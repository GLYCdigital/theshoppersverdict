If you've battled creeping bentgrass or crabgrass that shrugs off standard weed-and-feed products, you already know the frustration. Syngenta Tenacity isn't another me-too herbicide. It's the product lawn care professionals reach for when conventional options fail — and the 4.4-star rating across 220 Amazon reviews reflects that reputation. But it's also a product that requires patience, a sprayer, and a tolerance for watching your lawn look temporarily weird.

## What You Actually Get

The 8-ounce bottle is small, but it's concentrated. At the standard rate of 4 to 5 teaspoons per gallon of water, one bottle treats roughly 1 acre of established turf. You also get a measuring cap with clear markings — a small detail that matters when you're mixing chemicals. There's no surfactant included, so factor that into your cost. You'll want a non-ionic surfactant (about $10-15) for best results, especially on waxy-leaved weeds like clover.

## How It Performs in the Real World

Tenacity's active ingredient, mesotrione, works differently than most herbicides. It inhibits a specific enzyme pathway that weeds need to survive — and because established grass processes it differently, it targets the weed without torching your lawn. It's one of the few products you can apply at seeding time, which makes it uniquely valuable for new lawns or overseeding projects. You can prevent crabgrass AND kill existing broadleaf weeds in the same pass.

The tradeoff? The bleaching effect. Within 7-14 days, treated weeds turn white or pinkish before they die. That's normal — it's the herbicide working. But it also affects desirable grass in the treatment area. One reviewer noted: *"I panicked the first time I used this because my whole lawn looked white for about a week. It bounced back greener than before, but nobody warns you about that."* Multiple buyers echo this: the visual shock is real, but recovery is typically strong.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is that Tenacity solves problems other products can't. Crabgrass, bentgrass, nimblewill, and even tough perennials like wild violet — buyers report success where Roundup or standard broadleaf killers failed. As one verified purchaser put it: *"I tried everything on bentgrass for two years. Tenacity knocked it out in one season with two applications."* Several reviewers also note it's safe around established trees and shrubs, which broad-spectrum herbicides aren't.

## Where It Falls Short

This is not a quick fix. Expect to reapply after 2-3 weeks for heavy infestations. The bleaching effect is alarming if you haven't researched it — and the label doesn't emphasize it enough. Also, Tenacity is a selective herbicide, not a total vegetation killer. If you're clearing a patch of ground for a new garden bed, this is the wrong tool. And at roughly $60-70 per bottle, it's pricier than consumer-grade options. For a small lawn with minor weed pressure, you're overpaying.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The new-lawn builder**: Applying at seeding prevents weeds while your grass establishes — a huge advantage.
- **The crabgrass/bentgrass battler**: If you've tried pre-emergents and still get invasions, this is your next step.
- **The overseeder**: You can renovate thin lawns without waiting weeks between weed control and seeding.

**Skip it if** you have a small yard with basic dandelion pressure, or if you want a single-application spot treatment. You'll be frustrated by the cost and the wait.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does it take to see results?**
A: Weeds typically show whitening within 7-10 days and die within 3-4 weeks. Grassy weeds like crabgrass may need a second application at 2-3 weeks.

**Q: Can I reseed after applying Tenacity?**
A: Yes — this is its standout feature. You can apply it at seeding or immediately after, unlike most herbicides that require a 4-8 week wait.

**Q: Will it kill my good grass?**
A: Established turf may temporarily whiten but usually recovers within 1-2 weeks. Newly seeded grass is safe. However, Tenacity will harm bentgrass varieties, so check your lawn type first.
