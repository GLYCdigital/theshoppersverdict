Let’s talk about the one pool chemical most owners forget until it’s too late. You’ve balanced chlorine, shocked, and adjusted pH. But if your calcium hardness sits below 200 ppm, you’re not done. Low hardness makes water aggressive — it leaches calcium right out of your plaster, grout, and vinyl liner seams. That’s how you get etching and premature liner failure. Pool Mate’s 8-pound bucket is the fix, and with a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it’s clearly doing something right.

## What you actually get

A sealed white bucket holding 8 pounds of anhydrous calcium chloride — that’s the fast-dissolving form, not the slow granular stuff. The label gives you the key number: 1.4 pounds raises hardness by 10 ppm in 10,000 gallons. So this bucket treats roughly 57,000 gallons total, or about three full doses for a typical 18,000-gallon in-ground pool. No scoop included, so grab a measuring cup you don’t mind dedicating to pool duty.

## Real-world performance

I’ll cut to the chase: this stuff dissolves. Most hardness increasers sit on the pool floor like snow, leaving white clouds that take days to clear. Pool Mate breaks down in a few hours when broadcast over the deep end with the pump running. One buyer noted, *“Dissolved completely in about 3 hours with no residue on the steps or floor. My plaster pool finally stopped eating my pH.”* That’s the core job — and it does it cleanly.

The resealable lid matters more than you’d think. Calcium chloride is hygroscopic — it pulls moisture out of the air and turns into a brick. The gasket-style seal keeps the remaining product pourable into next season. Several reviewers mentioned storing it through winter and finding it still granular.

Buy a smaller container if that’s your setup.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do I add for my 15,000-gallon pool?**
A: To raise hardness by 20 ppm, you need about 2.1 pounds (roughly 4.5 cups if you’re measuring by volume). Test first — don’t guess.

**Q: Can I use this in a saltwater pool?**
A: Yes. Calcium hardness works independently of the salt chlorine generator. Just don’t add it near the salt cell intake while dissolving.

**Q: How long before I can swim after adding it?**
A: Wait at least 4 hours with the pump running, then retest. The product is safe once fully dissolved — no extended waiting period like some algaecides.
