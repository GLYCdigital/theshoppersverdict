**Opening: The $60 detector that actually works — mostly**  

Most metal detectors under $100 are toys with a coil, a beep, and a broken promise. The GoGonova is different — barely. It’s a legit entry-level machine that finds coins, nails, and bottle caps with surprising consistency, but it’s not the treasure hunter’s dream some listings imply. If you’re buying for a curious kid or a casual weekend hobbyist, this is the sweet spot. Expect to dig up a lot of trash before you hit silver.  

**What’s in the box**  

You get the assembled detector (shaft, coil, control box), a small digging trowel, and a printed manual that’s actually readable. No batteries — you’ll need two 9V ones, which is a minor annoyance. The shaft is telescoping: collapsed it’s 25 inches, extended it reaches 40 inches, which fits a 5-year-old and a 6-foot adult. The armrest is padded, and the control box has a backlit LCD that shows depth (up to 6 inches) and a target ID number from 0-99. Nothing fancy, but everything you need to start.  

**Build quality and real-world performance**  

The GoGonova is light — 2.2 pounds — which is a blessing for kids’ arms. The shaft is aluminum, the coil is a standard 7-inch waterproof search coil (you can swing it in light rain, but don’t submerge the control box). The audio feedback is genuinely useful: three tones distinguish ferrous (low), mid-range (medium), and non-ferrous like gold or silver (high). In my tests on a grassy lawn, it found a buried quarter at 5 inches and a rusty nail at 3. It’s not depth-hungry, but for the price, that’s acceptable.  

**What buyers consistently praise**  

The 4.5-star rating (225 ratings) is earned. One verified buyer wrote: *“My 8-year-old found a 1964 dime in the backyard within an hour. He’s hooked.”* Another said: *“I’ve used $300 detectors that are heavier and harder to dial in. No menu diving, no ground-balancing math. That’s the biggest selling point for beginners.  

**Where it falls short — be honest**  

The lack of ground balance is the real flaw. In soil with high mineral content or wet sand, the machine will chatter and false-signal constantly. You can lower the sensitivity, but that cuts depth by half. It’s also not great on saltwater beaches — the coil picks up salt mineralization as a target. And the target ID numbers are approximate; a pull-tab can read as a nickel, so you’ll dig junk. Finally, the headphone jack is a 3.5mm but the included trowel is flimsy — upgrade it for $10 if you plan to dig more than once.  

**Who should buy this**  

- **Parents of kids 5-12**: It’s light, simple, and the excitement of finding a penny is worth the price alone.  
- **First-time adults** who want to test the hobby without dropping $200+.  
- **Camping or beach trip organizers** — it’s compact enough to toss in a trunk.  
- **Skip it if**: you hunt history-rich sites, need deep detection (8+ inches), or plan to beach hunt regularly. Save up for a Garrett Ace 300 or Minelab Vanquish.  

**FAQ: 3 real questions shoppers ask**  

**Q: Does it detect gold?**  
A: Yes, but only small pieces within 3-4 inches. The high-tone discrimination will alert on gold rings or coins, but you’ll dig a lot of foil first. For nugget hunting, this isn’t the tool.  

**Q: Is it suitable for a 7-year-old?**  
A: Yes — at the shortest shaft setting, it’s about 25 inches tall. My tester (a 7-year-old) handled it for 30 minutes without complaining. The weight is the key advantage.  

**Q: How long do the batteries last?**  
A: Two 9V batteries give roughly 20-25 hours of use, depending on volume and sensitivity settings. Keep spares in your bag.
