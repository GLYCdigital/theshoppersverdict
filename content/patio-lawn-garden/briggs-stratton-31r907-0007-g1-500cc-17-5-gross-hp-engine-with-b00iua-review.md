**What you actually get**

Open the box and you'll find the complete engine: the 500cc overhead-valve block, a muffler, air filter assembly, 1.5-quart fuel tank, and a recoil start mechanism. The shaft is a standard 1-inch diameter by 3.19 inches long — that's the exact spec for hundreds of riding mowers and lawn tractors from MTD, Troy-Bilt, and Craftsman. If your old engine is a 31R907 or similar, this bolts on with no modifications.

The 3.44-inch stroke and 3.12-inch bore give you 17.5 gross HP at 3,600 RPM. That's enough grunt for a 42-inch deck cutting thick, damp grass. The cast iron bore liner and full-pressure lubrication (with a spin-on oil filter) are the heavy-duty details that separate this from cheaper engines.

**Real-world performance**

Owners consistently report it fires up and performs like a factory replacement. One verified buyer wrote: *"Put it on a 2008 Troy-Bilt pony. Bolted right up, started on the second pull, and it's been cutting for two seasons now."* Another noted: *"The power difference vs. my old 15.5 HP is noticeable — it doesn't bog down in tall grass."*

The overhead valve design runs cooler and cleaner than older L-head engines, which means less valve maintenance. The dual ball bearings on the crank shaft reduce vibration, so you'll feel less shake in the steering wheel at full throttle. Fuel consumption is typical for this class — roughly 0.5 gallons per hour under load, so the 1.5-gallon tank gets you about three hours of mowing.

**What buyers praise**

The dominant theme in reviews: **ease of installation.** Multiple users mention it's a direct swap. One review said: *"I'm not mechanically inclined. This took me an afternoon with basic wrenches. Everything lined up."* The cast iron bore gets consistent shout-outs for longevity — buyers expect 1,500+ hours of service. **Where it falls short**

Three honest complaints surface repeatedly. First, the recoil starter is stiff when cold — several buyers mention needing a firm, deliberate pull, and one user with a bad shoulder found it genuinely difficult. Second, there's no fuel shutoff valve, which means you'll want to drain the carburetor or run it dry before winter storage, or you risk gummed-up jets. Third, genuine Briggs parts (like the air filter and oil filter) cost 20-30% more than aftermarket — but they fit perfectly and last longer, so I consider that a fair trade.

One more note: this engine is **not** a zero-turn or pressure-washer unit. It's a vertical-shaft, horizontal-crank design strictly for ride-on mowers and tractors. If you need a horizontal shaft for a log splitter or go-kart, look elsewhere.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my 42-inch Craftsman mower?**  
A: Check your old engine's model number. If it starts with 31R9 (like 31R907), yes — this is the direct replacement. The 1-inch by 3.19-inch shaft fits most 42-inch deck spindles.

**Q: Does it come with a wiring harness or key switch?**  
A: No. You'll reuse your existing wiring and key switch. It has a standard 12V charging system with a flywheel magneto, but the plug is a simple two-wire connector.

**Q: How hard is the first start?**  
A: Prime the bulb 3-4 times, set the choke, and pull. Most buyers get it on the first or second pull. On cold days, expect a stiffer pull — that's the compression release working.
