## What You're Actually Getting

This is a standard roll of galvanized hexagonal wire mesh—the classic "chicken wire" pattern you've seen a thousand times. The galvanization matters here; it's a zinc coating that resists rust, and it's the main reason this wire can survive outdoor conditions for years rather than turning into a brittle, flaky mess after one wet season. It's flexible enough to bend around corners and wrap over frames without kinking, which is the primary reason chicken wire remains a go-to for so many backyard projects.

## Real-World Performance

I've seen this wire used for everything from reinforcing a raised garden bed against digging pests to enclosing a young orchard. The flexibility is genuinely useful—you can cut it with standard tin snips and shape it to fit odd spaces. The galvanized coating holds up well in direct sun and rain, which is where cheaper, non-galvanized options fail within months.

However, let's set expectations: this is *poultry* wire, not predator-proof fencing. The hexagonal mesh openings are small enough to keep chickens in and most rodents out, but a determined raccoon or a large dog will make short work of it. If you're protecting against serious predators, you'd need a welded wire or hardware cloth—this isn't that product.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires acknowledging the tradeoffs:

- **Sharp edges:** After cutting, the wire ends are genuinely sharp. Gloves aren't optional—they're mandatory. Multiple reviewers mention nicks and scratches.
- **Not predator-proof:** I've already touched on this, but it bears repeating. If your area has coyotes, bears, or determined raccoons, this wire will not stop them.
- **Roll can be unruly:** It arrives tightly coiled, and it will try to spring back to that shape while you work. You'll want a second person or some stakes to hold it down during installation.

## Three Questions Buyers Ask

**Q: Can I cut this with regular household scissors?**
A: You can, but it will dull them. Use tin snips or wire cutters—they're cheap and make the job faster and safer.

**Q: Will this rust?**
A: The galvanized coating resists rust, but it's not permanent. Scratches from cutting or installation can expose bare steel. Expect several years of outdoor life, not decades.

**Q: Is this safe for chicks?**
A: Yes, the mesh size keeps young chicks contained. Just ensure the openings aren't large enough for them to squeeze through—check the specific gauge you're purchasing.
