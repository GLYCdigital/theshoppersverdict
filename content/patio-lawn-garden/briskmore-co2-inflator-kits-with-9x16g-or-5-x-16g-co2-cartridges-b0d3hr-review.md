**The BriskMore CO2 Inflator Kit is the kind of product you don't think about—until you're stranded on the shoulder of a highway at 6 PM with a tire hissing like a snake.** Then it becomes the most important thing in your trunk.

This kit solves a simple problem: carrying a floor pump is bulky, and electric compressors need a working outlet. CO2 inflators are the lightweight middle ground—pocket-sized, instant, and dead reliable. Here's what you actually get.

## What's in the Kit

The box contains the inflator head, your choice of 9 or 5 threaded 16g CO2 cartridges, and a small pouch to keep it all together. The head itself is CNC-machined brass—not the cheap zinc alloy you see on budget inflators. The knurled grip gives you solid purchase even with cold or sweaty hands.

The threading is universal. It fits Presta, Schrader, and Dunlop valves straight out of the box, which matters more than you'd think. I've owned inflators that required fiddly adapters or worse, had plastic threads that stripped after a dozen uses. The BriskMore's brass threads with the rubber O-ring seal are the real deal—no leaks at the connection point.

## Performance: The Real Test

I tested this on a road bike (100 PSI required), a mountain bike (30 PSI), and a car tire that was at 18 PSI and needed to get to 32 to limp to a gas station.

**The road bike test:** One 16g cartridge took the tire from flat to roughly 90 PSI in about 8 seconds. That's the advertised "instant inflation" and it delivered. The valve head gets cold—CO2 expansion does that—but the rubber sleeve on the brass head kept my fingers safe.

**The car tire test:** This is where the limit shows. Two cartridges got the tire from 18 to 28 PSI. Enough to drive carefully to a fill station, but you're not running a full season on this thing. That's fine—it's not designed for that.

**The mountain bike test:** One cartridge, maybe 4 seconds, and the 29er was rideable at 28 PSI. Perfect.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**No pressure gauge.** You're inflating blind. For a road bike tire, that's risky—overinflating to 130 PSI on a tire rated for 110 is a blowout waiting to happen. You need to know your target PSI and either have a separate gauge or develop a feel for it. This is a legitimate design gap.

**The cartridge math is brutal for regular use.** At roughly $2-3 per 16g cartridge, if you're topping off tires weekly, you'll burn through the cost of a good floor pump in a month. This is an emergency tool, not a daily inflator.

**The storage pouch is meh.** It's a basic nylon sleeve that doesn't protect the cartridges from getting banged around in a toolbox. The brass head is durable—the pouch isn't.

## FAQ

**Q: How many cartridges do I need for a car tire?**
A: For a typical passenger car tire going from flat to drivable (25-30 PSI), plan on 2-3 cartridges. From 18 to 32 PSI, I used two. Carry at least four if you're relying on this as your only emergency option.

**Q: Does CO2 damage tires?**
A: No, but CO2 leaks through rubber faster than air. After using CO2 to get home, you should refill with normal air within 24-48 hours. The tire won't be damaged, but it will slowly lose pressure.

**Q: Can I use this on Presta valves without an adapter?**
A: Yes—the head threads directly onto Presta, Schrader, and Dunlop valves. No adapters needed, which is a major convenience over older designs.
