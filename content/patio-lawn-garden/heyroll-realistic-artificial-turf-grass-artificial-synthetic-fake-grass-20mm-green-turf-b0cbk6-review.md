**The Short Version:** If you've been staring at a dead patch of dirt or a sad concrete balcony and thinking "I could fake it," the Heyroll 20mm turf is one of the better budget-friendly options I've tested. It's not golf-course perfect, but it's convincingly real from a normal viewing distance — and that's the whole point.

## What You Actually Get

The Heyroll arrives as a tightly wound roll of 20mm pile turf. The box contains exactly that — no adhesive, no seam tape, no accessories. You're paying for the grass itself. The blade structure uses a mix of darker and lighter green polyethylene fibers, with a brown thatch layer woven through the base. That thatch layer is the secret sauce; it's what kills the "plastic rug" look that cheaper turfs scream.

Cut with a sharp utility knife, it slices cleanly without the fibers pulling loose from the backing. I'd budget for a sharp blade — a dull one will fray the edges and make seams visible.

## Real-World Performance

I installed this on a rental patio (concrete base) and a small backyard dog run. The 20mm pile height hits a sweet spot: tall enough to feel like real grass underfoot, short enough that it doesn't flop over and look matted after a week of foot traffic.

**The good:** The color is genuinely convincing. It's not neon green or cartoonishly dark. The tan thatch peeking through gives it that "dormant grass" depth. One buyer noted, *"My neighbor asked if I'd resodded the lawn — didn't believe it was fake until she touched it."* That tracks with my experience.

**The drainage issue:** This is the big one. The backing is solid — no perforations. On soil or sand, water drains through the gaps around the fibers. On concrete or a solid deck, you'll get standing puddles after rain. If your installation surface is sealed, you need to address drainage or expect pooling.

**The crease problem:** It ships folded, and those fold lines are stubborn. Expect to lay it flat in the sun for a day or two before installation. One reviewer complained, *"The creases never fully flattened out in the corners."* I found that leaving it weighted overnight in warm conditions solved it, but if you're installing in winter, budget extra time.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the drainage and crease issues, the turf is **thin on cushioning**. At 20mm with a lightweight backing, it's fine for walking but not for kids doing gymnastics or pets digging. One reviewer noted, *"My dog was able to dig at the edges and lift it up."* You'll want to secure the perimeter with staples or adhesive if you have a digger.

The **color fades slightly** in direct, all-day sun. Not dramatically, but after two months in Arizona heat, the darker blades lost some richness. Shaded installations won't see this.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters** wanting a non-destructive patio or balcony upgrade — it rolls up and moves with you
- **Pet owners** with a muddy yard patch who want a clean, low-maintenance fix
- **DIY landscapers** covering small to medium areas (under ~200 sq ft) who want a natural look without the cost of premium turf

**Skip it if** you're installing over a sealed surface without drainage planning, or if you need a plush, cushioned surface for kids' play areas. For that, look at taller pile (30mm+) with foam backing.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I install this directly on dirt?**
A: Yes, but do prep work first. Remove weeds, level the soil, and compact it. The turf will lay better and last longer. Consider landscape fabric underneath to block weed growth.

**Q: Will it survive a dog's claws and urine?**
A: Generally, yes. The polyethylene fibers hold up well to scratching. Urine drains through on permeable surfaces — just hose it down occasionally. On concrete, it'll pool, so rinse frequently or add drainage.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
A: For light debris, a leaf blower works. For deeper cleaning, a garden hose and mild soap solution — rinse thoroughly. A stiff broom against the grain fluffs the fibers back up every few weeks.

**Skip it if** you need cushioned play surface, have a persistent digger, or are installing over sealed concrete without a drainage solution.
