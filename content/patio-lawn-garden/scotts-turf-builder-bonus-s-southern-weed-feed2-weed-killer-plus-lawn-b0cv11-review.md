# Scotts Turf Builder Bonus S Southern Weed & Feed2, Weed Kill

If you've got St. Augustine, centipede, or Zoysia grass in your yard, you already know the struggle: most weed killers will torch your lawn right alongside the weeds. Scotts built this product specifically for Southern grass types, and that focus shows — but it comes with a narrow set of rules you need to follow.

## What's Actually in the Bag

The 14.5 lb bag covers up to 5,000 square feet. That's roughly a quarter-acre lot with some room to spare for touch-ups. The analysis is 29-0-10 — heavy on nitrogen for that deep green push, zero phosphorus, and a solid dose of potassium for root health. There's also 10% iron in the mix, which is what gives Southern lawns that dark, almost blue-green color within a week or two.

The "Weed & Feed2" branding means this is the second iteration of the formula — it uses a more targeted herbicide blend than the original Bonus S. It's designed to hit broadleaf weeds common in the South: dollar weed, clover, wild violet, chickweed, and henbit.

## How It Performs in Real Lawns

The key thing to understand: this is a post-emergent product. It kills weeds already growing in your lawn — it won't prevent new ones from sprouting. Apply it when weeds are actively growing and temperatures are between 60-90°F, which in the South means spring or early fall.

Application details matter enormously here. The bag specifies different spreader settings depending on your equipment — 5.5 for Scotts EdgeGuard, 7.5 for basic drop spreaders. Use a broadcast spreader at half that setting and do two passes in opposite directions for even coverage. Skip this and you'll get visible stripes — green where the granules landed, yellow where they didn't.

The iron does double duty: it greens up the grass AND makes the weeds more susceptible to the herbicide by stressing them. You'll notice weeds curling and yellowing within 7-10 days, with full die-off in 2-3 weeks. That's slower than a spray-on herbicide, but the fertilizing effect makes the wait worthwhile.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint in the reviews: misapplication damage. People who used a broadcast spreader at too-high settings, applied in temperatures above 90°F, or ignored the "water-in" requirement ended up with yellow patches. That's not a defective product — that's a user error issue — but it's worth flagging.

The grass-type restriction is a real limitation. If you have Bermuda or Bahia, this will damage your lawn. The bag says it clearly, but people still buy it without checking. Also, the weed control is selective — it won't touch grassy weeds like crabgrass. If that's your problem, you need a pre-emergent, not this.

You also can't use this on newly seeded or sodded lawns until the grass is established — typically 4-6 weeks after installation.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have St. Augustine, centipede, or Zoysia grass in the South — specifically zones 7-10 — and you're fighting broadleaf weeds like dollar weed, clover, or wild violet. You want a single-product solution that feeds while it kills, and you're willing to follow the spreader settings precisely.

**Skip it if:** You have Bermuda or Bahia grass, you need pre-emergent crabgrass control, or you're not confident calibrating a drop spreader. Also skip it if you want immediate weed die-off — this takes 2-3 weeks to fully work.

## FAQ

**Q: When should I apply this?**
Apply in spring when daytime temps are consistently 60-90°F and weeds are actively growing. In the deep South, that's typically March-April. A fall application around September-October also works well.

**Q: Do I need to water it in?**
Yes — but not immediately. Wait 24 hours after application, then water lightly with about ¼ inch. If rain is forecast within 24 hours, wait for a dry window. Watering too soon washes the granules into the soil before the herbicide has a chance to work on the weeds.

**Q: Will this kill crabgrass?**
No. This targets broadleaf weeds only. For crabgrass, you need a pre-emergent applied in late winter or early spring before soil temps hit 55°F.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you've got a Southern grass type and broadleaf weeds you're tired of fighting. It's a smart two-in-one that saves you an application pass and delivers visible greening. **Skip it** if you're not careful with spreader calibration or you need something for Bermuda lawns.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the narrow application window and the user-error risk that comes with granular application. But for the right lawn, this is the product that finally wins the weed war.
