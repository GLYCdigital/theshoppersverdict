Let me be direct: this is glyphosate. The same active ingredient in Roundup, at nearly double the concentration, for a fraction of the price. If you know what you're doing, that's a win. If you don't, you're about to nuke your flower bed.

**What you actually get**

The 16-ounce bottle arrives with a child-resistant cap that doubles as a measuring cup — marks on the side let you mix 1-ounce batches without hauling out a separate beaker. The concentrate is 41% glyphosate isopropylamine salt, which is the maximum strength available to home users. One ounce treats roughly 2 gallons of water, so a single bottle stretches to 32 gallons of finished spray. That's enough to cover about 8,000 square feet of established weeds, depending on how heavy you spray.

**How it performs in the real world**

This is not a contact killer. Glyphosate works systemically — the plant absorbs it through the leaves, and it travels down to the roots over the course of a week or two. You'll see yellowing in 4–7 days on annual weeds, but established perennials like poison ivy or Canadian thistle can take a full 14 days to show complete dieback. That's normal. The temptation is to re-spray when you don't see instant results, which wastes product and can actually slow the process down.

The concentration matters. Many generic herbicides sit at 18% or 20% glyphosate. This one is 41%, which means you use less per gallon and get better penetration on mature weeds. For brushy, woody growth or grass that's been established for years, that extra strength makes a noticeable difference.

**What buyers consistently say**

The Amazon reviews at 4.4 stars out of 220 ratings tell a consistent story. One verified purchaser put it plainly: *"Same active ingredient as the big name brand, half the price. Kills everything I spray it on within two weeks."* Another noted the value angle: *"I've bought Roundup for years. This is literally the same chemical. Wish I'd switched sooner."*

But the complaints are just as telling. Several reviewers mention accidental damage to desirable plants — one wrote, *"Sprayed on a windy day, and my hostas curled up and died. My fault, but I didn't realize how potent this is."* That's the tradeoff with any non-selective herbicide: it doesn't discriminate.

**Where it falls short**

First, this is not for lawn spot-treatment. If you want to kill dandelions in your fescue without killing the fescue, you need a selective broadleaf herbicide — this will leave brown patches. Second, the measuring cap is accurate but small; if you're mixing a full 32-gallon tank, you'll be filling that cap 16 times. For large properties, buy the larger jug instead. Third, the concentration means you must mix carefully. Getting the ratio wrong on the strong side can cause runoff damage to nearby plants.

**Who should buy this**

- **Budget-conscious gardeners** who want Roundup-level results without the brand markup
- **Property owners battling brush, poison ivy, or invasive weeds** along fences and driveways where precision doesn't matter
- **Anyone who already owns a pump sprayer** — mixing concentrate is far cheaper than buying ready-to-use formulas

Skip it if you're a casual gardener who wants a safe, targeted weed control for a lawn you care about. You need a selective product for that.

**Three questions shoppers actually ask**

*How long until I see results?* Annual weeds yellow in 4–7 days; perennials take 10–14 days. Don't reapply before two weeks — you'll just waste concentrate.

*Is it safe around pets and kids?* Once dry, glyphosate binds to soil and has low toxicity to mammals. But keep pets off treated foliage until it's completely dry, and store the concentrate out of reach.

*Can I use this in a garden?* Yes, but only for spot-treating weeds before planting or between rows on a calm day. It kills everything — cover or shield desirable plants.

**The verdict**

Compare-N-Save 41% glyphosate delivers exactly what it promises: industrial-strength weed control at a working-person's price. It's not fancy, it's not selective, and it demands respect in handling. But for clearing driveways, fence lines, and overgrown lots, it's the best value per ounce on the market.

**Buy it if** you want maximum weed-killing power per dollar and already understand basic herbicide safety.  
**Skip it if** you're a casual gardener looking for a gentle, selective solution — you'll burn more than weeds.
