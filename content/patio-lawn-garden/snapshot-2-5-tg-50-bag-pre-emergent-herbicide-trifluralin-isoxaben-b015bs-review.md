Snapshot 2.5 TG is a granular pre-emergent herbicide from Quali-Pro, the professional-grade arm of Control Solutions. The "2.5" refers to the active ingredient concentration — 2.5% isoxaben plus 12.5% trifluralin. That combination is what separates this from the cheap stuff at big box stores.

## What's Actually in the Bag

The 50-pound bag covers up to 20,000 square feet at the standard rate of 2.3 pounds per 1,000 square feet. That's the headline number, but here's what the label doesn't scream about: this product targets over 90 broadleaf and grassy weeds, including crabgrass, foxtail, henbit, chickweed, and oxalis. The trifluralin handles the grassy weeds while isoxaben gets the broadleaves — a one-two punch you rarely find in a single granular product.

The granules are a fine, sand-like texture that spreads evenly with a broadcast spreader. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward white and green bag with clear application rate instructions on the back. Nothing fancy, but the label is actually readable, which is more than I can say for some competitors.

## Real-World Performance

The chemistry works — if you time it right. Snapshot needs to go down before soil temperatures hit 55°F in spring, which is when weed seeds start germinating. Apply too late and you've wasted your money. Apply correctly and you'll see a visible weed barrier that lasts 6-8 months, which is significantly longer than most pre-emergents' 3-4 month window.

## Where It Falls Short

The watering-in requirement is non-negotiable. You need 0.5 inches of irrigation or rainfall within 24 hours of application. Miss that window and the granules just sit on the soil surface doing nothing. In dry climates or during drought restrictions, this can be a dealbreaker.

The dust factor is real. The fine granules kick up easily during spreading, and several reviewers mention needing a mask. One user wrote, *"Great product but wear a respirator. I coughed for two days after spreading without one."* That's fair warning.

Also, this is strictly pre-emergent. If you already have crabgrass or henbit growing, Snapshot won't touch it. You'll need a post-emergent product first, then apply Snapshot for future prevention.

## Common Questions

**When should I apply this?**
Late winter to early spring, before soil temps hit 55°F. For most of the South, that's February-March. For northern states, April-May. A second application in fall (September-October) for winter annuals is recommended.

**Can I overseed after using Snapshot?**
No. Trifluralin inhibits grass seed germination too. You need to wait 12-16 weeks after application before overseeding.

**Is it safe for vegetable gardens?**
The label says no. This is for ornamental lawns and landscapes only. Use a different product for edible beds.
