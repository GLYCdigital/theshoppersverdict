You've probably been burned by cheap feeders before — the ones that crack after one frost, leak in the rain, or collapse when a fat squirrel decides they're a snack bar. ## What You Actually Get

The box includes the feeder (pre-assembled in two main pieces), a sturdy hanging hook with a weather-resistant rope, and the Allen wrench needed for final assembly. The feeder itself measures roughly 12 inches tall with a 7-inch diameter seed tray. The build is powder-coated steel with decorative scrollwork — it's genuinely attractive and won't look like a tin can in your garden.

The design has a clear seed chamber (so you can see when it's empty), four feeding ports, and a perch ring that encircles the whole feeder. That ring is smart — it lets multiple birds feed at once without fighting for a single perch.

## Real-World Performance

The metal construction is the headline feature here. Unlike plastic feeders that yellow and become brittle in UV light, the IWNTWY's powder coating holds up. One buyer noted, "**Survived an Iowa winter and two hail storms without a scratch. My old plastic feeder was trash after one season.** " The drainage holes in the tray are a thoughtful touch — they prevent the seed soup problem that plagues many feeders.

The clear reservoir holds roughly two cups of seed. That's moderate — enough for a day or two with active birds, but you'll be refilling more often than with a hopper-style feeder. The four ports are generously sized, so cardinals and blue jays can access the seed comfortably, not just finches and chickadees.

The perch ring is the standout feature. It wraps completely around the feeder, giving birds a 360-degree landing zone. This reduces crowding and lets smaller birds escape quickly if a bully shows up.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 215 ratings, a few themes emerge. Durability is the big one — buyers repeatedly mention the feeder surviving storms, heat waves, and falls. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight: this is not truly squirrel-proof. The metal construction and perch design deter many squirrels, but there's no weight-activated mechanism to close the ports. A determined squirrel can still hang upside down and scoop seed. If squirrels are a serious problem in your yard, you'll need a baffle or a separate squirrel-proof feeder.

Assembly is straightforward but not tool-free. You'll use the included Allen wrench to attach the tray to the body, and the small screws can be frustrating on a cold morning. The hanging rope is functional but not premium — you may want to replace it with wire for extra security.

One buyer flagged a quality-control issue: "**The seams on my unit weren't perfectly flush, but it doesn't affect function.** " Worth noting, but not a dealbreaker at this price point.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you want a feeder that looks good, survives real weather, and gives multiple birds space to eat. It's ideal for casual birders who want a set-and-forget feeder that won't fall apart in 18 months.

**Skip it if** you're fighting a serious squirrel infestation — you need a weight-activated design — or if you want a high-capacity feeder for a busy yard that goes through seed fast.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this feeder really squirrel-proof?**
A: It's squirrel-resistant, not proof. The all-metal build and perch design deter casual climbers, but the seed ports don't close under weight. Pair it with a baffle for serious squirrel problems.

**Q: Does the powder coating peel or chip?**
A: Most buyers report no issues after a full season. One reviewer mentioned minor chips after a fall from a tree, but that's an edge case. The coating is baked on, not painted.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
A: The tray detaches with the Allen wrench for easy washing. The metal body can be wiped down with mild soap and water. Drainage holes prevent most mold buildup.
