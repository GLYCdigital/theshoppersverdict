**Review Body:**

Let's cut through the noise: if you're fighting powdery mildew on your roses, rust on your lawn, or anthracnose on your shade trees, you need a systemic fungicide — not a contact spray that washes off with the next rain. Atticus Gravex 20EW delivers that with 19.7% myclobutanil, the same active ingredient found in premium brands like Eagle 20EW, at a fraction of the per-ounce cost. The question is whether the savings come with compromises. After digging through the formulation, the math, and 225 verified buyer experiences, here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The 16 oz bottle contains a concentrated emulsifiable liquid. The label rate for most ornamental and turf applications is 0.5 fl oz per 25 gallons of water — that's incredibly concentrated. One bottle yields roughly 800 gallons of finished spray solution. For context, a typical homeowner with a 2-gallon pump sprayer will get an entire season's worth of coverage from a single bottle. The product label covers ornamentals, turf, fruit trees, and even some vegetable crops, making it one of the broader-spectrum homeowner fungicides available.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

Myclobutanil is a demethylation inhibitor (DMI) fungicide — it stops fungal sterol production, effectively halting spore germination and mycelial growth. Unlike contact fungicides like chlorothalonil that only protect what they touch, Gravex is absorbed into the plant's vascular system and translocates upward to new growth. That's why the label allows a 14-day application interval for most diseases rather than the 7–10 days typical of protectants.

Buyers consistently report visible results within 3–5 days on active powdery mildew. One reviewer noted, *"My crepe myrtles were white with mildew. Two applications, 10 days apart, and the new growth was completely clean."* Another user treating a 5,000 sq ft lawn for rust and dollar spot confirmed the coverage math: *"Half an ounce per 25 gallons sounds tiny, but it absolutely works. I mixed 2 oz for a 100-gallon tank and my fescue came back green in two weeks."*

The product mixes readily in water with moderate agitation, though it's an emulsifiable concentrate — it will separate if left in the tank overnight. Plan to mix fresh and use within a few hours.

**Where It Falls Short**

The concentration is both a blessing and a curse. Measuring 0.5 fl oz accurately without a graduated syringe is genuinely difficult — a tablespoon is 0.5 fl oz, but you'll often need less than that for small sprayers. A 1-gallon sprayer needs just 0.02 fl oz (about 0.6 mL), which is nearly impossible to measure with kitchen tools. Buy a 1 mL oral syringe before your first application.

Also, this is a synthetic fungicide. If you're managing an organic garden or selling produce as "organic," this isn't compliant. It's also worth noting that DMI fungicides carry a moderate resistance risk — rotating with a different mode of action (like chlorothalonil or sulfur) every few applications is smart practice, especially if you're treating the same plants season after season.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Homeowners with established ornamentals** — roses, crepe myrtles, phlox, and other mildew-prone plants. One bottle covers years of treatment.
- **Lawn enthusiasts fighting rust, dollar spot, or leaf spot** on fescue, bluegrass, or bermuda. The 25-gallon rate makes it economical for large turf areas.
- **Fruit tree growers** dealing with scab or powdery mildew on apples, pears, and peaches. Check the pre-harvest interval (typically 14 days) on the label.

**Skip It If**

You're strictly organic, you need an instant knock-down on heavy existing infections (this works best as a preventive or early-treatment product), or you're looking for a ready-to-spray hose-end product — this requires mixing and measuring.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is Atticus Gravex 20EW the same as Eagle 20EW?**
A: Yes, in active ingredient and concentration — both are 19.7% myclobutanil emulsifiable concentrate. The difference is price and labeling. Atticus is a generic equivalent, often 30–50% cheaper per ounce.

**Q: Can I use this on vegetables?**
A: The label lists several vegetable crops including cucumbers, squash, and tomatoes for certain diseases. Always verify the specific crop and disease match on the label before application, and respect the pre-harvest interval.

**Q: How long does it protect after application?**
A: The systemic activity provides 10–14 days of protection on new growth under normal conditions. Heavy rain doesn't wash it off, but it's not rain-fast immediately — allow 2–4 hours to dry.
