Here's the thing about log tongs: most are cheap stamped steel that bend the first time you pick up a wet oak round. The VEVOR Log Lifting Tongs are different. They're built from forged steel with a 22-inch jaw opening, and they actually work as advertised. After 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this is one of those rare tools that earned its reputation.

## What You Actually Get

The package is simple: one pair of tongs, no assembly, no instructions needed. The design is immediately obvious — two curved steel jaws with serrated teeth, connected at a spring-loaded hinge. The handles extend to roughly 36 inches total length, giving you enough leverage to lift a 100-pound log without bending your back. The jaws open to 22 inches, which handles everything from kindling-sized splits up to full rounds from a 16-inch diameter tree.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these on a mixed pile of seasoned oak, fresh-cut maple, and some gnarly pine rounds. The spring-loaded mechanism is the standout feature. You squeeze the handles to open the jaws, position them over the log, and release — the spring snaps them shut with enough force to bite into bark and hold firm. The serrated teeth dig in about 1/4 inch on hardwood, which is plenty for a secure grip.

The 110-pound rating isn't marketing fluff. I lifted a 90-pound oak round without any flex in the hinge block. The forged steel construction means the jaws don't spread or twist under load. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "These things are beasts. Grabbed a soaked log that must have weighed 80 pounds and carried it across the yard without any slipping."

Releasing is just as easy — set the log down and the spring tension releases the jaws automatically. No fumbling with latches or triggers.

## Where It Falls Short

The handles are bare metal. In winter, that means cold hands even with gloves, and wet gloves slip on the smooth surface. A rubber grip sleeve would fix this, but VEVOR didn't include one. You can add aftermarket bicycle handlebar grips for a few bucks, but you shouldn't have to.

The sharp jaw points are aggressive. On softwoods like pine or cedar, they'll leave visible dents if you clamp too hard. For firewood that's going to be burned anyway, this doesn't matter. If you're moving lumber you plan to mill or sell, you'll want to be more careful.

At 4.8 pounds, these aren't ultralight. For carrying a single log a short distance, it's fine. For hauling armloads across a large yard, your arms will feel it.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Firewood users** who split their own wood and need to move rounds from the splitting block to a cart or stack
- **Landscapers and property owners** dealing with storm downed branches and small trees
- **People with back issues** who need to lift heavy objects without bending — the 36-inch handles keep you upright
- **Anyone tired of wrestling logs bare-handed** or using a weak grabber tool that can't handle real weight

Skip these if you only move small kindling or pre-split firewood under 20 pounds — a basic pair of $15 tongs will do the job. Also skip if you need to carry logs more than 50 feet regularly; a log cart is the better investment.

## FAQ

**Can I use these with one hand?**
Yes. The spring-loaded mechanism lets you open the jaws with one squeeze, position over the log, and release. You'll want two hands for heavier logs, but one-handed operation works for anything under 40 pounds.

**Will they scratch my concrete or patio?**
The jaw tips are sharp steel — they'll leave marks on concrete if you drag the log. The handles themselves won't scratch, but the jaws will. Lift, don't drag.

**What's the maximum log size?**
The 22-inch jaw opening handles logs up to about 20 inches in diameter comfortably. For anything larger, you're better off splitting it first.
