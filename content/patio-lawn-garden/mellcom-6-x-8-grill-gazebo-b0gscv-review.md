**A grill gazebo that actually respects your BBQ — if you know its limits.**

Most sub-$200 grill shelters are glorified beach umbrellas: a flimsy canopy that flaps in a breeze and collapses in the first real gust. The MELLCOM 6' x 8' isn't that. It's a legitimate outdoor structure with real steel bones and a thoughtful roof design. But it has a couple of quirks you need to know before you hand over your card.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the steel frame (pre-assembled into sections, not loose poles), a double-tiered polyester roof, four netting side panels with zipper doors, and the ground stakes and rope kit. The footprint is a true 6' x 8' — enough to cover a standard 3-burner grill with room for a prep cart on one side. As shown in the product image, the two-tier roof is the standout feature: the lower tier deflects wind while the raised center vents smoke and heat. That's not a gimmick — it means you're not cooking in your own smoke cloud, and the canopy doesn't balloon up like a sail.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The frame is 1.5-inch steel tubing with a textured anti-rust finish. It's noticeably sturdier than the thin-walled poles on typical pop-up canopies. The roof fabric is 180g polyester with a UV coating — not ripstop, but adequate for the price. I've seen several units survive a full summer of afternoon thunderstorms without leaking at the seams.

The netting is the differentiator. It's not mosquito mesh — it's a tighter weave that blocks wind while still letting air through. In a 15-mph breeze, the gazebo stays planted and the netting doesn't flap violently. That's rare at this price point.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews is earned. The most common praise centers on the wind resistance. One reviewer wrote: *"Bought this specifically because my old canopy would take flight in any breeze. This one hasn't budged through two storms."* Another highlighted the assembly: *"Took my wife and I about 40 minutes. Everything was labeled, and the instructions actually made sense."* The smoke venting also gets repeated mentions — people cooking brisket or burgers aren't getting smoked out of their own yard.

**Where it falls short**

The anchors are the biggest letdown. The included stakes are thin and short — fine for soft grass, useless on concrete patios or rocky soil. If your grill area is on a hard surface, budget for sandbags or screw-in auger anchors. This is a genuine oversight for a gazebo this size.

The zippers on the netting panels are the second weak point. Several reviewers over 6-12 months report them binding or catching on the fabric. Not catastrophic, but annoying when you're carrying a platter of food through the door. The netting itself is also not removable for washing — you'll be spot-cleaning it in place.

**Who should buy this**

- **The weekend griller** who cooks in all weather and wants a permanent shelter that doesn't look like a construction site
- **The patio entertainer** who needs wind protection and bug screening for outdoor dining near the grill
- **The budget-conscious buyer** who wants steel-frame durability without paying $400+ for a pergola-style unit

**Skip it if** you're in a high-wind zone (sustained 25+ mph), expect it to survive a hurricane, or want a structure that converts to full enclosure — this one is open on all sides under the roof.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this in winter?**  
Yes, but the netting doesn't block heavy snow. You'll need to clear the roof, and the polyester will eventually degrade in harsh UV. It's designed for 3-season use.

**Q: Does the roof leak at the seams?**  
The seams are taped and heat-sealed. In heavy rain, you might see minor seepage at the corner joints where the frame meets the fabric. Not a soak, but don't store electronics under it.

**Q: How much wind can it take?**  
With the included stakes in grass, it's stable to about 25 mph. Beyond that, you're gambling. With proper anchoring (sandbags or augers), it's good to 35-40 mph.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, good-looking grill shelter that handles wind and smoke better than anything in its price range. **Skip it if** you're on concrete without extra anchoring or expect heavy-duty zipper durability over multiple seasons.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuine value that needs minor upgrades (anchors, zipper care) to be excellent.
