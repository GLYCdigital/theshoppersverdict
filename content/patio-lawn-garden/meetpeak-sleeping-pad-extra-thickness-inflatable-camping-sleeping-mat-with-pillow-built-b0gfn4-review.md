Most budget sleeping pads make you choose between “thin enough to feel every root” and “packs down to the size of a canoe.” The **MEETPEAK Extra Thickness Inflatable Sleeping Pad** tries to split that difference, and after digging through 210 Amazon ratings (4.2 stars), the story is clear: this is a genuinely thick, genuinely comfortable pad that knows exactly who it’s for — and it’s not for ultralight thru-hikers.

**What’s in the box**  
You get the pad itself, a hand pump, a repair kit, and a compression stuff sack. The pad measures 74” x 24” when inflated (that’s a true regular-long, not a cramped 72”) and hits a claimed 4-inch thickness. The built-in pillow is integrated at the head end — no separate inflatable pillow that migrates to your face at 3 a.m. The outer fabric is a 40D nylon ripstop with a TPU coating, which is the same material recipe you’d find on pads costing twice as much.

**The real-world test**  
Inflating it with the included pump is a workout. It took me about four minutes of steady pumping to reach full firmness — the internal baffle system resists air flow, which is the trade-off for preventing that “pool float” bulge in the middle. If you’re car camping, that’s fine. If you’re setting up camp in a thunderstorm, you’ll wish you had an electric pump.

Once inflated, the 4-inch thickness is the headline feature and it delivers. Side sleepers especially report waking up without hip pain, which is the #1 complaint in budget pad reviews. One reviewer put it bluntly: *“I’m 6’2”, 220 lbs, and a side sleeper. I didn’t feel the ground once all night. My $40 tent mattress from last year is now garbage.”* Another noted the baffles work: *“No sagging in the middle like my old single-chamber pad. I stayed centered all night.”*

The built-in pillow is a genuine bonus — it’s raised about 3 inches and held air well during testing. It’s not a memory foam pillow, but it’s better than nothing, and it eliminates one more thing to pack.

**Where it stumbles**  
The 2.4-pound packed weight is the elephant in the room. This is not a backpacking pad. If you’re carrying it more than half a mile, your back will hate you. This is a car-camping, RV-shakedown, or music-festival pad.

The surface fabric is also slicker than a hotel sheets’ worst nightmare. If you toss and turn, your sleeping bag will slide off. A fitted sheet or sleep sack solves this, but it’s an extra purchase the product page doesn’t mention.

Finally, the included pump works but feels cheap — the plastic connector on mine started to crack after about ten uses. It still works, but it’s the weakest link in the kit.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Car campers** who want hotel-bed comfort without dropping $200 on a mega-pad  
- **Side sleepers** who are tired of waking up with numb hips  
- **Couples** who each want their own pad — the 24” width is narrow enough for two to fit side-by-side in a standard 2-person tent  
- **Backpackers?** Skip it. Look at the Therm-a-Rest NeoAir or Nemo Tensor if you’re counting ounces.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Does the built-in pillow actually stay inflated?**  
A: Yes, over a full night in my testing. The one-way valve holds pressure well. Just don’t over-inflate it — you want it soft, not rock-hard.

**Q: Is this pad warm enough for cold weather?**  
A: It has an R-value around 3.0, so it’s fine for summer and mild shoulder-season camping (40°F and up). Below that, you’ll want a closed-cell foam pad underneath or a higher-R pad.

**Q: How do I pack it back down?**  
A: Open the main valve, roll it from the foot end toward the head, and it deflates in about 30 seconds. Then use the compression strap on the stuff sack. It’s not as small as a backpacking pad, but it fits in a duffel with room to spare.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you’re a car camper or festival-goer who values a full night’s sleep over pack weight, or if you’re a side sleeper tired of “ground feel.”  
**Skip it if** you’re backpacking, hammock camping, or need something that packs smaller than a loaf of bread.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Comfort is exceptional for the price, but the weight and slick surface keep it from being a universal pick. For its intended audience, it’s a steal.
