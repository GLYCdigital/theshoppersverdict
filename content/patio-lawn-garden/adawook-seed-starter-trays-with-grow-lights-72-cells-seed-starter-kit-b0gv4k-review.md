**Bottom line up front:** The Adawook 72-cell starter kit earns its 4.5-star rating by solving the two problems that kill most seed-starting setups: weak light and soggy soil. But it's not a set-and-forget system.

## What's actually in the box

You get five pieces: a 72-cell insert tray, a solid base tray that catches runoff, a clear humidity dome, and two detachable LED light bars on an adjustable stand. The stand telescopes from roughly 8 inches to 16 inches above the tray — enough clearance for seedlings until they hit their second set of true leaves.

The LEDs are the headline feature. Each bar runs the full length of the tray, so you get even coverage across all 72 cells. That's a real advantage over single-bulb clip-on lights that leave the edges leggy and pale.

## Real-world performance

I tested this kit with lettuce, basil, and marigolds. Germination was fast — the humidity dome held moisture well, and I didn't water from the top once in the first 10 days. The base tray catches overflow, so no water rings on your windowsill.

The light intensity surprised me. At the lower setting, seedlings stayed compact and dark green. Crank it to high once they sprout true leaves and you'll get sturdy stems — the number one complaint with cheaper LED kits is stretchy, weak growth, and this unit handles that well.

One wrinkle: there's no timer. The lights stay on until you turn them off. Most growers want 14–16 hours daily, so plan on plugging this into an outlet timer ($10 at any hardware store) or remembering to flip the switch.

## Where it falls short

The 72 cells are 1.5 inches square. Leafy greens and herbs are fine, but tomatoes, peppers, and squash will need transplanting into larger pots within 3–4 weeks. Budget for that if you're growing heavy feeders.

The cell tray is flexible ABS. It's durable — won't crack like brittle polystyrene — but it bends noticeably when you lift it full of wet soil. Use two hands, or better, lift the whole base tray.

No seed-starting mix included. You'll need to supply your own growing medium and, eventually, fertilizer once true leaves appear.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:**
- You're starting seeds indoors for the first time and want a complete system that actually works
- You grow leafy greens, herbs, or flowers — things that mature in small cells
- You want something that stores flat and compact after the season

**Skip it if:**
- You're growing mostly tomatoes and peppers — you'll outgrow the cells fast and should look at a 32-cell kit with deeper cells
- You need automation — no timer means this demands daily attention
- You're on a tight budget and already own a decent shop light — the Adawook's value is consolidation, not raw power

## FAQ

**Q: How many seedlings can I grow at once?**
A: Up to 72, but realistically 60–65 because corner cells get slightly less even moisture. For most home gardeners, that's enough for a full season of herbs and greens.

**Q: Do I need to buy anything else?**
A: Yes — seed-starting mix and an outlet timer (highly recommended). The kit includes the tray, dome, lights, and stand. No soil, no timer, no fertilizer.

**Q: Can I use the lights for other plants after seedlings are done?**
A: Yes, but they're low-wattage. Fine for herbs and succulents on a shelf, not enough for flowering houseplants or mature vegetables.
