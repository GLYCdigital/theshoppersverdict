A hammock that claims "waterproof" usually means one of two things: a cheap vinyl tarp that turns into a sauna, or a fabric that technically repels water but falls apart after one season. The Gafete Double Hammock with Stand sits somewhere in the middle — and for most buyers, that's exactly the sweet spot.

I've tested this unit for three weeks in my backyard, through two rainstorms and a stretch of 90-degree heat. Here's what you need to know before spending your money.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the hammock itself (a 210T polyester fabric measuring roughly 78 x 55 inches), a 10-foot steel stand that breaks into three sections, two attached suspension ropes with carabiners, and two small throw pillows. The stand's powder-coated finish is the real selling point — it's thicker than the cheap tubular frames you see on most sub-$100 hammocks. The base legs measure about 1.2 inches in diameter, which gives it a stable footprint.

Assembly is genuinely tool-free. The stand sections lock together with a push-button mechanism, and the hammock attaches via hooks at the top of the frame. I timed it: 12 minutes from box to swinging. No Allen wrenches, no cursing.

## Real-World Performance

The waterproofing claims check out. I left it out during a 20-minute downpour, and the fabric beaded water like a freshly waxed car. It dried completely within an hour of the sun coming back. The stitching around the edges is double-stitched and shows no signs of fraying after daily use.

The 450-pound weight capacity is the spec most buyers care about. Two average adults (say, 160 and 170 pounds) will comfortably fit, but here's the honest caveat: the frame starts to creak above 400 pounds combined. It won't collapse — the steel is solid — but the noise is distracting. If you're planning to use this as a couple's hammock, stay under that threshold.

The fabric's biggest weakness is breathability. That waterproof coating works both ways. On a 90-degree afternoon, you'll feel the heat trapped against your back. A cotton hammock breathes better, but it also absorbs rain and mildew. You're trading breathability for weatherproofing — know which matters more for your climate.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 215 ratings, a few themes emerge. Multiple buyers mention the stability of the stand on uneven ground — the curved base design distributes weight well, so it doesn't tip when you sit on the edge. One reviewer noted, *"I was worried the frame would feel flimsy like my last one. This thing doesn't budge when my kids jump in."* Another praised the waterproofing specifically: *"Left it out for a week straight. Two storms, no mold, still looks new."*

The most common praise centers on value — buyers consistently say it feels like a $150 product at its actual price point.

## Where It Falls Short

The included pillows are nearly useless. The straps that hold them in place are positioned so they pull the fabric tight and create a ridge under your neck. Most buyers will toss them.

The fabric's non-breathable nature also means it's not ideal for extended lounging in hot weather. You'll want a light blanket or sheet between you and the fabric on warm days.

And the stand, while sturdy, doesn't fold flat for storage. It breaks into three pieces, but the largest section is still about 5 feet long. If you're planning to pack this for camping, look elsewhere — this is a permanent backyard fixture.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Backyard loungers** who want a set-and-forget hammock that survives rain and humidity without maintenance
- **Couples** under 400 pounds combined who want a stable, non-wobbly frame
- **First-time hammock buyers** who want something that works out of the box without tools

**Skip it if** you live in an extremely hot climate and plan to nap in it daily, or if you need portability.

## FAQ

**Q: Can one person use this comfortably, or is it too big?**
A: It's a double hammock, so a single person gets plenty of room to stretch diagonally. The extra width is actually more comfortable than a single-person hammock.

**Q: Does it come with a rain cover or mosquito net?**
A: No. The fabric itself is waterproof, but there's no bug netting or canopy included. If mosquitoes are a problem in your area, you'll need to add a separate net.

**Q: How does the stand hold up on grass vs. concrete?**
A: The curved steel base sits well on both. On grass, it can sink slightly after heavy rain — place it on pavers or a patio if you want it to stay perfectly level.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a low-maintenance hammock that handles weather without babysitting. **Skip it if** you prioritize breathability or need something portable.

The Gafete earns a solid **4.2 out of 5**. It's not perfect — the pillows are useless and hot-weather comfort is compromised — but for the price, it delivers reliable waterproofing and a stable frame that most competitors at this price point can't match.
