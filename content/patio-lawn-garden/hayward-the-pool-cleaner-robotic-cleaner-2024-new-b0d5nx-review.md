The 2024 Hayward Pool Cleaner enters a crowded field of robotic pool vacuums, and the numbers tell a mixed story: 3.8 stars from 190 Amazon ratings isn't a home run, but it's not a strikeout either. After digging through the specs and customer feedback, here's what you actually need to know before dropping money on this thing.

**What's in the box**

You get the robotic cleaner itself, a 50-foot power cable, a control unit that sits poolside, and the top-loading filter basket assembly. The unit weighs about 15 pounds, which is lighter than the Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus (around 17 lbs) and noticeably easier to haul out of the water. The build uses a mix of ABS plastic and rubberized bumpers — nothing premium, but it feels solid enough for a seasonal product.

**How it actually cleans**

The headline feature is the dual brush roll system. On flat surfaces, this thing does work. Multiple buyers report it picking up fine sand, dirt, and even small pebbles that broom-style cleaners miss. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "Cleaned the bottom of my pool better than my old pressure-side cleaner ever did. The fine debris in the filter basket was proof." For a floor-only robot, the suction is genuinely strong.

But here's the catch: this is a floor-only cleaner. Despite some product imagery suggesting wall-climbing capability, the Hayward 2024 does not ascend pool walls or clean the waterline. If you have a vinyl liner pool with algae streaks on the walls, you're still scrubbing those by hand. That's a significant limitation for a robot in this price tier.

**Filter and runtime**

The top-loading filter basket is convenient — pop the lid, lift it out, rinse, done. No flipping the unit over or wrestling with clips. The tradeoff is capacity. The basket fills quickly if you have trees dropping leaves or a sandy yard. A few reviewers noted needing to empty it mid-cycle during heavy debris season. For normal weekly maintenance, one cycle should suffice.

Runtime is a solid 120 minutes with an auto-shutoff feature. That's enough to cover a standard 20x40 inground pool on a single charge cycle. The 50-foot cord is adequate for most residential setups, but reviewers consistently mention cord management as a minor annoyance — if you don't lay it out in a straight line, it can tangle and interrupt the cleaning pattern.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- Cleaning effectiveness on floors and coves: "It picked up stuff I didn't even know was at the bottom of my pool."
- Ease of filter cleaning: The top-load design is genuinely better than the bottom-access baskets on many competitors.
- Quiet operation: Several buyers noted it runs quieter than their old pressure-side cleaner.

**Where it falls short**

The wall-climbing limitation is the biggest issue. If you're coming from a Dolphin or a Polaris that scrubs walls, this will feel like a step down. Also, some users reported the unit getting stuck on main drains or pop-up heads — not universal, but common enough that the 3.8-star average reflects it. One critical reviewer wrote: "Works great on the floor but I had to babysit it around my pool's drain cover. Needed to reposition it twice per cycle."

**Who should buy this**

This is for pool owners with flat-bottomed, simple-shaped pools who primarily deal with sinking debris — sand, dirt, small pebbles. It's also a decent choice if you're replacing a pressure-side cleaner and want to cut your energy costs; robots use a fraction of the electricity. If you have a large pool with lots of corners, a light-colored liner that shows algae, or heavy leaf drop, keep looking — you'll want a wall-climbing model with a larger filter.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does this cleaner climb walls?* No. It's floor-only. It will clean the cove where the floor meets the wall, but it won't go up vertical surfaces.

*How long does the filter last?* With regular rinsing, a couple of seasons. The mesh is sturdy, but don't leave it sitting in chlorine water between uses — rinse and air-dry it.

*Will it work with my pool pump?* No. This is fully robotic — it plugs into a standard GFCI outlet and runs independently of your pool's circulation system.

**The verdict**

The Hayward 2024 Pool Cleaner is a competent floor cleaner with a frustrating identity problem. It does one thing — cleaning flat surfaces — very well, and it does it quietly, efficiently, and with minimal fuss. But the lack of wall climbing and the modest filter capacity mean it's not a complete pool-cleaning solution for most owners.

**Buy it if** you have a straightforward pool with mostly floor debris and want an energy-efficient robot that's easy to maintain. **Skip it if** you need wall scrubbing, have heavy leaf fall, or want a set-and-forget solution that doesn't require occasional repositioning.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A solid floor cleaner that's honest about its limits, but those limits are significant.
