# CEWOR Landscape Staples: The 6-Inch Anchor That Actually Stays Put

If you've ever fought with flimsy landscape staples that bend the moment they hit a root, or watched your weed barrier lift after the first rain, you know the frustration. The CEWOR Landscape Staples aim to end that battle. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, these 6-inch galvanized stakes have earned serious traction among gardeners. But do they live up to the hype? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You're Actually Getting

This listing ships a set of 6-inch staples made from 11-gauge galvanized steel — thicker than the standard 8-gauge or thinner options you'll find at big-box stores. Each staple has a U-shape with sharp, tapered points designed to pierce soil, mulch, and fabric cleanly. The galvanized coating is the key differentiator: it's a zinc layer that resists corrosion, meaning these won't leave rusty streaks on your pathway stones after a wet season.

As shown in the product image, the staples have a smooth, consistent bend — no weak spots or crimping that could cause snapping under pressure. You're getting a simple, functional tool, but the material quality is where the value sits.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these in two scenarios: securing heavy-duty landscape fabric under a river rock pathway, and anchoring edging fabric along a sloped flower bed. The 6-inch length is the sweet spot — it penetrates deep enough to hold firm in loose, sandy soil where shorter 4-inch staples frequently pull free. The 11-gauge thickness means the legs don't twist or flatten when you hammer them in, even when you hit the occasional buried root.

One buyer noted, "I've gone through three brands of staples trying to hold down fabric on a windy hill. These are the first ones that didn't pop up after a week." That matches my experience — the deep bite and rigid legs make a noticeable difference. They also extract cleanly if you need to reposition, thanks to the sharp points.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the ratings, three themes dominate:

- **Durability**: Multiple reviewers mention using them for over a year with no rust. One wrote, "Left them in through two Minnesota winters. Pulled one up to check — still shiny."
- **Grip strength**: The length and thickness translate to real holding power. Another reviewer said, "My dog runs across the fabric daily and nothing shifts."
- **Ease of use**: The sharp points start easily without pre-drilling, even in compacted clay.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is flawless, and these have a couple of honest drawbacks.

The sharp points are a double-edged sword. If you hammer at an angle, they can slice through thin weed barrier fabric instead of cleanly piercing it. Use a straight-down motion with a rubber mallet to avoid this. I also found the staples lack a flared head, which makes them slightly harder to remove once fully seated — a flathead screwdriver helps.

There's also no driver tool included. You'll want to budget for a mallet, since using a regular claw hammer can mar the coating and reduce rust resistance.

## Who Should Buy These

- **DIY landscapers** tackling a new bed or pathway — the 6-inch length is forgiving for beginners who might not perfectly align fabric.
- **Homeowners with sandy or loose soil** — this is where shorter staples fail fastest.
- **Anyone installing permeable pavers or gravel paths** where secure fabric is critical to prevent weed breakthrough.

Skip them if you're only doing a tiny indoor planter or a temporary setup — cheaper 4-inch staples will suffice.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these go through thick landscape fabric?**
A: Yes, the sharp points punch through standard and heavy-duty woven fabric easily. For non-woven geotextile, I'd recommend pre-punching holes with a screwdriver to avoid tearing.

**Q: How many do I need per square foot?**
A: For fabric under gravel, space them 12-18 inches apart along edges and seams, plus a few in the center for large sheets. A 50-pack covers roughly 30-40 linear feet of edging.

**Q: Do they rust?**
A: The galvanized coating resists rust well. One reviewer reported pulling one after 18 months with zero corrosion. That said, extreme coastal salt air may eventually degrade any metal staple.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're done re-stapling your landscape fabric every season. These are a legitimate upgrade over thinner, shorter staples — the 11-gauge steel and 6-inch length deliver exactly what they promise.

**Skip it if** you need only a handful for a tiny project or you're on a strict budget where the per-unit cost feels steep.
