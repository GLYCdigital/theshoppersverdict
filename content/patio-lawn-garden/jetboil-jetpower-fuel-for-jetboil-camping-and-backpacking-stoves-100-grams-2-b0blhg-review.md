## What you're actually getting

The 100-gram Jetpower canister measures about 4 inches tall and 3.5 inches in diameter — roughly the size of a soda can but denser. It's a proprietary blend of isobutane and propane, which matters more than you'd think. Standard butane stops vaporizing below freezing; the propane kick in this mix keeps the pressure up when temps drop. The threaded valve is the same Lindal B188 standard used across the camping industry, so it will physically fit other stoves. But the tolerances are noticeably tighter on Jetboil hardware — one buyer noted, "threads on perfectly, no hiss, no wobble, just a solid seal."

The package includes just the canister and a clear plastic valve cap. No adapter, no carrying case, no extras. That's fine — it's fuel, not a starter kit.

## Real-world performance

The math on this is straightforward. A 100g canister delivers roughly 20-25 minutes of full-burn time on a Jetboil Flash. That translates to about 6-8 boils of 0.5L water (the standard for freeze-dried meals and coffee). In practice, most buyers report getting 5-7 full boils before the flame starts to sputter.

The pressure regulation is where Jetpower earns its premium. One verified buyer wrote: "Used it at 9,000 feet in Colorado. First burn was as strong as the last. No cold-weather fade like I got with the cheap stuff." That's the isobutane/propane blend working as advertised. Another reviewer confirmed the 12-minute boil time in breezy 45°F conditions — consistent with Jetboil's published specs.

The reliability thread runs through nearly every positive review. People don't buy this fuel for excitement; they buy it because it works every time. Common praise points:

- **Consistent flame output** — no sputtering or pressure drops mid-canister

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**Price per gram is steep.** A two-pack of 100g Jetpower canisters typically runs $12-15. A generic brand like Coleman or Gas One costs half that for the same fuel type. If you're a weekend car camper who never sees temperatures below 50°F, you're paying a premium you don't need.

**No fuel gauge.** You cannot tell how much is left by shaking it. You'll need a small digital scale (about $10) or meticulous trip logging to avoid running dry mid-cook. For a product at this price point, a simple float gauge would be a welcome addition.

**The 100g size is limiting.** For a 3-day solo trip, you'll need two canisters minimum. Jetboil sells 230g versions, but they don't fit the smaller stoves as cleanly and cost disproportionately more.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You own a Jetboil system and prioritize reliability over cost. You're a winter backpacker who needs consistent performance below freezing. You're heading into remote backcountry where a failed seal or sputtering flame isn't an option.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual car camper using a stove a few times a year. You're on a budget and don't mind weighing canisters to manage fuel. You use a non-Jetboil stove — the premium is wasted on generic hardware.

**Q: Will this fit my MSR or Snow Peak stove?**
A: Yes, the thread is the industry-standard Lindal B188. It will physically seal on most stoves. However, you'll pay a premium for Jetboil-specific tuning benefits you won't fully realize on other hardware.

**Q: How many meals can I cook with one 100g canister?**
A: Realistically 5-7 full boils of 0.5L water. If you're doing one boil per meal (coffee + dinner), expect 3 days solo. Plan two canisters for a 3-4 day trip.

**Q: Can I recycle the canisters?**
A: Only if empty and punctured. Jetboil doesn't offer a take-back program. Your local recycling center may accept them if you pierce the valve and clearly mark them as empty. Most buyers just toss them.
