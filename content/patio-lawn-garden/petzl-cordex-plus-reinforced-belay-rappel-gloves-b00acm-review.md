If you've ever done a long rappel on a gritty rope with cheap gloves, you know the feeling: blisters forming, fabric pilling, and the quiet realization that your hands are about to be raw for a week. The PETZL CORDEX Plus exists to eliminate that problem entirely.

This isn't a garden glove or a light-duty work glove. It's a purpose-built tool for climbers, canyoneers, and anyone whose hands regularly meet dynamic rope. With a 4.6/5 average across 229 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. The question is whether it's right for *your* specific use case.

## What You Actually Get

The CORDEX Plus is a belay/rappel glove with a split-leather palm, synthetic leather reinforcement across the high-wear zones (thumb saddle and index finger), and a stretchy synthetic back that breathes. The elasticized wrist cinches snug without cutting off circulation.

As shown in the product image, the reinforcement isn't just cosmetic — it's double-stitched into the zones that take the most abuse when feeding rope through a device. The glove weighs almost nothing (roughly 60 grams per pair), which matters when you're racking gear for a multi-pitch.

## Real-World Performance

The split-leather palm is the smartest design choice here. Full-grain leather is tougher but stiff as a board until broken in; split leather gives you 80% of the durability with 100% of the flexibility right out of the box. You can tie knots, handle biners, and adjust devices without the "claw hand" feeling that plagues cheaper gloves.

Grip on wet or muddy rope is genuinely impressive. The leather bites into the sheath without slipping, which is critical when you're catching a falling partner on belay. The reinforced index finger and thumb mean the areas that normally wear through in a season will last two or three times longer.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reviewers repeatedly mention durability and comfort. One climber put it plainly: *"I've had these for three years of regular gym and outdoor use. Still going strong."* Another noted, *"The fit is perfect for belaying — you can feel the rope without losing dexterity."*

The consensus is that these gloves hit the sweet spot between protection and sensitivity. You can clip quickdraws, fiddle with a prusik, or handle a locking biner without stripping the gloves off every 30 seconds.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**Sizing is erratic.** Multiple reviewers report these run small. If you're between sizes, go up. If you have wide palms, definitely go up — the split-leather doesn't stretch much across the width.

**No touchscreen compatibility.** This is 2026; you'll be removing gloves to check your phone, adjust your music, or answer a call. It's a minor annoyance but worth knowing.

**They're not for heavy winter use.** The synthetic back breathes well, which is great for warm weather but means cold-weather climbers will want a liner or a different glove entirely.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these for rappelling on a figure-8 or ATC?**
Yes. The reinforced zones align perfectly with where the rope runs through a tube-style device. They also work well with GriGris, though you'll want to remove them for the fine manipulations the device sometimes requires.

**Q: How do they handle sweat?**
The synthetic back breathes well, and the leather palm doesn't get slippery when damp. They'll get stinky if you don't air them out, but that's true of any leather glove.

**Q: Do they fit women well?**
Petzl offers the CORDEX Plus in unisex sizing down to XXS. The fingers are proportioned for average hands; women with narrower palms should size down.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a regular climber who wants a durable, comfortable glove that won't fall apart after a season of regular use. The reinforced wear zones genuinely extend the lifespan, and the balance of dexterity and protection is the best in this price range.

**Skip it if:** You only climb occasionally and want to save money, or if you need a cold-weather glove for alpine environments. Also skip if you're between sizes and can't try them on — the sizing inconsistency is real.
