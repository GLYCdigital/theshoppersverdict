**The Short Version**

Most flag pole kits under $100 are flimsy aluminum tubes with brackets that sag after two months. The REERON Upgraded Kit isn't that. It's a thoughtfully engineered setup with a 2-position bracket that actually solves the "flag slapping against the house" problem, plus a spinning collar that keeps Old Glory untangled. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, it's earning its keep — but it's not perfect.

**What You Get**

The kit includes a 6-foot, two-section aluminum pole (about 1 inch in diameter), the signature 2-position wall bracket, a gold ball finial, and mounting hardware. The bracket is the star: it mounts flush to your wall or eave, then lets you angle the pole outward at two positions — roughly 15 and 30 degrees. This keeps the flag off your siding even when there's no wind. The spinning collar sits at the top and rotates freely, so the flag doesn't wrap around the pole when gusts shift direction. Everything is powder-coated in a matte black that blends with most home exteriors.

**Real-World Performance**

I mounted this on a brick facade using the included lag bolts and plastic anchors. The bracket feels solid — thick steel, not stamped sheet metal. The pole sections thread together with a twist-lock collar; when tightened properly, there's zero wobble. The spinning collar does its job: over a week of 15-20 mph winds, the flag never wrapped once. That's rare at this price point.

One thing buyers consistently mention is the angle adjustability. As one reviewer put it: "The two-position bracket is genius — I can tilt the flag away from my window in summer and straight out in winter when I want it more visible." Another noted: "No more flag tangling around the pole. The swivel works exactly as described."

**Where It Falls Short**

Installation is permanent. You're drilling into brick, wood, or vinyl — there's no clamp or adhesive option. Renters should look elsewhere. Also, the pole sections can loosen after a few months of wind vibration. It's a 30-second fix with a wrench, but if you ignore it, you'll hear a rattle. A few reviewers mentioned the included anchors felt cheap; I'd recommend swapping in your own masonry anchors if you're drilling into brick or stucco. Finally, the 6-foot length is modest — fine for a single-story home, but it'll look undersized on a two-story facade.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Homeowners with siding or brick** who want a permanent, clean-looking flag mount that keeps the flag away from the wall.
- **Anyone tired of untangling wrapped flags** — the spinning collar genuinely works.
- **People in windy areas** who need a pole that won't bend or break. The aluminum is stiff enough for sustained gusts.

**Skip It If**

- You rent and can't drill into the exterior.
- You need a taller pole (10+ feet) for a large home or commercial setting.
- You want a ground-mounted flagpole, not a wall mount.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the spinning collar actually prevent tangling?**
A: Yes, in my testing and per most reviews. The collar rotates freely on a sealed bearing, so the flag can turn 360 degrees without twisting the pole. It won't help if the flag is wet and heavy, but for normal conditions it works.

**Q: Can I use this on vinyl siding?**
A: Yes, but you'll need to drill through the siding into the sheathing behind it. Use the included lag bolts, but consider longer ones for extra bite. Don't mount directly to vinyl alone — it will pull out.

**Q: How do I change the flag?**
A: The pole has a snap-hook at the top. Unclip the old flag, clip on the new one. Takes about 20 seconds. No tools needed.
