If you own a Harbor Freight Predator 212cc engine — the workhorse in generators, log splitters, and mini bikes — you already know the stock plastic petcock is a ticking time bomb. It strips, it seeps, and it leaves you smelling like gasoline every time you wheel the generator out of the shed. The Savior Generator Petcock is the $10 fix that 229 Amazon reviewers have rated at 4.6 stars. Here's the real story after digging through the feedback and specs.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You're getting a machined brass petcock body, not a cheap zinc alloy casting. The package includes two thread sizes — M14-1.5 and M16-1.5 — which covers Predator engines, most Honda clones, and a surprising range of small generators and pressure washers. There's also a small fuel filter that threads into the tank side, which catches debris before it reaches your carburetor jet. No gaskets or thread tape included, so have your own PTFE tape handy.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The first thing you notice is the weight. This isn't the hollow, featherweight plastic you're replacing. The brass body feels substantial, and the 90-degree lever is positioned to clear generator frames — a common complaint with OEM petcocks that point straight down and interfere with the recoil starter housing.

Installation takes about five minutes. Remove the old petcock, clean the tank threads, wrap the new one with a couple layers of PTFE tape, and torque it hand-tight. The M14 fitting on my Predator 212 matched perfectly with zero wobble. The lever rotates through three positions: ON, OFF, and RES (reserve). Fuel flow in the ON position is generous — the generator starts on the first pull even after sitting for two weeks with a full tank.

One thing I noticed in the customer feedback: several buyers mention using this on Coleman and Sportsman generators, not just Predators. The dual-thread design is genuinely versatile. As one reviewer put it, "Fixed a leaking generator that had been sitting in my garage for a year. Replaced the plastic petcock with this and it fired right up."

**Where It Falls Short**

The lever detents aren't crisp. There's no satisfying "click" between positions — it's more of a soft resistance. In a dark garage, you might not be 100% sure you've fully shut off the fuel. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth checking visually after switching.

The included O-ring is thin. Multiple reviewers report needing an extra wrap of thread tape to get a perfect seal, especially on older tanks with slightly deformed threads. Budget for that when you install it.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is a no-brainer if you own a Predator 212 or any small engine with a leaking fuel shut-off. It's also ideal for anyone who stores their generator for months at a time — the brass body won't degrade from ethanol-blended fuel the way plastic does. If you're restoring an old mini bike or go-kart with a Honda GX clone, the M16 thread option makes this a solid pick.

Skip it if you need a petcock with a fuel gauge, or if your tank has a tapered thread that requires a specific tapered fitting — this is a straight-thread design.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Will this fit my Harbor Freight Predator 212?**
Yes, directly. Use the M14-1.5 thread. It's the exact replacement size for the stock petcock on the 212cc engine used in generators, water pumps, and log splitters.

**Q: Does it come with a fuel filter?**
Yes, a small in-tank filter is included. It threads into the tank side of the petcock and is replaceable.

**Q: Is this compatible with ethanol-blended gasoline?**
Yes. The brass body and internal components are resistant to ethanol corrosion, unlike the plastic OEM parts that crack and swell.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy It If, Skip It If**

**Buy it if** you're tired of fuel leaks, stripped threads, or that lingering gas smell in your garage. This is a permanent fix for a chronic problem on Predator engines, and the price is right.

**Skip it if** you want a factory-perfect aesthetic or need a petcock with a fuel level sight glass. This is a functional replacement, not a cosmetic upgrade.

For the money, this is the best insurance policy your generator can get. The dual-thread design adds versatility, and the brass construction means you'll never replace it again. Just bring your own thread tape and check the lever position before you store the machine.
