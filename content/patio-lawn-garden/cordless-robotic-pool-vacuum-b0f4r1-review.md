## The Cordless Pitch: Convenience That Comes With Compromises

A cordless robotic pool vacuum sounds like the perfect solution for anyone tired of wrestling with hoses, poles, and extension cords every weekend. And honestly, this unit delivers on that core promise — the freedom of no tether. But as the 3.7-star average across 185 ratings suggests, the reality of cordless pool cleaning involves some tradeoffs you need to know about before you click buy.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the robotic vacuum unit itself, a charging dock, and a small floating cable used to retrieve the unit if it gets stuck mid-cycle. There's no wall-mounted base station like some premium brands — you charge via the included adapter plugged directly into the unit.

**Key specs to know:**
- **Battery:** Rechargeable lithium-ion, advertised at 90 minutes
- **Weight:** Roughly 8-10 pounds depending on configuration
- **Drive system:** Dual motor, track-style wheels for climbing walls
- **Filter:** Fine mesh canister, washable and reusable

## Real-World Performance: What Buyers Report

The cleaning performance on flat surfaces earns genuine praise. One verified buyer noted, *"It picked up sand and small debris from my vinyl liner floor better than my old pressure-side cleaner ever did."* The wall-climbing capability is functional but not stellar — it handles gradual slopes fine but struggles with steep, slick surfaces.

**Where it wins:**
- Floor and cove cleaning on vinyl, fiberglass, and tile pools
- Fine debris like sand, silt, and pollen
- Small to mid-sized above-ground pools (up to roughly 20-24 feet)

**Where it struggles:**
- Large in-ground pools — the battery simply runs out before a full cycle
- Heavy leaf loads — the small filter canister fills fast, reducing suction
- Steep wall angles and stairs

## The Honest Downsides

**Battery life is the elephant in the pool.** Multiple reviewers report getting closer to 45-60 minutes of actual runtime, not the advertised 90. For a 30-foot in-ground pool, that means the unit dies before covering everything. You'll either run it twice or accept partial cleaning.

**The filter canister is genuinely undersized.** If your pool sees regular leaf fall or you skip a week between cleanings, expect to empty it mid-cycle — which means hauling the unit out, rinsing the filter, and sending it back in.

**No smart features.** There's no app, no scheduling, no Wi-Fi. You press a button, it runs, it stops. For the price point, that's acceptable, but worth knowing if you expected app control.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You have an **above-ground pool** or a small in-ground pool under 20 feet
- You want **cordless simplicity** — no hoses, no pump connections, no setup
- You're fine with **semi-regular manual intervention** (emptying the filter mid-run)

**Skip it if:**
- You own a **large in-ground pool** (over 25 feet) that needs full coverage in one pass
- Your pool accumulates **heavy debris** like leaves, twigs, or acorns
- You want a **set-and-forget** solution with smart scheduling and app control

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Will this climb pool walls?**
A: Partially. It handles gradual slopes and gentle wall angles, but steep vertical walls and stairs are hit-or-miss. Buyers report it works better on vinyl and fiberglass than on slick tile or gunite surfaces.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Real-world reports put it between 45-75 minutes depending on debris load and whether the unit is climbing walls. The advertised 90 minutes is optimistic in most conditions.

**Q: How often do I need to clean the filter?**
A: For normal weekly maintenance on a small pool, every 1-2 cycles. With heavy debris, plan on mid-cycle empties. The filter is easy to rinse out with a garden hose.
