**Performance and Real-World Use**  

Here's where the reviews split. The majority of buyers report strong germination — seeds sprout in 7–14 days when soil temperatures hover around 70°F, which matches the species' natural requirements. One reviewer put it plainly: "Planted in early May, had sprouts by the 10th day. By August I had a 3-foot tall patch of yellow blooms." That tracks with my research: Black Eyed Susans are tough, drought-tolerant once established, and don't need fussy soil amendments.

But the first-year bloom is genuinely underwhelming. This is a biennial/short-lived perennial in most zones, meaning the first season is mostly foliage. Multiple buyers echoed this frustration: "First year I thought I wasted my money — just leaves. Second year? Nonstop flowers from July to frost." If you're expecting instant gratification, this isn't the seed for you.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**  

**Q: Do I need to cold-stratify these seeds?**  
A: No. Unlike some native wildflowers, *Rudbeckia hirta* germinates fine without cold treatment in zones 3–9. Direct sow after last frost, or start indoors 6–8 weeks before.

**Q: How much sun do they really need?**  
A: Six hours minimum, but they'll thrive in full sun (8+ hours). Less than 6 hours and you'll get leggy plants with fewer blooms.

**Q: Will they spread and take over my garden?**  
A: They self-seed moderately, not aggressively. Deadhead spent blooms if you want to control spread; leave them in fall for birds to eat the seeds.
