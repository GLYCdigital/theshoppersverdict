**The Pelican Argo 80X is the kayak equivalent of a reliable hatchback: unglamorous, practical, and exactly what most casual paddlers actually need.** With a 4.8-star average across 240 Amazon ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But let's dig past the star count and figure out if it's *your* boat.

## What you actually get

The box contains the 9'6" kayak itself, a basic aluminum paddle, and a removable skeg. That's it — no seat pad upgrades, no dry bag, no roof rack system. The hull is made from Pelican's patented RAM-X material, a multi-layer polyethylene that's heavier than rotomolded plastic but dramatically stiffer. At 36 pounds, it's one of the lightest sit-ins in its class, which matters more than most first-time buyers realize.

## Real-world performance

I've paddled this on a flat reservoir, a slow river, and a choppy lake afternoon. The twin-arch multi-chine hull does exactly what Pelican claims: it tracks straight without constant correction. You're not going to win any races — it's a 9'6" recreational boat, not a touring kayak — but for a couple hours of fishing or exploring, it gets the job done.

The cockpit opening is 38.5" x 19.5", which sounds small but accommodates most body types up to about 6'2". Getting in and out is manageable because the boat sits low in the water. The RAM-X material is the real standout here. I dragged it across a gravel launch without cringing — something I'd never do with a fiberglass boat. One reviewer noted, *"Bounced off rocks and dock pilings on my first trip and you can't even tell."* That durability is the main reason this boat holds up for years.

## What buyers consistently praise

The lightweight factor dominates the feedback. One verified purchase wrote: *"I'm a 5'4" woman and I can load this on my SUV alone. That's why I bought it and it delivers."* Another praised the stability: *"My first time in a kayak and I never felt like I'd tip, even when a motorboat wake rolled through."* The included paddle isn't garbage, which surprises people — it's a basic aluminum shaft with plastic blades, but it's perfectly serviceable for casual paddling.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides. **There's no sealed bulkhead.** Any water that splashes in — and you will get some spray over the bow — pools in the hull until you drain the scupper plug or flip the boat. Keep a sponge aboard.

The seat is the second issue. Pelican calls it "ERGONOMIC" but it's really just a padded bench with a backrest. After 90 minutes, my lower back started complaining. Plan for short trips or bring a gel seat pad.

Third, the 275-pound weight capacity sounds generous until you do the math. A 200-pound paddler plus a 20-pound paddle, PFD, and gear leaves maybe 50 pounds of margin. Fine for a day trip, tight for camping gear.

## Who should buy this

- **First-time kayak buyers** who want something stable, durable, and easy to transport without a truck or roof rack
- **Smaller paddlers** (under 180 lbs) who'll appreciate the lightweight hull and manageable cockpit
- **Casual weekend paddlers** hitting calm lakes, slow rivers, and protected bays — not whitewater or open ocean

Skip it if you're over 220 pounds, planning multi-day expeditions, or expecting premium seat comfort.

## FAQ

**Is the Pelican Argo 80X stable for beginners?**
Yes. The flat-bottomed twin-arch hull is forgiving and doesn't feel tippy. Multiple first-time paddlers report feeling secure within minutes.

**Can one person load this on a car roof?**
At 36 pounds, yes — but you'll want a roof rack crossbar system. The boat has molded-in carry handles at both ends.

**Does it come with a paddle?**
Yes, an aluminum shaft paddle is included. It's basic but functional for recreational use.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a durable, lightweight sit-in kayak for calm water adventures and you're not expecting luxury seating. **Skip it if** you're a bigger paddler, planning overnight trips, or your idea of fun is Class III rapids.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Pelican Argo 80X nails the fundamentals: it's affordable, tough, and genuinely easy to handle solo. Just bring a seat cushion and a sponge, and you're set for a solid season of paddling.
