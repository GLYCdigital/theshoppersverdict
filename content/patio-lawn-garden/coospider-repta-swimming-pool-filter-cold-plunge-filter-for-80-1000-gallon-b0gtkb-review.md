You've seen the cold plunge trend. You've priced the $3,000 all-in-one tubs. Then you found this little filter for a fraction of that, wondering if it can actually keep a plunge tub clean. Short answer: it can — with caveats that matter.

The coospider-repta is a compact cartridge filter pump aimed at cold plunge tubs, inflatable pools, and small above-ground setups between 80 and 1,000 gallons. As shown in the product image, you're getting a self-priming pump with a clear lid over the filter cartridge — simple, no permanent plumbing required.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the filter pump unit, one replacement cartridge, two hose adapters (3/4-inch and 1-inch), and a short length of tubing. There's no separate pump — the filter and pump are integrated into one housing, which is both a plus (fewer connections to leak) and a minor annoyance (when one fails, both need replacing).

The 12V DC pump is the standout spec here. It draws minimal power, runs quiet enough for bedroom-adjacent use, and won't trip GFCI outlets like some 120V pool pumps can. For cold plunge users, that's genuinely useful — you don't want a lawnmower-loud pump ruining your ice bath zen.

## Real-World Performance

For a 100-300 gallon cold plunge tub, this filter moves water adequately — roughly 300 gallons per hour at peak. That's enough to turn over a small tub every hour, which is the minimum for keeping water clear.

Here's where it gets real: the cartridge loads up fast. Buyers consistently report the flow visibly weakens within days, not weeks. One reviewer noted, "I'm rinsing the cartridge every other day or the water gets cloudy." That's not a defect — it's physics. A small cartridge surface area means more frequent maintenance. Budget for replacement cartridges; they're cheap but you'll need them.

The pump is self-priming, which sounds great until you realize it needs the water level above the intake port. If your plunge tub water level drops, the pump loses prime and runs dry. Dry running burns out the impeller — several one-star reviews describe exactly this failure.

## What Buyers Praise

The quiet operation gets consistent shout-outs. One verified buyer wrote: "I can run this while sleeping in the next room — barely a hum." That's the 12V design paying off.

Setup simplicity also earns credit. The dual hose adapters mean it works with both standard pool hoses and the smaller tubing common in DIY cold plunge builds. For someone piecing together a budget plunge setup, compatibility matters.

## Honest Shortcomings

Beyond the cartridge maintenance and weak hose barbs, understand what this filter doesn't do. It's mechanical filtration only — no UV sterilization, no ozone injection. You still need to add chlorine or bromine to prevent algae and bacteria. Some cold plunge enthusiasts expect a filter to handle everything; this one won't.

The 1,000-gallon rating is optimistic. At that volume, you'd get less than one turnover per hour — marginal for keeping water clear. Realistically, this filter shines at 80-400 gallons.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're running a DIY cold plunge tub under 400 gallons, you value quiet operation, and you don't mind rinsing a cartridge every few days. It's also fine for small inflatable pools where you're already managing chemicals.

**Skip it if:** You want a set-and-forget system, you're filtering a pool over 500 gallons, or you expect zero maintenance. Look at sand filters or UV-equipped systems instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this filter handle a 500-gallon stock tank plunge?**
A: Technically yes, but you'll see weak turnover and frequent cartridge cleaning. Stick to 400 gallons or less for best results.

**Q: Does it come with a timer?**
A: No. You'll need a smart plug or mechanical timer to run it on a schedule, which I'd recommend — continuous running wears the cartridge faster.

**Q: How often do cartridges need replacing?**
A: With daily use, expect 2-4 weeks per cartridge before it stops cleaning effectively. Rinsing extends life but doesn't restore full performance.
