## What's actually in the box

You get the edger itself, a 7.5-inch double-edged steel blade, an adjustable depth guide, and a 10-foot power cord. That's it. No blade wrench (you'll need a 13mm socket), no gloves, no spare parts. The unit weighs about 11 pounds — light enough to carry one-handed, heavy enough to feel solid while cutting.

The design is straightforward: a fixed rear wheel, a front guide wheel, and the blade housing sits between them. The double-edge blade is the standout spec here — most edgers in this price range use single-edge blades that require flipping the unit or repositioning to cut the opposite direction.

## Real-world cutting performance

I tested this on a typical suburban lawn with 3-inch-thick St. Augustine along a 40-foot concrete driveway. The 12-amp motor spun up to speed in about two seconds and held RPMs through the thickest sections. No bogging, no smell of overheating, no tripping the breaker.

The depth adjustment lever is genuinely tool-free — you push it down, slide the blade assembly, and it locks with a satisfying click. The double-edge feature is real: I edged along a flower bed on the left, then flipped my stance and cut the opposite side of the walkway without rotating the machine. That saves real time on longer runs.

## Where it falls short

Be honest with yourself about your lawn's geometry. The fixed blade angle means you cannot tilt the head for angled cuts on sloped driveways or curved walkways. If your curb has a significant pitch, you'll be fighting the machine to keep a consistent depth.

The corded reality is the second issue. You need a heavy-gauge 14 AWG extension cord — a 16 AWG cord will cause voltage drop and noticeable power loss. And you will run over the cord at least once. It's manageable if you plan your path (start at the outlet, work outward), but it's a real annoyance.

Finally, there's no grass deflector or collection bag. Clippings spray outward onto the lawn. If you're a "clean edge, clean lawn" perfectionist, you'll be raking after every pass. Most buyers accept this as normal for an edger, but a few 3-star reviews specifically called it out.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You have a straight concrete or asphalt driveway, a standard sidewalk run, or defined flower bed borders. You're comfortable managing an extension cord and want a sub-$70 tool that cuts as clean as units costing twice as much. You don't edge more than 200 linear feet per session.

**Skip it if:** Your property has steep driveway slopes, curved paver edges, or tight corners where you need an angled blade. Also skip if you're buying for a rental property where you won't always have access to an outdoor outlet.

## FAQ: Three questions shoppers actually ask

**Q: Can this edge along brick pavers or is it only for concrete?**
A: It works on pavers, but the fixed blade angle means you'll get a straight vertical cut. For curved paver patterns, you'll need to make multiple shallow passes. The steel blade handles brick and stone contact without chipping — just don't force it deeper than the depth guide allows.

**Q: How does the double-edge blade actually help?**
A: Most edgers cut only in one direction, so you position yourself on the lawn side and push. With this, you can edge from either side of the walkway or flower bed without turning the machine around. It's a convenience feature that matters most on long runs.

**Q: What size extension cord do I need?**
A: Use a 14 AWG or thicker (lower gauge number) cord rated for outdoor use, and keep it under 100 feet. A 16 AWG cord will cause voltage drop, making the motor run hot and cut noticeably weaker at the far end of your run.
