Let's be direct about this 2026 robotic pool vacuum: it's not a premium Dolphin or Polaris, and it doesn't pretend to be. What it is — a cordless, self-contained robot that scrubs your above-ground pool floor and walls for about two hours before needing a charge. At a 3.9-star average across 195 ratings, buyers are mostly satisfied, but the complaints are consistent enough that you need to know what you're signing up for before you hit "buy."

**What you actually get**

The box contains the robot itself, a floating cable with a handle for retrieval, a charging adapter, and a small brush for cleaning the filter. No wall mount, no remote, no app. The unit weighs under 10 pounds, which matters because you'll be lifting it in and out of the pool by that tether. The 6,600mAh lithium battery is the headline feature here — the 130-minute runtime is real, not marketing math. One buyer confirmed: "I have a 24-foot round pool and it covers the whole floor and most of the walls before the battery dies."

**How it performs in real use**

The cleaning pattern is methodical: it drives straight, turns, and repeats in a grid-like path across the floor, then attempts wall climbs. On vinyl liners and standard above-ground pool walls, it climbs about 70% of the way up before falling back — enough to catch the green line of algae at the waterline but not enough to clean the top foot of the wall. The suction is genuinely strong for a cordless unit. Sand, silt, pollen, and fine dirt get pulled into the top-loading filter basket effectively. One reviewer noted it "picked up stuff my old suction-side cleaner left behind for years."

The catch: it's not designed for large debris. Acorns, twigs, and bigger leaves will clog the intake. If your pool sits under a tree, this isn't your machine. You'll also be emptying the filter basket regularly — on a moderately dirty pool, plan for a mid-cycle clean. The basket pops open from the top, which is convenient, but the mesh is fine enough that it clogs faster than you'd expect.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The runtime and the battery life get the most love. Multiple reviewers mention that the 130-minute claim holds up in practice, and charging takes about 4-5 hours. The cordless design is also a recurring plus — no tangled hoses, no connecting to your pool's pump system. It's genuinely plug-and-play: drop it in, press the button, come back in two hours. One long-term reviewer said: "I've had mine for three months, used it twice a week, and the battery still holds a full charge."

**Where it falls short**

The lack of any remote or scheduling feature is the biggest gap. You get one button. Press it once, it runs until the battery dies, then it stops wherever it is. You can't direct it to a specific dirty spot or tell it to skip the walls. Several buyers also reported that it gets stuck on pool steps or ladders — the climbing mechanism doesn't have the intelligence to navigate obstacles, so you may need to rescue it mid-cycle. And the filter basket, while easy to clean, is small. On a heavily used pool, you're emptying it every 30-40 minutes.

**Who should buy this**

This robot is for the above-ground pool owner who wants to stop manually vacuuming but doesn't want to spend $600+ on a feature-packed unit. It's ideal for pools up to 24 feet in diameter with flat floors and minimal obstacles. If you have a large, dirty pool with lots of trees nearby, or you want a smart device you can control from your phone, pass on this and save for a higher-end model.

**FAQ**

**Will it work with my pool's liner?**  
Yes, on vinyl, fiberglass, and most standard above-ground pool liners. If you have a sand or pebble bottom, the suction is strong enough to handle it, but the wheels can struggle for traction on loose surfaces.

**How long does the battery actually last?**  
Real-world feedback puts it at 2 to 2.5 hours on a full charge, matching the 130-minute claim. Charging takes about 4-5 hours from empty.

**Does it clean the waterline?**  
Partially. It climbs walls to about 70-75% of the height, so it gets the lower waterline area but won't scrub the top edge. You'll still need to brush the top rim occasionally.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have an above-ground pool under 24 feet, you want cordless convenience, and you're tired of manual vacuuming. The battery life and suction are genuinely impressive for the price.

**Skip it if:** Your pool is surrounded by trees, you need app control or scheduling, or you expect it to handle anything bigger than small leaves. This is a basic, reliable worker — not a smart cleaning system.
