## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. **Packet quantities are uneven.** Some varieties ship generously while others are noticeably thin — a few buyers reported as few as 12–15 seeds for certain crops. If you're planning to fill a large raised bed with a single variety, you'll likely need a second source.

**No growing guide or zone map is included.** The packets give you the essentials, but if you're in a short-season climate (zones 3–5), you'll need to research your frost dates and possibly start seeds indoors. This is a seed collection, not a gardening course — know that going in.

Finally, **variety composition can shift** between shipments. A few recent reviewers noted that their pack didn't perfectly match the advertised list, with one or two substitutions. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing if you have your heart set on a specific cultivar.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Are these seeds truly heirloom and non-GMO?**
A: Yes. Buyers and the manufacturer confirm these are open-pollinated, non-GMO heirloom varieties. They'll produce seeds you can save and replant next season, though cross-pollination between varieties in your garden can affect purity.

**Q: How long do the seeds stay viable?**
A: Stored in a cool, dry place, most varieties will maintain strong germination for 2–3 years. The resealable packets help, but consider transferring to an airtight container for long-term storage.

**Q: Will everything grow in my climate?**
A: Not necessarily. The pack includes warm-season crops (tomatoes, peppers) and cool-season crops (lettuce, spinach, carrots). You'll need to check your local frost dates and plan two planting windows — one in early spring for greens, one after the last frost for warm-season veggies.
