## What you actually get

Three separate shears, each with a different role:

- **Small (approx. 7 inches):** Precision tip for deadheading flowers, trimming herbs, and fine bonsai work
- **Medium (approx. 8 inches):** Your everyday workhorse for stems up to 1/2 inch
- **Large (approx. 9 inches):** Bypass-style cutting for branches up to 1 inch thick

All three share the same DNA: stainless steel blades (not the coated carbon steel you see on cheaper sets), a spring-loaded return mechanism, and an ergonomic handle with a sliding thumb lock. The handles have a soft-touch rubberized grip that runs the full length — not just a small pad. As shown in the product image, the orange accents make them easy to spot when you drop them in the garden bed.

## How they perform in real use

I put these through a full season of mixed work: rose bushes, tomato suckers, and some overgrown juniper branches. The stainless steel claim holds up. After a week of cutting sap-heavy plants, the blades wiped clean with a dry rag — no sticky residue left behind, no rust spots forming. That's the main reason to choose these over cheaper carbon steel options.

The cutting action is clean on green wood up to about 1/2 inch. The bypass design (where one blade slides past the other) gives you a precise cut that doesn't crush the stem — important for roses and other plants where ragged cuts invite disease. On branches closer to the 1-inch max, you'll feel resistance. The blades are sharp out of the box, but they're not surgical-grade. Expect to take a sharpening stone to them after a few heavy sessions.

One thing buyers consistently mention is the lock mechanism. One reviewer noted, "The thumb lock actually stays engaged — my old pruners would pop open in the garage and I'd find them on the floor." That matches my experience. The lock slides firmly into place and doesn't budge during storage or transport.

## Where it falls short

No product is perfect, and this one has two honest weaknesses.

First, there's no holster or belt clip. If you're moving around the garden constantly, you'll be juggling three shears or making repeated trips back to the pouch. For a set designed for "grab the right tool for the job," that's a miss.

Second, the blades need a break-in period. They're sharp enough out of the box for light work, but for serious pruning, you'll want to run them over a sharpening stone first. One buyer put it plainly: "Great value, but don't expect Felco-level sharpness right away."

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a home gardener who wants a dedicated shear for different tasks without paying premium prices. You hate rust and sap buildup. You lose tools in the yard and want a set you won't cry over.

**Skip it if:** You're a professional landscaper doing hours of cutting daily. You need surgical precision on delicate plants. Or you insist on a belt holster for hands-free carrying.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these actually stainless steel or just coated?**
A: The blades are solid stainless steel — not a coating that wears off. That's why they resist sap and rust so well.

**Q: Can I sharpen these?**
A: Yes. Standard sharpening stones or a carbide sharpener work fine. The stainless steel holds an edge well once you break it in.

**Q: Will the small shears handle thick branches?**
A: No. Use the small one for stems under 1/4 inch. Anything thicker, grab the medium or large shear. Trying to force the small one will bend the blade.
