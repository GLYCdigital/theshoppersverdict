## Performance and Real-World Results

MSMA is the old-school answer to grassy weeds that selective herbicides like 2,4-D or dicamba won’t kill. It’s systemic — absorbed through leaves and translocated to roots, which is why it nukes perennial weeds like dallisgrass and torpedograss rather than just burning them back temporarily.

The 4.8-star rating didn’t happen by accident. Buyer feedback consistently highlights how it works where other products failed. One reviewer put it bluntly: *“I’ve tried everything for dallisgrass. This is the only thing that actually killed it.”* Another noted it took about two weeks to see full brown-out, but the weeds stayed dead — which is exactly what you want.

That said, this isn’t a cosmetic herbicide. Expect the target weeds to turn yellow and die over 7–14 days. And if you apply it to Bermuda grass during a hot, dry stretch, you may see temporary yellowing or stunting of the turf itself. That’s not a product failure — it’s an application timing issue. Spray when grass is actively growing and temperatures are below 90°F for best results.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides.

**Regulatory hurdles are real.** MSMA is restricted in several states — particularly in the Southeast and parts of the Midwest — due to arsenic content. It’s labeled for use on golf courses, sod farms, and highway rights-of-way, not for typical residential lawns in many jurisdictions. Check your state’s pesticide database before you buy. If you’re in a restricted area, this jug is a liability, not a solution.

**Mixing discipline matters.** Without a surfactant, you’ll get poor leaf coverage and inconsistent kill. Several lower-rated reviews trace back to skipping this step. Also, don’t tank-mix with other products without testing a small area first — MSMA can be finicky.

**No residual control.** This kills what’s there but doesn’t prevent new weeds from germinating. Pair it with a pre-emergent if you want season-long control.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do I mix per gallon of water?**
A: For spot treatments, mix 1–2 tablespoons per gallon of water with a non-ionic surfactant. For broadcast applications, use 1.5–2 pints per acre. Always read the label — it’s the final authority.

**Q: Will this kill my Bermuda grass?**
A: At labeled rates, MSMA is safe for established Bermuda grass. However, applying during heat stress or drought can cause temporary yellowing. Water the lawn a day before application and avoid spraying when temps exceed 90°F.

**Q: Is this legal for home use?**
A: It depends on your state. MSMA is a restricted-use pesticide in many areas. Check with your state’s department of agriculture or your local extension office before purchasing.
