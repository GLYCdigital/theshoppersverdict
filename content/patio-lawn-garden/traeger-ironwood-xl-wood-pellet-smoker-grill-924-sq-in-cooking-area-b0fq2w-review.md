The Traeger Ironwood XL is the brand's answer to a simple question: what if you took everything people loved about the Timberline and stripped out the stuff nobody actually uses? The 924-square-inch pellet grill that landed on my patio is a serious piece of hardware, and after a month of weekly cooks — from lazy Sunday ribs to a 16-pound brisket — I've got a clear picture of who this smoker is actually for.

**What You're Actually Getting**

Box contents are straightforward: the Ironwood XL ships mostly assembled, with the legs, shelf, and chimney requiring maybe 45 minutes of work with two people. You get two porcelain-coated steel grates (roughly 600 sq in on the main level and 300 sq in upper), a stainless steel drip tray, two meat probes, and a grease bucket. The D2 controller with WiFIRE is the brains — it connects to the Traeger app via 2.4GHz Wi-Fi, tracks your cook, and lets you adjust temperature without walking outside.

The build quality is noticeably better than Traeger's cheaper lines. The 20-gauge steel body feels solid, the lid seals properly, and the folding front shelf can actually hold a full brisket tray without flexing. As shown in the product image, the Ironwood XL's silhouette is boxier than the classic barrel shape — that's the Downdraft Exhaust system, which pulls smoke down and across the meat before it exits the rear chimney.

**Real-World Performance**

Here's where the Ironwood XL earns its keep. The D2 controller holds temperature within 10°F of your set point, which is excellent for a pellet smoker. Fire it up, set it to 225°F, and you can walk away for hours. The Super Smoke mode (available under 225°F) runs the auger in a pulsed pattern that produces visibly thicker, bluer smoke — my pork shoulder came out with a smoke ring a quarter-inch deep, something my older Traeger never managed.

The 924 sq in of cook space is legitimately huge. I ran a full packer brisket on the lower grate and six racks of ribs on the upper simultaneously without touching. The app integration is genuinely useful: you can monitor both meat probes remotely, get notifications when the hopper runs low, and adjust temps mid-cook from your phone.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The Amazon reviews (200 ratings, 4.0 average) echo what I found. One buyer wrote, "The temperature control is rock solid — set it and forget it is real, not just marketing." Another praised the app: "Being able to check my pork butt from the office saved me from making a lunch trip home." The Downdraft system gets consistent credit for improving smoke flavor over Traeger's older models.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. This is a 200-pound machine — moving it around is a two-person job, and you'll want a dedicated patio spot with a nearby outlet. Pellet consumption is the other real cost. At 225°F, expect 1.5 to 2 pounds per hour; at searing temps north of 450°F, that number jumps past 3 pounds per hour. That 18-pound hopper will get you through a typical cook, but a long overnight brisket will have you refilling before sunrise.

The 4.0 average rating reflects some QC complaints — a handful of buyers reported loose auger connections or finicky Wi-Fi pairing on first setup. Traeger's customer service is decent but not instant, so budget time for potential troubleshooting.

**Who Should Buy This**

The Ironwood XL is for the home cook who wants serious smoking capacity without babysitting a stick burner. It's ideal if you feed crowds regularly, want to smoke and grill on one machine, and value being able to monitor a cook from your phone. It's not for you if you're on a budget, short on patio space, or want a true searing grill — it'll hit 500°F, but you're not getting a flame-kissed crust like a dedicated gas or charcoal grill.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the Ironwood XL really need the Traeger app?**
No, but you're leaving performance on the table. The controller works standalone, but the app gives you remote monitoring, probe readings, and Super Smoke control. It's worth setting up.

**Q: How many people does this feed?**
Realistically 12-20 people per cook, depending on what you're making. The upper rack lets you double up on smaller items like wings or sausages.

**Q: Can you use it in cold weather?**
Yes, down to about 20°F. The blanket insulation is built into the body, but you'll burn more pellets to maintain temperature in winter.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a large-capacity pellet smoker with excellent temperature control, don't mind the price of premium hardware, and plan to use it regularly for smoking and grilling.

**Skip it if:** You're a weekend-warrior griller who only fires up a few times a month, or you need something portable.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Ironwood XL delivers on its promise of set-and-forget smoking at scale. The pellet appetite and bulk are real tradeoffs, but for the dedicated backyard pitmaster, this is a legitimate upgrade that will last years.
