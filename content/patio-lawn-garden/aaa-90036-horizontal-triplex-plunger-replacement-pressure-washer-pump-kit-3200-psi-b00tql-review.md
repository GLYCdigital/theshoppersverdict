If your pressure washer pump just gave up mid-deck-cleaning, you're staring at a $200–400 repair bill for a machine that cost $300 new. The AAA 90036 horizontal triplex pump sidesteps that trap entirely. This is the component that resurrects dead pressure washers — and with 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's the most-discussed replacement pump in the budget tier. Here's what you actually need to know before clicking buy.

## What's in the Box

The kit includes the complete pump assembly, a thermal relief valve (though notably, not a thermal relief *bypass*—more on that below), and mounting bolts. No oil is included, so factor in a bottle of pump oil (SAE 30 or 10W-30 non-detergent) since the unit ships dry. The pump measures roughly 8.5 inches long and weighs about 9 pounds — the brass head is substantial, not the flimsy pressed metal you see on sub-$100 pumps.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The AAA 90036 is a genuine triplex plunger design, meaning three plungers driven by a wobble plate — this is the same architecture found in pumps costing twice as much. The brass head and stainless steel plungers are the standout features. Aluminum-head pumps corrode at the fittings within a season; brass doesn't. Buyers consistently note this durability: "Two years of heavy weekend use and it still holds pressure like day one," one verified purchaser reported.

Output is rated at 3200 PSI at 2.5 GPM. That's honest — owners testing with pressure gauges confirm it delivers within 10% of spec on a healthy engine. The pump runs smooth and quiet, with vibration levels noticeably lower than the wobble-plate aluminum units in this price range.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The installation fitment dominates positive reviews. The 3/4-inch keyed shaft with standard bolt pattern matches Honda GX160/GX200 engines and Harbor Freight Predator 212s directly. "Bolted right up to my 212 Predator in 20 minutes. No modification needed," reads one five-star review. Multiple buyers also confirm this is the same pump sold under the Powerhorse and Simpson brand names at 2–3x the price — it's a white-label part.

## Where It Falls Short

The quality control isn't flawless. Roughly 5–8% of reviews report seal leaks within the first few hours of use. It's not a design flaw — it's inconsistent assembly. If you get a leaker, Amazon's return policy covers it, but it's an annoyance when you've already drained your pump oil and mounted everything.

The metric threads on the inlet/outlet are the real gotcha. Standard American pressure washer hoses use NPT threads. Unless you already own metric adapters, budget an extra $15–20 for fittings. And the missing thermal bypass valve means the pump recirculates water internally when the trigger is released — run it idle for more than a minute and you'll cook the seals. This is a design omission, not a defect, but it's worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners with a dead big-box pressure washer** — if your engine runs but the pump is shot, this is your cheapest resurrection path.
- **Owners of Honda or Predator engine machines** — direct bolt-on fitment, no fabrication needed.
- **Anyone buying a used pressure washer** — a $120 pump turns a $50 junker into a reliable machine.

Skip it if you need commercial duty cycles (8+ hours daily) or if you're not comfortable with basic wrench work — there are no quick-disconnect fittings here.

## FAQ

**Will this fit my Craftsman/Generac pressure washer?**
If your engine has a 3/4-inch keyed shaft and the pump mounts with four bolts on 3-inch centers, yes. Measure before ordering — some Briggs & Stratton engines use a different shaft taper.

**What oil should I use?**
Non-detergent SAE 30 or 10W-30. The pump holds about 14 ounces. Change it after the first 50 hours, then annually.

**Does it come with the unloader valve?**
Yes, the unloader is pre-installed and adjustable. Set it to 3200 PSI max for the rated output.
