## Where It Falls Short

Be direct about these limitations:

**Larger birds can push through** — The 0.4-inch mesh blocks small birds, but crows and jays have been reported pecking through when the netting isn't taught. You'll need proper hoops or frames, not just draping it loose.

**Drip irrigation compatibility** — The netting has no grommets or access flaps. You must install drip lines before securing the netting, which is inconvenient for mid-season adjustments.

**Dust and pollen accumulation** — In dry climates, the fine mesh collects dust that reduces light penetration. Plan to rinse it occasionally with a garden hose.

## Who Should Buy This

This netting suits **home vegetable gardeners** protecting berries, brassicas, and leafy greens from birds and common insects. It's also excellent for **cut flower growers** who need pest barriers without ugly hardware.

Skip it if you're dealing with **heavy deer pressure** (you need 6-foot fencing, not netting) or if you need a permanent, multi-year solution — this is seasonal protection.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this netting harm birds or small animals?**
A: No. The mesh is soft and flexible. Birds can't get entangled like they can with loose monofilament netting. It's a physical barrier, not a trap.

**Q: How do I keep the netting off my plants?**
A: Use PVC hoops or rebar arches. Draping directly on plants causes abrasion and can trap humidity against leaves, promoting fungal issues.

**Q: Does it survive winter storage?**
A: Yes, if you fold it dry and store it out of direct UV light. Expect 2-3 seasons of regular use with proper care.
