Most pool poles are either too short to reach the deep end or so flimsy they bend under pressure. The Kokido Telescopic Pool Pole tries to solve both problems with a 16- to 32-foot aluminum extension system. After digging through 229 ratings and the technical specs, here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The pole ships in four interlocking anodized aluminum sections. Twist-lock collars connect each segment, and the whole thing collapses down to roughly 6 feet for storage. A universal thread adapter screws onto the business end, accepting both standard 1-1/4-inch and fine-thread pool brush heads — no separate adapters to lose in the garage.

The aluminum construction is the standout feature. At full extension, the pole maintains rigidity that cheaper fiberglass models lose after a few seasons. The anodized finish resists the corrosion that plagues bare aluminum poles in chlorinated environments. As one buyer noted, *"I've had this for two summers now and it still telescopes smoothly — my previous poles would seize up by June."*

**Real-World Performance**

The locking mechanism deserves scrutiny since it's the most common failure point on telescoping poles. Kokido uses a cam-lock twist system rather than the push-button detents found on budget poles. This design distributes pressure evenly around the collar, which prevents the "sudden collapse" problem you get when a spring-loaded button gives out mid-sweep.

That said, the twist-locks require deliberate effort. Each collar needs a firm clockwise turn to seat properly, and you'll want both hands for the job. If you're switching between skimming and brushing mid-session, the adjustment dance gets old fast. Several reviewers mentioned this exact friction point, though most considered it a fair trade for the stability.

The 32-foot maximum reach is genuinely useful for above-ground pools with wide diameters. For in-ground pools, you'll rarely extend past 20 feet, which means the pole operates well within its structural limits — less flex, more control when scrubbing algae off the deep-end walls.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

- **Longevity:** Multiple reviewers report 2+ years of regular use without the locking collars stripping or the aluminum pitting.
- **Stiffness at length:** Unlike fiberglass poles that whip and bend, this one tracks straight even with a heavy brush attached.
- **Universal fit:** The threaded end works with virtually every brand of pool accessory — no proprietary lock-in.

**Where It Falls Short**

The lack of a comfortable grip is the most common complaint. The bottom section is bare anodized aluminum, which gets slick when your hands are wet and sunscreen-coated. A foam grip or rubberized sleeve would cost pennies to manufacture and make a meaningful difference in control. Aftermarket handle wraps exist, but you shouldn't need to buy one on a pole at this price point.

The 6-foot collapsed length also makes storage awkward for smaller sheds or garage corners. It won't fit in most trunk-mounted pool equipment bags either, so don't plan on taking it to a friend's pool without a roof rack.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Above-ground pool owners** with wide diameters who need to reach the center without wading in
- **In-ground pool owners** who want a single pole that handles both skimming and deep-wall brushing
- **Anyone replacing a fiberglass pole** that bent or corroded within a season

**Skip it if** you prefer one-handed quick-adjust poles, or if you have a small pool where 16 feet of reach is overkill — a fixed-length pole will serve you fine for less money.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my existing pool brush?**  
A: Almost certainly. The universal thread adapter accepts both standard and fine-thread fittings. If your brush has one of those threaded ends, it'll screw on.

**Q: Does it float if dropped in the pool?**  
A: No. Aluminum sinks. Keep a firm grip, or attach a floating retrieval device if you're prone to dropping tools.

**Q: How long does assembly take?**  
A: Under a minute. Slide the sections together, twist each collar until snug, and you're ready to clean. No tools required.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need serious reach without the flex and corrosion issues that plague cheaper poles. **Skip it if** you want a lightweight pole for quick daily skimming — the tradeoff for rigidity is heft and slower adjustment.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid, durable, and built to last. The grip issue and two-hand locking are real annoyances, but they don't undermine the core job: getting your pool clean without the pole fighting you.
