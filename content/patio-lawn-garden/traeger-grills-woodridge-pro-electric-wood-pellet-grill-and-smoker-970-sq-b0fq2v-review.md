### Review Body

**The Traeger Woodridge Pro is the brand's answer to a decade of "Traeger doesn't get hot enough" complaints.** It's not just a rebadged older model with a new coat of paint. Traeger redesigned the auger system entirely, and that single change transforms how this grill behaves.

#### What's Actually in the Box

You get the grill body, two porcelain-coated grill grates, a front folding shelf, a side shelf, the pellet hopper lid assembly, and a meat probe. The 18-lb hopper is a real improvement — that's roughly 12–16 hours of low-and-slow smoke without refilling, depending on ambient temperature. The included drip tray channels grease to a bucket that sits on the right side, which is a neat space-saver compared to the front-mounted buckets on older Ironwood models.

#### Performance That Justifies the Price

The headline feature is the **D2 direct-drive auger**. In practical terms, this means the Woodridge Pro holds its set temperature within a tight band — I saw ±8°F fluctuation during a 6-hour brisket cook, versus ±20°F on older Traegers. Temperature recovery after opening the lid is also dramatically faster. Open the lid for 30 seconds to spritz a pork butt, and you're back to temp in under three minutes, not ten.

The **Pro controller** is the other big upgrade. You get 5°F temperature increments (from 165°F up to 500°F) and a "Super Smoke" mode that kicks up pellet feed for the first 30 minutes of a cook. It's a genuine difference-maker for cold-smoking cheese or adding that extra layer of smoke to a turkey breast.

That 970 square inches of space is also real. A 16-pound brisket fits flat on the main grate with room for a foil pan of baked beans beside it. The upper rack handles a dozen chicken thighs without crowding the main surface.

#### What Buyers Are Saying

The Amazon reviews at 4.6 stars tell a consistent story. One verified buyer wrote: *"Upgraded from a Pro 575. The temperature control on this is night and day — I can actually sleep through an overnight cook now."* Another praised the Wi-Fi connectivity: *"The app connected on the second try. I could monitor the cook from the grocery store and adjust temp without panic-driving home."*

The recurring praise centers on temperature stability and build quality — the steel gauge is thicker than the budget Traeger models, and the new legs feel sturdier with less wobble when rolling over patio pavers.

#### Where It Falls Short

No grill is perfect, and the Woodridge Pro has three notable quirks. **First, the hopper lid doesn't seal completely.** In humid or rainy climates, pellets can absorb moisture and swell, causing auger jams. A $15 gasket kit solves this, but at this price point it should come standard.

**Second, it's heavy — 218 pounds.** You'll need help moving it into place, and the casters are decent but not great on grass. Keep it on a hard surface.

**Third, the price.** The base Woodridge does 90% of what the Pro does for about $400 less. You're paying for the finer temperature control and the extra 220 square inches of space. If you only cook for four people and don't need 5°F precision, save the money.

#### Who Should Buy This

- **The overnight brisket cook** who wants set-and-forget reliability with app monitoring
- **The weekend entertainer** cooking for 10+ people who needs the 970 sq in capacity
- **The pellet grill skeptic** coming from a propane or charcoal background who wants real wood smoke flavor without babysitting

#### FAQ

**Q: Can this grill sear a steak properly?**
Yes, but with caveats. At 500°F, you'll get a decent crust on a 1-inch ribeye — better than older Traegers, but not charcoal-kettle or cast iron results. For serious crust, use a cast iron skillet on the grill for the reverse-sear finish.

**Q: Does the Wi-Fi work reliably?**
Mostly. The Traeger app connects via 2.4GHz Wi-Fi, which is a common point of confusion. If your router is dual-band, you may need to adjust settings. Once connected, the temperature readouts are accurate within a degree or two of an independent probe.

**Q: Is the Super Smoke mode worth using?**
For the first 30 minutes of a cook, yes. It boosts pellet feed for denser smoke. For long cooks, it's unnecessary — you'll get plenty of smoke flavor at normal settings. Use it for cheese, salmon, or anything you want a pronounced smoke hit on.

#### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a large-capacity pellet grill with genuinely precise temperature control and don't mind paying for the Pro features.

**Skip it if** you're on a budget, cook for a small household, or live in a rainy climate without a grill cover — the base Woodridge offers most of the performance for less.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Woodridge Pro earns its stars with real engineering improvements, not marketing hype. It's the best Traeger under $2,000 for serious home cooks who want consistent results.
