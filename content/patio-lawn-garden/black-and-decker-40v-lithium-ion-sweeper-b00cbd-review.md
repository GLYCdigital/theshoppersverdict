If you've ever wrestled a push broom across a two-car driveway only to watch dust resettle five minutes later, the Black and Decker 40V Lithium Ion Sweeper is aimed squarely at your frustration. This is not a leaf blower and not a shop vac — it's a powered push sweeper that flicks debris into a rear collection tray as you walk. With 225 ratings holding a 4.5-star average, it's clearly doing something right. The question is whether that something matches your specific yard situation.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the sweeper unit, a 40V lithium-ion battery, and the charger. That battery inclusion matters — plenty of competitors sell bare tools and make you pay another $100+ for power. The sweeper itself features a brushless motor, which is genuinely notable at this price. Brushless means fewer moving parts to wear out and better torque consistency when you're pushing through a layer of dried oak leaves.

The collection hopper is rear-mounted and detaches with one lever for emptying. You'll also get a pivoting head that angles side-to-side, letting you sweep along fence lines and under patio furniture without re-aiming the whole machine. The height adjusts via a simple knob, no tools required.

## How It Performs in Real Use

On dry concrete, asphalt, and pavers, this thing earns its keep. The rotating brush flicks debris — pine needles, grass clippings, acorns, gravel — into the hopper efficiently. You walk at a normal pace, not a forced shuffle. The 40V motor has enough grunt that you don't need to slow down for moderate debris density.

Battery life is the headline number: roughly 40 minutes of continuous sweeping on a full charge. That covers a standard suburban driveway plus walkways and a patio in one pass. Recharge time runs about 60 minutes, which is reasonable for a mid-session break.

The hopper holds a solid amount — you won't be emptying it after every pass. That said, if you're clearing a heavily treed half-acre, plan for three or four hopper dumps.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reviewers repeatedly highlight how much time it saves versus manual brooms. One verified owner put it plainly: *"I swept my entire driveway in 10 minutes — used to take me 40 with a broom."* Another noted the battery genuinely lasts: *"I did my driveway, sidewalk, and patio on one charge and still had power left."*

The pivoting brush gets frequent mentions too. People with pergolas, outdoor furniture, and tight corner spaces appreciate not having to crouch or reposition constantly. The tool-free height adjustment also earns credit from users with uneven concrete where the brush needs to sit slightly higher to avoid catching joints.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about limitations. This is a dry-debris machine. Wet leaves clump and jam the brush — you'll stop, flip the unit, and pull matted gunk out of the roller. Wet mud is worse; it cakes the brush and requires hosing down. If your fall cleanups happen after rain, this is the wrong tool.

The plastic housing is functional but not rugged. Several reviewers mention it feels lighter-duty than commercial sweepers, which is fair. It's built for homeowner use, not daily landscaping work. Drop it off a tailgate and you may crack the hopper mounts.

Runtime also drops when you hit thick, matted leaf layers. The motor works harder, the brush bogs slightly, and you lose a few minutes of effective charge. Buyers with heavy tree cover report closer to 25–30 minutes in those conditions.

## Who Should Buy This

This sweeper suits the homeowner with a paved driveway, concrete walkways, or a paver patio who wants to eliminate the dust-cloud broom routine. It's ideal for spring pollen season, post-mow grass clipping cleanup, and light-to-moderate fall leaves on hard surfaces.

Skip it if your property is mostly gravel (the brush will fling stones everywhere) or if you're clearing a massive leaf load weekly — you'd be better served by a leaf blower or a commercial-grade sweeper with a larger hopper and sturdier build.

## FAQ

**Can it handle wet leaves?**
No. Wet debris jams the brush and requires manual cleaning. Keep it to dry conditions.

**Does it work on grass?**
Not effectively. The brush is designed for hard, flat surfaces. On turf it digs in and stalls.

**How long does the battery actually last?**
Expect 30–40 minutes depending on debris density. Light sweeping on clean concrete hits the high end; thick leaves drop it to the low end.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're tired of manual brooms on paved surfaces and want a cordless, battery-included solution that genuinely saves time. **Skip it if** your debris is often wet, you need to sweep gravel, or you require commercial-grade durability.

At 4.6/5, this is the right tool for the right job — just make sure that job is dry pavement.
