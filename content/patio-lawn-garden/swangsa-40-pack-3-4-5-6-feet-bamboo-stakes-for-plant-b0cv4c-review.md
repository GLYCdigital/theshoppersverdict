## Real-World Performance

Bamboo is bamboo — it's lightweight, strong for its weight, and naturally rot-resistant for a season or two. In practice, these stakes push into soft garden soil with minimal effort. The surface is sanded smooth enough that you won't fight splinters when handling them, which matters more than you'd think after a few hours in the garden.

For most plants — peas, beans, dahlias, small tomatoes — these hold up fine. The jute twine ties securely without cutting into stems, and it degrades naturally by end of season, which is a nice touch for composters.

But here's the honest tradeoff: bamboo tapers. A 6-foot stake is maybe 0.5 inches at the base but noticeably thinner at the top. If you're growing heavy beefsteak tomatoes or a vigorous cucumber trellis, the top third of those tall stakes can bend under load. One Amazon reviewer noted, "The 6-foot stakes are great for support but I had to double up two stakes per plant for my heavy Brandywines." That's not a dealbreaker — it's just physics — but plan accordingly.

Second, there's no storage solution. The stakes arrive as a loose bundle wrapped in plastic. After the season, you'll need to find your own way to keep them organized — a bucket, a PVC pipe holder, or a rubber band. Minor, but worth noting.

Third, quality control isn't perfect. A handful of reviewers reported 1–2 split stakes per pack. SWANGSA's return policy covers it, but it's an inconvenience if you're mid-planting.
