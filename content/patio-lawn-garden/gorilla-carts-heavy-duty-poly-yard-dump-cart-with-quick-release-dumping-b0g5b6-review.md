The Gorilla Carts Heavy-Duty Poly Yard Dump Cart is the kind of tool that makes you wonder how you ever hauled mulch in a wheelbarrow. With a 600-pound capacity, a genuinely functional quick-release dump, and wheels that actually roll over lumpy grass, this cart solves the three biggest frustrations of yard work hauling. But it’s not perfect — and depending on your property, one missing feature could be a dealbreaker.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the cart frame, the poly bed, two 10-inch pneumatic tires (mounted on 13-inch wheels), the handle assembly, and the hardware kit. You’ll also find the quick-release dump lever pre-attached to the frame — that’s the piece that makes this cart different from a standard utility wagon. Assembly is straightforward but not tool-free: you’ll need a 10mm wrench and a socket set, and you should budget 30–40 minutes if you’re not mechanically inclined. The instructions are clear, but the bolt holes on the handle bracket don’t always line up perfectly — a little patience and a rubber mallet helps.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature is the quick-release dumping mechanism. Pull the lever on the handle, and the bed pivots forward to dump its load. In practice, it works exactly as advertised — one hand pulls, the bed tilts, and 600 pounds of soil or mulch slides out in a controlled pour. It’s not a violent tip like some carts; it’s more of a smooth, deliberate dump that you can partially control by how far you pull the lever. That’s a meaningful design win.

The 600-pound capacity is the real story. The steel frame is welded in all the right places, and the poly bed is UV-treated so it won’t fade or become brittle after a summer in the sun. The 10-inch pneumatic tires are the unsung heroes here — they’re wide enough to distribute weight, and the tread grips wet grass without sinking in. One reviewer noted, “I filled it to the brim with wet leaves and it rolled across my sloped backyard like it was empty.” That matches my experience: the cart tracks straight even when loaded unevenly.

## Honest Shortcomings

The most significant gap is the absence of brakes. On any slope steeper than about 10 degrees, a fully loaded 600-pound cart will develop momentum that you cannot stop with the handle alone. You end up wrestling it down hills or parking it sideways. This is a real limitation for anyone with a sloped property. If you have a flat yard, it’s a non-issue; if you don’t, budget for a chock block or a bungee-tied brake modification.

Second, the poly bed scratches easily. If you’re hauling jagged rocks, broken concrete, or using a shovel to unload, you’ll see surface scuffs within the first few uses. They don’t affect function, but they do make the cart look older than it is. Third, the tires arrive inflated to about half pressure — you’ll need a pump before first use, and it’s easy to miss that in the setup instructions.

## Who Should Buy This

This cart is ideal for suburban and rural homeowners with at least a quarter-acre of property who routinely move mulch, soil, compost, or firewood. It’s also excellent for gardeners who want to avoid repeated wheelbarrow trips. Skip it if you have a steeply sloped yard, if you need to haul the cart in and out of a truck bed (it’s heavy — about 60 pounds empty), or if you only move light loads a few times a year — a $50 wheelbarrow would suffice.
