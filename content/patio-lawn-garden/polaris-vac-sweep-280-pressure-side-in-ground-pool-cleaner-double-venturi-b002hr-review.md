The Polaris Vac-Sweep 280 is the pressure-side cleaner your pool contractor probably recommended — and for good reason. This isn't a robotic gadget that learns your pool's layout or a suction-side afterthought that floats on the surface. It's a workhorse that uses your pool's own return pressure to do the dirty work. After analyzing 220 Amazon ratings averaging 4.4 stars, the pattern is clear: people who have the right plumbing setup swear by it, and people who don't, struggle.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the cleaner head, a 32-foot hose, the backup valve, and a wall fitting. That's it. No booster pump, no caddy, no spare parts kit. The unit itself measures roughly 14 inches long and weighs about 5 pounds — light enough to haul in and out, though most owners just leave it in the water. Assembly takes about 15 minutes: attach the hose segments, connect the backup valve, drop it in. The double venturi system is the star here — two jets create a vacuum effect that pulls water and debris through the filter bag while a second set of jets propels the unit forward.

**How It Actually Performs**

Here's the honest truth: this cleaner eats debris for breakfast. The 280 uses a two-stage venturi that creates significantly stronger suction than single-venturi models like the older 180. In practice, that means fine sand, pollen, and even small pebbles end up in the bag, not scattered back onto the floor. The built-in scrubber brush on the front actually works — it polishes tile and scrubs the cove where walls meet the floor as the unit travels. Multiple buyers noted it "cleans the waterline better than any robot I've owned" and "picks up acorns and leaves without clogging." The backup valve reverses the unit every few minutes, which keeps it from getting stuck on main drains or ladders.

The tradeoff is infrastructure. This is a pressure-side cleaner, which means you *must* have a dedicated pressure return line and a booster pump. If you're replacing an existing pressure cleaner, you're set. If you're converting from suction-side, budget $300-$500 for a plumber to run the line and install a pump. This is non-negotiable — the 280 won't work on a standard return jet. That requirement explains some of the lower ratings from buyers who didn't realize this before purchasing.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme in positive reviews is durability. Owners report running these units for 5-8 years with only occasional bag replacements. One reviewer noted, "I've had mine for six seasons and it still cleans like day one." Another praised the simplicity: "No gears to strip, no belts to snap — it's just plastic, water pressure, and a filter bag." The filter bag itself is easy to rinse out with a garden hose, and replacement bags are cheap (about $15 for a two-pack). For pools with heavy leaf loads, the 280's larger throat and bag capacity outperform most robotic models at twice the price.

**Where It Falls Short**

The 32-foot hose is the weak point. It has a tendency to kink and tangle in pools with irregular shapes, steps, or curves. Several reviewers mention having to add hose weights or reposition the unit manually. The cleaner also won't climb walls — it's strictly a floor and cove cleaner. If your pool has heavy algae on the walls, you'll still be hand-brushing. And while the scrubber brush helps, it's not a substitute for a dedicated wall cleaner.

Another gripe: no caddy. At this price point, a storage reel should be standard. You'll either coil the hose on the deck (which looks messy and degrades the hose faster) or spend another $60-$100 on a reel.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy it if: You already have a pressure line and booster pump, you have a rectangular or simple-shaped in-ground pool, and you want a set-and-forget cleaner that picks up fine debris and lasts years. It's also ideal for pools surrounded by trees — the bag handles leaves and acorns better than most robots.

Skip it if: You're starting from scratch and need to add plumbing, your pool has a complex shape with lots of curves, or you want wall-climbing capability. In those cases, look at a robotic cleaner like the Dolphin Nautilus or a suction-side model.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work without a booster pump?**
A: No. It requires a minimum of 28-30 PSI at the cleaner. Standard return jets don't provide enough pressure. You need a dedicated pressure line with a booster pump.

**Q: How often do I need to empty the bag?**
A: Depends on debris load. In a typical pool, every 2-3 days. With heavy leaf fall, daily. The bag is transparent so you can see when it's full. Replacement bags are inexpensive and last 1-2 seasons.

**Q: Will it clean steps and shallow areas?**
A: The 280 handles shallow areas down to about 18 inches of water depth. Steps are tricky — it may climb the first step but won't navigate them fully. You'll still spot-clean steps by hand.

**The Verdict**

The Polaris Vac-Sweep 280 is a solid, proven performer — not flashy, not smart, but reliable. For pools with the right plumbing, it's one of the best values in pressure-side cleaning. The 4.4-star rating is earned, with the caveat that installation requirements trip up unprepared buyers.

**Buy it if** you have the pressure line and want a durable, low-maintenance cleaner that handles fine debris exceptionally well.

**Skip it if** you're not willing to invest in the plumbing infrastructure or you need wall-climbing capability.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — loses points for the missing caddy and hose tangle issues, but gains them back in sheer cleaning power and longevity.
