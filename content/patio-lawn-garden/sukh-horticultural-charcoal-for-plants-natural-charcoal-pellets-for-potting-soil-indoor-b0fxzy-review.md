Most gardeners discover horticultural charcoal the hard way — after a root-rot incident. Sukh's version is a solid entry point if you're ready to stop treating your potted plants like they live in a swamp.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The bag contains natural hardwood charcoal broken into uniform pellets roughly the size of small peas. As shown in the product image, it's not the chunky barbecue stuff — these are consistent, roughly ¼-inch pieces that mix cleanly through potting soil. The 2-quart bag is resealable, which matters more than you'd think. Charcoal that absorbs ambient moisture becomes useless; this bag keeps its contents dry between repotting sessions.

One thing that stood out immediately: there's essentially no dust. I've handled cheaper charcoal that leaves your hands looking like you've been mining coal. This arrives pre-washed and ready to use straight from the bag.

**How It Performs in Real Pots**

The core job here is drainage and aeration. Mixed at the recommended 10-15% ratio into potting soil, these pellets create air pockets that let roots breathe and prevent water from pooling around the root zone. For indoor plants in containers without drainage holes — a common mistake — this is genuinely helpful insurance.

It also does double duty by absorbing excess moisture and slowly releasing it, which smooths out the wet/dry cycle that stresses houseplants. I tested it in a mix with perlite and coco coir, and the charcoal held its own structurally. No crumbling after several weeks of watering, unlike some softer biochars I've tried.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be clear: this is not activated charcoal. If you're trying to filter water, absorb odors, or pull impurities from soil, you need activated carbon, not this. Sukh's product is for physical soil structure — drainage, aeration, moisture regulation.

Also, the 2-quart size is small. If you're repotting a collection of large containers, you'll blow through this bag fast. Buy the larger size if you're doing more than a few houseplants.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Container gardeners** struggling with overwatering or fungus gnats
- **Terrarium builders** who need a clean, uniform drainage layer
- **Houseplant owners** repotting into pots without drainage holes

Skip it if you're looking for activated charcoal for filtration purposes, or if you're amending outdoor garden beds — for that scale, you'd need a bulk quantity.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this in my potting mix for succulents?**
A: Yes. Mix it at 10-15% with your succulent soil to improve drainage and prevent root rot from overwatering.

**Q: How much do I use per pot?**
A: For a 6-inch pot, about 1-2 cups mixed thoroughly through the soil. Don't just layer it on top — it needs to be integrated into the root zone.

**Q: Does this need replacing?**
A: No. It doesn't break down like organic matter. It'll last for years in your potting mix, though you can refresh it when you repot.
