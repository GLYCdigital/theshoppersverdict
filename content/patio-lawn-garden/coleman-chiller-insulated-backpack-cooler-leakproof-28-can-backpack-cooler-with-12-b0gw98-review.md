Most backpack coolers are either glorified lunch bags or overpriced gear with a logo. The Coleman Chiller 28-Can sits somewhere smarter: it's a legitimately leakproof, comfortable pack that doesn't pretend to be something it isn't. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, it's not perfect — but for the price, it punches well above its weight.

**What you actually get**

The Chiller is a 28-can capacity backpack cooler with a welded, leakproof liner (Coleman's claim, and in testing, it holds). The exterior is a rugged polyester that shrugs off sand and gravel. You get adjustable padded shoulder straps, a padded back panel, and a front zippered compartment that opens wide enough to see what you've packed. The dimensions are roughly 17 x 12 x 8 inches — compact enough to fit under a stadium seat, but deep enough to swallow a full 12-pack plus ice.

**Real-world performance**

I loaded it with 24 cans, a bag of ice, and two sandwiches. The straps distributed weight well — no digging into shoulders after a 20-minute walk to the beach. The leakproof claim is the headline feature, and it's earned: I left it on its side in the trunk for three hours, and the interior stayed dry outside the liner. That's the difference between this and cheaper soft coolers.

Ice retention is where it gets honest. Coleman rates this for 12-18 hours, and that tracks. In 85°F heat, ice was mostly melted by hour 14. That's fine for a beach day, but if you're camping overnight, this isn't your cooler. It's a day-tripper.

The front zipper is the one design frustration. Because it's a backpack, you're accessing from the front panel, not the top. That means digging through the top layer to reach the bottom cans. It's manageable, but it's not elegant.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The leakproof liner is the recurring hero in reviews. One buyer wrote, "I've had three other backpack coolers and all leaked. This one actually doesn't — I can throw it in the back of my truck without worry." Another echoed the comfort: "Carried it a mile to the lake fully loaded and my back wasn't sore."

**Where it falls short**

No external pockets is a genuine miss. Keys, phone, wallet — they're going inside with your drinks or in your pants. That's a design oversight at this price point.

The insulation is adequate, not exceptional. Competitors like Yeti or Engel offer multi-day ice retention, but they cost three times as much and weigh twice as much. This is a tradeoff, not a flaw — but know it going in.

**Who should buy this**

- **Beach and pool goers** who need hands-free carrying and don't want soggy trunks
- **Tailgaters** walking from the parking lot to the stadium
- **Day hikers** who pack lunch and drinks, not overnight expeditions
- **Parents** hauling snacks and juice boxes for a park day

**Skip it if** you need multi-day ice retention, if you want organization pockets, or if you're carrying more than 28 cans — at that point, get a wheeled cooler.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it actually hold 28 cans with ice?**
A: Tightly, yes. 28 cans with minimal ice fits. With a full bag of ice, expect closer to 20-24 cans. That's standard for any cooler.

**Q: Is the liner really leakproof?**
A: Yes, in real-world testing. The welded liner is genuinely sealed. It's the strongest feature of this cooler.

**Q: How long does ice last?**
A: 12-18 hours in moderate heat, per Coleman and confirmed by user reports. Don't expect overnight performance.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, leakproof, comfortable backpack cooler for day trips — and you don't need freezer-grade insulation.

**Skip it if** you need multi-day ice retention or want organizational pockets.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, honest performer that nails the essentials and skips the extras. Coleman got the fundamentals right.
