If you've been eyeing that pile of wet oak leaves in your driveway and dreading the gas blower's pull cord, the RYOBI ONE+ 18V 100 MPH 325 CFM blower is worth a serious look. This isn't a toy — it's a legitimate leaf-clearing machine that happens to run on the same batteries as your drill. But it's not perfect, and I'll tell you exactly where it stumbles.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the blower body, a concentrator nozzle tip (useful for tight spots and stubborn debris), and an operator's manual. No battery or charger included — this is a bare tool, so it's only a smart buy if you already own RYOBI ONE+ batteries. If you're starting from scratch, factor in the cost of at least a 4Ah battery and a charger.

**Build and ergonomics**

The unit weighs 6.4 pounds with a 4Ah battery installed. That's heavier than some competitors, but the weight distribution is decent — the handle sits near the center of gravity. The variable speed dial is on the top rear, easy to thumb even with work gloves on. The trigger is progressive, so you can feather it for delicate work like blowing dust off a patio without launching mulch everywhere. The tube is 22 inches long, giving you good reach without bending over.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this on a mix of dry grass clippings, wet maple leaves, and gravel driveway debris. At full blast, the 100 MPH rating is honest — I moved a six-foot pile of damp leaves in about two minutes. The 325 CFM volume is the real star here; it moves a broad swath of air rather than a narrow jet, so you clear more ground per pass than with cheaper blowers.

That said, battery life is the tradeoff. On a 4Ah battery, I got roughly 14 minutes of continuous full-speed operation before the low-battery light kicked in. Dropping to medium speed extends that to about 25 minutes, which is fine for most suburban lots. If you have a larger property, you'll want a second battery or the 6Ah option.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Amazon reviewers with 225 ratings at 4.5 stars echo my experience. One buyer wrote, *"I was skeptical a battery blower could replace my gas one. It did. I cleared a half-acre of leaves in 20 minutes on a single 4Ah charge."* Another noted, *"The variable speed is the killer feature — I can blow off my deck without dusting the neighbors."* The most common compliment is the convenience: no gas, no pull cords, instant start every time.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond battery life, the noise is a legitimate gripe. At full power, it registers around 65 dB — not ear-splitting, but louder than some cordless rivals like the EGO 580 CFM. Wear ear protection for extended use. Also, the blower is noticeably front-heavy with larger batteries. After 15 minutes of overhead work (clearing gutters, blowing off a roof), my forearm was tired. The lack of a shoulder strap is a missed opportunity.

**Who should buy this**

- **RYOBI ONE+ owners** — This is a no-brainer if you already have batteries. It's the most affordable way to add a powerful blower to your arsenal.
- **Suburban homeowners** — If your yard is under a quarter-acre and you have a few trees, this handles fall cleanup without the gas hassle.
- **Apartment/condo dwellers** — Quiet enough for a patio or balcony, and it stores flat in a closet.

Skip it if you're clearing acreage or dealing with heavy, matted pine needles weekly — you'll want a backpack blower or a corded model for endurance.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this run with my old RYOBI 18V batteries?**  
A: Yes. Any RYOBI ONE+ battery from the last decade works. Older 1.5Ah packs will give you about 6 minutes of full power — enough for a quick driveway sweep but not much else.

**Q: How does it compare to a gas blower?**  
A: It's quieter, lighter, and zero-maintenance, but it won't match a 200+ MPH gas backpack blower for heavy, wet debris. For typical leaf blowing, it's more than adequate.

**Q: Can I use it to clean out my garage or shop?**  
A: Absolutely. The variable speed dial makes it ideal for blowing sawdust or dirt out of corners without creating a tornado.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're in the RYOBI ecosystem and want a genuinely powerful, convenient blower for typical yard work. **Skip it if** you need marathon battery life or have a big property — you'll be swapping batteries constantly.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The power-to-price ratio is excellent, and the variable speed control is the best in class. The only real penalties are battery thirst and noise. For most homeowners, this is the last blower you'll need to buy.
