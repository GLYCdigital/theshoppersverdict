**The Short Version:** If you're tired of renting a gas pressure washer every spring or fighting with a weak 1,200 PSI unit that just smears dirt around, the Westinghouse WPX2700e deserves a hard look. It's an electric unit that actually delivers gas-washer cleaning power without the noise, fumes, or maintenance headaches.

**What You Actually Get**

Out of the box, Westinghouse includes the pressure washer, a 25-foot high-pressure hose, a spray gun with a 3-foot lance, and five quick-connect nozzles (0°, 15°, 25°, 40°, and soap). There's also a 35-foot power cord with a GFCI plug — which is non-negotiable for any electric washer sold today. The unit itself sits on a wheeled cart with a telescoping handle, so it rolls like a piece of luggage rather than dragging like a cement block.

As shown in the product image, the WPX2700e has a low-profile, stable frame. The 10-inch wheels are never-flat — a detail that matters more than you'd think once you're pulling this thing over a gravel path or up a curb.

**Performance: Where the Rubber Meets the Concrete**

Here's the spec that matters: 2,700 PSI at 1.76 GPM. That's not marketing math — that's genuinely useful cleaning power. On a test run, the 25-degree nozzle stripped years of moss and grime off a south-facing concrete patio without needing to hover over any one spot. The 0-degree nozzle will etch concrete if you're not careful — respect it.

The brushless induction motor is the real highlight. Unlike the universal motors found in cheaper electric washers, this one doesn't scream like a leaf blower. It hums at a conversational volume, and it doesn't bog down when the trigger is held for extended periods. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "Quiet enough to use at 7 AM without the neighbors hating me."

The auto-stop feature kicks in when you release the trigger, which saves power and extends motor life. The unit also has a built-in thermal protection that shuts down before the pump overheats during long sessions — a failure point on many budget washers.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the 225 ratings (4.5 stars), the recurring themes are power and usability. A verified buyer wrote: "I've owned three pressure washers. This is the first one that didn't feel like a toy. It cleaned my driveway in an afternoon." Another noted, "The wheels and handle make it actually portable — I can drag it around my whole property without lifting."

The five-nozzle setup covers nearly every job: 40° for windows and cars, 25° for siding, 15° for patios, and 0° for stubborn stains. The soap nozzle works fine with a siphon hose, though you'll need to supply your own detergent.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the limitations. The 25-foot hose is functional but on the short side for larger properties. You'll likely need an extension cord (the 35-foot cord helps, but it's not enough for a big driveway). Make sure you use a 12-gauge or thicker cord rated for outdoor use — the manual is explicit about this, and ignoring it will cause nuisance trips on the GFCI.

There's no onboard detergent tank. For a unit at this price point, that's a minor annoyance. You can add a foam cannon (about $20) and solve the problem, but it's an extra purchase you shouldn't have to make.

A few reviewers noted that the plastic fittings on the spray gun feel less durable than the rest of the unit. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're rough on equipment, budget for a metal replacement gun down the line.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The patio-and-siding homeowner:** You clean concrete, vinyl siding, decks, and cars 4-6 times a year. You want gas-level power without storing a gas engine.
- **The noise-sensitive user:** If you have close neighbors or early-morning cleaning sessions, the quiet motor is a genuine advantage.
- **First-time pressure washer buyers:** The learning curve is gentle, the nozzles are color-coded, and the auto-stop prevents rookie mistakes.

**Skip It If:**

- You have a 2,000+ square foot driveway and no exterior water spigot within 50 feet — the hose/cord combo will frustrate you.
- You need hot water capability — this is cold-water only, and no electric unit in this class does hot water.
- You're a pro contractor — rent a gas unit or step up to a belt-drive model.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use it to wash my car?**
Yes, but use the 40° nozzle and keep the wand at least 12 inches from the paint. The 2,700 PSI is more than enough to strip wax or etch clear coat if you get careless. A foam cannon attachment helps add lubrication before rinsing.

**Q: Does it come with a detergent tank?**
No. It has a siphon hose for soap, but you'll need to supply your own detergent and either use the soap nozzle or a foam cannon. It's a minor inconvenience, not a functional flaw.

**Q: How loud is it really?**
Measured around 68-72 dB at the operator's ear — roughly the volume of a normal conversation. That's dramatically quieter than any gas unit (85-90 dB) and quieter than most electric washers with brushed motors.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy It**

**Buy it if:** You want a powerful, quiet, low-maintenance electric washer that handles real cleaning jobs without the gas-engine hassle. The brushless motor, solid cart design, and genuine 2,700 PSI output make it one of the best values in the electric class right now.

**Skip it if:** Your property layout demands a longer hose/cord reach, or you need hot water for grease-heavy jobs. Also skip if you're a professional who needs daily-duty durability — this is a premium homeowner tool, not a commercial one.

The WPX2700e sits in that sweet spot: enough power to feel like a real tool, enough refinement to not feel like a chore to use. At 4.5 stars from 225 buyers, the track record backs it up.
