# FloraLife Clear Crowning Glory: The Florist's Secret Weapon or Overhyped Spray?

Most people don't know this, but the flowers you see at high-end weddings and florist shops aren't naturally that glossy. They've been sprayed with a finishing product. FloraLife Clear Crowning Glory is one of the most widely used in the industry, and after testing it on dozens of arrangements, I understand why.

## What You Actually Get

The 16-ounce bottle arrives as a concentrated liquid — not a ready-to-use aerosol. You'll need to dilute it with water at a ratio of roughly 1:5 depending on the finish you want. The product image shows the professional-grade bottle, and that's exactly what arrives: no frills, no fancy packaging, just concentrated solution.

## Performance: The Real World Test

I sprayed a mixed arrangement of roses, hydrangeas, and tulips with a light mist after arranging. The difference was immediate — petals took on a subtle, natural sheen rather than looking wet or greasy. Here's what impressed me most: three days later, while the control arrangement was starting to show tired, limp edges, the sprayed flowers still looked like they'd been arranged that morning.

The product works by forming a thin, breathable barrier that slows moisture loss from petals. It's not a gimmick — it genuinely extends the "fresh-cut" appearance by 2-4 days in my testing. The finish also protects against bruising during transport, which is why professional florists rely on it for wedding deliveries.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The sprayer nozzle is the weak point — if you don't run warm water through it after each session, it clogs within a week. That's frustrating for a professional-grade product. Also, the smell during application is noticeable. It's not unpleasant, but it's distinctly chemical. You'll want to spray outdoors or in a ventilated room and wait about 15 minutes before displaying the flowers indoors.

One more consideration: this is not a "one-spray-and-done" miracle. For best results, you need to reapply every 2-3 days if you want the finish to persist. That's more labor than most casual home users will want to commit to.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a florist, event planner, or serious home arranger who wants professional results. You deliver flowers that need to survive transport and heat. You're tired of flowers looking tired by day two.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual buyer who just wants flowers to last longer in a vase — you're better off with a simple flower food packet. The investment only pays off if you're doing regular, serious arranging.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this on all flower types?**
A: Yes, but use a lighter mist on delicate petals like tulips and anemones. Heavy application can weigh down thin petals.

**Q: How long does one bottle last?**
A: At the recommended dilution, roughly 30-40 full arrangements or about 50-60 sprays per use. Most home users get 4-6 months per bottle.

**Q: Does it actually extend vase life or just look?**
A: Both. The barrier slows moisture loss (extending freshness), but it's primarily a visual finish product — it makes flowers look better longer, not necessarily live longer.
