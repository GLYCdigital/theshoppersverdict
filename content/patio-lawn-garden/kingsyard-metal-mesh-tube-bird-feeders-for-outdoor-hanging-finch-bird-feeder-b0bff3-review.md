The bird feeder aisle is full of flimsy plastic tubes that crack in the first frost or shatter when a squirrel decides they're a snack. The Kingsyard Metal Mesh Tube Feeder takes a different approach — a welded steel mesh body with a powder-coated finish that actually holds up. After 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's earned a spot as one of the better-value tube feeders on Amazon. But it's not perfect, and the mesh design has a specific audience.

**What's in the Box**

You get the feeder itself — a 16-inch metal mesh tube with six feeding ports, six wire perches, a removable base cap, and a top cap with a metal hanger. Assembly is minimal: slide the base cap on, fill from the top, hang. No screws, no tools, no instructions required. The hanger is a solid metal loop that swivels, which helps prevent seed spillage when the wind rocks it.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The selling point here is the welded steel mesh. It's rigid — no flexing, no bending. The powder coating is evenly applied and resists flaking, which matters because bare metal rusts quickly in rain and humidity. The top cap fits snugly with a rubber gasket that keeps moisture out; I've seen reviewers mention their seed stays dry through heavy downpours.

The mesh openings are sized for sunflower hearts and mixed seed blends. That's the sweet spot for attracting finches, chickadees, titmice, and nuthatches. But here's the catch: if you're planning to feed pure nyjer/thistle, this feeder will waste it. The mesh openings are too large, and the seed sifts through. One buyer put it plainly: "Great feeder, but don't put thistle in it. I lost half the seed the first day." Mix nyjer with sunflower hearts, or buy a dedicated finch feeder with smaller mesh.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The durability theme dominates the reviews. One long-term owner wrote: "Had this for two winters now. The squirrels have tried and failed. The finish still looks new." Another praised the capacity: "Holds way more than it looks like it should. I fill it once a week and it feeds a dozen birds daily." The easy refill design gets repeated shout-outs — the top cap lifts off cleanly, and you're not wrestling with twist-off lids or clamps.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the nyjer issue, the perches are the weak point. They're thin wire — functional for small birds, but frustrating if you're hoping to attract cardinals, blue jays, or woodpeckers. Those heavier birds will cling awkwardly or just move on. If your yard is dominated by larger species, this isn't the right feeder.

Also, the mesh means seed debris accumulates inside the tube. Without a removable bottom tray (which this model doesn't have), cleaning requires dumping the remaining seed and rinsing the tube thoroughly. It's manageable but not as convenient as a feeder with a lift-out base.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Finch and chickadee enthusiasts** who want a durable, squirrel-resistant feeder for mixed seed and sunflower hearts
- **Buyers in wet or snowy climates** who need a feeder that won't rust or warp after a season of exposure
- **Anyone replacing a plastic feeder that cracked** — this is a meaningful upgrade in longevity

**Skip It If**

- You primarily feed nyjer/thistle — you'll waste seed
- You want to attract cardinals and other heavy birds — the perches won't support them
- You need a feeder with a removable tray for easy cleaning — this one requires a bit more effort

**FAQ**

**Q: Will squirrels chew through the metal mesh?**
No. The welded steel is too tough for squirrel teeth. They might climb on it and shake it, but they won't gnaw through the mesh itself.

**Q: How much seed does it hold?**
Roughly 2-3 cups depending on the seed blend. That's enough for a week of steady feeding for a small flock of finches.

**Q: Can I hang this under a covered porch?**
Yes, but the powder coating handles direct sun and rain fine. Just note that bird droppings accumulate on the top cap — a porch position makes cleaning easier.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a no-nonsense, all-metal feeder that survives squirrels and weather while feeding small songbirds reliably. It's a solid 4.6-star product that delivers on its core promise.

**Skip it if** your bird menu is nyjer-heavy or you're hoping to host cardinals — you'll be disappointed by seed waste or wobbly perches.

At this price point, the Kingsyard metal mesh tube feeder is one of the better investments for backyard birders who are tired of replacing cheap feeders every season.
