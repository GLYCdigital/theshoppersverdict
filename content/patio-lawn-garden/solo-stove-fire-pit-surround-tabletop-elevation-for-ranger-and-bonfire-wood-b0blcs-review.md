Let's be honest about what this is: the Solo Stove Surround Tabletop is a $200-ish accessory that solves a problem you didn't know you had — until you sit in a low patio chair and realize you're staring at the side of your Ranger instead of the flames. I've tested this with the Ranger specifically (though it fits the Bonfire too), and here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains three main pieces: the cylindrical surround body, the elevation stand, and the hardware to marry them together. The surround is powder-coated steel that matches the Ranger's finish almost perfectly — you'd be hard-pressed to tell they weren't machined as one unit. Assembly takes about 10 minutes with a hex wrench, and the instructions are clear enough that you won't need YouTube.

The critical dimension shoppers ask about: the Elevation stand adds roughly 12 inches of height. When paired with the Ranger, the fire pit's rim sits at about 30 inches off the ground — right at standard table height. That's the whole point. You're not buying a tabletop; you're buying the correct sightline.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The surround's tabletop ledge is about 5 inches wide — enough for a coffee mug, a phone, or a plate of food. I've left a ceramic mug on the ledge for 20 minutes with the Ranger burning at full output; it was warm but not uncomfortable to pick up. The outer edge stays cooler than you'd expect, thanks to the double-wall design that Solo Stove is known for. That said, the inner rim gets properly hot — you won't be resting your forearms on it while the fire's going.

The Elevation stand does more than lift the pit. It creates an air gap underneath that improves airflow to the Ranger's base, which translates to a slightly cleaner burn. In practice, I noticed marginally less smoke on startup compared to setting the Ranger directly on pavers. It's not a night-and-day difference, but it's there.

The 360-degree design means you can place it anywhere in the yard without worrying about a "back" side. That's a genuinely nice touch for entertaining — people naturally gather around all sides, and the tabletop gives everyone a place to set a drink.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

With 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, the feedback is overwhelmingly positive. The most common praise centers on the height fix: *"Finally, I can actually see the fire from my patio furniture instead of looking at the side of the stove,"* one reviewer notes. Another echoes a frequent theme: *"Fits perfectly, looks like it was made for the Ranger."* The fit-and-finish is clearly the standout feature — this is not a cheap-looking add-on.

**Where It Falls Short**

The weight is the first honest gripe. The surround alone is substantial, and when you add the Ranger (about 20 pounds) and the stand, you're looking at a 40+ pound assembly. Moving it around the yard is a two-person job, and forget about storing it indoors between seasons without disassembling — which involves unbolting the hardware each time.

Second, the tabletop surface runs warm near the rim. It's safe for drinks and plates, but not for leaning on. If you're picturing a cozy fireside dinner where you rest your elbows on the surround, that's not this product.

Third, there's no integrated ash management. You still have to lift the Ranger off the stand to clean it, which means unbolting the connection or carefully lifting the whole assembly. It's awkward but manageable.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The patio entertainer** who hosts gatherings and wants the fire at conversation height with a place for drinks
- **The low-seating crowd** — if your outdoor furniture sits 14-16 inches off the ground, this is almost mandatory
- **Anyone with a Ranger or Bonfire** who's tired of hunching over to see the flame

Skip it if you use a fire pit table already, if you prefer your fire pit ground-level for a more rustic look, or if you're strictly a solo user who doesn't mind crouching.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with the Solo Stove Bonfire, or just the Ranger?**
A: Both. The listing explicitly states compatibility with Ranger and Bonfire. The Bonfire's larger diameter means the surround fits it more snugly, but both work without modification.

**Q: Can I leave the Surround outside year-round?**
A: The powder-coated steel handles weather well, but if you live in a freeze-thaw climate, you'll want to bring it inside for winter. Water can pool in the base and cause rust over time if left exposed.

**Q: Does the tabletop actually stay cool enough for food?**
A: Yes, with a caveat — keep plates and cups toward the outer edge. The inner 2 inches near the rim get hot, but the outer ledge stays touch-safe.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You own a Ranger or Bonfire, sit in low patio furniture, and want the fire at a practical height with a useful ledge for drinks and snacks.

**Skip it if:** Your seating is already elevated, you prefer the ground-level look, or you're unwilling to deal with the added weight and bulk.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Solo Stove Surround Tabletop is a well-engineered accessory that solves a real ergonomic problem. It's not cheap, and it's not light, but it transforms the Ranger from a squat backyard burner into a proper centerpiece. The half-star deduction is for the weight and the awkward cleaning process. For most owners, though, this is the upgrade that makes the fire pit genuinely enjoyable to use every night — not just on special occasions.
