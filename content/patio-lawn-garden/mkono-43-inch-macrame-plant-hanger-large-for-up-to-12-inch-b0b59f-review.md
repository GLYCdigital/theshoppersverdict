## The 43-inch macrame hanger that actually looks like it was made by hand

Most macrame plant hangers on Amazon look like they were cranked out by a machine — thin cord, uneven knots, and a plastic ring that screams "temporary." The Mkono 43-inch version is not that. The moment you unbox it, the weight and texture tell you this is the real thing. This is the hanger you buy when you want your trailing pothos or monstera to look like it's living in a curated bohemian space, not a dorm room.

## What you actually get

The package contains one pre-assembled hanger, roughly 43 inches from the top ring to the bottom knot. The rope is a thick, 100% cotton cord that's been hand-knotted into a classic four-strand cradle design. At the top, there's a sturdy metal ring — not the cheap, painted plastic you often see. The bottom features a reinforced knot that forms a basket-style cradle, designed to hold pots up to 12 inches in diameter.

I measured the drop at about 40 inches from the ceiling hook to the bottom of the cradle, which gives you generous clearance for tall plants. The hanger itself weighs just over half a pound — light enough to hang from a standard drywall anchor, but substantial enough to feel durable.

## Real-world performance

I hung this from a ceiling hook in a corner with decent indirect light and dropped in a 10-inch ceramic pot with a trailing heartleaf philodendron. The installation took under two minutes: loop the top ring over the hook, adjust the four strands to level the pot, and you're done. The cradle holds the pot snugly — no wobbling, no sliding, even when I gave it a gentle nudge.

The weight capacity is rated at 30 pounds, and I believe it. The knots are tight and consistent, and the cotton rope has enough friction that it won't slip through the cradle over time. I also tested it outdoors on a covered porch with a lighter plastic pot — it held steady through a few breezy afternoons without issue.

One thing to note: cotton stretches. After about four days, the hanger had dropped about an inch and a half as the knots settled. I re-tightened the top knot and it's been stable since. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you're hanging it at a precise height.

## Where it falls short

The white cotton rope is a double-edged sword. It looks clean and bright when new, but it'll show dust, dirt, and any water marks if you're not careful. If you're hanging this outdoors, expect to wipe it down regularly or choose a darker color option if available.

You'll also need a saucer or liner pot. The open cradle design means any drainage holes in your pot will leak directly onto your floor. Pair it with a catch tray or use pots without drainage holes.

Finally, the 43-inch length might be too long for standard 8-foot ceilings if you have a tall plant. You can shorten it by re-knotting the top, but that requires some DIY effort. For most people, it's fine — just measure your space first.
