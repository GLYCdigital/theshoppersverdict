# CLOROX® Pool&SPA™ Shock XTRABLUE®2: The Two-in-One Shock That Actually Earns Its Keep

Most pool shock products promise clarity but deliver a cloudy mess and a three-day wait. CLOROX Pool&SPA Shock XTRABLUE2 takes a different approach — it combines 50%+ available chlorine with a built-in blue clarifier that tackles both sanitization and water clarity in one shot. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. Here's the unvarnished truth after digging into the specs, the chemistry, and what real owners are saying.

## What You Actually Get

The container holds granular calcium hypochlorite shock — roughly 1 pound of product per treatment for a 10,000-gallon pool. The XTRABLUE2 formulation is the differentiator: those blue granules you see in the product image aren't just for looks. They're a copper-based clarifier that binds to dead algae and organic particles, making them easier for your filter to catch. You're essentially buying shock and clarifier in one bucket, which simplifies your chemical routine.

## Real-World Performance

The headline claim here is fast oxidation. CLOROX says this stuff works on algae, sunscreen, and body oils in under 15 minutes — and owners back that up. One reviewer noted, *"I shocked at dusk and by the time I finished dinner the water was visibly clearer. My sand filter was pulling out green gunk the next morning."*

The quick-dissolve formula matters more than most people realize. Traditional granular shocks can sit on the pool floor and bleach your liner before dissolving. XTRABLUE2 granules break down quickly, which reduces that risk significantly. For vinyl liner pools, this is a meaningful advantage.

One thing that surprised me in the feedback: several owners use this as their *weekly* maintenance shock, not just for algae emergencies. The blue clarifier keeps water polished between filter cleanings, which explains the high satisfaction scores.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating isn't accidental. Across the 240 reviews, three themes dominate:

- **Speed**: Multiple reviewers mention swimming within 15–30 minutes of shocking, which is unusually fast for a granular product.
- **Clarity**: The built-in clarifier gets consistent shout-outs. One long-term owner said, *"I stopped buying separate clarifier entirely. This does both jobs without me having to measure two products."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides — every product has them.

**The blue tint issue is real.** If you overdose or pour directly over a light-colored liner, you can get temporary blue staining. It fades within a day or two, but it's a shock if you're not expecting it. Dissolve the granules in a bucket of pool water first and pour it in front of a return jet.

**It's not stabilized.** XTRABLUE2 contains no cyanuric acid, so outdoor pools in direct sun will lose chlorine quickly. You'll need to maintain CYA levels between 30–50 ppm separately. This isn't a flaw unique to CLOROX, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

**The cost-per-pound math.** If you're on a tight budget and only need basic oxidation, plain calcium hypochlorite runs cheaper. You're paying a premium for the convenience of the combined clarifier.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do I use per treatment?**
A: One pound treats 10,000 gallons. For a 20,000-gallon pool, use two pounds. Shock weekly or after heavy use.

**Q: Can I use this in a saltwater pool?**
A: Yes, but monitor calcium hardness — cal-hypo adds calcium, which can cause scaling in salt systems. Test monthly.

**Q: How long before I can swim?**
A: Wait until free chlorine drops below 5 ppm. Most owners report swimming within 15–30 minutes for normal shock levels.
