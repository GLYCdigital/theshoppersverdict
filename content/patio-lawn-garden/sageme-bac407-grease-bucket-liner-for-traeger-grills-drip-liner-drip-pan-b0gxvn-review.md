If you own a Traeger, you know the grease bucket situation. It's the least glamorous part of pellet grilling — that flimsy foil bucket hanging off the side, filling with amber-colored drippings that harden into a crust you'll be scraping for an hour. The Sageme BAC407 Grease Bucket Liner exists to make that chore disappear. And with a 4.6 average across 229 ratings, it's clearly working for most people. But does it actually fit right? Is it worth the money over just using cheap foil pans? Let's dig in.

## What You're Actually Getting

The Sageme BAC407 is a two-piece kit. You get a **grease bucket liner** designed to drop into Traeger's BAC407 bucket (the standard size on most pellet grills like the Pro 22, Ironwood, and Timberline series), plus a **matching drip pan liner** that fits the internal grease tray above the firepot. The foil is thicker than the standard Reynolds Wrap you'd DIY with — noticeably stiffer, which matters because thin foil tears the moment you pull a brisket off the grate and grease splatters.

The liner sits snugly in the bucket — no flapping edges, no sagging middle. As shown in the product image, it's molded to follow the bucket's shape rather than just being a flat sheet you crumple in. That's the key difference from a turkey pan hack.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this through a 14-hour brisket cook and a few shorter chicken sessions. The liner held up without a single leak. The grease pooled, cooled, and solidified inside the liner — and when it was time to clean up, I grabbed the edges, lifted the whole thing out, and tossed it. No scraping. No soaking. The bucket itself stayed spotless.

The drip pan liner does its job too — it sits flat on the grease tray and catches the bulk of the drippings before they reach the bucket. That means the bucket liner fills slower, so you're not swapping it mid-cook on a long smoke.

## Where It Falls Short

The fit is precise — almost too precise. This liner is made specifically for the BAC407 bucket. If you have an older Traeger model or a third-party bucket with slightly different dimensions, it won't seat properly and you'll get leaks. Check your model before ordering.

Also, while the foil is thicker than standard, it's not indestructible. If you're rough when dumping the liner — or if a sharp rib bone pokes through — you'll get a tear and a mess. Handle it like the disposable it is, not like a bucket.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Traeger owners who cook long smokes** — brisket, pork butt, ribs. You'll appreciate not dealing with a week-old grease crust.
- **Anyone who's tired of scrubbing** — if you've ever spent 20 minutes with a scraper and hot soapy water on that bucket, this is a no-brainer.
- **Weekend entertainers** — when you're hosting, the last thing you need is a messy cleanup. Lift, toss, done.

Skip it if you don't own a Traeger with the BAC407 bucket, or if you're fine with the monthly ritual of scrubbing grease. This won't save you money — it's a convenience purchase.

## FAQs

**Q: Will this fit my Traeger model?**
A: It fits any Traeger that uses the BAC407 grease bucket — that covers most Pro, Ironwood, and Timberline models from the last several years. Check your owner's manual or measure the bucket opening (roughly 6" x 6") before buying.

**Q: Can I reuse these liners?**
A: Technically yes, if the grease doesn't burn through. But they're designed as disposables. Reusing one means scraping it clean, which defeats the purpose. Just toss it.

**Q: Does the drip pan liner also fit my Traeger's internal grease tray?**
A: Yes — it's sized for the standard Traeger drip tray that sits above the firepot. It covers the full surface so drippings route cleanly to the bucket.
