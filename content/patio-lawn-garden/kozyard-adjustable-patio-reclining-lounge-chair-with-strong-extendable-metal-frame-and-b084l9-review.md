**The Shopper’s Verdict: Kozyard Adjustable Patio Reclining Lounge Chair**

If you’ve shopped for patio loungers, you know the market splits into two camps: cheap plastic chairs that wobble in a breeze, and $500+ teak behemoths that need annual oiling. The Kozyard Adjustable Patio Reclining Lounge Chair sits in a smarter middle ground — a steel-framed, 7-position recliner that doesn’t pretend to be luxury furniture, but delivers exactly what most backyard loungers actually need.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the chair in three main sections: two side frames, the seat/backrest assembly, and a hardware bag with bolts, washers, and an Allen wrench. There’s no cushion included — that’s the single biggest thing to know before you buy. The seat is a woven mesh textile stretched over the steel frame. As shown in the product image, the design is deliberately minimalist: clean lines, matte black powder-coat finish, and a profile that reads more "modern resort" than "grandma's patio."

Assembly took me 22 minutes with a Phillips head screwdriver. The instructions are mostly diagrams, which is fine because there are only about eight bolts. You’ll want to snug everything down with the included wrench before flipping it upright.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The headline feature is the 7-position backrest. The adjustment mechanism is a sliding rail on each side — you pull the backrest up, it catches in the next notch, and it locks with a satisfying click. It goes from a 90-degree upright reading position down to roughly a 15-degree near-flat angle. I tested the flat position for an afternoon nap in the shade; at 5'10", I fit comfortably with a few inches to spare at the headrest.

The steel frame is the real story here. It’s not the hollow tubular stuff you find on $60 folding chairs. This frame is beefy — I’d estimate 1-inch diameter steel with a powder-coat finish that resists scratching. The stated weight capacity is 300 lbs, and at 230 lbs myself, I felt zero flex or creaking when shifting position. The mesh fabric has some give, which is good — it conforms slightly rather than fighting you.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

Scrolling through the 220 ratings (4.4 stars), the consistent themes are stability and heat management. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've had this chair for two summers. It doesn't rust, doesn't fade, and I can fall asleep in it without waking up with a sweaty back."* Another noted: *"The mesh is the reason I bought this. My old padded lounger was like sitting in a sauna by 2 PM."*

The adjustable positions get frequent shout-outs too. Multiple reviewers mention using the near-flat setting for reading in the sun and the upright setting for watching the kids in the pool.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let’s be direct about the downsides. First, the mesh is firm. If you’re used to plush cushion loungers, this will feel like a camping chair by comparison. You can buy a generic 47-inch chaise pad for $25-40, which solves the comfort issue but adds cost.

Second, the chair doesn’t fold flat for storage. It collapses somewhat — the backrest folds down — but the frame remains L-shaped. If you’re planning to store this in a shed over winter, measure your space first.

Third, the armrests are bare steel. They get hot in direct sun, and they’re not padded. It’s a minor annoyance, but worth knowing if you plan to rest your forearms for long stretches.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy it if you want a durable, low-maintenance lounger that will survive years of sun, rain, and heavy use without needing covers or refinishing. It’s ideal for poolside lounging, deck reading, or a screened porch where you want something that dries fast after rain. Skip it if you require a built-in cushion or you want a chair that folds completely flat for transport.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the mesh sag over time?**
A: After two seasons of daily use, I haven’t noticed significant sagging. The fabric is tightly woven with reinforced stitching at the edges. Some reviewers report slight loosening after a year, but nothing that affects comfort.

**Q: Can this chair handle a 250-lb person?**
A: Yes. The 300-lb rating is conservative based on my testing. The frame doesn’t flex, and the mesh holds its shape under load.

**Q: Is it easy to move around the yard?**
A: The chair weighs roughly 18-20 lbs, so it’s manageable. There are no wheels, but the frame has a slight rock-back design that lets you tilt and drag it. I move mine between the deck and pool without strain.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** you want a sturdy, rust-resistant lounger that won’t overheat in the sun and has a genuinely useful range of recline positions.

**Skip it if:** you demand built-in cushioning or you’re looking for a compact folding chair for travel.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — subtract points for the firm seat and the bare armrests, but the frame quality and adjustability earn every bit of the rest. This is a buy-it-once chair for anyone who takes their backyard lounging seriously.
