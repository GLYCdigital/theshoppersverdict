Let's be blunt: most wild bird seed bags are 40% filler — milo, red millet, and cracked corn that only grackles and starlings touch. Wagner's Songbird Supreme Blend takes the opposite approach, and at 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, it's clearly working. But is the premium worth it? Here's the real breakdown.

## What's in the bag

You get 8 pounds of seed in a resealable pouch — a detail more brands should copy. Flip it open and you'll see what you're paying for: black oil sunflower, sunflower chips, safflower, peanuts, and white millet. No wheat, no milo, no dyed seed. As one buyer put it, "I was shocked at the quality — this is what seed should look like, not the dusty mix from the big box store."

## How it performs at the feeder

The blend's strength is its targeted appeal. Black oil sunflower and safflower bring in cardinals, chickadees, nuthatches, and titmice. The peanut pieces attract woodpeckers and jays. White millet catches finches and juncos. That's a genuinely diverse yard — but it's not instant. Several buyers noted a transition period: "Took about 10 days for the cardinals to figure out the safflower. Now they're here every morning."

You'll notice less waste, too. Because there's no filler, you're not sweeping up piles of uneaten red millet under the feeder. One reviewer summed it up: "I refill less often with this 8-lb bag than I did with 20 lbs of cheap mix." That math checks out — you're paying for seed birds actually eat.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.7-star rating holds up across feedback themes. Quality and freshness come up repeatedly — the bag seals properly, and the seed doesn't arrive crushed or dusty. Bird variety is the second big win. "I've counted 11 species in three weeks," one reviewer reported. The resealable zipper also earns consistent mention, particularly from buyers who store seed in garages or sheds.

## Where it falls short

The 8-pound size is the main friction point. If you run a single tube feeder, it's manageable — roughly two to three weeks. But active yards with platform feeders will burn through this bag in a week. You'll pay more per pound than bargain blends. That's the honest tradeoff: premium ingredients, premium price.

Also, the peanut pieces are generous but uneven. Some buyers mentioned larger chunks that smaller birds struggle with. It's minor, but worth noting if your yard skews toward chickadees and wrens.

## Who should buy this

- **Backyard birders who want variety** — if you're tired of feeding only house sparrows and pigeons, this mix changes your visitor list
- **Cardinal enthusiasts** — the safflower-to-sunflower ratio is nearly perfect for keeping cardinals coming back
- **Anyone tired of seed waste** — the no-filler formula means less mess and better value despite the higher upfront cost

Skip it if you're on a strict budget and just want to put something out. This is a step-up product for people who care about what they're feeding.

## FAQ

**Will squirrels eat this?**
Yes, they'll go after the peanuts and sunflower. Use a baffle or squirrel-proof feeder — the seed itself won't deter them.

**Does it sprout under the feeder?**
Less than most blends because there's no milo or wheat. Some millet may sprout in warm weather, but it's minimal.

**Is this okay for a tube feeder?**
Yes, the seed sizes work well in standard tube feeders. The peanut pieces are small enough to flow through most ports.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely premium blend that pulls in a diverse bird population with minimal waste. **Skip it if** you need the cheapest seed per pound or have a single feeder with low traffic — the 8-pound size won't stretch far enough.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A quality mix that delivers on its promise. The price and bag size are the only real compromises.
