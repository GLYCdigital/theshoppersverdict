**What You Actually Get**

The box contains tumbled, reflective glass chunks ranging from roughly 1 to 2 inches — large enough to stay put in a breeze but small enough to pack tight around burner rings. **Where It Falls Short**

The reflective coating is durable but not immortal. After dozens of high-heat burns, some users report the gloss softening to a matte finish, particularly on pieces sitting closest to the burner. It's cosmetic, not structural — the glass won't crack or degrade — but if you're chasing that mirror-shine forever, expect some wear by year two.

Also, the packaging is heavy. Multiple boxes arrive for larger quantities, and some buyers noted shipping delays because of the weight. Not a dealbreaker, just budget extra delivery time.

**FAQ: Three Real Questions**

**Q: How much do I need for my fire pit?**
Measure the diameter of your burner area. For a 24-inch round pit, budget 15–20 pounds for proper coverage. For rectangular pits, calculate square footage and aim for 1 pound per 20 square inches.

**Q: Can I mix this with lava rock?**
Yes, and many do. Lava rock at the bottom for volume, fire glass on top for looks. It stretches your budget without sacrificing the reflective finish.

**Q: Will it melt or explode?**
No. This is tempered glass rated for continuous high heat. It won't pop like river rocks or melt into slag. Worst case is cosmetic dulling.
