If you own a Hot Springs Limelight spa, you already know the OEM filter costs more than it should. The REEPLAR PWK35B is the aftermarket answer — a direct replacement for the Watkins 1642301 that promises OEM-level performance at a fraction of the price. With 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly earning its keep. But is it truly a drop-in swap, or does "compatible" hide compromises? Here's what I found after digging through specs, reviews, and real-world usage patterns.

**What's in the box:** You get the filter cartridge itself — nothing more. No O-rings, no gaskets, no instruction manual. That's standard for this category, but it's worth knowing before you order: if your existing filter's O-ring is worn, grab a replacement while you're at it, because you'll need to harvest the old one. The unit measures 7.75 inches tall with a 2-inch flange, matching the PWK35B spec exactly. The pleated media is polyester, not the cheaper paper blend some budget filters use, and it's wrapped in a rigid PVC core that won't collapse under water pressure.

**Performance in practice:** The filtration is where this filter either wins or loses, and it wins. The pleats are tightly packed — one reviewer noted, "I compared it side-by-side with my old Watkins filter and the surface area looks identical. My water stays crystal clear for the full 3-month cycle." Another confirmed the fit: "Dropped right into my Limelight with zero wobble. The flange seated perfectly." That's the consistent theme across positive reviews: it fits, it seals, and it filters like the original.

Filtration efficiency is rated at the same 5-micron level as OEM, which matters for trapping the fine debris that clouds spa water. The polyester media also holds up better to repeated cleaning with a garden hose — it won't degrade after two or three pressure washes the way cheaper filters do. For most owners, that means a 12-month lifespan (four 3-month cycles) before replacement is genuinely realistic.

**Where it falls short:** The honest tradeoffs are minor but real. Some buyers reported the pleat count runs slightly high or low — one measured 108, another 112, versus the OEM's 110. In a hot tub, that's a non-issue; the surface area difference is under 2%. But if you're obsessive about matching factory specs, it's worth knowing. The other quibble: no included gasket. If you're coming from a filter that's seen better days, budget $5–10 for a new O-ring rather than reusing a compressed one.

**Who should buy this:** If you own any Hot Springs Limelight model (the Pulse, the Beam, the Glow, or the Solstice), this is a no-brainer. It's also ideal for anyone who does routine filter swaps every 3–4 months and wants to stop paying $60+ for the Watkins badge. If you're a first-time hot tub owner, this is a safe, proven entry point — the 4.6-star average isn't inflated by brand loyalty; it's earned.

**Skip it if:** You're running a commercial spa where every spec must match OEM to the letter, or if you refuse to reuse O-rings. Otherwise, there's little reason to pay double for the branded version.

Every 3–4 months under normal use (daily soaks, regular chemical balance). If you soak heavily or have hard water, lean toward 3 months. Clean it monthly with a garden hose to extend life.

*"Does it work with the Limelight's 24-hour circulation pump?"*  
Yes. The 2-inch flange and pleat density are designed for continuous flow. Multiple reviewers confirmed no pressure loss or bypass issues.
