**What's actually in the bag**

You get 5 pounds of tan-colored granules that dissolve into a suspension concentrate. The active ingredient is diuron at a potent 80% concentration — that's roughly four times stronger than the liquid consumer formulas at big-box stores. A single bag mixed at typical rates (2-4 lbs per acre for bare ground) covers an impressive area. The dry flowable formulation means no measuring cups of liquid — you weigh out what you need and mix with water in a pump sprayer or ATV rig.

**Real-world performance**

Diuron works two ways: it kills emerged weeds by disrupting photosynthesis, and it creates a soil barrier that stops germinating seeds for 3-6 months depending on rainfall and soil type. For the price per square foot of coverage, nothing else on Amazon comes close. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"Used on a 300-foot gravel driveway. Two applications per year and the weeds just stop. The first spray knocked down existing crabgrass in about a week."* Another noted: *"Mixed 2 tablespoons per gallon for spot treatment around the barn. Killed everything green in 10 days, including the thistle patches I'd been fighting for years."*

The dry flowable particles stay suspended in water far better than wettable powders — you won't spend the afternoon shaking your sprayer to keep sediment from clogging nozzles.

**Where it gets tricky**

This is not Roundup. Diuron is a soil-active herbicide with residual activity, meaning it stays put and keeps killing. That's great for gravel and industrial sites, but catastrophic if overspray drifts onto your lawn or flower beds. The label explicitly warns against use on residential turf — it will yellow Bermuda grass and kill fescue outright. Several reviewers discovered this the hard way. One wrote: *"Killed the weeds along my fence but the runoff stained the edge of the lawn. My mistake — I didn't read the residual warning carefully enough."*

You'll also need to buy a surfactant separately for best results on older, waxy-leaf weeds. And because diuron is a photosynthesis inhibitor, it works slower than glyphosate — expect 7-14 days for full browning, not 48 hours.

**Who should buy this**

- **Gravel driveway and fence line owners** who want season-long bare ground control
- **Small farm and orchard operators** doing pre-emergent strip treatments between rows
- **Property managers** maintaining rental lots, parking strips, and utility access areas
- **Anyone comfortable with PPE** — you need nitrile gloves, goggles, and an N95 or better respirator when mixing

**Skip it if** you're looking for a spot-treatment lawn weed killer, have kids or pets roaming untreated areas, or want fast visible knockdown. For those needs, look at a selective post-emergent instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: How much area does a 5lb bag cover?**
A: At the standard bare-ground rate of 2-4 lbs per acre, one bag covers roughly 20,000-40,000 sq ft. For spot treatment (1 tbsp per gallon of water), it lasts most homeowners a full year or more.

**Q: Is it safe around trees?**
A: Established trees with roots deeper than 6 inches are generally fine since diuron stays in the top few inches of soil. Shallow-rooted ornamentals and young transplants are at risk — keep applications at least 3 feet away from their drip line.

**Q: Can I use it in vegetable gardens?**
A: Not recommended. Diuron has a 60-90 day pre-harvest interval on certain crops, but the residual soil activity makes it risky for rotation. Use organic mulches or hand weeding in edible beds.
