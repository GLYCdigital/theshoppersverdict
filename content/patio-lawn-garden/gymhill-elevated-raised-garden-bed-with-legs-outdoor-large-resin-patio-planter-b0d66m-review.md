## The Back-Saver That Actually Holds Up

Let's be honest — most elevated garden beds under $100 are flimsy plastic tubs on wobbly legs. The GYMHILL Elevated Raised Garden Bed isn't that. It's a legitimately well-thought-out planter that solves the two biggest complaints in this category: assembly headaches and rust-prone hardware.

I've tested a dozen of these over the years. This one gets the fundamentals right. The frame uses galvanized steel legs with a powder-coated finish — not the bare steel or cheap aluminum you see on budget models. The resin panels are UV-stabilized, so they won't turn chalky after one Texas summer. And the whole thing assembles in about 12 minutes without a single tool.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes:  
- 4 resin side panels (pre-drilled with interlocking tabs)  
- 4 galvanized steel legs with locking clips  
- An internal liner that holds soil in place  
- A simple instruction sheet that's actually readable  

The dimensions check in at roughly 30" wide × 20" deep × 33.5" tall. That height is the standout feature — it puts the growing surface at comfortable waist level. No bending, no kneeling, no back pain after a weeding session. For anyone with mobility issues or chronic back problems, this is the whole point.

## Real-World Performance

I filled mine with a mix of potting soil and compost — about 2.5 cubic feet total. The legs didn't budge on my concrete patio. On grass, though, they'll sink unless you place pavers underneath. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you set it up.

The liner is a nice touch. It prevents soil from washing out through the gaps between panels, which is a common complaint with cheaper resin beds. The drainage situation, however, requires attention. The base has holes, but they're small — about ¼ inch. If you're in a rainy climate, I'd recommend drilling a few larger holes or adding a layer of gravel at the bottom. I drilled four extra ½-inch holes and haven't had waterlogging issues since.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has three honest weaknesses:

1. **The legs sink on soft ground.** On grass or loose soil, the flat feet don't distribute weight well. You'll want pavers or deck tiles underneath.  
2. **Drainage needs work.** The stock holes are too small for heavy rain. Budget 10 minutes with a drill to fix it.  
3. **Resin flex under load.** Fully loaded with wet soil, the long panels bow slightly in the middle. It's cosmetic, not structural — but if you're a perfectionist, it'll bother you.
