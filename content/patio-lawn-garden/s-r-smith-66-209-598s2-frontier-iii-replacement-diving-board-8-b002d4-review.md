If you're staring at a cracked, faded diving board and dreading the replacement cost, here's the honest truth: this S.R. Smith board is the right call for most homeowners, but it's not the cheapest option. At roughly $400–$450 depending on the seller, the Frontier III sits at the premium end of the replacement market. The question is whether the durability justifies the price tag. After digging through 225 ratings and the specs, I'd say yes — with a few caveats.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 8-foot board itself, pre-drilled mounting holes, a roll of non-slip grip tape, and mounting hardware. The board measures 96 inches long, 20 inches wide at the base, tapering to 18 inches at the diving end — dimensions that matter if you're replacing an existing 8-foot board. The fiberglass construction is the headline feature here: it's a solid core wrapped in marine-grade resin, which means no wood rot, no splintering, and no warping after a few brutal summers.

One thing worth noting: this board is designed specifically for S.R. Smith's Frontier III base and compatible with most 8-foot jig-style stands. If you're bolting it onto an older or off-brand base, double-check the bolt pattern before ordering. The pre-drilled holes are standard 6-inch on-center, but some older bases use different spacing.

## Real-World Performance

The spring rate is where this board earns its keep. It's stiff enough to give you a solid launch for a proper dive, but not so rigid that kids feel like they're jumping off a plank. Multiple reviewers note that the board "holds its bounce" even after several seasons of heavy use — one parent mentioned their teenage boys use it daily all summer, and it still feels like new.

The grip tape deserves special mention. It's applied in a strip down the center, and when installed correctly, it provides solid traction even when wet. But here's the catch: several reviewers complained about the tape peeling at the edges after a season. The fix is straightforward — clean the surface thoroughly with alcohol before applying, and press firmly, especially at the edges. Skip this step and you'll be re-buying grip tape within months.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The durability theme runs through nearly every positive review. One customer put it simply: "Replaced a 12-year-old board that finally cracked. This one feels even better — the fiberglass construction is noticeably more solid than the original wood-core." Another reviewer, who runs a vacation rental with a pool, noted they've "replaced boards every 2–3 years before this one. Three years in, zero issues."

Fitment is the other consistent win. Most buyers report the board bolts up perfectly to existing S.R. Smith bases with no shimming or modification needed. That "it just fits" factor saves real money if you're hiring someone for installation.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the price. You can find generic fiberglass boards for $250–$300, and some of them are perfectly serviceable. You're paying a premium here for brand confidence and warranty coverage — S.R. Smith backs this with a solid warranty, but you'll need to register the product and keep your receipt.

Second, the grip tape issue mentioned above. It's a minor annoyance, but when a $400+ product requires careful installation to avoid a peeling hazard, that's worth flagging.

Third, and this is niche but real: the board is *stiff*. If you're a casual swimmer who just wants to cannonball off the end, this board might feel more unforgiving than you'd like. It's built for actual diving, not just jumping.

## FAQ

**Will this fit my existing diving board stand?**
If you have an 8-foot jig-style base with 6-inch bolt spacing, almost certainly. S.R. Smith bases are the standard, but measure your existing board's mounting holes before ordering. Off-brand bases sometimes use different patterns.

**How hard is installation?**
Two people, a socket wrench, and about 30 minutes. The hardest part is aligning the grip tape straight. Have a helper hold the board level while you tighten the bolts.

**How long will it actually last?**
Based on reviewer feedback, 10+ years is realistic for residential use. The fiberglass core doesn't degrade like wood, and the resin resists UV damage. The grip tape will likely need replacement before the board does.
