**The dual-purpose fire pit that actually earns its keep**

Most fire pits claim to double as grills. Most are lying — you get a flimsy wire grate perched over a fire that's either too hot to cook on or too weak to warm you. The Gbekery Wood Burning Firepit is different. It's not perfect, but it's the rare 2-in-1 that does both jobs credibly, and at 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, buyers mostly agree.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the 24-inch steel fire bowl, a spark screen with a lift handle, a fire poker, and two separate BBQ grill grates — one fixed, one adjustable in height. That second grate is the headline feature. It swings over the fire on a pivot arm, letting you raise or lower it to control cooking heat without smothering the flames.

The build is legitimately stout. The steel is thick enough that the bowl doesn't warp under full loads, and the high-temp painted finish resists rust through a season of rain and ash. As shown in the product image, the legs bolt on with cross-bracing — this thing isn't going to wobble when you're stirring coals.

**Where it shines**

The adjustable grill changes the game. You can start burgers on high heat, then swing the grate up and let the fire die down to embers for slow-cooking. One buyer noted, *"Cooked steaks for six and still had a campfire going after dinner — never had that with my old pit."* Another praised the spark screen: *"The mesh is fine enough that I don't worry about my wood deck. First pit I've owned that feels safe."*

The fire bowl depth is also smarter than most. It's deep enough to hold a good bed of coals without sparks showering your guests, but shallow enough that you don't need a cord of wood to get a proper flame going.

**Where it falls short**

Assembly is the biggest friction point. The brackets that hold the legs require precise alignment, and a few reviewers mention holes that don't quite line up. One wrote, *"Took two of us and a rubber mallet to get the legs seated. Doable, but frustrating."* Budget 30–45 minutes and have a second set of hands ready.

The 24-inch bowl is also a compromise. It's perfect for a couple or a small family, but if you regularly host 8+ people, you'll find yourself feeding the fire constantly while also trying to cook. And there's no ash pan — when the night's over, you're scooping ashes out from under the grate or tipping the unit. Not a dealbreaker, but it's a messy chore.

**Who should buy this**

- **The backyard host** who wants one piece of gear that handles both cookouts and evening fires
- **Deck owners** who've avoided fire pits due to spark risk — the screen genuinely mitigates that
- **Small-space patios** where a separate fire pit and grill won't both fit

Skip it if you need a dedicated smoker-style grill with precise temperature control, or if you regularly entertain crowds that need a 30-inch+ fire bowl.

**FAQ**

**Is the grill grate hot enough for proper searing?**
Yes — with the grate lowered over a good bed of coals, you'll get a hard sear on steaks and burgers. It's not a kamado, but it outperforms most combo pits I've tested.

**Can you use it on a wood deck?**
With the spark screen in place, yes. The screen's mesh is fine enough to catch most embers. Still, keep a fire extinguisher or bucket of sand nearby — that's just responsible fire pit ownership.

**How much wood does it burn through?**
Standard split logs cut to 16–18 inches fit fine. A full evening burn (4–5 hours) will consume roughly half a face cord. It's not a fuel miser, but it's not a hog either.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely dual-purpose fire pit that cooks well and heats better, without spending premium-brand money. **Skip it if** you're a solo assembler, need to feed crowds, or want ash-free cleanup.

At 4.6 stars with 229 ratings, the Gbekery earns its score. It's not the cheapest pit on Amazon, but it's the rare one that does both jobs well enough that you'll actually use both functions. For the price of a mediocre grill and a flimsy fire pit, you get one solid piece of gear that replaces both.
