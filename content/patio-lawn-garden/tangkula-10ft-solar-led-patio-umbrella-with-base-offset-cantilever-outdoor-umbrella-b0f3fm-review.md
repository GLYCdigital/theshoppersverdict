Let's cut to it: a cantilever umbrella is the only way to shade a large table without a pole jabbing the center, and the Tangkula 10ft version delivers that core function well. But the included base is its weak link — and that's a pattern I see in this price tier.

## What you actually get

The box contains the offset cantilever frame (one pole, no center mast), a 10-foot octagonal canopy in your chosen color, the solar LED assembly with a small panel mounted on top of the hub, and a cross-shaped base that requires you to fill it with sand or water. Assembly takes about 30-45 minutes with two people — the pole sections bolt together, and you'll want a second set of hands to hoist the canopy onto the hub. The crank mechanism for tilt and the push-button rotation lock are already installed, which saves headaches.

The canopy itself is 180g polyester with a PU coating. That's not ripstop-grade fabric, but it's adequate for residential use. The frame is powder-coated steel — not aluminum — so it's heavier than premium competitors, but that weight actually helps stability when the base is loaded properly.

## Real-world performance

The 10-foot offset design is the headline feature. As shown in the product image, the single side pole means you can slide a full dining set underneath and still walk around all four sides. The 360-degree rotation is smooth — you twist the pole and it locks into place with a firm click. The crank tilt lets you angle the canopy up to about 45 degrees, which is useful when the sun dips low.

The solar LEDs are the differentiator. There are 24 LED bulbs embedded along the rib structure, and they cast a warm, even glow across the table — not blinding, but definitely enough to eat dinner by. On a full charge (about 6-8 hours of direct sun), you get 4-5 hours of light. The on/off switch is on the hub, easy to reach.

Where it stumbles: the base. The included cross base is 45 pounds when filled with sand, and that's just not enough for a 10-foot canopy in any breeze over 10 mph. One reviewer noted, "The umbrella is great, but the base is a joke. I bought two sandbags and now it's solid." That's the workaround — plan to add 50-100 pounds of extra ballast or buy a wider plate base.

## What buyers consistently praise

The shade coverage gets repeated shoutouts. Owners with 6-8 person tables report the canopy fully covers the seating area, which is rare at this price point. The LED lighting is also a hit — "The lights are what sold me. We eat outside almost every night now," one verified purchase noted. The crank mechanism and rotation get consistent praise for smooth operation.

## Where it falls short

Beyond the base, the solar panel is the other compromise. It's mounted flat on top of the hub, so it only gets optimal sun when the canopy is fully open and directly overhead. If you keep the umbrella tilted for afternoon shade, the panel gets less light, and you'll notice the LEDs dim after 2-3 hours. Also, the canopy fabric is thinner than premium brands like Coolaroo — you'll get shade, but it's not blackout-level. Some heat still radiates through on triple-digit days.

The powder coating on the frame is another watchpoint. It's fine for the first season, but owners in coastal or high-humidity areas have reported rust spots by month 8-10. A yearly touch-up with rust-inhibiting spray will extend its life.

## Who should buy this

- **The entertainer**: You host dinners and want ambient lighting without stringing extension cords. The built-in LEDs are a genuine convenience.
- **The flexibility seeker**: You want to reposition shade throughout the day. The 360-degree rotation and tilt make this easy.
- **The budget-conscious shopper**: At this price, you're getting cantilever function that typically costs 2-3x more — as long as you accept the base and fabric tradeoffs.

Skip it if you live in a consistently windy area (coastal, open plains) or demand premium fabric durability. You'll end up spending more on ballast or replacing it sooner.

## FAQ

**Does the umbrella need to be fully open for the LEDs to work?**
Yes. The solar panel is on the hub, so the canopy needs to be at least partially open to expose it to light. Closed, it won't charge.

**Can I replace the base with a heavier one?**
Absolutely. The pole is a standard 1.5-inch diameter, so it fits most aftermarket cantilever bases. Many buyers upgrade to a 100+ pound plate base for windy conditions.

**How long does the LED battery last?**
On a full day of direct sun, expect 4-5 hours of light. In partial shade, that drops to 2-3 hours. The battery is integrated into the hub and isn't user-replaceable.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want an affordable 10-foot cantilever umbrella with useful LED lighting and don't mind adding extra ballast for stability.
