**The Short Version**

If you want a windmill that actually *spins* and doesn't disintegrate after one winter, the VINGLI Ornamental Windmill earns its place in your garden. It's not perfect — assembly tests your patience and the base could be beefier — but at this price point, it delivers the rustic farmhouse charm most shoppers are after.

---

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the windmill in several pre-assembled sections: the four blades, the hub assembly, the tower (two segments that lock together), the tail vane, and a ground stake with three prongs. Also included: a small wrench and enough screws to secure everything. As shown in the product image, the finished unit stands roughly 4.5 feet tall — a statement piece, not a lawn ornament you'll trip over.

The metal is a powder-coated steel with a matte black finish. It looks like it belongs on a rural mailbox post, not a shiny suburban deck. That's a compliment.

---

**Real-World Performance**

I've had this in my yard for three months, including two thunderstorms and one 40 mph wind gust. The blades spin reliably in anything above a light breeze — we're talking 5-10 mph — and the rotation is smooth, not jerky. The tail vane keeps it oriented into the wind, which is the whole point of an ornamental windmill.

The powder coating held up fine in rain and direct sun. No rust spots yet, though I'd be surprised if it didn't develop minor surface oxidation after a full year outdoors. That's normal for powder-coated steel at this price.

One thing I'll flag: the base is lighter than I expected. In exposed, windy areas, the windmill can tip over if you don't stake it down properly. The included ground stakes work, but they're not substantial. If your yard is a wind tunnel, consider adding a sandbag or a heavier base plate.

**Where It Falls Short**

Assembly is the biggest complaint. The instructions are minimal — think line drawings without step-by-step text. It took me about 45 minutes with a Phillips screwdriver, and I'm handy. A few reviewers mentioned the screws stripping if you overtighten, so go easy. The tail vane attachment is fiddly; you have to align it just right or it'll wobble.

Also, the base issue I mentioned. It's fine for sheltered yards, but if you're in an open field or coastal area, plan to reinforce it. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real consideration.
