**The Flame King 100LB (23 Gal) Steel Propane Tank Cylinder with P** — a 68-pound steel beast that holds roughly 23.6 gallons of propane, enough to run a 40,000 BTU patio heater for about 50 hours straight. That's the headline. The question is whether this tank earns its keep in your backyard setup.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the tank itself — no regulator, no hose, no gauge. Just the cylinder with a standard POL valve (the left-hand thread type) and a protective collar that also serves as a lifting handle. The steel shell is 0.06 inches thick, which is noticeably beefier than the thin-walled aluminum tanks you'll see at camping stores. The foot ring is welded, not crimped, so it won't wobble on uneven patio stones.

One thing that stands out: the tank arrives with a plastic plug in the valve and a paint job that's actually baked on, not just sprayed. That matters for outdoor storage in rain or snow — cheap tanks get surface rust within months; this one holds up.

## Real-World Performance

The 100lb capacity is the whole story here. If you're running a high-BTU patio heater, a fire pit table, or a grill that sees weekly use, a standard 20lb tank is a chore — you're swapping it every couple of weekends. This tank eliminates that cycle. One buyer put it simply: *"I hooked this up to my 50,000 BTU patio heater and got three full months of nightly use before I had to refill."* That's the kind of feedback that shows up repeatedly in the 235 ratings.

The POL valve is a standard fit for most residential regulators — you won't need an adapter for the vast majority of grill or heater setups. The tank holds pressure well; no reports of leaks at the valve stem or the welded seams in the review data.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest talk: this tank is heavy. Empty, it's 68 pounds. Filled, you're hauling 168 pounds to the refill station. Unless you have a truck bed and a hand truck, you'll be wrestling this thing. That's a physical limitation of any 100lb tank, but it's worth saying plainly.

Second, there's **no fuel gauge**. You'll be doing the "hot water trick" down the side of the tank to check levels, or buying an aftermarket gauge that screws into the POL valve. For the price point, a basic gauge would have been a nice inclusion.

Third, a few buyers noted shipping scuffs — the paint can get scratched in transit. It's cosmetic, doesn't affect function, but if you're particular about aesthetics, it's a minor ding.

## Who Should Buy This

## FAQ

**Q: Does this tank come with a regulator or hose?**
A: No. It's just the cylinder with a POL valve. You'll need to purchase a regulator and hose separately, which typically costs $15–$30.

**Q: Can I get this refilled at any propane station?**
A: Yes, most refill stations handle 100lb tanks. They'll weigh it and charge by the gallon. Exchange programs (like Blue Rhino) won't take it — those are for 20lb tanks only.

**Q: How long will a full tank last?**
A: At 40,000 BTU/hour, expect roughly 50–60 hours of continuous use. At 20,000 BTU (a typical fire pit), double that. Your actual mileage depends on your appliance's efficiency.
