## The INTEX Excursion 4: More Boat Than You'd Expect for the Price

Most inflatable boats under $200 are pool toys with delusions of grandeur. The INTEX Excursion 4 isn't that. With a 4.4/5 rating across 220 reviews, this is the rare budget inflatable that anglers and weekend paddlers actually trust on real water. But it's not without compromises — let me break down exactly what you're getting.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The "set" includes the boat itself, two 54-inch aluminum oars with molded plastic grips, a high-output manual air pump, and a repair patch kit. Missing from the box: a carry bag (you'll want one) and any motor mount provisions. The boat measures 10 feet 4 inches long with a 4-foot 7-inch beam, and it's rated for four adults or a max payload of 1,100 pounds. Packed weight is 62 pounds — manageable for two people, a workout for one.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The Excursion 4 uses a three-layer laminated PVC construction (outer PVC, inner polyester mesh, more PVC). That's the same basic build as INTEX's pricier Mariner line, minus the reinforced floor. The floor here is an I-beam inflatable design — not a hard floor — which means it's lighter but slightly less rigid underfoot. In practice, it works fine for casting and paddling; just don't expect to stand up and walk around like you would on an aluminum john boat.

The three independent air chambers (two side tubes plus the floor) are a genuine safety feature. If you puncture one tube on a submerged branch, the other side and floor keep you buoyant. Reviewers consistently mention this as a deciding factor. As one buyer put it: *"I hit a submerged log and tore a 2-inch gash in the port side. The boat stayed upright, and I paddled 200 yards back to shore with zero panic."*

The oars deserve a mention. At 54 inches, they're properly sized for the boat's beam — not the undersized plastic paddles that come with cheaper inflatables. They're aluminum with molded plastic blades, so they'll flex a bit under hard strokes, but they're serviceable for a day of trolling or a short crossing.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Three things dominate the positive reviews. First, the setup time. Once you've done it once, inflation runs 10–15 minutes with the included pump. Second, the stability — the wide beam and inflatable floor make it forgiving for first-timers. Third, the price-to-capability ratio. One reviewer summarized it neatly: *"I've owned inflatables for 15 years. This tracks like boats twice the price."* Owners also repeatedly note the grab handles on all four corners, which make solo recovery from the water and tying off at a dock genuinely easier.

**Where It Falls Short**

The included hand pump is the biggest frustration. It works, but it's a single-action pump — every stroke only pushes air on the downstroke. Expect 15–20 minutes of continuous pumping to hit the recommended 1.25 PSI on the side tubes. If you're not in shape, you'll be sweating before you hit the water. Most owners buy a 12V electric pump within a month; budget for that if you value your time.

The lack of a motor mount is also worth noting. There's no transom reinforcement, and the floor isn't rigid enough for a trolling motor's weight distribution. This is a paddle boat, period. If you're planning to motorize, look at the Mariner 4 instead.

At 62 pounds, it's also heavier than you'd expect for an inflatable. Dragging it across sand or gravel will wear you out, and without a carry bag, the handles put all the weight on your hands. A $30 wheeled bag solves this, but it's an extra cost.

**Who Should Buy This**

The Excursion 4 fits three buyer profiles: the budget angler who needs a stable platform for lakes and slow rivers within a half-mile of the launch; the family camper who wants a boat the kids can't wreck and that packs into a trunk; and the first-time inflatable buyer who wants to test whether a soft boat works for them without dropping $400+.

Skip it if you need motor capability, if you're regularly carrying the boat more than 100 yards solo, or if you expect a rigid floor for standing and casting all day.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does it take to fully inflate?**
With the included pump, 15–20 minutes. With a 12V electric pump (recommended), under 5 minutes. Don't overinflate — follow the printed PSI on each chamber.

**Q: Can this handle a small outboard motor?**
No. There's no motor mount, and the floor isn't rigid enough for the torque. This is a paddle-only boat.

**Q: Is it safe for kids?**
Yes, with adult supervision. The three-chamber design adds a real safety margin, and the grab handles give kids something to hold. But maintain rated pressure — a soft boat is a tippy boat.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely capable four-person inflatable for under $200, you're paddling within sight of shore, and you don't mind a workout during setup.

**Skip it if:** You need motor capability, you're fishing solo and want a rigid floor, or you lack the patience for the hand pump.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, honest value that outperforms its price class. The pump and lack of a motor mount hold it back from greatness, but for the core use case of paddling and light fishing, this is one of the best budget inflatables available.
