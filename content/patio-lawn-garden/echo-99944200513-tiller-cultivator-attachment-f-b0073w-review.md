If you already own an Echo PAS-2620 or Pro Attachment Series power head, the 99944200513 tiller attachment transforms it into a legitimate cultivator for about half the cost of a standalone unit. If you don't — well, keep reading, because there's a catch you need to know before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the tiller head assembly: eight heat-treated steel tines arranged in a staggered pattern, a metal gear housing, and the drive shaft that locks into your Echo power head. No wrench, no grease, no instructions beyond a basic foldout. You'll need a 10mm socket and about five minutes to mount it. The tines span 10 inches of working width, which is narrow enough for raised beds and tight rows but wide enough to cover ground reasonably fast.

As shown in the product image, the tines have a slight curve that digs aggressively rather than just scraping the surface. This matters when you're breaking new ground.

## How It Performs in Real Dirt

I tested this on a patch of Georgia clay that hadn't been touched in two seasons. First pass was rough — the tines bounced on the hard crust until I let the machine's weight settle. Once the tines bit in, it chewed through to about 6 inches deep without stalling. The gear case runs in an oil bath, so it stays cool even after 20 minutes of continuous work.

The 10-inch width is a double-edged sword. It's perfect for between established rows — you won't slice into neighboring plants. But if you're trying to till a 4x8 bed, expect to make multiple passes. Plan on overlapping each pass by about 2 inches to avoid skipping strips.

**The $64 question:** Does it work with other brands? No. This is Echo's proprietary PAS (Power Attachment System) mount. It fits PAS-225, PAS-260, PAS-2620, and Pro Attachment Series power heads. If you own a Ryobi, Troy-Bilt, or Craftsman multi-tool system, stop right now — this won't bolt up, and no adapter exists.

## What 225 Owners Are Saying

The 4.5-star average holds up when you read the reviews. One verified buyer wrote: "I've used this to till three garden beds and it hasn't stripped a gear yet. My old MTD cultivator died after one season." Another echoed the depth complaint: "Works great but there's no way to lock the depth — your arms get tired holding it down."

The most common praise: durability. Multiple reviewers mention running it for years without maintenance beyond wiping off dirt. The most common complaint, beyond compatibility, is vibration. At full throttle, the tines transmit noticeable shake through the shaft. Wear gloves; your hands will thank you.

## Where It Falls Short

- **No depth control.** You regulate depth by lifting or pressing. On uneven ground, this means inconsistent tilling depth.
- **Narrow width.** 10 inches is slow for larger plots. If you're tilling more than 200 square feet, rent a walk-behind tiller instead.
- **Price-to-value depends on your power head.** If you're buying a power head *just* for this attachment, you're better off with a dedicated cultivator. The attachment only makes sense if you already have an Echo multi-tool.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Raised bed gardeners** with existing Echo PAS power heads
- **Small plot vegetable growers** who need to weed between rows without damaging crops
- **Homeowners** who want to avoid storing a second gas engine

**Skip it if** you don't own an Echo power head, or if your garden plot exceeds roughly 300 square feet.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my Echo SRM-225 trimmer?**  
No. That's a straight-shaft trimmer, not a PAS model. You need a power head with the removable attachment coupler — check for a silver collar near the engine.

**Q: Can it break through sod or new lawn?**  
It'll do it, but slowly. Take multiple shallow passes, and don't force it deeper than 2 inches on the first go. Wet sod will clog the tines quickly.

**Q: Does it come with the drive shaft lubricant?**  
No. You'll need to apply grease to the drive shaft splines before first use. Echo sells a tube, but any lithium-based grease works.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you own an Echo PAS/Pro power head and need a reliable, no-nonsense cultivator for beds, borders, and between-row weeding. The steel tines are built to last, the gear case holds up, and it's genuinely useful for small-scale work.

**Skip it if** you're starting from scratch or working large ground. The proprietary mount locks you into Echo's ecosystem, and the narrow width will frustrate you on anything bigger than a backyard plot.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does exactly what it claims, does it well, and doesn't break. The only real penalties are the lack of depth adjustment and the brand lock-in. At this price, that's a fair trade.
