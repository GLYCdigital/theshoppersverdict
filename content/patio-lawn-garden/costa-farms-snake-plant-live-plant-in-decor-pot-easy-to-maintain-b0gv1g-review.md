## The Snake Plant That Survives Your Schedule

If you've killed every plant you've ever owned, the snake plant (Sansevieria) is the botanical equivalent of a reset button. Costa Farms' version — a live plant already potted in a decor container — is one of the most reliable ways to test that theory without spending much.

Here's the honest breakdown before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains one live snake plant already planted in a decorative pot. Costa Farms ships it in a protective sleeve with the pot wrapped separately to prevent cracking. Most buyers report the plant arrives upright and intact — one reviewer noted, *"It came in perfect condition, no broken leaves, and the pot wasn't chipped. I was honestly surprised given how rough shipping can be."*

The decor pot is a matte ceramic-style container — not the flimsy plastic nursery pot you might expect. It has a clean, modern look that works on a desk, shelf, or side table without needing a cachepot. The plant itself is a standard snake plant (Dracaena trifasciata) with the classic upright, sword-like leaves featuring yellow margins and green centers.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Here's the thing about snake plants: they're nearly indestructible. This one tolerates low light, dry air, and inconsistent watering. It's a CAM plant, meaning it opens its stomata at night to exchange gases — which is why it's often recommended for bedrooms. It also purifies air by filtering formaldehyde, benzene, and other VOCs, though the effect is modest in a single plant.

The leaves are stiff and upright, so it won't flop over or require staking. It grows slowly — you'll see a new shoot every few months, not every week. That's a feature for low-maintenance buyers, not a bug.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the plant's condition on arrival and its resilience. One buyer put it simply: *"I've had this on my desk for three months. I've watered it maybe four times. It still looks exactly like the day it arrived."* Another noted, *"Perfect for my office — no windows, fluorescent lights only, and it's thriving."*

Multiple reviewers also appreciate that the pot is genuinely usable. It's not a cheap black nursery pot wrapped in plastic. It looks intentional, which matters when you're putting it in a visible spot.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest issue is the pot's lack of drainage holes. Snake plants are prone to root rot if their roots sit in water. You'll need to water sparingly — maybe every 2-3 weeks, and only when the soil is completely dry. If you're an overwaterer (most plant killers are), either drill a hole in the pot or repot into something with drainage. This isn't a dealbreaker, but it's a genuine design flaw.

The second issue is size inconsistency. Some buyers received a lush, multi-shoot plant; others got a smaller 2-3 leaf starter. If you're expecting a large floor plant, this isn't it. It's tabletop-sized. One reviewer expressed mild disappointment: *"The pot is nice, but the plant was smaller than the photos suggested. It's fine, just not what I pictured."*

## FAQ

**Q: How often should I water this snake plant?**
A: Every 2-3 weeks, and only when the soil is completely dry. In winter or low light, stretch it to 4 weeks. When in doubt, don't water.

**Q: Does it need direct sunlight?**
A: No. It tolerates low light, but bright, indirect light will make it grow faster and keep the yellow edges more vibrant. Avoid harsh afternoon sun, which can scorch the leaves.

**Q: Is the pot really decorative, or can I keep it long-term?**
A: You can keep it long-term, but because there's no drainage hole, you must water carefully. If you tend to overwater, repot into a pot with drainage holes — the plant will thank you.
