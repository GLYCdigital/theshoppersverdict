If you've ever priced a decorative wishing well planter at a garden center, you know they run $100–$150 for something that looks like it was assembled from a craft kit. The Best Choice Products version typically lands closer to $60, which raises an immediate question: what's the catch?

After digging through 215 ratings and the construction details, the short answer is — not much, provided you're willing to spend 20 minutes with a screwdriver and an afternoon with a can of sealant.

**What's actually in the box**

You get pre-cut cedar wood panels, a square base planter box, a roof assembly, a hanging basket, and all necessary hardware. The pieces are routed and drilled, so you're not cutting anything. As one buyer put it: *"I put this together by myself in under half an hour. The pieces fit perfectly, no gaps."*

The assembled footprint is roughly 21 by 21 inches, standing about 3 feet tall to the roof peak. That's compact enough for a corner of a patio or tucked against a porch railing, but substantial enough to read as a real garden feature rather than a tchotchke.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The cedar is the real selling point here. It's naturally rot-resistant, which is why most buyers report getting multiple seasons out of this thing. But — and this is critical — the wood ships untreated. The finish is raw, and the product listing doesn't scream about sealing it. That's the single biggest source of negative reviews.

One verified buyer warned: *"I didn't seal it and it warped badly by spring. My fault, but I wish the instructions had been more forceful about it."* Another countered: *"Two years outside in Tennessee, sealed with Thompson's, and it still looks great."*

The two-tier design is genuinely useful. The base planter is roughly 7 inches deep — enough for annuals or herbs — and the hanging basket hangs from the roof beam. The basket chain is thin, though. If you're loading it with wet potting mix and trailing petunias, consider swapping in a heavier chain from a hardware store for about $3.

Drainage is adequate. The base has pre-drilled holes, and the basket has a liner tray. No buyer complaints about waterlogged roots, which is more than I can say for several pricier planters I've tested.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the sealing issue, the wood is on the thinner side. The roof slats are decorative rather than structural — they'll handle a light snowfall but could crack under heavy accumulation. The paint/stain is entirely up to you; if you want a color other than bare cedar, factor in those supplies.

The instructions are minimal line drawings. Not a dealbreaker, but if you're not handy, watch a video tutorial first.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the gardener who wants immediate visual payoff without a big budget. It's perfect for:

- Renters who want a portable garden feature they can move or take along
- First-time homeowners filling a bare corner without dropping $150
- Anyone who enjoys a 30-minute weekend project with a visible result

Skip it if you want a zero-maintenance, leave-it-out-forever planter, or if you live somewhere with brutal winters and won't commit to sealing or storing it.

**FAQ**

*Does it hold up in rain?* The cedar handles moisture fine, but standing water against the base over years will eventually soften the wood. Wet feet plus dirt accelerates that — put a couple of bricks or pot feet under it.

*Can you paint it?* Yes, and you should. Any exterior latex or stain works. Seal before first planting, not after.

*What's the actual interior planting depth?* About 7 inches. Great for annuals, herbs, and small perennials. Not deep enough for a rose bush or anything with a taproot.

**The verdict**

The Best Choice Products Rustic Wooden Wishing Well Planter earns a **4.2 out of 5** — not because it's flawless, but because at this price point, the combination of pre-cut cedar, two-tier design, and honest assembly requirements delivers real value. The failures almost all trace back to skipping the sealant step.

**Buy it if** you're okay with a light DIY afternoon and want a charming, functional planter that looks like it cost twice as much.

**Skip it if** you want something that survives neglect, or you need a deeper planter for larger plants. This one rewards a little care — and punishes laziness.

For the money, it's one of the better decorative planters under $75 on Amazon right now. Just buy the sealant at the same time you hit "add to cart."
