The Thule 910XT Passage 2 is the Swiss Army knife of trunk-mounted bike carriers — but it's not without a few nicks. With a 4.1-star rating across 204 reviews, it's a solid performer, but the real question is whether it's the right fit for *your* car and *your* bikes. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the assembled carrier, four padded frame cradles, two anti-sway straps, and six ratcheting straps. That's it — no tools required. The whole unit folds down to about 28 x 10 x 3 inches, which means it tucks into a corner of your garage or trunk when not in use. At 9.5 pounds, it's light enough to move between vehicles if you own multiple cars.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The steel frame has a powder-coated finish that resists rust and scratches. The rubber cradles are thick enough to protect your top tube, and the anti-sway straps do their job — bikes stay planted even at 70 mph on bumpy interstates. The ratcheting straps tighten with satisfying clicks and hold tension well over a 200-mile trip.

The most impressive feature is the tool-free install. Hook the top straps over your trunk lid, thread the bottom hooks through the gap between trunk and bumper, and ratchet down. First-timers will have it mounted in under five minutes. The rack accommodates a 35-inch wheelbase and handles bikes up to 35 pounds each.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reviewers repeatedly mention the stability. One user wrote, *"Drove from Chicago to Colorado with two mountain bikes — no wobble, no movement, no issues. The anti-sway straps are genius."* Another noted, *"I've owned three trunk racks. This is the first one that doesn't bounce or shift on rough roads."* The build quality also draws consistent praise — multiple reviewers mention the rack surviving years of seasonal use without rust or strap failure.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest complaint in the reviews is paint damage. One reviewer reported, *"The straps left permanent rub marks on my trunk lid after a single trip."* The issue isn't the straps themselves — it's improper routing. If you let the straps rub against painted surfaces instead of following the diagram, you'll get wear. Some hatchback owners also noted that the top hooks can catch on spoilers, making contact with the paint. Also, there's no built-in locking mechanism. You'll need to buy Thule's locking strap kit or use cable locks to secure your bikes.

Fit is another consideration. The rack lists compatibility with most vehicles, but if you drive a late-model sedan with a sharply angled trunk or a prominent spoiler, check Thule's fit guide before buying. A few reviewers with newer Honda and Toyota models reported the straps wouldn't reach properly.

**Who should buy this**

The Passage 2 is ideal for the casual cyclist with two bikes who wants a reliable rack that doesn't require a hitch or roof crossbars. It's perfect for weekend trips, commuting to trailheads, or occasional family rides. It's also a strong choice for multi-car households because you can move it between vehicles in minutes.

Skip it if you haul bikes weekly, carry heavy e-bikes, or own a car with a carbon fiber or oddly shaped trunk. For those scenarios, a hitch-mounted or roof rack is a better investment.

**FAQ**

*Will it fit my car?*
Thule's website has a vehicle fit guide. Measure your trunk opening and check for spoilers — that's the main compatibility risk. The rack works on most sedans, hatchbacks, and minivans.

*Can I use it with a carbon fiber frame?*
Yes, but only if you use the included frame cradles correctly. The rubber padding protects the top tube, but if your bike has an oddly shaped or oversized tube, consider adding pipe insulation as a buffer.

*How do I prevent paint scratches?*
Route the straps exactly as the diagram shows — never let them contact painted surfaces directly. Also, clean your trunk before mounting to remove grit that can act as sandpaper.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a dependable, tool-free trunk rack for two bikes, value stability at highway speeds, and don't mind checking your strap routing once.

**Skip it if:** You need a locking system, drive a car with a tricky trunk design, or plan to carry bikes more than twice a week.

The Thule 910XT Passage 2 earns a 4.2 out of 5. It's not perfect — paint risk and lack of locks are real downsides — but for the price and convenience, it's one of the best trunk-mounted options available. Just read the manual and take five extra minutes to route everything correctly, and you'll get years of reliable service.
