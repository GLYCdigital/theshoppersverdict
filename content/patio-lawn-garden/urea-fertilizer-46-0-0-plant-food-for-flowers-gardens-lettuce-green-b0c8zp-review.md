## The 46-0-0 Urea Fertilizer: A Nitrogen Powerhouse That Demands Respect

If you've ever stared at a yellowing lawn or stunted vegetable bed and wondered why your plants look hungry, the answer is almost always nitrogen. This Urea Fertilizer 46-0-0 is the highest-nitrogen granular option you'll find on Amazon — 46% actual nitrogen by weight. That's not marketing hype; it's chemistry. Urea (CH₄N₂O) is the most concentrated dry nitrogen source available to home gardeners, and this bag delivers exactly what the label promises.

## What's Actually in the Bag

You get pure prilled urea granules — small, uniform white beads that look like coarse salt. The 46-0-0 analysis means zero phosphorus, zero potassium. It's a single-nutrient fertilizer designed for one job: pushing vegetative growth. The prills are consistent in size, which matters more than you'd think — uniform granules spread evenly with a handheld spreader, avoiding the striped lawn effect you get with poorly sized products.

## Where It Falls Short: Read This Before You Buy

This fertilizer will burn your plants if you misuse it. High nitrogen means high risk. Apply it to dry grass without watering, and you'll see brown scorch marks within days. The ammonia gas released during breakdown can also damage seedlings if granules land directly on foliage.

It's also incomplete. If your soil is deficient in phosphorus or potassium — common in sandy soils and heavy feeders like tomatoes — this alone won't fix it. You'll need to pair it with a balanced fertilizer or amend with bone meal and greensand. One reviewer learned this the hard way: "Great for my lawn, but my tomato plants stayed small until I added a phosphorus source."

Finally, it has a shelf-life consideration. Urea absorbs moisture from the air and can clump into hard chunks if the bag isn't sealed tightly. Store it in a cool, dry place and use it within a year.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I mix this with water and use it as a liquid fertilizer?**
A: Yes. Dissolve 1 tablespoon per gallon of water for a mild foliar feed. Always test on a few leaves first — full-strength solutions can burn foliage, especially in hot sun.

**Q: How often should I apply it?**
A: For lawns, once every 6–8 weeks during the growing season. For vegetables, side-dress every 4–6 weeks. Over-application leads to excessive leafy growth at the expense of fruit development and can leach into groundwater.

**Q: Is it safe for organic gardens?**
A: No. Urea is synthetic. If you follow organic certification standards, look for blood meal or composted manure instead.
