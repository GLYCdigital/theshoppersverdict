Let me start with what you see in the product images: a classic red-and-clear glass reservoir sitting on a molded plastic base with three feeding ports and perches. The glass holds roughly 8 ounces of nectar — enough for a couple of days with moderate traffic, but you'll be refilling more often than with larger 16-ounce feeders. That's the tradeoff for a compact design that hangs easily from a shepherd's hook or tree branch.

**What's in the box is refreshingly simple:** the glass bottle, the plastic base with integrated perches, the ant moat cup, and a wire hanger. No unnecessary doo-dads. Assembly takes about 30 seconds — screw the glass onto the base, hang it, fill it. The ant moat sits between the hanger and the reservoir, and it works as advertised. Fill it with water, and ants can't cross the gap. That's a genuinely useful feature that many feeders at this price point get wrong.

**Performance-wise, this feeder delivers where it counts.** The glass reservoir is easy to clean — a bottle brush reaches every corner, and unlike plastic, it won't develop that cloudy film after repeated sun exposure. The red base and feeding ports do their job attracting hummingbirds; the color reads clearly from a distance. The perches are long enough for two or three birds to feed simultaneously, which is a real plus if you have territorial hummers that chase each other off.

Buyers consistently praise the same things. Skip it if you need a high-capacity feeder for heavy traffic areas or if you have persistent bee problems that you don't want to manage.

**FAQ:**

**Does the ant moat actually work?** Yes, when you keep it filled with water. The water creates a barrier ants won't cross. Just check it every few days during dry spells.

**How often should I clean this feeder?** Every 2-3 days in hot weather, especially if you're using sugar water. The glass makes this easier than plastic — a quick rinse and a bottle brush does the job.

**Will bees ruin it?** They can be a nuisance if the nectar is too high in the ports. Keep the level below the port openings and consider a bee guard if your yard has a heavy wasp population.
