**First Saturday Lime Insect Deterrent Review: The 20-Minute Monthly Fix for Your Yard**

Most insect control products fall into two camps: chemical sprays that nuke everything (including beneficial bugs) or expensive monthly pest services that hit your wallet harder than the bugs. First Saturday Lime sits in a third category — a mineral-based powder that works with the biology of insects rather than against it. After digging through 235 ratings and the science behind it, here's what actually matters before you buy.

**What you actually get**

The product arrives as a fine, pale-gray powder — essentially pelletized limestone (calcium carbonate) with a proprietary blend of essential oils. It's not a bait, not a poison, and not a synthetic pyrethroid. The key mechanic is desiccation: the powder sticks to insects' exoskeletons and absorbs the waxy lipid layer that keeps them hydrated. They dry out and die. It also disrupts their scent trails, which is why ants and roaches stop marching in formation after application.

As shown in the product images, the 25-pound bag is substantial — roughly the size of a large dog food bag. Coverage is generous: one bag handles about 5,000 square feet. For context, that's a quarter-acre lot's worth of treated perimeter. You apply it with a standard broadcast spreader on a low setting, water it lightly (about one-tenth of an inch), and you're done for the month. No mixing, no spraying, no gear cleanup.

**Performance and real-world use**

In testing, the powder works best as a barrier treatment. Spread it along your foundation line, around the perimeter of the yard, under porches, and along fence lines. Where it shines is against crawling insects — roaches, ants, earwigs, silverfish, and palmetto bugs. Fleas and ticks in the yard also die on contact, which is a major win for dog owners.

The most common feedback from buyers confirms the "set it and forget it" appeal. One verified purchaser wrote: *"I've used every chemical spray on the market for palmetto bugs. This is the first thing that actually kept them out for more than a week. Two applications and I stopped seeing them entirely."* Another noted: *"My vet recommended it for fleas since my dog is allergic to chemical treatments. Spread it in March, reapplied in May, and no fleas all summer."*

There's a notable fringe benefit: the lime is food-grade calcium, so your lawn gets a mild nutrient boost. Multiple reviewers mentioned greener grass and healthier soil after a few months of use. That's a bonus you won't get from bifenthrin.

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about the limitations. This is not a contact killer — if you're staring at a roach infestation inside your kitchen, you need a spray or bait for immediate results. The dry-out process takes 24 to 72 hours, and it's not effective on flying insects like mosquitoes or wasps. The manufacturer is clear about that, but some buyers miss it.

The bigger issue is moisture. The powder must stay relatively dry to work. Heavy rain or daily sprinkler runoff will wash it into the soil, neutralizing the desiccant effect. If you live in a rainy climate or have an irrigation system, you'll need to reapply every two to three weeks rather than monthly, which cuts into the value proposition. Also, the powder is fine — apply it on a breezy day and you'll get a face full of chalky dust. A dust mask is non-negotiable.

**Who should buy this**

- **Dog owners** dealing with fleas and ticks who want to avoid chemical treatments on their lawn.
- **Southern homeowners** fighting palmetto bugs and roaches that laugh at standard sprays.
- **Homesteaders or organic gardeners** who want pest control without collateral damage to pollinators.
- **Anyone tired of monthly pest control bills** — one bag at roughly $30 replaces several service calls.

Skip it if you need immediate knockdown of an active indoor infestation, or if you live in a consistently wet climate and can't commit to reapplication.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is it safe for dogs and kids?**
A: Yes, once dry. It's food-grade calcium carbonate — the same stuff in antacids. The essential oils are plant-derived. Just keep pets off the treated area until the powder is watered in and dry.

**Q: Does it really work on fire ants?**
A: It kills them, but it's slower than dedicated fire ant baits. Spread it generously over the mound and give it a week. It won't eliminate the queen as fast as a poison bait, but it reduces mound activity significantly.

**Q: How often do I really need to apply it?**
A: Monthly in dry conditions. If you get heavy rain, treat it as a two-week product. Watch the product color — if the powder is no longer visible on the soil surface, it's time to reapply.
