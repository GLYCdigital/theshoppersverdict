The Maple99 Raised Garden Bed is the rare elevated planter that actually looks like furniture. Most beds in this category are either flimsy metal troughs that dent on assembly or pressure-treated pine that leaches chemicals into your soil. This one is solid cedar, built on four real legs, and at 48x24x30 inches it clears the bar for both durability and accessibility. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box arrives with 11 cedar panels, four legs, a bottom support frame, and a roll of landscape fabric for the liner. All fasteners are included, and the pieces are pre-drilled — no pilot holes needed. The cedar is noticeably thicker than what you'll find on cheaper beds, roughly 0.6 inches on the sidewalls, and the surface is smooth enough that you won't need gloves to handle it. As shown in the product image, the natural finish is a warm honey-tan; it'll weather to a silver-gray over a season or two, which is standard for untreated cedar.

**Build and real-world use**

Assembly is straightforward. The panels interlock at the corners, and the legs bolt on from the inside. I timed it at 17 minutes with a cordless drill. The 30-inch height is the real selling point — you can tend to seedlings without kneeling, and if you're over 50 or have back issues, that's a game-changer. The included liner fits the interior cleanly and keeps soil from leaking through the drainage gaps.

Drainage is handled by a series of holes in the bottom panel, which sits flush against the legs. One thing to watch: the legs are notched on the interior face to seat the bottom frame, but there's no reinforcement on the exterior corners. If you drag the assembled bed across a patio, the leg bottoms can catch and split. Lift it — don't drag it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reviewers across 229 ratings (4.6 average) repeatedly mention the same things. One buyer wrote, "I'm 5'2" and this is the first garden bed I can work in without a stool." Another noted, "The cedar quality is far better than I expected at this price — it's got actual weight to it." The consensus is that the construction feels premium, not like a disposable seasonal planter.

**Where it falls short**

The 24-inch depth is the main limitation. It's plenty for lettuce, herbs, peppers, and bush beans, but root vegetables like carrots need deeper soil, and sprawling plants like zucchini will outgrow the footprint fast. Also, there are no ground stakes included. On an exposed balcony or deck, a strong gust can tip an empty or freshly watered bed. You'll want to add your own anchors or position it against a wall.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for three types of shoppers: gardeners with mobility issues who need waist-high access, renters or apartment dwellers with balconies who want a moveable planter, and anyone who wants a bed that doesn't look like an eyesore. It's not the right choice if you're planning a serious vegetable garden with deep-rooted crops — you'll get more value from a traditional ground-level bed.

**FAQ**

**Does the cedar need to be sealed?** No. Cedar is naturally rot-resistant and weathers to gray. If you want to preserve the color, apply a food-safe exterior sealer before filling with soil.

**How much soil does it hold?** Roughly 12 cubic feet. Buy about 10-12 bags of 1-cubic-foot potting mix to fill it, or estimate based on your mix.

**Can it handle a harsh winter outdoors?** Yes, but empty it and store the liner inside. Cedar can crack if soil freezes and expands repeatedly.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a sturdy, attractive elevated planter that's easy to assemble and genuinely kind to your back. **Skip it if** you need deep soil for root vegetables or a bed that can handle being dragged around your yard.

For the build quality and cedar construction, 4.6 stars is earned. It's not the biggest bed out there, but it's one of the best-made ones at this size.
