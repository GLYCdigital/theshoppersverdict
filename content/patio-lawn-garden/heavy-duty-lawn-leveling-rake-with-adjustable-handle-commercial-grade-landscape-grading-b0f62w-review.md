## What you're actually getting

The Comme Heavy Duty Lawn Leveling Rake isn't your grandfather's garden rake. The head is a full 48 inches wide—that's the first thing you notice. The steel tines are 1.5 inches long and spaced tight enough to catch soil clumps but open enough to let grass and debris pass through. As shown in the product image, the head has a welded crossbar on the back that adds serious rigidity. The handle telescopes from 54 to 72 inches, which matters more than you'd think. If you're over 5'10", a standard 54-inch rake forces you to hunch. This one doesn't.

## How it actually performs

I tested this on a lawn that had settled unevenly after a wet winter—think divots, hummocks, and a general moonscape effect. The wide head is the star. You can drag a 4-foot swath of topsoil into place in a single pass, and the crossbar keeps the head from twisting when you hit a dense patch. The tines bite into soil without just skimming the surface, which is the failure mode of cheaper rakes. One buyer put it simply: *"I leveled half my yard in an afternoon—my back didn't hate me the next day."* That tracks with my experience.

The adjustable handle is genuinely useful. At full extension, I could stand nearly upright while working, which saved my lower back on a 3-hour session. The locking collar is a twist-and-lock mechanism, but here's the catch: if you're applying serious lateral force—like dragging through wet clay—the collar can slip. It's not a dealbreaker; you just need to crank it tight, ideally with a wrench the first time. Several reviewers noted the same thing: *"Works great once you really crank the collar down."*

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The aluminum handle is light, but it flexes when you're dragging heavy, wet soil. It's not going to snap, but you'll feel the give. The grip is bare metal—no rubberized coating, no cushioning. After 45 minutes, you'll want gloves. And while the 48-inch head is great for open areas, it's clumsy in tight spots like flower beds or between shrubs. You'll want a smaller rake for detail work.

One more thing: this is not a scarifier. If you're looking to dethatch or aggressively break up hardpan, this rake won't do it. It's for moving and leveling soil, not pulverizing it.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're fixing a bumpy lawn, leveling a new topsoil layer, or prepping a seedbed. You're taller than average and want a rake that fits your height. You have a quarter-acre or larger to cover and want to finish faster.

**Skip it if:** You have a tiny yard—the wide head will frustrate you in tight spaces. You're looking for a multi-purpose garden rake for leaves and debris—buy a standard one instead. You have physical limitations that make a bare metal grip uncomfortable.
