**What's Actually in the Box**

You get one 25-pound cardboard box of pool-grade diatomaceous earth — nothing more, nothing less. Dicalite is one of the largest DE producers in the world, mining from deposits in Nevada and Oregon, and this product is the calcined, milled powder specifically engineered for swimming pool filtration. The particle size is consistent, which matters more than brand loyalty. As the product image shows, it's a straightforward, no-frills box — the value is in what's inside, not the packaging.

**Performance: Where It Earns Its Keep**

DE filtration works because each microscopic diatom is a porous, labyrinthine shell that traps debris as water pushes through. Dicalite's material holds up its end of that bargain. Buyers consistently report crystal-clear water within hours of a fresh charge. One long-time pool owner noted, "I've used every major brand and this one filters just as well at a better price. My water stays clear all season."

The 25-pound size is the sweet spot for most residential pools. A standard charge for a 25,000-gallon pool runs 4-6 pounds, so you'll get roughly five full charges out of one box. If you backwash weekly during peak season, that's a summer's worth of filtration without a mid-July reorder.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme in reviews is cost-per-pound value. Shoppers repeatedly mention that Dicalite undercuts the pool-store house brands by a meaningful margin, often 20-30% less. Beyond price, buyers praise its dust control — the powder is less dusty than some competitors, though "less dusty" is relative. One reviewer summed it up: "Works exactly like the expensive stuff from the pool store. Same results, lower price."

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the powder is still a fine silicate dust. Pouring it into your skimmer without a dust mask will have you coughing. Do this downwind and you'll be dusting your patio furniture afterward. Second, the box has no resealable closure — once opened, it's a cardboard flap situation. If you don't transfer the remaining powder to a sealed plastic container, it will absorb moisture from humid air and clump, making it harder to measure accurately.

Third, and this is worth stating plainly: this is *not* food-grade DE. It's pool-grade, which means it's calcined at high temperatures and has different crystalline silica content. Don't use it for garden pest control or any food-related application. Buy the food-grade variety for that.

**Who Should Buy This**

**FAQ**

**Q: How much DE do I need for my filter?**
A: Check your filter's manual or grid area. A common rule of thumb: 1 pound of DE per 10 square feet of filter grid area. A typical 48-square-foot filter takes about 4-5 pounds. Don't overcharge — it can restrict flow.

**Q: Can I mix this with other brands of DE?**
A: Yes, DE is DE for filtration purposes. If you're topping off after a backwash, mixing brands is fine. Just ensure you're adding the right amount per your filter's specs.

**Q: How often should I add DE?**
A: After every backwash, and additionally when the pressure gauge reads 8-10 PSI above the clean baseline. That's the signal your filter is loaded and needs fresh media.
