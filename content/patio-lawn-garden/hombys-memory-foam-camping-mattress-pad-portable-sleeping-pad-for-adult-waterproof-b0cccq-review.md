**A 3.4-star camping mattress tells a story. Here's what 170 buyers actually found.**

The HOMBYS Memory Foam Camping Mattress Pad sits in that awkward middle ground of outdoor sleep gear. It's not a budget foam pad, and it's not a premium self-inflating mattress. It's something in between — and the user reviews reflect exactly that tension.

**What you actually get**

Unboxing the HOMBYS reveals a 2-inch thick memory foam pad, roughly 25 x 75 inches for the standard size (they offer larger versions too). The outer shell is a soft brushed polyester that feels decent against the skin, and the bottom layer is a waterproof TPU coating. It arrives compressed in a vacuum-sealed bag, with two compression straps included for re-rolling.

The pad weighs in around 4-5 pounds depending on size. That's heavier than an inflatable pad but lighter than a full camping cot. The roll diameter when compressed is about 10 inches — manageable, but not ultralight backpacking territory.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The memory foam core is the headline feature, and it does deliver on the basic promise. Multiple buyers describe sleeping significantly better than on air mattresses that lose pressure overnight. One reviewer noted, *"I woke up without the usual hip pain I get on inflatables."* The waterproof bottom held up against tent floor condensation, which several users confirmed.

But here's where the rating drops. The foam density is the weak point. At roughly 2 inches of memory foam with no support layer beneath, the pad compresses fully under heavier sleepers. Several reviews mention feeling the ground by 3 AM. One succinctly put it: *"Great for kids, not for a 200-pound adult."*

The folding issue is even more universal. Because the pad folds rather than rolls flat, the creases from packaging never fully disappear. Multiple buyers reported visible ridges that made themselves known on the lower back after a few hours. Heat retention is another common complaint — memory foam sleeps warm, and several warm-weather campers found it uncomfortable above 70°F.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- The genuine memory foam feel, not hollow inflatable comfort
- Waterproof bottom that actually works in damp tents
- Compact rolled size when compressed
- Easy to clean surface — a damp cloth handles most messes

**Where it falls short**

- Foam density insufficient for heavier adults
- Permanent fold creases that affect comfort
- No integrated pillow or inflation system
- Bulky compared to modern inflatable options
- Retains body heat in warm weather

**Who should buy this**

This pad makes sense for three specific people: car campers under 180 pounds who want foam comfort without inflating anything, kids transitioning from sleeping bags to pads, and RV owners needing a spare bed for guests. It also works as a stadium seat cushion or picnic pad in a pinch.

Skip it if you're a side sleeper over 200 pounds, an ultralight backpacker counting ounces, or someone who camps in hot climates. You'll be more comfortable with a quality inflatable pad or a thicker self-inflating model.

**FAQ**

**Does this pad work for side sleepers?**  
Side sleepers under 180 pounds generally find it acceptable. Heavier side sleepers report hip pressure points because the foam compresses fully.

**How do you clean it?**  
The outer shell is wipe-clean only. Spot clean with mild soap and water, then air dry. Do not machine wash.

**Is it truly waterproof on the bottom?**  
Yes, the TPU coating prevents ground moisture from seeping through. However, moisture can still get trapped between the pad and tent floor if left out in heavy rain.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a lighter camper wanting foam comfort at a reasonable price and don't mind the extra weight.

**Skip it if** you're a heavier adult, a warm sleeper, or need something packable for hiking.

The HOMBYS memory foam pad earns 3.3 stars. It's a solid budget option for the right user, but the foam density and folding issues keep it from being a universal recommendation. For car campers under 180 pounds, it's a reasonable choice — just know the limitations before you buy.
