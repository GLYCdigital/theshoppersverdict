If you've been gardening for more than a season, you've heard the pitch: mycorrhizal fungi attach to plant roots, extend the root system's reach, and help plants pull more water and nutrients from the soil. It sounds like magic, but it's actually biology. The question is whether Supercharge's granular product actually delivers on the promise—or whether it's just another bag of expensive powder destined to sit in the shed.

Here's what I found after digging through 229 ratings, customer photos, and the product specs.

## What You Actually Get

The product is a fine granular powder, not a liquid concentrate. That matters. Granular formulations are designed for one specific moment: when you're transplanting or planting. You sprinkle it into the planting hole, set the root ball on top, and backfill. The fungi need root contact to colonize. As one verified buyer put it, "I sprinkled a spoonful in each hole when planting my tomatoes and peppers. By midsummer, the plants were noticeably more vigorous than the ones I didn't treat."

The bag contains a blend of 25+ endomycorrhizal and ectomycorrhizal species, plus beneficial bacteria and trichoderma. That diversity is the selling point. Single-strain products are cheaper but less effective across different plant types. This one covers vegetables, trees, shrubs, and ornamentals.

## How It Performs in Real Gardens

The most common success stories involve vegetable gardens and newly planted trees. Gardeners consistently report stronger root systems at the end of the season, better drought tolerance, and fewer transplant shock symptoms. That last one is the biggest win. If you've ever watched a freshly planted shrub wilt for two weeks, you know the value of anything that reduces that stress.

Coverage is generous. One pound treats roughly 1,000–2,000 square feet depending on application rate. For a typical home vegetable plot, one bag lasts multiple seasons.

## What Buyers Praise

The overwhelming theme in positive reviews is root development. "I dug up my pepper plants in the fall and the root balls were twice the size of last year's," one reviewer noted. Another mentioned that their container-grown blueberries—notoriously finicky—finally thrived after two seasons of adding this at repotting.

Gardeners also appreciate the price-per-square-foot value. Compared to liquid mycorrhizal products that require multiple applications per season, one granular treatment at planting time is both cheaper and less labor-intensive.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: this is not a fertilizer. It will not make your plants greener in a week. The fungi take 4–6 weeks to establish a meaningful connection. If you're looking for a quick fix, buy something else.

The bigger limitation is application. Broadcasting it on top of existing lawn or garden soil does almost nothing—the fungi need root contact to survive and colonize. That makes it a product for new plantings, not existing beds. Some customers didn't read the instructions and were disappointed. That's on them, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Storage is another minor gripe. A few users reported the powder clumping after the bag was opened and exposed to humidity. Keep the bag sealed and it's fine, but it's not a set-and-forget product.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Vegetable gardeners** who plant annuals each spring and want bigger harvests
- **Anyone planting trees or shrubs** who wants to reduce transplant shock
- **Container gardeners** growing blueberries, raspberries, or other plants that need acidic, fungi-friendly soil
- **Organic gardeners** looking to reduce synthetic fertilizer dependence

Skip it if you're only maintaining an established lawn or existing flower beds without doing any new planting this season.

## FAQ

**Can I use this with chemical fertilizers?**
Yes, but avoid high-phosphorus synthetic fertilizers at the same time. Phosphorus can inhibit mycorrhizal colonization. Apply the fungi at planting, then wait 2–3 weeks before feeding.

**Does it work in containers?**
Yes, and it's actually where many gardeners see the biggest difference. Container soil lacks the natural fungal networks found in garden beds, so introducing them gives potted plants a real boost.

**How long does the powder stay viable once opened?**
Kept sealed in a cool, dry place, it remains effective for up to one season. After that, spore viability declines. Don't stockpile it.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're planting anything this season—vegetables, trees, shrubs, or perennials—and want stronger roots, better drought tolerance, and reduced transplant shock. The science is sound, the coverage is generous, and the price per square foot beats liquid alternatives.

**Skip it if** you have no new plantings planned, or if you expect visible results within days. This is a long-game product.
