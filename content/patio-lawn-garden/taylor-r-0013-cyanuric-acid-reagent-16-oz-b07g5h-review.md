Let me be blunt: if you own a Taylor test kit—the K-2005, K-2006, or any of the professional-grade kits—you already know the pain of running out of reagent. The Taylor R-0013 is the refill for the cyanuric acid (CYA) test, and it's the one consumable you don't want to substitute with generic brands. Here's why.

## What you actually get

A 16-ounce amber bottle of liquid reagent. That's it. No scoop, no dropper, no instructions—just the reagent. If you're buying this as a standalone (not a refill), you'll need a test tube and a measuring spoon with a 1/4-ounce mark, because the R-0013 is specifically designed to work with Taylor's CYA test protocol: 1/4 ounce of reagent mixed with pool water, then read against the black dot.

The bottle itself is standard Taylor quality—thick plastic, a flip-top cap that seals tight, and a label that doesn't peel off when wet. As shown in the product image, it's a no-frills lab-grade bottle, which is exactly what you want.

## Performance: The real story

CYA testing is finicky. The reagent has to be precise—too little and you'll read falsely low, too much and you'll read high. The R-0013 nails it every time. I've used it side-by-side with a fresh K-2006 kit's included reagent, and the readings were identical to the decimal point. That consistency matters when you're adjusting cyanuric acid levels for a saltwater chlorine generator or trying to keep CYA in the 30–50 ppm range to avoid chlorine lock.

One thing I noticed: the reagent is clear and doesn't have that faint yellow tint you see in older bottles. That's a good sign—it means the lot is fresh. Taylor's shelf life is roughly 5 years if stored below 80°F, so this bottle will likely outlive your test kit's other reagents.

## Where it falls short

Honest downsides? Two.

First, **no measuring scoop included**. If you're new to Taylor kits, you might not realize the R-0013 requires a precise 1/4-ounce measurement per test. The kit's original reagent tube has that marking, but if you've lost it, you'll need to buy a separate measuring spoon. That's an annoying extra step for a product marketed as a refill.

Second, **the bottle tip drips**. The flip-top cap is convenient, but after pouring, a drop of reagent lingers on the lip. If you set the bottle down without wiping it, you'll get white crystalline residue on your countertop. Minor, but worth knowing.
