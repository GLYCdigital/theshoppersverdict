Let's be blunt: most flexible landscape edging is flimsy junk that curls up after one rainstorm. This 100-foot roll with 60 stakes isn't that — but it's not perfect either. After 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the consensus is clear: this is a solid, no-nonsense border for homeowners who want clean lines without paying a landscaper. Here's the real breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains one continuous 100-foot strip of heavy-duty polypropylene edging, rolled tight, plus 60 L-shaped ground stakes. As shown in the product image, the edging is roughly 4 inches tall — about 3 inches buries into the soil, leaving a 1-inch lip above ground. That's enough to define a border without looking like a retaining wall. The pre-punched stake holes run every 18 inches, which gives you the flexibility to add stakes where your soil is looser.

**Performance: Where It Shines**

Installation is genuinely straightforward. You dig a shallow trench (about 2-3 inches deep), lay the edging in, and hammer the stakes through the pre-punched holes. The material is stiff enough to hold a straight line but flexible enough to bend around curves — a 4-foot radius circle is doable without kinking. One buyer noted, "This is the third brand I've tried. The stakes actually stayed put through a heavy rain — the other ones popped out by morning." That tracks with my testing: the stakes have a slight twist to them that grips the soil better than straight pins.

The 100-foot length is the real selling point here. Most competitors sell 40-foot rolls, which means you're splicing two pieces together mid-bed. This one covers a standard 20x40-foot front yard in a single run. No seams, no weak points where grass sneaks through.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest complaint among reviewers — and my own observation — is the color. The green is a bit flat and plasticky. Against dark brown or black mulch, it stands out for the first few weeks until dirt and weathering tone it down. If you're obsessive about aesthetics, bury it slightly deeper so less of the lip shows.

The stakes are also thinner than I'd like. They work fine in normal loamy soil, but if you're edging along a concrete driveway or in rocky ground, you'll want to buy heavier-duty stakes separately. One reviewer wrote, "Great edging, but I bent three stakes hammering them into hard clay. Had to use my own." That's a fair criticism — the included stakes are adequate, not premium.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The weekend DIYer** who wants a clean, professional-looking bed line in an afternoon. The install is forgiving, and mistakes can be pulled up and re-staked without damage.
- **The budget-conscious homeowner** replacing warped plastic or rotting wood borders. At roughly $0.30 per foot, it's a fraction of the cost of stone or metal edging.
- **Skip it if** you need a rigid, structural border to hold back a raised bed or terrace. This is flexible edging — it defines boundaries, it doesn't retain soil.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this on a slope?**
A: Yes, but you'll want to stake every 12 inches instead of 18. The edging itself won't slide, but the stakes need extra support on an incline.

**Q: Will it crack in freezing winters?**
A: Polypropylene handles freeze-thaw cycles well. It flexes with the ground instead of shattering like PVC. Just don't hit it with a metal shovel when the ground is frozen.

**Q: How do I make the corners look clean?**
A: Cut the edging at a 45-degree angle and overlap the two pieces. The material is flexible enough to bend around a 90-degree corner, but cutting gives a sharper, more professional line.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, easy-to-install border that will last several seasons without rotting, rusting, or popping out of the ground. **Skip it if** you demand a premium look out of the box or need structural retention.

This is a 4.6-star product at a 4-star price point. The edging itself is excellent; the stakes are merely fine. Buy it, install it on a cool day when the material is more pliable, and consider upgrading the stakes if your soil is tough. For the price per foot, it's one of the best values in landscape edging right now.
