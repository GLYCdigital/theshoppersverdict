**What you actually get**

The box contains one 1-pound resealable bag of fine, dark granules. That's it — no scoop, no measuring cup. The label says it covers 100 square feet, which for a typical row of 10–12 medium boxwoods is about right. The NPK ratio is 16-5-9 with chelated micronutrients — that's a heavy nitrogen hit with a controlled-release mechanism. What this means for your hedge: you'll see a deep green flush within 10–14 days, not overnight. The nitrogen is coated, so it releases over roughly 6–8 weeks rather than dumping all at once. This is exactly what boxwoods need, especially if you're dealing with winter bronzing or that sickly yellow-green look from iron deficiency.

Buy it if you have established boxwoods (3+ years old) showing chlorosis, slow growth, or winter stress. **FAQ**

*How often should I apply it?*
Twice per growing season: once in early spring (March–April) and again in late summer (August–September). Don't feed after September — you'll encourage tender new growth that frost will kill.

*Can I use it on other shrubs?*
Technically yes, but at your own risk. The ratio is tuned for boxwoods' specific needs. If you use it on azaleas or hollies, cut the rate in half and watch for leaf burn.

*Is it pet-safe after it dries?*
Once watered in and dried, the granules break down and pose minimal risk. But keep pets off the area for 24 hours after application, and store the bag out of reach — the concentrated product can cause stomach upset if ingested.
