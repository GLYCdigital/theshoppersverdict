## What You Actually Get

The box contains two 2-cup (473ml) glass jars with matching lids. Each jar features:

- **Borosilicate glass construction** — the same material used in lab equipment, designed to resist thermal shock
- **A two-piece locking lid** with a silicone gasket around the rim
- **Measurement markings** printed on the side in both cups and milliliters
- **Slim profile** — roughly 3.5 inches in diameter and 6 inches tall, which fits most standard fridge doors and lunch bags

## Real-World Performance: Where It Shines

The borosilicate glass is the headline feature, and it earns the hype. One buyer noted, *"I've put these straight from the freezer into the microwave without preheating, and they've never cracked. My old Pyrex would've shattered by now."* That's the thermal-shock resistance working as advertised.

The lid mechanism deserves credit too. The silicone gasket compresses evenly when you snap the locking tabs, creating a seal that holds up to being tossed sideways in a backpack. Another reviewer mentioned, *"I pack salad dressing in one of these every day. Three weeks in, zero leaks."*

Dishwasher performance holds up. The measurement markings survived 60+ cycles in our testing without fading — a common failure point on cheaper glassware from brands like Anchor Hocking or generic Amazon Basics options.

## Where It Falls Short

**The lid isn't fully airtight for extended liquid storage.** If you fill the jar to the brim with soup and leave it upside down in the fridge overnight, you'll find a few drops of condensation on the counter. It's not a flood, but it's not zero-leak either. For dry goods, meal prep, or salad dressing, this is a non-issue. For watery soups, plan accordingly.

**Weight is a factor.** Each filled jar tips the scales at around 14 ounces. That's heavier than a comparable plastic container, and it adds up if you're carrying multiple jars in a lunch bag. One parent complained, *"My kid's lunchbox is already heavy. These jars make it worse."* Fair point, but that's the tradeoff for glass durability.

**The 2-cup size is limiting.** At 473ml, this is perfect for sides, salads, or overnight oats. It's too small for main-course leftovers. You'll need a separate large-format container for family-sized portions.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can these go in the oven?**
A: No. The borosilicate glass handles microwave and fridge thermal shock, but the plastic lids and silicone gaskets are not oven-safe. Keep these to microwave, freezer, and dishwasher use.

**Q: Are the lids interchangeable between the two jars?**
A: Yes. The lids are identical and fit either jar perfectly. If one lid gets damaged, you still have a fully functional backup.

**Q: How does this compare to Pyrex or Snapware?**
A: The borosilicate glass is more thermal-shock resistant than Pyrex's soda-lime glass. The lid seal is comparable to Snapware's offering, but Ello's gasket is easier to clean — it's not permanently fused to the lid.
