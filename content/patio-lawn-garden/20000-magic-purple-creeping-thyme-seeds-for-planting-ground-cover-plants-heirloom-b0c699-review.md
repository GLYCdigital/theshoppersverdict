**What’s in the bag**

The packet contains a surprisingly generous volume of seeds — the count checks out. These are dust-fine, darker than typical thyme seed, and arrive in a simple resealable pouch. No stratification instructions printed on the label, no growing guide included. You get seeds and a basic plant name. That’s it.

**The honest reality of germination**

This is where the 3.0 rating comes from. Creeping thyme (*Thymus serpyllum*) is notoriously slow to germinate — 14 to 28 days minimum — and this seed lot doesn't do you any favors. Multiple verified buyers report the same pattern: planted directly in spring soil, waited three weeks, saw nothing. One reviewer wrote, *"I followed the packet directions exactly and got exactly zero sprouts. Waste of a season."*

The fix isn't complicated, but it's on you. Cold stratification for 4-6 weeks in the fridge before planting dramatically improves sprout rates. So does surface sowing — thyme needs light to germinate, so don't bury these seeds. Mix them with fine sand before scattering; they're so tiny they clump and drop in patches otherwise.

**What buyers praise when it works**

When germination happens, the results are genuinely lovely. The purple flowers are a deep, saturated violet — not the washed-out lavender you see in cheaper mixes. Established patches form a dense, aromatic mat that handles light foot traffic and stays green through mild winters. One happy customer noted, *"Took two seasons to fill in, but the third summer was a solid carpet of purple. Bees loved it."*

**Where it falls short**

Beyond germination struggles, patience is the real cost. This is not a quick ground cover. Expect bare soil for the first season while plants establish. Weeds will absolutely outcompete young thyme seedlings unless you stay on top of them. And if you're in a hot, humid climate (zone 8+), expect the plants to sulk through summer — thyme prefers cool, dry conditions.

**Who should buy this**
