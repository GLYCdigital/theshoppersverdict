If you've priced name-brand glyphosate concentrates lately, you know the pain. A quart of the blue-jug stuff runs $20-plus. Compare-N-Save 75324 gives you a full gallon of the same 41% active ingredient (isopropylamine salt of glyphosate) for roughly the same money. That's the headline. But let's dig into whether the savings cost you anything else.

**What's in the jug**

You get one gallon of milky-white concentrate. The label says it mixes at 2.5 to 5 ounces per gallon of water depending on weed toughness — so figure 25 to 50 gallons of ready-to-spray solution per jug. For context, that covers a LOT of fence lines, driveway cracks, and patio edges. A 1-gallon pump sprayer filled with the standard mix will knock out a typical suburban yard's worth of weeds several times over.

**Real-world performance**

The active chemistry here matches the industry standard. Spray it on a sunny day above 60°F, and you'll see weeds yellowing within 24 hours, fully crisped in 7-10 days. It's systemic, so it kills roots — not just leaves. That matters for perennial monsters like bindweed and poison ivy, where surface-only sprays just make them angry.

One caveat: rain. Check the forecast. If it pours within six hours of application, you're redoing that section. That's true of every glyphosate product, but worth stating plainly.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews holds up. The dominant theme in customer feedback is value. One reviewer put it simply: *"Same active ingredient as the big names, half the price. Why pay more?"* Another noted the jug lasted them two full seasons for a half-acre property — a detail that tracks with the dilution math.

Gardeners also repeatedly praise it for spot treatment before planting. Spray, wait ten days, till — no regrowth. That's the systemic action doing its job.

**Where it falls short**

This is not a selective herbicide. It doesn't know a dandelion from a daylily. Drift from a windy day, a sloppy spray pattern, or overspray onto lawn edges will leave brown streaks that take weeks to recover. Use a dedicated sprayer with a fan nozzle on a calm day, and you're fine — but treat it with respect.

The jug itself is functional, not fancy. The white plastic is thin, and the cap doesn't seal perfectly after the foil seal is removed. Store it upright, or you'll find a puddle of concentrate in your garage. Transferring to a labeled sprayer bottle is the smarter move anyway.

**Who should buy this**

If you maintain a fence line, gravel driveway, patio, or garden paths — and you're tired of paying premium prices for a chemical that's chemically identical — this is your jug. It's also a strong choice for property owners with invasive weeds (kudzu, Japanese knotweed, poison ivy) who need volume, not fancy additives.

Skip it if you're looking to selectively kill broadleaf weeds in your lawn. For that, you want a 2,4-D or dicamba-based product that leaves grass unharmed. This isn't that tool.

**FAQ**

*Q: How much does this make when mixed?*
A: At the standard 2.5-ounce rate, one gallon makes 51 gallons of spray. At the heavy 5-ounce rate for tough perennials, about 25 gallons. Both figures are on the label.

*Q: Is it safe around pets and kids?*
A: Once dried, glyphosate binds to soil and is essentially inert. Keep animals off the sprayed area until the solution dries (typically 1-2 hours). It's a contact herbicide, not a soil sterilant.

*Q: Can I use this in a spray bottle for small jobs?*
A: Yes, but mix it fresh and use it within a day or two. The concentrate is stable, but diluted solution loses potency over time. For tiny jobs, mix only what you need.

For everyone else: this is the value pick that actually delivers. The savings are real, the results are proven, and the only "catch" is that it does exactly what it says — kills everything green it touches.
