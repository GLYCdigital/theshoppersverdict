Let's be blunt: most trellis netting is flimsy green string that disintegrates by August. The AlpineReach 5x15 Heavy Duty Polyester netting is not that, and that's precisely why it holds a 4.6-star rating across 229 Amazon reviews.

**What You Actually Get**

The package contains one continuous sheet of 300-denier polyester netting measuring 5 feet tall by 15 feet long. The grid pattern is 6-inch squares, which is the sweet spot for supporting vining vegetables. As shown in the product image, the netting arrives rolled tightly but relaxes quickly once unrolled. There's no frame, stakes, or mounting hardware in the box — this is just the netting itself, so factor in zip ties or U-staples for your setup.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

Here's where AlpineReach separates itself from the $12 garden center nets. The 300D polyester weave is noticeably thicker and stiffer than the standard polyethylene mesh you'll find elsewhere. When I pulled on the grid, it held firm without stretching or deforming — that's a good sign for a season of heavy cucumber vines pulling against it.

The UV stabilization matters more than most shoppers realize. Cheap netting gets brittle and snaps after a few months of direct sun. Multiple reviewers mention hanging this in full Southern exposure and getting two full growing seasons out of it. One buyer noted, *"Used it for pole beans last year and it's still tight as a drum this spring."* That's the durability story in a nutshell.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 6-inch grid spacing gets repeated shout-outs in the reviews. It's large enough that you can reach through and harvest without wrestling the netting, but tight enough to support pea tendrils and cucumber vines. The 5-foot height is also practical — tall enough for most vining crops but short enough that you don't need a ladder to maintain the top edge.

**Where It Falls Short**

The white color is a double-edged sword. It reflects heat and looks clean on day one, but in humid climates it develops a greenish algae tint by midsummer. The green version of this netting hides that much better. Also, the absence of any tensioning hardware means you'll need to figure out your own mounting system. And if you're growing sweet peas or other delicate tendrils, the 6-inch squares are too wide — they'll need a finer mesh or additional string support.

**Who Should Buy This**

This netting is for gardeners growing vining vegetables — pole beans, cucumbers, squash, and indeterminate tomatoes. It's also solid for creating a quick privacy screen on a balcony or separating garden zones. If you're growing lightweight ornamentals like morning glories, you can make it work, but be ready to add supplemental support.

**FAQ**

*How do I install this netting?*
Attach it to a wooden or metal frame using zip ties, U-staples, or heavy-duty staples every 12-18 inches. Pull it taut before securing to prevent sagging. For a freestanding setup, mount it between two posts with crossbars.

*Will this survive winter outdoors?*
The polyester fabric itself will survive, but UV degradation accelerates in cold, sunny weather. Most buyers take it down in fall and store it indoors, which extends its life to multiple seasons.

*Is the 5x15 size accurate?*
Yes, the netting measures a full 5 feet by 15 feet. A few reviewers noted slight shrinkage after the first season, but that's normal for any woven material exposed to weather.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want trellis netting that survives a full growing season — or three — without turning into a brittle mess. The 300D polyester build and UV resistance justify the price over cheaper alternatives.

**Skip it if** you're growing fine-tendril plants that need a tighter grid, or if you want a complete trellis kit with mounting hardware included.
