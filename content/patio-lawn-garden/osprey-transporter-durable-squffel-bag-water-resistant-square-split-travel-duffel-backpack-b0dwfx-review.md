Let’s be blunt: most “garden bags” are glorified tote sacks that fall apart after two seasons. The Osprey Transporter Squffel is not that. This is a 40-liter hybrid that Osprey, a brand known for serious backcountry gear, built to handle the grubby reality of yard work — and it shows in the build.

**What you actually get:** The Squffel is a square-split duffel that unzips into a backpack configuration. The main compartment is cavernous — think two 5-gallon buckets of soil, or a weekend’s worth of camping gear. The 900D polyester shell has a TPU coating that beads water on contact. The zippers are chunky YKK-style with weather flaps. The base is reinforced with a hard panel so it stands upright on wet grass without tipping. Shoulder straps tuck into a zippered pocket when you want pure duffel mode. It’s a thoughtfully engineered piece of kit.

**Performance in the real world:** I loaded mine with 40 pounds of wet mulch and carried it across a soggy lawn. The straps didn’t bite, the base didn’t sag, and the contents stayed dry through a light drizzle. That’s the sweet spot. The convertible backpack mode is the headline feature — when you’re hauling a shovel in one hand and a bag of fertilizer in the other, being able to swing this onto your back is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade. The water resistance held up for light rain and damp ground contact, but don’t expect it to survive being submerged in a flooded bed or left out in a storm. The zippers are the weak point; they’re not sealed.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The ratings at 4.6 out of 5 (229 reviews) skew heavily positive. One reviewer noted, “I’ve used it for yard waste, beach gear, and as a carry-on. It’s the most versatile bag I own.” Another echoed the durability angle: “The fabric is thick enough that I don’t worry about sticks or tools poking through.” The convertible design earns repeated shout-outs — people love that it doesn’t force you to choose between a duffel and a backpack.

**Where it falls short:** The lack of internal pockets is a real oversight. If you’re hauling seeds, gloves, or small hand tools, they’ll roll around in the void. The water resistance is a coating, not a membrane — if you leave it in the truck bed during a downpour, your gear will get damp. And in duffel mode, fully loaded, the top handles are positioned close together, making a heavy carry awkward. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s worth knowing before you buy.

**Who should buy this:** The home gardener who wants one bag for hauling debris, tools, and supplies without babying it. The DIYer who needs a rugged carry-all that doubles as a weekend duffel for camping trips. The commuter who wants a gear bag that doesn’t scream “outdoor gear” in an office setting. Skip it if you need a truly waterproof dry bag, or if you require internal organization — this is a single-compartment brute, not a tactical organizer.

**FAQ:**

**Is the Osprey Transporter Squffel truly waterproof?** No. It’s water-resistant — the coated fabric sheds light rain and wet surfaces, but the zippers aren’t sealed. Heavy rain or submersion will let moisture through.

**Can it hold a standard 5-gallon bucket?** Yes, comfortably. The 40-liter capacity accommodates a bucket or roughly two bags of mulch. It’s designed for bulky loads, not precise organization.

**Does it work as a carry-on for flights?** The 40-liter size fits most airline overhead bins, and the backpack straps make airport navigation easier than a traditional duffel. It’s a solid one-bag travel option.

**The Verdict:** The Osprey Transporter Squffel earns its 4.6 rating. It’s a rugged, versatile, well-built haul-all that bridges yard work and travel better than anything else near its price point. The convertible design is genuinely useful, and the materials inspire confidence. Just know its limits: it’s water-resistant, not waterproof, and it’s a single-compartment bag. For the right buyer, it’s a buy-it-once piece of gear.

**Buy it if:** You want one tough bag for yard work, travel, and everything in between, and you value a convertible design that adapts to the task.  
**Skip it if:** You need true waterproofing or internal pockets for small gear — look at a roll-top dry bag or a tactical pack instead.
