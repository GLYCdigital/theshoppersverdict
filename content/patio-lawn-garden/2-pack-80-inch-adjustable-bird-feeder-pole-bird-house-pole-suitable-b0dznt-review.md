**The short version:** This 2-pack of 80-inch adjustable poles solves the most annoying bird feeding problem — squirrels and raccoons treating your feeders like a buffet. But it's not a set-and-forget product. You'll need to pay attention to how you tighten it.

**What's in the box:** Two complete pole assemblies. Each pole consists of three sections: a ground stake with a flat step plate (good for boot pressure when pushing into soil), a middle section, and the top section with a hanging hook. The total height runs from about 40 inches collapsed to 80 inches fully extended. There's also a twist-lock collar on each joint. No tools included — you'll want a rubber mallet for the stake and possibly pliers for the locking collars.

**Build quality and real-world use:** The steel is powder-coated matte black. It's not heavy-gauge pipe — think medium-weight fencing material — but it's adequate for standard tube feeders or a single platform feeder. The ground stake has a pointed tip that penetrates average lawn soil well. I'd recommend wetting the ground first if you have hard-packed dirt; the step plate helps but you'll still work for it.

The height adjustment is where this pole either shines or frustrates. The twist-lock mechanism works — when you actually tighten it enough. Hand-tightening alone is insufficient. Multiple Amazon reviewers noted the same thing: "The locking collar needs to be really cranked down or the pole slides after a day of wind." Take that seriously. Use pliers or channel locks on the collar, and it holds fine. Don't, and you'll be re-adjusting constantly.

The hanging hook at the top is simple and works with most feeders that have a wire hanger or chain. It's not a shepherd's crook — it's a single J-hook. So if you want to hang two feeders from one pole, you'll need an adapter.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The 2-pack value is the most common positive thread in the 215 ratings. One buyer summed it up: "Finally, a set that doesn't cost $40 per pole. I've got one by the deck and one near the vegetable garden." Others like the adjustability for different setups — shorter for ground-feeding birds like doves, taller for hanging finch socks out of cat reach. The 4.3-star average reflects genuine satisfaction with the core function: holding feeders steady off the ground.

**Where it falls short:** The finish is the biggest complaint. It's not a durable outdoor powder coat; it's more of a painted surface that chips when you scrape it against a rock or when the locking collar slides. You'll see bare metal within a season in high-use areas. The pole also has a slight wobble at maximum extension with a heavy feeder (think a 5-pound platform feeder in wind). It won't tip over — the stake holds — but it's not rock-solid like a welded steel post.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Backyard birders on a budget** who want two feeding stations without buying two expensive single poles
- **Squirrel-war veterans** who need a pole that can be greased or fitted with a baffle without much fuss
- **Renters** who don't want to pour concrete — this is removable and leaves minimal trace

**Skip it if:** You need a permanent, heavy-duty installation for multiple large feeders, or you live in an area with extreme wind that would test the twist-lock joints repeatedly.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Will this pole support a squirrel baffle?**
A: Yes, most slip-on baffles (like the 12-inch dome type) fit the 1-inch diameter post. Just account for the extra weight when tightening the collars.

**Q: Can I use this on a deck or patio instead of the ground?**
A: Not directly — it's designed as a ground stake only. You'd need a deck-mount flange adapter, which isn't included.

**Q: How long does the finish last outdoors?**
A: Expect visible wear within 6-12 months, especially where the adjustable collar slides. Touch-up paint or rust-resistant spray helps extend it.

**The Verdict:** This is a solid, functional product at a fair price for what you get — two poles that do the job. It's not premium, but it's not trying to be. The height adjustment is genuinely useful, and the ground stake holds firm. Just budget an extra five minutes to properly tighten the locking collars and you'll be satisfied.

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, adjustable pole setup and don't mind minor maintenance. **Skip it if** you demand heavy-duty, zero-fuss hardware that you'll never touch again.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Good value, functional, with honest limitations on the finish and locking mechanism.
