## The Gold Standard for Low-Acid Slicing? Almost.

Most yellow beefsteaks are pretty, but they taste like watery disappointment. The Burpee 'Sunny Boy' Hybrid flips that script. This is a tomato that actually delivers on the "sweet, mild" promise — and it does it with fruit big enough to cover a burger bun entirely. If you've been burned by pale, flavorless yellow tomatoes before, this one's worth a second look.

## What's in the Packet

You get 30 non-GMO hybrid seeds in a standard Burpee seed packet. No starter plants, no fertilizer — just seeds. The packet itself is the usual paper envelope with basic planting instructions on the back. Nothing fancy, but Burpee's germination rates tend to be reliable, and this batch is no exception based on grower feedback.

## Real-World Performance

Sunny Boy is a determinate-ish hybrid that behaves more like an indeterminate in practice — meaning it keeps producing until frost, but the main flush comes in a concentrated window. Gardeners report plants reaching 4–5 feet tall with heavy yields of 1–2 pound fruit. The tomatoes are a deep golden-yellow with a meaty, dense interior — few seeds, lots of flesh. That makes them ideal for slicing, not juicing.

Flavor-wise, the standout trait is the balance. It's not cloyingly sweet like some cherry varieties, but it has a noticeable honeyed note with almost none of the acidic bite that makes red beefsteaks tangy. One reviewer put it simply: *"Best tasting yellow tomato I've grown in 20 years."* Another noted, *"My wife hates raw tomatoes and she ate these like apples."* That tracks — the low-acid profile is the whole point here.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Heat tolerance:** Multiple reviewers in Texas and the Southeast reported fruit set continuing through 95°F+ stretches where red varieties stalled.
- **Texture:** Dense, meaty flesh that holds up on sandwiches without turning to mush.
- **Size consistency:** Most fruit hits the 1-pound mark; some growers reported 2-pound monsters with proper pruning.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, this is not a beginner-friendly plant. The vines are vigorous and heavy — if you don't stake or cage them within the first few weeks, you'll be wrestling broken branches and fruit rot by mid-season. Plan for a sturdy 6-foot cage or Florida weave trellis.

Second, germination is slow in cool soil. If you direct-sow before nighttime temps stay above 55°F, expect patchy emergence. Start seeds indoors 6-8 weeks before last frost, or wait for warm soil. Several reviewers complained about poor germination, but most had planted too early.

Third, the concentrated harvest window means you'll have more tomatoes than you know what to do with for 3-4 weeks, then a trickle. If you want steady supply all summer, stagger your planting or pair this with a longer-producing indeterminate variety.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Low-acid seekers:** If you avoid tomatoes due to acid reflux or just prefer milder flavor, this is a top-tier choice.
- **Slicer enthusiasts:** Sandwich, burger, and caprese lovers who want big, beautiful, flavorful slices.
- **Hot-climate gardeners:** If your summers regularly hit 90°F+, this hybrid's heat tolerance gives it a real edge over finicky heirlooms.

**Skip it if:** You're a container gardener with limited space (these need room to sprawl), or you want a continuous harvest rather than a big flush followed by a lull.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these seeds GMO?**  
A: No, they're non-GMO hybrids. That means they're crossbred for specific traits, but not genetically modified. You can save seeds, but the offspring won't grow true to the parent.

**Q: How many plants do I need for a family of four?**  
A: For fresh eating and occasional slicing, 3-4 plants is plenty. For canning or sauce, you'd want at least 6-8, but honestly, this variety is better for fresh use than preserving.

**Q: Do they really taste sweeter than red tomatoes?**  
A: Yes, but it's not a sugar bomb. The sweetness is subtle — the bigger difference is the lack of acidity. It's a mellow, rich flavor that lets the umami come through.
