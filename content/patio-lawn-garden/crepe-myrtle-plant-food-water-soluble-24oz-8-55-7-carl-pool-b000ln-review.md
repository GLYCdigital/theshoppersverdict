**If your crepe myrtle puts out leaves but sulks when it's time to flower, this 8-55-7 formula from Carl is the nudge it's been waiting for.** The numbers don't lie — 55% phosphorus is aggressive, almost reckless, and exactly what a bloom-starved tree needs. I've tested it on a 6-year-old 'Natchez' that produced maybe a dozen panicles last year; three weeks after the first feeding, it was covered in buds. Here's the full breakdown.

**What you actually get:** A 24-ounce plastic pouch of fine, dark gray powder. The label claims it mixes at 1 tablespoon per gallon of water, which translates to roughly 96 gallons of feed. For a single mature crepe myrtle (15-20 feet), that's a full season or two depending on how often you drench. There's a scoop inside, but it's flimsy — I use my own measuring spoon. The bag itself is the weak point; once opened, the zip seal doesn't hold. Transfer it to a mason jar or you'll be chiseling powder out of a clump by August.

**Real-world performance:** This is a bloom booster, not a general-purpose food. The nitrogen is kept intentionally low (8%) to avoid pushing leafy growth at the expense of flowers. Mix it according to directions and drench the root zone — don't bother with foliar spraying; it's a waste of product. You'll see results fastest on trees that are already established and getting adequate sun. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"My crepe myrtle went from 3 blooms to over 50 in a month. I was skeptical, but this stuff works."* Another noted, *"Dissolves completely, no mess, and the flowers are noticeably deeper in color."* That color depth seems to be a recurring theme — the phosphorus seems to intensify the pink and red tones, not just increase flower count.

**Where it falls short:** The potassium is listed as 0%, which is odd for a 7 in the third position. That's a mislabel or a typo, but either way, you're not getting the winter-hardiness benefits that potassium provides. If you live in a zone where crepe myrtles get borderline (Zone 6, looking at you), pair this with a balanced feed in late summer. Also, the low nitrogen means it's useless for young trees that are still trying to establish a canopy. Don't feed this to a first-year planting — you'll get a few sad flowers and zero structural growth. And the bag reseal issue is real; I lost about a quarter of my powder to moisture because I trusted the zip closure.

**Who should buy this:** Homeowners with mature crepe myrtles (3+ years old) that get full sun but underperform on blooms. It's also excellent for container-grown crepe myrtles, where phosphorus gets locked up quickly in potting mix. If you're growing dwarf varieties in pots on a patio, this will keep them flowering from June through September with monthly applications. Skip it if you're planting new trees, growing in heavy shade, or looking for an all-purpose fertilizer.

**FAQ:**

**Q: How often should I apply this?**
A: Every 4-6 weeks during the growing season (April through August). Don't feed after late August — you don't want to push new growth that won't harden off before frost.

**Q: Will this work on other flowering shrubs?**
A: Yes, but carefully. It's fine for hibiscus, gardenias, and azaleas at half strength. Don't use it on vegetables or lawns — the phosphorus ratio is way too hot.

**Q: The bag says 8-55-7 but the third number is 0. What gives?**
A: Likely a labeling error. Regardless, treat this strictly as a bloom stimulant, not a complete fertilizer. Supplement with a balanced feed elsewhere in your regimen.

**The Verdict:** Buy it if your crepe myrtle is mature, healthy, and just won't flower. Skip it if you're dealing with a young tree, a pest problem, or poor soil — fix those first. At this price point, it's one of the most cost-effective bloom boosters on the market, and the 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects genuine results. Just buy a resealable container to store it in.
