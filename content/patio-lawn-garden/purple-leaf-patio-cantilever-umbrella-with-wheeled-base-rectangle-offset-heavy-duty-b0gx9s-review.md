Most cantilever umbrellas are a compromise. You either get a cheap offset frame that wobbles in a light breeze, or you pay premium prices for a base that weighs more than your grill. The PURPLE LEAF Rectangular Cantilever Umbrella sits somewhere more interesting — it's the first offset umbrella in this price tier that actually solves the two biggest problems shoppers complain about: stability and repositioning.

Here's what I found after digging through the specs and 225 buyer reviews.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 10x13-foot rectangular canopy, the aluminum pole assembly, a cross-shaped base that takes four sand or water bags (not included), and the wheel kit. Assembly runs about 45 minutes to an hour — the pole sections bolt together, and the canopy attaches via a hub-and-rib system that's straightforward but requires two people for the final lift.

The frame is powder-coated aluminum, not steel. That's a deliberate tradeoff: lighter to move, but you must fill the base with 4 x 40 lb sandbags to hit the recommended 160 lbs of ballast. Do that, and the umbrella handles a 15 mph breeze without drama. Skip the ballast, and it'll tip. Buyers who skipped the sandbags and used water instead reported the exact same complaint: "It lifted in a gust and came down on my glass table."

## Real-World Performance

The offset design is the headline feature. As shown in the product image, the pole sits entirely outside the seating area, which means a full 10x13-foot rectangle of shade over your dining table — no center pole fighting with your chairs. The 360° rotation and six tilt positions work smoothly via a crank at the base. You can track sun from breakfast to late afternoon without dragging the unit.

That's where the wheeled base earns its keep. The four 2-inch casters lock individually, and rolling the whole assembly across a concrete patio is genuinely manageable for one person. This is the single most-praised feature in buyer reviews: "I'm 62 and can reposition this alone. That's why I bought it."

The 300D polyester canopy has a PU coating that sheds water and provides UV30+ protection. It's not a top-tier solution-dyed fabric, so expect some color shift over multiple seasons of direct sun. Darker colors hide this better than lighter ones.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.5-star average holds up across 225 ratings. Recurring themes:

- **Coverage**: "Shades my entire 8-person table with room to spare."
- **Ease of use**: "Crank is smooth, tilt is effortless, wheels actually work."
- **Build quality**: "No rust after a full season of rain and sun."

## Where It Falls Short

The wheels are the weakest point. They're hard plastic casters sized for patios and pavers. On grass, they sink. On uneven brick, they catch. If your setup is on turf, you'll want to install the base on a paver or deck board.

The crank handle is stiff at full extension — multiple reviewers noted needing two hands to crank the canopy open when it's fully tilted. It loosens up with use, but it's not a one-finger operation.

Assembly instructions are image-only. They're adequate, but a few buyers described the canopy-to-hub alignment as "fiddly." Budget an hour, not 30 minutes.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Patio dining hosts** who want full-table coverage without a center pole blocking chairs
- **Anyone with a concrete or paver patio** who's tired of dragging heavy umbrellas around
- **Shade-chasers** who want to follow the sun without moving furniture

Skip it if your setup is on grass, if you don't want to buy and haul 160 lbs of sand, or if you want a fabric that'll look new in year five.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the base come with sand or water bags?**  
No. The base is a hollow cross frame. You fill four separate bags with sand (recommended, ~160 lbs total) or water (heavier, but must be drained for winter storage).

**Q: Can I leave it out in the wind?**  
With proper ballast, it handles normal breezes. For storms or gusts over 20 mph, lower and secure the canopy. The umbrella comes with tie-down straps for the canopy when closed.

**Q: Is the 10x13-foot size accurate?**  
Yes, the rectangular canopy measures 10 feet by 13 feet at full extension. The offset pole means the entire footprint is usable shade, unlike center-pole umbrellas where the pole eats into the coverage.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you want serious offset shade coverage and value being able to reposition the whole setup by yourself. It's a well-built umbrella that nails the basics — stable, smooth operation, generous shade — at a price that undercuts most competitors with similar specs.
