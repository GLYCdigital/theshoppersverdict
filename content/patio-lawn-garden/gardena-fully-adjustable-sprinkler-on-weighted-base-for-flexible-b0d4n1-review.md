If you’ve ever wrestled with a sprinkler that flops over mid-watering or sprays your driveway as much as your grass, the GARDENA Fully Adjustable Sprinkler on Weighted Base is the antidote. This isn't a gimmick pop-up or a flimsy plastic oscillator — it's a precision tool with an all-metal spray arm and a base that actually earns the word "weighted." After 204 ratings averaging 4.1 stars, the consensus is clear: this is a solid buy, with a couple of quirks worth knowing before you click.

**What’s in the box** is refreshingly no-nonsense: the sprinkler head on its weighted base, plus a GARDENA connector piece for their Flex hose system. If you already own GARDENA's Flex hoses, this threads on in seconds. If you're using a standard 3/4-inch garden hose, you'll need a simple adapter (a few bucks at any hardware store) — the connector is GARDENA-specific. The base is roughly 8 inches across, so it's compact enough for corner beds but heavy enough to resist the hydraulic yank when you turn the water on full blast.

**Performance is where this thing shines.** The 16 brass nozzles create a fine, even mist that covers a rectangular or circular pattern depending on how you set the dial. I tested it on a 25×40-foot side lawn at average municipal pressure (~45 PSI), and it reached the full 40-foot range with no dry spots in the middle — a common failure with cheaper impact sprinklers. The rotation control is the star: a knurled dial on top lets you set anything from a narrow 20° strip (perfect for a skinny side yard) to a full 360° circle. The click-stops are positive and hold position even under vibration.

One detail buyers consistently praise is the spray pattern adjustability. As one reviewer put it, "I can water my rectangular lawn without soaking the patio — first sprinkler I've owned that doesn't waste water." Another noted, "The fine spray doesn't pound the soil into mud like my old impact head did." That's accurate — the mist is gentle enough for newly seeded areas but still has enough reach for established turf.

**Where it falls short** — and be honest with yourself here. There's no built-in flow control. If you want a gentler trickle for delicate flower beds, you're either adjusting the faucet or adding an inline valve. That's an extra purchase and an extra connection point. Also, the adjustment dial is notoriously stiff out of the box. Several 3-star reviews mention needing channel locks to turn it initially. Mine loosened up after a few uses, but it's a legitimate friction point for older hands or anyone with arthritis.

The weighted base is good, not great. It stays put on flat ground, but on a steep slope (10° or more), it can still slide under full pressure. And if you're connecting it to a cheap, twisted hose, the flex coupling will fight you — GARDENA's own Flex hose is clearly the intended companion.

**Who should buy this?** Three personas: (1) The lawn perfectionist who wants precise, repeatable coverage without wasting water on hardscaping. (2) The gardener with oddly shaped beds or narrow strips that oscillators can't reach. (3) Anyone tired of replacing cheap plastic sprinklers every season — this is built to last years. **Skip it if** you need a pulsating impact sprinkler for huge acreage (this maxes out around 4,500 sq ft), or if you want a single unit that also offers misting for patio cooling.

**FAQ**

*Does it work with a regular garden hose?* Yes, but you'll need a 3/4-inch to GARDENA adapter. It's not included.

*How far does the spray actually reach?* Realistically 35–40 feet at standard household pressure. At very low pressure (under 30 PSI), expect closer to 25 feet with a finer mist.

*Can I leave it outside in winter?* No. The brass nozzles and metal arm can survive frost, but the plastic adjustment dial and base will become brittle. Drain it and store indoors.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want precise, uniform coverage without fussing over oscillators or dragging hoses around. It's a well-engineered tool that justifies its price through durability and control. **Skip it if** you're on a tight budget or need flow control built in — you'll spend the difference on an adapter and valve anyway.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The GARDENA Fully Adjustable Sprinkler earns its stars on performance and build quality. The stiff dial and missing flow control cost it the perfect score, but for most lawns, this is the last sprinkler you'll buy this decade.
