You don't buy a fleece jacket for a patio. You buy it because you're headed outside — to the garden at 7 a.m., on a trail before the sun's over the ridge, or to the fire pit when the breeze picks up. The North Face Women's Glacier Fleece Jacket sits squarely in that sweet spot: warm enough to cut the chill, light enough to forget you're wearing it.

**What you actually get**

The Glacier is a 125-weight fleece with a full front zip, a stand collar that actually stays up, and two zippered hand pockets. It's not a technical shell — there's no hood, no waterproofing, no pit zips. What it is, is a clean, versatile midlayer or standalone piece for cool, dry conditions. The fabric has a smooth face that layers easily under a rain shell, and the cuffs are elasticized without being tight enough to leave marks.

**Real-world performance**

I've worn this through a spring of early-morning garden work and weekend trail hikes. At 48°F with a light base layer, it holds heat comfortably. The 125-weight construction is the Goldilocks of fleece — heavier than a fashion cardigan, noticeably lighter than a 200-weight expedition piece. The articulated sleeves matter more than you'd think: reaching for pruning shears or scrambling over a fallen log never pulls at the shoulders.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I live in this thing from September through May. It's not a winter coat, but for shoulder seasons it's perfect."* Another noted, *"I ordered my usual size and it fits — but just barely in the arms. If you have any muscle up top, size up."* That sizing observation came up repeatedly across the 225 ratings, so take it seriously.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The zippered pockets earn near-universal approval. They're lined, deep enough to swallow a phone, and positioned above the hip belt line — so they work on hikes without fighting a backpack's waist strap. The collar also deserves mention: it's tall enough to keep the wind off your neck without feeling like a turtleneck.

**Where it falls short**

Honestly? The lack of wind resistance is the biggest gap. On a breezy ridgeline at 45°F, you'll feel the air move through the knit. That's inherent to the fabric weight, not a defect — but it means the Glacier is a poor choice for exposed, windy conditions without a shell over it. There's also no interior pocket, which is a small but real annoyance if you carry a wallet or earbuds.

**Who should buy this**

- The **suburban gardener** who needs a warm layer for dawn watering and dusk weeding
- The **hiker** who runs warm and wants a breathable midlayer for 40–60°F trails
- The **commuter** who needs a clean-looking layer that works under a rain jacket
- The **gift-buyer** who wants a safe, high-quality option for an outdoorsy woman

Skip it if you need a winter coat, a windproof layer, or something with a hood.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this fleece warm enough for winter?**  
A: Alone, no — not below about 35°F unless you're active. As a midlayer under a shell, it works well into freezing temps.

**Q: How does the sizing run?**  
A: True to size for most, but snug in the shoulders and arms. If you're between sizes, go up. Multiple reviewers noted this.

**Q: Can I machine wash it?**  
A: Yes — cold, gentle cycle, and hang dry. It comes out looking new with minimal pilling.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, well-made fleece that handles spring mornings, cool summer nights, and fall hikes without fuss. **Skip it if** you need wind protection or a heavy winter layer.

At 4.6/5, the Glacier earns its rating. It's not flashy — it's just a damn good fleece that does exactly what it's supposed to do.
