## Performance and Build Quality

The frame is aluminum, not the hollow plastic you'll find on bargain-bin skimmers. It holds its shape under load. The mesh bag uses reinforced stitching at the seams — the exact spot where most nets fail after a season of dragging wet oak leaves. When you load this bag with wet, heavy debris, the frame flexes slightly but doesn't buckle.

The deep bag is the game-changer. On a standard skimmer, you're emptying every 3-4 scoops when dealing with fall leaf drop. With this one, you can make 8-10 passes before the bag fills. That's genuinely fewer trips to the yard waste bin and less time hunched over the pool edge.

The mesh does create some drag in the water. It's not terrible, but if you're skimming a large in-ground pool, your shoulders will feel it after 20 minutes. The tradeoff is that fine mesh catches more than just leaves — it grabs pine needles, bugs, and that mysterious sludge that sinks to the bottom.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my standard telescoping pole?**
A: Yes, if your pole has the standard 1-1/4" threaded end. Most poles from major brands (Pentair, Hayward, or generic) will connect fine. Just hand-tighten — don't force it.

**Q: Can I use this for algae or fine debris, or just leaves?**
A: The fine mesh catches small particles, but it's not a replacement for a dedicated algae net. For pollen and silt, you'll still want a vacuum or finer filter. This is primarily a leaf and debris tool.

**Q: How do I clean the bag?**
A: Hose it down after use, and let it dry fully before storing. The nylon mesh doesn't stain easily, but leaving wet leaves in it for days will cause mildew odors. A quick rinse takes 30 seconds and keeps it fresh.
