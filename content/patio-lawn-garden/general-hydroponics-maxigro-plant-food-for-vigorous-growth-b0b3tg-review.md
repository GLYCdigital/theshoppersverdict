If you're growing peppers in coco coir on a balcony or running a recirculating hydro system in the basement, you've probably noticed the price of liquid nutrients creeping up every year. General Hydroponics MaxiGro cuts through that noise with a dry powder that delivers the same results at a fraction of the cost. It's the blue-collar workhorse of the nutrient world — and for good reason.

**What's in the bag**

You get a resealable 2.2-pound pouch of fine blue powder. The NPK ratio sits at 10-5-15, which is deliberately dialed in for the vegetative growth phase. Unlike many one-part formulas that force you to buy separate calcium and magnesium supplements, MaxiGro includes both in the mix. One bag makes roughly 200 gallons of nutrient solution at standard strength. That's a significant savings compared to liquid concentrates that typically yield 20–50 gallons per bottle at a similar price point.

**How it performs in real grows**

The math is simple: dissolve 1 teaspoon per gallon of water for light feeding, 2 teaspoons for heavy feeders. The powder dissolves quickly in warm water, though you'll want to stir for a full minute or two. Some growers report a small amount of sediment if they mix it cold — this is normal and doesn't affect nutrient availability, but it's worth knowing if you're running drip irrigation where lines can clog.

The results speak for themselves. Gardeners using MaxiGro consistently report dense, dark green foliage and strong stem growth within the first week. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I switched from a popular liquid brand and saw my tomato seedlings double in size within 10 days. The cost savings alone made the switch worth it." Another grower praised its consistency: "Dissolves clean, plants respond fast, and I don't have to babysit pH as much as I did with other brands."

**Where it falls short**

MaxiGro is a vegetative-phase nutrient. It won't carry your plants through flowering or fruiting. You'll need to switch to MaxiBloom or another bloom formula after the light cycle flips or when buds start forming. That's not a flaw — it's a design choice — but budget buyers should factor in the second purchase.

The powder also demands a bit more care than liquids. It can cake if stored in humid conditions, and measuring by volume (teaspoons) is less precise than using a gram scale. For serious growers, a $15 digital scale removes any guesswork and makes dosing repeatable.

Finally, if you're running a large recirculating system, you'll want to mix nutrients in a separate reservoir and let the solution sit for 15–20 minutes before pumping. This lets any undissolved particles settle out and protects your pump.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for three types of growers:

- **Budget-conscious hydro growers** who want lab-grade results without premium liquid prices
- **Coco coir and soilless gardeners** growing leafy greens, herbs, or peppers who want a simple one-bag solution for veg
- **First-time hydroponic growers** who want a forgiving, well-documented nutrient with a massive community of support

It's less ideal if you want a single nutrient for the entire grow cycle, or if you prefer the convenience of pre-mixed liquids — you'll trade cost for convenience.

**FAQ**

*Can I use MaxiGro for flowering plants?*
You can, but it's not recommended. The nitrogen-heavy ratio encourages leaf growth over bud development. Use MaxiBloom for the flowering phase.

*Does MaxiGro work in soil?*
Yes. Mix the powder into your watering can at half the recommended hydro dose for soil applications. It's particularly effective for container vegetables.

*What's the shelf life?*
Stored in a cool, dry place with the bag sealed, the powder stays viable for years. It won't degrade or lose potency like some liquid nutrients.
