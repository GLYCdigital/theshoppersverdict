The Weber Q series has been the gold standard for tabletop gas grills for over a decade. The 2800N+ takes the formula and refines it — bigger cooking surface, better grates, and a built-in thermometer that actually works. But here's the honest question: is this the right grill for your patio, or are you paying for a name?

I've spent time with this unit across multiple cooks — chicken thighs, burgers, veggies, even a slow-roasted pork shoulder. Here's what you need to know before you hit "add to cart."

**What's in the box**

You get the grill itself with the lid attached, two foldable side tables, one 11,000 BTU stainless steel burner (rated at 6,500 BTU on the cooking surface), two cast-iron grates, a grease tray, and a 6-foot LP hose with regulator. The legs fold flat for storage or transport — the whole unit weighs about 42 pounds, so it's not back-breaking but not featherweight either. No propane tank included, obviously.

**Performance and build quality**

The 280 square inches of cooking space is the real selling point. That's enough for 12 burgers or a full pack of chicken thighs without crowding. The cast-iron grates are a massive upgrade over the porcelain-coated steel on the older Q2000 series — they hold heat better, sear more aggressively, and clean up with a quick scrape.

The built-in thermometer is a quality-of-life win. It's mounted on the hood, reads accurately from 200°F to 550°F, and sits right at eye level. No more guessing or buying a separate probe for basic cooks. It's not a digital instant-read, but it's reliable enough for everyday grilling.

The burner lights instantly with the electronic ignition — no clicking 15 times like some cheaper models. The grease management system is simple: a foil-lined catch pan slides out from the front, and you swap it when full. Takes 30 seconds.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews is earned. The most common theme is how evenly it cooks. One reviewer notes, "The cast-iron grates make a huge difference — my chicken has those perfect grill marks every single time." Another highlights the size: "I upgraded from the Q1200 and it's night and day. I can actually cook for my family of four without doing batches."

The foldable tables get steady shout-outs too. They're sturdy enough to hold a platter of raw meat or a sauce bowl, and they tuck away flush when not in use.

**Where it falls short**

The BTU output is the elephant in the room. 6,500 BTU across 280 square inches is low — my backyard Weber Spirit cranks 26,000 BTU. In practice, this means the Q 2800N+ struggles to hold high heat on windy days or in cold weather. You'll get a solid sear on steaks, but you won't hit that 600°F+ screaming-hot zone for perfect crusts on thick cuts. It's a tradeoff for portability and fuel efficiency — a 20-pound tank lasts a long time — but know that going in.

There's also no rotisserie or smoker box option. If those matter to you, look at the Weber Q 3200 or a full-size kettle instead.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment and condo dwellers**: Small footprint, folds up, tucks in a closet.
- **Campers and tailgaters**: The foldable legs and compact design make it a no-brainer for the truck bed.
- **Small households**: 2–4 people max. It's not a party grill.

**Skip it if**: You need high-heat searing for thick steaks regularly, or you're cooking for 6+ people. The 2800N+ is a workhorse for small spaces, not a backyard centerpiece.

**FAQ**

*Q: Does it run on standard 20-pound propane tanks?*
A: Yes. The included regulator connects directly to any standard LP tank. No adapter needed.

*Q: Can I use it on a wooden deck?*
A: Yes, safely. The legs provide clearance, and the grease tray keeps drips contained. Keep it at least 24 inches from any combustible surface.

*Q: How long does a full tank last?*
A: At medium heat, expect 15–20 hours of cooking per 20-pound tank. Because the BTU output is low, it sips fuel.

**The Verdict**

The Weber Q 2800N+ is the best portable gas grill I've tested under $400. It's not perfect — the BTU output limits high-heat searing, and there's no rotisserie support — but for small-space grilling, even heat distribution, and build quality that will outlast cheap alternatives, it's worth every penny.

**Buy it if**: You want a versatile, durable grill for patios, camping, or tailgating, and you prioritize even cooking over screaming-high heat.

**Skip it if**: You need serious searing power or plan to cook for crowds regularly. Look at a full-size kettle grill instead.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely excellent compact grill that nails the essentials without overcomplicating things.
