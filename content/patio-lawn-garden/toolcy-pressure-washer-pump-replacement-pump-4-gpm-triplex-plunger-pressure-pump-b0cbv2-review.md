## The Pump That Saves You $400

When your pressure washer pump dies—and they all eventually die—you're staring at a choice: buy a whole new unit for $500+, or replace just the pump for a fraction of that. The TOOLCY 4 GPM triplex pump sits firmly in the second camp, and with 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in garages across the country.

## What Actually Shows Up

The box contains the pump itself, pre-filled with a lightweight shipping oil, plus a small parts kit with O-rings and seals. You'll also get a basic manual that covers installation and break-in procedure. What you won't get: oil for the initial fill (the shipping oil is meant to be drained), mounting bolts, or any adapters for your existing hoses. The pump measures roughly 9.5 inches long and weighs about 15 pounds—solid, dense, and reassuringly heavy for what it is.

## Real-World Performance

This is a triplex plunger pump rated for 4 GPM at 3600 PSI, which puts it squarely in "serious homeowner / light commercial" territory. The brass head is the standout feature here—it handles heat better than aluminum and won't pit or corrode when you run detergent through it. The ceramic plungers and stainless steel valves are exactly what you'd expect from a pump at this price point, but the execution is clean and the tolerances feel tight.

I've seen multiple reports of buyers swapping out failed pumps on Generac, Simpson, and Craftsman machines with this one. The key spec to verify before ordering: the shaft. TOOLCY uses a 3/4-inch keyed shaft, which matches the vast majority of horizontal-shaft Honda and Predator engines. If your pump is bolted to a vertical-shaft engine, this won't work—check that first.

## What Owners Keep Saying

The praise in the reviews is remarkably consistent. One buyer noted, *"Dropped right in place of my dead pump. Same bolt pattern, same shaft size, and it's been running strong for months."* Another mentioned, *"I was skeptical of the price, but honestly this outperforms the OEM pump that came on my machine."* The common thread: people are surprised that an affordable replacement works as well as—or better than—the original.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the break-in procedure matters. You need to drain the shipping oil, fill with SAE 30 non-detergent oil, run it for 20 minutes, then change the oil again. Skip this and you risk premature seal wear. Some buyers missed this step and complained about short life—read the manual.

Second, the inlet and outlet threads differ (3/8-inch and 1/2-inch respectively). If your existing hoses use different fittings, you'll need adapters, and those aren't included. Check your setup before ordering to avoid a frustrating trip to the hardware store.

Third, there's no thermal relief valve or unloader built in. You'll need to use your existing unloader from the old pump, or the pump will dead-head and overheat if you run it with the trigger closed.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my Simpson or Generac pressure washer?**
A: Likely, if your engine is horizontal-shaft with a 3/4-inch keyed shaft. Measure your bolt pattern—it's a standard 4-hole pattern, but confirm against the dimensions listed before ordering.

**Q: Does it come pre-filled with oil?**
A: It ships with lightweight oil for protection, but you must drain it, fill with SAE 30 non-detergent oil, run for 20 minutes, then change again. This break-in is critical for longevity.

**Q: Can I run hot water through it?**
A: No. This is a cold-water pump. Max inlet temperature is around 140°F. Running hot water will destroy the seals quickly.
