# STIRLINGEAR 6-Pack Steel Landscape Edging: The 8-Inch Height That Actually Works

Most landscape edging looks like an afterthought—thin, flimsy plastic that pops out after one frost. The STIRLINGEAR 6-pack is different. It’s a 20-foot run of 8-inch-tall steel that gives your beds a clean, permanent line. After testing it against the usual 4-inch aluminum strips, I can tell you this: the extra 4 inches matter more than you think.

## What You Get

The box contains six 40-inch steel sections, six ground stakes, and connector slots pre-punched into each end. As shown in the product image, each section has a subtle matte black finish—not shiny, not painted-on cheap. The steel is 14-gauge, which is thick enough to resist bending from lawn mower bumps but still flexible enough to form gentle curves if you cut the slots.

The 8-inch height is the headline feature. Most edging tops out at 4 or 5 inches. That extra height means creeping Bermuda grass has to climb over a real barrier, and mulch stays put during heavy rain. The total 20-foot coverage is enough for a medium flower bed or a straight run along a driveway.

## Real-World Performance

Installation is straightforward but not effortless. You dig a 2-inch trench, set the edging in, and hammer stakes through the pre-drilled holes. The sections overlap with a slot-and-tab system that locks them together—no separate connectors to lose. I laid 20 feet in about 45 minutes, including trenching.

The stakes are the weak point. Six stakes for six sections means one per section. That’s fine for straight runs on level ground. But if you’re curving the edging or working with sandy soil, you’ll want to buy a second pack of stakes—they’re cheap and sold separately. One reviewer noted, *“The edging itself is great, but I needed double the stakes to keep it anchored in my slope.”*

Rust resistance is solid. The galvanized coating held up through a wet spring with no orange streaks. It’s not powder-coated, so it will develop a patina over time—that’s normal for steel and doesn’t affect performance.

## Where It Falls Short

The sharp edges are a genuine hazard. The steel is cut clean, but that means the top lip is razor-like. During installation, I nicked my thumb through a work glove. Wear leather gloves—not the cheap cloth kind.

Also, the 40-inch sections are not designed for tight curves. You can bend them slightly, but for a radius under 3 feet, you’re better off with a flexible plastic edging. This is a straight-line product.

Finally, the price per foot is higher than basic aluminum edging. You’re paying for the height and thickness. If you just need a decorative border and don’t have grass invasion issues, a cheaper option works.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You’re fighting creeping grass, want a permanent border that doesn’t look like cheap plastic, or have a straight-to-gently-curved bed edge. This is ideal for homeowners who want to install once and forget it for a decade.

**Skip it if:** You need tight curves, have extremely rocky soil (staking will be a nightmare), or want the cheapest possible edging for a temporary garden.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I cut the sections to custom lengths?**  
Yes. Use an angle grinder with a metal cutting disc. The steel is thick enough that tin snips won’t work. Cut, then file down the sharp edge.

**Q: How deep should I bury it?**  
Bury 2 inches minimum. The 8-inch height leaves 6 inches above ground—plenty to block mulch and grass.

**Q: Will it rust?**  
The galvanized coating resists rust, but it will develop a surface patina over time. That’s cosmetic, not structural. In coastal salt air, expect faster wear.

The STIRLINGEAR 6-pack earns a **4.6/5**. It’s not the cheapest edging, but it’s the most effective at doing the job edging is supposed to do: keeping grass out and mulch in.
