The Thule Chariot Lite isn't cheap, and it isn't small, and it isn't something you buy on a whim. But if you're an active parent who wants to keep running, cycling, or cross-country skiing with your kid in tow, this is the trailer that does all three without demanding a separate piece of equipment for each sport. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the consensus is clear: it's expensive, it's worth it, and it has a few quirks you should know about before you click buy.

## What you actually get

The box contains the trailer frame with the stroller wheel already mounted, a second 20-inch wheel for jogging mode, a front wheel fork for cycling conversion (the bike hitch arm is included), the canopy with a peek-a-boo window, and the Click 'n' Store folding system. You don't get the bike hitch itself — that's a separate purchase — which annoys some buyers. The ski kit is also sold separately. So factor an extra $60–120 into your budget if you plan to use it for cycling or winter sports.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The frame is aluminum, and it shows. At 21.8 pounds, it's light enough to lift into a trunk without grunting, but stiff enough that it doesn't flex when you're pushing a 40-pound kid at a jogging pace. The 27-inch rear wheel makes the stroller mode feel almost like a proper jogger — you're not fighting a wobbly front caster like you do on cheaper trailer/stroller hybrids.

One thing that stands out in customer reviews is the canopy. One buyer wrote: "We got caught in a surprise thunderstorm on a bike path. My daughter stayed completely dry. I was soaked. Worth every penny for that alone." The 75-denier polyester with PU coating is genuinely waterproof, not just water-resistant, and the UV protection is rated at UPF 50+.

The suspension is where the Lite model shows its budget positioning. On smooth pavement and groomed trails, the ride is fine. On gravel or rooty singletrack, the rigid frame sends a constant vibration through the cabin. If you're mostly a road cyclist or sidewalk jogger, you won't notice. If you plan to hit rough trails, spend the extra for the Chariot Cross.

## What buyers consistently praise

The folding mechanism gets near-universal praise. The Click 'n' Store system collapses the trailer flat in one motion, and it stands upright when folded, which means it takes up about as much floor space as a large suitcase. One reviewer noted: "I fold it in the trunk while my toddler waits in his car seat. Takes 20 seconds, no pinched fingers."

The harness system is also repeatedly mentioned. The 5-point harness has padded shoulder straps and a crotch pad, and the adjustment is tool-free. Kids can't wriggle out of it, which is the thing parents care most about.

## Where it falls short

The parking brake is genuinely stiff when new. Multiple reviewers mention needing two hands to engage it, which is awkward when you're holding a child. It loosens up after a few weeks, but it's a minor annoyance during the break-in period.

The storage pocket on the back is small — it fits a phone and keys, but not a diaper bag. Plan to use the included parent console or bring your own bag.

And the 20-inch rear wheels, while great for jogging, make the folded package longer than some competitors. If you drive a compact car, measure your trunk before buying. A few reviewers report the folded trailer barely fitting in their sedan.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a runner or cyclist who wants one piece of gear for multiple sports, you value genuine waterproofing, and you're willing to pay for a proven brand with excellent resale value.

**Skip it if:** You only need a stroller for neighborhood walks — the $200 Graco or Evenflo will do that fine. Also skip if you plan to tackle technical mtb trails; the lack of suspension will disappoint you.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this for newborns?**
A: Yes, with the infant sling insert (sold separately). The seat reclines flat enough for a sleeping infant, but you need the sling for proper head support.

**Q: How fast can I jog with it?**
A: The manual recommends a max speed of 10 mph for jogging and 15 mph for cycling. Realistically, most users jog at 8–9 mph comfortably. The fixed front wheel tracks straight without wobble.

**Q: Does it fit through standard doorways?**
A: The width is 30 inches, so no — standard interior doors are 28–32 inches, and this won't clear most of them. You'll be folding it or going through double doors.

## The Verdict

The Thule Chariot Lite earns its 4.6/5 score through sheer versatility and build quality. It's not the cheapest trailer, and it's not the most comfortable on rough terrain, but it's the trailer that does everything well enough that you'll actually use it. The waterproofing alone justifies the price for anyone who rides in real weather.

**Buy it if** you want one trailer for running, cycling, and strolling, and you value gear that lasts. **Skip it if** you're a casual user who needs a basic stroller, or you demand suspension for rough trails.
