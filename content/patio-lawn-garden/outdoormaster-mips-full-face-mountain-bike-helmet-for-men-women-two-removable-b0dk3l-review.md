Let me be direct: a full-face helmet with MIPS for under $100 usually means corners cut somewhere. The OutdoorMaster MIPS Full Face Helmet doesn't cut the corners that matter — but it does make a few compromises you need to know about before clicking buy.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the helmet, a spare set of thicker cheek pads, a helmet bag, and a visor. That spare pad set is a genuinely thoughtful inclusion — most budget full-face helmets forget it, and it lets you fine-tune the fit as the pads pack down after a few weeks of riding. The helmet itself weighs in around 850 grams (about 1.9 pounds). That's not a featherweight by any stretch, but it's within the normal range for a downhill-oriented shell with a chin bar.

**Safety Credentials That Actually Matter**

Here's the thing that separates this from the dozens of generic full-face helmets flooding Amazon: it carries ASTM F1952 certification — the downhill standard — not just the basic CPSC bike rating. That means it's been tested for the kind of high-impact crashes that happen at speed on rough terrain. Combined with the MIPS liner (that yellow slip-plane system that reduces rotational forces on impact), this is legit protection, not costume gear.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The shell is a polycarbonate outer over an EPS foam inner — standard construction, but the finish quality is better than the price suggests. The matte surface doesn't look cheap, and the adjustable visor stays put even at speed. The chin bar sits close enough to the face to feel protective without pressing against your jaw, and the closure uses a reliable double-D ring — the same system found on helmets costing three times as much.

The removable liner is the quiet hero here. After a hot summer ride, you can pull the whole thing apart and wash it. That's a feature I'd expect on a $200+ helmet, and it's here at roughly half that.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

Scrolling through the 210 ratings, a clear pattern emerges. One rider put it simply: "I was skeptical about the price, but the MIPS and the fit sold me. I've taken this on black diamond trails and it's held up perfectly." Another noted, "The spare cheek pads made the difference — I have a narrow face and the thicker ones locked everything in place."

Comfort during actual riding gets consistent shoutouts. Multiple reviewers mention forgetting they're wearing a full-face after a few minutes, which is the highest compliment you can give a helmet in this category.

**Where It Falls Short**

The sizing is the biggest complaint, and it's legitimate. This helmet runs small. If you're between sizes, go up — and if you have a rounder head shape, expect a snug fit even after break-in. Several buyers report having to return for a size up, so measure your head circumference carefully before ordering.

Ventilation is adequate but not impressive. The front vents pull air through reasonably well at speed, but if you're climbing with this thing strapped on, you'll notice the heat. It's a downhill helmet, not an enduro all-rounder — buy it with that expectation.

The chin strap padding is thin. It's functional, but on longer rides you might notice it digging in slightly. A simple aftermarket chin strap cover solves this cheaply if it bothers you.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the rider hitting bike parks, doing downhill runs, or progressing into jump lines who wants MIPS-level protection without spending $300+. It's also a smart buy for parents — the spare pads mean a growing teenager can get more life out of one helmet. If you're a cross-country rider doing long climbs, or if you ride mainly flat urban trails, the ventilation tradeoff makes this the wrong tool for your job.

**FAQ**

*Is this helmet safe for e-bike riding?*
Yes. The ASTM F1952 certification covers higher-speed impacts, and the full-face design protects your jaw and teeth — an often-overlooked risk area when riding at e-bike speeds.

*Will it fit a GoPro or action camera?*
Yes, the visor has enough space to mount a camera on top, and the chin bar can take a chin mount. Just be aware the added weight will shift the balance slightly.

*How do I clean the liner?*
The liner and cheek pads pull out easily — hand wash with mild soap and cold water, then air dry completely before reinstalling. Don't machine wash.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want certified downhill protection with MIPS at a price that makes sense, and you're willing to size up for the fit.

**Skip it if:** You're a climber needing maximum airflow, or you're between sizes and can't dial in the fit with the included pads.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — This helmet overdelivers on safety tech, underdelivers on ventilation, and makes you work for the right fit. For the price, that's a trade I'd make again.
