Look, I've tested enough leaf blowers to be skeptical of cordless claims that promise gas-level performance. But the EGO Power+ 56V Variable-Speed Blower is the rare exception that backs up the marketing. With 580 CFM of air volume at peak output, this thing moves wet autumn oak leaves like they're dry confetti. The catch? That kind of power demands a serious battery investment, and EGO makes you buy it separately.

**What's Actually in the Box**

This is the bare tool version. You get the blower body, a variable-speed trigger, cruise control lock, and a 5.5-inch flat nozzle. No battery. No charger. That's a $179+ commitment on top of the tool price if you're starting fresh. If you already own EGO's 56V ecosystem — like their mower or trimmer — this slots right in. If not, factor in the 2.5Ah battery and charger bundle, which typically runs $100–150 more.

**Real-World Performance**

The variable-speed trigger is the standout feature here. It's not a simple on/off switch — you get genuine proportional control from a gentle patio sweep to hurricane-force clearing. The cruise control lock is a lifesaver for long driveway runs; thumb it on and you don't have to hold the trigger at full squeeze. Owner feedback consistently highlights this control as the differentiator. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I can clear a half-acre lawn in 20 minutes that took 45 with my old gas blower, and I'm not deaf afterward."

Battery life is the honest tradeoff. At full speed, expect 10–15 minutes from a 2.5Ah pack. That's not a flaw — it's physics. Dropping to medium speed roughly doubles runtime. For typical suburban lots, one charged battery gets the job done. For acreage, you'll want the 5.0Ah or 7.5Ah packs, which EGO sells separately.

**Build Quality and Ergonomics**

The housing is impact-resistant polymer that feels solid, not cheap. The IPX4 weather resistance means light rain won't kill it — several owners report using it on damp grass without issues. The noise level is dramatically lower than gas; you can hold a conversation at normal volume while it runs. That's a genuine quality-of-life upgrade if you've ever annoyed neighbors at 7 AM.

Here's the honest downside: at 5.7 pounds without the battery, this gets heavy. Add a 2.5Ah battery (about 1.5 lbs) and you're holding 7+ pounds of equipment. The lack of a shoulder strap is a head-scratcher. My left arm was noticeably fatigued after 25 minutes of continuous use. If you have a large property, budget for a harness or take breaks.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy it if you're already in the EGO ecosystem, or if you're replacing a gas blower and want zero-maintenance convenience with real power. It's perfect for homeowners with 0.25–1 acre lots, patios, decks, and garage cleanup. The instant start and stop makes it superior for quick jobs — grab it, blow off the porch, hang it back up in 90 seconds.

Skip it if you have 3+ acres of heavy leaf cover, need all-day runtime for commercial work, or are on a tight budget. In those cases, a gas backpack blower or a corded electric with a long extension cord makes more financial sense.

**FAQ**

*Does it actually replace a gas blower?* For most residential use, yes. The 580 CFM matches mid-range gas handheld units. You lose the endless runtime, but you gain instant start, no fuel smell, and lighter weight than most gas equivalents.

*Which battery should I get?* Minimum 2.5Ah for light duty. For regular use, the 5.0Ah is the sweet spot — about 25–30 minutes at full power. The 7.5Ah exists but adds noticeable weight.

*Is the variable speed worth it over cheaper fixed-speed models?* Absolutely. The trigger control prevents scattering debris across the yard and lets you adjust on the fly from wet leaves to delicate flower beds.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want gas-level performance without gas-level maintenance, you're in the EGO ecosystem, or you value quiet, instant-start operation.

**Skip it if:** You need all-day runtime, hate buying batteries separately, or have a massive property requiring backpack-level air volume.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's a genuinely excellent blower with a serious battery cost catch and ergonomic oversight. The performance justifies the investment for the right buyer.
