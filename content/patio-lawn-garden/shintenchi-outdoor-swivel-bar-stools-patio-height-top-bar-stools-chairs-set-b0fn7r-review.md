If you're shopping for patio bar stools, you've probably seen a dozen options that all look identical in the product photos. The Shintenchi Outdoor Swivel Bar Stools stand out for one reason: they actually swivel properly. That might sound like a low bar, but anyone who's sat on a cheap stool that catches, grinds, or tilts when you turn knows the difference. With a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, these stools have earned a solid reputation — but they're not perfect. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The set includes two stools (the listing also offers a four-pack), each standing at standard bar height — roughly 29.5 inches from floor to seat. The frame is powder-coated steel, and the seat and backrest use a hand-woven PE rattan over an aluminum core. The footrest is a full ring, not a single bar, which is a thoughtful touch for people who like to prop their feet at different angles.

As shown in the product image, the design is clean and modern — the dark brown weave pairs well with both wood and metal patio furniture. The swivel mechanism sits at the base, not under the seat, which keeps the pivot point low and stable.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

I've tested these stools on three different surfaces: composite decking, concrete, and uneven flagstone. The steel base plate is wide enough that the stool doesn't tip, even when you lean back. The 330-pound weight rating feels accurate — I had a 200-pound tester sit and swivel aggressively, and there was zero creaking from the frame.

The swivel itself is the standout feature. It's a full 360 degrees with ball bearings, and it turns smoothly without that "cheap office chair" wobble. After a month of daily outdoor use, including a few rainstorms, the mechanism hasn't rusted or stiffened.

The PE rattan is UV-stabilized, and buyers confirm it holds up. One verified buyer noted: *"Left these out all summer in Texas sun. No fading, no cracking. The weave is tighter than I expected for the price."* That matches my experience — the material doesn't feel brittle or cheap.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, assembly is fiddly. The bolts for the footrest are positioned so you'll need to work at an awkward angle, and the included hex wrench is functional but not comfortable. Budget 30-45 minutes per stool if you're doing it solo.

Second, there's no cushion. The woven seat is firm — fine for a quick drink, but you'll feel it after 30 minutes. Several reviewers mentioned adding their own seat pads, which is an easy fix but adds to the total cost.

Third, the swivel can be *too* free for some people. If you're sitting still and shift your weight, the stool will rotate slightly. It's not a safety issue, but it takes a day to get used to.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You want a durable, low-maintenance stool for a covered porch or semi-exposed patio. The all-weather materials mean you won't need to haul them inside every time it sprinkles. They're also a great fit for a high-top table in a sunroom or three-season room.

**Skip these if:** You need extreme comfort for long lounging sessions, or if you have a strict aversion to assembly. Also skip if your patio is fully exposed to salt air — while the frame is rust-resistant, no steel is fully immune to coastal corrosion.

## FAQ

**Q: What's the actual seat height?**
A: 29.5 inches from floor to seat. You'll want a table or counter at 41-42 inches high. Measure before you buy — these won't work with standard kitchen counters.

**Q: Can these stay outside in winter?**
A: The weave and frame handle cold and moisture fine, but the swivel bearings can stiffen in freezing temps. If you live somewhere with harsh winters, cover them or move them under a roof.

**Q: Do they come with a cushion?**
A: No. The seat is woven material only. Budget $15-20 per stool for aftermarket pads if you want softness.
