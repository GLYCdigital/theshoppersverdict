**What you actually get**

The POOL BLASTER arrives as a single-piece handheld vacuum with a rechargeable lithium-ion battery, a fine-mesh filter cartridge, and a charging adapter. There's no hose, no pump connection, no skimmer basket to align — you charge it, press the trigger, and push it along the tub floor. The product image shows the compact, wand-style body with a wide suction head designed for flat surfaces. For a hot tub owner tired of wrestling with siphon hoses and losing prime, that simplicity is the entire appeal.

**Real-world performance**

This thing sucks — in the good way. The motor draws water through the intake head and traps debris in the internal filter basket. On a 300-gallon hot tub, I cleared a film of dead leaves, hair, and sunscreen residue in about 10 minutes. The suction is strong enough to pull sand from between acrylic ridges, though you'll need to move slowly over textured surfaces. The cordless design means no tripping hazards around the tub, and the 4-pound weight is balanced well enough for one-handed use along vertical walls.

Battery performance is the honest tradeoff. You get roughly 30–45 minutes of continuous runtime, which is plenty for a hot tub or small above-ground pool (up to 8–10 feet diameter). If you're eyeing this for a 15-foot or larger above-ground pool, expect to recharge mid-job. A full charge takes about 4 hours, so plan accordingly — this isn't a grab-and-go for heavy pool maintenance.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The convenience factor dominates the feedback. One verified buyer noted, *"I was tired of hooking up the garden hose and losing suction every time. This just works — charge it and go."* Another highlighted the filtration: *"The fine filter actually catches the cloudy stuff, not just the big chunks."* Owners repeatedly mention how quickly it handles routine touch-ups — a quick pass before guests arrive, or a weekly sweep of the tub floor without draining.

**Where it falls short**

The filter maintenance is the biggest gripe. After vacuuming fine silt or heavy algae, the pleated cartridge clogs fast, and you'll need to rinse it thoroughly — sometimes mid-session. Disassembly involves twisting off the collection chamber, which can be fiddly with wet hands. Also, the suction head is about 6 inches wide, so covering a large pool floor takes patience. And if you drop it in the deep end, it sinks — there's no floatation built in, so keep a firm grip.

**Who should buy this**

- **Hot tub owners** who want a 5-minute weekly cleanup without dragging out hoses — this is your tool.
- **Small above-ground pool owners** (up to 10 feet) who need spot-cleaning between full filter cycles.
- **Seniors or anyone with limited mobility** who can't bend over with a manual vacuum head attached to a pole.

**Skip it if** you have a large in-ground pool (this won't cover the floor efficiently), or if you expect hands-off robotic cleaning — this is manual labor, just cordless.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use it while people are in the hot tub?**  
Yes — it's fully submersible and safe. The motor seals are rated for underwater operation. Just don't run it dry.

**Q: How long does the battery last per charge?**  
Realistically 30–45 minutes of continuous use. Charging takes about 4 hours from empty.

**Q: Does it pick up sand or only leaves?**  
It handles both, but fine sand will clog the filter faster. Rinse the cartridge immediately after sand pickup to maintain suction.

**The Verdict**

The POOL BLASTER earns a 4.2/5 for doing exactly what it promises: cordless, no-setup suction for hot tubs and small pools. It's not a pool-maintenance replacement, but it's a genuinely useful spot-cleaner that eliminates the hose hassle. The filter cleaning and battery limits are real, but for the price, it beats the alternative of draining and scrubbing.

**Buy it if** you want a quick, low-effort way to keep your hot tub floor clean between deep cleans.  
**Skip it if** you need a whole-pool solution for anything larger than a 10-foot above-ground model.
