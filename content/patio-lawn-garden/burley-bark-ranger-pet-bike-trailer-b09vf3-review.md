**Does your dog deserve a first-class seat on your next ride?** The Burley Bark Ranger is the trailer you’ll see at every trailhead — and for good reason. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it’s not just popular; it’s proven. But before you drop serious cash, let’s talk about what it’s actually like to tow 75 pounds of happy dog behind your bike.

## What’s actually in the box

Burley sends you the trailer frame, two 20-inch wheels, the tow hitch arm, and a padded floor insert. You also get two internal leash tethers and a shoulder strap for carrying it folded. Missing from the box: tools. You’ll need a Phillips screwdriver and a 5mm Allen wrench — neither is included. The manual is clear, but plan on 45 minutes of assembly. The trickiest part is seating the wheel axles; they need firm, even pressure to click in. 

## How it performs on real roads

The Bark Ranger’s suspension is the headline feature. It’s a torsion-style system that absorbs bumps far better than the rigid trailers I’ve tested. On cracked asphalt and packed gravel, my 55-pound lab mix barely shifted. The tow hitch attaches to your bike’s rear axle in about 90 seconds, and the safety strap loops around your seat post as a backup. 

The interior is genuinely roomy: 29 inches long, 24 inches wide, and 24 inches tall. That fits a 70-pound dog comfortably, though a 90-pounder will be cramped. The floor pad is 1 inch thick and wipeable — a lifesaver after muddy paws. Two mesh windows roll up fully, and the rear flap opens for a second entrance. 

## What owners consistently praise

Across the reviews, three things keep coming up. First, the stability. One owner wrote, “I’ve taken this on 20-mile rides and my dog falls asleep in the back — it tracks straight even at 15 mph.” Second, the fold-flat mechanism: “I store it in my apartment closet. It folds smaller than a suitcase.” Third, the build quality — the aluminum frame feels bomber, and the 600-denier polyester fabric has survived two seasons of daily use without a tear.

## Where it honestly falls short

The lack of a parking brake is the biggest annoyance. Unhitch the trailer at a café and it will roll on any slope — I’ve chased mine twice. You’ll need to carry a wheel chock or always lean it against something. 

Assembly is another hurdle. Several buyers noted the axle alignment issue, and it’s real. One reviewer said, “I needed my spouse to push down on the wheel while I guided the pin — doable, but frustrating.” Also, the 75-pound weight limit is firm. If your dog is close to that, you’ll feel every pound on hills. The trailer itself weighs 22 pounds, so add that to your bike’s load.

## Who should buy this

- **City cyclists** who want a safe way to take a medium dog to the park — the hitch is rock-solid and the reflective trim is visible at dusk.
- **Weekend trail riders** with dogs under 70 pounds who value comfort — the suspension genuinely smooths out gravel paths.
- **Apartment dwellers** — the fold-flat footprint is roughly the size of a large suitcase.

**Skip it if** your dog is over 75 pounds, you ride on steep mountain singletrack (this is for bike paths and fire roads, not technical trails), or you lack patience for a two-person assembly.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use it as a jogging stroller?**  
A: No. It’s bike-only. Burley sells a separate jogging kit, but it doesn’t fit this model.

**Q: Is it safe for a puppy?**  
A: Yes, but wait until they’re fully grown. The tether system clips to a harness, not a collar, which is correct — but a puppy under 6 months may find the motion stressful.

**Q: Does it fit an e-bike?**  
A: Yes, as long as your rear axle is 12mm or smaller. If you have a thru-axle e-bike, you’ll need Burley’s separate thru-axle adapter.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a durable, comfortable trailer for a dog under 70 pounds and you’re willing to spend 45 minutes on assembly. The suspension, fold-flat design, and safety features justify the price.

**Skip it if** you need a parking brake, have a giant breed, or expect tool-free setup. 

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a near-perfect trailer held back only by the missing brake and finicky axle install. If Burley fixed those two things, this would be a 5.0. As it stands, it’s still the best dog trailer I’ve tested for the money.
