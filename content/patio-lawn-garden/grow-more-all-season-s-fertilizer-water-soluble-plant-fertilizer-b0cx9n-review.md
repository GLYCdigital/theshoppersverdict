## What's actually in the bag

Grow More sends you a heavy-duty foil pouch — the 2.5-pound size fits neatly on a shelf, unlike the flimsy paper bags Miracle-Gro uses. The product image shows a bright yellow and green bag with the NPK ratio front and center, which is accurate. Inside, you get fine blue-green crystals that dissolve almost instantly in lukewarm water.

The formula is straightforward: **20-20-20** — equal parts nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. That's the "all season" sweet spot. Unlike high-nitrogen lawn foods or bloom-boosting high-phosphorus mixes, this balanced ratio works for virtually everything you're growing. The bag also lists chelated micronutrients — iron, zinc, manganese, and copper — which most budget fertilizers skip. That matters if you're growing in containers or alkaline soil where nutrients lock up.

## Real-world performance

I've run this through three different use cases over the past few months:

**Container vegetables:** Tomatoes and peppers responded within 5 days — new growth was noticeably darker green than control plants fed a competing brand. Mixing at the recommended 1 tablespoon per gallon, I saw zero leaf burn even on heat-stressed plants in mid-July.

**Lawn spot treatment:** Diluted at half strength (1/2 tablespoon per gallon), it kicked stubborn patches of clover and crabgrass back without scorching the surrounding grass. The nitrogen release is fast, so you'll see results quickly, but don't expect slow-release longevity — you're feeding every 2-3 weeks, not once a month.

**Houseplants:** This is where it shines. The chelated micronutrients prevent the yellowing between veins that plagues indoor plants in winter. My monstera and pothos have held steady color through a full cycle.

## Where it falls short

**No scoop.** The bag is bare inside. You're expected to read the small chart on the back and measure with your own spoon. A tablespoon is the standard measure, but if you're using a different brand's scoop, don't assume it matches.

**The smell.** When first mixed, it has a sharp ammonia note that takes a couple minutes to dissipate. Outdoors, no problem. In a kitchen or enclosed porch, you'll notice it.

**Fast-acting means fast-fading.** This is a quick-release fertilizer. If you're looking for set-and-forget feeding, look at slow-release pellets instead. This requires consistency.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should I apply this?**
A: For outdoor containers and gardens, every 2-3 weeks during active growth. For houseplants, once a month at half strength is plenty. Always water the soil, not the foliage, to avoid burn.

**Q: Can I use this on seedlings?**
A: Yes, but dilute it to quarter strength (1/4 tablespoon per gallon) until the second set of true leaves appears. The full-strength dose is too hot for young roots.

**Q: Does it work in a hose-end sprayer?**
A: Yes, it dissolves fully with no residue. Use the highest dilution setting and run the sprayer for 5 seconds to clear the line before stopping.
