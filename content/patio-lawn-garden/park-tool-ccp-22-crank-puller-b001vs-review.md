## What You Actually Get

The CCP-22 is a compact, hand-held crank puller that weighs about 8 ounces and measures roughly 7 inches long. In the box you get the puller body with a reversible threaded tip, a built-in T-handle, and a small plastic storage case. The tip flips between two thread patterns: the standard 22mm x 1.0 for Shimano and most square taper cranks, and the 20mm x 1.0 for ISIS Drive and Octalink splined systems. Park Tool includes a knurled collar that locks the tip in place, so you won't accidentally switch threads mid-pull.

## Real-World Performance

The heart of this tool is the hardened steel threads. I've pulled cranks that had been sitting outside for three winters, and the CCP-22 didn't so much as hesitate. The T-handle gives you genuine mechanical advantage—you can lean into it without worrying about bending the tool. One reviewer put it simply: "Worked perfectly on a stuck crank that had been on for 20 years. No drama, just solid extraction."

The reversible tip is the standout feature. Instead of owning two different pullers, you flip the tip, lock it with the collar, and you're ready for a different crank standard. That's a $15 value baked into a single tool. The threads are cut cleanly and engage smoothly—no cross-threading anxiety when you're starting the pull.

## Where It Falls Short

The CCP-22 requires a 5mm hex wrench to drive the center bolt, and Park Tool doesn't include one. If you don't have a hex set, factor in an extra trip to the hardware store. It's a minor oversight, but annoying at 9 PM when your bike is disassembled on the floor.

Second, this tool won't work on cranks with self-extracting bolts—the kind that have an integrated hex socket in the bolt head. You'll need to remove those bolts and use the CCP-22 on the bare crank arm. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a step that catches people off guard.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home mechanics** working on one or two bikes: This is the only crank puller you'll need for the next decade.
- **Vintage bike restorers**: The hardened steel threads handle corroded, seized cranks that cheaper tools would strip.
- **Anyone with both Shimano and SRAM/ISIS cranks**: The reversible tip covers both standards without buying a second tool.

**Skip it if** you only work on bikes with self-extracting bolts (most modern high-end cranks) or you're a shop that needs a dedicated tool for each standard—though you'll likely buy the pro-grade CCP-44 anyway.
