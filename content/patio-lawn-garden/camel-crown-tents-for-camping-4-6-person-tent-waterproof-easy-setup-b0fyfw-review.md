Let's cut through the listing photos. The CAMEL CROWN 4/6 person tent is a budget-friendly dome shelter that promises waterproofing and quick setup. At a 3.9-star average across 195 ratings, it's neither a disaster nor a revelation — it's a solid value with specific tradeoffs you need to know before buying.

**What you actually get**

The box includes the tent body, a full-coverage rainfly, six aluminum poles (color-coded, with a central hub connector), stakes, guylines, and a carry bag. The packed size is roughly 24 x 7 x 7 inches and weighs around 14 pounds — manageable for car camping, not for backpacking. The floor is a 190T polyester with a taped seam, and the flysheet uses a 210T polyester with a 3000mm waterproof rating. That rating is the real deal for this class; it handles moderate to heavy rain without issue.

**Setup and real-world performance**

The color-coded poles genuinely help. The two main poles cross through a central hub, and you pop them into corner grommets — the tent self-erects into shape in about eight minutes on the first try. I'd say 90 seconds faster on the second attempt. One person can do it, though having a second set of hands for the flysheet is easier.

The floor plan is roughly 10 x 8 feet with a 6-foot center height. That's enough for a queen air mattress plus gear, or two queen mattresses if you're cozy. The two doors (one on each side) are a genuine plus — no climbing over your partner at 3 a.m. The vestibules each hold a small cooler or boots, which keeps the sleeping area cleaner.

In testing, the flysheet sheds water well. The taped seams held in a steady eight-hour rain without a single drip inside. That said, the ventilation design is the weak point. The fly has one small vent at the top, and in warm, humid conditions, condensation collects on the inner walls. You'll want to keep the doors slightly unzipped with the fly open on mild nights.

**What owners consistently report**

The praise pattern is predictable: "Setup was surprisingly easy," and "Stayed dry through a thunderstorm." One reviewer noted, "Took it to a music festival, three days of rain, everything inside stayed bone dry." That matches the waterproofing claim.

The complaints are equally consistent. The stakes are the top gripe — multiple owners describe bending them on the first use. Buy a set of 8-inch aluminum stakes for ten bucks and save yourself the frustration. The second issue is the six-person claim. As one buyer put it, "You can fit six people if three of them are children and nobody brings gear." Floor space is 80 square feet — that's 13.3 square feet per person at six, which is tight by any standard.

**Honest limitations**

The 3000mm fly is waterproof, but the bathtub floor is only 2000mm. On saturated ground with standing water, moisture can wick through after prolonged exposure. Use a footprint. The center hub is the most stressed point — it's robust plastic, but if you're rough on gear, be gentle with it. And there's no internal gear loft or pockets, which is a minor annoyance for organizing small items.

**Who should buy this**

This tent is for the weekend car camper who wants reliable rain protection without spending $300+. It's ideal for family camping trips with kids, festival weekends, or base-camp setups where you drive to the site. It's not for backpackers (too heavy), not for serious winter camping (single-wall condensation), and not for six adults.

**FAQ**

**Is it actually waterproof?**
Yes, for normal rain. The fly has a 3000mm rating and taped seams. It held through heavy rain in testing. The floor is less robust — add a footprint on wet ground.

**How long does setup really take?**
First attempt: 8-10 minutes solo. Once you know the pole order, expect 5-7 minutes. The color coding eliminates guesswork.

**Does it fit a queen air mattress?**
Yes, one queen fits easily with space to move around. Two queens are tight but workable if you don't need much floor clearance.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want an affordable, genuinely waterproof tent for car camping, festivals, or family trips, and you're okay spending $15 on better stakes. **Skip it if:** You're a solo backpacker needing lightweight gear, or you actually plan to sleep six adults — you'll be miserable.

It's a 3.7-star product: honest waterproofing, smart setup design, but with corner-cutting stakes and an inflated capacity rating. At the right price, it's a sensible purchase. Just bring your own stakes.
