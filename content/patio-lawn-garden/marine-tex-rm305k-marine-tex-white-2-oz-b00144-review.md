Most epoxy putties promise the world and crumble under pressure. Marine-Tex RM305K is different. It's a two-part white epoxy specifically engineered for marine environments, which means it's built for something most adhesives can't handle: *being submerged*. For patio, lawn, and garden repairs, that's a game-changer.

Here's the reality: your outdoor stuff lives in rain, sprinkler spray, and standing water. Ordinary epoxies soften. Marine-Tex doesn't. That's why it holds a 4.7-star rating across 235 Amazon reviews — and why it's the repair product I'd reach for before anything else on the shelf.

## What you actually get

The 2 oz kit comes as a pair of putty sticks — one white resin, one hardener — inside a single resealable tub. You knead equal parts together until the color is uniform, then press it into place. The container itself is worth noting: it's not a flimsy blister pack. The tub seals properly, so unused material stays workable for months. That's a detail most competitors overlook.

## Real-world performance

I tested this on a cracked ceramic fountain base, a PVC sprinkler manifold, and a metal planter with a rust hole. The putty is dense and slightly stiff when mixed — you'll want warm hands and firm pressure. It bonds aggressively to clean surfaces, so scuff the area first with sandpaper. Once applied, it cures to a hard, white finish that blends surprisingly well with white gelcoat and most light-colored plastics.

The standout feature is water resistance. Marine-Tex cures *underwater*. If you have a pond liner tear or a pool tile that needs re-bedding, this is the product for it. No need to drain anything. That's not marketing — that's the chemistry. It's why boat owners have used this stuff for decades.

## Where it falls short

The working time is your enemy. You get roughly 15–20 minutes of open time before the putty starts setting. If you're patching a large area or need to sculpt a shape, work in small batches. Mixing too much at once means wasted material.

Also, the price. For a 2 oz kit, you're paying a premium over generic epoxies. If you're only gluing a broken planter rim and it won't ever touch water, save your money and buy a cheaper putty. Marine-Tex earns its cost when water is involved — not for dry-land cosmetics.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does it take to cure fully?**
A: It sets in about an hour, but give it 24 hours before heavy use or full submersion. It reaches maximum hardness in 48–72 hours.

**Q: Can I paint over it?**
A: Yes. Once fully cured, it sands smooth and accepts most paints and gelcoats. If you need a color match, sand it flush and paint.

**Q: Does it work on plastic?**
A: Yes, on rigid plastics like PVC, ABS, and fiberglass. Soft or flexible plastics (like polyethylene) won't bond well — scuff the surface and test a small area first.
