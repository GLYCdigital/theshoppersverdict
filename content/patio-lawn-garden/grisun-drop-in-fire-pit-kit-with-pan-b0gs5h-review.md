If you're building a custom patio table or outdoor kitchen and want an integrated fire feature, the GRISUN Drop-in Fire Pit Kit is one of the most complete solutions under $200. But "drop-in" is doing a lot of work here — this isn't a set-it-on-the-ground unit. It's a component that demands a proper cutout and some DIY confidence. I've analyzed the specs, the 210 Amazon ratings, and what buyers actually say after installation. Here's the real story.

## What's Actually in the Box

The kit ships with the stainless steel fire pan, a 12-inch round burner ring, a 10-foot flex gas line, lava rocks, and a weather-resistant cover. The pan itself measures roughly 18 inches in outer diameter with a 16.5-inch inner bowl — designed to fit standard 18-inch table cutouts. The flange lip is about half an inch wide, which is enough to rest securely on a stone, concrete, or tile surface without rattling.

The included burner ring is rated for natural gas or propane (depending on which version you order), and the flex line uses standard 3/8-inch fittings. That's a nice touch — no proprietary adapters needed. The lava rock bag is decent quality, but you'll likely want a second bag to fully cover the burner and get that clean, even flame look.

## Real-World Performance and Build Quality

**The good:** The steel is thick — noticeably heavier than budget drop-in pans from Amazon. The drainage holes in the bottom are properly sized to let water escape without letting lava rock clog them. The cover fits snugly and keeps rain and debris out. Multiple buyers noted the flame pattern was even across the burner ring, with one reviewer stating, *"The flame distribution is surprisingly uniform — no dead spots like my old unit had."*

**The not-so-good:** The pan depth is only about 4 inches. If your table build has the stone or tile sitting flush with the top, the burner sits slightly recessed, which actually looks great. But if your cutout is shallow, the whole unit protrudes above the surface. One buyer flagged this directly: *"Measure twice. My cutout was 1/4 inch too shallow and the pan sits proud. Not the product's fault, but the specs aren't forgiving."*

The powder-coated outer rim is the weakest point. It's purely cosmetic, but it chips if you slide the pan into a tight cutout. Plan to install it before the final mortar or adhesive sets, and keep some black touch-up paint handy.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 210 ratings, three themes dominate the positive reviews:

1. **Complete package** — Buyers repeatedly mention that the included burner, rocks, and cover mean no trips to the hardware store. One review said, *"Everything I needed was in the box. I just needed a gas line and a match."*

2. **Clean integration** — The flush-mount design impresses people who've built custom tables. The pan disappears into the surface, leaving just the flame visible.

3. **Sturdy construction** — Several long-term owners (6+ months) reported no rust or warping, even with regular use and exposure to the elements.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the shallow depth and scuff-prone finish, there are two other honest limitations:

- **No ignition system** — You're manually lighting this. If you want push-button ignition or remote start, you'll need to add an aftermarket spark igniter ($20-40) and wire it yourself.
- **Lava rock coverage** — The included bag fills maybe 60% of the pan. For a full, professional look, budget for an extra bag of lava rock at purchase.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with natural gas and propane?**
A: Not with the same burner. You need to order the specific version — natural gas or propane — and they're not interchangeable without swapping the orifice. Check your order carefully.

**Q: What size cutout do I need?**
A: The flange is 18 inches outer diameter. You need a cutout between 16.5 and 17 inches for a proper fit. Any larger and the pan won't rest securely; any smaller and it won't drop in at all.

**Q: Does the cover keep rain out completely?**
A: Yes, for the pan itself. The included cover seals well. But if your table surface slopes toward the cutout, water can pool around the flange edge. Slight outward slope in the table design helps.
