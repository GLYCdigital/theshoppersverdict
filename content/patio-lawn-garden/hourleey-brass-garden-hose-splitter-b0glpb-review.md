**The Short Version: A Brass Splitter That Actually Earns Its Keep**

Most hose splitters under $20 are die-cast zinc or plastic that cracks by September. The Hourleey Brass Garden Hose Splitter takes the opposite approach — it's a solid brass block with two independent ball valves, and after 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clear this one holds up where cheap alternatives fail.

**What's in the Box**

You get the splitter itself with a female inlet that threads onto any standard outdoor spigot, and two male outlets for your hoses. The package includes a spare set of rubber washers, which tells you the manufacturer knows exactly where wear happens. No tools required for installation — the brass nuts are knurled for hand tightening, and the pre-installed washers do the sealing work.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The difference between this and cheaper splitters is immediately obvious when you hold it. It's heavy — solid brass throughout, not a brass-colored coating over pot metal. The quarter-turn valves have a positive stop, so you know when they're fully open or closed. No guessing.

Threads are cleanly machined. That matters more than most shoppers realize — cheap splitters often have rough threads that cross-thread on your spigot within a month. The Hourleey threads on smoothly and seats without needing plumber's tape.

At standard household pressure (40-60 PSI), the seals hold. No weeping at the connections, no drips from the valve stems. One reviewer put it simply: "Bought two — one for the front and one for the back. No leaks anywhere after three months of daily use."

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The independent valve control is the feature that gets the most love. You can run a soaker hose on one outlet while completely shutting off the other — no more crawling behind the shrubbery to turn off the main spigot. As one verified buyer noted: "Finally, I can water the garden and wash the car without playing the disconnect-and-reconnect game."

Durability is the second recurring theme. Multiple reviewers mention leaving it installed year-round through freezing winters without failure. That's not something you can say about the plastic splitters from the big-box store.

**Where It Falls Short**

The compact design has a tradeoff: there's not much handle to grip when you need to loosen it after months of sitting tight. If you're the type who cranks fittings down with a wrench, you'll regret it when removal time comes. Hand-tight is genuinely the right approach here.

There's also no flow adjustment. The valves are binary — full on or full off. If you need a gentle trickle for a drip irrigation line, you'll want a separate inline valve instead.

**Who Should Buy This**

You're the target buyer if:

- You're tired of replacing plastic or zinc splitters every season
- You need two independent outlets at one spigot (front yard + back yard, or hose + sprinkler)
- You want something that survives winter without being disconnected
- You appreciate tools that feel substantial in the hand

Skip it if you need precise flow control, or if you're only running one hose and don't need the splitter function at all.

**FAQ**

**Will this fit my existing hoses?**
Yes, if your hoses have standard 3/4-inch male fittings — which is virtually universal in the US. The two outlets are standard male hose threads, so any standard hose or accessory connects directly.

**Do I need plumber's tape?**
No. The pre-installed rubber washers create the seal. Adding tape on the male threads actually interferes with proper seating.

**Can it handle high water pressure?**
It's rated for standard residential pressure up to around 100 PSI. If you're on a well system with a pressure tank, you're fine. Commercial or agricultural pressure — not so much.
