## What You Actually Get

The package contains a single stainless steel mesh basket filter. That's it. No adapter rings, no brushes, no spare parts. The filter measures roughly 5.5 inches across and 1.5 inches deep, with a fine 304-grade stainless mesh wrapped around a rigid frame. The handle is a simple tab that folds flat for storage. It's lightweight — about 2 ounces — and feels solid in hand. The mesh is noticeably finer than some cheaper reusable filters I've seen, which matters for keeping grounds out of your cup.

## Real-World Performance

I brewed with this filter daily for two weeks in a Mr. Coffee 5-cup model. The good news: coffee comes out clean, with no sediment or grit. The fine mesh does its job, even with store-brand pre-ground coffee that tends to have finer particles. Flow rate is comparable to paper — a full pot brews in roughly the same time, maybe 10–15 seconds faster since there's no paper to saturate.

Cleanup is genuinely easy. A quick rinse under hot tap water removes 90% of the grounds. The stubborn oils that collect near the rim need a gentle wipe with a sponge every few days. I've seen complaints about staining, but that's cosmetic — it doesn't affect taste.

One thing to note: this filter is designed for 4–5 cup brewers, and it's not interchangeable with larger 10–12 cup machines. If you've got a bigger pot, this won't cover the basket properly, and grounds will wash around the edges. Check your brewer's basket dimensions before ordering.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. This isn't a perfect universal fit. Several reviewers with Cuisinart models report the filter sits slightly loose, allowing fine grounds to sneak past the edges into the carafe. It's not a dealbreaker — a slight press to seat it properly solves the issue — but it's worth knowing if you're a Cuisinart owner.

The second issue is drying. The mesh holds moisture in the frame, and if you store it in a closed cabinet while damp, you risk mold spots. I learned this the hard way. Rinse, dry thoroughly with a towel, or let it air dry before putting it away. It's a 30-second habit, but it's non-negotiable.
