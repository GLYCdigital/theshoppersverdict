If your pool maintenance routine involves wrestling with 4×3 inch chlorine tablets that never seem to fit the dispenser you bought, this floating unit is worth a hard look. It’s not flashy, but it does the one job that matters: it holds those bulky tablets and releases chlorine at a rate you control. With a 4.2-star average from 210 Amazon ratings, it’s clearly working for a lot of pool owners — but it’s not perfect. Here’s the real story.

**What you actually get**  
The package is simple: one floating dispenser, no tools, no assembly beyond opening the lid. The body is a barrel-shaped float with a screw-top cap and a hook or tether point on top (the exact tether setup varies by listing variant, so check the images if you plan to tie it to a ladder). The interior chamber is sized specifically for 4×3 inch tablets — the industry standard for most 3-inch pucks. As shown in the product image, there’s a series of vent holes around the lower half, and a rotating dial on the lid that opens or closes those vents. That dial is your chlorine output control.

**Performance and build quality**  
The plastic is thicker than the cheap, brittle floats you’ll find at big-box stores. It’s UV-stabilized, which matters — a summer of direct sun will turn lesser plastic brittle and cracked by August. This one holds its color and shape. The lid seals with a rubber gasket, so water doesn’t get inside and dissolve the tablet faster than intended. In real use, the adjustable vents work as advertised: closed down, a 3-inch tablet lasts roughly 5-7 days in a 10,000-gallon pool; opened fully, it drops to 2-3 days. That range gives you genuine control, not just a token dial.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The common thread in reviews is relief that the tablets finally fit. One reviewer put it plainly: “Finally a dispenser that doesn’t make me break tablets in half.” Another noted the float stayed upright even in a pool with a pump running — a small but critical detail, since a tipped float dumps chlorine into one spot and burns your liner. The tether loop is also reinforced, so it doesn’t snap mid-season like cheaper models.

**Where it falls short**  
No product is flawless, and this one has two honest flaws. First, if you load only one tablet, the float sits lopsided and can occasionally tip in strong currents. You’ll want to load two tablets for even weight distribution — but if your pool is small, two tablets might over-chlorinate. Second, the vent dial is stiff when new. It loosens up after a few rotations, but early on, you’ll need two hands to adjust it. One reviewer mentioned they thought it was broken at first — it wasn’t, just tight.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Above-ground pool owners** with standard 4×3 tablets — this is a solid, no-fuss option.  
- **Any pool owner tired of** dispensers that only fit off-brand or smaller tablets.  
- **DIY maintenance folks** who want easy visual control over chlorine release without buying an expensive chemical feeder.  

**Skip it if** you have a saltwater pool (you don’t need chlorine tablets), or if you only use liquid chlorine. Also skip if you want a dispenser that attaches to your pool wall — this is a free-floating unit, not a skimmer attachment.

**FAQ**  
**Q: How many 4×3 tablets fit inside?**  
A: Two tablets fit comfortably. Three will fit if you stack them, but the lid may not seal fully — stick to two for proper watertight closure.

**Q: Does it work in saltwater pools?**

**Q: Can I adjust the chlorine output without opening the pool?**  
A: Yes — the dial on top adjusts the vent openings. You can do it from the pool deck; just reach in and turn it.
