Let's be straight about pressure washer hoses: most are either too short, too stiff, or they burst on the third use. The Westinghouse 50 ft PVC hose sits in the middle of the market — not the cheapest, not the most premium — but it does exactly what the spec sheet promises. Given the 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, buyers largely agree. Here's the full picture before you spend.

**What's in the box**

You get the 50-foot hose itself, pre-fitted with brass M22 connectors on both ends (the standard for most gas pressure washers), plus rubber O-rings already seated in each fitting. No adapters, no extra washers, no carry bag. It's bare-bones — but for most gas washer owners, M22 is what you need, so you won't be hunting for adapters immediately. If your unit uses a quick-connect system, budget a few dollars for a separate adapter.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The hose is rated for 3400 PSI, which means it's built to handle the output of nearly all consumer gas pressure washers on the market — from 2000 PSI homeowner units up to the 3200-3400 PSI "prosumer" machines. The PVC construction is reinforced with a braided layer that resists bursting, and the 3/8-inch inner diameter keeps water flow strong at distance. At 50 feet, you'll notice some pressure drop compared to a 25-footer, but it's within acceptable range for washing driveways, siding, or fences without moving the machine constantly.

The fittings are the real highlight. They're solid brass with a machined feel, and they thread onto pressure washer wands snugly without cross-threading. Multiple reviewers noted the seals held tight immediately — no dripping at the connection, which is the most common failure point on cheaper hoses. One buyer put it plainly: "The fittings feel like they belong on a $200 hose, not a $40 one."

Flexibility is a mixed bag. The PVC is genuinely lighter and more maneuverable than rubber alternatives, and it stays pliable in cooler weather where rubber stiffens up. However, the hose arrives coiled tightly from the factory, and several owners reported needing to unroll it in the sun or run hot water through it for several minutes to lose the coil memory. Once it relaxes, it's easy to drag around, but the first use requires patience.

**Where it falls short**

The PVC outer jacket is the weakest link. It scuffs easily — dragging it over rough concrete or gravel leaves white marks and abrasions. This is purely cosmetic and doesn't affect performance, but if you're particular about your gear looking clean, it will annoy you. The lack of a swivel at the gun end is a more functional gripe: if you're moving in tight circles around a car or patio furniture, the hose can twist and develop a temporary kink. It usually works itself out with a shake, but it interrupts flow briefly.

A handful of reviewers reported leaking at the O-rings after several months of heavy use. The fix is simple — replace the rubber washers (a $3 pack of spares solves it) — but it's worth knowing before you buy. The hose is not covered by a lifetime warranty; Westinghouse offers a one-year limited warranty, which is standard but not exceptional.

**Who should buy this**

- **Gas pressure washer owners** with machines rated 2500-3400 PSI who need a longer reach without upgrading to a $100+ rubber hose.
- **Casual-to-moderate users** who wash their driveway, deck, or car a few times a month and want a hose that won't burst mid-job.
- **Anyone replacing a stock 25-foot hose** that's too short to reach the far side of the house without moving the washer.

**Skip it if** you're a professional using the hose daily — the PVC scuffing and O-ring maintenance will frustrate you. Also skip if your washer uses a proprietary quick-connect fitting; you'll need extra adapters and the cost advantage fades.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my pressure washer?**
Most gas models use M22 fittings, which this hose has. If your unit uses quick-connect or a smaller 1/4-inch thread, you'll need an adapter. Check your washer's manual or the connection point before ordering.

**Q: Does the 50-foot length reduce water pressure?**
Yes, some pressure drop is physics — you'll lose roughly 5-10% PSI compared to a 25-foot hose. For most cleaning tasks, this is negligible. If you're running high-pressure surface cleaners, stick closer to the machine.

**Q: Can I use this with an electric pressure washer?**
You can, but it's overkill. Electric washers typically output 1500-2000 PSI, and this hose is rated far beyond that. It'll work fine, but the heavier brass fittings add weight that a lighter-duty hose wouldn't.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a reliable 50-foot hose for a gas pressure washer and want solid brass fittings without paying premium prices. It's a workhorse that performs well out of the gate and holds up for typical homeowner use.

**Skip it if** you're a daily professional, need a swivel connection, or want a hose that stays pristine-looking after concrete work.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, honest product with a few rough edges. For the price, it's a smart upgrade over the stock hose that came with your washer.
