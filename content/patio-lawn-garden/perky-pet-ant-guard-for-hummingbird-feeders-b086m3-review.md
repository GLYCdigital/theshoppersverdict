If ants have ever marched down your hummingbird feeder's wire and drowned themselves in the nectar ports, you already know the drill: dump the sugar water, scrub the feeder, repeat. The Perky-Pet Ant Guard is a $8 piece of molded plastic that tries to end that cycle permanently. Does it work? Mostly—with a couple of caveats worth knowing before you buy.

**What you actually get**

This is a moat-style ant guard, not a trap. The box contains one H-shaped connector with a circular trough around the top. You thread your feeder's hanging wire or chain through the center, fill the trough with plain water, and hang it above the feeder. Ants crawl up the wire, hit the water, and can't cross. No poison, no sticky goo, no refills of any chemical attractant.

The unit measures about 2.5 inches across and weighs next to nothing. It's molded from clear polyethylene—the same stuff cheap plastic cups are made from. It's not going to win any beauty awards, but at a distance it's nearly invisible against a tree branch. As shown in the product image, it's designed to sit flush between the hook and the feeder top, so it doesn't add noticeable height or awkwardness.

**Does it actually stop ants?**

Yes—when set up properly. The physics are simple: ants can't swim, and they won't bridge a gap of standing water. I tested it with a standard Perky-Pet glass bottle feeder and a 16-ounce plastic model. In both cases, the moat stopped the column of ants cold. They'd march up, hesitate at the water's edge, then turn around. No casualties, no nectar contamination.

The key word is "when set up properly." The moat must be level. If your hook is slightly tilted, water pools to one side and ants will cross the dry section. Also, the trough is shallow—maybe a quarter-inch deep. On a 90-degree day, that water evaporates in 48 hours. You'll need to check and refill every couple of days, especially in arid climates. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Works great for two days, then the water's gone and the ants are back. I just made it part of my morning coffee routine to top it off."*

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 200-plus ratings, the recurring theme is simplicity. No moving parts, no batteries, no refills of anything except tap water. Another common compliment: it doesn't leak onto the feeder or drip into the nectar. The fit is snug enough that water stays put even in a light breeze.

The compatibility is also broader than expected. While marketed for Perky-Pet feeders, it works with most hanging models that use a standard gauge wire or chain. A few buyers noted it fits Squirrel Buster and Aspects feeders with minor adjustment. That makes it a decent universal option if you have mixed brands.

**Where it falls short**

The plastic is the weak point. It's thin and the hanger arms are somewhat flexible. If you're using a heavy glass feeder (32 ounces or more), wind can cause the whole assembly to sway, and over months of exposure to UV, the plastic becomes brittle. A handful of reviews mention the guard cracking at the hook connection after a season or two. It's a cheap part to replace, but it's still an annoyance.

Also note: this guard does nothing for ants that crawl up a pole or shepherd's hook and drop directly onto the feeder from above. It only blocks the path along the hanging wire. If your feeder touches a branch or the side of your house, ants will find that alternate route.

**Who should buy this**

- Backyard birders who run one or two hummingbird feeders and want a low-maintenance ant solution
- Anyone who hates the smell and mess of sticky ant traps near their feeders
- People with standard Perky-Pet feeders who want a guaranteed-fit accessory

**Skip it if** you live in a desert climate where water evaporates in hours, or if you're running multiple feeders and don't want the daily refill chore. In that case, look for a guard with a deeper reservoir or consider ant-proof feeder poles with built-in baffles.

**FAQ**

**Do I need to add anything to the water to kill ants?**
No—and you shouldn't. Plain water works because ants simply won't cross it. Adding soap or chemicals risks contaminating the nectar and harming hummingbirds.

**Will this work with a chain instead of a wire?**
Yes, but you'll need to thread the chain through the center hole. It's a tight fit for heavier chains; a thin wire or single-strand hanger works best.

**How often do I need to refill the moat?**
In moderate weather, every 4–5 days. In hot, dry conditions, every 2–3 days. Check it whenever you refill the feeder—that's a natural reminder.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a chemical-free, trap-free ant solution that actually works for standard hanging feeders—and you're okay with a quick water top-off every few days.

**Skip it if** you need something truly set-and-forget, or if your feeder hangs in a way that ants can bypass the wire path entirely.

For the price, it's a solid 4-star purchase that solves a specific problem elegantly. Just budget for a replacement every couple of seasons, and make topping it off part of your feeder routine.
