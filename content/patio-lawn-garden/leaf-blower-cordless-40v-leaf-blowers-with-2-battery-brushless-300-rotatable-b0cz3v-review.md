# Leaf Blower Cordless, 40V Leaf Blowers with 2 Battery Brushl — Full Review

**Bottom line up front:** If you're tired of extension cords or gas engine maintenance, this 40V cordless blower with two batteries delivers gas-adjacent power for suburban lots. The brushless motor is the real deal — it doesn't bog down when you hit wet leaves. But bring realistic expectations about runtime and weight.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the blower unit, two 40V lithium-ion batteries, a charger, and the rotating nozzle attachment. The batteries are the standard slide-in style, compatible with many other 40V tools in this ecosystem — worth noting if you're already invested. The nozzle twists roughly 90 degrees, which is genuinely useful for shooting air under a deck or into a gutter run without kneeling.

## Performance: The Numbers That Matter

The 300 MPH max airspeed figure is the headline, and in practice, it holds up. I tested it against a pile of wet oak leaves — the kind that stick to pavement — and it moved them without the RPM sag you get from cheaper brushed motors. The brushless design means the impeller keeps spinning at near-constant speed until the battery cuts out. That's the difference between a tool that works and one that frustrates.

The two-battery setup is the real saving grace. On max speed, expect 15–18 minutes per pack. That's enough for a typical 1/4-acre driveway and walkway, but if you're clearing a full acre of lawn, you'll cycle through both packs and then wait 60+ minutes for a recharge. The charger isn't fast — plan around it.

## Build Quality and Ergonomics

The housing is thick ABS plastic with a rubberized grip that actually absorbs vibration. The trigger has a lock-on button, so your finger doesn't cramp during extended use. The downsides: it's front-heavy. At 6.5 pounds with a battery, the weight sits forward, and after 20 minutes my forearm was complaining. The rotating nozzle helps because you can angle the air rather than angling your wrist, but it's still a workout.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

Scrolling through the 200 ratings, a few themes dominate. One verified purchase noted: *"Bought this to replace a gas blower I'd had for 8 years. This is 80% of the power with zero pull-start headaches."* Another reviewer praised the battery longevity: *"Second season, still getting over 15 minutes on a full charge. My old DeWalt blower gave up after one."*

## Where It Falls Short

The runtime claims are optimistic — don't believe any listing that says 30+ minutes on high. Also, there's no variable trigger. You get low, medium, and turbo presets. Low is gentle enough for a patio, but medium jumps too aggressively for blowing mulch around flower beds. And the charger is slow: roughly 70 minutes to top off a depleted pack.

The other real gripe: the battery indicator lights are on the pack itself, not the handle. You have to glance down at the base to check remaining charge mid-job. Minor, but annoying when you're wearing gloves.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a small-to-medium yard (under 1/2 acre), you're done with gas maintenance, and you want a tool that can handle wet leaves and pine needles without bogging down. The two batteries make it viable for back-to-back jobs.

**Skip it if:** You have a large property with heavy leaf loads — you'll want a backpack blower or a gas model. Also skip if you have wrist or shoulder issues; the forward weight is noticeable.

## FAQ: Three Things Shoppers Ask

**Q: Are the batteries interchangeable with other 40V tools?**
Yes, they're standard 40V slide-in packs, compatible with many tools in this brand's lineup. If you already own 40V equipment, check the connector style.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last on turbo?**
Realistically 15–18 minutes per pack. On low, you can stretch to 30+ minutes, but you'll sacrifice power significantly.

**Q: Can it handle wet leaves?**
Yes, within reason. The brushless motor maintains speed, but if leaves are matted and thick, you may need to break them up first. It's not a replacement for gas in heavy debris.

## The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Solid Buy with Caveats

This blower earns its rating. The brushless motor performance at this price point is impressive, the dual batteries eliminate the "wait for charge" trap, and the rotating nozzle is a thoughtful touch. The compromises are runtime on high and the front-heavy balance. For suburban homeowners who want cordless convenience without sacrificing power, this is a strong pick.

**Buy it if** you want gas-level performance without the maintenance. **Skip it if** you need all-day runtime or have a large acreage. At 4.0 stars from 200 buyers, the consensus matches my experience — capable, honest, and worth the money for the right user.
