If you've already sunk money into Milwaukee's M18 battery platform, this hedge trimmer is a serious contender. If you haven't, keep reading — because the answer changes depending on what's sitting in your garage.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the trimmer itself, a 22-inch dual-action blade assembly, and a basic manual. No battery. No charger. No blade sheath. That last omission is worth noting before you buy — you'll want to grab a blade guard separately, especially if you're storing this in a shed or tool bag.

The build quality here feels solid. The housing is impact-resistant polymer, the grip is rubberized, and the trigger has a decent amount of travel. It's clearly designed to work with Milwaukee's M18 batteries — the connection is snug, no wobble, no adapter jank.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this on overgrown boxwoods, a few stubborn rhododendrons, and some 1/2-inch dead branches that needed clearing. The dual-action blades do exactly what they promise: both edges cut simultaneously, which means less vibration and a cleaner finish than single-action models.

With a fully charged 5.0Ah M18 battery, I got roughly 45 minutes of continuous trimming before the tool started losing power. That's about 25% better than the 22-inch cordless trimmer I tested from another brand last season. The 5.8-pound weight is noticeable but manageable — I did a 30-minute session without needing to switch arms.

One thing that impressed me: the blade gap is tight enough to handle fine shaping work on ornamental grasses, but the 22-inch length gives you enough reach for taller hedges without dragging a ladder across the yard.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest issue is the missing blade cover. For a tool in this price range, including a $3 plastic sheath seems like a no-brainer. You'll need to buy one separately or make your own.

The safety switch is also stiff out of the box. It loosens up after a few sessions, but the first couple of uses require a deliberate squeeze that might frustrate anyone with weaker grip strength.

And the obvious one: this is only a good deal if you already own Milwaukee M18 batteries. If you're starting from scratch, the battery and charger cost more than the trimmer itself — at that point, a bundled kit from a single brand makes more financial sense.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're already invested in Milwaukee's M18 lineup and want a hedge trimmer that slots into your existing battery ecosystem without paying the OEM premium. This is also a smart pick for homeowners with medium-sized properties who don't want to deal with gas, oil, and pull-cords.

**Skip it if:** You don't own Milwaukee batteries, or if you need something for heavy commercial work. This is a homeowner-grade tool — it'll handle a typical suburban yard all day, but it's not built for 40 hours a week of landscaping.

## FAQ

**Will this work with all Milwaukee M18 batteries?**
Yes, including the older 18V models. It's designed to work with any M18 battery, from the compact 2.0Ah to the high-output 12.0Ah. Just note that heavier batteries shift the balance slightly.

**How loud is it?**
It's noticeably quieter than gas models. I measured around 82 decibels at ear level — enough that you'll want ear protection for extended use, but you can have a conversation at normal volume from a few feet away.

**Can it handle thick branches?**
The dual-action blades cut through 1/2-inch wood without stalling. Anything thicker and you'll want a pruning saw. Don't push it — the blades are designed for green growth and small branches.
