If your lawn edges look like a beaver's snack bar—ragged, overgrown, and creeping onto the driveway—you know the pain of fighting grass with a string trimmer. The WORX 12 Amp Electric Edger & Trencher exists to end that fight. It's a corded tool that trades convenience for raw, consistent power, and after digging through 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, here's what actually matters.

**What's in the box**

You get the edger body with the 7.5-inch hardened steel blade pre-installed, a blade wrench, and a depth adjustment knob. No battery, no charger—this is pure corded. The unit weighs about 10.5 pounds, which is light enough to maneuver one-handed when needed but heavy enough to feel substantial. The dual handles are positioned to let you push from behind or pull from the side, which matters more than you'd think when edging along a long driveway.

**Performance and real-world use**

The 12-amp motor is the star here. It spins the blade at roughly 6,000 RPM, and that's enough to chew through thick Bermuda grass, crabgrass, and even small tree roots without the motor straining. The depth adjustment is genuinely tool-free—you twist the knob and slide the front wheel to set cutting depth from 0.5 to 1.5 inches. For standard lawn edging, I'd start at 0.75 inches. For creating a clean trench for landscape lighting wire or drip irrigation, max it out.

One thing buyers consistently mention is how much cleaner this cuts compared to a trimmer. As one reviewer put it, "I used to spend 20 minutes with a string trimmer and it still looked like garbage. This thing gives me a crisp, professional edge in about 5 minutes." That matches the consensus—this tool doesn't just cut grass, it creates a defined separation between lawn and hardscape.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- **Cut quality**: The blade leaves a sharp, clean line without the shredded look you get from nylon line.
- **Ease of use**: The wheel-guided design means you don't need a steady hand to get a straight edge—just follow the curb or sidewalk.
- **Versatility**: The trenching capability is a legit bonus. One reviewer noted, "I dug a 30-foot trench for landscape lighting in under an hour. My back didn't hate me afterward."

**Where it falls short**

The cord is the elephant in the room. You're tethered to an outlet, which means extension cord management is part of the job. If you've got a standard suburban lot, a 100-foot heavy-duty cord will cover most of it, but you'll be dragging it behind you the whole time. It's a real limitation for larger properties or anyone who hates cord wrangling.

The plastic wheel housing also gets mixed feedback. It's fine for normal use, but if you hit a buried rock or a chunk of concrete, you risk cracking it. A metal housing would be more forgiving, but it would also add weight and cost. At this price point, it's a tradeoff, not a dealbreaker.

The blade guard partially obscures the cutting line. You can see enough to work, but getting right up against a curb or paver edge requires a bit of trial and error at first. Some users solve this by tilting the unit slightly, but it's not ideal.

**Who should buy this**

- **The weekend warrior** who wants crisp edges without the arm fatigue of a trimmer.
- **The landscaping DIYer** who needs to trench for wiring, edging, or irrigation and doesn't want to rent a gas trencher.
- **Anyone with a mid-size lawn (under half an acre)** who can tolerate a cord and wants consistent power without battery anxiety.

Skip it if you have a huge property, if you're deeply opposed to cord management, or if you need to edge in tight spaces with lots of obstacles—the 7.5-inch blade is nimble, but it's not a precision tool for intricate flower beds.

**FAQ**

**Can this actually trench, or is that marketing fluff?** It's real. Set the depth to 1.5 inches, guide it along your line, and it will cut a clean 1-inch-wide trench. It won't handle deep utility lines, but for landscaping fabric, wiring, or edging borders, it works.

**Does it handle thick, wet grass?** Yes, but you'll need to slow down. The motor won't stall, but pushing too fast in heavy grass will leave a ragged edge. Let the blade do the work.

**How loud is it?** About 85-90 decibels—comparable to a leaf blower. Wear ear protection if you're doing a long run.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want professional-grade lawn edges without paying for a gas unit or hiring a landscaper. It's powerful, easy to use, and the trenching capability adds genuine value. **Skip it if** you can't work around a cord or you need heavy-duty trencher performance.

The WORX 12 Amp Edger & Trencher earns a solid 4.2 out of 5. It's not perfect—the cord and plastic housing are real compromises—but for the price, it delivers clean cuts and real versatility that most homeowners will appreciate. If your edges are embarrassing you, this is the tool to fix them.
