The WaterStorageCube isn't the first collapsible water container you'll find on Amazon, but it might be the last one you need. At 5.3 gallons, it hits the sweet spot between a carryable jug and a stationary reserve tank. After digging through 229 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

The package includes the 20-liter (5.3-gallon) collapsible bag, a threaded spigot with twist-cap, and a small repair patch kit — a thoughtful inclusion that most competitors skip. The bag measures roughly 13.7 x 13.7 inches when full and collapses to about 2 inches thick when empty. The clear, food-grade PVC lets you see water levels at a glance, which is both a feature and a flaw (more on that below).

**Build quality and real-world use**

The 0.5mm PVC walls feel substantial — this isn't the crinkly, cheap plastic you get with budget camping jugs. Multiple buyers specifically mention the reinforced seams holding up through repeated use. One reviewer noted, "Used it for 6 months as my primary RV water tank. No leaks, no smell, and the spigot hasn't stripped." That spigot is a genuine highlight: it's a standard garden-hose thread size, so you can attach a hose directly without adapters.

The wide mouth is the unsung hero here. At 2.8 inches across, you can fill it from a fast-flowing hose without splashback, and cleaning is straightforward — you can actually get your hand inside to scrub. When full, the bag stands stable on its own; the flat base and gusseted sides prevent the drunken-lean problem that plagues cheaper collapsible containers.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring theme in positive reviews is versatility. Campers use it for shower water (leave it in the sun for a solar-heated rinse), gardeners use it for rain collection, and emergency-prep folks use it for long-term storage. The fold-flat design earns repeated praise — one reviewer said, "Stows behind my truck seat when empty. Takes up less space than a jacket."

**Where it falls short**

The clear plastic is the biggest tradeoff. Left in direct sunlight, algae will colonize your water within a week. You'll need to store it in a dark place or wrap it in something opaque for long-term water storage. Several reviewers also noted that the thin fill-cap tether broke off within a month — minor, but annoying since the cap itself seals fine.

The lack of a pressure-relief valve is a real design gap. If you overfill, the top seam takes stress that could eventually cause failure. Fill to about 90% capacity and you're fine, but it's a limitation worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

- **RV and van-dwellers** who need a reliable, stowable water reserve without permanent tank installation
- **Emergency-prep households** wanting a compact backup water source that doesn't eat closet space
- **Off-grid gardeners** collecting rain or hauling water from a distance

Skip it if you need a rigid, stackable container for permanent storage — the collapsible design trades structural rigidity for portability.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this actually food-grade and BPA-free?**
A: Yes, the product is made from BPA-free, food-grade PVC. Multiple reviewers confirm no plastic taste or odor transfers to the water.

**Q: Can I use this for drinking water long-term?**
A: Yes, but treat it like any stored water — use water preserver, keep it out of sunlight, and rotate every 6 months. The clear plastic accelerates algae growth, so storage conditions matter.

**Q: Does the spigot leak?**
A: The twist-cap spigot seals well when closed. The main leak risk is the fill-cap gasket, which can be dislodged if you over-tighten it.

**The Verdict: Buy it if you need portable water storage that disappears when empty. Skip it if you want a permanent, set-and-forget tank.**

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the algae susceptibility and missing pressure valve. Everything else performs above its price point. For campers, preppers, and RV owners, this is a solid, honest workhorse.
