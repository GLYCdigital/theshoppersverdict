Most flag pole holders are an afterthought — a $15 piece of stamped steel you bolt to the garage and forget about until it rusts. This one is different, and if you live anywhere near salt water, snow, or actual weather, that difference matters.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains the mounting bracket itself, four stainless lag bolts, and a set screw to lock your flag pole in place. The bracket is a one-piece casting — no welded seams to fail, no stamped thin metal that flexes under load. The collar opening is sized for standard 3/4-inch to 1-inch flag poles, and the depth is generous enough that a 5-foot pole sits rock-solid without excessive play.

## Build Quality That Justifies the Price

The headline here is the 316 marine-grade stainless. That's not marketing language — it's a specific alloy with molybdenum added specifically to resist chloride corrosion. If you've owned a 304 stainless flag holder near the coast, you know the drill: brown streaks, pitting, and eventual failure within 18 months. The 316 grade is what boat hardware uses because it survives salt spray that destroys lesser metals.

The machining is clean. Edges are rounded, the set screw threads smoothly, and the collar has an internal lip that prevents the pole from bottoming out at an angle. As shown in the product image, the mounting plate is substantial — roughly 4 inches tall with a 4-hole pattern that distributes load properly across your wall surface.

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't accidental. The dominant theme in customer feedback is longevity confidence. ## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The set screw that locks your pole in place uses a hex key (included), but it's a small one — easy to lose, and the screw can strip if you over-torque it. Go snug, not gorilla.

The satin finish is attractive but high-maintenance in terms of appearance — it shows fingerprints and water spots more than a brushed or matte finish would. Not a functional issue, but if you're particular about aesthetics, factor that in.

There's also no gasket between the plate and your wall. On an exterior installation, water can seep behind the mount. A $3 rubber gasket from any hardware store solves this — but at this price point, including one would have been a better look.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you live in coastal areas, snowy climates, or anywhere with significant weather exposure. This is also the right choice if you're mounting a heavier flag — think 4x6 feet or larger — where a flimsy holder will flex and fatigue.

**Skip it if** you're mounting a lightweight garden flag in a sheltered location. The premium cost buys marine-grade durability you simply won't need. A basic 304 steel holder will serve you fine under those conditions.

## FAQ

**Will this fit a 1-inch diameter flag pole?**
Yes, the collar accepts 3/4-inch to 1-inch poles. The set screw secures whatever diameter you insert.

**Can this be mounted on vinyl siding?**
Technically yes, but you'll need to mount it to a solid backing behind the siding. Directly into vinyl alone, it will pull out under wind load. Use a mounting block rated for the weight.

**Does the 316 stainless actually resist salt water better?**
Yes. The molybdenum content in 316 provides dramatically better chloride resistance than 304. For coastal installations, this is the difference between a one-time purchase and a yearly replacement.
