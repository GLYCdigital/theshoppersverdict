Here's the thing about the Gas One GS-3400P: it's not trying to be your kitchen range. It's trying to be the most flexible, no-nonsense portable stove you can throw in a trunk—and for the most part, it succeeds. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, this dual-fuel burner has clearly found its audience. The question is whether that audience includes you.

## What You Actually Get

The GS-3400P ships with the stove unit, a hard-shell plastic carrying case, and a butane canister to get you started. The stove measures 12.6 x 11 x 4.3 inches folded—roughly the footprint of a thick hardcover novel—and weighs about 3.5 pounds. The stainless steel burner surface is solidly built, and the fold-out legs lock into place with a reassuring click.

The dual-fuel capability is the headline feature here. The unit accepts both propane (via a side-mounted hose) and butane (via a rear canister slot), and switching between them takes about 30 seconds. No adapters, no tools, no fuss. That's genuine versatility for anyone who's ever stood in a camping aisle wondering which fuel to buy.

## Real-World Performance

The 7,650 BTU output isn't going to boil a gallon of water in 90 seconds, but it's more than adequate for most outdoor cooking tasks. A standard 10-inch skillet of eggs and bacon heats evenly across the burner surface, and the piezo ignition fires on the first click nearly every time. Temperature control via the dial is smooth and linear—you won't get dramatic flame jumps between settings.

The case is a genuine upgrade over soft pouches. It's rigid enough to protect the stove during transport, and the interior cutout fits the unit snugly. One buyer noted, "The hard case alone makes this worth it—I've had cheap soft bags tear on me before, but this thing has survived two seasons of car camping."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 235 ratings, three themes dominate the positive feedback:

- **Fuel flexibility**: "I can grab whatever canister is on sale and it just works. That's the whole reason I bought this over a single-fuel stove."
- **Reliability**: Multiple reviewers report years of trouble-free use, with the piezo ignition holding up long after cheaper units would have started needing a lighter.
- **Portability**: The combination of the compact footprint and protective case gets consistent shout-outs from campers and tailgaters.

## Where It Falls Short

The most significant issue is wind resistance—or rather, the lack of it. There's no integrated wind guard, so even a modest breeze can significantly reduce efficiency and make flame control frustrating. If you're cooking in exposed conditions, plan to bring a folding windscreen or position the stove strategically.

The butane performance also drops off in cold weather. Below about 50°F, butane canisters lose pressure noticeably, producing a weak, sputtering flame. Propane doesn't have this problem, so you'll want to switch fuels for shoulder-season camping. It's a limitation of the fuel itself, not poor engineering, but it's still worth knowing before you buy.

## Who Should Buy This

This stove is ideal for:

- **Car campers** who want a reliable burner that doesn't demand a specific fuel brand
- **Tailgaters** who need something compact that packs away cleanly
- **Emergency preparedness buyers** who want a stove that can run on either of the two most common portable fuel types

Skip it if you need serious wind protection, plan to cook in sub-freezing temps with butane, or expect restaurant-grade heat output.

## FAQ

**Can I use it indoors?** No. This is an outdoor appliance. Carbon monoxide risk is real, and the manufacturer explicitly warns against indoor use.

**How long does a butane canister last?** At medium heat, a standard 220g canister runs roughly 2-3 hours of continuous cooking. Propane lasts longer per ounce but the canisters are bulkier.

**Does it work with 1-pound propane cylinders?** Yes, the included hose connects directly to standard 16.4-ounce disposable propane cylinders.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely flexible dual-fuel stove that's built to last and pack away cleanly. The GS-3400P nails the fundamentals—reliable ignition, solid construction, and true fuel versatility—at a price that undercuts most competitors.

**Skip it if** you primarily cook in windy conditions or need consistent cold-weather performance. You'd be better served by a stove with integrated wind protection and a propane-only design. But for the vast majority of campers, tailgaters, and emergency kits, this is a smart, practical pick that earns its high rating.
