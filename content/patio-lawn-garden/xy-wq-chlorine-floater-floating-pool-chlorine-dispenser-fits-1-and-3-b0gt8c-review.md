# XY-WQ Chlorine Floater Review: The $15 Fix for Consistent Pool Chemistry

Most pool owners don't need a $300 chemical automation system. You need something that floats, holds a tablet, and doesn't crack in three weeks. The XY-WQ chlorine floater promises exactly that — and with 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's clearly doing something right. But does it earn a spot in your pool maintenance kit? Here's the breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the floater body, a screw-on lid with a handle, and an adjustable flow dial. That's it — no tablets included, so factor that into your first purchase. The unit measures about 7 inches in diameter and stands 5 inches tall, which is compact enough to stay out of the way but visible enough that you won't lose it in a 20-foot pool.

The key feature is the dual-size compatibility. The internal basket accepts both 1-inch and 3-inch chlorine tablets. That's a genuine convenience — most floaters force you to commit to one size, and switching means buying a whole new unit.

## Performance and Build Quality

The ABS plastic body feels substantial, not flimsy. The lid seals with a quarter-turn lock that requires deliberate force to open — that's good, because a loose lid means a tablet dissolves in one spot and bleaches your liner. In real-world use, the floater maintains a steady chlorine release for 5 to 7 days on a standard 3-inch tab, depending on your water temperature and sun exposure.

The adjustable vent dial is the standout feature. You can set it from near-closed (slow dissolve, good for cooler water) to fully open (fast release, ideal for shocking). One buyer noted, "I dial it down to two vents in spring and open it all the way in July — it's like having three different floaters in one."

That said, the dial has a flaw: on a new unit, it's stiff. Several reviewers mention needing pliers or a coin to twist it the first time. It loosens up after a few uses, but it's annoying on day one.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The most repeated positive feedback centers on durability and simplicity. One verified purchase wrote, "I've had cheap floaters crack by mid-summer. This one survived a full season including my nephew throwing it at the pool steps." Another highlighted the visibility: "The bright orange lid makes it easy to spot, even on a cloudy day when the water's murky."

The dual-tablet compatibility also earns frequent mentions. As one pool owner put it, "I bought this because I had a box of 1-inch tabs left over from my old floater but wanted to switch to 3-inch. This handled both without me buying another gadget."

## Where It Falls Short

The stiff dial is the main complaint, and it's legitimate. If you have arthritis or limited hand strength, you'll struggle with initial adjustments. The plastic also isn't rated for bromine or liquid chlorine — using either will cause premature cracking. That's a limitation, not a defect, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

There's also a minor design quirk: the floater sits low in the water, so if you run a skimmer with a high flow rate, it can get pulled toward the intake. It won't get sucked in, but you'll want to position it away from the return jet.

## Who Should Buy This

- **First-time pool owners** who want a no-fuss solution without learning chemical dosing math
- **Above-ground pool users** with small to medium pools (up to 15,000 gallons) that need steady weekly chlorination
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who want one device that works with whatever tablet size they already own

Skip it if you have a massive in-ground pool requiring precise chemical control — you'll want a floating feeder with larger capacity or an inline system. Also skip it if you use bromine; this floater is chlorine-only.

## FAQ

**How long does one 3-inch tablet last in this floater?**
With the dial set to medium (about 3 vents open), expect 5 to 7 days in warm water. In cooler water (below 70°F), it can stretch to 10 days. Direct sun accelerates dissolution, so adjust accordingly.

**Can I use it in a saltwater pool?**
Yes, the ABS plastic holds up fine in saltwater. Just rinse it with fresh water when you refill it to prevent mineral buildup on the vent dial.

**Will it fit in a skimmer basket if I want to use it that way?**
No — this is strictly a floating dispenser. The body is too tall for most skimmer baskets. If you want in-skimmer chlorination, look for a dedicated feeder.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, adjustable chlorinator that accepts both tablet sizes and won't disintegrate by August. It's a solid value for the price.

**Skip it if** you need a high-capacity feeder for a large pool, or if you prefer a fixed-rate flow without manual adjustment.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The stiff dial knocks off a few points, but the build quality, dual-size compatibility, and dependable performance make this a smart pick for most residential pools.
