**The Smart Grill That Actually Sells the Smart Part**

Most "smart" grills slap a Bluetooth thermometer on a mediocre firebox and call it innovation. The Monument Grills Denali is different — it's a legitimately capable 4-burner gas grill with Wi-Fi monitoring that doesn't feel like a gimmick. At 4.0 stars from 200 ratings, it's not perfect, but it's the rare connected grill that earns its tech badge.

**What You Actually Get**

Open the box and you'll find the main grill body, four stainless burners, a 12,000 BTU side burner, a 640-square-inch cooking surface (split between 480 sq in primary and 160 sq in warming rack), and a smart controller with two meat probes. Also included: a grease management tray, drip pan, and a propane tank scale indicator — a small touch that saves you from mid-cook fuel surprises. Assembly runs about 90 minutes if you've got a socket set and patience. The instructions are clear; the parts fit without forcing.

**Real-World Performance**

Firing all four burners hits 48,000 BTU, and the grill reaches 500°F in roughly 12 minutes on a 70-degree day. I seared ribeyes at 550°F — got a proper crust without charring the interior. The cast iron grates hold heat well, though they're not reversible like some pricier models. Temperature distribution is even across the main surface; the left side runs about 15°F hotter, which is minor and works fine for zone cooking.

The Wi-Fi app is the headline feature. You can set target temps, get alerts when the meat hits your number, and graph temperature over time. In testing, probe accuracy read within 3°F of an instant-read thermometer — genuinely useful for long smokes or overnight cooks. The app pairs quickly initially, but I did experience one drop mid-cook that required re-pairing. Not a dealbreaker, but know it's not flawless.

**What Buyers Praise**

Owners consistently highlight the value. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical about a smart grill at this price, but the app and probes work better than units twice the cost."* Another noted, *"Even heat across the grates — my burgers cook evenly without flare-ups."* The build quality gets repeated mentions: *"Feels solid, not wobbly like my old Char-Broil."* The grease tray system also earns points for being easy to remove and clean.

**Where It Falls Short**

The side burner is the weakest link — 12,000 BTU is adequate for simmering barbecue sauce but won't boil a pot of corn efficiently. If you planned on wok cooking, look elsewhere. The smart controller's Wi-Fi drops are real; if you're relying on remote monitoring for a 12-hour brisket, have a backup thermometer. And there's no rotisserie kit in the box — that's a $100+ add-on if you want it. The porcelain-coated grates are decent, but they won't last as long as full stainless or cast iron with enamel.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Tech-savvy home cooks** who want remote monitoring without dropping $1,500 on a Traeger or Weber SmokeFire
- **Weekend grillers** who value even heat and generous cooking space for parties
- **First-time smart grill buyers** who want a functional app without a steep learning curve

**Skip It If You Need:**

- High-BTU side burner for serious side dishes
- Built-in rotisserie capability
- Flawless, never-drops connectivity

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use the app without Wi-Fi?**
A: The grill works manually without the app — knobs operate as a standard gas grill. The smart features (remote monitoring, alerts) need Wi-Fi. Bluetooth is not supported; it's Wi-Fi or nothing for remote access.

**Q: Does it work with natural gas?**
A: The standard model is propane. A natural gas conversion kit is sold separately — factor that cost into your decision if you're converting.

**Q: How many people does it feed?**
A: The 480 sq in primary surface fits 12-16 burgers or 4 whole chickens comfortably. The warming rack adds space for buns or foil packets.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want smart grilling features that actually function, and you're okay with minor connectivity hiccups and a weak side burner. **Skip it if** you need rotisserie, high-BTU side cooking, or demand zero-tech-failure reliability — in which case, spend more for a Weber or a dedicated pellet smoker.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, smart gas grill that delivers on its core promise with a few forgivable compromises.
