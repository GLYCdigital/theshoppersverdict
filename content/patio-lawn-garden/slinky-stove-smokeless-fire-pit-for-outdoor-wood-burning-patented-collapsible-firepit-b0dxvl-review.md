**The Slinky Stove is the fire pit you buy when you've got a tiny patio, a hatchback trunk, or zero interest in wrestling a 40-pound steel bowl out of storage every time you want a fire.**

Here's the honest pitch: most smokeless fire pits are heavy, bulky, and expensive. This one collapses into a flat stack of stainless steel rings that looks like an oversized slinky. That's the whole gimmick, and it works. But the portability comes with real tradeoffs you need to know before you click buy.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the collapsible stainless steel body (roughly 18 inches in diameter when assembled), a solid steel base plate, and a carry handle. That's it. No spark screen, no poker, no storage bag. The burn chamber stands about 12 inches tall when fully stacked.

Assembly is genuinely tool-free: you stack the rings, drop in the base, and you're burning in under two minutes. Disassembly is just as quick — the rings nest flat and the whole thing slips under a car seat.

**Real-world burn performance**

I tested this with seasoned oak and kiln-dried firewood over three separate burns. The smokeless claim is legit — the double-wall design pulls air through the inner vents and reignites smoke particles before they escape. You'll still see a wisp of smoke during startup and when you add fresh logs, but once the fire settles, it burns remarkably clean. One reviewer put it plainly: "I was skeptical, but the smoke reduction is real. My wife sat downwind and didn't complain once."

Heat output is decent for the size — you'll feel warmth within three feet, but this isn't a primary heat source for a cold night. It's a vibe fire, not a furnace.

**Where it falls short**

The 18-inch chamber is the limiting factor. Standard 16-inch firewood logs need to be broken down to fit, and even then, you're adding wood every 20-30 minutes to keep a solid flame. That's fine for a relaxed evening, but annoying if you're trying to keep a fire going through a long conversation.

The lack of a carry bag is a genuine oversight. The steel gets hot during use, and you can't pack it up until it fully cools — which takes about an hour after the last flame dies. I ended up buying a $15 canvas bag separately.

Build quality is solid for the price point — the stainless steel felt substantial and showed no warping after multiple burns. But a few reviewers reported the base plate denting if dropped on hard surfaces, so treat it with care.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers with balconies** — this stores flat against a wall and won't trip a weight restriction
- **Campers and tailgaters** — packs smaller than a camp chair and sets up in minutes
- **Anyone who hates storing bulky fire pits** — this is the only smokeless option I've seen that truly disappears when not in use

**Skip it if** you want a permanent backyard centerpiece, need to burn full-size logs without cutting them, or expect it to survive years of heavy weekly use without showing wear.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can you cook on this fire pit?**
A: Yes, with caveats. The flat base accommodates a cast iron skillet or small grate, but the 12-inch height means you'll be crouching or sitting low. It's better for roasting marshmallows or hot dogs than serious camp cooking.

**Q: Does it really produce no smoke?**
A: No fire pit is truly smokeless. This reduces visible smoke by about 70% compared to an open fire. You'll still smell like campfire afterward, but you won't be coughing and wiping your eyes.

**Q: How long does it take to cool down enough to pack up?**
A: Plan on 45-60 minutes after the last flame. The steel retains heat well — which is great for warmth, but means you can't just dump it in your trunk after burning.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you value portability and storage convenience above all else and don't mind feeding the fire regularly. It's the smartest-designed compact smokeless fire pit I've tested at this price.

**Skip it if** you have the space and budget for a larger, more permanent fire pit — those offer bigger flames, longer burn times, and better build resilience.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Great engineering, honest smoke reduction, and unbeatable portability. Just budget for a carry bag and plan for frequent log-loading.
