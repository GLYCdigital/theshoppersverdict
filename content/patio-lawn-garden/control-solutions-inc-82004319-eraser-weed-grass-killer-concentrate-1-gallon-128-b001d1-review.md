## What's Actually in the Jug

You get one gallon of amber concentrate in a sturdy jug with a child-resistant cap that doubles as a measuring cup. The active ingredient is glyphosate at 53.8% — the same workhorse chemistry found in Roundup, but at a significantly higher concentration than most retail formulations. This is professional-grade stuff, the kind landscapers buy in bulk.

As shown in the product image, the jug is plain and utilitarian. No fancy nozzle, no sprayer included. You're expected to mix this yourself, and that's the point — it's a concentrate, not a ready-to-use product. One gallon mixes to 21 gallons of spray solution at the standard 5.5 ounces per gallon rate. That's roughly 21,000 square feet of coverage, depending on how heavy you apply.

## How It Performs in the Real World

Glyphosate is systemic, meaning it gets absorbed through the leaves and travels down to the root system. That's why Eraser kills the whole plant rather than just browning the tops. Most buyers report visible wilting within 5-7 days, with complete kill in 2-4 weeks. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Took about two weeks to fully see results, but everything it touched died. Completely. Including the roots."*

The 2-hour rainfast claim holds up in practice. That's a genuine advantage over some competitors that wash away in 30 minutes if a sprinkle hits. For fence lines, driveways, patios, and along sidewalks, this is reliable.

**The catch:** You need a surfactant. Waxy-leaf weeds like poison ivy and mature broadleafs will bead the spray right off without it. A few drops of dish soap per gallon works, or buy a proper non-ionic surfactant. This is a hidden requirement that the label mentions but doesn't emphasize enough. Skip it, and you'll wonder why some weeds laugh at your application.

## Where It Falls Short

Patience is required. This is not a "spray and watch it die tomorrow" product. Full kill takes weeks, and some users mistake the slow start for failure. If you need instant gratification, look elsewhere.

The non-selective nature is a double-edged sword. One gust of wind, one careless spray near a flower bed, and you've got dead perennials. Several reviews mention this as a cautionary tale, not a product flaw, but it's worth repeating: this stuff does not discriminate.

There's also the surfactant issue I mentioned. Factor in an extra $10-15 for a proper surfactant if you're tackling waxy-leaf species, or the product underperforms.

## Who Should Buy This

**Skip it if:** You need quick cosmetic results for a one-time touch-up, you're strictly organic, or you have a small yard where a quart of ready-to-use spray makes more sense than a gallon of concentrate sitting in your garage.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**How long after spraying can I plant new grass or flowers?**
The label specifies waiting 7 days before seeding or planting in treated areas. The glyphosate deactivates on soil contact, but give it the full week for safety.

**Is it safe around pets and kids?**
Once dry, it's considered safe. The 2-hour rainfast window aligns with the drying time. Keep pets and children off treated areas until the spray has fully dried.

**Will one application kill everything for good?**
Perennial weeds with deep roots may need a second application after 2-3 weeks. Annual weeds typically die with one dose. Seeds in the soil will still germinate — this kills growing plants, not future ones.
