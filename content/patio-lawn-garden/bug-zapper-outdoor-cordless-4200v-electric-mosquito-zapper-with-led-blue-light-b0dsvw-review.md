## What you're actually getting

The unit is compact — roughly the size of a large lantern. You get the zapper itself, a USB charging cable, and a hanging loop plus a flat base for tabletop placement. As shown in the product image, the double-layer grid surrounds a central LED blue light, which does the heavy lifting of attracting insects. The 4200V claim is real: the grid delivers a sharp, audible snap when a mosquito hits it. No, it won't clear your entire acre, but within a 10–15 foot radius, it noticeably cuts down the swarm around your seating area.

The rechargeable battery is the headline feature. No extension cords snaking across the lawn, no hunting for an outdoor outlet. A full charge gives you roughly 8–10 hours of run time — enough for a typical evening barbecue or camping trip. The USB charging is convenient, though it takes about 4 hours to top up.

## Where it falls short

Honesty time. This is not a weatherproof device. Rain, heavy dew, or sprinkler overspray can damage the electronics, and several reviewers report exactly that. You must bring it inside when not in use or when storms approach. That's a genuine inconvenience for a product marketed for outdoor use.

Battery life is adequate but not exceptional. If you're hosting a long evening gathering, the 8–10 hour runtime covers most situations, but heavy use means daily charging. The charging port is also uncovered — a minor design flaw that accelerates wear if you're careless about moisture.

Finally, the catch tray is small. You'll need to empty it every few days during peak mosquito season. It's a minor chore, but worth knowing.
