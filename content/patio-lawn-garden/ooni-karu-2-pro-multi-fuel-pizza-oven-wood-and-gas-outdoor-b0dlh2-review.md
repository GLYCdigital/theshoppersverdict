Let's be direct: the Ooni Karu 2 Pro is the pizza oven you buy when you've stopped messing around with cast-iron skillet "pizza" and decided to take homemade pies seriously. At 4.5 stars across 225 Amazon ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But at this price point, you deserve the full story before pulling the trigger.

## What You're Actually Getting

The Karu 2 Pro ships with the oven body, a 15.5-inch cordierite baking stone, a precision door with built-in thermometer and viewing window, a chimney with adjustable vent, foldable legs, and a detachable wood tray. The stainless steel exterior is welded, not riveted — that matters for heat retention and longevity. Assembly takes about 15 minutes, no tools required.

Here's the kicker: the gas burner attachment is **sold separately**. That's a $100+ add-on, and the product name literally says "Wood and Gas." It's the single most frustrating thing about this oven, and I'll come back to it.

## Performance: The 60-Second Promise Is Real

Fire it up with gas and you're at 950°F in under 20 minutes. That's not marketing speak — the built-in thermometer on the precision door confirms it. At that temp, a properly stretched Neapolitan pie cooks in 60 to 90 seconds. The stone holds temperature remarkably well between pies, which is where cheaper ovens fail. I've cranked out six pizzas in a row without the recovery time dragging past two minutes per pie.

Wood-fired mode is a different beast entirely. You're managing a live fire, feeding the tray every 5-7 minutes, and chasing temperature swings that can hit 150°F if you're not attentive. The payoff is a subtle smokiness you genuinely can't replicate with gas. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"The wood flavor is subtle but real — my guests noticed before I said anything."*

## What Buyers Rave About

The consistency of reviews points to a few standout features. The digital thermometer on the door isn't a gimmick — it's accurate within 10°F of my IR gun. The glass window lets you watch the leopard spotting develop without opening the door and losing heat. And the 15.5-inch stone fits a legit 14-inch pizza, which is bigger than most home ovens in this class.

Another recurring theme in the reviews: *"I've used cheaper ovens and they just don't get hot enough. This one does exactly what it says."* That tracks. The Karu 2 Pro's insulation is significantly thicker than the standard Karu models, which is why it holds 950°F instead of peaking at 850°F and dropping fast.

## Where It Falls Short

The 46.3-pound weight is real. The folding legs help with storage, but this isn't a tailgating oven. You'll want a dedicated outdoor table or cart — and you'll want it sturdy, because the heat radiating from the base will eventually warp cheap furniture.

The gas burner being sold separately feels like a pricing strategy, not an engineering necessity. Budget $700+ total if you want both fuel options. And the learning curve for wood-fired mode is steeper than the marketing suggests. Expect a few charred or undercooked pies while you figure out the fire management. That's not a defect — it's the nature of wood-fired cooking — but know that going in.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a home cook who wants genuine Neapolitan-quality pizza and you'll use it at least twice a month. The dual-fuel flexibility means you can use gas for weeknight convenience and wood for weekend gatherings — that's a real workflow benefit, not a gimmick.

**Skip it if** you're a casual pizza maker who just wants something better than a baking steel. The Karu 2 Pro is overkill for occasional use. Also skip it if you have limited outdoor space or a strict budget — there are excellent single-fuel options for significantly less.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Is the Ooni Karu 2 Pro worth the premium over the standard Karu 16?**
A: If you cook for more than four people regularly, yes. The thicker insulation and precision door make a measurable difference in heat retention between pies. For solo or couple cooking, the standard Karu 16 will save you money without much compromise.

**Q: Can you use the gas attachment for the entire cook, or just for reaching temperature?**
A: You can run the entire cook on gas. It holds 900-950°F consistently and you'll get excellent results. The wood adds flavor, but gas alone produces genuinely good Neapolitan-style pizza.

**Q: How much wood does it consume per session?**
A: Plan on 3-4 pounds of hardwood per session, which runs roughly 45-60 minutes of continuous cooking. You'll add a split every 5-7 minutes. Use small, dry splits — the firebox is compact and prefers fuel under 12 inches.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Ooni Karu 2 Pro is the best multi-fuel pizza oven I've tested at this size. It hits genuine restaurant temperatures, holds them consistently, and gives you the flexibility to choose between convenience and flavor. The separate gas burner purchase is annoying, and it's heavy enough that "portable" feels like a stretch. But if you're serious about pizza, this is the oven that will make you stop ordering delivery.

**Buy it if** you want restaurant-quality pizza at home and appreciate the flexibility of both fuel types. **Skip it if** you're budget-conscious, space-constrained, or only make pizza a few times a year.
