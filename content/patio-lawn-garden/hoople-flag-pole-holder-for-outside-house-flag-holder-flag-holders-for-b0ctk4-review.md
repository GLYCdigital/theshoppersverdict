The wall plate measures roughly 5 inches tall and 3 inches wide. It’s designed to mount flush against siding, brick, or wood trim. The cradle opening accepts poles between 3/4-inch and 1 inch in diameter — the standard range for residential aluminum and fiberglass flag poles.

**Where It Falls Short**

No product is perfect, and the HOOPLE has two honest limitations.

First, there is no locking mechanism. The flag pole sits in the open cradle and can be lifted straight out. In a front yard where you can’t watch the street, that’s a theft or vandalism risk. If you fly a quality pole, consider a locking holder instead.

Second, the included mounting screws are zinc-plated, not stainless steel. In coastal areas with salt air, they will corrode faster than the bracket itself. Upgrading to stainless steel lag bolts costs about $4 at any hardware store — do it before installation, not after.
