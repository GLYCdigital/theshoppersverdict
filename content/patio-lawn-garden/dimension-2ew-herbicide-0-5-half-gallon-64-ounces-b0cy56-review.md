If you've battled crabgrass every August, you know the frustration of a lawn that looks fine in May and turns into a straw-colored mess by Labor Day. Dimension 2EW is the chemical fix that lawn care pros have used for years, now available in a homeowner-friendly 64-ounce jug. The question isn't whether it works — it's whether you can apply it correctly. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You're Actually Getting

The 0.5 half-gallon jug contains 2EW formulation — that's 2 pounds of active ingredient (dithiopyr) per gallon, emulsified in water. You get a measuring cap on top and a product that mixes readily with water in any pump sprayer. At standard rates, this jug treats roughly 42,000 square feet, which covers the average suburban lot for a full season. That's about three times the coverage you'd get from a comparable granular product at the same price point.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The active ingredient, dithiopyr, is what sets this apart from cheaper pre-emergents. Most products like Prodiamine only stop weeds before they sprout. Dimension 2EW does that **plus** kills crabgrass for up to three weeks after emergence. That window matters — it forgives slightly late applications, which is why it's the standard for professional lawn crews.

You'll see results in two phases. Applied correctly in early spring, it forms a barrier that stops crabgrass, foxtail, barnyardgrass, and chickweed as they germinate. Buyers consistently report clean lawns through summer. One reviewer noted, "Applied in March, zero crabgrass by August. My neighbor's lawn looked like a hay field." Another mentioned it handled a stubborn goosegrass infestation that granular products missed.

## What Buyers Praise

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews holds up when you read the specifics. Homeowners repeatedly mention three things: the coverage value, the forgiveness window, and the lack of staining on concrete or sidewalks — a real issue with some liquid herbicides. Several reviewers with Bermuda and fescue lawns confirmed it's safe on established turf when mixed at labeled rates.

## Where It Falls Short

This is a **pre-emergent**, not a cure-all. If you already have mature crabgrass, this won't touch it — you'd need a post-emergent like quinclorac. The timing demands are real: soil temperatures need to hit 55°F for three consecutive days before application. Apply too early and the barrier degrades before germination; too late and you've wasted the product.

The other catch is watering. This needs irrigation within 14 days of application to activate. Skip that and you've sprayed money onto dry dirt. One frustrated buyer wrote, "Applied it, forgot to water, and got crabgrass anyway. My fault, but the instructions could be clearer." That's fair — the label does state it, but it's easy to miss in the fine print.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY lawn enthusiasts** who've used granular pre-emergents and want more precise, even coverage
- **Overseed-heavy yards** where you need season-long control without repeat applications
- **Anyone with a sprayer** — if you don't own one, factor in that cost; a basic 2-gallon pump sprayer runs about $25

Skip it if you're looking for post-emergent weed control or if you can't commit to the watering requirement. For those cases, look at quinclorac-based products instead.

## FAQ

**Q: When exactly should I apply this?**
A: When soil temps hit 55°F for three consecutive days — typically 2-3 weeks before your average last frost date. Check your local extension office for soil temperature data.

**Q: Can I overseed after applying?**
A: You'll need to wait 8-12 weeks, depending on grass type. Check the label for your specific turf — the waiting period varies between cool-season and warm-season grasses.

**Q: How long does one jug last?**
A: At standard rates, 42,000 square feet. For a typical 10,000-square-foot lawn, that's four seasons of applications.
