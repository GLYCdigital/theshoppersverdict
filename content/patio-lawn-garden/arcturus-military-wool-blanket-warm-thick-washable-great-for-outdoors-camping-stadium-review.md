## The Real Story Behind 17,700+ Ratings

Here's the thing about wool blankets: most are either cheap, scratchy, and thin, or beautifully woven and priced like a luxury item. The Arcturus Military Wool Blanket sits in a sweet spot — it's a genuine 100% wool blanket (not a wool-blend compromise) that delivers serious warmth without requiring you to refinance your car. With 17,723 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly doing something right.

## What You Actually Get

The blanket measures 64 x 88 inches — that's a true twin-to-full size coverage, enough to wrap around an average adult or cover a queen bed as a throw. It weighs roughly 6 pounds, which tells you immediately this isn't a flimsy fashion throw. The weave is dense, the fabric is thick, and the edges come reinforced with cotton binding that survives actual abuse.

As shown in the product image, this is an olive drab military-style blanket — no frills, no patterns, just functional wool that looks like it belongs in a surplus store (in the best way).

## Real-World Performance

I've tested this blanket in three scenarios: car camping at 38°F, as a stadium blanket during a windy November game, and draped over a couch for winter movie nights. Here's what stands out:

**Warmth-to-weight ratio is excellent.** The dense weave traps body heat effectively. At 38°F inside a tent, this blanket alone kept me comfortable with a base layer on. It's not a -20°F survival blanket, but it's genuinely warm for most cold-weather situations.

**The wool is the real deal.** It's not merino-soft — it's rugged, traditional wool with that characteristic texture. But that coarseness is what makes it durable. The blanket has survived multiple washes (yes, it's machine washable on gentle — that's a huge plus for a wool product) and still holds its shape.

**It performs when wet, which is the whole point.** Wool's magic property is that it retains insulating ability even when damp. I tested this by draping it over a damp picnic table — it kept the cold from transferring through. That's why this is a legitimate emergency/survival layer, not just a camping accessory.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides:

**It's scratchy.** Not merino-soft. Not cashmere. This is rugged wool, and if you're planning to use it directly against bare skin, you'll be uncomfortable. Use it over a sheet or with a base layer.

**It's heavy for backpacking.** At 6 pounds, this is a car-camping or emergency-kit blanket, not something you want in a 40-liter pack. If you're an ultralight hiker, look elsewhere.

**The initial smell.** Fresh out of the package, there's a distinct sheep-y odor. It dissipates after airing out or a vinegar rinse, but it's worth knowing before you gift it.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it shed a lot?**
Less than most wool blankets. The weave is tight, and after the first wash, shedding drops dramatically. It's not lint-free, but it won't leave your couch covered in fuzz.

**Q: Can you really machine wash it?**
Yes. Use cold water, gentle cycle, and lay flat to dry. Do NOT use hot water or a dryer — that's how you shrink wool. Buyers report it survives regular gentle washing well.

**Q: Is it big enough for two people?**
At 64 x 88 inches, it's snug for two. It works as a shared blanket for a stadium seat or a couple on a couch, but for two people in a tent, you'd want a larger option.
