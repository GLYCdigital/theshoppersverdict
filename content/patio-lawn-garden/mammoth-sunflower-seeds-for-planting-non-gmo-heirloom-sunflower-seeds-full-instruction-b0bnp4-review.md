## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. **These are not low-maintenance plants.** At full height, the stalks are thick but hollow—a stiff breeze can snap them like celery. You'll need to stake them if your garden gets any wind exposure. That's not mentioned prominently in the listing.

Second, spacing is a real constraint. At 18-24 inches apart, a 10-foot row only fits 5-6 plants. If you're working with a standard 4x8 raised bed, you're looking at maybe a dozen plants max, and they'll shade out everything else nearby. This is a "give them their own corner" plant, not a companion crop.

Finally, the packet lacks a germination date stamp. Seeds lose viability over time, and without a date, you're trusting the seller's turnover. Given the strong germination reports, this hasn't been an issue for most buyers, but it's a transparency gap worth noting.

## FAQ

**Q: How long until they bloom?**
A: About 75-90 days from planting. Early summer planting (May-June) yields August blooms. They're annuals, so you'll replant each year.

**Q: Will the seeds from these flowers grow true next year?**
A: Yes, since it's an open-pollinated heirloom. Let the heads dry fully on the stalk, then harvest and store in a cool, dry place. Just keep them away from cross-pollination with other sunflower varieties if you want pure Mammoth offspring.

**Q: Do they really reach 12 feet?**
A: In ideal conditions—full sun, rich soil, consistent moisture—yes. In average garden soil with partial shade, expect 6-8 feet. The height claims are accurate but depend on your growing conditions.
