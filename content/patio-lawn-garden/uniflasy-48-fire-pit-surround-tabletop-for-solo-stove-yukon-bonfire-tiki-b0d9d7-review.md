If you own a Solo Stove Yukon, you already know the problem: the stove throws heat in a 360-degree circle, but you have nowhere to set down your drink without kneeling. The Uniflasy 48" surround tabletop solves that—mostly. Here's what 220 buyers and four weeks of hands-on testing taught me.

**What you actually get**

The box contains a 48-inch round steel tabletop in two half-moon pieces, four foldable X-legs, a crossbar stabilizer, and the mounting hardware. The pieces are heavier than they look—each half weighs about 14 pounds—so this is a permanent fixture, not a carry-along accessory. Assembly is genuinely simple: lay both halves flat, bolt them together, flip it over, and screw on the legs. The included Allen wrench does the job, and you'll be done in under ten minutes. The center cutout measures 27 inches, sized specifically for the Yukon's body, leaving a roughly 1.5-inch gap around the stove for heat to escape.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The 1.5mm powder-coated steel is the right thickness for this job. It doesn't flex when you lean on it, and it doesn't warp after a few hours of sustained burn. I ran mine for three consecutive evenings, and the surface stayed flat. The powder coat has held up without bubbling or peeling, which is the failure point on cheaper surround tables.

The legs fold inward for storage, but here's the honest truth: you're not going to fold this up after every use. It's 48 inches across and weighs around 30 pounds assembled. Once it's out, it's out. The X-legs are sturdy enough on flat ground, but there's no rubber on the feet. On a wood deck, a firm bump will slide it a few inches. On concrete or pavers, it's rock solid.

The heat question is the one everyone asks. The outer 12 inches of the table stays cool enough to rest your forearm on during a normal burn. The inner 6 inches near the stove gets noticeably hot—not enough to melt a plastic cup, but enough that you won't want your skin there for more than a second. Keep drinks on the outer ring, and you're fine.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.4-star average holds up across 220 ratings. The praise consistently centers on fit and finish. One verified buyer wrote, *"Fits the Yukon like it was made for it. No gaps, no wobble, and the height is perfect for standard chairs."* Another noted, *"I was worried about the heat warping it, but three months in and it still looks brand new."* The most common compliment is the height—at 15.5 inches, it lines up with standard Adirondack and camp chairs, which is the difference between a comfortable reach and a stretch.

**Where it falls short**

The finish is the weak point. Matte black shows every ash speck and water ring, and it's not a wipe-and-forget surface. You'll be cleaning it regularly if you want it looking sharp. The lack of rubber feet on the legs is a minor but real oversight—one buyer mentioned their surround slid on a covered porch when someone leaned on it. And if you have the Yukon's heat deflector accessory, you'll need to check the clearance: the deflector adds height, and you may need to adjust the stove's position to keep the tabletop flush.

**Who should buy this**

Get this if you own a Yukon and want a proper social fire pit experience—a flat, stable surface for drinks, plates, and phones without buying a custom $400 patio table. It's also a strong pick if you've been using a folding side table and are tired of it tipping. Skip it if you have a smaller Solo Stove (Bonfire or Ranger)—the 27-inch cutout won't fit, and you'll need the smaller version. Also skip it if you want a portable setup; this is a semi-permanent piece.

**FAQ**

**Will it fit the Solo Stove Yukon with the stand?**
Yes. The center cutout is 27 inches, and the Yukon's body diameter is 27 inches. The surround sits around the stove's base, not under it. The stove's legs remain on the ground.

**Can I leave it outside year-round?**
The powder coating is weather-resistant, but not weatherproof. Cover it during heavy rain or snow; standing water on the surface will eventually find micro-scratches and start rust.

**Does the table get hot enough to melt plastic cups?**
The outer half stays cool. The inner 6-inch ring near the stove heats up but won't melt a plastic cup sitting on it. Keep skin away from that inner ring during active burns.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You own a Yukon and want a sturdy, well-fitting surround that turns your fire pit into a social hub without spending patio-furniture money.

**Skip it if:** You need portability, have a smaller Solo Stove model, or want a zero-maintenance outdoor table.
