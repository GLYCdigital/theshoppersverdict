**If you're serious about boiling crawfish at home — like, 30 pounds at a time serious — the Creole Feast CFB2001+CFB1001A is the setup that separates a backyard experiment from a proper boil.** This is a single-sack system built around a 60-quart stainless pot and a 100,000 BTU jet burner. It's not the cheapest option on Amazon, but after digging through 229 ratings and owner reports, it's clear why it holds a 4.6-star average.

## What's actually in the box

You get the 60-quart stainless steel pot (that's roughly 15 gallons), the matching lid, a single perforated basket rated for a 35-pound sack of live crawfish, a propane jet burner stand, and a 24-inch regulator hose. The pot measures about 18 inches in diameter and 15 inches deep — enough clearance that a full sack has room to circulate in the boil instead of sitting in a cramped pile. The burner frame is welded steel with three support arms that hold the pot steady; it folds flat for storage, which matters if your garage is already a disaster zone.

## Performance: the part that matters

The headline number here is the 100,000 BTU jet burner. For context, most patio turkey fryers put out 50,000 to 60,000 BTUs. Owners consistently report bringing 60 quarts of water to a full rolling boil in 15 to 20 minutes. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Boils faster than my stove ever could — we did 40 pounds in three batches and never waited more than 18 minutes between boils."* That's the difference between a long afternoon and a productive boil session.

The pot itself is 1.5mm stainless steel — a meaningful upgrade over the flimsy 1mm pots you'll find on cheaper kits. It distributes heat evenly across the bottom, so you don't get a hot spot that scorches the seasoning or burns the bottom layer of crawfish. The basket is welded, not spot-welded, and the crossbar gives you a solid grip point to lift a full sack without the handles bending under load.

## What buyers consistently praise

The unanimous theme across reviews is **speed and capacity**. Multiple owners mention hosting boils for 15 to 25 people and never feeling bottlenecked by the equipment. The basket design gets specific credit: *"The single sack basket is perfect — lift once, dump, reload. No scooping around with a strainer."* The burner's wind resistance is also mentioned repeatedly; the jet design holds a steady flame even on breezy patios, which isn't true of cheaper disc burners.

## Where it falls short

Two honest downsides. First, the regulator hose is only 24 inches. That's tight if your propane tank sits on the ground next to the burner. You'll likely want a longer hose (a 4-foot LP hose runs about $15) or a tank riser to keep the hose from kinking. Second, there's no thermometer or drain valve. You'll need a clip-on fry thermometer to monitor the boil, and you'll have to tilt the pot to dump the water — which is fine when it's just one pot, but a drain valve would make cleanup easier. Also note: this is a single-sack system. If you regularly boil more than 35 pounds at a time, the double-sack version is worth the upgrade.

## FAQ: What shoppers actually ask

**Q: Can I use this on a standard 20-pound propane tank?**
A: Yes — the burner connects to a standard LP tank with the included regulator. Just be aware of the short hose and position the tank accordingly.

**Q: How long does it take to boil 35 pounds of crawfish?**
A: Plan for 15–20 minutes to bring the water to a rolling boil, then 3–5 minutes for the crawfish to cook after you drop the basket and the boil returns. A full batch, from start to dump, runs about 25–30 minutes.

**Q: Is the pot dishwasher safe?**
A: The pot is stainless steel, so technically yes, but it won't fit in most residential dishwashers. Hand wash with soap and water — the basket and pot clean up fine without heavy scrubbing.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you host regular boils for 10+ people and want equipment that won't leave you waiting on water to boil or wrestling a flimsy basket. This is a buy-once piece of gear.

**Skip it if** you only boil a few pounds occasionally — a smaller 30-quart pot and a cheaper burner will save you money and take up less storage space. Also skip if you need a drain valve or built-in thermometer; this is a no-frills, high-performance workhorse, not a gadget-filled unit.
