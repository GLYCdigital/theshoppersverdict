If you've ever tripped over a coiled hose that mysteriously unraveled overnight, you already know the pain this product solves. The HOMIJIABRO Garden Water Hose Holder is a wall-mounted double-arm rack that claims to hold two 150-foot hoses simultaneously. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but let's talk about whether it's right for *your* setup.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the welded steel frame, four lag bolts with plastic wall anchors, and a small hex wrench for assembly. There's no reel mechanism here — this is a passive storage rack, not a winding system. You manually coil each hose over the two U-shaped arms, which are spaced wide enough that two 150-foot hoses can sit side-by-side without fighting for space. The arms are capped with rounded ends, so you're not snagging the hose material when you pull it free.

As shown in the product image, the design is straightforward: two horizontal arms extending from a wall plate, with a lower hook on each side for holding spray nozzles or quick-connect fittings. The powder-coated finish is matte black, which blends into most exterior walls without looking like an eyesore.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The steel gauge feels substantial — this isn't a flimsy wire rack that'll bend under a waterlogged hose. A full 150-foot hose weighs roughly 10–15 pounds depending on material, and two of them will put 25–30 pounds of load on this thing. The welded joints and four-bolt mounting plate handle that weight without flexing.

Installation is straightforward if you're mounting to wood siding or studs. The included anchors work fine for drywall or softwood, but several reviewers noted they upgraded to heavier toggle bolts for brick or stucco surfaces. One reviewer put it plainly: *"The rack is solid, but the screws it comes with are the weakest link. I swapped them for masonry anchors and it's been rock solid for six months."*

The fixed-arm design means you lose about 10 inches of clearance from the wall. If your hose bib sits close to a door frame or tight corner, measure before you drill. There's also no locking mechanism to keep hoses from sliding off — if you have kids who like to yank hoses, you'll want to coil them with the ends tucked under.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The most repeated compliment is capacity. Reviewers with large yards specifically call out being able to store both their main garden hose and a separate drip-irrigation line without resorting to a second rack. The phrase "finally organized" shows up more than a few times in the feedback.

The finish also earns credit. Multiple long-term owners report no rust or chipping after a full year of outdoor exposure, which is notable for a product at this price point. The hose-end hooks are a smaller touch, but they genuinely help keep connectors clean and off the dirt.

## Where It Falls Short

The mounting hardware is the clear weak point. For a product designed to hold 30 pounds of hose, the included plastic anchors feel cheap. If you're mounting to masonry or stucco, budget for better anchors.

The arms don't fold, which makes this awkward to pack away for winter in colder climates. You're either leaving it exposed to freeze-thaw cycles or unscrewing the whole plate each season. Also, if you have a single 100-foot hose, this rack is overkill — a single-arm or freestanding reel would take up less visual space.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have two long hoses serving different zones (front yard/backyard, or main line plus drip system) and want them off the ground and untangled. It's ideal for homeowners with wood or vinyl siding who want a permanent, no-moving-parts storage solution.

**Skip it if:** You're renting and can't drill into the wall, you have only one hose, or you want the convenience of a spring-loaded reel that winds automatically. This is storage only — it won't make coiling faster, just tidier.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this really hold two 150-foot hoses?**
A: Yes, if they're standard 5/8-inch garden hoses. The arms are each roughly 16 inches long, which gives enough coil depth for two full-length hoses. Heavier-duty 3/4-inch hoses will fit but bulge more in the center.

**Q: Can I mount this to a vinyl fence?**
A: Not directly. The mounting plate needs solid backing. You'd need to screw a plywood or metal backing board to the fence first, then mount the holder to that. For wood fences, mount directly into the posts, not the pickets.

**Q: Does it come with everything needed for installation?**
A: Yes — the rack, four lag bolts, wall anchors, and a hex wrench are included. But as noted, the anchors are best suited for wood. For masonry, buy your own anchors rated for the wall material.
