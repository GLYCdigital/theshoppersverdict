Let me be blunt: most "waterproof" metal detectors are splash-proof toys. The Nokta PulseDive is not that. With a 200-foot depth rating and pulse induction technology, this is a real tool for people who actually get wet.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the PulseDive unit itself, a lanyard, a belt clip, and an owner's manual. That's it. No batteries included — it takes one 9V alkaline, which runs roughly 30 hours. The unit is 11.4 inches long and weighs just 10.2 ounces, so it won't weigh down your wrist during a long dive.

The 2-in-1 claim in the product title is accurate: it functions as both a handheld pinpointer for recovering targets you've already located, and as a short-range detector for scanning small areas. The search coil is at the tip, and the entire housing is sealed with O-rings rated to IP68 standards.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this in both a saltwater bay and a freshwater river. The difference was night and day compared to a standard VLF pinpointer I'd used previously. The PulseDive's PI technology ignores salt entirely — no false signals, no screaming in black sand. In the bay, it picked up a corroded fishing weight buried six inches under packed sand without flinching.

The vibration motor is strong enough to feel through neoprene dive gloves, and the LED light on the tip actually helps in murky water. You can switch between vibration-only, tone-only, or both, which is a thoughtful touch for divers wearing full hoods where hearing is compromised.

For shallow-water wading, it's equally capable. The auto-tune feature means you just turn it on and sweep — no knob fiddling, no ground balancing. That simplicity is a double-edged sword, though. In freshwater lakes with heavy mineralization, the auto-tune occasionally chatters. It's not a dealbreaker, but it requires you to lift the unit away from the bottom and re-sweep to reset.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 220 Amazon ratings at 4.4 stars, three themes dominate. "This pinpointer is a beast," one verified buyer wrote. "Took it to 30 feet in the Gulf and it found a ring in the sand in under two minutes."

## Where It Falls Short

The battery cap is the weakest design element. It requires a coin or flathead screwdriver to open — not ideal when you're standing in ankle-deep water with wet hands. Several reviewers mention this exact frustration, and I'll echo it. Pack a coin in your wading pouch or you'll be making an awkward trip back to shore.

The lack of sensitivity adjustment is the other limitation. Advanced users who like to fine-tune their discrimination will find the auto-tune too aggressive in certain freshwater environments. You can't dial it down; you can only work around it.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a scuba diver or freediver who wants to recover targets without hauling a full-size detector. You hunt saltwater beaches and are tired of VLF units falsing on every grain of black sand. You want one device that does both pinpointing and small-area scanning.

**Skip it if:** You only hunt dry land (you'll overpay for waterproofing you don't need). You expect adjustable sensitivity like higher-end full detectors. You're a casual user who'd be better served by a $60 basic pinpointer for occasional backyard finds.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this for actual scuba diving?**
A: Yes. The 200-foot depth rating exceeds recreational diving limits (typically 130 feet). The buttons and vibration work under pressure, and the LED remains visible in clear water.

**Q: Does it discriminate between metals?**
A: No. PI technology detects all metals — it's designed for target recovery, not coin-and-jewelry discrimination. If you need to ignore iron, you need a different tool.

**Q: How long does the 9V battery really last?**
A: Expect 25-30 hours of active use. It drops faster if you rely heavily on the LED, so budget accordingly on extended dive trips.
