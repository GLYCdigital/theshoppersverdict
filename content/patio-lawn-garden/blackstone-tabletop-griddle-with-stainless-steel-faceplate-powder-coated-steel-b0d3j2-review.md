The Blackstone Tabletop Griddle with Stainless Steel Faceplate is the portable flattop that keeps showing up in campgrounds, tailgate lots, and apartment balconies for one reason: it just works. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, this 17-inch version hits the sweet spot between the tiny 10-inch single burners and the full-size 22-inch backyard units. But is it the right griddle for your specific setup? Let's break it down.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box, you're looking at a 17-inch rolled steel cooking surface sitting on a powder-coated steel frame with that upgraded stainless steel faceplate. The unit measures roughly 21.5 x 18.5 x 12.5 inches when the legs are folded, and it tips the scales at just over 17 pounds — manageable for one hand, though you'll want two when it's full of food.

The setup includes the griddle top, detachable grease cup, and folding legs that lock into place. The faceplate is the headline upgrade here. Older Blackstone tabletop models used painted steel that flaked after repeated heat cycles. The stainless version resists that corrosion and looks sharper sitting out on a patio table.

## Real-World Performance

The 17-inch surface gives you roughly 260 square inches of cooking area. That's enough for four burger patties, six strips of bacon, or about eight pancakes at once — functional for a family of four, tight for entertaining.

The dual burners are the star feature. Each side heats independently, so you can run one zone low for slow-cooked onions while the other rips hot for a proper sear. The 12,000 total BTUs bring the surface to temperature in about eight minutes, which is respectable for a portable unit. Reviewers consistently note the heat distribution is better than expected for this size — one buyer put it simply: "No hot spots. Everything cooked evenly, which surprised me for a tabletop unit."

The cold-rolled steel surface needs the standard Blackstone seasoning routine before first use. Skip this and you'll be scraping stuck eggs for weeks. Do it right — thin oil coats, high heat, repeat — and you'll get that naturally non-stick surface that makes this griddle worth owning.

## What Buyers Praise

The grease management system gets consistent shoutouts. The rear-mounted drip cup catches runoff cleanly, and the faceplate design makes cleanup less messy than older models. One verified buyer noted, "The stainless front makes wiping down after breakfast take 30 seconds instead of 10 minutes."

Portability is another recurring theme. The folding legs and 17-pound weight make it genuinely travel-friendly. Multiple reviewers mention using it for camping trips and tailgates, pairing it with the standard 1-pound propane canisters (not included).

## Where It Falls Short

The regulator is the weak point. Several buyers report flame sputtering in breezy conditions. One reviewer wrote, "Perfect on calm days, but the wind shuts it down quick. You'll need a windscreen if you're cooking outdoors regularly." This isn't a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you plan an exposed beach cookout.

The grease cup is also smaller than you'd expect. Cooking a full pound of bacon fills it quickly, and there's no tray door to swap it mid-cook — you're reaching behind the griddle while it's hot. Not ideal.

Legs are fixed-height with no adjustment. On uneven patio bricks, you'll need a shim or a level table. Minor, but it's the kind of thing that shows up in long-term reviews.

## Who Should Buy This

This griddle fits three buyer profiles:

- **The apartment griller** with balcony restrictions on open flames — a propane griddle is often allowed where charcoal isn't
- **The weekend camper** who wants real cooking capability without hauling a full-size grill
- **The breakfast enthusiast** who wants diner-quality pancakes, eggs, and hash browns without the indoor mess

Skip it if you regularly cook for more than four people, need a grill lid for roasting, or want something you can leave outside year-round without a cover.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this indoors?**
A: No. It's propane-fueled and designed for outdoor use only. Carbon monoxide risk makes indoor use dangerous and dumb.

**Q: Does it work with standard 1-pound propane canisters?**
A: Yes, the regulator connects directly to disposable green tanks. You can also get a hose adapter for larger tanks, sold separately.

**Q: How long does it take to cool down after cooking?**
A: About 10-15 minutes to drop below 200°F. The legs fold up once cool enough to pack, but give it a full 20 minutes before storing in a case.

**Buy it if:** You want a portable, capable cooking surface for small groups and don't mind the initial seasoning work. The stainless faceplate upgrade makes this the best-value version of Blackstone's tabletop line.

**Skip it if:** You need wind resistance, cook for crowds regularly, or want a unit that can live outdoors unprotected.
