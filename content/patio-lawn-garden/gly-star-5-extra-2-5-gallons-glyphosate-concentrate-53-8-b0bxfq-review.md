If you're tired of paying triple for half-strength weed killer in a spray bottle, this 2.5-gallon jug of Gly Star 5 Extra is the grown-up choice. At 53.8% glyphosate — the same active ingredient as Roundup but at roughly double the concentration — this is the stuff farmers actually buy, repackaged for serious homeowners.

**What's actually in the jug**

You get one 2.5-gallon container of amber liquid concentrate. No sprayer, no measuring cup, no gloves. Just the chemical. That's fine — you're not paying for plastic junk you'll throw away. The label calls for mixing 2 to 3 ounces per gallon of water for most weeds, which means this single jug stretches to roughly 80–100 gallons of finished spray. For reference, that's enough to treat a quarter-acre of dense vegetation, or about 20,000 square feet of driveway cracks and fence lines.

**How it performs in the real world**

Glyphosate works by disrupting the shikimic acid pathway — essentially, it blocks the plant's ability to produce essential amino acids, and the plant starves from the inside out. What that means practically: you'll see yellowing within 3–5 days on most annual weeds, and complete browning in 7–14 days. Perennial monsters like bindweed, Canadian thistle, and poison ivy will need a second application after 3 weeks, because the first pass kills the top growth but the roots can regenerate.

One buyer put it bluntly: *"I sprayed a fence line full of poison ivy and blackberries. Two weeks later it was crispy brown. Nothing survived. This is the real deal, not the watered-down stuff from the big box store."*

That tracks with my research. The 53.8% concentration means you're applying more active ingredient per drop, which translates to faster visible results and better root kill — especially on tough perennial weeds that laugh at 41% homeowner formulas.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the headline. Multiple reviewers note the price per ounce of active ingredient is roughly half what you'd pay for Ortho or Roundup concentrate. One long-time user said: *"I've used this for 3 years on my 5-acre property. One jug lasts me two seasons. The per-acre cost is unbeatable."*

Coverage is another recurring theme — the 2.5-gallon format is the sweet spot between the tiny 32-ounce bottles and the bulky 5-gallon farm jugs that are awkward to pour and store.

**Where it falls short — be honest with yourself**

First, this is not a selective herbicide. One drift of spray onto your prized hydrangea and it's toast. Don't spray on windy days, and use a shield if you're anywhere near desirable plants.

Second, there's no surfactant in the mix. On waxy-leaved weeds like poison ivy or mature broadleafs, the spray beads up and rolls off. Add a non-ionic surfactant (about $10 for a bottle that lasts years) to your tank mix. Several reviewers mentioned this exact issue — *"Works great on grass and dandelions, but I had to add dish soap to get it to stick to the burdock."*

Third, the smell. It's pungent, chemical, and unmistakable. Wear a respirator if you're spraying for more than 15 minutes. This isn't a pleasant garden chore — it's a chemical application.

**Who should buy this**

This is for you if: you have a large property with established weed problems, you're maintaining a fence line, gravel driveway, or pasture edge, or you're a farmer/rancher who needs bulk herbicide at a sane price. It's also ideal for folks managing invasive species like kudzu or Japanese knotweed.

Skip it if: you have a small suburban yard with spot weeds — you'll never use this much, and the concentrate requires careful measuring and mixing that a pre-mixed spray bottle sidesteps. Also skip if you have pets that roam the yard constantly; glyphosate is safe once dried, but wet spray is hazardous.

**Three questions shoppers actually ask**

*Is this the same as Roundup?*

Chemically, yes — the active ingredient is glyphosate, same as Roundup. But Gly Star 5 Extra is 53.8% concentration versus Roundup's typical 41%. You use less product per gallon, and it's a generic, so you avoid the brand markup. It's not identical — Roundup has proprietary surfactants — but for most uses, it's functionally equivalent at a fraction of the cost.

*How do I mix it?*

For most weeds: 2–3 ounces per gallon of water in a pump sprayer. For tough perennials or dense brush, go to 4 ounces per gallon. Add a surfactant at 1 teaspoon per gallon. Spray to wet, not to runoff. Wear gloves, long sleeves, and eye protection. Mix only what you need — the concentrate stores fine, but mixed spray degrades within 24 hours.

*How long until I see results?*

Visible yellowing in 3–5 days on annuals. Complete browning in 7–14 days. Perennials like poison ivy or thistle may need a follow-up application at 3 weeks. Don't pull weeds until they're fully brown — you want the glyphosate to translocate to the roots first.

**The verdict**

Gly Star 5 Extra is a no-nonsense, farm-grade herbicide that delivers on its promises. It's more potent, more economical, and more effective than anything you'll find in the garden center aisle. The tradeoffs — no surfactant, strong odor, and the need for careful handling — are manageable for anyone who reads the label.

**Buy it if** you've got serious weed problems on serious acreage and want professional results without the professional price tag.

**Skip it if** you need one bottle for spot-treating a small lawn, or you're not comfortable handling concentrated chemicals.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the missing surfactant and the aggressive odor, but the performance and value are undeniable.
