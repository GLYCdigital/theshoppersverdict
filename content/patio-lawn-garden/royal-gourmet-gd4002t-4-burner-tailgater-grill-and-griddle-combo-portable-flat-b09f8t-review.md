**The Two-Burner Tailgate Workhorse That Actually Does Double Duty**

Most "combo" grills are a gimmick — a tiny griddle bolted awkwardly next to a cramped grate. The Royal Gourmet GD4002T isn't that. This 4-burner unit splits its 560 square inches of cooking surface into two distinct zones: a flat-top griddle on the left and a traditional grill grate on the right. It's the rare product that earns its dual-purpose claim, and at 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, owners generally agree.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the assembled head unit, two folding leg assemblies, a detachable side shelf, and the grill/griddle surfaces. Total cooking area is 560 square inches — roughly 280 per side. Burner output is 25,000 BTU total, which breaks down to roughly 6,250 BTU per burner. That's moderate heat, not searing-station power, but it's consistent and manageable.

The griddle is porcelain-coated steel, not cast iron or cold-rolled steel like premium units. That matters: it heats faster than cast iron but won't hold heat as well during a big cook. The grill side uses standard porcelain-coated wire grates. Both surfaces are removable for cleaning, though the griddle is heavy — you'll want two hands.

**Real-World Performance**

I've cooked on this unit across a full tailgate season. The piezo ignition is the standout feature: it fires on the first click, every time, even in breezy parking lots. That's rare at this price point. Heat distribution is decent but not flawless — the griddle runs hotter near the front burners, so you'll develop a mental map of hot zones for different foods.

The side-by-side layout is genuinely useful. You can run bacon and eggs on the flat top while burgers sear on the grate, all at once. Multiple Amazon reviewers echo this: "Cooked a full breakfast for 8 people in under 20 minutes. The griddle side is the star here."

**Where It Falls Short**

The lack of a temperature gauge on the griddle side is the biggest annoyance. You're guessing until you learn your burner positions — and that learning curve costs a few overcooked pancakes. The grease tray is also under-engineered for heavy use. During a 12-strip bacon cook, the front tray overflowed onto the drip pan below. It contained the mess, but cleanup was messier than it should be.

Assembly is the other sore spot. Count on 45-60 minutes, and use a light touch with the screws — several buyers report stripping the holes by over-tightening. One reviewer put it plainly: "Took me an hour and a half and I broke a cotter pin. Works great now, but I was frustrated during setup."

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the tailgater who cooks for a crowd and wants breakfast AND lunch options without hauling two appliances. It's also solid for apartment balconies or small patios where space is tight. Skip it if you're a grill purist who wants high-temp searing — 25,000 BTU won't give you steakhouse crust. And if you're cooking for two, the 60-pound weight and large footprint are overkill.

**FAQ**

*Does the griddle side get hot enough for a proper sear?*
Hot enough for home cooking, not for restaurant searing. You'll get good browning on burgers and chicken, but don't expect a crust that rivals a 600°F cast iron skillet.

*Can you use both sides simultaneously?*
Yes, that's the point. All four burners are independently controlled, so you can run the griddle at medium while the grill side cranks on high.

*Is it truly portable?*
Portable in the sense that it folds and fits in an SUV trunk. At 60 pounds, it's not something you carry far. It's tailgate-portable, not backpack-portable.

**The Verdict**

The Royal Gourmet GD4002T is a legitimately useful dual-zone cooker that earns its keep for group cooking. The reliable ignition, generous surface area, and true side-by-side functionality outweigh the missing temperature gauge and mediocre grease management.

**Buy it if** you tailgate or host group cooks and want one unit that does breakfast and dinner without compromise. **Skip it if** you're a serious grill enthusiast chasing high-heat searing, or if you value quick setup over cooking flexibility.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a solid workhorse with real versatility, held back by minor design shortcuts.
