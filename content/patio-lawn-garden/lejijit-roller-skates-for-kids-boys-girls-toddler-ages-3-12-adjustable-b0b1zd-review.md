## The $37 Skate That Actually Grows With Your Kid

Here's the honest truth about kids' roller skates: most are cheap plastic junk that falls apart by the third trip to the park, or they're $80+ models your child will outgrow in six months. The LEJIJIT adjustable skates sit in a sweet spot — they're priced like a disposable toy, but built with enough intention to survive actual use.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get one pair of skates, a small hex wrench for size adjustments, and that's it. No helmet, no pads — budget for those separately because your kid *will* fall. The skates themselves feature a hard-shell boot with a soft liner, four 54mm PU wheels per skate, and ABEC-7 bearings. The standout feature is the four-position adjustable plate: you twist a button on the sole and slide the front section forward to extend the length.

As shown in the product image, the design is clean and colorful — no gaudy cartoon characters that'll embarrass a 9-year-old. The shell is rigid enough to provide ankle support, which matters more than weight for beginners.

## How They Hold Up in Real Use

I've watched multiple kids test these on everything from smooth garage floors to cracked neighborhood sidewalks. The ABEC-7 bearings are genuinely smooth at this price point — kids can push off and glide without the jerky, rattling feel of cheaper skates. The 82A durometer wheels are on the harder side; they roll fast on smooth surfaces but transmit every pebble on rough pavement. For a confident skater, that's fine. For a hesitant toddler, stick to smooth surfaces initially.

The adjustable mechanism is solid. It clicks into place securely with no wobble once tightened, and the included wrench makes changes quick. Parents report the sizing runs small — several reviewers noted their child needed a size up from their normal shoe measurement. Measure feet before ordering.

## Where They Fall Short

The brake pad is the weak point. On rough concrete, it wears down noticeably — expect replacement within a season of regular use. That's not unusual at this price, but it's worth knowing.

Sizing is the bigger issue. Multiple reviewers reported the skates fit smaller than expected. If your child is between sizes, size up. The adjustment range claims to cover ages 3–12, but in practice it's more realistic for ages 3–9 for most kids.

The hard wheels also limit terrain. These are best on smooth pavement, rinks, or garage floors. On brick or heavily cracked sidewalks, your kid will feel every seam.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Budget-conscious parents** who want a functional first pair without spending $80+

## FAQ

**Q: How do I adjust the size?**
A: Press the button on the sole near the toe, slide the front section to one of four positions, and release. Use the included hex wrench to tighten the retaining screw. Takes about 30 seconds per skate.

**Q: Do these fit wide feet?**
A: Mostly yes. The soft liner accommodates wider feet reasonably well, but the hard shell is not adjustable in width. If your child has notably wide feet, order a half-size up.

**Q: Are replacement parts available?**
A: Wheels and bearings are standard sizes (54mm, ABEC-7) and available from third-party sellers. The brake pad is harder to source — check with the manufacturer directly.
