## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the limitations.

**The bottle doesn't last.** At the recommended biweekly feeding schedule for a large Bird of Paradise (which needs about 1 gallon of water per session), a 32-ounce bottle covers roughly two months. If you have multiple tropical plants, you'll be reordering every six weeks.

**No slow-release mechanism.** This is an immediate-feed liquid. If you're the type of plant parent who forgets to fertilize for three months, you'd be better served by a controlled-release granule. This product demands consistency to deliver results.

**Not formulated for flowering.** The 3-1-2 ratio prioritizes foliage. If you're trying to force blooms on a mature Bird of Paradise, you'll want a bloom-boosting fertilizer with higher phosphorus during the pre-flower phase.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should I apply this?**
Every two weeks during spring and summer. Cut back to monthly in fall, and stop entirely in winter when growth slows naturally.

**Q: Can I use this on other plants?**
Yes—any tropical foliage plant benefits. It's particularly effective on Monstera, Calathea, and Alocasia. Avoid using it on flowering plants like orchids or African violets.

**Q: Is it safe for pets?**
The fertilizer is a synthetic mineral blend, not organic. It's not toxic in small amounts, but keep pets from drinking the runoff water. Rinse the saucer after watering.
