## What You're Actually Getting

The bucket holds standard 3-inch trichlor tablets—the industry workhorse. Each tab weighs approximately 8 ounces and delivers 99% available chlorine. That's the same active ingredient as Clorox or In The Swim, so the chemistry checks out. What matters is the binder: SpaChoice uses a slow-dissolve formulation that, based on user reports, holds up for nearly a week in summer water temperatures.

The bucket itself is a detail worth mentioning. It's not a flimsy cardboard tub with a foil seal—it's a sturdy, resealable container that actually keeps moisture out. If you've ever had tabs turn to mush in a humid garage, you'll appreciate this.

## How It Performs in Real Use

Pool chemistry is about consistency, not shock and awe. The tablets dissolve slowly enough to maintain a stable chlorine residual of 1-3 ppm without daily fiddling. For a 15,000-gallon pool, a single tablet in a floater typically lasts 5 to 7 days, matching the manufacturer's claim.

One buyer noted, "I've used these for two summers now. They dissolve evenly without leaving that white sludge on the pool floor." That's a common pain point with cheaper tabs—uneven dissolution leaves calcium debris that clouds water. The SpaChoice tabs avoid this because the trichlor is compressed with a stabilizer that keeps the tablet intact as it erodes.

The 99% available chlorine means you're getting what you pay for. Some budget brands cut corners with fillers that drop the active percentage to 90% or less. At that point, you're paying for filler, not sanitation.

## Where It Falls Short

Every pool owner needs to understand the CYA tradeoff. These are trichlor tablets, which means each tab adds cyanuric acid to your pool. If you're running a saltwater system or your CYA is already above 50 ppm, you will eventually hit a chlorine lock where the sanitizer stops working. This isn't a SpaChoice flaw—it's inherent to all trichlor tabs—but it's worth saying plainly.

Also, this is a 3-inch tab only. If you have a smaller above-ground pool (under 5,000 gallons), a full tablet may over-chlorinate. You'll need to break tabs or use a smaller dose, which is awkward.

One reviewer flagged: "The bucket is heavy and the tabs are packed tight—getting the first few out requires some effort." Minor, but real.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a mid-to-large in-ground pool (10,000+ gallons), you use a floating feeder or inline chlorinator, and you want a set-and-forget solution that won't leave your water cloudy or your walls scaled.

**Skip it if:** You're managing a small above-ground pool, you rely on a salt chlorine generator, or your CYA readings are already near maximum. In those cases, liquid chlorine or a cal-hypo alternative would serve you better.

## FAQ

**Q: How often do I add a tablet?**
A: For a 15,000-gallon pool at 80°F, expect 5-7 days per tablet. Hotter water and heavy swimmer load will shorten that. Test your chlorine levels every 2-3 days to adjust frequency.

**Q: Can I use these in a saltwater pool?**
A: Not recommended. Salt pools already generate chlorine, and the added CYA from tabs will build up over time, reducing the effectiveness of your salt cell. Stick with unstabilized liquid chlorine.

**Q: Do these work in a floater or only in a chlorinator?**
A: Both. The 3-inch size fits standard floaters and inline feeders. Just make sure the floater's opening is wide enough—some narrow models can restrict water flow and slow dissolution.
