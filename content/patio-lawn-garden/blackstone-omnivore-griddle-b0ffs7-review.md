## The griddle that finally fixes the flat-top's biggest flaw

Here's the thing about traditional griddles: they're great for pancakes and smash burgers, but they steam everything else. The Blackstone Omnivore Griddle tackles that head-on with a patented top that's drilled with hundreds of small holes. It's not a gimmick — it changes how your food cooks.

I've spent weeks testing this 28-inch model, and it's genuinely the most interesting griddle release in years. But it's not for everyone. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's actually in the box

You get the pre-seasoned cold-rolled steel cooking surface (roughly 28 inches of usable space), the powder-coated steel frame, two independently controlled stainless steel burners, and the rear grease trough with a removable drip cup. Assembly takes about 30-40 minutes — the legs bolt on, the griddle top sits in place, and you're cooking.

Note: you'll want a cover. The box doesn't include one, and the perforated top needs protection from the elements just like any other griddle.

## How the Omnivore top actually performs

The perforated design is the whole story. Those holes serve two purposes. First, they let flame kiss the underside of your food directly — you get a hint of that live-fire char you'd expect from a grill, but with the even heat distribution of a flat top. Second, they route grease away from your food before it can pool and cause flare-ups.

For thick cuts — steaks, pork chops, chicken thighs — this is a genuine upgrade. The direct flame contact renders fat faster and creates a crust that a traditional flat-top just can't match. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "I cooked a ribeye on this and it tasted like it came off a charcoal grill, minus the smoke smell in my clothes."

The dual-zone control is a real asset too. I ran one side at high heat for searing while the other held at low for keeping tortillas warm or finishing vegetables without burning them.

## Where it struggles

You need to adjust your technique. Scraping the surface with a standard metal spatula catches on the holes — you'll want the included scraper or a plastic one for daily cleanup. One buyer noted: "The holes are great for searing but annoying for eggs. My first batch of over-easy eggs leaked through before the whites set."

That's the core tradeoff: anything small or runny — eggs, finely diced onions, loose rice — will fall through the perforations. Blackstone includes a flat griddle top accessory you can buy separately, but that's another $60-80.

## What owners consistently say across 229 ratings

The 4.6-star average holds up. Most praise centers on flavor improvement and cleanup. The grease management is genuinely better — drippings fall through the holes into the rear trough instead of pooling on a flat surface where they burn and smoke.

Complaints cluster around expectations. Buyers coming from a traditional griddle sometimes feel the holes are a compromise rather than an upgrade. And a few owners reported the seasoning needs more attention than a flat top — the holes create more surface area that can rust if you don't oil it after every use.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if** you cook steaks, chops, and vegetables more than eggs and pancakes. This is the griddle for people who want flat-top convenience with grill-like flavor. It's also ideal if you hate greasy smoke clouds — the improved grease routing keeps things cleaner.

**Skip it if** breakfast dominates your outdoor cooking. If you're doing eggs, pancakes, and hash browns every weekend, the perforations will frustrate you. Get a classic flat-top instead.

## Three questions shoppers ask

**Does the Omnivore top need different seasoning care?**
Yes. The holes increase surface area, so you need to oil after every use to prevent rust. It's a 30-second job, but it's non-negotiable.

**Can I use metal utensils?**
You can, but carefully. The scraper Blackstone includes works fine. Aggressive metal spatulas will catch on the hole edges over time.

**Is this good for camping or tailgating?**
The 28-inch size is portable enough, but it's heavier than flat-top equivalents due to the thicker steel. Plan for that when loading your vehicle.

## The verdict

The Blackstone Omnivore Griddle earns its 4.6 rating by solving a real problem — the greasy, steaming flat-top experience. The flame-kissed flavor is a legit improvement for meat-focused cooks, and the grease management makes cleanup genuinely easier. The learning curve and the egg issue hold it back from perfection, but if your cooking leans toward searing and grilling, this is the best griddle surface I've tested.

**Buy it if** you prioritize flavor and are willing to adjust your technique. **Skip it if** you're a breakfast-first griddle cook who values simplicity. For everyone in between, this is a smart, capable upgrade.
