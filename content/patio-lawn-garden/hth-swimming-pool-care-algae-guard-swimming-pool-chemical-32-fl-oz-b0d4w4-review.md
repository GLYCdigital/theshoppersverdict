Algae is the single most frustrating problem in pool ownership. You brush, you shock, you vacuum, and a week later that green tint is back. HTH's Algae Guard claims to break that cycle with a single dose that protects for up to 60 days. After digging through 229 ratings and the chemistry behind it, here's what actually matters.

## What You're Actually Getting

The HTH Swimming Pool Care Algae Guard comes in a 32-fluid-ounce bottle with a clearly marked measuring cap. One full bottle treats a 15,000-gallon pool — that's the standard 18-foot round above-ground pool or a modest in-ground setup. The active ingredient is a polymer-based algaecide that works differently from the copper-based alternatives you'll find on the shelf.

As shown in the product image, the dosing instructions are printed directly on the label. You pour the full bottle around the pool perimeter while the pump runs, then walk away. No mixing, no waiting, no complicated ratios for standard applications.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The chemistry here is straightforward: this is a preventive algaecide, not a cure. It creates a protective layer in the water that stops spores from taking hold. The 60-day claim holds up in practice for most buyers, though your mileage varies with sun exposure and how often you run the pump.

One thing that separates this from cheaper options: it's chlorine-compatible. Many algaecides foam when they hit a shocked pool or turn the water cloudy. HTH formulated this to work alongside your regular shock routine without those side effects. Multiple reviewers specifically noted that they didn't see the sudsing that plagued their previous algaecide.

The broad-spectrum coverage matters too. It handles green algae (the common surface bloom) and does respectable work against yellow and black algae — those stubborn strains that cling to pool walls and require scrubbing to dislodge.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The feedback pattern across those 229 ratings is remarkably consistent. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Added this after a green pool nightmare. Shock, brush, vacuum, then this. Three weeks later, still crystal clear."* Another noted, *"I've used the copper stuff that stained my liner. This didn't stain anything."* That stain-free claim is significant — copper-based algaecides are notorious for leaving blue-green deposits on light-colored pool surfaces.

The 60-day protection is the other recurring theme. Pool owners who travel or just don't want to babysit their water every weekend appreciate that one application covers two months of normal summer use.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: this will not rescue a pool that's already green. If you pour this into a bloom, you'll be disappointed. The label says it clearly, but plenty of buyers skip that part and expect a miracle. You must shock, brush, and vacuum the algae out first, then add Algae Guard as the preventive layer.

The bottle size is also a practical limitation. At 15,000 gallons per bottle, a 25,000-gallon in-ground pool needs two bottles — at roughly $25–30 each, that adds up fast. And if you have heavy rain or a lot of swimmers, the 60-day window shrinks; you'll want to reapply after about 45 days in peak summer conditions.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the pool owner who has fought algae before and wants a reliable preventive that doesn't require weekly attention. It's ideal if you've dealt with recurring green water despite regular chlorination, or if you're opening the pool for the season and want a set-and-forget layer of defense.

Skip it if you're currently staring at a green pool and expecting a one-bottle fix, or if you have a very large in-ground pool where the per-dose cost becomes prohibitive compared to bulk alternatives.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work in saltwater pools?**
A: Yes. The polymer-based formula is compatible with salt chlorine generators. Just ensure your salt cell is clean and your stabilizer levels are within range for best results.

**Q: Can I swim immediately after adding it?**
A: HTH recommends waiting until the product is fully circulated — about 15–20 minutes with the pump running. Unlike some algaecides, it won't irritate skin if you wait that short window.

**Q: Will it prevent black algae on plaster pools?**
A: It helps significantly, but black algae has roots that embed in porous plaster. You still need to brush affected areas aggressively before application and spot-treat with a dedicated black algae product if you see it.
