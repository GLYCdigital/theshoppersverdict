Let's be honest: most roller skates under $100 look like they were assembled from a craft bin. The XUDREZ Double Row skates don't. The full-grain leather upper, contrast stitching, and high-top silhouette give them a vintage rink vibe that photographs well and holds up better than the pleather alternatives. But looks only get you so far — the real question is whether they skate as good as they look.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the pair of skates (pre-assembled — no wrench work required), a small hex key for future bearing maintenance, and a basic instruction card. No spare wheels, no extra toe stops, no carrying bag. That's fine at this price point, but know what you're buying.

The standout feature is the adjustable sizing system. A button on the heel releases a sliding mechanism that extends the boot length across four sizes — roughly US 3–6 in the small size, 7–10 in the large. It's a genuine money-saver if you're buying for a kid who's still growing or if multiple family members share a pair.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The leather is the headline here. It's not buttery-soft glove leather, but it's real, structured hide that holds its shape and doesn't tear at stress points. The padded collar sits high around the ankle, which gives solid lateral support — especially valuable for beginners who tend to roll their ankles inward.

The double-row (quad) wheel setup uses 54mm polyurethane wheels with ABEC-7 bearings. On smooth indoor surfaces — rinks, gym floors, garage epoxy — they glide predictably and hold speed well. Outdoors, the four-wheel configuration absorbs more shock than inline skates, and the wider stance makes balancing easier for new skaters. One experienced buyer noted, *"I've skated for 20 years and bought these for my daughter. The stability is genuinely impressive for the price — she was rolling confidently by day two."*

The XUDREZ skates also handle rough terrain better than the price suggests. The wheel durometer is soft enough to grip asphalt without that teeth-chattering vibration you get on harder wheels. You'll still feel cracks and pebbles, but you won't feel like your fillings are loosening.

## Where They Fall Short

The fixed toe stop is my biggest gripe. It's positioned for a neutral, feet-shoulder-width stance — which works fine for casual cruising. But if you want to do jam skating, dance moves, or even aggressive stops that require a specific toe-stop placement, you're stuck with the factory position. It's a design shortcut that limits the skates' versatility.

The break-in period is also worth mentioning. The leather upper is stiff out of the box, and the ankle collar can dig in during the first few sessions. Several reviewers mentioned this — one wrote, *"First two times out, my ankles were sore. By week two, they fit like a glove."* If you're buying these for a child, expect some complaints before the leather softens.

And at 4.5 pounds per boot, they're not light. You'll feel the weight on your feet during longer sessions, and carrying them by the attached loop gets old fast.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You're a beginner or intermediate skater who wants a stable, retro-styled quad skate for recreational rink sessions and smooth outdoor paths. Parents of growing kids will get the most value from the adjustable sizing.

**Skip them if:** You're an advanced skater who needs precise toe-stop placement, or you plan to do aggressive street skating or park tricks. The weight and fixed hardware will frustrate you.

## FAQ

**Q: How does the adjustable sizing actually work?**
A: Press the silver button on the heel, slide the front of the boot forward to extend the length, and release to lock. It adjusts in about two seconds and holds firmly — no accidental slipping during use.

**Q: Are these good for outdoor use?**
A: Yes, with caveats. The soft 54mm wheels handle asphalt and concrete well, but they'll wear faster outdoors than on rink floors. Stick to smooth surfaces for best performance and longevity.

**Q: What maintenance do they require?**
A: Wipe down the leather with a damp cloth after sweaty sessions, and check the wheel bearings every few months. The included hex key lets you pop the wheels off for cleaning — a 10-minute job.
