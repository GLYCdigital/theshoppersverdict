**The Review**

Most tow ropes for tubes are an afterthought — something you grab because the tube didn't come with one, or the one that did snapped on its third outing. The Wow Sports 2K Tow Rope is not that rope. With 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, this 60-foot, 16-strand line has become the default recommendation in boating forums for a reason: it does the one thing a tow rope must do — hold up — without costing wakeboard-rope money.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the 60-foot rope (16-strand woven polypropylene), a floating foam handle, and a heavy-duty snap hook on each end. One end connects to your boat's tow point; the other clips to the tube's D-ring. The rope is 3/8-inch thick, which sounds modest until you feel how dense the weave is. This isn't the flimsy twisted rope that comes bundled with budget tubes. It's a low-stretch line that stays taut through the pull, giving riders a firmer, more predictable ride.

**Performance on the water**

The standout feature is the 16-strand construction. Standard tube ropes are typically 8-strand or 12-strand, and they stretch noticeably under load. That stretch might feel forgiving, but it also means the tube dips toward the water mid-pull, creating drag and a mushy ride. The 2K's tighter weave keeps the tube planing higher, which riders feel immediately as more speed and less spray in the face.

The 6000-pound break strength is overkill for a single rider on a two-person tube, but it's exactly what you want if you're pulling a three-seater with a loaded boat. Several Amazon reviewers mention using it for years without visible fraying. One long-term owner noted: *"I've had this rope for three seasons, pulled tubes, skis, even a wakeboard occasionally. Still looks new."* Another praised the handle: *"The foam float makes grabbing it from the water actually possible — my old rope sank every time."*

**Where it falls short**

The biggest omission is a shock absorber. On calm water, you won't notice. But on a choppy lake with a driver who throttles aggressively, the lack of stretch means the tube and riders absorb every surge. If you frequently pull kids at low speeds, it's a non-issue. If you're pulling athletic adults who want to get tossed around, consider a rope with a bungee section or add a separate shock tube.

The 60-foot length is also worth scrutinizing. It's ideal for bowriders and smaller ski boats that throw a modest wake. Larger wake boats — 22 feet and up — create a wake that can interfere with the tube at this distance. For those setups, a 75-foot rope is the better call. One reviewer with a 21-foot boat noted: *"Works great, but I wish I'd gotten the longer version. The wake is right under the tube at speed."*

**Who should buy this**

This is the right rope for the majority of recreational boaters. If you own a 17- to 20-foot runabout or bowrider, pull tubes with two or three riders, and want a rope that will outlast your tube — this is it. It's also the smart pick for rental cottages or shared docks, where gear gets abused and replaced often. The 16-strand weave and 6000-lb rating mean it survives the "guests who don't know how to handle equipment" test.

Skip it if you need a longer rope for a large wake boat, or if you specifically want shock absorption built in. In those cases, spend more on a wake-specific tow rope.

**FAQ**

**Is this rope suitable for wakeboarding?**
It can pull a wakeboard, but it's not ideal. The low stretch is good, but there's no handle — you'd need to add a wakeboard handle. For occasional boarding, it works; for regular boarding, buy a dedicated wakeboard rope.

**Does the rope float?**
The foam handle floats, and polypropylene rope is naturally buoyant. It won't sink, though the handle is the only part that stays visibly on the surface.

**Can I connect two ropes together for more length?**
Technically yes, but don't. A knot or connector creates a weak point and adds drag. If you need more length, buy a 75-foot rope.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a durable, low-stretch tube rope that will last multiple seasons without breaking the bank, and you're pulling from a standard recreational boat.

**Skip it if:** You're running a large wake boat, need shock absorption, or plan to use it primarily for wakeboarding.
