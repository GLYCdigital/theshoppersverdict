# Ciokea Aluminum Chaise Lounge Chair Outdoor with Wheels: The Real Deal

Most patio loungers under $200 are a compromise: steel that rusts by year two, or plastic that fades and cracks by season three. The Ciokea Aluminum Chaise Lounge Chair sidesteps that trap with a genuinely rust-proof frame and a wheel system that actually works. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's not perfect — but it's close for the price.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the fully assembled chair (no tools needed), a matching headrest pillow, and a set of thin but serviceable seat cushions. The frame is welded 6063-grade aluminum — the same alloy used in decent bicycle frames — and it shows in the weight: roughly 18 pounds, light enough to drag around a deck without breaking a sweat.

The dimensions are generous: 76 inches long, 24 inches wide, with a seat height around 12 inches off the ground. That low profile is good for lounging but means getting up requires a bit of effort.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The headline feature is the wheels. Two rear wheels let you tilt the chair back and roll it like a wheelbarrow. In practice, this is brilliant — you can reposition the lounger one-handed while holding a drink in the other. On flat pavers or decking, it glides smoothly. On grass, the plastic wheels dig in a bit, but the tilt-and-drag motion still beats lifting.

The backrest adjusts through five positions, from near-upright to fully flat. The locking mechanism is a simple pull-pin design, and it engages with a satisfying click. One buyer noted, "I'm 230 pounds and the backrest has never slipped on me, even at the most reclined setting." That matches the general feedback — the frame is sturdy, with no wobble or creaking reported.

The aluminum frame stays cool to the touch even in direct afternoon sun, which is a huge plus over steel or dark plastic. The powder coat finish is smooth and even, with no sharp edges on the welds.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The portability is the runaway favorite. A verified purchaser wrote: "I bought this specifically because I have a bad back and can't lift heavy chairs. I just tilt it and roll. It's changed how I use my patio." Another reviewer highlighted the rust resistance: "Left it out all winter in Seattle rain. Looks new in spring."

The included cushion covers are removable and machine-washable, which several reviewers appreciated after a season of sunscreen and bird droppings.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the weak points. The cushions are thin — about 2 inches of foam — and you'll feel the crossbars under your thighs after an hour. The headrest pillow is more decorative than supportive; it's small and tends to slide around. Several reviewers recommend buying aftermarket cushions, which adds $40–60 to the total cost.

The wheels are plastic, not rubber. On wet grass, they can skid instead of roll. And the chair has no armrests, which is fine for lounging but makes it awkward to use as a reading chair.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Poolside loungers** who want to chase the sun across the deck without dragging furniture
- **Older adults or anyone with back issues** who needs a lightweight, rollable chair
- **Small patio owners** who need to move furniture for cleaning or entertaining
- **People in rainy climates** who want a chair that won't rust after one season

Skip it if you want plush, deep-cushion comfort for hours of reading, or if your yard is mostly uneven grass.

## FAQ

**Q: Is it actually rust-proof?**
A: The frame is aluminum, which doesn't rust. The hardware (bolts, pins) is stainless steel. The wheels and cushion components are plastic. In practice, it's as rust-proof as anything in this price range.

**Q: Can it lie completely flat?**
A: Yes, the fifth position is fully flat. It's long enough for most adults up to about 6'2" — taller folks will have their feet hanging off.

**Q: How hard is assembly?**
A: There isn't any. It arrives fully assembled. You unfold it, attach the cushions, and it's ready.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**
