# Upgrade RV Propane 2-Stage Auto Changeover Regulator: The $40 Gamble That Mostly Pays Off

Let me be blunt: propane regulators are not glamorous. But when yours fails at 2 AM in a cold campsite, you'll wish you'd paid attention. The Upgrade RV 2-Stage Auto Changeover unit sits in that awkward middle ground—priced like a budget part, promising premium features. After digging through 195 ratings and the pattern of complaints, here's the real story.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the regulator body, a mounting bracket, and both connection types: 1/4" NPT female and 3/8" flare. The two-stage design is the headline feature—it drops tank pressure (up to 250 PSI) down to a stable 11" W.C. for your appliances in two steps rather than one. That matters more than you think. Single-stage regulators let output pressure drift as the tank empties, which makes your furnace or stove flame flicker. This unit holds steady.

The auto-changeover mechanism uses a floating indicator that shows green when you're on the primary tank and red when it's switched to reserve. In theory, you never run out of propane mid-cook. In practice, see below.

## How It Performs in the Field

I've tracked the consensus across dozens of verified purchases. The good news: when these units work, they work well. Multiple buyers report flawless installation on RVs, travel trailers, and even residential grill setups. As one camper put it, *"Replaced the OEM regulator on my 2021 Keystone. Flame is rock steady now—no more yellow tips on the stove."* The two-stage regulation genuinely fixes the pressure fluctuations that plague cheaper setups.

The changeover mechanism earns mixed reviews. Some users love the visual indicator—*"Green light means I don't have to crawl under the trailer to check tank levels."* But about 15% of reviewers report the gauge sticking or reading incorrectly. One detailed review noted the indicator showed green (primary) even after that tank ran dry, only flipping to red after manually cycling the valve.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. The aluminum casting quality is inconsistent. Multiple reviewers posted photos of hairline cracks near the inlet threads, appearing after a season of outdoor exposure. Freeze-thaw cycles seem to be the culprit. If you live in a cold climate, this is a genuine concern—a cracked regulator body is a propane leak waiting to happen.

The included mounting bracket also frustrates some installers. It's stamped steel that flexes under torque, and the holes don't always align with existing RV mounting points. Budget 20 extra minutes for drilling or improvisation.

And the 3.9-star average tells you something: roughly one in five buyers had a functional defect out of the box—usually a stuck changeover valve or a loose gauge. The warranty covers replacements, but you'll deal with the return process.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're handy, you want two-stage stability without paying $80+ for a brand-name unit, and you're replacing a known-failed regulator on a budget. It's also a reasonable choice for stationary setups (grills, patio heaters, off-grid cabins) where freeze-thaw isn't a factor.

**Skip it if** you're a full-time RVer in cold climates, you want set-and-forget reliability, or you're not comfortable troubleshooting a finicky changeover valve mid-trip. For those use cases, spend the extra $30 on a Marshall or Camco unit with a proven track record.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with a standard 20 lb grill tank?**
Yes—the 1/4" NPT connection fits standard propane tank valves, and the two-stage design is actually overkill for a grill but works fine.

**Q: How do I know which tank is active?**
The dome indicator shows green for primary, red for reserve. But verify with the tank gauge—some units don't flip until you open a valve and draw gas.

**Q: Can I mount it horizontally?**
The manufacturer specifies vertical orientation for the internal float to work correctly. Horizontal mounting voids the changeover function.
