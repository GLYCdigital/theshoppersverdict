**The Short Version: This Is the Anti-Candy Dried Mango**

Most "dried mango" you find in stores is actually candied mango — soaked in sugar syrup, coated in sulfur dioxide to keep it bright orange, and chewy enough to stick to your teeth. Crispy Green takes the opposite route. The ingredient list on this 2.54-ounce bag contains exactly one word: mango. No added sugar, no preservatives, no sulfites. That's the whole pitch, and it delivers.

As shown in the product image, you get a foil pouch packed with pale, matte mango slices that have been freeze-dried rather than dehydrated. That's not a cosmetic detail — it fundamentally changes the texture. Where traditional dried mango is leathery and dense, these shatter like a crisp cracker when you bite into them, then dissolve into a fine, slightly fibrous powder that tastes intensely of ripe mango.

**What You're Actually Getting**

Physically, the bag is smaller than it looks in photos. At 2.54 ounces, this is roughly two to three handfuls of fruit — about two servings per the nutrition label. Each serving runs around 60 calories, which is genuinely impressive for something that tastes this sweet. The sweetness comes entirely from the fruit itself, which means each batch varies slightly depending on the harvest. Some bags lean tart, some lean honeyed. Neither is bad, but don't expect uniform flavor.

**How It Actually Performs**

The freeze-drying process removes about 97% of the water, which does two things. First, it makes the mango shelf-stable for months without refrigeration — toss it in a backpack, glovebox, or camping kit without worry. Second, it concentrates the flavor. The mango taste here is loud, bright, and unmistakably fresh, not muted like some cheaper freeze-dried fruit that tastes vaguely of cardboard.

Buyers consistently mention the crunch and the clean ingredient profile in their reviews. One verified purchaser noted, "My toddler asks for these over gummy fruit snacks," while another praised them for being "the only dried fruit I've found that doesn't have added sugar or oil." The resealable zipper on the pouch works as intended — though in practice, most people finish the bag before needing to reseal it.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be blunt about the tradeoffs. The texture is the dealbreaker for some. If you grew up on sweetened, chewy Thai dried mango, this will feel more like a crouton than a fruit snack. It's brittle, it crumbles, and it leaves a slightly chalky residue on your tongue for a few seconds before dissolving. Kids who expect squishy fruit leather might reject it outright.

The value proposition is also worth scrutinizing. At this size and price point, you're paying a significant premium over fresh mango — often two to three times the cost per ounce. You're paying for convenience, shelf stability, and zero-waste snacking, not for the cheapest way to get vitamin C into your diet. If you're feeding a family of four, you'll need to buy several bags at once.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for hikers who want lightweight, non-messy trail fuel. Parents who want a lunchbox snack that won't sugar-crash their kids. People doing whole30, paleo, or low-sugar eating who refuse to read a label full of additives. And anyone who's been burned by "fruit snacks" that are 30% corn syrup.

Skip it if you want something to snack on mindlessly while watching TV — you'll finish the bag too fast and feel shortchanged. Skip it if you hate crunchy fruit (it's genuinely polarizing). And skip it if you're on a tight grocery budget, because fresh mango is cheaper per serving.

**FAQ**

**Is this mango actually freeze-dried or just dried?**
Freeze-dried. The fruit is flash-frozen and then placed in a vacuum chamber where the ice sublimates directly to vapor. That's why it's crispy rather than chewy, and why the color stays pale instead of turning deep orange from heat processing.

**Does it taste like fresh mango?**
Close, but more intense. Removing the water concentrates the natural sugars and acids, so it tastes like a punchier, tangier version of fresh mango. Expect some natural variation between bags depending on the fruit harvest.

**How should I store it?**
Keep the pouch sealed in a cool, dry place. Once opened, the freeze-dried fruit will absorb moisture from the air and turn soft or chewy within a few days. The resealable zipper helps, but the bag rarely lasts that long anyway.
