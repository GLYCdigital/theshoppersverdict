If you’ve ever chopped scallions, tossed the white ends, and felt a twinge of waste — this gadget is aimed squarely at you. The Green Onion Regrower turns that guilt into a countertop science project. But does it actually work, or is it just another plastic doodad destined for a drawer? After digging through 225 ratings (averaging 4.5 stars), here’s the real story.

**What you actually get**

The kit arrives with a clear glass or acrylic vessel, a silicone top with slotted openings, and a base tray. The design is dead simple: you drop the root ends of your scallions into the slots, add water up to the root line, and wait. No soil, no grow lights, no electricity. The clear walls aren’t just for looks — they let you monitor root development and water levels at a glance, which is genuinely useful, not just aesthetic.

**Does it actually regrow onions?**

Yes, and better than you’d expect. The silicone lid is the standout feature. It holds the scallion bases upright and secure, so they don’t float or tilt sideways like they do in a mason jar. Buyers consistently report visible regrowth within 3–5 days. One reviewer noted, *“I was skeptical, but my scallions grew two inches in under a week. My kids check it every morning before school.”* That’s the recurring theme — it works, and it’s oddly delightful to watch.

The regrown greens are slightly thinner than store-bought scallions, which is normal for water propagation. You’ll get 2–3 harvest cycles before the bulbs exhaust their energy and need replacing. That’s not a flaw; that’s just plant biology.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The compact footprint gets mentioned a lot. It’s roughly the size of a large coffee mug, so it fits on a narrow windowsill without crowding your spice rack. The glass construction is also a plus — no staining like plastic, and it’s dishwasher-safe (though the silicone lid is easier to hand-wash).

As shown in the product image, the design is clean and modern enough to leave out on the counter, which matters if you’re the type who actually wants to use it daily rather than hide it in a cabinet.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be honest about the limitations. First, capacity. This holds about 5–7 standard scallion bases. If you’re cooking for a family and go through a bunch a week, you’ll still be buying store-bought. This is a supplement, not a replacement.

Second, water maintenance. You need to change the water every 2–3 days, or it gets cloudy and starts to smell faintly of onion rot. That’s not a huge chore, but it’s a commitment. If you forget, you’ll come back to slimy roots and a less pleasant experience.

Third, the price. Depending on the listing, this runs anywhere from $15–$25. You could replicate the function with a jar and a rubber band for free. What you’re paying for is the stability, the visibility, and the aesthetics. Whether that’s worth it depends on how much you value a polished tool versus a hack.

**Who should buy this**

- **Urban gardeners with limited space**: If you don’t have yard or balcony space, this gives you a tiny, productive green corner.
- **Families with kids**: Watching roots grow daily is genuinely educational. Multiple reviewers mention their children taking ownership of the watering routine.
- **Home cooks who hate waste**: If you buy scallions weekly and toss the bottoms, this will save you a few bucks over time and cut down on kitchen waste.

**Skip it if** you’re expecting a constant, unlimited supply of scallions, or if you’re not willing to change water regularly. Also skip it if you hate single-purpose kitchen gadgets — this does exactly one thing.

**FAQ**

**How long until I see regrowth?**  
Visible green shoots in 3–5 days. Full harvest-ready stalks in 10–14 days, depending on light and temperature.

**Can I regrow other vegetables in it?**  
Technically yes — leeks, lemongrass, and bok choy bases work. But the slot sizes are optimized for standard scallions, so anything thicker will be a tight squeeze.

**How many times can I regrow from one base?**  
Expect 2–3 cycles per base. After that, the bulb is spent. Just compost it and start fresh with your next store-bought bunch.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a functional, attractive way to reduce kitchen waste and you’ll actually maintain the water changes. **Skip it if** you’re looking for a high-volume solution or you know you’ll forget about it after week one.

The Green Onion Regrower is a niche product, but it executes its niche very well. It’s not a necessity — it’s a small, satisfying upgrade to a habit you should probably already have. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it’s delivering what it promises. I’m giving it a 4.6/5.
