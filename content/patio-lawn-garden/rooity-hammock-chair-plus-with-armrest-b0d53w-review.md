Most hammock chairs make you choose between lounging and actually sitting upright to drink your coffee. The ROOITY Hammock Chair Plus with Armrest tries to give you both. After digging through 204 ratings and the spec sheet, here's what you need to know before you hand over your cash.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the hammock chair itself — a woven cotton-polyester blend that feels more substantial than the cheap nylon options — plus two padded armrests that slide over the spreader bars, two heavy-duty 10-foot ropes, two carabiners, and the hardware needed for ceiling or beam mounting. No stand is included. If you don't have a porch beam, pergola, or sturdy tree branch, factor in another $50-80 for a standalone frame.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The 300-pound weight capacity is the headline spec, but what matters more is where that strength lives. The stitching around the fabric-to-rope connection points is reinforced with bartack stitching — the same technique used on climbing gear. That's where most cheap hammock chairs fail, and ROOITY got it right.

The armrests are the differentiator. They're padded with a dense foam core and wrap around the spreader bars with a hook-and-loop closure. In practice, they give you a stable surface to grip when you're swinging in or settling back. The fabric itself is a cotton-poly blend that breathes better than polyester-only options — important for afternoon naps in direct sun.

Installation is where the friction lives. The spreader bars need to align just right before you thread the ropes through the end caps, and the included instructions are serviceable but not stellar. Plan on 15-20 minutes with a ladder, and have a second person for the first hang if you're mounting it overhead.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The overwhelming theme in positive reviews is the armrests actually working as intended. One verified buyer wrote, *"I was skeptical about the armrests being gimmicky, but they're solid. I can actually sit up and read without the chair spinning me around."* Another noted, *"The fabric is thicker than my last two hammock chairs combined — zero stretch after three months of daily use."* Multiple reviewers also flagged the weight capacity as accurate, with heavier users reporting no sagging.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest complaint cluster is the lack of a storage bag. At this price point, competitors like the Vivere or La Siesta include a carry sack. You're paying a premium for the armrest feature, and it would cost ROOITY pennies to include a basic drawstring bag.

Second, the spreader bars are aluminum, not hardwood. That keeps weight down (about 6.5 pounds), but if you're leaving it out year-round in harsh weather, the metal can develop a chalky oxidation over time. A coat of clear outdoor sealant fixes it, but you shouldn't have to.

Finally, the mounting hardware is rated for the chair's weight, but the included screws are only meant for solid wood or concrete. Drywall installs need toggle bolts — not included.

**Who should buy this**

- **Porch loungers** who want to read, use a tablet, or drink something without spilling — the armrests are a genuine upgrade
- **Backyard readers** who want a chair that supports an upright position, not just a full recline
- **Campsite comfort seekers** — it packs flat (though not in a bag), and the 300-pound capacity handles gear on the chair with you

**Skip it if** you need a portable option with a carry case, or if you're on a tight budget — you can get a decent no-armrest hammock chair for 40% less.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this with a hammock stand?**
A: Yes, but you'll need a stand rated for at least 300 pounds and a spreader-bar-compatible connection. The included ropes work with most standard stands, but check the stand's hook height — you need about 7-8 feet of clearance.

**Q: How hard is it to install?**
A: Moderate. The chair assembly is straightforward — slide the armrests on, thread the ropes. The hang is the tricky part. You'll need a ladder, a stud finder, and ideally a second person to hold the chair while you secure the ropes.

**Q: Is the fabric weatherproof?**
A: No. The cotton-poly blend is water-resistant but not waterproof. Bring it in during rain or store it with a cover. Prolonged UV exposure will fade the color over a season or two.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you want a hammock chair that lets you sit upright comfortably, with armrests that make reading, drinking, and using your phone actually practical. The build quality is above average for the price bracket.

**Skip it if** portability matters, you need a stand included, or you're a set-and-forget type who won't bring it inside during bad weather.

The ROOITY Hammock Chair Plus is a solid mid-tier pick that solves a real problem — the armrests aren't a gimmick, they're the reason to buy this over cheaper options. Just budget for a storage solution and a bit of installation patience.
