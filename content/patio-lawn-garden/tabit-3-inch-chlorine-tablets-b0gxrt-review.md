## The 3-Inch Tablet That Actually Pulls Its Weight

Let's be honest — most chlorine tablets are a commodity. You grab a bucket, drop a few in a floater, and hope. The TABIT 3-inch tablets are different, and after digging through 235 reviews and the product specs, I can tell you exactly why pool owners are giving this 4.7 stars.

These are 99% pure trichloroisocyanuric acid — that's the active ingredient that matters. No calcium hypochlorite fillers, no inert binders padding the weight. That means you're paying for sanitizer, not filler. The 8-ounce tablets are pressed uniformly at 3 inches, so they fit standard feeders, skimmers, and floaters without jamming or breaking apart mid-cycle.

## What You Actually Get

A 5-pound bucket ships with roughly 10 tablets, each weighing 8 ounces. The bucket itself is food-grade HDPE with a gasket seal — moisture stays out, chlorine gas stays in. That's important because trichlor off-gasses if exposed to humidity, and this bucket holds the seal well.

The tablets are white, dense, and uniform. As shown in the product image, they stack cleanly in the bucket without crumbling at the edges — a common issue with cheaper compressed tabs that arrive half-powdered.

## Performance: The Numbers That Matter

The slow-dissolve formulation is the headline feature. In a standard skimmer basket with normal water flow, these tabs dissolve over 5–7 days. With a floater, expect closer to 7–10 days depending on your pool's temperature and circulation. Warmer water dissolves faster — that's physics, not a defect.

The 99% trichlor content also means these are fully stabilized with cyanuric acid (CYA). That's a double-edged sword: it protects your chlorine from UV breakdown, which is great for outdoor pools in full sun, but it means regular CYA monitoring is non-negotiable. If you're already running CYA above 50 ppm, switch to liquid chlorine until it drops.

## Where It Falls Short

The CYA issue is real. If you have a small above-ground pool (under 5,000 gallons), these will spike your cyanuric acid faster than you can drain and refill. You're better off with unstabilized liquid chlorine or a smaller dose of dichlor.

The bucket lid also gets criticism. It's sealed tightly enough that first opening requires a flathead screwdriver to pry up the edge. And if you store these in a humid garage, the tabs can absorb moisture and fuse together into a solid block. Keep the lid sealed and store them somewhere dry.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You have an in-ground or large above-ground pool (10,000+ gallons), you want a slow-release sanitizer that cuts maintenance frequency, and you're comfortable testing CYA levels monthly.

**Skip these if:** You have a small above-ground pool, a saltwater chlorine generator system, or you're not willing to monitor stabilizer levels. Also skip if you prefer the convenience of liquid chlorine for granular dosing control.

## FAQ

**Q: How many tablets should I use per week?**
A: For a 15,000-gallon pool, start with 2 tablets in a floater or skimmer. Test chlorine daily for the first week and adjust by one tablet. The goal is 1–3 ppm free chlorine.

**Q: Can I put these in my skimmer basket?**
A: Yes, but only if your pump runs frequently. Tablets in a skimmer dissolve faster with constant water flow, and the acidic solution can corrode metal components over time. A floater is gentler on equipment.

**Q: Do these raise pool pH?**
A: No — trichlor is acidic and will actually lower pH slightly. Test pH weekly and adjust with soda ash or borax as needed.
