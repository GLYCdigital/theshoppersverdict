# Mklsit Deer Netting Fence: 8ft x 100ft of Protection That Actually Works

If you've ever watched a deer casually decimate a season's worth of tomato plants in one night, you know the frustration. The Mklsit Deer Netting Fence promises a solution — an 8-foot tall, 100-foot long barrier that claims to keep the four-legged marauders out without turning your yard into a fortress. After digging through 220 ratings and the specs, here's the real story.

## What You're Actually Getting

Unboxing reveals a single roll of black polyethylene netting. The dimensions matter here: 8 feet tall by 100 feet long. That's enough to wrap a substantial garden — roughly 50 linear feet of double-sided protection, or one long boundary line. The mesh openings measure about 2 inches square, which is the sweet spot for deer and bird deterrence.

The material is UV-stabilized, which sounds like marketing speak until you've had cheap netting turn brittle and crumble after one summer. This holds up. The mesh is lightweight — noticeably lighter than welded wire fencing — which makes it manageable for one person to install, though a second set of hands helps with the length.

## Performance in the Field

Installation is straightforward: stake it, tie it, or zip-tie it to existing posts. The netting drapes naturally and doesn't sag excessively, though you'll want tension on the bottom edge to keep critters from pushing under. The 8-foot height is the standout feature — most deer netting tops out at 6 or 7 feet, and determined deer can clear those. At 8 feet, you're in the "not worth the effort" zone for most whitetails.

The 2-inch mesh does double duty. It stops deer cold, but it also keeps out birds, rabbits, and groundhogs. Pollinators like bees and butterflies still get through, which is critical if you're protecting fruiting plants. One reviewer noted, "The netting is sturdy enough to keep deer out, but it's not so dense that it blocks sunlight or rain."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The height-to-value ratio is the recurring theme. Multiple reviewers specifically called out that this is one of the few netting rolls that actually measures a full 8 feet — some cheaper options come up short. The UV resistance also earns consistent mention, with buyers reporting it stayed flexible and intact through an entire growing season.

Another frequent positive: it's nearly invisible from a distance. The black mesh blends into shadows and foliage, so your garden doesn't look like a construction site. For suburban yards where HOA aesthetics matter, that's a real advantage.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the weaknesses. The netting is thin — about what you'd expect at this price point, but it will tear if you stretch it tight over jagged branches or metal fence posts. Use protective caps or wrap the edges with tape where it contacts sharp surfaces.

Second, there's no installation hardware in the box. No stakes, no zip ties, no clips. If you don't already have a support system — T-posts, wooden stakes, or an existing fence — factor in that additional cost. This is a common complaint in the reviews, and it's fair.

Finally, the black color is a double-edged sword. It's discreet, but it's also hard to see in low light. Deer can run into it before they register it's there, which can bend posts or tear the netting. Brightly colored flagging tape at intervals solves this.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for you if: you have a medium-to-large garden or orchard, you're fighting deer and birds simultaneously, and you want a season-proof barrier that doesn't require a contractor. It's also ideal for protecting tall plants — raspberry canes, young fruit trees, climbing beans — that shorter netting can't cover.

Skip it if: you need a permanent, livestock-grade barrier, or if your deer are particularly aggressive and prone to pushing through lightweight barriers. For extreme pressure, you'd want welded wire or an electric fence setup.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this keep out small animals like rabbits?**
A: The 2-inch mesh stops rabbits and groundhogs in most cases, but determined chewers can gnaw through it. It's primarily designed for deer and birds; small rodents are a bonus, not a guarantee.

**Q: Can I reuse it after the season?**
A: Yes. Take it down, shake off debris, and store it indoors. The UV stabilization means it won't degrade in storage, and many reviewers report getting two or three seasons from a single roll.

**Q: How do I install it without it sagging?**
A: Run a tension line (wire or rope) along the top and bottom edges, and attach the netting to it every few feet with zip ties. This distributes the weight and keeps the mesh taut.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5 (★★★★)**

This is solid, reliable deer netting at a fair price. It's not the toughest option on the market, and the lack of included hardware is annoying, but the full 8-foot height and UV-stabilized material make it a genuinely effective garden protector for a season or two of hard use.

**Buy it if** you need tall, affordable netting for a large area and don't mind sourcing your own stakes and ties.

**Skip it if** you need something bulletproof for high-pressure deer zones — or if you want a complete kit out of the box. For everyone else, this is a smart, cost-effective choice that gets the job done.
