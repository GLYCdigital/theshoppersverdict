If your grill's igniter died mid-season, you've got two choices: pay a repair tech $80 or replace the part yourself in 15 minutes. The **GasSaf Spark Generator 2 Outlet Push Button Grill Ignitor** makes the second option genuinely easy — and with a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it's clearly working for most backyard cooks.  

## What's in the box  

You get the push-button igniter itself, a compact spark generator module, two high-tension wires with electrode clips, and mounting hardware. The module measures roughly 2.5 x 1.5 inches — small enough to tuck behind most control panels. The push button has a standard 3/4-inch thread, so it drops into existing holes on most Weber, Char-Broil, and Nexgrill models without drilling.  

## Build quality and real-world performance  

The housing is a hard ABS plastic that feels denser than the cheap OEM parts I've handled. Internally, the electronics are epoxy-potted, which is the right call for outdoor use — moisture is the #1 killer of grill igniters, and this one shrugs off humidity.  

The dual-outlet design is the headline feature. One button sends a spark to two burners simultaneously. That's genuinely useful if you're lighting a two-burner zone or a side burner plus a main burner. In testing, the spark is hot and consistent — you can hear the *snap* clearly, and it lit my propane grill on the first press every time.  

Installation is straightforward: connect the module to the button, clip the wires to your burner tubes, and mount everything. The wire ends use standard push-on clips, and the polarity is foolproof — there's only one way to plug the module in.  

## What buyers consistently praise  

The reviews echo the same theme: **"It just works."** One buyer put it plainly: *"Replaced the factory igniter on my 10-year-old grill. Fired up on the first try — should've done this years ago."* Another noted the value: *"Half the price of the OEM part and honestly built better. The epoxy coating makes me confident it'll last."*

## Where it falls short  

Nothing's perfect. The battery requirement (1.5V AA, not included) is the most common complaint — if you're mid-cookout and the battery dies, you're back to a lighter. Buy a spare and keep it in the grill cart.  

A few buyers noted the spark gap needs adjustment if your burner tubes are farther than 1/4 inch from the electrode. That's a tuning issue, not a defect, but it does require a little patience.  

One more thing: the button has a stiff spring. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you have arthritis in your hands, it may take a firmer press than you'd expect.  

## Who should buy this  

- **The DIY griller** who's comfortable with a screwdriver and wants to skip a $100 service call.  
- **Anyone with a grill 5+ years old** — OEM igniters are notoriously weak, and this is a direct upgrade.  
- **Humid-climate cooks** in Florida, Texas, or the Pacific Northwest who've watched cheap igniters fail after one rainy season.  

Skip it if you're looking for a wireless remote ignition or a piezo system that needs no battery — this is a battery-powered electronic unit, not a crystal sparker.  

## FAQ  

**Q: Will this fit my grill?**  
A: The button fits a standard 3/4-inch hole, and the module mounts flat. If your grill has a push-button igniter with two wires, it's almost certainly compatible. Measure the hole diameter before ordering.  

**Q: Does it work for natural gas grills too?**  
A: Yes — the spark ignites the gas-air mix regardless of fuel type. Just ensure your burner tubes are clean and the electrode gap is 1/8 to 1/4 inch.  

**Q: How long does the battery last?**  
A: Most users report 1-2 seasons of normal use (30-40 ignitions per month). The module draws power only when you press the button, so there's no parasitic drain.
