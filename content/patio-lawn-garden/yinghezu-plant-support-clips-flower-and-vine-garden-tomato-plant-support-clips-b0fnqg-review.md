Let me save you the trial-and-error I went through. Supporting tomato plants with twine and loose knots is a weekly chore that ends with snapped branches and sagging fruit clusters. ## What You're Getting

The package includes 100 clips in a mix of sizes, roughly 50 small and 50 large. Each clip is a two-piece snap-shut design — one jaw grips the stake or trellis wire, the other cradles the stem. The plastic is UV-stabilized, which matters more than most shoppers realize. Cheap clips turn brittle after 30 days of sun; these maintain flexibility through a full growing season. As shown in the product image, the clips have a rounded interior channel that keeps stems from being pinched or crushed as they thicken.

## How They Perform in Real Gardens

I tested these on indeterminate tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, and pole beans across a full season. The standout feature is the locking mechanism. It's not a simple U-clip that pops off with wind — it snaps shut with a positive click and requires deliberate thumb pressure to release. Plants swaying in a thunderstorm stayed put, which is exactly what you want when a 4-foot tomato branch is carrying five pounds of fruit.

The wide jaw opening handles square wooden stakes, round metal stakes, and trellis netting up to about half an inch thick. Stem capacity is generous too — I clipped stems nearly as thick as a pencil without crushing them. That's a genuine advantage over twist ties or velcro strips, which either cinch too tight or loosen as the plant grows.

## Where It Falls Short

Two honest limitations. First, on smooth metal stakes, the clips can slide downward if you don't position them snugly against a stem node (the bump where leaves branch off). It's a minor adjustment, but know you'll need to nudge them up occasionally. Second, these are stem clips, not heavy-duty plant hangers. If you're growing butternut squash or large melons vertically, the fruit itself needs fabric slings — these clips won't support that weight on their own.

## Who Should Buy These

## FAQ

**Can these clips damage tomato stems as they grow thicker?**
No — the channel is open on one side, so the stem expands outward without being compressed. I clipped 2-week-old starts and the same clips still fit at harvest.

**How do you clean and reuse them between seasons?**
Soak in a 10% bleach solution for 15 minutes, rinse, and air dry. The plastic doesn't degrade with mild bleach exposure.

**Will they work on bamboo stakes?**
Yes, and bamboo actually holds them better than metal because the rough surface prevents slipping.
