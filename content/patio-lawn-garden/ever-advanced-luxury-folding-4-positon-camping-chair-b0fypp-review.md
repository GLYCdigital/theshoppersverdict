Let's cut through the noise: most folding camp chairs under $100 are flimsy, shallow-seated, and collapse after one season. The EVER ADVANCED Luxury Folding 4 Position Camping Chair is not most camp chairs. Priced as a step above the budget bin, it promises the reclining versatility of chairs that cost twice as much. After digging through 225 ratings and the specs, here's what you're actually getting.

## What's Actually in the Box

The chair ships with a padded carrying bag. That's it — no cup holder add-ons or accessories, because the cup holder is already integrated into the right armrest. The chair unfolds to 26.4 inches wide, 23.6 inches deep, and stands 42.5 inches tall at the headrest. The seat height sits at 19.7 inches, which is standard, but the 31-inch backrest is noticeably taller than typical camp chairs — that matters if you're 5'10" or taller and tired of your head floating above the chair back.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The frame is 0.9mm-thick steel tubing with a powder-coated finish. That's the kind of spec you'd find on chairs in the $150 range. The fabric is a 600D Oxford weave with a double-stitched seam at stress points. I've seen cheaper chairs blow out at the armrest joint within weeks; this one has reinforced corner stitching that should hold up to regular weekend use.

The four-position mechanism is the headline feature. You push the armrests forward to release the lock, then lean back to select your angle — from upright to a full 150-degree recline. The transition is smooth, not jerky. One buyer noted, "The recline mechanism is actually smooth, not the clunky ratchet system on my last chair." That matches the design: it uses a locking pin system rather than the notched teeth you find on cheaper recliners.

**Where it shines:** The lumbar support. There's a built-in padded curve at the lower back that actually aligns with your spine — not just a flat slab of fabric. Combined with the high back, this is a chair you can sit in for a full afternoon without the usual butt-numbness.

**Where it struggles:** Weight and portability. At 13.7 pounds, this is not a "grab and go" chair. It's a car-camping, tailgating, backyard-lounging chair. If you're hiking a mile to a fishing spot, this will be miserable. Also, the carrying bag is a tight fit — you'll need to fold the chair precisely or wrestle with the zipper.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.5-star average (225 ratings) reflects genuine enthusiasm. Recurring themes in positive reviews:

- **Comfort for larger frames:** Several reviewers over 250 lbs reported the 330-lb capacity felt conservative, not optimistic.
- **The recline genuinely works:** "I napped in this chair for two hours in my backyard. Worth every penny."
- **Stability on uneven ground:** The anti-sink feet are wide and grippy, earning specific praise from beach users.

## Honest Shortcomings

Beyond the weight, there are two real gripes. First, the cup holder is shallow — a standard 12-ounce can fits fine, but a 32-ounce tumbler wobbles. Second, the armrests are uncovered steel. They're comfortable when you're sitting, but in direct sun, they get hot enough to be uncomfortable. A simple padded sleeve would fix this; as-is, bring a towel.

Also, note the "4 Position" claim. It's accurate, but the two middle positions are closer together than you'd expect. The real options are effectively upright, mid-recline, and lie-flat. Don't buy this expecting five distinct angles.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Car campers and tailgaters** who want a chair that doubles as a nap station
- **Backyard loungers** who read or watch outdoor movies for hours
- **Taller folks** (5'10"+) who need actual head support
- **Petite users** — the 19.7-inch seat height means your feet will dangle

**Skip it if** you're a ultralight backpacker, or if you need a chair that packs into a small trunk space. Also skip if you prefer chairs with padded armrests — there's no easy way to retrofit them.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this chair actually comfortable for tall people?**
A: Yes, up to about 6'2". The 31-inch backrest and lumbar curve are the standout features. At 6'4", your head will still extend past the top, but the lumbar support keeps you comfortable.

**Q: Does the chair stay reclined without holding it?**
A: Yes, the locking pin mechanism holds firm. One buyer reported the chair holding his 220 lbs in full recline without slipping.

**Q: How do you fold it back up?**
A: Push the armrests forward to release the lock, fold the backrest down, then fold the legs together. It takes about 30 seconds once you get the hang of it. The bag is snug but workable.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely comfortable, lie-flat reclining chair for car camping, tailgating, or backyard lounging, and you don't mind the 13.7-lb weight. This is a $80-$100 chair in performance, likely priced below that.

**Skip it if** you need portability, or you're expecting aircraft-grade polish. The hot armrests and snug carry bag are real annoyances, but they don't outweigh the comfort and build quality.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A strong value that outperforms its price point, with minor compromises that won't matter for most buyers.
