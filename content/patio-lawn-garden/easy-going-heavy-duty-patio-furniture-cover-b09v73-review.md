You can spend $200 on a "premium" patio cover, or you can spend a fraction of that on the Easy-Going Heavy Duty and get 90% of the same protection. That's the short version. Here's the long one.

This is a water-resistant, UV-stabilized cover designed for outdoor sofas, sectionals, chairs, and tables. It's not a tarp — it's a tailored cover with reinforced corners, stitched hems, and adjustable straps. The fabric is a 300D Oxford weave with a PU (polyurethane) backing. That combination gives it a thicker, more canvas-like feel than the crinkly 90-gram polyester sheets most budget covers use.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the cover, a small stuff sack for off-season storage, and a set of straps with plastic buckles already attached. There's no assembly — just drape, cinch, and walk away. The hem has a drawstring that runs the full perimeter, which is the single most important feature for wind-prone yards. The buckles let you tighten the cover around the base of your furniture without crawling underneath.

One thing to check before ordering: measure your furniture. Easy-Going lists dimensions for every size on the listing, and buyers who ignore them end up with a slack fit that pools water. As one verified buyer put it: *"I ordered the 96-inch for my 84-inch sofa and it fits like a glove. My neighbor ordered by 'feel' and got one that flapped all winter."* The extra fabric around the base is good — it lets the cover tuck under the legs — but you don't want more than 6-8 inches of overhang.

**Real-world performance**

The water resistance is legit for the price. The PU backing isn't a full waterproof membrane, but in testing, water beads and rolls off rather than soaking through. This matters because a cover that absorbs water becomes heavy, saggy, and prone to mildew. The tradeoff is breathability: on smaller sizes without vents, you can get condensation underneath during freeze-thaw cycles. If you live somewhere humid — think Gulf Coast or Pacific Northwest — I'd add a small vent or leave the cover loosely cinched for a few days after a rain.

Durability is where this cover punches above its price. The seams are double-stitched with an extra reinforcement strip at the stress points — the corners and where the straps attach. Several reviewers reported surviving full winter seasons with snow loads: *"We had 18 inches of snow and this didn't rip or tear. The straps held tight and the whole thing just shed the snow when it melted."* That's the kind of feedback that tells you the materials are doing their job.

**Where it falls short**

No cover at this price point is perfect. The lack of vents on smaller sizes is a genuine issue for humid climates. The storage bag is flimsy — treat it as a bonus, not a feature. And while the fabric is tough, it's not immune to punctures. If you have pets that jump on the furniture, or kids who use the cover as a trampoline, you'll get holes. That's not a defect; it's physics.

Also note: this is not a "100% waterproof" cover. That term gets thrown around loosely in the category. Easy-Going doesn't claim it, and neither should you. In heavy, wind-driven rain, some moisture can seep through the PU coating over time. For 95% of users, the water resistance is plenty. For the 5% who live in monsoon zones, you'd want a higher-end marine-grade cover.

**Who should buy this**

- **The weekend homeowner** who wants a no-fuss cover that survives a season or two without falling apart — this is your sweet spot.
- **The bargain hunter** replacing a cheap tarp that shredded in year one. This will feel like a serious upgrade.
- **The meticulous measurer** who wants a tailored fit across a specific sofa or sectional set — the size variety makes that easy.

Skip it if you need a truly waterproof cover for a boat or RV, or if you live in a hurricane corridor where 100-mph winds are routine. The straps are good, but they're not storm-proof.

**FAQ**

**Will this fit my 3-seater sofa?**
Measure the width and depth first. A 3-seater typically runs 72-84 inches wide. Easy-Going's medium sizes (70-90 inches) cover that range. Order the next size up if you have deep cushions or armrests that add height.

**Does it really keep water out?**
It sheds water and resists soaking through, but it's not a sealed membrane. Water won't pool on top if you cinch it tight, and the fabric won't saturate like a cheap tarp. For normal rain and snow, you're covered.

**Can I leave it on all year?**
Yes, but if you live in a sunny climate, UV exposure will eventually degrade the coating — typically after 12-18 months. Consider a UV-protectant spray to extend the life.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want solid, season-over-season protection without paying premium prices. It's a well-constructed cover that fits properly, sheds water, and survives wind and snow.

**Skip it if** you need absolute waterproofing, live in a tropical climate where condensation is a bigger threat than rain, or you're covering furniture that costs more than your car — in that case, spend up.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A top-tier budget pick that outperforms its price point. The vent issue and flimsy storage bag keep it from a perfect score, but for most shoppers, this is the right call.
