**The Review**

If you've ever fought a losing war against fungus gnats, you already know the frustration: sticky traps, yellow cards, and desperate YouTube remedies that never quite work. The Mosser Lee ML1112 Black Sand takes a different approach — it physically blocks the problem. This isn't decorative fluff; it's a barrier strategy that actually works.

**What's actually in the bag**

You get 5 pounds of fine, matte black sand that looks more like aquarium substrate than typical garden grit. The grain size is consistent — roughly 1-2mm — which matters. Too coarse and gnats slip through; too fine and it compacts into a crust that repels water. Mosser Lee got the sweet spot right. The color is a deep, true black, not a washed-out gray.

**Real-world performance**

The primary job here is gnat control, and it delivers. One buyer put it simply: *"I've tried everything for fungus gnats. This is the only thing that actually stopped them."* The mechanism is straightforward — the sand creates a dry, inhospitable layer that adult gnats can't penetrate to lay eggs, and larvae can't escape from below.

But there's a secondary benefit that surprised me: it looks genuinely good. The matte finish doesn't glare like glass-based sand, and it doesn't wash away when you water from the top. Another reviewer noted, *"I was skeptical about the price per pound, but it's been on my plants for six months and still looks freshly applied."*

The weight is a double-edged sword. At 5 pounds, this bag has real heft — which means it won't blow off your balcony in a gust of wind, unlike bark or perlite. But it also means you'll get less coverage per bag than you might expect. Plan for roughly 8-10 six-inch pots per bag at a ½-inch depth.

**Where it falls short**

Here's the honest tradeoff: black sand absorbs heat. If your plants sit in direct afternoon sun, the top layer can get uncomfortably warm — think baking on a black car dashboard. For succulents and cacti that like it hot, this is fine. For ferns or calatheas with shallow root systems, it's a legitimate risk.

Also, this is a top-dressing, not a soil amendment. Mix it into your potting mix and you'll get poor drainage and compaction. Use it as intended — a ½-inch cap on existing soil — and it works beautifully.

**Who should buy this**

- **Houseplant parents fighting gnats**: This is your off-switch. No pesticides, no chemicals, just a physical barrier.
- **Succulent and cactus growers**: The heat retention is actually a bonus for these sun-lovers.
- **Balcony gardeners**: The weight means it stays put in wind, unlike lightweight mulches.

**Skip it if** you have moisture-loving plants that need frequent top-watering, or if you're looking for a soil amendment rather than a top dressing.

**FAQ**

**Q: How much do I need per pot?**
Apply a ½-inch layer. A 5-lb bag covers roughly 8-10 standard 6-inch pots. For larger containers, budget accordingly.

**Q: Will this hurt my plants?**
No, as long as you don't mix it into the soil. It's inert and won't change pH. Just keep it on top.

**Q: Does it fade over time?**
The black color holds surprisingly well. Several buyers report it looks fresh after months of watering. Direct UV exposure may lighten it slightly, but not noticeably.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're tired of fungus gnats and want a permanent, chemical-free solution that also elevates your potted plants' appearance. **Skip it if** your plants sit in intense all-day sun or you need bulk coverage for large planters — you'll go through this bag fast.

At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, the Mosser Lee ML1112 earns its score. It's a niche product that does exactly what it claims, with a couple of caveats that are easy to work around. For the gnat-plagued houseplant owner, this is worth every penny.
