**Bottom line up front:** If you've ever stood in a crowded camping aisle staring at empty butane shelves while propane tanks sit untouched, the Gas One GS-3400P solves that exact problem. This dual-fuel burner accepts both butane canisters and standard propane cylinders, which is rare at this price point. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it's clearly doing something right.

## What's in the box

The GS-3400P arrives as a self-contained unit. You get the stove itself, a hard plastic carrying case that doubles as a storage base, a propane hose with regulator, and a piezo ignition built into the control knob. No tools, no assembly. Pop it open, screw in your fuel source, and you're cooking.

The dimensions are roughly 13 x 11 x 4 inches when closed — small enough to toss in a trunk corner or pack on top of a cooler. Weight comes in around 4 pounds, which isn't ultralight backpacking territory, but for car camping, tailgating, or patio use, it's perfectly reasonable.

## Performance and real-world use

The 8,000 BTU burner is the star here. For context, that's comparable to a standard home stovetop burner. One verified buyer noted, "Boils water faster than my indoor stove." Another mentioned using it for a full Thanksgiving spread outdoors without issues.

The dual-fuel capability isn't a gimmick — it genuinely works. In butane mode, the stove uses standard 8-ounce canisters that click into place. In propane mode, the included hose connects to a 1-pound cylinder. The switch takes about 30 seconds. This flexibility matters more than you'd think; fuel availability varies wildly by region and season.

The piezo ignition is reliable in my testing — one click, no repeated sparking. The control knob offers decent flame adjustment from a low simmer to full blast, though it's not infinitely precise like high-end Japanese butane stoves.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 229 ratings, several themes emerge. Portability gets frequent shoutouts — the hard case protects the unit in transit and stores the propane hose neatly. The dual-fuel design earns repeated thanks from people who got stuck with the wrong fuel type on past trips.

One reviewer summed it up: "Perfect for hurricane season power outages. I can use either type of canister I find at the store." Another praised its stability with a cast-iron skillet, noting the four anti-slip feet kept things steady.

## Where it falls short

The lack of a wind guard is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. In even a light breeze, the flame flickers and heat disperses. You'll want a windscreen or a sheltered spot. This limits its usefulness for exposed beach or mountaintop cooking.

The propane hose is another weak point. It's short — about 12 inches — and the rubber is stiff in cold weather. This makes canister placement awkward. You can't just set the propane tank anywhere; it needs to sit close to the stove, which can create a tipping hazard on uneven ground.

There's also no built-in pressure regulator for butane mode. That's standard for this price range, but it means performance can drop when the canister is low or cold.

## Who should buy this

**The car camper.** You want one burner that handles both fuel types without carrying adapters or multiple stoves.

**The emergency prepper.** Power outages happen. This stove runs on fuels you can stockpile, and it packs away into a small case.

**The tailgater.** Compact enough to bring along, powerful enough to cook for a group.

**Skip it if** you're a backpacker counting every ounce, or if you primarily cook in exposed, windy conditions without natural shelter.

## FAQ

**Can I use a full-size propane tank with this stove?**
Not directly with the included hose. It's designed for 1-pound disposable cylinders. However, you can buy a separate adapter hose to connect a 20-pound tank — several reviewers report doing this successfully.

**Does it work at high altitude?**
Yes, but you'll notice reduced output. Butane performs worse in cold conditions, so switch to propane below 40°F for best results.

**Is the carrying case durable enough for checked luggage?**
The hard plastic case is sturdy, but I wouldn't trust it for airline baggage handling. It's fine for car trunks and RV storage.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, affordable burner that shrugs off fuel shortages and handles both camping and emergency cooking duties.

**Skip it if** you need serious wind resistance or precise low-temperature simmering — look at higher-end Japanese butane stoves for those features.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The GS-3400P nails the basics, offers genuine dual-fuel flexibility, and comes at a price that undercuts most single-fuel competitors. The wind guard omission and stiff hose keep it from perfection, but for the vast majority of users, this is the only portable stove you'll need.
