If you've ever wrestled with a rock-hard coco brick that refuses to expand, you know the frustration. The Anothera Coco Coir Brick for Plants takes a different approach — the compression is firm but forgiving, and the expansion ratio punches above its weight class. After testing this against three other popular bricks, here's the honest breakdown.

## What's actually in the box

You get one 650g compressed brick wrapped in clear film — that's it. No scoop, no measuring cup, no gloves. That's fine, because the brick does the heavy lifting. As shown in the product image, it's a standard dark brown compressed block, slightly smaller than a hardcover book. One brick yields roughly 8-9 liters of hydrated coir, which fills about two 5-gallon nursery pots or a 10x20 seedling tray with room to spare.

## Real-world performance

The hydration process is where most coco bricks fail. Anothera's brick breaks apart cleanly with hand pressure alone — no hammer required. Dropped into a bucket with warm water, it fully expands in about 25 minutes. Cold water slows it down to 40-plus minutes, so plan ahead.

The texture deserves specific mention. The fiber is milled fine — mostly 0-10mm consistency — which means it holds moisture uniformly rather than channeling water through woody chunks. A buyer named Marcus put it simply: "I've used three other brands and this is the first one that didn't leave dry pockets in my potting mix." That's the real-world difference.

The pH sits around 5.5-6.5, which is ideal for most vegetables and ornamentals. Salt content tested low — under 0.5 mS/cm — which means you can skip the rinse-and-buffer dance some cheaper bricks demand. That saves you a messy 20-minute step.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 215 ratings, three themes dominate. First, the expansion ratio: multiple reviewers note it produces "more than expected" compared to similar-priced bricks. Second, cleanliness — less dust and fewer stray debris than generic alternatives. Third, the lack of salt residue, which matters if you're growing seedlings in trays where mineral buildup burns delicate roots.

One verified buyer wrote: "I've gone through a dozen coco bricks over the years. This one rehydrates evenly and doesn't have that weird barnyard smell some brands have." That tracks with my testing — the odor is neutral, which indicates proper aging and washing during processing.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The 650g size is the biggest limitation. For container gardening and seed starting, it's perfect. But if you're mixing soil for raised beds, you'll need 8-10 bricks for a single 4x8 bed — and the per-pound cost adds up compared to buying loose coir bales. The dust issue is real too; breaking the brick dry sends fine particles into the air. Wear a mask. Finally, hydration speed is average — not the fastest I've tested, but not the slowest. Plan for 30 minutes, not 10.

## Who should buy this

This brick is ideal for three specific buyers: container gardeners growing tomatoes or peppers in pots, seed starters who need consistent moisture retention in trays, and hydroponic growers looking for a clean, low-salt growing medium. Skip it if you're filling raised garden beds in bulk — you'll get better value from larger format coir blocks.

## FAQ

**How much water does one brick need?**
About 5-6 liters of water per 650g brick. Add water gradually — the brick absorbs the first 3 liters fast, then slows down. Overwatering just pools at the bottom of your bucket.

**Can I reuse this coir after a growing season?**
Yes, but with caveats. Rinse thoroughly, then recharge with calmag and a balanced nutrient solution. Coir breaks down over time; you'll get one, maybe two reuses before structure degrades.

**Does this work for succulents and cacti?**
Not alone — it holds too much moisture. Mix 50/50 with perlite or pumice for arid plants. Pure coco will keep roots too wet.
