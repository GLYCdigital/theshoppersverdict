**The short version:** Weber took their 36-inch gas griddle frame and gave it the flat-top treatment — and it shows. This isn't a griddle attachment bolted onto a grill. It's a purpose-built cooking surface with the heat output to back it up.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the griddle body, four stainless steel burners rated at 12,000 BTU each, a rolled-steel cooking surface that's pre-seasoned, two fold-down side tables, a grease tray, and a full LP tank setup with regulator. The cooking surface measures 36 by 15 inches — that's 524 square inches of flat-top.

The pre-seasoning matters more than you'd think. Most griddles ship bare and require you to spend an hour burning off oils and layering seasoning. This one arrived ready to cook. I wiped it down, fired it up, and had a full breakfast going within 20 minutes.

## Real-World Performance

The four burners are independently controlled, which gives you genuine temperature zones. I ran the left side at 400°F for pancakes while cranking the right side to 550°F for smash burgers. The rolled steel holds heat better than the thinner cold-rolled surfaces on cheaper units — I didn't get the hot spots I've seen on comparable Blackstone models in this price range.

One thing that surprised me: the grease management. The rear-to-front slope channels runoff into a removable tray that's actually big enough to matter. On my previous griddle, I was scraping grease into a bucket every 20 minutes. Here, a full cookout of bacon and burgers left the tray maybe half full.

## Where It Falls Short

**It's heavy.** At 92 pounds, this is a two-person assembly job. The caster wheels help once it's together, but lifting the body onto the cart frame is awkward alone.

**No surface thermometer.** Weber gives you a hood thermometer on their grills, but here you're flying blind on surface temp. For high-heat searing, that's fine. For delicate low-temp cooking — holding eggs at 275°F — you'll want an infrared thermometer. That's a $20 add-on they should have included.

**The side tables are functional but not spacious.** Two fold-downs give you about 12 inches of work space each. It's enough for a plate and tongs, but don't plan on staging a full mise en place out there. I use a separate prep cart for bigger cooks.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you cook for groups regularly and want a flat-top that can handle volume without temperature crashes. The four independently controlled burners make this genuinely versatile — you can do a full diner-style breakfast for six people without anything coming out at the wrong temp.

**Skip it if** you're a solo cook or only make the occasional batch of burgers. At this price point, a 22-inch griddle serves a single person fine, and you'll save significant counter space and money.

## Quick Answers

**Q: Does the pre-seasoned surface really work?**
Yes. It's a proper factory seasoning, not a token coating. My first batch of eggs slid around like they were on Teflon. You'll still want to maintain it with a thin oil layer after each use, but the initial work is done.

**Q: Can I use this on a wooden deck?**
The grease tray catches runoff, but the unit itself gets hot underneath. I'd recommend a grill mat or heat shield if you're on composite decking. The caster wheels are plastic and won't mar the surface, but radiant heat is a real concern.

**Q: How long does a 20-pound propane tank last?**
At medium heat across all four burners, expect 10-12 hours. At full blast on two burners, closer to 15. I get about four solid cookouts per tank with normal use.
