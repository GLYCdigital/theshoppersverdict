## Performance and Real-World Use

The adapter works by connecting your 20LB tank's ACME thread (the big nut you turn by hand) to the small threaded valve on a refillable 1LB cylinder. You open the bulk tank valve, fill the small tank, then crack the bleed valve to vent trapped air before disconnecting. That bleed valve is the key detail — without it, you get a partial fill and a frustrating mess.

Customer feedback consistently confirms this works as intended. One verified buyer noted, *"I've refilled the same 1lb tank at least 15 times now. It fills to about 80%, which is exactly what you want for safety."* Another user praised the build: *"The brass feels substantial, not like the cheap aluminum ones that strip after a few uses."*

The math is compelling. A 20LB refill costs around $15–$20. That fills roughly 15–18 one-pound cylinders. At store prices, those same cylinders would run you $90–$140. The adapter pays for itself on the first two tanks.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest catch: **this only works with refillable 1LB tanks.** Standard disposable Coleman-style green cylinders are not designed to be refilled, and attempting to do so is both dangerous and often physically impossible because the valve construction differs. Flame King and a few other brands sell refillable 1LB tanks specifically for this purpose — you'll need to buy those separately.

A second limitation: the adapter requires a 20LB bulk tank with a standard ACME thread (the large green nut). If you have an older tank with a POL valve (the left-hand threaded one), this won't connect without an additional converter. Also, a few users mentioned that over-tightening the hand knob can warp the O-ring over time, so use firm hand pressure, not a wrench.

## FAQ

**Is it legal/safe to refill 1LB tanks?**
Yes, when using certified refillable cylinders. The DOT-approved refillable tanks from Flame King and similar brands are designed for this exact purpose. Never attempt to refill a disposable green cylinder — the valve isn't designed for repeated pressurization cycles, and it's a serious safety hazard.

**How do I know when the tank is full?**
Weigh the tank before and after. A full 1LB tank weighs about 1.4 pounds more than empty. You can also listen for the flow to slow — but weighing is the reliable method.

**Will this fit my propane tank?**
It fits any standard 20LB tank with an ACME thread (the big green nut). If your tank uses a POL valve, you'll need an adapter. Check your tank's valve type before ordering.
