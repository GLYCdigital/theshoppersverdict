**If you've ever spent a January morning guessing where your driveway ends after a 14-inch dump, these Anley markers are the $30 insurance policy that pays for itself the first time the plow truck comes through.**

I'm not going to bury the lede: the reason to buy these over the cheaper plastic stakes at your local hardware store is the fiberglass construction. Plastic snaps. Fiberglass flexes. That's the entire game.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 20 individual 48-inch poles, each with a pointed steel base for ground insertion and a factory-applied reflective strip running the upper portion of the shaft. The fiberglass is roughly 0.25 inches in diameter — thin enough to bend significantly under pressure, thick enough to stay upright in wind. The reflective material is a standard adhesive tape, not a molded sleeve, which matters for installation and longevity.

As shown in the product image, the reflective band is positioned near the top third of the pole. That's a deliberate design choice: it keeps the reflective surface above the deepest snow line while the lower two-thirds remain buried for stability.

## Real-World Performance

I've tested these through a full New England winter — the kind where the driveway becomes a luge run if you miss your line by six inches. The fiberglass flexes dramatically when the plow blade catches one, then springs back straight. No splintering, no cracking, no permanent bend. That's the difference between buying these once and buying replacements every March.

The reflective tape does its job. At night with headlights on, the markers light up from 100+ yards out. Multiple buyers echo this, with one noting: *"I can see my driveway from the road before I even turn onto my street — they're that bright."* Another long-term reviewer reported: *"Second winter with these. Still standing, still reflective, no fading."*

## Where They Fall Short

Honest downsides, because there are always downsides:

**Installation is a workout.** The pointed steel base helps, but in frozen ground you'll need a pilot hole. A cordless drill with a 1/2-inch bit or a pry bar works. Don't try to hammer these directly into hard-packed earth — you'll bend the steel tip. Buyers who complained about difficulty almost universally tried to install without preparing the ground.

**The tape can peel.** The reflective material is adhesive-backed, not embedded. If you install in warm weather (above 50°F), the adhesive softens and the tape can slide or bubble. Clean the pole with rubbing alcohol before installation and you'll avoid 90% of these issues. Also, don't leave them out year-round — the UV exposure will eventually yellow the tape and reduce reflectivity.

**No height adjustment.** At 48 inches, they're tall enough for most snowfalls, but if you live somewhere that regularly sees 36+ inches, the reflective band will get buried. That's physics, not a flaw, but worth knowing.

## FAQ

**Q: How deep do these install?**
A: The steel base is about 6 inches of pointed rod. You'll want the pole to sit roughly 12 inches deep for stability in wind, so bury the bottom quarter of the fiberglass shaft. That leaves about 36 inches above ground — plenty for snow visibility.

**Q: Will these damage my lawn mower or snowblower?**
A: They're flexible enough that a mower deck will knock them flat without breaking them, but they'll scratch paint. Mark them clearly in summer or pull them up before mowing season. The fiberglass won't shatter like plastic, so no shard hazard.

**Q: Can I reuse them next year?**
A: Yes, if you store them indoors. Pull them up in spring, wipe them down, and store in a dry place. The fiberglass and tape will handle multiple seasons. Leaving them exposed to UV year-round will degrade the tape within 18 months.
