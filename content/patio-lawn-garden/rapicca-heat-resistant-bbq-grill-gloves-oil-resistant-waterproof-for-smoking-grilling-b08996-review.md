## Where They Fall Short

Let's be honest about the limits. These are not welding gloves. The heat rating assumes brief contact, not sustained soaking. Dunking them in boiling water or grabbing a screaming-hot cast iron skillet straight from a 500°F oven will test their limits fast. The sizing also runs snug — several reviewers advised ordering a size up if you have larger hands or plan to wear glove liners underneath. And if you're doing fine detail work, like pulling a tiny hook off a smoker grate, the thick rubber reduces dexterity compared to thinner leather gloves.

## FAQ

**Can I use these to grab a cast iron skillet straight from the oven?**
Brief contact, yes. Holding it for extended time, no. The rating assumes you're moving something, not holding it steady. If you're transferring a hot pan to a stovetop, you're fine. If you're carrying it across the kitchen while it's still blazing hot, use a different tool.

**Are they truly waterproof?**
Yes, in practice. The rubber exterior is fully waterproof, and the cotton lining stays dry even when handling dripping meat or a submerged water pan. Just don't submerge them past the cuff.

**How do I clean them?**
Hand wash with soap and warm water, then air dry. The lining is not machine-washable, and tossing them in the washer will likely degrade the rubber over time.
