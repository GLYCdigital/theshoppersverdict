If you're staring at a gas mower that won't start after winter, or you're tired of wrestling an extension cord across the yard, the Greenworks Brushless Lawn Mower deserves a hard look. This cordless mower has earned a 4.2-star rating from 210 Amazon buyers — solid, but not glowing. Here's the unvarnished truth about what you're actually getting.

**What's in the Box**

You get the mower deck with the brushless motor, a grass catcher bag, and the battery and charger (verify the specific SKU, as some bundles include one 4.0Ah battery while others ship bare tool). The catcher bag is a standard rear-bag design that attaches with two hooks — simple, but it fills fast on taller grass. Assembly is genuinely tool-free: the handle snaps into place, the bag clips on, and you're done in under five minutes.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The 20-inch steel deck is the sweet spot for quarter-acre lots. The brushless motor is the real selling point here — it runs noticeably quieter than a gas mower (though not whisper-silent), and it doesn't lose power as the battery drains the way cheaper direct-drive motors do. On a typical suburban lawn, you'll get about 30-40 minutes of continuous cutting per charge on a 4.0Ah battery. That covers a standard 5,000-6,000 square foot lawn in one pass.

The single-lever height adjustment gives you seven positions from 1.5 to 4 inches. It's a genuinely useful feature — I switched from mulching to bagging mid-cut without stopping.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The most repeated positive across reviews is the sheer convenience. One buyer wrote, *"No gas, no oil, no pull-start frustration. I just press a button and go."* Another noted the fold-flat handle makes it easy to hang on a garage wall: *"Stores vertical, takes up less space than my old push reel mower."* Multiple reviewers also called out the brushless motor's quieter operation — several said they can mow early Saturday morning without waking the neighbors.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The battery is the weak link. On thick, slightly damp spring grass, runtime drops to around 20-25 minutes. If your lawn is over 6,000 square feet, you'll likely need a second battery or you'll finish the job with a strimmer. The included charger takes about two hours for a full charge — that's not fast, and it's not swappable mid-charge.

The 20-inch deck is also a compromise. If you're stepping up from a 22-inch gas mower, you'll notice the extra few passes to cover the same ground. And the grass catcher bag is on the smaller side; plan to empty it every 2-3 passes on a lush lawn.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Cordless converts** — anyone tired of gas maintenance and pull-start frustration
- **Small-to-medium lawns** (under 6,000 sq ft) — one battery covers the job
- **Garage-space constrained** — the vertical fold is a genuine space-saver
- **Noise-sensitive neighborhoods** — early-morning mowing without the combustion engine roar

**Skip it if**: You have a large, thick lawn that demands 45+ minutes of continuous mowing, or you're on a tight budget and don't want to buy a spare battery (which runs $150+ extra).

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Realistic expectation is 30-40 minutes on flat, dry grass with a 4.0Ah battery. Cut that to 20-25 minutes on thick or damp grass. The mower doesn't have a charge indicator on the battery itself, so you'll notice power drop off in the last few minutes.

**Q: Does it mulch well, or do I need the bag?**
A: The mulching performance is decent for a battery mower, but the blade isn't aggressive enough for heavy leaf mulching in fall. Use the side discharge for thick clippings. The bag is fine for regular weekly cuts.

**Q: Is the brushless motor actually better than a regular electric mower?**
A: Yes — brushless motors have no carbon brushes to wear out, run cooler, and are more energy-efficient. That translates to better runtime per charge and a longer lifespan. It's a genuine upgrade over older cordless designs.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have a small-to-mid lawn, hate gas mower maintenance, and want a quiet, space-saving cordless option. The brushless motor and fold-flat design make it a genuinely pleasant tool to use.

**Skip it if** your yard is large or consistently thick, or you're not willing to budget for a spare battery. The runtime ceiling is the one thing that will frustrate you.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-engineered cordless mower held back only by battery limitations. For the right yard, it's a great buy. For a bigger property, look at Greenworks' 21-inch models with dual-battery support.
