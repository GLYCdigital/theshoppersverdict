The Polaris Freedom is the pool cleaner equivalent of a wireless vacuum — no hose, no booster pump, no tangled cable snaking across your patio. That's the pitch, and when it works, it's genuinely liberating. But with a 3.6-star average across 180 ratings, the real story is more complicated. Here's what you need to know before dropping serious money on a cordless pool robot.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the Freedom unit itself, a charging dock with power adapter, and a small floating caddy for retrieval. There's no wall-mounted base station like you get with some premium robots — the charger sits on your patio or in a garage. The unit weighs roughly 15 pounds, which is manageable but not light. Setup is genuinely plug-and-play: charge it for 4-6 hours, drop it in the water, and it starts its cleaning cycle automatically.

**Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Honest**

When it's running correctly, the Freedom does solid work on typical pool grime. It scrubs the floor, climbs walls, and tackles the waterline with a rotating brush that leaves a visible clean line. Battery life lands in the 2-3 hour range depending on how much wall climbing it does — enough for most 15,000-25,000 gallon pools in a single cycle. It returns to the water's edge when done, which is a nice touch.

But here's where the Amazon reviews get uncomfortable. A significant portion of the 180 ratings are 1-star, and the pattern is consistent: units that stop working after 3-6 months. One buyer wrote, "Worked great for two months, then just stopped charging. Customer service was unhelpful." Another noted, "The motor sounds like it's struggling after a few weeks of use." This isn't a fluke — it's a reliability concern you need to weigh.

**What Owners Consistently Praise**

The cordless convenience is the headline, and it delivers. No hoses to coil, no pump connection, no wrestling a cable. As one verified buyer put it: "Finally a cleaner I can just toss in the pool and walk away. No setup, no fuss." Wall climbing is also better than many corded robots in this price range, and the waterline scrubbing is a feature you'd normally pay more for.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the reliability issues, the Freedom has a debris-size problem. It's designed for fine particles — dirt, sand, algae — not leaves, twigs, or acorns. If you have trees anywhere near your pool, you'll still be netting debris before this goes in. There's also no remote control or app scheduling; you physically drop it in each time you want it to run. For a product at this price point, that feels dated.

**Who Should Buy This**

The Freedom makes sense if you: have a smaller in-ground pool, hate dealing with hoses, and are willing to gamble on longevity — buy it with a warranty extension or from a retailer with a good return policy. It's also a solid fit for someone who only needs a "maintenance cleaner" that runs a few times a week on fine debris.

**Skip It If**

You have a pool surrounded by trees (leaf season will defeat it), you want app control or scheduling, or you expect 3+ years of trouble-free service. Given the mixed reliability reviews, this isn't a set-and-forget investment.

**FAQ**

*Does it climb stairs?* No. It handles walls and waterline but won't climb steps. You'll hand-clean those.

*How long does the battery actually last?* Real-world reports say 1.5-2.5 hours, less if the pool has heavy wall algae. A full charge takes about 5 hours.

*Is it OK for saltwater pools?* Yes, the unit is rated for saltwater use, though you should rinse it after heavy use to extend brush life.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You prioritize cordless convenience, have a manageable pool size, and accept that this may be a 1-2 year product rather than a decade-long investment.

**Skip it if:** Reliability is your top concern, you have heavy debris, or you want modern smart features. Consider a corded robot like the Dolphin line for proven longevity.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — When it works, it's excellent. When it doesn't, you'll wish you'd read the 1-star reviews before buying.
