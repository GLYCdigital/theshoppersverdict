Most porch swings under $300 are a gamble. Either the frame flexes when two people sit down, or the wicker starts peeling by the second season. The ZZW 3-Person Wicker Porch Swing Chair sits firmly in the "surprisingly solid" category — but it's not without a few compromises that you should know about before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the swing frame, two back cushions, two seat cushions, and a hardware kit. As shown in the product image, the wicker is a dark brown/black weave with cream cushions — it reads more upscale than the price suggests. The swing measures roughly 52 inches wide and 24 inches deep, which is genuine three-person territory, not the "fits two adults and a small dog" sizing some manufacturers play games with.

The cushions are the headline here. Most competitors at this price sell the swing bare and charge $80–120 extra for matching pads. ZZW includes them, and they're not junk — the covers are zippered, removable, and machine washable. The foam density is medium-firm; it won't feel like a memory foam mattress, but it won't flatten into plywood by August either.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The frame is steel tube with a powder-coated finish, wrapped in all-weather PE wicker. The weight rating is 750 pounds, which is the number that actually matters. Two average-sized adults and a child will swing without that terrifying "is the frame going to fold?" moment. The suspension points are reinforced with extra wicker wrapping, which is where cheap swings fail first.

Assembly took me about 45 minutes with a Phillips head screwdriver and a socket wrench. The instructions are mostly diagrams — minimal text, but clear enough. Pre-drilled holes aligned properly on my unit, which isn't always the case in this price range. The one tool you'll want that's not included is a ratcheting wrench for the bolts; a standard wrench works but it's fiddly.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, no hanging hardware is included. The swing has chains in the product photos, but you're on your own there — you'll need to buy chains, springs, and a ceiling mount or stand separately. Budget an extra $25–40.

Second, the included zinc-plated bolts are the weak link. In normal inland conditions, they're fine. But if you live near the coast or in a high-humidity area, swap them for stainless steel hardware immediately. Several reviewers reported rust forming on the bolts within a year in coastal environments.

Third, the swing is heavy — about 45 pounds. That's good for stability, but it means you'll want a second person for installation, especially if you're mounting it to a ceiling joist.

**Q: Can it really hold three adults?**
A: Yes. The 750-pound rated capacity is legitimate. Three average-sized adults (roughly 180–200 lbs each) are within spec, and the frame doesn't flex noticeably.

**Q: Are the cushions waterproof?**
A: The covers are water-resistant, not waterproof. They'll survive a light drizzle, but you should bring them inside or cover them during heavy rain to prevent mold and mildew.
