Let's be blunt: most fire pits under $150 are flimsy, rust-prone, and barely hold a flame. The Hykolity 35-inch model, with its 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews, claims to be different. After digging through the specs and what owners actually say, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the fire pit bowl, legs, two cooking grates, a charcoal pan, a spark screen, and a fire poker. That's a genuinely complete package — most competitors make you buy the cooking grates separately. The bowl is 35 inches in diameter and 12 inches deep, which is substantial enough for a real fire, not just a decorative flame. The steel is 0.8mm thick with a high-temperature powder coating, which is the key spec to check here — thinner steel warps and rusts out within a season.

## Performance and Build Quality

In real-world use, this pit performs like a piece of equipment, not a toy. The dual cooking grates are the standout feature: you can use one for direct grilling and the other to keep food warm, or flip them both over for a full cooking surface. Owners consistently mention this in reviews. One buyer wrote, "We cooked burgers and hot dogs for eight people at once — the second grate kept the buns warm. Perfect setup."

The charcoal pan sits at the bottom of the bowl, which serves two purposes: it lifts the fire for better airflow, and it makes cleanup dramatically easier since ash collects in a removable tray. That's a thoughtful design choice that most budget fire pits skip entirely.

The spark screen is functional but not perfect. It's held in place by gravity and a small lip, so in gusty conditions it can shift. Several reviewers noted this, with one saying, "The screen rattles in the wind. I put a brick on it when it's breezy."

As for the fire bowl itself, the 35-inch width gives you a genuinely large burn area. You can fit standard 16-inch logs without cutting them, and the depth keeps the flames contained. The powder coating held up well in reviews — no complaints about peeling or bubbling after several months of use, which is a common failure point on cheaper pits.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest gripe. The legs bolt to the bowl with small nuts and bolts, and several reviewers mentioned stripped threads — you'll want a socket wrench, not a basic screwdriver. Plan for 45 minutes and an extra set of hands to hold the bowl steady while you tighten everything.

The lack of a weather cover is another real omission. The powder coating is good, but it's not impervious to standing water. If you live somewhere with regular rain, budget another $20–$30 for a cover or plan to move it under shelter.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the backyard entertainer who wants a fire pit that does double duty as a grill. If you host small gatherings and want to cook over an open flame while keeping food warm, the dual-grate setup justifies the price on its own. It's also a solid choice for first-time fire pit owners who want a complete kit without researching and buying accessories separately.

Skip it if you're a serious griller who needs precise temperature control — this is a wood or charcoal fire pit, not a propane grill. And if you're mainly looking for a decorative piece, the utilitarian design won't win any beauty contests.

## FAQ

**Is the charcoal pan necessary, or can I burn wood directly in the bowl?**
You can burn wood directly, but use the pan. It improves airflow for a hotter, cleaner burn and makes ash cleanup a five-minute job instead of an hour of scrubbing.

**Can this be left outside year-round?**
Not without protection. The powder coating resists rust, but standing water will eventually find a weak point. Cover it or store it under a patio roof during wet months.

**How hot does the cooking grate get? Can I use cast iron on it?**
The grates get hot enough to sear meat, and yes, cast iron skillets work fine on them. The steel is thick enough to handle the weight without bowing.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a multi-functional fire pit that cooks as well as it burns, and you value getting a complete kit without nickel-and-diming for accessories.

**Skip it if:** You need something you can assemble solo in under 20 minutes, or you want a decorative showpiece rather than a working outdoor appliance.

The Hykolity 35-inch fire pit earns its 4.5-star rating. It's not perfect — the assembly and loose spark screen are legitimate annoyances — but for the price, the dual cooking grates, charcoal pan, and sturdy steel construction make it one of the better values in this category. It's a buy.
