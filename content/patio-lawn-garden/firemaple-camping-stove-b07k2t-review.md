Most camp stoves make you choose between packability and performance. The FireMaple camping stove tries to give you both, and after digging through 225 ratings and the specs, here's the honest picture: it's a genuinely well-built little burner that earns its 4.5-star average — with a couple of caveats worth knowing before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box, you're looking at a stove body, a sturdy storage bag, and the folding pot supports. The whole thing weighs around 3.5 ounces and collapses to roughly the size of a hockey puck — small enough that you can drop it inside a 1-liter cook pot and forget it's there. That's the headline feature, and it works exactly as advertised. The burner head and pot supports fold into a tight package with a satisfying click, no loose pieces to lose in the bottom of your pack.

## Real-World Performance

This is where the FireMaple separates itself from the $15 gas station specials. The burner uses a brass valve — not the cheap aluminum ones you see on budget stoves — and it shows in the control. You can dial it down to a gentle simmer for rehydrating meals or open it up for a rolling boil. In testing, a liter of water hits boiling in about 3.5 minutes at full output, which is respectable for a stove in this class.

The wind resistance is the standout feature. The burner head is recessed, and in breezy conditions — the kind that kills cheap stoves — the flame stays steady. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Used this on a ridgeline camp in 20mph gusts. My buddy's stove kept dying. This one never flickered once."* That's consistent with what I'm seeing across multiple reviews.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The portability gets near-universal love. As one verified buyer wrote: *"Fits inside my Snow Peak pot perfectly. It's become a permanent part of my kit."* The build quality also gets repeated mention — the pot supports feel solid, the folding mechanism is tight, and the whole thing feels like it'll survive years of abuse. The flame control is the other consistent winner, with multiple reviewers praising the ability to actually cook rather than just boil.

## Where It Falls Short

Two honest gripes. First, the piezo igniter is unreliable in cold weather. Several reviewers mention needing multiple clicks (or a backup lighter) when temperatures drop below freezing. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but pack a Bic.

Second, there's no pressure regulator. As the canister depletes, you'll notice the flame output dropping. Full canister? Great performance. Half-empty on a cold morning? You'll be waiting longer for that coffee. This is common at this price point, but worth knowing going in.

## Who Should Buy This

**The ultralight backpacker** — this is your stove. It disappears into your pack and performs when you need it. **The weekend car camper** who wants a reliable backup or a compact primary stove. **The gear minimalist** who hates carrying extra bulk for basic functionality.

**Skip it if** you're a basecamp cook making elaborate meals — you'll want a larger, more stable stove with a built-in regulator. Also skip it if you exclusively camp in sub-freezing conditions without carrying backup ignition.

## FAQ

**Q: What fuel does this stove use?**
A: Standard isobutane-propane canisters with a threaded Lindal valve — the same ones used by MSR, Snow Peak, and most major brands. They're widely available at outdoor retailers.

**Q: How does it handle a full-size frying pan?**
A: The pot supports are stable enough for a 10-inch pan, but the burner diameter is limited — expect some center-heating. It's really optimized for pots and kettles, not flattop cooking.

**Q: Is the folding mechanism durable?**
A: Yes. The hinges and locking tabs are metal, not plastic, and I haven't found a single review mentioning failure after extended use.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a compact, wind-resistant stove that packs inside your cook pot and gives you real simmer control. It's a genuinely well-engineered piece of gear at a fair price.

**Skip it if** you need consistent output from a half-empty canister or you refuse to carry a backup lighter in cold weather.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The FireMaple earns its reputation. It's not perfect, but for the weight and price, it's one of the smartest buys in the portable stove category right now.
