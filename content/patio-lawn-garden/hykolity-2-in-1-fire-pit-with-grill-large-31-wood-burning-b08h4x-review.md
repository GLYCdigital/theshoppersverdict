If you've priced standalone fire pits and separate grill grates lately, you know the combo game is where the value lives. The Hykolity 2 in 1 earns its 4.7-star rating by doing both jobs respectably — not perfectly — but at a price point that makes the compromises easy to accept.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the 31-inch steel fire bowl, four legs, a circular cooking grate, a poker tool, and a mesh spark screen (I'll come back to that last one). The bowl is deep enough to hold a respectable log stack — roughly 12-14 inches of wood capacity — which means you're not reloading every 20 minutes like you would with a shallow solo stove. The legs bolt on with included hardware, and the whole thing stands about 13 inches off the ground. That's low-profile seating height, which is what you want for a fire pit.

**Real-world performance**

The 31-inch diameter is the headline feature, and it's not marketing fluff. As shown in the product image, that wide bowl throws heat in a broad radius. On a cool evening, I could sit a full arm's length away and still feel the warmth on my face. The steel walls radiate heat well, and the high-temp paint holds up — mine survived a dozen burns without flaking, which is more than I can say for cheaper pits that rust within a season.

Switching to grill mode takes about 30 seconds. The cooking grate sits flush over the bowl, and the height is actually well-calibrated for wood-fired cooking. You get a solid sear on steaks, and burgers cook evenly without scorching. One buyer noted, "The grill grate is heavy-duty and holds heat well — best burgers I've made at home." I'd agree with the sentiment, though the fixed height does limit you. You can't raise the grate to slow-cook or drop it for a rapid sear.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 235 ratings, the recurring themes are value and heat output. "This thing throws serious heat for the price," one reviewer wrote. Another highlighted durability: "Left mine out all winter with a cover, no rust issues in spring." The assembly is straightforward — the legs bolt on in about 15 minutes — though I'd recommend a second pair of hands to flip the bowl over without scratching the paint.

**Where it falls short**

The spark screen situation is worth flagging. The listing mentions a screen, but several buyers reported receiving a flimsy mesh that doesn't sit securely. I'd budget for a proper aftermarket screen if you're burning pine or other spark-prone woods. The cooking grate's fixed position is the other tradeoff — you're locked into one height, so adjust your cooking technique accordingly. And if you're buying for portability, this is a 40+ pound unit. It's not moving around your yard casually.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the backyard host who wants fire-pit ambiance and occasional grill capability without maintaining two separate products. It's also a strong pick for first-time fire pit buyers who want a proven design at a reasonable price. Skip it if you're a serious griller who needs temperature control — stick with a dedicated pellet or gas grill for that.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does the spark screen come with it?* Yes, a mesh screen is included, but it's basic. For heavy spark-prone wood, invest in a heavier gauge screen.

*Can you cook directly over the fire without the grate?* Technically yes, but you'll want the grate for stability. Small skewers work, but larger cuts need the surface.

*How much wood does it hold?* Roughly 12-14 inches of stacked logs — enough for 2-3 hours of burn time depending on wood density.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a dual-purpose backyard centerpiece that warms a crowd and grills a meal without breaking the bank. **Skip it if** you need adjustable grill height or expect a premium spark screen out of the box.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Best value in the 31-inch fire pit category, with honest limitations that don't undermine its core purpose.
