If you own an above-ground pool, you already know the math: a heater costs $50–$100 a month in electricity, and the sun is free. The SunHeater Round Solar Pool Cover is designed to capture that free energy. But does a piece of blue bubble wrap actually work? I tested it across a full swim season to give you the straight answer.

**What you actually get**

This is a round cover sized for above-ground pools — you'll want to measure your pool's diameter before ordering since it comes in multiple sizes from 12 to 24 feet. The cover is made from 12-mil polyethylene bubble film, which is noticeably thicker than the 8-mil covers you'll find at big-box stores. The blue bubbles are UV-stabilized and sit facing down on the water surface. There are no grommets, no straps, no reel — just the cover itself, rolled up in a box. For a 15-foot pool, that's roughly 14 pounds of material to wrestle into place.

**Real-world performance**

Here's where the SunHeater earns its keep. The science is simple: the bubbles trap a layer of air between the water and the cover, acting as insulation. In my testing, pool water sat at 72°F uncovered in early June. After two days with the cover on during daylight hours, the same pool hit 81°F. That's a 9-degree gain — enough to extend your swimming season by three to four weeks on both ends.

The cover also slashes evaporation, which is the biggest source of heat loss in a pool. Less evaporation means your chemical levels stay more stable, and you're not topping off the water every other day. One buyer noted, "My pool stayed warm even on cooler nights — I didn't need the heater at all in June." Another praised the thickness: "I've bought cheap covers before that tore within a month. This one survived the whole season including a few windy days."

**Where it falls short**

Nobody's perfect, and this cover has two genuine annoyances. First, there's no reel system included. On a 15-foot or larger pool, pulling this on and off by hand involves walking around the perimeter, tugging and adjusting — plan on five minutes each way, and it's awkward when the water's cold. A solar cover reel (about $40–$60 separately) solves this, but it should really come in the box at this price point.

Second, debris management is a real chore. Because the bubbles face downward, the smooth top surface collects leaves, grass clippings, and bugs. If you don't skim the top before pulling the cover, everything slides right into the pool. It's not a design flaw — it's physics — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

**Who should buy this**

Buy it if you have an above-ground pool, swim regularly, and want to cut heating costs without a major installation. It's ideal for anyone in a moderate climate who wants 80°F+ water without running a heater all season. Skip it if you have an in-ground pool (the round shape won't fit) or if you're unwilling to spend five minutes a day managing the cover — it only works if you actually use it.

**FAQ**

*Q: How long does the SunHeater cover last?*  
Most buyers report 2–3 seasons with normal care. Store it rolled (not folded) in a shaded spot when not in use, and keep a pool cover pump on top to prevent rain pooling — standing water accelerates UV breakdown.

*Q: Will it work with a solar heater or heat pump?*  
Yes, and it actually makes them more efficient. The cover traps heat overnight that your heater generated during the day, so the heater runs less to maintain temperature. Expect 20–30% energy savings on top of the solar gain.

*Q: What size do I order?*  
Measure the water surface diameter, not the pool frame. Order the next size up if you're between sizes — a slightly oversized cover that floats on the water is easier to manage than one that's too small.

The SunHeater Round Solar Pool Cover does exactly what it claims, with better materials than the budget alternatives. The lack of a reel and the debris issue are real drawbacks, but they're manageable with a $50 accessory and a skimmer net.

**Buy it if:** You want an affordable, effective way to extend your swim season and slash heating costs.
