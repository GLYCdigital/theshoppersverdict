## The "Made in USA" claim actually holds up here

Most flags on Amazon slap "Made in USA" on the listing and ship you something stitched in a Vietnamese factory. This one doesn't. Every component — the nylon, the thread, the embroidered stars — is sourced and assembled domestically. If you care about where your money goes, that alone justifies the premium over the $12 flags clogging the search results.

## What's actually in the package

You get the flag itself. That's it. No pole, no grommets, no mounting hardware. The flag arrives folded with a simple cardboard header. The fabric is a 200-denier nylon — noticeably heavier and stiffer than the cheap polyester flags you'll find at big-box stores. It has that crisp, professional snap when the wind catches it.

The stars are the headline feature. They're embroidered — dense, tight stitching that creates real texture and depth. In the product image, you can see the clean contrast between the deep blue field and the white stars. Up close, it looks like a flag you'd see outside a government building, not something from a discount bin.

## Real-world performance and build quality

I've been flying this flag for about eight months now, and the difference from cheaper flags is obvious. The fly end — the edge that takes the most abuse — is double-stitched and reinforced. That's where most flags die first, and this one shows no fraying yet.

The embroidered stars have held their color through direct sun exposure. The blue field hasn't faded to that washed-out gray you see on budget flags after a single summer. The stripes are sewn, not printed, so there's no risk of the design cracking or peeling as the fabric flexes in the wind.

One thing to flag (pun intended): this is a heavier fabric. On a tall, rigid pole, it flies beautifully. On a cheap 5-foot collapsible pole or a flimsy garden stand, the extra weight can make it hang limply in light breezes and put stress on the pole in strong gusts. Match it to a proper pole and you'll be happy.

## Where it falls short

The missing grommets are the most common complaint, and it's a fair one. For a flag at this price point, not including brass grommets feels like an oversight. You'll need to buy a set separately or use a clip-style flagpole mount. It's a minor hassle, but worth knowing before you buy.

The heavier fabric cuts both ways. If you live in a consistently windy area, this flag will hold up better than lighter options. But on a lightweight pole, it won't fly as proudly in gentle breezes. That's a tradeoff, not a flaw — just match it to your setup.
