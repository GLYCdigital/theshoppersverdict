**What you get**

The 2-pack contains two 32-ounce ready-to-use bottles with attached spray wands. Each can covers roughly 1,500 square feet, so the pair handles about 3,000 square feet total — enough for a typical quarter-acre lot with a patio, garden beds, and a perimeter fence line. There's no hose attachment required; the trigger sprayer delivers a fine mist that coats foliage, grass, and outdoor surfaces. One can typically lasts several applications if you're spot-treating, or about one full treatment if you're covering the whole yard.

**Performance and build quality**

The active ingredient is permethrin, a synthetic pyrethroid that targets the nervous system of insects. It kills mosquitoes, fleas, ticks, and listed ants on contact — not just repels them. That's the key difference from citronella candles or ultrasonic gadgets. In practice, you'll notice the mosquito activity drop significantly within minutes of spraying, especially around shrubs, under decks, and along fence lines where they like to rest.

The sprayer itself is well-designed — the wand locks in place, the trigger doesn't fatigue your hand mid-treatment, and the nozzle produces a consistent mist without clogging. One caveat: permethrin is a contact killer, not a barrier treatment. Rain, heavy dew, or even a thorough lawn watering will wash it away. Expect to reapply every 2–4 weeks depending on your climate and how much rain you're getting.

**Where it falls short**

Honest tradeoffs exist. The chemical smell is noticeable for a few hours after application — it's not overpowering like some commercial-grade products, but if you want to host a dinner party immediately after spraying, you'll want to plan ahead. Some reviewers mentioned that it's less effective on *very* dense vegetation or if you don't spray the undersides of leaves where mosquitoes actually rest. And if you have a vegetable garden, you'll want to avoid spraying directly on edible plants, since permethrin isn't labeled for that use.

**FAQ**

A: Expect 2–4 weeks of effectiveness, less if you get heavy rain. It kills on contact, so it doesn't leave a residual barrier like some professional sprays.

**Q: Is it safe around pets after it dries?**  
A: Once the spray is fully dry (usually 1–2 hours), it's generally safe for dogs and cats. That said, keep pets away during application and avoid spraying directly on animal bedding or food bowls.

**Q: Can I use it indoors?**  
A: No — this is strictly for outdoor use. The label is explicit about that, and using it inside risks overexposure to the chemicals.
