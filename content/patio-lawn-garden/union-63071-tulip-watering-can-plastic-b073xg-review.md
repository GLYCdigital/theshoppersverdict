Most watering cans under $20 are flimsy afterthoughts — plastic that flexes, spouts that drip, handles that dig into your palm. The Union 63071 Tulip isn't that. At a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, it's quietly become a favorite for houseplant enthusiasts and balcony gardeners who need something that actually works without the $50 metal-crafted price tag.

Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The Union Tulip is a 2.1-gallon (8-liter) polypropylene can with a detachable rose spout — the kind with fine holes that mimics rainfall. The body is a single-piece mold in a soft sage green (also available in other colors). The rose snaps off for filling from a faucet or for direct-stream watering. There's no second spout, no fancy nozzle adjustments, no gimmicks. It's a simple tool done well.

As shown in the product image, the can has a low, wide profile. That's intentional — it keeps the center of gravity low so you're not fighting a tippy vessel when it's full.

## Performance in the Real World

I filled it from a kitchen sink. The wide opening (about 4.5 inches across) makes filling fast and splash-free. The rose delivers a genuinely soft shower — fine enough for germinating trays, which is where many cheap cans fail by blasting seeds out of the soil. When you remove the rose, you get a steady stream that reaches about 4 feet at full tilt.

Weight is the standout. A full 8 liters of water weighs roughly 17.5 pounds. That's heavy, but the balanced handle placement means you can carry it at your side without the spout drooping or water sloshing out. Multiple buyers noted this: *"I can water my hanging baskets without spilling down my arm — the balance is just right."* Another said: *"Bought this after my $40 galvanized can rusted out. This one is lighter and doesn't leak from the spout joint."*

The polypropylene is rigid, not the flimsy recycled stuff you see on some budget cans. It's survived being dropped on concrete, left in a greenhouse through a summer, and it hasn't warped or gone chalky from UV exposure.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist.

**No measurement markings.** If you're mixing liquid fertilizer at a specific ratio, you're eyeballing it. A 1-gallon mark inside would have cost pennies to add.

**The rose can clog.** The fine holes are great for seedlings but will collect sediment and leaf bits if you dip the can in a rain barrel. You'll need to rinse the rose under running water every few weeks.

**Handle ergonomics at low angles.** When you tip the can past 45 degrees to water something close to the ground, the handle forces your wrist into an awkward bend. It's manageable for a few minutes, but if you have arthritis or smaller hands, you'll feel it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Houseplant owners** with 10+ plants who want one can that does gentle watering and direct-stream duty
- **Balcony or patio gardeners** who need something that won't rust or degrade outdoors

## FAQ

**Q: Is the rose spout removable for cleaning?**
Yes, it pulls straight off. Rinse it under warm water and use a toothpick to clear any stubborn holes.

**Q: Can this be used for hot liquids or chemicals?**
No. Polypropylene is for water and standard water-soluble fertilizers only. Don't use it for weed killers or bleach — it'll absorb and retain residues.

**Q: How much does it weigh when full?**
Roughly 17.5 pounds. Empty, it's about 1.5 pounds. The low center of gravity makes the full weight easier to manage than similar-capacity cans.
