When you need to boil 10 gallons of water for a crawfish boil or crank out 50 ears of sweet corn for a family reunion, your kitchen stove isn't going to cut it. The ARC 55,000 BTU propane burner promises restaurant-grade output at a backyard-friendly price point. After digging through 229 ratings and the specs, here's what you actually need to know before adding one to your cart.

**What Shows Up at Your Door**

The box contains the burner head, three detachable steel legs, a 3-foot regulator hose with a standard 20-pound tank connector, and a flimsy-looking windscreen that's more decorative than functional. Assembly takes about two minutes — the legs thread in by hand and lock with a satisfying click. The whole unit weighs in around 8 pounds, so it's portable enough to toss in a truck bed for tailgating, though it's not exactly backpacking gear.

The cast iron burner head is the star of the show. It's a wide, four-pronged design that distributes flame evenly across a large surface area. The pot support is generously sized — you can fit a 30-quart pot with room to spare without worrying about it teetering.

**Real-World Performance**

This thing gets serious quickly. In testing, five gallons of cold water went from tap temperature to a rolling boil in roughly nine minutes at full output. That's the kind of performance you'd expect from a commercial unit, not a $50 camping stove. The flame is fierce — you can hear the whoosh when you crack the valve open.

The adjustable valve gives you decent control across the range, but it's not a precision instrument. The low end is touchy; getting a gentle simmer requires patience and small adjustments. If you're planning to fry turkeys or boil seafood, that won't matter. If you're trying to keep a pot of chili at a low bubble for hours, you'll be fiddling with the knob more than you'd like.

**What Buyers Are Saying**

The 4.6-star average is backed up by consistent praise for raw power. One reviewer put it plainly: "This burner replaced my expensive Bayou Classic and performs just as well for half the cost." Another noted the build quality surprised them: "The steel is thicker than I expected for the price, and the legs feel solid when locked."

The most common complaint, beyond the missing ignition, is the short hose. Several buyers mentioned needing to buy a longer one to reach from their tank to the cooking area. A few also flagged that the paint on the burner head chips after a few uses — purely cosmetic, but worth noting if you're particular about appearances.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the downsides. There's no piezo ignition, so you're fumbling with a lighter every time you fire it up. The included windscreen is almost useless in anything beyond a light breeze — it's thin aluminum that bends easily and doesn't fully wrap the burner. The 3-foot hose is a real limitation if your propane tank placement is awkward.

Also, this is a high-output burner, which means it drinks fuel. A full 20-pound tank will get you through a solid afternoon of boiling, but don't expect it to last a weekend of heavy use.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is a no-brainer for home brewers, turkey fryers, and anyone who does serious outdoor cooking in volume. It's also a solid pick for preppers who want an emergency cooking option that can handle large pots. If you're a casual camper who just needs to boil water for coffee, you're paying for way more power than you need — a smaller, lighter stove would serve you better.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with a standard 20-pound propane tank?**
Yes, the included regulator hose connects directly to the standard BBQ-style tank valve. No adapter needed.

**Q: Can I use this on a wooden deck or patio?**
You shouldn't. The radiant heat from the burner is intense, and the legs are bare steel. Place it on concrete, gravel, or bare ground — never directly on wood or composite decking without a heat shield.

**Q: What size pot can this hold safely?**
The pot support spans about 12 inches across, and the legs provide enough height for pots up to 32 quarts. A 100,000 BTU-rated turkey fryer pot fits without issue. Anything larger starts to feel top-heavy.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need serious heat output for large-batch cooking and don't want to drop $150+ on a premium brand. The ARC delivers 90% of the performance at half the price.

**Skip it if** you require precise temperature control, want push-button ignition, or you're working with limited space — the footprint is larger than you might expect.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The missing ignition and short hose are minor annoyances, not deal-breakers. For raw power and value, this is one of the best propane burners in its price range.
