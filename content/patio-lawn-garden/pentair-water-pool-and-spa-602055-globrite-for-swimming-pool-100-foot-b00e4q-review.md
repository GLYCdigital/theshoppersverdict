If you're staring at a dark pool with a dead Pentair AmerLite fixture, the Globrite 602055 looks like the obvious fix. It's the same brand, same niche, supposedly a direct swap. But at this price point, "obvious" deserves a second look.

The 602055 is a 12-volt, 27-watt LED pool light rated at 1,500 lumens, designed to replace older incandescent Pentair fixtures. The package includes the light assembly with a 100-foot cord, a gasket, and mounting screws. That's it — no transformer, no junction box. As shown in the product image, it's a compact, low-profile white ring that sits nearly flush with the pool wall, which is a nice upgrade over bulbous older housings.

**Installation reality check**

If you're handy with pool equipment, the swap is straightforward — once the water level is below the niche. The 100-foot cord is genuinely useful; several buyers noted it let them run a clean path to the transformer without splicing, which matters because splices in pool lighting are failure points. One reviewer put it plainly: "The cable length saved me from having to replace the entire conduit run." That's real value if you're in a retrofit situation.

But here's the catch: this light *requires* the Pentair transformer (sold separately) and uses a proprietary connector. You cannot wire it to a standard 120-volt pool light circuit or use a generic 12-volt transformer. If you're replacing an old incandescent Pentair system, you're likely fine. If you're switching brands, factor in the transformer cost and the fact that you're locked into Pentair's ecosystem for future replacements.

**Performance: The good and the cloudy**

When it works, the Globrite is excellent. The white light is crisp and bright — noticeably cleaner than the warm yellow of old incandescents. Multiple owners describe it as "daylight in the pool" and praise the even distribution without dark spots. For a 20-foot pool, one fixture is genuinely sufficient.

The problem is longevity. A significant cluster of reviews mention moisture getting inside the lens within 6 to 18 months. One buyer wrote: "After 8 months, water vapor collected in the lens and now it looks foggy." Another said: "The gasket failed, and I had to take it apart to dry it out." That's not a fluke — it's a recurring pattern in the review data. Pentair's warranty covers this, but the hassle of draining the pool or diving to remove the fixture is real.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the moisture issues, the biggest complaint is value. At roughly double the price of comparable aftermarket LED pool lights (like the ColorMatch or niche-compatible alternatives), you're paying for the Pentair name and the clean fit. You're not getting better durability — the 3.7-star average reflects that. Also note: this is a white-light-only fixture. No color-changing modes, no remote. If you want colored lighting, look elsewhere.

**Who should buy this**

Buy it if: You already have Pentair pool equipment, you're replacing a dead AmerLite, and you want a no-brainer fit without adapters. The install is clean, the light is bright, and the 100-foot cord solves real-world routing problems.

Skip it if: You're on a tight budget, you want color options, or you're replacing a non-Pentair fixture. The proprietary transformer requirement and moisture track record make the premium hard to justify. Also skip if you live in a freeze-thaw climate — the seal stress seems worse there based on review patterns.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this replace my old Pentair AmerLite directly?**
A: Yes, if you have the same niche size. It bolts into the existing housing with the included screws. You'll need the matching Pentair transformer, though — it won't work with your old 120V wiring.

**Q: Can I install it myself?**
A: If you're comfortable with basic electrical work and can drain the pool below the niche, yes. Budget 2-3 hours. If you're not sure, hire a pro — pool light wiring mistakes can be dangerous.

**Q: How long does it actually last?**
A: The LED chip is rated for years, but the seal is the weak point. Realistic expectation is 1-3 years before moisture appears, based on buyer reports. Pentair's warranty covers it, but you'll eat the labor.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're already in the Pentair ecosystem and want a bright, clean white light with a genuinely useful 100-foot cord. **Skip it if** you want color, value, or long-term reliability without warranty claims.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A great light when it's sealed properly, but the moisture issues and proprietary lock-in keep it from being a confident recommendation.
