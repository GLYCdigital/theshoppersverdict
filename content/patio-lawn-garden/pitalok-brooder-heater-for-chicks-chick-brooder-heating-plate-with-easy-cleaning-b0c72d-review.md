**Bottom Line Up Front:** If you're tired of replacing heat lamp bulbs, worrying about fire risk in your coop, or dealing with chicks that pile up in corners, the Pitalok brooder heater is a genuinely safer alternative. It's not perfect — the height adjustment mechanism feels cheaper than the price suggests — but for the 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, this is a solid mid-tier radiant heat plate that most backyard flock owners will appreciate.

## What You Actually Get

The Pitalok arrives mostly assembled. You get the heating plate itself, two adjustable legs that snap into the base, and a standard power cord. No thermometer, no humidity gauge, no brooder ring — just the heater. The plate surface is roughly the size of a standard sheet pan, which means it comfortably covers about 15–20 chicks depending on their age. As shown in the product image, the legs raise the plate anywhere from a couple inches to about 8 inches off the ground, which is the critical adjustment range for the first 4–6 weeks.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The biggest difference between this and a heat lamp is the physics. A lamp radiates heat in all directions — hot, dry air that stresses chicks and creates dangerous temperature swings. The Pitalok uses a radiant plate that warms from above, just like a hen's body. Chicks naturally tuck underneath when they're cold and wander out when they're warm. That self-regulating behavior is why so many buyers report calmer, healthier chicks.

One buyer noted: *"My chicks figured it out in about 10 minutes. They sleep under it in a little pile and come out to eat and drink like they're on vacation."* That matches the consensus across the 220 ratings — the learning curve for chicks is nearly zero.

The heating element warms up in about 10 minutes and stays consistent. There's no automatic shutoff, which is fine for continuous brooding but worth knowing if you're the forgetful type. Power draw is minimal — around 60 watts — so running it for weeks won't wreck your electric bill.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The safety angle comes up constantly. No exposed bulb means no fire risk from knocked-over lamps, no shattered glass, no burned bedding. For anyone brooding in a dry, dusty barn, that's a meaningful upgrade. The easy-clean surface also gets consistent love — chick droppings wipe off with a damp cloth, no scrubbing around a bulb fixture.

The height adjustability is the other standout. You can raise the plate as chicks feather out, which extends the usable life well past the first month. Several reviewers mentioned using it through 6 weeks without needing to switch to a heat lamp.

## Where It Falls Short

The height adjustment mechanism is the weak point. The knobs are plastic and several reviewers report they feel like they'll strip if you crank them too hard. One said: *"Works great, but the adjustment screws are cheap. I tightened one a bit much and it cracked. Still functions, but I'm careful now."* That's a fair warning — handle the legs gently.

Also, there's no included thermometer. You'll need to buy a separate brooder thermometer and check temperatures under the plate at chick level. That's an extra $8–10 and a minor hassle, but essential for getting the height right.

Finally, it's not designed for large hatches. If you're brooding 50+ chicks at once, you'd need multiple units or a commercial-grade heater.

## Who Should Buy This

## FAQ

**Q: What wattage is this, and how hot does it get?**
A: It draws about 60 watts. The plate surface itself gets warm but not scalding — the key is adjusting the height so the area under the plate stays around 95–100°F in the first week. Lower for warmer, raise for cooler.

**Q: Can I leave it on 24/7?**

**Q: How do I clean it?**
