Oscillating sprinklers are a dime a dozen, but most of them share the same fatal flaw: plastic nozzles that clog, strip, or spray unevenly within a season. The Meekamar takes a different approach with 18 solid brass nozzles—a material choice that immediately sets it apart in the under-$50 category. After digging through 215 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the sprinkler unit itself, a metal base plate, and the necessary connectors for standard garden hoses. No tools required for assembly—the base snaps on in about ten seconds. The oscillator bar spans roughly 16 inches, and the whole unit sits about 5 inches tall when deployed. It's compact enough to store in a garage shelf but substantial enough that it won't blow over in a moderate breeze.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature is those 18 brass nozzles. Unlike plastic or rubber alternatives, brass resists mineral buildup from hard water—the primary cause of uneven spray patterns in cheaper units. The coverage claim is up to 4,500 square feet, which translates to a standard 50-foot by 90-foot lawn. In practice, buyers report strong, consistent water distribution across the full sweep, with no dry spots at the edges.

The oscillation mechanism is gear-driven, which means smoother motion and fewer breakdowns than spring-based systems. The width-adjustment slides on the spray bar are genuinely useful—you can narrow the pattern to water a garden bed without soaking the fence line. Directional control lets you angle the spray up to 25 degrees, which helps clear tall grass or reach slightly raised beds.

One buyer noted: *"I've had this for two months of daily use and the spray pattern hasn't changed. My old Orbit started skipping within three weeks."* That durability impression is consistent across the reviews, with multiple users reporting a full season of trouble-free operation.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires mentioning the connection joints. The brass nozzles are excellent, but the plastic fittings where the hose attaches and where the oscillator bar meets the base are the weak points. Several reviewers mentioned needing to be careful when tightening the hose connection to avoid stripping the threads. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth a gentle hand during setup.

There's also no built-in timer. For a product in this price range, that's expected, but if you want automated watering while you're at work, budget for a separate hose timer. And while the 4,500 square foot rating is accurate, that's at ideal water pressure—if your home has low pressure (under 40 PSI), expect reduced coverage to around 3,000 square feet.

## Who Should Buy This

This sprinkler fits three buyer profiles well:

- **The lawn perfectionist** who wants even coverage without constantly repositioning the sprinkler. The 18-nozzle design delivers a uniform curtain of water.
- **The value seeker** who's tired of replacing $15 sprinklers every spring. The brass nozzles and gear drive justify the modest premium.
- **The water-conscious gardener** who needs precise width control to avoid drenching patios or driveways.

It's less ideal for anyone with a complex, multi-zone irrigation setup—this is a single-zone tool. And if you have very hard water, you'll still want to occasionally clean the nozzles with vinegar, though far less frequently than with plastic units.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this sprinkler work with low water pressure?**
A: It functions below 40 PSI but coverage drops noticeably. At 30 PSI, expect roughly 2,500–3,000 square feet. For best results, use a short, wide-diameter hose to minimize pressure loss.

**Q: How do I clean the brass nozzles if they get clogged?**
A: Soak the oscillator bar in white vinegar for 30 minutes, then rinse. A paperclip can clear stubborn debris. Avoid metal brushes—they'll scratch the brass.

**Q: Can I leave it outside over winter?**
A: Technically yes, but you shouldn't. Drain it fully and store indoors. Brass handles cold well, but the plastic fittings and internal gears will crack if water freezes inside.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a durable oscillating sprinkler with even coverage and don't want to replace it every season. The brass nozzles are a genuine upgrade, and the adjustability makes it versatile for both lawns and garden beds.

**Skip it if** you need automated scheduling without buying extra accessories, or if you're on a tight budget and only need to water a small patch—a basic spike sprinkler would suffice.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, well-built sprinkler that earns its keep. The plastic fittings keep it from a perfect score, but for most homeowners, this is the last oscillating sprinkler you'll need to buy for several years.
