**What's in the jug**

You get 5 pounds of wettable dispersible granules (WDG) in a foil-lined bag inside a sturdy jug. That's enough for roughly 27,500 square feet at the standard 0.83 oz per 1,000 sq ft rate — or about 13,750 sq ft at the higher 1.5 oz rate for heavy crabgrass pressure. The math matters here: a typical 1-acre lawn needs about 3.6 pounds per application at the standard rate. This one jug covers you for a full season plus a fall split if you dose correctly.

**Real-world mixing and application**

WDG formulations are more forgiving than wettable powders, but they're not foolproof. You'll want to premix the granules in a small container with water before dumping into your sprayer. Add the product to water slowly while agitating. A few reviewers noted clumping issues when they rushed this step or used hot water from a hose-end sprayer. Use cool water, mix thoroughly, and you'll get a clean suspension that won't clog nozzles.

The product labels rates clearly, but there's no scoop included. Invest in a cheap gram scale — you'll need it for split applications. Many lawn care veterans run a spring application at 0.5-0.6 oz per 1,000 sq ft, then a follow-up in late summer at the same rate. This staggered approach extends control well into fall.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The cost-per-application math is the headline. One reviewer put it plainly: "Same chemical as the big brand, less than half the price. I've used it for three seasons and my lawn has never been cleaner." Another noted, "The 5lb size means I don't run out mid-season like I did with the 2.5lb jugs."

Crabgrass control is where this product shines. Prodiamine is one of the most effective pre-emergents for crabgrass, foxtail, and annual bluegrass. Multiple reviewers mentioned that a single spring application held their lawn weed-free through September, even in hot Southern climates.

**Where it falls short**

Pre-emergents only work if they're down before germination. Miss your window — typically when soil temps hit 55°F for three consecutive days — and you've wasted money. Also, this product must be watered in within 14 days of application. If you're in a dry spell and can't irrigate, you're gambling. One reviewer learned this the hard way: "Applied in April, got two weeks of no rain, and my crabgrass came up anyway."

The WDG formulation also means you need a sprayer, not a spreader. If you don't own a backpack or pump sprayer, factor that into your total cost.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the lawn enthusiast who's past the beginner stage. If you're already tracking soil temps, using a sprayer, and understand split applications, this is a no-brainer value play. It's also ideal for large properties where the 5-pound size saves you from buying multiple small jugs. Skip it if you're a casual homeowner looking for a quick granular spread-and-go product — you'd be better served by a granular prodiamine product that works with a broadcast spreader.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Can I use this on vegetable gardens?*
No. Prodiamine is for established turf and ornamental landscapes only. It will damage vegetable crops. Use a different pre-emergent for edible beds.

*When exactly should I apply it?*
Apply when soil temperatures hit 55°F consistently in spring. For fall applications, aim 6-8 weeks before the first frost to catch winter annuals. Your local university extension office publishes soil temp data online.

*How long does one jug last?*
At the standard rate, one 5-pound jug covers about 27,500 sq ft, which is roughly a half-acre to three-quarter-acre lawn depending on your split strategy. Most homeowners get 2-4 years from one jug.
