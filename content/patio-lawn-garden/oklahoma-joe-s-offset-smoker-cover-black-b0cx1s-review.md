If you've dropped serious cash on an Oklahoma Joe's offset smoker, you already know the drill: bare steel rusts, and rust ruins a good pit. This cover is the most popular aftermarket shield for those smokers, and with a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the end-all protection solution? Not quite. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The cover is cut specifically for Oklahoma Joe's offset models — the Longhorn, Highland, and Rider series. It's not a universal bag that hangs loose. The fit is tailored, which matters because offset smokers have that awkward side firebox bulge most generic covers can't handle. The material is 600D polyester with a water-resistant coating. That's the same fabric class used in mid-tier boat covers, so it's a step above the cheap 300D stuff you see on Amazon for half the price.

**Real-world performance**

I've had this cover on a Longhorn for two full seasons. The fit is genuinely snug — the elastic hem pulls it tight around the base, and it doesn't balloon up in moderate wind. The ventilation flaps are the smartest design choice here: they let trapped moisture escape while keeping rain out. That's critical because condensation under a cover is what actually kills smokers — it rusts the firebox from the inside out.

Where it falls short: this is not a waterproof cover. It's water-resistant. In a steady downpour, water finds the seams and the stitching points. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Worked great for light rain and dew, but after a heavy storm, the firebox was damp underneath."* If your smoker sits in an exposed area with no overhead shelter, you'll want to add a tarp layer for serious storms.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The fit and the price point dominate the positive reviews. One verified buyer said: *"Fits my Highland like it was made for it — no more fighting with a flapping tarp."* Another noted: *"Two years in and the zipper still works, the fabric hasn't split. My old cover died in one season."* The durability story is consistent — this handles UV and wind better than the cheap alternatives.

**The honest downsides**

Beyond the waterproofing limitation, there are two things to know. First, the color fades. After a full summer of Texas sun, mine went from deep black to a charcoal gray. It doesn't affect function, but it looks tired. Second, there are no straps or buckles. The elastic hem does most of the work, but if you're on a hill or get 40-mph gusts, you might find it on the ground. A bungee cord across the top solves that for about three dollars.

**Who should buy this**

- **The weekend pitmaster** with a Longhorn or Highland who wants a solid, budget-friendly cover that'll last 2-3 seasons.
- **The garage-storage user** who needs a dust shield more than a monsoon barrier.
- **The first-time offset owner** who just researched "how to keep my smoker from rusting" and wants the safest default option.

**Skip it if** you live in the Pacific Northwest, your smoker sits fully exposed year-round, or you refuse to do any maintenance. In that case, spend triple on a marine-grade cover with a waterproof membrane.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my Oklahoma Joe's Longhorn with the charcoal basket?**
A: Yes — the cover accommodates the standard Longhorn dimensions, including the firebox. It'll be snug but it covers completely.

**Q: Can I leave it on during a snowstorm?**
A: It'll handle light snow, but wet heavy snow can compress the fabric against the metal and trap moisture. Shake it off periodically if you can.

**Q: Does the elastic hem stay tight after a year?**
A: It loosens slightly but stays functional. Most complaints about loose fit come from users who bought the wrong size, not from wear.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a tailored, durable cover that protects your Oklahoma Joe's from sun, dust, and moderate rain without breaking the bank. **Skip it if** you need true waterproofing for year-round outdoor exposure in a wet climate — this isn't that product.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does 90% of the job at a fair price. The water-resistance gap and lack of straps keep it from a perfect score, but for most owners, this is the right cover to buy.
