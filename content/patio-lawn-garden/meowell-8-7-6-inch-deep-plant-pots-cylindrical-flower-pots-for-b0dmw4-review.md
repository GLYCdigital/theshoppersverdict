The problem with most plastic plant pots is that they're flimsy. You pick one up with a damp root ball and the sides cave in, or the rim flexes so much you nearly drop the whole thing. The Meowell 8/7/6 inch deep plant pots don't have that problem. These are thick-walled, rigid cylinders that actually hold their shape under load — and that matters more than you'd think.

**What you're actually getting**

The set includes three sizes: 8-inch, 7-inch, and 6-inch pots, all with the same tall, cylindrical profile. As shown in the product image, these aren't your typical nursery-style pots with tapered sides. They're straight-walled and deep, which means more vertical root room and less surface area for moisture to evaporate. Each pot is made from a dense, matte-black plastic that feels closer to a ceramic planter in weight than a disposable nursery pot.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The standout feature here is the wall thickness. Most pots in this price range use thin, recyclable plastic that cracks after a season. The Meowell pots are noticeably more substantial — I'd estimate the walls are roughly twice as thick as standard nursery pots. The rim is reinforced, so lifting a fully saturated pot by the edges doesn't cause it to bow inward.

The drainage setup is straightforward: four small holes in the base. They're adequate for most houseplants, but here's the catch — the pots sit completely flat. If you place them directly on a solid saucer with no gap, water can pool and clog the holes. A layer of gravel or a raised drip tray solves this, but it's something to know before you buy.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, the same themes keep coming up. One reviewer put it simply: "Finally, pots that don't feel like they'll crack if you look at them wrong." Another noted the depth was the selling point: "My tomato seedlings have never had this much root room in a pot this size." Multiple people mention using these for orchids and other plants that prefer deep, narrow root channels — the straight walls deliver that.

**Where it falls short**

No saucers. That's the big one. For a product positioned for indoor use, skipping drip trays feels like an oversight. You'll spend extra money and time finding saucers that fit the 8-inch pot properly — and because the bases are flat, water sits directly on the soil line if you're not careful.

The finish is also purely functional. These are matte black, no texture, no decorative rim. If you want something that looks good on a windowsill without a cachepot, these aren't it. They're workhorse pots, not display pieces.

**Who should buy these**

- **Container vegetable gardeners** — the depth is ideal for tomatoes, peppers, and herbs with long taproots
- **Houseplant collectors** who like to pot up progressively — the 6-7-8 progression means you can move a plant up a size without buying a whole new set
- **Anyone tired of flimsy pots** that crack or warp after a season of outdoor use

Skip them if you need pots for shallow-rooted succulents or if you want a decorative planter you can put directly on your coffee table.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Do these pots have drainage holes?**
Yes, each pot has four small pre-drilled holes in the base. They work fine, but because the pots sit flat, you'll want a raised saucer or a layer of pebbles underneath to ensure proper flow.

**Are the 8-inch pots actually 8 inches deep?**
The pots are sized by diameter, not depth. The 8-inch pot is roughly 8 inches wide and about 9 inches deep — the depth-to-width ratio is what makes them "deep" pots.

**Can these handle outdoor winter weather?**
The plastic is thick enough to survive a season or two outdoors, but it's not UV-stabilized. Extended sun exposure will eventually make it brittle. Indoor or sheltered use is where they'll last longest.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you need sturdy, deep pots for root-hungry plants and don't want to pay ceramic prices. **Skip it if** you require saucers included or want a decorative finish.

At 4.6/5, these earn their rating. They're not flashy, but they do exactly what a plant pot should do: hold soil, drain properly, and not fall apart when you move them. For the price, that's a solid deal.
