If you grow cut flowers, you already know the drill: you plant zinnias, they bloom beautifully for two weeks, then they fizzle and you're back at the farmer's market spending $8 on a bouquet. The **Zinnia Cut & Come Again Seed** mix promises to break that cycle. After digging through 229 ratings and real grower feedback, here's whether it actually delivers.

## What's in the Packet

You're getting roughly 50 seeds of a curated mix — not the generic "California Giants" blend you'll find in the bargain bin. The packet includes a solid range of bloom types: dahlia-flowered, cactus-style, and button-sized pom-poms. As shown in the product image, the color range spans cream, coral, hot pink, and a few bicolor varieties that look striking in a vase.

Seeds are raw (not pelleted), so they're tiny and dark — easy to scatter but a bit fiddly to space individually if you're starting them in trays.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this mix earns its keep. The "cut and come again" claim isn't marketing fluff. Multiple buyers note that harvesting stems down to the first set of leaves triggers vigorous side-shoot growth, extending bloom production from a typical 3-week window to 6-8 weeks. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I cut bouquets every other day from July through September. These just keep pumping out flowers."*

Germination is fast and reliable. Most growers report sprouting in 7-10 days with soil temps around 70°F, with germination rates between 85-95%. The plants themselves reach 36-42 inches tall — substantial enough for cutting but not so tall they flop over without staking.

Disease resistance is decent but not exceptional. In humid climates, powdery mildew showed up late in the season for several buyers, though good airflow and spacing mitigated most of it. One reviewer in Florida noted: *"They got mildew in August but by then I'd already gotten months of blooms. Worth it."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The vase life is the standout. Cut stems hold for 7-10 days in water — better than most commercial zinnias. The color variety also gets consistent love. Beyond the basic oranges and pinks, the mix includes peach-toned and lime-green varieties that look almost tropical in arrangements.

Direct sowing works well, which is a relief for gardeners who don't want to fuss with indoor seed starting. Several buyers reported scattering seeds in mid-May and getting their first blooms by early July.

## Where It Falls Short

The seed count is the main gripe. Fifty seeds at this price point is stingy compared to the 200-500 seed packets you can grab for less at garden centers. If you're planting a full cutting garden row, you'll need to buy multiple packets.

Color consistency is also a lottery. A handful of reviewers noted the mix leaned heavily toward orange and magenta, with fewer of the advertised cream and soft-pink varieties. If you're designing a specific color palette for wedding arrangements or market bouquets, this unpredictability could be a dealbreaker.

Finally, "cut and come again" requires actual effort. If you don't harvest regularly, plants go to seed and production drops off fast. This isn't a plant-and-ignore situation.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a reliable, long-blooming cutting flower for home bouquets, don't mind deadheading, and value variety over uniformity. Also great for beginners — these are forgiving seeds that don't need pampering.

**Skip it if:** You need a specific color palette for events, want a bulk quantity at the lowest cost per seed, or live in a high-humidity zone and can't provide good air circulation.

## FAQ

**Q: How many seeds are in the packet?**
Roughly 50 seeds. Enough for a small cutting bed, but plan to buy two or three packets for serious production.

**Q: Can I start these indoors or should I direct sow?**
Both work. Start indoors 4-6 weeks before last frost for earlier blooms, or direct sow after soil warms. Zinnias resent root disturbance, so use biodegradable pots if starting indoors.

**Q: Do these attract pollinators?**
Yes — the open-faced blooms are magnets for bees, butterflies, and beneficial wasps. A good addition to a pollinator garden, not just a cutting patch.

## The Verdict

**4.6/5 stars.** This is a solid, performance-driven zinnia mix for home gardeners who actually cut their flowers. It delivers on its core promise — extended bloom production — with better-than-average vase life and a prettier color range than most seed packets in this category. The stingy seed count and unpredictable color ratios are real downsides, but neither undermines the core value.

**Buy it if** you want reliable cutting flowers from mid-summer through fall. **Skip it if** you're seeding a large plot on a budget — buy bulk seeds for that and save this mix for a dedicated cutting bed.
