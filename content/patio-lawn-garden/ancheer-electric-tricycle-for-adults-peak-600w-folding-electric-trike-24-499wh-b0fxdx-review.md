**The honest take on a three-wheeled e-trike that trades sportiness for stability**

Most electric trikes for adults look like medical devices. The ANCHEER doesn't. With its 24-inch wheels, step-through aluminum frame, and matte finish, it reads more like a utility bike with training wheels than a mobility scooter. That distinction matters if you're buying this for independence rather than necessity.

But looks only get you so far. Here's what 210 Amazon reviewers (averaging 4.2 stars) actually discovered after riding it.

**What's in the box**

You get the trike with the front wheel and handlebar detached, a 499Wh battery (removable, key-locked), the charger, a basic tool kit, and an owner's manual that's surprisingly readable. Assembly takes about 45 minutes if you're mechanically comfortable; the front brake cable routing is the fiddliest part. The folding mechanism is a lever at the mid-frame — it hinges the front half over the rear, reducing length by roughly a third for storage or transport.

**Real-world performance**

The 600W peak motor (rated 500W continuous) lives in the rear hub. On flat ground, you'll hit the 15.5 mph top speed without pedaling. Hills are where this trike earns its keep — the motor handles moderate grades (5-8%) without bogging down, though steep climbs will drain the battery noticeably faster. Pedal assist has five levels, and the throttle is a twist-grip on the right side. Both work independently, which is genuinely useful when your legs need a break but you don't want to stop.

The 499Wh battery is the headline spec. ANCHEER claims 55 miles max range; that's at the lowest assist level with a 150-lb rider on flat pavement. Realistic use — mixed terrain, throttle-heavy riding, a bit of cargo — lands between 30 and 40 miles. One reviewer noted, "I'm 190 lbs and ride mostly flat streets with the throttle, and I get about 28 miles before the battery drops to 20%." Plan accordingly.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Stability is the recurring theme. Three wheels mean zero balancing anxiety, and the low step-through frame makes mounting and dismounting easy even with stiff knees or a bad back. One verified buyer wrote: "My 72-year-old mother rides this to the farmers market twice a week. She hasn't used her car in a month." Multiple reviewers also highlighted the smooth, comfortable ride — the 24" tires and front suspension fork absorb sidewalk cracks and potholes better than you'd expect from a folding bike.

**Where it falls short**

At roughly 70 pounds, the "folding" feature is a mixed blessing. It folds, yes, but you're still lifting a heavy machine. A 5'2" reviewer noted, "Getting this into my SUV trunk alone is a workout. I've started leaving it in the garage and using the included charger there instead."

The cargo setup also underwhelms. The rear rack is rated for 55 lbs, and the front basket is small — enough for a purse or a six-pack, not a Costco run. If hauling is your priority, budget for aftermarket panniers or a larger front basket.

Finally, the seat. It's padded and adjustable, but several reviewers found it uncomfortable on rides over 30 minutes. The "comfort" saddle is firm; you may want a gel cover.

**Who should buy this**

- **Seniors or mobility-limited riders** who want to stay active without balance risk — this is your machine
- **Car-free suburbanites** running errands within a 10-mile radius
- **RV or boat owners** who need a compact electric vehicle that stores in a small space

**Skip it if** you need serious cargo capacity, weigh over 280 lbs (the 330-lb limit is generous but the frame feels more confident at lower weights), or you expect to regularly lift it into a vehicle solo.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it ride like a regular bicycle?**
A: No. The steering is heavier than a two-wheeler, especially at low speeds, and you can't lean into turns. You'll slow down for corners. It's not bad — just different.

**Q: Can you pedal it without power?**
A: Yes, but it's a 70-lb trike with a motor and battery. The gearing is low enough for gentle pedaling, but you won't want to do a 10-mile ride unassisted.

**Q: How long does a full charge take?**
A: About 6-8 hours from empty to full. The charger is standard 2A; there's no fast-charge option.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you value stability and ease of use over speed and range. This is a capable, comfortable e-trike for errands, leisure rides, and maintaining mobility — at a price point that undercuts most three-wheeled competitors.

**Skip it if** you need serious hauling capacity or plan to transport it frequently. The folding mechanism is convenient for storage, not for carrying.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a solid, honest machine that does exactly what it promises, with a few practical compromises you should know about before buying.
