**The Verdict: A Promising Cordless Cleaner That Falls Short on Endurance**

At first glance, the Cordless Pool Vacuum with Telescopic Pole looks like the answer to every pool owner's prayers. The 26.5 gallons/min flow rate is genuinely impressive on paper, and the telescopic pole—which extends up to 40 inches—suggests thoughtful ergonomics. But with a 3.0-star average across 150 ratings, the reality is more complicated. Here's what you need to know before spending your money.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the vacuum head, the telescopic pole, a rechargeable battery pack, a charging adapter, and two filter bags. That's it. No wall mount, no storage hook, no spare filters. The unit weighs about 5.5 pounds fully assembled, which is reasonable. The pole locks securely with a twist mechanism, and the head swivels 180 degrees, making it easy to navigate corners and steps.

**Performance: The Good and the Frustrating**

When it works, it works well. The 26.5 gallons/min suction is no marketing fiction—it picks up pine needles, small twigs, and sand in a single pass. One reviewer noted, "I was shocked at how much debris it grabbed on the first run. My pool floor looked brand new." The cordless design is a genuine blessing; no wrestling with a 50-foot cord that keeps catching on ladder steps.

The problem is endurance. The advertised 60-minute runtime assumes you're running it on the lowest suction setting. Crank it to full power—which you'll need for anything beyond light dust—and you're looking at 25–30 minutes, max. For a 20-foot pool, that's doable. For anything larger, plan on a recharge break. Another reviewer put it plainly: "Great suction, but I had to recharge twice to finish my pool. That wasn't in the description."

**Build Quality and Maintenance**

The housing is ABS plastic, which feels sturdy, but the filter bag is the weak link. It's a fine-mesh fabric that clogs quickly with algae or fine silt, and you'll need to rinse it out mid-session. The charging port has a rubber cover that fits snugly, but several buyers reported water seeping in after a few months of use. Not a dealbreaker, but worth noting if you plan to store this outdoors.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest complaint across reviews is the battery life discrepancy. Amazon's listing claims "up to 60 minutes," but that's only at the lowest setting—a setting that barely moves the vacuum. On the practical settings, you get half that. The second recurring gripe is the lack of a caddy or wall bracket. For a $200+ product, asking you to figure out storage yourself feels cheap.

**Who Should Buy This**

This vacuum suits owners of small to medium above-ground pools (up to 18 feet) who want cordless convenience and don't mind a mid-session recharge. It's also a good pick if you have light debris like leaves and dust rather than heavy silt or sand. Skip it if you have a large in-ground pool, need all-day runtime, or want a set-and-forget cleaning solution. For those scenarios, a robotic cleaner is a better investment.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: On the lowest setting, expect 50–60 minutes. On high suction, 25–30 minutes. Plan your cleaning session around that reality.

**Q: Does it work on pool steps and walls?**
A: The swivel head helps with steps, but it's primarily a floor vacuum. It won't climb walls, and it struggles with steep angles.

**Q: Can you replace the filter bag?**
A: Yes, but replacements aren't included. Check the manufacturer's page for compatible bags—generic ones may not fit the housing properly.

**The Bottom Line**

The Cordless Pool Vacuum with Telescopic Pole has the suction power to do the job, but its battery endurance and filter maintenance demand patience. It's a solid choice for small pools and light debris, but it's not the hands-off solution the marketing suggests.

**Buy it if:** You have a small pool and want cordless convenience with genuine suction power.
**Skip it if:** You need reliable runtime for a larger pool or want minimal maintenance.

**Rating: 3.3/5** — A capable cleaner held back by battery and filter shortcomings.
