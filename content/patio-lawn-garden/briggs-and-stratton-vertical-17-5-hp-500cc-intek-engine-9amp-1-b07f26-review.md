If you're staring at a dead riding mower with a seized engine, you already know the drill: replacing the whole mower costs $2,500+, but a bare engine runs about a third of that. The Briggs & Stratton Vertical 17.5 HP 500cc INTEK with 9-amp starter sits right in that sweet spot — it's the workhorse replacement that keeps a perfectly good deck, transmission, and frame alive for another decade. With 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly doing something right. Here's what you need to know before you click buy.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the bare engine: the 500cc INTEK powerhead with a vertical 1-inch crankshaft (3-5/32-inch length), a 9-amp starter, and the 3.5-inch bolt pattern that matches most MTD, Cub Cadet, and Craftsman chassis from the last 15 years. That's it. No fuel tank, no muffler, no air filter assembly. As shown in the product image, you're buying the heart, not the whole body. If you're replacing a seized engine, you'll transfer those parts over from your old motor — and that's where the hidden costs start if your old parts are rusted beyond reuse.

**Performance and real-world use**

The 500cc displacement is the real story here. That's a full 20 cubic inches of iron — this isn't a lawn mower engine pretending to be a tractor motor. It produces 17.5 HP at 3,600 RPM, which is plenty for a 42-46 inch deck on flat or gently rolling terrain. The cast iron cylinder liner is a genuine upgrade over the aluminum bore engines Briggs sells at the low end; it handles heat better and won't oval out after a few hundred hours. Dual ball bearings on the crankshaft mean the PTO side can handle a heavy deck without flexing.

The 9-amp starter is noticeably stronger than the older 5-amp units. One buyer noted, "After sitting all winter with old gas in it, I charged the battery and it fired on the second crank. My old engine wouldn't even turn over in the spring." That's the kind of feedback that shows up repeatedly in the review data — this engine starts, and it keeps starting.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme in positive reviews is that this is a genuine Briggs & Stratton, not a Chinese knockoff. Buyers who've been burned by cheap replacements from no-name brands appreciate the OEM fit. As one reviewer put it, "Bolted right up to my 2008 Craftsman. All the holes lined up, the wiring harness matched, and I was mowing in an afternoon." The oil filter — a real spin-on filter, not a screen — also draws consistent praise. It's a small thing until you've changed oil on an engine without one.

**Where it falls short**

The carburetor is the weak point. The INTEK series uses a plastic float bowl design that's prone to gumming if fuel sits for more than a month. This isn't a defect — it's a design choice that trades a few dollars of cost for a maintenance headache. Run non-ethanol fuel or add stabilizer religiously, or you'll be pulling the carb apart by August. That's not speculation; it's the most common complaint in the one-star reviews.

The bare-engine format also trips up first-time buyers. If you're replacing a blown motor, you need to be comfortable transferring the fuel tank, exhaust, and wiring. If your old engine was damaged in a fire or rollover, those parts may be trash too — and you'll be buying them separately. Factor $60-100 in extra parts if you're starting from scratch.

**Who should buy this**

This engine is for the home mechanic with a mid-2000s or newer riding mower who wants to skip the $2,000 replacement cost. It's also ideal for someone with a mower that's otherwise in good shape — solid deck, good transmission — who doesn't want to buy a whole new machine because one component failed. If you're not comfortable with basic wrench work, this isn't your project; budget for a small engine shop to do the swap.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my Craftsman/MTD/Cub Cadet?**
A: If your old engine is a Briggs vertical shaft with a 1-inch x 3-5/32-inch crank and a 3.5-inch bolt pattern, yes. That covers most 42-46 inch decks from 2000-2020. Measure your bolt pattern before ordering.

**Q: Does it come with a fuel tank and muffler?**
A: No. It's a bare engine. You transfer those from your old motor or buy them separately. Check the condition of your existing parts before you order.

**Q: Is 17.5 HP enough for my hilly property?**
A: For 42-46 inch decks on moderate slopes, yes. If you're on steep terrain or running a 50+ inch deck, step up to a 21-24 HP twin. This is a single-cylinder engine; it'll lug but not pull like a twin.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have a solid mower with a dead engine, you're comfortable with basic mechanical work, and you'll commit to fuel maintenance. This is a genuine OEM replacement that will outlast the chassis it's bolted to.

**Skip it if:** You expect a plug-and-play experience with all accessories included, or you can't commit to using fuel stabilizer. This engine rewards owners who maintain it — and punishes those who don't.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect, but for the price of a new mower, this engine delivers honest, reliable power for the next decade.
