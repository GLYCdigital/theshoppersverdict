**Robarb R20154 Super Blue Clarifier Review: Does This 1-Quart Bottle Actually Deliver Crystal Clear Pool Water?**

Cloudy pool water is the fastest way to ruin a summer afternoon. You've shocked, you've brushed, you've vacuumed — and it's still that milky haze that makes the deep end look like a fog bank. Robarb's Super Blue Clarifier promises to fix that in a single quart. Here's whether it actually earns the 4.6-star rating from 229 Amazon shoppers.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The R20154 comes as a one-quart bottle of concentrated blue liquid. That's it — no measuring cup, no applicator, no test strips. You pour it directly into your skimmer or across the pool's surface while the pump runs. The label claims one quart treats up to 20,000 gallons, which covers the vast majority of residential in-ground pools and larger above-ground models. For a typical 15,000-gallon pool, you're looking at roughly one bottle per season if you dose weekly.

**How It Performs in Real Use**

The chemistry here is straightforward: this is a polymer-based clarifier that binds microscopic particles — dead algae, dirt, oils, sunscreen residue — into larger clumps your filter can actually catch. It's not a shock treatment and it's not an algaecide. It's the polish step after you've already done the heavy lifting.

What makes Super Blue different from generic clarifiers is the speed. Multiple buyers report visible improvement within hours, not days. One reviewer noted, "Added it at 7 PM, by morning the water was noticeably clearer — by the next evening it was absolutely crystal." That tracks with the product's design: the polymer system is aggressive at flocculating fine particles that standard cartridge and sand filters miss.

The product works across all three common pool types — chlorine, saltwater, and bromine. That's a genuine advantage over single-system clarifiers. It's also safe for all filter types, though you should expect to backwash or clean your filter within 24–48 hours after dosing. The clumped particles have to go somewhere, and that somewhere is your filter media.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme in positive reviews is simplicity and results. A 4.6 average across 229 ratings is solid for any pool chemical, and repeat buyers are common. One verified purchase wrote: "I've tried three other clarifiers this season. This is the only one that actually made the water sparkle. Will buy again." Another noted it cleared a stubborn haze that had resisted two rounds of shock treatment.

Cost-per-use is also a recurring point. At roughly one ounce per 5,000 gallons, a single bottle stretches further than most competitors' products. For pool owners who dose weekly during peak season, that's meaningful savings.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about limitations. This is not a rescue product. If your pool is green — as in, you can't see the bottom step — you need to shock, brush, and possibly use an algaecide *before* you reach for this bottle. Several negative reviews reflect this misunderstanding. One frustrated buyer said, "Pool was green, added this, didn't change anything." Correct — because that's not what it's for.

Some users with heavily clouded water report needing a second dose. That's not a failure of the chemistry but it does double your cost per treatment. And as mentioned, you'll need to be proactive about filter maintenance. Skip the backwash and you'll see pressure rise and flow drop within a day.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The weekend pool owner** who keeps up with chlorine and pH but battles a persistent haze after heavy use or rain
- **Above-ground pool owners** with cartridge filters that struggle to catch fine particles
- **Anyone preparing for a backyard party** who needs clear water in 24 hours, not a week

**Skip it if** your pool is green, you've neglected sanitation, or you're looking for an algaecide. This is a finisher, not a fixer.

**FAQ**

**Q: How often should I use Super Blue Clarifier?**
Weekly dosing is standard for pools in heavy use. For light use, every two weeks is sufficient. One quart at 4 ounces per 10,000 gallons gives you roughly eight treatments.

**Q: Can I use this with a saltwater chlorine generator?**
Yes. The polymer chemistry is compatible with salt systems, and multiple reviewers confirm it works without issues.

**Q: Does it work in cold water?**
It's most effective above 60°F. Below that, the polymer reaction slows significantly — wait for warmer water if possible.
