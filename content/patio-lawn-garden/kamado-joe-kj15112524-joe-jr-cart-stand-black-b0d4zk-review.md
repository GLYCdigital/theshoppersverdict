Let's be blunt: the Kamado Joe Joe Jr. is a fantastic little ceramic grill, but sitting flat on the ground, it's a back-breaking chore to use. You're bending over for every flip, every check, every basting session. The Kamado Joe KJ15112524 Cart Stand fixes that problem — but at a price. Here's the real breakdown after digging through 215 ratings and buyer feedback.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the powder-coated steel cart frame, two locking caster wheels, two fixed wheels, a folding side shelf, and all necessary hardware. The cart measures roughly 24" wide by 20" deep and stands about 27" tall — putting the grilling surface at a comfortable waist height for most adults (5'8" to 6'0"). The wheels are 5-inch rubber casters, which is the sweet spot for navigating patio stones, deck boards, and even packed grass without catching.

Assembly is straightforward but not quick. Plan for 30–45 minutes. The frame bolts together with 10mm hardware, and the instruction sheet is diagram-heavy with minimal text. One reviewer noted, "The pictures are clear enough, but you'll want a magnetic tray for bolts — there are about 20 of them and they're easy to lose in the grass." No tools are included, so have a wrench and socket set ready.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The frame is genuinely sturdy. The steel gauge is thicker than typical aftermarket grill carts, and the powder coating resists chipping when you scrape a wheel against a concrete step. The locking casters engage firmly — no rolling away mid-cook on a sloped driveway. The Joe Jr. weighs about 80 pounds, and the cart handles it without any flex or wobble.

The folding side shelf is the standout feature. It locks into place with a simple pin mechanism and holds a surprising amount — a full platter of prepped meat, a sauce bottle, tongs, and a thermometer all fit without crowding. When you're done, it folds flat against the frame, reducing the cart's footprint to nearly nothing for garage storage.

One thing buyers consistently praise: the portability. A verified purchaser wrote, "I wheel this from my patio to the driveway for tailgate-style cooks with my buddies. The locking wheels hold it in place on the sloped driveway." Another reviewer appreciated the height: "I'm 6'2" and no longer hunch over. Worth every penny for my back alone."

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest complaint is the price. At roughly $200 (it fluctuates), you're paying a premium for a branded accessory rather than a generic table. Several reviewers noted they found cheaper alternatives on Amazon for half the price, though most admitted the Kamado Joe version feels more solid.

Assembly is the second gripe. The manual lacks torque specs, and a few buyers reported stripping bolts by overtightening. Go gently — snug is enough. Also, there's no storage: no hooks for tools, no shelf for charcoal or wood chunks. You'll still need a separate caddy.

Finally, the cart is purpose-built for the Joe Jr. only. If you're thinking about upgrading to a larger Kamado Joe model later, this cart won't transfer over — you'll be selling it or repurposing it as a plant stand.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Back-conscious grillers** who use their Joe Jr. multiple times a week and are tired of kneeling
- **Patio entertainers** who want to wheel the grill to the table for serving and socializing
- **Joe Jr. owners** who've already invested in accessories and want a matched, cohesive setup

Skip it if you're a budget-first buyer who doesn't mind a basic metal table, or if your Joe Jr. lives permanently on a built-in outdoor kitchen counter.

**FAQ**

*Does this cart fit the Kamado Joe Jr. only, or other grills?*
It's designed specifically for the Joe Jr. The base dimensions and bolt pattern match that grill exactly. Other kamados or kettle grills won't sit securely.

*Can I leave the cart outside year-round?*
The powder coating resists rust, but the wheels and locking mechanism will benefit from occasional lubrication. If you live in harsh winters, store the cart covered or in a shed.

*Does the side shelf hold a cutting board?*
Not natively — it's a flat metal surface. You can lay a small cutting board on it, but there's no built-in cutting board slot like on larger Kamado Joe carts.

**The Verdict**

**4.2/5** — Buy it if you own a Joe Jr., use it regularly, and value ergonomics and portability. The build quality is excellent, the locking wheels are genuinely useful, and the shelf makes a real difference during cooks. Skip it if you're on a tight budget or your grill never moves — a $60 table from the hardware store will do the job adequately, just with less polish.
