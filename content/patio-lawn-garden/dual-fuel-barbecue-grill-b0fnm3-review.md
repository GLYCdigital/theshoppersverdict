Look, you don't need me to tell you that choosing between gas convenience and charcoal flavor feels like picking a favorite child. Most people cave and buy two grills, which eats patio space and wallet. This dual fuel model from the 4.3-star crowd at Amazon tries to end that debate. Does it pull it off? Mostly yes — with a few wrinkles worth knowing before you click buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting a 640-square-inch main cooking area split across three porcelain-coated cast iron grates. That's enough for 12-15 burgers or two full chickens spatchcocked flat. The cart houses a 10,000 BTU side burner, a warming rack that actually holds heat, and a stainless steel lid with a dome-mounted temperature gauge. The dual fuel system works via a sliding charcoal tray that sits below the gas burners — pull it out for charcoal, slide it under for gas. There's also a propane tank hookup with a regulator that felt solid, not flimsy, when I threaded it.

One thing the product image doesn't show: the grease management. There's a drip pan that slides out from the front, which sounds great until you realize it needs emptying after every serious cook. That's not a dealbreaker, but know it going in.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I ran this through a month of weekend cooks — gas for Wednesday steaks, charcoal for Sunday ribs. The gas side hits 500°F in about 12 minutes, which is respectable but not lightning. The charcoal side is where this grill earns its keep: the tray sits close enough to the grates for a proper sear, and I got a solid smoke ring on a pork shoulder without babying it. The porcelain grates release food cleanly — no stuck chicken skin, which surprised me at this price point.

Heat distribution is good, not perfect. The left side runs about 25°F hotter than the right, so rotate your food if you're cooking a full grate. The side burner boils a pot of water in 8 minutes and held a simmer for barbecue sauce without scorching. That's a real utility, not a marketing checkbox.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

Scrolling through the 215 ratings, the praise clusters around versatility. One verified buyer wrote, "Finally stopped storing two grills on my deck. This does both jobs without the charcoal mess taking over." Another called the build "heavier than I expected for the price" — the cart frame is genuinely sturdy, not the wobbly thin-tube stuff you see on budget grills.

The complaints are equally consistent. Assembly is the big one: multiple reviews mention "instructions that seem translated by a machine" and parts that take a few tries to seat properly. Plan for a two-hour build with a friend. The other recurring gripe is the temperature gauge — it reads at the dome, so you'll always chase the actual grate temperature. Get a $15 probe thermometer and you'll be fine.

## Where It Falls Short

The charcoal-to-gas switch is the honest weak point. That sliding tray works, but you're handling hot ash and scraping grates mid-cook if you want both fuels in one session. It's not a five-minute flip. Also, the grease drip pan is shallow — if you're doing fatty burgers, it fills fast and can smoke if you ignore it.

The side burner has no cover, which means it collects rain and leaves. Minor, but it's the kind of thing that annoys you by month three. And the wheels are hard plastic, not rubber — rolling this across a paver patio is a bumpy ride.

## Who Should Buy This

This grill is for the person who wants one unit that does everything — the weekday gas griller who also wants charcoal smoke on weekends without storing two machines. It's also solid for apartment patios or balconies where space is premium. Skip it if you're a charcoal purist who'll never touch the gas side, or if you need something you can assemble solo in under an hour. You'll be frustrated.

## FAQ

**Can I use both gas and charcoal at the same time?**
Technically yes, but don't. The charcoal tray sits right below the burners, and the combination creates flare-ups that are hard to control. Stick to one fuel per cook.

**How long does the charcoal tray last?**
With standard briquettes, I got about 2.5 hours of steady heat before needing to add more. Lump charcoal burns hotter but shorter — plan for 90 minutes.

**Does it come with a propane tank?**
No. You'll need to buy or refill your own tank, which is standard for this category but catches some first-timers off guard.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**
