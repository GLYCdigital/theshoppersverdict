Gardenias have a reputation problem: they’re gorgeous for three weeks in May, then sulk for the rest of the summer. The Southern Living Jublilation Gardenia (2 Gal) is explicitly bred to break that cycle. It’s a reblooming variety that pushes fragrant white flowers from late spring through fall, which is the single biggest reason it holds a 4.2-star average across 210 Amazon ratings.

**What’s actually in the box**

You’re getting a live plant in a 2-gallon nursery pot — not a seed packet, not a bare-root stick. Expect a shrub roughly 12–18 inches tall at arrival with established roots and glossy, deep-green foliage. The Jublilation is part of the Southern Living Plant Collection, which means it’s been trialed for heat tolerance and southern growing conditions. It ships in a sturdy box with the pot secured; most buyers report arrival in good shape, though a few noted broken branches in transit — normal for live plants, and the shrub recovers quickly.

**Performance and build quality**

This is a compact gardenia, maturing at 3–4 feet tall and wide. That’s significantly smaller than older varieties like August Beauty or Frostproof, which can hit 5–6 feet. For a patio planter, foundation planting, or a tight corner bed, that size is a genuine advantage — you won’t be wrestling it with pruners every summer.

The fragrance is the headline act. Multiple buyers describe it as stronger and sweeter than standard gardenias. One reviewer put it plainly: “The scent carries across the whole porch. My neighbors asked what I planted.” The reblooming habit is the other big draw. Instead of one spring flush, you get scattered blooms through summer and into fall, especially if you deadhead spent flowers and feed with an acid-loving fertilizer.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The repeat blooming is the most-cited win. One verified purchase noted, “It’s bloomed three times since I planted it in April. My old gardenia bloomed once and quit.” The fragrance gets consistent love too, with buyers using words like “intoxicating” and “better than the nursery variety.”

Cold hardiness to Zone 7 is workable for much of the Southeast and mid-Atlantic. If you’re in Zone 8–9, this plant will thrive with minimal fuss — full sun to part shade, acidic soil, consistent moisture.

**Where it falls short**

The honest downsides: this is not a Zone 5–6 plant. Northern gardeners will need to grow it in a container and overwinter indoors or in a garage, which is doable but adds hassle. Several reviewers in colder zones reported leaf drop after an early frost.

Second, the 2-gallon size is a starter plant. You’re not getting an instant hedge. Expect modest growth the first year as the root system establishes; the real show comes in year two. A few buyers were disappointed by the initial size — read the listing carefully so you’re not expecting a 3-foot shrub on day one.

Finally, gardenias are not low-maintenance. Acidic soil, consistent watering, and occasional feeding are non-negotiable. If you want a zero-care shrub, this is the wrong plant.

**Who should buy this**

- **Southern gardeners (Zones 7–9)** who want a fragrant, reblooming shrub with manageable size
- **Patio container growers** on a deck or balcony who can move it to a sheltered spot in winter
- **Gardenia lovers** tired of one-and-done spring bloomers

**Skip it if** you’re north of Zone 7 without indoor space, or you want an instant mature hedge.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it really bloom all summer?**
A: Yes, in flushes rather than continuously. It blooms in spring, then repeats in waves through fall if you deadhead and fertilize. Not as dense as the spring show, but real flowers.

**Q: Can I plant it in full sun?**
A: In Zones 7–8, morning sun with afternoon shade is ideal. Hot afternoon sun can stress it, especially in Zone 9. In cooler zones, more sun is fine.

**Q: How fast does it grow?**
A: Moderate — roughly 6–12 inches per year. It’ll reach full size in 2–3 seasons from the 2-gallon pot.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a compact, intensely fragrant gardenia that blooms repeatedly and fits a small space. **Skip it if** you’re in a cold zone or want minimal maintenance.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a strong, well-bred shrub with a real fragrance payoff. Just respect its zone limits and give it a season to settle in.
