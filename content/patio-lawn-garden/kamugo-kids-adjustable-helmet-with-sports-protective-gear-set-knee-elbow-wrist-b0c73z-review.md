## What’s Actually in the Box

You get eight pieces: one adjustable helmet, one pair of knee pads, one pair of elbow pads, and one pair of wrist guards. The helmet uses a dial-fit system on the back — turn the knob, and the internal cradle tightens around the child’s head. That’s a feature you typically find on $40+ helmets. The pads use elastic straps with hook-and-loop closures. Nothing here requires tools, and the whole set weighs about 2 pounds combined, so a 4-year-old won’t feel like they’re wearing a suit of armor.

## Real-World Performance

The helmet’s ABS hard shell sits over EPS foam — the same basic construction as pricier helmets, just with a thinner foam layer. For bike rides, scooters, and tricycle spills, it’s adequate. The 11 ventilation holes actually move air; one parent noted their kid “stopped complaining about a sweaty head” compared to a previous foam-lined set.

The pads are where this set earns its keep. The knee and elbow guards use a hard plastic cap over a thin EVA foam pad. They stay put during normal riding — several reviewers mention their kids testing them on pavement with no injuries. One buyer wrote: “My son wiped out on gravel going downhill and the pads took the scrape, not his knees. Zero tears.”

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the limitations. The wrist guards run small — several parents of 7 and 8-year-olds report the straps barely close. If your child is on the upper end of the size range, measure their wrist circumference before ordering.

The padding on the knee and elbow pads is thinner than dedicated skate brands. For balance bikes, scooters, and casual neighborhood rides, it’s fine. If your kid is hitting a skatepark or doing serious downhill runs, this isn’t the set — upgrade to Triple Eight or 187 Killer Pads for that.

Also note: the S size is labeled for ages 3-8, but that’s optimistic. Most buyers report the best fit for ages 3-6. A tall, broad-shouldered 7-year-old will likely find the pads snug.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the helmet meet safety certifications?**
A: Yes, it’s CPSC-certified for bike riding and ASTM-tested for multi-sport use. That covers scooters, skateboards, and roller skates. It’s not rated for climbing or skiing.

**Q: Can the pads be washed?**
A: The pads wipe clean with a damp cloth. The straps hold up to hand-washing, but skip the machine — the hook-and-loop closures will collect lint and lose grip.

**Q: Will this fit my 2-year-old?**
A: The dial can tighten down small, but the helmet’s minimum circumference is roughly 48cm. Most 2-year-olds have heads around 46-48cm, so it’s borderline. Measure first.

## The Verdict
