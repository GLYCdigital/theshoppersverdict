Let's be blunt about pool chlorine: most granular options are either slow-dissolving calcium hypochlorite that clouds your water, or unstabilized dichlor that evaporates in an afternoon of full sun. Leslie's Chlor Brite sidesteps both problems with a 99% sodium dichlor formula that's stabilized and fast-acting. If you're tired of shocking your pool only to see chlorine readings drop to zero by dinner, this is worth a serious look.

**What you actually get**

The bucket contains concentrated sodium dichlor granules — no fillers, no calcium. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward white bucket with clear dosing instructions on the label. The granules are fine and uniform, which matters more than you'd think. Coarse granules take forever to dissolve and bleach your pool floor; these break down quickly in a bucket of water. You're getting a dual-purpose product here: use it as a weekly shock or as a daily sanitizer, depending on your pool's needs and your CYA levels.

**Real-world performance**

Here's where this product earns its keep. Sodium dichlor is one of the few chlorine types that dissolves completely without leaving calcium buildup on your pool walls or equipment. That's a huge advantage over cal-hypo, which is notorious for scaling and cloudy water if you're not careful. The built-in cyanuric acid stabilizer means the chlorine won't degrade as fast in direct sunlight — a genuine benefit for above-ground pools in open yards.

One buyer put it simply: *"Dissolves clear in about five minutes, no cloudiness. My water tests perfect the next morning."* That tracks with my research. The 99% concentration means you need less product per treatment than lower-grade options, which offsets the price per bucket.

**Where it falls short**

The stabilizer is a double-edged sword. Every time you use Chlor Brite, you're adding cyanuric acid to your pool. CYA above 50-70 ppm actually makes chlorine less effective, not more. If you're using this as your only chlorine source all summer, you'll need to test CYA monthly and be prepared to partially drain and refill your pool to reset levels. That's not a flaw unique to this product — it's chemistry — but it's a real consideration.

Also, you should pre-dissolve these granules in a bucket of water before adding to skimmers or any vinyl-lined pool. Dumping them directly can bleach liner patterns or sit on the floor undissolved. It's a minor extra step, but worth knowing before you buy.

**Who should buy this**

- **Above-ground pool owners** who want a quick-dissolving shock that won't cloud the water
- **Hot tub and spa users** needing a fast-acting sanitizer that leaves no residue
- **First-time pool owners** who want one product that handles both daily chlorination and weekly shocking without buying two separate chemicals

**Skip it if** you already have high CYA levels from years of tablet use, or if you prefer unstabilized chlorine to keep your cyanuric acid completely in your control.
