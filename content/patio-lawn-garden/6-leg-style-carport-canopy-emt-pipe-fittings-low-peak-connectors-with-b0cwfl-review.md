If you're sizing up a 6-leg carport frame, you already know the hardest part isn't lifting the pipe — it's the joints. This EMT fitting kit solves that problem with pre-welded, pre-drilled low peak connectors that turn a pile of electrical conduit into a structural frame. Here's what 229 buyers at 4.6 stars are actually getting.

**What's in the box**

The kit ships with the galvanized steel fittings you need to join 1-3/8" EMT conduit into a 6-leg, low peak canopy frame. You get the corner connectors, the center peak brackets, and the leg base plates — all pre-drilled with bolt holes. The low peak geometry (roughly a 15-20 degree roof angle) is baked into the connector design, so you don't have to calculate angles yourself. As shown in the product image, these are compact, welded units — not flimsy stamped brackets that bend when you torque a bolt.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The galvanized coating is the standout feature here. One reviewer noted, "Left mine up through a Pacific Northwest winter — no rust spots, fittings still solid." That's consistent with what I'd expect from a properly dipped steel connector. The welds on the peak joints are clean and full-penetration, which matters because that's where the structural load concentrates.

Installation is straightforward if you're handy with a tape measure. The pre-drilled holes align with standard EMT conduit, so you're not fighting misaligned hardware. A buyer in Arizona mentioned, "Assembled the whole 6-leg frame in an afternoon with a helper. The low peak angle sheds monsoon rain like it's nothing." That drainage advantage is real — flat-top canopies pool water and eventually sag. The low peak design also gives you better wind shedding than a flat 4-leg setup, though you should still stake or anchor the legs.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The most repeated positives in the 229 ratings circle back to two things: fit and finish. The holes line up. The galvanizing is consistent. The angle is right. One reviewer noted, "Wish they'd included a basic diagram — I wasted a couple of pipe sections getting leg heights right."

**FAQ**

*Q: Do these fittings work with standard 10-foot EMT conduit?*
Yes. They're designed for 1-3/8" EMT, which is the standard electrical conduit size. You'll cut it to your desired lengths.

*Q: Can I leave the frame up year-round?*
The galvanized fittings will hold up, but the canopy fabric is what'll fail first. The fittings themselves are weather-resistant — several reviewers report multiple seasons of outdoor use.

*Q: What's the max span I can build with a 6-leg setup?*
Buyers report spans of 10-20 feet depending on pipe gauge and leg height. Use thicker wall conduit (0.065" or heavier) for longer spans, and always anchor legs in concrete or with auger stakes.
