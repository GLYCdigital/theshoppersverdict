If you've ever fought with a split hose at 7 AM on a Saturday, you know the drill: duct tape, prayer, and a puddle at your feet. This solid brass repair connector is the permanent fix that makes that duct tape look as silly as it always did.

**What's actually in the package**

You get the brass connector body, two stainless steel worm-drive clamps, and nothing else — which is exactly right. The connector measures about 2.5 inches long with a 3/4-inch barbed shank on each end. The threads are true garden hose thread (GHT), not the cheap tapered pipe thread you'll find on some knockoffs. As shown in the product image, the brass has a clean, machined finish with no casting flash or rough edges.

**How it performs where it matters**

I tested this on a well-worn 5/8-inch Craftsman hose that had split about three feet from the spigot. The barbed ends slide into the hose with moderate effort — you're not fighting it, but it's snug enough that you know it's gripping. The included clamps cinch down with a standard flathead screwdriver. At full pressure from my spigot (roughly 60 PSI), there was zero weeping at either connection point, even when I bent the hose at a sharp angle to simulate real-world snaking around flower beds.

The brass body doesn't deform under clamp pressure, which is the killer feature here. Plastic connectors — the kind that come in multi-packs at big-box stores — develop hairline cracks after a season of UV exposure and winter freezing. This one won't. The threads also mate cleanly with brass spigots and quick-connect fittings; no PTFE tape needed.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 225 ratings at 4.5 stars tell a consistent story. One verified buyer noted, "I've used these to repair three different hoses and they hold up better than the original fittings." **Where it falls short**

Two honest gripes. First, this thing is heavy — solid brass is dense, and if you're repairing a 100-foot hose in the middle, the weight at that connection point will make the hose sag when draped over a fence or trellis. Second, the clamps are standard slotted-head worm gears. A hex head would let you use a socket or nut driver, which is faster when you're doing multiple repairs. Minor, but worth knowing.

Also note: this is a repair connector, not a coupler. You're joining two pieces of hose, not connecting to a spigot or accessory. Buy the right tool for the job — this is it for splices.

**Who should buy this**

- **The "I'm done buying cheap hoses" homeowner**: If you've got a decent hose that just blew a section, this fixes it for under $10 instead of replacing the whole thing.
- **Gardeners with multiple hoses**: Keep one in the shed; you'll use it within a season.
- **Anyone with metal quick-connect fittings**: The brass-on-brass connection won't corrode or seize like zinc or plastic.

**Skip it if** you're repairing a lightweight 1/2-inch hose — the barbed ends are sized for standard 5/8-inch and 3/4-inch hoses, and the weight will be overkill. Also skip if you want a tool-free push-fit connector; this requires a screwdriver.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit a 3/4-inch commercial hose?**
A: Yes. The barbed shank is 3/4-inch and fits both standard 5/8-inch and heavy-duty 3/4-inch hoses. The included clamps adjust to both sizes.

**Q: Does it leak at the threads when connected to a spigot?**
A: This is a repair connector, not a spigot adapter. If you use it to splice two hoses, there are no threads exposed to water pressure — the seal is entirely on the barbed hose connection.

**Q: Can it handle winter freezing?**
A: The brass won't crack, but any water left inside can freeze and expand, potentially separating the hose from the barbs. Drain your hoses before storage — same as you would with any fitting.

**Buy it if** you want a one-time, leak-free fix for a split hose that will outlast the hose itself. **Skip it if** you're on a tight budget and only need a temporary patch — but understand you'll likely buy this anyway next season.
