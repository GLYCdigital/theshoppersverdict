A rotted fence post is the kind of problem that starts as a small lean and ends with your entire panel sagging into the neighbor's yard. The AKingKong Fence Post Repair Kit promises a fix without digging out the old concrete footer. After digging through 229 ratings and the reported experiences, here's what you actually need to know before buying.

**What's in the Box**

The kit includes two galvanized steel U-shaped brackets, two pointed ground spikes, and all necessary bolts and washers. The steel is thick — noticeably heavier than the cheap repair sleeves you'll find at big-box stores. As shown in the product image, the design sandwiches your existing post between two plates, which is the key difference from flimsier wrap-around fixes.

**How It Performs in Real Life**

The concept is straightforward: drive the spike into the ground beside your rotted post, attach the brackets to the post, and bolt everything together. The spike transfers the load to the ground while the bracket supports the post at ground level — the point where rot typically does its worst damage.

Buyers consistently report that the kit eliminates post wobble. One reviewer noted, "My fence was swaying in the wind before this. After installation, it's rock solid — I can lean on it without any give." Another mentioned using it to brace a gate post that had pulled loose, and the fix held through a full winter.

The adjustable design is a genuine plus. The brackets accommodate both 4x4 and 6x6 posts, so you're not locked into a single size. That flexibility matters if you're repairing mixed-size fencing.

**Where It Falls Short**

The ground spike is the weak point. Driving it into compacted soil or clay requires serious effort — several reviewers mentioned bending or mushrooming the spike top with a sledgehammer. If you have rocky ground, consider pre-drilling a pilot hole or wetting the soil first. It's doable, but it's not the effortless install the marketing suggests.

The metric bolts are another annoyance. You'll need a 13mm socket, and most American homeowners don't have one in their standard set. It's a small thing, but it means a trip to the hardware store mid-project.

**Who Should Buy This**

This kit is ideal for the DIY homeowner with a post that's still structurally sound above the ground line. If the wood is soft or crumbling above the soil, you need to replace the post entirely — no bracket will save it. It's also a strong choice for renters who want a reversible fix, since the brackets can be unbolted and the spike removed.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Can I use this on a concrete-set post?**
Yes, but with a caveat. The spike needs to be driven into soil beside the concrete. If your concrete footer is wider than the spike, you'll need to position the bracket offset — which reduces its effectiveness. Measure your footer first.

**Will this work on a metal post?**
No. The brackets are designed for wood posts only. The U-shape won't grip a round or square metal post securely.

**How long does installation take?**
Most buyers report 30 to 60 minutes per post, not counting the time spent wrestling with the spike in hard ground.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if:** You have a rotted post base, solid wood above the ground line, and you want a fix that doesn't involve a jackhammer and a bag of Quikrete.

**Skip it if:** Your post is rotten above the soil line, you have extremely rocky ground, or you're expecting a zero-effort install. This is a solid product with honest limitations — not a miracle worker.

The AKingKong Fence Post Repair Kit does exactly what it promises: it stabilizes a failing post without the mess of concrete work. The quality steel and adjustable sizing justify the cost over cheaper alternatives. Just budget for a sledgehammer and a metric socket before you start.
