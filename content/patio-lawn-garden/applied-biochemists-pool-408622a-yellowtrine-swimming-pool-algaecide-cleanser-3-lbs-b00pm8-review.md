**Yellow algae is the stubbornest problem in pool ownership.** Regular chlorine shock treats green algae fine, but that yellow-brown dust that clings to walls and laughs at your chlorine levels? That's mustard algae, and it needs a different weapon entirely. Applied Biochemists Yellowtrine is that weapon — a granular algaecide designed specifically for this one frustrating problem. With 215 ratings averaging 4.3 stars, it's clearly working for many pool owners. But it's not magic, and understanding its limitations will save you a wasted weekend.

**What you actually get:** A 3-pound jug of granular sodium tetraborate-based algaecide. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward white container with clear dosing instructions. That 3 pounds treats up to 30,000 gallons — enough for two full applications on a typical 15,000–20,000 gallon residential pool. The math matters here: you need a double treatment for stubborn blooms, so don't buy one jug expecting to also treat a large neighbor's pool.

**How it performs in real use:** Yellowtrine is a flocculant and algaecide combo that works by settling the algae to the pool floor so your filter and vacuum can remove it. The critical step most people skip is brushing. This product only works if you brush the affected walls and floor first to loosen the algae's grip, then broadcast the granules over the deepest part of the pool. Users who follow the instructions report visible clearing within 24–48 hours. One pool owner noted, *"I fought yellow algae for three weeks with shock. Yellowtrine cleared it in two days after I actually brushed first."* That's the pattern in most positive reviews — it works, but only when you follow the prep steps.

**Where it falls short — honestly:** The pH requirement is non-negotiable. You must drop your pH to 7.2–7.4 before applying, or the product loses effectiveness. Several reviewers learned this the hard way. One wrote, *"Didn't work the first time because my pH was 7.8. Lowered it, reapplied, worked perfectly."* That's not a product defect — it's chemistry — but it means you need a reliable test kit and patience. Also, this is a reactive treatment, not a preventive. If you're looking for something to add weekly to prevent yellow algae, you're buying the wrong product.

**Who should buy this:** Pool owners with a confirmed mustard algae problem — that yellowish-brown, powdery algae that brushes off easily but returns within days. Also anyone who's shocked repeatedly with no results. If you're dealing with green algae, save your money and fix your chlorine levels instead. If you're dealing with yellow algae specifically, this is one of the most targeted products on the market.

**Common questions from shoppers:**

**Q: Is Yellowtrine safe for all pool types?**
A: Yes for vinyl, fiberglass, and gunite pools. But it's not compatible with salt chlorine generators that require copper-based algaecides. Check your system before buying.

**Q: How often do I need to apply it?**
A: For an active bloom, two applications 24 hours apart is the standard protocol. After that, the pool should be clear. It's not a weekly maintenance product.

**Q: Can I swim immediately after adding it?**
A: No. The label requires waiting until the product has fully dispersed and been filtered — typically 24 hours. Plan around swim times.

**The Verdict:** Yellowtrine earns its 4.2/5 rating because it solves a genuinely hard problem that standard products can't touch. It's not cheap, it demands prep work, and it's useless if you skip the brushing and pH adjustment. But for the specific job it's designed for — killing mustard algae — it's one of the most reliable products available.

**Buy it if:** You have confirmed yellow/mustard algae and you're willing to brush, test pH, and follow the label exactly.

**Skip it if:** You're looking for a general preventive algaecide, you have a salt system with copper restrictions, or you won't do the prep work. You'll just waste money and get frustrated.
