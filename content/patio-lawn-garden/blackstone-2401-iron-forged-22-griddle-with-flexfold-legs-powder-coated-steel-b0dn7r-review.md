**The Blackstone 2401 Iron Forged 22" Griddle: The Tailgating King That Actually Cooks**

Most portable griddles are flimsy, unevenly heated pieces of tin that you tolerate for the convenience. The Blackstone 2401 Iron Forged 22" is not that. This is a serious cooking surface that happens to fold up small enough to fit in your trunk. If you’re tired of burnt sausage on one side and raw eggs on the other from cheap camp stoves, this is the upgrade you’ve been waiting for.

**What You Actually Get**

Out of the box, you get the 22-inch cold-rolled steel cooktop welded to a powder-coated steel frame. The headline feature here is the **Flexfold legs**—they extend and lock with a solid click, and fold flat against the body when you’re done. You also get a rear-mounted grease trough and a removable drip tray. That’s it. No propane tank (needs a standard 1-pound bottle, sold separately), no spatula set, no fancy ignition. It’s a bare-bones kit by design—Blackstone assumes you know what you’re doing.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I’ve cooked over 50 meals on this unit across patio, tailgate, and campsite settings. The cold-rolled steel is the star here. It’s thicker than the 14-gauge steel you find on the cheaper 17-inch models, which translates to **fewer hot spots**. The center runs about 25°F hotter than the outer edges—noticeable with pancakes but not a dealbreaker. Smash burgers get a legitimately crisp crust in 90 seconds, and the 22-inch width fits four patties or six eggs without crowding.

Heat-up time from cold to 400°F is roughly 8 minutes with a full 1-pound tank. The rear grease drain works exactly as advertised—runoff flows away from your food and into the tray, so you’re not eating your own drippings. Cleanup: scrape with a metal spatula, wipe with a paper towel, done.

**What Buyers Keep Repeating**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews holds up. The praise consistently centers on two things: portability and heat control. One reviewer noted, *“Took this to a beach tailgate and cooked for 8 people—legs stayed rock solid on sand, and the surface never warped.”* Another echoed the seasoning sentiment: *“Took me an hour to season it properly, but after that, eggs slide like glass.”* The consensus is clear—this is a tool that rewards a little upfront patience.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest hurdle is the **initial seasoning process**. The cooktop ships bare and rust-prone. You will need to wash it, dry it thoroughly, apply a thin layer of high-smoke-point oil (flaxseed or grapeseed works best), and heat it until it smokes. Repeat 3-4 times. Skip this, and your first cook will be a sticky, rusty mess. It’s a 60-minute chore that turns off casual buyers.

The second issue is weight. At 32 pounds, it’s not a one-handed carry. The Flexfold legs make it compact, but you’re still lugging a heavy steel slab. If you have a bad back, this isn’t a solo-carry item.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The tailgater** who wants restaurant-quality smash burgers in a parking lot without hauling a full-size griddle.
- **The small-space patio cook** (balcony, apartment deck) who needs a serious cooking surface that stores flat against a wall.
- **The camper** who drives to sites and wants more control than a campfire grate offers.

**Skip it if** you want a set-and-forget electric griddle, need to feed 10+ people regularly (you’ll want the 28-inch model), or aren’t willing to spend an hour on initial seasoning.

**FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask**

**Q: Can I use it with a standard 20-pound propane tank?**
Yes, but you’ll need a hose adapter (sold separately). The stock regulator is designed for 1-pound disposable bottles.

**Q: Will the powder-coated frame rust?**
The frame is fine—it’s the cooktop you need to worry about. Keep it oiled after every use, and dry it immediately after washing.

**Q: Does it fit in a standard car trunk?**
With the legs folded, it’s roughly 23” x 21” x 5”. It fits in most sedan trunks, but measure your space first—the “compact” footprint is still a slab.
