## What you're actually getting

Each case contains four 1-gallon jugs of 12.5% liquid chlorine. That's roughly 17 pounds of pool shock. The jugs are standard pool-chemical containers — white, opaque, with a basic screw cap. There's no measuring cup included, which is worth noting because you'll need one (or as shown in the product image, a dosing cup). The concentration is the headline here: 12.5% vs. the 10% you'll find from many competitors. That extra 2.5% means you're pouring less chemical to achieve the same free chlorine residual.

## How it performs in the real world

Liquid chlorine is fundamentally different from granular shock. It's already dissolved, so it disperses instantly when you pour it into the pool — no cloud of white powder, no waiting for granules to break down. This makes it ideal for shocking at dusk (chlorine degrades in sunlight, so evening application is the standard practice).

Buyer experiences skew overwhelmingly positive. One reviewer put it simply: "Turned my green pool clear overnight." Another noted, "I've tried every granular shock on the market. This liquid is the only thing that keeps my pool clear without the residue." The pattern across ratings is consistent — it works, and it works quickly.

The 12.5% concentration also matters for cost-per-treatment. You'll use roughly 25% less product per shock compared to 10% liquid chlorine. Over a pool season, that difference adds up.

## Where it falls short

Liquid chlorine has a shelf-life problem. Unlike granular shock, which can sit in a garage for years, liquid chlorine loses potency as it sits. Heat accelerates this degradation. If you buy a case in April and don't use it until August, don't expect full 12.5% strength. Buy what you'll use within a few months and store it in a cool, dark spot.

The jug design is also a minor annoyance. There's no spout, and the screw cap doesn't seal tight enough to prevent drips. A funnel makes pouring manageable, and you'll want to wipe the jug before storing it. A few buyers reported shipping delays during peak summer months — this is a heavy product, so don't expect Prime-speed delivery to be the norm.

One more note: this is strong stuff. At 12.5%, it's a skin and eye irritant. Wear gloves and eye protection when handling. That's true of all pool shock, but the higher concentration demands extra respect.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do I use per shock?**
A: For a standard 10,000-gallon pool, use about 1 quart (32 oz) for regular shocking. For a green pool, double it — 2 quarts. Check your free chlorine levels after 24 hours and adjust. Always shock in the evening.

**Q: Can I just pour this directly into the pool?**
A: Yes, but pour it around the perimeter while walking the edge, or add it to the skimmer with the pump running. Don't pour it all in one spot — you'll get localized high chlorine that can fade a vinyl liner. Never mix with other chemicals.

**Q: Does this work in saltwater pools?**
A: Yes, but you likely don't need it as often since your salt cell generates chlorine. Use this for shock treatments, not routine chlorination.
