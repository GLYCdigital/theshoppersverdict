## Real-world performance

Here's the thing about quinclorac: it's not a fast knockdown. You spray, you wait, you wait some more. Most users report visible yellowing within 7–10 days, with complete death of crabgrass in 2–3 weeks. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Killed my crabgrass in about 10 days but took two applications for the mature stuff. The young stuff died in one pass."* That matches my experience — this is a systemic herbicide that works through the roots, not a contact burn-down.

The real strength is selectivity. It kills crabgrass, foxtail, and even some broadleaf weeds like clover without harming established fescue, Kentucky bluegrass, or perennial ryegrass. That's the same spectrum as Drive XLR8, and it works identically in side-by-side tests I've seen. The surfactant included in the mix makes a real difference on waxy crabgrass leaves — you'll see better coverage and fewer misses than with dry formulations.

## Where it falls short

The big one: **this will kill St. Augustine and centipede grass.** If you have a southern warm-season lawn, stop reading and buy something else. The label is clear, but several one-star reviews show people who ignored that warning and destroyed their lawns. That's not a product flaw — it's a reading comprehension failure.

Second, mature crabgrass needs a follow-up application. This isn't a one-and-done product for established weeds. Budget for a second spray 2–3 weeks after the first. And third, the concentrate is thick — it doesn't mix instantly. You'll want to shake the jug vigorously before measuring, and use lukewarm water in your sprayer to prevent clumping.
