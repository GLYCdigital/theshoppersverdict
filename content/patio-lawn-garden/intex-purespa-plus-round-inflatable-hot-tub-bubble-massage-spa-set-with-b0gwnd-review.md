# INTEX PureSpa Plus Round Inflatable Hot Tub: The $600 Backyard Spa That Actually Works (Mostly)

Let's be straight about what this is: a 4-person inflatable hot tub that promises a luxury spa experience for a fraction of a permanent installation. The INTEX PureSpa Plus delivers on the core promises — hot water, strong bubbles, easy setup — but it comes with maintenance responsibilities that some buyers underestimate.

## What's Actually in the Box

When the box arrives (and it's heavy — roughly 130 pounds), you're getting the full package:

- **The spa itself**: A 77-inch diameter, 28-inch deep round tub that holds 210 gallons
- **The pump/heater unit**: This is the brain — a 1,260W heater and 140-jet bubble system combined
- **Two filter cartridges** (Type S1, rated for 7 days each)
- **Thermal cover** with locking clips
- **Ground cloth** to protect the underside
- **Carrying bag** for off-season storage
- **Repair patch kit** (you'll likely need it eventually)

The assembly is genuinely simple. Lay out the ground cloth, spread the tub, and start inflating. The included pump handles inflation, but here's the honest truth: it takes 10-15 minutes and sounds like a vacuum cleaner the entire time.

## Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Hot

**Heating speed** is where this unit surprises. The 1,260W heater brings cold tap water to 104°F in roughly 24-48 hours depending on ambient temperature. That's not fast, but it's consistent. One Amazon reviewer noted: *"Took about 30 hours to reach 100 degrees from our well water. After that, it held temperature well even on cool nights."*

The **140 bubble jets** are genuinely impressive. Unlike cheaper spas that feel like a gentle fizz, these push real pressure. Sit over the jet rows and you'll feel a legit massage on your lower back. The control panel on the pump unit lets you toggle between heating, bubble massage, and combined mode — simple and effective.

**Heat retention** is decent but not stellar. The thermal cover with locking clips does its job, but expect the heater to cycle on every 30-45 minutes in cooler weather. This shows up on your electric bill — one buyer calculated an additional $30-40 monthly cost. Budget for that.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The strongest recurring themes in positive reviews:

- **Setup is genuinely easy** — no tools, no plumbers, no permits
- **The bubble massage is powerful** — multiple reviewers compared it favorably to commercial spa jets
- **Temperature control is accurate** — the digital display and controls work reliably
- **The carrying bag and compact storage** — it packs down to roughly the size of a large suitcase

One 4-star review summed it up: *"For the price, this is unbeatable. We use it 3-4 times a week. The jets are strong enough to work out knots, and it's held up for two seasons so far."*

## Where It Falls Short

Now the honest part. This isn't a luxury spa — it's an inflatable tub with real limitations:

**The floor is hard.** There are no molded seats or cushions. After 30 minutes, your backside will feel it. Several reviewers recommend buying a spa mat or waterproof seat pad separately. That's an extra $30-50 you should budget for.

**Water chemistry is a full-time job.** Without proper chemical treatment, the water turns cloudy within 3-4 days. You'll need test strips, chlorine or bromine, pH adjusters, and shock treatment. This isn't optional — it's a weekly maintenance ritual. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"If you don't want to deal with chemicals, don't buy a hot tub. This isn't a pool — it's a warm bath, and bacteria love warm baths."*

**The pump is loud.** During heating cycles, it emits a constant hum that's noticeable if the spa is near a bedroom window. It's not unbearable, but it's not quiet either.

**Durability concerns.** The PVC and polyester laminate material is 3-ply, but sharp objects and dog claws are a real threat. The included repair kit works, but it's a patch, not a permanent fix. Treat the floor area with the ground cloth and consider a padded mat underneath.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You want a hot tub experience without the $8,000+ installation cost
- You're a renter or plan to move — this packs up and goes with you
- You're okay with weekly water chemistry maintenance
- You want strong bubble massage, not just warm water

**Skip it if:**
- You expect a permanent, low-maintenance spa
- You have pets or kids who might puncture the material
- You're sensitive to noise during operation
- You want a tub for more than 4 people (this fits 4 adults tightly)

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: How long does it take to heat up from scratch?**
A: Plan on 24-48 hours to reach 104°F from cold tap water. Starting with warm water from a hose (if you have a hot water source) cuts this to 12-18 hours. Once heated, it maintains temperature with periodic heater cycles.

**Q: What chemicals do I need and how often?**
A: You'll need test strips, chlorine or bromine tablets, pH increaser/decreaser, and shock treatment. Test water every 2-3 days, add chemicals as needed, and shock weekly. Budget about $20-30 per month for consumables.

**Q: Can this survive winter?**
A: The unit is rated for operation down to about 40°F. Below that, the heater works harder, and freezing is a real risk if you lose power. For winter use, you'll need a spa blanket and possibly a winter cover. Most owners drain and store it indoors when temps drop below freezing.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5 stars**

The INTEX PureSpa Plus is the best value in inflatable hot tubs if you understand what you're buying: a portable, bubble-massage spa that requires ongoing maintenance and has a firm floor. It's not a permanent installation, and it won't fool anyone into thinking it's a $10,000 Jacuzzi.

**Buy it if** you want affordable hot water therapy with strong jet pressure and don't mind the chemical routine. **Skip it if** you want a low-maintenance luxury experience or have concerns about material durability.

For the price, it delivers honest value — just keep your expectations realistic, and buy a seat pad while you're at it.
