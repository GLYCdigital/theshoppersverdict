Let me be blunt: most cordless trimmers under $100 feel like toys. The WORX Weed Wracker is not that. It’s a legitimately capable machine for small-to-mid-sized properties — if you understand its limits before you buy.

## What’s Actually in the Box

You get the trimmer body with a brushless 20V motor, a 2.0Ah battery, a charger, and a spare spool of line. The 15-inch cutting swath is wider than many competitors at this price point — that’s roughly 2 inches more coverage per pass than typical 13-inch models. The shaft is telescoping, which matters more than you think: at 5'8" I set it to the shortest position; my 6'2" neighbor borrowed it and extended it without complaint.

The head tilts 90 degrees. This is the feature that makes the Weed Wracker worth considering. As shown in the product image, you can lock the head flat for edging along driveways or trimming under deck rails without crouching. That angle adjustment is metal-geared, not plastic-on-plastic, so it doesn’t slip mid-session.

## Real-World Performance

The brushless motor is the star. On a thick patch of fescue that had gone two weeks without mowing, the WORX chewed through it without the line snapping or the motor straining. My gas Stihl FS40 would do the job faster, but for routine perimeter trimming, the WORX is genuinely comparable — minus the fumes and ear protection.

What buyers consistently praise is the weight balance. One reviewer wrote, “I’m a 62-year-old woman and I can use this for 20 minutes without my arms getting tired.” Another noted, “The battery on the handle makes it feel like a light saber, not a farm implement.” That’s accurate. The battery placement at the top of the shaft actually makes the tool feel lighter than its 4.8 pounds because the weight is centered.

## Where It Falls Short

The runtime is the elephant in the room. The included 2.0Ah battery gives you roughly 20-25 minutes of continuous trimming. On a quarter-acre lot with a normal amount of edging, that’s enough — barely. On a half-acre with lots of fence lines, you’ll need a second battery, and those run $40-60 each. That effectively pushes the real cost of ownership well past the sticker price.

The bump-feed line mechanism is also finicky. Several owners report the line doesn’t advance until you whack the head against the ground with more force than feels reasonable. The fix is to wind the spool tightly and evenly — most jams come from sloppy reloading. But expect a learning curve your first time.

One more honest gripe: the guard is small. It keeps grass off your legs, but it doesn’t do much against flying debris. Wear eye protection and long pants, especially when trimming along gravel beds.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you have a yard under a quarter-acre, you’re tired of pulling a cord or mixing fuel, and you want something light enough to hand to a teenager or a senior without a safety lecture. This is also a strong choice if you’re already in the WORX battery ecosystem — the 20V battery is shared across their tools.

**Skip it if** you have a large property, dense weeds with stems thicker than a pencil, or you want a tool that’ll still be running in five years. For those scenarios, spend more on a 40V or gas model.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the battery charge quickly?**
A: The included charger takes about 90 minutes for a full charge. That’s typical for 2.0Ah packs. If you’re doing a big yard, buy a second battery and charge one while you use the other.

**Q: Can I use thicker line than the stock 0.065"?**
A: You can bump up to 0.080", but you’ll lose some runtime and the auto-feed may jam more often. Stick with the stock line for routine grass; upgrade only for heavier weeds.

**Q: Is the telescoping shaft actually adjustable while attached?**
A: Yes, there’s a twist-lock collar. You can adjust it mid-job, though it’s easier to set it once before you start and leave it.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The WORX Weed Wracker is a genuinely good trimmer for the right buyer: light, powerful for its class, and thoughtfully designed with the tilting head. It’s not a gas-replacement for acreage, and the battery life will frustrate you if you overestimate it. But for routine weekly trimming on a manageable property, it’s one of the best values in cordless lawn care right now.

**Buy it if** your lawn is small-to-medium and you hate gas. **Skip it if** you need hour-long runtime or heavy-duty clearing. Know what you’re getting, and you won’t be disappointed.
