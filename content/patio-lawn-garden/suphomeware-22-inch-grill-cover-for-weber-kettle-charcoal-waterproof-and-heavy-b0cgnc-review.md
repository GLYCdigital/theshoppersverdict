Let’s cut through the “premium cover” marketing. You own a Weber Kettle — the most iconic charcoal grill in America — and you need something that survives a Midwest thunderstorm, a week of 90-degree sun, and the neighbor’s cat using it as a scratching post. The Suphomeware 22 Inch Grill Cover claims to do all that for less than half the price of Weber’s branded option. I’ve dug through 235 verified ratings and the spec sheet to see if it actually holds up.

**What you’re actually getting**

The package is straightforward: one cover, folded flat. No straps, no bungees, no storage bag — just the cover. That’s fine, because the design compensates. It’s built from 600D Oxford polyester with a PVC backing, which is the same fabric family used in quality boat covers. The seams are taped and heat-sealed rather than just stitched, which is where cheap covers fail first. You get two webbed side handles for easy removal, and two buckled vents near the top that let heat and moisture escape — a feature that prevents the internal condensation that rusts out grill grates.

**Fit and real-world performance**

As shown in the product image, this is cut specifically for the 22-inch Weber Kettle — not a generic “fits up to 24-inch” cover. That precision matters. Buyers consistently note it drops down past the ash catcher and covers the top half of the legs, which means wind won’t get under the rim and flip it off. One reviewer said, *“Fits my 22” Weber like it was tailored. The vents actually work — no more mildew smell when I lift it in the spring.”* Another noted the fabric thickness: *“You can tell this isn’t a $12 tarp. It’s got real weight to it.”*

In terms of weather resistance, the PVC backing does the heavy lifting. Multiple owners reported leaving it out through heavy rain and snow, finding the interior bone dry. The fabric doesn’t absorb water, so it won’t get heavy and sag into the grill. That’s the difference between a cover that lasts two seasons and one that lasts five.

**Where it falls short**

The most common complaint in the reviews isn’t about materials — it’s about wind. There’s no drawstring or elastic hem at the base. In exposed, gusty areas, the bottom flaps around and can catch a strong breeze. One reviewer warned, *“Great cover, but I had to add two bungee hooks to the bottom loops during a storm. Without them, it shifted sideways.”* There are no dedicated tie-down loops, so you’ll have to get creative with clips or weights.

Second, the fabric is stiff when new. It arrives folded with hard creases that take a few warm days to relax. It won’t drape like a soft fabric cover right out of the box. This isn’t a functional issue, but if you’re expecting a glove-like fit immediately, adjust your expectations.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the grill owner who leaves their Kettle outside year-round and wants a cover that actually protects the investment without spending $60+ on a branded option. It’s also ideal for anyone in rainy or snowy climates who’s dealt with a cheap cover that wets through and leaves rust spots on the lid. If you live in a moderate climate and store your grill in a garage or under a patio overhang, you’re probably overbuying — a lighter cover would suffice.

**FAQ**

*Does this fit the Weber Jumbo Joe or other sizes?*  
No. It’s cut specifically for the 22-inch Kettle (the Original and Premium models). For smaller portable kettles, look for a 14-inch cover.

*Will it handle a New England winter?*  
Based on reviews, yes. The PVC-backed fabric sheds snow and ice without cracking. Just knock heavy snow off periodically to prevent weight stress on the handles.

*Is the inside padded?*  
No, it’s a single layer of coated fabric. It protects against moisture and UV but won’t cushion against impacts. That’s typical for this price point.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely weatherproof cover for your 22-inch Weber Kettle that won’t disintegrate after one season. The fabric quality, sealed seams, and functional vents justify the price, and at 4.7 stars with 235 ratings, the consensus is clear.

**Skip it if** you live in a persistently windy area and don’t want to rig your own tie-downs — without bottom cinching, it’s not fully wind-secure out of the box.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It nails the essentials (waterproofing, fit, durability) and only stumbles on the lack of a drawstring. For the price, it’s the smart buy for Weber Kettle owners who actually cook outdoors.
