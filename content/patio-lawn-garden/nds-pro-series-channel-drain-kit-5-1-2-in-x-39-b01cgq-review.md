If standing water on your patio is turning every rainstorm into a mosquito breeding project, the NDS Pro Series Channel Drain Kit is the fix you've been circling. This 5-1/2-inch wide by 39-3/8-inch long trench drain kit is the contractor-grade solution that DIYers eventually graduate to after fighting with flimsy plastic gutters. ## What's Actually in the Box

NDS doesn't make you hunt down pieces. The kit includes the 39-3/8-inch polypropylene channel body, a snap-in ductile iron-style grate (the polymer version, not actual cast iron), two end caps with knockouts, a 3-inch outlet adapter with a 4-inch option, and stainless steel screws. The grate sits flush with the channel edges — no tripping hazard, no rocking when you walk on it. As shown in the product image, the grate has a subtle mesh pattern that handles water flow without looking like a utility drain bolted to your patio.

## Real-World Performance

The standout engineering here is the pre-molded 2% slope built into the channel. You don't need to calculate pitch when setting the concrete — just level the top edge and let the internal slope do the drainage work. Water moves efficiently toward the outlet, and the 5-1/2-inch width captures runoff from a surprisingly large surface area. One reviewer noted, "I installed this along my driveway edge and it handled a 2-inch downpour without backing up. My basement stayed dry for the first time in years." Another contractor-type buyer mentioned using four sections in series for a 13-foot run — the connection joints are gasketed and seal cleanly without adhesive.

The grate holds up to real traffic. It's rated for light vehicle use, and buyers consistently report it surviving lawn mower passes and occasional car tires without cracking. The stainless steel screws are a thoughtful touch — no rust streaks bleeding into your concrete.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. At 5-1/2 inches wide, this is a heavy-duty product. If you're draining a modest 10x12 patio, you're paying for capacity you don't need — NDS makes a 4-inch version that's cheaper and plenty for light residential use. Some installers have reported the channel bowing during concrete pours if they didn't brace the interior with temporary supports. You can't just set it and dump a wheelbarrow of mix on top; you need to hold it in place while the concrete sets.

The grate slots are narrow enough to catch small debris. Pine needles, pea gravel, and mulch will accumulate, and the grate doesn't hinge open — you'll need a screwdriver to lift it for cleaning. It's a 10-minute chore, but it's worth budgeting for in the fall.

## Who Should Buy This

This kit is ideal for the homeowner tackling a **driveway edge drainage** or a **large patio slab** with significant runoff. If you're already pouring concrete or setting pavers, the integrated slope saves you real time. It's also a solid pick for **basement waterproofing projects** where you need a reliable channel at the foundation perimeter.

Skip it if you're doing a tiny walkway or a spot drain — you'll overpay for width you don't need. And if you don't want to commit to a concrete pour, consider a simpler French drain system instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I connect multiple sections together?**
Yes. The channels lock together with a tongue-and-groove joint. Use the included gaskets and screws between sections. For runs over 8 feet, you'll want a center outlet to prevent water pooling at the ends.

**Q: Does the grate rust?**
No. It's a polymer composite — same look as cast iron, zero rust. The screws are stainless steel, so no discoloration there either.

**Q: What's the minimum concrete depth needed?**
You need at least 2-1/2 inches of concrete below the channel base and about 1 inch over the side flanges. For a 4-inch slab, that works cleanly.
