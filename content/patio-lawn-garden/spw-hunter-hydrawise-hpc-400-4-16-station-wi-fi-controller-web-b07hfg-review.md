Most smart irrigation controllers promise "set it and forget it." The Hunter Hydrawise HPC-400 actually delivers on that promise — but it demands a bit of setup patience and a solid Wi-Fi signal to earn its keep.

**What's in the box**

You get the HPC-400 base controller (4 stations), a plug-in transformer, mounting screws, and a quick-start guide. The 4.3-inch color touchscreen is pre-installed. To expand beyond 4 zones, you'll need to buy Hunter's HPC-ADJ expansion modules separately — they slot into the side panel and bring you up to 16 stations total. The unit itself is a compact 10.5 x 7.5 x 2.5 inches, so it mounts cleanly on a garage wall without looking like a piece of industrial equipment. As shown in the product image, the front face has a clean, modern layout with the screen centered and status LEDs tucked below.

**Performance and real-world use**

This is where the HPC-400 separates itself from cheaper Wi-Fi controllers. The Hydrawise platform uses predictive weather data — pulling from local stations, not just a single forecast feed — to automatically skip or adjust watering cycles before rain hits. In practice, that means you're not watering your lawn 20 minutes before a thunderstorm. One reviewer noted, "It saved my water bill the first month. The controller saw the rain coming and canceled two cycles. My old timer would have just run."

The app is the control center. You can pause zones, run manual cycles, adjust run times by percentage, and get push notifications for flow alerts (if you add the optional flow meter) or system faults. The touchscreen on the unit itself is responsive and backlit, which matters if you're programming it at dusk in a dark garage. Setup takes about 15 minutes if your Wi-Fi reaches the controller location — that's the single biggest caveat.

**Where it falls short**

The Wi-Fi dependency is real. If the controller is mounted in a detached garage or a metal enclosure, expect connection drops. Hunter recommends a 2.4 GHz network only — if your router broadcasts combined 2.4/5 GHz bands, you'll need to split them in your router settings first. That tripped up several buyers. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: "Took me an hour to figure out why it kept losing connection. The 5 GHz band was the problem. Once I separated the networks, it's been flawless."

Also, there's no built-in flow sensing or soil moisture detection. For a controller in this price range, that's not a dealbreaker — Hunter sells add-on modules — but know that "smart" here primarily means weather-based, not soil-based.

**Who should buy this**

- **Tech-savvy homeowners** with 6+ zones who want granular control from their phone. The Hydrawise app is among the best in the category.
- **People in unpredictable climates** — the predictive weather adjustments are the standout feature.
- **DIY installers** who don't want to pay an electrician. The wiring terminals are clearly labeled, and the included manual is genuinely helpful.

**Skip it if** you have a weak Wi-Fi signal where the controller will live, or if you want a plug-and-play setup with zero app configuration. This is not that product.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it work with the Hunter Hydrawise app on both iOS and Android?**  
Yes, both platforms are supported. The app is stable and regularly updated. Some iOS users report that using the QR code scan during setup is faster than manual entry — do that.

**Q: Can I control it manually without the app?**  
Absolutely. The touchscreen lets you run zones, set schedules, and adjust watering times directly on the unit. The app is a convenience, not a requirement.

**Q: Will the HPC-400 work with my existing Hunter solenoid valves?**  
Yes, it's compatible with standard 24 VAC irrigation solenoids — that includes Hunter, Rain Bird, and Orbit valves. You don't need to replace your valve wiring.

**The Verdict**

The SPW Hunter Hydrawise HPC-400 is a genuinely smart irrigation controller that earns its 4.5-star rating — not through gimmicks, but through reliable weather-based automation and a polished app ecosystem. The expansion capability from 4 to 16 stations means it can grow with your property, and the touchscreen makes on-device programming painless.

**Buy it if** you have solid Wi-Fi at the controller location, want to cut your water bill without thinking about it, and appreciate a controller that learns your climate instead of just following a timer.

**Skip it if** your mounting spot has weak connectivity or you want soil moisture sensing out of the box. For everyone else, this is one of the best smart irrigation controllers you can install today.
