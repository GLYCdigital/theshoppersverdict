Let me be blunt: the GreenSweep Vac & Blow is a tool for a very specific person. If you have a quarter-acre of mature oaks, keep scrolling. If you've got a postage-stamp patio, a few shrubs, and a growing resentment toward your leaf blower's cord, this 3-in-1 deserves a look.

The pitch is simple. One compact unit that vacuums, blows, and mulches — no gas, no cord, no separate leaf shredder taking up garage space. At 6.8 pounds with a claimed 10:1 mulch ratio, it promises to turn autumn annoyance into compost-ready mulch. The question is whether the execution matches the idea.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the main unit with a detachable blower nozzle, a 13-gallon collection bag, and a shoulder strap. Nothing else. No extra filters, no wall mount, no storage bag. The carry handle doubles as the attachment point for the strap, and the whole thing collapses into a roughly 2-foot-long package that slides into a utility drawer. For apartment dwellers and condo owners, that footprint is the real selling point.

**Real-world performance**

The vacuum mode is where this thing earns its keep. Suction is genuinely impressive for a cordless unit — it picked up dry oak leaves, pine needles, and even the occasional stray receipt without clogging. The mulching impeller chews material into fine confetti, and the collection bag's zipper design means you're not chasing debris across the patio when you empty it.

The blower mode is... adequate. It clears light debris from concrete and decking, but don't expect to move wet matted leaves or gravel. On grass, it's borderline useless — the airflow just isn't there. One reviewer noted, "It's fine for the patio but I still need my gas blower for the lawn." That tracks.

Battery life hovers around 25 minutes of continuous use in vacuum mode, which is fine for small spaces but frustrating if you're in the middle of a job. Recharge time is roughly 90 minutes, so plan accordingly.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Three themes dominate positive reviews. First, the weight. Multiple owners describe using it one-handed for gutter cleaning and under-deck work — something heavier units make miserable. Second, the mulch quality. "The shredded leaves are perfect for my compost bin," one verified buyer wrote. "I was skeptical about the 10:1 claim, but it's close." Third, the build quality of the housing — the ABS plastic feels dense and well-put-together, not like a toy.

**Where it falls short**

The collection bag is too small, full stop. Wet leaves clump and fill it in under five minutes, and even dry debris demands emptying every few minutes on anything beyond a small patio. That's the tradeoff for the compact design, but it's worth knowing.

More concerning: the impeller is plastic. Multiple reviews describe it shattering after sucking up a small acorn or a piece of gravel. One reviewer wrote, "Worked great for three weeks, then a pebble destroyed the fan. Can't find replacement parts." At this price point, a machine that dies on a stray pebble is a legitimate liability.

**Who should buy this**

- **Patio and balcony owners** with 200 square feet or less of hardscaping
- **Gutter cleaners** who want a lightweight vacuum for downspout clogs
- **Composters** who want pre-shredded browns without a separate chipper

**Skip it if** you have a real lawn, mature trees that drop more than a few bags per season, or any history of sucking up things that aren't leaves.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a compact, lightweight, genuinely useful 3-in-1 for small spaces and you're careful about what you vacuum. **Skip it if** you need a workhorse for real yard maintenance — the small bag and fragile impeller will have you reaching for the old rake by October.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A clever concept with real utility for small-space owners, undermined by a fragile impeller and a collection bag that's too small for serious use. At the current price, it's a reasonable gamble for the right buyer; the wrong buyer will be frustrated within a month.
