If you're chasing a green pool all summer, the problem isn't always chlorine — it's that the sun is eating your chlorine before it can do its job. That's the gap the Robelle 2604B Pool Stabilizer is designed to fill. This is cyanuric acid (CYA) in granular form, and at 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it's clearly doing something right for pool owners.

## What you actually get

The Robelle 2604B comes as a 2.5-pound container of white granular cyanuric acid. That's enough to stabilize a 10,000-gallon pool at the recommended 30-50 ppm level. The product image shows a straightforward white jug with clear dosage instructions on the back label. There's no scoop included, so grab a measuring cup from the kitchen before you start.

## Performance and build quality

Here's where this product earns its keep. Most granular CYA products are notoriously slow to dissolve — you often need to hang them in a sock in front of the return jet for days. The Robelle 2604B dissolves noticeably faster. Multiple buyers noted they dissolved it in a bucket of warm water and poured it directly into the skimmer with good results. One reviewer put it simply: "Dissolved quickly in a bucket, added to pool, and my chlorine loss dropped significantly within a week."

The chemical itself is fine-grade and consistent, which matters because clumping or inconsistent particle size can clog skimmers or settle on pool floors. I didn't see reports of either issue in the feedback.

## What buyers consistently praise

The dominant theme in positive reviews is simple: it works, and it works fast. Pool owners report that after adding this stabilizer, their chlorine residual holds much longer between treatments. One reviewer noted: "I was adding chlorine every other day. Now I'm at twice a week and my water stays clear." That's the real-world benefit — fewer chemical costs and less maintenance time.

Another consistent positive is the value. At a price point that undercuts many name-brand stabilizers, buyers feel they're getting comparable performance without the premium markup.

## Where it falls short

No product is perfect, and this one has two real caveats. First, the lack of a measuring scoop is a genuine annoyance. CYA dosing is weight-based, and guessing wrong can push your stabilizer too high — which actually *reduces* chlorine effectiveness. You'll need a kitchen scale or a known-volume cup to dose accurately.

Second, and this is critical: if you use chlorine tablets (trichlor) or shock with dichlor, those already add CYA to your pool. Adding this stabilizer on top can push you into over-stabilized territory (above 70 ppm), which makes chlorine nearly useless. You need a separate CYA test kit to monitor levels monthly. This isn't a flaw in the product itself, but it's a real risk for unaware buyers.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do I need for my pool?**
A: The general rule is 1 pound per 10,000 gallons to raise CYA by about 10 ppm. The 2.5-pound container treats a 10,000-gallon pool to the recommended 30 ppm starting level. Test your water first — if you're already at 20 ppm, you won't need the whole container.

**Q: Can I pour it directly into the skimmer?**
A: You can, but pre-dissolving in a bucket of warm water first is safer. Pouring directly can cause undissolved granules to sit on the pool floor and bleach vinyl liners. The Robelle dissolves fast enough that the bucket method takes only a few minutes.

**Q: How often do I need to add stabilizer?**
A: CYA doesn't evaporate or get used up like chlorine. You only need to add more when you backwash heavily, drain water, or experience splash-out losses. Test monthly and add only when levels drop below 30 ppm.
