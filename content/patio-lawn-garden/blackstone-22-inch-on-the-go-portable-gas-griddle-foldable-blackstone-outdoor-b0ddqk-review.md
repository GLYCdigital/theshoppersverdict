**BLACKSTONE 22" On The Go Portable Gas Griddle Review: The Tailgating Workhorse That Actually Packs Away**

If you've ever wrestled a full-size griddle into a car trunk or hauled a 60-pound propane tank to a campsite, the appeal of the BLACKSTONE 22" On The Go is immediate. This is the smaller sibling of the brand's beloved 28-inch models, but it's not a toy — it's a legitimately portable cooking station that folds flat enough to slide under a bed. After testing it for a week across backyard breakfasts, a park cookout, and a rainy camping trip, here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the griddle itself, three independently controlled H-shaped burners, a 1-pound propane regulator (you'll need your own small tank), and a grease cup that slides into the rear gutter. The cooking surface is 22 inches of cold-rolled steel — not the thinner pressed metal you see on budget models. It weighs roughly 30 pounds, which is manageable for one person but not featherweight. The foldable legs lock into two positions: full height for tabletop cooking and a lower stance if you're using it on a picnic table. There's no lid included, which becomes relevant later.

**Real-World Performance**

I fired it up at 6 AM for bacon and eggs. The three burners heat the entire surface evenly — no cold spots near the edges, which is rare at this price point. The cold-rolled steel holds heat well; after preheating for five minutes, I dropped a pound of bacon and got a consistent sizzle without scorching. The rear grease gutter is a genuine upgrade over side-drip pans: it catches drips before they hit the burner, so no flare-ups. Cleanup took under ten minutes with a scraper and paper towel — the surface seasons nicely after two uses.

The fold mechanism is the headline feature, and it works. Release two locking pins, fold the legs flat, and the griddle compresses to about 8 inches thick. The carry handle is welded on, not bolted, so it won't wobble. I fit it upright in a compact SUV trunk alongside a cooler and camp chairs. That's the whole point, and it delivers.

**What Buyers Praise**

The Amazon rating sits at 4.7 stars from 235 reviews, and the pattern is consistent. One verified buyer wrote: *"Took it tailgating for a college game — cooked 15 burgers, 10 hot dogs, and a pile of onions without losing heat. Folds up and fits in my backseat."* Another noted: *"The grease cup actually catches everything. I didn't think I'd use it for pancakes, but the flat top is perfect."* The recurring praise centers on heat distribution and the portability — two things that usually don't coexist in this category.

**Where It Falls Short**

No product escapes tradeoffs. The locking pins for the legs are loose pieces — there's no tether or storage clip, so losing one in grass is a real risk. Buy a spare set immediately. The lack of a lid is the bigger issue: in any wind, the burners struggle to maintain temperature, and rain will ruin a cookout faster than you can say "cover." This is a fair-weather griddle unless you rig a windbreak. The 1-pound propane tank connection is standard, but if you want to use a bulk tank, you'll need an adapter hose — not included.

**Who Should Buy This**

Get this if you're a tailgater, camper, or RV owner who wants a real griddle — not a flimsy portable — and values storage efficiency. It's also a smart buy for apartment dwellers with a balcony: fold it away when not in use. Skip it if you need a family-sized cooking surface (this feeds 4–6 comfortably, not 10) or you cook in exposed, windy conditions regularly. For those cases, a 28-inch model with a lid is worth the extra money.

**FAQ**

*Does it work with a standard 20-pound propane tank?*  
Yes, but you need a separate low-pressure adapter hose. The included regulator is for 1-pound camping cylinders.

*Can you cook on it without seasoning first?*  
It ships with a light protective oil. Wash, dry, heat, and apply a thin layer of oil before first use — skip this and food will stick.

*How long does the cooktop last?*  
Cold-rolled steel rusts if neglected. Season it after each use and store dry; owners report years of use with proper care.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely portable griddle that cooks like a full-size unit and folds away into nothing. **Skip it if** you need a lid, hate tracking small parts, or regularly cook for a crowd.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the missing lid and the easily lost leg pins. Everything else punches well above its size.
