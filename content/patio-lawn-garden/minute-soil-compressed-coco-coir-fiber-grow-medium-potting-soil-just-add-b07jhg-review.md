If you've ever wrestled with a 40-pound bag of soggy potting soil, the Minute Soil compressed coco coir brick is going to feel like a revelation — or at least a serious back-saver. This is a 650-gram block of dehydrated coconut husk fiber that expands to roughly 9 liters of growing medium when you add water. That's the entire pitch: just add water, wait five minutes, and you've got a fluffy, aerated substrate ready for seeds, cuttings, or container plants.

## What You Actually Get

The package is deceptively small — a dense brick about the size of a hardcover book, wrapped in a shrink-sealed plastic sleeve. Inside, that's it. No perlite, no compost, no fertilizer. This is 100% coco coir, nothing else. As shown in the product image, the brick is uniform and tightly compressed, which is why shipping costs stay low and storage is a non-issue. One brick yields enough to fill three or four 6-inch pots, or two 10-inch hanging baskets.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Hydration is fast. Drop the brick in a 5-gallon bucket with about 3 liters of warm water, wait five minutes, and break it apart with your hands. It expands dramatically — expect it to roughly double in volume before you even start fluffing it. The coir itself is fibrous but soft, with good water retention and aeration. For seedlings and microgreens, this is about as close to ideal as you'll get without mixing your own blend.

One thing I appreciate: this coir is pre-buffered. Raw coco coir often has high sodium and needs rinsing with calcium and magnesium before use. Minute Soil skips that headache — it's ready to hydrate and plant. That's a meaningful time-saver, especially if you're starting seeds in bulk.

The ratings back up the core claims. At 4.4 stars across 220 reviews, the pattern is clear: people love the convenience and the expansion ratio. ## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. This is not a complete potting mix. If you plant directly into hydrated Minute Soil and expect lush growth, you'll be disappointed — there are zero nutrients in this brick. You must amend it with compost, worm castings, or a balanced slow-release fertilizer. For seed starting, that's fine because cotyledons don't need feeding. But for anything beyond week two, you're on your own.

Also, plan your hydration container carefully. Some reviewers warned that the brick swells faster than expected and can overflow a standard 2-gallon bucket if you add too much water at once. Start with less, add more as it absorbs.

## Who Should Buy This

This is perfect for three types of shoppers: urban gardeners with limited storage, hydroponic growers who want a clean, inert medium, and anyone who's tired of hauling heavy bags from the garden center. It's also a solid choice for emergency seed starting — the brick stores indefinitely on a shelf.

Skip it if you want a true "just add water" single-bag solution for mature houseplants or vegetable containers. You'll need to buy fertilizer separately, which adds cost and complexity.

## FAQ

**Does this need to be rinsed before use?**  
No. It's pre-washed and buffered. Just hydrate and plant.

**How much water do I add to the brick?**  
About 3 liters (roughly 3 quarts) per 650g brick. Add slowly and let it absorb before mixing.

**Can I reuse this coir after a harvest?**  
Yes, but you'll need to rinse out old salts and re-amend with nutrients. It breaks down slowly and can last 2-3 growing cycles with proper care.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're starting seeds, growing microgreens, or need a lightweight, sterile medium for container gardening and want to save storage space.

**Skip it if:** You want an all-in-one potting mix with nutrients already included — this is a base ingredient, not a complete solution.
