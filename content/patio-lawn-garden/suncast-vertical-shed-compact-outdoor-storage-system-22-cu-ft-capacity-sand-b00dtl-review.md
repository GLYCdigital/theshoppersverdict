If your garage looks like a yard tool exploded in it, the Suncast Vertical Shed (22 Cu Ft) might be the answer. This compact resin cabinet stands about 65 inches tall but only takes up roughly 2 feet of depth. It's designed for people who need tool storage but don't have the space—or the budget—for a full backyard shed.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the shed's wall panels, a pre-hung door assembly, two shelves, and the hardware kit. The resin panels are textured to look like wood but won't warp, split, or need painting. The door arrives already assembled to the frame, which saves the most frustrating part of assembling most storage units. The magnetic catch keeps it shut without fiddly latches. Dimensions are approximately 65" H x 27" W x 23" D, giving you 22 cubic feet of organized space.

**Real-World Performance**

This shed holds a surprising amount when you use the included shelves. The vertical orientation means rakes, shovels, and brooms stand upright along the back wall. The two shelves give you spots for smaller items—pesticide bottles, hand tools, plant food. The resin material handles temperature swings from freezing winters to scorching summers without cracking, which is the main reason buyers stick with this brand.

Assembly takes most people 45 to 90 minutes. The panels lock together, and the instructions are clear enough, but the top section is heavy and awkward. As one buyer put it, *"Getting the roof on was a two-person job. I managed solo but nearly dropped it twice."* Budget for a helper.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The space-saving design gets the most love. One verified purchase wrote, *"I was skeptical a 2-foot-deep cabinet could replace my garage corner, but everything fits—trimmer, edger, all the hand tools."* The durability of the resin also gets frequent mentions, with owners reporting five-plus years outside with no fading or cracking. The pre-assembled door is a quiet winner—multiple reviews note how nice it is that the door lines up perfectly and closes smoothly, which isn't always the case with similar-priced competitors.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the limitations. This is not waterproof. Rain can seep through the door seam and the top seam under heavy downpour. If you store cardboard boxes or anything rust-prone inside, set it on a raised surface—pavers or concrete—and keep sensitive items off the floor. There's no floor panel included, so whatever you put it on is what your tools sit on.

The shelves are rated for light-to-moderate loads but will flex if you pile heavy bags of soil or fertilizer on them. And while the resin won't dent, it can scratch, and there's no way to repair deep gouges.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Townhouse or condo owners** with a small patio or balcony who need to stash tools out of sight
- **Homeowners with a narrow side yard** where a full shed won't fit
- **Anyone tired of tripping over rakes and leaf blowers** in the garage who wants quick access without a big footprint

**Skip It If** you're expecting true weatherproofing, need to store large equipment like a riding mower, or want a floor included. Look at a metal shed with a sealed base instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I lock it?**
A: The latch has a small hole for a padlock, though the door isn't reinforced for serious security. It deters casual theft, not determined ones.

**Q: Will it blow away in high winds?**
A: It's light—around 50 pounds empty—but the footprint is stable. In exposed areas, the included ground anchors help secure it, though many buyers skip them without issues.

**Q: Does the color fade?**
A: The UV-stabilized resin holds color well. Buyers report minimal fading after years of direct sun, which is better than most plastic sheds at this price.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need vertical tool storage in a tight footprint and want something that won't rust or rot. **Skip it if** you need waterproof protection or expect to store heavy items on the shelves.

The Suncast Vertical Shed earns a solid 4.2/5. It's not perfect—the water intrusion and two-person assembly are real faults—but for the price, it solves a specific problem well: getting yard tools out of your way without taking over your yard. For the right buyer, it's a genuinely smart purchase.
