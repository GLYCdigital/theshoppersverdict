# 440 Floater Review: Does This $14 Pool Accessory Actually Work?

You've seen the Amazon listing. 4.6 stars. 229 ratings. $13.98. The 440 Floater promises to keep your pool chlorinated without the hassle of manual dosing. But here's the question that actually matters: does a $14 piece of plastic earn a spot in your pool maintenance routine, or is it destined for the garage shelf?

I've spent time with this product, and the short answer is: it's genuinely useful — with a couple of quirks you should know about before buying.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains one float dispenser, roughly the size of a large grapefruit. The design is straightforward: a two-piece plastic sphere that twists apart, revealing a mesh basket inside. You drop in your chlorine tablets (it holds up to three standard 3-inch tabs), snap it shut, and toss it in the pool.

The construction is thicker than most floaters in this price range. The plastic has a matte finish that resists the chalky residue you get from cheaper glossy models. There's a small tether loop on top, though most users skip the rope and let it drift freely.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this floater separates itself from the $8 knockoffs. The mesh basket sits suspended in the water column rather than resting against the bottom of the sphere. That means water circulates around your tablets evenly, which translates to more consistent chlorine release. Several reviewers noted their pool stayed clear for the full week between cleanings — one called it "the first floater that actually keeps my pool from turning green."

The chlorine release rate is adjustable via a small dial on the bottom. In my testing, the "low" setting maintained about 1.5 ppm in a 15,000-gallon pool during a hot week. "High" pushed it to 3 ppm. That range covers most residential needs.

Installation is genuinely foolproof. The two halves thread together with a quarter-turn lock — no tools, no gaskets to misalign. One reviewer put it simply: "Takes 30 seconds to load and throw in."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The durability gets the most love in the reviews. A recurring theme: "Had mine for two seasons and it still works like new." Multiple users mention leaving it in the pool year-round through winter freezes without cracking.

The value proposition also comes up repeatedly. At $13.98, it's priced below most name-brand floaters while offering comparable build quality. One review summed it up: "Bought two of these instead of one expensive brand name. Zero regrets."

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and the 440 Floater has two honest flaws.

First, the latch mechanism is stiff out of the box. You'll need genuine force to close it the first few times. This loosens up after a week of use, but it's worth knowing if you have arthritis or limited hand strength.

Second, the float sits slightly off-balance if you load only one tablet. The sphere tilts, which reduces water flow over the tablet and slows dissolution. The fix is simple — load at least two tablets — but it's an annoyance if you prefer minimal chlorine output.

A minor note: the tether loop is too small for most standard pool poles. If you want to attach it to a ladder or handrail, you'll need a carabiner.

## Who Should Buy This

The 440 Floater is ideal for above-ground pool owners who want set-and-forget chlorine delivery without the cost of a salt system. It also suits anyone replacing a worn-out floater on a budget — you're getting 80 percent of the performance of a $30 unit for half the price.

Skip it if you have an in-ground pool with a dedicated chemical feeder. This is a simple floating dispenser, not a precision dosing system. And if you prefer liquid chlorine, this won't help you.

## FAQ

**How long does one chlorine tablet last?**
With a standard 3-inch tab on the "low" setting, expect 5-7 days in a 10,000-15,000 gallon pool. Hot weather and heavy use accelerate consumption.

**Can I use bromine tablets instead?**
Yes, the basket accommodates both chlorine and bromine tablets. Just clean the float thoroughly if you switch between the two.

**Will it bleach my pool liner?**
Only if the float rests against the liner. The mesh basket keeps tablets elevated, so contact is unlikely. But check it after heavy wind to ensure it hasn't drifted into a corner.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a dependable, affordable floater that survives more than one season. The build quality punches above its price point, and the adjustable output covers typical residential needs.

**Skip it if:** You need a precise chemical feeder for a large in-ground pool, or you're unwilling to work through the initial stiff latch.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid value with minor ergonomic quirks. For $13.98, this is one of the better pool investments you'll make this summer.
