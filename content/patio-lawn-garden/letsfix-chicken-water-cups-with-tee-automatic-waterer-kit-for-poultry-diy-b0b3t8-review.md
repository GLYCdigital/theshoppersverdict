**The $20 hack that ends daily water refills (if you're willing to DIY)**

If you're tired of hauling 5-gallon buckets to the coop twice a day in July heat, the letsFix Chicken Water Cups with Tee kit is worth a hard look. This is a PVC-based automatic watering system that lets your birds drink on demand — no electricity, no float valves, no daily refills. But it's not a plug-and-play product. It's a component kit that rewards a little DIY effort.

**What's actually in the box**

You get 10 water cups, 10 tee connectors, and a roll of PTFE thread tape. That's it. As shown in the product image, each cup has a push-in barbed stem that fits into a standard ¾-inch PVC pipe. The tees let you branch off your main water line to position cups where your birds actually gather. You supply the PVC pipe, a water reservoir (bucket or barrel), and a way to elevate it.

The clever part is the push-in design. No drilling, no threading, no messy PVC cement. You push the cup's stem into a pre-drilled hole in the pipe, wrap the base with the included tape, and the barbed grip holds it watertight. Most buyers report a 10-minute setup per cup.

**How it performs in real coops**

The cups hold roughly 2 ounces of water each, and the angled design lets chickens drink without dunking their beaks into a shared reservoir — which means less poop contamination than open troughs. The 90° cup angle also cuts down on splashing significantly compared to bare nipple drinkers.

Performance depends entirely on your water pressure setup. The cups work on gravity feed: raise your reservoir at least 6–12 inches above the cups, and water flows. One buyer noted, “I hung a 5-gallon bucket from the coop rafters, drilled a hole in the bottom, attached a hose to the tee, and ran lines to three cups. My 12 hens haven't needed a refill in four days.” That's the typical use case, and it works.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value is the headline. A comparable branded kit from RentACoop or Little Giant runs $50–$70. This letsFix kit sits well under half that. Reviewers also repeatedly mention the leak-free seal once properly installed. One 5-star review stated, “No drips after two weeks of use. The tape seal is the key — don't skip it.” Another praised the cup depth: “Even my bantams can reach the water, but the chicks can't drown because it's shallow.”

**Where it falls short (honestly)**

The cups' plastic feels thin. Several reviewers reported cracked cup bases when they over-tightened into PVC fittings. Use hand-tightening only, and you'll likely avoid this. Also, there are no mounting brackets included — you'll need zip ties or metal clamps to secure your PVC lines to coop walls or fencing. That's an extra trip to the hardware store.

Most importantly, this won't work if you have a low-pressure setup. If your water source is a shallow tray or a bucket sitting at ground level, the cups won't fill. You need elevation. And during freezing winters, the cups will ice over just like any other waterer — no heat options here.

**Who should buy this**

- **Backyard chicken keepers with 4–20 birds** who want a low-cost gravity watering system and don't mind basic PVC work.
- **Duck or quail owners** — the cups work for any poultry that can reach the 2-ounce reservoir.
- **DIY enthusiasts** who already have PVC pipe and a drill lying around.

Skip it if you want a ready-to-hang system out of the box, or if you keep birds in freezing climates without a heated water source.

**FAQ**

*Q: Does this work with a standard 5-gallon bucket?*  
Yes. Drill a hole in the bucket's bottom, insert a barbed hose adapter (not included), and connect it to the tee line. Elevate the bucket for gravity flow.

*Q: How many cups can I run off one bucket?*  
Most users run 4–6 cups comfortably off a 5-gallon bucket. Beyond that, water pressure drops and refill frequency increases.

*Q: Will chicks drown in these cups?*  
No. The cups hold about 2 ounces and are shallow enough for chicks to drink safely, though you may need a small step for day-olds.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a hands-on keeper who wants to eliminate daily watering chores for under $25. **Skip it if** you want a fully assembled system or deal with hard freezes without a heated option.
