Let me be direct: most landscape edging is plastic junk that cracks by year two or thin aluminum that bends if you look at it wrong. The Edge Right Cor-Ten Steel system is a different animal entirely. At 1/8-inch thick, this is real steel — the kind of material that makes you wonder why you ever settled for less.

What you actually get: Each 48-inch strip has the stakes already welded in place. No separate pins to lose, no assembly. You're buying 4-foot sections that overlap slightly at the seams, so plan for about 44 inches of effective coverage per strip. A 50-foot garden bed needs roughly 13-14 sections. That's the math nobody mentions in the marketing photos.

**Installation is simpler than it looks, but not effortless.** You hammer the stakes straight down using a rubber mallet or dead-blow hammer — a regular metal hammer will mar the surface. The steel is rigid enough that it won't crumple on rocky soil, which is the #1 complaint I see with cheaper alternatives. Buyers consistently note it goes in cleanly even in compacted clay. One reviewer put it simply: "It took me about 20 minutes per section, and I'm not handy at all."

Now, the elephant in the room: **the rust.** This is Cor-Ten steel, which means it's designed to oxidize. Yes, it rusts. That's the point. But here's what the product images don't tell you: during the first 3-6 months, that rust can bleed onto adjacent concrete or pavers, leaving orange stains that are a pain to remove. If your edging sits against a light-colored patio or walkway, you've been warned. When it weathers to a stable patina (usually within a season), the staining stops. It looks genuinely good — like a deliberate design choice, not neglect.

The praise is consistent across the 229 ratings. Buyers love the **heft and durability**. "I've used plastic edging for years and it always buckles," one verified purchase wrote. "This doesn't move. Period." Another noted the visual payoff: "The rust color matches my garden aesthetic perfectly. It looks like it's been there for decades." The 4.6-star average holds up because the product does what it claims.

Where it falls short — and you need to hear this before ordering:

- **Sharp edges are genuine hazard.** The factory-cut steel has burrs. Multiple reviews mention cut gloves, and one buyer reported a gash requiring stitches. Wear heavy leather gloves and handle carefully.
- **Curves are difficult.** This is straight steel. Bending it around circular beds requires either cutting notches or buying a separate flexible product. If your landscaping is all sweeping curves, this isn't your solution.
- **Cost per foot adds up.** This is premium priced. You're paying for steel that will outlive every plastic alternative on the market, but the upfront cost is real.

**Who should buy this:** Homeowners with straight-edged beds or defined borders who want a permanent solution — not a "re-buy in three years" solution. Anyone who's replaced flimsy plastic edging more than once will appreciate the finality of steel. It's also ideal for contemporary or desert landscaping where the rusted metal aesthetic fits naturally.

**Skip it if:** You need tight curves, have light-colored hardscaping adjacent to the edging, or you're on a tight budget for a temporary fix.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Will the rust ruin my lawn or plants?**  
No. The oxidation is surface-level and doesn't leach harmful compounds. The steel stabilizes over time and won't kill grass or flowers.

**Q: Can I cut these to custom lengths?**  
Yes, with an angle grinder or hacksaw. Cut edges will rust faster than the factory patina initially, but they'll blend in within a season.

**Q: How deep does it sit in the ground?**  
The total height is 4 inches, with roughly 1.5 inches remaining above ground once properly installed. That's enough to define a border without creating a trip hazard.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy it if you want edging that will outlast your mortgage. Skip it if you're not prepared for the rust process or need flexible curves.**

This is the last edging you'll ever buy. The rust patina is either a feature or a flaw depending on your taste, but the durability is beyond question. Just budget for gloves and accept the staining risk on nearby hardscape. That's the honest tradeoff for steel that doesn't flinch.
