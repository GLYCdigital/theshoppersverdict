**The portable grill that respects your tailgate — but demands a backup plan**

The Masterbuilt SH19030819 isn't trying to replace your backyard kettle. It's aiming at a simpler target: giving you real grilling power somewhere other than your patio. At 14.5 pounds with a 260-square-inch cooking surface, it sits in the sweet spot between toy and full-size rig. But — and this is the part the marketing photos skip — it has quirks you need to know about before you click buy.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the grill itself, two folding legs that lock into place with a satisfying click, a porcelain-coated cooking grate, a shallow grease tray, and a regulator hose that connects to standard 1-pound disposable propane canisters. Assembly takes about ten minutes with a Phillips head screwdriver. No tools beyond that. The lid locks shut with a latch that also doubles as a carry handle — a detail that matters more than it sounds when you're hauling it from truck bed to picnic table.

**Heat performance and real-world grilling**

The single burner delivers 10,000 BTUs. That's not astronomical, but for a tabletop unit it's honest output. You'll sear burgers at 450–500°F after a 10-minute preheat, and the porcelain grates hold heat evenly across the surface. I've seen reviewers report cooking eight burgers at once without hot-spot drama, which tracks with the burner design.

One thing buyers consistently note: this grill runs *hot* and fast. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "Cooks great but you have to watch it — it'll char your chicken if you walk away." That's not a bug; it's a portable grill doing what it should. But it means you're babysitting, not socializing.

**Where the 4.2-star rating gets honest**

The biggest recurring complaint isn't about heat — it's about the gas connection. Multiple verified buyers report that certain 1-pound canister brands (Coleman, in particular) don't seat properly in the regulator, causing weak flames or no ignition at all. The fix involves jiggling the canister or using a different brand, which is annoying mid-cook. Pack a backup canister brand.

Also missing: any kind of built-in thermometer. For a grill in this price class, that's a real oversight. You'll need an instant-read probe to hit medium-rare with confidence. And the grease tray is shallow — expect flare-ups if you're cooking fatty meats like brats or bone-in chicken without trimming first.

**Who should buy this**

This grill is for the person who grills at tailgates, campsites, or apartment balconies where a full-size propane unit won't fit. It's also a solid choice for RVers who want something that stows flat in a storage bay. If you're a weekend warrior who needs a dependable, packable grill that doesn't require a PhD in assembly — this fits.

Skip it if you're looking for a primary backyard grill, want precise temperature control, or prefer charcoal's smoke profile. This is a tool with a narrow job, and it does that job well.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Can I use a standard 20-pound propane tank with this grill?**
A: Not out of the box. The regulator is designed for 1-pound disposable canisters. You can buy an adapter hose separately, but that adds cost and complexity — and it voids the warranty.

**Q: How long does a 1-pound canister last?**
A: At full output, roughly 1.5 to 2 hours. At medium heat, closer to 3. Plan your cook accordingly or bring spares.

**Q: Does it come with a carrying case?**
A: No. The folding legs and latch handle make it carryable, but there's no included bag. If you're rough on gear, budget for a separate case.

**The verdict: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you want a lightweight, genuinely portable propane grill that sears hard and folds up small — and you're willing to work around the canister quirk with a backup brand.

**Skip it if** you need a thermometer, plan to use 20-pound tanks, or expect a grill that doesn't demand your attention mid-cook.

The Masterbuilt SH19030819 earns its rating through solid heat output and genuinely thoughtful portability design. The gas connection issue is the kind of flaw that's tolerable if you know about it going in — and now you do.
