**The 165'' inflatable pool that's either a backyard win or a frustrating chore — here's the truth**

Let's cut straight to it: this pool is *big*. At 165 inches long and 76 inches wide, it takes up serious real estate in your yard. But size alone doesn't make a good pool — the real question is whether the construction and included pump justify the hassle. After digging through 185 ratings and the spec sheet, here's what actually matters.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains the pool itself, an air pump, and a repair patch (though some buyers report the patch is missing — worth checking on arrival). The pool measures 13.75 feet by 6.3 feet with 22-inch walls. That's roughly 1,800 gallons of water capacity, which sounds impressive until you realize filling it takes hours and runs up your water bill. The material is 0.4mm dual-layer PVC — thicker than the flimsy 0.2mm pools that puncture if you look at them wrong, but still not "commercial grade" despite the marketing language.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The reinforced side walls and I-beam construction keep the pool stable once inflated. Buyers consistently note the walls hold their shape even with kids pushing against them. The triple-layer coating on the bottom is a genuine upgrade over cheaper pools — one reviewer said: *"We've had it on a slightly rough patio surface for two months without a leak, which surprised me."*

But here's the catch: the included pump is weak. Multiple buyers report inflation takes 15-20 minutes of babysitting, not the 5 minutes claimed in the listing. And the pump heats up fast — you'll likely need breaks to let it cool. If you have a leaf blower or a better air pump, use that instead.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The size is the headline. Families with 3-4 kids report it fits everyone comfortably without the constant "stop splashing me" arguments. One verified purchaser wrote: *"This is the only pool we've found that actually fits two adults lounging with a kid swimming between us."* The 22-inch depth is also a sweet spot — deep enough for real swimming and cannonballs, shallow enough that younger kids can stand safely.

The drain valve placement is a genuine design win. It's positioned at the side, so you don't have to tip the entire pool over to empty it — a feature that saves your back.

**Where it falls short**

The drain plug sits about 4 inches above the bottom. That means you'll never fully empty it by gravity alone. Plan on sponging out the remaining water or tilting the pool — annoying when you need to pack it away for the season.

The included repair patch is a nice idea, but the PVC glue that comes with it is weak. If you get a puncture, you're better off buying a proper vinyl repair kit from a hardware store. The storage bag is also thin — don't expect it to survive multiple seasons of packing and unpacking.

**Who should buy this**

This pool is for families with enough yard space to dedicate 14 feet of it to summer fun, who plan to leave it up for weeks at a time. It's also a solid pick for anyone who's been burned by ultra-thin budget pools that tear on day two. Skip it if you're looking for a quick splash pool you'll set up and tear down every weekend — the fill and drain cycle is too tedious for that. And definitely skip it if you have a rough or rocky surface without a ground tarp; the bottom is durable but not indestructible.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does it actually take to inflate?**
A: With the included pump, plan on 15-20 minutes. The walls are thick, which is good for durability but bad for fast inflation. A separate high-volume pump cuts that to 3-4 minutes.

**Q: Can this pool handle a lawn with small rocks?**
A: The triple-layer bottom helps, but you're gambling. A cheap tarp or ground cloth underneath is non-negotiable if your lawn isn't perfectly smooth. One reviewer lost their pool to a single sharp pebble on day one.

**Q: Is 22 inches deep enough for adults?**
A: For lounging, yes. For swimming, no. It's a splash-and-cool-off depth, not a lap pool. Kids can wade and sit comfortably; adults will be sitting rather than floating.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have the space, want a durable above-ground family pool that'll survive a full summer, and don't mind the 20-minute inflation ritual.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget, have a small yard, or want something you can easily pack up each weekend. The size and weight make this a "set it and forget it" purchase.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — Solid construction and genuinely useful size, but the weak pump and incomplete drainage hold it back from greatness. Worth it if you're prepared to work around those flaws.
