Green water is the fastest way to ruin a pool weekend. The EASYCARE Algatec Super Algaecide-Clarifier promises a one-bottle fix for both the algae bloom and the cloudy aftermath that follows. At 64 ounces with a 4.2-star average across 210 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in most backyards—but it's not magic in a jug.

Here's what you actually get and whether it deserves a spot in your pool maintenance kit.

## What's in the Bottle

One 64-fluid-ounce container of concentrated algaecide-clarifier blend. The label claims coverage for pools up to 30,000 gallons, which means a typical 15,000-20,000 gallon residential pool gets roughly three to four full treatments per bottle. That's decent value compared to the single-dose specialty products at similar price points.

The formula is designed for all pool surfaces—vinyl, fiberglass, plaster, and even above-ground pools. It's non-foaming, which matters more than most people realize; foaming algaecides create a mess that takes hours to clear.

## Performance: What Buyers Actually Report

The split personality of this product is what makes it interesting. It's a **two-in-one**: the algaecide kills active algae, and the clarifier binds dead particles so your filter can catch them. That combination means you're not doing two separate chemical treatments.

Buyer feedback leans heavily positive on the "works as advertised" front. One reviewer noted, "Added this after shocking, and the water went from pea soup to clear in about 36 hours." Another said, "I've used it every spring for the past three years to open my pool—zero green water issues."

The pattern is clear: **this product shines as a shock-and-clarify follow-up**, not as a standalone rescue for a fully green pool. Buyers who got best results paired it with a chlorine shock first. The algaecide then finished the job and cleared the dead algae residue.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the limitations.

**Black algae and yellow/mustard algae resist this product.** Several reviewers with plaster pools reported needing a second application and aggressive brushing for black algae spots. This is a general-purpose algaecide, not a specialty black algae killer—don't expect it to handle every strain.

**It's not a preventive.** If you're looking for something to add weekly to keep algae from ever starting, this will work at lower doses, but it's overkill. You're paying for the clarifier action you may not need routinely.

**Dosage confusion.** The label directions aren't the clearest. Some buyers reported underestimating the initial dose and seeing limited results, then re-treating successfully. Read the label carefully and measure for your actual pool volume—don't guess.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Seasonal pool openers**: If you deal with green pool water every spring, this is your shortcut. Shock first, then add Algatec to clear it fast.
- **Above-ground pool owners**: The non-foaming formula works well with cartridge filters common on these setups.
- **Anyone fighting recurring cloudy water**: If algae isn't your issue but cloudiness is, the clarifier half of this formula handles that effectively.

**Skip it if** you're dealing with a severe black algae infestation on plaster, or if you want a true preventive algaecide for ongoing maintenance. There are cheaper, more targeted products for those specific jobs.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I swim after adding this?**
Wait until chlorine levels are in the normal range (1-3 ppm) and the water is clear. Typically 24-48 hours after treatment, but the clarifier needs time to bind particles and get filtered out.

**Q: How much do I use for a 10,000-gallon pool?**
The label calls for roughly one quart per 10,000 gallons for the initial treatment. For maintenance, you'd use about half that. Measure precisely—this is where most buyer complaints start.

**Q: Does it work in cold water?**
Algaecides work slower below 60°F. If you're opening a pool in early spring, the water temperature will extend the clear-up time. Expect 2-3 days instead of 24-36 hours.
