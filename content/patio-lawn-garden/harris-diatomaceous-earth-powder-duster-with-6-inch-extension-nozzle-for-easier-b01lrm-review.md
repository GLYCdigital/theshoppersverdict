If you've ever tried to dust diatomaceous earth along a baseboard with a spoon and a paper towel, you already know why the HARRIS Duster exists. This is a purpose-built bellows applicator that turns a messy, dusty chore into a controlled, targeted operation. The question is whether it earns a spot in your pest-control arsenal or just becomes another gadget in the garage.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes the duster itself — a steel-bodied bellows pump with a threaded nozzle cap and a 6-inch rigid extension tube. There's no powder included, no measuring cup, no storage bag. It's bare-bones, which is fine at this price point. The build is where HARRIS earns its keep: the body is powder-coated steel, not the thin plastic you find on garden-store bellows. The extension screws on securely, and the trigger mechanism feels solid in hand.

**Real-World Performance**

This duster shines in tight spaces. The 6-inch extension is the star feature — it lets you reach under appliances, into cracks along window frames, and behind toilets without contorting yourself. For outdoor use, it's equally handy for dusting around plant stems or along the foundation line of your house.

The bellows action delivers a surprisingly fine, even mist of powder. You control the volume by how hard and how fast you pump — a light squeeze gives you a dusting, a firm press lays down a visible line. The steel construction means the bellows won't crack after a season of UV exposure, a common failure point on cheaper plastic units.

One thing to note: this is a two-handed tool. You'll hold the body with one hand and guide the nozzle with the other. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you were hoping to dust one-handed while holding a flashlight in the other, you'll be disappointed.

**FAQ**

**Can I use this with liquids, or only powders?**
Only dry powders. Liquids will clog the bellows mechanism and ruin the steel body. Stick to DE, boric acid, sulfur, or similar fine, dry materials.

**How do I clean it between different powders?**
Disassemble the nozzle, tap out the loose powder, and wipe the interior with a dry cloth. Avoid water — moisture will cause the powder to cake inside the bellows.

**Will this work with food-grade DE for kitchen use?**
Yes, and that's a common use case. The steel body doesn't react with food-grade DE, and the fine nozzle lets you apply thin, even layers along pantry shelves and behind appliances.
