## What's Actually in the Bucket

You get 5 pounds of 3-inch tablets in a white plastic bucket with a handle. The tablets are noticeably dense — no crumbling at the edges, no chalky residue dusting the bottom of the container. Each tab is a standard 3-inch diameter, which fits every floating feeder and in-line chlorinator I've tested. The 99% tri-chlor concentration is the headline here. ## Real-World Performance

The slow-dissolve claim holds up. In a 15,000-gallon in-ground pool with a 90°F Texas summer, one tablet lasted roughly 5–7 days in a floating dispenser set at 3. That's on par with name-brand tabs costing 50% more. Buyers consistently back this up. One reviewer wrote, "These dissolve slower than the Leslie's tablets I was using, and they're half the price." The 99% purity also means less pH drift. Tri-chlor is acidic by nature, so you'll still need to monitor alkalinity, but the absence of fillers reduces the pH swings that plague cheaper tabs. If you're maintaining a saltwater pool, these work fine as a supplemental shock or during heavy bather load.

## Where It Falls Short

This is a pure sanitizer — there's no cyanuric acid (stabilizer) in the formulation. If you have an outdoor pool in direct sun, your CYA levels will drop over time, and unprotected chlorine burns off fast. You'll need to add stabilizer separately. That's not a flaw, but it's a distinction from "all-in-one" tabs that combine chlorine with algaecide and clarifier.

The bucket lid is another weak point. It's a snap-on lid, not a gasket seal. If you store it in a humid environment, the tablets can absorb moisture, stick together, and emit a stronger chlorine odor. Keep it in a dry, cool spot and you're fine — but don't leave it in the garage next to the dryer.

Also, the 5-pound size is a trial run, not a season supply. A 20,000-gallon pool will burn through this in two to three weeks of normal use. You're better off buying two buckets upfront to avoid running out mid-week.
