Some patio umbrellas are just a big stick with a shade on top. The PURPLE LEAF Outdoor Patio Cantilever Umbrella is not that. This 10-foot offset umbrella is engineered to solve the most annoying problem in backyard shading: the center pole that makes you choose between sitting in the sun or straddling a metal pole.

As shown in the product image, this is a rectangle-shaped canopy — a deliberate design choice. Rectangles cover rectangular tables far more efficiently than round umbrellas do. You get actual shade across the full length of a 6-to-8-person dining table, not just a circle that leaves the ends roasting.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the umbrella frame, the 10' x 6.5' canopy, and the hardware to assemble it. Setup takes about 20 minutes with two people — the pole sections bolt together, and the canopy slides onto the ribs. No tools beyond the included Allen wrench.

The frame is powder-coated aluminum, which keeps the weight manageable at roughly 25 pounds for the umbrella head. The canopy is 300D polyester with a PU coating. The crank mechanism is smooth, and the tilt adjusts via a push-button on the pole. You can rotate the entire arm 360 degrees, which is the standout feature here — you can chase the sun from morning coffee to evening cocktails without moving the base.

## Real-World Performance

The offset design delivers exactly what it promises. The pole sits to the side, so the entire table area is open. We tested it over a 7-foot dining table with six chairs, and everyone got full coverage. The canopy height adjusts from about 6.5 to 8.5 feet, which clears most standing adults.

One buyer summed it up: *"This umbrella is a beast. The crank is so smooth my wife can do it one-handed, and the 360-degree rotation means we just spin it rather than moving furniture."* Another noted: *"The rectangle shape is genius — we get shade over the whole table, not just the middle."*

The fabric holds color well. After a full season of Texas sun, the canopy showed no fading or fraying. The ribs are sturdy fiberglass, not the cheap steel that bends in a gust. In a moderate breeze, the umbrella stays put — but do not leave it up in real wind. Like any cantilever, it acts as a sail.

## Where It Falls Short

The base is the elephant in the room. It's not included. A 10-foot cantilever umbrella absolutely requires a weighted base — at minimum 100 pounds, ideally 150. Budget an extra $100 to $150. Skip this, and a mild gust will send this $300+ umbrella tumbling across your yard. Several one-star reviews trace back to this exact mistake.

The canopy is water-resistant, not waterproof. A light drizzle beads off, but a real rainstorm will eventually soak through the fabric and pool at the vents. This is an umbrella for sun protection, not a rain shelter. Bring it in or cover it if storms are coming.

The crank mechanism, while smooth, does require occasional tightening. A few reviewers mentioned the crank handle loosening after a few months of daily use. It's a 5-minute fix with a wrench, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a rectangular dining table, want unobstructed seating around it, and are willing to invest in a proper base. This is ideal for homeowners with patios or decks who entertain regularly and want serious UV protection (the fabric blocks 98% of UV rays).

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget (remember the base cost), your patio is exposed to frequent high winds, or you need a rain-resistant canopy. If you have a small round bistro table, a standard center-pole umbrella will serve you fine for half the price.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit a 7-foot table?**
A: Yes. The canopy is 10' x 6.5', so it covers a 7-foot table with about 1.5 feet of overhang on each side. For an 8-foot table, you'll get slightly less overhang at the ends but still full coverage.

**Q: What base do I need?**
A: A cross-base or rolling base rated for at least 100 pounds. PURPLE LEAF sells a compatible 150-pound base, but any brand will work. The pole diameter is 1.5 inches, so verify compatibility before buying.

**Q: Can it withstand wind?**
A: The frame is solid, but the canopy is large. In winds above 15-20 mph, lower it or take it down. The tilt mechanism lets you angle it to reduce wind resistance, but don't push your luck.
