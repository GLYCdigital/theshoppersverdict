## What You're Actually Getting

The listing offers a set of two 10-gallon bags (though check the current configuration — some listings ship three). Each bag measures roughly 16 inches in diameter and 13 inches tall. The material is a 300g non-woven polypropylene fabric — heavier than the flimsy 150g bags you'll find for half the price.

The construction details matter here: double-stitched side seams, reinforced bottom, and two heavy-duty carrying handles per bag. The handles aren't decorative — they're bar-tacked and can actually support the weight of a fully loaded bag, which will hit 40-50 pounds by harvest time.

Each bag includes four drainage holes at the base. Combined with the breathable fabric, this creates the air-pruning effect that prevents roots from circling — the primary reason potatoes in bags outperform those in buckets.

## Real-World Performance

The 10-gallon size hits a sweet spot for potatoes. You can plant 3-4 seed potatoes per bag, and the depth supports the hilling process (adding soil as plants grow) that's essential for tuber production.

Buyers consistently report strong yields. One reviewer noted: *"Harvested about 6 pounds of Yukon Golds from a single bag — way more than I expected for the price."* Another praised the durability: *"These survived two full seasons on my deck in Texas heat. No UV degradation, no tearing at the seams."*

The fabric does what it's supposed to: keeps soil moist without waterlogging, stays cool in afternoon sun, and lets excess water escape immediately. Multiple reviewers mentioned that overwatering was nearly impossible with these bags — a common killer for container potatoes.

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**No harvest access.** There's no flap, window, or zipper to check tuber development. You're growing blind until the plants die back, then you dump the entire bag into a wheelbarrow or tarp to harvest. This is standard at this price point, but worth knowing.

**The 10-gallon limit.** Indeterminate potato varieties (like Russets and some fingerlings) produce better with 15-20 gallons of root space. These bags work best with determinate varieties — Yukon Gold, Red Pontiac, most early-season types. If you're set on growing storage potatoes, size up.

**Handle durability questions.** While most buyers report no issues, a handful noted the handles pulled loose after a season of heavy use and sun exposure. Not a dealbreaker, but don't expect these to last forever as moving tools.

## FAQ

**How many seed potatoes fit in a 10-gallon bag?**
Plant 3-4 seed potatoes (cut and cured) per bag. More than that creates overcrowding and smaller tubers. For 10-gallon bags, err toward 3.

**Can these bags be reused?**
Yes — most buyers get 2-3 seasons. Empty the soil, let the bags dry completely, and store indoors over winter. UV exposure is the main killer, so don't leave them out in summer sun when empty.

**Do I need to add drainage holes?**
No. The four existing holes plus the breathable fabric handle drainage effectively. Adding holes risks soil loss through the bottom.
