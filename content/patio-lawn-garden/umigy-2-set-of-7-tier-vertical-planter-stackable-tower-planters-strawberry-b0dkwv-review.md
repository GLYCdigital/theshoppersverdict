If you've priced vertical planters lately, you know a decent single tower runs $35–$50. This Umigy set delivers two complete 7-tier towers for roughly the same money. That math alone pulls in a lot of shoppers. But price per tower only matters if the thing actually holds dirt and keeps plants alive — and on that front, the experience is more mixed.

**What's actually in the box**

Each of the two towers breaks down into seven interlocking ring sections, four planting cups per tier, one top cap, and a base reservoir. Assembled, each tower stands 24 inches tall with a 10-inch footprint. The cups slide into slots around each ring, giving you 28 planting pockets per tower, 56 total across the set. The polypropylene plastic is rigid and surprisingly thick — 2.5mm at the walls — so it doesn't flex like cheap blow-molded planters. Assembly takes about 10 minutes per tower with no tools. The instructions are picture-only, but the logic is intuitive: stack rings, align the internal channel, insert cups.

**Real-world performance**

The self-watering mechanism is the standout feature. Each tier's ring base holds water that wicks upward through the soil in the pocket above. In practice, this means you're not hovering with a watering can daily. One reviewer noted, "I filled the top and let it trickle down — went on a 4-day trip and my basil looked better when I got back than when I left." That matches the design intent: pour water into the top cap and it cascades through the internal reservoirs.

The watering cascade has a flaw, though. Water flows down through the center channel, but the pockets on the sides only receive moisture via capillary action from the ring reservoir below them. If you pack the soil loosely, the top tier's pockets dry out fast while the bottom tier stays soggy. Several buyers reported root rot in the lower cups, specifically with strawberry plants, after following the "water from the top" instruction. The fix is to water each tier's pockets individually — tedious but effective.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The space savings get consistent shout-outs. "I grow lettuce, cilantro, and green onions on my apartment balcony — this replaced four separate pots and looks neat doing it," one verified purchase reads. Another reviewer appreciated the 2-pack value: "One tower for herbs, one for strawberries — and I still have six empty pockets for flowers." The UV-stabilized plastic has held up through a full season without fading or becoming brittle, per multiple long-term reviews.

**Where it falls short**

The alignment pins are the weak link. Each ring connects via two shallow tabs that don't snap — they just rest in grooves. A moderate wind gust on an uncovered balcony will twist the tower, and the planting cups spill soil. You'll want the towers against a wall or staked if you're in an exposed location.

The base basin leaks. It's not a catastrophic failure, but a slow seep around the seam after a few weeks of regular watering. On a wooden deck or indoors, that's a problem. On concrete or pavers, it's a minor annoyance. Buyers who placed the towers on railing-mounted stands reported water stains on the railing below.

Pocket depth limits what you can grow. At 2.2 inches deep, these are for shallow-rooted crops: lettuce, herbs, strawberries, scallions. Anything more ambitious — peppers, dwarf tomatoes, zucchini — will struggle. One reviewer summarized it plainly: "Great for greens, useless for anything that needs real root space."

**Who should buy this**

This set suits apartment gardeners, renters, and anyone with a small balcony or patio who wants to grow salad greens and herbs without a big footprint. It's also a solid option for vertical strawberry patches — the pockets are sized right and drainage holes prevent crown rot, a common strawberry killer. Skip it if you're growing anything deep-rooted, if your only space is a windy exposed rooftop, or if you need a planter that won't dribble water onto a wooden deck.

**FAQ**

**Can these stay outdoors through winter?** The PP plastic tolerates freezing, but the water in the internal reservoirs will expand and crack the seams. Drain thoroughly and store indoors if you get hard freezes.

**How often do I actually need to water?** In 70–80°F weather, every 3–4 days if you fill each tier's reservoir. In peak summer heat, check daily — the top tier dries fastest.

**Do the tiers come apart for winter storage?** Yes, they pull apart without tools, and the cups remove individually. They nest flat for compact storage.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want maximum planting capacity per square foot at a budget price, and you're sticking to shallow-rooted crops. **Skip it if** you need a leak-proof, wind-stable structure or plan to grow anything with serious root depth. This is a capable starter vertical planter with genuine self-watering benefits, undermined by assembly tolerances and a leaking base. At the 2-pack price, the value still tips positive for the right use case.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — Good value, smart design, imperfect execution.
