# HTH Spa 86121 Clarifier Review: Does This Concentrated Clarifier Actually Fix Cloudy Spa Water?

Let’s be blunt: no clarifier is magic. If your hot tub looks like pea soup because you skipped shocking it for three weeks, no chemical will fix that overnight. But if your water is *slightly* hazy, dull, or full of tiny particles that your filter keeps missing, the HTH Spa 86121 Clarifier is one of the most effective fixes I’ve tested — and the 4.7-star rating from 235 buyers backs that up.

This is a **cationic polymer clarifier** — a concentrated liquid that binds microscopic particles (dead skin, oils, mineral dust) into larger clumps your filter can actually catch. It’s not a sanitizer, it’s not a shock. It’s the finishing touch after you’ve balanced your water chemistry.

## Where It Falls Short

**It won’t fix a sanitizer problem.** If your chlorine or bromine levels are zero, or your water has a green tint (algae), you must shock first. Clarifying algae-laden water just makes the dead algae sink to the floor — then you have a different mess. Buy this *after* you’ve fixed your chemistry, not instead of.

**Overdosing is a real risk.** The instructions say to add in small increments. If you dump in a full cap on a small spa, the polymer can temporarily cloud the water further — some buyers report this and panic. Start with half the recommended dose, wait 12 hours, then add more if needed.

**Not a replacement for filter cleaning.** This works by making your filter work harder. If your filter cartridge is clogged or dirty, the clarifier has nowhere to push the particles. Clean your filter first.
