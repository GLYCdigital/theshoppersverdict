Let me be direct: most sub-$200 charcoal grills are flimsy tin cans that rust out in two seasons. The Feasto Heavy-Duty 30-Inch isn't that. But it's not without quirks. Here's what you need to know before buying.

**What's actually in the box**

The Feasto arrives mostly disassembled. You get the main barrel body, two porcelain-coated cooking grates (each roughly 15x15 inches), a charcoal pan, ash catcher, two wheels, two fixed legs, and the side shelf. The lid is pre-hinged, which helps during assembly. Total cooking space comes to 443 square inches — enough for 12 burgers, two whole chickens, or a 12-pound brisket flat.

One thing I'll note: the side shelf is a nice touch at this price, but it's not a full prep station. It's roughly 12x16 inches — fine for a platter or tongs, not for rolling out dough.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The heavy-gauge steel body is the headline here. At 30 inches wide, this grill has real mass — it doesn't wobble when you flip a steak, and the barrel doesn't flex when the fire gets hot. The porcelain-coated cooking grates are genuinely non-stick after a proper seasoning, and they clean up with just a wire brush.

Temperature control is where this grill punches above its weight. The top damper and bottom intake vent actually work together. With practice, you can hold 225-250°F for a 4-hour pork shoulder — something most grills in this class simply cannot do. Crank both vents open and you'll hit 500°F+ for searing steaks. The charcoal pan sits low in the barrel, giving you decent vertical clearance for indirect cooking.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 210 Amazon ratings, three themes keep coming up. First, durability. One verified buyer writes: *"Had mine for 9 months in the Pacific Northwest rain with a cover, zero rust. My old Weber kettle was rusted through by now."* Second, heat retention — the thick steel holds temperature through wind gusts that would kill a thinner grill. Third, value: multiple reviewers note this outperforms grills costing twice as much.

**Where it falls short**

No thermometer is the biggest miss. The lid has a mounting hole, but Feasto doesn't include a gauge. You'll spend $15-20 on an oven probe or instant-read thermometer immediately. Factor that into your budget.

The wheels are basic solid plastic with no brakes. On a flat patio, fine. On even a slight slope, the grill rolls when you bump it. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying.

Assembly is tedious. The instructions are minimalist line drawings, and the bolts aren't labeled. Budget 45-60 minutes, and have a socket set handy — a Phillips screwdriver won't cut it for all fasteners.

Also note: no ash pan door. You'll need to lift the charcoal pan out to dump ashes, which is messy mid-cook. The ash catcher tray below helps, but it's not a hands-free system like some pricier kettles.

**Who should buy this**

This grill suits three types of buyers: budget-conscious families who grill 2-3 times weekly and need reliable output; beginner- to intermediate-level charcoal cooks who want to learn temperature control without spending $400+ on a kamado; and anyone replacing a rusted-out cheap grill who's tired of buying new equipment every 18 months.

Skip it if you need portability (it's heavy — around 75 pounds assembled), if you want a built-in thermometer, or if you're a hardcore low-and-slow enthusiast who needs precise airflow control beyond what two dampers can offer.

**FAQ**

*Is the 443 sq ft enough for a family of four?* Yes. You can cook 12 burgers or 3 racks of ribs flat. For a large gathering, you'll cook in batches — that's expected at this price point.

*Can I use lump charcoal or briquettes?* Both work. Briquettes hold steadier temps for long cooks; lump charcoal gets hotter faster for searing. The charcoal pan has sufficient airflow for either.

*Does it come with a cover?* No. Buy a 30-inch universal grill cover ($15-25) — it'll extend the life of this grill significantly.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely heavy-duty charcoal grill under $200 that can handle both weeknight burgers and weekend slow-smoked ribs. The steel is thick, the cooking surface is generous, and the dual-damper system gives you real control.

**Skip it if** you refuse to buy a separate thermometer, need a portable grill for tailgating, or expect Swiss-watch build quality at this price point. The wheels and ash management system are reminders that you're paying budget-tier money — even if the core cooking performance feels mid-tier.

**4.2/5 stars.** The Feasto Heavy-Duty 30-Inch is the rare budget grill that respects the craft of charcoal cooking. It's not perfect, but it's honest — and that's worth your money.
