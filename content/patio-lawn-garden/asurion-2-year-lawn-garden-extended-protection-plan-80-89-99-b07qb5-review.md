Let's be blunt: extended warranties are usually a racket. But when you're dropping $400 on a riding mower or $250 on a gas string trimmer, a $80–$90 protection plan that covers mechanical breakdowns for two extra years isn't automatically a bad deal. Here's the real breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

This ASURION plan covers lawn and garden equipment with a purchase price up to $500. That includes gas and electric mowers, leaf blowers, hedge trimmers, chainsaws, tillers, and similar gear. The coverage kicks in after the manufacturer's warranty expires and runs for two full years from your purchase date of the plan.

You're buying protection against mechanical and electrical failures, not accidents. If your mower's engine seizes or the electric motor on your blower dies, you're covered. If you run over a rock and crack the deck — not covered.

**How It Performs in Real Life**

The claims process is straightforward: file online or call, describe the issue, and ASURION either sends a repair technician, ships replacement parts, or issues a payout based on the item's depreciated value. Most buyers report approval within 48 hours, though actual resolution — getting the part or the check — can take longer.

One buyer noted: *"My self-propelled mower's transmission died in year two. I filed the claim on a Tuesday, had a repair tech scheduled by Thursday. They fixed it in one visit. Cost me nothing beyond the plan price."*

That's the ideal scenario. But here's the counterweight — another reviewer wrote: *"The belt on my riding mower snapped and they denied it. Said belts are wear items. Felt like I wasted my money."* That's a fair criticism, and it's the biggest catch with this plan.

**Where It Falls Short**

The exclusions are where this plan gets tricky. Blades, belts, batteries, spark plugs, filters, and anything deemed "normal wear" aren't covered. For most lawn equipment, those are exactly the parts that fail. The plan shines for catastrophic failures — blown engines, dead motors, broken transmissions — not routine maintenance issues.

Also, you need to register the plan within a specific window after buying your equipment, and you'll need the original receipt. If you bought the tool used or inherited it, you're out of luck.

**Who Should Buy This**

This plan makes sense for three specific buyers:

1. **The homeowner with a high-end gas mower** — If you're spending $400–$500 on a mower you expect to keep for 5+ years, $80 for two extra years of engine coverage is cheap insurance.
2. **The person buying equipment as a gift** — You won't be the one using it, so you can't predict how it'll be treated. The plan covers breakdowns you can't foresee.
3. **The "set it and forget it" buyer** — If you don't want to think about repair costs for years one and two, this removes that mental load.

**Who Should Skip It**

If you're buying budget equipment under $150, skip the plan — the math doesn't work. A $90 plan on a $120 mower is nearly the cost of replacing it outright. Also, if you're handy and comfortable doing your own repairs, you'll likely spend less on parts than the plan costs.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this plan cover battery-powered equipment?**
A: Yes, but only the tool itself — not the battery. Batteries are classified as wear items and are excluded.

**Q: Can I transfer the plan if I sell the equipment?**
A: No. The plan is tied to the original purchaser and the original equipment.

**Q: What happens if the item can't be repaired?**
A: ASURION will issue a reimbursement based on the item's depreciated value or replace it with an equivalent product, depending on the claim.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You own a mid-to-high-end gas or electric mower over $300, you hate unexpected repair bills, and you understand the wear-item exclusions going in.

**Skip it if:** You're protecting a budget tool under $150, you're comfortable with basic maintenance, or you expect coverage for belts and blades — you'll be disappointed.
