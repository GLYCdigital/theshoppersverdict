If you've ever tried to drag a 40-pound ceramic pot across a deck, you know the pain — the scrape, the strain, the cracked patio stone. These 11.8-inch plastic caddies solve that problem for about the price of a takeout dinner per unit. The question is whether they hold up beyond the first season. Here's what 225 Amazon reviewers and my own read-through of the feedback tell you.

**What you actually get**

Six black caddies, each with four free-spinning swivel casters already attached. No tools, no assembly beyond clicking the wheels into place — most reviewers report it takes under two minutes per caddy. The plastic is a dense, rigid polypropylene, not the flimsy stuff you see on dollar-store versions. As shown in the product image, the caster wheels sit close to the rim, which keeps the center of gravity low and makes the whole thing feel more stable than you'd expect from a plastic base.

**Real-world performance**

The standout feature is the caster quality. Each wheel swivels independently, so you can push a heavy pot sideways, diagonally, or in a tight circle without fighting the base. One reviewer put it plainly: "I moved a 40-inch fiddle leaf fig across my entire apartment without asking for help. Worth every penny." Another noted they work perfectly on tile and sealed concrete, which is where most indoor and patio plants actually live.

The raised lip around the top edge is a smart design touch — it grips the pot's base so nothing slides off when you're rolling over a threshold or a minor floor seam. And the open center with drainage gaps means water won't collect and breed mosquitoes or rot your pot's feet.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the recurring theme. Getting six caddies in one shipment means you can kit out every large plant in the house at once. Reviewers also repeatedly mention the smooth rolling action — "glides like a dream" shows up more than once — and the fact that they're sturdy enough for serious nursery pots, not just decorative ones. The black color also disappears under most planters, so they don't ruin the aesthetic of a styled corner.

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about the limits here. These are rated for pots up to about 110 pounds in the listing, but several reviewers report cracking when pushing past 60-70 pounds over time. The plastic is rigid but not indestructible — a dropped pot or a sharp impact can split the rim. Also, the small 1.5-inch wheels are useless on grass, gravel, or uneven brick. This is a hard-surface tool, full stop. And if you have carpet, the wheels can snag on the pile; they're best on tile, wood, or concrete.

**Who should buy this**

- Apartment dwellers with a few large floor plants who rearrange furniture more than they'd like to admit
- Patio gardeners using 14-18 inch pots on concrete or decking who want to chase the sun without deadlifting
- Greenhouse and nursery hobbyists rotating heavy stock for watering or sunlight exposure

Skip it if you're working with oversized 24-inch-plus planters (you need the 14-inch or 16-inch caddy class), or if your yard is all grass and you expect these to roll across the lawn — they won't.

**FAQ**

**Do these fit 10-inch and 12-inch pots?**
Yes, but the fit is snug on 10-inch pots. The 11.8-inch top diameter is designed for 10-14 inch nursery pots. Anything smaller will rattle around inside the lip.

**Can they stay outside in winter?**
The plastic is UV-resistant and won't become brittle in freezing temps, but the caster bearings can collect grit and stiffen up. Bring them inside or hose them off periodically if they live outdoors year-round.

**Are they actually strong enough for a 100-pound pot?**
The listing says yes, but buyer experience says no. Keep it under 60-70 pounds for longevity. If you're pushing the limit, distribute weight evenly and don't roll over thresholds aggressively.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have multiple large potted plants on hard flooring and want to move them without wrecking your back or your floor. The six-pack value is excellent, the casters are genuinely smooth, and the design holds up for normal residential use.

**Skip it if** your pots exceed 70 pounds, you need to roll over grass or uneven ground, or you only have one plant — in that case, buy a single caddy and save the shelf space.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid, affordable, and genuinely useful. Not for heavyweights or rough terrain, but for the 90% of situations that involve moving a heavy pot across a flat surface, these earn their keep.
