**The tall planter that actually earns its floor space**

Most planters are boring. They sit low, blend into the background, and do nothing for your patio’s visual rhythm. The LEWIS&WAYNE Tall Rattan Planter Rectangular is different — it’s a vertical statement piece that brings greenery up to eye level. But is it worth your money, or just another pretty Pinterest prop that falls apart by September? I dug through the specs, the build, and 215 Amazon ratings to give you the straight answer.

**What you actually get**

The box contains one rectangular planter, a pre-installed interior liner tray, and the hardware you’ll need for assembly. The frame is powder-coated steel, wrapped in a synthetic rattan weave — the kind that mimics natural cane but won’t crack or peel after a season of sun. The interior liner is a separate plastic insert that keeps soil and moisture away from the rattan itself. That’s a smart design choice, because it means you’re not relying on the weave to hold water — which it wouldn’t do well.

At roughly 24 inches tall, this planter is built for drama. You’re not planting low-growing annuals here; you want tall grasses, faux olive trees, or a climbing vine that can cascade down the sides. The rectangular footprint is generous enough for a substantial root ball, but it’s not a giant — measure your space first.

**Real-world performance**

The standout feature is the drainage system. Unlike many decorative planters that force you to drill your own holes or risk root rot, this one has pre-drilled drainage covered by a liner. Water flows through the liner into the bottom tray, so your plants stay healthy without staining your deck. Buyers consistently mention this as a key reason they chose it over cheaper alternatives.

One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *“I’ve gone through three planters from big-box stores that cracked by winter. This one has survived two seasons in direct Texas sun and still looks new.”* That tracks with the materials — the powder-coated frame resists rust better than plain galvanized steel, and the synthetic rattan doesn’t fade or fray like natural cane.

But here’s the honest catch: the liner is not a sealed waterproof barrier. If you leave standing water on the rim after heavy rain, moisture can seep into the weave over time. It won’t destroy the planter overnight, but it can cause discoloration. Use a saucer underneath if you’re in a rainy climate, or keep it under an eave.

**Where it falls short**

Assembly is required, and it’s not a five-minute job. You’ll need a Phillips head screwdriver and about 20 minutes of patience. The instructions are adequate but not great — the diagrams are small and the hardware isn’t labeled. If you’re not handy, budget a bit more time.

The other issue is size perception. The product photos make it look larger than it is. It’s tall, yes, but the width is narrower than you might expect. If you’re planning to drop a wide ceramic pot inside, measure the interior dimensions first — some buyers found their existing pots didn’t fit.

**Who should buy this**

- **Small-space dwellers** — the vertical design gives you height without eating up balcony square footage.
- **Renter-friendly decorators** — it’s lightweight enough to move easily when you relocate, and it doesn’t require permanent installation.
- **Anyone tired of flimsy planters** — the steel frame and synthetic weave genuinely outlast the $30 plastic alternatives.

**Skip it if** you want a fully waterproof, leave-it-out-in-a-hurricane vessel, or if you expect it to arrive ready to plant without any assembly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this outdoors year-round?**
A: Yes, but with caveats. The synthetic rattan and steel frame handle freezing temps and UV exposure fine. However, if you live in a climate with heavy, prolonged rain, bring it under cover or add a saucer — the liner isn’t a fully sealed waterproof barrier.

**Q: Does it come with drainage holes?**
A: Yes. The interior liner has pre-drilled holes that allow excess water to drain into a bottom tray. You don’t need to drill anything yourself.

**Q: Will my existing pot fit inside?**
A: Measure first. The planter is tall but relatively narrow. If you’re using it as a cachepot (hiding a plastic nursery pot inside), check the interior width against your pot’s diameter before ordering.
