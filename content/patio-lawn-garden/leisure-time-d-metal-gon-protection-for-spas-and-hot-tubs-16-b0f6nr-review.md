**Bottom line: If your spa water turns green, brown, or leaves rust-colored rings around the shell, this is the bottle you've been missing.**

Metal ions in your fill water don't care how much you spent on your hot tub. They'll stain the shell, cloud the water, and corrode internal components if left unchecked. Leisure Time's Metal Gon is a liquid sequestering agent that grabs onto iron, copper, and manganese ions and holds them in suspension so they can't settle on surfaces or oxidize into ugly discoloration.

**What's actually in the bottle**

It's a 16-ounce liquid concentrate. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward white bottle with clear dosing instructions on the back label. You use 2 ounces per 500 gallons at initial fill, then 2 ounces weekly or after every water change. That means one bottle handles roughly eight full treatments on a standard 400-gallon spa — or a full season of maintenance for most households.

**How it performs in the real world**

The chemistry here is the selling point. Unlike chelating agents that can interfere with your sanitizer levels, Metal Gon's sequestering formula works alongside chlorine or bromine without nuking your free chlorine readings. It's also safe for all spa surfaces — acrylic, fiberglass, vinyl, and even inflatable models.

Buyers consistently report two things: it prevents new stains from forming, and it clears up mild existing discoloration within a day or two. One verified purchaser noted, "Had brown water from well iron. Added this and it cleared within 24 hours. Now I add it weekly and haven't had a stain since." Another long-term owner mentioned, "I've used this for three years. It's the only thing that keeps my copper-rich well water from turning the shell green."

**Where it falls short**

This is a prevention product first, a cure second. If your spa already has heavy, crusty staining — the kind that feels rough to the touch — Metal Gon alone won't dissolve it. You need Leisure Time's Metal Gon Stain Remover (the granular version) for the initial deep clean, then this liquid to keep it from coming back. That's an extra purchase some shoppers don't anticipate.

Also, don't skip the weekly schedule. Skip two weeks and the metals can re-sequester and stain again. It's a small chore, but it's a recurring one.

**Who should buy this**

- **Well-water users** with iron or manganese issues — this is non-negotiable for you
- **Hot tub owners** in areas with hard or metal-heavy municipal water
- **Anyone noticing green water, brown rings, or blue-green staining** on a white acrylic shell
- **Inflatable spa owners** who want cheap insurance against shell discoloration

Skip it if you're on consistently soft, low-TDS municipal water with zero staining history, or if you're looking for a one-time stain remover rather than ongoing protection.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use Metal Gon with saltwater chlorine generators?**
Yes. It's compatible with all sanitizer types, including salt systems. Just add it after the salt has fully dissolved and the system is circulating.

**Q: How often do I really need to add it?**
Weekly is the manufacturer's recommendation. If you change water frequently or your source water is low in metals, bi-weekly may suffice — but most users report the best results keeping to a seven-day schedule.

**Q: Will it affect my pH or alkalinity readings?**
No. It's pH-neutral and won't shift your water balance, which is why it's safe to use alongside typical weekly balancing routines.
