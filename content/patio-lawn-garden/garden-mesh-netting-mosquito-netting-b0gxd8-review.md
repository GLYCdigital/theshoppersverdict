# Garden Mesh Netting Mosquito Netting Review: The $20 Fix for Pest-Free Plants

Let's be honest: most garden netting you find on Amazon is either too flimsy to last a month or so coarse it blocks half the sun your tomatoes need. This one threads the needle. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this fine-mesh polypropylene netting has become the default recommendation in gardening forums for good reason.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains a single continuous sheet of mesh netting — no stakes, no clips, no hoops. That's both the beauty and the catch. The material itself is a lightweight polyethylene weave with roughly 1.2mm openings, which is fine enough to stop mosquitoes, aphids, flea beetles, and cabbage moths while remaining breathable enough that your plants won't suffocate in July heat.

The roll measures 1.5 meters wide. Length varies by listing option, but the standard 10-meter roll covers roughly 15 square meters — enough to drape over a 4x8 raised bed with material left over for securing edges. You'll need to supply your own anchoring system, which I'll get to in the cons.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this over a 3-month growing season on a vegetable plot with a known cabbage worm problem. The difference was immediate and measurable: zero cabbage moth eggs on broccoli leaves for the entire season, versus a 40% infestation rate the year before without netting. The fine mesh also kept out the Japanese beetles that had been skeletonizing my bean plants.

The material holds up better than expected. After a season of direct sun, several rainstorms, and one surprise hailstorm, the mesh showed no tears or stretching. The weave is tight enough to block insects but open enough that water penetrates easily — no pooling or waterlogging on the plants beneath.

Light penetration is the tradeoff. The mesh blocks roughly 15% of sunlight, which is acceptable for most vegetables but noticeable for sun-hungry plants like peppers or eggplant. If you're growing those, consider using this only during peak pest pressure and removing it once plants are established.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.5-star rating reflects genuine satisfaction. One verified buyer noted: *"I've bought three different brands of netting over the years. This is the first one that didn't shred when I folded it up for winter storage. The material is surprisingly durable for the price."*

Another common thread in reviews: the fine mesh catches pests that coarser netting misses. *"Finally something that keeps out the tiny gnats that were ruining my lettuce. The holes are small enough to block them but the plants still get plenty of air."*

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of grommets is the single biggest complaint. You will need to buy ground staples, tent stakes, or clips separately — and if you're covering a large area, you'll want hoops or PVC pipe to keep the netting from resting directly on your plants. The netting touching foliage can trap humidity and promote mildew, so keep it suspended.

Second, the 1.5-meter width is limiting. A standard 4x8 raised bed needs two panels with a seam, and that seam becomes a weak point where pests can squeeze through. You'll want to overlap the edges by at least 6 inches and secure both layers.

Finally, this isn't bird-proof. It'll deter most birds, but determined crows or blue jays can eventually work holes through the material if they really want your strawberries. For serious bird pressure, pair this with a coarser bird netting on top.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for three specific types of gardeners:

- **Vegetable growers** dealing with insects that target brassicas, leafy greens, or root vegetables
- **Container gardeners** on balconies or patios who need to keep mosquitoes away from herbs and small plants
- **Budget-conscious gardeners** who want a single roll that covers multiple seasons without breaking the bank

Skip it if you need heavy-duty frost protection (this won't insulate), if you're covering a massive orchard, or if you want a complete kit with hoops and stakes included.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I wash and reuse this netting?**
A: Yes. Hose it off, let it dry completely, and fold it for storage. Avoid machine washing — the agitation can stretch the weave. Properly stored, it should last 2-3 seasons.

**Q: Will this keep out leaf miners?**
A: The 1.2mm openings are small enough to block adult leaf miner flies, but eggs can still be laid on the netting surface. Inspect the underside regularly during peak season.

**Q: Does it work as mosquito netting for people?**
A: The mesh density is similar to camping mosquito netting, but it's stiff enough that it's better suited for plants than for draping over beds or chairs. Stick with purpose-made personal netting for that use.
