## What You're Actually Getting

Two identical blades, each measuring 21.25 inches long with a 1-inch center hole and a 0.25-inch thickness. The steel is heat-treated, which is the first thing I look for in an aftermarket blade — cheap ones are often soft metal that rolls over on the first rock. The cutting edge is sharpened from the factory, though it's not razor-honed like some premium brands. It's mower-sharp, which is what matters.

The spindle pattern is the critical spec here, and MaxPower got it right. The 5-point star pattern on the center hole matches the standard 46-inch AYP (American Yard Products) spindle used across Craftsman, Husqvarna, and Poulan models from the mid-2000s through current production. As one buyer put it: *"Bolted right up to my 2018 Craftsman 46-inch, no modification needed. Cut my install time from 45 minutes to 15."*

## Real-World Performance

I've seen enough mower blades to know the difference between a blade that cuts and one that shreds. This MaxPower set cuts clean. The 0.25-inch thickness gives it the stiffness needed for a clean shear across the grass instead of the tearing action you get from thinner blades. The lift wings are aggressive enough to stand the grass up before the cut, which means a more even finish on taller fescue or Bermuda.

Where these blades really earn their keep is longevity. The heat-treated edge held up through a full season of weekly mows on a half-acre property with occasional acorn strikes, and it still had decent life left. That's the complaint I hear most about OEM blades — they're fine, but they dull fast. This set doesn't.

One thing I'll flag honestly: the factory paint is cosmetic only. It chips off the first time you hit anything harder than grass. That's normal for mower blades, but if you see flaking paint and think rust, it's not. The steel underneath is fine.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest gotcha is compatibility. While the AYP spindle is common, it's not universal. Check your deck's part number before ordering. The product description lists specific models, and the reviews show a few buyers who ordered without checking and got a non-fit. One wrote: *"Doesn't fit my 2021 Husqvarna YTH22V46 — the spindle pattern is different on the newer decks."* That's not a flaw in the blade; it's a flaw in not verifying compatibility.

Also, no hardware is included. You'll reuse your existing bolts and washers, which is fine unless they're worn or stripped. If you're replacing blades because you hit something hard, inspect your spindles and bolts while you're under there.

## FAQ

**Will these fit my 46-inch Craftsman?**
If your mower uses the AYP spindle (most Craftsman 46-inch decks from roughly 2005 onward), yes. Check your deck's part number against the MaxPower fitment list before ordering. The center hole is 1 inch with a 5-point star pattern.

**Are these blades sharp out of the box?**
They're mower-sharp — they'll cut grass cleanly immediately. They're not scalpel-honed like some premium aftermarket blades, but you don't want razor edges on a mower blade anyway; they dull too fast.

**How often should I replace these?**
Every 25–30 hours of mowing, or once a season for the average homeowner. If you're hitting rocks or roots regularly, inspect the edge monthly. When the edge shows visible rounding or nicks that tear grass, it's replacement time.
