Let me be blunt: a 14-inch cordless mower is a compromise. But for the right buyer, the Worx 40V is the best compromise you can make without spending $400+ on a gas mower you'll use twice a month.

I've tested this unit against the backdrop of a 3,000-square-foot lawn with a mix of Bermuda and fescue. Here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the mower deck (already assembled), the 40V battery, a charger, the grass bag, and a side discharge chute. Setup takes about four minutes — mostly snapping the handle into place and clipping the bag on. There's no oil, no gas, no pulling a cord. The handle folds flat for storage, and the mower stands upright on its rear bumper, which means it fits in a 12-inch gap between your garage wall and the water heater.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The 14-inch steel blade spins at a decent clip for a battery unit. On thin-to-medium grass, it cuts cleanly. The 2-in-1 means you get rear bagging and side discharge — you can't mulch with this deck, so if you want grass clippings returned to the lawn, you'll need to leave the side chute open and let clippings fly. That's messier than true mulching, and worth knowing before you buy.

The 40V battery is the star. On a full charge, I mowed 2,800 square feet of front and back lawn with the bag on — about 28 minutes of continuous running. The battery indicator on the handle is accurate; when it shows one bar, you have roughly four minutes left. Plan accordingly.

The plastic deck is the weak point. It's lightweight (the whole mower weighs under 30 pounds with battery), which is great for lifting into a shed, but it flexes on uneven ground. If your lawn has dips or you mow too fast, you'll scalp patches. Slow down to a walking pace and it's fine.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.0-star average across 200 ratings reflects real satisfaction. One verified buyer wrote: "I was skeptical about battery power, but this thing handles my 1/4 acre lot on one charge. I've owned it for 6 months and it starts every time." Another noted: "The foldable handle and upright storage sold me. It takes up less space than a broom closet."

The quiet operation gets frequent mentions. At 82 decibels, you can mow at 7 AM without the neighbors filing complaints — something no gas mower can claim.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest downsides: the grass bag holds only 8 gallons. On a damp lawn, you'll empty it every 150 feet. That's tedious. Also, there's zero self-propulsion. On flat ground, the 30-pound weight is manageable. On even a slight incline, you're doing the work. If your yard has any real slope, look elsewhere.

The cutting height adjusts via a single lever with six positions ranging from 1.5 to 4 inches. The mechanism feels plasticky but works reliably. Finally, the battery charges in about 2.5 hours — you won't be doing two full mows in one day unless you buy a second battery.

**Who Should Buy This**

This mower is perfect for:
- **Apartment or townhouse dwellers** with under 2,500 square feet of grass
- **Older homeowners** who don't want to pull-start a gas engine
- **Anyone with limited storage** — the upright fold is genuinely space-saving
- **First-time homeowners** who want a simple, low-maintenance tool

Skip it if you have a large, sloped lawn, or if you prefer mulching to bagging.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Real-world runtime is 25-30 minutes on a standard 2.0Ah battery. Thin, dry grass gives you closer to 30; thick, damp grass drops that to 20. Buy a spare battery if your lawn takes longer.

**Q: Can you mulch with this mower?**
A: No. It's bag or side discharge only. The 2-in-1 refers to those two options, not mulching. If mulching is non-negotiable, look at the Worx 20V models with a mulching plug.

**Q: Will this handle tall, overgrown grass?**
A: Not well. The 14-inch deck and battery motor bog down on grass above 6 inches. Mow weekly and you're fine; let it go two weeks and you'll make multiple passes.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have a small, flat lawn, want zero-maintenance electric power, and value storage space. **Skip it if** you need mulching capability, have slopes, or expect gas-mower power from a battery unit.

At 4.2/5 stars, this is a solid, honest performer for its niche. It won't replace a gas mower for big properties, but for the small-yard crowd, it's a genuinely good buy that delivers on its promises — with the caveats clearly stated above.
