Let me be blunt: bat guano is one of those fertilizers that sounds like a gimmick until you actually use it. I've tested dozens of organic amendments over the years, and Down To Earth's 7-3-1 formulation is the real deal for one specific job—getting plants to flower and fruit. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box**

You get a 5-pound box of prilled granules (small, uniform pellets that look like dark coffee grounds). As shown in the product image, the box is straightforward and utilitarian—no fancy marketing, just the NPK ratio and OMRI organic seal plastered on the front. The granules are dry, not sticky, and they pour cleanly. No measuring scoop is included, so grab a tablespoon from your kitchen drawer.

**The real-world performance**

This is where Down To Earth separates itself from cheap guano products. The prilled format means you don't get the dusty, clumping mess that plagues powdered guano. It dissolves readily in water for liquid feeding or works as a top-dress for slow-release. I used it on tomatoes, peppers, and a container fig tree. Within three weeks, flower set noticeably increased on the tomatoes, and the peppers stopped dropping blossoms in the heat.

The 7-3-1 ratio is the story here. The nitrogen is moderate, but the phosphorus (3%) and the trace micronutrients from the bat guano source drive root development and bloom production. It's not a complete fertilizer—you'll still need a balanced feed during vegetative growth. But as a targeted bloom booster, it performs.

**Where it falls short**

Honest drawbacks, because they exist. First, the nitrogen is too low for leafy greens or lawns—this is a specialist tool, not a generalist. Second, the box packaging is not resealable, and I've seen two complaints about corners splitting in transit. Transfer it to a mason jar or plastic bucket immediately. Third, the fine dust that kicks up when you measure it is irritating. Wear a dust mask, especially if you have respiratory sensitivities.

**Who should buy this**

- **Container gardeners** growing flowering ornamentals, tomatoes, or peppers—you'll see the difference in bloom count.
- **Organic gardeners** who need an OMRI-listed phosphorus boost without synthetic salts.

Skip it if you're growing leafy greens, maintaining a lawn, or want a single all-purpose fertilizer to handle everything.

**Frequently asked questions**

*How often should I apply this?*
For liquid feeding, mix 1 tablespoon per gallon of water and apply every 2–3 weeks during the growing season. As a top-dress, work 2–3 tablespoons into the top inch of soil around the drip line once a month.

*Is it safe for edible plants?*
Yes, it's OMRI-listed for organic production. Just follow the label rates and avoid applying within 2 weeks of harvest to let the nutrients fully break down.

*Does it expire or lose potency?*
Unopened, it keeps indefinitely in a cool, dry place. Once opened, moisture will cause it to clump, but it won't spoil. Just break up clumps before use.
