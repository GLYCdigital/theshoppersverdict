# Landzie Lawn Leveling Rake Review: Is the 72-Inch Handle Worth It?

Most leveling rakes feel like toys — flimsy aluminum heads, handles that snap after a season, and a design that assumes you’re 5'8" with a perfect back. The Landzie 72-inch powder-coated version is different. It’s the rake you buy when you’re done fighting cheap tools.

I tested this on a 2,000 sq. ft. lawn that needed leveling after aeration, plus topdressing with a sand-compost mix. Here’s the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains one assembled rake — no tools required. The handle is a single continuous steel tube, 72 inches long, wrapped in a powder coat that feels grippy even with gloves. The head measures 15 inches wide with 1.5-inch steel tines spaced evenly. Weight lands around 3.5 lbs, which is light enough for one-hand dragging but substantial enough to feel solid.

The powder coat isn’t just cosmetic. It prevents rust on the handle where your hands sweat, and it adds a slight texture that stops the rake from slipping when your palms get damp.

## Real-World Performance

Leveling is where this rake shines. The wide head pulls a surprising amount of soil per pass — I cut my leveling time by roughly 30% compared to my old 12-inch rake. The rounded tine tips are the standout design choice. They push soil and compost into low spots without tearing up grass crowns, which is critical if you’re leveling after overseeding.

Topdressing was equally smooth. The rake distributes a thin, even layer of compost without that annoying "clumping" you get with cheaper rakes that have sharp-edged tines. For dethatching, it works but isn’t ideal — the tines are too widely spaced to pull heavy thatch. Use a dedicated dethatcher for that job.

One surprise: the 72-inch handle genuinely helps. I’m 6'1" and didn’t bend at the waist once during a 45-minute session. My wife (5'4") found it slightly long but manageable — she just gripped lower on the shaft.

## Honest Shortcomings

The fixed head angle is the biggest limitation. At roughly 15 degrees, it’s perfect for flat ground but awkward on slopes or against edging. You can’t adjust it, so tight corners require more manual maneuvering.

The powder coat isn’t indestructible. Scrape it against a concrete curb and you’ll see bare metal. It won’t rust immediately, but touch-up paint is a good idea if you’re rough on tools.

Finally, this is a leveling rake, not a multi-tool. If you need a single rake for leveling, thatch, and leaf cleanup, you’ll be disappointed. It does one job exceptionally well.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners with uneven lawns:** If you’re filling low spots or leveling after aeration, this is your tool.
- **Tall users:** Anyone 5'10" or above will appreciate the handle length more than they expect.
- **Topdressing enthusiasts:** The tine design makes compost and sand spreading nearly effortless.

Skip it if you have a tiny postage-stamp lawn (under 500 sq. ft.) where a smaller rake suffices, or if you need an adjustable-angle head for sloped terrain.

A: Both. The rounded tines won’t rip out existing grass. It’s safe for leveling over living turf, though you’ll want to avoid heavy dragging on stressed or dormant lawns.

**Q: How does the 72-inch handle compare to standard 48-inch rakes?**  
A: The extra 24 inches reduces bending by roughly 60% and increases leverage. It does add a bit of swing weight, but the balance is well-designed — no tip-heavy feel.

**Q: Will the powder coat chip off during normal use?**  
A: On soil and turf, no. It only chips if you scrape hard surfaces like concrete, pavers, or rocks. Minor chips won’t affect function.
