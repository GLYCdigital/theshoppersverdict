## What You Actually Get

Four 5-foot round tables. Each measures 60 inches in diameter with a tabletop height of 30 inches — standard banquet height. The top is blow-molded high-density polyethylene, which is a fancy way of saying it’s a hollow, impact-resistant plastic shell that won't absorb moisture. The legs are 18-gauge steel with a baked-on powder coat. Each table weighs 46 pounds. That’s heavy, and it’s a feature, not a bug.

As shown in the product image, the tables fold in half with a locking mechanism on the underside. When collapsed, each table is about 30 inches tall and 5 inches thick. Stack all four, and you’re looking at a footprint smaller than a single upright table.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. At 46 pounds per table, the 4-pack means 184 pounds of gear. If you’re hauling these solo, you’ll be making two trips and possibly regretting your life choices. Also, the white surface is a scuff magnet. Dark shoe marks and pen ink require a bit of elbow grease to remove completely. And because the legs aren’t height-adjustable, a truly uneven patio will produce a slight rock — shim it or accept it.

**Q: Do they fit standard tablecloths?**
A: Yes. A 90-inch round cloth fits perfectly over a 60-inch table with a nice drape. For a floor-length look, go with a 120-inch cloth.

**Q: What’s the weight capacity really like?**
A: The 1,000-lb rating is static (evenly distributed). For dynamic loads — people leaning or kids climbing — that number drops. For typical buffet spreads, you’ll never approach the limit.
