**Monument Grills** sits in a strange spot in the gas grill market: it's not the cheapest option, not the flashiest, and not a big-box-store household name. Yet 225 Amazon reviewers have pushed it to a 4.5-star average, which says something. After digging through the specs and owner feedback, here's what I found.

**What you're actually getting**

The box includes the grill body, four stainless steel burners, a cast-iron cooking grate, a side burner, and a folding side shelf. The cooking surface measures roughly 640 square inches—enough for 20+ burgers or a couple of beer-can chickens. The cart has two locking casters and two fixed wheels, so it rolls predictably without wandering mid-cook.

The build quality is where Monument separates from the sub-$300 grills. The burners are heavy-gauge stainless (not the thin, stamped metal found on bargain units), and the firebox is welded, not folded. Owners consistently note the lid fits flush and stays aligned after a season of use.

**Real-world performance**

Heat-up to 500°F takes about 12 minutes on a 70°F day—not class-leading, but respectable. The key advantage is distribution: the four burners run front-to-back rather than side-to-side, which means the outer edges stay hot. One reviewer put it plainly: "I've cooked on Weber, Char-Broil, and Napoleon. This beats my old Weber Spirit for evenness, no question."

The cast-iron grates retain heat well for searing. A 1-inch ribeye gets a proper crust in about 90 seconds per side at full blast. Low-and-slow is workable too—the burners hold a steady 250°F for hours without the flame flickering out, which is a common complaint on cheaper units.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Three themes dominate the 225 reviews:

- **Assembly**—"Instructions are actually readable. Had it together in under an hour by myself." This isn't nothing; most gas grills eat a Saturday afternoon.
- **Heat control**—Multiple reviewers mention precise flame adjustment, even at the low end. That's rare under $500.
- **Value for money**—"Feels like a $700 grill, paid $400." That sentiment appears repeatedly.

**Where it falls short**

The side shelf and side burner are the weak points. The burner puts out decent heat, but the shelf uses thin gauge metal and flexes if you lean on it. Don't rest a full platter on the edge. The grease tray is also shallow—if you're cooking fatty cuts for a crowd, plan to empty it mid-session or it overflows into the drip pan below.

There's no thermometer in the hood, which is annoying at this price point. You'll want a $15 probe for indirect cooks. The igniter is piezoelectric (push-button), and while it fires reliably for most owners, a few report needing two or three clicks on cold mornings.

**Who should buy this**

- **The weekend host** who cooks for 6–10 people regularly and wants even heat without dropping $800+
- **The upgrade buyer** moving from a cheap propane grill who wants better build without the premium brand markup
- **The low-and-slow beginner** who wants a grill that holds temperature without constant babysitting

**Skip it if** you need a rotisserie (not included, no motor mount), or if you plan to move the grill frequently—the cart is sturdy but heavy, and the side shelf won't survive being dragged around a deck.

**FAQ**

**Is the 10-year burner warranty legit?** Yes. Monument honors it on the stainless burners and firebox against rust-through. You'll need your proof of purchase, and the process takes a few emails, but owners report replacement parts arrive in about two weeks.

**Does it fit a standard 20-lb propane tank?** Yes, and the tank sits inside the cabinet with room to spare. The regulator hose reaches easily, and there's a tank strap to keep things stable.

**Will the cast-iron grates rust?** They'll develop surface rust if left wet, same as any cast iron. Season them with oil after cleaning and they'll hold up fine. A few reviewers skipped this and got rust spots within months—that's user error, not a defect.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a workhorse gas grill that cooks evenly, assembles without a fight, and won't fall apart after two seasons. **Skip it if** you need a built-in thermometer, rotisserie, or a side shelf that can double as a workbench.

At 4.6/5, this is one of the better mid-tier gas grills available right now. It's not perfect, but for the price, it's genuinely hard to beat.
