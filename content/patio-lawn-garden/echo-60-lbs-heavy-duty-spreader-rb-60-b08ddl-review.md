Let me be blunt: most homeowner spreaders are glorified plastic toys that crack by the second season. The Echo RB-60 is not that. This is a legit piece of equipment that sits in the gap between big-box store throwaways and commercial-grade units costing three times as much.

## What you actually get

The box contains the spreader frame, 60-pound capacity hopper, 13-inch pneumatic tires, handle assembly, and a small hardware bag. As shown in the product image, this is a full-size broadcast spreader — roughly 35 pounds assembled, standing about waist-high. The hopper opening is wide enough to take a full bag of fertilizer without funneling gymnastics.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The headline feature here is the all-metal gear train. Most spreaders in this price range use nylon gears that strip when you hit a rock or turn too sharply. The RB-60's gears are enclosed in a sealed housing, and the stainless steel hardware won't rust out after a few wet seasons.

The pneumatic tires are the other standout. If you've ever pushed a plastic-wheel spreader across a damp lawn and watched it bog down, you'll appreciate what 13-inch air-filled tires do. They roll over soft ground, small ruts, and mild slopes without fighting you. Buyers consistently mention this — one reviewer noted, "The tires make it feel like pushing a wheelbarrow, not dragging a plow."

Spread coverage is even across a roughly 8-10 foot swath at normal walking speed. The shutoff lever is positioned on the handle and engages positively — no half-closed flap dribbling fertilizer as you turn.

## Where it falls short

Assembly is the biggest complaint across reviews. The instructions are printed small, and the diagrams don't always match the parts visually. Budget 45 minutes and have a second person help flip the frame during the tire install. It's not hard, just tedious.

The rate dial has no numbered stops — it's a smooth rotation with an arrow indicator. You'll need to calibrate your own settings for different products. Same issue with the spread pattern: you'll start with a mid-setting and adjust. Not a dealbreaker, but a minor inconvenience.

## Who should buy this

- **The acreage owner** spreading fertilizer, lime, or ice melt across a large yard — the 60-pound capacity saves you from refill trips.
- **The equipment hobbyist** who maintains tools and wants something that'll last a decade, not a season.
- **The hillside dweller** dealing with sloped terrain where plastic wheels skid and slip.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it spread sand or ice melt in winter?**
Yes, but note that the metal gear train handles the weight fine. Just clean the hopper thoroughly after salt use to prevent corrosion.

**Q: Is the spreader accurate for small lawns?**
The spread width is wide, so a small yard will see you turning frequently. It's overkill for under a quarter-acre.

**Q: Does the Echo RB-60 come assembled?**
No — it ships in a box and requires assembly. Most buyers report 30-45 minutes with basic hand tools.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you have a large property, hate replacing cheap spreaders every couple years, and want something that feels like real equipment.

**Skip it if** you're on a small lot, have limited storage space, or prefer a lightweight unit you can hang on a hook. This one takes up space and takes effort to move.

The Echo RB-60 is a solid 4.6/5 — it's not perfect, but it's the last spreader you'll buy for a long time. The build quality justifies the price premium over budget units, and the pneumatic tires alone are worth the upgrade.
