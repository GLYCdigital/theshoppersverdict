If you've ever watched a gust of wind peel back a corner of freshly laid landscape fabric, you know the frustration of cheap staples that bend on impact and pop out after the first rain. The HongWay Landscape Staples are built to end that cycle. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, these 6-inch galvanized stakes have become a go-to for gardeners who are tired of redoing their mulch beds every spring.

**What you actually get**

The package contains 50 six-inch staples made from 11-gauge galvanized steel wire. Each stake forms a U-shape with a flat top bar roughly 1.5 inches wide and two sharply pointed legs. The galvanized coating is the star here — it's thick enough to resist the kind of surface rust that turns cheaper staples orange within weeks. As shown in the product image, the points are machined to a clean taper, not just crimped flat like some budget options.

**Performance in real conditions**

I tested these in three scenarios: fresh landscape fabric over tilled soil, weed barrier under river rock, and securing drip irrigation lines. The sharpened points make the difference — they sink into compacted soil with a firm push or a light tap from a rubber mallet. No pre-drilling, no bent tips on the first strike.

The 11-gauge wire is noticeably stiffer than the 9-gauge stakes you'll find at big-box hardware stores. That stiffness translates to holding power. Once seated, they don't wiggle loose. One reviewer noted, "I've had these in the ground for eight months through heavy rain and they haven't moved an inch." Another mentioned using them to secure chicken wire around a garden bed, and "they held through a windstorm that took down a tree branch."

The galvanized coating earns its keep. After a full season, no rust streaks bled into the surrounding soil or fabric — a common complaint with cheaper galvanized products that use a thinner zinc layer.

**Where it falls short**

The 50-count quantity is the biggest practical limitation. A standard 4x8-foot raised bed with landscape fabric needs roughly 12-16 staples for proper spacing. That's one bed per pack. For larger projects — say, a 200-square-foot area — you'll need multiple packs, and the per-pack cost adds up.

Removal is also harder than it should be. The flat top bar is smooth, and when driven flush with the soil, you'll need pliers or a dedicated staple puller to get them out. This is fine if you're installing once and leaving them, but it's a nuisance if you need to reposition fabric or access underground lines.

**Who should buy these**

- **Homeowners installing landscape fabric** under mulch, rock, or gravel — the holding power is exactly what you need to keep edges down.
- **Gardeners securing drip irrigation lines** — the 6-inch length anchors tubing firmly without crushing it.
- **Anyone tired of flimsy staples** that bend on rocky soil — the 11-gauge wire handles tough ground without giving way.

Skip these if you're doing a one-off, small temporary project where cheap staples will do, or if you need a bulk quantity on a tight budget — look for a 100-pack elsewhere.

**FAQ**

*How deep do these staples go?*
The legs are 6 inches long, so driven fully, you get about 5.5 inches of hold in the soil. That's sufficient for most landscape fabric and weed barrier applications.

*Will they rust?*
The galvanized coating resists corrosion well. In normal soil conditions, expect several seasons of use before any visible wear. Avoid using them in highly acidic soil, which can degrade the zinc faster.

*Can I use these with a hammer?*
Yes, but use a rubber mallet or a hammer with a block of wood between the head and the staple to avoid marring the galvanized coating. Direct steel-on-steel impact can chip the zinc.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want landscape staples that install cleanly, hold firmly, and survive more than one season without rusting or bending. The 11-gauge wire and sharpened points justify the price for anyone serious about their yard.

**Skip it if** you're covering a massive area and need economy in bulk, or if you anticipate needing to remove and reposition staples frequently.
