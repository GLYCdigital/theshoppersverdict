If your patio is squeezed against the house, a traditional center-pole umbrella is a space hog. The JEAREY 9ft Half Umbrella solves that by mounting flush against a wall or fence, giving you shade where a full umbrella simply won't fit. With 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, it's clearly working for a lot of people. But is it the right shade solution for your setup? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the half-umbrella canopy (9-foot diameter at the widest point), a steel pole, and the crank-and-tilt assembly. That's it. No base, no wall bracket, no mounting bolts. The canopy is made from a polyester fabric with a UV coating, and the ribs are steel — not the flimsy aluminum you see on budget umbrellas. The pole is designed to fit standard 1.5-inch mounting brackets, which you'll need to source separately.

## Real-World Performance

Setting this up requires a wall or fence. The crank mechanism is smooth — several reviewers noted it opens and closes without the usual grinding or sticking. The tilt function is a simple push-button release that lets you angle the canopy to chase the sun. It's not infinite-adjust, but it covers the morning-to-afternoon range most people need.

The fabric feels substantial and has a decent water-repellent finish. In light rain, it beads and rolls off. In wind, the steel frame holds its shape up to about 15-20 mph before you should close it. One reviewer wrote, "I've had it through two thunderstorms and it didn't budge," while another cautioned "you still need to close it if the wind picks up — it's not a permanent structure."

The half-circle design is the star here. Mounted against a wall, it shades a 9-foot arc of deck, which is perfect for a small bistro table or a couple of lounge chairs. It keeps the center of your patio open — no pole in the middle of your walking path.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The most repeated positives in the reviews are the ease of assembly and the space-saving design. "Took 15 minutes to mount and it looks like it came with the house," one buyer noted. Another said, "Finally, shade on my narrow balcony without blocking the door." The crank-and-tilt combo is also called out repeatedly as a major upgrade over pull-string or push-up mechanisms.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest gotcha: it doesn't come with mounting hardware. You'll need to buy a wall mount or pole bracket separately, and if your wall is stucco, brick, or concrete, you'll need the right anchors and drill bits. A few reviewers were caught off guard by this and had to make a second trip to the hardware store.

The 9-foot half-circle also covers less than you might expect. A full 9-foot umbrella shades a circle; this shades half of one. For a 6-foot patio table, you'll likely need two of these mounted on opposite walls to get full coverage. And the canopy color options are limited — mostly basic neutrals, so don't expect a vibrant pattern to match your outdoor decor.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Balcony and small patio owners**: If you have a narrow space against a building wall, this is the smartest shade option.
- **Anyone with a wall-mounted bracket already**: If you have the mounting infrastructure, this is a drop-in upgrade.
- **People who hate center poles**: If you're tired of a pole interfering with your table legs, this design removes it entirely.

Skip it if you need a freestanding umbrella for a large table, or if you're not comfortable drilling into your wall.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this without a wall?**
A: No. This is a half-umbrella designed specifically for wall or fence mounting. You'll need a mounting bracket (not included) to attach the pole to a vertical surface.

**Q: What size pole does it need?**
A: The pole is 1.5 inches in diameter, which fits standard umbrella wall mounts. Double-check your bracket's inner diameter before buying.

**Q: Is the canopy replaceable?**
A: Yes, the canopy is removable from the frame, and replacement canopies are available directly from JEAREY. It's a nice touch for extending the umbrella's life.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you have a wall or fence and want efficient, space-saving shade without the hassle of a center pole. The crank and tilt work well, the build is solid, and the design genuinely solves a real patio problem.

**Skip it if** you need a freestanding umbrella, want full-circle coverage for a large table, or aren't prepared to buy mounting hardware separately.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-built, thoughtfully designed half-umbrella that delivers exactly what it promises, with the only real annoyance being the missing mounting hardware.
