**The 6-inch staple that actually stays put — most of the time**

If you've ever pinned down landscape fabric with cheap 4-inch staples only to find them peeking out after the first good rain, you know the frustration. The FEED GARDEN 6-inch galvanized staples are the "buy once, cry once" answer to that problem. At 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, these are clearly doing something right — and for the most part, they deliver exactly what the product photos promise: thick, long, rust-resistant stakes that hold their ground.

**What's actually in the box**

You get a solid handful of 6-inch galvanized steel staples with a black powder coating, a set of rubber gaskets, and a small pick hook tool. The staples have the classic U-shape with sharpened tips and a flat top bar. The gaskets slide onto the top bar and act like washers — they spread the holding force so the staple head doesn't chew through thin woven fabric. The pick hook is a simple but thoughtful addition: it's curved steel with a handle grip, designed to wedge under the staple's top bar for clean removal.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these in two scenarios: pinning down heavy-duty landscape fabric along a mulched border, and securing chicken wire around a vegetable bed. In both cases, the 6-inch length made a noticeable difference. The staples sank fully flush into packed clay-loam soil with a few solid hammer strikes, and they stayed put through a week of heavy rain and 30 mph wind gusts. The galvanized coating held up fine — no rust blooms after several weeks of ground contact.

The gaskets are the unsung hero here. Without them, the staple head can pull through lighter fabric under tension. With them, the pressure spreads nicely and the fabric stays taut. One buyer put it well: *"I've used cheap staples before and they always pulled through the weed barrier. These with the gaskets actually stay flat and hold."* That matches my experience.

The pick hook deserves a mention too. Removing a 6-inch staple from compacted soil without bending it is genuinely hard with pliers. The hook gives you leverage and a comfortable grip, and it works on both the gasketed and bare staples. It's a small thing, but it makes reusing staples practical.

**Where it falls short**

No product is perfect, and these have a couple of honest drawbacks. First, the black powder coating is tough but not indestructible. If you hammer at an angle and catch the tip on a rock, the coating chips — and that exposed spot can rust over time. It's cosmetic in most cases, but worth knowing if you're working in rocky soil.

Second, 6 inches is a lot of staple. For lightweight jobs — holding down a tarp for a weekend, pinning floating row cover, or securing thin netting — these are overkill. They're harder to drive and harder to remove than shorter staples, and you'll likely bend a few if you're not paying attention. This is a heavy-duty tool for permanent or semi-permanent installations, not a quick-fix solution.

Third, the gaskets are small and surprisingly easy to misplace. If you drop one in the grass, it's gone. Order a pack with extras or store them in a small jar after each use.

**Who should buy these**

- **Landscapers and gardeners** installing weed barrier, edging, or drip irrigation lines in permanent beds
- **Homesteaders** securing chicken wire, hardware cloth, or netting against animals and weather
- **DIYers** working with heavy fabric or multiple layers that need serious anchoring

**Skip them if** you only need temporary hold for a season or two — a shorter, cheaper staple will do the job without the extra effort.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these rust in the ground?**
A: The galvanized coating plus black powder layer gives solid protection. They'll last several seasons in normal soil. If the coating chips (common in rocky ground), you may see surface rust eventually — but it won't compromise the staple's strength.

**Q: How hard are they to hammer in?**
A: They need a firm hand but go in cleanly in normal soil. In hard, compacted clay, you'll want to pre-drill or wet the soil first. The sharpened tips help, but they're not magic.

**Q: Can I reuse them after pulling them up?**
A: Yes, especially if you use the pick hook. Straighten any slight bends with pliers and they're good to go. The gaskets can be reused too, assuming you don't lose them.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need permanent, weather-proof anchoring for landscape fabric, netting, or edging and want staples that won't pop out after a storm.

**Skip it if** you're doing light, temporary work — you'll be fighting the extra length more than enjoying it.
