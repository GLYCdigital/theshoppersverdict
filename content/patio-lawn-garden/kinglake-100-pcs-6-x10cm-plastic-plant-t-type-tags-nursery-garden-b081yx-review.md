**What you actually get**

The box contains 100 identical white plastic tags, each measuring 6cm wide by 10cm tall. The "T-type" design means a wide rectangular label surface sits atop a tapered pointed stem — you push the stem into soil and the label stands upright. The plastic is roughly 0.5mm thick, which feels noticeably more substantial than the flimsy sheets you sometimes get in cheap seedling kits. These are rigid enough that they won't flop over in wind or when brushed by watering can spouts.

**Real-world performance**

The writing surface is where these tags shine. The matte finish grips ink aggressively — permanent marker dries in seconds without beading, and pencil works beautifully if you want something erasable. One verified buyer noted, "I used a fine-tip Sharpie and the writing stayed crisp through an entire rainy spring and into July." That matches the general consensus in the reviews: legibility holds up for at least one full growing season.

The pointed stem deserves specific mention. It tapers to a sharp tip that slices through compacted garden soil without requiring a screwdriver or dibber to pre-poke holes. In raised beds and container pots, you can push these in with one hand while holding a plant in the other. That's a small thing that makes a big difference when you're planting 40 tomato seedlings in a row.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reading through the feedback, three themes dominate. First, the value proposition: at roughly 10 cents per tag, losing or breaking one is a non-event. **Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. These tags are only available in the 6x10cm size — there's no smaller variant for seed-starting trays or compact herb pots, where a 10cm stem is overkill. The white plastic also shows soil stains quickly; after a season of being pushed into dark, damp earth, the stems look permanently grubby even if the label surface stays clean. And while the plastic survives a season in full sun, some long-term users report brittleness after 12-18 months of continuous UV exposure. For most hobby gardeners, that's acceptable — you'll likely have repurposed or replaced them by then anyway.

**Who should buy these**

This pack is ideal for three specific buyer types: vegetable gardeners who plant dozens of varieties each spring and need clear, lasting labels; nursery owners or plant sellers who need professional-looking tags that don't scream "cheap"; and anyone who's tired of re-writing labels every few weeks because the ink washed off. If you're a casual indoor plant parent with five houseplants, this is overkill — a smaller pack or reusable chalk labels would serve you better.
