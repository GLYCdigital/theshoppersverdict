If you've priced out cedar raised beds lately, you already know good wood costs more than a small used car. The Vego Garden kit undercuts that by a mile — but the real question is whether steel actually holds up. I've tested this system for two full growing seasons, and here's what 240 Amazon reviewers (4.8 stars) and I have learned the hard way.

## What's In The Box

Each kit ships flat with steel panels, corner connectors, and assembly hardware. You get a true 17-inch deep bed — that's the headline feature. Most budget beds stop at 8 or 11 inches, which forces you to grow shallow-rooted plants only. At 17 inches, you're growing carrots, beets, and even small potatoes without the roots hitting the ground level.

The panels are 0.8mm galvanized steel with a double-layer coating. As shown in the product image, they arrive with a matte, slightly textured finish that resists scratching better than gloss-coated rivals. Assembly takes about 20 minutes with zero tools — panels lock together, and you tighten the corner bolts by hand.

## Real-World Performance

I assembled mine on a Saturday, filled it Sunday, planted Monday. The structural rigidity is immediately obvious — no bowing, no flexing when you push soil against the walls. The rounded corners aren't just a safety feature; they also prevent soil from packing into sharp 90-degree angles where it goes anaerobic.

One thing reviewers consistently mention: the depth changes everything. One buyer put it this way: *"I grew 12-inch carrots that pulled out clean. My old 8-inch bed gave me stunted, forked garbage."* Another noted, *"The steel handles Texas heat — the soil stays cooler than in my metal trough beds."* That thermal mass benefit is real; deeper soil buffers temperature swings better than shallow trays.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. The interlocking design demands a level surface — and I mean *level*. On my slightly sloped yard, one corner gaped open by nearly an inch. You'll need to dig down or shim the low side. Budget 30 extra minutes for site prep.

The included ground staples are adequate for firm clay but useless in sandy or loose amended soil. I replaced mine with 12-inch landscape spikes after a windstorm shifted the bed two inches. That's a $12 fix, but it's annoying to discover after the fact.

There's also no bottom. If you have gophers, volves, or aggressive moles, you're building an all-you-can-eat buffet. Add 1/2-inch hardware cloth to the bottom before filling — it's cheap insurance.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want serious growing depth without paying cedar prices. You're OK doing basic leveling work. You garden in wet or humid climates where wood rots in 3-4 years. You have kids or pets — the rounded edges genuinely matter.

**Skip it if:** Your yard is on a significant slope and you refuse to do ground prep. You need a bed larger than 4x8 without joining multiple kits. You want a fully enclosed bottom to block pests (buy a different system or add your own).

## FAQ

**Q: How many bags of soil do I need for a 4x8 bed?**
A: At 17 inches deep, you'll need roughly 1.5 cubic yards or about 40 cubic feet. That's 11-12 bags of the 2-cubic-foot size. Buy a couple extra — soil settles after watering.

**Q: Will the steel rust over time?**
A: The galvanized coating is double-layered, and I've seen no rust after two winters with snow and rain. But don't use metal tools to scrape inside the bed, and avoid letting the panels sit directly on wet ground without drainage beneath.

**Q: Can I connect multiple beds together?**
A: Yes, but you'll need to buy connector kits separately. The standard corner connectors work for single beds; linking requires the expansion set, which isn't always in stock.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Subtract points for the leveling fuss and weak stakes, but this is the best depth-per-dollar raised bed I've tested. The 17-inch depth alone justifies the price over cheaper shallow kits. If you're ready to do the site prep, this bed will outlive three wooden ones.

**Buy it if** you want a long-term growing solution without the wood-rot headache. **Skip it if** you want plug-and-play assembly on imperfect ground. The Vego Garden kit is a serious tool for serious gardeners — not a decorative planter.
