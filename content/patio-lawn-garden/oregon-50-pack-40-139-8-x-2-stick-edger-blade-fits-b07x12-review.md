## Fifty blades you won't think about again until next spring

Let's be honest: stick edger blades are the spark plugs of lawn care. Nobody gets excited about them. But when one snaps mid-stroke on a stubborn root, you'll suddenly care a lot. The Oregon 40-139 50-pack solves that problem permanently — or at least for the next couple of seasons.

## What's actually in the package

You get 50 individual 8-inch by 2-inch blades, each stamped from heat-treated steel. The mounting hole is a standard 0.25-inch diameter, which is the universal fit for most straight-shaft edgers. As shown in the product image, each blade has the classic flat bar design with a sharpened leading edge — no fancy curves, no gimmicks.

The blades ship in a plain cardboard box, not a plastic clamshell. That's fine. You're not displaying these; you're storing them in a shed.

## Real-world performance

I've run these on an Echo PE-260 and a Ryobi 790r, and the mounting is genuinely tool-free. The pre-drilled hole lines up with the arbor bolt without any filing or persuasion. That's the kind of detail that separates Oregon from the cheap generics that require a drill and some choice words.

The steel is noticeably harder than the OEM blades that came with my Echo. On a typical suburban lawn with clay soil and the occasional oak root, a single blade handles roughly 200 to 300 linear feet of edging before it needs flipping. Buyers report similar numbers. One verified purchaser notes: *"I edge about 100 feet of driveway every two weeks and I'm still on the first blade after three months."*

The flip side: these are not quick-change blades. You'll need to loosen the arbor bolt, slide the old blade out, slide the new one in, and retighten. That's about 30 seconds per swap if you're coordinated. Not a big deal, but don't expect a tool-free release like some curved-shaft models offer.

## Where it falls short

The lack of depth-adjustment slots is the most common frustration. Some edgers — particularly older Troy-Bilt models — use blades with elongated slots that let you change cutting depth. These Oregon blades are fixed-position only. If your edger requires slotted blades, this pack won't work.

The light oil coating is a minor annoyance. The blades come with a rust-preventive film that transfers to your hands and can fling onto concrete during the first few minutes of use. A quick wipe with a rag solves it, but it's worth knowing before you grab one mid-project.

## FAQ

**Will these fit my Craftsman edger?**
Craftsman straight-shaft edgers use the same 0.25-inch arbor hole and 8-inch blade length. If your model takes a standard flat blade, these will work. Verify your existing blade measures 8 x 2 inches before ordering.

**How often should I replace the blade?**
Rotate the blade when it stops cutting cleanly — typically after it hits a rock or root hard enough to dull the edge. Most homeowners get 200 to 400 linear feet per blade. The 50-pack is realistically a two-to-three-year supply.

**Are these blades reversible?**
Yes, both ends are sharpened identically. Flip the blade when one edge dulls, doubling the usable life of each piece.
