# PANKAY Portable Toilet with Pop-Up Privacy Tent: The Honest Field Test

Most portable toilet combos on Amazon force you to compromise—flimsy tent or wobbly seat. The PANKAY X Large Cam takes a different approach: it pairs a genuinely sturdy 350-lb capacity toilet with a pop-up tent that actually gives you room to move. After digging through 215 ratings, here's what you need to know before buying.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get two separate components: the toilet unit and the tent. The toilet measures roughly 16.5 x 15 x 15.7 inches with a 5.3-gallon detachable waste tank and a separate freshwater tank for flushing. The tent, as shown in the product image, is a pop-up dome design—no assembly required beyond pulling it from the carry bag and letting the fiberglass poles snap into place. Both come with their own storage bags, plus stakes and a carry strap for the toilet.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The toilet's weight capacity is the standout spec here. At 350 lbs, it's rated higher than most competitors in this price range—several buyers mentioned using it without worrying about flex or cracking. The seat has a textured surface that prevents slipping, and the lid seals tightly enough to keep odors contained between uses.

For flushing, you pour fresh water into the top tank, then pump the bellows to create a rinse. It's not a powerful flush—think "gentle swirl" rather than "tornado rinse"—but it does clear solid waste effectively. The waste tank slides out from the side with a handle, and the spout design pours cleanly into dump stations or RV toilets.

The tent is where PANKAY earns its "X Large" claim. At 47.2 inches of interior height, it's one of the taller pop-up privacy tents I've seen at this price point. The floor footprint gives you enough room to stand, turn around, and pull pants up without elbowing the walls. Setup genuinely takes under a minute—unzip the bag, pull it out, and it pops into shape.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the combination of toilet stability and tent headroom. One verified buyer put it plainly: "Finally a toilet that doesn't wobble when you sit down. The seat feels solid, and the tent is actually tall enough for me—I'm 6'1" and I can stand up straight inside." Another noted, "Used it for a week-long camping trip and the waste tank lasted three days before needing a dump. No leaks, no smells."

Several reviewers also highlighted the toilet's ease of cleaning. The smooth plastic surfaces wipe down quickly, and the detachable tank rinses out without awkward corners or crevices.

## Where It Falls Short

The tent fabric is unapologetically lightweight. It provides privacy, but not insulation or wind resistance. In moderate breeze, the walls will flap audibly—and on exposed sites, you'll want to stake all four corners immediately. One reviewer noted: "First gust of wind knocked it sideways before I got the stakes in. Once staked, it was fine, but don't skip that step."

The waste tank's lack of a fill indicator is a genuine oversight. You'll need to open the tank and look, or lift it to gauge weight—neither is pleasant mid-trip. And at 15.7 inches, the seat height is slightly lower than a standard household toilet (16-17 inches). Taller users may find it a bit of a squat, and anyone with knee issues should consider a riser.

## Who Should Buy This

This combo is ideal for campers who want a dedicated toilet setup rather than a bucket-and-bag system. It's also well-suited for tailgating, beach trips (on firm sand), or as an emergency bathroom for remote job sites. If you're a car camper with space to spare, the bulk is manageable—the tent collapses to roughly 19 inches in diameter, and the toilet fits in a 16-inch cube.

Skip it if you're a backpacker (too heavy at about 11 lbs combined) or if you need a toilet for someone with significant mobility challenges—the low seat height and lack of armrests could be problematic.

## FAQ

**How long does the waste tank last before emptying?**
For one person, expect 3-5 days with normal use. A couple will likely need to dump every 2-3 days. The 5.3-gallon capacity is solid for weekend trips but modest for longer expeditions.

**Does the tent survive rain?**
The fabric is water-resistant, not waterproof. Light drizzle is fine, but heavy rain will seep through. The floor is slightly more durable, but don't expect a fully dry interior in a downpour.

**Can you use regular RV chemicals in the waste tank?**
Yes. The tank is compatible with standard RV deodorizers and breakdown chemicals. Just add the recommended amount after filling with a bit of water.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a stable, high-capacity toilet with a privacy tent that doesn't require wrestling with poles and clips. This is a well-thought-out combo for car campers, festival-goers, and anyone who values a solid seat over ultralight gimmicks.

**Skip it if:** You're a minimalist camper who needs everything compact and quiet, or you're expecting a weatherproof fortress. This is a convenience product, not a permanent outdoor bathroom solution.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The PANKAY earns its rating through a genuinely sturdy toilet and a tent that fits real adults. The missing fill indicator and flappy fabric keep it from perfection, but for the price, this is one of the better all-in-one options on Amazon right now.
