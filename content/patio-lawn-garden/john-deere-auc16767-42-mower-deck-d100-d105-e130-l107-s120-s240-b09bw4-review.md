If you own a John Deere D100, D105, E130, L107, or S120, you already know the factory 42" deck is the weak link. The stamped steel shell eventually cracks at the spindle mounts, the belt pulleys seize, or the whole undercarriage rusts out after a few seasons of wet grass. ## What's Actually in the Crate

You get the complete 42-inch deck assembly: the 10-gauge stamped steel housing, three spindles, three mandrels, pulleys, and the belt guard. It arrives ready to bolt on — no assembly required beyond attaching it to your tractor's lift linkage.

Here's the critical detail most aftermarket listings get wrong: **the AUC16767 is specific to the 42" Edge XT deck used on these five models**. All holes lined up perfectly, didn't need to modify anything."* That's the difference between an OEM part and a universal deck that requires shims and prayer.

## Real-World Performance and Durability

The deck shell is 10-gauge steel with a powder-coated finish. That's thicker than the 14-gauge steel on most aftermarket decks selling for half the price. It handles rock strikes and the occasional hidden sprinkler head without denting. The spindles are pre-greased and sealed — owners report 200+ hours of mowing before any bearing noise.

Cut quality matches factory. The three-blade design with the standard 1/2-inch blades gives you a clean, even cut at moderate ground speed. It's not a mulching deck out of the box (you'll need the separate mulch plug kit if that matters), but bagging and side-discharge performance are identical to what your tractor shipped with.

## Where It Falls Short

Three honest tradeoffs. First, **the deck does not include the drive belt or the blade engagement cable**. If yours snapped or stretched, that's another $40–60 and a trip to the dealer or a parts order. Second, **it's heavy** — roughly 85 pounds. You'll want a second set of hands or a floor jack to position it under the tractor. Most garages have a jack, but it's worth planning for.

Third, the price. Genuine John Deere parts carry a premium, and this deck is no exception. You can find no-name decks for $150–200 less. But those often have thinner steel, misaligned holes, and no warranty support. For a part that takes two hours to swap, the OEM fit guarantee is worth real money.
