**Is an extended warranty on lawn equipment ever worth it?** For a $2,000–$3,000 mower, tiller, or chainsaw, the math gets interesting. ASURION's 2-Year Lawn & Garden Protection Plan costs a fraction of what a single repair might run — but the 3.8-star average with 190 ratings tells you the experience isn't universally smooth. Here's what you're actually buying.

**What the plan covers (and what it doesn't)**
This plan kicks in after the manufacturer's warranty expires, covering mechanical and electrical failures for two years. That includes parts and labor — the big-ticket items. If your riding mower's transmission goes at month 14, ASURION pays for the repair. The coverage is transferable if you sell the equipment, which adds resale value.

The exclusions are where shoppers get caught. Normal wear items — belts, blades, spark plugs, filters — are not covered. Neither is cosmetic damage or anything that fails because of improper maintenance. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"They denied my claim because the mower hadn't been serviced at a dealership. The manual just says regular oil changes — nobody told me a dealer had to do them."* That's the fine print that matters.

**How the claims process actually works**
You file online or by phone, get a case number, and take the equipment to an authorized repair center. ASURION pays the shop directly once approved. Here's the catch: if the technician determines the issue isn't covered — say, it's wear and tear — you're on the hook for the diagnostic fee, typically $50–$100.

Several buyers describe the process as slow. *"Took three weeks to get approved, then another two for the shop to get parts,"* reported one verified purchase. *"The protection itself is fine, but don't expect a fast turnaround during peak mowing season."* That's worth factoring in if your equipment is how you earn a living.

**What buyers consistently praise**
When claims go through, they go through properly. Multiple reviewers note that approved repairs were paid in full with no nickel-and-diming. The transferability gets positive mentions, and the flat pricing — no deductible per claim — beats many competitors that charge $25–$50 each visit.

**Where it falls short**
Beyond the claim delays, the biggest frustration is the manufacturer warranty overlap. If your equipment has a 3-year factory warranty, this 2-year plan is redundant for most of its term. Check your original warranty before buying. Also, this plan is tied to the price bracket ($2000–$2999.99) — if you paid less than $2,000, this specific listing won't apply to your purchase.

**Who should buy this**
- **Weekend homeowners** with a $2,500 riding mower they plan to keep for 5+ years. One transmission repair easily exceeds the plan cost.
- **Anyone buying used equipment** where the factory warranty has lapsed — this restores some peace of mind.
- **Sellers** who want a transferable warranty to make their equipment more attractive on the resale market.

**Who should skip it**
- **Pros who maintain their own equipment.** If you're comfortable with basic repairs, the coverage math doesn't work.
- **Buyers with long manufacturer warranties already in place.** Check that overlap first.
- **Anyone needing coverage for wear items** — this isn't a maintenance plan, it's a failure plan.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I cancel and get a refund?**
A: Yes, within 30 days of purchase for a full refund. After that, you get a prorated amount minus any claims already paid.

**Q: Does it cover commercial use?**
A: No. This plan is for residential use only. If the equipment is used for business, claims will be denied.

**Q: What if my equipment is no longer in production?**
A: ASURION will cover the repair cost up to the current market value of the equipment. If the cost exceeds that, they may offer a replacement or payout instead.
