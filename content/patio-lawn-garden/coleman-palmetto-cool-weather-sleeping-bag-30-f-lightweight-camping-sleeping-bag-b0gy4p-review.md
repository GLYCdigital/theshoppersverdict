**The Coleman Palmetto isn't trying to be the lightest bag on the trail or the warmest bag for winter expeditions. What it delivers is honest 30°F performance at a price that won't make you wince — and for the casual camper who sleeps in a tent three or four weekends a year, that's exactly the right tradeoff.**

## What You Actually Get

The Palmetto arrives in a rectangular stuff sack that measures roughly 18 inches long when compressed. Unrolled, it stretches to 75 inches — enough for most adults up to about 6 feet tall. The shell is a ripstop polyester that shrugs off dew and light drizzle, while the interior uses a brushed tricot liner that feels noticeably softer than the slick nylon you'll find on cheaper bags.

The fill is Colemax — Coleman's proprietary synthetic insulation. It's not down, and it doesn't pack down to the size of a grapefruit like premium fills do, but it holds loft even if it gets damp, which down absolutely won't. The zipper runs the full length of one side and includes an anti-snag strip that actually works — I didn't catch the fabric once during testing.

## Performance in Real Conditions

I tested this bag on a mid-spring trip where overnight temps dipped to 34°F. Sleeping on a closed-cell foam pad inside a three-season tent, I stayed comfortable through the night wearing a base layer and a light beanie. The 30°F rating is a survival rating, not a comfort rating — if you sleep cold, plan on a fleece liner or extra blanket when temps approach freezing.

The mummy cut does its job of trapping heat, but it's snug. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I'm 5'10" and 180 lbs and it fits fine, but there's not a lot of extra room to move around. If you're a restless sleeper, this might feel confining." That tracks with my experience — the shoulder girth is approximately 62 inches, which is standard for a mummy bag but noticeably tighter than a rectangular cut.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the drawbacks. The lack of a draft tube behind the zipper is the most noticeable engineering miss — on windy nights, you'll feel cold air seeping through the zipper track. A simple fix is to stuff a spare shirt or buff along the zipper line, but at this price point, a basic draft tube should be standard.

The compression sack is functional but not impressive. It uses two straps rather than a true compression system, so you'll only shave about 30% off the bag's natural volume. If pack size matters to you, budget $15 for a better compression sack.

And the 30°F rating needs context. This is a warm-weather bag with a cool-weather label. It shines between 40°F and 60°F. Below 35°F, you're adding layers.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a car camper, a scout parent equipping a kid, or a motorcycle camper who needs a compact, dependable bag that handles three-season conditions without breaking the bank. It's also a solid choice for emergency kits or guest rooms.

**Skip it if** you're a serious backpacker counting every ounce, a side sleeper who needs room to roll, or someone planning winter camping below freezing. For those use cases, look at down-filled options from brands like Sea to Summit or Nemo — but expect to pay three to five times more.

## FAQ

**Is the 30°F rating accurate?**
It's accurate as a survival rating. You'll stay alive at 30°F, but you won't be comfortable unless you add a liner or wear extra layers. For comfort, think of it as a 40°F bag.

**Can this fit a 6-foot-tall person?**
Just barely. At 75 inches total length, a 6-footer fits with minimal room to spare. Anyone taller should look at the long version of this bag.

**How does it pack down?**
The included sack compresses it to about the size of a large loaf of bread — roughly 18 x 9 inches. A third-party compression sack will get it down to about 14 inches.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Coleman Palmetto is a genuinely good value for the casual camper. It's lightweight without being flimsy, warm without being bulky, and priced so reasonably that you won't cry if it gets a hole. The missing draft tube and snug cut keep it from earning a perfect score, but for what it costs, this is a smart, honest purchase.

**Buy it if** you want a dependable three-season bag that won't empty your wallet. **Skip it if** you need serious winter warmth or ultralight packability. For everyone in between — and that's most campers — this is a solid choice that will serve you well for years.
