## The Deep-Bed Solution That Actually Delivers

Most raised beds max out at 11 inches of soil depth — enough for lettuce and herbs, but a compromise for carrots, tomatoes, and anything with serious roots. The Vego Garden 17" changes that math. At nearly a foot and a half of growing depth, this bed gives your plants room to stretch while sparing your lower back. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, it's clearly resonating with serious gardeners. Here's the full story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the pre-cut steel panels, corner connectors, a rubber mallet, and assembly hardware. There's no bottom — this is an open-base design meant to sit directly on soil or grass. You're getting the 17-inch deep panels, which is the standout feature. The steel is a heavy 0.8mm gauge — noticeably thicker than the flimsy 0.4mm beds you'll find at big-box stores. Assembly is genuinely tool-free: the panels lock together, you tap the connectors with the included mallet, and you're done. Most buyers report being fully assembled in 10 to 15 minutes.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The construction is where this bed separates itself. The galvanized steel core gets a double-layer powder coat finish — that's two passes of baked-on coating, not a single spray. This matters because it directly addresses the #1 killer of metal raised beds: rust. The finish resists chipping during assembly and holds up to repeated watering contact.

The 17-inch depth also solves a practical problem: soil temperature stability. Deeper soil mass buffers against sudden temperature swings, which means healthier root zones through spring and fall transitions. One reviewer noted, "My tomatoes went in three weeks earlier than in my old 11-inch beds, and they didn't stunt." That tracks — the extra soil volume holds heat longer.

The rounded corners aren't just aesthetic. They eliminate the sharp-edge hazards found on cheaper rectangular beds, which matters if you have kids or dogs in the yard.

## Where It Falls Short

The 17-inch depth is a double-edged sword. You'll need significantly more soil than a standard bed — roughly 1.5 cubic yards for a 4x8 configuration. At current bagged soil prices, that's a real cost to plan for. Bulk delivery is cheaper but requires planning.

The open bottom means you'll contend with whatever lives beneath — bindweed, bermuda grass, or gophers. A hardware cloth liner is a smart add-on before you fill, but that's extra work and expense. Also, the "modern" aesthetic — clean lines, no visible fasteners — is sleek, but it won't suit everyone's garden style. If you prefer a rustic, natural look, this might feel too industrial.

## Who Should Buy This

This bed is ideal for three specific buyers: **serious vegetable gardeners** who want deep-rooting crops without back strain; **perennial growers** planting fruit bushes, asparagus, or rhubarb that need long-term soil structure; and **anyone replacing cheap metal beds** that rusted out within a season or two. It's also great for gardeners with mobility issues — the height genuinely reduces bending.

Skip it if you're growing only shallow-rooted greens in a small space, or if you're on a tight budget and need to minimize soil costs.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the 17-inch depth require more soil than standard beds?**
Yes, significantly. A 4x8 bed needs roughly 1.5 cubic yards of soil. Factor that into your budget — it's often more than the bed itself.

**Q: Can I assemble this alone?**
Yes. The panels are manageable by one person, though the included mallet is essential for seating the connectors. Most buyers assemble solo in under 15 minutes.

**Q: Will this rust in humid or coastal climates?**
The double-layer powder coating over galvanized steel handles humidity well. For coastal salt air, expect a longer lifespan than any non-galvanized alternative, but no metal bed is truly maintenance-free.
