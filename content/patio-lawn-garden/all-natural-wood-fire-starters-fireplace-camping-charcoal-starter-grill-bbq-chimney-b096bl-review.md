The package contains individual solid wood sticks — not the sawdust-and-wax pucks you're used to seeing. Each stick measures roughly the size of a fat crayon on steroids, about 3-4 inches long. **How they perform in the field**

This is where these starters separate themselves from the cheap stuff. A single stick burns consistently for 30-40 minutes. That's not a flash-and-gone affair — that's a sustained burn that actually gets your charcoal ashed over properly. I tested one in a standard Weber chimney with lump charcoal and it had the coals ready in about 20 minutes, faster than newspaper and with zero fuss.

The wood is kiln-dried, which means no moisture issues, and it burns clean with minimal smoke. The scent is neutral — you won't catch any pine resin or chemical undertones. This matters if you're using them for cooking, since you don't want your ribs tasting like lighter fluid.

**FAQ**

*How many sticks do I need for a chimney starter?*
One stick is sufficient for a standard-sized chimney. For extra large chimneys or windy conditions, use two — they burn long enough that you won't need to add more.

*Can I use these in a wood stove or fireplace insert?*
Yes. The clean burn and low smoke make them safe for indoor use. Just follow standard fireplace safety practices.

*Do they work in wet conditions?*
The wood is dry and the burn is sustained, but if the sticks themselves have been exposed to moisture, they'll struggle. Keep them dry before use.
