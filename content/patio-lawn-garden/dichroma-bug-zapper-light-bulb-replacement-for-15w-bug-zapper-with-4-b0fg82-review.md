UV bulbs lose their attractive power long before they burn out, and most people don't realize their zapper has been running on a dead light for months. The Dichroma 15W replacement is a straightforward fix for a very common problem, and at 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, it's earned its place as a solid aftermarket option. ## What You Actually Get

The Dichroma ships as a single 15-watt UV replacement bulb with a standard E26 base. That's it — no adapters, no gimmicks. The tube itself is a classic T-shape design that slots into most major bug zapper brands (Flowtron, Aspectek, and generic patio models included). The glass has that characteristic blue-purple hue you expect from UV bulbs, and the base is solid brass-plated ceramic — not the cheap plastic you sometimes see on budget replacements.

One thing worth noting: this is a straight swap for 15W models. If your zapper takes a different wattage, don't force it. The electrical draw and UV output are calibrated specifically for this spec.

## Performance: Does It Actually Kill Bugs?

Here's the honest truth about UV bug zappers: they work, but only if the bulb is fresh. UV output degrades over time, and once it drops below a certain threshold, the zapper becomes decoration. The Dichroma puts out legitimate 15W of UV-A light at 365nm — the wavelength that actually attracts flying insects. In side-by-side comparisons, users report it's noticeably brighter than the OEM bulb that came with their zapper, especially if that original had been running for a season or two.

One buyer put it this way: *"I didn't realize how dim my old bulb had gotten until I swapped in this Dichroma. The zapper sounds like it's working twice as hard now — it's popping all night long."* That tracks with how UV bulbs degrade. The Dichroma restores full output, and the zapper's performance follows.

## Build Quality and Installation

The bulb feels well-made in hand — decent weight, clean seams on the glass, and a base that threads smoothly into standard sockets. Installation is genuinely tool-free: unscrew the old bulb, screw in the new one, done. The whole process takes about 30 seconds. The glass tube is the weak point, though. It's thin, and if you're not careful threading it in, you can crack it. Several reviews mention exactly this happening. Go slow, align the threads properly, and you'll be fine.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be clear about the downsides. First, the lifespan claim of 8,000 hours is optimistic. Under continuous nightly use (8-10 hours per night), multiple users report noticeable dimming around the 5,000-6,000 hour mark. That's still a full season or two of use, but don't expect the full 8,000.

Second, the glass fragility is a real concern. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you buy. If you're clumsy with small glass components, order an extra one while you're at it.

Third — and this is more of a heads-up than a flaw — this bulb attracts bugs to the zapper. It doesn't repel them from your patio. If you're looking for a mosquito repellent, this isn't it. It's a killer, not a deterrent.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my Flowtron or Aspectek zapper?**
A: If your zapper uses a standard 15W bulb with an E26 base, yes. This is the most common configuration for residential bug zappers. Check your manual if you're unsure — the wattage and base type are listed there.

**Q: How often should I replace the bulb?**
A: Replace it every 12-18 months of regular use, or when you notice the zapper's activity dropping. Even if the bulb still lights up, UV output fades over time. If you can't remember the last time you replaced it, it's time.

**Q: Is this safe to touch?**
A: The bulb emits UV-A, not the harmful UV-C used in germicidal lamps. Still, don't stare directly at it while it's on, and handle the glass carefully during installation — the oils from your skin can create hot spots that shorten its life.
