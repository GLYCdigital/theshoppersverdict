If you’ve fought with a hummingbird feeder that drips, grows black mold in unreachable corners, or tips in the wind, the More Birds Jewel is the corrective you’ve been looking for. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, this isn’t a flashy gadget — it’s a thoughtfully engineered tool that respects both the birds and your patience.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains the glass reservoir, a red plastic base with four feeding ports, the perch ring, and a hanging hook. No tools, no assembly beyond a quarter-turn twist. The reservoir holds 8 ounces of nectar — modest, but deliberate. As shown in the product image, the glass is clear, letting you see nectar level at a glance. The base is a single molded piece with no seams or gaskets to fail.

**Why it works better than the $12 plastic feeders**  
The key innovation is the integrated ant moat — a raised channel that sits *above* the feeding ports. Fill it with water and ants physically cannot cross to reach the nectar. That’s a small feature that saves you from a constant battle. The glass reservoir also solves the two biggest complaints in cheap feeders: staining and lingering odors. Glass doesn’t absorb nectar residue like plastic does, so a quick rinse with hot water (no soap needed) keeps it clean. One buyer noted, *“I’ve had mine for three months and it still looks new — my old plastic one was permanently cloudy by week two.”*

**Real-world performance**  
The Jewel uses a vacuum seal design — the reservoir screws down tight, and the nectar only flows out as birds sip. In practice, this means minimal dripping. Several reviewers confirmed: *“Hang it over a deck railing and you won’t get sticky drips on the wood.”* The four feeding ports are wide enough for hummingbirds to perch comfortably, and the red base does its job attracting them before they even spot the nectar.

**Where it falls short — honestly**  
No product is perfect, and the Jewel has two tradeoffs. First, the 8-ounce capacity is small. If you have a busy yard with multiple feeders, you’ll refill every couple of days in summer. Second, the glass makes it heavier — roughly 1.2 pounds filled. On a flimsy shepherd’s crook, it will sway in a breeze. Plan for a solid mount. Also, the moat needs refilling every few days as water evaporates — set a mental reminder.

**Who should buy this**  
- **The new hummingbird host** who wants a no-fuss, leak-resistant feeder that’s easy to clean and maintain.  
- **The gardener who values aesthetics** — the Jewel looks like a proper garden ornament, not a plastic science experiment.  
- **Anyone who’s given up on cheap feeders** that mold or crack within a season. This one will outlast them.

**Skip it if** you want a high-capacity feeder for a large flock (look for a 32-ounce model) or if you need something unbreakable for a high-wind location.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Does the ant moat actually work?**  
A: Yes, when kept filled with water. It creates a physical barrier ants won’t cross. Check it every few days in hot weather.

**Q: Can I put it in the dishwasher?**  
A: The glass reservoir is dishwasher-safe (top rack). The plastic base is not — hand-wash it to avoid warping.

**Q: Will it leak if hung at an angle?**  
A: The vacuum seal holds up to about 15 degrees of tilt. Beyond that, you’ll get drips. Hang it level.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a durable, low-maintenance feeder that looks good and keeps ants out.  
**Skip it if** you need massive capacity or a shatterproof option for kids’ play areas.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely well-built feeder that solves the real problems. The small size is the only real compromise, and for most yards, it’s a fair trade for the ease of cleaning.
