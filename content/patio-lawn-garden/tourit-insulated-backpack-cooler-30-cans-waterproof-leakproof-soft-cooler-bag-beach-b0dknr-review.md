**The Short Version: A Backpack Cooler That Actually Works Like a Cooler**

Most soft-sided coolers are just insulated grocery bags with straps. The TOURIT Insulated Backpack Cooler is different. It's a legitimately waterproof, properly insulated pack that holds a full case of drinks and keeps ice frozen through a full day at the beach. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it's earned its reputation. But it's not perfect — let's talk about where it shines and where it stumbles.

**What You Actually Get**

The TOURIT arrives as a single backpack-style cooler. You get the main 30-can compartment, a smaller front zipper pocket for utensils or a phone, and two mesh side pouches (more on those later). The exterior is a thick, rubberized TPU fabric — not the flimsy nylon most coolers in this price range use. The interior is food-grade PEVA lining with welded seams, which is what makes the "leakproof" claim believable. The zipper is a heavy-duty water-resistant YKK-style closure that runs across the top and down one side, giving you a wide opening for loading.

Dimensions come in around 12 x 9 x 16 inches. That's compact enough to be a personal item on most airlines, which is a feature you'll appreciate if you travel with perishables.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I loaded this with 24 cans plus a bag of ice, and the shoulder straps handled the weight without digging in. The back panel has decent airflow padding, but you'll still sweat if you're hiking more than half a mile in summer heat — that's physics, not a design flaw.

Ice retention is where this cooler earns its keep. In 85-degree weather with the pack sitting in partial shade, ice held for about 26 hours. That's not the advertised 48, but it's genuinely better than most backpack coolers I've tested. The leakproof zipper is the real deal — after the ice fully melted, I tipped the bag upside down over a sink and nothing dripped out. That alone justifies the purchase if you've ever had a cheap cooler soak your car trunk.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The reviews are remarkably consistent. Buyers repeatedly mention three things:

- **The leakproof seal works.** One reviewer noted: "I left it in the car overnight with melted ice and expected a mess. Not a drop. This is the first cooler bag that's actually leakproof."
- **Capacity is honest.** "It holds exactly what it says. I fit 30 cans with a small ice pack on top."
- **Comfort for a loaded pack.** "Carried it a mile across the sand with 12 beers and lunch. Way more comfortable than the shoulder-strap coolers."

**Where It Falls Short**

No product is flawless, and the TOURIT has a few annoyances. The side mesh pockets are too shallow for standard water bottles — they'll fall out when you bend over. You'll need to keep your personal drinks in the main compartment, which means unzipping the whole thing to grab a sip.

The ice retention claim of 48 hours is a stretch. In my testing, and in several customer photos showing melted ice at 30 hours, you're realistically looking at 24 to 30 hours depending on ambient temperature and how often you open it. If you need multi-day ice retention, you want a rotomolded cooler, not this.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is the right cooler for three specific buyers:

1. **The beach-goer** who walks more than 50 yards from parking to sand and doesn't want to drag a wheeled cooler.
2. **The festival attender** who needs hands-free carrying and a reliable seal for a long day of walking.
3. **The weekend kayaker or hiker** who needs a waterproof, packable option that fits in a boat hatch or backpack frame.

Skip it if you need to keep ice for a full weekend without replenishment, or if you want external bottle holders for quick access.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it fit under an airplane seat?**
A: Yes, when fully packed it's roughly the size of a large personal item. You'll get away with it on most carriers, though it's tight if you have long legs.

**Q: Can I put hot food in it?**
A: Technically yes, but the insulation is optimized for cold retention. Hot items will stay warm for about 2-3 hours, not all day.

**Q: How do you clean it?**
A: Wipe the interior with warm soapy water and a cloth. The welded seams mean you can't pull the liner out, but the smooth PEVA surface doesn't trap odors.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely leakproof, comfortable backpack cooler that holds a case of drinks and keeps ice overnight. The build quality justifies the price.

**Skip it if:** You need 48-hour ice retention or external bottle pockets for quick-draw hydration.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The TOURIT is one of the best backpack coolers under $60. The leakproof zipper and welded seams are the differentiators; the shallow side pockets and optimistic ice-retention claims are the tradeoffs. For a day at the beach, park, or festival, this is a solid, honest purchase.
