**The 18-inch patio table that disappears into your deck — until you need it**

Most outdoor side tables are either flimsy aluminum that blows over in a breeze or heavy wood that demands yearly staining. The MXIMU Round Outdoor Side Table takes a third path: molded HDPE plastic with the look of Adirondack furniture and none of the maintenance. After 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, it's clear this little table earns its keep. But it's not perfect — and here's what you should know before clicking buy.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the tabletop, four legs, a crossbar support, and a small hardware bag. Assembly takes 10–15 minutes if you've got a Phillips screwdriver handy — the included Allen wrench starts the job, but the final bolts need the screwdriver for proper torque. The finished table stands 18 inches tall with an 18-inch diameter top. That's chairside height, meaning it lines up with most Adirondack chairs and standard outdoor seating.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The HDPE construction is the headline here. Unlike resin tables that feel hollow and cheap when you knock them, this has a solid, dense feel. The textured surface hides water spots and pollen. The center hole — designed for an umbrella pole — doubles as a drainage point, so rain won't pool on the top. One reviewer noted, "I use it with my cantilever umbrella base and it's perfect for holding my drink and phone while I read." Another said, "Assembly took under ten minutes and it looks like it cost three times the price."

At 8.5 pounds, it's light enough to move with one hand but has enough heft that it won't skitter across the deck in moderate wind. I'd still bring it inside for a serious storm, but it's fine for everyday breezes.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star average holds up in the review text. Owners repeatedly mention three things: the color fidelity (the black and teak options don't look like cheap plastic), the easy assembly, and the sturdiness once assembled. Multiple reviewers mention using it with existing Adirondack chairs and having the heights match perfectly. One wrote: "Finally a table that's actually the right height for my Adirondack chairs — most are too tall or too short."

**Where it falls short**

Two honest caveats. First, the legs have no adjustable feet. On perfectly level decking or concrete, it's rock solid. On uneven patio pavers or a slightly sloped yard, you'll get a wobble. A couple of rubber shims or furniture pads solve it, but it's an oversight at this price point. Second, the 18-inch top is compact — it holds two drinks and a small plate, but it's not a dining surface. If you want a table for meals, size up.

**Who should buy this**

- **Adirondack chair owners** — this is the exact height match you've been hunting for.
- **Renters** — it's lightweight and portable; it moves with you without hassle.
- **Low-maintenance folks** — hose it off, done. No staining, no sealing, no rust.

Skip it if you need a full-size patio table, or if your patio surface is significantly uneven and you don't want to fuss with shims.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this hold a heavy planter?**
A: The listed weight capacity is 110 pounds. A large ceramic planter with wet soil will approach that limit. Stick to lighter pots or use it for drinks and decor.

**Q: Does the umbrella hole fit standard patio umbrellas?**
A: It fits poles up to 1.5 inches in diameter. Most market umbrellas are in that range, but measure your pole before assuming it fits.

**Q: Will the color fade in direct sun?**
A: HDPE is UV-stabilized, so fading is minimal. One reviewer reported "two full summers in Texas sun and it still looks new." That aligns with what I've seen from this material.

**The Verdict: Buy it if you want a no-fuss, right-height side table for chairside use. Skip it if you need adjustable legs or a bigger surface.**
