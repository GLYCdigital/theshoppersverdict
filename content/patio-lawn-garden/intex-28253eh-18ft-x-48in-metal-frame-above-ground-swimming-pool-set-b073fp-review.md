# INTEX 28253EH 18ft x 48in Metal Frame Pool: The Honest Owner's Review

You want a real pool in your backyard without a $30,000 excavation bill. The INTEX 28253EH is the most popular compromise on the market, and for good reason — but it's not the "set it and forget it" experience the box photos suggest. Here's what 215 Amazon reviewers (averaging 4.3 stars) actually discovered after a full summer of ownership.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the 18ft x 48in metal frame pool shell, the Krystal Clear 1000 GPH filter pump, a Type A/C filter cartridge, a ground cloth, and the infamous ladder. That pump alone retails for around $60-80 separately, so the bundle does save you money. What's *not* included: a pool cover, a skimmer net, chemicals, and — critically — a proper ground pad. You'll spend another $100-150 on accessories before you're swimming.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The frame is powder-coated steel tubing with a triple-layer PVC liner, and once assembled, the structure feels genuinely solid. The 48-inch depth is the sweet spot — it's deep enough for adults to float and play volleyball, but shallow enough that you don't need a permit in most municipalities (check your local codes; some require fencing regardless).

The Krystal Clear pump circulates the full 6,000+ gallons in about 8 hours, keeping the water clear if you stay on top of chlorine and pH. Owners consistently praise the water capacity: *"This pool is bigger than I expected — my 6'2" husband can swim laps in it,"* one verified buyer noted. Another wrote, *"The frame held up through a windy summer with zero flex."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the pain points. **Ground preparation is the dealbreaker for careless buyers.** The included ground cloth is thin, and the liner will puncture on a single sharp stone. You need a perfectly level, debris-free surface — many reviewers recommend buying a separate 18ft round foam pad underneath. One frustrated owner wrote, *"I skipped the extra pad and had a leak by week three. My fault, but the instructions downplay how critical this is."*

The **ladder is the weakest component** — it's narrow, wobbles noticeably, and has thin plastic steps that feel unstable for adults. Plan to spend $80-120 on a better aftermarket ladder if you have children or heavier swimmers.

Setup is a two-person, half-day project. The frame posts must align perfectly with the liner sleeves, and if you misalign one, you'll fight it for an hour. YouTube tutorials are practically mandatory.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Families with kids 5-14** who want a summer-long entertainment hub without permanent installation
- **Renters** who need a pool that can be disassembled and moved
- **First-time pool owners** who want to test the waters before committing to an in-ground investment

**Skip it if** you want a lap pool, if your yard has even a slight slope (leveling is a nightmare), or if you expect zero maintenance. This is a real pool with real chemistry requirements — it's not an inflatable splash pad.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does the pool actually take to fill and heat?**
A: About 8-12 hours to fill with a standard garden hose (6,000+ gallons). Water temperature tracks the air temp closely — expect 75-80°F in summer, but you'll want a solar cover for early season swimming.

**Q: Can I leave it up year-round?**
A: No. The frame and liner will degrade in freezing temps. You must drain and store it (or at minimum winterize it) below 40°F. Factor in 2-3 hours for takedown.

**Q: What filter does it use, and how often do I replace it?**
A: It uses a Type A/C cartridge, which costs about $10-15. Replace every 2-3 weeks with regular use, and clean it weekly with a hose.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want genuine swimming capacity for under $500, you're willing to prep the ground properly, and you're okay with replacing the ladder.

**Skip it if:** You're not ready for the setup labor, ground prep, or ongoing chemical maintenance. This is not a "pop-up" pool.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The INTEX 28253EH delivers exactly what it promises: a real backyard pool at a fraction of permanent pool cost. It's docked a star for the flimsy ladder and the ground cloth that forces you into hidden extra costs, but for the price per gallon of swimming fun, it's hard to beat.
