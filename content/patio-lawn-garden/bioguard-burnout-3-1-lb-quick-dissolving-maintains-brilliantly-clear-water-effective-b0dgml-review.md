**BioGuard Burnout 3 Review: The Quick-Dissolving Shock That Won't Mess With Your CYA**

Most pool owners have been burned by "quick dissolve" shock that leaves chalky clouds settled on the floor or takes 20 minutes of stirring to break down. BioGuard Burnout 3 sidesteps that entirely — and it's why this 1 lb container has earned a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The box contains one 1 lb container of granular calcium hypochlorite (65% available chlorine). That's a full shock dose for a 10,000-gallon pool. No measuring cups, no guesswork — dump the whole container in at dusk and you're done. The granules are fine and uniform, which is why they dissolve fast without the clumping you see with cheaper brands.

**Performance: The Real-World Test**

Here's what separates this from generic pool shock: zero cyanuric acid. Most granular shocks contain CYA as a stabilizer, which builds up over time and forces you to drain water. Burnout 3 skips it entirely, so long-term users report their CYA levels stay manageable. That's a big deal for anyone fighting persistent algae blooms.

The dissolving speed is legit. Buyers consistently note it's gone within five minutes — one reviewer said, *"I didn't even need to brush the walls afterward. It was fully dissolved before I put the brush down."* Another long-time pool owner mentioned, *"Swam the next morning, water was crystal clear, no residue on the liner."*

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The recurring theme in positive reviews is clarity and speed. People appreciate that they can shock in the evening and swim the next morning without waiting days for the water to clear. One customer noted, *"This is the only shock I use now. Two hours after adding it, the water was blue again after a green algae start."* Another praised its performance in hard water, saying it didn't leave the calcium scaling they'd seen with other brands.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest tradeoff is the packaging. One pound is a single dose for a standard pool. If you shock weekly, you're buying multiple containers — there's no bulk option here, so the per-use cost is higher than buying a 5 lb bucket of a similar product. Also, this is not a tablet. You can't toss it in a floater. For plaster or gunite pools, you need to pre-dissolve it in a bucket of water before adding, which adds a step some people won't want.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The algae fighter:** If your pool keeps turning green despite regular chlorine, the no-CYA formula is a genuine fix, not a band-aid.
- **The weekend pool owner:** You want one product, simple dosing, and no measuring. This delivers exactly that.
- **The test-strip perfectionist:** If you track CYA levels and want to keep them under 50 ppm, this is the shock that won't sabotage your numbers.

**Skip It If:**

- You have a very large pool (20,000+ gallons) — you'll need two containers per shock, which gets pricey.
- You prefer a tablet you can drop in a skimmer and forget about.
- You want the cheapest per-ounce option available.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this in a saltwater pool?**
A: Yes, but it's redundant. Salt cells generate chlorine continuously; adding Burnout 3 for a quick boost after heavy use is fine, but you shouldn't need it regularly.

**Q: How long after shocking can I swim?**
A: Wait until the free chlorine level drops below 3 ppm, typically 8-12 hours. Most users report swimming the next morning safely.

**Q: Will this bleach my liner?**
A: Not if you pre-dissolve it in a bucket before adding to plaster pools. For vinyl liners, sprinkle it over the deep end while the pump runs. Direct contact with undissolved granules can lighten fabric.

**Skip it if:** You need bulk quantities or prefer a no-fuss tablet. This is a precision tool, not a bargain bin purchase.
