If you've ever watched a squirrel do a gymnastic routine just to empty a "squirrel-proof" feeder, you know the marketing claims usually don't survive contact with reality. This one comes closer than most.

The Upgraded Squirrel Proof Bird Feeder uses a two-pronged defense: a full metal cage that physically blocks access, plus a weight-sensitive perch that closes the seed ports when something heavier than a bird lands. It's a proven design, but execution matters. Here's how it actually holds up.

## What You Actually Get

The feeder arrives fully assembled — no hex keys, no confusing diagrams. The main body is a clear polycarbonate reservoir holding 3 pounds of seed, wrapped in a welded steel cage with roughly 1.5-inch spacing between bars. The base has a removable tray and the top features a metal ring for hanging. As shown in the product image, the perch mechanism sits below the seed ports and drops the protective shroud when triggered. It's a straightforward, no-nonsense build.

## Real-World Performance

The 3-pound capacity is the standout feature. Most tube feeders hold 1-2 pounds, so this one cuts refill frequency significantly. One reviewer noted, *"I filled it Sunday and didn't touch it again until Friday. My previous feeder needed topping off every other day."* That tracks with the math — active backyards will see 5-7 days between refills.

The squirrel defense works, but with nuance. The weight trigger is calibrated around 3-4 ounces, which is roughly where squirrels start. Blue jays and grackles at the upper end of bird weight can occasionally trip it, but the cage prevents them from forcing their way in regardless. The real test — a determined squirrel hanging upside down — reportedly results in the perch closing and the squirrel sliding off. Multiple buyers confirmed this: *"My squirrel spent 20 minutes trying every angle. He eventually gave up and went for the ground feeder."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Durability**: The steel cage holds up to weather and determined wildlife. Buyers report zero chewing damage after months of use.
- **Seed savings**: Because the ports close under weight, spillage drops dramatically. One reviewer estimated they went through 40% less seed in the first month.
- **Easy cleaning**: The removable base tray and wide mouth make emptying and wiping out the reservoir simple — no bottle brushes required.

## Where It Falls Short

The cage spacing is the double-edged sword. At roughly 1.5 inches, it keeps squirrels out but also blocks smaller birds. Chickadees, finches, and nuthatches can slip through, but they often struggle to reach the perches comfortably. A few buyers noted goldfinches simply gave up and went elsewhere. If your yard is primarily small songbirds, this feeder will favor cardinals, jays, and woodpeckers instead.

The lack of drainage holes is a real oversight. If you live somewhere humid or use straight black-oil sunflower, moisture can build up in the base and cause seed to clump. A few buyers drilled their own holes — an easy fix, but you shouldn't have to.

Finally, the fixed hook means the feeder doesn't swivel freely. In gusty conditions, the chain can wrap around the cage and tangle. It's cosmetic rather than functional, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Backyard birders battling squirrels**: This is your front-line defense. It won't stop every squirrel forever, but it will stop most of them most of the time.
- **People who hate refilling daily**: The 3-pound reservoir is genuinely convenient for busy schedules.
- **Anyone feeding medium-to-large birds**: Cardinals, blue jays, woodpeckers, and mourning doves will all feed comfortably.

**Skip it if** your primary audience is tiny songbirds — finches and chickadees will struggle with the cage design.

## FAQ

**Does the weight mechanism scare birds away?**
No. The perch only closes when something heavy lands on it. Birds under 3 ounces feed normally. The slight movement when a jay lands might startle them initially, but they adjust within a day or two.

**Can squirrels chew through the plastic reservoir?**
No. The metal cage completely surrounds the plastic. Squirrels can't reach the reservoir to chew it. The only plastic exposed is the base tray, which is thick and has held up in buyer reports.

**Is it weatherproof?**
The materials hold up to rain and sun, but the lack of drainage holes means you should monitor moisture levels in humid conditions. Consider drilling two small holes in the base tray for ventilation.
