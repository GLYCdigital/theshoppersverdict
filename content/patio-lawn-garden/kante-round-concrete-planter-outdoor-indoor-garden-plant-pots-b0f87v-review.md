If you’ve ever watched a cheap plastic pot blow across your patio in a gust of wind, you know why concrete planters exist. The Kante Round Concrete Planter is a 12-inch diameter, 10-inch tall cylinder that weighs roughly 20 pounds empty. That heft is the whole point — it stays put.

The design is straightforward: a smooth, matte-gray concrete cylinder with clean lines and a pre-drilled drainage hole in the bottom. As shown in the product image, it looks best with a single statement plant — think a fiddle-leaf fig, a dwarf citrus, or even a cluster of succulents. The neutral color works with brick, wood decking, or indoor tile.

---

### What You Actually Get

One planter. That’s it. No saucer, no liner, no stand. The concrete is about 0.5 inches thick at the walls, which gives it solid weight and decent frost resistance — but it’s not sealed. If you live somewhere with hard freeze-thaw cycles, you’ll want to bring it inside or cover it for winter, or you risk hairline cracks as trapped moisture expands.

The drainage hole is a welcome touch. Many concrete planters ship solid, forcing you to drill through an inch of aggregate. This one arrives ready to plant. But because there’s no saucer, you’ll need to add a tray underneath or plan to water sparingly — water will bleed out and stain wood or stone.

---

### Real-World Performance

Buyers consistently praise the stability. One reviewer wrote, *“I put a 5-foot tall olive tree in this and it hasn’t budged through two windstorms.”* That tracks — the low center of gravity and heavy walls make it ideal for top-heavy plants.

The finish is also better than most budget concrete. It’s smooth, not chalky, and the color is consistent across the surface. A few buyers noted that the rim has a slight irregularity — a minor casting imperfection, not a functional issue.

Where it falls short: the weight cuts both ways. You’re not moving this around once it’s planted. If you rearrange your patio seasonally, this is a permanent fixture, not a portable pot. And in colder climates, the lack of a sealant is a real concern. One owner reported, *“Left it out in a Michigan winter and it developed a crack by March.”*

---

### Who Should Buy This

- **Minimalist decorators** who want a clean, sculptural planter that doesn’t compete with the plant.
- **Windy patio owners** who are tired of knocking over lightweight pots.
- **Container gardeners** growing trees, shrubs, or tall perennials that need a stable base.

Skip it if you need something lightweight, if you live in a deep-freeze zone and won’t cover it, or if you want a self-watering or saucer-included system.

### FAQ

**Q: Does the drainage hole come plugged or open?**  
A: Open. It’s roughly 0.75 inches wide — ready for gravel or a mesh screen.

**Q: Can I leave it outside all year?**  
A: In mild climates, yes. In areas with repeated freezing, no — cover it or bring it inside. Concrete is porous and water expansion will crack it.

**Q: Will it stain my deck?**  
A: It can. The unsealed base absorbs water, and mineral deposits can leach out onto wood or stone. Use a saucer or rubber feet.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a stable, modern planter for a permanent spot and don’t mind the weight. **Skip it if** you need portability, winter durability without maintenance, or a complete kit with a saucer.

At 3.9 stars, this is a solid mid-tier concrete planter — better than most big-box options for the same price, but not the last word in frost-proofing. For a statement piece that stays put, it earns its place.
