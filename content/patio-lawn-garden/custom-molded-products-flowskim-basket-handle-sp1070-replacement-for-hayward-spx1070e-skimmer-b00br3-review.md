## What you actually get

The package contains one injection-molded polypropylene handle, pre-drilled with the correct hole spacing. That's it — no hardware, no instructions. You'll reuse the original screws or pins from your existing basket. The handle measures roughly 6 inches across the grip with a 3-inch span between the mounting holes, matching the Hayward spec exactly. As shown in the product image, the grip has a subtle ergonomic curve that keeps your fingers comfortable when hauling a wet basket full of leaves.

## Real-world performance

Installation takes about 90 seconds. You press out the old retaining pins, slide the new handle into the basket's molded slots, and reinsert the pins. The fit is snug — not loose, not binding. One buyer noted, "Replaced two of these on my Hayward skimmers and they fit perfectly. No modification needed." That mirrors my experience: the handle aligns with the basket's pivot points without any shaving or sanding.

The polypropylene material is the same grade used in the OEM part, which means it has some flexibility — that's a feature, not a flaw. When you lift a basket holding several pounds of wet debris, the handle flexes slightly rather than cracking. The UV stabilizers keep it from becoming brittle after a full season of Texas sun, which is where cheap knockoffs typically fail.

## Where it falls short

This handle is specifically molded for Hayward SP1070 and SP1071 series baskets. If you have a Pentair, Jandy, or off-brand skimmer, this won't fit — measure your basket's handle slot width before ordering. Also, the new handle is a brighter white than a basket that's been in the sun for years. It's purely cosmetic and fades within a few weeks, but it will look mismatched initially. Finally, this is a 5-dollar piece of plastic — treat it accordingly. Dropping a full basket on the pool deck edge will crack it, just like the OEM part.
