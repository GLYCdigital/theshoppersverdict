The Weber Performer Premium isn't just another charcoal kettle. It's the grill that answers the one question every charcoal cook eventually asks: *"Do I really need to mess with a chimney starter and lighter fluid every single time?"* No. You don't. And that's the entire story here.

## What You Actually Get

Unbox this and you'll find the classic 22-inch porcelain-enameled kettle — the same cooking chamber that's been the gold standard for decades — mounted on a sturdy steel cart. The headline feature is the Touch-N-Go gas ignition system: a small propane tank (not included, roughly $10 at any hardware store) feeds a burner ring inside the charcoal bowl. Flip a switch, push a button, and your coals are lit in about 8–10 minutes. No chimney, no newspaper, no fluid aftertaste.

The cart itself is the other half of the value. You get a large worktable on the left side with integrated tool hooks and a lid holder, plus a sealed charcoal storage bin underneath that swallows a full 20-pound bag. There's also a built-in thermometer in the lid, a one-touch ash cleaning system, and two locking caster wheels with two fixed wheels for maneuverability.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where the Performer earns its keep. Once those coals are lit, this grill holds temperature like a champ. The porcelain-enameled bowl retains heat, the dampers give you precise airflow control, and I've held 225–250°F for a 6-hour pork shoulder without babysitting it. The 363-square-inch cooking grate fits 9 burgers, 4–6 steaks, or one whole chicken spatchcocked flat.

The gas ignition isn't a gimmick. It's genuinely faster than a chimney starter (which takes 15–20 minutes) and completely hands-free. One buyer put it plainly: *"I've owned Weber kettles for 20 years. The gas ignition is the reason I'll never go back to a plain kettle."* Another praised the worktable: *"The prep space is huge — I can stage 8 burgers and all my tools without running inside."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the assembly. This is not a 45-minute job. You're looking at 2–3 hours with a Phillips head screwdriver, a wrench, and ideally a second person to hold panels while you align bolts. The instructions are clear, but there are a lot of parts.

The 22-inch size is also a real limitation for bigger cooks. A full packer brisket will hang over the edges unless you use a rib rack or cook indirect with creative placement. And the lid thermometer measures dome temperature, not grate temperature — plan on a separate probe thermometer if you're serious about low-and-slow.

One more thing: the propane tank for the igniter is empty on arrival, and some buyers were caught off guard. It's a $10 trip to the store, but it's an extra step before your first cook.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The convenience seeker** who loves charcoal flavor but hates the startup ritual — this is your grill.
- **The backyard entertainer** who wants prep space and tool storage built in, not bolted on later.
- **The Weber loyalist** upgrading from a basic kettle who wants the classic cooking experience with modern convenience.

Skip it if you're strictly a gas griller, need more than 363 square inches for frequent large gatherings, or you're on a tight budget — the Performer Premium typically runs $350–$450, significantly more than a standard kettle.

## FAQ

**Does the gas ignition really work, or is it a gimmick?**
It works. The burner ring surrounds the charcoal, and it lights evenly in about 8–10 minutes. You'll need a standard 16.4-oz propane cylinder, and one tank lasts roughly 20–30 grilling sessions.

**Can you smoke on this grill?**
Absolutely. Set it up for indirect cooking with coals on one side, add wood chunks, and use the dampers to hold 225–275°F. The Performer excels at this — it's just a kettle with a nicer cart.

**Is the charcoal bin worth it?**
Yes. It's sealed, keeps charcoal dry, and holds a full bag. It's one of those features you don't think you need until you use it.
