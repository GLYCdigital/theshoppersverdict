Look, if you're reading this, you've probably already thrown a cheap trimmer head across your yard in frustration. The Echo Genuine OEM Speed Feed 400 is the part that ends that cycle. It's not flashy, it doesn't have LEDs, and it won't mow your lawn for you. But it does one thing exceptionally well: it feeds line when you tap it, and stops feeding when you don't. That's the entire job, and the Speed Feed 400 nails it.

**What you actually get**

This is the real deal — a genuine Echo part, not an aftermarket knockoff. The package includes the complete bump-feed head assembly with the spool, spring, and housing. The thread pattern is machined to match Echo's 10mm shaft standard, so it spins onto your trimmer without adapters or shims. As shown in the product image, the head has a recessed bump knob that's easy to reach even with gloves on. The spool accepts 0.095" or 0.105" line, which is the sweet spot for residential trimming.

**How it performs in the real world**

The headline feature is the tool-free reload. You push the two tabs, pull the spool out, wind the line through the eyelets, and snap it back. No screwdrivers, no taking the whole head off, no cussing. First-time users report getting the hang of it in under five minutes. One verified buyer put it simply: "Loaded in 30 seconds. No tools. Works exactly as advertised."

The bump feed is where this head separates from the $12 generic options. The spring tension is calibrated so a firm tap on the ground releases line — but it won't accidentally dump line when you're just hitting a gopher mound. Another reviewer noted, "Holds line tight, never free-spools, and the bump feed is consistent every time." That consistency matters because the whole point of a bump feed is that you don't have to stop and manually pull line mid-job.

**What buyers keep praising**

Across the 235 ratings at 4.7 out of 5, the same themes keep coming up. Fit is flawless on Echo trimmers — SRM-225, SRM-230, SRM-260, and similar models with the 10mm shaft. Durability is strong for normal residential use, with several buyers reporting a full season of weekly trimming without issues. The bump knob is wide enough to hit reliably without looking down, which sounds minor until you've tried it on a narrow knob. The line feeds in small increments, which means less waste and fewer stops.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. This head is limited to 0.105" line maximum. If you're cutting through blackberry thickets or heavy weeds, you'll want a heavier-duty head that accepts 0.130" line. The Speed Feed 400 is a residential tool, not a brush-clearing solution. Second, the plastic housing is durable but not indestructible — several reviewers mentioned cracked covers after aggressive curb impacts. The replacement cover is cheap, but it's still an extra purchase. Finally, the pre-wound spools are convenient but pricey. The cost advantage only appears if you buy bulk line and wind it yourself, which defeats the "tool-free" convenience for some users.

**Who should buy this**

Get this head if you own an Echo trimmer and want a direct OEM replacement that just works. It's also ideal if you're tired of fighting with aftermarket heads that require tools to reload. Skip it if you need heavy-duty line for serious brush work, or if you're on a non-Echo trimmer — you'd need an adapter, and at that point the fit advantage disappears.

**FAQ**

**Does this fit my Echo SRM-225?**
Yes. The Speed Feed 400 fits Echo trimmers with a 10mm right-hand thread shaft, which includes the SRM-225, SRM-230, SRM-260, and most current Echo gas trimmers. Check your owner's manual to confirm shaft diameter.

**Can I use thicker 0.130" line?**
No. The spool is designed for 0.095" or 0.105" line only. Forcing thicker line will jam the feed mechanism.

**Is this the same as the head that came with my trimmer?**
It's the OEM replacement for the Speed Feed 400, which ships on many Echo models. If your original head was a Speed Feed 400, this is an exact match.
