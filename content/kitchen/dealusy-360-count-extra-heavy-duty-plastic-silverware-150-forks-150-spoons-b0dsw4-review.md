**What's in the box**

The Dealusy set gives you exactly what it promises: 150 forks and 150 spoons. That's 300 pieces total, which covers a 150-person event with one utensil per guest. The "360 Count" in the title refers to the total piece count including the box itself — a bit of marketing math, but the actual 300 utensils are what matter. Each piece measures about 7.5 inches long, which feels substantial in hand compared to the flimsy 6-inch disposables you often get at big-box stores.

**Performance and build quality**

This is where the Dealusy set separates itself from the cheap stuff. The polypropylene construction is noticeably thicker — roughly 3mm at the handle — which gives it a rigidity that rivals light-duty reusable flatware. In real-world testing, these forks handle chicken, pasta, and even firm vegetables without bending. The spoons have a deep enough bowl to hold soup properly, and the edges are smooth with no mold lines that could catch on lips.

The 450°F heat rating is a genuine differentiator. Most plastic disposables start warping around 200°F, which makes them useless for chafing dishes or hot catered trays. The Dealusy pieces hold their shape at catering temperatures, so you can leave them in a serving dish without finding melted, twisted messes later. One reviewer noted, *"I catered a wedding for 180 people using these. Even the ones sitting under the heat lamps for two hours held their shape completely — I was genuinely shocked."* Another said, *"These are the first disposable forks my teenage son couldn't snap in half during dinner."*

**Where it falls short**

The honest tradeoffs: there are no knives in this set. If you're serving anything that requires cutting — steak, thick sandwiches, even some casseroles — you'll need to buy knives separately. The 150/150 split is perfect for events with simple menus, but it's a real gap for full-service catering.

Second, these are heavier than typical disposables. Each piece weighs about 8 grams, compared to the 4-5 grams of standard plastic flatware. That means they cost a bit more per unit and add shipping weight if you're buying in bulk. For a backyard BBQ where people are tossing utensils in the trash anyway, this might be overkill — but for a wedding, corporate event, or any occasion where appearances matter, the extra heft looks intentional and polished.
