The 85kPa suction number is the reason you're looking at this Mesliese, and here's the honest truth: it's not marketing fluff. Most budget sealers sit in the 50–60kPa range, which works fine for dry goods but turns wet marinated chicken into a frustrating game of "why won't this seal." This unit's 120W motor pulls hard enough that you'll see the bag contract visibly within two seconds of pressing the button. That's the difference between sealing dinner and fighting dinner.

## What Actually Arrives

The box includes the sealer itself, a roll of vacuum bags, a bag cutter, and the accessory hose for canisters and mason jars. The body is stainless steel-clad — it looks more expensive than the price point suggests, and it's compact enough to slide into a drawer without you having to rearrange the entire utensil organizer. The control panel is straightforward: Auto, Dry, Moist, Seal-only, and Cancel. No touchscreen gymnastics, no Bluetooth app that you'll never open.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this through three weeks of meal-prep testing. The standout feature is the Moist setting. It handles marinades, raw fish, and even slightly juicy ground beef without the "liquid in the seal zone" failure that plagues cheaper machines. The 85kPa motor doesn't choke — it pulls the air out aggressively, then the sealing strip presses down with enough heat to create a clean, airtight weld. As one Amazon reviewer put it: *"Seals better than my previous FoodSaver that cost twice as much. I've vacuum-sealed soup bags and they held up perfectly in the freezer for two months."* That tracks with my testing — frozen bags came out with zero frost burn.

The built-in bag cutter is a genuine convenience. You roll out what you need, cut, seal one end, fill, and vacuum. It's not a gimmick — it saves you from buying pre-cut bags at a premium.

## Where It Stumbles

No product this affordable gets everything right. The accessory hose has no storage compartment — it just dangles from the side when not in use, which is a minor annoyance if you're sliding the unit in and out of a cabinet. The drip tray is also shallow. If you overfill a bag with juicy meat past the marked fill line, liquid can get pulled into the tray and overflow. It's not a dealbreaker — it's a "read the fill line" lesson — but it's worth knowing before your first brisket.

A few reviewers noted the unit runs loud. It's not deafening — think "small vacuum cleaner" — but if you're sealing at 6 AM before work, you might wake the household. The bags are also a bit thin compared to name-brand options; they work fine, but I'd recommend upgrading to thicker bags (like FoodSaver brand) for long-term freezer storage.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You meal prep weekly, you sous vide regularly, or you buy meat in bulk from Costco and want it to last beyond the "use by" date. The 85kPa suction and Moist setting make it genuinely capable for wet foods, which is where most budget sealers fail.

**Skip it if:** You only need to seal dry goods once a month, or you require whisper-quiet operation. This is a workhorse, not a show pony — it makes noise, takes up counter space, and rewards you with months of frost-free freezer organization.
