Let's be honest: most potato ricers are flimsy aluminum gadgets that bend the first time you encounter a stubborn russet. The PriorityChef Large Potato Ricer is not that. This is a serious piece of stainless steel kitchen equipment that weighs in at just over two pounds and feels like it could outlast your oven.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the ricer body, two interchangeable stainless steel discs (coarse and fine), and a cleaning brush. That's it — no unnecessary accessories. The large hopper is the headline feature here: it holds roughly two cups of cooked potatoes, which translates to a full pound in a single press. For reference, most standard ricers handle half that, meaning you're cutting your mashing time roughly in half.

The ergonomic handles are angled for natural wrist position, and the discs lock in with a quarter-turn mechanism that's intuitive. As shown in the product image, the whole thing is mirror-polished stainless — no cheap coating to chip or flake.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this with Yukon Golds, russets, and even cauliflower. The fine disc produces genuinely silky purées with zero lumps — the texture you'd expect from a restaurant kitchen. The coarse disc is better for rustic mash or gnocchi where you want some structure. The large hopper is the real differentiator; filling it once for a family-sized batch of mashed potatoes beats the old press-and-scrape cycle of smaller ricers.

Cleanup is straightforward: rinse immediately, and the included brush clears the disc holes in about 30 seconds. The stainless steel doesn't retain odors or stains. One buyer noted, *"I've owned three ricers in my life. This is the first one that doesn't make my wrist ache by the end of a batch."* Another praised the build: *"Feels like a commercial tool, not a home gadget."*

**Where It Falls Short**

The hinge is stiff out of the box. Multiple reviewers mention needing significant grip strength for the first several uses, and one said, *"I had to ask my husband to press the first batch."* It does loosen up with use, but if you have arthritis or weak grip, this may not be your tool.

The coarse disc clogs with fibrous vegetables like celery root or broccoli stems. The fine disc actually handles these better because the smaller holes shear through fiber more effectively. Also, the large hopper means you need a larger pot — it won't fit in a standard 2-quart saucepan for direct ricing.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home cooks making mashed potatoes weekly**: The capacity alone justifies the upgrade
- **Gnocchi and dumpling makers**: Fine disc gives the consistent texture you need
- **Baby food preparers**: Steam vegetables and rice them — smooth, lump-free, no additives
- **Anyone replacing a bent aluminum ricer**: This is the last one you'll buy

**Skip it if** you have limited hand strength, mostly make single servings, or want something that fits in a drawer — this needs cabinet space.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can it rice hot potatoes directly in the pot?**  
A: Yes, but the hopper is large, so use a pot with at least a 6-inch opening. The handles stay cool during use.

**Q: Is it dishwasher safe?**  
A: Technically yes, but hand washing is recommended. The discs can warp slightly in high heat, affecting the fit over time.

**Q: Does it work for applesauce or tomatoes?**  
A: It'll do applesauce beautifully. For tomatoes, you'd want a food mill — the discs are too fine for seeds.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you cook for more than two people, hate lumpy mash, or want a ricer that won't bend, rust, or need replacing in three years. **Skip it if** you have hand strength issues or only cook for one.

At 4.6/5, this earns its rating. It's not perfect — the stiff hinge and coarse disc clogging are real tradeoffs — but for the core job of making perfectly smooth mashed potatoes, it's among the best home ricers available. The stainless construction alone justifies the price over cheaper aluminum alternatives that leach metallic taste into acidic foods.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A heavy-duty workhorse that makes lump-free mash effortless, with minor ergonomic caveats.
