Let’s be honest: a washing machine outlet box is not a glamorous purchase. It’s a behind-the-wall piece of plastic and metal that you’ll never see again after the drywall goes up. But get it wrong, and you’re dealing with water damage, a flooded laundry room, and a plumber’s bill that makes the box itself look like pocket change. The EFIELD Washing Machine Outlet Box with Center Drain has earned a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, and after digging into what buyers actually report, there’s a clear story here.

## What You Actually Get

The EFIELD box arrives fully assembled — a 18-gauge galvanized steel frame with a powder-coated finish, pre-installed shut-off valves, and the standout feature: factory-installed 1/2-inch crimp-style water hammer arrestors. You’re not just buying an outlet box; you’re buying the arrestors that normally cost $15 to $20 each. That’s real value baked into the price.

The center drain configuration is the key design decision here. As shown in the product image, the drain outlet sits dead-center between the two water supply lines. That means you can mount this directly between standard 16-inch on-center studs without offsetting anything. The drain outlet itself is a 2-inch PVC elbow with a hose clamp included.

## Performance and Build Quality

The metal box frame is genuinely sturdy — several reviewers mention the solid feel compared to cheaper all-plastic alternatives. The powder coating isn’t just cosmetic; it protects against the humid environment of a laundry room and prevents the sharp edges you sometimes find on cheaper stamped steel boxes.

The water hammer arrestors are the real selling point. One verified buyer noted, “I didn't realize how noisy my old setup was until I replaced it with this box. The arrestors actually work — no more banging when the washer kicks off.” That’s the experience most owners report: the integrated arrestors eliminate the pipe knock that plagues standard outlet boxes.

Installation is straightforward for anyone comfortable with basic plumbing. The 1/2-inch crimp connections require a crimping tool — if you don’t own one, factor that into your planning. The valves turn smoothly and don’t feel cheap, and the drain outlet has a proper 2-inch connection that mates cleanly with standard washer hoses.

## Where It Falls Short

Nothing’s perfect, and the EFIELD has a couple of genuine weaknesses.

The plastic drain outlet elbow is the weak point. It’s functional, but it doesn’t match the quality of the metal box. One reviewer warned, “Don’t overtighten the hose clamp on the drain elbow — the plastic can crack.” That’s a real concern if you’re heavy-handed with tools.

The included mounting hardware is underwhelming — basic screws that are better suited for drywall than stud mounting. You’ll want to grab #10 wood screws for a proper install. It’s a minor annoyance, but worth knowing before you start.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this box fit standard 16-inch stud spacing?**
Yes. The center drain design is specifically engineered for 16-inch on-center framing. It fits cleanly between studs without modification.

**Q: Do I need a crimping tool for the 1/2-inch connections?**
Yes. The supply lines use crimp-style connections, so you’ll need a PEX crimp tool or a compression adapter if you don’t have one. It’s a standard plumbing tool, but don’t assume you can wing it with pliers.

**Q: Are the water hammer arrestors reusable or serviceable?**
They’re sealed, pre-charged units. They’re not meant to be serviced, but they’re rated for the life of the box. If they fail, you’d replace the whole box — which is still cheaper than buying separate arrestors and a box.
