If you're staring down a stack of laundry and a coin-op machine that eats $3 per load, the COSTWAY Portable Twin Tub is the kind of product that makes you question why you didn't buy one sooner. This 26lbs-capacity unit (18lbs wash, 8lbs spin) is aimed squarely at apartment dwellers, RV owners, and anyone tired of laundromats. It's not a full-size washer replacement — but for a single person or couple, it's close.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the washer unit itself (roughly 25" wide × 14" deep × 29" tall), a drain hose, a fill hose with adapters, and a lint filter for the spinner basket. The twin-tub design is exactly what it sounds like: a wash tub on the left, a spin dryer on the right. Both have their own lids, and the whole thing sits on four locking casters. Assembly is basically zero — attach the hoses, plug into a standard 120V outlet, and you're operational.

**Real-world performance**

The wash tub handles 18lbs of laundry — that's a queen duvet cover, or about five days of clothes for one person. It has three wash settings (gentle, normal, heavy) plus a 15-minute mechanical timer. The pulsator at the bottom gets clothes genuinely clean; reviewers consistently note it handles everyday soil without issue. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical, but it got my gym clothes and towels noticeably cleaner than my building's machines."*

The spinner is the real workhorse. At 8lbs capacity, it removes enough water that clothes come out damp, not dripping — most reviewers report line-drying times cut to a few hours. The catch? You're manually transferring wet clothes from wash tub to spinner. There's no drain pump; the wash tub drains via gravity, so you'll need the unit elevated or at least near a floor drain or sink.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The simultaneous operation is the standout feature. While one load spins, the next is washing — total time for a full day's laundry drops to about an hour of active involvement. Portability is also a recurring positive: *"Fits in my closet, wheels roll smoothly, and it's light enough to move to the kitchen sink for filling."* The build quality exceeds expectations at this price point — stainless steel tubs, no sharp edges, and the lid seals properly.

**Where it falls short**

The gravity drain is the biggest frustration. If you're not near a floor drain, you'll be propping the unit on a sturdy table or box to get the hose angled downward — and the hose is short (about 4 feet). The fill hose also needs a threaded faucet; most modern kitchen sinks require a $10 adapter. The mechanical timer is imprecise — you'll learn to estimate "that's about 10 minutes" by feel. And the spinner basket is small; bulky items like a thick towel need to be folded carefully to fit.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers** with a bathtub or utility sink — you'll save $100+/month in laundromat fees
- **RV owners** who need off-grid washing without hookups
- **Singles or couples** who generate 2-3 loads per week
- **Anyone with sensitive skin** who wants control over detergent and rinsing

Skip it if you have a family of four, if you lack any way to elevate the unit for draining, or if you expect full automatic operation — this is a semi-manual machine, period.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I hook this up to my kitchen sink permanently?**
A: Not really. It's designed for temporary connection — attach hoses, fill, wash, drain, disconnect. For permanent installation, you'd need a dedicated laundry sink and a Y-connector.

**Q: How much water does it use per load?**
A: Around 8-10 gallons for the wash tub at full capacity, plus another 2-3 for the rinse cycle. That's roughly a third of a standard washer's usage.

**Q: Does it damage delicate clothes?**
A: The gentle cycle is genuinely gentle, but the spinner can be rough on lace or thin knits. Use a mesh bag for delicates and avoid spinning them at full capacity.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're renting, you're tired of laundromats, and you have a sink or tub nearby. The COSTWAY pays for itself in about six weeks of avoided coin-op fees, and the twin-tub design is genuinely efficient.

**Skip it if:** You need hands-off operation, you have a large household, or you have zero tolerance for manual water management.

It's not a full appliance replacement — it's a smart compromise. At 4.0 stars from 200+ ratings, the consensus is clear: this is a solid, honest portable washer that does exactly what it promises, provided you understand its limitations going in. For the price, that's a fair trade.
