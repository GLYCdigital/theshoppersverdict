If you've ever had a lasagna volcano over a standard 9x13 pan, you already know why this Pyrex Deep exists. This 5.2-quart version takes the classic dimensions and adds nearly two inches of extra depth — enough to layer a serious lasagna, roast a whole chicken with vegetables, or bake a double batch of brownies without risking a molten mess on your oven floor.

**What You Actually Get**

The set includes the main dish (measuring 9x13 inches with 3.1-inch walls) and a glass lid that's not just a cover — it's a second, slightly shallower baking dish. The lid nests onto the base with a snug fit, but when flipped over, it works as a standalone pan for cobblers, sheet cakes, or roasted veggies. That's the "2-in-1" pitch, and it's genuinely useful, not marketing fluff.

Both pieces are made from borosilicate glass, which handles the freezer-to-oven transition far better than standard soda-lime glass. Pyrex rates this up to 450°F. The glass is nonporous, so it won't absorb food odors or stains, and it cleans up easily after baked-on cheese or caramelized sauces.

**Real-World Performance**

In testing, the deep walls are the standout feature. A standard 9x13 holds about 3 quarts; this one holds 5.2. That means you can build a four-layer lasagna, a thick shepherd's pie, or a big batch of mac and cheese with room to spare. The glass heats evenly, and you can actually watch your food brown through the sides — a small perk that's surprisingly handy.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I've burned through three cheap metal pans in two years. This thing has survived weekly lasagnas, holiday casseroles, and my clumsy hands. It still looks brand new."*

Another noted the lid's versatility: *"I bought this for the depth, but I use the lid as a second pan more than I expected. It's perfect for cornbread while the main dish holds the chili."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The lid is heavy — when it's full of food, you need two hands and a steady grip. There are no handles on either piece, so moving a hot, full dish from oven to counter requires oven mitts and care.

The lid also has a subtle metal rim (for a tight seal), which means it's not microwave-safe. You'll need to remove it before reheating leftovers. And while the base is dishwasher-safe, Pyrex recommends hand-washing the lid to preserve the rim's integrity over time.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the home cook who entertains, meal-preps, or feeds a family that actually eats. It's ideal if you regularly make baked pasta, large roasts, or desserts that need generous vertical space. It's also a smart pick for anyone who's tired of replacing warped metal pans — borosilicate glass lasts for decades if you don't drop it.

Skip it if you only bake occasional small batches, or if you have limited cabinet space — the deep walls and lid take up more room than a standard 9x13.

**FAQ**

**Can this go from freezer directly to a hot oven?**
Yes. The borosilicate glass handles thermal shock well. Just avoid placing it on a cold, wet surface immediately after baking — that's what actually causes glass to crack.

**Is the lid really a usable baking dish?**
It's about 1.5 inches deep — enough for cornbread, brownies, roasted vegetables, or a small cobbler. It won't hold a full lasagna, but it's a legitimate second pan.

**Does the glass stain or retain odors?**
No. This is the big advantage over ceramic. Tomato sauce, curry, or garlic won't leave any trace after a normal wash.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a workhorse baking dish that does double duty, handles big recipes, and will outlive your current cookware collection. **Skip it if** you need a lightweight, easy-to-handle pan for quick weeknight meals.

This is one of the rare products where the 4.7-star rating feels earned. It's not flashy, but it's the kind of kitchen tool you'll reach for every single week — and still own a decade from now.
