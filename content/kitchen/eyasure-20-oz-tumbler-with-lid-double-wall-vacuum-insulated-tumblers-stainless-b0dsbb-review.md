The 20 oz tumbler market is brutally crowded. Every brand promises 24-hour ice retention and leak-proof lids, yet most deliver lukewarm coffee by noon. The Eyasure 20 oz Tumbler doesn't reinvent the wheel — it just executes the basics better than most, and at a price that won't make you wince.

Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 220 ratings and real-world usage patterns.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the stainless steel tumbler itself, a clear polycarbonate lid with a sliding closure, and a reusable straw. No fancy extras, no carrying sleeve, no brush. The double-wall vacuum construction is standard 18/8 stainless — the same grade you'd find on tumblers costing twice as much.

The dimensions matter: at roughly 6.5 inches tall with a 3.5-inch base, this fits standard car cup holders. That's a genuinely rare feature for 20 oz tumblers, which often taper too wide at the bottom. The powder-coated finish comes in several matte colors, and it resists fingerprints better than glossy alternatives.

## Real-World Performance

Cold retention is the headline feature, and it delivers. Multiple reviewers report ice still present after a full workday. One buyer noted: *"I filled it at 7 AM with iced coffee and there was still ice at 5 PM. My old Yeti doesn't even do that."* That tracks with the double-wall vacuum construction — there's no structural shortcut here.

The lid deserves specific attention. It uses a slide-lock mechanism rather than a push-button. That means no accidental opens in your bag, and it seals firmly enough that I'd trust it in a laptop bag with electronics. The straw opening accommodates standard reusable straws, not just the included one.

Hot liquids work too, but with a caveat: the exterior gets noticeably warm after 30+ minutes with hot coffee. That's not a defect — it's physics with any vacuum-insulated cup. But if you're looking for a true travel mug that stays cool to the touch, this isn't it.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.4-star average (out of 220 ratings) clusters around three themes:

- **Ice retention** — the most-cited positive, with multiple reviewers comparing it favorably to brands costing twice as much
- **Lid security** — the slide-lock design gets repeated shout-outs for not leaking in bags
- **Cup holder fit** — the narrow base means it works in cars, strollers, and bike cages

One reviewer summed it up: *"I've bought three of these now. One for home, one for work, one for my car. They just work."*

## Where It Falls Short

The lid is the weak point. The clear polycarbonate scratches easily — several reviewers mention visible scuffs within weeks. It also fogs up with hot liquids, which obscures the contents. And while the lid seals against leaks, it's not truly airtight; if you tip it upside down aggressively, you'll get a few drops.

The bigger issue: **this is hand-wash only.** The manufacturer explicitly states it's not dishwasher safe, and the powder coating will degrade in the dishwasher. For anyone who's relied on the dishwasher for their daily mug, this is a genuine inconvenience, not a minor nitpick.

The straw is also on the thin side — roughly 0.3 inches in diameter. Fine for water or iced coffee, but too narrow for smoothies or boba.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Commuter** — fits standard cup holders and survives bag tosses
- **Iced coffee loyalist** — 8+ hours of ice retention means your last sip is as cold as your first
- **Minimalist** — basic design, no gimmicks, does exactly what it claims

**Skip it if:** you're a dishwasher-or-bust person, or you need a lid you can see through clearly after months of use.

## FAQ

**Does it actually keep drinks cold for 24 hours?**
No. Expect 8–12 hours for ice retention with cold liquids. Anyone claiming 24 hours is either testing in a lab with no lid openings or exaggerating.

**Will it fit in my car's cup holder?**
Most likely yes, thanks to the tapered base. But if you drive a car with unusually small cup holders, measure your existing tumbler first.

**Can I put this in the dishwasher?**
Technically yes, but it'll ruin the powder coating and potentially compromise the vacuum seal over time. Hand washing is strongly recommended.

## The Verdict

**Skip it if:** hand-washing is a dealbreaker, or you need a fully airtight seal for aggressive travel.
