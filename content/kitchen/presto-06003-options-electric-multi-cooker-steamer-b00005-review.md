You don't need a $200 multi-cooker to steam vegetables properly. The Presto 06003 Options proves that with a straightforward, no-nonsense design that's been a budget kitchen staple for years. But "simple" cuts both ways — let's talk about what you gain and what you sacrifice.

## What's Actually in the Box

The Presto 06003 ships with the 6-quart base unit, a stainless steel steaming basket, a rice bowl, and a tempered glass lid. There's no digital display, no touchscreen, no Bluetooth. You get a manual heat dial with three settings — steam, rice, and slow cook — plus a keep-warm position. The base doubles as a warming tray when you remove the steaming basket, which is a clever space-saver for dinner parties.

The build is classic Presto: heavy-gauge aluminum base, cool-touch handles, and a power cord that stores in the base. It's not pretty, but it's sturdy. The 6-quart capacity is the sweet spot for a family of four — you can steam a full head of broccoli florets or a dozen ears of corn without stacking them into submission.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Struggles

Steaming is this unit's superpower. The water reservoir heats fast — you'll see steam within 3-4 minutes — and the perforated basket allows even circulation. Dumplings, fish fillets, and asparagus come out consistently cooked. The interior water window is genuinely useful; you can see when you're running low without lifting the lid and losing heat.

The rice function works but requires attention. You'll need to nail your water-to-rice ratio (typically 1:1.5 for white rice), and there's no automatic shutoff. Set a timer. The slow-cook setting is the weakest link — it runs hotter than a dedicated slow cooker. A 6-hour stew on "low" cooked closer to a 4-hour simmer, and liquid reduced more than I'd like. If you're a serious slow-cooker, keep your Crock-Pot.

## Honest Shortcomings

Beyond the slow-cook issue, there are real quirks. The heat dial is imprecise — there's no "medium" setting, just three fixed positions. The keep-warm function can dry out food if you forget it's running. And the glass lid is heavy; it needs two hands to handle safely when the unit is full of steam. One reviewer summed it up: *"Great steamer, but don't expect set-and-forget convenience. You need to be in the kitchen."*

## Who Should Buy This

## FAQ

**Can I use it to reheat leftovers?**
Yes, but it's not ideal. The steam setting reheats without drying out, but the keep-warm tray is better for holding already-cooked food at temperature.

**Is the basket dishwasher safe?**
The stainless steel basket is dishwasher safe. The base must be hand-washed — never submerge it in water.

**Does it make good rice?**
Acceptable, not great. White rice comes out fluffy if you watch the timing. Brown rice requires more water and patience. Don't expect fuzzy-logic rice cooker results.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, affordable steamer that also handles basic rice and buffet warming. It's a workhorse for vegetables, tamales, and seafood.

**Skip it if** you need a hands-off slow cooker or want precise digital temperature control. The Presto demands presence, not automation.
