If you're shopping for a 5-quart stock pot and you're tired of thin-bottomed pots that scorch soup in one corner while the other side sits lukewarm, the DELARLO 5QT is worth your attention. This is a genuine tri-ply pot — a magnetic stainless steel exterior, an aluminum core, and an 18/10 stainless interior — cladded across the entire body, not just a cheap disc welded to the base. That difference matters more than most shoppers realize.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the 5-quart pot, a matching stainless steel lid with a tight-fitting rim, and two riveted side handles. No glass lid, no steamer insert, no extras — this is a focused, no-nonsense piece of cookware. The pot measures roughly 9 inches in diameter and stands about 6 inches tall, which puts it in the sweet spot for batches of soup, chili, or boiling pasta for a family of four. It's induction-ready, which you'd expect at this level, but it also works on gas, electric coil, and ceramic glass tops without complaint.

**How it performs in real kitchens**

The tri-ply cladding does what it's supposed to: heat spreads evenly across the base and up the sidewalls. On a gas burner set to medium, a full pot of chicken stock comes to a gentle simmer without hot spots scorching the bottom. On induction, the response is quick — you're not waiting around for the aluminum core to catch up. The 18/10 interior is a workhorse: it's non-reactive, so you can simmer tomato sauce or braise with wine without that metallic aftertaste cheaper pots produce.

Oven safety is rated to 500°F, which makes this a legitimate one-pot option for braising — sear a chuck roast on the stovetop, then slide the whole thing into a 325°F oven for three hours. The riveted handles hold firm; they're sturdy enough for a full pot when you're careful, though I'd always recommend two hands with 5 quarts of liquid.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews reflects a pattern: people are surprised by the build quality at this price point. One verified buyer wrote, "I've owned All-Clad for years and bought this as a backup — honestly can't tell the difference in heating performance." Another noted, "Finally a stock pot that doesn't warp after a year of induction use." Multiple reviewers mention the lid fit specifically — it's snug enough to trap steam without rattling.

**Where it falls short — honestly**

No pour spouts. That's my biggest gripe. When you're transferring a full 5 quarts of stock into storage containers, the liquid catches the rim and runs down the outside of the pot. It's controllable if you pour slowly, but it's a design miss at this level.

The interior also requires technique. Stainless steel is not non-stick — you need fat or liquid before adding ingredients. One reviewer put it bluntly: "My eggs stuck like glue the first time I tried. Once I figured out to preheat and add oil, zero issues." That's a learning curve, not a defect, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Handles stay cool on gas but warm noticeably on induction at high heat. Use a mitt, not your bare palm.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home cooks who make stock, soup, or chili weekly** — this pot handles volume without scorching
- **Induction stove owners** — the magnetic base is solid and warp-resistant
- **Anyone graduating from non-stick** — if you're willing to learn stainless technique, this rewards you with durability

**Skip it if** you only boil pasta occasionally, or if you're looking for a non-stick surface. Also skip if you need pour spouts — you'll want a different design.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this go in the dishwasher?**
A: Technically yes, but hand-washing is better. High-heat dishwasher cycles can discolor the stainless exterior and dull the finish. Five minutes with soap and a sponge keeps it looking new.

**Q: Does it work on glass-top electric stoves?**
A: Yes. The base is flat and polished — no warping scratches the surface. Just lift the pot rather than sliding it to avoid marking the glass.

**Q: What's the capacity for pasta?**
A: 5 quarts handles a full pound of dried pasta comfortably, with room for the water to boil over if you're not watching. Cook 2 pounds max, and you'll need to stir more frequently.
