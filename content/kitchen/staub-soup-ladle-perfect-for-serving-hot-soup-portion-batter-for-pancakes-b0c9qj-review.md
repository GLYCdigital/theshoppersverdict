## What You Actually Get

The STAUB Soup Ladle is a single-piece cast iron construction with the brand's signature matte black enamel finish. It measures roughly 12 inches from handle tip to bowl edge, and the bowl is deep and substantial — think restaurant-grade portioning rather than the shallow scoops you get from cheaper ladles. ## Real-World Performance

Here's where this ladle separates itself from the $8 stainless steel options at Target. The cast iron body holds heat. That sounds like a minor detail, but when you're serving a pot of chili to six people, the ladle itself doesn't cool down between scoops. It stays warm, which means your last bowl is as hot as your first.

The portion control is genuinely impressive. Each full scoop delivers roughly 4 ounces — about half a cup. If you're portioning soup for meal prep or serving batter for pancakes, that consistency matters. One buyer noted, *"I use this for pancake batter on weekend mornings. The deep bowl pours cleanly without dripping down the sides, and I get perfectly uniform pancakes every time."* That tracks with my experience — the pour spout on one side of the bowl is subtle but effective for controlled dispensing.

The handle balance deserves mention. STAUB clearly engineered this with weight distribution in mind. Even when the bowl is full, the handle stays stable and doesn't torque your wrist. It's not light — this is a substantial piece of cast iron — but it never feels unwieldy.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. This ladle requires hand washing. You cannot toss it in the dishwasher — the high heat and harsh detergents will degrade the enamel over time. It also needs to be dried thoroughly after washing to prevent rust on the exposed cast iron rim. If you're someone who prefers low-maintenance kitchen tools, this adds a small chore.

The weight is another consideration. At roughly 1.2 pounds, it's noticeably heavier than typical stainless steel ladles. Most adults won't have an issue, but if you have arthritis or wrist weakness, extended serving sessions could get tiring.

One more thing: there's no hanging loop. The handle ends in a flat, rounded tip, so you'll need drawer space or a countertop crock for storage. It won't hang neatly on a pegboard.

## FAQ

**Can I use this ladle on nonstick cookware?**
Yes, the cast iron is smooth and won't scratch nonstick surfaces. Just avoid dropping it or banging it aggressively against the pan edges.

**Does it leave a metallic taste in food?**
No. The enamel coating prevents any metallic transfer. This is a common concern with cast iron, but the STAUB finish completely addresses it.

**Is it safe for high-acid foods like tomato soup?**
Absolutely. Unlike bare cast iron, the enamel coating won't react with acids. You can simmer and serve tomato-based dishes without any flavor alteration.
