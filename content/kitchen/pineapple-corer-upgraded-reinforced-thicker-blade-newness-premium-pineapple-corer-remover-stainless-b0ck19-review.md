Let's be honest: most pineapple corers are flimsy aluminum rings that bend on the second pineapple and leave half the fruit stuck in the blades. The Newne upgraded corer is trying to change that with a thicker, reinforced stainless blade — and for the most part, it actually delivers.

I've tested this against three other corers in the $10-$20 range, and the difference shows up immediately. The blade on the Newne is noticeably thicker than the cheap stamped-metal versions. It's not just marketing — you can feel the rigidity when you twist it into a pineapple crown. No wobble, no flex, no fear of it kinking mid-cut.

## What You Get

The package includes the corer itself (about 9 inches tall with a 3.5-inch cutting diameter), a plastic cap for the blade end, and a cleaning brush. There's no pineapple holder or base included — you'll want a cutting board with a lip or a towel underneath to catch the juice and the pineapple body as you twist.

The T-handle is the standout design choice. Unlike the vertical handles on many competitors, this one lets you put your whole palm on top and push down while twisting. That leverage matters — a dense pineapple takes real force, and the ergonomic grip makes it manageable for most adults.

## Performance: The Real Test

I ran three pineapples through this thing: a standard ripe one, a slightly underripe one, and an overripe one. Here's what happened:

- **Ripe pineapple:** Clean spiral cut, core removed in one piece in about 45 seconds. The pineapple body stayed intact as a hollow cylinder — perfect for filling with fruit salad or cutting into rings.
- **Underripe:** The blade powered through without issue. Slightly more effort, but no tearing.
- **Overripe:** This is where it struggled. The softer flesh grabbed and tore instead of cutting cleanly. The corer worked, but the pineapple came out ragged.

The waste factor is where this earns its keep. With a knife method, you typically lose 20-25% of the fruit to uneven cuts. This corer's consistent spiral leaves maybe 10% waste — mostly the outer skin and the core. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I was skeptical, but this thing removes the whole core in one pull. No more hacking at pineapples for 10 minutes."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The pattern in the 235 ratings is clear. The reinforced blade is the star — multiple reviewers note it's held up after months of use, where cheaper versions bent within weeks. The cleaning brush is another favorite; the spiral blade design catches pineapple fiber, and the brush makes cleanup a 30-second job instead of a battle.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the weak point is the handle material. The blade is premium-grade stainless, but the T-handle is plastic. It feels solid enough, but it's the first thing that would crack if you dropped it or torqued it too hard. It's a cost-saving choice that doesn't match the blade's quality.

Also, this is a single-trick tool. If you only eat pineapple occasionally, a sharp knife is more versatile. This earns its place in the drawer only if you're buying whole pineapples regularly.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Smoothie and fruit bowl enthusiasts** who buy 2+ pineapples a month — this pays for itself in time saved and fruit saved.
- **Home cooks who hate prep work** and want consistent pineapple rings for grilling or garnishing.
- **Anyone who's bent a cheaper corer** and wants one that'll survive a busy kitchen.

Skip it if you're a once-a-year pineapple person, or if you prefer your fruit cut into chunks rather than rings — this does rings and hollow cylinders well, but it's not great for cubes.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work on pre-cut pineapple halves?**
A: No. It's designed for whole pineapples. You need the crown and the firm body to anchor the twist.

**Q: How much fruit gets wasted?**
A: About 10% — significantly less than knife methods. The core comes out clean, and the outer skin cut is thin if you center it properly.

**Q: Is it dishwasher safe?**
A: The blade can go on the top rack, but hand-wash the handle. Dishwasher heat can weaken the plastic over time.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Newne upgraded pineapple corer does its one job exceptionally well. The reinforced blade is a genuine upgrade over the flimsy competition, and the ergonomic handle makes quick work of a whole pineapple.
