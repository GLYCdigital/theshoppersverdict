## What You Actually Get

The package contains exactly what you'd expect: one apple cutter/corer/slicer unit, no assembly required. The frame is constructed from stainless steel with a welded joint at the top — noticeably sturdier than the flimsy cast-aluminum versions that bend after a month. The blades are sharpened on both edges, which matters more than you'd think. The handles are coated with a non-slip rubberized grip that extends slightly past the frame, giving you leverage without digging into your palms.

As shown in the product image, the design is straightforward: eight stainless blades arranged in a circle around a central corer tube. Total height is about 4 inches, and it weighs roughly 8 ounces — substantial enough to feel durable, light enough to store in a drawer without hassle.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this on Honeycrisp, Granny Smith, and a slightly mealy Red Delicious. The results were consistent. Firm apples — the kind you actually want for snacks or baking — cut through with a satisfying *thunk* and no resistance. The corer tube punches out the seeds and stem cleanly, leaving a neat center hole. No apple chunks stuck inside the core tube, which was my biggest concern based on cheaper models I've used.

For softer apples, the blade still cuts cleanly but you'll feel slightly more drag. One press is enough for standard medium-sized apples. Large Honeycrisps — the ones that look like they could star in a monster movie — required a second press with repositioning. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

Cleanup is straightforward. Rinse under warm water, and the blades don't trap apple flesh the way some competitors' designs do. The stainless steel doesn't discolor or retain odors, even after sitting for a few minutes before washing.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The fixed 8-section wedge design means you can't get thinner slices for dehydrating or finger-food for toddlers. If you need adjustable thickness, this isn't your tool.

The hand-wash requirement is real. Several reviewers noted blade dulling after dishwasher cycles, despite the product description suggesting it's dishwasher-safe. I recommend hand-washing to extend blade life — it takes ten seconds.

One more thing: the blades are sharp. That's a pro for cutting, but a con if you have kids helping. There's no safety lock or guard. Store it with the blades facing down or in a utensil divider.

## Who Should Buy This

## FAQ

**Q: Will it core the apple completely?**
A: Yes, the center tube removes the entire core including seeds and the stem. The bottom stem might need a quick flick with your finger if it doesn't release automatically, but it doesn't end up in your slices.

**Q: Does it work on pears or other fruit?**
A: It works on firm pears, but softer fruits like peaches or very ripe plums will squish rather than cut cleanly. Stick to apples and firm pears.

**Q: How hard do you have to press?**
A: For standard apples, one firm press with both hands on the handles is sufficient. For large apples, you'll need to rock it slightly or use a second press.
