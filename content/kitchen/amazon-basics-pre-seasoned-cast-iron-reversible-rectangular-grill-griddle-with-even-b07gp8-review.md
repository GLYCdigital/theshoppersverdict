## Two pans in one, or a compromise on both?

The Amazon Basics Pre-Seasoned Cast Iron Reversible Rectangular Grill/Griddle is the budget answer to a question every home cook eventually asks: do I really need a separate grill pan *and* a flat griddle? At roughly half the price of Lodge's equivalent, it's tempting. But "cheaper" doesn't always mean "smarter." Here's the real story after digging through 220 ratings and the specs that matter.

## What you actually get

This is a 20-inch long, 10.5-inch wide rectangle of solid cast iron weighing about 20 pounds. The reversible design means one side has raised ridges for grilling (steak marks, chicken, veggies), and the flip side is flat — perfect for pancakes, eggs, or searing fish. It's pre-seasoned with vegetable oil, so it's technically ready to use out of the box. You get the pan itself and nothing else — no cover, no handle mitt, no scraper. Budget that in.

The size is genuinely useful. It spans two burners on most gas stoves, which means you can cook a full batch of bacon and eggs simultaneously. That's the selling point, and it delivers.

## Performance and build quality

Cast iron's superpower is heat retention, and this slab has it in spades. The 20-pound heft means no hot spots — you'll get even browning across the entire surface, provided you preheat it properly (5-7 minutes on medium, not high). On gas and electric coils, it's a workhorse. Induction works too, though the flat bottom sits flush.

The grill ridges are tall enough to leave genuine char marks and let fat drain away — a real advantage over flatter grill pans. The flat side sears beautifully once the factory seasoning gets a couple of coats of oil. Several reviewers noted that after two or three uses, eggs slide around like they're on Teflon.

The seasoning out of the box is functional but thin. Multiple buyers mentioned the first cook required extra oil and a watchful eye to avoid sticking. That's not a defect — it's cast iron reality — but worth knowing before you expect non-stick perfection on day one.

## What buyers consistently praise

The dual-function design is the runaway favorite. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I use the flat side for breakfast and the ridged side for dinner — it's replaced two pans I never use anymore."* Another noted, *"Preheats evenly and holds heat through a full batch of smash burgers. No warping after months of use."* The size gets consistent love from larger households — cooking for four on one surface is realistic.

## Where it falls short

The lack of a helper handle is the biggest practical complaint. At 20 pounds, lifting this thing one-handed from a hot stove is awkward and a little dangerous. You'll want a sturdy mitt and both hands on the short ends — but those ends get hot too. Plan your grip strategy before you start cooking.

The factory seasoning isn't Lodge-level. It's thinner, and the first few cooks will test your patience. If you're new to cast iron, expect some sticking. A quick oven-seasoning pass before first use eliminates most of this, but it's an extra step Amazon doesn't advertise.

Glass-top stove owners should think twice. The weight and flat bottom can scratch ceramic surfaces if you slide it, and the ridges on the grill side can trap debris that scuffs. It's doable with care, but it's not ideal.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're on a gas or electric coil stove, you cook for 2-4 people regularly, and you want one pan that does both flat-top griddling and grill-marked searing without spending Lodge money.

**Skip it if:** You have a glass-top stove, you're not comfortable managing cast iron's learning curve, or you need something lightweight enough to handle with one hand.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I put this in the dishwasher?**
No. Ever. Cast iron requires hand washing with minimal soap (or just hot water and a stiff brush), thorough drying, and a light oil coat. Dishwasher detergent strips seasoning and rusts the iron.

**Q: Does it work on induction cooktops?**
Yes. Cast iron is induction-compatible, and the flat bottom sits flush. Just be careful lifting — it's heavy and can scratch the glass surface if you slide it.

**Q: How do I clean the grill ridges without a special brush?**
A stiff nylon brush or a chainmail scrubber works. For stuck-on bits, deglaze with water while the pan is still warm — the steam lifts debris. Never soak it.
