If you've ever tried to make chocolate cookies that actually look like an Oreo — with that distinct waffle pattern and the cream center peeking out — you know it's nearly impossible freehand. This two-pack of silicone molds solves that problem directly. Each mold gives you 12 cavities with separate recesses for the chocolate wafer and the cream filling, so your finished pieces have real depth instead of looking like flat buttons.

**What's actually in the package**

You get two identical molds, each with 12 round cavities. The cavities are roughly 1.5 inches in diameter — slightly smaller than a real Oreo, but close enough that nobody will squint at your finished product. The silicone is food-grade and noticeably thicker than the flimsy molds you'll find at craft stores. It doesn't flop around when you pick it up, which matters when you're pouring melted chocolate.

The design has a clever detail worth calling out: the cream layer has its own slot that sits flush inside the chocolate base. You don't have to glue two halves together with extra melted chocolate — the pieces nest into each other. That's what makes the final result look like an actual sandwich cookie rather than a chocolate medallion with a white dot on top.

**How it performs in real use**

I tested these with melted dark chocolate and a homemade white chocolate filling. The key is letting the chocolate base set completely before adding the cream layer — roughly 15 minutes in the fridge. The cream slot is deep enough that you can overfill slightly and scrape the excess flat, which gives you that clean, factory-made edge.

Release is where these molds earn their keep. Because the silicone is flexible without being flimsy, you can push from the bottom and the cookie pops out in one piece. No twisting, no prying, no broken wafers. One reviewer noted, "I've bought three different brands of cookie molds and these are the only ones where the pattern actually shows up crisp on the finished chocolate." That matches my experience — the waffle texture transferred cleanly every time.

Cleanup is straightforward: warm water and a drop of dish soap, or you can toss them on the top rack of the dishwasher. They don't retain odors, which is a common issue with cheaper silicone.

The smaller cavity size is also worth flagging. If you were hoping to make full-size truffles or oversized cookies, these won't do it. They're perfect for standard bite-size pieces, but measure your expectations before you buy.

**FAQ**

*Do these work with white chocolate for the cream layer?*
Yes, but white chocolate is softer than dark. Let the base layer fully harden before adding the cream, and refrigerate the finished pieces until serving.

*Can I use these for soap or wax?*
The silicone will handle it, but food-grade molds are more expensive than craft-specific ones. If you're only doing soap, buy a cheaper mold.

*Are they dishwasher safe?*
Yes, top rack is fine. Hand washing is gentler and will extend the life of the mold.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want cookies that look professionally made without buying specialized equipment. The two-mold set, crisp pattern transfer, and easy release justify the price.

**Skip it if** you need larger cavities or want a project that comes with full instructions. This is a straightforward tool for bakers who already know their way around melted chocolate.
