Let's be clear about what this is: a 32-ounce borosilicate glass teapot designed for direct stovetop use. Not a kettle with a whistle. Not an electric unit. A teapot you put on the burner, which means you need to pay attention. That's the tradeoff baked into the design — and for many buyers, it's exactly why they bought it.

## What you actually get

The PARACITY arrives as a single-piece glass pot with a removable stainless steel infuser basket and a glass lid. The 32oz (950ml) capacity is printed clearly on the side — no guesswork. The infuser sits deep enough to get full leaf contact with the water, and it lifts out with a simple hook handle. That's it. No plastic parts, no rubber gaskets, no hidden components that'll degrade after six months.

The borosilicate glass is the real story here. It's the same material lab beakers are made from — low thermal expansion, which is what lets it survive the jump from a cold counter to a medium flame without shattering. Multiple reviewers confirmed they've used it on gas, electric coil, and ceramic cooktops without incident. One buyer put it this way: *"I've boiled water in this thing at least twice a week for five months. Not a crack, not a chip. The glass stays clear — no clouding."*

## Performance and real-world use

Boil time on a standard gas burner runs about 6–8 minutes for a full pot. That's slower than a metal kettle, but the visual payoff is significant — you watch the water roll from tiny bubbles to a proper boil, then drop your leaves and watch the color bloom. For loose-leaf drinkers who obsess over steep time, this transparency is genuinely useful.

The pour is where this teapot earns its keep. The spout is narrow enough to give you a controlled stream — no dribbling down the side of the pot, no sudden gushes. That matters when you're pouring 190°F water into a ceramic cup and don't want to wear it.

The removable infuser holds roughly 2 tablespoons of loose leaf comfortably. It's fine for a single steep of most teas, though if you're brewing something that expands dramatically (like jasmine pearls or rolled oolong), you'll want to leave the infuser out and strain after.

## What buyers consistently praise

The recurring theme in reviews is clarity and durability. Owners repeatedly mention how the glass stays pristine — no mineral staining, no tea residue clinging to the walls. That's not typical for glass teaware. The other consistent compliment is the heat distribution: no hot spots, no sudden boiling over, just even, controlled heating.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides, because they're real.

**No whistle, no automatic shutoff.** If you walk away while this is on the burner, you'll come back to steam hissing out of the spout and water evaporated down. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"I burned my hand reaching for the handle because I forgot it was hot. This isn't a set-it-and-forget-it appliance."* The handle stays warm — not scalding, but warm enough to be uncomfortable for a bare-handed pour. A mitt or trivet is non-negotiable.

**The lid has no lock.** It sits in place with a simple glass knob, but if you pour aggressively or the pot jostles, it can shift. Pour with one hand on the lid. Always.

**Thin glass at the spout.** A few buyers reported chips forming at the spout lip after months of use — typically from clinking against metal sinks or scrubbing too aggressively. Treat it gently and it'll last. Treat it like a cast iron skillet and you'll be shopping for a replacement.

## FAQ

**Can you use it on an induction cooktop?**
No. Borosilicate glass doesn't work with induction — the magnetic field has nothing to grab. Gas, electric coil, and ceramic glass cooktops are fine.

**Does the infuser rust?**
The basket is stainless steel, not coated. Multiple long-term reviewers report no rust after months of use, provided you dry it after washing. It's also dishwasher-safe, though hand-washing is gentler on the glass.

**How do you clean tea stains from the glass?**
Baking soda paste or a denture tablet with warm water. Avoid abrasive scrubbers — the glass is durable but the spout area is thinner. White vinegar works for hard water spots, but rinse thoroughly.
