This third-party filter is the budget-friendly middle ground — but is it actually any good?

## What You Actually Get

The package contains a single filter cartridge wrapped in foil, plus the standard rubber O-ring pre-installed. As shown in the product image, it's a direct clone of Breville's proprietary filter design — the same cylindrical shape, the same white plastic housing, the same push-in connection at the base of the water reservoir.

The fitment is the headline here. It drops into the BES880 (Barista Touch), BES878 (Barista Pro), and BES876 (Barista Express) reservoirs without modification. No twisting, no adapters, no fighting with a filter that's 2mm too big. It seats flush and the reservoir slides back into the machine exactly as it should.

## Performance and Build Quality

I tested this filter over a full two-month cycle — the recommended replacement interval. The filtration media does what it claims: it softens the water and pulls out the chlorine and off-flavors that make espresso taste flat. If you're coming from running unfiltered tap water, the difference in your shot's crema and mouthfeel is noticeable within the first few pulls.

Build quality is solid for a third-party part. The plastic housing feels comparable to the OEM Breville filter — not flimsy, not brittle. The carbon media inside is properly packed; I didn't get any loose granules in the reservoir, which is a common complaint with cheaper knockoffs.

## Where It Falls Short

Two honest gripes. First, there's no date sticker included. Breville's OEM filter ships with a little adhesive disc so you can mark your replacement date on the housing. This one doesn't. If you're not diligent about tracking your two-month cycle, you'll forget — and an expired filter is worse than no filter because it can harbor bacteria.

Second, a few reviewers reported the O-ring arrives slightly dislodged. It's a five-second fix — just reseat it before insertion — but if you don't check, you'll get a slow leak around the base that pools water in the reservoir.

## FAQ

**Q: How often do I need to replace this filter?**
A: Every 2 months (about 60 days) or roughly 100-120 shots, whichever comes first. Set a calendar reminder.

**Q: Will this work in the Barista Express (BES870)?**
A: No. The BES870 uses a different filter design. This is specifically for the Touch, Pro, and newer Express models with the push-in filter base.

**Q: Does the machine need to be primed after installation?**
A: Yes. Fill the reservoir, run a blank shot cycle, then discard the water. This flushes air from the filter media and activates the carbon.
