You don't need to spend $40 on a linen runner to get that airy, coastal-farmhouse look. At $8.99, the Socomi Pink Cheesecloth Table Runner is the kind of find that makes you wonder why you ever paid more. With 6,815 ratings holding a 4.8-star average, this isn't a niche product — it's a proven crowd-pleaser. Here's the honest breakdown after examining the specs and what real buyers report.

**What You Actually Get**

The runner measures 120 inches long — that's ten full feet — and arrives folded into a compact rectangle. The pink is a dusty, muted blush rather than bubblegum, which photographs beautifully and pairs with both warm wood tables and white marble. The cheesecloth weave is loose and open, giving it that textured, rustic boho character that's been dominating summer tablescapes.

**Fabric Quality and Real-World Performance**

Cheesecloth is cotton, and this is 100% cotton through and through. The weave is consistent — no thin spots or pulled threads — and the edges are finished with a simple hem that doesn't pucker. In hand, it feels substantial but not heavy, with that slight gauze-like transparency that defines true cheesecloth.

One buyer noted, "I was shocked at how soft this is right out of the package. No chemical smell, no stiffness." Another echoed, "The texture is exactly what I wanted — casual but intentional."

The real test is washing, and this is where the Socomi earns its keep. Multiple reviewers confirm it survives machine washing on cold without shrinking or fraying. The color stays true — no pink bleeding onto white tablecloths. The fabric softens noticeably after each cycle, which is a feature, not a bug.

**Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself**

This is a sheer fabric. If your table has stains, water rings, or a busy pattern, they'll show through. Several reviewers mentioned needing a solid tablecloth underneath. One put it plainly: "I love the look but I have to pair it with a white cloth or my wood table's scratches ruin the aesthetic."

Wrinkling is the other genuine annoyance. The cheesecloth creases easily, and if you forget it in the dryer, you'll spend ten minutes with an iron or steamer. Buyers who hang-dry report fewer wrinkles. This isn't a dealbreaker — it's just the nature of lightweight cotton.
