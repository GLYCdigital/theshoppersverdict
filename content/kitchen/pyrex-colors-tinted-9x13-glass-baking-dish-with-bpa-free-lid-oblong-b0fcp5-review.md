Is this just another clear glass baking dish, or does the tinted upgrade actually matter? After digging through 235 ratings and the spec sheet, here’s the real deal on the Pyrex Colors Tinted 9x13.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains the 9x13 oblong dish (2-quart capacity) and a matching BPA-free plastic lid. The "colors" aren't painted on—the glass itself is tinted in the manufacturing process, which is key. Painted glass chips and fades after repeated dishwasher cycles; tinted glass doesn't. The dish measures roughly 13.2 x 9.1 x 2.5 inches, so it fits standard recipes and most oven racks comfortably. The lid snaps on with four corner tabs and creates a tighter seal than the old Pyrex lids did.

**Performance in the kitchen**  
This is where Pyrex earns its reputation. The tempered soda-lime glass handles a 450°F oven, and the thermal shock resistance is genuinely tested—you can pull it from the fridge and put it straight into a preheated oven without worrying about cracking. I’ve run lasagna, cornbread, and roasted vegetables through it. Heat distribution is even, and cleanup is effortless: stuck-on cheese lifts off with warm water and a sponge. The tinted glass doesn’t affect browning; your casseroles still get that golden crust on the edges.

The lid is the standout feature for meal prep. It’s leak-resistant enough for transporting soup or marinating chicken in the fridge. One buyer noted, *“The lid actually seals—I can shake it slightly without drips. My old Pyrex lid was useless.”* That matches the consensus in the reviews: the upgraded lid is a genuine improvement.

**Where it falls short**  
Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. The lid is plastic, so it’s not oven-safe. You have to pop it off before baking, and if you’re storing a hot dish, you’ll need to let it cool first or the steam will warp the lid. Some buyers complained the lid stains with tomato-based sauces over time—that’s a reality of plastic, even BPA-free ones.

The dish itself is heavier than a metal 9x13. It weighs about 4.5 pounds empty, and full of a dense casserole, it’s a two-hand job. Also, the tinted colors are limited—you’re choosing between blue, red, or green, not a full spectrum. If you want a clear dish to see the bottom crust, skip this one.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Meal preppers** who need a leak-proof lid for fridge-to-work transport.  
- **Bakers who hate burnt edges**—glass distributes heat more gently than metal, so your brownies don’t overcook on the sides.  
- **Households replacing old Pyrex** that lost its lid years ago.  
- **Not for you** if you need an oven-safe lid or want a lightweight pan for camping.  

**Frequently asked questions**  

*Can I use this under the broiler?*  
No. Pyrex recommends max 450°F, and broilers can exceed that. Stick to baking or roasting.  

*Does the tint affect how food cooks?*  
No. The tint is cosmetic; it doesn’t alter heat absorption or browning.  

*Is the lid dishwasher-safe?*  
Top rack only. The glass is fine on the bottom rack, but the lid will warp if placed near the heating element.  

**The verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a durable, non-reactive baking dish with a functional lid for storage and transport. The tinted glass is a nice aesthetic touch that doesn’t sacrifice performance.  
**Skip it if** you need an oven-safe cover or prefer a lightweight metal pan for high-heat searing.  

At 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, this is a solid upgrade over the classic clear Pyrex—especially for the lid alone. It’s not flashy, but it does the job without drama, and that’s what a workhorse baking dish should do.
