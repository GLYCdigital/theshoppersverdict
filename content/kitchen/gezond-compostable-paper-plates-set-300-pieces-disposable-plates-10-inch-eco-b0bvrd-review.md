## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides exist. First, the natural brown look is utilitarian — if you're hosting a dinner party where presentation matters, these will look like you raided the recycling bin. They're fine for everyday meals, cookouts, and kids' parties, but not for impressing guests.

Second, these are not ideal for extended microwave use. While they're technically microwave-safe, hot oily foods can cause staining or slight softening if left for several minutes. Heat food, then serve — don't cook directly on them.

Finally, the compostability claim requires the right conditions. In a backyard compost pile that's actively managed, they'll break down. In a landfill, they won't decompose any faster than anything else. If you're buying these for landfill disposal, you're paying a premium for a benefit you're not actually getting.

## FAQ

**Are these really compostable, or is that greenwashing?**
Yes, genuinely. They're made from bagasse (sugarcane pulp) with no plastic lining. In a proper compost environment with heat and moisture, they break down in 60-90 days. They've earned BPI certification, which requires third-party testing.

**Can I use these for hot foods like soup or chili?**
Yes, but with a caveat. The depth helps hold liquid, and they won't leak through. However, extremely hot liquids over a long period can soften the fiber. For soup, use the bowl version of these, not the flat plate.

**Will these hold up to a steak knife?**
Reasonable use, yes. The fiber is dense enough to resist scratching from cutting meat. But they're not cutting boards — heavy sawing motion will eventually cut through.
