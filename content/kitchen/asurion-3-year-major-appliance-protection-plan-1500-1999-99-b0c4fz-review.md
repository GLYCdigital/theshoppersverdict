# ASURION 3-Year Appliance Protection Plan: Read This Before You Click "Add to Cart"

You just dropped $1,700 on a refrigerator. The checkout page offers you a 3-year protection plan for roughly the cost of a nice dinner out. Tempting, right? But here's the uncomfortable truth: this ASURION plan has a 2.8-star rating from 150+ verified buyers. That's not a fluke. Let's dig into why.

## What You're Actually Buying

This specific ASURION plan covers major appliances priced between $1,500 and $1,999.99. The "contract" (that's what it is — an insurance policy, not a product) extends your coverage to 3 years total from the original purchase date. It kicks in after the manufacturer's warranty expires.

What it covers: mechanical and electrical failures, parts and labor, and — notably — **replacement cost up to your purchase price**, not depreciated value. That's genuinely better than what most store-brand warranties offer.

What it doesn't cover, per the fine print: cosmetic damage (dents, scratches), pre-existing issues, improper installation, and "normal wear and tear." That last one is doing a lot of heavy lifting in their denial letters.

## The Real-World Experience

Here's the pattern that emerges from buyer feedback. The plan is easy to buy. Filing a claim is where things get rocky.

Multiple verified purchasers report the same sequence: call the claims line, wait on hold, get a repair appointment scheduled for 1–3 weeks out, then have the technician arrive and declare the part needs ordering. That part takes another week or two. If you're waiting on a dead refrigerator in July, that's not a theoretical inconvenience — that's spoiled food and a very unhappy household.

One buyer put it bluntly: *"Took 3 weeks and 4 phone calls to get someone to even look at my washer. When the tech finally came, he said the part would take another 10 days. I could have bought a new machine in that time."*

To be fair, not every experience is negative. Some buyers report smooth claims and quick reimbursement. But when you're rolling the dice, you need to know the odds — and at 2.8 stars, the odds aren't in your favor.

## Where ASURION Actually Delivers

The replacement-cost guarantee is the standout feature. If your $1,899 dishwasher dies irreparably in year two, ASURION cuts you a check for what you paid — not what a used one is worth. That's rare and valuable.

The 24/7 claims line is also legitimately useful. If your oven dies on a Sunday night, you can at least start the process immediately rather than waiting for business hours.

## The Honest Downsides

Beyond the claims delays, there are structural issues worth flagging.

**You're paying for coverage you might never use.** The average major appliance lasts 10–15 years. Most failures happen either in the first year (covered by the manufacturer) or after year 8. The 3-year window this plan covers (years 2–4) is statistically the most reliable period of an appliance's life.

**The denial language is broad.** "Improper installation" is a subjective call. If your installer didn't follow the manual to the letter, ASURION can — and reportedly does — use that to deny claims.

**No refunds for unused coverage.** Unlike some competitors that prorate refunds if you sell the appliance, this is a sunk cost.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're buying a high-end appliance with complex electronics (think smart refrigerators or induction ranges) where repair costs can easily exceed $500. The replacement-cost guarantee makes this a reasonable bet if you plan to keep the appliance for the full 3 years and you're buying from a brand with a shaky reliability record.

**Skip it if:** You're buying a basic, proven model (a standard top-load washer, a simple gas range). The likelihood of a major failure in years 2–4 is low enough that you're probably better off self-insuring — putting that $200 in a savings account for future repairs.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I transfer this plan if I sell my appliance?**
A: No. The plan is tied to the original purchaser and the original appliance. If you sell the appliance, the coverage effectively ends.

**Q: How do I file a claim?**
A: Call the 24/7 claims line. You'll need your order number and the appliance's model/serial number. Be prepared for hold times, and document everything in writing.

**Q: Does this cover labor costs for repairs?**
A: Yes, parts and labor are covered after you pay the service fee (which is $0 for this plan — no per-visit deductible). Just be aware that "covered" doesn't mean "fast."

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.3/5**

The ASURION plan offers genuinely strong coverage terms — full replacement cost, no deductibles, 3 full years. That's the good news.

The bad news is execution. A protection plan is only as good as its claims process, and the consistent complaints about slow response times, scheduling delays, and broad denial language are hard to ignore. You're not just buying coverage; you're buying a customer service experience, and that experience is rated at 2.8 stars.

**Buy it if** you're purchasing a complex, expensive appliance from a brand with reliability question marks, and you value the replacement-cost guarantee over a smooth claims process.

**Skip it if** you're buying a mainstream appliance from a reputable brand, you're comfortable with minor DIY repairs, or you'd rather keep that $200 for something you'll definitely use.

The math is simple: this plan costs about 10% of your appliance's value. If you're the kind of person who keeps appliances for the long haul, that's a steep insurance premium for a 3-year window. If you're risk-averse and want peace of mind, it's a calculated bet — just know the payout might take a while to arrive.
