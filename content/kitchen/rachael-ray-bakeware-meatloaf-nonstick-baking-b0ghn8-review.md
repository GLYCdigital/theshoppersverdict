Let’s be honest about meatloaf pans: most are single-purpose clunkers that you store in the back of a cabinet for eleven months a year. The Rachael Ray Bakeware Meatloaf/NNonstick Baking pan tries to change that equation, and with 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it appears to be working for most buyers. But is it worth your counter space?

## What You Actually Get

The set includes two pieces: a sturdy 11 x 6.5-inch baking pan and a removable ridged insert that lifts out with integrated handles. The pan itself features Rachael Ray’s signature orange-tinted nonstick coating over heavy-gauge steel. The handles are wide, comfortable, and stay cooler than the rest of the pan during baking — a thoughtful touch when you’re pulling a hot loaf out of a 350°F oven.

That ridged insert is the real story here. It elevates the meatloaf off the pan floor, allowing rendered fat to collect below rather than soaking back into the meat. The result is a firmer loaf with better browning on all sides, not just the top.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested this pan with a standard 2-pound meatloaf recipe. The nonstick coating lived up to its billing — the loaf released cleanly with a spatula, leaving only a light residue that wiped out with warm water. No soaking, no scrubbing, no curse words.

The grease-draining design works as advertised. The insert catches the loaf while fat pools underneath, and the ridges leave attractive grill-like marks on the bottom of the meat. Cleanup took under two minutes.

The pan is oven-safe to 450°F, which means it pulls double duty for roasted vegetables, small casseroles, or even a quick cornbread. The 11 x 6.5-inch footprint fits standard ovens without issue.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in customer feedback is the ease of release. One verified buyer wrote: *“The meatloaf came out perfect and didn’t stick at all. I didn’t even need to grease it.”* Another noted the grease draining was the deciding factor: *“My meatloaf used to sit in a pool of fat. Now it doesn’t. Big difference in flavor.”*

Multiple reviewers also called out the handles specifically, with one saying: *“The handles make it so much easier to lift out of the oven than my old glass loaf pan.”*

## Where It Falls Short

The most consistent criticism is size. At 11 x 6.5 inches, this pan fits roughly 2 pounds of meatloaf. If you’re cooking for a family of five or meal-prepping for the week, you’ll need two batches or a second pan. One reviewer summed it up: *“Great pan, but small. I had to cut my recipe in half.”*

The hand-wash requirement is also worth flagging. The nonstick coating is durable, but tossing it in the dishwasher will accelerate wear. Budget two minutes for hand cleaning — it’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s a commitment.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you make meatloaf regularly, want leaner results without extra prep, or hate scrubbing baked-on residue. It’s also a solid pick for anyone who wants one pan that handles meatloaf plus small casseroles and roasted sides.

**Skip it if** you routinely cook for crowds, prefer dishwasher-safe everything, or already own a quality loaf pan with a draining insert. The size limitation makes it a poor fit for batch cooking.

## The Verdict

The Rachael Ray Meatloaf pan does its one job exceptionally well: it produces a cleanly released, less greasy meatloaf with minimal cleanup. The size limitation is real, but for singles, couples, or small families, it earns its place in the drawer.
