**The 5-in-1 coffee maker that tries to do everything — and mostly pulls it off**

Most "multi-function" coffee makers are really just a drip machine with a single-serve attachment bolted on. The Hamilton Beach Flexbrew Advanced 5-in-1 is different. It genuinely attempts five distinct brewing methods: traditional drip, single-serve grounds, K-Cup pods, iced coffee, and cold brew. That's ambitious for a machine at this price point, and the 3.6-star average rating tells you it's not flawless. But for the right buyer, this could be the only coffee maker you need.

**What you actually get**

Out of the box: the main unit, a 12-cup glass carafe, a single-serve cup adapter, a permanent mesh filter basket, and a scoop. The footprint is substantial — roughly 13 inches deep and 11 inches wide — so measure your counter space before committing. The water reservoir holds 48 ounces, which is enough for a full carafe or roughly four single cups before refilling.

The control panel is straightforward: a digital display, program timer, strength selector (bold or regular), and a dedicated mode button cycling through the five brewing options. Setup takes about five minutes, but you'll want to run a full cleaning cycle with vinegar before first use — the machine ships with manufacturing residue.

**Performance and real-world use**

Drip coffee is the core function, and it performs respectably. A full carafe brews in about 10 minutes, and the bold setting genuinely produces a stronger cup — not just slower dripping, but a noticeably more extracted flavor. The permanent filter is fine-mesh and doesn't let silt through, which is a common complaint with cheaper reusable filters.

The single-serve mode is where this machine gets interesting. You can brew directly into a mug using either ground coffee in the included cup or a K-Cup pod. The water temperature hits the right range for proper extraction — around 195°F — which is more than many pod-only machines manage. It's not as fast as a dedicated Keurig (about 3 minutes vs. 1), but it's acceptable.

Iced coffee mode brews a concentrated shot directly over ice, which actually works — the ice melts into the concentrate rather than diluting weak coffee. Cold brew, however, is the weak link. It takes 25-35 minutes for a single serving of cold concentrate. That's faster than traditional 12-hour cold brew, but it's not "convenient." Plan ahead or use it as a weekend treat.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The versatility is the standout. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"I replaced three appliances with this one machine. Drip for the family, single cup for me, and cold brew on weekends."* Multiple buyers echo that the ability to brew a full carafe and a single cup without changing anything is the main reason they bought it.

The strength control also gets consistent positive mentions. As one verified purchaser noted: *"The bold setting actually makes a difference. My wife likes regular, I like bold — we just flip the button."*

**Where it falls short**

The 3.6-star rating isn't for nothing. The most common complaint is leaking. Several reviewers report water pooling around the base after brewing, and a few mention the reservoir seal failing within months. If you get a unit with this defect, it's annoying — not dangerous, but you'll be wiping down your counter every morning.

The carafe lid is also poorly designed. It doesn't snap on securely, so heat escapes faster than it should, and pouring can cause drips down the side of the carafe. It's a minor annoyance, but for a machine this size, you expect better build quality.

Finally, the cold brew mode's 25+ minute cycle is a real drawback. If you want cold coffee on demand, this isn't the machine for you — you'd be better served by a dedicated cold brew maker or just brewing strong drip and refrigerating it.

**Who should buy this**

This is for households with mixed coffee preferences. If you have a drip drinker and a pod user under one roof, this replaces two machines. It's also good for occasional cold coffee drinkers who don't want a separate cold brew system taking up cabinet space. If you're a purist who only drinks pour-over or espresso, skip it — this machine does many things adequately, not one thing perfectly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it use K-Cup pods or just ground coffee?**
A: Both. The single-serve adapter accepts K-Cup pods, and there's a separate mesh cup for ground coffee. You don't need an additional filter basket for pods.

**Q: How long does the cold brew actually take?**
A: The cycle runs 25-35 minutes depending on the amount of water and strength setting. It produces a concentrate you dilute with water or milk, so one serving stretches to about two drinks.

**Q: Can I brew a full carafe and a single cup at the same time?**
A: No. The machine is single-task — you select one mode and brew. The advantage is switching modes without changing filters or components, not running both simultaneously.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want one machine that handles drip, pods, and occasional cold coffee without cluttering your counter with three appliances. The versatility is genuinely useful, and the drip quality is above average for this class.

**Skip it if** you're a daily cold brew drinker, you're sensitive to potential leaking defects, or you want a machine that will last a decade without issues. The Flexbrew Advanced is a capable workhorse, but it's not built like a tank.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A genuinely versatile coffee maker with a few quality-control headaches. Worth it for the right household, frustrating for others.
