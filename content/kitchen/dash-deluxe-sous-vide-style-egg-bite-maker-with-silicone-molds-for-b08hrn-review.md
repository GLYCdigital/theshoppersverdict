# Dash Deluxe Sous Vide Style Egg Bite Maker Review: Better Than Starbucks?

You know those $5 sous vide egg bites you grab at the drive-thru? The Dash Deluxe claims to replicate them at home for pennies. After 200 Amazon ratings averaging 4.0 stars, the consensus is clear: it mostly delivers — but with a few caveats worth knowing before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

The Dash Deluxe is a countertop egg bite cooker with a 7-egg capacity. In the box: the base unit, a clear lid, and two silicone molds — each holds 3-4 egg bite cavities. The silicone molds are the star here. Unlike cheaper non-stick trays, these pop eggs out with a simple flex, no butter or spray required. The unit measures roughly 8 x 8 x 7 inches, small enough to tuck beside a toaster, and the cord wraps into the base for storage.

## How It Performs

The cooking process is straightforward: whisk eggs with your mix-ins, pour into molds, add water to the heating tray, close the lid, and wait about 20 minutes. The result is a custard-like egg bite with a smooth, velvety texture — noticeably softer than a scrambled egg muffin but slightly denser than the Starbucks original. One buyer noted, *"The texture is close enough that I stopped buying them out. I add bacon and cheddar, and they're 90% as good at a fraction of the cost."*

The 7-egg capacity is genuinely useful. That's a full week of breakfast meal prep in one cycle. I've also seen buyers use them for protein-packed snacks or even mini crustless quiches for a crowd.

## What Buyers Praise

The cleanup wins consistently. The silicone molds are dishwasher-safe, and because eggs slide out cleanly, most buyers report zero scrubbing. One reviewer summed it up: *"I was skeptical about another single-use appliance, but honestly the cleanup is faster than making scrambled eggs."* The speed is also a recurring theme — from counter to finished bites in under 25 minutes, hands-off.

## Where It Falls Short

**The heat issue is real.** The exterior gets warm — not burn-your-hand hot, but warm enough that you shouldn't leave it within reach of toddlers. Multiple reviewers mention this. It's a design tradeoff for the compact size.

**The "sous vide style" is marketing.** This is a steam cooker, not a true sous vide circulator. You won't get the exact same texture as a professional immersion cooker. It's close, but if you're a texture purist, adjust expectations.

**No timer, no auto-off.** You must remember to unplug it. If you walk away, you'll get rubbery eggs. This is the most common complaint in critical reviews, and it's a fair one for the price point.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Meal preppers** who want a week of protein-heavy breakfasts in one cycle
- **Starbucks egg bite addicts** looking to cut a $5/day habit
- **Keto or low-carb eaters** who want a quick, portable protein snack
- **Small households** — 7 bites is perfect for 1-2 people

**Skip it if** you want true sous vide precision, need something that operates unattended, or have a family larger than 3 people (you'd be running back-to-back batches).

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use liquid egg whites instead of whole eggs?**
Yes, works perfectly. Just don't overfill the molds — leave 1/4 inch of headroom for expansion.

**Q: Are the silicone molds BPA-free?**
Yes, Dash specifies BPA-free silicone. They're also dishwasher-safe top rack.

**Q: Can I make sweet versions, like vanilla or cinnamon?**
Absolutely. The base recipe adapts well to sweet mix-ins like berries or a dash of maple syrup. Just keep total liquid volume consistent.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a fast, low-mess way to make protein-packed egg bites at home and don't mind remembering to unplug it.

**Skip it if** you need true sous vide precision, want set-and-forget operation, or already own a multi-cooker that does the same job.

At 4.2/5, the Dash Deluxe is a solid value for its niche. It won't replace a full kitchen setup, but for egg bite lovers, it's a worthwhile countertop addition that pays for itself in about 10 drive-thru visits.
