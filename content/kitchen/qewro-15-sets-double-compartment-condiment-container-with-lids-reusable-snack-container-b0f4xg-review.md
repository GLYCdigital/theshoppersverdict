**Qewro 15 Sets Double Compartment Condiment Container With Lids Review: Small Containers, Big Convenience**

You know the drill: you pack a salad, throw in a plastic baggie of dressing, and by lunch it’s a soggy mess. The Qewro 15-set double compartment containers aim to kill that problem — and for most buyers, they largely succeed. With a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, these tiny 2-ounce cups have become a lunchbox staple. But they’re not perfect. Here’s the real breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

Fifteen individual containers, each with a snap-on lid that splits the interior into two equal compartments. The plastic is BPA-free, translucent, and feels thicker than the cheap deli cups you’re used to. Each side holds roughly 1 ounce — about two tablespoons. That’s enough for a single salad dressing serving, a portion of hummus, or a kid’s snack of berries and yogurt. The lids have a raised center ridge that acts as a grip point, which helps when prying them open mid-meal.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The headline feature is the dual-compartment design. It genuinely works. One side for ranch dressing, the other for croutons — no soggy lettuce. The divider is molded into the container, not a flimsy insert, so there’s no cross-contamination risk. The plastic is durable; I’ve run mine through the dishwasher top rack at least ten times with zero warping or clouding. They also microwave safely in 30-second bursts, though I’d recommend removing the lid first.

The sealing mechanism is where opinion splits. The lids snap on with a firm click, and when properly pressed down, they’re leak-resistant. I tested them with runny Italian dressing in a sideways position — no drips. But you *must* press the center and all four edges. Several Amazon reviewers noted the same: “Great product, but you need to push hard on the lid or you’ll find dressing in your bag.” That’s accurate. It’s not a fatal flaw, but it’s a learning curve.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The most common praise revolves around the two-in-one utility. One reviewer wrote, “Perfect for my kid’s lunch — no more separate containers for applesauce and crackers.” Another highlighted the value: “15 sets for this price is a steal. I’ve paid more for four.” The stackability is also a recurring compliment; the flat tops and rounded bottoms nest neatly, keeping your pantry drawer organized.

**Where It Falls Short**

The capacity is the main tradeoff. Two tablespoons per side is small. If you’re meal-prepping full portions of dressing for a week of salads, you’ll need to refill these daily. They’re snack-sized, not meal-sized. Also, the lids aren’t airtight enough for long-term storage of wet ingredients — I wouldn’t trust them for a week-old homemade salsa. The plastic, while sturdy, can stain with turmeric or tomato-based sauces, though a soak in warm soapy water handles it.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Parents packing school lunches** — the portion control is ideal for kids, and the leak-resistant seal survives a backpack toss.
- **Meal preppers who hate soggy salads** — keep dressing separate until the last second.
- **Travelers** — TSA-friendly at 2 ounces total, so you can bring your own hot sauce or vinaigrette on a plane.
- **Not for you** if you need large portions or want a single container that holds more than a few tablespoons.

**FAQ**

*Are these really leakproof?*  
They’re leak-resistant when the lid is fully snapped down. I’d trust them in a lunchbox upright, but not in a checked suitcase for hours. Press firmly on all edges before closing.

*Can they go in the microwave?*  
Yes, but remove the lid and heat in short bursts. The plastic is microwave-safe, but the lid can warp if exposed to high heat for long periods.

*Are they truly dishwasher safe?*  
Top rack only. The heat of the bottom rack can distort the lids over time. Hand washing is safer if you want them to last.

For the price, these are a solid upgrade over single-use plastic baggies.
