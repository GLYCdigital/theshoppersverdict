Let me cut through the listing photos and give you the real story on the GASLAND 5 Burner. With 215 ratings averaging 4.3 stars, it's clearly doing something right — but the reversible cast iron grill/griddle is the headline feature, and whether it's worth your kitchen remodel depends on how you actually cook.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting the cooktop unit itself (30-inch drop-in), the reversible cast iron grill plate that spans two burners, four standard grates, and the necessary mounting hardware. The grill plate is the star — one side has raised ridges for searing steaks, the flip side is a flat griddle for pancakes or smash burgers. It's heavy, and I mean *properly* heavy, which is what you want from cast iron. The unit is natural gas compatible out of the box, but GASLAND includes LP conversion nozzles if you're on propane.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The layout is practical: five burners across a 30-inch footprint. The center-left burner cranks out 15,000 BTU, which is genuinely enough for a screaming-hot sear on a cast iron skillet. The other four range from 5,000 to 9,500 BTU — nothing extreme, but adequate for simmering sauces and boiling pasta water simultaneously.

Here's where the reversible plate earns its keep. Slide it over the two left burners and you've got a 12x20-inch cooking surface. The griddle side handles a dozen pancakes or eight eggs without crowding. Flip it and the grill ridges give you those char lines on chicken thighs — though don't expect outdoor grill flavor without smoke. You'll want your range hood on max.

The 110V ignition is a small quality-of-life win. No fumbling for a lighter or replacing AA batteries every few months. Push the knob, it lights. Simple.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the griddle's versatility. One buyer noted, *"I use the flat side for breakfast every single morning — eggs, bacon, hash browns all at once. The cast iron holds heat so much better than my old griddle pan."* Another mentioned the build quality: *"Surprisingly solid for the price. The grates feel thick and the knobs have a satisfying click."* Multiple reviewers mention how evenly the griddle side heats across the full surface, which is rare at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

First, the cast iron plate needs seasoning before first use. It ships with a protective coating that will make your food taste metallic if you skip the oil-and-heat ritual. Some buyers missed this and left frustrated reviews — read the manual, spend the 20 minutes, and it's fine.

Second, when the grill plate is in place, those two burners are covered. You can't use a stockpot on that zone simultaneously. It's a tradeoff, not a flaw, but worth knowing if you regularly run three pots at once.

Third, installation is not trivial. It's a drop-in unit needing a 30-inch cabinet cutout. If you're swapping out an existing cooktop, measure twice. A few reviews mention the cutout dimensions being slightly off from their old unit, requiring minor cabinet adjustments.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a breakfast-for-dinner household, love smash burgers at home, or want a single appliance that replaces both a griddle pan and a grill pan cluttering your cabinets. **Skip it if** you rarely use griddles and just need straightforward burners — you'll pay extra for a feature you won't touch, and there are cheaper 5-burner options without the cast iron plate.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the grill plate fit over the high-BTU burner?**
A: No — it covers the two left burners (5,000 and 9,500 BTU). The 15,000 BTU burner stays exposed for searing with your own skillet.

**Q: Can I use it with propane?**
A: Yes. LP conversion nozzles are included, but you'll need a wrench and about 15 minutes per burner. If you're not comfortable, hire a pro — gas is gas.

**Q: Is the cast iron plate dishwasher safe?**
A: Absolutely not. Hand wash, dry immediately, and re-oil lightly after each use. Treat it like a cast iron skillet, because that's exactly what it is.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The GASLAND 5 Burner delivers genuine versatility at a competitive price. It's not perfect — the seasoning requirement trips up careless owners, and the covered burners during griddle use is a genuine limitation. But if you'll actually use both sides of that plate, this cooktop earns its counter space.

**Buy it if** you want grill marks and flat-top cooking without buying a separate appliance. **Skip it if** you're a minimalist cook who just needs five reliable burners — simpler options exist for less.
