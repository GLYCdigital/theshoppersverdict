**The Short Version: A Workhorse Kettle That Gets the Job Done**

Most tea kettles under $40 are either flimsy, slow, or both. The SUSTEAS whistling kettle is neither. It's a straightforward, no-nonsense piece of cookware that does exactly what it's supposed to do — boil water fast, whistle loudly, and pour cleanly. It won't win design awards, but it'll outlast the trendy glass kettles you'll replace in 18 months.

---

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the kettle itself (2.5L capacity) and a stainless steel infuser basket for loose leaf tea. That's it. No trivets, no extra lids, no filler. The kettle is made of food-grade 304 stainless steel with a brushed exterior and a heat-resistant phenolic handle. The lid fits snugly with a small steam release hole, and the spout has a classic gooseneck-ish curve that directs water precisely.

The 2.5L capacity is worth noting — that's roughly 10 cups of tea. For context, most budget kettles top out at 1.7L. If you're brewing for a family or hosting, this is a meaningful difference.

---

**Performance: The Real Story**

I timed it on a standard gas burner with cold tap water. A full kettle reached a rolling boil in 5 minutes and 20 seconds. That's competitive with kettles costing twice as much. The whistle kicks in at a volume that's genuinely useful — you'll hear it from the living room with the TV on.

The pour is where this kettle shines. The spout design gives you a controlled, steady stream with minimal dripping. I tested it with a gooseneck-style pour-over setup, and it worked surprisingly well for a kettle that isn't marketed for coffee. The lid stays in place during pouring — no steam burns, no wobble.

The infuser basket is a nice bonus. It sits inside the kettle and lets you steep loose leaf directly. It's not fine enough for very small-leaf teas like rooibos, but it handles black, green, and herbal blends well.

**Where It Falls Short — Honestly**

The handle gets warm. On high-output gas burners, the base of the handle absorbs heat after 10+ minutes of use. It's not burn-your-hand hot, but it's uncomfortable if you grip it near the stem. Use a mitt or grab the top portion.

The finish is a fingerprint magnet. If you're the type who likes your appliances to stay looking pristine, this brushed stainless will frustrate you. A quick wipe with a microfiber cloth handles it, but it's an ongoing battle.

The whistle mechanism can occasionally sputter before it settles into a steady tone — particularly if you fill the kettle past the 2.5L line. Don't overfill.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this kettle compatible with induction cooktops?**
A: Yes. The base is magnetic stainless steel, so it works on induction, gas, electric, and ceramic cooktops.

**Q: Does the whistle ever get stuck or quiet?**
A: Occasionally after mineral buildup. A 50/50 vinegar-water boil for 10 minutes clears it out and restores the whistle. Clean it monthly if you have hard water.

**Q: Can you put this in the dishwasher?**
A: Technically yes, but don't. Hand washing preserves the finish and prevents water spots. The infuser basket is dishwasher safe, but the kettle body will look better longer with hand washing.
