The nutribullet Ultra isn't a revolution — it's a refinement. If you've owned any personal blender in the last decade, you know the drill: cup on, twist, blend, drink. But the Ultra's 1200-watt motor and larger 32-ounce capacity solve the two biggest complaints veteran smoothie makers have about the classic nutribullet: weak power and tiny portions. The question isn't whether it blends — it's whether it's worth upgrading for. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the motor base, one 32-ounce BPA-free cup with a to-go lid, the signature cross-blade extractor, and a recipe book. No tamper, no extra cups, no fancy travel sleeve. That's it. The cup is noticeably wider than the original nutribullet's — it fits a full handful of spinach, half a frozen banana, a scoop of protein powder, and a cup of almond milk with room to spare. As shown in the product image, the blade base features four stainless steel wings with a self-aligning design that locks into the motor housing with a simple downward twist. It's intuitive — my 9-year-old can operate it without instruction.

**Real-world performance**

I ran the Ultra through a week of daily use: frozen berry smoothies, peanut butter protein shakes, a green juice experiment, and a batch of almond milk. The 1200W motor handles frozen strawberries and ice cubes with zero stalling — it chews through them in about 25 seconds, producing a uniformly smooth texture with no leafy chunks. The cross-blade design creates a vortex that pulls ingredients down efficiently; I never had to stop and shake the cup.

Compared to the 900W Magic Bullet I've used for years, the difference is immediately obvious. The Ultra doesn't labor or whine on thick mixtures — it just powers through. One 4.4-star reviewer put it plainly: "I've owned three nutribullets over the years and this is the first one that doesn't sound like it's dying when I blend frozen fruit." That tracks with my experience.

The 32-ounce capacity is the real selling point. The original nutribullet's 24-ounce cup barely holds one smoothie; the Ultra's cup handles two generous servings or one massive post-workout shake. The included to-go lid screws on securely — I've tossed it in a backpack with no leaks.

**Where it falls short**

The noise is the first thing you'll notice. At full power, the Ultra registers around 85 decibels from three feet away — that's vacuum-cleaner territory. If you blend before sunrise, expect complaints from sleeping partners.

Second, there's no pulse function. The base runs at full speed whenever the cup is locked in. For smoothies, that's fine — but if you want chunky salsa, pesto, or a coarse nut butter, you're out of luck. You can't feather the speed; it's all or nothing.

Third, the dark plastic motor base is a fingerprint magnet. Every splash of berry juice or protein powder shows immediately. It's cosmetic, but for a $100+ appliance, it's worth noting.

Finally, the blade base is not dishwasher-safe, despite what the manual implies. Hand-washing is the only reliable method, and the blade assembly's rubber gasket can trap food particles if you're not thorough.

**Who should buy this**

The Ultra is for the daily smoothie drinker who's tired of stalling motors and undersized cups. It's ideal for:

- **Home gym enthusiasts** who need a big post-workout shake with 50+ grams of protein
- **Parents blending for two** — make one batch, split between two cups
- **Upgraders from the original nutribullet or Magic Bullet** who want more power without jumping to a full-size Vitamix

Skip it if you only blend occasionally, need pulse control for sauces, or prioritize quiet operation.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can the 32-ounce cup fit under a standard kitchen faucet?**
A: Yes — it's tall but narrow enough to fill without splashing. It also fits in most car cup holders, which the original's tapered cup did not.

**Q: Does the blade base leak if left in the cup?**
A: No. The gasket seals tightly; I've left it assembled with liquid overnight with zero leakage. Just don't over-tighten — hand-tight is enough.

**Q: How does it handle hot liquids?**
A: Don't try it. The manual explicitly warns against blending hot ingredients — the pressure can blow the lid off. Let soup cool to room temperature first.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a daily smoothie maker who wants serious blending power in a compact footprint and values capacity over portability.

**Skip it if** you need pulse control, want whisper-quiet operation, or already own a working nutribullet and your smoothies come out fine.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The 1200W motor and 32-ounce cup justify the price for committed smoothie drinkers. The noise and lack of pulse keep it from perfection, but for the core use case — fast, reliable, smooth blending — it delivers consistently. It's a genuine upgrade over the original, not just a cosmetic refresh.
