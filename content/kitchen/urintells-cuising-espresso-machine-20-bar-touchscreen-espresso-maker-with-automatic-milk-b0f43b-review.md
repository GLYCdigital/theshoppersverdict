Let's cut through the noise: a 20-bar espresso machine with a touchscreen at this price point usually means one of two things — either a rebadged pump machine with a fancy face, or a genuine step up from the $99 starter units. The URintells Cuising lands somewhere between, and after digging through 204 ratings and the spec sheet, here's what actually matters.

**What's in the box:** The machine itself (roughly 13" tall, 11" deep — compact enough for tight counters), a 1.5L removable water tank, a 58mm portafilter (the pro-standard size, which is a big deal for replacement parts), single and double baskets, a tamper, and the automatic milk frother wand. No grinder — you'll need pre-ground coffee or a separate burr grinder. That's expected at this tier, but worth stating plainly.

**Real-world performance:** The 20-bar Italian pump is the headline spec, and it mostly delivers. Extraction pressure peaks around 15 bars during pre-infusion and settles into the 9-bar sweet spot for the actual shot — that's how the good machines work. Users consistently report thick crema and proper body on medium-to-dark roasts. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Pulls shots as good as my friend's $600 Breville — I genuinely wasn't expecting that."* The thermoblock heats up in about 45 seconds, which beats most pump machines in this class.

The touchscreen is the differentiator here. It's responsive, not laggy, and the 8 presets (espresso, Americano, latte, cappuccino, etc.) actually adjust brew time and temperature per drink. The auto milk frother is a wand-style, not a one-touch jug — you submerge it in your milk pitcher and it steams to your chosen texture. It produces microfoam that's noticeably better than the cheap auto-frothers on sub-$200 machines.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The crema quality, the intuitive touchscreen, and the milk frother's consistency. Multiple reviews mention how easy it is to hand to a guest who's never used the machine — the presets remove the learning curve. Heat-up speed gets called out too.

**Where it falls short:** The 1.5L water tank is genuinely small. If you're making two lattes a day, you're refilling every other day. The drip tray is shallow — there's a removable cup platform that you'll need to take off for standard 12oz mugs, and even then, the clearance is tight. And there's no pressure gauge or shot timer. That's fine for beginners, but if you're chasing precise espresso extraction, you'll be guessing.

**Who should buy this:** 
- **The upgrade buyer** — moving from a $79 starter machine and wants real crema without jumping to the $500+ tier
- **The convenience-focused home barista** — wants one-touch drinks for the whole family, not manual lever control
- **The counter-space-conscious** — this footprint is genuinely compact for what it does

**Skip it if:** You're a hobbyist who needs shot timers and pressure profiling, or you entertain heavily and need a bigger reservoir.

**FAQ:**

*Does it work with ground coffee or only pods?* Ground coffee only. The 58mm portafilter takes standard espresso grounds — no pods, no capsules. That's a pro for freshness, a con if you wanted capsule convenience.

*How loud is it during operation?* The pump is audible but not obnoxious — comparable to a mid-range drip coffee maker. The steam wand is quieter than most.

*Can you make a real cappuccino with the auto frother?* Yes, but you'll want a separate milk pitcher. The wand steams and textures simultaneously, producing foam dense enough to hold latte art — a handful of reviewers confirmed this.

**The Verdict:** The URintells Cuising is a legitimate step up from entry-level machines, with a 20-bar pump, proper 58mm portafilter, and a touchscreen that actually improves the experience rather than complicating it. It's not perfect — the small tank and lack of extraction feedback hold it back from greatness. But at this price, it delivers 85% of what a $600 machine does for a fraction of the cost. That's solid math.

**Buy it if** you want café-quality shots at home without the learning curve. **Skip it if** precision dialing or high-volume entertaining is your priority.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A capable, well-thought-out machine held back by small-but-real compromises.
