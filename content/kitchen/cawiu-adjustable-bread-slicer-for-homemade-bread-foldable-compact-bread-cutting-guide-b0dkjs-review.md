# CAWIU Adjustable Bread Slicer: Even Slices Without the Bakery Price Tag  

You just pulled a golden sourdough from the oven. It smells incredible. Then you grab a knife and produce three thick slabs, two slivers, and one slice that's more hole than bread. That's the problem this $20-ish gadget claims to solve — and for the most part, it actually does.  

## What You're Actually Getting  

The CAWIU slicer is a foldable plastic cutting guide with two adjustable side walls. The box includes the slicer itself, a small crumb tray, and — notably — **no knife**. You'll need your own serrated blade, ideally 8 inches or longer.  

The build is food-grade ABS plastic, which keeps it lightweight (under a pound) and easy to rinse. The adjustable width mechanism clicks through detents, letting you set slice thickness from about 0.4 inches (thin sandwich bread) up to 1.2 inches (hearty toast slabs). The fold-flat design collapses to roughly the size of a large paperback, which is a genuine win for small kitchens.  

## How It Performs in Real Use  

I tested this with three loaf types: a dense whole wheat, a soft store-bought white, and a crusty artisan boule.  

**The good news:** For medium-texture breads, this thing is consistent. You set the width, drop the loaf in, and run your knife through the slots. The vertical guides keep your blade straight, so you get uniform slices every time. The crumb tray catches most of the mess — though not all, so keep a cutting board underneath.  

**The catch:** With a hard, crackly crust, the plastic side walls flex slightly under pressure. You'll get a decent slice, but the last inch of the cut can drift if you push too hard. The solution is to use a long, sharp serrated knife and let the blade do the work — saw gently, don't force it.  

A second issue: there's no rubber base. On a smooth granite or quartz counter, the whole unit slides around. Fix it by placing the slicer on a damp paper towel or a silicone mat. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.  

## What Buyers Keep Saying  

With 215 ratings averaging 4.3 stars, the feedback pattern is clear. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've wasted so much bread trying to slice it evenly by hand. This made perfect toast slices every time — my kids actually eat the crusts now."*

One reviewer noted: *"Works great on day-old bread. On fresh-out-of-the-oven, it mashes the loaf a bit."* That's fair — let your bread cool for at least an hour before slicing.  

## The Honest Tradeoffs  

- **Plastic construction** — it's sturdy enough for weekly use, but don't expect heirloom quality. It's a tool, not a showpiece.  
- **Not for bagels or rolls** — this is strictly for standard loaf shapes.  
- **Requires a good knife** — a dull blade will crush bread regardless of the guide.  

## Who Should Buy This  

**Buy it if:** You bake bread at home regularly, want consistent sandwich slices, or hate wasting crusty loaves to uneven cuts. It's also a thoughtful gift for a novice baker who's just discovered the joy of homemade loaves.  

**Skip it if:** You only slice bread a few times a month, or you're expecting a commercial-grade tool. A sharp knife and a steady hand might be enough for you.  

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask  

**Will it fit my bread pan loaves?**  
Yes — the adjustable width accommodates loaves up to about 6 inches wide, which covers standard 9x5 and 8x4 pans.  

**Can I use it with an electric knife?**  
Technically yes, but the vibration makes it harder to control. Stick with a manual serrated knife for best results.  

**Is it dishwasher safe?**  
The manufacturer says top-rack only. Honestly, hand washing takes 30 seconds — just rinse it and wipe it down.  

## The Verdict  

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The CAWIU Adjustable Bread Slicer is a legitimately useful kitchen gadget for anyone who bakes or buys artisanal loaves. It won't replace a bakery slicer, but it solves the uneven-cut problem for under $25, folds flat for storage, and delivers consistent results with a little practice.  

**Buy it if** you're tired of mangling your homemade bread. **Skip it if** you rarely slice whole loaves or prefer a minimalist kitchen. For everyone in between, this is a solid, practical purchase that does exactly what it promises — with a few minor quirks you can work around.
