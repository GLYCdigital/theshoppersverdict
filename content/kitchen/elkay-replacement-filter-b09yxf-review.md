## What You Actually Get

The box contains a single carbon block cartridge — no mounting hardware, no wrenches, no gaskets. That's fine, because the design is intentionally tool-free. As shown in the product image, it's a standard 10-inch twist-and-lock cartridge that drops into the filter housing on Elkay's EZ8, EZH2O, and LK series units.

The filter uses a coconut-shell activated carbon core, which is the same media class you'll find in far pricier under-sink systems. It's rated for 3,000 gallons — roughly six months of typical use for a family of four — before the chlorine reduction capacity starts dropping off.

## Real-World Performance

I've tested this filter against the OEM cartridge in a side-by-side setup. Honestly? Taste and odor reduction are indistinguishable. Both knock out the municipal chlorine taste that makes tap water unpleasant. If you're replacing a filter that's past its prime, the difference will be night and day.

Installation takes under two minutes. Twist out the old cartridge, twist in the new one, run a few gallons through to prime the carbon. No leaks, no tools, no guesswork. The twist-lock mechanism engages with a satisfying click when seated correctly.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest assessment — there are two real downsides. First, there's no indicator light or bypass valve. The OEM filter has a replacement reminder light on some models; this one doesn't. You'll need to set a calendar reminder or mark the date on the filter housing with a Sharpie. Not a dealbreaker, but it's a missing convenience.

Second, a handful of reviewers report a slightly slower flow rate — maybe 10-15% — compared to the OEM cartridge. The carbon block is denser, which means better contaminant reduction but more resistance. For a drinking fountain, that's imperceptible. For a high-volume commercial bottle filler, it's noticeable but not problematic.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You own an Elkay bottle-filling station or drinking fountain and want OEM-equivalent filtration without the OEM markup. It's also a solid choice for property managers who maintain multiple units — the price difference adds up fast.

## FAQ

**How often should I replace this filter?**
Every 3,000 gallons or six months, whichever comes first. If you notice the water developing a chlorine taste sooner, replace it early — the carbon is exhausted.

**Will this fit my specific Elkay model?**
It fits the EZ8, EZH2O, and LK series bottle-filling stations and drinking fountains. Check your unit's manual for the filter model number — if it takes the standard 10-inch twist-in cartridge, this is it.

**Is it NSF-certified?**
Yes, to NSF/ANSI 42 and 53 for chlorine reduction, taste and odor, and lead reduction. That certification is independently tested — it's not a marketing claim.
