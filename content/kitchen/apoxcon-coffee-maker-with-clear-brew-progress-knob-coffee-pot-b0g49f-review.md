Most coffee makers treat brewing like a black box. You press a button, wait, and hope for the best. The APOXCON Coffee Maker with Clear Brew Progress Knob takes a different approach — it lets you watch the entire extraction happen through a transparent window on the brew basket. That single design choice tells you a lot about what this machine is: a straightforward, no-drama brewer for people who actually care about their morning cup but don't want to fuss with a $300 gadget.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the coffee maker itself, a glass carafe, and a permanent mesh filter. No paper filters included, no scoop, no descaling kit — just the machine and the essentials. The carafe holds 8 cups (measured by the 5-ounce coffee-cup standard, so realistically about 4 decent mugs of coffee). The body is mostly plastic but feels solid for the price point, and the warming plate has a nonstick coating that wipes clean easily.

The headline feature is the brew progress knob mounted on top of the water reservoir. As water heats and flows through the system, a small indicator rises in the clear tube, giving you real-time visibility into where things stand. It's not a gimmick — it's genuinely useful when you're making coffee for multiple people and want to know if you can start pouring yet.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this through a two-week test with a mix of light, medium, and dark roasts. Brew time for a full carafe came in around 7-8 minutes, which is average for a drip machine. The water temperature held steady in the 195-205°F range — hot enough for proper extraction without bitterness. The showerhead distributes water evenly across the grounds; I didn't notice any dry spots or channeling, which is a common failure on cheaper machines.

The permanent mesh filter catches fines reasonably well. You'll still get a bit of sediment at the bottom of your cup — that's normal with mesh filters — but nothing that ruins the experience. If you prefer cleaner coffee, you can swap in a #4 paper filter; it fits fine.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides exist, and you should know them before buying. The carafe lid has a loose fit. If you pour aggressively or tilt it much past 45 degrees, coffee will dribble down the side of the pot. Annoying, but not a dealbreaker — just pour with intent.

The warming plate runs hot. Coffee left on it for more than 40 minutes develops a scorched, burnt taste. There's no auto-shutoff either, so if you're the type to forget you made coffee, you'll come back to a crusty, overheated pot. Plan to drink it fresh or transfer to a thermos.

The water reservoir has no markings beyond a MAX line. There's no cup-by-cup measurement guide, so you'll need to count using the carafe lines or just eyeball it. Minor, but worth noting.

## Who Should Buy This

This machine fits three types of people: First-time coffee makers who want something uncomplicated. Older users who appreciate large, clear controls and visible feedback. And anyone who hates guessing whether their coffee is done — the progress knob genuinely helps.

Skip it if you want programmability, a thermal carafe, or a machine that keeps coffee hot for hours. This is a focused, simple brewer that does one thing well.

## FAQ

**Does the brew progress knob actually work or is it decorative?**
It works. The indicator rises as water heats and the brew cycle progresses, giving you a clear visual cue of where things stand. It's genuinely useful, not just a design flourish.

**Can I use paper filters instead of the mesh one?**
Yes. A standard #4 cone filter fits without issue. You'll get cleaner coffee with less sediment, though you'll need to keep buying filters.

**How do I descale this machine?**
Run a 50/50 mix of white vinegar and water through a full brew cycle, then follow with two cycles of clean water. The reservoir and brew basket are both removable and rinse easily.
