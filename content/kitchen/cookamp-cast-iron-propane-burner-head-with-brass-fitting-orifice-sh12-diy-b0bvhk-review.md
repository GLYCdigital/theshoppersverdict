# COOKAMP Cast Iron Propane Burner Head: A Serious Upgrade for DIY Burners

Most burner heads under $30 are stamped steel that warps after a season. The COOKAMP cast iron unit takes a different path. It's heavy — noticeably so when you lift it out of the box. That weight translates directly to heat retention and durability, and for anyone building a turkey fryer, crab cooker, or homebrew stand, it's the kind of foundation you want.

## What You Actually Get

The package is straightforward: the cast iron burner head itself, a brass orifice pre-installed, and the brass fitting that threads onto your propane hose. No igniter, no hose, no regulator — this is a component, not a complete cooking system. If you're replacing a warped burner or building from scratch, you'll need to supply the rest of the setup. The SH12 thread pattern is the standard for most DIY stands, which is good news for compatibility.

## Build Quality and Performance

The cast iron body is the star here. It doesn't flex when you crank the gas, and it distributes heat evenly across the burner ring. In practice, that means fewer hot spots on your pot bottom. The brass orifice is where precision matters most — a poorly drilled jet can cause flame sputtering or incomplete combustion. This one holds a clean, steady blue flame at low and high settings.

One thing to note: this burner is *loud*. At full output, you'll hear a distinct roar. That's normal for cast iron burners in this class, but worth knowing if you're planning to use it near a patio where conversation happens.

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings reflects real satisfaction. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Replaced the cheap stamped burner on my turkey fryer — night and day difference. ## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has specific tradeoffs. First, the rust factor. Cast iron requires care — dry it after use and give it a light oil wipe. Skip that and you'll see orange flakes within weeks, especially in humid climates. Second, the brass fitting is a specific size (SH12), so if you're working with a non-standard hose, you'll need an adapter. Third, the lack of an igniter is mildly annoying for the price point; a piezo spark would cost them pennies to add.
