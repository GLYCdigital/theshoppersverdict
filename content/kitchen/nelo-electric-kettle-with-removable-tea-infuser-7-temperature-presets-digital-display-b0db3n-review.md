Most electric kettles boil water and call it a day. The NELO Electric Kettle with Removable Tea Infuser tries to be smarter than that—and for the most part, it succeeds. If you've ever scorched a pot of delicate green tea by using rolling-boil water, this is the appliance that fixes that recurring mistake.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the 1.0L kettle base, a stainless steel infuser basket with fine mesh, and a glass lid. The body is brushed stainless steel with a matte finish that resists fingerprints better than most. The digital display sits at the base of the handle—easy to read but not in your face. The infuser drops into the chamber from the top and hooks onto the rim; you don't need to reach inside a hot kettle to remove it.

**Performance and real-world use**

The temperature presets are the headline feature. Seven settings range from 160°F for white tea up to 212°F for boiling. The internal thermostat holds water within roughly ±5°F of your target—good enough for proper steeping. Boiling a full liter takes about 4 minutes, which is average for a 1200W unit. The hold function keeps water at temperature for up to 60 minutes, and it cycles on and off rather than constantly boiling—so you don't get that flat, overcooked taste that comes from repeated reheating.

The infuser is genuinely useful for loose leaf. The mesh is fine enough to catch most fannings and broken leaves, though very fine dust can still escape and settle at the bottom of your cup. For whole-leaf teas, it works beautifully. You steep directly in the kettle, pull the basket out when the timer goes off, and pour. One design note: the basket sits low, so if you overfill with leaves that expand significantly, they can crowd the spout and slow pouring. Keep your leaf amount moderate.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reviewers at 4.4 stars across 220 ratings repeatedly mention the temperature accuracy and the convenience of the infuser. One buyer noted, "Finally stopped burning my green tea—the 175° setting is spot on and I use it daily." Another commented on the build quality: "Feels heavy and well-made, not like the cheap plastic kettles I've had before." The 60-minute hold function also gets frequent mention from people who brew multiple cups throughout a workday.

**Where it falls short**

The capacity is the most common complaint. At 1.0L, you're getting about two standard mugs of water—not enough for a family or for filling a large teapot. Several reviewers wished for a 1.5L version. The infuser basket also has a minor flaw: it doesn't have a dedicated drip tray or rest, so when you pull it out, you need a saucer or plate handy. And if the kettle loses power—even briefly—the temperature setting resets to the default, which is annoying if you're mid-brew.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the tea drinker who owns more than one type of loose leaf and has ruined at least one batch with boiling water. It's also solid for pour-over coffee enthusiasts who want precise water temperatures (the 195°–205° range is well covered). It's not for large households, heavy multitaskers who need to boil a liter-plus at once, or anyone who wants a barebones budget kettle.

**FAQ**

*Can I use the infuser for coffee or cold-brew tea?*
Yes for cold-brew—steep in the fridge without heat. For coffee, skip it; the mesh is too fine for ground coffee and will clog quickly.

*Is the kettle body cool to the touch during boiling?*
The exterior stays warm but not scalding—about what you'd expect from a double-wall design. The handle stays cool.

*Does the hold function keep water at the exact set temperature?*
It holds within a few degrees, cycling on and off. It's not lab-precise, but it's fine for tea.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're serious about tea temperature, want an all-in-one steep-and-pour solution, and don't mind the smaller 1.0L capacity.

**Skip it if** you need to boil large volumes, prefer a separate teapot for steeping, or want the cheapest possible kettle that just boils.

The NELO earns a 4.2/5. It does its job with precision where it matters, stumbles on capacity, and provides a genuinely useful infuser that most competitors lack. For the dedicated tea drinker, it's a worthwhile upgrade over a basic kettle.
