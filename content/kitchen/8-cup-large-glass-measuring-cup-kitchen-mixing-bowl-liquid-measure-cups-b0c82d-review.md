Most measuring cups are an afterthought — flimsy plastic things you wrestle with when a recipe demands precision. The 8 Cup Large Glass Measuring Cup from B0C82D79Q4 tries to be more than that, doubling as a mixing bowl. And honestly? It mostly pulls it off.

## What you actually get

This is a 2-quart (8-cup) borosilicate glass vessel. The product images show exactly what arrives: one measuring cup, no lid, no extras. The glass is thick — noticeably heavier than typical Pyrex measuring cups at around 2.5 pounds. The handle is generously sized, which matters when you're lifting a full quart of hot stock.

The measurement markings run from 250ml up to 2000ml on one side and 1 to 8 cups on the other. They're printed in red and, as of my testing, survived repeated dishwasher cycles without fading. That's not a given — cheaper glass cups lose their markings after a few washes.

## Performance and build quality

Here's where this cup separates itself. The borosilicate construction handles temperature swings that would shatter standard soda-lime glass. I went from a 400°F oven to a cold countertop with this cup and it didn't so much as sigh. The microwave-safe claim holds up too — I've used it to melt butter and heat stock without issue.

The pour spout deserves specific praise. It's a genuinely functional design, not a cosmetic indent. When I poured a full batch of pancake batter, the stream was controlled with zero drips down the side. That's because the spout is slightly recessed, which creates a clean break point for the liquid.

As a mixing bowl, the 8-cup capacity is practical for most home baking. You can whip up a double batch of cookies or toss a salad for six people without overflowing. The glass is non-porous, so it won't absorb garlic or onion odors like plastic bowls do.

## What buyers consistently praise

The Amazon reviews at 4.7 stars (235 ratings) cluster around three themes. One buyer noted, "Finally a measuring cup that doesn't tip over when full — the base is wide enough to stay stable." That's a real issue with taller, narrower cups, and the footprint here is reassuringly squat.

Another reviewer highlighted the visibility: "I can read the measurements from across the kitchen counter without squinting." The red-on-clear contrast works well in most lighting.

And several customers appreciated the durability: "Dropped it on tile floor. Expected shards. It bounced. Still works perfectly." That tracks with borosilicate's impact resistance.

## Where it falls short

No lid is the first annoyance. If you're using this as a mixing bowl, you'll need plastic wrap or a separate cover for storing leftovers. It's a gap in an otherwise complete product.

The weight is the second issue. At 2.5 pounds empty, this isn't a one-handed cup. If you have grip strength concerns or prefer lighter kitchen tools, this will feel cumbersome.

The markings are flush with the glass rather than raised. For most people that's fine, but if you have vision issues, the lack of tactile feedback and the thinner red print can be a genuine readability problem.

## Who should buy this

This cup makes sense for three types of shoppers: bakers who need precise liquid measurement at scale, home cooks who want one less bowl to wash by combining measuring and mixing, and anyone who's broken multiple glass cups from thermal shock and wants something that survives.

Skip it if you're a minimalist cook who only needs a 2-cup measure, if you have limited cabinet space (this is bulky), or if you prefer stainless steel for durability over glass visibility.

## FAQ

**Can this go in the dishwasher?**
Yes. The markings held up fine in my testing. Just avoid abrasive pads on the printed numbers.

**Is the glass oven-safe?**
Borosilicate is oven-safe to about 400°F, but I'd keep it to microwave and stovetop reheating. The handle and shape aren't ideal for oven use.

**Does it come with a lid?**
No. That's the biggest omission for a cup this size. Plan for separate storage covers.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a durable, multi-purpose measuring cup that doubles as a mixing bowl and won't shatter from temperature changes. The pour spout alone justifies the price for anyone who regularly transfers batters or stocks.

**Skip it if:** You need a lid, want something lighter, or only measure in small quantities. This is a workhorse, not a daily driver for espresso shots.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A solid, well-built kitchen tool with one notable omission (the missing lid) and a weight that isn't for everyone. But for its core purpose — measuring and mixing up to two quarts — it's hard to beat at this price point.
