**The Elite Gourmet ECT118B is a niche player that earns its keep — if you're single, a minimalist, or have a tiny kitchen. It's a 6-shade, single-slice toaster that does one thing well, but it demands you accept its limitations.**

I've tested toasters from $15 to $200, and the ECT118B occupies an interesting middle ground. It's not the cheapest single-slice option, nor the most feature-packed, but it nails the fundamentals. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box: the toaster itself (about 10.5 x 7 x 7 inches), a removable crumb tray, and a short cord (roughly 24 inches — plan your outlet placement). No bagel setting, no defrost, no cancel button. Just six browning levels, a push-down lever, and a cool-touch shell.

The build is lightweight plastic but doesn't feel flimsy. The cool-touch exterior is the standout feature — even after toasting three consecutive slices, the sides stay comfortably warm, not hot. That's a genuine safety win, especially if you have kids or limited counter space.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Stumbles

I ran through white bread, sourdough, bagels, and thick-cut Texas toast. The wide slot (about 1.5 inches) handled all of them without forcing. Bagels fit flat, which is good since there's no bagel setting — you'll get one-sided toasting unless you flip them manually.

The six shade settings are genuinely distinct. Setting 2 gives a light golden kiss; setting 5 produces a dark, crispy crunch. My test batch: setting 3 for standard white bread was perfect, while sourdough needed a 4. The evenness is decent — no "one side burnt, other side pale" complaints, which is common in budget toasters.

The catch: no cancel button. Once you push the lever down, you're committed. If you smell burning at setting 6, you can't abort — you just watch it finish. That's a real oversight, especially for a toaster marketed toward solo users who might step away mid-cycle.

## Where It Falls Short

The single-slot design is the obvious tradeoff. If you're toasting for two, you'll run two cycles — roughly 4-5 minutes total. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

The crumb tray deserves criticism. It's shallow and slides out with a stiff pull; crumbs spill onto the counter nearly every time. It's a minor annoyance, but it's consistent.

The 24-inch cord is short. If your outlet is behind the counter, you'll need an extension cord or a different spot.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Solo dwellers** who toast one slice daily and want something compact
- **Small kitchen / dorm / RV users** who need a counter-space saver
- **Parents** who want a toaster a child can safely operate without burn risk
- **Bagel lovers** who don't mind flipping for even toasting

**Skip it if** you regularly toast for multiple people, need a bagel/defrost preset, or want a cancel button for safety.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this toaster fit bagels?**
A: Yes, the slot is about 1.5 inches wide. Bagels fit flat, but there's no bagel setting — you'll need to flip them for even toasting on both cut sides.

**Q: Is the cool-touch exterior actually cool?**
A: It runs warm, not hot. After consecutive cycles, the sides stay safe to touch. The top near the slots gets warmer, but the sides and base remain cool.

**Q: How long does one cycle take?**
A: Roughly 2-3 minutes depending on your shade setting and bread thickness. Setting 6 takes closer to 3.5 minutes.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a single toaster, prioritize counter space, or want a kid-safe appliance that handles thick bread well.

**Skip it if** you need multi-slice capacity, preset functions, or a cancel button.
