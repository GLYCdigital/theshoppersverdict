## What You Actually Get

The set includes three calderos in graduated sizes — roughly 2-quart, 3.5-quart, and 5-quart capacity. Each pot comes with a tempered glass lid that fits snugly. ## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where the caldero earns its keep. The thick aluminum walls heat evenly and retain temperature well. That's the entire secret to getting perfect rice: even heat distribution that creates that crusty bottom layer without burning the rest of the pot. The 5-quart pot handles a full family-sized batch of arroz con gandules or paella without crowding.

The glass lids are a practical upgrade over the traditional metal ones. You can check your rice without releasing steam — a small detail that makes a real difference. The lids fit well across all three sizes, too.

One thing I noticed: the set feels heavier than typical budget cookware. That's a good sign — it means the aluminum is actually thick enough to do its job, not stamped paper-thin like some cheap imports.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the aluminum interior. It reacts with acidic ingredients — tomatoes, citrus, vinegar-based marinades. Over time, you'll see pitting and discoloration. This is inherent to uncoated aluminum cookware, not a defect. If you cook a lot of tomato-heavy dishes, you'll want to rotate these with stainless steel or enameled pots.

The handles are bare metal and get dangerously hot. There's no silicone grip or insulated coating. Every single use requires oven mitts — plan accordingly.

Also, this set will not work on induction cooktops. If your kitchen is all-induction, this is a non-starter. Look for the stainless steel versions instead.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You cook Latin, Caribbean, or Spanish rice dishes regularly and want authentic results. You're cooking on gas or electric coil burners. You appreciate traditional cookware over modern nonstick alternatives.

**Skip it if:** You're on induction, cook mostly acidic dishes, or prefer low-maintenance dishwasher-safe cookware. (These should be hand-washed — the aluminum will discolor and degrade in the dishwasher.)

## FAQ

**Can you use these on high heat?**
Yes, but you don't need to. Medium heat works best for rice — the thick aluminum retains heat so well that high heat will just scorch your food. Start on medium, and once you hear the rice crackling, drop it to low.

**Do these work on glass-top stoves?**
They work, but be careful. The aluminum base can scratch glass cooktops if you slide the pot. Lift, don't drag.

**Will the aluminum affect food taste?**
With neutral foods like rice, beans, and meat, no. With acidic foods, you may notice a metallic taste, especially in the first few uses. Simmering tomatoes for a few minutes and discarding can help season the surface.
