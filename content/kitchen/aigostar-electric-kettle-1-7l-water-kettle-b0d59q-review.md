Let's be honest: most electric kettles are boring. You plug them in, water boils, you pour. The Aigostar 1.7L doesn't reinvent the category, but it gets the fundamentals right—and a few details wrong. Here's what 220 Amazon buyers and I found out.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the 1.7L kettle, a 360-degree swivel base, and a user manual. That's it. No filters, no descaling kit, no extras. The kettle body is brushed stainless steel with a matte finish that resists fingerprints better than most. The lid flips open with a button on the handle, and there's a blue LED ring that lights up during boiling—subtle, not obnoxious. The spout is a gooseneck-style pour spout, which gives you decent control for pour-over coffee, though it's not as precise as a dedicated gooseneck kettle.

**Real-world performance**

The 1500W heating element is the star here. A full 1.7L goes from tap temperature to a rolling boil in about 5 minutes. That's not the fastest on the market—some 1800W models shave off a minute—but it's respectable for the price point. The auto shut-off is genuinely reliable: the kettle clicks off within 2 seconds of boiling, and boil-dry protection kicks in if you forget to add water. I tested that twice; it didn't burn out or smell.

The 360-degree base means the kettle sits down in any orientation, which sounds trivial until you're fumbling with one hand and a coffee mug in the other. The stainless steel interior is seamless, so there's no crevice for scale to hide in. After three weeks of daily use with hard tap water, a light vinegar rinse brought it back to spotless.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme in owner feedback is the lack of plastic taste. One verified buyer wrote: "Finally a kettle that doesn't make my tea taste like a swimming pool. The water comes out clean and neutral." Another mentioned: "It's quiet. My old kettle sounded like a jet engine. This one hums softly, and the boil is calm." Build quality gets repeated shout-outs—the handle stays cool during boiling, and the lid seal doesn't leak when pouring aggressively.

**Where it falls short**

The lid is the biggest annoyance. When the kettle has just boiled, the steam pressure makes the lid stiff to open. You'll need a towel or an oven mitt to flip it up, which is a minor hassle every single time. The base also lacks cord storage—the 28-inch cord just hangs off the back. On a tight countertop, that's clutter you'll have to manage. A few buyers also noted the exterior gets warm to the touch near the top (not hot enough to burn, but enough to notice), though the handle remains cool.

**Who should buy this**

If you're a tea drinker who boils water multiple times daily, this is a solid pick. It's also great for pour-over coffee enthusiasts who want a budget-friendly electric kettle with decent pour control. Families will appreciate the 1.7L capacity—enough for four large mugs or a full pasta pot. Skip it if you need a variable temperature kettle for green tea or French press precision; this is a simple boil-only model.

**FAQ**

**Is the interior really all stainless steel?** Yes. The water chamber is fully stainless, with no plastic components inside. The only plastic contact is the exterior housing and the lid rim.

**Does it leak from the spout when pouring?** No. The spout is designed well—no dribbling even when you tilt it to a sharp angle. Just don't overfill past the MAX line; that's when you'll get splashing.

**How do I descale it?** Mix 1:1 water and white vinegar, boil it, let it sit for 20 minutes, then rinse thoroughly. Do this monthly if you have hard water. The wide opening makes cleaning easy with a sponge.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a reliable, no-frills electric kettle with a clean stainless interior, fast boil times, and safety features that actually work. It's a solid mid-range choice for daily tea, coffee, and cooking water.

**Skip it if:** You need temperature presets for specialty teas, or you're easily annoyed by a stiff lid and cord clutter. Pay more for a variable-temperature model instead.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good kettle that nails the essentials but stumbles on minor ergonomics. For the price, it's hard to beat.
