**The glow-up your dessert table didn't know it needed**

Let's be honest — cotton candy already carries a party. It's pink, it's fluffy, it's nostalgic. But if you're throwing a wedding, a Sweet 16, or a Halloween bash and you want that extra "wow" factor without renting a fog machine, these LED cotton candy cones deliver exactly what the product photos promise. The glowing stick turns a $3 bag of sugar floss into the most photographed item at the party.

**What you actually get**

The package includes six LED cone bases with removable clear cones on top. Each base houses a small battery compartment (button cell batteries included) with a simple push-button switch. The cones themselves are food-grade plastic — thick enough to handle a generous serving of cotton candy without flexing or cracking. The LED unit sits at the base of the cone, shining upward through the candy itself. It's a smart design: the light diffuses through the cotton candy fibers rather than glaring directly at you.

**Performance and build quality**

I tested these at a backyard birthday party and a dimly lit indoor gathering. The light output is genuinely pleasant — a soft, even glow that makes the cotton candy look like a neon cloud. There are multiple color modes: steady red, blue, green, purple, and a slow color-cycle option, plus a couple of flash settings. The steady modes are the winners; the flashing feels a bit cheap for an upscale event, but kids absolutely love it.

The waterproof claim holds up. Cotton candy melts, hands are sticky, and condensation drips — I wiped these down with a damp cloth and they kept working. The switch is recessed enough that accidental activation in a bag or purse is unlikely.

Here's where the product image doesn't tell the whole story: the light is not blindingly bright. In a well-lit outdoor afternoon setting, the glow is subtle. These shine at dusk, in tents, or under string lights. If you're planning a daytime pool party, temper your expectations.

**Where it falls short**

The battery replacement process is annoying. You need a tiny Phillips screwdriver to open the compartment, and the screws are small enough that you'll likely lose one during your first battery change. Buy a spare screwdriver set and keep it with the cones.

Also, the clear cones are sold separately from the LED bases — check the listing carefully because some configurations ship only the bases. If you're planning to serve cotton candy immediately, pre-thread the cones before guests arrive. Fumbling with a paper cone of sugar floss while trying to press the button is a mess waiting to happen.

**Who should buy this**

- **Wedding planners and event hosts**: These elevate a dessert table for minimal cost, and you can resell them or reuse them for multiple events.
- **Parents of sensory-sensitive kids**: The glow is soft and non-flickering on steady mode — no strobe-effect meltdowns.

**Skip it if** you're serving cotton candy outdoors in full sun, or if you expect commercial-grade brightness. These are an accent, not a spotlight.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these dishwasher safe?**  
A: No. Hand wash with warm soapy water and dry thoroughly. The battery compartment is waterproof but not dishwasher-proof — the heat and water pressure will damage the contacts.

**Q: How long do the batteries last?**  
A: On steady mode, roughly 6-8 hours of continuous use. On flashing mode, less — about 4 hours. Turn them off between events to stretch battery life.

**Q: Do they fit standard cotton candy bags?**  
A: The cones are sized for the included sticks. Standard machine-spun cotton candy on paper cones won't fit — you'll need to remove the candy from the paper cone and re-thread it, or buy cotton candy made on sticks.
