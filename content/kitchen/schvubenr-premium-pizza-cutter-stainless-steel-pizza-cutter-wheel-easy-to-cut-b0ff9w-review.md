**What You Actually Get**

This is a one-piece stainless steel construction — the entire body, from the handle to the wheel fork, is metal. The cutting wheel measures 4 inches in diameter, which is noticeably larger than the typical 3-inch wheel on budget cutters. That extra inch matters: it gives you more leverage and means the blade rolls further per push, so you're not sawing back and forth.

The handle is ergonomically shaped with a textured grip. It's not rubber-coated (which degrades over time), but rather a contoured stainless design with ridges. Weighing in around 8 ounces, it has enough heft to feel substantial without being tiring.

**Real-World Performance**

The blade arrives sharp — genuinely sharp, not "sharp for a pizza cutter." It cleanly slices through thick Chicago-style crusts and crispy thin-crust pizzas without dragging toppings across the pie. The single-pass cut is the differentiator here; you won't need to rock the cutter back and forth, which is what tears cheese and shifts pepperoni.

One buyer noted: *"I've owned $30 pizza cutters that couldn't touch this. Cuts through my homemade sourdough crust like it's paper."* Another mentioned it handled a frozen pizza straight out of the oven without bending — a common failure point for cheaper wheels.
