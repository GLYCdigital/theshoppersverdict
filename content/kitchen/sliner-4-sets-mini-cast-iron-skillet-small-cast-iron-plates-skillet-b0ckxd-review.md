If you've ever watched a skillet cookie sizzle in a cast iron pan and thought, "I want that at my table, not just in a restaurant," the Sliner 4-piece mini skillet set is worth a serious look. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, this isn't a gimmick purchase — it's a genuinely useful set for anyone who cooks for one, entertains often, or wants portion-controlled cast iron without the storage headache of full-size pans.

## What You Actually Get

The set includes four 6.5-inch mini skillets and four small trivet plates (which is where the naming gets tricky — more on that below). Each pan is pre-seasoned with flaxseed oil, and the cooking surface is smoother than what you typically find at this price point. The handles are the classic long cast iron style, which means they're oven-safe but absolutely require a mitt.

As shown in the product image, these are proper mini skillets — not the tiny 3-inch novelty pans you see at tourist shops. The 6.5-inch size is genuinely useful: it fits a single egg, a personal cornbread portion, or a decent scoop of cookie dough for one.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these across three cooking methods: stovetop, oven, and campfire. The heat distribution is what you'd expect from cast iron — excellent once they're preheated, with a 2-3 minute warm-up time on medium heat. The seasoning held up through my first week, and eggs released cleanly with just a light butter swipe.

The trivets deserve a specific mention. They're not enameled, so they'll protect your table from heat but won't handle acidic spills without staining. The pans themselves are a touch lighter than Lodge's mini skillets — about 1.2 pounds each — which makes them easier to handle but slightly less durable-feeling in hand.

## Where It Falls Short

The naming is the biggest issue. These are not "plates" in any functional sense — they're trivets, and if you're expecting something you can eat directly from without a wooden board underneath, you'll be disappointed. The handles also run hot with no included silicone covers, which is a minor safety concern if you're serving directly from oven to table.

One more honest note: the pre-seasoning is adequate but not exceptional. I'd recommend a quick oven-seasoning pass (coat with oil, bake at 400°F for an hour) before your first use to build a more robust non-stick layer.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a solo cook who wants cast iron without committing to a full-size pan. You entertain and want individual serving vessels that double as cookware. You camp or use a toaster oven where compact cast iron is a genuine advantage.

**Skip it if:** You need one workhorse skillet for daily family cooking — buy a 10-inch Lodge instead. Or if you're expecting actual serving plates rather than trivets.

## FAQ

**Can these go in the dishwasher?** No. Hand wash only, and dry immediately to prevent rust. This is standard for all cast iron.

**Are they pre-seasoned enough to use right away?** Yes, but a quick oven-seasoning pass improves performance noticeably.

**What's the actual cooking capacity?** About 1.5 cups — perfect for one egg, a small cornbread muffin, or a personal skillet cookie.
