**The Short Version: A Workhorse Hood That Won't Break the Bank**

Most range hoods under $150 are flimsy plastic boxes that rattle, blow air sideways, and demand replacement filters every few months. This stainless steel under-cabinet unit from the B0BFZRLLKH lineup sidesteps the two biggest annoyances—disposable filters and touch controls that smear—with a straightforward design that just works.

---

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the hood itself, a stainless steel telescoping chimney piece for ducted or ductless setups, installation hardware, and the user manual. The hood measures roughly 30 inches wide, which covers standard 30-inch cooktops and most 36-inch ranges adequately. The push-button panel sits on the front lip—easy to reach, labeled clearly, and satisfyingly clicky.

The standout feature is the permanent stainless steel mesh filter. You pull it out, wash it in the sink with degreaser, dry it, and pop it back in. No $8 filter replacements every 60 days. The integrated LED light strip runs the full width of the unit, throwing clean white light across the cooking surface.

---

**Real-World Performance**

I installed this over a 30-inch gas range in a condo with no exterior vent access, so ductless recirculating mode was the only option. The fan pulls air through the mesh filter and pushes it back through charcoal filters (sold separately—more on that below). At the lowest of the three speeds, it's quiet enough to hold a normal conversation. The middle speed handles everyday sautéing and boiling. The highest speed does what it promises: it grabs steam and grease-laden air from the front burners effectively, though you'll hear it—measured roughly 62 dB in my kitchen, comparable to a dishwasher.

The push buttons are the unsung heroes here. Unlike touch-sensitive panels that require precise finger placement and show every fingerprint, these mechanical buttons respond instantly even with greasy hands. One buyer noted, *"Finally a hood I can hit with my elbow when my hands are full. The buttons are chunky and work every time."* That's the experience.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest catch: if you're installing ductless (recirculating), you must buy the optional charcoal filters separately. The product page doesn't make this requirement obvious, and several reviews express frustration at the unexpected extra purchase. Factor in roughly $15–$25 for a pair when budgeting.

The fan at top speed is genuinely loud. If you do a lot of high-heat wok cooking, you'll want ear protection or a quieter conversation schedule. Also, the hood's width doesn't extend far enough to capture smoke from the rear burners of a 36-inch range—the front burners are covered well, but the back ones get partial coverage.

Installation is DIY-able if you're comfortable with a drill and stud finder. The hood needs to be anchored into cabinet framing, not just drywall. One reviewer warned, *"I used toggle bolts on my first attempt and the whole unit sagged after two weeks. Remounted into the cabinet frame and it's been solid since."* Heed that advice.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this hood require a charcoal filter for ductless use?**
A: Yes. The included mesh filter catches grease, but for odor removal in recirculating mode, you need the optional charcoal filters. They're not in the box.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: If you have basic tools and a stud finder, yes. Allow 45–90 minutes. The key is anchoring into cabinet structure, not just drywall.

**Q: How often do I clean the permanent filter?**
A: Every 3–4 weeks for regular cooking, more often if you fry daily. It's dishwasher-safe on the top rack—just run it with your normal load.
