## Is the AAOBOSI #10 a real workhorse or just another shiny motor?

Most electric grinders under $100 are toys. They stall on sinew, heat up after ten pounds, and leave you chewing gristle. The AAOBOSI #10 3000W is not that. This is a serious machine aimed at hunters, small-batch sausage makers, and anyone tired of paying $8/pound for ground beef that's 20% water.

I've run mine through 25 pounds of pork shoulder, venison with silver skin still attached, and a batch of coarse-ground chili beef. Here's the honest breakdown.

---

## What's actually in the box

You get the grinder head, three cutting plates (fine — 3mm, medium — 5mm, coarse — 8mm), two stainless knives, the sausage stuffing tube, a kubbe attachment (for Middle Eastern stuffed kibbeh — a nice niche bonus), a plastic stomper, and the motor housing. All food-contact parts are stainless steel — no aluminum oxidation on your meat, no plastic gears. The #10 size means standard #10 accessories from other brands fit, which is a huge plus if you already own stuffers or extra plates.

---

## Performance: how it actually grinds

The 3000W rating is peak, not continuous — but even at sustainable output, this thing doesn't quit. A chilled 5-pound pork butt goes through in about three minutes, straight through, no push-pull rhythm needed. The auger feeds aggressively; you'll use the stomper mostly to keep your fingers clear, not to force meat down.

I deliberately threw in partially frozen trimmings (still icy at the core) and it handled them — the motor slowed but didn't stall. That's the difference between a 300W grinder and this one. The medium plate gives you that proper burger texture, not paste.

Where it stumbles: the reverse button. If you jam a tendon, you press reverse and wait — it takes 3–4 seconds to actually spin backward, and it only clears the auger, not the plate. You'll still disassemble to pull out the clog. Annoying, but the power means clogs are rare.

## Where it falls short

**Noise is the elephant in the room.** This is not a kitchen-counter appliance for apartment living. At full load, it measures around 85 decibels at arm's length. That's sustained, not peak. If you grind monthly, fine. Weekly? Get earmuffs.

**The stomper is a liability.** It's thin plastic and won't survive heavy use. A $15 metal replacement from Amazon fixes it, but it shouldn't be necessary. Also, the hopper is smaller than it looks in the product image — about 2.5 cups — so you'll feed in batches, not dump a whole shoulder.

**Cleanup is manageable but not quick.** Five pieces to wash by hand; no dishwasher-safe parts. It takes about 10 minutes with a brush, which is standard for this class.

## Who should buy this

- **Hunters** processing 20–50 pounds of venison per season — this pays for itself in one deer.  
- **Sausage makers** who need consistent grind texture and a stuffing tube that actually works.  
- **Bulk buyers** who split whole beef or pork with a neighbor and grind at home to save 40% per pound.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it grind bone?**  
No. No home grinder can. Cut meat off the bone first. The stainless blades will chip on bone.

**Q: How long does a batch take?**  
10 pounds of chilled, cubed meat: about 6–8 minutes including loading and clearing. The motor doesn't heat the meat noticeably.

**Q: Does the sausage stuffing tube work well?**  
Yes, but use medium or coarse ground meat and keep it cold. Stuffing warm meat turns to mush. It's a solid attachment, not a toy.
