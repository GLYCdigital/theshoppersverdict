## Real-World Performance

The motor does what it's supposed to do: rotates the ejector blades to push ice into the bin, then cycles back to let the tray refill. The metal gear housing is genuinely better than the plastic gears on the original motors — those strip out after about 18 months of daily use, which is the most common failure mode. Multiple buyers confirmed this holds up longer. One reviewer noted, "The original motor lasted 14 months. This one's been going strong for 8 months with zero issues."

Noise is a pleasant surprise. Several buyers specifically mentioned the replacement runs quieter than their factory motor. Check your machine's model number against the compatibility list before ordering — this is not a universal part despite what the listing suggests.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my [specific brand] ice maker?**
Check the compatibility list in the listing against your machine's model number. It covers most GE, Frigidaire, and NewAir units from 2020 onward, but older models or off-brands may need modification.

**Q: How do I know if my ice maker needs a motor vs. something else?**
If you hear the machine humming but ice isn't dropping, it's usually the motor. If there's no sound at all, check the control board or power supply first — the motor isn't getting power.

**Q: How hard is the install?**
Moderate DIY. You'll need a Phillips screwdriver and about 20 minutes. Unplug the machine, remove the top cover, disconnect the old motor's wiring harness, swap the mounting screws, and reconnect. Basic wiring knowledge helps.
