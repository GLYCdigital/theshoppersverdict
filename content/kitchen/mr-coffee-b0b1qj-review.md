Let's be blunt: the Mr. Coffee name carries decades of baggage. It's the machine your grandparents had, the one that sat on motel counters, the one that brews "fine" coffee and nothing more. But this particular model — the B0B1QJG72F — has quietly accumulated a 4.2-star rating across 210 reviews. Is it actually good, or is it just cheap enough that people forgive its flaws?

I've spent time with this machine, and here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the coffee maker itself, a 12-cup glass carafe, a permanent filter basket, and a scoop. That's it. No pod adapter, no milk frother, no smart features. The unit measures roughly 11 x 8 x 14 inches — compact enough to slide under most upper cabinets. The water reservoir is fixed (no removable tank), which means filling it requires either pulling the whole machine forward or using a pitcher with a spout.

The control panel is refreshingly analog: an on/off switch, a programmable timer, and a "brew now" button. There's also a 2-hour auto shutoff, which is a genuinely nice safety touch for forgetful mornings.

## Brew Performance: The Good and The Lukewarm

Here's where this machine splits opinion. The brew cycle is fast — a full pot finishes in about 7 minutes, which beats many machines in this price range by a couple minutes. The showerhead distributes water evenly across the grounds, and the permanent filter does a respectable job — you'll get a few fine particles at the bottom of your cup, but nothing that ruins the experience.

The problem is temperature. This brewer consistently produces coffee in the 165-170°F range. For reference, the Specialty Coffee Association recommends 195-205°F for optimal extraction. What this means practically: your coffee will taste slightly under-extracted — a bit more acidic and less rich than what you'd get from a $100+ machine. For daily drinkers who add cream and sugar, you won't notice. For black coffee purists, you will.

## What Buyers Actually Say

Scrolling through the 210 reviews, a pattern emerges. The praise is consistent: "Simple to use, easy to clean, and makes a decent cup for the price," one reviewer wrote. Another noted, "The timer is reliable — I set it before bed and wake up to fresh coffee every single morning."

The complaints are equally predictable. The most common gripe: "The carafe lid came off while I was pouring and coffee went everywhere." This happened to me too — the lid is held on by friction alone, and if you tilt the carafe past about 80 degrees, it pops off. It's a design flaw, not a manufacturing defect.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the temperature and lid issues, the water reservoir's fixed design is genuinely annoying. You can't remove it to rinse out mineral buildup, and cleaning the interior requires a bottle brush and patience. The warming plate also runs hot — leave a pot on for an hour and you'll get a burnt, bitter brew that tastes closer to scorched than coffee.

## Who Should Buy This

This machine is for three specific people:

1. **The set-it-and-forget-it commuter** who needs hot coffee on a timer and doesn't care about third-wave extraction science.
2. **The college student or first-apartment renter** who wants a functional brewer under $40 and will replace it in two years without regret.
3. **The office break room** where the machine will be abused and nobody wants to cry over a $200 espresso machine.

Skip it if you're a coffee enthusiast, if you drink black coffee exclusively, or if you want a machine that'll last a decade.

## FAQ

**Does the timer actually work?**
Yes, and surprisingly well. It holds its programming through power outages, which is rare at this price point. Set it once, and it'll fire up at the same time daily.

**Can I use paper filters instead of the permanent one?**
Yes. The basket accepts standard #4 cone filters, which will give you a cleaner cup with virtually no sediment.

**Is the carafe dishwasher safe?**
The glass carafe is, but the lid and brew basket are not — they'll warp in the heat. Hand wash those.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, no-frills drip brewer that makes decent coffee with minimal fuss and doesn't cost a fortune. **Skip it if** you're chasing specialty-grade extraction or need a carafe that won't redecorate your counter with hot coffee.

At 4.2 stars, this Mr. Coffee model earns its rating honestly — it's exactly what it claims to be, no more, no less. For the price, that's a fair deal.
