# SUSTEAS Juicer: Does This Budget Centrifugal Actually Deliver?

Most juicers under $100 fall into one of two categories: frustratingly flimsy or surprisingly capable. The SUSTEAS Juicer sits firmly in the second group — with a few caveats worth knowing before you add it to your cart.

This is a centrifugal juicer, meaning it shreds produce at high speed and spins the pulp against a mesh strainer. That's the right approach for most home users who want quick, everyday juice from affordable fruits and vegetables. It's not the machine for wheatgrass shots or year-round leafy green juicing — that's a masticating juicer's job. But for apples, carrots, oranges, and beets? This handles them with ease.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the motor base, juicing basket, feed tube with pusher, pulp container, juice pitcher with a froth separator lid, and a cleaning brush. The motor base has a brushed stainless finish that looks more expensive than the price suggests. It's compact — roughly 12 inches tall and 10 inches deep — so it won't dominate your countertop.

Assembly is straightforward: the basket locks in with a quarter-turn, the lid clicks into place, and you're running. Total setup takes under a minute the first time.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The headline feature here is the 3-inch feed chute. This is a big deal for daily use. I threw in whole apples, halved oranges, and full carrots without any chopping. That saves genuine prep time — probably five to eight minutes per juicing session compared to machines with narrow chutes.

The 800-watt motor spins at 17,000 RPM, and output is fast. A full pitcher of apple-carrot juice takes about two minutes from start to cleanup. The juice comes out smooth with minimal foam — the froth separator lid on the pitcher actually works, which is rarer than it should be at this price point.

One pleasant surprise is the noise level. Centrifugal juicers typically sound like a blender fighting a lawnmower. This one runs at roughly 60dB — conversation-level noise. You can run it early morning without waking the household.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest tradeoffs matter. The pulp comes out noticeably wet — squeezing it by hand yields significant juice still trapped inside. A masticating juicer would extract 15-20% more from the same produce. If you're juicing expensive organic produce in bulk, that loss adds up.

The juicing basket is plastic, and a few reviewers report cracks after several months of use. The stainless exterior suggests durability, but the internal basket is the weak point. Handle it gently during cleaning.

There's no reverse function. When I fed celery stalks, the machine occasionally jammed and required disassembly to clear. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if celery is a staple in your juice rotation.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want fast, convenient juice from common fruits and vegetables without prep work. You're new to juicing and don't want to invest $200+ in a masticating machine. You value quick cleanup and quiet operation.

**Skip it if:** You're committed to daily green juice with heavy leafy greens. You want maximum extraction efficiency for pricey organic produce. You need a machine that can handle continuous heavy use — this is best for 2-4 juice sessions per week, not commercial-scale output.

## FAQ

**Is the SUSTEAS Juicer easy to clean?**
Yes — it breaks down into six dishwasher-safe parts with no hidden crevices. Most users report a two-minute rinse-and-dry routine.

**Can it handle whole fruits without chopping?**
The 3-inch feed chute handles whole apples, carrots, and halved oranges. Larger produce like whole pineapples still needs cutting.

**Does it work for leafy greens like kale or spinach?**
It works, but mediocre. Centrifugal juicers extract poorly from leafy greens, and you'll get wet pulp. For occasional greens, fine. For daily green juice, get a masticating juicer instead.
