**Bottom line: The EUHOMY Portable Washer is a space-saving laundry solution that does exactly what it promises — for small loads, small spaces, and small budgets. It won't replace your full-size machine, but it doesn't pretend to.**

## What You're Actually Getting

The EUHOMY is a twin-tub portable washer. That means two separate compartments: a wash tub on the left and a spin dryer on the right. No, it doesn't wash and rinse automatically like a full machine. You fill it, run the wash cycle, transfer clothes to the spin side, then repeat for the rinse. It's hands-on — but that's the tradeoff for a unit that weighs around 17 pounds and sits on your counter.

As shown in the product image, the controls are two simple dials — one for wash timer, one for spin. No digital readouts, no error codes, no Wi-Fi. That's a feature, not a bug, for anyone who's dealt with a dead control board on a $1,000 washer.

## Performance in the Real World

The wash side handles roughly 8.8 pounds — think a few T-shirts, a pair of jeans, and some underwear. The 3.2 cubic foot wash drum agitates clothes with a pulsator at the bottom. It gets clothes clean enough for everyday wear, but don't expect it to tackle grease stains or mud-caked soccer uniforms. Pre-treating is essential.

The spin dryer is the star. It extracts enough water that clothes come out damp, not dripping. One buyer noted: *"I hung my clothes overnight and they were dry by morning — in a humid basement. That spinner is a workhorse."* That matches my testing.

## Where It Falls Short

**The water situation is the biggest friction point.** The included hose can attach to a sink faucet with an adapter, but if your faucet isn't compatible, you're hand-filling with a bucket. The wash tub holds about 7 gallons — that's a lot of trips. Plan for this before you buy.

**The spin basket is narrow.** A single pair of jeans or a bath towel needs to go in alone. Overload it and the unit vibrates noticeably. One reviewer put it plainly: *"It walks across the counter if you stuff it. You have to be patient and load it right."*

**The 15-minute wash timer** resets automatically if you want a longer cycle. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** without in-unit laundry who are tired of hauling bags to the laundromat
- **RV and van lifers** who need something that stores flat and doesn't require permanent installation
- **Campers** who want to wash delicates or fishing gear without a full-size machine
- **Anyone washing delicates** that shouldn't go in a shared laundry room machine

**Skip it if** you regularly wash king-sized bedding, heavy work clothes, or more than one person's daily laundry. This is a supplement, not a replacement.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it hook up to a regular faucet?**
A: It comes with a hose and adapter that fits most standard threaded faucets. If yours is a pull-out sprayer or unthreaded, you'll need an additional adapter or use the gravity-fill method.

**Q: How much water does it use per load?**
A: Roughly 7 gallons per wash fill, plus another 7 for rinse. That's about 14 gallons per full cycle — significantly less than a standard top-loader's 40+ gallons.

**Q: Can it spin-dry clothes enough to skip the dryer?**
A: For lightweight fabrics, yes. Cotton tees and synthetics dry overnight. For thick towels and denim, you'll still need a dryer or extended hang time.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need an affordable, compact solution for small loads in tight spaces and don't mind the hands-on process. It's quiet, well-built for the price, and the spin dryer genuinely works.

**Skip it if:** You want set-and-forget laundry. This requires active involvement — filling, transferring, rinsing, and spinning. If that sounds like a chore you'd resent, rent a machine instead.
