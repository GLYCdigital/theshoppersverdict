Let me cut through the greenwashing. "Biodegradable" and "unbleached" are buzzwords slapped on plenty of mediocre kitchen products. But this filter from the Natural Unbleached Brown line actually earns its eco-friendly claims, and more importantly, it brews a damn good cup.

I've run through two boxes of these in my Chemex and a standard #4 cone brewer. Here's what you're actually paying for.

**What's in the box**
You get a stack of extra-large cone filters sized for 10-14 cup brewers. The paper is a warm, natural brown — no chlorine-brightened white here. The texture is noticeably thicker than grocery-store generic filters, with a visible fiber structure that suggests real unbleached pulp rather than recycled mystery paper. Seams are crimped tightly, and the filters lie flat without the curl you sometimes get from cheaper packs.

**Real-world performance**
This is where the filter separates itself. The extra-large sizing isn't marketing fluff — it genuinely covers bigger baskets. I tested it on a 12-cup Mr. Coffee and a larger commercial-style brewer, and the filter extended past the basket edges with room to spare. No overflow, no grounds sneaking around the sides.

The thickness matters. Thin filters collapse under the weight of saturated grounds, especially if you're doing a slow pour. These held their structure through a full bloom and three-stage pour without a single tear. The brew time ran slightly longer than with bleached filters — about 20-30 seconds more for a full pot — but the resulting cup was cleaner, with zero sediment at the bottom of the carafe.

**Where it falls short**

Second, some batches lack perforated seams along the bottom edge, which makes popping the filter into a cone shape more fiddly than it should be. You'll need two hands and a bit of patience.
