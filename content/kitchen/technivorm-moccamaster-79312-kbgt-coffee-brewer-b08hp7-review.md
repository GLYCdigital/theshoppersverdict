**The $350 question: does a coffee maker with zero electronics and no programmable timer deserve that price tag?**

Here's the short answer: if you care about what's actually in your cup, yes. If you want convenience features, absolutely not. The Technivorm Moccamaster 79312 KBGT is a purpose-built machine that does exactly one thing — brew excellent coffee — and refuses to apologize for ignoring everything else.

**What you actually get**

Unboxing reveals the machine assembled, a 40-ounce glass carafe, and a paper filter starter pack. That's it. No measuring scoop (odd for a brewer this precise), no descaling kit, no fancy accessories. The KBGT is the "glass carafe" model — the thermal carafe version costs more and holds heat longer, but this one keeps things simple.

The build is immediately impressive. The copper boiler is visible through the housing, the aluminum drip arm has nine holes machined into it, and the whole thing feels like commercial equipment scaled for a home kitchen. It weighs about 8 pounds — not flimsy, not a toy.

**How it actually brews**

The Moccamaster's claim to fame is temperature stability. It holds water between 195–205°F through the entire brew cycle, not just at the start. The 9-hole drip arm showers water evenly across the coffee grounds — no channeling, no dry spots, no under-extracted edges. A full carafe finishes in about 5–6 minutes.

Here's what that means in the cup: you get a clean, bright, fully extracted brew. Dark roasts taste smoother. Light roasts show their origin character. It's not dramatically different from a good pour-over — it's just that this machine does it consistently, every single day, without you thinking about it.

One real quirk: the brew basket has a manual stop valve. You can pull the carafe mid-brew to pour a cup, and the coffee stops dripping. That's a thoughtful touch most machines skip.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Long-term owners are fanatical about this machine. One Amazon reviewer who's owned theirs for six years wrote: *"Still brewing like day one. I descale twice a year and it's never missed a beat."* Another noted: *"I've had $50 drip machines die in 18 months. This thing is a tank."*

The 5-year warranty gets mentioned constantly. Technivorm backs the copper boiler and heating element, and they honor it — replacement parts are available for every component, which is rare in this category.

**Where it falls short**

The glass carafe is genuinely thin. It's not Pyrex; it's standard borosilicate that will break if you tap it against the sink. Replacement carafes run $40–50.

The lack of a programmable timer is the biggest frustration for many. At this price, you'd expect a "wake up to fresh coffee" feature. You don't get one. You get a switch. That's it.

Descaling matters more here than with cheaper machines. The copper boiler and brass fittings will scale up if you ignore it. Technivorm recommends citric acid descaling every 100 brews. Skip it, and you'll notice slower brew times and potentially void your warranty.

**Who should buy this**

Buy it if you're a coffee enthusiast who wants café-quality drip coffee without learning pour-over technique. It's ideal for someone who drinks 2–4 cups daily, appreciates build quality, and doesn't mind a manual routine.

Skip it if you want programmable brewing, a thermal carafe for all-day heat, or you're fine with "good enough" coffee from a $100 machine. Also skip if you have low overhead cabinets — this brewer stands about 15 inches tall, and the top-loading water reservoir needs clearance.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the Moccamaster actually brew at the right temperature?**
A: Yes. Independent testing confirms it maintains 195–205°F throughout the cycle. Most $100 brewers drop below 190°F within the first minute, which under-extracts coffee and makes it taste sour or weak.

**Q: Is the glass carafe model better than the thermal version?**
A: Glass is easier to clean and you can see the brew level. Thermal keeps coffee hot for 2+ hours but costs about $50 more and requires preheating to get the full benefit. Pick based on how fast you drink the pot.

**Q: Can I use pre-ground coffee or do I need a grinder?**
A: You can use pre-ground, but you're wasting the machine's potential. The Moccamaster extracts fully, which means stale pre-ground coffee will taste flat and bitter. A burr grinder is strongly recommended — it's the difference between good coffee and great coffee.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a buy-it-for-life brewer that delivers consistently excellent coffee, you're willing to descale a couple times a year, and you don't need programmable convenience.

**Skip it if:** You want a set-and-forget machine with a timer, you're on a tight budget, or you don't think you'll notice the difference between this and a $150 brewer.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's expensive, it's minimalist, and it demands a bit of maintenance. But for pure brew quality and longevity, nothing else in this category touches it. This is the last drip coffee maker you'll buy — if you take care of it.
