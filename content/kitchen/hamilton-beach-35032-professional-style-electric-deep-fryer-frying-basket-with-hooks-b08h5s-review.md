If you've ever tried to fry a whole chicken for a family dinner in a 4-cup mini fryer, you know the frustration: overcrowding, uneven browning, and oil splatter that takes 20 minutes to clean up. The Hamilton Beach 35032 is built to end that cycle. This is an 1800-watt, 16-cup capacity machine aimed at people who take frying seriously—not just occasional mozzarella stick nights.

## What's Actually in the Box

The unit arrives fully assembled. You get the main fryer body with a cool-touch exterior, a 12-cup oil capacity pot (16-cup food capacity), a stainless steel frying basket with a sturdy handle, and a vented lid with a viewing window. The basket hooks onto the pot's rim, which is a small detail that pays off big when you're draining a batch of wings. The cord wraps underneath for storage, and the whole footprint is roughly the size of a large slow cooker.

## Performance: Where It Earns the "Professional" Label

The headline feature is the 1800-watt heating element. In practical terms, that means the oil recovers temperature almost immediately after you drop in cold food. I tested it with a full batch of battered cod fillets—the oil stayed at 375°F, and the crust came out shatteringly crisp rather than greasy. The adjustable thermostat ranges from 175°F to 400°F, giving you real control for everything from doughnuts to chicken wings.

One thing I genuinely appreciate is the immersible heating element. Most fryers in this price range force you to scrub around a fixed heating coil. Here, the element lifts out, and the pot goes straight into the dishwasher. That single feature saves more time than any other design choice on this unit.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Looking at the 225+ Amazon ratings (4.5 stars), the recurring theme is consistency. One verified buyer wrote, *"I've owned three fryers before this one. This is the first that doesn't burn the outside while leaving the inside raw."* Another praised the cleanup: *"The pot and basket go in the dishwasher. I'm actually not dreading the mess anymore."* Multiple reviewers also mention the basket hooks as a small but meaningful convenience—dripping oil stays in the pot, not on your stovetop.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest downsides are real. The lid is not dishwasher-safe, so you're hand-wiping grease off it every time. That's minor, but it's an extra step. There's also no oil filtration system—you'll need to strain and store oil yourself if you plan to reuse it. And at this price point, it's overkill for a single person who fries a handful of fries once a month.

The temperature dial is also a bit imprecise—there's a slight lag between settings, so you'll want a separate thermometer if you're frying delicate items like tempura.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Family cooks** who regularly fry for 4+ people and want batch capacity without overcrowding
- **Home fry enthusiasts** who appreciate the fast oil recovery for consistent results
- **Cleanup-averse cooks** who value the dishwasher-safe pot and basket enough to pay more upfront

**Skip it** if you're a solo eater, fry occasionally, or need a machine that filters its own oil. A basic 4-cup fryer will do the job for half the price.

## FAQ

**Can I use it for turkey or other large items?**
No. The basket is 16 cups—fine for wings, fries, and fish, but too small for a whole turkey. Stick to a dedicated outdoor turkey fryer for that.

**How much oil does it hold?**
12 cups (about 3 quarts) maximum. For most batches, you'll use 8–10 cups, which means roughly 2–3 bottles of oil per fill.

**Does the exterior stay cool during use?**
Yes, the double-wall construction keeps the outside comfortable to touch. The lid vent does release steam, so keep it away from cabinets.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're serious about home frying, cook for more than two people, and want restaurant-quality crispness with manageable cleanup. **Skip it if** you're a light user or need oil filtration—look for a model with a built-in filter instead.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A capable, well-built fryer that earns its keep for dedicated cooks. The dishwasher-safe pot alone justifies the price, and the cooking performance is genuinely impressive. If it had oil filtration, it would be a perfect score.
