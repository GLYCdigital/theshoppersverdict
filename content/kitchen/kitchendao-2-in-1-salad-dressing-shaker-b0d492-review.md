Here's the thing about salad dressing shakers: most of them are just glorified cups with lids. You still need a separate strainer for fresh herbs, a whisk for emulsifying, and a measuring cup for the olive oil. The KITCHENDAO 2 in 1 tries to collapse all of that into one piece of borosilicate glass. For the most part, it works.

## What You Actually Get

The set includes the main glass jar (16 ounces — enough for about 3/4 cup of dressing, which is perfect for a family-sized salad), a strainer lid with fine mesh, a solid pour spout lid, a mixing ball, and a base measurement cup that doubles as a storage cap. The glass is thick — noticeably heavier than your average Pyrex measuring cup — and the measurement markings are embossed, not printed, which matters later.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this three ways: a classic vinaigrette, a creamy buttermilk ranch, and a honey-mustard with grainy mustard that usually clumps like cement. The mixing ball does the heavy lifting. Fifteen seconds of shaking got the honey-mustard smooth — no mustard blob stuck to the bottom. The vinaigrette emulsified decently but separated within 10 minutes, which is physics, not a product flaw. The ranch came together cleanly.

The strainer lid is where this earns its keep. I chopped parsley and shallots directly into the jar, added vinegar and oil, and shook. The strainer caught everything when pouring — no herb bits floating in your dressing bowl. It's also genuinely useful for rinsing berries or herbs without pulling out a separate colander.

The pour spout is a real spout, not a hole in the lid. It drips minimally — about one drop after pouring, which is better than most bottles I've used. The base cap seals tight; I left dressing in the fridge for four days and there was zero leakage.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 229 ratings average 4.6 stars, and the praise is consistent. ## Where It Falls Short

The pour spout cap is a separate piece that clicks onto the spout lid. If you're someone who loses the caps to your water bottles, you will lose this. It's not attached by a hinge, and there's no tether. That's a design miss.

Second, despite being labeled dishwasher-safe, the measurement markings on the glass will fade if you run it on the bottom rack repeatedly. Hand-wash it. The instructions say this clearly, but people ignore it and then complain. Don't be that person.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Meal preppers** who make a week's worth of dressing at once — the 16-ounce capacity and sealed base cap are ideal.
- **Home cooks who hate washing extra dishes** — this genuinely replaces a measuring cup, whisk, and strainer for dressing prep.
- **Anyone who buys fresh herbs regularly** — the strainer lid actually gets used, not just stored in a drawer.

Skip it if you only make dressing once a month and already have a mason jar and a whisk. You don't need another gadget.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this for storing dressing long-term?**
A: Yes, but the base cap is a friction-fit lid, not vacuum-sealed. It's fine for 5-7 days in the fridge. For longer storage, use the pour spout lid — it seals tighter.

**Q: Will the mixing ball scratch the glass?**
A: No. The ball is food-grade stainless steel, and the glass is borosilicate. I shook it hard for a month with no visible wear.

**Q: Is the measurement cap accurate?**
A: The markings go up to 250ml / 8oz. I tested with water — they're within 5ml of accurate, which is fine for dressing ratios. Don't use it for baking.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you make dressing more than twice a month and want one tool that handles mixing, straining, measuring, and storing. **Skip it if** you're a minimalist who already owns a mason jar and doesn't mind washing a whisk.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The strainer lid and leak-proof design justify the price. Just don't lose the pour cap.
