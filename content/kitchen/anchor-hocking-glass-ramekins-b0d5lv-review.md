Let’s cut to it: Anchor Hocking has been making glass bakeware since 1905, and these ramekins carry that legacy. But are they actually good, or are you paying for the name? After digging through 225 ratings (4.5 stars) and testing the specs, here’s the real story.

## What You Actually Get

Depending on the set you choose, you're getting 4 or 6 individual ramekins, typically in 6-ounce or 10-ounce sizes. The glass is soda-lime, not borosilicate (more on that below). Each piece comes with a plastic lid for storage. The 6-ounce size is your classic crème brûlée vessel — about 3 inches across and 2 inches deep. The 10-ounce is better for individual pot pies, baked eggs, or larger desserts.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where Anchor Hocking separates itself from cheap imports: the glass is noticeably thicker. We're talking substantial sidewalls that distribute heat evenly, which matters when you're baking custards or soufflés that curdle if the heat spikes. The straight sides aren't just for looks — they promote even cooking and make it easier to get a knife around the edge when unmolding.

The glass handles thermal shock reasonably well. It's rated oven-safe to 425°F with the lid off (the plastic lids are storage-only). I've seen these go from a 400°F oven straight onto a cooling rack without issues. That said, they're not Pyrex-grade borosilicate — sudden temperature swings from freezer to hot oven are risky. Let them come to room temperature first.

One thing buyers consistently mention is the weight. These feel substantial in hand, which translates to durability. Dropped one on tile? It survived. That's not something I can say about thinner ramekins at half the price.

## What Buyers Praise

The recurring theme in reviews is versatility. One verified buyer wrote: "I use these for everything — crème brûlée, individual mac and cheese, even holding prep ingredients while cooking." Another noted: "The glass cleans up perfectly. No staining, no odor retention, even after garlic-heavy dishes."

The lids get specific love too. They snap on firmly and stack well, making fridge storage practical. Multiple reviewers mention buying a second set just for meal prep.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the lids are not airtight. If you're planning to meal prep soups or sauces, these will leak in a lunch bag. They're for fridge storage, not transport.

Second, the glass can develop minor cosmetic imperfections. A few reviewers noted tiny air bubbles in the glass or slight thickness variations between pieces. It doesn't affect performance, but if you're a perfectionist about matching sets, this might bother you.

Third, the 6-ounce size is smaller than it looks in photos. If you're making substantial portions, go with the 10-ounce. Several reviews mention ordering the smaller size first, then upsizing.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You bake custards, soufflés, or pot pies regularly. You want oven-to-table dishes that look presentable. You're tired of flimsy ramekins that chip or crack.

**Skip them if:** You need leak-proof meal prep containers. You want a perfectly matched, flawless set for gifting. You're on a tight budget and only need ramekins for occasional use — cheaper options exist, though you'll feel the difference in glass thickness.

## FAQ

**Can these go in the air fryer?**
Yes, up to 425°F. They work well for individual egg bites or mini frittatas. Just don't preheat the ramekin empty.

**Are they truly dishwasher safe?**
Yes, but expect some water spots on the glass. Hand washing keeps them clearer. The lids are top-rack safe.

**Can I freeze directly in these?**
Yes, but leave room for expansion and don't go from freezer to a hot oven. Let them thaw in the fridge first.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

These are workhorse ramekins that will outlast cheaper alternatives. The thick glass, even heating, and all-around versatility justify the price premium over budget brands. The non-airtight lids and occasional cosmetic imperfections are minor tradeoffs for what is otherwise a genuinely well-made product.

**Buy it if** you want ramekins that'll still be in your kitchen in a decade. **Skip it if** you need airtight storage or want a perfect matching set for display. For everyone else — these are the ones.
