If you buy microwave popcorn in boxes of pre-buttered bags, you're paying for paper, chemicals, and a fake-butter coating that lingers on your fingers. The W&P Microwave Silicone Popcorn Popper Maker takes a different angle: pop loose kernels in plain silicone, add your own fat and salt, and skip the disposable waste entirely. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, it's clearly working for a lot of people. But it's not a perfect replacement for your bagged-popcorn habit — here's the honest breakdown.

## What you actually get

The popper is a collapsible silicone bowl, roughly 9 inches across when open, with a vented lid that has a built-in butter well on top. The design is simple: kernels go in the bottom, butter goes in the top tray, and the whole thing goes in the microwave. As the butter melts, it drips through small holes onto the popping corn. The silicone collapses flat for storage, which is genuinely useful if your cabinets are packed.

It's BPA-free food-grade silicone, and the entire thing goes in the dishwasher. No plastic parts, no metal, no non-stick coating to flake off over time.

## How it performs in real use

The popper handles about 1/3 cup of kernels per batch — that yields roughly 8 cups of popped corn. Pop time runs 2.5 to 4 minutes depending on your microwave wattage. The first time you use it, you'll need to listen carefully; the popping slows noticeably when it's done, and pulling it early leaves a frustrating number of unpopped kernels.

The butter tray is the standout feature. It melts and distributes evenly without you having to stop the microwave, shake the bowl, and risk burning yourself on steam. For a gadget under $20, that's thoughtful design.

Cleanup is genuinely easy — rinse it, run it through the dishwasher, done. No greasy paper bag to throw away, no chemical residue.

## What buyers consistently praise

Reviewers repeatedly mention how much money they save buying kernels in bulk instead of boxed bags. One top reviewer notes, "I've had this for three months and it's already paid for itself in what I'm not spending on microwave bags." Another calls out the storage benefit: "It folds flat as a pancake — I hang it on a hook inside my cabinet door."

The lack of weird aftertaste also comes up often. Because you control the oil and salt, the popcorn tastes like actual popcorn, not artificial butter flavoring.

## Where it falls short

The batch size is the biggest limitation. At 1/3 cup of kernels, you're making a snack for one or two people, not a movie-night bowl for the family. If you're popping for more than two, you'll run back-to-back batches.

Unpopped kernels are also a real issue if you're not paying attention. The window between perfectly popped and slightly burnt is narrow — maybe 20 seconds. Undercook it and you're picking out a dozen hard kernels per bowl. Several reviewers mention this exact frustration.

One more thing: silicone absorbs odors. If you leave it sitting dirty for a day, it picks up butter smells that linger. Wash it promptly and this isn't a problem, but it's worth knowing.

## Who should buy this

This is for the person who eats popcorn a few times a week, wants to stop buying boxes of single-use bags, and doesn't mind a little trial-and-error to nail the timing. It's also great for small kitchens, dorm rooms, RVs, or anyone with limited storage — the collapsible design is a real space-saver.

Skip it if you regularly make popcorn for a crowd, or if you want set-it-and-forget-it convenience. A microwave bag is still more foolproof.

## FAQ

**Can you use it without oil?**
Yes, but the popcorn will be drier. A teaspoon of oil or a pat of butter in the bottom helps with flavor and slightly reduces unpopped kernels.

**Is it really dishwasher safe?**
Yes, top rack or bottom rack both work. Hand washing is faster and prevents odor buildup.

**How long does a batch take?**
Typically 2.5 to 4 minutes depending on microwave wattage. Start at 2.5 minutes and listen — when pops slow to one every 2-3 seconds, it's done.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a regular popcorn eater who wants to cut waste, save money on bulk kernels, and don't mind dialing in your microwave timing. The collapsible design and butter tray make it a genuinely well-thought-out gadget.

**Skip it if** you need family-sized batches or want zero learning curve. This is a two-serving tool that rewards a little attention.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a solid, practical upgrade from bagged popcorn with real limitations on batch size, but excellent value for solo snackers and small households.
