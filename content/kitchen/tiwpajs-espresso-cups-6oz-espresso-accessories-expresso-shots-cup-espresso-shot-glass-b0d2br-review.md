If you’ve ever burned your fingertips on a flimsy demitasse cup or watched your espresso go cold before you finished the first sip, the TIWPAJS 6OZ Espresso Cups are worth a serious look. These aren’t your average shot glasses dressed up as coffeeware — they’re borosilicate glass cups that punch well above their price point.

**What You Actually Get**

The set includes six 6-ounce cups. That’s the headline feature: 6 ounces, not the usual 2-3 ounces you get with most espresso cup sets. This matters more than you’d think. A traditional double shot lands around 2 ounces, but many home espresso machines — especially the popular Breville models — pull shots with more crema and volume. A 6oz cup gives you room for a proper lungo, an Americano, or a flat white without switching vessels.

The cups are made from borosilicate glass, the same material used in lab glassware. It handles thermal shock well, meaning you can go from a cold rinse straight into a hot shot without worrying about cracking. The set also includes matching saucers, which are surprisingly sturdy and don’t wobble on uneven surfaces.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested these with a Gaggia Classic Pro pulling shots at 200°F. The borosilicate glass handled the heat without issue — no cracks, no stress marks. The cups warmed up quickly, and here’s the thing that surprised me: they held temperature better than the ceramic cups I was using before. The thin glass transfers heat to the coffee more slowly than thick ceramic, so your last sip is still hot.

One thing you’ll notice immediately: there are no handles. That’s a deliberate design choice that works for most people — you grip the cup by the body, and because it’s thin glass, the heat dissipates quickly enough that you can hold it comfortably. But if you fill it to the absolute brim, your fingertips will feel it. Don’t overfill and you’ll be fine.

The saucers are a nice touch. They’re not just decorative — they catch drips from the portafilter and give you a place to rest a spoon. The glazed surface wipes clean easily, and the cups themselves are dishwasher safe. Multiple reviewers confirmed they’ve run them through hundreds of cycles without clouding or etching.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

Scrolling through the 229 ratings (4.6 stars average), a few themes keep coming up. One reviewer put it simply: “Finally, a shot glass that doesn’t feel like a toy.” Another mentioned using them for both espresso and matcha — the 6oz size works for a lot more than just coffee. A third noted the packaging was “overkill but appreciated” — each cup comes individually wrapped, which matters if you’re gifting these.

**Where They Fall Short**

Honest downsides: the rim is thin. If you’re someone who clinks cups together when storing them in a cabinet, you risk chipping the edge. It’s not fragile — borosilicate is tough — but it’s not indestructible. Also, there’s no handle, which I already mentioned. Some people with larger hands find gripping a 2.5-inch diameter cup awkward. And if you’re strictly a traditionalist who wants a 2oz demitasse, these are bigger than you’re used to — that’s a feature, not a bug, but worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Home espresso enthusiasts** pulling shots on machines like the Breville Barista Express or Gaggia Classic — the 6oz size fits your workflow.
- **People who drink Americanos or lungos** without switching cups mid-drink.
- **Gift-givers** who want a practical, attractive set that won’t sit in a box. The packaging is genuinely gift-ready.

Skip these if you only drink straight ristretto shots and want a traditional 2oz demitasse experience. Also skip if you’re clumsy — the thin rim won’t survive a drop onto tile.

**Q: Can I use these for cappuccinos or lattes?**

**Q: Are they really dishwasher safe?**
