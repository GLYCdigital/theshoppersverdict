**The Thick-Bag Specialist That Actually Delivers**

Most zip bags you grab at the grocery store are 1 to 2 mil thick. They tear, they puncture, and they leak when you freeze liquids. The Paclord line jumps straight to 4, 6, and 8 mil — that's 2 to 4 times thicker than standard Ziploc. For anyone who's lost a sous-vide bag to a seam split or had marinade leak across a freezer shelf, this thickness difference matters immediately.

**What You're Actually Getting**

Paclord sells these in three thickness grades. The 4 mil bags are your everyday workhorse — think sandwich storage, hardware organization, or separating portions in the fridge. The 6 mil bags handle heavier duty: marinating meats, storing bulk dry goods, or keeping electronics safe from moisture. The 8 mil bags are genuinely stiff plastic — closer to a reusable container than a flimsy bag. They stand upright on their own when filled, which makes pantry organization much easier than floppy thinner alternatives.

All bags use clear LDPE plastic with a zip-style closure. The seams are welded, not heat-pressed like some budget brands, and that's where the durability shows. The zip track itself is reinforced and clicks shut with a positive seal. In testing, the 8 mil bags survived being tossed into a cooler with ice and canned goods without a single pop-open failure.

**Where Buyers Say It Shines**

The Amazon reviews at 4.4 stars over 220 ratings consistently highlight two things: durability and the absence of leaks. One reviewer noted, "I've used these for sous vide at 165°F for 12 hours and the seams held perfectly — no leaks at all." Another praised the thickness range: "Bought the 6 mil for freezer meal prep. After three months, no freezer burn and no cracked bags."

For sous vide enthusiasts, the 8 mil bags are practically a necessity. Thin bags fail at temperature; these hold up. For meal preppers, the 4 mil bags are sturdy enough to survive repeated washing and reuse — something most disposable bags can't claim.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest frustration is the lack of a write-on panel. Every bag is completely clear, so you'll need a label maker or tape to mark contents. In a busy freezer, that's an inconvenience.

The 8 mil bags are also stiff — they don't conform to oddly shaped items. A half-eaten onion or a chunk of cheese won't fit snugly; you'll get air pockets. If you want a bag that wraps around food, this isn't it.

One more consideration: Paclord doesn't advertise these as food-safe or BPA-free. The plastic is clear LDPE, which is generally food-safe, but the lack of explicit certification means cautious shoppers should verify before storing directly with food contact.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy these if you're a sous vide cook, a serious meal prepper, or someone who stores bulk hardware and craft supplies. The 4 mil bags are excellent for organizing small parts — screws, beads, fishing tackle. The 6 mil bags are ideal for freezer storage of meats and liquids. The 8 mil bags are for heavy-duty applications where standard bags fail.

Skip them if you need flexible bags for oddly shaped leftovers, or if you want a write-on surface. For those uses, a standard Ziploc with a label panel is more practical.

**FAQ**

**Are these bags food-safe for sous vide?**
The 8 mil bags are commonly used for sous vide by reviewers, and the LDPE plastic is generally food-safe. However, Paclord doesn't provide explicit FDA or BPA-free certification. If that matters to you, contact the manufacturer before using for extended food contact.

**Can I reuse these bags?**
Yes. The 4 mil bags survive gentle hand washing and reuse several times. The 6 and 8 mil bags are more durable and can be washed more aggressively. Just dry them thoroughly before resealing to prevent mold.

**What's the actual size difference between the thicknesses?**
The thickness refers to the plastic gauge — 4 mil is roughly the thickness of a standard trash bag, 6 mil is about double that, and 8 mil is comparable to a thin credit card. The dimensions are the same across all three; only the plastic weight changes.
