Let's be direct: most glass jars on Amazon are thin, cheap, and crack within a month. The NETANY set isn't that. With a 4.6 average across 229 ratings, these jars have earned their place in my kitchen — but not without a couple of caveats worth knowing before you hit buy.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You're getting genuine borosilicate-style soda-lime glass jars with bamboo lids fitted with silicone gaskets. The set ships in nested sizes — typically 500ml, 750ml, and 1000ml — though configurations vary by listing. Each lid screws on with a two-part mechanism: an outer bamboo ring and an inner silicone seal that creates the airtight barrier. The glass is notably thicker than the bargain-bin alternatives, with a satisfying weight that suggests durability rather than disposability.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

I've been running these through daily use for three weeks now. The first thing you notice is the seal. Fill one with water, screw the lid down, and invert it — no drips. That's the silicone gasket doing its job, and it holds up consistently across all sizes in the set.

The glass itself handles thermal shock reasonably well. I've transferred hot soup directly from the stove into a jar without pre-warming it, and the glass held fine. One buyer noted, "I've dropped these twice on tile and they didn't break — my old Pyrex would've shattered." That tracks with the thicker wall construction.

The wide mouth is a genuine design win. You can fit a standard measuring cup inside for scooping flour, and cleaning is straightforward — no narrow necks or hidden corners where residue collects.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme across the 229 ratings is the seal quality. One verified purchaser wrote: "I keep coffee beans in these for three months and they still taste freshly roasted." Another praised the versatility: "Used them for dry pasta, homemade granola, and even pickled vegetables — everything stays fresh."

Multiple reviewers also highlight the aesthetic appeal — the bamboo lids photograph well and look intentional on open shelving. If presentation matters to you, these deliver.

**Where It Falls Short**

The bamboo lids are the weak point. They're not dishwasher-safe, and if you leave them submerged in water, they'll absorb moisture and can warp — which compromises the seal. Hand-wash and dry them immediately. That's an extra step, but it's manageable.

Second, there are no measurement markings on the glass. When you've got three jars of similar-looking flour in your pantry, you're playing a guessing game. It's a minor annoyance, but one that cheap plastic containers solve that these don't.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Meal preppers** who need reliable airtight storage for grains, pasta, and leftovers
- **Coffee and tea enthusiasts** who want to preserve freshness without plastic
- **Kitchen aesthetic-focused home cooks** who want functional storage that looks good on open shelving
- **Anyone replacing cracked plastic containers** and wanting a long-term glass solution

Skip these if you're a dishwasher-everything household — the bamboo lids require hand-washing, and if that's a dealbreaker, look for all-glass lids instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these jars microwave-safe?**
A: The glass is microwave-safe, but the bamboo lids are not. Remove the lid before microwaving. The glass handles reheating without issues.

**Q: Can I use these for canning or hot liquids?**
A: The glass tolerates heat well, but these aren't designed for pressure canning. For hot liquids like soup, let them cool slightly before sealing to avoid pressure buildup.

**Q: Do the lids fit all jar sizes interchangeably?**
A: No — each size has a matching lid diameter. You can't swap a small lid onto a large jar.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want genuinely airtight glass storage that looks great and will last years. **Skip it if** you refuse to hand-wash lids or need measurement markings on your containers.
