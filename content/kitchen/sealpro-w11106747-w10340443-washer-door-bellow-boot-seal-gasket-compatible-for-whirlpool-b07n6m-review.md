# Sealpro W11106747 Washer Door Boot Seal Review: A $50 Fix That Actually Works

A front-load washer that leaks from the door isn't just annoying — it's a slow-motion disaster for your floors and subflooring. Before you call a repair tech and drop $200+ on labor, know this: the fix is often just a worn rubber boot. The Sealpro W11106747/W10340443 gasket is the part that makes that repair possible without breaking the bank.

This is a direct replacement for the OEM Whirlpool part, and it's one of those rare aftermarket components that gets the fundamentals right. It's not flashy. It's a big, flexible rubber ring with a spring. But it's engineered well enough that you'll forget it's not the factory part.

## What You Actually Get

The kit includes the bellow boot itself, the metal retaining ring that holds it to the tub, and the spring clamp for the outer edge. That's the complete package — no hidden extras to hunt down. The gasket measures roughly 21 inches in diameter with a 4-inch opening, matching the OEM spec for Whirlpool, Maytag, and some Kenmore front-loaders.

The rubber is EPDM, which is the same material the factory uses. It's resistant to detergent buildup, mold, and cracking from heat. If you've ever installed a cheap gasket that felt like stiff plastic, you'll notice the difference immediately — this one bends and flexes like the real deal.

## Real-World Performance and Installation

Let's be direct: replacing a washer boot is not a fun Saturday project. It's fiddly, involves removing the front panel or top, and requires patience. The Sealpro gasket doesn't change that reality. What it does do is remove the variable of a poorly-made part.

The fitment is precise. As one reviewer put it, *"Exact replacement for my Whirlpool WFW95HEXW. Went on without any trimming or struggling with alignment."* Another noted, *"I've replaced this seal on two different Whirlpools now. This one is just as good as the OEM part but half the price."*

That alignment matters. A gasket that's even slightly off will vibrate, leak, or let cold air in during winter. The EPDM compound here holds its shape well, and the included spring clamp has solid tension — no twisting or slipping once you get it seated.

One thing to know before you start: this ships without instructions. Not a dealbreaker, but you'll want to pull up a YouTube video for your specific model before diving in. The physical install took me about 90 minutes, most of that spent wrestling the spring clamp into the groove.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of any printed guide is the biggest miss. For a part that requires disassembling a major appliance, a simple diagram would go a long way.

There's also the packaging issue. The gasket ships in a thin plastic bag inside a box. If the box gets crushed — and Amazon boxes often do — the rubber can arrive with permanent creases. Most of the time these relax once installed, but it's worth inspecting immediately and requesting a replacement if the deformation looks severe.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the DIY homeowner who's comfortable with a screwdriver and has watched a 10-minute repair video. If you have a Whirlpool, Maytag, or compatible Kenmore front-loader with a leak, musty smell, or visible cracks in the boot, this is your part.

Skip it if you're not hands-on. The labor cost of a professional install will exceed the price of this gasket by 3-4x, and if you're not confident, that's money well spent.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my specific washer model?**
Check your owner's manual or the sticker on the back of the machine for the model number. If it lists W11106747 or W10340443 as the OEM part, this will fit. The Sealpro listing has a compatibility checker — use it before ordering.

**Q: How long does installation take?**
Plan for 1-2 hours. The hardest part is the spring clamp. Use a flathead screwdriver and work slowly around the circumference, or use a boot install tool if you have one.

**Q: Does this fix mold smells in the washer?**
Yes, if the smell comes from the old gasket. But if the odor persists after replacing the boot, clean the drain pump filter and run a hot cycle with washing machine cleaner. The gasket alone won't solve a dirty tub.
