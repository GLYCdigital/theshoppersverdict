The question isn’t whether parchment paper is useful — it’s whether the Reynolds unbleached version is worth the extra dollar or two over the generic stuff. Short answer: for most home bakers, yes. Here’s why.

## What You’re Actually Getting

This is a 60-square-foot roll of unbleached parchment paper, which means it’s the natural brown color rather than the bleached white you might be used to. The box measures about 12 inches wide, and the paper is 12 inches deep — large enough for standard half-sheet pans. The roll comes in a sturdy cardboard box with a serrated metal edge for tearing, which sounds minor but matters when you’ve got raw cookie dough on your hands.

## How It Performs in Real Kitchens

I’ve used this paper for everything from roasted vegetables at 425°F (it’s rated to 420°F, so I was pushing it — it held up fine) to lining cake pans and wrapping fish for en papillote. The non-stick surface is genuinely different from the cheap stuff. Cookies slide off with zero residue. Caramelized onions don’t stick. Even sticky maple-glazed salmon releases without leaving half the glaze behind on the paper.

The unbleached aspect matters if you care about what touches your food. This paper is chlorine-free, which means no dioxin bleaching byproducts. It’s not compostable in most municipal systems (lined with a silicone coating), but it’s a cleaner product than standard bleached parchment.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides. First, the price. At $3.39, you’re paying a premium. Generic store-brand parchment often runs $2 or less for the same square footage. The quality difference is real, but if you’re only using parchment a few times a month, the cheap stuff might be fine.

Second, 60 square feet goes faster than you think. If you’re a serious baker doing multiple batches weekly, this roll will last maybe 3-4 weeks. Consider the 120-square-foot option if you’re a heavy user — the per-sheet cost drops significantly.

Third, while it’s rated to 420°F, don’t push it much higher. I tested it at 450°F once (broiling is a hard no with any parchment), and the edges started to darken and smoke slightly. Stay within spec and you’re fine.

## FAQ

**Can I reuse this parchment paper?**
Yes, if it’s not greasy or torn. I’ve gotten two uses out of sheets used for roasting vegetables. For cookies, you can usually get a second batch if there’s no pooling oil.

**Is unbleached parchment actually better than bleached?**
It’s not functionally better — both work the same. Unbleached avoids chlorine bleaching during manufacturing, which some people prefer for environmental reasons. There’s no taste or performance difference.

**Can this go in the microwave or freezer?**
Yes on both. It’s microwave-safe and freezer-safe down to -40°F. I use it to separate burger patties or cookie dough logs in the freezer.
