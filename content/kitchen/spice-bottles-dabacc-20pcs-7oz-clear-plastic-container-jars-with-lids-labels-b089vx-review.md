**Full Review:**

Let’s be honest: most spice jars are either flimsy glass that shatters when you drop them or cheap plastic that stains and warps. The Dabacc 20-piece set sits in a sweet spot — it’s a bulk solution for organizing a chaotic spice drawer without breaking the bank.

**What You Actually Get**

This isn’t just a pile of jars. The kit includes 20 clear 7oz containers, 20 black shaker lids, 20 airtight storage lids, 20 pre-printed labels (think oregano, paprika, cumin, garlic powder — the usual suspects), plus a set of blank labels and a marker. You also get a small funnel, which sounds trivial until you’ve tried pouring oregano from a bag into a narrow jar. That funnel saves real mess.

The jars are made from a thick, food-grade PET plastic. I’m not going to claim they’re indestructible, but they have a substantial heft that immediately signals “not disposable.” The 7oz capacity is the standout spec here — it fits a full standard spice bottle refill or a bulk-bin purchase without needing to decant halfway through.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

I’ve been using these for three months in a working kitchen. The double-wall design keeps their shape even after repeated use. The shaker lids have a flip-top mechanism that clicks open and closed, and the perforations are sized well for whole spices like cumin seeds or dried basil.

However, there’s a catch. If you’re storing fine powders — cinnamon, cocoa, confectioner’s sugar — the shaker holes are too large. You’ll get a cloud of powder instead of a controlled sprinkle. For those, use the solid storage lids and a separate measuring spoon. It’s a limitation, but not a dealbreaker for most.

The airtight lids are the real workhorses. They press on with a satisfying click, and I’ve had zero issues with moisture clumping — even in a humid coastal kitchen. That said, you must press firmly until you hear the click. Half-pressed lids will let air in, and reviewers have noted this too. One buyer said, *“The lids are great once you figure out they need a solid push — not a gentle tap.”* That matches my experience exactly.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The overwhelming theme in 235 ratings is organization achieved. One reviewer wrote, *“I finally threw away the mismatched baggies and old jars. My spice rack looks like a professional chef’s setup.”* Another noted the size as the key selling point: *“Most sets are tiny 4oz jars. These 7oz containers actually hold enough that I’m not refilling every week.”*

The label quality also gets consistent props. They’re printed on a matte, water-resistant stock that survives wiping with a damp cloth. After a few weeks, mine haven’t smudged or curled at the edges.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let’s talk about durability over time. The plastic is sturdy, but it’s still plastic. If you run these through the dishwasher regularly, you’ll notice micro-scratches on the clear walls that dull the “glass-like” look. Hand washing is easy — they’re not greasy if you’re storing dry goods — so just do that.

The labels, while nice, are not fully waterproof. Soaking them will make the ink run. Again, wipe-clean only.

And one more thing: the shaker lids have a small gap around the hinge. If you store the jars on their side in a drawer, fine powders can sift out over time. Store them upright and you’re fine.

**Who Should Buy This**

This set is ideal for three specific shoppers:

- **The bulk buyer** — if you buy spices in bulk from co-ops or online, these jars have the capacity to hold a full pound of most herbs.
- **The kitchen organizer** — if you’re tired of mismatched, rusting tins, this set gives you instant visual uniformity.
- **The meal prepper** — these work great for storing dry rubs, seasoning blends, or even homemade taco seasoning in batches.

Skip it if you primarily use ground powders and want a dedicated shaker for every spice — you’ll need a separate set of fine-hole lids. Also skip if you insist on glass for every storage need; this is a plastic product, through and through.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these jars truly leak-proof?**
A: With the solid lids pressed firmly until they click, yes. The shaker lids are not travel-proof — don’t toss them in a bag with loose contents.

**Q: Can I use these in the microwave?**
A: No. They’re PET plastic, which is not microwave-safe. Decant spices before heating.

**Q: Will the labels fit standard spice names?**
A: They cover about 20 common spices. The blank labels and marker cover everything else — just write clearly and let them dry before sticking.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a no-nonsense, affordable way to declutter your spice drawer and don’t mind hand-washing plastic containers. The 7oz size is generous, the build is solid for the price, and the included labels make setup a ten-minute job.

**Skip it if** you need fine-powder shakers, prefer glass, or you’re the type to throw everything in the dishwasher.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely useful kitchen organizer with a couple of minor design quirks that are easy to work around. For the price, it’s hard to beat the capacity-to-cost ratio.
