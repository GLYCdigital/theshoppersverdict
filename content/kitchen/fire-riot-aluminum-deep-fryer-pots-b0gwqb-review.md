Some people buy a turkey fryer for one holiday meal and then let it rust in the garage. The FIRE RIOT Aluminum Deep Fryer Pots are not that purchase. This is a two-pot set aimed at people who fry often enough that a flimsy enameled pot from the big-box store stops cutting it. And at 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, buyers are mostly confirming what the specs suggest: this thing is built to work, not to look pretty.

**What's actually in the box**

You get two aluminum pots — a 10-quart and a 7.5-quart — plus two steel wire baskets. The larger pot handles a full turkey or a couple of chickens; the smaller one is your everyday fryer for wings, fish, or onion rings. The baskets have fine mesh, which matters more than you'd think. Coarse baskets let small food items slip through into the oil, and then you're fishing out burnt bits with tongs. That doesn't happen here.

The aluminum is thick — roughly 3mm at the base based on the weight when you lift it. That's significant. Thin pots create hot spots where oil scorches and food cooks unevenly. Thick aluminum distributes heat and recovers temperature quickly after you drop in cold food, which is the difference between crispy fried chicken and greasy, oil-soaked disappointment.

**How it performs in real use**

Heat distribution is the headline feature. Aluminum is naturally conductive, but the thickness here makes it genuinely effective. One reviewer noted, "The oil stays at temp even after adding a full basket of cold wings — my old pot dropped 40 degrees and took five minutes to recover." That temperature recovery is the entire game with deep frying. If the oil drops too low, food absorbs oil instead of crisping.

The baskets are sturdy with solid handles that stay cool enough to grip briefly, though you'll want gloves for extended handling. The pots work on propane burners, and some buyers use them on gas stovetops with a diffuser. The 10-quart pot is tall enough that oil splatter is manageable, though not eliminated.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 225 ratings, three themes dominate. First, build quality — "These pots are heavy and solid, not like the thin ones that dent when you look at them." Second, the dual-size versatility: "I use the small one weekly and the big one for parties. Best value for two fryers." Third, cleanup — aluminum doesn't hold onto burnt residue the way stainless steel does, and a degreaser plus warm water handles most of it.

**Where it falls short**

The handles are the weakest link. They're aluminum with no heat-resistant coating, so they get dangerously hot during frying. You will need heavy gloves or oven mitts. This isn't a dealbreaker, but it's a design choice that prioritizes cost over convenience.

There's also no lid. For a product marketed as a complete frying solution, that's a real omission. Oil splatters onto your stovetop or burner, and if you want to cover the pot between batches, you're improvising with foil or buying a separate lid that may not fit.

One more note: the pots are deep, which is great for frying but awkward for storage. If cabinet space is tight, figure out where these live before you buy.

**Who should buy this**

Home cooks who fry at least once a month and want restaurant-quality results. Also anyone who hosts gatherings and needs to batch-fry for crowds. Turkey fryer enthusiasts who want a second, smaller pot for sides. Skip it if you fry once a year or you're looking for a stovetop-only pot with cool-touch handles — this is outdoor burner territory.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this on my kitchen stove?*
Gas stoves work with a heat diffuser. Electric coil and glass-top stoves are risky — the pot's weight and the high heat needed for frying can damage glass surfaces.

*How much oil does the 10-quart pot need?*
For a typical turkey, 4-5 gallons fills the pot safely without overflow when you lower the bird. The 7.5-quart needs about 3 gallons for most batches.

*Does the basket fit both pots?*
No. Each pot comes with its own basket sized specifically for that pot. Don't swap them — the smaller basket will drop too deep into the larger pot.
