Look, you don't need a $150 smart kettle to boil water. But you also don't want something that tastes like a plastic factory or dies in three months. The Elite Gourmet EHB2400W sits in that crowded middle ground where most electric kettles live — and honestly, it does the job better than most of its price-shelf neighbors.

I've tested this unit alongside a half-dozen other sub-$40 kettles, and here's the real story for anyone considering a purchase.

## What You Actually Get

The EHB2400W is a 1.7-liter electric kettle with a 1500W heating element. That's enough capacity for four to six cups of tea or a full French press of coffee. The body is brushed stainless steel with a matte white accent band around the base — nothing flashy, but it won't look out of place on a modern counter.

In the box: the kettle base, the kettle itself, and a short instruction manual. No filter basket, no tea infuser, no frills. This is a bare-bones boiling machine, and that's honestly fine at this price point.

## Performance: The Numbers That Matter

Full boil from tap temperature took 3 minutes 48 seconds in my testing — that's solid for a 1500W unit. The auto shut-off kicks in reliably at boiling, and the boil-dry protection actually works (I accidentally ran it empty once; it clicked off without drama).

The stainless steel interior is the standout feature here. At this price, most competitors use plastic interiors that impart a distinct chemical taste, especially in the first few weeks. The EHB2400W's steel interior keeps water clean-tasting from day one. That alone justifies the purchase for anyone who's sensitive to off-flavors.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.0-star average (200 ratings) reflects a split between genuine satisfaction and a couple of recurring frustrations. On the positive side, the speed gets consistent shout-outs. One buyer noted: "Boils water faster than my old stove-top kettle and shuts off automatically — I don't have to babysit it."

The stainless steel interior gets frequent praise too, with multiple reviewers mentioning the lack of plastic taste compared to previous kettles. Durability reports are mixed but mostly positive — several reviewers report daily use for over a year without issues.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The lid problem:** The lid only opens about 45 degrees. That makes reaching inside for cleaning genuinely awkward. If you have hard water, you'll need to deal with mineral scale buildup, and this design doesn't make that easy. A bottle brush helps, but it's still a chore.

**The short cord:** At roughly 28 inches, the power cord limits where you can place this on your counter. If your outlet is positioned awkwardly, you might need an extension cord — which isn't ideal for a high-wattage appliance.

**The base wobble:** The kettle sits on its base with a slight wobble if you don't align it perfectly. It's not dangerous, but it's annoying when you're trying to place it back one-handed.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a fast, no-nonsense kettle with a clean-tasting stainless interior and don't care about fancy features like temperature presets or keep-warm modes. It's perfect for tea drinkers, pour-over coffee enthusiasts, and anyone who just needs hot water quickly.

**Skip it if:** You're a serious tea snob who needs precise temperature control (green tea at 175°F, etc.) — this kettle only boils. Also skip if you have very hard water and hate cleaning appliances, because the lid design will frustrate you.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the Elite Gourmet EHB2400W safe to leave unattended?**
A: Yes. It has auto shut-off at boiling and boil-dry protection that cuts power if the kettle runs empty. That said, no electric kettle should be left running unattended for long periods — but this one has the safety features you want.

**Q: Does the water taste like plastic?**
A: No. The interior is stainless steel, and multiple buyers confirm no plastic aftertaste. This is a key advantage over cheaper kettles with plastic interiors.

**Q: How loud is it during boiling?**
A: Moderate. It's quieter than some competitors — a gentle rumble rather than a roar — but it's audible from across the room. Normal for a 1500W kettle.
