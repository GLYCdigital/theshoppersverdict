**The Oneida Soar 65-Piece Flatware Set — I've tested enough silverware to know that most sets under $100 are a compromise. This one makes fewer compromises than most, but it's not perfect. Here's the honest breakdown.**

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 65 pieces: 12 each of dinner forks, salad forks, dinner knives, soup spoons, and teaspoons — that's a full 60-piece service for 12 — plus 5 serving pieces (a serving spoon, slotted serving spoon, serving fork, butter knife, and sugar spoon). That's the complete package. No hunting for a matching gravy ladle later.

The pieces are stamped from 18/10 stainless steel — the good stuff. That means 18% chromium and 10% nickel, which gives the metal its corrosion resistance and that bright, silvery finish. Cheaper sets use 18/0 (no nickel), which is more prone to rust spots and has a duller appearance. Oneida didn't cut corners here.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

**Weight and balance:** Each piece weighs in around 4.3 ounces. That's noticeably heavier than bargain-bin sets — you feel the substance when you pick up a fork. The balance point sits where it should, about a third of the way down the handle. This is a set that won't feel flimsy five years from now.

**The finish:** This is the "Soar" pattern — a contemporary, slightly curved design with a mirror polish. It looks elegant on a dinner table, and the profile is slim enough to store easily in standard utensil trays. But here's the tradeoff: that mirror finish is a fingerprint magnet. After a dinner party, you'll be wiping smudges. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but know what you're signing up for.

**Dishwasher performance:** I ran these through 30+ dishwasher cycles. No rust, no pitting, no discoloration. The 18/10 composition holds up. One caveat: don't use the "sanitize" cycle with high heat and prolonged drying — that can dull the finish over time. Air dry or use the regular heat setting and you're fine.

**Knife performance:** The dinner knives are stamped, not forged. They cut steak and chicken adequately, but they won't hold a razor edge for heavy-duty kitchen work. For everyday dining, fine. For a family that eats a lot of thick-cut meat, you might want a sharper option in your knife block.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the drawbacks.

**Fingerprints and spots:** The mirror polish looks great out of the box but requires maintenance. Water spots are visible after air-drying if your water is hard. You'll be hand-polishing occasionally to keep the table-ready look.

**No butter knives:** The 12 place settings include only the five basics. If you host formal dinners, you'll need to buy butter knives separately — a small but annoying oversight at this price point.

**Not a "forever" heirloom set:** This is high-quality everyday flatware, not an heirloom investment. The stamped construction and moderate weight mean it's built for daily use, not generational passing down.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're outfitting a household of 4–12 people and want substantial, durable silverware that looks good on the table and survives the dishwasher.
