Most sparkling water makers look like they escaped from a 1980s sci-fi set — all glossy plastic and aggressive angles. The Mysoda Woody takes the opposite approach. As shown in the product image, it's wrapped in real birch veneer, giving it the warmth of a wooden cutting board rather than a lab instrument. But does this Finnish-designed machine back up its looks with performance? I've spent weeks testing it against the usual suspects from SodaStream and Drinkmate, and here's what actually matters.

**What's in the box**

You get the carbonation unit itself, a 1L Tritan plastic bottle, a 60L CO2 cylinder, and a user manual. Note that the CO2 cylinder ships partially filled — Mysoda includes just enough for a few test batches, so budget for a refill or exchange almost immediately. The machine measures roughly 17 inches tall and weighs about 8 pounds with the cylinder installed, so it's stable on the counter but not something you'll want to move around frequently.

**The silent claim is real**

The headline feature here isn't carbonation — it's the absence of noise. Press the button on a SodaStream and you get that theatrical hiss that sounds like a punctured tire. The Woody produces a soft, muffled click and a gentle fizz. It's not completely silent — physics won't allow that — but it's dramatically quieter than anything else in this category. If you carbonate early in the morning while others sleep, or you live in a small apartment where every kitchen sound carries, this matters more than you'd think.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The birch veneer isn't just decorative — it's sustainably sourced and feels substantial. The carbonation mechanism uses a push-button design with no lever to slam, which contributes to both the quiet operation and the solid feel. Carbonation takes three to five presses depending on how fizzy you like it. The downside: there's no pressure gauge, so you're learning by trial and error. After a dozen batches, I found my rhythm — three presses for light fizz, five for aggressive bubbles.

The 1L bottle is dishwasher-safe (top rack only) and uses a standard thread, so replacement bottles are cheap and easy to find. This is a genuine advantage over machines that lock you into proprietary bottles costing $20+ each.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects real satisfaction. One Amazon reviewer wrote, "I was skeptical about the wood finish holding up in a busy kitchen, but six months in it still looks brand new. The silence is the real deal — my old machine sounded like a fire extinguisher." Another noted, "The carbonation is actually stronger than my previous SodaStream. I use fewer presses for the same fizz."

The most common praise centers on three things: the quiet operation, the aesthetic appeal, and the cost of ongoing use. Because it takes standard 60L cylinders, refills run $15–20 at most hardware stores — cheaper than proprietary exchange programs.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the wood veneer, while beautiful, is not indestructible. One reviewer reported scratches after moving the machine repeatedly. It's a countertop appliance — it should live in one spot. Second, the lack of any carbonation indicator means consistency requires attention. If you're the type who wants set-and-forget precision, this machine will frustrate you. Third, the included CO2 cylinder is barely enough for testing — plan for an immediate refill purchase.

**Who should buy this**

This machine is ideal for three specific buyers: the design-conscious homeowner who wants kitchen appliances that don't look like appliances, the light sleeper or early riser who carbonates before the household wakes, and the cost-conscious user who wants to avoid proprietary CO2 systems. It's less ideal for heavy users who carbonate multiple liters daily — the plastic bottle replacement cycle every two years adds up, and the lack of a gauge becomes more annoying with frequent use.

**FAQ**

**Is the Mysoda Woody actually silent?**
Not silent — quiet. It produces a soft click and gentle fizz rather than the loud hiss of most carbonators. It's about 70% quieter than a SodaStream in my testing.

**Can I use SodaStream bottles or CO2 with it?**
No. The Woody uses its own 1L bottles and standard 60L CO2 cylinders. The cylinders are widely available at hardware stores and are cheaper than SodaStream's exchange program.

**How long does the CO2 last?**
A 60L cylinder makes roughly 60 liters of sparkling water — about 60 bottles. Heavy users will refill monthly; casual users every two to three months.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You value quiet operation, want a carbonator that looks like furniture instead of plastic, and don't mind learning carbonation by feel.

**Skip it if:** You need precise carbonation control, plan to use it multiple times daily, or prefer a machine with digital feedback.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Woody delivers on its two promises — silence and style — with solid build quality and lower ongoing costs. The lack of a pressure gauge and the two-year bottle replacement cycle keep it from perfection, but for most home users, this is the most pleasant sparkling water maker you can put on your counter.
