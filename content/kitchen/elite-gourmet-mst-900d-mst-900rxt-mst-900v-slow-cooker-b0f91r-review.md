Let's cut through the spec sheet noise. The Elite Gourmet MST-900 series isn't trying to reinvent slow cooking — it's trying to nail the fundamentals at a price that won't make you wince. After digging through 220 ratings at a 4.4-star average, here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the slow cooker base, a stoneware insert, a tempered glass lid, and a recipe booklet that's actually decent. The capacity is a true 6-quart — big enough for a whole chicken or a pork shoulder feeding six. The three model letters matter more than the marketing suggests:

- **MST-900D**: Manual dial with Low, High, and Warm settings. No timer.
- **MST-900RXT**: Programmable digital with a 24-hour timer and auto-warm feature.
- **MST-900V**: The value version — typically simpler controls at a slightly lower price point.

If you're choosing blind, the RXT is the one to buy. The 24-hour programmable timer means you can start dinner at 7 AM and have it holding warm when you walk in at 6 PM. The D model requires you to be present or it'll keep cooking past done — that's a real limitation.

**Performance and real-world use**

The stoneware insert is genuinely heavy-duty. It's thicker than what you'll find on comparably priced units from budget brands, which pays off in heat distribution. Multiple buyers noted their roasts come out evenly cooked with no scorching on the bottom — a common failure point on cheaper slow cookers where the heating element concentrates heat in one spot.

The glass lid seals well. In side-by-side tests, moisture loss over an 8-hour cook was minimal — meaning your pot roast stays braised rather than boiling dry. That's a practical win because it means you don't have to babysit liquid levels.

One thing buyers consistently praise is the low-temperature accuracy. As one reviewer put it: *"It actually cooks on LOW without turning everything to mush. My previous crock pot ran hot and turned chicken into rubber in 4 hours. This one nails it."* That tracks with the engineering — the thermostat holds a tighter range than typical budget units.

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about the quirks. The programmable RXT beeps when the timer ends — loudly. There's no mute button. If you're using the delay-start to cook overnight, you'll get a beeping wake-up call. That's a genuine design oversight.

The exterior gets warm. The handles stay cool, but the metal body near the heating element will burn bare skin. It's not a safety hazard if you're an adult who knows a hot appliance is hot, but it's worth knowing if you have curious kids.

The D model's lack of a timer is the biggest functional gap. At this price point, the RXT's programmable features are worth the small upcharge — the manual version feels dated once you've used a timer-equipped slow cooker.

**Who should buy this**

- **The budget meal-prepper**: Someone who wants set-and-forget cooking without dropping $100+ on a smart slow cooker. The RXT gives you 90% of the functionality at half the price.
- **The Sunday-batch cooker**: If you're making chili, pulled pork, or bone broth on weekends, the 6-quart capacity and even heating make this a workhorse.
- **The first-time slow cooker buyer**: The controls are intuitive, the insert is dishwasher-safe, and the learning curve is basically zero.

**Skip it if** you need a searing function (this doesn't have it — brown your meat in a separate pan first), or if you want Wi-Fi/app control. This is an analog cooking tool, not a smart home gadget.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I put the stoneware insert in the dishwasher?**
Yes, both the insert and glass lid are dishwasher-safe. The base must be wiped clean by hand.

**Q: How long does it take to cook a whole chicken on low?**
Typically 6-7 hours for a 4-5 pound bird. Check internal temperature reaches 165°F before serving — the RXT's timer lets you set this and walk away.

**Q: Does the auto-warm feature kick in automatically?**
On the RXT, yes — after the cook time ends, it shifts to warm automatically. On the D model, you must manually switch the dial to Warm before leaving.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want dependable, even slow cooking at a budget-friendly price and don't need smart features. The RXT variant is the sweet spot — programmable timer, auto-warm, and consistent low-temp performance that rivals units costing twice as much.

**Skip it if** the beeping timer will drive you insane, you need to sear in the same pot, or you're shopping for a gift where presentation matters as much as function — the aesthetic is utilitarian, not premium.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, honest workhorse that earns its 4.4-star Amazon average. It's not glamorous, but it does the job right.
