The ChocoVision Revolation 2 isn't a toy. At roughly the price of a mid-range espresso machine, it's a serious investment for anyone who's tired of wrestling with double boilers and marble slabs. The question isn't whether it tempers chocolate — it does, and it does it well. The real question is whether your chocolate-making volume justifies the cost and the learning curve.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the main tempering unit, a 3kg (about 6.6 lbs) capacity reservoir, the auger assembly, and a clear lid that lets you watch the chocolate flow. As shown in the product image, the white finish is clean and compact — it takes up roughly the footprint of a large bread maker. The digital control panel gives you temperature readouts and adjustment buttons. You supply your own chocolate and your own patience for the initial setup.

**How it performs**

Here's the thing about tempering: it's precision work. Cocoa butter has six crystal forms, and only one of them — Form V — gives you that glossy snap. The Revolation 2's digital controller maintains temperature within about a degree, which is the difference between perfect enrobing and dull, streaky chocolate. It cycles through the stages automatically: melting, cooling, and reheating to working temperature.

The 3kg capacity is the standout feature. Once it's running, you can keep adding chocolate and it'll keep tempering. This matters if you're making batches of bonbons or dipping hundreds of truffles. Smaller machines force you to stop and reload, which breaks your rhythm and lets your tempered chocolate cool.

Cleanup is genuinely better than most competitors. The reservoir lifts out and the auger disassembles without tools. You'll still be wiping down chocolate residue, but you won't be fighting a sealed unit that requires disassembly just to rinse.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The consistency gets repeated praise. One reviewer notes, "This is the only way I can get professional results at home. My chocolates have that snap and shine now." Another confirms the longevity factor: "I've had mine for three years and it still holds temperature perfectly."

**Where it falls short**

The 3.8-star average isn't an accident. The most common complaint is jamming — white chocolate, in particular, tends to gum up the auger. One frustrated buyer writes, "It clogged within ten minutes of adding white chocolate, and the cleanup took longer than the tempering." That's a legitimate design limitation. White chocolate's lower melting point and higher milk fat content can cause it to seize in the auger mechanism.

There's also no timer. For a machine at this price, you'd expect programmable cycles. Instead, you're watching the temperature readout and setting your own reminders. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's an odd omission.

The warranty situation is worth noting. ChocoVision offers a two-year warranty, but several Amazon reviews report slow response times on customer service. If you're buying for a business, factor in the potential downtime.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the serious home chocolatier or small-batch business owner who's already comfortable with chocolate work and wants to scale up. If you're making more than five pounds of chocolate per session, the Revolation 2 pays for itself in saved time and reduced waste. The 3kg continuous capacity is the differentiator — you can't get that in cheaper machines.

**FAQ**

**Can I use this for compound chocolate or candy melts?**
Yes, but you're wasting the machine's capability. The precise temperature control matters most for real couverture chocolate. Compound coatings don't need tempering — save your money and use a slow cooker for those.

**How long does a tempering cycle take?**
Plan for 45-90 minutes total, depending on chocolate type and starting temperature. Dark chocolate takes longer than milk or white because it requires a higher final temperature to reach Form V crystals.

**Does it really handle 3kg at once?**
The reservoir holds 3kg, but you'll want to start with about 1.5-2kg for best results. Overloading at the start can cause uneven heating. Once running, you can add more chocolate gradually.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're making chocolate regularly in large batches, you value consistent professional-grade results, and you've budgeted for a real tool rather than a novelty appliance.

**Skip it if:** You're an occasional hobbyist, you mainly work with white chocolate (the jamming issue is real), or you're expecting set-and-forget automation.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The Revolation 2 delivers where it counts, but the white chocolate jamming issue and missing timer keep it from being the definitive choice at this price point. For dark and milk chocolate production, it's still the best continuous-capacity option in the home market.
