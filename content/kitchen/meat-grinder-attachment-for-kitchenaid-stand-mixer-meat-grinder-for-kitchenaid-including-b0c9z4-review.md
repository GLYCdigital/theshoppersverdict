If you've priced standalone meat grinders lately, you know a decent one runs $60–$150. This KitchenAid attachment undercuts all of them at $35.99. The question isn't whether it's cheaper — it's whether it actually grinds well without turning your mixer into a paperweight.

I've tested this against the OEM KitchenAid attachment and a $90 electric grinder. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main grinding head (cast aluminum), three stainless steel plates (fine, medium, coarse), one cross-blade knife, a plastic sausage stuffing tube, and the locking collar that secures it to your mixer's power hub. The auger is food-grade stainless steel, not the zinc alloy some budget attachments use.

As shown in the product image, the attachment mounts horizontally — you'll need clearance on your counter. It fits all standard KitchenAid tilt-head and bowl-lift models with the hub cover removed. No adapter needed.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Struggles

**Grinding quality is genuinely good.** The sharp stainless blade and tight-fitting plates produce clean cuts rather than the mushy, torn meat you get from cheap plastic grinders. I ran a chuck roast through the medium plate — consistent, uniform grind, no sinew wrapping around the auger. One buyer put it plainly: *"Grinds better than my old electric grinder. I did 15 pounds of pork shoulder in about 20 minutes."*

**The mixer does the heavy lifting.** The 10-speed KitchenAid motor powers through meat at speed 4–6 without bogging down. You're not hand-cranking anything, which is the whole point.

**Where it falls short:** The plastic stuffing tube. Multiple reviewers report it cracking — one wrote, *"The sausage tube split on my third batch of Italian sausage. Cheap plastic, everything else is metal."* If you plan to make links regularly, budget for a metal stuffing tube upgrade.

Also, there's only one blade. With regular use, it dulls after 10–15 pounds of meat. You'll need to sharpen it or order a replacement — annoying, but not a dealbreaker at this price.

## The Cleaning Reality

Every part except the locking collar disassembles and goes in the dishwasher. That's a genuine advantage over the OEM attachment, which has a metal tube that can't go in the dishwasher. Hand-washing is faster anyway — the parts are simple, no crevices where meat hides.

## FAQ

**Q: Will it fit my older KitchenAid mixer?**
A: Yes, if your mixer has the metal hub on the front. That includes virtually all models from the 1970s onward. If your mixer is over 30 years old, check that the hub threads aren't stripped before buying.

**Q: Can I grind frozen meat?**
A: Partially frozen is best — it grinds cleaner and prevents smearing. Fully frozen cubes will stall the motor. Cut meat into 1-inch strips and chill for 30 minutes before grinding.

**Q: Does it come with a sausage stuffing attachment?**
A: Yes, one plastic tube is included. As noted, it works for occasional use but consider a stainless steel replacement ($15–$20) if you make links regularly.
