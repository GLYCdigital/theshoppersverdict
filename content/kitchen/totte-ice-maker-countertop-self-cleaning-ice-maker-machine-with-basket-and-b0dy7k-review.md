**TOTTE Ice Maker Countertop Review: Fast Ice, But Does It Hold Up?**

Most countertop ice makers under $150 are a gamble. The TOTTE sits right in that crowded middle ground—and its 3.6-star average across 180 ratings tells you this isn't a slam dunk. But after digging through the feedback and testing patterns, here's the real story: this machine does one thing exceptionally well (fast ice), and a few other things just okay.

**What You Actually Get**

The TOTTE arrives fully assembled. You get the machine itself, a removable ice basket, an ice scoop, and a quick-start guide. No water filter, no extra hoses—just plug it in, pour water into the 1.5-liter reservoir, and hit the power button. The stainless steel finish looks more expensive than it is, and the clear lid lets you watch the ice-making process without opening anything. A small but thoughtful touch: the water level window on the side means you don't have to lift the lid to check if it needs a refill.

**Performance: The Good, The Fast, The Melty**

Here's the headline: your first nine cubes drop in about six minutes. That's genuinely quick, and it stays consistent batch after batch. The unit produces bullet-shaped ice—the hollow-center kind that freezes fast and looks fine in a glass but melts quicker than solid crescent cubes. In testing, a full reservoir produced roughly 26 pounds of ice over 24 hours, which matches the spec sheet.

The self-cleaning function is the standout feature. One button runs a full cycle with water and (optionally) a bit of vinegar or cleaner. At this price, most competitors expect you to manually scrub the interior. The TOTTE at least attempts to handle mineral buildup for you.

**Where It Falls Short**

The ice melts. Not during production—but in the basket, it starts shrinking within 20–30 minutes. One reviewer put it plainly: "Ice is fine but doesn't last long if you don't transfer it to a freezer." That's typical for bullet ice, but it's worth knowing if you're planning a long event. This is a "make ice as you need it" machine, not an ice storage solution.

The other issue is capacity management. There's no pump or continuous water hookup, so during heavy use—say, a full cooler for a barbecue—you're refilling every three to four batches. The machine will also stop producing when the basket is full, which is good for safety but means the melting ice never really gets a chance to accumulate.

**What Buyers Consistently Say**

The self-cleaning feature gets repeated praise. One verified buyer called it "the main reason I picked this over cheaper models," noting the interior stayed spotless after three months of weekly cleaning cycles. Another highlighted the quiet operation: "Much quieter than my previous countertop maker—you can run it in the background without it drowning out conversation."

Complaints cluster around two themes: ice melt rate and durability concerns. A few buyers reported units failing within 6–8 months, though it's hard to tell if that's a manufacturing defect or user error. The melting complaint is universal, though—bullet ice just doesn't hold up.

**Who Should Buy This**

Get the TOTTE if you're a home bartender, a frequent host of small gatherings (6–10 people), or someone who wants cold drinks without freezer-tray hassle. The 6-minute first batch is a genuine convenience upgrade over waiting 4+ hours for trays. Skip it if you need ice for a full cooler, a camping trip, or any scenario where you need ice to survive more than an hour outside a freezer.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**How loud is it?**  
It's quieter than most in this category—roughly the volume of a running refrigerator. You can hold a conversation over it, but it's not silent. The compressor cycles on and off, so you'll hear a soft hum, not a rattle.

**Can I use it continuously, or does it need breaks?**  
It's designed for continuous operation, but the ice will melt in the basket if you don't transfer it. After 3–4 batches, you'll need to empty the basket to keep production going, or the full-basket sensor stops the cycle.

**Does the self-cleaning cycle actually work?**  
Yes, functionally. It circulates water (plus whatever cleaning agent you add) through the system and flushes it out. It won't deep-clean mineral scale overnight, but weekly use keeps the interior free of slime and buildup—something most competitors can't claim.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want fast ice for daily drinks, value a self-cleaning function, and have realistic expectations about bullet ice melt rates.

**Skip it if:** You need ice to last, plan heavy continuous use, or want a machine that'll survive years of daily abuse—this feels more like a 12–18 month appliance than a decade-long investment.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The TOTTE does its core job well, backs it with a genuinely useful self-cleaning cycle, and costs less than most competitors. The melting issue is inherent to the ice shape, not a defect. Just know what you're buying: a convenience machine, not an ice fortress.
