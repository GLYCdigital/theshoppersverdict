Most apple slicers are flimsy plastic things that bend the moment you hit a stubborn Honeycrisp. The AUBENR 4.72-inch takes a different approach: it's built like a tool, not a toy. If you've ever fought with a slicer that left half the apple stuck in the blades while the core stayed stubbornly in place, this is worth a close look.

## What You Actually Get

The AUBENR arrives as a one-piece unit — no assembly, no tiny screws to lose. The frame is cast aluminum with a matte finish, and the 10 blades are stainless steel with serrated edges. The handles have a slight ergonomic curve and rubberized grips. The cutting diameter measures 4.72 inches across, which is notably larger than the 3.5–4 inch standard on most slicers.

There's also a built-in core ejector — you press down, and the core pops out the bottom rather than requiring you to dig it out with your fingers. That's a genuinely useful feature that many budget slicers skip.

## How It Performs in Real Use

I tested this on a range of apples: small Galas, medium Granny Smiths, and a massive Honeycrisp that barely fit on a dinner plate. The AUBENR powered through all of them. The serrated blades grip the skin rather than sliding off, and the weight of the frame does most of the work — you press down with moderate force, and it cleanly produces 10 uniform wedges.

The core ejector works as advertised, though it does require a firm push. On smaller apples, the fruit can shift slightly before the blades bite in, so you'll want to center it carefully. The 4.72-inch diameter is the real selling point here — standard slicers simply cannot handle the jumbo apples that are increasingly common at grocery stores.

One thing to note: this is not a lightweight gadget. At roughly 1.5 pounds, it has heft that inspires confidence but might be genuinely difficult for someone with arthritis or weak grip strength to operate.

## Where It Falls Short

The hand-wash requirement is the most common complaint. The cast aluminum frame will develop cloudy spots if you run it through the dishwasher, and some buyers found that out the hard way. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's an extra step after every use.

A few reviewers also mentioned that the core ejector can be stubborn. It works, but it requires deliberate force rather than a casual push. And if you're buying this specifically for small apples or pear slices, the large diameter means the fruit can shift around before the blades engage.
