**Bottom line: If you need a no-frills microwave that heats evenly, fits a tight space, and doesn't cost a fortune, this BLACK+DECKER 0.9 cu ft model is a solid pick — provided you're not expecting smart features or a fingerprint-proof finish.**

I've tested dozens of countertop microwaves, and the first thing that struck me about the BLACK+DECKER 0.9 Cu Ft model is how unapologetically straightforward it is. No WiFi, no voice control, no sensor cooking wizardry. Just 900 watts, six preset buttons, and a dial-free digital keypad that does exactly what it says.

**What's in the box**

You get the microwave itself (about 17.3 inches wide, 12.9 inches deep, and 10.8 inches tall), a 10-inch glass turntable, a removable crumb tray, and a user manual that's actually readable. The 0.9 cubic foot interior is smaller than the 1.1–1.3 cu ft models most families default to, but it fits a standard dinner plate (up to 10 inches) without scraping the sides. If you regularly heat large casserole dishes or family-size bowls, measure your cookware before ordering.

**Real-world performance**

The 900 watts is the sweet spot for this size. It reheats a 12-ounce mug of coffee in about 90 seconds, defrosts a pound of ground beef in roughly 6 minutes (with the auto defrost setting), and handles a frozen dinner in the advertised 5–6 minute window. The six auto menus — popcorn, potato, pizza, beverage, frozen dinner, and reheat — aren't just decorative. The popcorn setting actually stopped before burning a standard 3.5-oz bag in my testing, which is more than I can say for some $150 units.

One thing buyers consistently mention is even heating. "I've had microwaves that leave cold spots in the middle of leftovers," one verified reviewer wrote. "This one heats everything uniformly — no more stirring halfway through." The fixed turntable helps here, though it means you can't pause rotation for odd-shaped dishes. If you frequently heat rectangular baking dishes, you'll need to transfer to a round plate.

**Where it stumbles**

The stainless steel finish looks premium in product photos, but it's a smudge magnet. After three days of normal use, mine looked like a crime scene of fingerprints. The door release button also needs a deliberate press — not a dealbreaker, but annoying when you're holding a hot dish and trying to open it one-handed.

More significantly, there's no child lock. For a microwave in this price range, that's not unusual, but if you have kids who can reach the counter, you'll want to keep this unplugged or out of reach. The interior LED light is bright and stays on for a few seconds after the door opens, which is a nice touch.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for:
- **Dorm rooms and small apartments** where counter space is premium
- **Singles or couples** who mostly reheat leftovers and make popcorn
- **Home offices** where a full-size microwave is overkill
- **Budget buyers** who want a reliable no-nonsense appliance without smart features

Skip it if you cook large batches, need a child lock, or are bothered by visible fingerprints. Also skip it if you want a microwave that doubles as a convection oven or air fryer — this is strictly a standard microwave.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the 0.9 cu ft size fit a standard dinner plate?**
A: Yes, plates up to 10 inches in diameter fit comfortably on the turntable. Tall mugs (over 5 inches) are fine too, but you'll need to remove the turntable for very tall items.

**Q: How loud is it during operation?**
A: It's about average — a low hum while cooking and a slightly louder fan noise during the last 30 seconds. Not whisper-quiet, but not disruptive.

**Q: Can you adjust the power level?**
A: Yes, there are 10 power levels accessible through the "power" button before starting a cook cycle. The presets use their own optimized levels, but manual control is there if you want it.

**Skip it if:** You need child safety features, hate wiping fingerprints, or regularly heat oversized cookware.
