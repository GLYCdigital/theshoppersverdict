Your dryer spins, tumbles, and beeps — but the clothes come out cold and damp. That's the classic symptom of a failed heating element or thermal fuse. **What's actually in the box**

This is a full repair kit, not just a coil. You get the 8544771 heating element assembly (the coiled wire inside the metal housing), the 279816 high-limit thermostat, the 279973 thermal cut-off, and the 3392519 thermal fuse. That's the complete "no heat" fix for most Whirlpool-built dryers — which includes Kenmore, Maytag, and Amana badges. The wiring harness comes pre-terminated, so it plugs directly into your dryer's existing connectors. No crimping, no soldering, no guesswork.

**Performance and build quality**

The element itself is a standard 240V, 5,400-watt unit — same spec as the OEM part. When it works, it works exactly as expected: heats to temperature, cycles off, holds. The thermal components are the more critical pieces, and this is where I want you to pay attention. The 279816 thermostat is a generic replacement, not a Whirlpool-stamped part. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've installed these kits a dozen times and this one's fine, but I always advise people to check the coil spacing before installing. One element I got had the coil bunched up too tight in one spot."* That's a fair warning.

The coil alignment issue is worth checking. Before you mount the element, eyeball the wire loops — they should be uniformly spaced. If any two coils are touching, the element will overheat and trip the thermal fuse again within a week. That's a 30-second visual check that saves you a second disassembly.

If you're comfortable with a possible follow-up, the kit is fine as-is.

**FAQ**

**Q: How do I know if my dryer needs this part?**
A: If the drum spins but no heat comes, test the element with a multimeter (should read 8-12 ohms) and check the thermal fuse for continuity. If either is open, replace the whole kit.

**Q: Is this safe to install myself?**
A: Yes, if you unplug the dryer first. It's a simple plug-and-play swap. If you're uncomfortable with electrical work, hire a pro — but you'll lose most of the savings.

**Q: Will this work with a 3-prong or 4-prong dryer cord?**
A: The heating element doesn't care about your cord configuration. It runs on 240V regardless. The cord is a separate issue entirely.
