# Air Fryer Paper Liners: The 10-Minute Cleanup Hack That Actually Works

Let’s be honest: the worst part of air frying isn't the cooking—it’s scraping burnt cheese off the basket afterward. If you’ve been avoiding paper liners because you think they’ll ruin your crisp, this 125-count pack from Amazon ($9.99) might change your mind. It’s the highest-rated liner on the market for a reason, but it’s not perfect for everyone.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes 125 pre-cut round liners. As shown in the product image, these are unbleached parchment papers with a subtle waffle texture and punched air holes. They measure approximately 7.9 inches in diameter at the top, tapering slightly at the base—a design specifically engineered for standard 4-6 quart round air fryer baskets (think Instant Pot Vortex, Cosori, and Ninja models).

The paper is chlorine-free and coated with a food-safe silicone layer, which is the key to their non-stick performance. You won't get that bleached-white look, but you also won't get chemical leaching into your food.

## Real-World Performance: Crisp or Soggy?

I tested these with frozen french fries, breaded chicken tenders, and a messy garlic butter salmon. The perforations are the star here. Unlike solid parchment sheets that block airflow and steam your food, these holes allow hot air to circulate. The fries came out with a solid 90% of the crispiness of a bare basket—a trade-off I’m happy to make given the cleanup factor.

The non-stick coating is legitimate. The salmon left a slight oily residue on the liner, but zero transfer to the basket itself. When I was done, I lifted the liner out and tossed the whole thing—no soaking, no scrubbing.

## Where It Falls Short

Here’s the honest tradeoff: **size matters**. These liners are designed for round baskets up to 6 quarts. If you have an 8-quart model or a square/rectangular basket (like some Philips or Gourmia models), these will crumple and fold, blocking airflow and potentially touching the heating element. That’s a fire hazard, not a convenience.

Additionally, the paper is lightweight. If you preheat your fryer without food inside, the fan will blow these around like tumbleweeds. You *must* place food on top immediately, or anchor them with a fork. One reviewer complained, *"They flew up and touched the coil, causing a burning smell."* This is user error, but it’s worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Busy parents** making nightly nuggets, tater tots, or grilled cheese.
- **Clean-freak cooks** who hate soaking greasy baskets.
- **Meal preppers** cooking marinated proteins that tend to stick and char.

**Skip it if** you own a large, rectangular, or dual-basket fryer, or if you strictly require maximum crispiness (like reheating pizza).

## FAQ: 3 Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can I use these in my 5.8-quart Instant Pot Vortex?**
A: Yes. The 7.9-inch top diameter fits most round 4-6 quart baskets. Check the inner diameter of your basket; if it's 8 inches or more, these will be too small.

**Q: Are they safe at high temperatures?**
A: Yes, they're rated for up to 400°F (428°F max). That covers 99% of air fryer settings. Don't use them under a broiler.

**Q: Do they affect the cooking time?**
A: Slightly. You may need to add 1-2 minutes for items like fries because the paper absorbs a tiny bit of heat. For most foods, you won't notice a difference.
