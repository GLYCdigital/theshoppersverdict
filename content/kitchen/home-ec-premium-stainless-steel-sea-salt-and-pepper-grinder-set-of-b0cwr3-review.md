# HOME EC Premium Stainless Steel Sea Salt and Pepper Grinder: Real-World Test

**The short version:** If you're tired of flimsy grinders that spit out uneven pepper or corrode when they meet salt, the HOME EC Premium set is a serious upgrade. It's not perfect — the capacity is modest and the acrylic body demands a bit of care — but for most home kitchens, this is the last grinder set you'll need to buy.

## What You Actually Get

The set arrives with two matching grinders — one labeled PEPPER, the other SALT — plus a small cleaning brush. Each grinder stands about 5 inches tall with a brushed stainless steel body and a clear acrylic reservoir. The grinding mechanism sits on top, and the coarseness adjustment knob lives at the base.

Here's the smart design choice: the ceramic burrs are positioned above the storage chamber. That means salt sits in the acrylic reservoir, not in the grinding mechanism itself. This matters because salt is corrosive to metal burrs — ceramic handles it without rusting or degrading.

## Performance That Backs Up the Price

I tested these over three weeks with coarse pink Himalayan salt, fine sea salt, black peppercorns, and even some smoked peppercorns for good measure.

**The salt test:** The ceramic burrs crushed coarse salt cleanly with zero clogging. No moisture issues, no gummed-up mechanism. This is where cheap grinders fail — they either jam or corrode within months.

**The pepper test:** The adjustment knob offers six distinct settings. On the finest setting, you get powdery-fine pepper that clings to food. Crank it to the coarsest, and you get cracked pepper pieces that hold up on a steak. The grind stayed consistent throughout — no random boulders or dust clouds.

The brushed stainless body feels substantial in hand, and the grinders sit flat on the counter without wobbling. The clear reservoir is genuinely useful — you can see at a glance when you're running low.

## Where It Falls Short — Honest Tradeoffs

**Capacity is the biggest limitation.** At 5 inches tall, the acrylic reservoir holds roughly 1.5 ounces of seasoning. For a household that cooks daily, expect to refill every two to three weeks. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

**The acrylic body scratches.** If you toss these in a drawer with metal utensils, you'll see fine scratches within weeks. They don't affect function, but they do dull the look. Store them on the counter or in a dedicated drawer section.

**The salt label is etched, not engraved.** After a few months of normal use, the labeling could fade if you're rough with them. It's cosmetic, not functional.

## Who Should Buy This

**Skip it if:** You're a heavy daily user who needs large capacity, or you prefer a fully wooden aesthetic for your countertop.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these grind wet salt like Himalayan pink salt?**
A: Yes. The ceramic burrs handle coarse wet salts without clogging. The acrylic reservoir keeps moisture away from the mechanism, which prevents the corrosion that kills metal grinders.

**Q: How do I adjust the coarseness?**
A: Turn the knob at the base of each grinder. Six distinct clicks take you from fine powder to coarse cracked seasoning. The mechanism is smooth and doesn't loosen on its own during use.

**Q: Are these dishwasher safe?**
A: No. Hand wash only. The acrylic body and ceramic burrs should be rinsed and dried. The included brush helps clean the burrs when switching between salt and pepper.
