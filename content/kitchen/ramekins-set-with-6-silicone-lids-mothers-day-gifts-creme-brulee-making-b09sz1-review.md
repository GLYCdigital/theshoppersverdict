If you've ever burned your fingers trying to pull a hot ramekin out of a water bath, you already know why this set is worth a look. This 6-piece ramekin set with silicone lids targets a specific pain point: portioned cooking that doesn't require a roll of plastic wrap every time you stash leftovers in the fridge. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, it's clearly resonating with home cooks. The question is whether it earns a spot in your cabinet.

**What you actually get**

The box contains six 6-ounce ceramic ramekins and six matching silicone lids. The ceramic is thick, glazed, and dishwasher-safe. The lids are food-grade silicone with a slight lip that creates a seal when pressed down. No gimmicks, no extra accessories you'll never use. The creme brulee branding on the listing is accurate — these are the right size for standard crème brûlée recipes, which typically call for 5- to 7-ounce dishes.

**Performance and build quality**

I tested these for crème brûlée first, since that's the headline use. The ceramic distributes heat evenly in a water bath, and the 6-ounce capacity leaves enough room for the custard to set without overflowing. The dishes survived the broiler step to caramelize the sugar topping — the ceramic handled the temperature swing from cold custard to high broil without cracking. That's the moment cheap ramekins fail, and these held up.

The lids are the differentiator. They seal well enough that I stored leftover custard base in the fridge for two days with no skin forming on top. That's the test — if a lid allows air in, you get that rubbery film. These didn't. They're also stackable, which saves fridge space.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring theme in reviews is versatility. One buyer noted, "I use these for everything — individual pot pies, chocolate lava cakes, even holding prepped garlic and herbs while I cook." **Where it falls short**

The lids are not oven-safe. That's stated in the listing, but it's easy to forget when you're moving from fridge to oven. You must remove them before baking. Also, the ceramic is not broiler-safe with the lids on — obviously — but some buyers have complained the lids are harder to clean than expected. The silicone can hold onto grease if you use these for dips or oily foods. Hand-washing the lids is recommended, which is slightly annoying if you're used to tossing everything in the dishwasher.

*Can I microwave these?* The ceramic is microwave-safe, but remove the lids first. The silicone lids are not designed for microwave use.

*Are they dishwasher-safe?* The ceramic ramekins are dishwasher-safe. The lids are hand-wash only to preserve the seal.
