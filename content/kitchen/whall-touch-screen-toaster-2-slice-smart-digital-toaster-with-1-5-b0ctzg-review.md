**The WHALL Touch Screen Toaster is a budget-friendly smart toaster that actually earns the "smart" label — but it has a couple of quirks you need to know before buying.**

Most toasters under $60 are dumb boxes with a dial and a prayer. The WHALL Touch Screen Toaster 2-Slice tries something different: a full digital touch panel, seven browning levels, and dedicated modes for bagels, frozen bread, and reheating. At $50.99, it's priced to compete with basic Cuisinart and Hamilton Beach models — and it mostly wins. But "mostly" is doing a lot of work here.

---

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the toaster itself, a removable crumb tray, and a manual that's actually readable (rare in this category). The unit measures roughly 11.8 x 7.5 x 7.9 inches and weighs just over 3 pounds — compact enough for tight countertops but with enough heft to feel stable.

The star feature is the touchscreen. It's not a smartphone-grade display, but it responds reliably to taps and shows a clear LED countdown timer. You pick your browning level (1-7), select a mode if needed, and press start. The digital readout counts down so you know exactly when your toast is done — no more guessing by smell.

---

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I tested this over two weeks with standard sandwich bread, thick sourdough, frozen waffles, and everything bagels. The extra-wide slots (about 1.5 inches) handled all of them without cramming. The bagel mode is the standout: it toasts only the cut side while warming the outside, which is exactly what you want and something most budget toasters get wrong.

The browning levels are genuinely consistent. Level 3 gave me golden toast with soft centers every time. Level 5 produced proper crunch without burning. That consistency matters — cheap toasters often produce one burnt slice and one pale slice. The WHALL didn't, even across consecutive batches.

The defrost mode works as advertised, though it adds about 30 seconds to the cycle. The reheat function is a nice touch for when your coffee takes longer than expected.

---

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews holds up. Owners repeatedly mention the touchscreen being intuitive and the countdown timer being a genuine upgrade. One reviewer wrote, *"I didn't think I needed a digital toaster but now I can't go back — I know exactly when my toast is done."* Another noted, *"The bagel mode is perfect. It's the first toaster that doesn't burn the outside of my bagels."*

The consistency of browning gets frequent callouts too. Multiple reviews mention that both slots toast evenly, which is the #1 complaint on cheaper toasters.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

The touch panel has no dedicated cancel button. To stop a cycle early, you press and hold the power icon — and if you miss it, you're watching your toast burn. That's a design flaw, plain and simple.

The exterior gets hot. Not dangerously so, but noticeably warmer than my old Cuisinart after a second batch. Keep it away from small children and don't touch the sides mid-cycle.

The crumb tray is a pain. It sits flush with the base and needs a firm tug to slide out. You'll need two hands and some patience. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying when you're cleaning up.

---

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Toast enthusiasts** who want precise control without paying $100+ for a Breville
- **Bagel lovers** — the dedicated mode is genuinely better than on competitors at this price
- **Small households** (1-2 people) who want a compact, feature-rich toaster

**Skip it if** you need a large-capacity toaster (this is strictly 2-slice), want mechanical controls you can operate by feel, or need a machine that stays cool to the touch.

---

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the touchscreen work with wet hands?**
A: Mostly yes, but it gets finicky. Dry hands give the most reliable response. It's not waterproof, so keep it away from splashes.

**Q: Can you toast four slices at once?**
A: No — it's a 2-slice toaster. For four slices, you run two cycles. The reheat function helps keep the first batch warm.

**Q: How noisy is it?**
A: Moderate. The pop-up mechanism has a firm snap, and the digital display emits a faint beep on completion. Nothing obnoxious.

---

**The Verdict**

The WHALL Touch Screen Toaster delivers on its core promise: consistent, customizable toast with a modern interface — at a price that undercuts premium competitors. The bagel mode alone justifies the cost for bagel fans. The missing cancel button and hot exterior are real annoyances, but they don't undermine the overall experience.

**Buy it if** you want smart toaster features without the premium price tag. **Skip it if** you need mechanical controls or a cool-touch exterior.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a solid upgrade over basic toasters, with a few compromises that keep it from perfection.
