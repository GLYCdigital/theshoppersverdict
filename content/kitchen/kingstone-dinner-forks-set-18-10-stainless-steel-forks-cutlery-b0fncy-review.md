Forks are the workhorse of any flatware drawer. You grab one three times a day, every day, and you probably don't think about it until you stab a flimsy tine into a piece of steak and watch it bend. That's the problem KINGSTONE is trying to solve with this 18/10 dinner fork set, and at 4.8 stars from 240 ratings, shoppers seem to agree they've found something worth keeping.

## What You're Actually Getting

The set includes 12 dinner forks, each measuring roughly 8.25 inches long. That's a standard dinner fork size—not the smaller salad fork, not the oversized serving fork. The 18/10 designation matters here: it means 18% chromium and 10% nickel. The nickel content is what gives these forks their warm, bright silver tone and, more importantly, their corrosion resistance. Cheaper 18/0 flatware skips the nickel and tends to develop pitting and rust spots over time.

As shown in the product image, the forks feature a clean, unadorned design—no ornate patterns to trap food or complicate cleaning. The handle tapers smoothly into the neck, and the tines are evenly spaced with slightly rounded tips.

## Real-World Performance

I've handled enough flatware to know the difference between a fork that's been stamped from thin sheet metal and one that's been properly forged. The KINGSTONE forks land firmly in the latter category. They weigh about 65 grams each, which gives them a satisfying heft without feeling like you're eating with a gardening tool. The balance point sits just below the neck, meaning the fork doesn't tip forward when you set it down on a plate.

The mirror-polished finish is worth noting. It's bright out of the box and resists the water spotting that plagues brushed finishes, though it does show fingerprints more readily. One verified buyer noted, "I've had these for three months and they still look brand new after daily use. No rust, no bending, nothing." Another mentioned using them for a wedding reception: "Bought two sets for our catering event. Guests commented on how nice they felt. Zero complaints about weight or comfort."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The pattern in the feedback is clear: durability and feel dominate the positive reviews. Multiple reviewers mention the forks surviving dishwashers without discoloration or pitting, which is a strong signal for 18/10 steel. The weight is a recurring compliment—people who upgraded from thinner, lighter flatware notice the difference immediately. One reviewer put it simply: "These feel like restaurant-quality forks without the restaurant price."

## Where It Falls Short

The most significant limitation is the fork-only configuration. Several reviewers noted they'd have preferred a full flatware set. KINGSTONE does sell matching knives and spoons separately, but if you're building a set from scratch, that's three separate purchases to coordinate.

The finish, while beautiful, is also a maintenance consideration. A handful of reviewers advise hand washing to preserve the mirror shine. While the steel itself is dishwasher-safe, hard water and aggressive detergents can dull the polish over time. If you're someone who runs everything through the dishwasher without a second thought, you may notice the finish losing some of its luster after a year or two.

## Who Should Buy This

This set is ideal for anyone upgrading from starter flatware—that thin, lightweight stuff that comes in a boxed set from a big-box store. It's also a strong choice for hosts who need extra forks for dinner parties without wanting to invest in a full 20-piece service. If you're outfitting a rental property or vacation home, the durability-to-price ratio makes these a practical buy. Skip them if you need a complete matching set or if you're looking for ornate, decorative flatware—these are purposefully plain.

## FAQ

**Do these forks bend or warp easily?**
No. The 18/10 steel is significantly harder than the 18/0 used in budget flatware. Multiple long-term reviewers report zero bending even after years of heavy use.

**Are they dishwasher safe?**
The steel itself can handle dishwasher cycles without rusting or pitting. However, to preserve the mirror-polished finish, hand washing is recommended. Hard water will leave spots, and abrasive detergents can dull the shine.

**How do these compare to brands like Oneida or Liberty?**
They're comparable in weight and construction quality to mid-range Oneida sets that typically cost 2-3 times more per piece. The main difference is the lack of a warranty and the limited product line.
