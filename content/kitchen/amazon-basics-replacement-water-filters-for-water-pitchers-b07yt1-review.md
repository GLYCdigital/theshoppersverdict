**Where it falls short**

Now the honest part. These filters are **not** NSF 53 certified, meaning they don't reduce lead, mercury, or other heavy metals. If you're on well water or live in an older home with lead pipes, this is not your filter. You need a certified option for that.

The lifespan claims are also inconsistent. Amazon says 40 gallons or 2 months, but buyer reports vary wildly. Some get 3 months of regular use; others report the filter stops improving taste after 3–4 weeks. There's clearly some batch variation here. My advice: mark your calendar and replace at 6–8 weeks regardless, because the taste drop-off is gradual and easy to miss.

One more quirk: the fit isn't universal despite the marketing. Most standard pitchers work fine, but a few buyers with older Brita models report the filter sitting slightly loose. If you have an obscure pitcher brand, measure your filter cavity before ordering.
