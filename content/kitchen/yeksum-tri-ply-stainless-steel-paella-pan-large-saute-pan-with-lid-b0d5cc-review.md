**A paella pan that pulls double duty — if you respect its learning curve.**

Most paella pans are cheap carbon steel that rust if you look at them wrong. The Yeksum takes a different route: it's a full tri-ply stainless construction (magnetic stainless exterior, aluminum core, stainless cooking surface) with a glass lid thrown in. That means it's not just for paella — this is a serious saute pan that can go from stovetop to oven without complaining.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the 12.6-inch pan and a snug-fitting tempered glass lid. No helper handle, no utensils, no frills. The pan itself is about 2.2 inches deep at the walls, which is shallow for a saute pan but deeper than a traditional paella pan. The riveted stainless handles are solid and stay cool during stovetop use — I tested with a gas burner on high for 10 minutes and grabbed the handle bare-handed without issue.

**How it performs in real kitchens**

I cooked three things in this pan: a proper seafood paella, a batch of crispy-skin chicken thighs, and a frittata. The tri-ply construction delivers on the even-heating promise. The paella rice cooked uniformly across the full surface — no scorched center, no undercooked edges. The socarrat (that crispy bottom layer paella fans chase) developed evenly with minimal fuss.

Searing is where this pan shines. The stainless surface hits 400°F quickly and holds it. Chicken thighs released cleanly once the skin rendered, and I got a deep golden crust without burning the fond. That fond deglazed beautifully into a pan sauce — something you can't do with nonstick.

But here's the tradeoff: stainless steel is unforgiving. Eggs will weld themselves to the surface if you don't preheat properly and use enough fat. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"If you've never cooked on stainless before, expect frustration. You need to preheat until the Leidenfrost effect kicks in, then add oil. Once I figured that out, everything changed."* That's accurate. This is not a nonstick pan, and it doesn't pretend to be.

**What owners consistently praise**

Across the 220 ratings (4.4 stars), the most common compliments are the even heat distribution and the included lid. One verified buyer noted: *"I bought this for paella but use it more for one-pan chicken dinners. The lid turns it into a mini braiser."* The oven-safe capability up to 500°F also gets frequent mentions — it's rare to find a paella pan that can go from stovetop sear to oven finish.

**Where it falls short**

The weight. At roughly 5 pounds empty, it's manageable for a gas stove but feels heavy when full. The lack of a helper handle is a real oversight — tilting it to baste or drain requires two hands on the main handle, which is awkward and a bit risky. The glass lid is oven-safe only to 350°F, so if you're doing high-heat oven work, you'll need to leave it off.

Cleanup is also a commitment. Stainless shows every water spot and fingerprint, and stuck-on food requires either Bar Keepers Friend or a solid soak. It's not a "rinse and done" pan — it demands care.

**Who should buy this**

- **Paella enthusiasts** who want a dedicated pan that also handles weeknight searing
- **Stainless steel converts** who already own All-Clad or Demeyere and want a wider, shallower option
- **One-pan cooks** who want a single vessel that goes from stovetop to oven for braises and roasts

**Skip it if** you're used to nonstick and aren't willing to learn proper stainless technique, or if you have wrist issues — this pan is heavy and unbalanced when full.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this on an induction cooktop?**  
Yes. The magnetic stainless exterior makes it fully induction-compatible. The 12.6-inch base covers most induction burners well, though smaller burners may heat unevenly at the edges.

**Q: Does the lid fit other pans?**  
The included lid is roughly 12 inches across and fits standard 12-inch pans from other brands, but it's not universal. It seals well on the Yeksum itself, which is what matters.

**Q: How do I get the nonstick-like performance?**  
Preheat the pan on medium for 2-3 minutes until a drop of water beads and skates across the surface. Then add oil and swirl to coat. This is the single most important step for stainless cooking.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, even-heating stainless pan that handles paella, searing, and oven finishing in one piece — and you're willing to learn stainless technique. **Skip it if** you prefer nonstick convenience or need a lighter pan with better balance.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong, affordable alternative to expensive European tri-ply pans, held back only by the missing helper handle and stainless's inherent stickiness. For the price, it outperforms expectations.
