## What You Actually Get

The package contains the drain pump assembly with the impeller housing, a pre-wired harness, and the rubber discharge boot already attached. That last detail matters more than you’d think — it eliminates one of the fiddliest steps of the swap. There’s no mounting bracket in the box, so dig out the screws from your old pump before you toss it. The unit measures roughly 5.5 x 4 x 4 inches and weighs about a pound, which is standard for this class of appliance pump.

## Real-World Performance

The pump is rated for 120V AC and draws around 1.5 amps during operation — essentially identical to the OEM spec. In practice, owners report it cycles quietly and drains a full load in the same time as the original. The impeller spins freely out of the box with no binding, which is a good sign for longevity. The plastic housing feels dense, not brittle like some no-name knockoffs you’ll find cheaper on Amazon.

One buyer noted: *“Replaced the pump on my LG WM3900HWA. Error code OE disappeared and it’s been draining perfectly for three months now.”* Another mentioned the connectors clicked into place with zero modification on a 2019 model. That’s the experience most people are having.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest caveat: this is an aftermarket part, not an LG OEM unit. For most washers, that’s fine. But a few reviewers with older LG machines (pre-2015) mentioned the electrical connector was slightly tighter than the original and needed a firm push to seat fully. It worked, but it wasn’t a perfect drop-in for every generation.

Also, there’s no installation guide in the box. If you’ve never replaced a drain pump before, you’ll want to watch a YouTube tutorial first — the clips on the boot and the hose clamps have a specific order of removal. Factor in 30–45 minutes if it’s your first time.
