# Giantex Full-Automatic Washing Machine Review: The Real Deal for Small Spaces

Let's be honest about what this is: the Giantex 1.34 cu ft portable washing machine is not a full-size washer. It holds about 8 pounds of laundry. Your king-size comforter won't fit, and you'll run it two or three times a week instead of one big weekend load. But if you live in a studio apartment, an RV, or a dorm with no laundry facilities, this little machine can save you $30–$50 a month in laundromat fees — and it pays for itself in about four months.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the washer, a drain hose, a faucet adapter (both threaded and snap-on connectors), and an instruction manual that's honestly better than most budget appliance documentation. The unit itself measures 19.5" x 19.5" x 32" and weighs about 48 pounds. It's on casters, which is a relief — you'll want to roll it to the sink, hook it up, run your load, then roll it back into a closet. The transparent lid lets you watch the wash cycle, which sounds gimmicky but is genuinely useful for spotting a too-full load before it becomes a problem.

## How It Actually Performs

The control panel offers six wash programs: Normal, Heavy, Quick, Spin Only, Drain Only, and a Wool/Delicate setting. The water level selector gives you three options (Low/Medium/High), which matters more than you'd think — a small load with too much water wastes detergent and leaves residue.

Here's the real-world breakdown after testing and reading through 220 Amazon reviews:

**Wash quality:** It gets clothes clean. Not "commercial front-loader" clean, but genuinely clean. The agitator is effective, and the 1.34 cu ft drum handles a queen sheet set plus a couple of pillowcases without tangling into a knot. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"My work clothes come out looking better than they did from the laundromat's industrial machines — and I'm not paying $4.50 per load anymore."* That sentiment shows up repeatedly.

**Spin cycle:** At 700 RPM, it's not going to wring every drop out. Expect clothes to come out damp, not dripping. A 3-star reviewer flagged this: *"Spins okay but not great — jeans take a full day to dry on a rack."* That's accurate. Budget for a drying rack and a fan.

**Noise:** During wash, it hums at around 60 dB — comparable to a dishwasher. The spin cycle gets louder, roughly 70-75 dB, but it's brief (5-7 minutes). Apartment-dwellers in the reviews consistently report no complaints from neighbors.

## Where It Falls Short — Honest Tradeoffs

**The hookup is the bottleneck.** You need a standard threaded sink faucet within 4-5 feet of an outlet. The included adapter works, but if your faucet is a pull-down sprayer style, you'll need to buy an additional adapter (about $10 on Amazon). This is the #1 complaint in negative reviews. One 2-star review said: *"The machine works but the hose leaked at the connection point no matter how tight I made it. Had to buy a separate brass adapter to fix it."*

**No water heating.** This machine draws from your faucet's hot and cold supply. If your water heater is far from the kitchen or bathroom sink you're hooking up to, you'll waste water running the tap until it's hot. A small annoyance, but worth knowing.

**Capacity discipline required.** Overload it and you'll get poor wash results and potential balance issues during spin. The machine will occasionally shut off mid-spin if the load is unbalanced — you'll have to redistribute and restart.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if you're:**
- An apartment dweller without in-unit laundry who's tired of laundromat trips
- An RV owner who needs a real washing machine (it runs on standard 110V household power)
- A renter who doesn't want to install a permanent appliance
- Anyone washing for 1-2 people who values the convenience of at-home laundry

**Skip it if you're:**
- A family of 4+ (you'll be running it daily)
- Someone who needs to wash bulky items like comforters or heavy blankets
- Expecting a dryer — this is wash and spin only

## FAQs

**Q: Does it need a special outlet?**
A: No. Standard 110V household outlet. Draws about 350 watts during wash, peaks around 500 during spin.

**Q: How much water does it use per load?**
A: Roughly 12-14 gallons on the high setting. That's about a third of what a full-size top-loader uses.

**Q: Can I hook it to a bathtub?**
A: Not easily. It needs a threaded faucet or a showerhead adapter kit (sold separately). Sink hookup is the intended setup.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Giantex 1.34 cu ft portable washing machine delivers exactly what it promises: a functional, full-automatic washer that fits small spaces. It's not perfect — the spin cycle is weak, setup requires a compatible faucet, and capacity is limited. But at its price point, it beats every manual/hand-crank alternative and most competing portable washers on both wash quality and program variety.

**Buy it if** you're a single person or couple wanting to cut laundromat costs in a space-constrained home. **Skip it if** you need true capacity or have an incompatible faucet setup.

For the money, this is one of the most reliable portable washers on Amazon — and with 220 ratings holding at 4.4 stars, buyers overwhelmingly agree.
