# Breville Bambino Plus & Baratza Encore ESP Bundle: The Real Deal for Home Espresso

Let's cut through the noise. The single biggest mistake new espresso enthusiasts make is buying a decent machine and pairing it with a $40 blade grinder. You get sour, inconsistent shots and blame the machine. This bundle fixes that problem before it starts — pairing Breville's fastest-heating entry-level machine with Baratza's purpose-built espresso grinder.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting two complete units. The **Breville Bambino Plus** (BES500) measures roughly 7.7" wide and 12.6" deep, with a 54mm portafilter and that famous 3-second thermocoil heat-up. The **Baratza Encore ESP** — the updated version of their legendary entry grinder — adds 40mm conical steel burrs, 260 grind settings, and an espresso-specific adjustment range that the original Encore lacked.

Also included: dual-wall and single-wall filter baskets, a razor dose trimmer tool, milk jug, and the grinder's dosing cup. The bundle typically saves you $50–80 versus purchasing separately, though that margin shrinks when Amazon's pricing dips on individual items.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The Bambino Plus's thermocoil system is the real headline. From cold start to first shot in about 3 seconds — no joke, and no 20-minute warm-up like traditional boiler machines. Steam power is surprisingly robust for its size, producing microfoam that pours into proper latte art. The auto milk texturing function handles temperature and texture automatically; it's not perfect, but it's remarkably consistent.

**The grinder changes everything.** The Encore ESP's stepless-ish adjustment lets you dial in fine enough for espresso without the clumping that plagued older Encore models. In testing, I found the sweet spot around setting 14-16 for medium roasts, pulling 1:2 ratios in 27-30 seconds consistently.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

Looking through the 229 ratings (4.6 stars), a few themes dominate. ## Honest Shortcomings

The **water tank** is genuinely small. At roughly 64 ounces, you'll refill it every 2-3 days with moderate use. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying.

The **included portafilter is pressurized** — fine for learning, but you'll outgrow it fast. A bottomless portafilter (around $30-40) will show you channeling and improve your shots dramatically. Budget for that upgrade.

And the **auto milk texturing**, while clever, can't match manual steaming control. If you're a perfectionist about microfoam, you'll likely disable the auto function and do it yourself.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're serious about espresso but new to the game. You want a setup that grows with you — the grinder alone justifies the bundle's value. You're short on counter space but don't want to sacrifice shot quality.

**Skip it if:** You're happy with capsule machines and don't want a new hobby. Or if you're already experienced and know you'll want a dual-boiler machine within a year — invest in a higher-tier setup from the start.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the Baratza Encore ESP actually good for espresso?**
Yes — unlike the original Encore, this version has the finer adjustment range and burr set needed. It produces consistent, clump-free grounds at espresso settings.

**Q: Can I steam milk while pulling a shot?**
Not simultaneously — the Bambino Plus is a single thermocoil. It switches between brew and steam modes in about 10 seconds, which is acceptable but not true simultaneous operation.

**Q: How many shots can I pull before the machine needs a break?**
You can pull 2-3 back-to-back shots without issue. The thermocoil recovers quickly, but the group head and portafilter do get hot — give it a minute between shots if you're pulling more than three.
