If you're squeezing more than a single orange for breakfast, a hand press will wear out your wrists by Tuesday. The Breville BCP600SIL is a motorized citrus press that actually does the work for you — but it's not the silent, set-it-and-forget-it machine the marketing implies. After digging through 229 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

**What's in the box**  
You get the motorized base, a stainless steel reamer cone, a clear pulp container, a 1-liter (about 34 oz) juice jug, and a dust cover. The reamer is the key upgrade from manual presses: it rotates in both directions, so citrus flesh is scraped clean rather than just squashed. The pulp control dial on the side offers five settings, from "extra smooth" to "lots of pulp." Assembly is tool-free — the parts snap on and off for rinsing.

**Performance and build quality**  
The motor is the headline. At 100 watts, it's not a commercial beast, but it handles 10–12 oranges in a sitting without overheating. The dual-direction rotation genuinely extracts more juice — one buyer noted, "I got almost a third more juice from the same bag of lemons compared to my old hand press." The stainless reamer is sharp enough to pierce a halved orange cleanly, and the juice jug has a froth separator, so you're pouring liquid, not foam.

Build quality is classic Breville: heavy base, brushed metal trim, and a motor that carries a lifetime warranty. That warranty isn't marketing fluff — it covers the motor, which is the part that usually dies on cheap juicers.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The extraction efficiency is the most common compliment. One verified review said, "I was skeptical about the price, but it pays for itself if you juice daily — no waste, no pulp swimming in the glass." Another praised the cleanup: "Three parts, all dishwasher-safe, and I'm done in 30 seconds." The pulp control also gets love — being able to make a smooth mimosa base or a chunky juice for a smoothie without a second appliance is a legit convenience.

**Where it falls short**  
The noise is real. At 65–70 dB, it's louder than a manual press and comparable to a basic blender. If you're juicing before the family wakes up, they'll hear it. The press arm is manual — you push down on the fruit yourself, and the motor stops when you release. That's fine for control, but it's not hands-free, and there's no sensor to stop at the rind, so you have to watch it. The pulp bin is also small; for a family batch, you'll empty it twice. And halved fruit is required — this won't handle a whole orange in one motion.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Daily citrus drinkers** who make fresh OJ, lemonade, or lime juice more than three times a week — the extraction efficiency and cleanup speed justify the cost.  
- **Home bartenders** who need consistent lemon/lime juice without hand cramps.  
- **Anyone with arthritis or grip issues** — the motorized reamer eliminates the twisting motion that hurts.  

**Skip it if**  
- You juice once a week — a $20 manual press is fine.  
- You need silence — this isn't it.  
- You want a fully automatic juicer that you can walk away from — this requires physical interaction for each fruit half.

**FAQ**  

*Can it juice pomegranates or other non-citrus?*  
No. It's designed for citrus only (oranges, lemons, limes, grapefruit). The reamer won't break down pomegranate seeds or apples. Don't try it — you'll clog the pulp bin.

*Is the juice jug actually dishwasher-safe?*  
Yes, the jug, reamer, and both containers are top-rack dishwasher-safe. The base is not — wipe it down only. Some buyers reported the plastic jug clouding after repeated dishwasher cycles, so hand-washing the jug is safer long-term.

*How loud is it, really?*  
Measured around 65–70 dB at arm's length — that's a loud conversation or a vacuum on low. Short bursts (10–15 seconds per fruit) mean it's not continuous noise, but it's noticeable.

**The Verdict**  

**Buy it if** you're a regular citrus juicer who values extraction efficiency, easy cleanup, and a motor that's backed for life. It's a genuine upgrade from manual presses and cheap electric models.  

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget, juice rarely, or need a whisper-quiet appliance for early mornings.  

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The noise and manual press arm keep it from a perfect score, but the juice yield and build quality make it the best motorized citrus press under $100 for daily use.
