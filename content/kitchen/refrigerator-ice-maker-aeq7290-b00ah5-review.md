## Performance and real-world use

The install process is straightforward but demands a bit of patience. You'll need to unplug the fridge, remove the old unit (typically two screws and a quarter-turn on the water line), then plug in the new one and secure it. Most buyers report 10–15 minutes total. ## Where it falls short

The biggest gotcha is compatibility. This part fits a specific list of GE models — most commonly the GSS, GSF, and GBS series from roughly 2005–2015, plus some Hotpoint models. If your fridge is older than that, you may actually need the previous revision (WR30X10094), and the connector will not match. Check your model number against the fitment list before ordering.

Second, there's no printed manual in the box. The wiring harness is keyed so it only plugs in one way, but if your existing connector has corroded or you're not comfortable with basic appliance wiring, you'll want to pull up a YouTube video or your fridge's service manual. It's not hard, but it's not plug-and-play for everyone.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my GE model GSS25GSH?**
A: Yes, that model is on the confirmed fitment list. If your fridge's model number starts with GSS, GSF, or GBS and was built between 2005–2015, this is almost certainly your part. Double-check by pulling the model number from the tag inside the fresh food compartment.

**Q: Do I need to replace the water line or filter?**
A: No. The water line connection uses a standard quarter-turn fitting that seals with an o-ring. If you're replacing a unit that had a slow leak, check the o-ring on the fridge side for cracks, but most buyers reuse the existing line without issue.

**Q: Why is my new ice maker not making ice after 12 hours?**
A: Give it a full 24 hours. The unit needs time to cool down, the water line needs to fill, and the thermostat has to cycle. If it's still dry after 48 hours, verify the wiring harness is fully seated and that the fridge door switch is functioning — a faulty door switch will prevent the ice maker from cycling.
