# 1 Gallon Glass Cookie Jars + Labels & Marker — Set of 2 Cani Review

## The Problem With Most Cookie Jars

Most cookie jars on Amazon are either too small (that cute ceramic pig holds maybe 8 cookies) or made of plastic that makes your baked goods go stale in three days. The Cani 1-gallon glass set of 2 solves both problems with serious capacity and a proper airtight seal. After testing these for three weeks, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two 1-gallon glass canisters, two metal clamp lids with silicone gaskets, a sheet of chalk labels (you get a dozen, which is generous), and one liquid chalk marker. The jars measure about 9 inches tall with a 6-inch diameter opening — wide enough to reach into with your whole hand, which matters when you're fishing out the last cookie from the bottom.

The glass is thick. We're talking restaurant-quality borosilicate, not the thin stuff you get with cheap pasta jars. As shown in the product image, the glass is completely clear, so you can see exactly what's inside without opening the lid.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these with three different scenarios: a full batch of chocolate chip cookies, a 5-pound bag of flour, and a 2-pound bag of brown sugar.

**Cookies:** Twelve standard-sized cookies fit with room to spare. The airtight clamp lid kept them soft for 11 days — that's genuinely impressive. By day 12 they started to firm up, but that's longer than any plastic container I've used.

**Flour:** The 1-gallon capacity holds a full 5-pound bag with about 2 inches of headroom. The wide mouth makes scooping easy, and the glass doesn't absorb odors like plastic can.

**Brown sugar:** This is where the airtight seal really shines. The silicone gasket kept brown sugar soft and clump-free for two full weeks. No rock-hard brick of sugar.

The included chalk labels are a thoughtful touch. You write directly on the label with the marker, then wipe it clean when you want to change what's inside. One caveat: the marker is permanent-ish if you let it dry for more than a few hours. Wipe it off immediately after writing, or you'll need rubbing alcohol to remove it.

## Where It Falls Short

**Weight when full:** A full jar of flour weighs about 9.5 pounds. You're not moving these around casually. They're meant to sit on a counter or shelf and stay there.

**Clamp lids need muscle:** The first few times you close these, you'll think you're breaking them. The clamp requires a firm, deliberate press to fully seal. It loosens up after a week of use, but it's not a one-finger operation.

**No pour spout:** If you're using these for flour or sugar, you'll need a scoop. There's no pour-friendly lip, so transferring to a measuring cup means getting flour everywhere if you're not careful.

## FAQ

**Can these hold a full batch of cookies from a standard recipe?**
Yes. A recipe making 24-36 cookies fits easily with room to spare. You can stack them two layers deep.

**Are the labels really reusable?**
Yes, if you wipe them clean within a few hours of writing. Once the chalk marker dries completely, it's very difficult to remove without rubbing alcohol.

**Are these dishwasher safe?**
The glass jars are. The lids are not — hand wash those. The silicone gasket can warp in the dishwasher, which would break the airtight seal.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want proper, airtight cookie storage that actually keeps baked goods fresh for over a week. The build quality is excellent, the capacity is generous, and the included labels add genuine convenience.

**Skip it if** you need lightweight, portable storage or you only bake occasionally. These are substantial jars that earn their counter space through regular use.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the stiff clamps and the weight when full. Everything else performs exactly as advertised, and the airtight seal is the best I've tested in this price range.
