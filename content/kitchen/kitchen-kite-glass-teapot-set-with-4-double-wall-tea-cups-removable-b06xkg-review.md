**Is this the prettiest teapot set on Amazon that actually works?** After digging through 229 ratings and testing the geometry of this thing, here's the real picture.

The Kitchen Kite set is a 750ml borosilicate glass teapot with a removable stainless infuser, matched with four 200ml double-wall cups. What you're paying for is the thermal engineering — not just looks. The borosilicate glass is the same material lab beakers use, which means it takes boiling water without shattering. That's the single biggest reason this set holds a 4.6-star average instead of a 3-star one.

**What's actually in the box:** The teapot, infuser basket, four double-wall cups, and a glass lid. No tray, no coasters, no bamboo anything — despite some product photos suggesting otherwise. The infuser is fine-mesh stainless, not the coarse plastic kind that lets leaf bits through. That matters if you drink loose-leaf oolong or a broken-leaf black tea.

**Real-world performance:** The double-wall cups are the star. They're 200ml each — small enough to be proper tea cups, not mugs. Tea stays hot for roughly 20 minutes in these versus 8-10 in a standard ceramic cup. The exterior stays cool enough to hold even with boiling liquid inside. That's physics doing its job, and it works.

The teapot pours cleanly with a drip-free spout — a detail cheap glass teapots get wrong. The handle is comfortable for a right-handed pour, though lefties will find the thumb rest placement slightly awkward.

**What buyers consistently say works:**
- "The glass is thick and feels premium — not like the dollar-store stuff"
- "Infuser catches everything — no more tea dust floating in my cup"
- "Cups are the perfect size for a proper gongfu session or afternoon tea"

**Where it falls short — honestly:** The 750ml capacity is the biggest limitation. That's two decent cups of tea, or three if you're pouring small. For a household of four, you'll be re-steeping constantly. The double-wall cups are also brittle — they're two thin layers of glass with an air gap, and a knock against a stone counter has chipped more than one set. Finally, the infuser lid has no locking mechanism. Overfill it with expanding leaves like jasmine pearls, and it'll pop open mid-steep.

**Who should buy this:**
- **The loose-leaf convert** who's tired of metal infusers that rust or plastic that stains
- **The host who serves tea visually** — watching the leaves unfurl through clear glass is half the experience
- **The small household** — one to two people who want a proper pot, not a giant brewer

**Skip it if** you regularly serve more than two people, or if you're clumsy — this is not a drop-it-on-tile product.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Can you put this on a stovetop or in a microwave?**  
A: The borosilicate glass is microwave-safe for heating water, but do NOT put it on a direct flame or electric coil. The metal infuser will arc in a microwave — remove it first.

**Q: Does the double-wall cup design affect the taste?**  
A: No. The inner layer is plain borosilicate, same as the pot. No metallic or plastic taste. Some reviewers note tea cools to sipping temperature faster in double-wall because the air gap doesn't hold as much heat as thick ceramic — but it stays at that perfect drinking temp longer.

**Q: Is the infuser dishwasher-safe?**  
A: Yes, the stainless infuser is. But hand-wash the teapot and cups — the dishwasher's heat cycles can weaken the glass-to-glass seal on the double-wall cups over time.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you want a visually stunning, thermally competent teapot set for small servings and you'll treat the glass with care. **Skip it if** you need a family-sized pot or you're buying this as a gift for someone who's hard on kitchenware.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The engineering is solid, the materials are right, and the only real knocks are capacity and fragility. For the price, you're getting better thermal performance than most ceramic sets at double the cost.
