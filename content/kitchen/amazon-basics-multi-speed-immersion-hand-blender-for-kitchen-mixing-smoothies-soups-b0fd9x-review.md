Here's the thing about immersion blenders: you don't need a $150 Breville to puree soup. You need something that won't splatter hot liquid across your stove, won't die mid-smoothie, and won't cost more than the pot you're blending in. The Amazon Basics Multi-Speed Immersion Hand Blender sits squarely in that budget-friendly sweet spot, and with a 4.2-star average across 210 ratings, it's proving that you don't need to overspend to get the job done.

**What's actually in the box**

The package includes the main motor unit with a detachable stainless steel blending shaft, a whisk attachment for whipping eggs or light cream, and a 600ml plastic measuring beaker. The blending shaft is fully submersible and the blade guard is designed to sit slightly above the blade — that's a deliberate detail that helps prevent suction to the bottom of your pot, which is a common annoyance with cheaper stick blenders.

**Real-world performance**

The motor runs at two speeds via a slide trigger, plus a turbo button for when you need maximum power. For most tasks — tomato soup, pancake batter, protein shakes — the standard speeds handle it cleanly. The turbo mode is genuinely useful for breaking down frozen fruit in smoothies, which is where many budget immersion blenders fall apart.

The stainless steel shaft is the right call here. It won't stain from beets or turmeric, and it won't scratch the enamel coating on a Dutch oven. The blade guard does a decent job of keeping splatter down, though you should still keep the blades fully submerged when blending hot liquids.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reviewers regularly highlight the value proposition. One verified buyer noted, "I was skeptical at $25 but this handles everything I throw at it — soups, smoothies, even blending tomatoes for sauce." Another mentioned that the turbo button "actually works, unlike my old one that just made noise." The included beaker gets specific praise for being wide enough to blend without splashing — a detail that matters more than you'd think.

**Where it falls short**

The plastic motor housing is the main compromise. During extended blending of thick mixtures — think hummus or frozen fruit — it runs warm to the touch. It's not dangerous, but it signals the motor's limits. Several reviewers mentioned the whisk attachment is serviceable for eggs but bogs down on heavy cream or stiff batters. If you're planning to whip cream regularly, buy a dedicated hand mixer instead.

Also worth noting: the beaker is plastic, not glass, and it's a bit small for batch cooking. For a single smoothie or two servings of soup, it's plenty. For meal-prepping a week of soup, you'll want to blend directly in your stockpot.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the home cook who wants a reliable immersion blender for everyday tasks without spending appliance-showcase money. It's ideal for:

- **Soup lovers** who want to puree right in the pot without transferring hot liquid to a countertop blender
- **Smoothie drinkers** who want a quick single-serve option that's easier to clean than a full blender
- **New cooks** building a starter kitchen kit — the included whisk and beaker cover most basic needs

Skip it if you need professional-grade power for heavy-duty tasks like grinding nuts into butter or emulsifying large batches of mayonnaise daily. Those jobs need a higher-end model with a metal motor housing.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can it blend frozen fruit?**
Yes, but use the turbo button and add some liquid first. It handles frozen berries fine in a smoothie; it's not designed for crushing ice on its own.

**Q: Is it dishwasher safe?**
The blending shaft and whisk are top-rack dishwasher safe. The motor unit must be wiped clean by hand — never submerge it.

**Q: Will it scratch my nonstick pot?**
No. The stainless steel shaft and blade guard are smooth and rounded at the edges, so they won't damage nonstick coatings or enameled cast iron.
