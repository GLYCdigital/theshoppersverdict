Let me be blunt: a 170-ounce paper bucket is not a subtle product. At 223 mm across the top, this Karat bucket is closer to a small trash can than what most people picture when they think "food storage." But that's exactly the point. If you're feeding a crowd, running a food truck, or catering a backyard wedding, this thing holds over five gallons of pulled pork, coleslaw, or fried chicken — and that's a real capability most kitchen containers simply don't have.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the bucket and a matching lid. No inserts, no liners, no scoop. The bucket itself is made from a heavy-duty paperboard material with a waxed interior coating — that's the key detail. The coating is what keeps the grease from soaking through the first time you fill it with hot wings. The lid is a rigid plastic piece that snaps around the entire circumference, not just a flimsy dome that sits on top.

## Real-World Performance

I've seen these used in three distinct scenarios: food truck prep, church potlucks, and commercial kitchen bulk storage. In every case, the bucket does its job without drama. The paper walls are thick enough that you won't feel the heat of hot food through the sides, and the waxed lining holds up against sauces and marinades that would destroy a standard cardboard container in minutes.

The lid fit deserves specific praise. It's not a universal "one size fits all" lid that wobbles and gaps. It clicks into place around the rim and creates a seal that's good enough to keep a bucket of soup from leaking in a cooler — as long as you're not flipping it upside down. For transport, you'll want to keep these upright, but that's a reasonable expectation for a bucket with no locking mechanism.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The 223 mm diameter is a real problem for home refrigerators — standard fridge shelves are around 400-500 mm deep, but the bucket's width means it takes up a disproportionate amount of space. You're not fitting this on a door shelf, and you might struggle to fit two of them side by side on a single shelf.

The paper construction also means these are single-use in practice. You can rinse them out and reuse them for dry goods, but hot liquids and greasy foods will degrade the lining after one use. If sustainability is a priority, look at stainless steel or food-grade plastic alternatives — they'll cost more but last years.

There's also no handle. A 170 oz bucket filled with soup weighs roughly 12-14 pounds, and without handles, you're relying on a two-handed grip around the sides. It works, but it's awkward, especially when the bucket is hot.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these go in the microwave?**  
No. The paper construction and waxed lining are not microwave-safe. Transfer food to a microwave-safe dish before reheating.

**Q: Are these leak-proof?**  
The lids seal well enough for transport, but they're not airtight. If you're carrying liquids, keep the bucket upright and expect some seepage if the bucket gets jostled.

**Q: How much food does 170 oz actually hold?**  
Approximately 5.3 quarts, or about 13-15 pounds of pulled pork, 8-10 pounds of soup, or a full batch of fried chicken for 20-30 people.
