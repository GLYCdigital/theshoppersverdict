**Bottom line:** The Buttermelt is a solid mid-tier stainless saucepan that outperforms its price point on heating evenness, but demands more upkeep than nonstick alternatives. Here's the full breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The Buttermelt arrives as a 3-quart saucepan with a snug-fitting stainless lid, plus a second helper handle opposite the main one. That's it — no extras, no silicone trivets, no nonsense. The pan itself is tri-ply clad: stainless steel exterior, aluminum core, stainless interior. The riveted handles are hollow-cast stainless, which does help them stay cooler than solid steel.

The 3-quart capacity is the sweet spot for most home cooks. It handles a batch of rice, a quart of soup, or a full package of pasta without crowding. The base diameter is wide enough for even heat distribution but not so wide that it's awkward on smaller burners.

## Performance in the Kitchen

I tested this pan over two weeks on gas and induction. The standout trait is heating evenness. A béchamel sauce held at a gentle simmer for 15 minutes showed zero scorching at the edges — a problem I've hit with cheaper single-ply pans. The aluminum core does its job; the bottom reaches temperature quickly and distributes it uniformly.

Pouring is where this pan earns its keep. The flared rim pours cleanly, and the helper handle gives you real control. I'm a chronic spiller, and I didn't dribble once. That's rare enough that it deserves calling out.

The pan is oven-safe to 500°F, which is useful if you're finishing a sauce under the broiler or braising something that starts on the stovetop. Induction compatibility is a plus for anyone who's made the switch.

## What Buyers Are Saying

Across 225 ratings at 4.5 stars, the praise is consistent. One buyer noted: *"Heats more evenly than my All-Clad that cost three times as much. No hot spots, period."* Another mentioned the handle design specifically: *"The helper handle makes a real difference when you're pouring gravy. I don't know why every pan doesn't have this."*

The recurring complaint, as expected with stainless, is maintenance. *"Looks terrible after the first tomato sauce,"* one reviewer wrote, *"but a little Bar Keepers Friend and it's back to new."* That's not a defect — it's the nature of the material — but buyers should know it going in.

## Where It Falls Short

The handle stays cooler than most stainless pans, but it's not cool to the touch. On a 30-minute simmer, the handle gets noticeably warm — not burning, but uncomfortable to grab bare-handed. Use a towel or potholder.

The interior will show water spots, fingerprints, and discoloration from acidic foods. If you're someone who wants a pan that looks pristine without effort, this isn't it. Nonstick is easier to maintain; stainless is more durable and better for high-heat searing. Know which you value more.

The lid fits well but doesn't have a steam vent, so you'll need to crack it slightly when boiling to prevent condensation buildup. Minor, but worth noting.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're building a stainless cookware set and want a mid-price saucepan that performs like a premium pan. You cook sauces, grains, and soups regularly. You don't mind occasional polishing to keep it looking sharp.

**Skip it if:** You're used to nonstick and want zero-maintenance cleanup. You only boil water and pasta. You need a smaller pan — this is 3 quarts, which might be oversized for single servings.

## FAQ

**Is this pan dishwasher-safe?**
Technically yes, but hand-washing is strongly recommended. Dishwasher detergent will accelerate water spotting and can dull the exterior finish over time. A quick hand wash takes 30 seconds.

**Will it work on my glass-top electric stove?**
Yes. The base is flat and fully clad, which distributes heat evenly across the glass surface. Just be careful not to slide it, as stainless can scratch glass cooktops.

**How does it compare to All-Clad?**
It's about 60% of the price with roughly 85% of the performance. The heating is comparable; the handle design is actually better. All-Clad has a slightly more polished finish and a longer warranty, but for most home cooks, the Buttermelt delivers the same results.

## The Verdict

The Buttermelt is a genuinely well-engineered saucepan that punches above its price point. The heating evenness, pour control, and induction compatibility are features you'd expect from pans costing twice as much. The maintenance demands of stainless are the tradeoff, but that's true of every stainless pan on the market.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Buy it if you want performance without the premium brand markup. Skip it if you prefer the low-maintenance life of nonstick.
