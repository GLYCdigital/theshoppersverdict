If you've ever fished a soggy paper tea bag out of your mug and wondered why you're paying for crushed floor sweepings when whole-leaf tea tastes dramatically better, this infuser is the $10 fix that makes sense. At 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, it's clearly working for most people. But let's talk about whether it'll work for you.

**What you actually get**

The package contains the infuser itself — a 2.5-inch diameter basket made from 304 stainless steel mesh — plus a small silicone-tipped chain and hook that drapes over your mug's rim. There's no saucer, no drip tray, no fancy box. It's a utilitarian tool, and that's fine. The mesh is noticeably fine — we're talking 200+ holes per square inch — which matters more than most people realize. Coarse infusers let tea dust through, and you end up chewing your last sip. This one holds the leaf back properly.

**How it performs in real use**

I tested it three ways: grandpa style (leaf in basket, water poured directly over), traditional steep-and-remove, and cold brewing overnight. The basket's size is the sweet spot — big enough that tea leaves have room to unfurl fully, which is what actually releases flavor. Cramped infusers produce weak, flat tea because the leaves can't expand. This one gives whole-leaf oolong and rolled jasmine pearls room to open up properly.

The hinged clamp lid is the standout feature. It snaps shut with real pressure, so you can shake the basket to agitate the leaves mid-steep without the top popping off. When you're done, the same hinge flips open for easy leaf disposal — no unscrewing a threaded cap while your fingers are wet and the sink is filling up.

**What buyers keep saying**

The consensus in those 235 reviews is consistent. One buyer put it simply: "Finally an infuser that doesn't let tea leaves into my cup. The mesh is fine enough that even my dusty English Breakfast comes out clear." Another noted the durability: "Had mine for two years, used daily, still looks new. The mesh hasn't torn or stretched."

**Where it stumbles**

The chain is genuinely too short for large mugs. On a standard 12-ounce ceramic mug it's fine, but on deeper 16-ounce options the hook barely reaches the rim and the basket can end up fully submerged. That's fine for steeping but annoying when you want to pull it out mid-brew. Also, despite being stainless steel, this needs hand-washing. The fine mesh will warp in the dishwasher's heat cycle, and once it warps, the lid won't clamp properly anymore. That's the #1 complaint in the negative reviews — people who ignored the hand-wash instruction and paid for it.

**Who should buy this**

Buy it if you drink loose leaf tea daily, use standard-sized mugs, and want something that'll last years instead of the flimsy $4 ball infusers that rust after a month. It's also great for tea drinkers who like fine-ground blends — the mesh handles them without creating murky cups. Skip it if you exclusively use extra-tall travel mugs, or if you refuse to hand-wash anything. For those cases, look at a basket-style infuser with a longer handle or a disposable filter system.

**FAQ**

*Can this handle herbal teas and fruit pieces?* Yes, but the basket is 2.5 inches in diameter. Large cut pieces of apple or big hibiscus flowers will need breaking up first. It's not designed for chunky blends.

*Will it stain or retain flavors?* The stainless mesh resists both. A quick rinse after use and it's ready for the next tea. Even after steeping strong black tea, a rinse clears it completely.

*How much tea does it hold?* One to two teaspoons of loose leaf, depending on leaf size. That's roughly one standard mug's worth. For a pot, you'd need to re-steep.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, reliable daily infuser that filters well and won't fall apart — this is the one. **Skip it if** you need something for oversized mugs or refuse to hand-wash your kitchen tools.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does its job exceptionally well at a fair price, with one genuine flaw (the chain length) that's easy to work around.
