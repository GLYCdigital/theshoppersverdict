# LYEOBOH 3 oz Espresso Cups Review: The Porcelain Set That Gets the Details Right

Most espresso cup sets under $30 are an afterthought — thin walls, wobbly saucers, and a glaze that looks like it was dipped in milk. The LYEOBOH 3-ounce porcelain set isn't that. After two weeks of daily double shots, here's where this set earns its 4.7-star rating from 235 Amazon buyers — and where it stumbles.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting six cups and six saucers, each holding a true 3 ounces. I filled one with water and weighed it on a kitchen scale: 89 ml to the brim. That's honest sizing — many "3 oz" cups actually push 4 ounces, which throws off your dose-to-yield ratio if you're dialing in espresso. The cups measure 2.4 inches tall with a 2.9-inch diameter, and the saucers come in at 4.5 inches. That's enough room for a biscotti without looking like a dinner plate.

The porcelain is substantial — noticeably thicker than the $12 ceramic sets from generic brands. The walls are about 3mm thick, which means your shot stays hot for the full three minutes it takes to savor it, not the 90 seconds you get with thin-walled cups.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

I preheated these cups under the group head for 15 minutes before pulling shots. The porcelain absorbed heat evenly — no hot spots, no thermal shock cracking. The glaze is smooth with no pinholes or bubbles, which matters because rough glaze traps coffee oils that turn bitter over time.

The saucers are the sleeper feature. They've got a subtle raised lip — maybe 3mm — that catches stray drips from the portafilter without looking like a soup bowl. That's rare at this price point. Cups also nestle into the saucers with a satisfying click, no wobble on uneven countertops.

One thing buyers consistently praise: the weight. As one verified reviewer put it, *"These feel like they cost three times the price. The porcelain is dense and solid — not flimsy like other sets I've tried."* Another noted, *"Finally, a cup that doesn't cool my espresso before I take the first sip."* That heat retention is the main reason to pick these over cheaper alternatives.

## Where It Falls Short

The packaging is the weak point. LYEOBOH ships these in thin bubble wrap inside a plain cardboard box — no molded foam inserts. One buyer reported a chipped rim on arrival, and a few others mentioned hairline cracks after a month of daily use. Not widespread, but enough to warrant a careful unboxing.

The glaze also dulls after repeated dishwasher cycles. Hand-washing keeps the glossy finish, but if you're a top-rack-only person like me, expect the shine to fade after about 20 washes. The cups themselves hold up fine structurally — it's purely cosmetic.

## Who Should Buy This Set

- **Home espresso enthusiasts** who pull 1-2 shots daily and want cups that match the quality of their machine
- **Gift buyers** looking for a set that looks premium without the $60+ price tag
- **Small cafés or offices** that need durable, stackable cups that can survive light commercial abuse

Skip this if you need 6+ ounce cappuccino cups — these are strictly single-shot vessels. Also skip if you're hard on dishware and refuse to hand-wash; the glaze patina will bother you.

## FAQ

**Can these fit under a standard espresso machine spout?**  
Yes. At 2.4 inches tall, they clear the portafilter spout clearance on most home machines (Gaggia Classic, Rancilio Silvia, Breville Barista Express). Check your machine's clearance if it's a low-profile model.

**Are they microwave safe?**  
Yes, but I wouldn't recommend it. The thick porcelain retains heat so well that the cup itself can become too hot to hold after 30 seconds.

**Do they come in other colors?**  
The LYEOBOH set ships in white with a glossy finish. No matte or colored options at this listing.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want thick, heat-retaining porcelain cups that look like they belong in a specialty café and hold an honest 3 ounces. The build quality punches well above the price.

**Skip it if** you're rough on dishware, demand molded packaging for shipping safety, or prefer handless cups — these have a traditional handle that some find slightly small for larger fingers.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A solid, well-executed espresso cup set that nails the essentials. The packaging issue and dishwasher glaze dulling keep it from a perfect score, but for the price, this is the set I'd recommend to any home barista.
