Let's be honest: you don't need an egg cooker. You have a pot, you have water, you have a stove. So why does the Cuisinart Egg Cooker (CEC-7, in brushed stainless steel) have 225 ratings at 4.5 stars? Because it removes the guesswork. If you've ever peeled a hard-boiled egg that came out with a green ring around the yolk, or cracked one open to find it still runny in the center, you know the pain. This $50 countertop gadget solves that problem with steam.

**What you actually get**

The unit is compact — about 9 inches wide and 7 inches tall — and the brushed stainless steel finish (as shown in the product image) makes it look more at home next to your espresso machine than most plastic rivals. The box includes the base with a built-in heating plate, a 7-egg steaming tray, a poaching tray with three wells, an omelet insert, and a measuring cup with a built-in egg piercer. The piercer is a small detail that matters: puncturing the shell before steaming prevents cracking and makes peeling dramatically easier.

**How it performs**

The operation is dead simple. Pierce the large end of each egg with the pin on the measuring cup, place eggs in the tray, fill the cup with water based on desired doneness (hard, medium, soft), and pour into the heating plate. Press the button. In about 12–15 minutes, the water boils off, steam cooks the eggs, and a buzzer sounds. The eggs come out consistently cooked, and the shells practically fall off — this is the single biggest advantage over stovetop boiling. Cold water rinse after the buzzer stops the cooking process.

The poaching tray is genuinely useful, not a gimmick. Grease it lightly, crack an egg into each well, and you get tidy poached eggs in about 6 minutes. The omelet insert works for a single-egg omelet or scrambled eggs, though it's not a replacement for a proper pan.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Looking through the reviews, the recurring theme is reliability. One buyer put it plainly: "I've had this for a year and use it three times a week. Perfect hard-boiled eggs every single time, and my kids can operate it themselves." Multiple reviewers mention how easy it is to clean — the trays and inserts are dishwasher safe, and the brushed stainless exterior just wipes down.

**Where it falls short**

Two honest complaints. First, there's no auto shut-off. The buzzer sounds, but the unit stays on (though the heating element is small and the risk is minimal). You must remember to unplug it or press the button. Second, the water measuring cup has small print. If your eyes aren't what they used to be, you'll squint to find the "hard" line versus the "medium" line. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

**Who should buy this**

- **Meal-preppers** who batch-cook a week of hard-boiled eggs for snacks or salads. This is your tool.
- **Parents** who want a foolproof way to get protein into kids without supervising a boiling pot.
- **Anyone who eats eggs more than twice a week** and is tired of inconsistent results from stovetop timing.

**Skip it if** you only eat eggs occasionally at breakfast restaurants, or if you have a microwave egg cooker that already works for your soft-boiled needs.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can it make soft-boiled eggs for ramen?**
Yes. Use the "soft" water line and you'll get a jammy yolk in about 9 minutes. Rinse immediately in cold water to stop the carryover cooking.

**Q: How many eggs fit?**
Seven in the steaming tray, three poached eggs at a time in the poaching tray. The omelet insert handles one to two eggs.

**Q: Is it really dishwasher safe?**
The trays and inserts are top-rack dishwasher safe. The base should be wiped with a damp cloth only — never submerge it.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you value consistent, easy-to-peel eggs without babysitting a pot on the stove. At $49.95, it's not the cheapest egg cooker on Amazon, but the brushed stainless build and functional accessories justify the premium over $20 plastic alternatives.

**Skip it if** you're a minimalist with limited counter space and eggs are an occasional treat, not a staple.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely useful countertop appliance that does one thing very well, with minor quibbles that don't affect its core purpose. The Cuisinart Egg Cooker earns its place on your counter.
