**The Chefman Countertop Microwave: Small Footprint, Honest Performance**

Most budget microwaves make you choose between counter space and actual cooking power. The Chefman Countertop Microwave tries to split that difference with a 0.7-cubic-foot interior squeezed into a body that won't dominate your kitchen. After digging through 220 ratings and the specs, here's the real story for shoppers.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the microwave itself, a 10-inch glass turntable, and a basic manual. That's it — no crisper tray, no extra racks. As shown in the product image, you're getting a compact stainless steel unit with a digital display and a row of preset buttons. The control panel is straightforward: time cook, defrost, and one-touch presets for popcorn, potato, beverage, and reheat.

**Real-World Performance**

At 700 watts, this isn't a speed demon. A baked potato that takes 5 minutes in a 1200W microwave will run closer to 7 here. That's the tradeoff for the smaller footprint. But here's the thing — it heats evenly. The turntable rotation is smooth, and multiple buyers noted their food comes out without cold spots.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical about the wattage, but it reheats leftovers perfectly. Just add 20-30 seconds compared to my old microwave."* That matches what most owners report — this is a reliable reheater, not a powerhouse cooker.

The preset buttons deserve mention. On many budget microwaves, presets are guesswork. Here, the popcorn setting actually stops before scorching the bag, and the beverage button warms coffee to drinking temperature without boiling it over. Small wins, but they matter daily.

**Build Quality and Design**

The stainless steel exterior resists fingerprints reasonably well — not perfect, but better than the glossy black plastic found on comparably priced units. The door opens with a firm but not stiff button press. The interior is easy to wipe down, and the removable turntable is dishwasher-safe.

The child safety lock is a genuine feature, not an afterthought. Hold the stop button for three seconds and the panel locks. Parents who bought this specifically for that feature mentioned it in reviews as a deciding factor.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the downsides. The 700-watt output is the biggest one. If you regularly cook large meals or defrost big cuts of meat, you'll feel the difference. One reviewer admitted: *"It takes longer to defrost a pound of ground beef than I'd like — plan ahead."*

The interior light is dim. Checking on food mid-cycle means opening the door and losing heat. It's a minor annoyance, but one you'll notice every single day.

And there's no convection, no air fryer mode, no grill element. This is a basic microwave. If you want a multi-function countertop appliance, look elsewhere — this won't replace your toaster oven.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Apartment dwellers and students**: The compact size fits dorm rooms, small kitchens, and rental counters.
- **Singles or couples**: Portion sizes match the 0.7 cu ft interior perfectly.
- **Parents needing a child-safe unit**: The lock feature works and is easy to engage.
- **Budget-conscious shoppers**: You're getting a reliable, honest microwave without paying for features you won't use.

**Skip It If**

- You regularly cook for 4+ people or need to fit large casserole dishes.
- You want faster cook times — look for at least 900W.
- You expect multi-function capabilities like convection or air frying.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the 700-watt power really matter?**
A: Yes, for large items. Reheating a dinner plate takes about 30 seconds longer than a 1000W unit. For single servings and beverages, the difference is negligible.

**Q: Is the turntable glass or plastic?**
A: Glass. It's dishwasher-safe and doesn't warp or stain like plastic trays on other budget models.

**Q: Can it fit a standard dinner plate?**
A: A 9- to 10-inch plate fits fine. Larger platters won't clear the interior height, so measure your largest dish before buying.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a compact, reliable microwave for everyday reheating and small meals, and you're okay with slightly longer cook times.

**Skip it if** you cook big batches, need fast defrosting, or want a multi-function appliance.
