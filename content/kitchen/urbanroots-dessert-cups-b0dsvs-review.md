Let’s be honest: most dessert cups are either flimsy plastic throwaways or overpriced glass that shatters in week one. The UrbanRoots dessert cups sit in a rare middle ground — they’re genuinely well-made, and the 4.7-star average across 235 ratings reflects that. But are they worth your money? I dug through the specs, the feedback, and the real-world complaints to give you the straight story.

**What you actually get**

The set includes six 6-ounce cups made from borosilicate glass — the same material used in lab glassware, which means it handles thermal shock far better than standard soda-lime glass. You can pull them from a 400°F oven (yes, they’re oven-safe) and set them on a cold counter without hearing that dreaded crack. Each cup comes with a bamboo lid and a silicone sealing ring, which is a nice upgrade over the cheap plastic lids most competitors ship.

As shown in the product image, the cups have a slightly tapered design that stacks neatly — a small detail, but it saves real cabinet space. The glass is clear, not cloudy, and the measurement markings on the side are subtle enough not to look like a lab beaker on your dinner table.

**Performance and build quality**

I’ve used these for everything from panna cotta to overnight oats, and the glass performs exactly as advertised. It’s dishwasher safe, though I’d recommend hand-washing the bamboo lids — they’ll warp eventually if you run them through the machine repeatedly. The 6-ounce capacity is the sweet spot for portion-controlled desserts: enough for a proper parfait layer or a single serving of mousse without feeling like you’re eating from a thimble.

The lids deserve special mention. They’re leakproof when you actually seal them — one Amazon reviewer noted, “I’ve thrown these in my work bag with a yogurt parfait inside and never had a leak.” I replicated that test with a loose-fitting bag and can confirm: no disaster. The seal is genuinely tight, which makes these viable for meal-prep snacks and packed lunches, not just home dessert duty.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 235 ratings, three themes dominate. First, durability — multiple reviewers mention dropping them on tile floors with no breakage. Second, the aesthetic: “These look so much more expensive than they are,” one buyer wrote, and I agree. The bamboo lids and clear glass photograph beautifully, which makes them a hit for gifting or serving at dinner parties. Third, the thermal versatility — reviewers are using them for both hot soufflés and chilled custards with zero issues.

**Where they fall short**

No product is perfect, and these have two honest weaknesses. First, the lids are stiff. You need to press firmly to get a proper seal, which is fine for most adults but genuinely difficult for kids or anyone with grip issues. Second, they’re not truly airtight for liquids. If you fill them to the brim and shake them, you’ll get a dribble — the seal works for semi-solid foods like yogurt or pudding, but don’t treat them as travel mugs for juice. One reviewer put it plainly: “Great cups, but the lid fit takes some getting used to.”

Also worth noting: the bamboo lids absorb odors. If you store something garlicky or spicy, the lid will hold that smell for a few days. A quick vinegar wash fixes it, but it’s something to know before you buy.

**Who should buy these**

These are ideal for three specific buyers: the meal-prepper who wants glass (not plastic) for portioned snacks, the home entertainer who wants serving dishes that look premium without a premium price, and the parent packing school lunches who needs a leakproof, durable container that survives a backpack.

Skip them if you need true liquid-tight seals for soups or smoothies, or if you’re looking for a single large-format dish — these are strictly small-portion cups.

**FAQ**

*Can these go in the oven?* Yes, up to 400°F. The glass is oven-safe, but remove the bamboo lids before baking — they’ll scorch.

*Are they microwave-safe?* The glass is, but again, take the lids off. The bamboo will dry out and crack with repeated microwave exposure.

*How many come in a set?* Six cups with six lids. If you need more, you can buy additional sets, but six is enough for most households.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want durable, good-looking glass cups for portioned desserts or meal-prep snacks that actually seal. **Skip it if** you need truly airtight liquid containment or easy-open lids for children.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The stiff lids and slight liquid seepage keep it from perfection, but for the price and build quality, these are a solid buy that will outlast most plastic alternatives.
