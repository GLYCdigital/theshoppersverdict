# Instant Pot Duo Plus 6-Quart Review: The 9-in-1 Kitchen Workhorse

If you've browsed kitchen appliances in the last decade, you've seen the Instant Pot. It's not just popular — it's practically a cultural phenomenon. The Duo Plus sits in the middle of the lineup, offering more features than the entry-level Duo without the Wi-Fi connectivity of the Pro. After six months of daily use, here's our full Instant Pot Duo Plus review.

## Design and Build Quality

The Duo Plus 6-Quart is Instant Pot's sweet spot. At 12.2 x 12.2 x 12.5 inches, it occupies about the same counter space as a large Dutch oven but stands taller. The brushed stainless steel body feels durable and resists fingerprints better than the glossy models.

The control panel is the most significant physical upgrade over the basic Duo. A larger LCD display shows cooking time, pressure level, and temperature clearly. The dial-and-button interface is intuitive — turn to select, press to confirm. New users will figure it out in under five minutes.

## Nine Functions, One Appliance

The Duo Plus does pressure cooking, slow cooking, rice cooking, steaming, sautéing, yogurt making, warming, sterilizing (glass jars and baby bottles), and the unique NutriBoost function for soup stock. That's genuinely useful versatility.

**Pressure Cooking:** This is what Instant Pot does best. Beans that normally require overnight soaking cook in 30 minutes. Tough cuts of beef become fork-tender in 45 minutes. Rice comes out perfectly every time. The pressure release valve offers natural release (gradual, for slow-cooked dishes) and quick release (instant, for vegetables and delicate foods).

**Sautéing:** The sauté function lets you brown meat and aromatics before pressure cooking — all in the same pot. It reaches a decent temperature but won't match a dedicated skillet for deep searing. For everyday cooking, it's more than adequate.

**Yogurt:** The Duo Plus makes excellent yogurt. Heat milk to 180°F (the pot does this automatically), cool to 110°F, add starter, and let it incubate for 8–12 hours. The result is thick, tangy yogurt better than most store brands, at a fraction of the cost.

**Slow Cooking:** It works, but the heat distribution isn't as gentle as a traditional slow cooker. The stainless steel inner pot transfers heat more aggressively than ceramic. Use the "Less" setting for longer, slower cooks.

## Cooking Performance

The 1000-watt heating element brings the pot to pressure quickly and maintains it reliably. Temperature fluctuations are minimal — the element cycles on and off to stay within 1–2°F of the target. Food cooks evenly from batch to batch.

The 6-quart capacity handles a 4-pound roast, a whole chicken, or about 3 cups of dry beans with room to spare. For couples and families of four, it's the perfect size. Larger families might prefer the 8-quart model.

## Cleaning and Maintenance

The stainless steel inner pot is dishwasher safe. In practice, a quick hand wash with a soft sponge removes most residue. Stubborn stuck-on food releases with a short soak and a splash of white vinegar.

The sealing ring absorbs odors — your cheesecake will taste subtly of chili after you've made pulled pork. Instant Pot recommends keeping two rings: one for savory dishes and one for sweet or neutral foods. Extra rings cost about $10.

## The Downsides

The sauté function is functional but not exceptional. For serious searing, you'll still want a cast iron skillet. The quick-release valve emits a jet of steam that's genuinely dangerous — always use a long utensil to flip the valve, and never put your face near it.

The accessories situation is also mildly annoying. The Duo Plus comes with a steam rack with handles (a genuine upgrade from the standard Duo) but you'll want extra sealing rings, a glass lid for slow cooking and storage, and silicone mitts for lifting the inner pot. These add $20–30 to the effective cost.

## Final Verdict

The Instant Pot Duo Plus 6-Quart is the appliance that actually earns its counter space. It pressure cooks, slow cooks, steams, sautés, and makes yogurt better than any single-purpose device at its price point. The sauté function could be hotter and the accessories add up, but for $99.95, this is the best value in pressure cooking today.
