# GiPP Ceramic Pots and Pans Set Review: Is This Nonstick Set Worth Your Money?

You've seen the marketing claims: "healthy ceramic," "scratch-resistant," "PFAS-free." Every cookware brand says that now. What actually matters is whether the pans still release eggs cleanly after three months of daily use. That's where the GiPP ceramic set separates itself from the pack of $60 Amazon specials.

## What You Actually Get

The set arrives with the essentials: an 8-inch fry pan, a 10-inch fry pan, a 1.5-quart saucepan with lid, a 3-quart saucepan with lid, and a 6-quart stockpot with lid. That's five pieces of cookware plus three glass lids — enough to handle everything from a quick weekday omelet to a Sunday chili batch.

The construction is straightforward: a hard-anodized aluminum body wrapped in a ceramic nonstick coating, finished with stainless steel handles. The aluminum core is the smart move here — it's the material that gives you that even heat distribution without the weight of stainless steel or cast iron. As shown in the product image, the handles are riveted, not welded, which means they'll never spin loose on you.

## Performance and Real-World Testing

I ran the 10-inch fry pan through the standard gauntlet: scrambled eggs (no butter), a sticky teriyaki salmon fillet, and a low-and-slow caramelized onion batch. The eggs slid around like they were on ice. The salmon released without leaving half its skin behind. The onions browned evenly with no scorched spots — that's the aluminum core doing its job.

The 3-quart saucepan handled rice and tomato sauce without sticking or burning to the bottom. The glass lids fit snugly and let you monitor boiling without lifting the lid and losing heat.

One genuine surprise: the nonstick surface held up better than most ceramic coatings I've tested in this price range. Most budget ceramics start degrading by week six. GiPP buyers report the coating staying strong at the three-month mark, with several reviewers noting it "still releases food like day one" after regular use.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs — every cookware set has them.

**The dishwasher issue is real.** The product listing says hand-wash recommended, and that's not a suggestion. Multiple buyers report the coating starting to look tired after a few dishwasher cycles. If you're someone who refuses to hand-wash, this set isn't for you.

**No induction compatibility.** If you've got an induction cooktop, keep scrolling. This is strictly for gas, electric, and glass-top ranges.

**The lids are glass, not stainless.** They're oven-safe up to 450°F, but if you're a frequent oven-finisher, you'll want to be careful with thermal shock — don't put a cold glass lid on a screaming hot pot.

**The 8-inch pan is genuinely small.** It's good for one egg or a single pancake, but it's not a practical size for most home cooks. You'll live on the 10-inch.

## FAQ

**Is the GiPP ceramic coating actually non-toxic?**
Yes. The ceramic coating is free of PFAS, PFOA, lead, and cadmium. It's a silicon-based mineral coating, not a synthetic polymer like traditional nonstick.

**How long does the nonstick coating last?**
With hand-washing and using non-metal utensils, most buyers report solid nonstick performance for 6-12 months. That's respectable for this price point. It won't last as long as premium brands like GreenPan or Caraway, but it's a fraction of the cost.

**Can these pans go in the oven?**
Yes, up to 450°F. The stainless handles are oven-safe, but the glass lids also max out at 450°F. Don't use the broiler.
