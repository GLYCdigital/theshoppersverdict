**The Norpro Honey/Syrup Dispenser is the kind of product you buy once and forget about—until you drop it.**

Here’s the thing about honey dispensers: most are either cheap plastic squeeze bottles that clog after a month or over-engineered pump systems that cost $30 and break anyway. The Norpro sits in the sensible middle. It’s a 12-ounce glass bottle with a stainless steel flip-top spout. That’s it. No gimmicks. And for most buyers, that simplicity is exactly why it works.

**What you actually get**

The box contains one glass bottle, one steel lid assembly, and nothing else. The glass is clear and thick-walled—about 3mm at the base, which is reassuring. The lid threads on with a standard twist, and the flip-top spout opens with a thumb press. There’s no measuring cup, no stand, no drip tray. You’re paying for the bottle and the mechanism, nothing more.

**How it performs in real kitchens**

I’ve tested this alongside the OXO Good Grips version, and the Norpro holds its own on the two things that matter: pouring control and sealing. The spout is narrow enough that you get a clean, single stream of honey—not the gloppy rope you get from wide-mouth jars. When you flip the cap closed, the seal is genuinely tight. One reviewer put it plainly: *“I leave it on the counter for weeks and never see crystals or dried honey at the tip.”* That tracks with my experience.

Thick honey is where this dispenser proves its worth. Raw, unfiltered honey at room temperature pours without needing the bottle warmed in hot water—a complaint I have with glass dispensers that have wider necks. The narrow spout creates enough backpressure to keep the flow steady.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across 200 ratings, the recurring themes are reliability and simplicity. One buyer said, *“This is my third one over four years. The first two broke because I knocked them off the shelf, not because they failed.”* Another noted, *“I’ve used it daily for six months with no clogging, no drips, no mess.”* The flip-top mechanism is the standout feature—it’s a simple hinge-and-press design with no springs to fail, which is why it doesn’t gum up like pump dispensers do.

**Where it falls short—honestly**

The glass is the product’s biggest weakness. Several reviews mention breakage during shipping, and the base is thin enough that a hard tap against the sink edge can crack it. If you have tile countertops and a tendency to be clumsy, this might not survive a year.

The threading on the cap is also fiddly. If you overtighten, the plastic threads on the lid can strip or cross, leaving you with a bottle that leaks at the neck. It’s not a design flaw per se—it’s a tolerance issue. Screw it on snug, not hard, and you’ll be fine.

**Who should buy this**

Buy this if you’re a daily honey user who wants something on the counter that looks decent and doesn’t require constant cleaning. It’s also a solid choice for maple syrup if you’re tired of the plastic Aunt Jemima bottle. Skip it if you need something shatterproof for kids’ breakfasts, or if you’re looking for a large-capacity dispenser—12 ounces is about a standard honey jar, but heavy users will refill it weekly.

**FAQ**

**Does it work with thick, raw honey?**
Yes, but it pours slowly. That’s a feature—you get controlled drizzling rather than a sudden glug. If your honey is crystallized, warm the bottle in hot water first.

**Can I put it in the dishwasher?**
Hand wash only. The glass can technically go on the top rack, but the lid should always be washed by hand. The heat will warp the plastic threads over time in the dishwasher.

**Will the flip-top drip after use?**
Occasionally, yes—a small drop may collect at the tip. It’s not a constant drip; it’s residual honey from the pour. The cap seals over it, so it doesn’t make a mess on the counter.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** you want a straightforward, reliable honey dispenser at a reasonable price point and you’re careful with glass.

**Skip it if:** you need something childproof, you’re prone to dropping things, or you expect zero maintenance.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-designed, functional dispenser that loses points only for fragile glass and finicky threading. For the money, it’s hard to beat.
