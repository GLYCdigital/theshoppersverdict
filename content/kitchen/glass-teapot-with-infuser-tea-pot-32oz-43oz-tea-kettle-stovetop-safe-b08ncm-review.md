**A Glass Teapot That Actually Earns Its Place on Your Stove**

Most glass teapots are glorified decanters — pretty to look at, but strictly countertop-only. This one from the 32oz/43oz line actually goes on the burner. That single distinction changes how you brew. Instead of boiling water separately and transferring it, you put the whole pot on the stove, watch the water come to a rolling boil, drop in your leaves, and steep. One vessel, zero extra dishes.

**What You Get in the Box**

The package includes the teapot body, a stainless steel infuser basket, and a fitted glass lid. You choose between the 32oz (1 liter) or 43oz (1.3 liter) version at checkout — the larger size handles a full kettle's worth of water, enough for three generous mugs or a full pot for two. The infuser is fine-mesh stainless steel with a hook that rests securely on the pot's rim. It's not flimsy; the mesh holds its shape even after repeated washes.

The defining feature is the material: borosilicate glass. That's the same stuff lab beakers and high-end baking dishes use. It tolerates rapid temperature changes far better than standard soda-lime glass, which is why this pot can sit on a stovetop burner at all.

**Real-World Performance**

I brewed a batch of loose-leaf oolong at around 195°F and a full boil for black tea. The glass heated evenly with no hot spots, and the infuser basket gave the leaves room to unfurl — a win over those cramped ball infusers that strangle the tea. The spout pours clean with no dribble down the side, though you should pour with the lid held in place. It's not a tight seal; gravity is doing the work here.

**Where It Falls Short**

No product is flawless, and this one has two honest caveats.

First, **the lid isn't a sealed fit.** It sits on the rim with a small gap for steam to escape. If you tilt the pot aggressively while pouring, you'll get a few drips down the side. The fix is simple — hold the lid with your thumb — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Second, **stovetop use demands respect.** This is borosilicate glass, but it's not indestructible. High heat on a large burner can create thermal stress. The instructions say low-to-medium flame, and that's not a suggestion. If you're the type to crank the burner to max and walk away, get a metal kettle instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this on an induction cooktop?**  
No. The pot is glass — induction requires a magnetic base. This works on gas, electric coil, and ceramic glass stovetops only.

**Q: Will the glass stain from tea over time?**  
Borosilicate glass is non-porous, so it resists staining. A quick rinse with warm water after each use keeps it clear. For stubborn residue, a baking soda paste works without scratching.

**Q: Does the infuser work for fine teas like rooibos or chamomile?**  
Yes. The mesh is fine enough to contain small-leaf teas and herbs. You might get a few specks of dust in the first cup, but nothing that ruins the brew.
