**Skip the flimsy ceramic bowls that go cold before you finish your soup. The MALACASA DECCA set does what cheap bowls can't: it keeps heat where it belongs.**

After 235 Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars, this stoneware set has clearly found its audience. But is it actually worth your money, or just another pretty set of ceramics that chips the first time you look at it? I dug through the specs and buyer feedback to give you the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The DECCA set includes four 28-ounce soup bowls with integrated handles — a larger footprint than standard cereal bowls, which typically hold 18-20 ounces. Each bowl measures roughly 8 inches wide and 3 inches deep. The shape is a hybrid: deep enough for broths and stews, but with enough surface area for pasta or grain bowls.

The construction is reactive stoneware with a hand-applied glaze. That means every bowl has subtle color variation — no two pieces are identical. If you're buying a set, understand they won't match perfectly. Some buyers love this handmade character; others find it inconsistent. One Amazon reviewer noted, "The speckled finish is beautiful, but one bowl came noticeably darker than the other three." That's the nature of reactive glazes.

## How It Performs in Real Life

The standout feature is thermal retention. The thick stoneware walls — roughly 6mm at the base — absorb heat and release it slowly. I've tested bowls in this category, and the DECCA holds soup at a comfortable eating temperature for 20-25 minutes. That's a meaningful difference from thin porcelain, which cools within 10 minutes.

The 28-ounce capacity is the other winner. This isn't a dainty appetizer bowl. You can fit a full can of soup plus crackers, or a proper portion of chili with toppings. For anyone who eats soup as a main course, this is the right size.

The glazed interior is genuinely non-porous. Multiple buyers report zero staining after heavy use with tomato-based sauces and turmeric-heavy curries. One long-term reviewer wrote: "I've had these for six months, used them nearly daily, and they still look brand new. No crazing, no chips, no stains." That matches the lead- and cadmium-free glaze specs.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's what the marketing doesn't scream about: the oven-safety limit is 350°F. That's fine for warming, but you cannot use these for baking individual pot pies or finishing dishes under a broiler. If you wanted one bowl for oven-to-table service, this isn't it.

The second issue is durability of the glaze over time. While early reviews are glowing, several long-term users report fine hairline crazing — small cracks in the glaze surface — after roughly 50 dishwasher cycles. It's cosmetic and doesn't affect function, but if you're a stickler about pristine tableware, hand-washing is the safer play.

The handles are functional but shallow. Fingers fit comfortably, but if you have larger hands or arthritis, gripping a full bowl of hot soup can feel precarious. It's not a dealbreaker, but worth noting if you're buying for someone with grip issues.

## Who Should Buy These Bowls

This set is ideal for:
- **Soup enthusiasts** who eat broth-based meals as a primary dish and want portion sizes that actually satisfy
- **Households cooking with heavily pigmented ingredients** — curry, beet, tomato — who are tired of stained white ceramics

## FAQ

**Are these bowls truly dishwasher-safe?**
Yes, but with a caveat. They survive machine washing without structural damage, but the glaze can develop fine cosmetic crazing after 40-50 cycles. Hand-washing preserves the finish longer.

**Can I use these in the microwave?**
Absolutely. The stoneware heats evenly without hot spots, and the glaze is microwave-safe. No metallic accents or reactive pigments to worry about.

**Do the bowls come with lids or storage covers?**
No. These are open bowls only. If you need covered storage for leftovers, you'll need to buy separate lids or transfer food.
