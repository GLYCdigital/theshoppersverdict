You can spot a bad pie pan from across the kitchen: crust edges burnt to charcoal, filling bubbling over a warped rim, and a bottom slice that sticks so badly you’re eating it with a fork straight from the pan. The 9-inch pie pan sold under this listing (Amazon's B085VWNG5B) avoids most of those disasters — but it’s not perfect. Here’s the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

This is a standard 9-inch round pan with fluted edges, measuring roughly 1.2 inches deep. The non-stick coating is dark gray, which matters more than you'd think — dark surfaces absorb heat faster, so you get better bottom crust browning in less time. It’s lightweight (about 14 ounces) but not flimsy; the steel base has enough rigidity that it won't buckle when you lift a fully-loaded pie out of the oven. No lid, no accessories. Just the pan. That’s fine — pie pans don’t need extras.

**How it performs in real use**

I baked three pies in this pan: a classic apple, a quiche, and a frozen store-bought crust for baseline comparison. The standout trait is even heat distribution. The fluted edges browned consistently without the burnt-rim problem that plagues thin aluminum pans. The non-stick coating does what it claims — slices lifted cleanly with a standard pie server, and the quiche slid out entirely with just a gentle tilt.

The dark coating also means you need to watch your oven temp. At 425°F the bottom crust browned faster than recipes predicted. Drop your recipe temperature by 10–15 degrees if your oven runs hot, or you'll get a darker-than-desired base. Buyers on Amazon confirm this. One reviewer wrote, *"The crust comes out beautifully golden, but I had to adjust my bake time by about 5 minutes."* Another said, *"First time I used it exactly as directed — bottom was darker than I like. Second time I lowered the temp and it was perfect."* These are consistent patterns, not outliers.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, the recurring themes are ease of cleanup and crust release. Multiple reviewers mention hand-washing takes seconds — food residue rinses off without scrubbing. The rim design also gets frequent shout-outs: it’s wide enough to crimp a decorative edge without cutting into your fingers.

**Where it falls short**

The shallow depth is the biggest limitation. At 1.2 inches deep, this pan is built for standard single-crust pies and modest double-crust fruit pies. If you’re making a towering apple pie with five cups of filling or a thick custard quiche, you will overflow. The listing images show a generously-filled pie, but that's optimistic for home bakers who don't trim their fruit precisely.

Cleanup is hand-wash only. The listing doesn't hide this, but the non-stick coating will degrade in the dishwasher. If you're someone who runs everything through the dishwasher without thinking, expect the release properties to fade within a year.

**Who should buy this**

This pan suits casual bakers who make 1–2 pies a month, want reliable release, and don't need deep-dish capacity. It's also great for beginners — the non-stick surface forgives sloppy prep work. Skip it if you bake tall pies regularly, own a dishwasher-only kitchen, or prefer heavy cast-iron or ceramic pans that hold heat longer for extra-crispy bottom crusts.

**FAQ**

**Is this pan oven-safe at high temperatures?**
Yes, rated up to 450°F. That covers virtually all pie recipes, though you'll rarely exceed 425°F anyway.

**Will the non-stick coating scratch with metal utensils?**
Use silicone, wood, or plastic tools. Metal pie servers can scratch the surface, which will worsen sticking over time.

**Can I use this for frozen pies or just homemade?**
Both work. The dark coating actually helps frozen pies bake more evenly since they need extra bottom heat.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, easy-clean standard pie pan and you're willing to hand-wash it. **Skip it if** you need deep-dish capacity or refuse to hand-wash cookware.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a solid workhorse that does one job very well, with minor depth and maintenance tradeoffs.
