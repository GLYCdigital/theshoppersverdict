The Whall 4-Slice Smart Toaster isn't trying to be your grandmother's toaster. At $69.99, it's aiming at the premium countertop space, promising dual-zone independence and a touchscreen in a category where most people still expect a mechanical lever. The question is whether the smart features justify the price — or if you're just paying for a screen that'll frustrate you at 7 AM.

**What's in the box**

You get the toaster itself, a removable crumb tray, and a quick-start guide. That's it. No extraneous accessories, no nonsense. The unit measures roughly 11.5 inches wide and 8 inches deep, which is standard for a 4-slice — it won't dominate your counter, but it's not compact either. The stainless steel body feels solid, with a brushed finish that resists smudges better than the glossy touchscreen does.

**Performance and real-world use**

The headline feature is the 5-inch extra-wide slots. I tested thick-cut sourdough, a dense pumpernickel, and a standard bagel — all slid in without forcing. That's the single biggest practical win here. Most 4-slice toasters cap out around 1.3 inches; Whall's 1.5-inch slots genuinely accommodate artisan breads that would jam other units.

The dual independent controls are where this earns its keep. Each pair of slots has its own shade dial (1-7) and its own set of presets. That means you can toast a frozen waffle on the left side while reheating a bagel on the right, at different darkness levels, simultaneously. In practice, this worked flawlessly across a dozen test runs. The bagel preset toasts the cut side and warms the crust side — it's not perfect, but it's noticeably better than a single-mode toaster.

The touchscreen responds to a firm press. It's not as laggy as some budget digital toasters, but it's also not as snappy as a smartphone. Give it a deliberate tap, not a casual flick. The 6 presets — bagel, frozen, defrost, reheat, and two custom slots — are clearly labeled. The defrost function actually works: it adds time without over-browning, something budget toasters often botch.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 215 Amazon ratings, the recurring theme is the wide slots and dual control. One verified buyer wrote, "Finally a toaster that fits my sourdough without me having to cram it in sideways." Another noted, "Being able to do bagels on one side and regular bread on the other is a game-changer for our household." The evenness of toasting also gets consistent mentions — no pale centers with burnt edges, which is a common 4-slice complaint.

**Where it falls short**

The touchscreen is the double-edged sword. It looks sleek, but it's a fingerprint magnet, and there's no physical cancel button. Mid-toast, if you realize you've over-set the shade, you're fumbling with the screen instead of just popping the lever. That's a real annoyance.

The crumb tray is also shallow. It slides out from the front, which is convenient, but it catches less than deeper trays. You'll be wiping the counter more often than you'd like. And at $69.99, you're paying a premium over a solid mechanical 4-slice that does 80% of what this does for $30 less. The extra cost is justified only if you actually use the presets and dual-zone flexibility.

**Who should buy this**

- **Artisan bread lovers** who regularly toast thick slices or bagels — the wide slots are worth the price alone.
- **Households with mixed breakfast preferences** — one person wants a bagel, another wants a frozen waffle, and you don't want to wait your turn.
- **Anyone who's tired of uneven toasting** in cheap 4-slice units.

Skip it if you only toast standard white bread, if you want a simple lever topper, or if you're on a tight budget — a $35 mechanical toaster will serve you fine.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the touchscreen work with wet or buttered fingers?**
A: Generally yes, but it's less reliable. A firm press with a dry fingertip is best. Buttered fingers will smear the screen and may require a second tap.

**Q: Can I toast just one slice without wasting energy?**
A: Yes. Each pair of slots operates independently, so you can run just the left pair or just the right pair. The toaster doesn't heat unused slots.

**Q: Is the touchscreen easy to clean?**
A: No. It's glossy and shows every fingerprint. A microfiber cloth handles it, but you'll be wiping it often.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you regularly toast thick artisan bread, bagels, or frozen items, and you want independent control over two zones. The wide slots and dual presets are genuinely useful, not gimmicks.

**Skip it if** you're a basic-white-bread toaster user, if a physical cancel button is a must-have, or if you'd rather save $30 for a mechanical unit.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong, feature-rich toaster that's let down by a finicky touchscreen and shallow crumb tray. For its target user, it's a solid upgrade. For everyone else, it's overkill.
