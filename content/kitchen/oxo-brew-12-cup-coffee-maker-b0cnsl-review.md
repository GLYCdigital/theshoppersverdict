The OXO Brew 12-Cup Coffee Maker doesn't look flashy. It's a rectangular stainless box with a carafe, a water tank, and one button. But this machine has quietly become the default recommendation for coffee enthusiasts who want café-quality drip without spending $300 on a high-end brewer. Here's why.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the main brewer, a 12-cup double-wall stainless thermal carafe, and a permanent mesh filter. That last part matters — no paper filters to rebuy. The machine measures roughly 11" wide, 14.5" deep, and 14" tall, so it fits under standard upper cabinets. The 60-ounce water reservoir is removable, which makes filling and cleaning significantly easier than fixed-tank designs.

**Performance: where it justifies the price**

The OXO Brew holds Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) certification, which isn't marketing fluff. The brewer pre-heats water to between 197.6°F and 204.8°F — the exact range for proper coffee extraction. Most sub-$100 drip machines cycle water at inconsistent temperatures, which is why your cheap brewer produces bitter or weak coffee. This one doesn't.

The "Rainmaker" shower head is the real hero. It distributes water evenly across the entire coffee bed rather than dumping a stream in the center. You'll notice it the first time you lift the basket: the grounds form a flat, uniform puck instead of a cratered mess. That even saturation means you get more flavor from the same beans.

The thermal carafe is a genuine upgrade too. Double-wall stainless keeps coffee hot for roughly two hours, and because there's no warming plate, the coffee doesn't develop that scorched, metallic taste you get from glass carafes left on heat. One reviewer summed it up: "I used to drink my coffee fast because it went bad. Now I can pour a cup at noon and it still tastes fresh from my 7 AM brew."

**The praise is consistent**

Across 215 ratings at 4.3 stars, buyers repeatedly mention three things: temperature consistency, the thermal carafe, and the even extraction. As one verified purchaser wrote: "This is the first drip machine that makes coffee comparable to my pour-over. I've owned $30 brewers and $200 brewers. This beats both." Another noted the build quality: "Everything feels solid. The water tank slides smoothly, the carafe seats securely, and nothing rattles."

**Where it falls short**

The biggest gap: no programmable timer. You can't set this to brew at 6:30 AM. You press the button and wait about 10 minutes for a full pot. That's a dealbreaker for some.

The carafe lid is a multi-piece assembly that's genuinely annoying to clean. Coffee oils build up in the nooks, and since the lid isn't dishwasher-safe, you're hand-scrubbing it weekly. The brew basket is also hand-wash only. If you're a "everything goes in the dishwasher" person, this machine will test your patience.

The pause-and-pour feature has a quirk: it only works while brewing. Once the cycle finishes, you can't pull the carafe out to grab a cup without the basket dripping onto the hot plate. It's a small thing, but you'll notice it.

**Who should buy this**

- **The coffee enthusiast on a budget**: You want proper extraction temperature and even saturation without paying $300+ for a Technivorm.
- **The thermal carafe convert**: You're tired of burnt coffee from warming plates and want a carafe that actually holds heat.
- **The no-fuss brewer**: You don't need smart features or timers. You want consistent, great coffee with one button.

Skip it if you need a programmable timer, refuse to hand-wash parts, or prefer the convenience of paper filters and a glass carafe you can see through.

**FAQ**

**Is the permanent filter as good as paper?**
It produces slightly more sediment in the cup — some drinkers notice it, most don't. The tradeoff is zero ongoing cost and no waste. If you're sensitive to sediment, you can use a #4 paper filter in the basket instead.

**How long does the thermal carafe actually keep coffee hot?**
Real-world reports say about two hours to "hot," and up to three to "warm." It's not as long as some insulated travel mugs, but it's far better than any glass carafe on a warming plate.

**Does the 12-cup capacity mean 12 standard mugs?**
No. It's 12 five-ounce cups, which is the industry standard. That's about 6-7 standard 8-ounce mugs — roughly 60 ounces total. If you drink large mugs, treat this as a 6-cup machine.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want café-quality drip coffee at home, value even extraction and proper temperature, and don't mind hand-washing a few parts. **Skip it if** you need a programmable timer, want dishwasher-safe components, or prefer the ritual of paper filters.

The OXO Brew 12-Cup delivers 90% of what a $300 brewer offers for roughly half the price. It's not perfect — the timer omission is genuinely frustrating — but for the coffee it produces, it's one of the best-value brewers on Amazon right now.
