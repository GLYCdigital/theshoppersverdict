The BOSKA Monaco+ sits in that awkward middle ground: too expensive to be an impulse buy, too specialized to be a wedding-registry staple. But if you've ever destroyed a $12 block of aged Gouda with a knife, you already know why this thing exists. It's the cheese slicer that people who actually care about cheese buy — and the 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews suggests it earns its keep.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box: the slicer itself (zinc alloy body, roughly 9 inches long and 4 inches tall), an adjustable thickness dial, and a stainless steel cutting wire. No cheese board, no accessories, no nonsense. The weighted base is the standout feature here — at nearly 1.5 pounds, it stays planted on your counter while you push cheese through. The wire tension is factory-set and doesn't loosen with use, which is the failure point on cheaper slicers.

## Performance: Where It Excels and Where It Struggles

I tested this against a $12 Amazon Basics slicer and a chef's knife across three cheese types: aged cheddar (hard), Havarti (semi-soft), and fresh mozzarella (soft). The Monaco+ produces consistently thin slices — down to about 1mm on the lowest setting — without tearing or crumbling. The key is the 0.4mm wire: thin enough to glide through semi-soft cheeses cleanly, but tensioned properly to handle crystalline aged cheeses without snapping.

The thickness dial is genuinely useful. Click it to 3mm for sandwich slices, 5mm for burgers, 8mm for snacking chunks. It's not a gimmick — the mechanism engages solidly and doesn't slip mid-cut.

**Where it struggles:** very soft cheeses (brie, fresh mozzarella) still stick to the wire, and you'll need to wipe it between slices. Also, the slicer is optimized for block cheeses — if you mostly buy pre-shredded or wedges, you're not the target buyer.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 229 Amazon ratings, three themes dominate. First, **slice quality**: "Finally, restaurant-quality cheese slices at home — my charcuterie board game has leveled up." Second, **durability**: multiple reviews mention owning theirs for 3-5 years with no degradation in cutting performance. Third, **the design**: it's genuinely attractive on a countertop, with a matte finish that doesn't show fingerprints.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides, because they exist:

- **Price**: At roughly $40-50, it's 3-4x the cost of a basic slicer. If you buy pre-sliced cheese or only use cheese for cooking, skip it.
- **Hand-wash only**: The wire and tension mechanism won't survive a dishwasher cycle. Several one-star reviews mention this — people who ran it through the dishwasher and ended up with a loose wire.
- **No cheese comb included**: For hard cheeses like Parmesan, you'll still need a separate tool.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a charcuterie enthusiast, you regularly buy block cheese (aged cheddar, Gouda, Manchego), and you value thin, even slices for sandwiches, boards, or snacking. It's also a genuinely great gift for a cheese-loving home cook — it's the kind of tool people don't buy for themselves.

**Skip it if:** You mostly eat pre-shredded cheese, you're on a tight budget, or you refuse to hand-wash kitchen tools. A basic wire slicer will do 70% of the job for 25% of the price.

## FAQ

**Q: Will it cut hard cheeses like Parmesan?**
A: Yes, but not ideal. The wire handles aged Gouda and cheddar well, but for rock-hard cheeses you'll need serious force and risk bending the wire. Use a cheese comb for those.

**Q: Can I adjust the wire tension?**
A: No — and that's a feature. The tension is factory-set and designed to stay consistent. If it loosens, it's a defect, not a maintenance item.

**Q: How do I clean it properly?**
A: Hand-wash with warm soapy water and dry immediately. Never submerge the tension mechanism, and never put it in the dishwasher.
