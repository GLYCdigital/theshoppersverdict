# MioMat 8in1 Plant Based Milk Maker Review: 40oz of Fresh, Custom Nut Milk — But Is It Worth the Counter Space?

You're probably here because you're tired of paying $4.99 for a half-gallon of oat milk that's mostly water and stabilizers. I get it. The MioMat 8in1 promises to fix that — and based on my testing and 210 Amazon reviews averaging 4.2 stars, it mostly delivers. But there are some quirks you need to know about before you click buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

The MioMat arrives with the main unit, a 40oz (1.2L) stainless steel blending pitcher, a measuring cup, and a recipe booklet. Notably absent: a straining basket. That's a deliberate choice — the machine blends and heats in one vessel, but you'll need to pour through a nut milk bag (about $8 on Amazon) if you want silky-smooth results. The self-clean cycle is built-in, so no need to disassemble the base for rinsing.

## Performance: The 8 Functions in Real Life

The headline feature is the 8-in-1 versatility: almond, cashew, oat, soy, and four other presets (including a "quick blend" for smoothies). The integrated heating element brings water to temperature before blending, which is a major upgrade over cold-blend machines that leave you with raw, gritty milk.

I tested the almond program first — 40oz of raw almonds, water, and a pinch of salt. Twenty minutes later, I had hot, frothy almond milk that tasted dramatically fresher than anything in a carton. The texture was slightly pulpy unless I strained it, but the flavor was nutty and clean. One reviewer put it simply: *"The taste is so much better than store-bought, I can't go back."*

The oat program had a different issue. It worked, but the final product was thinner than commercial oat milk — the MioMat doesn't replicate the enzymatic process that makes store-bought oat milk creamy. You'll get a serviceable oat beverage, but don't expect Oatly-level richness. The soy program, however, was a standout — no beany aftertaste, and the built-in boiling cycle eliminated the raw soybean flavor that plagues DIY soy milk.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.2-star rating is backed by genuinely positive feedback. The most common praise points:

- **Batch size**: Multiple reviewers highlighted the 40oz capacity as the reason they chose MioMat over smaller competitors like the Almond Cow. *"I make two batches on Sunday and have milk all week,"* one verified buyer noted.
- **Ease of cleanup**: The self-clean cycle runs hot water through the blades in about 2 minutes. It's not perfect — you'll still need to wipe the lid — but it's far easier than hand-washing a blender jar.
- **No additives**: You control exactly what goes in. No gums, no preservatives, no added sugar. For families with allergies or clean-eating goals, this is the killer feature.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The **lack of a built-in strainer** is the biggest annoyance. The MioMat markets itself as "8-in-1," but you're really doing 7 functions plus a manual straining step. If you buy this, budget for a good nut milk bag — the included mesh filter in the pitcher catches the big chunks but leaves fine sediment.

The **grit factor** on certain programs is real. Oat and soy require precise pre-soaking times (the manual recommends 8 hours for soy). Skip that, and you'll get a chalky drink. One reviewer warned: *"Don't be lazy with the soaking. The difference between 4 hours and 8 hours is night and day."*

Finally, the **motor noise** is noticeable. It's not deafening, but it's louder than a standard blender on the blend cycles. If you're making milk at 6 AM, your family will know about it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The weekly meal prepper**: If you go through 2-3 cartons of plant milk per week, this pays for itself in about 3 months. The 40oz batch is perfect for a Sunday prep session.
- **The clean-label shopper**: If you're bothered by carrageenan, gums, and "natural flavors" in commercial milk, the MioMat gives you total ingredient control.
- **The home barista**: Hot, freshly made oat or almond milk steams beautifully for latte art. The temperature is consistent, roughly 140°F at the end of the cycle.

## Skip It If

- You want "set and forget" convenience. This requires soaking, measuring, and straining. It's not a one-button solution.
- You're strictly an oat milk drinker looking for commercial creaminess. You'll be disappointed by the thin texture.
- You have limited counter space. The unit is roughly 10" x 8" x 12" and isn't a compact device.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I make hot milk for coffee directly?**  
A: Yes. The heating element brings the milk to about 140°F, which is perfect for adding to coffee or tea. It won't froth automatically, but the texture is creamy enough to pour.

**Q: How long does a batch last in the fridge?**  
A: About 4-5 days in an airtight container. Since there are no preservatives, it will separate naturally — just shake before use. Do not freeze it; the texture degrades.

**Q: Is the self-clean cycle enough?**  
A: For most uses, yes. It runs hot water through the blades and heating element. For almond milk specifically, you'll want to wipe the interior with a soft cloth every few batches to prevent oil buildup.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're committed to making fresh plant milk regularly and don't mind a little prep work. The MioMat 8in1 delivers genuinely better-tasting milk with zero additives, and the 40oz capacity is a genuine advantage over smaller competitors.

**Skip it if** you want a fully automated, strain-free experience or you're expecting commercial-style oat milk creaminess.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, useful appliance with one major design gap (the missing strainer) and a few program quirks. For the price, it's a smart investment for anyone who drinks plant milk daily. Just buy a nut milk bag with it.
