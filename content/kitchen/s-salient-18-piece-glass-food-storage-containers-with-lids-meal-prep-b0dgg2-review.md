**What's Actually in the Box**

You're getting 18 pieces total: nine glass containers and nine matching lids. The lineup breaks down as:

- 3 large rectangles (roughly 30 oz) — perfect for main dishes or big salads
- 3 medium squares (roughly 22 oz) — the workhorse size for leftovers and meal prep
- 3 small rounds (roughly 15 oz) — good for sides, overnight oats, or kids' portions

The glass is borosilicate, which is the key spec here. That's the same material used in lab glassware—it handles thermal shock far better than standard soda-lime glass. You can pull a container straight from the freezer and pop it in a preheated oven without worrying about it fracturing. The lids are BPA-free plastic with four locking tabs on each side, which is more than the typical two-tab design you see on cheaper sets.

**Real-World Performance**

I've been rotating these through daily meal prep for about six months now. The locking mechanism is the standout feature. Each lid snaps down with four points of contact, creating a seal that's genuinely leak-proof. I've tested it with thin chicken broth and a vinaigrette dressing—zero seepage, even when tossed sideways in a cooler. The containers stack neatly in the fridge, and the lids nest inside the bowls when you're storing them empty, which frees up cabinet space.

The glass cleans up easily. Tomato sauce, turmeric-heavy curries, even burnt cheese—all come off with a quick soak and a sponge. No staining on the glass itself, which is a common complaint with cheaper tempered glass sets. The glass is also oven-safe up to 400°F, so you can reheat leftovers directly without transferring to a baking dish.

**Honest Shortcomings**

The lids are the weak point, and you need to know this going in. They're plastic, which means they'll stain with tomato-based dishes and hold onto odors from garlic or fish. Hand washing the lids is recommended—dishwasher heat can warp the locking tabs over time. That said, the lids are replaceable, and the 18-piece configuration means you have spares if one cracks.

The smallest container is genuinely small—about a cup of volume. It's fine for dips, sauces, or a single egg, but don't expect it to hold a real serving of leftovers. You'll get more practical use from the three medium squares.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Ask**

**Q: Can these go in the dishwasher?**
A: The glass is dishwasher-safe, but hand-wash the lids. The heat and water pressure can warp the plastic locking tabs, which breaks the airtight seal over time.

**Q: Are the containers truly oven-safe?**
A: Yes, the glass is rated to 400°F. But always remove the plastic lid before putting a container in the oven—the lids are not heat-safe.

**Q: Do the lids stay sealed when stacked in the fridge?**
A: Yes, the four-tab design holds the seal even with weight on top. Just make sure each tab is fully snapped down before stacking.
