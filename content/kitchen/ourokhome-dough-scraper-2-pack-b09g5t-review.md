I’ve burned through more plastic dough scrapers than I care to count. They snap at the handle, flex uselessly against stiff dough, and end up in the trash after a few months of weekly baking. So when the Ourokhome 2-pack showed up, I put them through the wringer: sourdough at 75% hydration, sticky brioche, and a full sheet-pan batch of cinnamon rolls. Here’s the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

Two identical scrapers, each with a full-tang stainless steel blade and a contoured wooden handle. The blade measures roughly 4.5 inches wide by 3 inches tall — compact but substantial. One edge is sharpened for clean cutting; the opposite edge is rolled for scraping. The handles are shaped with a slight finger groove, which makes a real difference when you’re bearing down on a stiff dough. No packaging waste beyond a simple cardboard sleeve, which I appreciate.

**Performance: where it shines and where it stumbles**

The rolled edge is the star. It glides under dough without gouging your countertop, and it doesn’t flex or chatter the way thin stamped-steel scrapers do. On a wet sourdough at 75% hydration, it scooped and folded cleanly without the dough clinging to the blade — a problem I’ve had with cheaper stainless models that lack any coating.

The cutting edge divides dough cleanly. I portioned a 2-pound batch of brioche into 12 equal pieces, and each cut was sharp and precise, no squishing or tearing. For bench work — scraping flour, cleaning up stray bits, transferring chopped vegetables — the rolled edge handles it all without scratching a wooden cutting board.

The handle is where Ourokhome got it right. Most scrapers under $20 have slick plastic or unfinished wood that gets slippery when your hands are floured or wet. This one has a smooth but grippy finish that held firm through a full kneading session. The contoured shape fits my hand naturally, and it didn’t dig into my palm during extended use.

**Where it falls short**

The wood handles are the main tradeoff. They’re not dishwasher-safe — hand-wash only — and they’ll need occasional mineral oil to prevent drying and cracking, especially in low-humidity kitchens. That’s a maintenance step most metal-handled scrapers don’t require.

The 4.5-inch blade width is also slightly narrow for serious home bakers working with 1,000-gram flour batches. You’ll need multiple passes to scrape a wide bench clean. It’s not a dealbreaker, but if you regularly work with large dough batches, a 6-inch bench scraper might serve you better.

**Who should buy this**

If you’re a home baker making bread, pizza, or pastries once or twice a week, this is the scraper to buy. It’s also ideal for anyone who’s frustrated by flimsy plastic tools that bend or snap. The 2-pack means you can keep one dedicated to dough and one for general kitchen tasks — a setup that makes sense for anyone who bakes regularly.

Skip it if you need a scraper for industrial-sized batches, or if you refuse to hand-wash any kitchen tool. For everyone else, this is a solid upgrade that’ll last years with basic care.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this on a cast iron skillet or nonstick pan?**
A: The rolled edge is safe for cast iron, but avoid it on nonstick coatings — the metal edge can scratch the surface. Use a silicone scraper for nonstick.

**Q: Is the blade sharp enough to cut through cold butter or frozen dough?**
A: The cutting edge is sharp, but it’s not a knife. It cuts room-temperature dough cleanly, but frozen or very cold butter will require serious pressure. Use a heavy chef’s knife for that task.

**Q: How do I clean the wooden handles without damaging them?**
A: Hand-wash with warm soapy water, dry immediately, and apply a thin coat of food-grade mineral oil once a month. Never soak them or run them through the dishwasher.
