The surface features a textured pattern designed to grip hot cookware while creating an air gap between the pot and your countertop.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline spec here is heat resistance up to 450°F (232°C). That covers everything you'd normally pull off a stovetop — sauté pans, saucepots, even a screaming-hot cast iron skillet. The silicone doesn't warp, melt, or transfer heat to your surfaces. One long-term user reports: *"I've used these daily for six months with my cast iron. No warping, no melting, no heat transfer to my butcher block. They just work."*

The non-slip grip is the standout feature. Unlike cork trivets that slide around or wooden ones that rock on uneven surfaces, the Walfos silicone clings to both the counter and the cookware. Another reviewer notes: *"Set a heavy Le Creuset pot on these and they don't budge. The grip is genuinely impressive."*

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The light-colored trivets in this set will show scorch marks and staining. Dark pans leave gray smudges, and if you're prone to setting down a pan that's hotter than the rated limit, you'll see discoloration. It's cosmetic — doesn't affect performance — but if you're particular about aesthetics, go with a darker color option.

Second, the 450°F rating has limits. This is not an oven-safe product in the way you might expect from a silicone baking mat. Don't use these under a broiler, on a grill grate, or in the oven above that temperature. Some buyers have melted trivets by using them as pot rests directly on a hot burner. That's user error, but the marketing could be clearer about the distinction between stovetop-safe and open-flame-safe.
