# Kitchen in the box Food Processor Review: Small Meat Grinder That Earns Its Counter Space

Let's be honest: most budget-friendly food processors under $50 are either glorified onion choppers or plastic machines that walk across your counter. The Kitchen in the box Food Processor with its small meat grinder attachment sits in a smarter middle ground. It's not going to replace a 14-cup Cuisinart, but it doesn't try to. It does one thing well — small-batch prep — and does it without breaking your budget.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the motor base, a 2-cup work bowl with lid and feed chute, the S-blade chopper, and a stainless steel grinding plate with auger for meat. There's also a pusher tool that doubles as a measuring cup — a small detail that saves you from digging through drawers. The bowl is BPA-free plastic, and the blades are stainless steel. Everything except the motor base goes on the top rack of the dishwasher, which matters more than any spec sheet.

As shown in the product image, the whole unit is compact — roughly the size of a large coffee mug on its side. If cabinet space is tight, this tucks into a corner drawer without complaint.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this through a standard prep gauntlet: onions, garlic, fresh herbs, and a pound of beef chuck for burgers. The chopping blade handled onions and herbs in 3-4 pulses with consistent results — no giant chunks left lurking at the bottom. The meat grinding function is where this unit earns its keep. Cut the chuck into 1-inch cubes, freeze for 20 minutes, and the auger pulls it through the plate cleanly. A full pound takes about 90 seconds with a few pauses to clear the feed tube.

The motor is rated at 200 watts, which is modest. It'll power through ground meat and soft vegetables fine, but it bogs down on dense stuff. Carrots need pre-cutting. Frozen meat is a no-go unless you want to hear the motor strain and smell that hot-electric smell. Several Amazon reviewers echo this — one noted, "Works great for ground pork, but don't try to grind anything with bones or heavy sinew." Fair warning.

## Where It Falls Short

Three honest complaints, and they're consistent across reviews.

**The motor is the bottleneck.** It's fine for intermittent use — think two batches of meatballs or a quick salsa — but it's not built for marathon processing sessions. Running it continuously for more than a minute invites overheating. Give it rest breaks between batches.

**The short cord is genuinely annoying.** At roughly 2.5 feet, you'll need an outlet close to your prep area. If your kitchen has limited counter outlets, factor in an extension cord or a different placement.

**Noise level is noticeable.** It's not a jet engine, but it's louder than a blender on low. One reviewer described it as "a loud hum with a rattle on top." If you're grinding meat at 6 AM, your household will know.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the home cook who wants fresh-ground meat for burgers, meatballs, or sausage without investing in a dedicated grinder. It's also great for:

- **Small households** (1-2 people) who don't need a 10-cup processor that dominates the cabinet
- **Apartment dwellers** with limited storage
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want functionality without the $100+ price tag of premium brands

Skip it if you regularly process large batches, need to grind through tough cuts, or want a machine that can double as a full-size food processor for dough and heavy mixing. This is a specialist, not a generalist.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can it grind a whole chicken or meat with bones?**
A: No. The motor isn't strong enough, and the grinding plate will clog. Stick to boneless cuts cut into 1-inch cubes. This is for ground meat, not bone-in processing.

**Q: How does it compare to a full-size food processor?**
A: It's faster and easier to clean for small batches, but it can't handle large volumes or dough. The bowl is 2 cups, so you'll work in batches for anything bigger than a single meal's worth of ingredients.

**Q: Is the meat grinder attachment actually useful, or just a gimmick?**
A: It's genuinely functional. The auger and grinding plate are solid stainless steel, not cheap plastic. For a pound of meat, it produces texture equal to store-bought ground beef. Just keep the meat cold and cut it small.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a compact, two-in-one chopper and meat grinder for small-batch cooking without spending big money. It delivers honest performance for the price, cleans up easily, and earns its place on a small counter.

**Skip it if** you need heavy-duty grinding capacity, process large quantities regularly, or demand whisper-quiet operation. This is a capable little workhorse, not a commercial-grade appliance. The motor limitations and short cord are real tradeoffs, but for the price, they're acceptable ones.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid value that does exactly what it promises, with a few compromises you should know about before buying.
