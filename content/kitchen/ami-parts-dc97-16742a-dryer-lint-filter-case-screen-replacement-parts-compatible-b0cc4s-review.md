The mesh is the star here. It's fine enough to catch the lint that normally escapes through a torn screen—which means less buildup in your dryer's internal vent and exhaust duct. That's not just a cleanliness issue; restricted airflow is what makes dryers run longer, overheat, and eventually trip thermal fuses. Buyers back this up. One reviewer noted, "My clothes are drying faster than they did with the cracked OEM filter I replaced." Another said, "Fits perfectly, snaps in tight, no gaps around the edges."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be plain: this is not OEM. The plastic is noticeably thinner than Samsung's original part. For the average user who pulls the filter out once a week, that's a non-issue. But if you're rough with it—yanking it out one-handed, dropping it on the floor—you might crack the frame. A handful of reviewers mention exactly that, so handle it with the same care you'd give the original.

The lack of an included gasket is the other quibble. On some Samsung models, the rubber seal is molded into the filter itself. On others, it's a separate strip. If your dryer needs that seal and you don't transfer it, you'll get lint bypassing the filter and settling in the blower housing. Check your old part before you toss it.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my Samsung dryer?**
Check your model number against the DC97-16742A compatibility list. It covers most Samsung dryers from roughly 2011 onward, but verify before ordering. If your model uses DC97-14486A or DC97-16742B, this won't match.

**Q: How often should I replace my lint filter?**
Every 6–12 months, or as soon as you notice tears, warping, or a poor seal. A damaged filter makes your dryer work harder and is a fire hazard.

**Q: Is this washable?**
Yes. Rinse it under warm water with a soft brush every few weeks to keep the mesh clear. Don't put it in the dishwasher—the heat can warp the plastic frame.
