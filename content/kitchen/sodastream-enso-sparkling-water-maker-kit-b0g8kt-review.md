**Is the SodaStream Enso actually worth your counter space?** With 235 Amazon ratings holding a 4.7-star average, this is one of the better-reviewed entry-level carbonation machines on the market right now. But a good rating doesn't tell you whether *you* should buy it. Here's the full picture after digging into the specs, the customer feedback, and how it compares to the rest of the SodaStream lineup.

## What's in the Box

The Enso kit gives you the carbonation unit, one 1L BPA-free carbonating bottle, and a 60L CO2 cylinder (that's roughly 60 liters of sparkling water before you need a refill). The bottle uses SodaStream's newer "Quick Connect" system — push, lock, carbonate. It's genuinely tool-free and takes about five seconds to seat properly.

**Key spec worth knowing:** The Enso works with SodaStream's standard 60L and 60L+ cylinders, but it does *not* take the smaller 40L ones. That's fine for most households, but if you're replacing an older model and have a stockpile of 40L canisters, check before you buy.

## Performance and Build Quality

The carbonation itself is consistent. Three presses gives you light fizz; five to six gets you that aggressive, restaurant-style bite. The button is responsive, and the unit locks into place with a satisfying click — no wobble, no guessing whether you've seated the bottle correctly.

What actually impressed me — and what shows up repeatedly in the Amazon feedback — is the **quick-release valve**. Here's a direct quote from a verified buyer: *"I upgraded from a 5-year-old Fizzi and the difference is night and day. No more foam spraying everywhere when I take the bottle off."* That's the Enso's main engineering win. Older SodaStreams vent pressure with a violent hiss that pushes water up the nozzle. The Enso releases it more gradually, which means less mess and less wasted CO2.

The body is a matte plastic that doesn't feel cheap, though it's not metal. It's light enough to move around but stable enough that it doesn't slide when you press down. The footprint is about 5 inches wide and 16 inches tall, so it hides under most cabinets.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate:

1. **Ease of use.** Multiple buyers note their kids can operate it without help — the lever-style button is easier for small hands than the old push-button designs.
2. **Bottle design.** The 1L bottle is slimmer than the old wide-base ones, so it fits in refrigerator door shelves. That's a small thing that matters daily.
3. **Value vs. canned sparkling water.** As one reviewer put it: *"I was spending $40+ a month on LaCroix. This paid for itself in six weeks."* The math checks out if you drink more than a liter a day.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The single-bottle problem:** The kit ships with one 1L bottle. If you drink sparkling water throughout the day, you'll carbonate, drink, wash, repeat. A second bottle runs about $15–20, so budget for that.

**The drip tray is shallow.** If you carbonate right after filling from a tap with very cold water, you'll get some overflow. It's manageable, but it's not a sealed system — keep a towel nearby.

**No flavor system included.** This is a *water* maker. If you want SodaStream's flavor drops, that's a separate purchase. Some competitors bundle a few samples; the Enso doesn't.

**Cylinder exchange logistics.** SodaStream's exchange program is convenient but requires a trip to Target, Best Buy, or a participating grocery store. If you live rural, factor in the drive.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The daily sparkling water drinker** who's tired of hauling 12-packs from the store
- **Families** — the simplicity means kids can make their own drinks without help
- **Small kitchens** — the compact footprint is genuinely space-saving

Skip it if you want a built-in flavor system, need a machine that takes the older 40L cylinders, or you're fine with your current setup and don't drink enough fizzy water to justify the upfront cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Enso use the same CO2 cylinders as other SodaStream models?**
Yes, it uses the standard Quick Connect 60L and 60L+ cylinders. It does not work with the older screw-in 40L models.

**Q: How many liters can I make before the CO2 runs out?**
With the included 60L cylinder, you'll get roughly 60 liters at standard carbonation. If you prefer heavy fizz (6+ presses), expect closer to 45–50 liters.

**Q: Can I carbonate anything other than water?**
Technically yes, but SodaStream explicitly warns against carbonating juice, tea, or anything with pulp — it will foam over and can damage the unit. Stick to water and add flavor *after* carbonating.
