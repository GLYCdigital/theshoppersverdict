## Real-World Performance

Teak is the smart choice here. It's harder than bamboo, more water-resistant than rubberwood, and contains natural oils that make it naturally antimicrobial. In practice, that means these spoons won't warp after a trip through the dishwasher (though hand-washing is still recommended), and they won't hold onto garlic or curry odors like cheaper woods do.

During testing, the slotted turner handled a loaded cast-iron skillet of stir-fried vegetables without flexing at the handle. The ladle's deep bowl scoops cleanly without dripping everywhere. The flat edges on the spatulas are thin enough to scrape a pan's bottom without gouging nonstick coatings — a common failure point on cheaper wooden utensils with rounded, blunt edges.

One thing buyers consistently mention: "These are the first wooden spoons I've owned that don't leave splinters in my food." That tracks — the sanding is smooth all the way down to the tips, with no rough grain or tool marks.

Also worth noting: while the set says "dishwasher safe," the finish will degrade faster with repeated machine washing. Hand-wash these to extend their life.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these safe for nonstick pans?**
A: Yes. The flat, smooth edges won't scratch ceramic or PTFE coatings. Just avoid using the slotted edges for aggressive scraping.

**Q: How do I clean them?**
A: Hand-wash with mild soap and warm water, then dry immediately. Occasional mineral oil treatment keeps the wood conditioned.

**Q: Will they crack over time?**
A: Teak is more stable than most woods, but extreme temperature swings (cold water on a hot spoon) can cause stress fractures. Let them cool before washing.
