A 24-quart dual-zone air fryer sounds like the answer to every weeknight dinner problem. Two baskets. Two temperatures. Rotisserie. It’s the Swiss Army knife of countertop cooking. But with a 3.6-star average across 180 ratings, buyers are split — some call it a game-changer, others are dealing with hot spots and motor noise. Here’s what you need to know before you clear off that counter.

## What You're Actually Getting

This is not a compact unit. The 24Qt Dual Zone Air Fryer Oven measures roughly 16 inches deep and about 14 inches tall, and it weighs in around 28-30 pounds. You're getting two independent 12-quart baskets that slide into a stainless steel housing — each with its own temperature control (typically 150°F to 450°F), timer, and cooking presets. The included rotisserie kit consists of a spit rod, two sets of forks, and a removable handle to lift the rod out. There's also a crumb tray, a rotisserie basket for smaller items like wings or vegetables, and two air fryer racks that nest inside the baskets.

As shown in the product image, the dual-zone setup is the headline feature: you can run one basket at 400°F for crispy fries while the other simmers at 325°F for a roast chicken breast. The LED digital display on each side is straightforward — dials for time and temp, plus a few one-touch presets (fries, chicken, steak, fish, bake, reheat). No WiFi, no app, no smart nonsense. It's a manual appliance dressed up with a digital face.

## Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Uneven

Let's start with what works. The rotisserie function genuinely performs. Multiple reviewers noted that a 4-5 pound chicken comes out with crisp, evenly browned skin and juicy meat in about 60-70 minutes at 350°F. The spit rod is sturdy, the forks grip the bird securely, and the motor has enough torque to handle that weight without stalling. If you buy this for rotisserie chicken, you'll likely be satisfied.

The dual-zone cooking also works as advertised when you're running both baskets at the same temp. "Cooked chicken thighs in one side and roasted potatoes in the other — both came out perfect at the same time," one reviewer wrote. "It genuinely replaced my toaster oven and air fryer." The 12-quart capacity per side is real — you can fit a full 12-inch pizza flat on the rack, or a whole 6-pound roast in one basket.

But here's where the 3.6-star rating starts making sense. The most common complaint across the reviews is uneven doneness — specifically in the upper basket. "The rear of the basket near the heating element browned faster; the front was noticeably undercooked," one verified purchase noted. This isn't a one-off defect; it's a design characteristic of how the heating elements are positioned. If you're cooking something that requires even heat across the entire basket — like a tray of cookies or a batch of chicken wings — you'll need to shake, flip, or rotate mid-cycle. That's annoying but manageable.

The second issue is quality control. Several reviewers reported receiving units with rattling rotisserie motors or basket rails that didn't align properly. One buyer said their unit's motor sounded "like a dying blender" after three uses. This isn't universal — plenty of units work fine — but it's frequent enough that you should inspect yours immediately and be ready to use the warranty.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the hot spot issue, there are two practical problems. First, the footprint. At nearly 30 pounds and with a deep footprint, this is not a "tuck it in a corner" appliance. You'll need dedicated counter space, and it draws enough wattage (typically 1700W) that it should have its own outlet — running it on a power strip with other appliances can trip breakers.

Second, the baskets are large but not dishwasher-safe — the nonstick coating requires hand washing. That's a real chore when you're cleaning up after a full rotisserie chicken. The crumb tray helps, but the interior walls and the heating element area are hard to reach and will accumulate grease over time.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You cook for 4-6 people regularly, want to replace multiple countertop appliances, and are willing to babysit the baskets for even cooking. The rotisserie alone justifies the purchase for chicken lovers — it's genuinely good.

**Skip it if:** You're a precision cook who needs consistent heat, you're short on counter space, or you want a set-and-forget appliance. The uneven heating in the upper basket will frustrate you, and the QC issues make it a gamble.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the two baskets run at completely different temperatures simultaneously?**
Yes, each zone has independent temperature controls and timers. This is the core feature and it works — just be aware of the hot spot issue in the upper basket.

**Q: How long does a rotisserie chicken take?**
Expect 60-70 minutes for a 4-5 pound bird at 350°F. It won't be as fast as an air-fried spatchcocked chicken, but it's hands-off and produces good results.

**Q: Does it actually fit a 12-inch pizza?**
Yes, the basket is large enough to lay a 12-inch pizza flat on the rack without folding. This is one of the most frequently praised features.

## The Verdict

The 24Qt Dual Zone Air Fryer Oven with Rotisserie is a genuinely capable appliance with one significant flaw: inconsistent heat distribution in the upper basket. At its best, it replaces three appliances and produces excellent rotisserie chicken. At its worst, it's a counter hog that requires constant attention. Given the 3.6-star average and the QC complaints, I'd rate it **3.7/5**.

**Buy it if** you want the rotisserie and dual-zone convenience and don't mind shaking baskets mid-cook. **Skip it if** you expect even, set-and-forget results — you'll be disappointed. Check the unit thoroughly on arrival and test the rotisserie motor before your return window closes.
