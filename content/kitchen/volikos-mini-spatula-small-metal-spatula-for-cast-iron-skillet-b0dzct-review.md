Here’s where the Volikos earns its keep. The thin, beveled edge is the star. It slips under pancakes, quesadillas, and even fragile fish fillets without tearing them apart. ## Where It Falls Short

No tool is perfect, and the Volikos has two honest drawbacks. First, the blade flexes noticeably if you try to use it as a pry bar for stuck-on food. It’s designed for flipping and serving, not scraping burnt bits off a pan — use a dedicated scraper for that. Second, the wooden handle means *no dishwasher*. If you’re someone who throws everything in the dishwasher, this will frustrate you. Hand washing is quick, but it’s an extra step. A few reviewers mentioned the handle can develop a slight patina over time if you don’t dry it thoroughly, so keep that in mind.
