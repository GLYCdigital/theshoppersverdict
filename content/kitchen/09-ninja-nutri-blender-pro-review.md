Let me save you some scrolling: the Ninja Nutri-Blender Pro BN401 is the blender you buy when you want smoothie-shop results without paying smoothie-shop prices. I've tested enough personal blenders to know the difference between a toy and a tool. This is the latter.

## What You Actually Get

The BN401 is a countertop unit with a 1200-watt motor — that's the "Pro" part. It comes with two 24-ounce Nutri Ninja cups (one with a regular spout lid, one with a flip-top), plus a 4-cup blending bowl with a handle. That bowl is the sleeper feature here; it's perfect for making salad dressings, sauces, or single-serving soups that would slosh around in a narrow cup.

The base itself runs about 6.2 pounds. It's not portable, and you won't want to stash it in a cabinet — plan for permanent counter space.

## Real-World Performance

I threw the obvious stuff at it: frozen berries, a frozen banana, spinach, Greek yogurt, almond milk. The 1200-watt motor chewed through it in about 25 seconds, producing a texture that was smooth with no unblended berry skin chunks. The Auto-iQ programs (there are three: Smoothie, Puree, and Crush) pulse automatically, so you can walk away and come back to a finished drink. That's genuinely useful, not gimmicky.

The 4-cup bowl deserves a shoutout. I made a batch of chimichurri in it — herbs, garlic, oil, vinegar — and it emulsified beautifully. The wide base means you're not scraping food out of a narrow tube.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. The blade assembly is permanently attached to the cups. That means you can't pop the blades out for a thorough scrub. The cups are dishwasher-safe (top rack), but if you're someone who hand-washes everything, you'll be using a bottle brush and reaching around the blades. It's manageable, not ideal.

The base is also louder than I'd like. It's not deafening, but if you're blending at 6 AM, your household will know about it. And the cup capacity is 24 ounces — that's a 20-ounce smoothie after you account for blade displacement. If you want a massive 32-ounce drink, this isn't your blender.

## Who Should Buy This

Buy it if you're a daily smoothie drinker who wants frozen-fruit crushing power without the $300+ price tag of a high-end personal blender. Buy it if you want the versatility of a 4-cup bowl for sauces and purees. Buy it if you value one-touch programs over manual pulse control.

Skip it if you're blending for a family of four every morning — the 24-ounce cups mean you're making drinks one at a time. Skip it if you refuse to put plastic cups in the dishwasher and hate hand-washing around fixed blades.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can it crush ice without liquid?**
A: Yes, but it works better with some liquid. The Crush program handles a cup of ice alone, but you'll get a more consistent snow-cone texture with a splash of water or juice.

**Q: Are the cups BPA-free?**
A: Yes, the cups and lids are BPA-free. They're also dishwasher-safe on the top rack.

**Q: How does this compare to a larger Ninja blender?**
A: The BN401 is faster for single servings and takes up less space. The larger Ninja models can handle bigger batches and hot liquids, but they're heavier and pricier. For individual smoothies, this is the better buy.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 8.8/10**

**Buy it if** you want professional-grade smoothie performance for under $100 and don't mind the fixed blades. **Skip it if** you need batch blending or want dishwasher-safe removable blades — in that case, look at the Ninja BL660 or a full-size Vitamix.

The BN401 earns its 4.6-star rating. It's powerful, consistent, and the 4-cup bowl makes it more versatile than most personal blenders. The blade design is a minor annoyance, but for the price, it's a tradeoff most buyers will happily make.
