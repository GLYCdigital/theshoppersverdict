**Review Body:**

Let's be honest about what a protection plan actually is: a bet. You're wagering that your $1,500–$2,000 kitchen appliance will break within four years, and ASURION is wagering that it won't. With a 3.1-star average across 155 ratings, this particular bet has a lot of frustrated gamblers on both sides.

**What You're Actually Paying For**

This plan covers mechanical and electrical failures on kitchen appliances (refrigerators, ranges, dishwashers, built-in microwaves) priced between $1,500 and $1,999.99. It kicks in after your manufacturer's warranty expires and runs for four years total. The coverage includes parts and labor, and claims are handled entirely through your Amazon account — no service calls, no waiting on hold. That digital-first approach is genuinely convenient, and it's the reason many buyers chose this plan over retailer alternatives.

**The Real-World Experience**

Here's the pattern from verified reviews: when the plan works, it works well. Multiple buyers report quick approvals and full reimbursements for failed compressors and control boards. One reviewer noted, "Filed a claim on a Tuesday for a refrigerator that stopped cooling. They approved it the same day and sent a check within a week. Painless." That's the experience ASURION wants you to have.

But the 3.1 average tells a different story for a significant minority. The most common complaint? Claim denials citing "wear and tear" — a term the policy defines broadly enough to exclude many legitimate failures. Another reviewer wrote, "My dishwasher's heating element died at 14 months. Denied. They said it was normal wear and tear. A heating element isn't a consumable part." That frustration echoes across roughly a third of the ratings.

**Where It Falls Short**

The coverage exclusions are worth reading twice. Cosmetic damage, pre-existing conditions, and damage from improper installation are excluded — which is fair. But "wear and tear" is the catch-all that's causing the most friction. If your appliance fails due to a defective part that happens to be a wear item (seals, gaskets, heating elements), you may find yourself on the wrong side of a denial.

There's also the transferability issue. This plan is tied to the original purchaser and original product. If you sell your home with the appliances included, the new owners get no coverage. If the appliance is replaced under a manufacturer's recall, you'll need to work through ASURION's claims process to get the remaining coverage moved — and that's not automatic.

**Who Should Buy This**

You're a good candidate if: you're buying a high-end appliance with known reliability issues (particularly refrigerators with ice makers or smart features), you hate dealing with repair appointments, and you'd rather have a hassle-free digital claim process than a phone call with a technician. This plan also makes sense if you're buying a floor model or open-box unit where the manufacturer's warranty is already partially expired.

Skip it if: you're buying a basic, mechanically simple appliance (a standard range or dishwasher with few electronic components), you plan to move within four years, or you're the type who reads the fine print carefully — because the fine print here has teeth.

**FAQ**

*Does this plan cover accidental damage like a dropped pot cracking a stovetop?*
No. This is a mechanical and electrical failure plan, not accidental damage protection. Drops, spills, and power surges are excluded.

*Can I use any repair service, or am I locked into ASURION's network?*
You can use any licensed repair service. You pay upfront, submit the receipt and diagnosis, and ASURION reimburses you. That's a flexibility advantage over plans that force you into their network.

*What happens if my appliance can't be repaired?*
ASURION will replace the product or reimburse you for its current value — which is depreciated, not what you paid. For a $1,800 refrigerator in year three, expect a reimbursement closer to $900–$1,100.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** you're purchasing a complex, electronics-heavy appliance that's expensive to repair, and you value a smooth digital claims process over saving the premium.

**Skip it if:** your appliance is mechanically simple, you're a careful reader who'll be frustrated by "wear and tear" denials, or you don't plan to own the appliance for the full four years.

**Rating: 3.3/5** — A convenient, well-priced plan when claims are approved, but the ambiguous "wear and tear" exclusion creates real risk that you'll pay for coverage that doesn't pay out when you need it most.
