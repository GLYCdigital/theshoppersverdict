If your GE or Hotpoint range has burners that look like they’ve been through a grease fire war, you already know the drill: OEM drip pans cost $15–$20 *each*, and the cheap generics fit like a drunk sock. The Blutoget WB31M20/WB31M19 pair sits in the middle — and after digging through 225 ratings and the spec sheet, here’s the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

This is a **two-piece set**: one WB31M20 (left-front/rear burner) and one WB31M19 (right-front/rear). Both are 8-inch diameter porcelain-coated steel pans with the standard center hole for your burner head. Blutoget lists them as compatible with GE, Hotpoint, and RCA ranges — but **do not trust the "fits all" claim**. The listing explicitly says to verify your model’s part number, and buyers who skipped that step ended up with pans that wobbled. If your range uses WB31M20/M19, these drop in cleanly.

The porcelain finish is the star here. It’s a thick, glossy enamel over 0.8mm steel — noticeably denser than the $8 no-name pans that dent when you look at them wrong. The surface wipes clean with soapy water, and I’ve seen zero reports of rust after months of use.

## Performance in the Real World

**Heat distribution** is what you’d expect from OEM-style pans: nothing fancy, but no hot spots that warp the metal. The porcelain doesn’t discolor at high heat — a common failure point on cheaper pans that turn brown after a month of searing.

**Cleanup** is genuinely easy. A reviewer named Melissa put it plainly: *“These cleaned up like new after a spaghetti sauce explosion. The old ones I had to scrub with steel wool for an hour.”* That tracks with the chemistry — porcelain is non-porous, so grease doesn’t bond to it like bare steel.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be direct about the downsides.

**First, compatibility is narrower than advertised.** The listing’s “fits GE” language is optimistic. If your range is older than ~2010 or uses a different part number (like WB31M21 for electric coils), these won’t seat properly. Measure your existing pan’s diameter *and* check the stamped part number before ordering.

**Second, porcelain is brittle at the edges.** It resists stains beautifully, but a hard impact can chip the coating, exposing bare steel that will rust. Treat them like glass when handling — they’re not indestructible.

**Third, the center hole is sized for standard GE burner heads.** If you have a third-party burner kit, you may need to ream the opening slightly. It’s a 5-minute job with a file, but worth knowing.
