# John Boos RA-Board Series Rectangular Wooden Maple Cutting Board Review

You've seen this board in every professional kitchen on television. The distinctive maple surface, the heft, the way chefs treat it like a prized possession. But is the John Boos RA-Board Series worth the premium price for a home cook? After digging through 229 ratings and real user experiences, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The RA-Board Series is a edge-grain cutting board crafted from North American hard rock maple — the same wood used in butcher blocks for over a century. It arrives in a sealed plastic wrapper, often with a light oil sheen already applied. You're getting just the board itself; no feet, no handles, no accessories. The surface is smooth to the touch with a satisfyingly tight grain pattern.

Depending on the size you choose, expect anywhere from 12 to 20 inches of working surface. The 1.75-inch thickness is the standout feature — this isn't a flimsy bamboo board that warps after three months. It's a serious piece of kitchen equipment.

## Performance That Justifies the Price

Here's where the Boos earns its reputation. The maple surface is remarkably forgiving on knife edges. Multiple reviewers note their blades stay sharper longer compared to glass or plastic boards. One shopper put it simply: "**I've owned this board for six months and my knives still feel like they did the day I bought them. My old plastic board was eating my edges alive.** "

The reversible design is genuinely useful. Use the juice-groove side for carving roasts and poultry — the channel catches liquid runoff effectively, keeping your counter dry. Flip it over for vegetable prep on the flat, un-grooved surface. It's a simple dual-function system that works.

Stability is another strong point. At 15+ pounds, this board doesn't skate around while you work. The weight absorbs impact from heavy chopping without transferring shock to your hands. It feels like a professional tool, because it is one.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across verified reviews, three themes dominate:

1. **Longevity** — Owners report years of heavy use with minimal wear. The edge-grain construction hides knife marks better than surface-cut boards.
2. **Knife preservation** — The soft-yet-dense maple is kind to blades. This is a recurring comment from both home cooks and former culinary professionals.
3. **Aesthetic appeal** — "**This is the prettiest thing in my kitchen,** " one reviewer wrote. "**I leave it out on the counter because it looks like it belongs in a butcher shop.** "

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The lack of rubber feet or grip pads is the most common complaint. On smooth granite or quartz countertops, the board slides unless you anchor it with a damp towel. That's a surprising oversight at this price point — even budget boards include feet.

Maintenance is another consideration. This is not a dishwasher-safe product. It requires hand washing, immediate drying, and regular treatment with food-grade mineral oil (Boos sells their own Mystery Oil, but any food-safe mineral oil works). Skip the conditioning for a few months and you'll see the wood dry out and potentially crack. One reviewer noted: "**I didn't oil it for four months and a small crack appeared in the end grain. My fault entirely, but worth knowing before you buy.** "

The weight is also a factor for some. At roughly 15 pounds, it's awkward to lift and store. If you have limited cabinet space or wrist issues, this might be more board than you need.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're serious about cooking, own quality knives, and want a single cutting surface that will outlast your cabinets. It's ideal for home cooks who prep daily, weekend warriors who host large gatherings, and anyone who appreciates professional-grade kitchen tools.

**Skip it if:** You prefer dishwasher-safe cutting boards, have limited counter space, or aren't willing to commit to regular oiling. If you're a casual cook who chops occasionally, a $30 bamboo board serves the same purpose.

## FAQ

**How often do I need to oil this board?**
Monthly for the first few months, then every 4-6 weeks with regular use. Apply food-grade mineral oil generously, let it soak in overnight, and wipe off excess.

**Can I use this for raw meat and vegetables?**
Yes, the reversible design handles both. Use the juice-groove side for meats and the flat side for produce to minimize cross-contamination. Clean with hot soapy water after raw meat contact.

**Will knife marks show?**
Yes, they will — that's normal and expected. The edge-grain construction makes marks less visible than surface-cut boards, and they sand out easily if you want to restore the surface.

## The Verdict

The John Boos RA-Board is a premium product with a premium price tag. It's not for everyone — the maintenance requirements and lack of grip are genuine drawbacks. But for cooks who value knife performance and want a cutting surface that will last decades, it's a worthy investment. The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects real satisfaction from owners who understand what they're buying.

**Buy it if** you're a dedicated home cook ready to maintain a serious tool. **Skip it if** you want low-maintenance convenience.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Points off for the missing grip pads and the maintenance commitment, but this is still the gold standard for wood cutting boards.
