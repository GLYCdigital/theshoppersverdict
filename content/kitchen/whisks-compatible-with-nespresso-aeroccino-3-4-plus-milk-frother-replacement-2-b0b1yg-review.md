Let's be honest: the Nespresso Aeroccino is a great little machine, but the proprietary replacement parts are priced like they're machined from gold. A single OEM whisk runs you $15–20, which is absurd for a coiled piece of wire. This two-pack from a third-party seller costs about the same as one genuine part — and after testing them across three weeks of daily lattes, they hold up surprisingly well.

**What's in the box**

You get two complete whisk assemblies: the spring-style whisk for hot milk frothing and the flat disc whisk for cold foam. Both are made from stainless steel with a magnetic base that snaps onto the Aeroccino's drive shaft. They're compatible with the Aeroccino 3, 4, and Plus models — which covers most units sold in the last decade. The packaging is minimal, just a clear plastic sleeve, but that's fine. These aren't heirloom pieces.

**Real-world performance**

I ran these through the full gauntlet: hot frothed milk for cappuccinos, cold foam for iced lattes, and even a few oat milk tests (which are notoriously punishing on whisks). The hot whisk produces the same dense, microfoam texture as the original — my latte art didn't suffer one bit. The cold disc whipped up the same thick, spoonable foam on the cold setting. No metallic taste, no scratching on the carafe's nonstick coating.

The build quality is the real story here. The spring coils are tightly wound with no sharp edges, and the magnetic base uses a strong magnet that engages firmly with the drive shaft. After 21 uses and six dishwasher cycles, there's zero rust, no discoloration, and the spring hasn't lost its tension. That's the failure point on cheap knockoffs — they go limp after a few weeks. These didn't.

**What buyers are saying**

**Where it falls short**

The spring whisk sits marginally looser on the drive shaft than the OEM part. During operation, you'll notice a slight wobble at max speed — nothing that affects performance, but it's audible if your machine is on the counter next to your workspace. Also, this set doesn't include the silicone gasket that sits under the whisk base. If yours is cracked or worn, you'll need to source that separately. Some buyers also reported that the whisk can pop off if you overfill the carafe past the MAX line — but that's user error more than a product flaw.

**Q: Can I put them in the dishwasher?**

**Q: Do they work with oat milk and other plant milks?**
A: Yes, though plant milks froth differently than dairy regardless of the whisk. These performed comparably to the OEM with oat and almond milk in my testing.
