## What You're Actually Getting

The package contains 100 square, parchment-style liners. They're designed specifically for dual-basket Ninja models, but the dimensions are forgiving enough that they'll likely fit similar 6-8 quart baskets. Each liner has a pre-cut center seam, which is meant to be folded down to create a flat surface over the basket floor.

The material is food-grade parchment with a light silicone coating. It's not the flimsy stuff you see on a baking sheet—these have a bit of heft. They arrive flat, not in a roll, which makes them easier to store but slightly awkward to separate at first.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this product wins or loses. In my testing, these liners handle the standard stuff beautifully: frozen french fries, chicken tenders, tater tots, and reheating leftovers. The silicone coating does appear to resist sticking, and cleanup becomes a thirty-second matter of lifting the liner out and tossing it.

The center seam is the clever part. Instead of the liner bunching up and blocking airflow, you fold the two halves down against the basket walls. This keeps the food surface flat and allows hot air to circulate around the edges. It's a small design detail that makes a real difference in cooking consistency.

However, there's a ceiling to what these can handle. High-fat foods—bacon, fatty sausages, or heavily marinated chicken—will cause grease to pool on the liner's surface. The paper doesn't absorb much, so you end up with food sitting in its own oil, which can make the underside soggy. For crispy bacon, you're better off skipping the liner.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 229 ratings, the recurring themes are convenience and fit. One verified buyer noted, "These fit my Ninja Dual perfectly. I don't have to scrub the basket anymore, and the food still gets crispy." Another mentioned, "I was skeptical about the seam, but it works—food doesn't fall through, and cleanup takes seconds."

The durability is also a common point of praise. A number of reviewers mention using them multiple times before discarding, which stretches the value even further. If you're using them for lower-grease items, you can often flip and reuse the same liner for two or three batches.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. First, the price per liner is reasonable, but it's a recurring cost. You're trading scrubbing time for an ongoing supply purchase.

Second, the fit isn't universal. While they're marketed for the Ninja Dual, the square shape can leave gaps in rounder baskets, which means smaller food items can slip underneath the liner and still make contact with the basket. That defeats the purpose and creates a mess anyway.

Third, as mentioned, high-grease cooking is a no-go. The liners don't wick oil away—they just hold it. If your air fryer routine is mostly bacon and burgers, these will disappoint.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Are these safe to use at high temperatures?**
A: Yes. They're rated up to 428°F (220°C), which covers the maximum temperature on the Ninja Dual. Just don't crank the heat beyond that and leave them empty—the parchment can scorch.

**Q: Can I reuse these liners?**
A: Technically, yes, for low-grease items. Many buyers report flipping and reusing them 2-3 times. But once they're darkened, absorb grease, or tear, toss them.

**Q: Will these work in a non-Ninja air fryer?**
A: Likely, if your basket is roughly square and 6-8 quarts. For round baskets, measure the diameter first—the fit will be less precise.
