## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 6-cup moka pot (assembled), a stainless steel coffee scoop, and a bonus 100g bag of their Colombian whole-bean coffee. The pot itself is brushed 18/8 stainless steel — no aluminum anywhere in the brew path. That's the headline feature. The base is compatible with all cooktops including induction, which aluminum models can't claim. The silicone handle sleeve stays cool enough to grip mid-brew, something I tested by leaving it on a gas flame for a full cycle.

## How It Performs

Brewing takes about 4–5 minutes on medium-low heat. The key difference from cheaper moka pots: the water chamber has a marked fill line, and the gasket seals snugly without needing to be cranked down with a wrench. The result is a steady, even extraction with a proper crema layer — not the watery, sputtering output you get from misaligned aluminum threads.

One thing to know: "6 cups" in moka pot language means 6 tiny espresso cups, roughly 2 ounces each. You're getting about 12 ounces of concentrated coffee total, enough for two proper Americanos or four small cups. Buyers consistently note this. As one verified purchaser put it: *"I knew moka pot sizes were small, but this still surprised me — it's perfect for two people, not a family."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The stainless steel construction is the recurring theme in positive reviews. People who switched from aluminum pots report a cleaner taste with no bitterness. The handle staying cool is another frequently mentioned win. ## Where It Falls Short

The finish is the first compromise. The brushed exterior develops a patina of water spots and fingerprints that needs regular wiping. Dishwasher use is explicitly discouraged — the heat and detergent will strip the finish and degrade the silicone gasket. That's fine for coffee people, but annoying if you're used to tossing everything in the dishwasher.

The gasket itself is a proprietary size. Replacement seals are available on Amazon, but they're not as ubiquitous as Bialetti's universal parts. If you lose the gasket, you're waiting on shipping rather than grabbing one at a local kitchen store.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use it on an induction cooktop?**
A: Yes. The stainless steel base is fully magnetic and induction-compatible. That's a major advantage over aluminum moka pots.

**Q: Does it make real espresso?**
A: No. It makes strong, concentrated coffee at around 5–6 bars of pressure. True espresso requires 9 bars. But for milk drinks and Americanos, it's indistinguishable to most palates.

**Q: What grind size should I use?**
A: Fine — slightly coarser than espresso machine grind, slightly finer than table salt. Too fine and it'll clog; too coarse and you'll get weak, bitter extraction.
