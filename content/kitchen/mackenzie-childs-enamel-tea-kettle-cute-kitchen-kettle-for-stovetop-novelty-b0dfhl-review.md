**Performance and real-world use**

The kettle heats water efficiently on gas, electric coil, and ceramic stovetops. Note that it is *not* induction-compatible — check your cooktop before ordering. The whistle is genuinely loud and reliable, which matters more than you'd think; it's the difference between catching your water at a rolling boil and walking into a kitchen full of steam.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. Enamel is glass fused to steel — it's beautiful, but it's also brittle. Drop this kettle or scrape it with a metal whisk and you'll chip it. A couple of reviewers reported chips arriving in transit, which is more about packaging than product quality, but it's worth inspecting yours immediately upon arrival.

The capacity is modest at 2 quarts. If you're brewing for a crowd or making multiple pots of tea back-to-back, you'll be refilling frequently. And the fixed whistle spout means you can't install a clip-on infuser — you'll need to brew loose leaf separately.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this on an induction stovetop?**  
A: No. The enamel coating over steel doesn't respond to induction magnetic fields. Stick to gas, electric coil, or ceramic glass cooktops.

**Q: How do I clean it without damaging the finish?**  
A: Hand wash with warm, soapy water and a soft sponge. Avoid abrasive scrubbers, steel wool, and dishwasher cycles — the harsh detergent and high heat will dull the enamel and could cause chipping.

**Q: Does the whistle actually work?**  
A: Yes, and it's loud. Several reviewers note they can hear it from other rooms, which is exactly what you want when water's boiling unattended.
