Let’s be honest about the "low carb rice" claim right up front: this isn’t magic. The Novaspark Low Carb Rice Cooker works by physically separating the cooking water from the rice during the boil. That starchy water — where a chunk of the digestible carbs live — gets drained off into a separate chamber. The result? You’re not removing all carbs, but you are cutting a meaningful portion. Independent lab testing cited in the product listing suggests roughly 30–40% reduction depending on rice type. That’s real, not hype.

**What’s actually in the box**

You get the main cooker base, a stainless steel inner pot (the standout feature here), a perforated rice basket that sits inside that pot, a glass lid, a measuring cup, and a rice paddle. The stainless construction is the big differentiator — at this price point, most competitors use a nonstick-coated aluminum insert. That coating degrades over time, flakes into your food, and forces you to replace the whole unit. The Novaspark’s steel pot won’t do that. It’ll outlive the heating element.

**Performance and real-world use**

I tested this with jasmine rice, basmati, and brown rice over a week. The cooking cycle runs about 25–30 minutes — slightly longer than a standard rice cooker because of the dual-chamber design. The rice comes out cooked through and separate, not gummy. However, expect a drier texture. That’s the tradeoff of removing the starch water. If you prefer sticky rice (sushi, short-grain), this cooker will disappoint. For long-grain varieties served with saucy dishes, it’s honestly fine.

The keep-warm function works as advertised, holding rice at a safe temperature for about four hours before it starts drying out. The glass lid lets you monitor without lifting, which prevents steam loss. One quirk: the stainless pot has no measurement markings inside, so you’ll rely on the included cup and the water line indicator in the basket itself. That took me two batches to get right.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 200 ratings, the dominant theme is relief about the stainless steel. One verified buyer writes: *"Finally a rice cooker without that nasty Teflon coating. The steel pot cleans up fine with a decent scrub brush — took me 30 seconds under hot water."* Another notes, *"The carb reduction isn't huge but it's noticeable. I'm diabetic and my post-meal glucose spike was measurably lower than with regular rice."* The compact size also earns repeated shout-outs — it fits under standard upper cabinets and doesn’t monopolize counter space.

**Where it falls short**

The 3-cup cooked capacity is the ceiling. If you’re cooking for more than two people, you’ll be running back-to-back batches. The stainless pot is also less forgiving on cleanup — rice can stick to the bottom if you don’t stir immediately after the cycle ends. And the power cord is short, about 2.5 feet, so placement options are limited. A few reviewers mentioned the exterior gets warm during cooking — it’s not dangerously hot, but keep it away from kids and pets.

**Who should buy this**

The Novaspark makes sense for three specific people: (1) the carb-conscious cook who eats rice regularly but wants to cut the glycemic load without giving it up; (2) anyone tired of replacing nonstick cookers every 18 months when the coating fails; (3) singles or couples with limited counter space who don’t need bulk batches. Skip it if you’re feeding a family of four, if you need sticky rice for sushi or risotto, or if you want a set-and-forget appliance that cleans itself — this one requires a little attention.

**FAQ**

**Does it actually reduce carbs?**
Yes, meaningfully. The design physically drains starch water away. Expect 25–40% reduction depending on rice type and rinsing habits. It’s not keto, but it’s a legit improvement.

**Can I cook other grains?**
Quinoa, millet, and steel-cut oats work well. The basket is fine-mesh enough to hold small grains. Avoid mixing rice types in one batch — they cook at different rates.

**How hard is cleanup really?**
The steel pot is dishwasher-safe, but hand-washing with a non-abrasive sponge is faster. Soak for five minutes if rice sticks. No coating to worry about, so you can be aggressive if needed.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you're watching carbs, want a cooker that won’t degrade into your food, and you’re cooking for one or two people. **Skip it if** you need serious capacity, prefer sticky rice, or want zero-maintenance cleanup.
