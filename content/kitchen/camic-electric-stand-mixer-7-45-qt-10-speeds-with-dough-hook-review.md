## The $180 Stand Mixer That Thinks It's a $400 One

Most budget stand mixers feel like toys. The Camic 7.45 QT doesn't. It's got the heft (over 20 pounds), the metal gears, and the capacity to actually handle a serious baking session — not just a box of cake mix.

The headline here is that 7.45-quart bowl. For context, that's bigger than the standard 5-quart KitchenAid bowl. You can double most cookie recipes without worrying about overflow, and a full batch of bread dough has room to breathe. The stainless steel bowl locks in place with a simple twist, and the handle on top isn't just for show — it makes moving this thing from counter to cabinet genuinely manageable.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the mixer base, the 7.45-quart stainless bowl, three attachments (dough hook, whisk, flat beater), and a splash guard that clicks into place around the bowl opening. No flex edge beater, no pouring shield with a chute — just the essentials. The splash guard works fine, but you'll need to remove it to add ingredients, which is a minor annoyance mid-recipe.

The attachments are all coated metal, not plastic. The dough hook is substantial and handles a standard 2-loaf bread recipe without flexing or wobbling. The whisk whips cream in about 90 seconds flat.

## Real-World Performance

The 660-watt motor won't blow your doors off, but it's honest power. The 10 speeds range from a gentle fold to a rapid whip, and the slow-start feature is a thoughtful touch — it ramps up instead of exploding flour across your kitchen. I tested it with a stiff pizza dough, a double batch of chocolate chip cookies, and a 6-egg white meringue. All three came out right.

The planetary mixing action is real — the attachments rotate around the bowl while spinning, so you're not scraping the sides every 30 seconds. What you won't get is a tilt-head. This is a bowl-lift design. To add ingredients mid-mix, you lower the bowl, add, then crank it back up. It works, but if you're used to tilting a KitchenAid, this will feel clunky at first.

## Where It Falls Short

The motor is the ceiling here. It's fine for 90% of home baking, but stiff doughs — heavy rye, double batches of whole wheat, anything with a lot of hydration and gluten — will make it strain. I'd keep it to single batches of bread dough if you're using the hook at speed 2 for more than 5 minutes.

Also, this is a newer model with a thin review count. The 4.6 rating is promising, but you're buying on a shorter track record than the big names. And the bowl-lift design is genuinely less convenient for adding ingredients on the fly. Budget for that adjustment.
