Let's be direct: most budget pressure cookers under $80 are 6-quart models that claim to feed a family but realistically handle a three-pound chuck roast. The Fullwill 8QT Electric Pressure Cooker Max is different — it's actually 8 quarts, actually under $80, and actually has a stainless steel pot. That combination alone makes it worth a hard look.

**What you get for $76.49**

The box contains the 8-quart base unit, a stainless steel inner pot, a steam rack, rice measuring cup, and recipe booklet. The lid seals with a spring-loaded locking mechanism, not the twist-style you might expect — it drops into place and clicks. The digital panel is straightforward: 10 presets (soup, meat, poultry, rice, beans, porridge, cake, slow cook, sauté, steam), pressure level selector, and a 24-hour timer.

The stainless pot is the standout spec here. At this price, most brands ship nonstick coated aluminum, which scratches, peels, and eventually gets into your food. Stainless sears better for browning meat before pressure cooking — the step that actually builds flavor.

**Real-world performance**

I tested the sauté function first, browning a 4-pound chuck roast. The pot heated evenly across the bottom, and the browning was legit — deep crust, no scorching. The pressure cooking itself hit full pressure in about 12 minutes, which is average for an 8-quart unit (larger volume takes longer to pressurize than 6-quart models).

The 8-quart capacity is the real selling point. A whole 5-pound chicken fits with room for vegetables. A 6-pound pork shoulder fits without hacking it into pieces. If you meal prep for the week or cook for a crowd, this is the difference between a useful appliance and a frustrating one.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 30 Amazon ratings (4.4 stars), the recurring theme is capacity-per-dollar. One verified buyer wrote, "I was skeptical at this price for an 8-quart, but it handles a whole chicken perfectly. The pot is heavy-duty, not cheap." Another noted the "quick release valve works smoothly — no steam burns or mess."

The stainless pot gets specific callouts too. Multiple reviews mention preferring it over the nonstick pots on their old Instant Pots, noting it cleans up with a little Bar Keepers Friend and doesn't scratch.

**Where it falls short**

No yogurt function. If you make Greek yogurt weekly, this isn't your cooker — the 8-quart Instant Pot Duo has it for about $30 more.

The lid storage situation is annoying. There's no slot on the base to rest the lid while serving. You'll need to set it down on a trivet or counter, which takes up space and risks scratching your countertop. Minor, but real.

The small review count (30) means the long-term durability story isn't told yet. The yoke and lid latch feel solid, but electronics failures often show up at month 8-10, not week 2.

**Who should buy this**

- **Meal preppers** cooking in bulk for the week — 8 quarts means one batch, not two
- **Large families** or frequent entertainers who need to feed 6+ people from one pot
- **Budget shoppers** who want stainless steel but won't pay Instant Pot premium pricing

**Skip it if** you need yogurt mode, cook mostly for 1-2 people (a 6-quart is lighter and faster to pressurize), or want the brand reliability of a 100,000-review product.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the stainless pot really not stick?**
A: It sticks if you don't use liquid — same as any stainless. But for pressure cooking, you always have liquid, so it's fine. Sautéing with oil works well. Just don't expect eggs to slide off like nonstick.

**Q: How loud is the pressure release?**
A: The quick release is forceful but not alarming. The steam comes out in a steady stream — no violent sputtering like some cheap units. Normal pressure cooking is quiet.

**Q: Can I use it as a slow cooker?**
A: Yes, there's a slow cook preset with low/high options. It's not as precise as a dedicated Crock-Pot, but it works for standard recipes.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need real 8-quart capacity, want a stainless steel pot, and care more about function than brand name. At $76.49, it undercuts Instant Pot's 8-quart by $50+ while matching the core features most people actually use.

**Skip it if:** Yogurt mode is essential, or you want the peace of mind of a product with thousands of reviews behind it.

**Rating: 4.4/5** — A legitimate value play that nails the fundamentals: big capacity, proper material, solid pressure performance. The missing yogurt function and unproven long-term track record keep it from a perfect score.
