If you've ever spent an afternoon hand-scooping spice blends into 50 tiny jars for a market or a wedding favor, you already know why a powder filling machine exists. The Fanlocn Powder Filling Machine promises to end that tedium — and for the most part, it delivers. But like any specialized kitchen gadget, it has quirks you need to know about before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the filling machine itself, a stainless steel hopper, an auger screw, a control box, and a foot pedal for hands-free operation. No funnel is included — that's the first thing you'll need to order separately if you're filling narrow-mouth containers. The unit measures about 13 x 8 x 20 inches, so it fits on a countertop without dominating your kitchen. The hopper holds roughly 3 liters, which is enough for a solid batch of supplement powder or spice blend before you need to refill.

## Real-World Performance

The Fanlocn works on a simple auger-driven system. You set the fill time (in 0.01-second increments) and the auger speed, then tap the foot pedal or hit the start button. In my testing with coarse sea salt and dried herbs, the accuracy was genuinely impressive — consistent fills within about ±2-3% once you dial in the settings. That's the difference between this and a $30 scoop-and-weigh setup.

The stainless steel hopper and auger are the standout build features. They're easy to disassemble and wipe down, which matters more than you'd think when you're switching between cayenne and cinnamon. The control box, however, is plastic and feels noticeably cheaper than the metal components. It works fine, but don't expect industrial-grade durability.

**The honest downside:** fine powders are this machine's weakness. Flour, confectioner's sugar, and other ultra-fine particles tend to bridge at the bottom of the hopper and stop feeding. You'll need to tap the hopper or stir the powder manually every few fills. Coarse granules and larger particles — think salt, sugar, coffee beans, capsule supplements — work flawlessly.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a small-batch food producer, spice blend seller, or serious home canner who fills more than 20 identical containers at a time. This machine pays for itself in saved time within the first few sessions. It's also ideal for supplement makers who need consistent capsule or powder doses.

**Skip it if** you fill containers only occasionally — a simple funnel and digital scale will do the job for less money. And if you work exclusively with fine powders, look for a machine with a vibration feature or plan to babysit the hopper.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it handle both powders and larger particles?**
Yes, but not equally well. Coarse materials (salt, sugar, seeds, capsules) work great. Fine powders require manual tapping to prevent clogging.

**Q: How accurate are the fills?**
Once calibrated, expect ±2-3% variation. The fill time and auger speed are adjustable in small increments, so you can fine-tune for your specific material.

**Q: Is it easy to clean between different products?**
The hopper, auger, and dispensing nozzle are all stainless steel and come apart without tools. A quick rinse and wipe-down is all you need between batches.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Fanlocn Powder Filling Machine earns its place in a serious kitchen.
