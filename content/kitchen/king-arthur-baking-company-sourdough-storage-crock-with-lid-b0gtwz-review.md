## What You Actually Get

The box contains one glazed stoneware crock and its lid. The 2-quart capacity is the headline here—it's sized to hold an entire King Arthur sourdough starter recipe (the one that starts with 113 grams each of flour and water) with plenty of headroom for the starter to double or triple. As shown in the product image, the crock has a classic, slightly rounded profile with a warm earthy glaze. It's roughly 7 inches tall with the lid on, and the wide mouth makes scooping and stirring genuinely easy.

The stoneware is thick—noticeably heavier than a typical ceramic bowl. That's a feature, not a flaw. The mass helps buffer temperature swings, which matters when your starter is sitting on a counter that gets chilly overnight.

## Real-World Performance

The practical test is simple: does it keep a starter alive and happy between feedings? Based on buyer reports, yes. One reviewer noted, *"My starter has never been more consistent. The thickness of the crock keeps it at a steady temp even in my drafty kitchen."* Another mentioned the size as the deciding factor: *"I'd been using a mason jar and constantly scraping overflow off the counter. This thing holds a full batch with room to spare."*

The glazed interior is smooth and non-porous, which means cleanup is quick—a warm water rinse usually does it. No soap drama, no lingering sour smells in the clay. That's a real advantage over unglazed ceramic crocks, which absorb odors and can harbor bacteria in tiny pores.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The lid doesn't seal. It's a loose cover that lets air circulate—which is actually correct for sourdough, since a sealed container can trap pressure and cause the lid to pop off. But it also means your starter's surface can dry out if you only feed it once a week. A few buyers solved this by placing a damp paper towel under the lid or using a cloth cover instead.

There are no measurement markings inside. For a product from a baking company, that feels like a missed opportunity. You'll need a scale anyway (which you should be using for sourdough regardless), so it's not a dealbreaker—just an inconvenience.

The price point is higher than a basic glass jar, and some shoppers may balk at paying for what is essentially a specialized bowl. But given the build quality, this is a buy-it-for-years item, not a disposable gadget.

## FAQ

**Does the lid need to be airtight?**
No. Sourdough starter needs airflow—a sealed container can cause pressure buildup and even crack the crock. The loose lid is intentional.

**Can I wash this in the dishwasher?**
Technically yes, but hand-washing is gentler on the glaze. If you do use the dishwasher, place it on the top rack.

**Will it fit in my fridge?**
It's about 7 inches tall, so it fits standard refrigerator shelves. Measure your shelf height first if you plan to refrigerate it.
