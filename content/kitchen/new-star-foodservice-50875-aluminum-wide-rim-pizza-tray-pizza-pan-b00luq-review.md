# New Star Foodservice 50875 Aluminum Wide Rim Pizza Tray Review: The Commercial Standard for Home Ovens

If you've ever pulled a pizza from a cheap dark pan and found the bottom pale while the edges char, you understand the frustration. The New Star Foodservice 50875 takes the opposite approach — this is the same heavy-gauge aluminum tray pizzerias use, and it solves the two biggest home pizza problems: uneven heat and soggy crusts.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 16-inch round pan made from 0.8mm thick commercial-grade aluminum. The standout feature is the corrugated (fluted) bottom — those ridges aren't decorative. They lift the dough slightly off the pan surface, allowing hot air to circulate underneath. That's what creates a crisp, evenly-browned crust instead of a steamed, pale one.

The wide rolled rim serves double duty: it's sturdy enough to grip with oven mitts, and it lets the pan double as a serving tray. You can take it straight from oven to table without transferring the pizza. The entire pan weighs about 1.5 pounds — substantial but not unwieldy.

## Performance and Build Quality

I've tested this pan side-by-side with a standard non-stick round pan, and the difference is night and day. The aluminum conducts heat aggressively — preheat it in the oven for 15 minutes before sliding your pizza on, and you'll get a blistered, pizzeria-style bottom in 6-8 minutes at 500°F. The corrugation also helps with frozen pizzas, which typically suffer from soggy centers; the airflow prevents that moisture from pooling.

The build quality is legitimately commercial. The aluminum won't warp under high heat, unlike thinner pans that buckle after a few uses. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I've had this pan for three years and use it weekly — it still looks brand new." Another mentioned, "The wide rim makes it easy to grab with oven mitts, and the pizza slides right off onto a cutting board."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. **The pan is hand-wash only.** Aluminum reacts with dishwasher detergent, turning dark and pitted. If you're someone who throws everything in the dishwasher, this will look rough within months.

Second, the 16-inch size is a problem for many home ovens. Standard ovens have about 18 inches of interior width, but the rack slides can reduce usable space. I had to angle the pan to fit mine — workable but annoying. Measure your oven before buying.

Finally, there's no non-stick coating. High-hydration doughs (above 65% water) will stick without oil or cornmeal. Use a light brush of olive oil on the pan, or line it with parchment for sticky doughs.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home bakers who make pizza weekly** — this pan will outlast three cheap non-stick pans
- **People who want pizzeria-style crusts** — the corrugation genuinely improves crispness
- **Anyone hosting pizza nights** — the wide rim makes serving and cleanup easy

**Skip it if** you exclusively use frozen pizzas (the pan works, but you won't notice the difference), or if your oven is smaller than standard width.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this on a grill or in a wood-fired oven?**
A: Yes, up to about 550°F. The aluminum handles high heat well, but avoid direct flame contact — the pan is meant for radiant heat, not open fire.

**Q: Will this scratch my pizza stone or baking steel?**
A: The corrugated bottom has slightly sharp edges. Place it on a sheet pan if you're stacking it in storage, and avoid dragging it across your stone.

**Q: Does it make frozen pizza taste better?**
A: It helps — the airflow prevents the soggy center that plagues frozen pizzas on flat pans. Use the preheat method and watch the crust brown faster than the instructions suggest.
