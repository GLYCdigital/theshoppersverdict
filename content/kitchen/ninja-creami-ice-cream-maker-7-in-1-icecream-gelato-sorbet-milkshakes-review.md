The Ninja CREAMi isn't an ice cream maker. It's a frozen dessert processor. That distinction matters because the 7-in-1 NC299AMZ doesn't churn and freeze simultaneously like traditional machines. Instead, you freeze a liquid base solid for 24 hours, then the machine's blade system shaves and kneads that frozen block into whatever texture you select. It's a fundamentally different approach — and for most home users, it works better.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the motor base (which is compact — about 7 inches wide and 15 inches tall), one 16-ounce pint container with a storage lid, and the paddle assembly with two blades. That's it. No extra bowls, no measuring cups, no recipe book beyond the basics. The pint is dishwasher-safe on the top rack, which is nice, but here's the catch: if you want to keep multiple flavors ready, you're buying additional pints at roughly $13 each.

## How It Performs

The key to the CREAMi is the pre-freeze step. You blend your base (milk, cream, sugar, flavorings), pour it into the pint, and freeze for a full 24 hours. The result is an ice cube the size of a soup can. Then you lock it into the machine, press a button, and the blade assembly slowly descends, shaving the frozen block into the texture you selected.

In practice, the ice cream setting produces a smooth, dense scoop that's noticeably creamier than what you'd get from a $40 churner. The gelato setting yields that characteristically dense, slow-melting Italian texture. Sorbet and smoothie bowl modes handle fruit-heavy bases without leaving icy chunks. The milkshake function is genuinely impressive — it turns a frozen pint of ice cream into a thick, drinkable shake in under a minute.

The mix-in function is where it shines. You can fold chocolate chips, cookie dough, or fruit into an already-frozen pint, and the machine evenly distributes them rather than pulverizing them. One Amazon reviewer noted, "The mix-in feature actually works — my chocolate chunks stayed intact, not ground into dust like my old machine did."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 16,112 ratings, the dominant theme is control. You decide exactly what goes into your ice cream — sugar level, fat content, dairy or non-dairy, flavor intensity. As one reviewer put it, "I've made keto ice cream, oat milk sorbet, and protein shakes that taste like actual dessert. My kids don't know they're eating healthy." The single-serving format is also a hit. You're not locked into a quart of one flavor. You can make one pint of pistachio and one pint of mango in the same day (if you have multiple pints).

## Where It Falls Short

The 24-hour freeze requirement is the biggest hurdle. You cannot decide at 7 PM you want ice cream at 8 PM. This is a planning appliance. If you forget to freeze a base, you're out of luck.

The 16-ounce capacity is also limiting. A full batch produces about two generous scoops per person. For a family of four, you're making two pints or accepting smaller portions. The single pint included in the box means you're buying more containers immediately if you want variety.

Noise is another consideration. The blade motor is loud — comparable to a blender on medium. It operates in short bursts (about 2-3 minutes per cycle), so it's tolerable, but you won't run it while the baby sleeps. The first cycle also requires you to press the container down firmly, and some users report the base can shift on countertops during operation.

## FAQ

**Do I need to use the Ninja recipe book, or can I use my own bases?**
Use your own. The machine just shaves frozen liquid — any base that freezes solid works. Just keep sugar or sweetener in the mix; it lowers the freezing point and prevents a rock-hard block.

**Can I make sorbet from fresh fruit?**
Yes, but puree the fruit with liquid (juice, water, or simple syrup) first. Whole fruit chunks won't process evenly and can leave icy bits.

**Is the pint really dishwasher safe?**
The pint, lid, and paddle are top-rack dishwasher safe. The motor base must be wiped clean — never submerge it.
