Most pressure canners on the market are stuck in the 1990s — heavy steel, finicky dials, and zero compatibility with modern induction ranges. The Presto 01784 23-Quart Induction Compatible Pressure Canner fixes the biggest pain point (induction compatibility) while keeping the classic design that's made Presto a household name for over a century. But is it the right canner for your kitchen? Here's what 240 buyers at a 4.8-star average are actually saying.

## What's in the Box

You get the 23-quart aluminum canner body, a locking lid with a gasket, the weighted gauge regulator (your primary pressure control), a dial gauge for reference, a jar rack, and a comprehensive instruction manual. The base is what sets this apart — it's a thick, magnetic stainless-clad disc that works on induction, gas, electric, and glass-top ranges. As shown in the product image, the base is noticeably thicker than the aluminum walls, which is how Presto solved the induction problem without turning this into a $300 stainless unit.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The 23-quart capacity is the headline here. You'll fit 19 pint jars or 7 quart jars in a single batch — that's roughly double what a standard 16-quart canner handles. For anyone putting up a summer garden's worth of tomatoes or green beans, this cuts canning day from 8 hours to 4.

The weighted gauge regulator is the star of the show. Unlike dial-only canners where you're constantly adjusting heat to chase a needle, this rocker maintains a steady 10 PSI with a gentle jiggle. Set your burner, wait for the rocker to start rocking, and you're basically hands-off. One buyer noted: "The weighted gauge is a huge relief after years of babysitting a dial. I set it and walk away."

The induction base does its job well. On a 1,800W induction burner, the canner reaches pressure in about 15–20 minutes for a full load — comparable to gas, and faster than you'd expect from aluminum construction.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring themes in reviews are capacity and reliability. "I canned 14 quarts of chicken stock in one run and it held pressure perfectly the entire time," reported one verified buyer. Another wrote: "This is my third Presto canner in 20 years. The induction base is the upgrade I've been waiting for."

Build quality gets solid marks too. The aluminum body heats evenly thanks to the thick base, and the lid gasket seats firmly with no steam leaks — a common complaint on cheaper canners.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. Aluminum dents. If you drop this or bang it against a cabinet, you'll see a dent, and deep dents in the body could compromise sealing. Stainless steel would be more durable but would triple the price.

The dial gauge is secondary — you'll rely on the weighted regulator for actual pressure control, and that dial can drift out of calibration over time. Plan on getting it checked annually at a county extension office if you want precise readings.

Finally, this is a big, heavy unit. At 17 pounds empty, a full load of quart jars pushes past 40 pounds. Draining it after a batch requires real strength or a second person. The handles are sturdy, but they're not ergonomic miracles.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have an induction range and have been frustrated that most canners won't work on it. You process large batches — 10+ jars at a time — and want to cut canning time in half. You're comfortable with a manual pressure regulator and don't need electronic automation.

**Skip it if:** You only can a few jars occasionally — a 16-quart model is lighter and easier to store. You're looking for a set-and-forget electric unit (this requires stovetop attention). You're rough on equipment and need stainless durability.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work on glass-top electric stoves?**
A: Yes. The flat, magnetic base is designed for all cooktops, including glass and ceramic. Just make sure you don't slide it — lift it to avoid scratching.

**Q: Can I use it for water bath canning too?**
A: Absolutely. The 23-quart capacity makes it excellent for large water bath batches of jams and pickles. Just don't use the pressure regulator for those — vent the steam with the weight off.

**Q: Is the dial gauge accurate enough for safe pressure canning?**
A: The weighted regulator is your primary safety control and it's accurate by design. The dial is a reference tool; get it checked annually if you're doing low-acid foods like vegetables or meats.
