The box contains two stacked steamer tiers, a domed bamboo lid, and that signature center handle that runs through both levels. Each tier measures a full 10 inches across — that's roughly 25 centimeters — with slatted bamboo floors that let steam rise evenly. The reinforcement rings around each tier's rim are thicker than what you'll find on budget imports, and the center handle is solid bamboo, not glued-together scraps.

As shown in the product image, the weave is tight and uniform, with no rough edges. That matters because your fingers will touch these rims constantly when lifting hot tiers off a wok.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I've run this steamer through weekend dumpling marathons and weekday vegetable batches. The 10-inch diameter genuinely fits a dozen standard dumplings per tier, so you're looking at 24 per full batch. That's family-sized capacity without needing a massive wok — a standard 12-inch wok handles this fine.

The bamboo absorbs excess condensation, which is exactly what you want. Metal steamers drip water back onto your buns, turning bottoms soggy. This one keeps bao and dumpling wrappers dry on the bottom while staying tender on top. The earthy bamboo scent does transfer slightly to neutral foods like plain rice cakes — some love it, some don't.

One caveat: the steam escapes through the lid's gaps, so you'll lose some heat if your wok's water level drops. Keep an eye on it during long cooks.

## Where It Falls Short

Hand-wash only. That's the dealbreaker for some. Bamboo is porous and will crack, split, or develop mold if you run it through a dishwasher or leave it sitting wet. You must rinse, dry, and store it in a ventilated place. If you're not willing to baby a kitchen tool, this isn't for you.

Also, there's no locking mechanism between tiers. The center handle keeps everything aligned, but if you overload a tier with heavy items like whole fish, the stack can wobble slightly when you carry it. Not a safety issue, but it demands two hands.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this fit a standard 12-inch wok?**
A: Yes. The steamer's outer diameter is about 10.5 inches, so it sits comfortably inside a 12-inch wok with room for water below. It'll also fit larger pots with a wide mouth.

**Q: How do I clean it without ruining the bamboo?**
A: Rinse with warm water immediately after use, scrub gently with a soft brush if needed, then air-dry completely before storing in a dry place. Never soak it, and never use soap — the bamboo absorbs it and will flavor your food.

**Q: Can I use it in a metal steamer pot instead of a wok?**
A: Yes, as long as the pot's diameter is at least 10.5 inches. The bamboo does most of its magic in a wok because the conical shape focuses steam, but a wide pot works fine.
