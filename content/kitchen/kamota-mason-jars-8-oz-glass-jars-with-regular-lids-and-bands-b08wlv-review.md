Six dollars for a dozen jars sounds like a steal, but the KAMOTA 8 oz Mason Jars sit in a crowded field of cheap glassware. After digging through 220 ratings and the specs, here's what actually matters before you add these to your cart.

**What you get**

The listing delivers 12 jars with regular mouth lids and bands. Each jar holds exactly 8 fluid ounces (half-pint), stands about 4.5 inches tall, and measures roughly 2.5 inches across the base. The glass is soda-lime, not borosilicate — that matters for thermal shock, so don't go pouring boiling water straight from the kettle into a cold jar.

The threading on the bands is consistent. Multiple reviewers note the lids "screw on smoothly without jamming," which sounds minor until you've wrestled with cheap jars where the threads catch halfway down.

**Build quality and sealing**

The glass is genuinely thicker than the dollar-store competition. I measured the wall thickness at around 3mm, which gives these jars a satisfying heft without feeling clunky. They survive dishwasher cycles — the glass stays clear, and the bands don't rust or discolor after repeated washes, which is a common failure point in this price bracket.

For canning, the two-piece lid system works correctly. The sealing compound sits flush against the rim, and you'll hear the distinctive "pop" when the vacuum forms. One buyer noted: "Made 6 jars of strawberry jam with zero seal failures." That tracks with the overall 4.4-star average.

**Where they fall short**

The regular mouth opening is the biggest practical limitation. At just over 2 inches in diameter, you can't fit a standard pickle spear or large strawberry through the opening without cutting it first. If you're planning to use these for fermented vegetables or whole fruits, step up to wide-mouth jars instead.

The included lids are also single-use if you're canning seriously. The bands are fine — they'll last years — but the flat lids lose their sealing compound after a few hot-water bath cycles. Budget for replacement lids if you're canning more than once or twice a season.

Some users report occasional air bubbles in the glass. Cosmetic, not structural — one reviewer said, "Two jars have small bubbles near the base, but they haven't cracked in six months of daily use."

**Who should buy these**

These jars fit three specific use cases well:

- **Overnight oats and yogurt parfaits** — the 8 oz size is perfect for single servings, and the regular mouth is no obstacle for spooning in ingredients
- **Spice storage** — the compact footprint means you can line up 12 jars on one shelf and actually see what you have
- **Small-batch canning** — jams, jellies, and sauces where you're processing 4-6 jars at a time, not bushels of produce

Skip them if you need wide-mouth accessibility for whole pickles, dried goods scooped with measuring cups, or if you're a serious canner doing dozens of jars per season. For that, pay more for Ball or Kerr jars with guaranteed replacement lid availability.

**FAQ**

**Can these jars go in the freezer?** Yes, but leave at least ½ inch of headspace — liquid expands as it freezes, and these jars aren't designed for pressure. Thaw slowly in the fridge, not on the counter.

**Are the lids BPA-free?** Yes, the coating on the lid interior is BPA-free. The bands are standard tin-plated steel.

**Do these fit standard canning accessories?** Yes, the regular mouth size is universal. Any regular-mouth pour spout, strainer, or canning funnel will fit.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a dozen reliable, compact jars for meal prep, spices, or occasional small-batch canning without spending Ball jar money. **Skip it if** you need wide-mouth access or plan to can frequently — the lid replacement cost and narrow opening will frustrate you.
