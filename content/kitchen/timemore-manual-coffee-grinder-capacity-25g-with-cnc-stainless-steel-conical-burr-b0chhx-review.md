## What's Actually in the Box

You get the grinder body, a glass catch cup, the crank arm, and a cleaning brush. No dosing funnel, no travel case, no bellows. The grinder itself is 4.7 inches tall and weighs just over a pound — compact enough to toss in a backpack for camping or office use. The body is CNC-machined aluminum with a brushed finish that resists fingerprints better than you'd expect.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The headline feature here is the CNC stainless steel conical burrs. In practical terms, this means you're getting a grinding mechanism that's machined to tighter tolerances than cast burrs found on cheaper grinders. I ran 20 grams of medium-roast Ethiopian beans through it and sifted the results — the particle distribution was notably uniform, with minimal fines. That translates to cleaner cups with less bitterness.

The external adjustment dial is where this grinder shines. It sits on top and clicks through 40+ settings without needing to realign anything. You can go from a fine espresso grind (setting 8-10) to a coarse French press grind (setting 30+) in about five seconds. That's the kind of flexibility that makes this a viable single-grinder solution for multiple brew methods.

## Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself

**The ergonomics are real.** Grinding 25 grams of light-roast beans for espresso will take you 90 seconds of serious cranking. Your shoulder will feel it. If you're a daily espresso drinker using light roasts, this gets old fast. Medium and dark roasts are significantly easier, but the physics of hand grinding doesn't disappear.

**The glass catch cup is a liability.** It's threaded metal on top and glass underneath — fine for countertop use, but if it slips from your hand, you're ordering replacement parts. Several reviewers note this exact failure mode.

**Retention is about 0.5g.** Not a dealbreaker, but if you're single-dosing and weighing output, you'll notice the difference between what goes in and what comes out. A quick tap on the counter or a bellows attachment solves it, but neither is included.

## Who Should Buy This

**The pour-over enthusiast** who brews 600ml batches and wants consistent grounds without dropping $200+ on an electric grinder. The 25g capacity genuinely eliminates the mid-brew refill annoyance.

**The travel brewer** who needs something compact and durable enough for campsites or hotel rooms. The aluminum body handles travel better than plastic alternatives.

**The espresso beginner** who wants to experiment with dialing in shots without committing to a $500 setup. The adjustment range is wide enough to learn on.

**Skip it if** you're a high-volume brewer making more than 4 cups at once, or if you have wrist/shoulder issues — hand grinding 25g daily will aggravate both. Also skip if you only drink espresso: the effort-to-output ratio is poor compared to an entry-level electric grinder like the Baratza Encore ESP.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Is this grinder good for espresso?**
Yes, with caveats. The burrs produce fine, uniform grounds suitable for espresso. But hand-grinding fine for espresso is physically demanding, especially with light roasts. If you're making one or two shots daily, it works. If you're pulling back-to-back shots for a family, get an electric.

**Q: How long does it take to grind 25g?**
Medium-dark roast: 30-40 seconds. Light roast for espresso: 90 seconds to 2 minutes. Your arm speed and bean density are the variables.

**Q: Can I use it for pour-over and French press?**
Absolutely. The 40-click adjustment range covers everything from Turkish (finest) to cold brew (coarsest). The click detents are tactile and hold position well during grinding.
