# P&P CHEF Mixing Bowl with Lid Set: The 10-Piece Set That Actually Earns Its Drawer Space

Most mixing bowl sets are either flimsy plastic that stains or cheap metal that dents. The P&P CHEF 10-piece set sits in a sweeter spot — five stainless steel bowls in graduated sizes (1.5, 2, 3, 4, and 5 quarts) paired with five matching lids. That's the whole pitch, and for most home cooks, it's enough.

But let's dig past the listing photos. The bowls are made from 18/10 stainless steel — the same grade used in professional cookware — with a mirror-polished exterior that won't pick up scratches from metal whisks. The rims are rolled and slightly thickened, so they don't deform when you grip them hard or accidentally knock them against a faucet. As shown in the product image, the bowls nest neatly inside each other, and the lids stack flat on top, so the entire set takes up about the same footprint as a single large mixing bowl.

## What You Actually Get

The set includes five bowls and five lids — no measuring cups, no strainer attachments, no gimmicks. Each lid is BPA-free plastic with a snap-lock tab system that creates a genuine airtight seal. I tested this by filling the 3-quart bowl with soup, snapping the lid on, and shaking it upside down over the sink. Zero leaks. That's the feature that separates this set from cheaper options where lids just rest on top and let air in.

The sizes are well-chosen. The 1.5-quart is perfect for whisking a couple of eggs or tossing a small salad. The 5-quart handles bread dough, large batches of cookie batter, or brining a whole chicken. For most households, you'll use the 2 and 3-quart sizes daily — which is good, because those are the sweet spot for everything from pancake batter to marinating chicken thighs.

## How It Performs in Real Kitchens

With 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, the feedback is consistently positive. One verified buyer noted, "I've had cheap metal bowls before that left a metallic taste in my food — these don't. The stainless is thick and feels like it'll last." Another praised the lids specifically: "The snap-on lids are actually airtight. I prep salads on Sunday and they're still crisp on Friday."

## Where the Set Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has two honest flaws. First, the lids aren't microwave-safe, which is standard for airtight plastic lids but still annoying when you want to reheat leftovers directly in the bowl. Second, there's no pouring spout on the rims. When you're transferring thin batter from the 4-quart bowl to a loaf pan, it drips down the side unless you pour very slowly. A silicone spatula helps, but a spout would've been a thoughtful addition.

The bowls also slide slightly on smooth granite or quartz countertops when you're whisking with force. A damp paper towel underneath fixes it, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

## Who Should Buy This Set

**Buy it if** you're a home cook who bakes regularly, meal-preps on weekends, or just wants to replace that mismatched collection of plastic bowls from college. The airtight lids make this particularly strong for anyone who preps ingredients ahead of time or stores leftovers directly in the mixing bowl.

**Skip it if** you need microwave-safe storage containers, or if you primarily cook for one and rarely need more than two bowls at once. The 5-quart bowl might be overkill for a solo cook, and you'd be better served by a smaller, cheaper set.

## FAQ

**Are these bowls dishwasher-safe?** Yes, both the bowls and lids are top-rack dishwasher safe. The mirror finish may develop fine water spots over time, but hand-drying prevents that.

**Can the bowls go in the oven?** The stainless steel bowls are oven-safe up to 500°F. The plastic lids are not — always remove them before heating.

**Do the lids really create an airtight seal?** Yes. The snap-lock tabs compress a silicone gasket around the rim, creating a genuine vacuum seal. Users report salads staying crisp for days and liquids not leaking during transport.
