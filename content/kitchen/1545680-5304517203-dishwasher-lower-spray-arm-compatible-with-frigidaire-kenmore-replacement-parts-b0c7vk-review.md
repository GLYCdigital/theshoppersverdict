Let me be blunt: when your dishwasher's lower spray arm cracks, you have two options. Pay $80+ for a genuine OEM part and wait a week, or roll the dice on a $25 aftermarket replacement. This one's a winner — but there are caveats worth knowing before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains exactly one item: the lower spray arm assembly. That's it. No mounting clip, no gasket, no hardware. The part itself is molded ABS plastic — the same material class as the OEM piece — with the two standard spray arms, a reinforced center hub, and the mounting collar that snaps onto your dishwasher's riser tube.

As shown in the product image, the design mirrors the Frigidaire/Electrolux original: curved arms, angled nozzles, and a single center pivot point. It's listed as compatible with 1545680 and 5304517203 — the two most common Frigidaire part numbers — which covers most Kenmore, Electrolux, and Crosley dishwashers manufactured in the last decade.

## Fit and Installation

This is where aftermarket parts live or die, and this one mostly delivers. The collar diameter matches the OEM spec, and it snaps onto the riser with a satisfying click — though you'll need to push harder than you'd expect. One reviewer noted, "Fit perfectly on my Frigidaire Gallery. I had to press firmly to get it to seat, but it hasn't moved since."

The key thing to verify before ordering: measure your existing spray arm's length. This part measures 13.5 inches tip-to-tip. If your current arm is substantially longer or shorter, this won't work. Check your model number against the compatibility list — the seller includes a chart in the listing images.

## Performance in Real Use

Once installed, the arm spins freely and distributes water evenly across the lower rack. The spray jets are angled to create the rotating force, and the through-holes align with the original pattern.

But here's the honest tradeoff: the jets are slightly narrower than OEM. On normal loads — plates, bowls, silverware — the difference is negligible. On heavily baked-on casserole dishes, the narrower spray pattern means you may need to pre-rinse more aggressively. One long-term reviewer put it this way: "Cleans better than my cracked original, but doesn't have quite the same water pressure as when the dishwasher was new."

That's not a dealbreaker at this price point, but it's real.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint? The lack of a retaining clip. On many Frigidaire models, the spray arm is held in place by a separate push-clip on the riser. If your clip is intact, you're fine. If it's broken, this part won't fix it — order a new clip simultaneously or you'll be waiting another shipping cycle.

The looser tolerance on the mounting collar is a minor annoyance during installation but hasn't caused any wobble or detachment issues in long-term use.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my Kenmore model?**
Most Kenmore dishwashers are rebadged Frigidaire/Electrolux units. Check your model number against the compatibility list in the listing — if it's not listed, measure your old arm's length and compare to 13.5 inches.

**Q: Do I need to replace the clip too?**
If your existing clip is intact, no. If it's missing or deformed, yes — order one separately. The part doesn't include it.

**Q: How long does installation take?**
About 10 minutes. Remove the bottom rack, unscrew the filter screen, pop the old arm off the riser, snap the new one in place. No tools beyond a screwdriver.
