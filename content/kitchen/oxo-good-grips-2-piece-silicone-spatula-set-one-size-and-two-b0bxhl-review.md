The OXO Good Grips 2 Piece Silicone Spatula Set has earned its 4.7-star rating across 235 Amazon reviews for a simple reason: it does what a spatula should do, without fuss, and it survives the heat. Let's get into what you're actually paying for.

**What's in the box**

You get two spatulas — one large and one small. The large one measures about 12.5 inches long with a 3-inch-wide head; the small runs roughly 10.5 inches with a 2.25-inch head. Both feature a one-piece silicone head bonded to a stainless steel core inside the handle. That's the key design detail: no plastic handle to melt, no wooden handle to crack, no crevice where the head meets the handle for food to lodge into.

**Performance and build quality**

These spatulas are heat-resistant up to 600°F, which means you can rest one on the edge of a screaming-hot cast iron skillet and walk away without coming back to a puddle of goo. The silicone heads are firm enough to flip a pancake or scrape a pan clean, yet flexible enough to get under a fried egg without shredding it.

The handles are what OXO calls "Good Grips" — soft, nonslip, and contoured. They're comfortable even when your hands are wet or greasy, and the stainless steel core gives them a reassuring heft. They're not flimsy; they feel like tools, not toys.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, durability — people repeatedly mention these have lasted years without warping, cracking, or losing their shape. One reviewer wrote, "I've had these for two years and they still look brand new despite daily use." Second, the seamless one-piece design. **Where it falls short**

No product is perfect, and this set has two honest tradeoffs. First, the flexibility is moderate — not flimsy, but not extreme. If you need to scrape the absolute bottom corner of a mixing bowl, you might find the head doesn't bend quite enough to get that last bit of batter. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker. Second, the large spatula's head is noticeably wide. It's great for big pots and skillets, but it feels clumsy when you're trying to stir a small jar of sauce or a narrow container. The small spatula handles those jobs, but you'll want to keep both within reach.
