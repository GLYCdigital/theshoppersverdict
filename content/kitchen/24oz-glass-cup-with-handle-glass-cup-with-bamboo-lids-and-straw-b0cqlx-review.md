## What You Actually Get

The package includes the 24oz glass cup itself, a bamboo lid with a silicone gasket, and a glass straw. No plastic, no cheap metal — the whole thing is glass and wood. The cup is made from borosilicate glass, which is the same material used in lab equipment because it handles temperature swings without shattering. That matters if you're the type to pour hot coffee into a cup that was just holding iced water.

The bamboo lid fits snugly and has a hole for the straw. There's also a smaller silicone stopper for the straw hole when you're not using it. The handle is integrated into the glass — not glued on — which is a good sign for durability.

## Real-World Performance

I'll cut to the chase: this cup performs well for what it is. The borosilicate glass is noticeably thicker than the cheap soda-lime glass you'll find in dollar-store tumblers. It's clear, doesn't retain odors, and cleans up easily with soap and water. The 24oz capacity is genuinely useful — it holds a full can of soda plus ice, or a large iced coffee from most coffee shops.

The bamboo lid is where things get interesting. Several buyers specifically mentioned that the silicone gasket creates a proper seal. One reviewer noted, *"I was skeptical about the bamboo lid, but it actually stays put and doesn't leak when I tip it over in my bag."* That's the kind of feedback that separates a well-made product from a novelty item. Just make sure you press the lid down firmly — it's not a screw-top, so a loose press will let liquid escape.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 220 ratings, three themes keep coming up. First, the glass quality — buyers repeatedly mention that it feels substantial and doesn't have the "thin, fragile" feel of cheaper cups. Second, the aesthetic — the bamboo lid and clear glass look genuinely nice on a desk or kitchen counter. Third, the size — 24oz is enough to actually hydrate without constant refills.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. The bamboo lid is hand-wash only. If you throw it in the dishwasher, the wood will warp and the seal will break. That's a real inconvenience for people who rely on dishwashers for everyday cleanup.

The glass is also heavy. At roughly 2.5 pounds when full, this isn't a cup you'll want to carry around all day. It's better suited for a desk, nightstand, or kitchen counter than a commute. And while the glass straw is elegant, it's fragile — dropping it on tile will likely end in pieces.

One more note: the lid isn't fully airtight. It's leak-resistant, not leak-proof. If you're planning to toss this in a backpack, you're taking a risk. For stationary use, it's fine.

## FAQ

**Is the bamboo lid really leak-proof?**
No. It's leak-resistant when the lid is pressed down firmly, but it's not airtight. Don't lay it on its side in a bag for extended periods.

**Can I put this in the dishwasher?**
The glass cup is dishwasher safe, but the bamboo lid and glass straw should be hand-washed. Heat and moisture will warp the wood lid over time.

**Does it fit in a standard car cup holder?**
The base is slightly tapered, so it fits most standard cup holders. But the height — about 8 inches — can be an issue in smaller vehicles.
