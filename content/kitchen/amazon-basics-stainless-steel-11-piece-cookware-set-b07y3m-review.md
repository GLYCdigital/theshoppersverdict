# Amazon Basics Stainless Steel 11-Piece Cookware Set Review: The Budget Set That Punches Above Its Weight

Let's be direct: most cookware sets under $150 are either flimsy nonstick that warps within a year or thin stainless that scorches everything. The Amazon Basics 11-piece stainless steel set lands in a different category — it's the rare budget offering that uses the same tri-ply construction you'd find in sets costing three times as much. With 215 ratings averaging 4.3 stars, it's earned a reputation. Here's whether it deserves a spot in your kitchen.

## What You Actually Get

The 11 pieces break down to six cookware items and five lids: a 1.5-quart saucepan, 2-quart saucepan, 3-quart sauté pan, 6-quart stockpot, 8-quart stockpot, and a 10-inch frying pan. Every pot gets its own lid except the fry pan, which is open. The set covers the essentials for a home cook — you can boil pasta, sear meat, simmer sauces, and roast in the oven without reaching for another pan.

The build is legit tri-ply: stainless steel exterior, aluminum core, stainless interior. That's the same structure used by Calphalon and All-Clad's entry lines. The riveted handles are stainless and stay cool on the stovetop, though they do conduct heat in the oven — use a mitt above 400°F.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Heating evenness is where this set surprises. The aluminum core distributes heat well enough that you won't get hot spots in the sauté pan when searing chicken thighs. The 6-quart stockpot brings water to a boil faster than my old nonstick set — roughly a minute quicker for the same volume.

The 10-inch fry pan is the one to watch. Stainless steel doesn't release food like nonstick, so eggs are a no-go unless you preheat properly and use butter or oil generously. The technique: preheat the pan on medium for 2-3 minutes, add fat, then food. Get that right and you'll get a good sear and easy cleanup. Get it wrong and you'll be scrubbing.

Oven safety is a genuine strength here — the set handles up to 500°F including the lids. That means you can start a braise on the stovetop and move the whole pot into a 425°F oven without transferring to another vessel.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The overwhelming theme in reviews is value for money. One verified buyer put it plainly: "I was skeptical at this price point but the weight and feel rival my sister's Cuisinart set that cost double." Another noted the dishwasher-safe convenience: "Everything except the frying pan goes in the dishwasher without spotting issues."

The even heating gets repeated shoutouts. Multiple reviewers specifically mention the sauté pan handling high-heat searing without warping — a common failure point on cheaper sets.

## Where It Falls Short

The 8-quart stockpot lacks a helper handle opposite the main one. When filled with pasta water, it's genuinely heavy — you'll need two hands on the main handle or a towel to grip the rim. This is a real design oversight.

The lids aren't perfectly flush on the smaller saucepans. You'll notice slight steam escaping from the 1.5-quart during simmering. It's not a dealbreaker, but it does mean slightly longer cooking times for sauces.

And the learning curve on stainless is real. If you're coming from nonstick, expect a week of sticking and frustration before the technique clicks.

## Who Should Buy This

This set is ideal for:

- **Budget-conscious home cooks** who want professional-grade construction without the price tag
- **Stainless steel converts** who are ready to learn proper preheating technique
- **Anyone replacing a worn nonstick set** — this will outlast three of those

Skip it if you're a nonstick purist, if you only cook eggs and delicate fish, or if you need induction compatibility (this set is not induction-ready).

## FAQ

**Is this set dishwasher safe?**
Yes, all pieces are dishwasher safe. That said, hand washing preserves the exterior finish better — dishwashers can leave mineral spots on stainless.

**Does it work on induction cooktops?**
No. This set is compatible with gas, electric, and ceramic glass cooktops, but the stainless base isn't magnetic enough for induction.

**Will food stick immediately?**
Yes, if you don't preheat properly. Stainless steel needs a preheat of 2-3 minutes on medium before adding oil, then food. Once you master this, most foods release cleanly.

**Skip it if:** You prefer nonstick convenience, cook induction, or need a helper handle on your largest stockpot.
