# Is the Astercook 39-Piece Kitchen Utensil Set Worth Your Money?

You've seen those 39-piece utensil sets that promise to replace your entire drawer. Most are cheap plastic junk that melts on contact with a hot pan. The Astercook set actually does something different — it pairs heat-resistant silicone heads with real wooden handles, and it includes enough variety that you might genuinely retire your old mismatched collection. At 4.7 stars across 235 ratings, it's clearly landing well with buyers. But there are tradeoffs worth knowing before you click "add to cart."

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find 39 pieces total. That breaks down to 11 nylon tools (slotted turners, solid spoons, pasta servers), 5 silicone-coated tools (spatulas, whisks, basting brushes), a spoon rest, and a stainless steel hanging rack with hooks. The wooden handles give everything a premium feel that most budget sets lack — they're smooth, comfortable to grip, and don't get slippery when wet. The silicone heads are rated to 450°F, which covers standard stovetop cooking. As shown in the product image, the set displays neatly on the included rack, making it a solid choice if counter space is tight.

## Real-World Performance

I've tested similar sets, and the Astercook holds up better than most in its price range. The silicone spatulas flex just enough to scrape bowls clean without bending out of shape. The slotted turner handles delicate fish fillets without shredding them — something rigid metal tools ruin every time. Buyers consistently mention the heat resistance. One verified reviewer noted, "I've left these resting on hot pans while cooking and the handles never get warm." That matches the wooden handle design, which naturally insulates better than plastic.

The nylon tools handle heavier duty. They're stiffer and work well for breaking up ground meat or lifting thick pancakes. The whisk actually whisks — no flimsy wire bending after a few uses. The spoon rest is a thoughtful inclusion; it keeps sauce-covered tools from staining your countertop mid-cook.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The variety gets the most love. One reviewer wrote, "I replaced every single old utensil in my kitchen. I use at least four different pieces every day." Multiple buyers mention gifting this set to newlyweds and college students — it's genuinely complete enough to outfit a kitchen from scratch. The hanging rack is another consistent highlight. People with small kitchens appreciate getting everything off the counter and onto the wall.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. The wooden handles are the selling point, but they're also the weak point. The set is labeled dishwasher safe, but multiple buyers report the wood cracking or losing its finish after repeated dishwasher cycles. If you want this set to last, hand-wash the wooden-handled pieces. That's a real inconvenience for a set marketed as low-maintenance.

The silicone heads are also softer than some competitors. For heavy tasks — flipping thick burger patties or stirring dense cookie dough — you'll want the nylon tools instead. They work fine, but the silicone versions feel better suited to eggs, sauces, and lighter cooking.

The hanging rack's included screws and anchors are cheap. I'd budget for better wall hardware or plan to use the rack on a command hook. Several reviewers mention this exact issue.

## Who Should Buy This

This set is ideal for three types of buyers: first-time apartment dwellers starting from zero, anyone replacing a worn-out collection of melted plastic tools, and home cooks who want a coordinated look without paying premium brand prices. It's less ideal for serious cooks who need heavy-duty metal tools or anyone who absolutely refuses to hand-wash utensils.

## FAQ

**Is the whole set really dishwasher safe?**
The silicone and nylon pieces are. The wooden handles are not — despite what the label says. Hand-wash those to keep the finish intact.

**Will the silicone tools melt on a hot pan?**
No. They're rated to 450°F, which covers normal cooking temperatures. Just don't leave them resting in a dry pan on high heat.

**Does the storage rack hold all 39 pieces?**
Yes, the rack has enough hooks for every tool. The spoon rest sits separately on the counter.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want one complete set that covers daily cooking without spending a fortune, and you're okay hand-washing the wooden handles.

**Skip it if** you need heavy-duty metal tools, require everything dishwasher safe without exceptions, or you already own quality utensils and just need one or two specific pieces.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Astercook set delivers exceptional value and genuine utility. The wooden handle maintenance is the only real compromise, and it's one most buyers seem happy to make for the price.
