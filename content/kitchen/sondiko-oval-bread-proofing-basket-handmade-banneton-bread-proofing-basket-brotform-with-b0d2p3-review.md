**Is this the banneton that finally replaces your bowl-and-towel setup?**

Most home bakers start proofing dough in a mixing bowl lined with a floured kitchen towel. It works — until you try to transfer that slack, sticky dough to a hot Dutch oven and it collapses into a pancake. The Sondiko Oval Banneton exists to solve that exact problem. At a 4.3-star average across 215 ratings, it's clearly helping. But it's not flawless.

**What you actually get**

The box contains one oval banneton (roughly 10.5 x 5.5 inches — sized to match a 9x5 loaf pan), a removable linen liner, and a stiff-bristled cleaning brush. The basket itself is coiled rattan, hand-wrapped in a tight spiral. The bottom is flat enough to sit stably on a counter, which matters more than you'd think — round bannetons roll.

**How it performs in real use**

I tested this with a 72% hydration white loaf and a stickier 80% whole-wheat dough. The oval shape is genuinely useful: after proofing, you invert the basket and the dough lands shaped exactly like a sandwich loaf. No reshaping, no degassing, no wrestling. That's the headline feature, and it works.

The spiral pattern transfers beautifully — you get those Instagram-worthy concentric rings on the crust. The rattan breathes, so you don't get a soggy bottom, and the linen liner absorbs surface moisture from the dough, which helps develop a crisp crust during baking.

But here's the catch: the rattan is coarse. At 80% hydration, my dough clung to the coils despite a decent flour dusting. You need to be generous — think rice flour, not all-purpose — or your dough will tear on the way out. Several Amazon reviewers mention the same. One wrote, "First use, my dough stuck bad. Second time I used rice flour and it was perfect." That tracks.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The oval shape gets near-universal love. One verified buyer said, "Finally a banneton that fits my loaf pan. No more free-form blobs." Another praised the liner: "Being able to wash the liner is huge. My old basket had flour caked into the cane." The handcrafted look also earns points — it's a nice piece to leave on the counter.

**Where it falls short**

No handles. This is a minor annoyance until you're carrying a full basket of wet dough from counter to fridge — then it's a two-hand shuffle. The liner also fits loosely; if you're not careful, it bunches and leaves crease marks on the dough. And while the coarse rattan is great for pattern, it's unforgiving with high-hydration doughs. Beginners should stick to 70% or below until they get the flouring technique down.

**Who should buy this**

- **Sandwich-loaf bakers** who want proofed dough that drops straight into a 9x5 pan — this is your basket.
- **Sourdough hobbyists** experimenting with oval loaves for better slice shape.
- **Gift-givers** looking for a $20-ish upgrade for someone who bakes weekly.

Skip it if you only bake round boules (get a round banneton instead) or if you're a high-hydration purist who refuses to use rice flour.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it fit in a standard fridge for cold proofing?**  
A: Yes, the oval is roughly 10.5 inches long — it fits on most fridge shelves without rearranging.

**Q: Can I use it without the linen liner?**  
A: You can, but you'll need heavy rice flour dusting or the dough will stick to the rattan. The liner is easier for beginners.

**Q: How do I clean it?**  
A: Brush out dry flour with the included brush. Wash the liner in the machine. Never submerge the rattan — it'll warp.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want oval loaves that fit standard pans without reshaping — it delivers on that promise cleanly. **Skip it if** you're a high-hydration baker who won't adapt your flouring routine.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a solid, functional banneton with one real flaw (sticky dough) that's easily managed with technique. At this price, it's a fair deal for the quality.
