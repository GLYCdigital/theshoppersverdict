If you've ever tried to make chocolate that looks like an Oreo, you know the struggle: store-bought candy molds are either too shallow (the detail vanishes) or too stiff (the chocolate won't release). The Sidosir 3-pack Oreo cookie mold sits in a sweet spot — literally and figuratively — and it's currently holding a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

Three identical silicone molds, each with 12 cavities. That's 36 total cavities per pour session — not 12. That matters more than you'd think. Most single molds force you to wait for chocolate to set before unmolding and starting again. With three, you can fill all 36, walk away, and come back to a full batch. Each cavity is roughly 1.5 inches in diameter, which is slightly smaller than a standard Oreo. The design captures the wafers' crisscross pattern and the cream layer's scalloped edge — the two-tone look is achievable because the mold has a natural separation line.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The silicone is food-grade and BPA-free, and it's flexible in the right ways. When the chocolate sets, you can push from the back of the cavity and the piece pops out cleanly — the deep embossing holds up. Multiple buyers noted this specifically. One reviewer wrote, "The detail on the cookies is amazing, the chocolate comes out looking just like the real thing." Another said, "I've used it for both chocolate and soap, and both release perfectly."

The thin walls are a double-edged sword. They make unmolding easy, but they also mean the mold lacks rigidity. If you fill it while holding it in one hand, the whole thing bends and chocolate sloshes. Set it on a flat tray or baking sheet first. That's a minor workflow adjustment, not a dealbreaker.

**Where It Falls Short**

The size catches people off guard. If you're picturing full-size Oreo replicas, you'll be disappointed. These are more like oversized truffles or bonbon-sized pieces. Fine for gifting or dessert tables, not for pretending you made actual cookies.

The molds also don't come with a storage box. Silicone attracts dust and pet hair like a magnet. Toss them in a zip-top bag between uses or you'll be rinsing them before every session. That's a small annoyance, but worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Chocolate makers** who want a reliable, detailed mold for truffles, bonbons, or molded chocolates — this delivers consistent results.
- **Bakers** making cake pops or Oreo-themed treats who want the cookie look without the cookie texture.
- **Soap and resin crafters** — the same detail that makes good chocolate also works for melt-and-pour soap bases or epoxy resin projects.
- **Gift-givers** — 36 pieces in one session means you can produce a full holiday or party favor batch quickly.

Skip it if you need full-size Oreo replicas or you're looking for a rigid mold you can handle one-handed.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this mold with candy melts, or does it need real chocolate?**
A: Both work. Candy melts set faster and release just as cleanly. Real chocolate needs proper tempering to avoid bloom (white streaks), but the mold itself handles either fine.

**Q: Is the mold dishwasher safe?**
A: Yes, but hand washing is gentler on the detailed embossing. Hot water and mild soap will keep the pattern crisp longer.

**Q: Can I make two-tone cookies (dark wafer, white cream)?**
A: Yes — fill the bottom half with dark chocolate, let it set briefly, then add white chocolate for the cream layer. The mold's separation line makes this easier than most.
