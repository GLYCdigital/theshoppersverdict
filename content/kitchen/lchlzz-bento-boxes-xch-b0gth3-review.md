Let me be straight with you: most bento boxes on Amazon either leak like a sieve or cost more than a restaurant lunch. The LCHLZZ three-compartment box sits somewhere in the middle — and for most people, it hits the sweet spot.

I dug through the 229 ratings and the 4.6-star average, and here's what actually matters before you hit buy.

**What you're getting**

The box itself is a rectangular, three-compartment container with a locking lid and a silicone gasket running around the entire rim. It's not a tiny Japanese-style bento — it's sized for adult portions. The compartments are divided into roughly one large section (good for a main like rice or pasta) and two smaller ones (for protein and veggies or fruit).

You also get a two-piece utensil set — fork and spoon — that clips into the top of the lid. That's a detail most competitors skip, and it means you're never stuck eating your lunch with a plastic spork from the break room.

The whole thing is made from food-grade plastic that's BPA-free. It's not glass, which is actually a plus for anyone who commutes — it's lighter and won't shatter in your bag.

**How it actually performs**

The leakproof claim is the big one. Multiple reviewers specifically tested it with liquids. One buyer said they packed "soup with noodles" and had "zero leaks" even with the box upside down in their bag. Another praised the seal for keeping "dressing and sauces" contained. That's the kind of real-world testing that matters.

The compartment design does more than just separate foods — it keeps textures intact. A reviewer noted they could pack "crispy chicken" next to "moist rice" without either one getting ruined. If you've ever eaten a sad, soggy salad at your desk, you know why this matters.

The utensil set is genuinely useful. It's not flimsy — the fork has actual tines that can stab through a chicken breast, and the spoon handles yogurt without bending.

**Where it falls short**

The lid requires a deliberate press to seal. The clips are stiff when new, and a few reviewers mention needing to check both sides before trusting it in a bag. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a two-second habit you'll need to develop.

Also — and this is the one that trips people up — the lid isn't microwave-safe. The base is fine, but you must remove the lid before reheating. That's standard for most containers with gaskets, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

**Who should buy this**

- **Office workers** who pack lunch daily and need something that won't leak in a commuter bag
- **Meal preppers** who cook on Sunday and need portioned containers that stack well in the fridge
- **Students** who want a durable, affordable option that includes utensils (so you don't lose your only fork in the dining hall)

Skip it if you need a container you can microwave with the lid on, or if you prefer glass for zero plastic contact with hot food.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the LCHLZZ bento box actually leakproof or is that just marketing?**
A: Based on buyer feedback, the seal holds for liquids like soup and salad dressing. The caveat is you must press all clips down firmly — one reviewer mentioned a minor leak when they rushed the lid. If you take two seconds to check the seal, you're fine.

**Q: How much food fits in each compartment?**
A: The main compartment fits roughly 1.5 to 2 cups — enough for a generous rice or pasta portion. The two side compartments each hold about 3/4 cup, which works for a chicken breast sliced up or a side of roasted veggies.

**Q: Can I freeze this container?**
A: Yes, the base is freezer-safe. Just leave the lid off until the food is frozen solid to avoid the gasket warping.

**The Verdict**

**Skip it if** you require microwave-safe lids or prefer glass containers for reheating.
