**Is this the one juicer that actually does two jobs well, or is the sorbet function just a gimmick? I spent two weeks putting this 2-in-1 cold press juicer through real-world testing to find out.**

Most "2-in-1" kitchen gadgets compromise on both functions. This one doesn't — and that's what makes it worth your attention. It's a slow masticating juicer that also churns frozen fruit into sorbet, and both modes deliver legitimate results.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main motor base, the masticating auger assembly, two different strainer baskets (one for juice, one for sorbet), a pulp container, juice pitcher with foam separator, and the sorbet pusher. Assembly takes about 90 seconds once you've done it a couple times.

The motor base has a solid, weighted feel — it doesn't walk across the counter even when processing dense ingredients. The 3-inch feed chute is the standout practical feature here. I fed whole cored apples and baby carrots without any pre-chopping, which cuts prep time significantly compared to narrow-chute masticating juicers.

## Real-World Performance

Running at roughly 80 RPM, this is a true slow juicer. The auger crushes and presses rather than shredding with high-speed blades. The difference shows in yield and pulp texture. My test batch of kale, cucumber, apple, and ginger produced visibly drier pulp than any centrifugal juicer I've used — you can squeeze the leftover pulp by hand and barely get moisture. That translates to more juice in your glass.

Juice quality holds up too. Because there's no heat from friction, the juice stays cold and doesn't oxidize as quickly. I left a glass of green juice in the fridge for 24 hours and it still looked vibrant, not brown.

The sorbet function is the real differentiator. You swap in the sorbet strainer, drop in frozen fruit chunks, and it extrudes a proper soft-serve texture. Frozen bananas and mango made genuinely scoopable sorbet with no added liquid. The key is patience — fruit needs to be frozen solid overnight. I tried with semi-frozen strawberries and got a watery puree instead of sorbet. That's a limitation, but it's also physics.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. Cleanup is the biggest friction point. The mesh strainer has fine holes that catch pulp, and if you don't rinse it within 10 minutes of use, the dried pulp becomes stubborn to remove. The included brush helps, but this is a 7-part cleanup process — not a quick rinse-and-go situation.

Noise level is moderate but not quiet. It's not as loud as a centrifugal juicer, but working through fibrous celery or frozen banana chunks produces a definite grinding sound. If you're juicing before the household wakes up, you'll hear it.

The sorbet texture depends entirely on your freezer discipline. As mentioned, partially frozen fruit ruins the result. Plan ahead or accept inconsistent sorbet output.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it juice citrus fruits with the peel on?**
A: No — peel oranges, lemons, and grapefruit first. The peel contains bitter oils and pith that will ruin the flavor. This applies to all masticating juicers.

**Q: How long does the sorbet take to make?**
A: A pint of sorbet takes about 3-4 minutes of active processing, plus 12+ hours of freezer time for the fruit. No added liquid or sweetener needed if you start with ripe fruit.

**Q: Is the juice really "cold pressed"?**
A: The slow auger operates without heat-generating friction, so juice stays at near-room temperature or cooler. That's the functional equivalent of cold pressing for home use — no oxidation from blade heat.
