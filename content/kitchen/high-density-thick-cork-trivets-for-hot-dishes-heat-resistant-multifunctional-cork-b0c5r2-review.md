## Where It Falls Short

Cork has honest limitations, and this product doesn't escape them. First, it's porous. If you set a cold, damp pan on it repeatedly, you'll see water rings develop. One reviewer warned, "I left a sweating pitcher on mine overnight and it left a dark stain. It still works, but it's not as pretty."

Second, no dishwasher. These need hand washing with mild soap and occasional air drying. In a humid climate, cork can develop mold if left wet in a closed drawer. That's a tradeoff of the material, not a manufacturing flaw — but you should know it going in.

Third, the surface will develop character over time. Scorch marks and small indentations from heavy cookware are normal. If you want a trivet that stays pristine forever, buy the silicone.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these handle direct flame or a grill grate?**
No. They're for hot dishes and cookware, not open flame. Direct contact with a burner will char the cork.

**Q: How do I clean them?**
Hand wash with warm water and mild dish soap. Pat dry immediately and let them air out completely before storing.

**Q: Will they scratch my table?**
Cork is softer than wood, so no. That's actually the advantage — it won't scratch finishes, unlike some metal and ceramic trivets.
