# West Bend Electric Percolator Review: Is This Retro 36-Cup Workhorse Worth It?

**The short version:** If you need coffee for a crowd — think tailgates, church basements, or a hunting cabin — this West Bend percolator delivers old-school flavor in volumes drip machines can't touch. But it asks for a bit of babysitting in return.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the percolator itself, a glass knob on the lid, a stainless steel perk tube and basket, and a detachable cord. No measuring scoop, no filter papers — you'll supply your own. The body is polished chrome over steel, with a black plastic base and a stay-cool handle that actually stays cool, even when the pot is roaring.

At 36 cups, this thing is *big*. We're talking 4.5 liters of coffee. It's roughly 14 inches tall and needs real counter space. This isn't a daily driver for a couple — it's a party appliance, and it knows it.

## How It Actually Performs

Percolators work differently than drip machines: water boils up through the tube, splashes over the grounds, and cycles back down. The result is a more extracted, bolder cup — closer to cowboy coffee than a pour-over. With a coarse grind and a 1:15 coffee-to-water ratio, you get a rich, full-bodied brew that holds up to cream and sugar.

One buyer summed it up: *"Tastes like the coffee my grandma made — strong, hot, and honest. No fancy sensors, just coffee."* That's the appeal.

Brew time for a full pot runs about 12–15 minutes, and it keeps coffee hot indefinitely on the warm cycle. But here's the catch: there's no auto-shutoff. Leave it plugged in too long, and the coffee turns bitter and scorched. You need to be present — or use a timer plug.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Durability.** Multiple reviewers mention inheriting one from a parent or grandparent. The chrome exterior wipes clean, and the heating element is sealed and reliable.
- **Volume.** "I made 30 cups for a family reunion and still had leftovers," wrote one verified buyer. Another used it for a 50-person brunch and said it kept up.
- **Flavor.** If you're tired of watery drip coffee, the percolator's extraction is a revelation. One reviewer said: *"I forgot how good perked coffee is. Rich, dark, no bitterness if you watch the clock."*

## Where It Falls Short

- **The glass knob.** It's the weak link. It sits right on the lid, and if you drop the lid or tighten it too hard, it cracks. Replacement knobs are about $8, so it's not a dealbreaker — just a known fragility.
- **No auto-shutoff.** This is the biggest modern convenience gap. Set it and forget it? Not here. You'll get a burnt pot if you walk away for 30 minutes.
- **No warming plate indicator.** The on/off light is small and easy to miss, especially on a countertop.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Tailgaters and campers** who need gallons of strong coffee without a generator-hogging drip machine.
- **Hosts of large gatherings** — family reunions, church events, holiday breakfasts.
- **Retro enthusiasts** who want the look and taste of a 1970s kitchen, minus the avocado green.

Skip it if you're a single-serve drinker, if you need programmability, or if you're prone to walking away from the kitchen.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Ask

**Q: Is the coffee stronger than drip?**  
A: Generally yes, percolation extracts more oils and compounds. Use a coarse grind to avoid over-extraction and a muddy cup.

**Q: Can I use paper filters?**  
A: No — the basket is designed for loose grounds. Use a medium-coarse grind, about 1 tablespoon per cup.

**Q: How do I clean it?**  
A: The basket, tube, and lid are dishwasher-safe (top rack). Wipe the chrome body with a damp cloth. Run a vinegar-water cycle monthly to descale.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need 20+ cups of bold, traditional coffee, you value durability over smart features, and you'll stay near the pot while it brews.

**Skip it if:** You want programmability, you're a small household, or you're not willing to manage the brew time manually.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a classic, capable workhorse with one modern convenience gap and one fragile knob. For the price and volume, it's hard to beat.
