Cold brew is the easiest coffee you'll ever make — water, grounds, time. The hard part is filtering out the sludge, controlling strength, and storing the result without it turning bitter. KitchenAid's cold brew maker tackles all three with a design that's smarter than most of the glass-jar-and-cheesecloth setups on Amazon. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, it's clearly working for most people. Here's the full breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the brushed stainless steel brewing chamber (holds 6 cups of water), a glass carafe with a silicone seal, the brew basket with a fine stainless mesh, and the strength dial that sits between the two. The dial is the standout feature — you rotate it to set brew strength (concentrate on one end, ready-to-drink on the other), and it physically adjusts the water flow rate through the grounds. It's not a gimmick; it changes the coffee-to-water ratio that actually reaches the carafe.

Assembly is tool-free. The brew basket clicks into the chamber, the dial locks in place, and everything separates for cleaning. The whole unit stands about 11 inches tall assembled, with a footprint smaller than a standard dinner plate.

## Real-World Performance

I ran three batches through it: a 24-hour concentrate, an 18-hour medium, and a 12-hour ready-to-drink. The concentrate came out rich and syrupy — perfect for cutting with milk or water. The ready-to-drink setting produced something closer to a strong iced coffee that needed no dilution. The mesh filter caught essentially all grounds; I found maybe three fines at the bottom of the carafe across all batches. That's better than most French press cold brew setups I've tested.

One thing that surprised me: the brew time is genuinely flexible. KitchenAid recommends 12–24 hours, but the flavor stayed clean at the shorter end. Some cheaper cold brew makers get sour if you under-extract. This one stayed smooth.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in 220 ratings is **ease of cleanup**. One verified buyer wrote, "Everything comes apart and rinses clean in under a minute. No scrubbing coffee oil off glass." Another noted, "The strength dial actually works — I didn't have to guess how much water to add anymore." Multiple reviewers mention the compact size fitting neatly in a corner of the counter.

## Where It Falls Short

The glass carafe feels like the weakest link. It's thin-walled, and several reviewers report chips after normal handling. Hand-wash it despite the label — the dishwasher cycle will eventually cloud the glass. Also, if you use pre-ground coffee, stick to a coarse grind. Fine grounds will clog the mesh and slow the drip to a crawl. One reviewer noted, "I had to stir the slurry halfway through because the flow stopped." Budget for a burr grinder if you don't own one.

The 6-cup capacity is also smaller than it sounds — you'll get about four 12-ounce servings of concentrate. For a household that drinks cold brew daily, you'll be rebatching every couple of days.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The concentrate drinker** who likes mixing their own ratios (latte, iced, straight)
- **The weekend meal-prepper** who wants a batch ready by Monday morning
- **The cleanup-averse** who has abandoned other brewers because of scrubbing

Skip it if you need more than 6 cups at once, or if you refuse to hand-wash glass. A $15 mason jar setup will technically work, but you'll fight the sludge filter forever.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I brew hot coffee with this?**
No. It's strictly cold brew. The chamber isn't insulated and there's no heating element.

**Q: How long does the brew last in the fridge?**
KitchenAid says up to two weeks. Realistically, concentrate stays fresh 7–10 days. The ready-to-drink setting fades faster — aim for 5 days.

**Q: Does the strength dial change the flavor or just the yield?**
Both. The concentrate setting yields less liquid but stronger coffee. The ready-to-drink setting yields more, weaker coffee. It's a dilution control, not a flavor profile changer.

## The Verdict

**Score: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you want a counter-friendly cold brew maker with real strength control and genuinely easy cleanup. It's a solid upgrade over jar-and-filter methods.

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget, need bulk batches, or won't commit to hand-washing the glass carafe. The $30 plastic alternatives won't taste as clean, but they'll survive the dishwasher.

For the price, the KitchenAid delivers a noticeably better cup than cheaper competitors — just treat the carafe like the fragile thing it is.
