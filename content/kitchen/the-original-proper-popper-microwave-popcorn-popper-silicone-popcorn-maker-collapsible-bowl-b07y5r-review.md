## What You Actually Get

The box contains the silicone popping bowl (collapses flat to about 1 inch thick), a vented silicone lid with steam-release slits, and a red measuring cup that doubles as a butter melter. The whole thing is food-grade platinum silicone — no BPA, no phthalates, no weird off-gassing when heated. As shown in the product image, the bowl expands into a roughly 11-inch diameter, 3-quart capacity vessel when in use.

It's not heavy — about 12 ounces total — and the silicone is flexible enough to collapse easily but rigid enough to hold its shape when full of kernels.

## Performance: The Real Test

The Proper Popper works on a simple principle: kernels + oil + microwave + trapped steam. You add 1/4 cup kernels, 1–2 teaspoons of oil (I use coconut or avocado), pop the lid on, and microwave on high for 2–4 minutes. The lid's steam vents prevent the pressure from building to dangerous levels, and the silicone bowl handles the heat without melting.

My first batch took exactly 2 minutes 45 seconds on a 1200-watt microwave. Out of 1/4 cup kernels, I counted 6 unpopped — that's a 90%+ pop rate, which beats any bagged popcorn I've tested. The texture was crisp, not chewy, and there was zero of that artificial "butter" coating that leaves your fingers orange.

**One critical tip:** Start at 2 minutes and add 15-second increments. Every microwave is different, and burnt popcorn in this thing smells like death for days because the silicone absorbs odors. Several Amazon reviewers mentioned this exact issue — one wrote, "First batch I left it too long and the smell lingered for a week. Now I undercook slightly and it's perfect."

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs, because every product has them:

**1. Microwave turntable heat marks.** The silicone bowl sits directly on the glass plate. On high power, I noticed faint circular marks on my turntable after about 8 uses. They wiped off, but it's a real concern for rental situations or sensitive microwaves. A silicone trivet underneath helps but adds cleanup.

**2. Butter pooling.** If you add butter before popping (the included cup is designed for melting it separately), it sinks to the bottom and creates soggy, greasy kernels. You must toss the popcorn immediately after popping with melted butter drizzled in — not before.

**3. Batch size.** 3 quarts is fine for 1–2 people. For a family of four, you're running two batches. That's 5–6 minutes of total popping time, which isn't terrible, but it's not "set it and forget it."

**4. Learning curve.** The first 2–3 batches will likely involve trial and error with your microwave's wattage. Don't judge it on batch one.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can you use it without oil?**
Yes, but the pop rate drops to about 60–70%. The oil helps conduct heat evenly. Use a tiny amount (1 teaspoon) — it's worth it.

**Q: Does it fit in all microwaves?**
The expanded bowl is 11 inches wide. It fits in standard microwaves with a full-size turntable, but check your microwave's interior width first. Compact microwaves (under 1.0 cu ft) may not accommodate it.

**Q: Is it really dishwasher safe?**
Yes, the silicone is top-rack safe. But hand-washing with warm soapy water preserves the surface texture longer — eventually, the silicone gets slightly sticky after dozens of dishwasher cycles.
