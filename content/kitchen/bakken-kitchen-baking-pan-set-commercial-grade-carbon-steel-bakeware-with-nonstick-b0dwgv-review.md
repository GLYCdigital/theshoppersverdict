## Real-World Performance

Carbon steel's superpower is heat conductivity. It heats faster than glass or ceramic and distributes heat more evenly than most aluminum pans. For baking, that means your cake edges won't set before the center catches up—the most common cause of domed, cracked tops.

The nonstick claim deserves nuance. Carbon steel is *not* nonstick out of the box. After a few uses (and proper seasoning), it develops a natural patina that releases food cleanly. One reviewer noted, "After seasoning, my banana bread slid right out. No greasing needed." Another said, "First bake stuck a bit, but by the third use it was flawless." That's the honest learning curve.

Browning is where these pans shine. Carbon steel produces a deeper, more even crust than aluminum—ideal for cornbread, sourdough loaves, and frittatas. The loaf pan, in particular, gives you that golden, crispy exterior that's hard to achieve in coated pans.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I have to season these before first use?**
Yes. Wash with hot soapy water, dry thoroughly, then lightly coat with a neutral oil (grape seed or canola) and heat in the oven at 400°F for an hour. It takes 20 minutes of active time.

**Q: Can these go in the dishwasher?**
No. Carbon steel rusts. Hand-wash, dry immediately, and apply a thin oil layer after each wash.

**Q: Are they safe with metal utensils?**
Yes. Unlike nonstick, you can cut directly in these pans without damaging the surface. Just don't use a knife that will scratch the seasoned patina.
