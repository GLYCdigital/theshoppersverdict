**The Shopper's Verdict: Melitta #4 Cone Coffee Filters, Bamboo (480 Count)**

Most coffee filters are an afterthought. You grab the cheapest pack, jam it in the brewer, and hope it doesn't collapse mid-brew. The Melitta #4 Bamboo filters are different — and not just because they're green. After running through six boxes of these over the past few months, here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

This is a pack of six boxes, each holding 80 filters — 480 total. That's roughly four to five months of daily brewing for a typical household. Each filter is a #4 cone size, which fits standard size-4 pour-over drippers like the Melitta Pour-Over, Hario V60-02 (with some finagling), and most ceramic cone brewers that accept the #4 spec. They're made from unbleached bamboo fiber, which gives them that natural tan color rather than the bleached white of standard Melitta filters.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

Here's where these filters justify their price premium. Standard paper filters at this price point are flimsy — one aggressive pour and they sag, tear, or collapse into the carafe. The bamboo fiber construction is measurably sturdier. The side seams hold up under pressure, and the filters maintain their cone shape even when you pour aggressively from a gooseneck kettle. I've pushed these with a medium-fine grind and a heavy 1:15 ratio, and they never blew out.

The tradeoff? Drip rate. The bamboo fiber is denser than standard paper, which means your brew will run slower. In my testing with a standard Melitta dripper, a 350ml brew went from roughly 2:30 with paper filters to nearly 3:00 with bamboo. That extra 30 seconds isn't a dealbreaker, but if you're impatient in the morning, you'll notice it.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.8-star average across 240 ratings is earned. The most common thread in positive reviews is the combination of durability and clean taste. One reviewer noted, *"I was skeptical about bamboo filters, but these don't tear like the cheap ones and my coffee tastes cleaner — no paper flavor at all."* Another long-term user mentioned, *"I've been through three boxes now. Consistent quality, no rips, and they compost nicely."* The unbleached construction is a genuine plus — there's no chlorine taste leaching into your cup.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the negatives. First, the slower drip rate — if you're dialing in a pour-over recipe with precise timing, these will alter your extraction profile. You may need to grind slightly coarser to compensate. Second, the storage situation. The boxes are compact, but the filters inside can develop a subtle curve if you stack heavy items on top of them in a tight pantry. This doesn't affect performance, but it's a minor annoyance when you're trying to grab a clean filter.

Third — and this is worth knowing — the bamboo material has a slight natural stiffness. If you're used to paper filters that mold instantly to your dripper's ridges, these take a second to seat properly. Not a problem, just an adjustment.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Eco-conscious daily brewers** who want a genuinely unbleached, compostable filter without sacrificing strength
- **Pour-over enthusiasts** who are tired of flimsy filters that tear mid-brew
- **Anyone brewing for multiple people** — the 480-count supply means you won't re-order for months

**Skip these if** you're brewing with an automatic drip machine that has a tight filter basket (the stiffer bamboo can be finicky to seat) or if you're hypersensitive to brew time variations in your pour-over routine.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these fit the Hario V60?**
A: The #4 size is slightly wider than a standard V60-02 filter, so they'll work but won't sit perfectly flush. They're ideal for Melitta drippers and other #4 cone brewers.

**Q: Are they actually compostable?**
A: Yes, bamboo fiber is naturally compostable. Many reviewers report successful backyard composting, though the breakdown takes a few weeks longer than standard paper.

**Q: Do they affect coffee taste?**
A: No — the unbleached bamboo adds zero flavor. If anything, the denser weave does a better job filtering fine grounds, yielding a cleaner cup than standard paper.

**The Verdict**

These are the best cone filters I've tested if you value strength and clean taste over lightning-fast brew times. They're not the cheapest option on the shelf, but the durability means fewer mid-brew disasters, and the 480-count bundle makes the per-filter cost reasonable.

**Buy it if** you want a sturdier, chemical-free filter that won't tear and doesn't affect flavor.
**Skip it if** you're on a strict budget or your brewer requires a specific thin paper filter.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Half a star off for the slower drip rate and slightly awkward storage, but the build quality and clean cup make this an easy recommendation for anyone serious about pour-over coffee.
