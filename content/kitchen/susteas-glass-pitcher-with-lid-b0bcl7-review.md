## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 68-ounce (2-liter) glass pitcher, a BPA-free plastic lid with a silicone gasket, and nothing else — no straw, no brush, no extras. That's fine, because the pitcher itself is the star. The glass is thick, noticeably heavier than standard soda-lime pitchers, and the handle is molded as part of the body rather than glued on. In the product image, you'll see the sleek, tapered silhouette with a wide mouth — that opening is about 4 inches across, which makes ice cubes and fruit slices drop in easily.

## Performance and Build Quality

I tested this pitcher with boiling water straight from the kettle, then transferred it to the fridge after it cooled. No cracking, no sweating, no stress fractures. That's the borosilicate advantage — it tolerates thermal shock that would shatter lesser glass. The lid is where SUSTEAS did their homework: the silicone gasket creates a vacuum-style seal that holds liquid even when you tilt the pitcher aggressively. I shook iced tea for 10 seconds straight — zero leaks.

The pour is clean with no dribble down the side, thanks to the slightly flared spout lip. Cleaning is straightforward — the wide mouth lets you reach in with a sponge, and the glass is dishwasher-safe (top rack). The lid, however, is not. That's the one maintenance catch.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews cluster around three themes. First, durability: one buyer wrote, *"I've dropped this twice on tile — the first time I held my breath, but it bounced and survived. The second time it chipped the floor, not the glass."* Second, the seal: *"Finally a pitcher that doesn't leak in my fridge door. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. There are **no measurement markings** on the glass. If you're portioning out exactly 2 liters for a recipe, you'll need a separate measuring cup. The lid gasket, while effective, must be hand-washed — one reviewer noted theirs warped after a few dishwasher cycles, compromising the seal. Also, at 68 ounces, it's on the heavier side when full (about 5 pounds of liquid), so if you have arthritis or grip issues, this isn't your pitcher. Finally, the black lid is plastic, not glass — if you're buying this specifically for aesthetic purity, that contrast might bother you.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You make iced tea, lemonade, or infused water in bulk and need something that survives daily use. You're tired of pitchers that leak in the fridge or crack when you pour hot liquids. You want one piece that goes from stovetop to table without drama.

**Skip it if:** You need precise volume markings, you only serve cold drinks (standard glass would save you money), or you refuse to hand-wash any component. Also skip if you need something lighter — look for a plastic or Tritan pitcher instead.

## FAQ

**Is the lid truly leak-proof when tipped over?**  
In my testing, yes — the silicone gasket holds liquid even inverted. But that only works if the gasket is properly seated and undamaged. Hand-wash it and it'll stay leak-proof for years.

**Can I put this in the freezer?**  
The glass can handle cold, but freezing liquid in it risks cracking because water expands. Leave at least 2 inches of headspace if you must freeze, but honestly, refrigeration is the safer play.

**Does it fit in a standard refrigerator door?**  
Yes — it's 9.5 inches tall with a 4.5-inch base diameter. Most door shelves accommodate that easily, though the 68-ounce capacity means it'll take up a decent chunk of shelf space.
