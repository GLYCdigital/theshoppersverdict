**The CASABREWS 20 Bar: A $150 Espresso Machine That Respects You Enough to Try**

Most espresso machines under $200 are glorified coffee makers with a fancy nozzle. They hiss, they sputter, and they produce a bitter, thin shot that makes you wonder why you bothered. The CASABREWS 20 Bar is not that machine. It's not perfect — no machine at this price is — but it's the rare budget option that treats espresso like a craft rather than an afterthought.

**What You Actually Get**

Out of the box, you get the machine itself, a 51mm portafilter, two baskets (single and double), a stainless steel milk pitcher, a tamper, and a measuring scoop. The build is mostly stainless steel with a matte black plastic housing that looks surprisingly premium for the price. The water tank holds 1.4 liters and is removable from the top, which means you don't have to wrestle the machine away from the wall to refill it. A small detail, but one that matters when you're half-awake at 6 AM.

**The Real Performance Test**

The 20-bar Italian pump is the headline feature, and unlike some machines that advertise pressure they never actually deliver, this one produces genuine crema. I pulled a double shot with a medium roast from a local roaster, and the result was a thick, honey-colored crema that held together for a full minute. The shot itself was balanced — not too bitter, not too sour, with a pleasant caramel sweetness.

The steam wand is where this machine surprises. It's a single-hole wand that actually textures milk rather than just blowing bubbles into it. It takes about 45 seconds to build steam pressure, and with a little practice, you can get microfoam that's respectable for latte art. One reviewer put it simply: *"I've spent more on a single latte at a hipster café than this machine costs, and honestly, the milk texture is close to what I get there."* That's a fair assessment.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

Consistent themes emerge across the 220 Amazon ratings. First, the learning curve is forgiving. The pressurized portafilter basket means you don't need a $300 grinder to get a drinkable shot. Pre-ground coffee from a grocery store works fine. Second, the temperature stability is genuinely good for the price. Multiple reviewers noted the machine holds temperature between shots, which is rare at this level. One said: *"I've owned two cheaper machines before this. They'd overheat or underperform after the first shot. This one stays consistent."*

**Where It Falls Short**

The single boiler is the biggest limitation. You can't pull a shot and steam milk simultaneously. After pulling your shot, you wait roughly 30-60 seconds for the boiler to switch to steam mode. For a single drink, that's fine. For making four lattes for guests, it becomes tedious.

The pressurized portafilter is the other compromise. It makes dialing in easier, but it also caps the ceiling on quality. If you're using freshly roasted, specialty-grade beans, you won't get the full flavor profile extraction that a non-pressurized basket offers. This machine is best with medium-to-dark roasts; light roasts often come out underdeveloped.

The steam wand also has a quirk: the included milk pitcher is a bit small, and the wand's pivot point is limited. You'll want to angle the pitcher carefully to get proper vortex action.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is the machine for someone who wants a genuine upgrade from pod-based coffee without diving into the deep end of espresso hobbyism. It's also great for a small household — one or two drinks per session. If you're a single person who wants a proper cortado before work, this is ideal. If you're hosting brunch for eight and need espresso-drink assembly lines, look elsewhere.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use pre-ground coffee from the grocery store?**
A: Yes, and honestly, that's the intended use case. The pressurized basket compensates for inconsistent grind size. Just avoid the extra-fine "espresso" grind from supermarket brands — a regular drip grind works better here.

**Q: How long does the machine take to heat up?**
A: About 25-30 seconds from cold start. The thermocoil heating system is quick. You'll spend more time grinding and tamping than waiting for the machine.

**Q: Does it come with a warranty?**
A: CASABREWS includes a 1-year warranty, and their customer support is responsive — multiple reviewers mention getting replacement parts quickly without hassle.

**The Verdict**

The CASABREWS 20 Bar is a 4.2/5. It's not a high-end espresso machine, and it doesn't pretend to be. But it delivers genuine espresso with proper crema, a functional steam wand, and enough consistency to build a morning ritual around — all at a price that won't make you wince.

**Buy it if:** You want real espresso at home without spending $500+ on equipment, you're fine with pressurized baskets, and you make one or two drinks at a time.

**Skip it if:** You're a coffee snob with a dedicated grinder and expectations of dialing in single-origin light roasts, or you need to crank out multiple milk drinks in a row.
