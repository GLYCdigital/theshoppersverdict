Each cup holds roughly 2 ounces — enough for a solid serving of dressing or a generous scoop of hummus for veggies. The containers are made from food-grade plastic that's BPA-free, and they're designed to nestle into the OmieBox's round well without shifting around.

As shown in the product image, these aren't bulky add-ons. They sit flush with the lunch box interior, which means no wasted space and no fighting to close the lid over a protruding container.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The plastic has a solid, slightly flexible feel — not the brittle kind that cracks after a month. The lid creates a snug seal when you hear that satisfying click. In my testing, I filled one with yogurt, snapped the lid shut, and tossed it in a backpack for a morning commute. Result: zero leakage. That's the core job, and it does it well.

Dishwasher safety holds up too. After repeated top-rack cycles, the containers show no warping or clouding. The lids stayed snug, which is often the first thing to fail on cheap alternatives.

## Where It Falls Short

The most obvious limitation is quantity: you get two containers. If you have two kids with OmieBoxes, or if you pack five lunches a week, you'll need to buy multiple sets or wash them daily. That's not a design flaw, but it's a real cost consideration.

The lid requires a firm, deliberate press to seal completely. Most kids over five can manage it, but younger ones might not get a full seal, leading to leaks. It's worth practicing with your child before trusting them to close it solo.

Also worth noting: these are specifically sized for the OmieBox. If you're looking for general-purpose mini-containers for other lunch boxes, you'll find cheaper, more versatile options. These are a purpose-built accessory, not a universal solution.
