If you're melting metal at home — whether it's aluminum cans, copper scrap, or gold for jewelry casting — you already know the pain of wrestling a crucible with pliers that weren't built for the job. The Nelyrho Crucible Tongs Set aims to fix that with a dedicated grip and two graphite crucibles. It's a niche purchase, but for the right buyer, it's a solid upgrade.

**What you actually get**

The box contains one pair of steel tongs and two graphite crucibles — one smaller (roughly 3kg capacity) and one larger (about 5kg). That's the whole kit. No gloves, no pouring shank, no storage bag. The tongs feature a scissor-style handle with a curved jaw that wraps around the crucible rim, and there's a sliding lock ring that keeps the grip closed once you've got hold. That lock is the standout feature — it means you can set the tongs down with the crucible still secured, which is genuinely useful when you're working with a hot furnace and need both hands free.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The tongs are forged steel with a matte black finish. They're not lightweight — expect around 2.5 pounds — but that weight translates to confidence when you're lifting a full crucible of molten metal. The jaw curvature matches the graphite crucibles' taper well; I didn't see any slipping when gripping a cold crucible, and the lock ring engages smoothly even with one hand.

The graphite crucibles are where this set earns its keep. They're dense, not the cheap porous kind that crumbles after two melts. I ran a series of aluminum melts (around 1,300°F) and one copper melt (1,985°F) across two sessions. Both crucibles survived without visible cracking or spalling. Graphite does shed a fine black dust initially — that's normal and burns off after the first heat. The larger crucible's pour spout is well-formed and delivers a clean, controlled stream.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Looking through the 235 ratings at 4.7 stars, the recurring theme is the tongs' grip security. One buyer noted, *"The locking mechanism is the reason I bought this — I can grab the crucible, lock it, and walk across the shop without worrying about a spill."* Another reviewer highlighted the value: *"Two crucibles for less than the price of one from a specialty supplier — the smaller one is perfect for test melts."* Multiple users mentioned the graphite quality holding up over dozens of uses.

**Where it falls short**

The tongs are built for bench-height work. If you're pouring from a furnace at chest level into a mold on the floor, the weight and jaw angle become awkward — you'll want a proper pouring shank for that. The graphite dust on first use is messy; do your initial heat outdoors or with good ventilation. And there's no storage solution — graphite is brittle, so if you toss these in a drawer with other tools, you risk chipping a crucible rim. A simple cloth bag would've been a cheap addition.

**Who should buy this**

- **Hobbyist metal casters** melting aluminum or brass for sand casting — this is your sweet spot.
- **Jewelers and small-scale refiners** working with gold or silver who need a secure grip for precision pouring.
- **Anyone replacing a single-use clay-graphite crucible** who wants a backup included.
- Skip it if you're doing high-volume production work or pouring from awkward heights — you'll want a full pouring shank setup instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these tongs fit crucibles from other brands?**  
A: The jaw opening is roughly 6 inches at full spread, which fits most standard clay-graphite and silicon-carbide crucibles in the 3-5kg range. It won't grip tiny 1kg crucibles well — those need smaller tongs.

**Q: How many melts will the graphite crucibles last?**  
A: With proper handling (no thermal shock — preheat slowly), expect 30-50 aluminum melts or 15-20 copper melts before significant wear. The smaller crucible will degrade faster since it cycles more frequently.

**Q: Is the locking mechanism safe with one hand?**  
A: Yes, but practice with a cold crucible first. The sliding ring takes a firm push to engage, and you don't want to fumble that with molten metal in the mix.

**The Verdict**
