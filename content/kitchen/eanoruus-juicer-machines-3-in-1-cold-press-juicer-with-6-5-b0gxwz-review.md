**The EanOruus 3-in-1 Cold Press Juicer: A Wide-Mouth Contender That Mostly Delivers**

Most budget juicers make you choose between convenience and juice quality. The EanOruus 3-in-1 tries to give you both with a 6.5-inch feed chute that's genuinely rare at this price point. After digging through 220 ratings and the detailed user feedback, here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

You get the main juicing base, a stainless steel strainer basket, the auger, a pulp cup, a juice pitcher with a foam separator lid, and a sorbet-making attachment. The 6.5-inch chute is the headline feature — it's wide enough to drop in a whole cored apple or a halved orange, which directly addresses the biggest annoyance with masticating juicers: the endless chopping.

**Performance: The slow-squeeze payoff**

This is a true masticating juicer, meaning it crushes and presses produce at around 60-80 RPM rather than shredding it with fast blades. The tradeoff is time — juicing takes longer than with a centrifugal model — but the yield is visibly better. Multiple reviewers noted getting noticeably more juice from the same amount of produce compared to their old machines.

The 3-in-1 claim holds up. The sorbet attachment isn't a cheap add-on; buyers report it handles frozen bananas and mango chunks into a creamy, soft-serve consistency. That said, the machine does have a ceiling. If you're juicing fibrous greens like celery, you'll need to cut them into smaller pieces — the wide chute doesn't mean the auger can handle oversized fibrous stalks without struggling.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The wide feed chute is the runaway favorite feature. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical about the 6.5-inch opening, but I threw in whole apples and it just worked. Prep time is cut in half compared to my old juicer."*

Quiet operation gets repeated mentions too. At slow speeds, it runs at a conversational volume — you won't wake the house at 6 AM. And the dry pulp is another frequent positive, confirming good extraction efficiency. If you're composting or baking with pulp, you'll get a lot of it.

**Where it falls short**

The assembly process is the biggest recurring complaint. The auger and strainer basket need to align perfectly before the locking mechanism engages. Several reviewers described a learning curve of 3-4 uses before it became muscle memory. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a daily friction point.

The lack of a reverse button is a genuine oversight. When something jams — usually dense carrots or thick celery — you can't just hit reverse. You have to disassemble, clear the auger, and reassemble. That's frustrating mid-session.

Cleanup is manageable but not quick. You're washing the auger, strainer basket, and two containers by hand. The included brush helps, but it's a 5-7 minute process per use. If you're juicing daily, factor that in.

**Who should buy this**

- **The weekly juicer** who wants more yield than centrifugal offers but doesn't want to chop everything into tiny cubes first
- **The green-juice enthusiast** on a budget — this handles spinach and kale well with proper prep
- **The sorbet-curious** who wants a machine that does double duty beyond just juice

**Skip it if** you juice every single day and can't tolerate a 5-minute cleaning routine, or if you plan to juice heavy celery constantly — you'll be cutting it into small pieces anyway, which somewhat defeats the wide chute advantage.

**FAQ**

**Is the 6.5-inch chute truly wide enough for whole produce?**
Yes, for round produce like apples, pears, and oranges. For long, fibrous items like carrots or celery, you'll still cut them into 2-3 inch pieces to prevent jams.

**How loud is it compared to a centrifugal juicer?**
Significantly quieter. It's a low hum rather than a high-pitched whine. You can comfortably hold a conversation while it runs.

**Does the sorbet attachment actually work?**
Yes, but only with fully frozen fruit. Partially frozen fruit will just puree. Bananas and mangoes work best; berries can be too small and slippery.

**Buy it if** you want cold press quality with minimal prep time and don't mind a 5-minute cleanup. It's a solid value for the wide chute alone.

**Skip it if** you're a daily heavy user who needs reverse functionality and quick assembly — those small frustrations compound with frequent use.
