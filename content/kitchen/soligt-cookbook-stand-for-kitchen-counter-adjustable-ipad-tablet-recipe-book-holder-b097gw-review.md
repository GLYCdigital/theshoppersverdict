If you've ever tried to follow a recipe on your phone while your hands are covered in flour, you already know the pain. The Soligt Cookbook Stand for Kitchen Counter is designed to end that struggle — and based on 240 Amazon ratings averaging 4.8 stars, it's doing a decent job.

Here's the honest breakdown after digging into what owners actually say.

## What You Actually Get

The Soligt arrives fully assembled. No Allen wrenches, no confusing diagrams. It's a stainless steel frame with a tempered glass shelf measuring roughly 12 inches wide — big enough for an open cookbook or a tablet up to 13 inches. The base is about 9 inches deep with non-slip silicone pads on all four corners.

The adjustable mechanism uses a simple ratcheting system. You squeeze the release lever, tilt the glass shelf to one of six positions, and let go. It locks into place with a satisfying click. The whole unit weighs around 4 pounds, so it's heavy enough to stay put but light enough to move from counter to island.

## Real-World Performance

The headline feature here is versatility. The glass shelf is wide enough that you can prop an iPad against the back lip while an open cookbook sits flat in front of it. That's rare — most stands force you to choose between a book OR a tablet. Owners consistently mention this dual-use capability.

One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "I use it for my iPad while cooking and my heavy Le Creuset cookbook fits with room to spare. The glass shelf doesn't budge."

The stainless steel frame resists fingerprints and wipes clean easily — important in a kitchen where splatter happens. The silicone feet grip smooth countertops well, and the angled base design means you can still reach under the stand to grab items stored behind it.

The six tilt positions are genuinely useful. The most upright angle works for standing prep. The flatter angles let you read from a stool or while seated at a kitchen island.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The stability gets the most love. Multiple reviewers mention using it with heavy hardcover cookbooks — think 1,000-page baking bibles — without any wobble. The tempered glass shelf doesn't flex under weight.

The build quality also impresses. Owners describe the stainless steel as "solid" and "premium-feeling" compared to cheaper plastic alternatives. One buyer noted, "It feels like it'll outlast my kitchen renovation."

The adjustable angles earn consistent praise, too. Being able to switch from a nearly vertical position for standing to a flatter angle for sitting makes it genuinely useful beyond just cooking — several reviewers use it on their desk for textbooks.

## Where It Falls Short

The page-holding clips are the weak point. They're mounted on the back edge of the glass shelf, which means you have to reach around the front to adjust them. It's awkward if you need to flip pages mid-recipe. Some owners report the clips work fine for thin paperback pages but struggle with glossy magazine pages that curl.

Height is another consideration. The stand tops out around 15 inches. For taller cooks working at standard 36-inch countertops, that puts the screen slightly below eye level, forcing a slight hunch. It's not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you're 6 feet or taller.

A few reviewers also mention the glass surface can create glare under direct overhead lighting. Positioning it away from bright fixtures helps.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for home cooks who follow recipes from tablets or phones while cooking. It's also great for bakers who need hands-free access to multi-step instructions.

It's a smart pick for anyone with a deep cookbook collection — the glass shelf holds heavy hardcovers open without damaging the spine.

Skip it if you're very tall and need eye-level viewing, or if you primarily use thin, glossy magazine-style cookbooks that need aggressive page-holding.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can it hold an iPad with a thick case?**
Yes, the shelf is deep enough (about 3 inches of lip) to support a 13-inch iPad in a standard protective case. The back lip prevents sliding, and the angle is adjustable to keep it propped securely.

**Is the glass tempered or will it shatter?**
The product specifies tempered glass. Multiple reviewers mention dropping heavy cast-iron cookware near it without issue. It's rated to hold up to 15 pounds.

**Does it fold flat for storage?**
It doesn't fold completely flat. The frame remains an L-shape, but it's compact enough to slide into a cabinet or pantry. It's not a travel accessory.
