Most loaf pans are flimsy sheet metal that buckles under high heat and gives you pale, unevenly baked bread. The Northriver Casting Enamel Cast Iron Bread Pan is the opposite of that. It's a 4.5-pound slab of cast iron wrapped in a smooth, colorful enamel coating — and it changes how your bread bakes.

I've tested dozens of loaf pans over the years, and the difference here is physics. Cast iron holds heat far longer than aluminum or steel. The walls radiate heat back into the dough from every side, giving you crust development that thin pans simply cannot achieve.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The weight is a real issue.** If you have arthritis, wrist problems, or simply prefer lightweight cookware, this pan will frustrate you. Lifting it in and out of the oven requires two hands and care.

**Cleanup is high-maintenance.** Despite the enamel coating, this pan does not go in the dishwasher. You need to hand-wash with mild soap and dry thoroughly. The enamel is durable, but it can chip if you drop it or bang it against metal utensils.

**Price consideration.** This pan costs significantly more than a standard loaf pan. If you bake bread once a month and are happy with your current results, the investment might not make sense.
