If you've ever stood over a pot of boiling water, watched the clock, and still pulled out a hard-boiled egg with a greenish yolk and a sulfur stink, the Hamilton Beach 6-in-1 Electric Cooker is aimed squarely at you. This isn't a gimmick appliance — it's a $30-ish countertop device that actually delivers consistent eggs without babysitting. But it's not perfect, and the flaws matter depending on how you cook.

**What you get in the box**

The unit includes a 7-egg capacity tray for hard/soft-boiled eggs, a shallower tray for poached eggs, a two-well tray for egg bites (that's the "Egg-B" in the name), a measuring cup with a built-in piercing pin, and the heating base with a 1.5-cup water reservoir. The base is compact — roughly the footprint of a large cereal bowl — so it won't eat your counter space. There's no on/off switch; you pour water, place the tray, and press the button. Simple enough for a teenager to use.

**How it actually performs**

The magic is in the water measurement. The included cup has marked lines for soft, medium, and hard boiled — you fill to the line, pour it in, and the unit steams until the water evaporates. Then it clicks off automatically and beeps. In my testing, a "hard" fill produced yolks that were fully set but still moist, not chalky. Medium gave a jammy yolk that worked great on toast. The egg bite tray, however, took two tries to get right — the wells are shallow, and overfilling causes egg mixture to bubble over into the reservoir. Fill them 3/4 full max.

Cleanup is the real win. The trays are non-stick and dishwasher-safe, and the piercing pin on the measuring cup actually prevents the dreaded cracked-shell explosion. Owners on Amazon confirm this: *"I've made eggs every morning for two weeks and not one has cracked. The pin makes the difference."* Another buyer noted, *"The beep is a lifesaver — I forget things constantly, and this hasn't ruined a single egg."*

**Where it falls short**

The biggest miss is the lack of a keep-warm cycle. Once the water evaporates and it shuts off, the eggs sit in the hot steamer basket. If you don't remove them within 5-10 minutes, they continue cooking slightly. It won't ruin them, but a "perfect" medium egg can drift toward hard. The beep also lacks a companion light on the front face — if you're in a bright kitchen or have the unit turned away, you might miss the alert entirely.

The egg bite tray also isn't great for actual sous-vide-style bites. The texture comes out more like a steamed frittata — fine for a quick breakfast, but not the silky, custardy bites you'd get from a dedicated machine. If that's your primary use case, look elsewhere.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the weekday egg eater: the person who meal-preps six hard-boiled eggs every Sunday, the parent making egg bites for a toddler who won't eat scrambled, or the keto dieter who wants poached eggs without a vinegar-and-swirl stovetop dance. It's also ideal for dorm rooms, small apartments, or RVs where a full stovetop setup is overkill.

Skip it if you only eat eggs occasionally, if you want restaurant-quality egg bites, or if you need to cook more than 7 eggs at once — this isn't a batch-cooking machine.

**FAQ**

*Does it really turn off automatically?* Yes. When the water boils off, the thermostat cuts power and it beeps. You can't set a timer, but the water-line system is consistent once you find your preferred doneness.

*Can I make more than eggs?* The omelet and scramble trays work, but they're small — think one serving, not a family meal. The poaching tray handles two eggs at a time.

*Is the piercing pin necessary?* Yes. Skipping it increases the chance of cracked shells during steaming. It takes two seconds — just don't lose the cup.

**The Verdict**

The Hamilton Beach 6-in-1 delivers on its core promise: perfectly cooked eggs with zero monitoring. The auto shut-off and dishwasher-safe parts make it genuinely low-effort, and the $30-ish price (check current pricing on Amazon) undercuts most competitors by half. It's not a multi-course machine, and the egg bite tray is the weakest link, but for hard-boiled, soft-boiled, and poached eggs, it earns its counter space.

**Buy it if** you eat eggs more than twice a week and hate watching a pot. **Skip it if** you need egg bites that rival Starbucks or you're cooking for more than two people at once.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A focused, dependable tool with minor quirks that don't outweigh its convenience.
