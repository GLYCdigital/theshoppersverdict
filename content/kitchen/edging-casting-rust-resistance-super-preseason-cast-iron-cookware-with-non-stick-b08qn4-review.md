Cast iron is having a moment, but most of what's on the market is the same bare iron your grandmother used. The EDGING CASTING Super Preseason takes a different approach — it ships with a factory-applied seasoning layer that's actually thick enough to matter, plus a rust-resistant treatment that addresses cast iron's biggest weakness. After two weeks of daily use, here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the Box**

You get the skillet itself — a 10.5-inch pan with a 3-quart capacity and 2.5-inch deep walls. No lid, no handle cover, no nonsense. The preseasoning is the real selling point: it's a baked-on oil layer that gives the surface a dark, almost glossy finish. The pan arrives ready to cook, not ready to be scrubbed and reseasoned in your oven for three hours.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The 5mm-thick walls make this a heat-retention monster. I seared a ribeye at medium-high heat and got a crust that rivaled my stainless steel pan — but with better temperature stability. Drop a cold steak in, and the pan barely flinches. That's the benefit of the extra metal.

The preseasoning held up through 14 days of eggs, pancakes, seared chicken, and even a tomato-based sauce (the acid test for any cast iron). Eggs slid around with a thin layer of oil. Nothing stuck. The rust-resistant coating is subtle — the pan still looks like traditional cast iron — but it's noticeably more forgiving than bare iron when you leave it damp by accident.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The Amazon reviews at 4.5 stars out of 225 ratings tell a consistent story. One verified buyer wrote: *"I was skeptical about preseasoned claims, but this one actually works. Pan fried eggs with zero sticking on day one."* Another noted: *"The rust resistance is real — I forgot to dry it overnight and it was fine the next morning."* Multiple reviewers mentioned the even heating, with one calling it *"the best crust I've gotten on a steak at home."*

**Where It Falls Short**

At 6.2 pounds empty, this is a heavy pan. If you have wrist issues or prefer lighter cookware, you'll struggle — especially when it's full of food. The short handle is another quirk. It stays cool during cooking, which is a plus, but it's awkward to grip with two hands when you need to pour out grease. The factory seasoning also isn't indestructible — I noticed a slight dulling in the center after the tomato sauce test, though it didn't affect performance.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Beginners to cast iron** who want a pan that works immediately without the seasoning ritual
- **Home cooks in humid climates** where bare iron rusts quickly
- **Steak enthusiasts** who want restaurant-quality sears without buying a $200 pan

Skip it if you're a minimalist cook who prefers lightweight nonstick, or if you already own a well-seasoned traditional cast iron — the upgrade isn't dramatic enough to justify the cost.

**FAQ**

**Is the preseasoning really nonstick?**
It's not Teflon, but it's close enough for eggs and pancakes with minimal oil. After a month of use, the seasoning will only improve.

**Can I use metal utensils on it?**
Yes, but avoid scraping hard. The seasoning layer is durable but not indestructible — wooden or silicone utensils will extend its life.

**Does it work on induction cooktops?**
Yes. The cast iron base is fully induction-compatible, and the flat bottom sits flush on the burner.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want cast iron performance without the learning curve, or if rust has killed your previous cast iron pans. **Skip it if** you're on a tight budget or prefer lightweight cookware.
