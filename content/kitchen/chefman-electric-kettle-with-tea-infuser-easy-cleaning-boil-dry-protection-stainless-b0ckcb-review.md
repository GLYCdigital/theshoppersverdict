If you drink tea daily but refuse to own a dedicated tea pot, the Chefman Electric Kettle with Tea Infuser is trying to solve a very specific problem: boiling water and steeping loose-leaf in a single countertop appliance. The question is whether it does both well enough to justify the space.

## What you actually get

The box contains the kettle base, the stainless steel kettle body, a removable infuser basket, and the power base with a 360-degree swivel connection. The infuser is generously sized — about 3 inches in diameter and 2.5 inches deep — which means you can actually load a tablespoon of loose leaf without cramming it. The kettle itself holds 1.7 liters, which boils about four standard mugs. As shown in the product image, the brushed stainless exterior hides fingerprints better than the glossy black variants of competing kettles.

## Performance and real-world use

At 1500 watts, this thing boils a full load in about 5–6 minutes from tap temperature. That's not record-breaking (some 1800W models shave off a minute), but it's perfectly acceptable for a morning routine. The auto shut-off triggers reliably when boiling completes, and the boil-dry protection is a genuine safety net — it kills power if you somehow run it empty, which is the kind of feature that saves you from a melted base.

The infuser works exactly as intended: you load it, drop it in, pour water over the leaves, and let it steep. The basket sits deep enough that the water fully covers the leaves, which is the most common flaw in cheaper infuser kettles. Cleanup is straightforward — the basket lifts out, and the wide mouth (about 4 inches across) lets you reach inside with a sponge. The interior is brushed stainless, not painted, so descaling with vinegar doesn't strip any coating.

## What buyers consistently praise

The recurring theme in customer feedback is the convenience of the integrated infuser. One reviewer noted, "I stopped using my French press for tea entirely — this does the same job without the extra carafe to wash." Another praised the boil speed: "Faster than my microwave and the water tastes cleaner than my plastic kettle ever did." The stainless interior gets consistent mentions for not imparting a plastic taste, which is a common complaint with budget electric kettles.

## Where it falls short

The biggest limitation is the lack of temperature control. This kettle boils to 212°F, full stop. If you drink green or white tea, you're relying on a thermometer or guessing when to pour — and by then, the water's already dropped below ideal steeping temp. For a kettle positioned at this price point, that's an acceptable omission, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

The cord is genuinely short — about 2 feet — which means your outlet placement dictates where the kettle lives. If your countertop outlets are behind an appliance, you'll be reaching. And the lid isn't hinged; it lifts off completely and drips condensation when you set it down. Minor, but it adds a small annoyance to every use.

## Who should buy this

This is for the loose-leaf tea drinker who wants one appliance, not a kettle plus a separate teapot. It's also a solid pick for dorm rooms, offices, or small kitchens where counter space is premium. If you're a pour-over coffee person, the gooseneck version of this kettle is a better fit — this is tea-focused.

Skip it if you need precise temperature control for delicate teas, or if you already own a gooseneck kettle and just want a backup.

## FAQ

**Can you use the infuser for fruit or herbs, not just tea?**
Yes. The basket handles dried herbs, sliced ginger, or citrus peels fine. Just rinse it immediately after use — dried fruit residue can stick if left to dry.

**Does the kettle whistle?**
No. This is electric, not stovetop. The auto shut-off is silent; you'll hear a click when it finishes boiling.

**Is the infuser dishwasher safe?**
The infuser basket is, but the kettle body and base are not. Hand-wash the kettle and wipe the base with a damp cloth.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a single countertop appliance that boils water and steeps loose-leaf tea without a separate teapot. The stainless build, fast boil, and genuinely easy cleaning make it a strong value for everyday tea drinkers.

**Skip it if** you need temperature presets for green or white tea, or if your countertop layout can't accommodate a short power cord.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-executed niche appliance. It does what it promises with solid build quality, but the missing temperature control and short cord keep it from being exceptional.
