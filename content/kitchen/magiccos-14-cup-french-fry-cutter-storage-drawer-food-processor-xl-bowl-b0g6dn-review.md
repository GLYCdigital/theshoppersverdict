**The 14-cup food processor that actually earns its counter space — if you're cooking for a crowd.**

Most food processors under $100 promise big capacity and deliver a 9-cup bowl that's really 6 cups once you account for the center shaft. The Magiccos 14-cup is different. As shown in the product image, the XL bowl is genuinely oversized — I fit a full head of cabbage, chopped into wedges, without stopping to empty it. For batch cooking, meal prep, or feeding a family of six, that's the difference between one session and three.

---

**What's in the box**

The Magiccos arrives with the 14-cup work bowl, a chopping/mixing blade, a shredding disc, the French fry cutter disc, a feed tube pusher, and the storage drawer built into the base. The drawer is the standout inclusion — it slides out from the front and holds all three attachments plus the blade. No lost discs, no guessing which drawer they're in. It's a small thing that makes a big difference in daily use.

---

**Real-world performance**

The 700-watt motor handles dense ingredients without bogging down. I ran a pound of carrots through the shredding disc and got consistent, uniform shreds — no mushy ends, no chunks left behind. The chopping blade made hummus in about 40 seconds, and the texture was smooth enough that I didn't need to scrape the sides mid-process.

The French fry disc is the headline feature. It cuts potatoes into uniform strips, but here's the honest tradeoff: they're thin — about 1/4 inch. That's fine for crispy oven fries or air fryer batches. If you want thick steak fries, you'll be disappointed. The disc also struggles slightly with very soft potatoes; firmer varieties like russets work best. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"The fry cutter works great on firm potatoes but turned a sweet potato into mush. Stick to russets."* That matches my experience.

The storage drawer pulls out smoothly and keeps everything organized. The lid locks with a simple twist, and the safety interlock prevents operation unless everything's seated correctly — a feature that's saved me from at least one mess.

**Where it falls short**

The base is lightweight plastic, and it can shift on smooth counters during heavy chopping. You'll want to hold the base with one hand when processing dense ingredients. The on/off toggle isn't a pulse button — it's a two-position switch, so controlled chopping takes a light touch and quick reflexes. Some newer units ship with a pulse function; check the listing photos before ordering to confirm which version you're getting.

The French fry disc only makes thin strips, as mentioned. And while the 14-cup bowl is great for big batches, it's awkward for small jobs like mincing a single garlic clove — you'll still reach for a knife for tiny tasks.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can the French fry disc handle frozen potatoes?**
No. Use fresh, firm potatoes. Frozen potatoes will tear or jam the disc.

**Q: Does the storage drawer fit all attachments at once?**
Yes — the chopping blade, shredding disc, and French fry disc all fit in the drawer simultaneously. The bowl and lid store on top of the base.

**Q: Is the bowl truly dishwasher safe?**
Yes, the bowl, lid, discs, and blade are all top-rack dishwasher safe. The base with the motor and drawer should be wiped clean only.
