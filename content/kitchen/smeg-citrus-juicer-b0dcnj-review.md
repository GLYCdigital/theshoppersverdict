## The Retro Juicer That's More Than a Pretty Face

Let's be honest: you're considering the Smeg Citrus Juicer because it looks like it belongs in a Mad Men set. That's fair — it's genuinely one of the most striking small appliances on the market. But at this price point, you need to know if it actually juices as well as it photographs. After digging through 215 Amazon ratings and the technical specs, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the juicer base, a dual-sided cone (large for oranges and grapefruit, small for lemons and limes), a clear plastic pulp container, and the stainless steel juice spout. The die-cast aluminum body gives it real heft — this isn't a hollow plastic toy. The 31W motor is modest, but for citrus specifically, that's not the drawback it would be for other juicers.

## Performance: The Good, The Pulpy, The Honest

The standout feature is the **adjustable pulp control lever** — a small dial on the front that lets you regulate how much pulp makes it into your glass. Crank it one way for cloudy, fiber-rich OJ; the other for a smoother pour. Real buyers consistently praise this: one reviewer noted, *"I can finally make my kids' orange juice without them complaining about pulp chunks."*

The dual-sided cone is genuinely useful. Flip the large side for oranges, flip it to the small side for lemonade or lime margaritas — no part swapping mid-recipe. The self-reversing motor direction helps extract more juice per fruit half, which buyers noticed: *"I get noticeably more juice out of the same oranges compared to my old hand juicer."*

**Where it stumbles:** The 31W motor is the weak link. Thick-skinned navel oranges require firm, *slow* pressure — rush it and the cone stalls. It's not broken; it's just not a powerhouse. One frustrated buyer put it plainly: *"Works great on lemons and soft oranges. My thick-skinned California navels? I have to go slow or it stops."*

The spout height is another quirk. It's designed for standard juice glasses. Tall tumblers or mason jars won't fit underneath — you'll be pouring from the built-in measuring cup instead, which adds a step.

## Build Quality and Cleanup

This is where Smeg earns its premium reputation. The aluminum body feels solid, the cone locks in place securely, and the finish (available in pastel blue, cream, red, and black) resists fingerprints better than you'd expect. The removable parts are dishwasher-safe, and because there's no mesh filter to scrub, cleanup takes about 30 seconds. That's a genuine advantage over centrifugal juicers that require immediate disassembly and rinsing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a citrus-forward household — daily orange juice, lemon water, lime for cocktails. You value countertop aesthetics and don't mind paying a premium for design. You prefer a compact appliance that does one thing exceptionally well.

**Skip it if:** You need a high-volume juicer for thick-skinned produce, you're on a tight budget (this commands a significant premium over functional alternatives), or you want to juice anything beyond citrus. This is a specialist, not a generalist.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it handle grapefruit?**
A: Yes, but with the same caveat — the large cone accommodates grapefruit halves, but the 31W motor means you'll be pressing slowly and firmly. Expect to work for it.

**Q: Is the pulp control actually effective?**
A: Yes, it's a real mechanical lever that adjusts the gap between the cone and the strainer. It's not a gimmick — the difference between low and high pulp settings is clearly visible in the glass.

**Q: Can I juice limes and lemons without the small cone?**
A: The small cone is included and snaps onto the same base. It's necessary for small fruit — using the large cone on a lime wastes juice and makes a mess.

## The Verdict: 4.2/5

The Smeg Citrus Juicer is a case of form *and* function — mostly. It's genuinely pleasant to use daily, produces quality juice with adjustable pulp control, and looks stunning doing it. The motor's limitations on dense fruit and the short spout are real drawbacks, but for the typical citrus-juicing household, these are minor annoyances, not deal-breakers.

**Buy it if** you want a beautiful, reliable citrus juicer and accept its limits with thick-skinned fruit. **Skip it if** you need raw power or only juice occasionally — in that case, a $25 hand juicer will do the job. For everyone else, this is the rare appliance that makes you want to make breakfast.
