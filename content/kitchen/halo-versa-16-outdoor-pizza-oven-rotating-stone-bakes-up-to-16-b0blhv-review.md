# HALO Versa 16 Outdoor Pizza Oven: The Rotating Stone Actually Works — Here's the Full Story

Look, most "rotating" pizza ovens are gimmicks. A motorized turntable that jams after three uses, or a manual crank that requires you to hover over the oven like a hawk. The HALO Versa 16 is different — it uses a gravity-fed rotating stone that spins continuously without electricity or batteries. I've tested a dozen ovens in this price range, and this is the first one where the headline feature genuinely delivers.

## What's in the Box

You get the oven body, a 16-inch rotating cordierite stone (more on that in a sec), a foldable metal stand with locking legs, a propane burner assembly, and an integrated thermometer that reads up to 950°F. Missing: the propane hose and regulator. That's a $25–40 add-on at any hardware store, and it's annoying that a $400+ oven doesn't include it. Also missing: a pizza peel. You'll need to buy one separately — budget another $20.

## Real-World Performance

The rotating stone is the star. It spins at a slow, steady pace — about one full rotation every 90 seconds. That means you launch the pizza, close the door, and walk away. No babysitting, no turning, no burnt side. In my testing, this produced noticeably more even browning than my Ooni Karu 16, which requires manual rotation every 30 seconds.

Preheat time is solid: 15–20 minutes to hit 900°F on the stone with the burner on high. At that temperature, a Neapolitan-style pizza with 62% hydration dough cooks in 90 seconds flat — leopard spotting on the crust, melt-in-your-mouth texture, properly charred but not bitter. I also tested New York-style pies at 650°F; they took about 4 minutes and came out with a crisp, chewy crust that would hold its own at any slice shop.

The temperature gauge is accurate to within 25°F, which is better than most ovens in this class. One quirk: the thermometer reads the air temp near the top of the dome, not the stone surface. Expect the stone to run 50–75°F cooler than the gauge shows.

## Build Quality and Design

The stainless steel body is thick — 1.2mm gauge, not the flimsy tin you see on budget ovens. The legs lock firmly, and the whole unit feels stable even on uneven patio pavers. The rotating mechanism is a simple ceramic bearing assembly; no complex parts to fail. I've run 30+ pizzas through it over two months with zero issues.

Storage is a genuine plus. The legs fold, the chimney detaches, and the whole thing collapses to roughly 23×23×20 inches. It takes up less corner space than a Weber kettle grill.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs you need to know:

1. **The 16-inch spec is misleading.** The rotating stone's actual cooking surface is 14.5 inches. That's still big enough for a 12-inch pizza with room to spare, but don't expect to bake a true 16-inch pie.
2. **It's heavy.** 42 pounds, mostly in the stone and burner assembly. Not a dealbreaker, but if you're planning to move it from garage to patio every weekend, your back will notice.
3. **Gas-only, no wood option.** Unlike the Ooni Karu 16, you can't burn wood chunks for that smoky flavor. The gas gives you convenience and consistency, but purists will miss the wood-fired aroma.
4. **The stone needs a 10-minute warm-up between pizzas.** If you're cooking for a crowd, that adds up. The thermal mass is high (good for even cooking), but it recovers slowly.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this on a wooden table or deck?**  
No. The bottom gets hot enough to scorch wood. Use the included stand or a non-combustible surface like concrete pavers.

**Q: How long does the rotating mechanism last?**  
The ceramic bearing is sealed and requires no maintenance. HALO offers a 1-year warranty on the mechanism. Based on owner reports, it's holding up fine past 12 months.

**Q: Does the stone crack from cold water or cold dough?**  
Cordierite handles thermal shock better than standard ceramic. I've launched cold, refrigerated dough onto a 900°F stone without issues. Still, let the stone preheat for a full 20 minutes on first use.
