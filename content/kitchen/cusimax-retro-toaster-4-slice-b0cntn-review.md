Let's be honest: most four-slice toasters are bulky aluminum boxes that burn one side and under-toast the other. The CUSIMAX retro toaster tries to be different — it's got the pastel enamel looks of a 1950s diner and a dual-control system that actually makes sense. After 220 Amazon ratings holding a 4.4 average, it's worth a closer look. Here's what you're actually getting.

**What's in the box**

The toaster itself, a crumb tray, and a manual that's refreshingly brief. No accessories, no filler. The unit measures roughly 11.5 inches wide, 8 inches deep, and 7.5 inches tall — compact enough for a standard counter but takes up real estate if you're working with tight space. The retro enamel body (mint, cream, or red) is the headline feature, and it looks better in person than in photos. The chrome dials and lever have a satisfying mechanical click that cheap toasters lack.

**Real-world performance**

The standout feature is the independent dual controls. Each pair of slots has its own shade dial and carriage lever. That means you can toast two slices dark for yourself while keeping two slices light for a picky eater — or just use the right side for a single slice without wasting energy heating all four slots. This is a genuine usability win that most toasters in this price range skip.

The extra-wide slots accept thick artisan bread, bagels, and even Texas toast without scraping the sides. The self-centering guides keep slices upright, which sounds basic but is surprisingly rare at this price point. Browning is reasonably even across the surface, though the outer elements run slightly hotter than the center — a minor quibble most people won't notice unless they're toasting a single slice in the middle.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The aesthetic is the hook, but the function holds up. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I bought this for the looks but kept it for the even toasting. My old toaster burned everything on setting 3; this one nails it at 3.5 every time."* Another noted the quiet operation: *"It doesn't sound like a lawnmower starting up. The lever drops smooth and it pops gently."* Multiple reviewers mention the dual controls as the deciding factor over cheaper four-slice models.

**Where it falls short**

The bagel function is the weak point. The toaster only heats the inner elements when set to bagel mode, so the cut side gets toasted while the outer crust side stays soft — but the exposed side of a split bagel comes out noticeably lighter than the side facing the element. If you like aggressively crispy bagels, this won't satisfy you.

The crumb tray is my other gripe. It slides out from the bottom, but it sits flush against the base with no lip to grab. You'll need a butter knife or a small brush to pry it free, and crumbs inevitably scatter across your counter in the process. It's a small annoyance that becomes a weekly ritual.

The 28-inch cord is genuinely short. If your outlet is behind the toaster, you'll need a power strip or an extension cord — or you'll be rearranging your counter layout.

**Who should buy this**

This toaster is for someone who wants appliance aesthetics without sacrificing function. It's ideal for a kitchen with a retro or mid-century design scheme, for households where different people prefer different toast levels, and for anyone who regularly toasts thick artisan bread. It's also a solid gift option — the enamel finish and chrome accents photograph well and look expensive.

Skip it if you're a bagel purist who needs aggressive single-side toasting, if your counter space is at a premium, or if you want a toaster that's easy to clean without fiddling.

**FAQ**

**Does the CUSIMAX toaster handle frozen waffles and toaster pastries?** Yes, the frozen setting adds time and reduces heat slightly. Waffles come out crispy without burning, though you'll want to watch the first cycle to dial in your preferred shade.

**Can you toast just one slice without wasting energy?** Absolutely. The independent controls let you lower just the left or right pair of slots. For a single slice, use one slot in either pair — the self-centering guide handles it fine.

**How loud is the pop-up?** It's a soft, cushioned pop — noticeably quieter than the aggressive spring-loaded snap on most budget toasters. It won't startle the dog or wake a sleeping baby.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a four-slice toaster that looks like a design piece, toasts evenly, and offers real dual-control flexibility. The wide slots, quiet operation, and enamel finish justify the premium over basic stainless models.

**Skip it if** you need aggressive bagel toasting, have limited counter space, or want a toaster with a longer cord and easier crumb access.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good-looking toaster that performs above its price class, held back by a mediocre bagel function and a finicky crumb tray. If those two issues don't matter to you, this is one of the best retro toasters on Amazon right now.
