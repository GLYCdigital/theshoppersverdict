# Victoria Cast Iron Griddle Review: Is the Double Burner Workhorse Worth Your Kitchen Space?

Cast iron griddles are having a moment, and for good reason. But most of the ones flooding Amazon are thin, warped, or too small to feed more than two people. The Victoria Cast Iron Double Burner Griddle aims to fix that with a genuinely massive cooking surface and a reversible design. After digging through 220 ratings and the specs, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains one pre-seasoned cast iron griddle measuring 21.5 inches long by 10.5 inches wide. That's the full span of a standard gas or electric stove's two front burners. The flat side handles pancakes, eggs, and grilled cheese; flip it over and you get raised ridges for steaks, chicken, and vegetables with those classic grill marks.

The seasoning is flaxseed oil, which Victoria applies at the factory. It's not the thickest seasoning you'll ever see — one reviewer noted, "It arrived well seasoned but I still added 2-3 coats of my own before first use to be safe." That's smart advice, not a dealbreaker.

## How It Performs in Real Kitchens

The flat side distributes heat evenly across the entire surface. No hot spots near the center burner, no cold corners. One Amazon reviewer summed it up: "I've cooked 10 pancakes at once with zero sticking. My old nonstick could never."

The ribbed side does what it's supposed to — those ridges sear meat properly and keep it elevated from its own grease. But here's the honest catch: the grease channel is shallow. If you're cooking a pound of bacon, fat will pool and smoke. You'll want a splatter guard and moderate heat, not full blast.

Cleanup is what you'd expect from cast iron: hand wash, dry immediately, thin oil coat. No dishwasher, no soap soaking. If you're not already a cast iron person, this is a lifestyle adjustment, not a set-and-forget pan.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The size is the headline feature. Multiple reviewers mention cooking for families of four to six without batch-cooking. One wrote: "Finally a griddle that fits my 30-inch stove properly. The handles don't hang over the edge."

Build quality gets consistent praise too. This is thick, heavy cast iron — 12.5 pounds of it — that won't warp on high heat. That's 5 pounds heavier than many competitors, and it shows in heat retention.

## Where It Falls Short

The weight is real. At 12.5 pounds, it's awkward to lift, especially since there's no helper handle on the short ends. A few reviewers mentioned nearly dropping it while carrying it to the sink. You'll want to store it flat in a dedicated drawer or on a lower shelf.

The seasoning can also be finicky out of the box. Some units arrive with minor seasoning inconsistencies — nothing a quick maintenance coat won't fix, but don't expect perfection at this price point.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You cook breakfast for a crowd, love cast iron's durability, or want one pan that covers two burners for searing, grilling, and griddling. It's ideal for gas stoves, though it works on electric and induction too.

**Skip it if:** You're short on storage, prefer lightweight nonstick, or only cook for one or two people. A smaller 10-inch skillet would serve you better.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Does it fit my stove?**
Measure your burner span first. The griddle is 21.5 inches long with handles included. It fits standard 30-inch stoves, but you'll need roughly 22 inches of clear space. The handles themselves are modest, so overhang is minimal on most ranges.

**Can I use it on an induction cooktop?**
Yes. Cast iron is fully induction-compatible, and Victoria explicitly states this model works on induction, gas, electric, and even campfire cooking. Just be careful — the griddle gets hot fast on induction, so start with low heat.

**Is it really pre-seasoned enough to use immediately?**
Yes, but with a caveat. You can cook eggs on it day one with enough butter or oil. However, the factory flaxseed seasoning is relatively thin. Most experienced users recommend doing one or two maintenance coats in your oven before the first serious use.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Victoria Cast Iron Double Burner Griddle is a legitimate kitchen workhorse if you have the storage space and arm strength. It outperforms cheaper griddles in heat distribution, build quality, and cooking capacity. The shallow grease channel and heavy weight are real drawbacks, but they're tradeoffs, not dealbreakers.

**Buy it if** you're upgrading from a cramped skillet and want one piece of cookware that handles everything from Sunday pancakes to weekday steak dinners. **Skip it if** you're a casual cook who can't commit to cast iron maintenance — this isn't the pan that'll convert you.

For the price and the 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, this griddle earns its place on your stovetop. Just clear a shelf before it arrives.
