## What You Actually Get

The CURTA is a full-size commercial sheet pan (18" x 26" exterior, 17.75" x 25.75" interior — the industry-standard size that fits most commercial ovens). It's made from 14-gauge aluminum, which is the same thickness used in professional kitchens. The rolled edges aren't just for looks; they prevent the pan from bending when you grip it with oven mitts, and they create a rigid frame that resists the twisting forces that destroy thinner pans.

The NSF certification matters more than most shoppers realize. It means the aluminum alloy meets strict food-contact safety standards — no reactive metals leaching into acidic batters, no questionable coatings. The pan comes bare, with no seasoning or non-stick layer. That's intentional. Commercial kitchens don't use coated pans because coatings fail under high heat and heavy scrubbing.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this pan separates itself from the competition. Because aluminum conducts heat roughly 10 times faster than steel, the CURTA eliminates hot spots. Your cookie sheets bake evenly edge-to-edge, with no burnt corners and raw centers. The flat, untextured bottom means cakes release cleanly when properly greased — one reviewer noted, "I've baked over 200 sheet cakes on these in my home kitchen, and they still look brand new."

The weight is the tradeoff. At about 3 pounds, this is not a pan you'll casually toss in a cabinet. But that mass is precisely why it doesn't warp. Thin pans buckle because they can't distribute heat stress; this one absorbs it. Users consistently praise how it handles high temperatures: "These have been through my oven at 500°F for pizza multiple times. Dead flat, no warping, no discoloration."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: if you're looking for a non-stick pan, this isn't it. The bare aluminum surface requires greasing, parchment paper, or a silicone mat for delicate items like madeleines or macarons. One reviewer mentioned this as a drawback: "I forgot to line it for a sheet cake and had to scrape. My fault, but worth knowing."

The size is also a consideration. A full sheet pan doesn't fit standard home ovens — you'll need at least an 18" deep oven to accommodate it. If you have a typical 30" home range, you'll want the quarter-size or half-size versions instead. And the bare aluminum will develop a patina over time; that's normal oxidation, not damage, but some buyers mistake it for rust.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this in a home oven?**
A: Only if your oven is at least 18 inches deep. Measure before you buy. If your oven is smaller, look for the half-size (13" x 18") or quarter-size (9" x 13") versions.

**Q: Does it need seasoning like cast iron?**
A: No. Bare aluminum doesn't require seasoning. Just wash with soap and water before first use. Some bakers rub a light coat of oil on it after cleaning to maintain the surface, but it's optional.

**Q: Is the aluminum safe for acidic foods like tomato-based dishes?**
A: Aluminum can react with highly acidic foods, which may impart a metallic taste. For tomato-heavy recipes, line the pan with parchment or use a different pan.
