## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where acacia earns its reputation. It's one of the harder domestic hardwoods available — significantly denser than bamboo or rubberwood. That density translates to two things: excellent knife edge retention (your blades stay sharper longer) and real durability against deep gouges.

The board stays flat. I've seen cheaper boards warp within weeks, especially if you occasionally let them sit in water. The thick construction here resists that tendency, though I'd still recommend not soaking it.

One thing that surprised me: the board handles heavy use without developing that "rough patch" feeling that cheaper wood boards get after a month of chopping. The surface stays smooth, which means less friction when you're mincing garlic or dicing herbs.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires me to flag the tradeoffs. The board is heavy. If you have wrist issues or prefer a lightweight board you can flip around easily, this will be a workout. It's a feature of the thick construction that gives it durability, but that doesn't help you if you're struggling to lift it.

The lighter acacia color shows wear more visibly than dark walnut. Citrus juice can leave temporary light spots, and you'll see knife marks accumulate over time. This is cosmetic — the board performs fine — but if you want a board that hides its battle scars, look to darker woods.

Finally, you need to commit to oiling every 3-4 weeks. This isn't optional maintenance; skipping it will eventually lead to drying and cracking. Budget for a bottle of food-grade mineral oil.

## Who Should Buy This

This board is ideal for home cooks who cook daily and want a board that will genuinely last years. It's also great for anyone who's been annoyed by boards that warp, splinter, or absorb odors. The handle makes it a smart pick for grill cooks who need to transport food outside.

Skip it if you want a lightweight board for quick tasks, if you're not willing to do periodic oiling, or if you prefer the darker aesthetic of walnut.

## FAQ

**Does this board dull knives faster than plastic?**
No — it's actually gentler on edges than plastic or glass. The wood fibers give slightly under the blade, which preserves sharpness. You'll sharpen less frequently than with a plastic board.

**How do I clean it?**
Hand-wash with warm water and mild soap immediately after use. Dry standing on its side. Never put it in the dishwasher — the heat and moisture will eventually split the wood.

**Is it safe for cutting raw meat?**
Yes, with one caveat: acacia is naturally antimicrobial and food-safe, but you should still wash it thoroughly after raw meat contact. The wood will absorb some juices, which is why the oiling routine matters for hygiene.
