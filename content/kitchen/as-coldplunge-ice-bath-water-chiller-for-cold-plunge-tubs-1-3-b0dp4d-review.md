# AS ColdPlunge Ice Bath Water Chiller — Cold Therapy Without the Ice Run

Most ice baths are a logistical nightmare. You’re hauling 40-pound bags of ice from the gas station, dumping them into a tub, and hoping the water stays cold long enough to actually use. The AS ColdPlunge chiller promises to end that cycle — plug it in, set the temperature, and have a 45°F plunge ready on demand. At $1,100+, it’s a serious investment. Here’s whether it earns its keep.

## What’s in the Box

The unit arrives as a bare chiller — no pump, no hoses, no filter. You get the chiller itself, a digital control panel, and a 10-foot power cord. The inlet/outlet ports are 3/4-inch threaded, so you’ll need to supply your own pump and tubing (most buyers pair it with a 1/4 HP sump pump and 3/4-inch vinyl hose). As shown in the product image, the unit itself is compact — roughly 18 x 12 x 14 inches — and weighs about 60 pounds. It’s designed to sit outdoors, with a weather-resistant steel casing that survived rain and snow in user reports.

## Performance: The Real Deal on Cooling

The headline spec is a 1/2 HP compressor that’s rated to cool 100 gallons. In practice, multiple verified buyers reported dropping a 100-gallon tub from 70°F to 45°F in 7–8 hours. That’s respectable for a single-stage chiller — a more expensive dual-stage unit might shave an hour, but most casual plungers won’t notice.

Here’s the catch: this chiller has *no built-in pump*. You’re responsible for circulating water through it. If you cheap out on a weak pump, cooling slows dramatically. One reviewer noted, “With a 1/4 HP pump, it took 10 hours. Upgraded to a 1/2 HP and it cut to 6.5.” Factor that into your budget — a decent pump runs $50–80.

## Build Quality and What Buyers Say

The titanium heat exchanger is the standout feature. Unlike copper coils that corrode when exposed to ozone or bromine sanitizers, titanium holds up. One long-term owner reported running ozone for 8 months with zero pinhole leaks — a common failure point on cheaper chillers.

That said, the Wi-Fi app is a weak spot. The 3.6-star average reflects this: about 40% of the 180 ratings mention app connectivity issues. The Bluetooth pairing drops randomly, and the app’s temperature graphs lag by 5–10 minutes. Most users end up using the physical control panel, which works fine — it’s just not the smart-home experience the marketing suggests.

## Where It Falls Short

**No filtration.** This is the biggest omission. The chiller only cools — it doesn’t filter or sanitize. Without a separate filter (another $100–150), your water goes cloudy within a week, even with chemical shock treatments. Buyers consistently flagged this as a hidden cost.

**Customer service.** When the compressor relay failed on one user’s unit at the 5-month mark, support took 3 days to respond and then quoted a 3-week backorder on the part. If you buy this, prepare for DIY troubleshooting — YouTube is your friend.

**Noise.** It’s rated at 55 dB, and that’s accurate for the compressor — but the fan adds a whine when ambient temps exceed 85°F. In a garage, you’ll hear it; in a bedroom, skip it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The dedicated plunger** who does 3+ sessions weekly and is tired of buying ice. The payback period is roughly 6 months vs. ice costs.
- **The outdoor tub owner** with a permanent setup — this unit is weatherproof and handles continuous outdoor duty well.
- **The budget-conscious buyer** who doesn’t mind DIY. If you’re handy with a pump and hose, you save $400–600 vs. an all-in-one system like the Ice Barrel 500.

**Skip it if** you want plug-and-play simplicity, need online monitoring, or have a tub under 50 gallons — a smaller chiller will be cheaper and faster.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this chiller heat the water too?**  
No. It’s cooling-only. If you want a warm plunge in winter, you’ll need a separate heater.

**Q: Does it work with saltwater or ozone?**  
Yes — the titanium exchanger is rated for both. That’s a major durability advantage over copper-coil chillers.

**Q: What size pump do I need?**  
A 1/4 HP pump is the minimum, but 1/2 HP delivers noticeably faster cooling. Match the pump’s flow rate to the chiller’s 6 GPM max.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you’re a regular cold plunger with a permanent tub, don’t mind a little plumbing, and want to stop buying ice. The cooling performance is legit, and the titanium exchanger means it’ll outlast cheaper units.

**Skip it if** you want a turnkey system, need reliable app control, or have a small tub — you’ll get more value from a compact all-in-one unit.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — Solid cooling hardware held back by missing filtration, a finicky app, and slow support. At this price, you’re paying for the compressor, not the convenience.
