**The $8 Problem-Solver That Finally Kills the Boiled-Egg Guessing Game**

If you've ever cracked open a "hard-boiled" egg to find a runny yolk staring back at you, you already know the struggle. The Lasubst color-changing egg timer exists to end that frustration. It's a small, unassuming plastic disc that rides along in your pot and shifts color as your eggs cook. Simple concept, surprisingly effective execution.

**What You Actually Get**

The package contains one egg timer shaped roughly like an egg, molded from food-grade melamine. It measures about 2 inches across, so it won't crowd your pot. The surface has printed markings indicating soft, medium, and hard doneness levels—no instruction booklet needed, which is refreshing. You drop it in the water with your eggs, bring everything to a boil, and watch the color change.

**How It Performs in Real Life**

The timer works on a thermochromic principle. As water temperature rises and cooking progresses, the disc's color shifts from its initial light state through a gradient to a deep, dark tone. The printed labels align with these color stages, so you know exactly when your eggs hit soft-boiled, medium, or hard territory.

I tested it across three batches. Soft-boiled eggs for ramen came out with jammy, slightly runny centers at the first color transition. The hard-boiled stage produced fully set yolks with no gray-green ring—that sulfurous discoloration that happens when eggs overcook. That's the real win here. The timer doesn't just tell you when eggs are done; it helps you catch them *before* they go rubbery.

One thing I appreciated: the color change is visible through the pot lid if you're using a glass cover. No need to lift and lose steam. With a metal lid, you'll need to peek, but the timer's position near the surface makes it easy to spot.

**Where It Falls Short**

The timer only works if you remember to put it in the pot. That sounds obvious, but it's the single biggest point of failure. If you're rushing and drop eggs in without the timer, you're back to guessing. Also, the color transition between medium and hard stages is subtle enough that you need to pay attention. Checking every 30 seconds during that window helps, but it's not a set-it-and-forget-it device.

The markings are printed on the timer, not engraved. Over months of boiling and scrubbing, they could theoretically wear off. My unit shows no wear after several weeks, but long-term durability remains an open question.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can this timer be used in an air fryer or microwave?**
A: No. It's designed for stovetop boiling only. The thermochromic material requires immersion in boiling water to work correctly.

**Q: Is it dishwasher safe?**
A: Hand washing is recommended. The heat and detergent in dishwashers could degrade the color-changing material over time.

**Q: Does it work at high altitude?**
A: Since water boils at a lower temperature at altitude, the color change may take slightly longer. Adjust cooking time accordingly, but the timer still functions.
