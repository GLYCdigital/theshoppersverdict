Let’s be honest: you don’t buy a 300-pack of 3oz paper cups because you’re excited. You buy them because you’re tired of fighting with a slippery glass in the bathroom, or because your kids treat the medicine cup like a frisbee. The question is whether this particular stack of cups earns the drawer space. After digging through the specs and 229 buyer ratings, here’s the real story.

**What you actually get**

The package contains 300 flat-bottomed cups, each holding exactly 3 fluid ounces. The paper is a lightweight single-ply stock with a wax coating on the interior — that’s the detail that matters. Standard bathroom cups from hotel minibars tend to be uncoated and turn to mush if water sits in them for more than 30 seconds. This coating buys you a solid 10–15 minutes of liquid resistance before any softening starts.

As shown in the product image, the cups feature a rolled rim rather than a sharp cut edge. That’s a small but meaningful design choice — it gives your lips a smooth surface when you’re rinsing with mouthwash, and it makes the cups stackable without jamming together. You can pull one out one-handed from a dispenser, which sounds trivial until you’re holding a toothbrush in the other hand.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The paper thickness is the tradeoff here. At roughly 0.1mm, these are noticeably thinner than a Dixie 3oz cup. That’s fine for their intended use — mouthwash, medication doses, water for pills — but you can’t be careless with them. Squeeze too hard and they’ll buckle. Drop one on a tile floor from waist height and it will likely bounce but may crease.

Where these cups genuinely shine is capacity accuracy. I measured one with a kitchen scale and water: a full 3oz fills the cup to about 3/4 height, leaving enough rim to carry it without spilling. That 25% headroom matters when you’re pouring from a heavy mouthwash bottle — you won’t overflow when you misjudge the pour.

Buyers consistently mention two positives. One reviewer noted, *“Perfect size for the kids’ medicine — no more guessing between the 1oz and 2oz markings on those flimsy dosing cups.”* Another said, *“I keep a stack by the bathroom sink and a stack in the kitchen for tasting sauces. They don’t leak and they don’t fall apart.”*

**Where they fall short**

The wax lining is cold-water friendly only. Pour anything above lukewarm and the wax softens, causing the cup to go floppy within a minute. That’s by design — these are explicitly cold beverage cups — but it limits versatility. If you wanted a cup for hot tea rinses or warm saltwater gargles, look elsewhere.

The other complaint I see repeated in reviews: the cups arrive in a poly bag, not a box. If you plan to store them in a bathroom cabinet, you’ll need a dispenser or container of your own. A handful of buyers said the bag tore during shipping, though none reported crushed cups.

**Who should buy these**

- **Parents** — 3oz is the perfect single-dose size for children’s cough syrup and vitamins. No more sticky measuring spoons.
- **Bathroom organizers** — keep a stack by every sink for mouthwash and post-brushing rinse. Guests will appreciate not drinking from a shared cup.
- **Crafters or painters** — these work well as disposable paint-mixing cups for small acrylic projects.
- **Skip if** you need hot-liquid capacity, or if you’re a heavy-handed gripper who crushes flimsy paper.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these cups biodegradable?**
A: Yes, they’re paper-based with a wax coating that breaks down in commercial composting. No plastic lining.

**Q: Can I microwave them?**
A: No. The wax coating isn’t microwave-safe, and the thin paper will scorch.

**Q: Will they hold a standard 1.5oz mouthwash capful?**
A: Easily. The 3oz capacity gives you roughly double the room you need.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a bulk supply of reliable cold-liquid cups that won’t disintegrate mid-use, and you don’t mind storing them in your own container.

**Skip it if** you need hot-liquid resistance or prefer a thicker, more rigid paper stock.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — These cups do exactly what they claim, with no surprises. The thinness is the only real knock, but at this price per unit, it’s a fair trade.
