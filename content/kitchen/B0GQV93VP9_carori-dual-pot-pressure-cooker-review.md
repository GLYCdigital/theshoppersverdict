Two pots, one lid, and a whole lot of dinner potential. The Carori Dual Pot Electric Pressure Cooker 9-in-1 is trying to solve the oldest weeknight problem: the protein and the sides never finish at the same time. At $89.99, it's priced like a single-pot pressure cooker but gives you twice the cooking surface. Here's what 223 Amazon reviewers and my own research say about whether it actually delivers.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main unit with two 6-quart stainless steel pots, one pressure lid, one glass lid, a steam rack, rice paddle, soup ladle, and measuring cup. Each pot is independently controlled via the central digital panel — you set different temperatures, times, and functions for each side. The 9-in-1 functions break down to pressure cook, slow cook, rice, steam, sauté, yogurt, warm, and two preset modes.

The stainless steel pots are triple-layer construction and feel substantial. They're dishwasher safe, which reviewers consistently mention as a win. The unit itself measures about 15 inches wide and 12 inches tall — this is not a compact appliance.

## Real-World Performance

The dual-pot setup genuinely works for its intended purpose. Multiple reviewers describe the classic scenario: meat in one pot, rice and vegetables in the other, both finishing at the same time. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "I made a full pot roast with potatoes and carrots in one side and rice in the other. Everything was hot and ready together. This replaced my Instant Pot and rice cooker."

Pressure cooking performance is solid. Both pots reach pressure in roughly 10-12 minutes based on reviewer reports, which is comparable to single-pot units. The sauté function gets hot enough to brown meat properly before pressure cooking — a step many budget dual cookers skip.

The 9-in-1 functionality isn't just marketing. Owners report using the yogurt function successfully, the slow cooker works as expected, and the steam function is reliable. The digital controls are straightforward: dial to select the pot, then choose the function and time.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is time savings. "This is the only way I can get dinner on the table by 6 PM after work," one reviewer noted. Others highlight the build quality at this price point — the stainless steel pots feel premium, and the unit runs quieter than expected during pressure release.

The value proposition is strong. You're getting the functionality of a pressure cooker, slow cooker, rice cooker, and sauté pan for less than the cost of a single high-end Instant Pot.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest limitation is that you cannot pressure cook in both pots simultaneously. Only one pot can be under pressure at a time; the other must use non-pressure functions. This means you can't cook two separate pressure-dependent dishes at once. For a dual cooker, that's a meaningful constraint.

The second issue is footprint and weight. This thing is heavy — around 18 pounds with pots in place. It's not something you'll stash in a cabinet between uses. You need dedicated counter space, which is a dealbreaker for small kitchens.

Some reviewers also mention the control panel isn't backlit, making it hard to read in dim kitchen lighting. Minor, but worth noting.

## Who Should Buy This

The Carori Dual Pot is ideal for busy families cooking for 4-6 people who want a complete meal in one appliance. It's also great for meal preppers who can cook a protein in one pot and grains or vegetables in the other for the week ahead. If you're short on counter space or rarely cook full meals, skip it.

## FAQs

**Can both pots cook at the same time with different settings?**
Yes, both pots can run simultaneously with independent settings. The catch is only one can use pressure cooking at a time — the other must run a non-pressure function like sauté, slow cook, or steam.

**Is the lid safe? One lid for two pots seems risky.**
The pressure lid seals onto whichever pot you're using, and it latches securely. Reviewers report no steam leaks or pressure release issues. It's a well-engineered design, though you can't cover both pots simultaneously with the pressure lid.

**Does it replace a rice cooker?**
Yes, for everyday rice needs. Reviewers report consistent results with white rice, brown rice, and quinoa. It won't match a dedicated fuzzy-logic rice cooker for perfect sushi rice, but for standard meals it's more than adequate.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want to cut weeknight dinner time in half, cook complete meals in one appliance, and have the counter space to leave it out permanently. The dual-pot design genuinely delivers on its promise, and at $89.99 it undercuts buying separate appliances.

**Skip it if** your kitchen is tight on space, you only cook for 1-2 people, or you need to pressure cook multiple dishes simultaneously.

**Rating: 4.5/5** — A genuinely useful dual-pot cooker that earns its counter space for busy households, with minor limitations that don't undermine its core value.
