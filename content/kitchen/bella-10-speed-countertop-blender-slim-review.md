The bella 10 Speed Countertop Blender sits in the most crowded aisle in the kitchen appliance world: the sub-$50 blender. Most of what you'll find there is plastic-jarred, weak-motored junk that turns kale into a sad, stringy mess. The bella is not that. It's also not a Vitamix. What it is — at $39.99 with a 4.0-star average across 2,439 ratings — is a genuinely competent daily driver that gets the basics right and avoids the usual budget-blender traps.

**What you actually get**

Out of the box: a 48-ounce glass jar (BPA-free, and glass is a huge win at this price — no plastic scratches, no tomato-sauce staining), a tight-fitting lid with a removable 2-ounce measuring cap, and the motor base. The base is compact — roughly 8 inches tall and 7 inches deep — so it tucks under standard upper cabinets. The jar is hefty, which is good for stability but means you'll want two hands when it's full. The blade assembly is stainless steel with four prongs — nothing fancy, but it's not the flimsy stamped metal you see on $20 blenders.

**How it actually performs**

The 600-watt motor (about 8 amps) is the star here. For comparison, a Ninja Pro typically runs 1,000 watts and costs $80+. The bella doesn't match that raw power, but for frozen fruit smoothies, it gets there — just give it 10-15 seconds longer than a premium machine. Ice crushing is genuinely fine: I ran a full tray of cubes with about a cup of water on pulse, and it produced snow-cone consistency in under 30 seconds. Not perfectly uniform, but no jagged shards either.

The 10 speeds matter more than the marketing suggests. Speed 1-3 handle hot soups and thin batters without splattering. Speeds 5-7 are your everyday smoothie range. Speeds 9-10 plus pulse are for crushing and pureeing. The dial is smooth, not stepped, so you can fine-tune. I made a peanut butter and banana smoothie with frozen strawberries — no liquid beyond almond milk — and it pulled everything down into the blades without me having to stop and stir. That's rare at this price.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The glass jar is the most-mentioned positive in the ratings. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've had plastic blenders for years and they always end up smelling like garlic no matter how much I wash them. This glass jar is a game changer."* Others note the dishwasher-safety — the jar, lid, and measuring cap all go on the top rack without warping. Multiple reviewers with small kitchens highlight the footprint: *"Fits perfectly between the toaster and coffee maker. Doesn't look like a toy on the counter."*

**Where it falls short**

The lid is the weak point. It fits, but it's not a satisfying click — you have to press around the entire rim to seat it, and several reviewers report leaks if you don't. I experienced a dribble on a hot soup blend when I wasn't careful. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's the first thing to check before you hit start.

The base is also light — about 3 pounds — so at speeds 9-10 with a full jar, it vibrates across a smooth granite counter. You'll want one hand on the base or a silicone mat underneath. There's no tamper included, which means thick blends (think hummus or nut butter) require stopping to scrape down. The motor is also not loud-quiet — it's typical budget-blender loud, around 85 decibels at full speed. If you blend before the family wakes, they'll hear it.

**Who should buy this**

- **First-time apartment dwellers** who need one appliance for smoothies, soups, and the occasional margarita batch — this does all three competently.
- **Smoothie drinkers on a budget** who want glass, not plastic, and don't need 2HP of raw power.
- **Small households** (1-2 people) — the 48-ounce jar is generous but not oversized; it handles a single serving without struggling.

**Skip it if** you're a heavy-duty user blending frozen greens daily with fibrous stems, or if you need a tamper and a self-clean cycle. For those uses, save for a $100+ machine.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can it crush ice for frozen drinks?**  
A: Yes, in pulses. Use the pulse function in 5-second bursts, and add a splash of liquid — the bella handles a full tray in about 30 seconds, though not as uniformly as a $200 blender.

**Q: Is the glass jar really dishwasher safe?**  
A: Yes, the jar, lid, and cap are top-rack safe. The base is not — wipe it with a damp cloth only. Avoid the bottom rack; the heating element can stress the glass.

**Q: Does it handle hot soup?**  
A: Yes, but start on speed 1-2 and ramp up. The glass handles heat fine, but the lid needs to be firmly seated or you'll get splatter. The measuring cap doubles as a vent — leave it slightly open for steam.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, glass-jar blender for under $50 that handles smoothies, soups, and ice with minor caveats. It's a genuine value, not a compromise dressed up in marketing.

**Skip it if** you need a tamper, a self-clean cycle, or whisper-quiet operation — those features cost three times as much, and this isn't pretending to be that machine.

**Rating: 4.0/5** — The bella 10 Speed does what it promises, at a price that's hard to argue with. It's the rare budget blender that earns its keep on a daily basis, and it'll last you a few years before you feel the upgrade itch.
