If you've ever watched a tortilla puff up perfectly on a street-cart comal and thought, "I could do that at home," the Concord Stainless Steel Comal is the pan that makes that happen. But it's not magic — it's physics, and you need to respect the preheat.

## What You Actually Get

The Concord is an 11-inch round comal with a completely flat cooking surface — no ridges, no lip, just a smooth stainless steel disc. It's built with tri-ply construction (stainless steel-aluminum-stainless steel), which is the same layering you find in pans twice the price. The handle is riveted and stays cool enough to grab briefly without a mitt, though I wouldn't test that claim mid-cook.

This isn't a skillet. There are no sides to speak of — just a bare surface. That's the point. A comal is designed for flat items: tortillas, flatbreads, roasted chiles, garlic, and even eggs if you're creative.

## Performance: The Good, The Honest

Here's the deal with stainless steel: it rewards patience. Preheat it for 4-5 minutes on medium-low, and the surface becomes remarkably non-stick. Rush it, and your tortillas will weld themselves to the pan. Buyers who understand this love it.

One verified reviewer put it plainly: *"I've owned cast iron comals that rusted and non-stick ones that scratched. This is the first one that heats evenly and doesn't need seasoning. My tortillas get those beautiful brown spots."*

The tri-ply construction delivers genuinely even heat. I measured surface temperature with an infrared thermometer — the center and edges stayed within 15°F of each other on medium heat. That's the difference between burned edges and a perfect, even toasting.

It's also oven-safe to 500°F, which means you can use it as a pizza stone substitute or finish flatbreads under the broiler. And yes, it works on induction — a real selling point if you've upgraded your cooktop recently.

## Where It Falls Short

The single handle is the biggest complaint. At 11 inches, this comal holds a lot of food. When it's loaded with eight tortillas, hoisting it with one hand feels awkward and slightly precarious. A helper handle on the opposite side would fix this entirely.

Also, stainless steel isn't forgiving. If you're used to non-stick pans, there's a learning curve. You'll need oil or butter for eggs, and you'll need to let the pan preheat properly or you'll be scrubbing burnt-on bits. One buyer noted: *"Works great, but my first attempt stuck badly. You have to be patient with it."*

Cleaning requires some effort. A quick wipe while warm works, but if you burn something on, you'll be reaching for Bar Keepers Friend.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if you're a tortilla enthusiast** — you make fresh tortillas or want to char chiles and garlic for salsas. This is the correct tool for the job.

**Buy it if you cook flatbreads regularly** — naan, roti, chapati, or even pancakes. The flat surface is versatile.

**Buy it if you're building a multi-pan setup** — it complements a cast iron skillet well. Use cast iron for searing, this for flat cooking.

**Skip it if you want a one-pan-does-everything solution** — no sides means no sauces, no shallow frying. This is a specialist tool.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this dishwasher safe?**
Technically yes, but hand washing is better. Dishwasher detergent can discolor stainless steel, and the flat surface is easier to care for with a quick rinse and wipe.

**Q: Can I use it on an induction cooktop?**
Yes. The tri-ply construction includes a magnetic stainless steel layer, and multiple reviewers confirm it works on induction.

**Q: Does it come with a lid?**
No. A comal is open by design — there's nothing to cover. If you need a lid for other cooking, this isn't the pan for that job.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want an even-heating, non-seasoning comal that handles tortillas and flatbreads beautifully and you're willing to master stainless steel preheating.

**Skip it if** you prefer non-stick convenience or need a pan with sides for saucy cooking.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid construction, even heating, and genuine versatility for flat cooking. Loses points for the awkward handle and the stainless steel learning curve. At this price point, it's a worthwhile addition to any serious home cook's arsenal.
