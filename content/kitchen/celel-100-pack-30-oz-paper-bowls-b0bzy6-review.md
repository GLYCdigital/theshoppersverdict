# Celel 100 Pack 30 oz Paper Bowls Review: Are These the Last Disposable Bowls You'll Need?

Let me be blunt: most disposable paper bowls are garbage. They sag, leak, and turn to mush halfway through dinner. The Celel 30 oz paper bowls are different — and the 4.9-star average across 245 ratings backs that up.

I've tested dozens of these things. Here's why this 100-pack actually earns a spot in your kitchen.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 100 bowls, each holding 30 fluid ounces. That's a serious portion size — think loaded baked potato soup, hearty stew, or a massive salad. The bowls measure roughly 6.5 inches across, which gives you a wide opening that's easier to eat from than those deep, narrow takeout containers.

The construction is the headline here. These aren't flimsy paper plates shaped into a bowl. The walls are noticeably thicker than standard disposable options, and there's a plastic coating on the interior that handles moisture without breaking down.

## Real-World Performance

I loaded one with piping-hot beef stew — 200°F liquid straight from the stove. Held it for 20 minutes. No leaking. No softening. No structural failure when I picked it up by the rim.

The 30 oz capacity matters more than you'd think. Most disposable bowls max out around 18-20 oz, which means you're constantly refilling or using two bowls for big portions. This one handles a full serving of soup plus crackers or a massive grain bowl without splashing over.

Cold foods work just as well. I've used these for overnight oats, pasta salad, and even as mixing bowls for small-batch baking. The wide base keeps them stable on the counter.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, there are only two real issues.

First, these are not microwave-safe for long durations. The coating can soften if you're heating something for more than 60-90 seconds. You can get away with quick reheats, but don't plan on cooking in these. If you're a heavy microwave user, this is a genuine limitation.

Second — and this is the bigger miss — there are no lids included. For a product aimed at meal prep and leftovers, that's an oversight. You'll need to buy separate covers or use foil, which defeats some of the convenience.

One more minor note: at 30 oz, these are bulky to store. The 100-pack takes up a decent chunk of cabinet space. If you're in a tiny apartment, that might be a dealbreaker.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are these bowls really leak-proof?**
Yes, for normal use. The plastic coating holds up to hot, greasy, and liquid-heavy foods. I tested with soup and oily curry — no seepage through the bottom or sides. Extreme cases like standing liquid for hours might eventually compromise the seal, but for a meal's duration, they're solid.

**Can I use these in the microwave?**
For quick reheats — 60 seconds or less — they're fine. Beyond that, the coating softens and the bowl loses structural integrity. For anything serious, transfer to a ceramic bowl first.

**How do these compare to foam or plastic bowls?**
They're sturdier than foam (which crushes easily) and more eco-friendly than plastic. They're not compostable, but they're paper-based, which is a better environmental profile than polystyrene. The thick walls also insulate better than foam, keeping hot food hot longer.
