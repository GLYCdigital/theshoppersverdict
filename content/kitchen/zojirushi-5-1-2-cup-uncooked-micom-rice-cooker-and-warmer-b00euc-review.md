## Performance: Where It Earns the 4.7 Stars

The core technology here is the "Micom" (microcomputer) system. Unlike basic cookers that just boil water until it's gone, this unit uses sensors to adjust temperature in real time. The result? Rice that's distinctly fluffy, with each grain separate and chewy — not clumped, not mushy.

I tested the multiple settings: **white, brown, sushi, porridge, and quick cook**. The brown rice cycle runs about 55 minutes, but the texture is genuinely nutty and tender without being al dente. The sushi setting produces the sticky-but-separate texture you need for rolls. The **porridge setting** is a standout — it's not a gimmick; it produces creamy congee without constant stirring.

One buyer noted, *"I've owned this for three years and it still makes rice like day one. The texture is better than restaurants I've eaten at."* That longevity is a recurring theme in customer feedback — the nonstick pan doesn't peel, and the heating element doesn't degrade.

## Where It Falls Short

**Capacity reality check:** The "5-1/2-Cup" refers to *uncooked* rice. Cooked, you get about 10 cups — enough for 4-6 people as a side. If you're meal-prepping for a family of 6+ or want leftovers for the week, look at the 10-cup model instead.

**The beeping is aggressive.** When cooking finishes, it emits a series of beeps that are genuinely startling. There's no way to mute it. One reviewer joked, *"The rice is done, and so is my quiet afternoon."*

**Cord management and footprint:** The cord is fixed (no wrap-around storage), and the unit is wide enough that it won't fit under most upper cabinets. It's a countertop resident, not a cabinet dweller.

**No built-in steamer basket:** For a cooker in this price range, the omission is noticeable. You'll need to buy the Zojirushi steamer basket separately if you want to cook vegetables simultaneously.
