Plastic straws are on the way out, and metal ones come with that undeniable tinny taste that ruins a good iced latte. The ANNVCHI 16-pack glass straw set sits in the sweet spot between eco-consciousness and actual drinking pleasure. But is a glass straw practical, or just fragile aspiration? I dug through two hundred-plus buyer experiences and the spec sheet to find out.

## What's Actually in the Box

This isn't a minimalist set. You get 14 drinking straws plus 2 cleaning brushes. The breakdown: 6 straight straws (10.5 inches), 6 bent straws (10.5 inches), and 2 wide-diameter smoothie straws (8mm versus the standard 6mm). Every straw is made from borosilicate glass — the same material used in lab beakers — which means it can handle thermal shock better than standard soda-lime glass.

The bent straws have a tapered bend, not a sharp U-turn. That matters. Sharp angles create weak points and make cleaning a nightmare. The gradual curve here keeps the straw structurally sound and lets the brush slide through without snagging.

As shown in the product image, the set ships with color-coded tips on the straight straws. Those aren't just decorative — they help you tell which straw is yours in a shared household, a genuinely thoughtful touch that most competing sets skip.

## Real-World Performance

Boronsilicate glass is the right call. It doesn't retain flavors, doesn't leach anything into your drink, and — unlike stainless steel — it doesn't make everything taste faintly metallic. One buyer put it plainly: "I forgot I was drinking out of glass. It just feels like sipping, not like a science experiment."

The 10.5-inch length handles standard 16-ounce cups without the straw disappearing into the drink. The 8mm smoothie straws actually pull thick liquids — think protein shakes and acai bowls — without the exhausting suction you get from narrow straws.

The cleaning brushes deserve a shoutout. Most glass straw sets include one cheap nylon brush that bends after two uses. These have stiff wire cores and dense bristles that reach the full length. One reviewer noted, "I make a daily green smoothie with chia seeds. The brush gets everything out. I haven't had a clogged straw yet, and I've had these for six months."

## Where It Falls Short

Glass is glass. These will break if you drop them on tile. The borosilicate construction makes them more resilient than cheap glass, but you're still handling a material that shatters. A few buyers reported cracked straws from dishwasher mishaps — which brings me to the bigger issue.

The manufacturer says these are dishwasher-safe, but multiple long-term reviewers report clouding and etching after repeated dishwasher cycles. The heat and abrasive detergent slowly degrade the glass surface. Hand washing with the included brushes takes thirty seconds and keeps them crystal clear. Consider the dishwasher claim marketing, not gospel.

There's also no travel case. If you're buying these to take to work or use at restaurants, you'll need to buy a separate protective pouch. That's a minor cost, but it's an omission worth knowing before you order.

## Who Should Buy These

**The smoothie enthusiast** — The wide straws handle thick blends that regular straws choke on. **The household trying to eliminate single-use plastic** — Sixteen pieces means you'll always have a clean straw, even with a family of four. **The cocktail host** — These look genuinely elegant in a highball glass, and the bent straws sit properly in stemware without tipping.

Skip these if you have young kids who drink from open cups, if you're looking for a grab-and-go travel solution (buy a separate pouch), or if you're prone to dropping things in the kitchen. A shattered glass straw in the sink is not a fun cleanup.

## FAQ

**Can I use these with hot drinks?**
Yes. Borosilicate glass handles temperatures up to 400°F. Hot coffee or tea won't crack them. That said, sipping boiling liquid through any straw is a bad idea — you'll burn your mouth.

**How do I clean the bent straws?**
The included brushes have enough flexibility to navigate the curve. Run warm soapy water through first, then follow with the brush. The wide smoothie straws need a few extra passes but aren't difficult.

**Do these fit standard cups and tumblers?**
The 10.5-inch length fits most 16-20 ounce cups. For taller 24-ounce tumblers, the straw will sit shorter — check your cup height before ordering.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a durable, taste-neutral straw set that handles everything from iced coffee to thick smoothies, and you're willing to hand-wash. **Skip it if** you're accident-prone, rely on your dishwasher for everything, or need a travel-ready solution out of the box.

The ANNVCHI set earns its 4.8-star rating through smart design choices — the tapered bends, the stiff cleaning brushes, and the borosilicate material. For roughly $20, you're replacing hundreds of plastic straws with something that actually improves the drinking experience. Just keep it out of the dishwasher and away from tile floors.
