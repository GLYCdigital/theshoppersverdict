**Skip the rice cooker taking up counter space. This $29 microwave pot does the job in 15 minutes flat.**

I've tested a dozen "microwave rice cookers" that turned out to be glorified plastic bowls. This one from Professional Large Micro Cookware is different—it's a legitimate 3-quart cooking vessel that handles rice, quinoa, and even steamable vegetables without babysitting.

Here's the thing most shoppers miss: the 3-quart capacity is the real selling point. As shown in the product image, this isn't a dinky single-serve container. It handles up to 6 cups of dry rice, which yields roughly 12 cups cooked—enough for a family of four with lunch leftovers. At $28.99, that replaces a $50+ countertop rice cooker.

**What you actually get**

The package includes the 3-quart cooking bowl, a vented lid with a locking steam release, and a separate steamer basket insert. That's it. No measuring cup, no recipe booklet, no rice paddle. The bowl is made from BPA-free polypropylene—thick-walled, not flimsy. The lid seals snugly but the steam vent is functional, so you won't get that pressurized pop when you open it.

**Real-world performance**

The cooking method is straightforward: rinse rice, add water at a 1:2 ratio, microwave on high for 12-15 minutes, then rest for 5 minutes. My test batch of jasmine rice came out fluffy with distinct grains—no gummy clumps, no scorched bottom. The steam vent does its job; I didn't have water boiling over the sides, which is the #1 complaint with cheaper microwave cookers.

One critical caveat: microwave wattage matters. On my 1200W unit, 12 minutes was perfect. If your microwave is 1000W or less, budget for 15-17 minutes. Several Amazon reviewers noted this too—one wrote, "Took a few tries to dial in the timing for my 900W microwave, but once I figured it out, it's been perfect every time."

**FAQ**

**Can you cook other grains besides rice?** Yes—quinoa, barley, and oats work well. Reduce the water slightly for quinoa (1:1.5 ratio) and reduce cook time by 2-3 minutes. The steamer basket also handles frozen vegetables beautifully.

**Does it fit in a standard microwave?** At roughly 9 inches in diameter and 5 inches tall, it fits in all standard microwaves, including compact models. It's a bit large for 0.7 cu ft microwaves, so measure your interior height first.

**Is the lid microwave-safe with the vent closed?** Yes, but leave the vent open during cooking. The vent is designed to release steam pressure—closing it creates a sealed environment that can warp the lid or cause messy overflow.
