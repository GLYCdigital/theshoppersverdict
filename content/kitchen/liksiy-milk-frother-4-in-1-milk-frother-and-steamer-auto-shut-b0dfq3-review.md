**The 4-in-1 jug that does everything — except please everyone.**

The Liksiy Milk Frother isn't the first electric frother to promise café-quality foam at home, but it's one of the few under $60 that bothers with four distinct functions: hot milk, hot foam, cold foam, and steaming. That breadth is both its selling point and its Achilles heel.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the 300ml stainless steel jug, a detachable whisk assembly, a clear lid, and a measuring cup. The base has a simple two-button interface — one for temperature, one for foam density. Nothing fancy, and that's fine. The jug's non-stick interior is a genuine plus; it makes cleanup less of a chore than the bare-metal interiors on cheaper rivals.

**Performance: the good, the bad, the foamy**

Hot milk hits a solid 150°F in about four minutes, which is respectable. The hot foam function produces dense, micro-foam-style bubbles that hold shape for a solid 20 minutes — genuinely usable for latte art if you're patient. Cold foam is the surprise standout; it whips cold milk into a creamy, stiff foam that doesn't collapse in iced drinks. That alone justifies the purchase for iced-coffee loyalists.

But here's where the cracks show. The frothing consistency varies wildly by milk type. Whole milk and barista blends come out thick and velvety. Oat milk? Thin, watery foam that dissipates fast. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: *"Great with whole milk, but if you're plant-based, prepare to be underwhelmed."* Another noted, *"The cold foam setting is why I bought this, and it works, but the hot foam burns slightly if you leave it past the auto shut-off."*

**What buyers consistently praise**

The auto shut-off is a genuine safety win — it triggers reliably at the end of each cycle, preventing the burnt-milk disaster that plagues cheaper frothers. The build quality of the jug itself drew repeated compliments; the stainless steel feels substantial, and the base doesn't wobble during operation. Buyers also appreciated the clear max-fill lines, which eliminate guesswork. One reviewer summed it up: *"Simple, does what it says, and the shut-off is a lifesaver when you're distracted by kids."*

**Where it falls short**

The whisk assembly is the weak link. Multiple reviewers report the plastic shaft cracking or the whisk detaching after 2-3 months of daily use. It's replaceable, but at roughly $15 for a replacement part, that's a real cost to factor in. Cleaning is also finicky — the whisk must be rinsed immediately after each use, or dried milk film sets in and requires soaking. The 300ml capacity is fine for single servings but tight if you're making two drinks back-to-back.

**Who should buy this**

- **Iced-coffee enthusiasts** who want proper cold foam without a $200 dedicated frother
- **Single-serve households** making one latte or cappuccino daily
- **Home baristas** who stick to dairy milk and want a budget-friendly steam alternative

**Skip it if**

- You're primarily plant-based — the oat and almond milk results are disappointing
- You're a heavy daily user who needs a durable workhorse — the whisk fragility is a dealbreaker
- You're making drinks for multiple people — 300ml max capacity means sequential cycles

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I heat milk without frothing it?**
A: Yes, the "hot milk" setting heats without agitating the whisk aggressively. It won't scald, but it doesn't get as hot as a dedicated steamer — expect around 150°F, which is fine for most drinks.

**Q: Does the cold foam work with non-dairy milk?**
A: It works, but results vary. Oat milk produces thin, quick-dissolving foam. Coconut milk does better, but soy is the most reliable plant-based option here.

**Q: How loud is it during operation?**
A: Noticeably buzzier than higher-end frothers — about the volume of an electric toothbrush. Not disruptive, but don't expect whisper-quiet operation.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a dairy-drinking single-serve latte lover who wants hot and cold foam in one affordable device. The cold foam function alone is worth the price of admission.

**Skip it if** you're plant-based, need heavy daily durability, or expect barista-grade consistency across all milk types.

The Liksiy is a capable budget frother with one standout feature and one structural weakness. At 3.7/5, it earns a cautious recommendation — just budget for a replacement whisk and stick to whole milk.
