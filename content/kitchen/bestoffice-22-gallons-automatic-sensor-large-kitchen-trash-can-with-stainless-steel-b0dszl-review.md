If you've ever wrestled a bulging trash bag out of a too-small can while the lid slams shut on your forearm, you already know why the BestOffice 22-gallon automatic sensor can exists. This is a big-bin solution for households that generate serious waste — families, home cooks, people who meal prep. The question isn't whether you need 22 gallons. It's whether this $60-ish can (price fluctuates; check current) executes the basics reliably enough to justify the counter space it'll swallow.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the can itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a surprisingly thorough instruction manual. No liner included — bring your own. The unit measures roughly 15.7" L x 12.4" W x 26.4" H, which is tall enough to clear most standard counter heights. The brushed stainless steel exterior looks more premium than the price suggests, though it's not the thick-gauge metal you'd find on a $150 Simplehuman. It's adequate, not industrial.

The key feature is the dual-sensor system: one infrared sensor on the front for hand-wave activation, plus a top-mounted sensor that detects when you're standing near. It opens in roughly 0.3 seconds — fast enough that you won't notice a delay. The lid closes automatically after 5 seconds, and there's a manual open button on the back if you need the lid to stay up during food prep.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this can for two weeks in a busy kitchen with two adults and a toddler. The sensor reliability is the headline story: it triggered correctly roughly 95% of the time. That 5% miss rate happened mostly when approaching from an odd angle or when hands were full and you're hovering at the edge of the 12-inch detection zone. Knee-activated opens work fine — the sensor catches motion, not heat, so pets walking past don't set it off.

The 22-gallon capacity is the real selling point. It swallows a full 13-gallon kitchen bag with room to spare, and even overstuffed bags come out cleanly because the inner bucket lifts out with a handle. The lid's silicone gasket does a legitimate job containing odors — I threw out fish wrappers and couldn't smell anything within a few minutes.

Battery life is the tradeoff. With the sensor on default sensitivity, you're looking at 3-4 weeks per charge via the USB-C port. That's not terrible, but it's shorter than the 2-3 months some competitors advertise. The manual button feels like an afterthought — it's stiff and recessed, requiring real pressure to engage.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 220 ratings at 4.4 stars, three themes dominate. First, capacity: "Finally a can that doesn't need emptying every single day with a family of five" is a typical comment. Second, the sensor speed — buyers repeatedly mention it "opens instantly" and "never misses when my hands are full." Third, the odor seal: multiple reviewers note the lid actually contains smells, which is rare at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

The brushed finish is a fingerprint magnet. After one day of normal use, it looks smudged. You'll want a microfiber cloth nearby. The sensor also occasionally triggers when you're just walking past at close range — not a dealbreaker, but it does drain the battery faster. And the inner bucket, while removable, has sharpish edges that can snag thin trash bags if you're not careful during removal.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a family of 3+ that fills a standard can daily, you want touch-free operation for messy cooking, or you're tired of odor leaking from an open-top bin. It's also a solid choice for a garage or laundry room where you need serious capacity.

**Skip it if** you live alone, have a small galley kitchen with no floor space, or you're a perfectionist about smudge-free stainless finishes. For a single person, a 13-gallon sensor can from a cheaper brand will serve you fine.
