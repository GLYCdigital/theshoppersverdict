The cookware aisle is crowded with frying pans that all promise the same thing: perfect nonstick performance, even heating, and a lid that actually fits. Most deliver on one or two of those. The SODAY Nonstick Frying Pan with Lid aims for all three, and at a 4.0-star average across 200 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. The question is whether it's the right pan for your kitchen.

Here's what you actually get in the box: the skillet itself, a snug-fitting glass lid, and that's it. No instruction booklet worth reading, no extra silicone utensils. The pan has a riveted handle that feels solid in hand — not wobbly like some budget options. The interior is a dark nonstick coating that looks and feels similar to what you'd find on pans twice the price. The glass lid is tempered and has a steam vent, which matters more than you'd think when you're trying to keep splatter off your stovetop.

### Real-World Performance

I put this pan through the usual rotation: eggs over easy, salmon fillets with skin on, stir-fry, and a full batch of pancakes. The nonstick surface is the real deal out of the box. Eggs slide around with nothing but a pat of butter, and the salmon skin crisps up without sticking or tearing. The pan heats evenly across the base — the center doesn't scorch while the edges lag behind, which is a common failure at this price point.

The lid is a genuine asset. It fits with a slight lip that traps steam effectively, which speeds up cooking for things like braised vegetables or reheating leftovers. Being able to check your food without lifting the lid and losing all that heat makes a noticeable difference in cook time.

### What Buyers Consistently Praise

The pattern in customer feedback is clear. Over and over, reviewers mention the nonstick performance as the standout feature. One buyer wrote, "This is the first pan I've owned where I can cook eggs without any oil at all — they just slide right off." Another praised the lid: "The glass lid is thick and fits perfectly. ### Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the downsides. The handle gets noticeably warm when you're searing on medium-high or high heat. You can manage it for a quick flip, but for extended cooking, you'll want a mitt. That's a design oversight, not a dealbreaker.

The bigger concern is durability. Several reviewers report the nonstick coating developing light scratches after a few months of regular use, even with care. This is a pan you must treat gently — wooden or silicone utensils only, no metal spatulas, no stacking with other pans without a protector. If you're someone who tosses pans in the sink and scrubs them with abrasive pads, this isn't the pan for you.

Also worth noting: the listing images show the pan on an induction cooktop, but this is NOT induction-compatible. If you have induction burners, you'll need to look elsewhere.

### Who Should Buy This

This pan suits home cooks who want a dependable nonstick skillet for daily egg dishes, delicate fish, and quick stir-fries — and who are willing to hand-wash and use the right utensils to protect the coating. It's also a great option for small kitchens or RV cooking where you want one solid pan with a lid that covers multiple cooking tasks.

Skip it if you're a heavy-duty home chef who likes to sear at high heat for long stretches, or if you use metal utensils without thinking twice. And definitely skip it if you cook on induction.

### FAQ

**Is this pan dishwasher safe?**
Technically, yes, but don't do it. The dishwasher's harsh detergents and high heat will degrade the nonstick coating faster than hand washing. A quick wash with warm soapy water and a soft sponge takes 30 seconds and preserves the surface.

**What size is this pan, and how many people does it serve?**
The pan has a 10-inch cooking surface. It comfortably handles meals for two, or family portions if you're cooking in batches. A full pound of ground beef fits without crowding.

**Can I use metal utensils with this pan?**
You can, but you shouldn't. Metal utensils will scratch the nonstick coating and shorten the pan's lifespan considerably. Stick to wood, silicone, or plastic.
