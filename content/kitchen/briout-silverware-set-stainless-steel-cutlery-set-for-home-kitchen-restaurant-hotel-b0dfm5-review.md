The flatware aisle is a minefield. Either you're paying restaurant-supply prices for pieces that feel like surgical instruments, or you're grabbing a budget set that bends when you look at it wrong. The Briout 24-piece set sits in that sweet spot — it's not luxury, but it's not junk. Here's what 229 Amazon reviewers and I actually found.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get 24 pieces: eight dinner forks, eight dinner knives, and eight tablespoons. That's service for eight, which covers a full table or a family of four with backups. The set is marketed for home, restaurant, and hotel use — that's a hint at the build philosophy. These aren't delicate heirloom pieces; they're workhorse cutlery with a polished look.

Each piece is stamped from 18/8 stainless steel (18% chromium, 8% nickel). That's the same alloy class used in most mid-range commercial flatware. The mirror finish is uniform across the set — no warping, no rough edges along the tines or bowl rims.

## How It Performs in Real Kitchens

The first thing you notice is the weight. These aren't feather-light airline utensils. The handles are solid and slightly weighted, which gives you confidence when cutting through a thick pork chop or scooping dense casserole. The fork tines are sturdy enough to pierce roasted vegetables without bending, and the spoon bowls are deep enough for soup without feeling like a ladle.

I ran them through the dishwasher three times a week for a month, mixed with regular cookware. No pitting, no discoloration, no rust spots. The mirror finish held up — which is more than I can say for cheaper sets that develop that cloudy film after a few cycles.

One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"I've had these for two months and they still look brand new. My old set from [a big-box store] started spotting after three washes."* That sentiment shows up repeatedly in the reviews — durability is the consistent theme.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations.

The knives are the weakest link. They're serviceable for butter, soft cheeses, and most cooked meats, but they're not steak knives. You'll find yourself sawing at a tough ribeye or a thick pork loin. If you regularly serve steak, buy a separate set of serrated steak knives and use these for everyday meals.

The mirror finish is beautiful, but it shows fingerprints and smudges almost immediately. If you're particular about presentation, you'll be wiping them down before guests arrive. Also — and this is a small thing — there's no storage tray in the box. Plan on a drawer organizer or a countertop caddy.

Finally, the tablespoons are a bit large for soups served in shallow bowls. They're perfect for cereal or serving dishes, but you might want a smaller spoon for broth.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're setting up a first apartment, replacing a mismatched drawer full of orphaned utensils, or outfitting a vacation rental. It's also a solid choice for a wedding registry gift at a reasonable price point.

**Skip it if:** You need steak knives, you want a specific pattern (these are clean and modern only), or you're looking for ultra-lightweight utensils — these have a substantial feel that some find too heavy.

## FAQ

**Are these dishwasher safe?**
Yes. The 18/8 stainless steel resists corrosion and pitting in standard dishwasher cycles. Just don't mix them with silver-plated items, which can cause blemishes from galvanic reaction.

**Do the knives actually cut food?**
They handle everyday tasks — cutting pancakes, chicken breast, vegetables. They won't replace steak knives for thick cuts of meat. Keep a separate set for special meals.

**Is this a complete 24-piece set?**
Yes — 8 forks, 8 knives, 8 spoons. That's service for 8 people. If you entertain often, consider buying two sets.
