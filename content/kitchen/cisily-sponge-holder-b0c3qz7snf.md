**Is the Cisily Sponge Holder just another $14 gadget that'll end up in a drawer? Not quite. Here's the honest breakdown.**

You've seen the problem: a wet sponge sitting in a puddle on the sink edge, growing bacteria, smelling like last week's dishwater. The Cisily Sponge Holder for Kitchen Sink tackles this by mounting directly onto your faucet neck, keeping your cleaning tools elevated and aired out. With over 12,700 ratings and a 4.5-star average, it's clearly working for a lot of people. But it has limitations you need to know before buying.

**What you actually get**

The package includes the main caddy body — a single-piece plastic unit with multiple compartments. The design is straightforward: a larger basin for standard sponges, a tall cylindrical holder that fits bottle brushes and scrub brushes, and a few smaller slots for things like steel wool or dish scraper pads. The unit slides over your faucet neck and sits on the deck of the sink. No screws, no adhesive strips, no tools. It's about 8 inches long and 4 inches wide, so it takes up minimal counter space. As shown in the product image, the whole thing is designed to sit right at the base of the faucet, keeping everything within arm's reach.

**Performance and real-world use**

The genius of this design is the ventilation. The caddy has drainage holes and raised slots, so water doesn't pool around your sponge. It drips straight into the sink. That alone eliminates the biggest complaint people have with countertop sponge holders — the sludge that collects underneath. One verified buyer put it simply: "No more soggy sponges. It actually dries out between uses. I don't have to wring it out every time I finish washing dishes."

The high brush holder is the standout feature. Tall scrub brushes and dish brushes usually tip over in short holders, but this one keeps them upright and drying properly. It's a small thing that makes a real difference if you use multiple brushes regularly. The caddy also holds a full-size dish soap bottle in some configurations, though the fit depends on your bottle shape.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across hundreds of reviews, the same themes keep coming up. First, installation is genuinely effortless — you just slide it over the faucet and it's done. Second, the drying function works. Multiple reviewers note that sponges last longer and don't develop that mildew smell. Third, it frees up counter space. One reviewer wrote: "I was skeptical about another 'as seen on TV' style product, but this cleared my sink area completely. Everything has a home now."

**Where it falls short — be honest with yourself**

This isn't a universal fit. The most common complaint is incompatibility with pull-down or pull-out sprayer faucets. If your faucet has a sprayer head attached to a hose, the caddy won't fit over it properly — the sprayer gets in the way. Check your faucet type before ordering. Also, the plastic is lightweight. It's not flimsy, but it's not premium either. If you scrub aggressively near the faucet, you might knock the caddy off its perch. And the sponge slots are sized for standard sponges. Those thick, dense scrub pads from the hardware store are a tight squeeze.

**Who should buy this**

This is perfect for renters who can't drill into counters, homeowners who want a simple no-tool solution, and anyone who hates the smell of a wet sponge. It's also great for small kitchens where counter space is precious. Skip it if you have a pull-down sprayer faucet or if you use oversized, heavy-duty cleaning tools that won't fit the slots.

**FAQ**

**Will this fit my faucet?**
It fits standard faucet necks up to about 1.5 inches in diameter. If your faucet has a pull-down sprayer head, it won't fit. Measure your faucet neck before ordering.

**Does it rust or corrode?**
No — it's made of plastic, so rust isn't an issue. It won't scratch your sink or faucet finish either.

**How do I clean the caddy itself?**
Just rinse it under running water or toss it in the dishwasher on the top rack. It's one piece, so no crevices where gunk hides.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have a standard faucet, you're tired of soggy sponges, and you want a zero-install solution for under $15. It's a functional, well-executed design that does exactly what it promises.

**Skip it if:** You have a pull-down sprayer faucet, or you prefer a more robust, heavier-duty caddy that mounts permanently. 

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, practical buy for most kitchens, docked slightly for the faucet compatibility limitation and lightweight plastic. For the price, it's hard to beat.
