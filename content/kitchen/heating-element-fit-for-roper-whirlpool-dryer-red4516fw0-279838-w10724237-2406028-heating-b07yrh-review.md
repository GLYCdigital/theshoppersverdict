**The 20-minute fix that keeps a $500 dryer out of the landfill**

When your Whirlpool-built dryer (Roper, Maytag, or Amana — they share the same guts) stops heating but still tumbles, you've got a dead heating element. The part number you need is 279838, and this aftermarket replacement aims to match it exactly. At roughly half the price of the OEM part from a local appliance shop, it's worth a closer look.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the complete heating element assembly — the coiled nichrome wire wrapped in its mica frame, plus the thermal cutoff and high-limit thermostat pre-wired to the element. The part measures about 8 inches long and 4 inches wide, fitting the rear access panel of dryers using the RED4516FW0-279 platform. It's a direct swap for the 279838, W10724237, and 2406028 part numbers.

**Real-world performance**

The element's job is simple: pull 240 volts, glow red hot, and push air over the coils. This one does that reliably. The nichrome wire gauge matches OEM spec, meaning you'll get the same 5,400-watt heat output as the factory part. Multiple buyers confirmed it restored full drying times — one noted their "loads that took 90 minutes now finish in 45."

Installation is straightforward if you're comfortable with basic appliance repair: unplug the dryer, pull the rear panel, disconnect the two wires, and swap the element. The whole job takes about 20 minutes with a screwdriver and nut driver. The thermal fuse (the white plastic piece with two wires) is separate — if that's blown, this kit won't fix it, but the high-limit thermostat included here is a common failure point too.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The fitment is the star. One verified purchaser wrote: "Exact match to the original, right down to the bracket holes. The dryer heats up faster than it did with the factory element." Another confirmed it cured their "no heat, clothes still wet after 2 cycles" issue on a Kenmore Elite with the same chassis.

The included thermostat is a genuine value-add. On OEM parts sites, that sensor sells for $15-20 alone — getting it bundled here means you're covered if the thermostat was the actual culprit rather than the coil.

**Where it falls short**

The high-limit thermostat's plastic housing is slightly different from OEM — it has a smaller mounting ear. Several installers reported having to reuse the original metal bracket or zip-tie the new one in place. It's a 5-minute workaround, but it's annoying when you expect a bolt-for-bolt swap.

You'll also need to keep your old element's screws and wire connectors. This kit ships bare — no hardware, no wire nuts, nothing. If you lose the original mounting screws during disassembly, you're making a second trip to the hardware store.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY homeowners** with even basic tool skills — you'll save $150+ in service call fees

**Skip it if** your dryer won't heat AND won't tumble — that's a different failure (likely the drive belt or motor), and this part won't help.

**FAQ**

**Q: How do I know if my heating element is actually dead?**  
A: Unplug the dryer, access the element, and check continuity across the two coil terminals with a multimeter. No continuity (infinite resistance) means a broken coil — this kit will fix it. Also check the thermal fuse (located near the exhaust vent) for continuity; if it's blown, replace it too, or the new element will burn out immediately.

**Q: Will this fit my Whirlpool, Maytag, or Amana dryer?**  
A: If your model uses part number 279838, W10724237, or 2406028, yes. That covers most Whirlpool-built dryers from 2010-2020. Check your model number against the compatibility list — the RED4516FW0-279, WED4815EW, and similar platforms all use this element.

**Q: Is this a genuine Whirlpool part?**  
A: No, it's aftermarket. But it's made to OEM specs and performs identically. The only difference is the thermostat housing, which is a minor fitment quirk, not a functional issue.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you've confirmed your element is open (no continuity) and you're comfortable with basic appliance repair. It's a solid, cost-effective fix that gets your dryer back to factory heat output.

**Skip it if** you'd rather pay a pro for peace of mind, or if your dryer is from the early 2000s — the extra wear on bearings and seals might make replacement, not repair, the smarter call.
