## Small cups, serious coffee ritual

Most espresso cup sets under $30 are flimsy afterthoughts — thin ceramic that cools your shot in 30 seconds, or cheap porcelain that chips the first time you look at it sideways. The YHOSSEUN set isn't that. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, this demitasse set is earning its place on real countertops. Here's what you actually need to know before buying.

## What's in the box

You get six espresso cups with matching saucers, plus a wooden holder that keeps the whole set organized. The cups hold roughly 2.5 ounces — proper demitasse size, not those oversized "espresso" mugs that are really just small coffee cups. The saucers are about 4.5 inches across, sized to match. The wooden holder is a nice touch; it's not fancy hardwood, but it's functional and keeps the stack tidy on your counter.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The ceramic is thick. That's the first thing you'll notice. It's heavier than typical cheap espresso cups, and that weight translates to heat retention — your shot stays hot through the sip, not lukewarm by the second taste. The glaze is smooth and glossy, easy to wipe clean.

These cups are designed for actual espresso machines. They're short enough to slide under standard portafilter spouts without tilting, which is a detail cheap sets often get wrong. If you're pulling double shots, the 2.5-ounce capacity means you'll need to split between two cups or use one for a lungo.

As shown in the product image, the wooden holder creates a clean presentation on the counter — it's the kind of thing that makes your coffee station look intentional rather than cluttered.

## What buyers consistently praise

The heat retention is the recurring theme in positive reviews. One buyer wrote: *"These cups are thicker than I expected — my espresso stays hot for the whole drink instead of going cold in two minutes."* Another noted: *"Finally, cups that don't feel like I'm drinking from a toy."* The included holder also gets consistent mentions — buyers appreciate not having to buy a separate organizer.

## Where it falls short

The saucers are shallow. If you're using a bottomless portafilter and get a messy pull, that overflow is going to end up on your counter, not in the saucer. It's a minor issue for most home users, but worth knowing if you're a perfectionist with your shots.

The glossy glaze gets slippery when wet. The handle is fine, but if you're carrying a full cup, grip the body too — a dropped espresso cup is a bad way to start the day.

There's no clear dishwasher-safe guarantee. The cups will likely survive a dishwasher cycle, but hand washing preserves the glaze finish longer. Don't risk it if you want these to last.
