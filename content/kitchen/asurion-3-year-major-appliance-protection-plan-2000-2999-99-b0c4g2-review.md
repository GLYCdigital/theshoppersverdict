Let's be direct: a 2.4-star average across 150 ratings is a red flag you shouldn't ignore. But the ASURION 3-Year Major Appliance Protection Plan for items priced $2,000–$2,999.99 is more nuanced than a single number suggests. Here's what you actually need to know before handing over your cash.

## What you're actually buying

This is a post-warranty service contract, not an extended manufacturer warranty. It kicks in after your appliance's standard warranty expires and runs for three years total. Coverage includes mechanical and electrical failures, parts, and labor for in-home repairs. You're not getting a physical product — you're buying a promise that a technician will show up when your $2,500 refrigerator dies at month 14.

The plan covers major appliances like refrigerators, ranges, washers, dryers, and dishwashers in that price bracket. Claims are filed by phone or online, and ASURION arranges for a vetted local repair tech to visit your home.

## Where it works

When the system functions as designed, it's genuinely convenient. Customers who had smooth claims report quick response times and competent technicians. The 24/7 claims line is real — one buyer noted, "Called at 9 PM on a Sunday, had a tech scheduled by Tuesday morning." That's the experience you're paying for.

The transferability is a legitimate perk. If you sell your house or the appliance mid-coverage, the remaining plan transfers to the new owner. For a high-end appliance, that's a small selling point.

## Where it falls apart

The negative reviews cluster around two themes: denials and hidden costs.

**Claim denials.** The most common complaint is ASURION refusing coverage for what buyers considered valid failures. The contract explicitly excludes rust, corrosion, cosmetic damage, and issues from improper installation. In practice, some customers report denials for "pre-existing conditions" on appliances that were working fine at purchase. One verified review stated: "They sent a tech who said the part was backordered, then denied the claim saying it was a pre-existing issue." That's a frustrating loop.

**Fees you didn't expect.** Some claims require a deductible or out-of-pocket shipping fees if the part must be ordered. These aren't always disclosed prominently at checkout. Read the terms before buying — the "what's not covered" list is longer than the "what's covered" list.

**The math problem.** For a $2,500 appliance, this plan typically costs $200–$350. If your appliance is reliable (most major brands last 7–10 years), you'll likely never file a claim. You're essentially betting the appliance fails within three years and the claim gets approved. Given the denial rate in reviews, that's a bet with poor odds.
