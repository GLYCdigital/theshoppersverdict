Let's be honest: most sink strainers are cheap metal discs that rattle, leak, or get lost. The OXO Good Grips 2-in-1 Sink Strainer Stopper tries to be the last drain cover you'll ever need. At 4.2 stars from 210 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but is it worth the upgrade over a $3 basic strainer? Here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains a single stainless steel strainer with a clever twist: a flip mechanism reveals a solid stopper on the reverse side. The entire unit measures about 4.5 inches across with a soft silicone rim that grips the sink opening. No tools, no installation — it drops right into a standard 3.5-inch drain hole.

The build quality is classic OXO. The stainless steel body feels substantial, not flimsy like some dollar-store options. The silicone rim is thick enough to create a genuine seal but flexible enough to remove without fighting it. As shown in the product image, the design is clean and understated — it won't clash with any kitchen aesthetic.

## Performance in Real Kitchens

I tested this in daily use for two weeks. The strainer side handles coffee grounds, rice scraps, and vegetable peels without clogging — the holes are fine enough to catch small particles while still letting water flow freely. The stopper side holds water for soaking dishes or filling the sink with no noticeable leakage around the edges.

The flip mechanism is the standout feature. One twist converts it from straining to stopping, which sounds gimmicky but genuinely works. You never have to hunt for a separate stopper or balance a lid on a wet counter. It's the kind of thoughtful design that makes you wonder why no one thought of it sooner.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the convenience factor. One verified buyer noted, *"I love that I don't have to dig under the sink for a separate stopper anymore — it's all right there."* Another praised the seal: *"Finally a stopper that actually holds water without me having to press down and hold it."* The silicone edge gets consistent mention for preventing the rattling noise that plagues metal-on-metal strainers.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has legitimate flaws. The silicone rim collects food debris in the crevice where it meets the metal — if you don't wipe it after heavy use, it starts to smell. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's an extra cleaning step you won't have with a plain metal strainer.

More significantly, this doesn't play well with garbage disposals. The stopper side creates suction that makes removal awkward when water is standing, and the silicone rim can deform if you're not careful. If you have a disposal, you'll want to stick to the strainer side exclusively — or look elsewhere.

Finally, the price. It's not outrageous, but you're paying a premium for the dual-function design. If you never fill your sink with water, a $5 basic strainer gets you 80% of the way there.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it fit all standard sinks?**
A: It fits standard 3.5-inch openings. If you have an oversized or custom drain, measure first — the silicone rim is flexible but won't stretch to fit a much larger hole.

**Q: Can I put it in the dishwasher?**
A: Yes, the stainless steel and silicone are dishwasher-safe. However, hand-washing the silicone rim prevents discoloration and extends the seal's lifespan.

**Q: How do I remove the stopper when water is standing?**
A: Use the small tab on the edge to break the suction before lifting. Pulling straight up while water is above it creates resistance — tilt it slightly to release the vacuum.
