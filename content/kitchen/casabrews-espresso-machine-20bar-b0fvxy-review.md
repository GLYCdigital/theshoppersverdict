If you've been scrolling past $500 espresso machines wondering if there's a shortcut, the CASABREWS 20Bar is the answer — with a few asterisks. This is a machine that takes the "budget espresso" category seriously: real portafilter, actual pump pressure, and a steam wand that doesn't just spit hot water. It's not a toy, but it's also not a prosumer rig. Here's what 225 buyers and my own teardown of the specs tell you before you hit checkout.

## What's actually in the box

You get the machine itself (stainless steel, about 13 inches tall), a 58mm portafilter with two baskets (single and double shot), a tamper, a 34oz water tank, and a steam wand with a plastic frothing pitcher. No pods — this is a ground-coffee-only machine, which is exactly what you want at this price. The 58mm portafilter is the standout; it's the same size used on machines three times the cost, so you can upgrade to a better basket or bottomless portafilter later if you get serious.

## Performance and real-world use

The 20-bar Italian pump is the headline, and it delivers. In practice, you'll get 9–10 bars at the group head, which is the sweet spot for espresso extraction. Pull a double shot with a medium-dark roast ground fine, and you'll see thick, honey-colored crema — the kind you'd normally pay café prices for. Heat-up time is about 3–4 minutes, which is acceptable for a single-boiler unit.

The steam wand is where this machine surprises. It produces dry, forceful steam — not the weak, wet puffs you get from cheap thermoblock machines. You can texture milk for a flat white or cappuccino in about 30 seconds, though you'll need to purge the wand after steaming to avoid clogging. One buyer put it bluntly: *"I've owned two machines in this price range. This one actually steams instead of just bubbling."*

## What buyers consistently praise

Scanning the 225 ratings, three themes dominate. First, build quality: *"The stainless steel body feels like it cost twice as much."* Second, shot quality: *"Crema on every single shot, even with grocery store beans."* Third, ease of cleaning — the drip tray and water tank pop out without fuss, and the portafilter is heavy enough to rinse clean in seconds.

## Where it falls short

Be honest about the tradeoffs. The single boiler means you can't steam and pull shots at the same time. If you're making multiple milk drinks back-to-back, you'll wait 60–90 seconds between steps — fine for a home barista, frustrating for a brunch crowd. The pressure gauge on the front is cosmetic; it doesn't let you adjust brew pressure. And while the included tamper works, it's lightweight plastic — you'll want a metal one eventually for consistent tamping.

Also, no pre-infusion. That means you won't get the gentle bloom that pricier machines offer, so very fresh, light-roast beans may channel (water finding an easy path through the puck). Stick to medium-to-dark roasts and you'll be happy.

## Who should buy this

- **The first-time espresso buyer** who wants real espresso without a $400 commitment.
- **The milk-drink lover** who makes 2–3 cappuccinos a day and wants proper microfoam.
- **The upgrade seeker** moving from a pod machine or a cheap 15-bar "espresso" that just makes strong coffee.

Skip it if you're a purity-obsessed espresso nerd who needs PID temperature control or dual boilers — this isn't that machine, and it's not pretending to be.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this machine use pods or ground coffee?**  
A: Ground coffee only. It comes with single and double shot baskets in a 58mm portafilter. No pod adapter included.

**Q: Can I make a latte or cappuccino with the steam wand?**  
A: Yes, and it's actually good at it. The wand produces dry steam, so you can create microfoam, not just hot bubbles. Just purge it after use.

**Q: How long does it take to heat up?**  
A: About 3–4 minutes from cold. If you're making multiple drinks, it stays hot, so the wait is only on the first shot.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want café-quality espresso and milk drinks at home without spending more than $150. **Skip it if** you need to pull back-to-back shots while steaming simultaneously, or if you insist on professional-level control over pressure and temperature.

The CASABREWS 20Bar earns a 4.6/5. It's the best sub-$200 espresso machine I've tested for shot quality and steam power. The single boiler is a real limitation, but for the price, it's a compromise worth making.
