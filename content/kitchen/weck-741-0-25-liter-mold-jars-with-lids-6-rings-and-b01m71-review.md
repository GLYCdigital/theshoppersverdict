If you've been eyeing those iconic glass jars with the wire clips and rubber gaskets, you've probably landed on the Weck 741. This is the 0.25-liter (about 8.5 ounces) version of the German classic, sold here as a set of six jars with six glass lids, six rubber rings, and twelve stainless steel clips — enough to run a full canning batch or organize your entire spice drawer.

## What's Actually in the Box

The Weck 741 set gives you six complete jar units: the molded glass jar itself, a glass lid, a rubber sealing ring, and two clips per jar. The glass is noticeably thick and heavy for its size — this isn't cheap soda-lime glass. It's borosilicate, which means it tolerates dramatic temperature swings without cracking. You can technically put these in the oven (up to 180°C/350°F), microwave, freezer, and dishwasher, though the rubber rings should come off before any high-heat use.

The 0.25L size is smaller than it looks in photos. To visualize: it holds about one cup of liquid. Perfect for single servings of yogurt, individual jams, or storing small dry goods like chia seeds, spices, or loose-leaf tea.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these for both canning and everyday storage. The sealing mechanism is straightforward: place the rubber ring on the jar rim, set the glass lid on top, then flip the two clips up to lock it down. For canning, the rubber ring creates an airtight seal as the jar cools — you'll hear the satisfying "pop" as the vacuum forms. One buyer noted, "I've made three batches of jam with these and every single lid sealed perfectly. No failures."

The wide mouth is the standout feature here. At 70mm across, you can get a spoon or spatula in easily, and cleaning is a breeze — no narrow necks trapping residue. The glass is also completely non-porous, so no staining or odor retention after garlic-heavy pickles.

## Honest Downsides

The rubber rings are the weak point. For canning, Weck recommends using fresh rings each batch — they're cheap (about $6 for a pack of 10), but it's an ongoing cost and a hassle if you're mid-project and run out. The clips also require a bit of practice; they're spring-loaded and can shoot off when you're releasing the seal on a hot jar. I nearly lost one down the garbage disposal on my first try.

Also, don't expect measuring marks or a pouring spout. These are straight-sided jars, and liquid pours drip down the edge without a spout. If you're using them for dressings or syrups, decant first.

## FAQ

**Can these go in a pressure canner?**
No. Weck recommends these for water-bath canning only (high-acid foods like jams and pickles). The glass can handle heat, but the clamping system isn't designed for pressure canning's high PSI.

**Are the replacement parts easy to find?**
Yes — Weck sells replacement rings, lids, and clips separately on Amazon. The 0.25L size uses size 70mm rings and standard clips, all of which are widely stocked.

**Do they leak if stored on their side?**
The rubber seal holds liquids when properly sealed, but I wouldn't trust them sideways for long-term storage. The seal is designed for upright use; laying them down increases leak risk if the ring isn't perfectly seated.
