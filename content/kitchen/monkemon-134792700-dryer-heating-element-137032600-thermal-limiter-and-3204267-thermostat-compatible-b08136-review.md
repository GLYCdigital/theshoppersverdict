## What's Actually in the Box

This isn't just the heating element. The kit ships with three components: the coil assembly (part 134792700), the thermal limiter (137032600), and the high-limit thermostat (3204267). That matters more than it sounds — when a heating element burns out, it often takes the thermal limiter with it. If you only replace the coil, the dryer might still refuse to heat because the safety switch is blown. Monkemon bundles all three, so you fix the root cause and the collateral damage in one shot.

The element itself measures roughly 12 inches long with the standard two-prong connector on one end and a mounting bracket on the other. It's designed for Whirlpool, Kenmore, Maytag, and Admiral dryers with the 279838, 279816, or 3403668 crossover numbers — so before you buy, flip your dryer around and check the model plate. ## Real-World Performance

The heating performance is where this kit earns its keep. The coil pulls the correct resistance for a 240V circuit, meaning it heats to the same 350–400°F range your original element hit. ## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my Whirlpool WGD4800BQ?**
A: Yes — that model uses the 134792700 element and the 137032600 limiter. Cross-check your model number against the compatibility list in the listing before ordering, but this covers most Whirlpool and Kenmore gas and electric dryers from the last 15 years.

**Q: How do I know if my heating element is actually the problem?**
A: Unplug the dryer, access the element, and test the coil terminals with a multimeter. A good element reads 12–20 ohms. If you get infinite resistance (OL), the coil is broken internally — this kit is your fix.

**Q: Do I need to replace the thermal limiter if it looks fine?**
A: Yes, replace it every time. Thermal limiters are one-time safety devices that blow when the element fails. A limiter can look perfect and still be dead. The $6 part isn't worth a second disassembly when it fails in two weeks.
