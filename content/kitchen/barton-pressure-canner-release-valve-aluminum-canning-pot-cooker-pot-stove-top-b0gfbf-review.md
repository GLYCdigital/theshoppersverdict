**Performance and Build Quality**

Aluminum is the right choice here for two reasons: it heats quickly and evenly, and it's significantly lighter than stainless steel. When you're lifting a full canner off a burner, that weight difference matters. The weighted gauge is the star of the show — it jiggles gently at pressure, telling you at a glance (and by sound) that you're in the safe zone. Unlike dial gauges that need annual calibration, a weighted gauge is essentially foolproof.

Real-world users consistently highlight how quickly the Barton reaches pressure. One reviewer noted, "I was shocked at how fast this hit 10 PSI compared to my old dial-gauge canner. **Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself**

First, this is a stovetop canner. If you have a glass-top electric range, check your manufacturer's specs before buying. Aluminum pots can scratch glass cooktops, and many ranges have weight limits that a full canner will exceed. This is the single most common complaint in the reviews — and it's a legitimate one.

Second, the weighted valve is functional but not quiet. It rattles and jiggles throughout processing. If you're noise-sensitive or canning while others sleep, you'll hear it. It's not a defect — it's how the system works — but it's worth knowing.

Third, aluminum requires care. You can't run it through the dishwasher, and acidic foods (like tomato-based sauces) can discolor the interior if left sitting. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a maintenance consideration.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Can I use this on an induction cooktop?**
No. Aluminum is not magnetic, so induction burners won't heat it. You need a gas or electric coil range.

**Q: Does the pressure regulator need periodic replacement?**
The weighted gauge itself is essentially maintenance-free. The gasket will eventually wear — typically after a few years of regular use — and replacements are readily available. The safety vent should be checked annually but rarely needs replacing.

**Q: How many jars fit inside?**
The standard config handles 7 quart jars or 9 pint jars on the included rack. Some users report fitting 10 pints by adjusting the rack arrangement, but don't count on it.
