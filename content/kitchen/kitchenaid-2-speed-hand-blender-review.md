# KitchenAid 2-Speed Hand Blender Review: Simple, Capable, Essential

Immersion blenders hover in that curious space between nice-to-have and kitchen essential. You don't miss one until you've used one — and then you wonder how you ever made soup without it. The KitchenAid 2-Speed Hand Blender promises to deliver that revelation at an approachable $49.99. We tested it across three months of daily use to see if it lives up to the KitchenAid name and its impressive 30,000+ Amazon reviews.

## Design and Build Quality

The KitchenAid 2-Speed Hand Blender follows a familiar silhouette: a tapered motor body above a stainless steel shaft with a bell housing covering the blade. The motor body is matte plastic with KitchenAid's signature soft curves. At the top, two clearly labeled buttons — Low and High — sit under your thumb naturally when gripping.

The build quality is reassuring. The shaft is thick-gauge stainless steel that doesn't flex. The bell housing is metal rather than plastic, which resists cracking and discoloration. The overall weight (about 1.4 pounds) is light enough for comfortable one-handed use.

## Blending Performance

In the pot, this blender performs admirably. A batch of roasted tomato soup goes from chunky to velvety in about 20 seconds on Low speed. The bell housing prevents splashing effectively when the head is fully submerged — an important safety feature that even some premium immersion blenders get wrong.

On High speed, it handles tougher tasks. Fresh ginger and garlic blend into pastes. Cooked vegetables puree to baby-food smoothness. The blade design creates an effective vortex that pulls ingredients toward the blades without excessive aeration (which can introduce bitter flavors).

**Smoothies:** This is not an immersion blender's strong suit compared to a countertop blender, but the KitchenAid handles soft-fruit smoothies in a tall jar reasonably well. Frozen fruit works if it's partially thawed. Solid ice cubes will frustrate it — stick to crushed ice or chilled liquids.

**Whipping:** The included whisk attachment is genuinely useful. It whips cream to stiff peaks in about 90 seconds and aerates eggs for omelets effectively. The gearbox that drives the whisk is metal, not plastic, which should hold up better over years of use.

## Speed and Control

Two speeds. That's it. Low runs at about 6,000 RPM, High at about 12,000 RPM. For most tasks, you'll use Low for soups and sauces (where you want control) and High for pureeing tough vegetables or making emulsified sauces.

The limitation is obvious: you can't fine-tune speed for delicate emulsions. Making mayonnaise with this blender is doable but requires a careful hand on the trigger. The Variable Speed model is a better choice if you regularly make aioli or hollandaise.

## Cleaning and Maintenance

The stainless steel shaft rinses clean under running water. Milk-based soups leave a film that requires a quick soap-and-sponge pass, but nothing sticks stubbornly. The whisk attachment has more crevices but still cleans easily.

The motor body should never go in the dishwasher or under running water. A damp cloth wipe-down is all it needs. The cord is 36 inches — adequate but not generous for reaching outlets on crowded countertops.

## How It Stacks Up

At $49.99, the KitchenAid 2-Speed Hand Blender competes directly with the Cuisinart CSB-175 (around $55) and the Breville BSB510 ($79). The Cuisinart has 15 speeds and comes with a blending jar; the Breville has a clever bell-magnet system that resists splashing. The KitchenAid is simpler but arguably more durable, with fewer plastic parts that can break.

It also benefits from KitchenAid's massive accessory ecosystem. The attachment collar (the connection point for the whisk gearbox) is compatible with KitchenAid's stand mixer attachments, though adapters vary by generation.

## Final Verdict

The KitchenAid 2-Speed Hand Blender is exactly what it promises: a well-built, simple immersion blender that handles soup, sauces, and light pureeing with ease. The two-speed limitation is real but irrelevant for most home cooks. At $49.99, it's an easy recommendation for anyone who makes soup, baby food, or smoothies and wants a blender that doesn't require digging out the whole countertop machine.
