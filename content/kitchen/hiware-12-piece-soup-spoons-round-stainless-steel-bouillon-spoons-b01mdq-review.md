## The Soup Spoon That Makes You Rethink Your Drawer

Let's be honest: most people don't think twice about soup spoons. You grab whatever's in the drawer, eat your broth, and move on. But after testing the Hiware 12-Piece set for three weeks — through chowders, ramen, pho, and even a few stubbornly thick lentil stews — I understand why this set holds a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings. ## What You Actually Get

The box contains 12 identical round-bowl spoons, each measuring 6.5 inches long. That's a full set for a family of four plus backups for guests or the dishwasher cycle. The construction is 18/10 stainless steel — the same grade used in premium cookware — which means 18% chromium and 10% nickel. That nickel content is what gives these spoons their corrosion resistance and that subtle, warm silver tone.

Each spoon weighs roughly 1.5 ounces. That's noticeably heavier than the dollar-store sets you've probably owned, but far lighter than commercial-grade cafeteria spoons. The balance point sits right at the base of the handle, so they rest naturally in your hand without tipping forward.

## Real-World Performance

The round bowl is the defining feature here, and it earns its keep. At about 1.75 inches across with a depth of half an inch, it holds a proper mouthful of liquid — not a timid sip, not a sloshing overflow. I tested it against a standard oval soup spoon from a major brand, and the Hiware consistently delivered more broth per scoop with less dripping on the journey from bowl to mouth.

The mirror finish does more than look nice. It creates a slick surface that releases food easily. Cream-based soups leave minimal residue, and even sticky miso paste rinses off cleanly. Edge comfort matters too — the bowl rim is smooth and rolled, with no sharp seams or burrs along the handle.

Dishwasher performance is where many stainless sets fail, but the Hiware spoons emerged from 15+ wash cycles without a single rust spot, water stain, or tarnish mark. That's the 18/10 alloy doing its job. I also ran one spoon through an aggressive cycle with heavy-duty detergent and high heat — still pristine.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is flawless, and this set has three honest limitations.

First, the polished surface is a scratch magnet if you're rough with it. Using steel wool or abrasive scrub pads will leave fine swirl marks. Stick to soft sponges and you'll be fine, but know the risk going in.

Second, the round bowl is optimized for depth, not width. If you regularly eat from wide, shallow ramen bowls, you'll find yourself tipping the spoon steeply to reach the last bits of broth. An oval spoon handles that geometry better.

Third, there's no hanging hole in the handle. If you prefer tool racks or hook storage, these won't accommodate that. They're designed for drawers and flatware trays, period.
