Some products promise convenience and deliver frustration. The YUHFERA 360 Rotatable Sofa Table sits firmly in the "actually works" camp — with a few caveats worth knowing before you hit buy.

Here's what you're getting: a C-clamp mount that secures to your couch arm, a vertical height-adjustable pole, and a 360-degree swiveling tabletop. The whole assembly weighs just over 4 pounds, and the tabletop measures roughly 16 inches by 10 inches — about the footprint of a large cutting board. The clamp opens wide enough for most sofa arms, and the padded grips mean it won't gouge your leather sectional the way cheaper metal clamps do.

**The rotation is the star feature.** Unlike fixed side tables that force you to reach awkwardly, this one spins freely and locks into position at any angle. Working from the couch, I rotated it from over-the-arm to perpendicular in about two seconds. The locking pin engages with a satisfying click, and once set, the table holds firm — no sagging, no gradual droop while you type. That stability matters more than you'd think, and it's the reason most buyers stick with this over cheaper alternatives.

The height adjustment range is genuinely useful. The pole telescopes roughly 10 inches, which covers everything from a low-slung recliner to a standard-height sofa. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"I have a deep sectional and was worried it wouldn't reach over the arm. It adjusted perfectly to my height and actually fits my laptop."* That tracks with my experience — the extension mechanism uses a threaded collar that locks tight, though it does take two hands to adjust.

**Where it falls short:** Assembly is the biggest hurdle. The C-clamp bolts are accessed from underneath, and if your couch arm is thick, you'll be contorting to tighten them properly. Budget 10–15 minutes and keep the included hex wrench handy — you'll need it again if the table loosens over time. The 15-pound weight limit is also a real constraint. A laptop and phone charger are fine. A gaming laptop, external drive, and a full mug of coffee? You're pushing it, and the swivel hinge will start to feel less secure.

Size is the other tradeoff. The 16-inch depth is fine for a tablet or a bowl of snacks, but a 15-inch laptop plus a drink means juggling. Several buyers mentioned this: *"Great for my iPad and coffee, but I can't fit my work laptop and a plate at the same time."* If you need a full work surface, this isn't it — it's a convenience table, not a desk replacement.

**Who should buy this:**

- **Apartment dwellers** with limited floor space who want a side table that disappears when not needed
- **Streamers and binge-watchers** who want a stable surface for a tablet, remote, and drink within arm's reach
- **Anyone with a deep sectional** — the clamp design solves the "nothing fits over the chaise" problem that plagues traditional end tables

**Skip it if** you need a dedicated workspace, have a couch arm narrower than 2 inches or wider than 7 inches, or plan to rest anything heavier than a standard laptop on it.

**FAQ:**

**Will it scratch my leather couch?**
The clamp has padded rubber inserts, and they stay put during normal use. As long as you don't overtighten the bolts, your upholstery is safe. I'd still recommend placing a thin cloth between the clamp and delicate leather for long-term peace of mind.

**Does the rotation lock in place or spin freely?**
Both. There's a locking mechanism that lets you fix the table at any angle. Leave it unlocked and it spins smoothly; lock it and it stays put until you release it.

**Can I mount it on a recliner's arm?**
Yes, as long as the arm is within the clamp's 7-inch maximum width. The height adjustment helps position the table correctly even when the recliner is partially tilted.

**The Verdict:**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely stable, adjustable table that mounts to your couch arm and disappears when you're done. The 360-degree rotation and solid height range justify the price over flimsier fixed-arm alternatives.

**Skip it if** you need a full-size work surface or own furniture with unusually thick or thin arms.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-designed convenience product with a real weight limit and a fiddly assembly process. For its intended use — holding your laptop, snack, and drink within easy reach — it does the job admirably.
