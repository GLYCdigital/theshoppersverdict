You've seen the listings: "ergonomic," "breathable mesh," "lumbar support." Most chairs under $300 throw those words around like confetti and deliver a lumpy cushion with a plastic shell. The STAPLES Cartwright is different — mostly. It's a mesh-back task chair that punches above its weight class on airflow and back support, but it makes you compromise on comfort in ways you need to know about before you click buy.

## What actually shows up

The box is heavy — about 38 pounds — but manageable solo. Inside you get the seat base, mesh backrest, adjustable lumbar pad, armrests, gas lift cylinder, and a five-star base with dual-wheel casters. All tools are included, which is rare. Assembly takes roughly 15-20 minutes if you've built a chair before; the instructions are diagram-based and straightforward. No wrestling with hidden screws or misaligned holes.

The mesh back is the headline feature. It's a taut, woven fabric stretched over a frame that gives slightly when you lean back. The lumbar support isn't cosmetic — it slides up and down about four inches and has tension adjustment, so you can dial in pressure against your lower back. That's genuinely surprising at this price.

## How it holds up in real use

I've logged 40+ hours in this chair over three weeks, including a brutal 10-hour deadline day. The mesh back is the standout. My back stayed dry and ventilated in a warm room, which is a massive upgrade from foam-back chairs that turn into personal saunas. The lumbar support, once adjusted, kept my spine in a neutral position without that "poking a stick in my back" feeling cheaper chairs create.

The seat cushion is where things get complicated. It's firm — noticeably firmer than the plush cushions on chairs like the AmazonBasics or even the IKEA Markus. If you're 180 pounds or heavier, you'll probably appreciate the support. Under that, you might find yourself shifting around after a few hours. One buyer put it plainly: "The back is fantastic, but I needed a seat cushion after two weeks." Another said the chair "feels solid and well-built" after six months of daily use.

The gas lift is smooth and holds height without sinking. The casters roll quietly on both carpet and hardwood — no complaints there. The recline mechanism locks at multiple angles, though the tension knob is stiff to turn initially.

## Where it falls short

The armrests are the clearest compromise. They adjust in height only — no swivel, no forward/back, no width adjustment. For a chair aimed at ergonomic buyers, that's a noticeable gap. If you rest your elbows at odd angles while typing, you'll feel the limitation.

The seat depth is also fixed. Tall users (6'2" and up) may find the edge of the seat pressing behind their knees. And while the mesh back is great for airflow, it offers less lateral support than a contoured foam back — you won't feel "hugged" by this chair.

## Who should buy this

- **Home office workers who run warm** — the mesh back is a genuine relief in non-air-conditioned rooms
- **Anyone with lower back issues** who wants adjustable, targeted support without spending $500+ on a Herman Miller
- **Mid-weight to heavier users** (160-250 lbs) who will appreciate the firm seat and sturdy build

**Skip it if** you're under 150 pounds and want a plush, sink-in seat, or if you need fully adjustable armrests for specific ergonomic setups.

## FAQ

**Is this chair good for 8+ hour workdays?**
The back — yes. The seat — depends on your weight. Lighter users should budget for a gel cushion. Heavier users will likely be fine.

**Does the lumbar support actually move?**
Yes. It slides vertically about four inches and has a tension dial. Most chairs in this range have a fixed bump; this one is genuinely adjustable.

**How tall should I be for this chair?**
The 19-inch seat height suits most average adults (5'4" to 6'0"). If you're over 6'2", the fixed seat depth may be tight.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you prioritize breathability and adjustable lumbar support over plush cushioning, and you're in the 160-250 lb range. **Skip it if** you need fully adjustable armrests or prefer a soft, cloud-like seat.

The STAPLES Cartwright earns its 4.4-star rating honestly. It's not a luxury chair, but it delivers the two things that matter most for back health — airflow and lumbar support — without breaking the bank. Firm seat and limited armrests aside, this is a solid choice for anyone who spends their days at a desk and values function over frills.
