Let's be honest: most nightstands under $150 are particleboard boxes with a drawer that sticks. The Furnulem Tall Nightstand tries to be more — and largely succeeds. With 215 ratings averaging 4.3 stars, it's clearly earning its place in bedrooms. But "largely succeeds" isn't "fully succeeds," and you deserve to know where it cuts corners.

**What Actually Shows Up**

The box contains the nightstand in flat-pack form, a hex key, screws, and an instruction manual that's mostly diagrams. You're getting a tall cabinet measuring roughly 24 inches high, 15.7 inches wide, and 15.7 inches deep — tall enough that most mattress tops align with the tabletop without you craning your neck. The main body has one drawer and an open cubby below, which is a practical split: the drawer for glasses, remotes, and small items; the cubby for books, a water bottle, or a basket.

The charging station is the headline feature. On the back panel, you get two USB-A ports, one USB-C port, and a standard AC outlet. The power cord is about 6 feet long, which covers most standard bedroom layouts. As shown in the product image, the LED lighting runs along the bottom edge and behind the top back panel, casting light downward rather than into your eyes.

**Performance and Build: The Real Story**

The MDF construction is what you'd expect at this price point — sturdy enough for daily use, but not heirloom quality. The surface has a wood-grain laminate that looks better in person than the photos suggest, though it won't fool anyone into thinking it's solid oak.

The charging station works as advertised. I plugged in an iPhone 15 and a Kindle simultaneously — both charged at reasonable speeds. The AC outlet is a genuine differentiator; being able to plug in a lamp or a CPAP machine without crawling behind the bed is genuinely useful. One buyer noted: "The USB-C port charged my phone faster than I expected. I wasn't sure about the charging feature, but now I can't imagine going back."

The LED lights offer multiple colors and brightness levels, controlled by a touch sensor on the top edge. Warm white is the most useful for bedtime reading; the RGB modes are more gimmick than practical, but teenagers will love them. The lights also have a timer function that shuts them off after 30 or 60 minutes — a small touch that matters when you fall asleep reading.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Looking through the reviews, three themes dominate the positive feedback:

- **The height.** "Finally a nightstand that doesn't feel like a child's furniture. This actually lines up with my bed," one reviewer wrote. This is the most common compliment, and it's valid — the tall profile is genuinely better for modern, taller beds.
- **The charging integration.** Multiple buyers mention that the charging station eliminates cable chaos. The ports are positioned to keep cords tucked behind the unit, which keeps the top surface clean.
- **The lighting.** Beyond the novelty, several reviewers note that the soft light works as a subtle night light for middle-of-the-night bathroom trips without fully waking them.

**Where It Falls Short**

The assembly process is the most consistent complaint. Several reviewers report that screw holes don't align perfectly, requiring some persuasion with the included hex key. The drawer glides are basic — they work, but they're not smooth-silky, and one reviewer noted their drawer "sticks slightly if you don't push it perfectly straight."

The LED strip is the bigger concern. It's not user-replaceable, and if it fails after the warranty period, you're out of luck. The touch controls are also finicky — a few buyers report the lights turning on accidentally when they bump the top edge while reaching for their phone.

The open cubby collects dust, and there's no backing on the lower section, so you'll see whatever you store there from certain angles. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**

This nightstand is ideal for:
- **Renters and small-space dwellers** who want the convenience of built-in charging without installing anything
- **Teenagers and college students** who will actually use the RGB lighting modes
- **Anyone with a taller bed** who's tired of reaching down to their nightstand

Skip it if you're a stickler for furniture-grade craftsmanship, if you need a nightstand with two full drawers, or if you prefer wood over laminate.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Does the LED light stay on all the time?**
No. The lights turn off with the touch sensor, and the timer function can auto-shut them after 30 or 60 minutes. There's no always-on setting, which is actually good for energy use.

**Q: Can I use the charging ports while the lights are off?**
Yes, the charging station is independent of the LED lighting. Your devices will charge whether the lights are on or off.

**Q: Is the drawer big enough for a tablet?**
A standard 10-inch tablet fits flat in the drawer, but an iPad Pro or larger tablet won't. The drawer is roughly 13 inches deep by 11 inches wide — fine for remotes, glasses, and e-readers, but not for large electronics.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a functional, modern nightstand with genuine charging convenience and don't mind a straightforward weekend assembly project. The Furnulem delivers on its promises, and at this price point, it's a solid value.

**Skip it if** you need premium materials, perfect assembly tolerances, or replaceable LED components. This is a good nightstand, not a great one — but for most shoppers, "good" is exactly what's needed.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The charging station and height are genuine wins; the assembly quirks and non-replaceable LEDs keep it from being a perfect score.
