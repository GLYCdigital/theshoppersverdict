**The Shaker 5-Drawer Chest from Grain Wood Furniture is a rare find at this price point: honest-to-goodness hardwood construction in a category flooded with veneer-over-cardboard. But the 4.0-star average tells you there's a catch — and it's mostly in the assembly.**

## What You Actually Get

The box arrives flat-packed, which is the first hint this isn't a "delivered assembled" piece. Inside you'll find:

- Solid rubberwood frame and drawer fronts (not pine, not MDF pretending to be wood)
- Five full-extension drawers with soft-close metal glides
- A solid wood top — no veneer, no laminate
- Wooden drawer boxes with dovetail joints (real ones, not glued butt joints)
- Hardware included: knobs, screws, and cam locks

Dimensions are 30"W x 18"D x 48"H — a proper dresser scale, not a nightstand pretending to be a chest. It weighs around 80 pounds assembled, which tells you there's actual material here.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The construction is where this chest separates from the $200 particleboard crowd. The rubberwood frame is dense and heavy. Drawer fronts are solid panels with routed edges — they're not hollow shells. The dovetail drawer boxes are a genuine surprise at this price, and they slide smoothly on ball-bearing glides.

The soft-close mechanism is the standout feature. Drawers glide closed with a controlled, quiet stop — no slamming, no jamming. In testing, all five drawers aligned properly once assembled, and the action stayed consistent over repeated use.

The Shaker design is executed faithfully: recessed panel fronts, minimal hardware, clean vertical lines. It works in modern farmhouse, Scandinavian, and transitional spaces. The available finishes (natural oak, walnut, gray) all use a matte, low-sheen topcoat that doesn't look or feel plasticky.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The consistent themes in positive reviews are the wood quality and the soft-close hardware. One verified buyer wrote: *"Finally a dresser that doesn't wobble or feel hollow. The drawers are heavy and solid — you can tell it's real wood."* Another noted: *"Assembly took a while, but the end result looks like it cost twice the price."* The dovetail joints and rubberwood construction get called out repeatedly as reasons people chose this over big-box store alternatives.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**Assembly is the biggest friction point.** Plan for 2–3 hours. The instructions are adequate but not great — some buyers reported confusion with cam lock orientation and drawer slide alignment. If you're not handy, budget for a second person or consider a professional assembly service.

**The back panel is thin hardboard.** It's painted to match, but it's not structural. In humid environments (basements, coastal homes), several reviewers reported the back panel developing slight warp or bowing over time. It won't collapse, but it's the one place where cost-cutting is visible.

**No anti-tip hardware is included.** For a 48-inch-tall chest, this is a genuine safety oversight. Buy a furniture wall anchor separately, especially if you have kids.

**The finish is matte and can scratch.** The wood is real, but the topcoat is not a hard lacquer. Heavy objects dragged across the top will mark it. Use coasters or a protective pad.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Is this real wood or veneer?**  
A: The frame, drawer fronts, and top are solid rubberwood. The drawer boxes are also wood with dovetail joints. The only non-wood components are the back panel (hardboard) and drawer bottoms (thin plywood). This is genuinely a hardwood piece, not a veneer-over-MDF product.

**Q: How long does assembly take?**  
A: Realistically, 2 to 3 hours for one person with basic tools. The included Allen wrench and cam locks work fine, but drawer alignment requires patience. A cordless drill with a hex bit will speed things up significantly.

**Q: Will this fit standard clothing?**
