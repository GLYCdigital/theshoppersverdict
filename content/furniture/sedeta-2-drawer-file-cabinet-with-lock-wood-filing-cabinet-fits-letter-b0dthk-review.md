If you’ve been hunting for a file cabinet that doesn’t look like it was rescued from a 1980s office liquidation sale, the SEDETA 2 Drawer File Cabinet deserves a look. This is a compact, mid-century-inspired wood unit that actually blends into a home office or bedroom corner. At 4.1 stars across 204 ratings, it’s not perfect — but for the price point, it solves a very specific problem: storing hanging files without sacrificing aesthetics.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the cabinet in flat-pack form: two drawer bodies, the outer shell, cam locks, dowels, a hex wrench, and two keys. The package weighs roughly 30 pounds. The cabinet measures 18.5 inches wide, 23 inches deep, and 25 inches high — small enough to tuck under a desk or beside a bookshelf. The drawers are sized for letter documents (12.5 x 9.5 inches interior), so do not plan on legal-size files here.

Assembly is straightforward. The cam lock system means you’re not fighting with dozens of screws. Most buyers report finishing in 20–35 minutes. One reviewer noted, *“I put it together alone in about 25 minutes. Everything lined up perfectly — no stripped holes, no swearing.”* That matches the general sentiment across the ratings.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The cabinet is made from engineered wood (particleboard with a wood-grain laminate finish). It’s not solid oak, and it doesn’t pretend to be. The laminate looks convincing from a few feet away — the walnut finish has a warm tone that photographs well and looks better in person than the stock images suggest.

The drawers run on metal slides with full extension. That’s a meaningful feature at this price; many budget cabinets only open 75% of the way, which makes reaching the back of the drawer awkward. The SEDETA opens fully. The slides are smooth, and the drawers don’t wobble when fully extended.

The locking mechanism is a single cylinder lock that secures both drawers simultaneously. The included keys are basic but functional. One buyer mentioned, *“The lock is tight — no play in it. It’s not a security vault, but it keeps my kids and curious guests out.”* That’s the right expectation to have.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the 204 ratings, three themes dominate the positive feedback:

First, the aesthetic. Multiple reviewers mention that it looks far more expensive than it is. The clean lines and wooden top surface mean it doubles as a small side table or plant stand — not just a filing unit.

Second, the smooth drawer operation. The full-extension slides get consistent shout-outs. One reviewer wrote, *“I was expecting cheap metal rollers. These glide like my $400 desk drawers.”*

Third, the footprint. At under 19 inches wide, it fits in narrow spaces where standard 22-inch cabinets won’t. Apartment dwellers and small-home office setups specifically call this out as the deciding factor.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honesty time. The engineered wood surface is prone to scratching. If you set a coffee mug on top without a coaster, you’ll see a ring. If you drag anything metal across the top, it will leave a mark. A cheap desk mat or felt liner solves this, but it’s an extra step.

The letter-size-only limitation is real. Legal documents won’t fit, and even some expanding file folders are too wide. Measure your files before ordering.

A few reviewers also reported that the drawer bottoms are thin — around 1/8 inch. They hold hanging files fine, but don’t load them with heavy binders stacked directly on the bottom. Use the hanging bar system as intended.

**Who Should Buy This**

This cabinet is for you if:
- You work from home and need letter-size file storage that looks like furniture, not office equipment
- You have a narrow space (under 19 inches wide) that a standard cabinet won’t fit
- You want something under $150 that doesn’t require professional assembly

Skip it if you need legal-size storage, or if you plan to stack heavy items on top. The top surface is rated for light decor, not a printer and a stack of binders.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it fit hanging file folders?**
A: Yes — letter-size hanging files fit perfectly on the included rails. The drawers are 12.5 inches deep, which accommodates standard Pendaflex folders without bending.

**Q: Can the drawers be locked separately?**
A: No. Both drawers lock simultaneously with the single key mechanism. You cannot lock one drawer while leaving the other accessible.

**Q: Is the wood real or laminate?**
A: It’s engineered wood with a laminate wood-grain finish. It looks like walnut but it’s not solid wood. Treat the surface gently — it scratches.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a compact, good-looking letter-size file cabinet that assembles easily and stores hanging files without drama. It’s a solid 4-star product that beats the metal-file-cabinet aesthetic for a home office.

**Skip it if** you need legal-size capacity, plan to abuse the top surface, or expect solid-wood construction at this price.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful, decently-built cabinet that earns its place in a home office. Just respect its limits.
