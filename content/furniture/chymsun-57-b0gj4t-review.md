# CHYMSUN 57" TV Stand Review: Solid Storage Without the Showroom Price

Most TV stands under $150 are flimsy, particleboard disasters that wobble the moment your subwoofer hits a bass note. The CHYMSUN 57" is not that. With a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, this unit earns its place in the conversation — but it's not perfect. Here's the straight story before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 57-inch wide main console, two drawers, an open center shelf, and two side cabinets with adjustable shelves. The package includes all hardware, a hex wrench, and clearly labeled parts. As shown in the product image, the design is a clean mid-century modern silhouette — flat top, angled legs, and matte wood-grain finish.

The top surface measures 57" wide by about 16" deep, which comfortably fits most 55-65 inch TVs. The unit stands roughly 20 inches tall — a good height for eye-level viewing from a standard sofa. Total weight capacity on the top is rated for 110 pounds, so you're not going to stress it with a modern LED television.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The frame is a combination of engineered wood with a laminated wood-grain finish. It's not solid oak, but it doesn't pretend to be. The legs are metal and screw into pre-drilled brackets — this is where the stability comes from. Once assembled, the unit feels solid under load. I've seen several buyer reviews noting they "expected it to feel cheap" but were surprised by how rigid the structure is after assembly.

One buyer put it plainly: "I was skeptical at this price point, but this thing doesn't budge when my kids are roughhousing in the living room." That tracks with my assessment. The metal legs and cross-bracing make a noticeable difference compared to all-particleboard competitors.

The drawer glides are basic metal runners, not soft-close, but they operate smoothly. The open center shelf is tall enough for a soundbar or cable box, and the side cabinets each have an adjustable shelf — useful for game consoles or media storage.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The most common positive themes across the 225 reviews are stability and appearance. Multiple reviewers mention the wood grain finish "looks more expensive than it is" — a common refrain for this style of furniture. The 57-inch width is also a sweet spot; it's wide enough for larger TVs but not so massive that it dominates a small apartment living room.

Assembly is another bright spot. Most buyers report completing it in 30-45 minutes with basic tools. One reviewer noted, "The instructions are actually readable, which is rare. All the holes lined up." That's higher praise than it sounds — misaligned holes are the #1 complaint across budget furniture.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The back panel is thin fiberboard that you attach with small nails. It does its job, but if you ever need to move the unit fully assembled, that panel will likely bend or separate at the corners. Plan to partially disassemble before relocating.

The cable management holes in the back are smaller than stated in the marketing photos. Standard HDMI cables fit, but thick power bricks or surge protector plugs will not pass through. You'll likely need to route those around the side or leave the back panel slightly loose.

Finally, the finish, while attractive, is a matte laminate that shows fingerprints and dust more readily than a gloss or textured surface. Keep a microfiber cloth handy.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** who need a wide TV stand that fits through standard doorways and doesn't require a crew to assemble
- **Home theater starters** who want clean cable management and drawer storage for accessories
- **Shoppers on a budget** who refuse to accept wobbly, cheap-feeling furniture as the only option under $200

**Skip it if** you need a solid wood piece that will survive multiple moves, or if you require soft-close drawers and premium hardware. This is value-tier furniture that overperforms — it's not heirloom quality.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this hold a 65-inch TV?**
A: Yes, as long as your TV's feet are within the 57-inch width and the weight is under 110 pounds. Most 65-inch LED TVs weigh 50-70 pounds, so you're fine. For heavier OLEDs, check the spec sheet first.

**Q: How long does assembly actually take?**
A: Most buyers finish in 30-45 minutes. The parts are labeled, and the instructions are clear. You'll need a Phillips screwdriver; the hex wrench is included.

**Q: Can the shelves be adjusted?**
A: The two side cabinets have one adjustable shelf each. The center open shelf is fixed. The drawers are fixed position and cannot be moved.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a stable, good-looking 57-inch TV stand that stores your gear without emptying your wallet. **Skip it if** you demand solid wood, premium drawer hardware, or plan to move frequently.

The CHYMSUN 57" delivers exactly what the 4.5-star rating suggests: dependable performance at a fair price, with minor compromises you can live with. For most shoppers, this is a smart buy.
