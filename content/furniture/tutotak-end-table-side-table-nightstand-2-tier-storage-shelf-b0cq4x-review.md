## What You Actually Get

The box contains the tabletop, two shelf panels, four legs, a crossbar, and the hardware — plus an Allen wrench. No drill required. The assembled footprint is roughly 15.7 x 15.7 inches with a height of 23.6 inches, which puts it right in the sweet spot for next to a sofa or bed. The lower shelf sits about 6 inches off the floor, giving you genuine storage without making the piece feel bulky.

The frame is powder-coated steel, and the tabletop and shelf use a water-resistant particleboard with a wood-grain laminate. It's not heirloom furniture, but nothing about it feels flimsy either.

## Build Quality and Daily Use

I've tested my share of budget end tables, and the TUTOTAK holds up better than most in its price range. The steel legs are welded properly — no sharp edges, no wobble at the joints. The crossbar underneath adds real stability; you can lean on it without the whole thing swaying. The laminate surface handles a sweating glass of iced tea without leaving rings, which is more than I can say for some pricier options.

Assembly is genuinely painless. The instructions are mostly diagrams, but the parts are intuitive enough that you'll be done before your coffee cools. All screw holes lined up on my unit — a small win that isn't guaranteed at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. The "wood grain" is clearly printed laminate. Up close, the pattern repeats and there's no real texture. If you want natural wood, this isn't it — but the price reflects that.

The 15.7-inch top is functional but tight. A large lamp plus a book and a coffee mug will crowd it. One reviewer mentioned their 18-inch lamp base left no room for anything else. Plan accordingly.

Finally, the screws are on the softer side. Use the included Allen wrench and don't overtighten — a couple of buyers stripped a screw head by cranking too hard. Hand-tighten until snug and you'll be fine.
