**La-Z-Boy Nova Executive Office Chair Review: Comfort You Can Sink Into, With a Few Caveats**

The La-Z-Boy name carries weight. You know the recliners, the living room furniture, the promise that your backside will thank you. But does that reputation translate to a $400-ish office chair? Short answer: mostly yes. After digging through the specs and what 200 Amazon reviewers are actually saying, here's the real story on the Nova Executive.

**What You Get**

Out of the box, you're looking at a high-back chair (roughly 45 inches tall) with bonded leather upholstery, a padded headrest, and the kind of thick seat cushion you'd expect from a company that built its name on sitting. Assembly runs about 20-30 minutes — standard five-star Allen wrench work, nothing cryptic. The base is a polished chrome five-star with caster wheels that roll smoothly on both carpet and hardwood (a detail several reviewers specifically called out).

**The Sitting Experience**

This is where the Nova earns its keep. The seat cushion is genuinely plush — not that rock-hard foam you get from budget office chairs. The high back supports your entire spine, and the built-in lumbar curve hits the right spot without needing a separate pillow. The recline is the standout feature: the tilt tension adjusts, and when you lean back, it glides smoothly rather than jerking. One reviewer summed it up: "It's like sitting in my living room recliner but at my desk. I've fallen asleep in it twice." That's the La-Z-Boy DNA showing.

**Where It Shines**

For tall users, this chair is a godsend. The high back actually reaches your shoulder blades and the headrest aligns with your neck — rare in this price range. The padding density is consistent across the seat, back, and armrests, so you don't get that "foam bottoming out" feeling after a few weeks. Multiple reviewers noted the chair "still looks and feels brand new after six months of daily 9-to-5 use."

**Where It Disappoints**

Now the honest part. The armrests are fixed — period. No height adjustment, no width pivot. If you're shorter or need your arms at a specific angle for typing, this will annoy you daily. One reviewer put it bluntly: "Great chair, but the armrests are positioned for a 6-foot man. I'm 5'6\" and my shoulders ache by 3pm." That's a real ergonomic limitation, not a nitpick.

The bonded leather is the other tradeoff. It feels nice initially, but bonded leather is essentially ground-up leather fibers glued to a backing. In high-friction areas — the seat edges, the armrest tops — expect cracking or peeling around the two-year mark. Several long-term reviewers confirmed this. If you want durability, you'd need to step up to a genuine leather or mesh chair.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The tall desk worker** (5'10" or taller) who's tired of chairs that end mid-back
- **The comfort-first buyer** who prioritizes plush seating over adjustability features
- **Home office users** who want that "executive" look without paying Herman Miller prices

**Who Should Skip It**

- **Shorter users** (under 5'6") — the fixed armrests will fight you
- **Ergonomics purists** who need adjustable lumbar, armrests, and seat depth
- **Long-haul buyers** expecting 5+ years of heavy daily use from bonded leather

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the Nova good for 8-hour workdays?**
Yes — the cushioning holds up and the high back supports your spine. But if you're under 5'6", the armrest height may cause shoulder strain over long sessions.

**Q: Does the recline lock into position?**
Yes, it locks in multiple angles. You're not stuck in one upright position all day.

**Q: How loud are the casters on hardwood floors?**
Quiet — reviewers consistently praise the smooth, silent rolling on both carpet and wood.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're taller than average, want genuine La-Z-Boy comfort, and accept that fixed armrests and bonded leather are the tradeoffs for the plush feel and price point.

**Skip it if:** You need full ergonomic adjustability or plan to put 2,000+ hours a year into this chair and need it to last a decade.

At 4.0 stars from 200 ratings, the Nova is a solid 4.2 in my book — a genuinely comfortable executive chair with honest limitations. It's not the most adjustable chair out there, but for pure sitting comfort, La-Z-Boy still knows what they're doing.
