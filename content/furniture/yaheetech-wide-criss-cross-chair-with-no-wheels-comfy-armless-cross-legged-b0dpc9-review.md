Let me be direct: the criss cross chair trend usually means sacrificing width for style. Most of these chairs squeeze you into a 19-inch seat and hope you don't notice. The Yaheetech Wide version isn't playing that game — and that's why it's sitting at 4.6 stars across 229 ratings.

**What actually shows up at your door**

The box contains the chair base, seat cushion, backrest, and a small hardware bag with an Allen wrench. No tools beyond that needed. The fabric is a linen-look polyester that feels denser than the photos suggest — not that cheap, slippery stuff you find on budget floor chairs. The frame is powder-coated steel, and the whole thing weighs about 22 pounds. As shown in the product image, the silhouette is low-profile with a wide, flat seat that angles slightly toward the back.

Assembly is straightforward: four bolts for the base, four for the backrest. Expect 10–15 minutes if you're not rushing. One thing worth noting — the seat cushion is attached to the frame with Velcro strips. It's secure enough for daily use, but if you have pets that love to knead fabric, keep an eye on it.

**How it actually performs**

The defining spec here is the 23.6-inch seat width. That's roughly two inches wider than most competitors in this category, and it makes a real difference. You can sit cross-legged, kneel, or stretch one leg out without your hips hitting the side rails. The foam is medium-firm — supportive enough for a 4-hour work session, soft enough that your tailbone doesn't complain by hour two.

The no-wheels design is a deliberate tradeoff. On hard floors, wheeled chairs drift as you shift weight. This one stays put. That stability matters if you're using it for gaming or desk work where you don't want to re-adjust every few minutes. Downside: you won't be gliding across the room to grab something. You'll stand up. That's the deal.

**What buyers keep mentioning**

The praise in reviews clusters around two things: the width and the sturdiness. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I'm 6'2" and 240 lbs, and this is the first floor chair that doesn't feel like it's going to collapse."* Another noted, *"The fabric actually looks expensive in person — way better than the listing photos."* The no-wheels aspect gets consistent love too, with multiple people calling out that it doesn't slide during intense gaming sessions.

**Where it falls short**

The backrest is the main compromise. At roughly 19 inches tall, it stops mid-back for anyone over six feet. You're not getting neck support — this isn't a lounger. If you're tall and want to lean back fully, you'll feel the top edge pressing into your shoulder blades. Also, the armless design means your arms rest on your lap or the seat itself. Some people love the open feel; others will miss having somewhere to plant their elbows while typing.

The Velcro-attached cushion is a minor annoyance. It stays put during normal use, but if you drag the chair across carpet, the seat can shift slightly. Easy to fix, but worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

- **Gamers** who sit cross-legged and need a stable, wide base for long sessions
- **Remote workers** with back pain who want an alternative to a rigid office chair
- **Plus-size users** who've been burned by narrow floor chairs that pinch at the hips
- **Anyone with hard floors** who's tired of wheeled chairs rolling away mid-task

Skip it if you're over 6 feet and need upper-back support, or if you absolutely require armrests.

**FAQ**

*Is this chair suitable for sitting on the floor or at a desk?*
Both. At floor height, it's about 13 inches to the seat. Paired with a low coffee table, it works great. At a standard desk, it sits low enough that you'll be looking up at your monitor — not ideal for primary work use.

*Can the cushion cover be removed for washing?*
No. The cover is not zippered or removable. You'll need to spot-clean with a damp cloth and mild detergent. Keep drinks away from it.

*How much weight can it hold?*
The steel frame is rated to 300 pounds. Buyers in the 240–260 range report no wobble or flex.

**The Verdict**

Buy it if you want a genuinely wide, stable floor chair that doesn't roll, doesn't wobble, and won't collapse under you. Skip it if you need backrest height or armrests — this chair isn't built for those needs.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The width and build quality justify the price. It's not perfect, but for the core use case — comfortable, stable floor seating — it delivers.
