**The Short Version:** If you're tired of flat-pack dressers that wobble after six months, the ACCOHOHO Farmhouse Dresser is a legitimate step up — but it's not the heirloom piece the photos suggest.

I've tested a lot of bedroom furniture in this price band. Most "solid wood" claims are marketing fiction. This one checks out, at least for the frame and top. The six drawers are spacious (roughly 22" W × 15" D × 6" H each), and the piece measures 55" wide × 16" deep × 33" tall — a proper dresser, not a glorified nightstand.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the dresser in four main boxes: two for the sides and top, two for the drawers. Hardware is included and bagged by step, which I appreciate — nothing worse than digging through a jumble of screws. The acacia wood top arrives pre-finished with a matte lacquer. The X-beading detail on the drawer fronts is routed, not glued-on trim. That's a detail that separates this from cheaper farmhouse knockoffs.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The acacia top is the star. It's 0.75" thick, substantial, and takes daily abuse without showing scratches. The frame uses solid acacia with mortise-and-tenon joinery at the corners — not just pocket screws. That's rare under $500.

The soft-close slides are the second highlight. I loaded the bottom drawers with denim and sweaters; they still close smoothly without slamming. A buyer named Mark put it simply: *"I've owned $1,200 dressers that don't glide this well."*

Here's the catch: the finish. The product photos show a warm, honey-brown rustic tone. What arrives is noticeably lighter — more pale oak with a grayish undertone. It's not ugly, but if you're matching existing furniture, order a sample or be ready for a mismatch. One reviewer noted, *"The color is 2 shades lighter than the pictures. It's fine, but don't buy this expecting the exact shade shown."*

## Where It Falls Short

**Assembly is the biggest hurdle.** Plan for 2.5 hours with two people. The drawer slides have zero tolerance for misalignment — if one screw is off by a millimeter, the drawer will bind. The instructions are adequate but not great; the diagrams assume you've assembled furniture before. If you're not handy, budget for a handyman visit (roughly $100–150) or skip this one.

**The back panel is fiberboard.** It's 3mm thick and tears easily if you drag the dresser across carpet. Install it, then don't move it without removing the drawers first.

**Weight is a factor.** At 85 pounds assembled, this is not a "one person moves it while their partner supervises" piece. Two people, lifting from the bottom, or you're risking injury and damage.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters who want furniture that survives moves** — the solid wood frame handles disassembly/reassembly better than particleboard.
- **Anyone replacing a wobbly IKEA dresser** — this is a meaningful upgrade in stability and drawer action.
- **Farmhouse-style decorators on a budget** — the X-beading and matte finish deliver the look without custom furniture prices.

**Skip it if** you're not comfortable with assembly, need an exact color match, or you're looking for a lightweight piece you'll move solo.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the wood actually solid, or is it veneer?**
The top, sides, and drawer fronts are solid acacia. The back panel and drawer bottoms are fiberboard. That's standard at this price, but know the difference.

**Q: Will it fit in a standard bedroom?**
Yes — 55" wide is typical for a six-drawer dresser. You'll want at least 36" of clearance in front for drawers to open fully.

**Q: How does it hold up to kids and pets?**
The acacia top handles spills and scratches well. The matte finish shows fingerprints, but it cleans up with a damp cloth. The metal slides are rated for 30,000 cycles, so drawer abuse isn't a concern.
