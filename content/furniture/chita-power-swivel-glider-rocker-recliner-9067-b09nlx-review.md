This chair does a lot. The question is whether it does it *well* — and for whom. After digging through the specs, build data, and what 229 buyers actually said, here's the real story on the CHITA Power Swivel Glider Rocker Recliner 9067.

**What you actually get**

The box arrives with the chair in two main pieces (base and body), a power adapter, and a small hardware kit. There's no ottoman — the footrest is built in and extends via the power mechanism. The chair measures roughly 41 inches tall with a 22-inch seat width and a 20-inch seat depth. The backrest reclines to about 150 degrees, and the footrest extends independently, which is a feature you don't always get at this price point. The swivel base rotates a full 360 degrees, and the gliding mechanism works without needing a separate glider base — the whole unit moves as one piece.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The frame is a combination of solid wood and engineered wood — not the cheap particleboard you sometimes find in sub-$400 recliners. The upholstery is a bonded leather (PU) that looks and feels more premium than the price suggests. I'll be direct about this: it's not genuine leather, and it won't age like one. But for a living room chair that gets daily use, it's acceptable — just don't expect it to look pristine after three years.

The power recline is the standout feature. The motor is quiet — buyers consistently mention they can recline without waking a sleeping baby or spouse. One reviewer wrote, *"The motor is almost silent. My husband can recline while I'm watching TV next to him and I barely notice."* The controls are simple two-button paddles on the side — one for the backrest, one for the footrest. They're intuitive enough that you won't need to read the manual.

The swivel and glide combination works smoothly on both carpet and hardwood. The base has a wide footprint (about 29 inches), so it feels stable even when fully reclined. If you're over 6 feet or north of 250 pounds, you'll appreciate the 22-inch seat width — this isn't a cramped chair. The lumbar support is built into the backrest curve rather than being a separate pillow, which means it's less adjustable but also less likely to shift around.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The independent recline controls come up repeatedly — being able to raise just the footrest without reclining the back is a small thing that makes a big difference for reading or watching TV. The swivel is also a frequent highlight; one Amazon reviewer said, *"I can turn to face my kids on the floor or the TV without scooting the chair at all."* The assembly, while time-consuming, is straightforward — no weird proprietary tools, just a standard Allen wrench and socket set.

**Where it falls short**

The bonded leather is the biggest compromise. Several long-term reviews mention peeling or cracking starting around the 18-month mark, particularly on the armrests where skin oils and friction take their toll. If you have pets with claws, this chair will show it fast. The power cord is also a practical annoyance — it runs from the base and needs to reach an outlet. If your seating area doesn't have a floor outlet, you'll need a power strip or extension cord, which can be a trip hazard.

Assembly is a two-person job. The base and body need to be aligned and bolted together, and while the instructions are clear, the bolts require patience to thread correctly. Budget an hour if you're not handy.

**Who should buy this**

This chair is ideal for nursery duty — the gliding motion is smooth enough to soothe a baby, and the swivel lets you reach for a bottle or burp cloth without contorting. It's equally good for a home theater setup where you want a recliner that isn't a massive theater seat. If you're a taller or broader person (up to about 300 pounds), the dimensions work in your favor.

Skip it if you want a chair that will look brand new in five years — you need fabric for that. Also skip it if you're not comfortable with a power cord in your living space.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Is the fabric removable for cleaning?**
No. The upholstery is not removable for washing. Spot-clean with a damp cloth and mild soap. The PU material wipes down easily, but avoid harsh chemical cleaners that can degrade the surface.

**Does it come with a warranty?**
Yes, CHITA provides a one-year warranty on the motor and a one-year warranty on the frame. The upholstery is not covered. Register your purchase on their website — some users report needing proof of purchase for warranty claims.

**Can I use it while pregnant?**
The power recline and glider motion are generally safe, but the footrest extends forward — you'll want to test clearance if you're in the third trimester. The gliding motion is smooth and gentle, not jarring.

**The verdict**

The CHITA Power Swivel Glider Rocker Recliner 9067 delivers strong value in the sub-$500 power recliner category. The quiet motor, independent controls, and smooth swivel-glide combo outperform chairs costing twice as much. The bonded leather is the tradeoff — it won't last forever, but at this price, it's a fair compromise.

**Buy it if** you want a multi-functional power recliner for a nursery, home theater, or den without spending premium money. **Skip it if** you demand long-term durability from upholstery or can't accommodate the power cord location.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely comfortable, feature-rich chair held back only by its material longevity.
