You're looking at a glass dining set, which means you've already accepted one truth: you'll be wiping fingerprints off the table for as long as you own it. The Niern 5-piece set leans into that tradeoff with a tempered glass top that's genuinely easier to maintain than any wood finish I've tested. But is it worth your money? Let's break down what you're actually getting.

**What's in the Box**

The set ships as five separate pieces: one rectangular dining table (54" L x 28" W x 29.5" H based on the product listing) and four matching chairs. The tabletop is tempered glass — this matters because tempered glass shatters into small, relatively harmless pebbles rather than dangerous shards if it breaks. The frame is powder-coated steel in a matte black finish, and the chairs have padded seats with a faux leather or fabric upholstery depending on the variant you order.

Assembly is the first hurdle. The legs bolt directly into pre-drilled holes on the underside of the glass top. You'll need a Phillips head screwdriver and ideally a second person to hold the glass steady while you align the bolts. Plan for 45 to 75 minutes. The instructions are adequate but not generous — there are no labeled parts, so you'll be matching bolts by sight.

**Real-World Performance**

The glass top is the star here. It handles hot plates, spilled red wine, and the aftermath of a toddler's spaghetti dinner with zero staining. A damp microfiber cloth and a few seconds of effort and it looks brand new. That's a genuine advantage over wood, which stains, and laminate, which chips at the edges.

The steel frame is solid at the joints. I didn't notice wobble on a level floor, and the table held a full Thanksgiving spread without flexing. The chairs, however, are the weak point. They're functional — the padding is comfortable enough for a 45-minute meal — but the construction feels lighter than the table. The legs are thinner gauge steel, and the seat-to-leg connection relies on small bolts that could loosen over time. Tighten them every few months or you'll feel a subtle rock.

**Where It Falls Short**

The smudging issue is real. Anyone who's owned a glass desk knows the drill — every handprint, every water drop, every dust particle is visible. If you're a "set it and forget it" cleaner, this table will annoy you daily.

The chair durability question is my bigger concern. This is a 4.0/5 product because of it. For a set that likely costs in the mid-hundreds, you're getting furniture that will serve you well for 3-5 years with care, but not a decade. The table will outlast the chairs.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the glass strong enough for daily use?**
Yes. Tempered glass is roughly four to five times stronger than standard glass of the same thickness. It handles normal dining activity fine, but avoid dropping heavy cast-iron cookware directly on it.

**Q: How do I clean the glass without streaks?**
Use a 50/50 water and white vinegar solution with a microfiber cloth. Wipe in one direction, then buff dry with a clean cloth. Avoid ammonia-based cleaners — they leave a film.

**Q: Can I fit six chairs around this table?**
Technically you could squeeze two extra chairs on the ends, but it'll be tight. The table is designed for four. Stick with the included chairs.
