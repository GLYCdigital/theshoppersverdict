## What You Actually Get

The REDEARTH is a round, knitted pouf with a cotton cable texture that reads more premium in person than most flat-woven alternatives. As shown in the product image, the chunky knit pattern gives it serious visual weight — it looks like something you'd find in a boutique hotel lobby, not a mass-market Amazon listing.

The dimensions clock in around 15-16 inches in diameter and roughly 12-14 inches tall, which puts it in the standard pouf territory. It's light enough to kick around a room with your foot, but the dense cotton fill keeps it from sliding across hardwood floors like some understuffed competitors.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The cotton cable knit isn't just decorative — it's the structural shell. That means it's breathable and soft to touch, but it's also a fabric that demands a bit of care. Several buyers noted the knit catches on jewelry, belt buckles, and pet claws. One reviewer mentioned their cat used it as a scratching post within the first week, which shredded the yarn. That's worth flagging if you have claw-happy roommates.

The fill is firm. Like, sit-on-it-with-a-book-firm, not sink-your-feet-in-firm. Some buyers wanted a softer footrest and were surprised by the rigidity. Others appreciated that it held its shape well over months of daily use. If you're planning to use it primarily as a footrest, know that it won't compress much — it's more of a stable platform than a plush cushion.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The most common thread in positive reviews is the aesthetic payoff. One buyer wrote, "It instantly upgraded my reading corner — looks way more expensive than it was." Another praised the size: "Perfect for a small apartment — doesn't overwhelm the space but actually holds my weight when I perch on it."

The zippered, removable cover is a genuine plus. Being able to unzip and machine-wash the knit shell solves the biggest practical problem with fabric poufs — they get gross. A few reviewers specifically called out washing it after a wine spill and having it come out looking new.

## Where It Falls Short

The firmness is the biggest divide. If you want a squishy, beanbag-style footrest, this isn't it. The cotton fill is dense and doesn't break in much over time. One reviewer noted, "It's basically a solid block wrapped in a sweater." That's a fair criticism if comfort is your top priority.

The knit texture also shows every speck of dust and lint. Light-colored versions will need more frequent cleaning, and while the cover is machine-washable, it's not a quick process to unzip, wash, dry, and re-cover the whole thing. Plan for a full day of drying time.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're decorating a boho, coastal, or eclectic space and want a functional accent piece. It works great as a footrest, a low stool for guests, or a plant stand when you're not using it. It's also a solid choice for small spaces where a full ottoman would overwhelm the room.

**Skip it if:** You need a plush, sink-into-it footrest after long workdays, or you have pets that love to scratch textured fabrics. Also skip if you prefer a more structured, non-knit look — the cable pattern is very specific and will anchor your room's vibe whether you want it to or not.
