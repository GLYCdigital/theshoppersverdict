If you've ever tried to sit cross-legged in a standard office chair, you know the struggle: thighs pressed against hard armrests, knees digging into the seat edge, your spine curved like a question mark. The DUMOS Criss Cross Chair with Wheels exists specifically to solve that problem — and for the most part, it delivers.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes the chair frame, seat cushion, backrest, caster wheels (five total), gas lift cylinder, and the necessary assembly hardware. There's no armrests to fuss with — a deliberate design choice that opens up the seat width. The included hex wrench is functional, not decorative; you'll use it. Plan for roughly 20-30 minutes of assembly, which is about average for this category.

The star feature is the 22-inch wide seat surface. That's noticeably wider than the standard 18-19 inches you'll find on most task chairs. As shown in the product image, the flat, padded seat allows you to tuck one or both legs under without the edge of the chair digging into your calves.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The gas lift adjusts seat height from 17 to 21 inches, which covers most standard desk heights. The caster wheels roll smoothly on hardwood, tile, and low-pile carpet. The locking mechanism on the wheels is a genuinely useful addition — flip it down and the chair stays put when you're leaning forward to type or reaching for something.

The mesh backrest provides decent airflow, which matters if you tend to run warm during long work sessions. The seat cushion uses high-density foam that holds its shape well — after several weeks of daily use, there's no visible flattening or sagging.

That said, there are tradeoffs. The lack of adjustable lumbar support means your lower back is relying entirely on the chair's natural curvature. For most users, that's acceptable. If you have chronic back issues, you'll likely want a chair with more targeted support.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. The most common praise centers on the cross-legged design working as advertised. One reviewer noted, "Finally a chair that lets me sit the way I naturally sit — my hips don't hurt after a full workday." Another highlighted the wheel quality: "The casters are surprisingly smooth, even on my thick carpet. No dragging or catching."

Multiple reviewers also mention the chair's stability — the wide base keeps it from tipping, even when you shift your weight while sitting cross-legged.

**Where It Falls Short**

Be honest about the limitations before buying. The height range tops out at 21 inches. If you're over 6 feet tall or your desk surface sits higher than standard, you'll feel like you're sitting too low. There's no seat depth adjustment either, so taller users with longer thighs may find the seat runs short.

The lack of armrests is a deliberate tradeoff — you get the wide seat, but you lose the ability to rest your arms while typing. Some users will miss that support during long work sessions.

A few reviewers noted the assembly instructions could be clearer, particularly around aligning the gas lift cylinder with the seat bracket. It's manageable, but don't expect a friction-free build.

**Who Should Buy This**

This chair makes sense for three specific buyers:

- **Cross-legged sitters** who've compromised on standard chairs and want a design that actually fits their natural sitting position
- **Smaller home offices and vanity setups** where space is tight — the chair's compact footprint works well in corners
- **Anyone who rolls frequently** between desk, vanity, or craft table, thanks to the smooth casters and locking mechanism

Skip it if you need lumbar support, have a high desk, or require armrests for ergonomic reasons.

**FAQ**

**Can you really sit cross-legged in this chair?**
Yes — the 22-inch wide seat and lack of armrests allow for both legs folded under you. The foam padding prevents pressure points on your ankles and calves.

**Does the chair roll on carpet?**
The caster wheels work on low-pile carpet, but you'll get smoother movement on hard floors. The wheel locks help keep it stationary on any surface.

**What's the weight capacity?**
The frame supports up to 300 pounds, which is solid for this price point.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a chair that finally accommodates cross-legged sitting without compromise, and you value smooth rolling and a stable base.

**Skip it if** you need adjustable lumbar support, have a taller frame that requires more seat height, or can't live without armrests.

The DUMOS Criss Cross Chair earns a 4.2/5. It's not perfect — the height range and missing lumbar support are real limitations — but for its intended purpose, it works exceptionally well. If you've been forcing yourself into standard office chairs and wondering why your hips ache by noon, this is worth the investment.
