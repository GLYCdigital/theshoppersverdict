# Rolanstar Computer Desk with Power Outlet & LED Strip and File Drawer: A 4.5-Star Workhorse With One Big Caveat

Most desks in the $150–$250 range promise "built-in power" and deliver a flimsy hub that buzzes. The Rolanstar 68-inch desk actually does it right—but only if you read the assembly notes before you buy.

This is a serious piece of furniture for a home office, not a dorm-room afterthought. The 68" x 24" carbon fiber-textured surface gives you genuine workspace for a dual-monitor setup plus a laptop. The steel frame is rated for 220 pounds, and in testing it didn't wobble even with aggressive typing on a mechanical keyboard.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the desktop, steel legs, a crossbar for stability, the LED strip (pre-mounted under the front edge), the power outlet module, a sliding file drawer, and all hardware. The power hub is the standout: two AC outlets, two USB-A ports, and one USB-C port, all integrated into the desktop itself—no dangling adapter.

The LED strip is controlled by a small remote that also handles brightness. It's warm white only, which is actually a plus for eye strain during late-night work sessions. The file drawer slides on ball bearings and accommodates standard hanging file folders—rare at this price.

## Real-World Performance

**The good:** The power hub delivers consistent charging. I ran a 65W laptop charger through the AC outlet while charging a phone and tablet via USB simultaneously—no voltage drop, no heat issues. The USB-C port outputs 15W, enough for fast-charging modern phones.

Customer reviews back this up. One verified buyer wrote: *"The power outlet is the reason I bought this, and it's been flawless for three months. My laptop charger stays plugged in at the desk, and I never think about it."* Another noted: *"Assembly took me 45 minutes alone, but the instructions were actually clear—unlike most IKEA furniture."*

**The not-so-good:** The cable management tray is my biggest gripe. It's a shallow metal channel that fits standard power strips but *not* bulky laptop chargers. You'll need to zip-tie bricks to the underside of the desk instead. Also, the desk ships with a slight chemical smell from the carbon fiber laminate—it dissipates in 2–3 days with windows open, but it's noticeable initially.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the real friction point. The 68-inch desktop is heavy (about 28 pounds alone), and aligning the pre-drilled holes with the legs requires patience. One reviewer said: *"Do yourself a favor and get a second person. I managed alone, but the desk shifted when I was tightening the last leg."* You'll also need a Phillips screwdriver—the included Allen wrench is fine, but the screws that attach the drawer glides are awkward to reach.

The LED strip isn't RGB. If you wanted color-changing gamer lighting, this isn't it. And the remote is IR, not RF—you must point it at the sensor on the desk's right side.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're setting up a dedicated home office, you hate crawling under your desk to plug in devices, and you want a desk that doesn't wobble. This is ideal for remote workers, students with dual monitors, or anyone who needs a file drawer within arm's reach.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget (there are adequate desks under $100, but they won't have power integration), you need RGB lighting for gaming aesthetics, or you live in a small apartment where assembly space is limited.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the power outlet have surge protection?**
A: No. It's a standard outlet, not a surge protector. For expensive electronics, plug a surge protector into the desk's AC outlet—the built-in USB ports are for convenience, not protection.

**Q: Can the LED strip be replaced if it fails?**
A: Yes—it's a standard 12V LED strip with a connector at the right side. Replacement strips cost $10–15 on Amazon and take five minutes to swap.

**Q: What's the actual desktop thickness?**
A: 0.6 inches of particleboard with a melamine coating. It's not solid wood, but it's denser than most budget desks—the 220-pound weight capacity is realistic, not marketing fluff.

## The Verdict: 4.6/5

This is the best value in the "desk with integrated power" category right now. The power hub is genuinely useful, the file drawer is functional, and the build quality justifies the price. The assembly hassle and shallow cable tray cost it a perfect score, but for most buyers, those are minor annoyances—not dealbreakers.

**Buy it if** you want a sturdy, powered desk that looks professional and handles a real workload. **Skip it if** you need RGB lighting or you're assembling solo in a cramped space. At 4.5 stars from 225+ ratings, the consensus is clear: this desk earns its place in your office.
