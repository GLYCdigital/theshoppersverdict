If your dining room has been drowning in clutter — stacks of plates, that random collection of wine glasses, the table linens with nowhere to go — the ONBRILL 63-inch sideboard is built to solve exactly that problem. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in real homes. But 4.5 stars means some buyers found flaws. Here's the unvarnished picture before you commit.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the sideboard in three main sections (left cabinet, right cabinet, center drawer bank), four tapered gold metal legs, two adjustable shelves, and all hardware. The unit measures 63 inches wide by 15.7 inches deep and stands 31.5 inches tall — a classic mid-century proportions that works under a TV, against a dining room wall, or as a hallway console.

The left cabinet features one adjustable shelf, giving you flexibility between tall bottles and stacked plates. The right cabinet has a fixed shelf. Between them, three soft-close drawers offer catch-all space for cutlery, napkins, or remote controls. The cabinet doors use magnetic catches rather than mechanical latches — a minor detail, but worth knowing.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The frame is engineered wood with a wood-grain PVC veneer, and the legs are solid metal with a gold finish. In person, the veneer does a surprisingly convincing job mimicking oak — several buyers commented they had to touch it to confirm it wasn't real wood. The soft-close drawer mechanism works smoothly; no slamming, no wobble.

One structural note: the back panel is thin hardboard. It's fine for stability once assembled, but it won't stand up to heavy pushing or moisture. If you're placing this near a damp area or in a high-traffic hallway where it might get bumped, keep that in mind.

Assembly is the most polarizing aspect. Most buyers reported 45–60 minutes with two people. The instructions are clear, and pre-drilled holes align properly. But the final step — tipping the assembled cabinet upright — genuinely requires two sets of hands. One reviewer wrote: "Tried to flip it myself and the side panel cracked. My fault for not reading the instructions, but I wish they'd warned me more clearly." Take that seriously.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The storage capacity is the runaway favorite feature. One verified purchase noted: "Fits my entire dinner service for 12 plus a full bar setup in the left cabinet, and the drawers hold all our serving utensils." Another praised the aesthetic payoff: "Looks like a $900 piece from West Elm. Guests always ask where I got it."

The soft-close drawers also earned repeated shout-outs for their smooth action at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the thin back panel and two-person assembly requirement, two issues surface in reviews. First, the gap issue: if your baseboards are thicker than about half an inch, the cabinet won't sit flush against the wall. Plan for a visible gap or add furniture shims. Second, the gold leg finish is prone to minor scratching if you drag the unit across hard flooring — lift, don't slide.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** needing multifunctional storage that doesn't dominate a room — the 63-inch width fits most standard walls without overwhelming them
- **Home entertainers** who need serious dish and glassware capacity with some drawer organization
- **Mid-century modern enthusiasts** on a budget who want the look without the designer price tag

## Skip It If

- You're assembling solo and lack a second person for the final step
- You need water-resistant construction for a sunroom or covered patio
- Your baseboards are unusually thick and you're unwilling to modify them

## FAQ

**Q: Can the shelves support heavy items like a microwave or large mixer?**
A: The manufacturer rates each shelf for approximately 30 pounds. A microwave works fine, but a full-sized stand mixer plus accessories might push it. Use the top surface for lighter décor.

**Q: Does the wood-grain finish scratch easily?**
A: The PVC veneer resists normal wear well — utensils, plates, and glassware won't mark it. But sharp objects like a dropped knife or dragging a metal vase across the top will leave permanent marks. Use felt pads under heavy items.

**Q: What's the actual assembly time with two people?**
A: Most buyers report 45–60 minutes. The drawers come pre-assembled, which saves significant time. Budget an extra 30 minutes if you're a first-time furniture assembler.
