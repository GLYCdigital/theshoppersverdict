If you're a nail tech who's been working off a folding table and a clamp-on lamp, the OmySalon Manicure Table is the kind of upgrade that pays for itself in the first month. This is a purpose-built workstation, not a repurposed desk, and it shows in the details that matter: the glass surface, the wrist rest, and the built-in ventilation.

**What you actually get**

Unboxing reveals a surprisingly heavy package — roughly 45 pounds of MDF frame, tempered glass, and hardware. The box includes the tabletop frame, the 6mm tempered glass top, a padded leather wrist rest, a dust collector unit with flexible hose, a power strip with USB ports, and two side shelves. The glass top is the centerpiece here: it's not a thin overlay but a substantial piece that sits flush on the frame, giving the whole table a premium feel that photographs well for your salon's Instagram.

**Performance and build quality**

The tempered glass surface is the standout feature. It handles acetone spills, gel polish drips, and alcohol wipes without staining or scratching. One reviewer noted, "I've spilled monomer on it twice and it wiped right off — no residue, no clouding." That's something you can't say about most laminate tables in this price range.

The dust collector is genuinely functional, not decorative. The adjustable suction arm pulls acrylic and gel dust away from your breathing zone while you file. It's not industrial-grade — you won't be clearing a full acrylic set with heavy filing — but for routine manicures and light gel work, it keeps the air noticeably cleaner. The motor hums at a low level; it won't bother clients, but you'll hear it working.

The wrist rest deserves special mention. It's padded with a dense foam core and wrapped in a smooth leather-look material. After a four-hour shift, your forearms won't feel like they've been through a marathon. The height is right for most techs — the table sits at 29.5 inches, which works for standard salon chairs.

**Where it falls short**

Assembly is the biggest hurdle. The instructions are adequate but not great, and getting the glass top seated correctly requires a second pair of hands. Budget 45 minutes to an hour, and don't attempt it alone — the glass is heavy and unforgiving if you drop it.

The dust collector's filter is a minor annoyance. It's washable, which is good, but you'll need to clean it weekly if you do multiple clients a day. Let it go for two weeks and suction drops noticeably.

The side shelves are functional but shallow — they hold polish bottles and files fine, but don't expect to stack towels or a lamp on them.

**Who should buy this**

This table is ideal for:
- **Mobile nail techs** who need a sturdy, professional-looking station they can set up at client homes or events
- **Salon owners** outfitting a new station — the glass top and dust collector justify the price over cheaper laminate options
- **Home-based techs** who want to separate work from living space without building a permanent station

Skip it if you're a beginner doing occasional hobby manicures — a simpler desk will do. Also skip if you primarily do acrylic sculpting with heavy filing; you'll want a stronger ventilation system.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can the table support a nail drill and lamp simultaneously?**
Yes. The frame holds up to 110 pounds, and the built-in power strip has three outlets plus two USB ports, so you can run a drill, lamp, and charger without extra extension cords.

**Q: Is the glass top really shatterproof?**
It's 6mm tempered glass, which is the same spec used in shower doors. It won't shatter into dangerous shards if something heavy hits it, but it can chip on the edges if you drop a metal tool on it. Treat the edges with care during assembly.

**Q: Does the dust collector work for gel polish fumes?**
It's designed for dust, not fumes. It will pull some vapor away, but it's not a fume extractor. For gel and monomer fumes, keep your ventilation fan running.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a working nail tech who wants a professional-grade station that looks sharp, cleans up fast, and keeps dust out of your lungs. The glass top and wrist rest alone are worth the investment over cheaper alternatives.

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget, work solo with light client volume, or hate assembling furniture. For everyone else, this is a solid 4.6-star workstation that will last years with proper care.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuine upgrade for working professionals, with minor assembly and maintenance quirks that don't overshadow the overall value.
