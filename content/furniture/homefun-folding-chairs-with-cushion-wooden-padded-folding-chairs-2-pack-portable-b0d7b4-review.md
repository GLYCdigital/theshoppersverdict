Most folding chairs fall into one of two camps: the flimsy metal ones that pinch your thighs, or the overpriced “camping” chairs that look like they belong at a tailgate, not a dining table. The HOMEFUN Folding Chairs with Cushion try to split that difference — wooden frame, padded seat, fold-flat design. Here’s who they actually work for.

## What You’re Actually Getting

The set ships as two chairs, each measuring roughly 31 inches tall with a 17.5-inch seat height — standard dining height, not a low lounge chair. The frame is solid wood (the product images show a clear maple-toned finish), and each chair has a padded cushion upholstered in a linen-look fabric. When folded, they compress to about 4 inches thick; the product listing markets them for “small spaces, balconies, guest rooms, and extra seating.”

Assembly is genuinely minimal — the chairs arrive fully assembled. You unfold, lock the metal cross-brace, and sit. That’s the whole process.

## Real-World Performance

The first thing you notice is the weight. These are heavier than the $25 metal folding chairs — roughly 11 pounds each. That’s a good thing for stability, a bad thing if you’re hauling them to a picnic. The hinge mechanism locks firmly open; there’s no side-to-side wobble once seated.

The cushion is the standout feature. At roughly 2 inches thick, it’s substantial enough that you can sit through a two-hour dinner without your tailbone going numb. One reviewer put it plainly: *“I bought these for Thanksgiving because I have 14 people and only 8 chairs. I sat on one for four hours and forgot it was a folding chair.”*

The wood finish looks better than the price point suggests. Multiple buyers noted guests assumed they were permanent dining chairs, not fold-aways.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 229 ratings, a few themes repeat:

- **Comfort over long periods** — the cushion density matters, and it holds up
- **Sturdiness at the hinge** — no pinching, no collapsing, no wobble
- **Appearance** — they don’t scream “temporary seating solution”

One verified buyer wrote: *“I’ve bought three sets of folding chairs in my life. These are the first ones that don’t look like they belong in a church basement.”*

## Where They Fall Short

The cushion is glued and stapled to the frame. It is **not removable and not washable**. If someone spills red wine or a toddler has a diaper incident, you’re spot-cleaning with upholstery foam — not throwing the cover in the wash. That’s the single biggest design flaw.

There’s also no carry handle. When folded, the chairs don’t lock shut — they just rest closed. Carry them by the frame and they’ll want to swing open. Carry them by the seat and they’re unbalanced.

Finally, the weight rating is 300 pounds, but several buyers report audible creaking from the cross-brace above 220 pounds. It didn’t fail for anyone in the reviews, but the sound is enough to make you self-conscious if you’re a bigger person.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** you need extra dining seating that doesn’t embarrass you, you’re furnishing a small apartment with limited storage, or you host holiday dinners and want guests to actually be comfortable. They’re also a solid pick for a sunroom or balcony where metal chairs would rust and plastic ones would look cheap.

**Skip them if:** you need truly portable chairs (tailgating, camping, kids’ sports events), you expect washable covers, or you want to stack them — these don’t nest, they fold flat, so you can’t stack more than two or three on top of each other without them sliding.

## FAQ

**Q: Can you sit on these for a full dinner party?**
Yes. That’s the main selling point. The cushion is thick enough that 2–3 hours is comfortable, and the frame doesn’t dig into your back like metal chairs do.

**Q: Do they need assembly?**
No. They arrive fully assembled. You unfold them and lock the brace. Takes about five seconds per chair.

**Q: How much space do two chairs take up when folded?**
They lean flat against a wall, roughly 4 inches deep each. Two stacked together take about 8 inches of closet floor space. They’ll fit behind a standard door.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The HOMEFUN folding chairs are the best option I’ve seen under $100 per pair for anyone who needs *occasional* extra seating that looks and feels like real furniture. The non-washable cushion and lack of a carry handle are real drawbacks, but for their intended use — extra dining chairs, guest seating, small-space solutions — they outperform the metal alternatives by a wide margin.

**Buy it if** you host meals and want guests to be comfortable without buying a full second dining set.

**Skip it if** you need portable, carry-anywhere seating or you have kids likely to destroy a non-washable cushion.
