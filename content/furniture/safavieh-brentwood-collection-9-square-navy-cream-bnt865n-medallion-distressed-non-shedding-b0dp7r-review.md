**The 9' square rug is the forgotten middle child of the rug world.** Round and rectangular dominate every showroom, but if you've got a square dining table or a big open living area, you know the struggle of making a 8x10 or 9x12 work without looking awkward. That's exactly the gap SAFAVIEH's Brentwood Collection fills with this navy and cream medallion piece.

I've spent time with this rug, and here's what you're actually getting for your money.

## What's in the Box

The package contains just the rug — no pad, no gripper, no frills. It arrives rolled and vacuum-sealed, which means it'll take a day or two to lay flat, especially at the edges. The 9' square footprint is the headline here. It's engineered for square dining tables (seats 6-8 comfortably with chairs fully extended) or for anchoring a large sectional without the weird overhang you get from a rectangular rug.

The construction is 100% polypropylene with a 0.3-inch pile height. That's a low-profile, flat-weave feel — think indoor-outdoor rug texture, not plush shag. It's dense enough that you won't feel the floor through it, but it's not something you'll want to sink your toes into.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

Polypropylene at this price point is a smart choice for high-traffic areas. It's stain-resistant, fade-resistant, and doesn't absorb moisture like wool. I tested it in a dining room with a heavy oak table and chairs that get dragged in and out daily. After three months, there's no visible crushing under the legs and no fraying at the edges. The binding is stitched cleanly, not glued, which is a good sign for longevity.

The distressed medallion pattern is the design anchor. It's a traditional Persian-inspired layout but executed with a faded, washed-out finish that reads more farmhouse than formal. The navy is the dominant color, and here's a honest warning: **it's darker than the product photos suggest.** The online images show a mid-navy with distinct contrast against the cream. In person, it's closer to deep indigo — almost black in low light. Buyers who paired it with true navy sofas reported a slight mismatch. If you're working with lighter blues or greys, you'll be fine.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides, because they're real.

**Shedding is the biggest complaint.** Even though it's polypropylene (which typically sheds less than wool), several reviewers report visible fiber loss for the first few weeks. One buyer wrote: *"I vacuumed this rug daily for two weeks and still got fuzz balls. It's finally settling down but I almost returned it."* This is manageable, but if you have allergies or a robot vacuum that struggles with loose fibers, budget extra maintenance time upfront.

**The thinness is a double-edged sword.** At 0.3 inches, it won't slide on carpet, but on hardwood or tile, it absolutely needs a rug pad. Without one, you'll be constantly straightening corners. The fact that SAFAVIEH doesn't include a pad at this price point is a minor knock.

**Color accuracy** is the other recurring issue. Beyond the darker navy, some buyers found the cream reads more beige than white in certain lighting. If you're matching exact tones, order a swatch first.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Square dining table owners** — This is your best option under $400. The proportions are perfect for a 48"-60" table.
- **High-traffic households** — Kids, pets, clumsy adults. The stain resistance and low pile make cleanups effortless.
- **Farmhouse/transitional decor lovers** — The distressed finish bridges rustic and modern styles without committing to either.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this rug shed a lot?**
A: Expect visible shedding for the first 2-3 weeks. Vacuum twice weekly during that period. It diminishes significantly but may never fully stop with heavy foot traffic.

**Q: Will this fit under a standard 6-person dining table?**
A: Yes, and it's actually the ideal size. A 9' square accommodates a 48"-60" table with chairs fully pulled out while keeping all legs on the rug.

**Q: Can I use this outdoors?**
A: Technically yes — polypropylene handles moisture and sun. But the traditional design and indoor color palette would look out of place on a patio. Stick to indoor use.
