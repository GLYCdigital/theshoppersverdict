## What You Actually Get

The box drops off with the table, a cushioned stool, a large mirror, and all the hardware. The tabletop is a generous size — you can spread out a makeup case, jewelry organizer, and still have room for a coffee mug (though I’d keep liquids away from the edges). The mirror tilts, which sounds minor, but it’s genuinely useful for finding the right angle without craning your neck.

The stool is where this set surprises. The cushion is thick enough that your tailbone won’t complain during a 40-minute skincare routine. That’s rare at this price point — most bundled stools are an afterthought.

## Build Quality and Daily Use

The frame is engineered wood, and it feels denser than the $100-and-under competition. The table doesn’t wobble when you lean on it, and the legs have a solid footprint. Drawers glide on basic metal slides — they work fine for brushes, palettes, and small accessories, but don’t stuff them full of heavy electronics.

One thing the photos don’t show: the ivory white finish is matte and soft. It scratches if you drag a metal jewelry box across it, and dark denim can transfer dye if you brush against it. A clear protective mat on the tabletop and a touch-up pen in your cart are worth the extra $10.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest hurdle. You’re looking at 45–60 minutes, and the drawer slides are the fiddly part. One reviewer said, *“Getting the drawers aligned took two tries — they’re not self-closing, so you have to be precise.”* Budget for that time, or bribe a friend with pizza.

The drawer slides themselves are the weakest link. They’re not soft-close, and they clack if you push them shut quickly. For daily use, they’ll hold up, but don’t expect premium hardware on a budget piece.
