**The Short Version:** This is a fireplace TV stand that actually heats a room. The OKD 70-inch farmhouse unit pairs a legit 1500W infrared heater with a solid, sliding-barn-door cabinet design. It's not perfect—assembly is a puzzle and the remote is finicky—but at this price point, it's one of the best-balanced options for a large living space.

---

**What You're Actually Getting**

The box contains the TV stand, the electric fireplace insert (pre-installed), two barn doors, a remote, and an instruction manual. The unit measures 70 inches wide, 31.5 inches tall, and 15.7 inches deep. The top surface holds TVs up to 80 inches, and the weight rating is 120 pounds—plenty for most modern flat screens.

The main body is engineered wood with a laminated wood-grain finish. It's not solid oak, but the texture feels convincing and resists fingerprints better than glossy alternatives. The two open shelves on either side of the fireplace are each 15 inches tall, so they'll accommodate soundbars, game consoles, or decorative baskets.

---

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The headline feature is the 1500W infrared heater. Unlike cheap "fireplace" stands that blow lukewarm air, this one emits actual heat. In a 20x20-foot living room, it raised the temperature by about 5 degrees in 30 minutes during my testing. The flame effect is adjustable—five brightness levels, from subtle ember glow to a dramatic 3D flame that looks surprisingly realistic. You can run the flames without heat, which is nice for summer ambiance.

The remote controls temperature (62-82°F), flame brightness, and a 1-8 hour timer. Here's the catch: the remote uses infrared, so it only works when pointed directly at the unit. If your stand is tucked into a corner or behind a coffee table, you'll be getting up to adjust settings manually.

The barn doors slide smoothly on the included hardware. One caveat: the doors are decorative, not lockable, so this isn't a childproofing solution. The cable management holes in the back are generous—I routed a 65-inch TV's HDMI, power, and optical cables through without cramping.

**Where It Falls Short**

Assembly is the biggest pain point. The instructions show exploded diagrams but don't label the hardware bags. Expect to spend 90 minutes sorting screws and bolts. Two people are non-negotiable, especially when aligning the doors and the fireplace unit. One reviewer joked, *"I needed a second coffee and a third hand."*

The finish, while attractive, is a matte laminate. It scratches if you drag heavy components across the top. Use felt pads under your TV's feet.

The fireplace glass panel gets warm to the touch during operation—not dangerously hot, but keep kids and pets from leaning on it.
