Let's be blunt: most bar stools under $100 are wobbly, cheaply welded, and look like they'd collapse under a heavy toddler. The HAOBO Home Industrial Metal Barstools aren't that. After digging through 225 ratings (4.5 stars) and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two pre-assembled stools (the frame and seat come attached), the footrest bar, and an Allen wrench. You're looking at roughly 30 pounds per stool — noticeably heavier than the hollow-tube junk you'll find at big-box stores. The legs are 1.2-inch diameter steel with a matte black powder coat, and the seat is a 12-inch round rubberwood plank.

These are counter-height, meaning the seat sits at 24 inches. That's a match for standard 36-inch kitchen counters and most kitchen islands. If you have a 42-inch bar-height counter, these will feel too low — measure first.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where HAOBO separates itself from the $40 specials. The frame uses welded cross-bracing rather than simple X-brackets bolted on as an afterthought. In practice, that means no lateral sway when you lean back or shift your weight. Multiple buyers confirmed this — one wrote, "I'm 230 pounds and these don't budge. My last set from [big box store] felt like they were going to fold."

The footrest sits 7 inches off the floor, which is actually ergonomically correct for most adults. It's not just decoration — you can rest your feet without your knees hitting the underside of the counter.

The wooden seat is comfortable for about 45 minutes to an hour. It's not padded, so if you're planning marathon game nights, grab a cushion. The seat surface is smooth and splinter-free, and the slight contour (it's not perfectly flat) keeps you from sliding forward.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The most consistent thread in the reviews is the stability. I'm seeing repeated comments like "rock solid" and "no wobble at all." That's rare at this price point. The powder coat finish is also getting positive mention — it doesn't chip when chairs bump into each other, which is the death of cheaper painted stools.

One reviewer noted the stools "look way more expensive than they are" — the industrial aesthetic with exposed bolts and matte black steel reads as intentional design, not cheapness.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The seat finish is vulnerable.** The rubberwood is sealed, but it's not a durable lacquer. A reviewer mentioned that a spilled glass of red wine left a mark within 30 minutes. If you're a messy drinker, apply your own polyurethane coat before first use.

**No floor protection.** The feet are bare metal. On hardwood or tile, you'll want to add felt pads — dragging these will scratch. HAOBO doesn't include them, which feels like an oversight at this price.

**Assembly is easy but not zero.** The footrest requires aligning bolt holes and tightening four bolts. It took me about 12 minutes. Doable, but don't expect it fully assembled.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You have a 36-inch counter or island, want industrial-style seating that won't wobble, and prefer the look of wood over fabric or leather. They're ideal for a kitchen with a concrete or tile floor, a home bar setup, or a loft-style dining area.

**Skip them if:** You need bar-height (30-inch seat) stools, have a wood floor you refuse to add pads to, or want cushioned seating for extended sitting. Also skip if you have small kids who will treat the seat as a climbing toy — the wood can dent if dropped on.
