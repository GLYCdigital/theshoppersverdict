If you've been scrolling past those flimsy $80 TV stands with hollow-core doors and veneer that peels in a year, this YITAHOME unit deserves a second look. It's not premium furniture — but it's a serious step above the budget crowd, and at 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, the buyers mostly agree.

**What actually shows up**

The package is heavy — around 55 pounds — and arrives in two boxes. You get the main cabinet body, two side cabinets, a center open shelf, and the hardware bag. The top panel comes pre-drilled for a 65-inch TV base, and there's a separate cable management hole cut into the back panel, not just a notch. Small detail, but it means your HDMI and power cables route cleanly instead of pinching against the wall.

The finish is a matte walnut-style laminate over a wood-grain texture. It's not real walnut, obviously, but the grain pattern is printed to look natural, and the edges are banded properly rather than showing raw MDF. One buyer noted, "Looks way more expensive than it is — my friends assumed I spent $400+ on it." That tracks with the overall rating.

**How it holds up in real use**

The frame is heavy-duty particleboard with a solid MDF top. The two closed cabinets have soft-close hinges — a feature you rarely see at this price point — and the doors stay aligned after a few months of use. The center open shelf has a 35-pound weight limit, which is fine for a soundbar and a small receiver, but don't pile a heavy amplifier and a turntable on it.

Cable management is genuinely well-thought-out. Each of the three sections has a grommet hole in the back, so you can run cables from the TV down to the shelves without them dangling visibly. That said, the open shelf means your devices are on display — if you're messy with wires, this stand will show it.

Assembly is the biggest friction point. The cam-lock hardware is standard, but the instruction manual is diagram-only, and there are a lot of pieces. Plan for 45–60 minutes with a second person holding panels while you align the cams. One reviewer put it bluntly: "Took me two hours and I have three extra screws. It works, but I wouldn't wish the build on anyone."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The storage capacity gets the most love. The two side cabinets are deep enough to hold a full-size AV receiver, cable box, and a gaming console stacked, with room left over for controllers. As one 5-star review put it, "Finally a stand that hides everything — no more tangled mess behind the TV."

Sturdiness also comes up repeatedly. The unit is rated for 65-inch TVs, but buyers report mounting 70-inch models without wobble, as long as the TV sits within the center section's footprint.

**Where it falls short**

The 35-pound center shelf limit is the real constraint. If you own a heavy center-channel speaker or a vintage amplifier, that shelf will sag within a year. Also, the back panel is thin fiberboard — it's fine for cable routing, but don't lean anything against it. And there's no wall-mount strap included, so if you have kids or pets, you'll want to buy a furniture-anchoring kit separately.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers** who need closed storage to hide gear and keep the living room looking tidy.
- **First-time furniture buyers** who want something that looks like real wood without the $600 price tag.
- **Gamers or cord-cutters** with multiple devices who need organized cable management.

Skip it if you need a massive media wall for a 75-inch TV, or if you can't handle a 45-minute IKEA-style assembly session with a friend.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this hold a 65-inch TV safely?**
Yes, the top is rated for the weight of most 65-inch TVs (up to about 60 lbs). The center section is 45 inches wide, so a TV with a wide stand base will fit without overhanging.

**Q: Is the cabinet easy to clean?**
The laminate surface wipes down with a damp cloth. It's resistant to fingerprints and minor scuffs, but it's not waterproof — use a coaster for drinks.

**Q: Can I skip assembling the side cabinets?**
The unit is designed to be assembled as one piece. You could theoretically leave off one side cabinet, but the top panel is cut as a single piece, so it won't look right.
