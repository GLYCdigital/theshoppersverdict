**Is this the “cheaper Aeron” you’ve been eyeing? Yes — but only if you know what you’re giving up.**  

The Mirra is Herman Miller’s “second chair,” often bought by people who want the Aeron’s pedigree without the $1,500 price tag. This particular listing—the “Fully Featured W Forward Tilt Adjustable Arms” config—is the mid-tier trim with the most useful ergonomic features. Here’s the real-world breakdown after testing it for three weeks and cross-referencing owner feedback.

## What’s actually in the box (and what’s not)  
You get the chair fully assembled, which is a relief—no Allen wrench marathons. The base is polished aluminum, the casters roll smoothly on carpet and hardwood alike (dual-wheel design). The armrests are the “fully adjustable” type: they move up/down, slide in/out, and rotate ~30 degrees inward. The forward tilt lever sits on the right side; pull it and the whole seat tips ~5 degrees, pushing your hips forward. The backrest is the signature Butterfly back—a flexible mesh-like polymer that flexes with your spine.  

Missing from this config: **no adjustable lumbar support.** That’s a separate $100 add-on. The seat is a foam-injected cushion, not the Aeron’s Pellicle mesh.

## Real-world performance: 8-hour days, tested  
The forward tilt is the headline feature, and it’s genuinely useful. When you lean into it, it stops the slouch-hunch cycle—your core engages automatically. I found it ideal for focused typing sessions, less so for reading or reclining. The armrests are the best part of the chair: they lock tight with zero wobble, unlike the Aeron’s plastic arms that flex under pressure.  

The Butterfly back is a love-it-or-hate-it tradeoff. It flexes with your spine, which feels great for people who move around a lot. But it’s **not** adjustable—if you need extra lumbar pressure, this chair won’t give it to you. The seat cushion is the weakest link. At 185 lbs, I felt the foam compress noticeably by week three. Owners on forums report that heavier users (200+ lbs) start feeling the plastic seat pan underneath within a year.

## What buyers consistently say  
Positive themes dominate, but with caveats:  
- *“The forward tilt saved my back—I can type for hours without my shoulders creeping up.”*  
- *“Armrests are rock solid. Better than the Aeron’s, honestly.”*  
- *“If you have a herniated disc, skip this. No lumbar adjustability is a dealbreaker.”*  

The most common complaint? **Price for what you get.** At $800–$1,000 used (new is ~$1,200), buyers expect perfection. The thin seat foam and lack of lumbar adjustment feel like cost-cutting, and replacement parts are pricey—a new armrest runs ~$150.

## Where it falls short  
- **No lumbar adjustment:** The Butterfly back’s curve is fixed. If your spine needs more or less support, you’re out of luck.  
- **Seat foam longevity:** Multiple owners report the cushion compressing within 12–18 months. It’s not a plush seat—it’s firm, and that firmness fades.  
- **Armrest replacement cost:** They’re durable, but if one breaks, you’re spending 15% of the chair’s used price on a single part.  

## Who should buy this  
- **Desk workers who type 6+ hours/day** and want forward tilt to engage their core.  
- **People who move around a lot** while sitting—the Butterfly back flexes with you.  
- **Anyone who’s tried the Aeron and found it too stiff or too mesh-heavy.**  

**Skip it if:** You have existing lower-back issues (get the Aeron or add the lumbar kit), you’re over 220 lbs (seat foam will disappoint), or you prefer a cushioned, plush seat (this is firm, not soft).

## FAQ: 3 questions shoppers actually ask  
**Q: Is this better than the Aeron?**  
A: Not better—different. The Aeron has adjustable lumbar and a mesh seat that breathes better. The Mirra wins on armrests and forward tilt. If you have back pain, pick the Aeron. If you want core engagement, pick the Mirra.

**Q: Does the forward tilt really work?**  
A: Yes, but it’s not magic. It tips your pelvis forward, which naturally straightens your spine. It’s great for active sitting, but you’ll still need to take breaks.

**Q: Is this chair worth buying used?**  
A: Yes, if you can find one under $700. Check the seat foam for compression (press down—if it doesn’t rebound, walk away) and test the forward tilt mechanism for stiffness.

## The Verdict  
**Buy it if:** You want a well-built ergonomic chair with exceptional armrests and forward tilt, and you don’t need lumbar adjustability.  
**Skip it if:** You have back issues, you’re heavy-set, or you want a plush cushion.  

**Rating: 4.0/5** — A great chair for the right person, held back by a thin seat and missing lumbar support. If you can find one used with the lumbar kit included, it’s a 4.5.
