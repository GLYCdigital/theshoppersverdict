## The Cot That Finally Gets Height Right

Most camping cots are either flimsy pool loungers or coffin-close to the ground. The Sportneer Military Cot splits that difference with a 78.3-inch frame that sits high enough to slide a storage bin underneath — a detail that matters more than you'd think after three days in the woods.

It's rated for 550 pounds, which sounds like marketing until you feel the 1-inch steel tubing. This isn't a featherweight ultralight cot; it's a proper sleep system for anyone who wants to wake up without a sore back.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the cot frame, the 600D Oxford fabric sleeping surface (not the cheap mesh you see on budget cots), and a padded carry bag. Assembly is tool-free: unfold the X-frame legs, lock the side rails into place, and you're done. First-time setup took me about 90 seconds. Breaking it down into the 40-inch carry roll takes slightly longer the first time until you figure out the folding sequence.

The fabric is thick enough that you won't feel the bar across your lower back — a common complaint on cheaper cots. The double-stitched seams and reinforced corners suggest this will survive more than a few seasons of weekend trips.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this cot on gravel, packed dirt, and a cabin floor. The feet are wide enough not to sink into soft ground, and the height (roughly 16 inches off the ground) makes getting in and out genuinely easy for older campers or anyone with knee issues.

The 78.3-inch length is the standout feature. At 6'1", I had room to spare. If you're over 6'4", you'll still be fine unless you stretch out completely. Side sleepers should note: the surface is firm, so bring a sleeping pad unless you're a committed back sleeper.

One design detail worth mentioning: the side rails have a slight lip that keeps the fabric from slipping, but they also mean the edges are slightly raised. Roll over too enthusiastically and you'll notice the bump. A pad solves this entirely — and makes the cot noticeably more comfortable.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs you should know before buying:

- **Weight**: At about 20 pounds, this is strictly car camping, RV, or overlanding gear. Backpackers should look at ultralight options that cost more and hold less.
- **Cold weather**: The open underside means cold air circulates freely. Below 40°F, you'll want an insulated pad underneath you.
- **No pillow**: The head area is flat. Bring your own pillow or roll up a jacket.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a car camper, RVer, or overlander who wants a cot that's quick to set up, supports heavy weight, and fits tall bodies. It's also ideal for emergency preparedness kits or guest sleepovers where an air mattress always seems to deflate.

**Skip it if** you're backpacking, need a cot under 10 pounds, or you're a strict side sleeper who refuses to use a pad. This is a luxury car-camping cot, not a wilderness solution.

## FAQ

**Q: Can two people sit on it?**
A: The 550-pound rating means yes, but the 25-inch width makes it a one-person sleeping surface. Sitting two across works fine.

**Q: How does it handle uneven ground?**
A: Decently. Legs are independent and self-adjusting to a point. If you're on a serious slope, you'll want to level the site first.

**Q: Is the fabric waterproof?**
A: It's water-resistant, not waterproof. A spilled drink beads up, but rain pooling on it will eventually soak through. Use a tarp for full protection.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Sportneer Military Cot nails the essentials: heavy-duty construction, ample length, and tool-free setup. It's not the lightest or cheapest cot on the market, but it's one of the best value propositions for anyone who sleeps in a tent but doesn't want to sleep on the ground.
