# HOMREST Heated Massage Office Chair Review: Real Relief or Gimmick?

You've stared at that stiff-backed office chair for two years. Your lower back screams by 3 PM, and you've already tried two lumbar pillows that slid down your spine. The HOMREST Heated Massage Executive Chair promises something different: built-in heat, an 8-point massage system, and a frame rated for users up to 400 pounds. At under $300, that's a bold claim. Here's whether it delivers.

## What You Actually Get

The box is heavy—about 55 pounds—so plan for a two-person carry. Assembly takes roughly 40 minutes with the included hex wrench and illustrated instructions. Nothing's ambiguous, though the screws require firm torque. As shown in the product image, you're getting a high-back chair with a segmented PU leather seat, flip-up armrests, a separate headrest pillow, and a wired remote that attaches to the side.

The massage unit is the headline. It's positioned in the lumbar and mid-back zones, not the seat. You get three intensity levels and a heat function that warms the lower back area. The remote is straightforward—no app, no Bluetooth, just buttons. That's refreshing.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 400-pound weight capacity comes from a reinforced steel base and a 21.7-inch-wide seat. That's genuinely wider than most executive chairs in this price range. The pneumatic gas lift is smooth, and the tilt mechanism locks at three positions. The headrest pillow is memory foam and actually contours to your neck—not the flimsy stuff you usually get.

The massage function is the differentiator. It's a rolling, kneading motion that cycles through eight points along your spine. On the lowest setting, it's subtle enough to use while typing. On high, it's firm—almost aggressive. The heat warms up in about two minutes and stays consistent. One buyer reported, "The heat and massage combo genuinely relieved my sciatica flare-ups during a 10-hour workday." Another noted, "It's not a spa chair, but for the price, the massage is shockingly effective."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scanning through the 210 ratings, three themes emerge. First, the massage-heat combination is the reason most people bought it, and it doesn't disappoint. Second, the width and weight capacity are repeatedly cited by larger users who previously couldn't find a chair that fit. Third, assembly—while time-consuming—results in a stable, wobble-free chair.

## Where It Falls Short

The PU leather is the weakest link. It's stiff for the first two weeks and doesn't breathe. If you work in a warm office or run hot, your back will sweat against the material. Multiple reviewers mentioned this. Also, the massage motors emit a low hum—not loud, but noticeable in a silent room. If you take calls or record audio, that's a problem. The armrests are padded but not adjustable in height, which limits ergonomic positioning for taller users. And the flip-up feature is handy for sliding the chair under a desk, but the padding is thin.

## Who Should Buy This

This chair fits three specific buyers. The big-and-tall professional who's been squeezed into standard chairs and needs real width. The chronic back-pain sufferer who wants heat and massage without spending $1,000 on a dedicated massage chair. And the home-office worker who logs 8+ hours and wants a chair that does more than hold them up.

Skip it if you need breathable fabric, if your office is dead silent, or if you want adjustable lumbar support. The lumbar pillow is fixed-position—no vertical adjustment.

## FAQ

**Does the massage function work while the chair is reclined?** Yes, but the intensity diminishes slightly at full recline because the rollers lose contact with your back. Best results are upright or at a slight tilt.

**How loud is the massage motor?** Around 45–50 decibels—comparable to a low-volume fan. Most buyers found it acceptable, but two reviewers specifically mentioned it bothered them.

**Can you replace the PU leather if it peels?** Not easily. It's bonded to the foam. With standard use, expect 2–3 years before visible wear. Use a leather conditioner monthly to extend that.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a larger-framed user who wants heat and massage without spending four figures, or you're willing to trade breathability for genuine back relief.

**Skip it if:** You work in a hot or silent environment, or you need adjustable lumbar positioning.

At 4.2 stars, the HOMREST is a solid value that outperforms its price point in massage function and weight capacity—but it's not a premium ergonomic chair. The heat alone is worth the cost for anyone with chronic lower-back tension. Just give the leather a few weeks to break in.
