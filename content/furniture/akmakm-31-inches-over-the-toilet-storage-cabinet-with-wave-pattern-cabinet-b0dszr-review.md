Let me be blunt about the over-the-toilet cabinet category: most of them look like cheap dorm-room afterthoughts. The AKMAKM 31-inch version is different. Not dramatically different — it's still a flat-pack particle board piece — but the wave-pattern door fronts and the proportions make it one of the better-looking options under $100 (assuming pricing stays in that range).

**What you're actually getting**

The package includes the cabinet body, three shelves (one interior shelf per cabinet side, plus a bottom open shelf), two cabinet doors with the signature wave design, and all mounting hardware. The 31-inch height is the sweet spot — tall enough to clear most standard toilet tanks, short enough that it doesn't loom over the room. The depth is 11.8 inches, which means it won't interfere with your seat. You also get a top surface that's actually usable for decorative items or a small plant, which is more than many competitors offer.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The cabinet uses engineered wood with a white laminate finish. The wave pattern isn't a sticker — it's pressed into the panel surface, which means it won't peel or chip like some glued-on decals do. The hinges are metal and feel reasonably sturdy; they won't sag after six months of use like some budget cabinets I've tested.

Assembly is the main friction point. It's not difficult, but it's tedious — you're looking at 45 minutes minimum, and the included Allen wrench is the weak point. Use your own screwdriver with a hex bit and you'll save yourself 15 minutes of frustration. The instructions are mostly diagrams with minimal text, which is fine, but the labeling on panels could be clearer. One buyer noted: "The instructions are a bit confusing at first, but once you figure out which panel goes where, it's straightforward."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The space-saving function is the headline. In a small bathroom, this thing transforms vertical dead space into actual storage. Multiple reviewers mention fitting toilet paper, cleaning supplies, and toiletries with room to spare. Here's what one verified buyer said: "I was skeptical about the quality for the price, but it looks much more expensive than it is. The wave design really elevates it from typical bathroom storage."

Another common thread: the open bottom shelf is the MVP. It's perfect for the stuff you grab daily — plunger, extra rolls, air freshener — while the closed cabinets hide the ugly stuff. Smart design choice.

**Where it falls short**

The laminate finish is not waterproof. This is the biggest caveat. A bathroom is a humid environment, and if you regularly splash water on the bottom panel without wiping it down, you'll eventually see swelling. One reviewer warned: "Don't let water sit on the bottom shelf. I learned the hard way after a leaky bottle ruined the finish." Use a mat or tray on the bottom shelf and you'll be fine.

Also, the included wall-anchoring straps are a must. The cabinet is stable when fully assembled, but with the top shelf loaded, it can tip if a kid pulls on it. Anchor it — it takes two minutes and isn't optional.

**Who should buy this**

This is for anyone with a compact bathroom who's tired of clutter on the tank top. It's also a strong pick for renters — the 31-inch size mounts without permanent modification to the toilet itself, just the wall. If you want something that looks decent, stores a lot, and won't break the bank, this fits.

Skip it if you're expecting solid wood furniture or if your bathroom has a humidity problem you can't control. Also skip if you hate assembly — this isn't a 10-minute setup.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit a standard toilet?**
A: The 31-inch height and 11.8-inch depth accommodate most standard toilets. Measure from the floor to the top of your tank first — if that's under 28 inches, you're fine. Elongated bowls are okay since the cabinet mounts to the wall, not the toilet.

**Q: Can it hold a lot of weight?**
A: Each shelf is rated for about 15 pounds. That's fine for towels, toilet paper, and bottles. Don't put heavy books or tools on it — the particle board will flex.

**Q: How hard is assembly really?**
A: Plan for 45-60 minutes. It's mostly screwing panels together with cam locks. The included Allen wrench is junk — have your own tools ready. Two people make it easier but it's doable solo.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need vertical storage in a small bathroom, want something that looks pricier than it is, and don't mind an hour of assembly.

**Skip it if:** Your bathroom has serious moisture issues, you hate particle board, or you want a piece you'll never have to think about again.

This is a solid 4-star product. It does exactly what it claims, looks better than most competitors, and the price is right. Just anchor it to the wall, keep water off the bottom shelf, and you'll get years of use.
