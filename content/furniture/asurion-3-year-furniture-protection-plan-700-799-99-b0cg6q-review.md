# ASURION 3 Year Furniture Protection Plan Review: Is the Peace of Mind Worth $700+?

Here's the uncomfortable truth about furniture protection plans: they're insurance, and insurance companies make money by denying claims. The ASURION 3 Year Furniture Protection Plan for items in the $700-$799.99 range costs roughly the same as a nice dinner for two — but unlike that dinner, you might never get anything out of it.

With a 3.0-star average across 150+ verified ratings, this plan sits in a murky middle ground. It's not a scam, but it's not the safety net the marketing suggests. Let me break down what you're actually paying for.

## What You're Actually Buying

This plan covers furniture priced between $700 and $799.99 for three years from delivery. The coverage includes:

- **Structural failures** — broken frames, sagging springs, joint separation
- **Fabric damage** — accidental stains, rips, and tears
- **Mechanical components** — recliner mechanisms, swivel bases, lift functions

You file claims directly through your Amazon account. No phone calls to a third-party call center, no waiting on hold for 45 minutes. That's genuinely better than most extended warranty programs.

## The Real-World Experience

Here's where things get complicated. The 3.0-star rating isn't random noise. Reading through customer feedback reveals a consistent pattern: claims get denied for reasons that feel arbitrary.

One buyer wrote: *"My sofa's frame cracked after 14 months. ASURION said it was 'normal wear and tear' and refused the claim. The frame shouldn't crack in 14 months."*

Another: *"Stain protection didn't work. Wine spilled, I filed a claim within a week, and they asked for a professional cleaning receipt first. Then they said the stain wasn't 'significant enough.'"*

The coverage language matters. ASURION explicitly excludes "gradual deterioration" and "normal wear and tear." In practice, that gives them plenty of room to deny claims — because what furniture owner can prove a rip wasn't gradual?

## What Buyers Actually Praise

To be fair, some customers had positive experiences. The claims that get approved tend to be:

- **Obvious structural failures** — a recliner that stops reclining, a bed frame that snaps
- **Large, visible damage** — a pet tear that spans several inches, a chemical burn spot
- **Early claims** — filed within the first 6-8 months of ownership

One satisfied buyer noted: *"Our sectional's stitching came apart at the seams after 8 months. Claim was approved in 4 days, and they sent a repair technician. Took two visits but it's fixed."*

The approved repairs are handled by local technicians, not shipped back to a warehouse. That's a plus if you don't want to crate up a bulky sofa.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest issue is value. This plan costs roughly 12-15% of the furniture's price. For comparison, most furniture retailers offer their own extended protection plans at 8-10% of the purchase price. You're paying a premium for the Amazon integration — and the 3.0-star rating suggests that premium doesn't translate to better service.

There's also a 30-day waiting period before coverage kicks in. If your $750 sofa arrives with a manufacturing defect, you're dealing with the seller, not ASURION. The plan only covers issues that emerge after day 31.

And here's a critical detail: the $700-$799.99 range is oddly specific. If your furniture costs $699.99, this plan likely won't match. You need to verify the exact price point before adding it to your cart.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Pet owners** with cats that scratch or dogs that chew — if the damage is obvious and documented, you'll likely get covered
- **Families with young kids** — juice spills, marker stains, and jumping-induced frame damage are common enough to justify the cost
- **Buyers of recliners or power furniture** — mechanical failures are the most consistently approved claims

## Who Should Skip It

- **Anyone buying a solid wood piece** — hardwood frames rarely fail within 3 years
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** — put the 12-15% premium toward a better-built piece of furniture instead
- **Anyone who reads the fine print carefully** — the exclusions for "wear and tear" give ASURION broad discretion

## FAQ

**Q: Does this cover pet damage?**
A: It covers accidental tears and scratches if they're documented with photos and submitted within 30 days of the damage occurring. But intentional chewing or scratching may be classified as "pet damage" and denied. File the claim immediately.

**Q: What's the process for filing a claim?**
A: Log into your Amazon account, go to Your Orders, find the plan, and click "File a Claim." You'll upload photos and a description. Expect an initial response within 2-3 business days. Approved claims involve a technician visit within 1-2 weeks.

**Q: Can I get a refund if I never use the plan?**
A: No. The plan is non-refundable after the 30-day cooling-off period. If you cancel within the first 30 days, you get a full refund. After that, you're committed.
