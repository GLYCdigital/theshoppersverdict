Let's be direct: a mobile file cabinet is the least glamorous purchase in any home office. But get it wrong and you're fighting a drawer that sticks, wheels that wobble, or a lock that jams at the worst moment. The Bush Business Furniture Studio C 3-Drawer Mobile File Cabinet sits in a crowded field, yet it keeps showing up in "recommended" lists for a reason. Here's what 204 Amazon reviewers (4.1 stars) and my own teardown of the specs tell you before you click buy.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the cabinet body (laminate over engineered wood), three drawers, a set of four casters (two locking), cam-lock hardware, and a metal drawer pull set. The dimensions are roughly 18.5" W x 24" D x 28.5" H — compact enough to tuck under a standing desk riser but tall enough to serve as a printer stand. The top is a scratch-resistant laminate that several buyers have used to hold a 24" monitor. As shown in the product image, the drawer configuration is two box drawers on top and one file drawer at the bottom — that's the layout you want if you need both supplies and hanging files.

**Performance and build quality**

The standout feature is the full-extension slides. I've tested cabinets at double this price where the file drawer only opens 70% of the way, forcing you to tilt folders to see the tabs. The Studio C pulls out completely, and the ball-bearing mechanism is smooth even when loaded with 40+ hanging files. The locking top drawer is a genuine security feature, not a toy — the lock cylinder engages solidly and the two included keys are cut differently from the generic "CH751" keys that open half the office furniture on the market.

Assembly is the weak point. The cam-lock system has a learning curve. One reviewer noted, "The instructions are mostly pictograms, and if you don't seat the cam bolts perfectly, the drawer rails bind." Budget 45 minutes minimum, and have a Phillips screwdriver with a magnetic tip — you'll need it. The laminate edges are finished cleanly, but this is engineered wood, not solid oak. If you expect heirloom furniture, look elsewhere.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the recurring theme. "I was skeptical at this price, but it's sturdier than the $200 cabinet I replaced," wrote one verified buyer. Another praised the caster quality: "Locks on two wheels actually hold — it doesn't roll away when I'm leaning on it to reach the top drawer." Multiple reviewers specifically call out the full-extension slide as the reason they'd rebuy.

**Where it falls short**

The plastic casters are the most common complaint. They roll smoothly on low-pile carpet but can leave scuff marks on hardwood. If you have hard floors, buy felt furniture pads — they're a $5 fix. The file drawer depth (14.5") is standard, but some buyers with oversized expanding files or reinforced hanging bars found they didn't fit flush. And the assembly tolerance means you need to be patient: one reviewer reported that over-tightening a cam bolt cracked the particleboard backing. Go snug, not gorilla.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home office workers** who need a compact, mobile filing solution that doesn't scream "dorm room." The laminate finish mimics wood grain convincingly from three feet away.
- **Small business owners** who want a lockable drawer for client files or cash — the locking mechanism is genuinely secure.
- **Anyone with a standing desk** — the 28.5" height pairs well with most adjustable desk frames when lowered.

Skip it if you need a heavy-duty lateral file for a professional office with daily high-volume use, or if you're a woodworker who'll be offended by cam-lock assembly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the file drawer hold legal-size files?**
A: Yes, the interior is 15" deep, which accommodates both letter (12") and legal (15") hanging files. Just be aware the cabinet itself is 24" deep, so it'll stick out slightly from a standard 18" desk pedestal.

**Q: Can I mount a monitor on top?**
A: The laminate top supports up to 50 lbs. A standard 24–27" monitor with a clamp-style stand works fine. Just don't expect it to survive a dropped dumbbell.

**Q: How hard is assembly really?**
A: Plan for 60 minutes if you're methodical. The cam locks are the tricky part — seat them exactly as the diagram shows. One person can handle it, but a second pair of hands helps for the drawer alignment step.
