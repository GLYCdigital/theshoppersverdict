The Alolo Drafting Chair-907 occupies a weird middle ground. It's not cheap enough to be disposable, not expensive enough to be "serious" ergonomic equipment. At 3.6 stars from 180 ratings, the truth is in the distribution — plenty of people love it, plenty regret it. Here's what you need to know before clicking buy.

## What you actually get

The box contains the chair disassembled: seat base, backrest, pneumatic cylinder, five caster wheels, the distinctive foot ring, and armrests. The build is straightforward — aluminum base, mesh back, padded seat with roughly 3 inches of cushion foam. As shown in the product image, the foot ring is angled for ergonomic foot placement, which is a thoughtful touch you don't see on most drafting chairs under $200.

The headline spec is the seat height range: roughly 24 to 34 inches. That's genuinely tall. If you're working at a standing desk or a traditional drafting table, this is one of the few chairs that actually gets you to the right height without a booster seat.

## How it holds up in real use

The first impression is decent. The mesh back breathes well, the seat padding is firm but tolerable for a full workday, and the pneumatic lift adjusts smoothly when new. The casters roll fine on hard floors and low-pile carpet.

But here's where the 3.6-star rating starts making sense. A significant cluster of reviews mention the gas cylinder failing after 6–9 months of daily use. One buyer put it bluntly: "Chair dropped to the lowest setting in month 7 and would not stay up. Contacted seller, got no response." That's not an isolated complaint — it's a pattern.

The armrests are another weak point. They're attached with hardware that develops lateral play quickly. They won't snap off, but they'll wobble. If you rest your elbows while working, you'll feel it.

## What buyers consistently praise

The height range is the star. Multiple reviewers specifically call out using this with standing desks and drafting tables. One verified purchase wrote: "Finally a chair that actually goes high enough for my 42-inch desk. Most 'drafting' chairs top out way too low."

The foot ring also gets consistent love. It's positioned at an angle that actually supports your feet at drafting height, which reduces leg strain during long sessions. That's a detail most budget chairs skip entirely.

## Where it falls short

Beyond the cylinder and armrest issues, the assembly experience frustrates people. The instructions are minimal — you'll likely figure out the gas spring installation through trial and error. It's not hard, but it's not the "15 minutes" the listing claims.

The seat cushion also compresses noticeably within a few months. Heavier users (over 200 lbs) report bottoming out on the seat pan. The chair's advertised weight capacity is 250 lbs, but reviews suggest it's more comfortable under 220.

## Who should buy this

This chair makes sense for three specific people: students or hobbyists who need drafting height occasionally, shorter users who need the low end of the range, and anyone on a budget who understands they're buying a 2-year chair, not a 10-year chair.

Skip it if you're a full-time designer or drafter working 40+ hours weekly. The cylinder failure pattern is too risky for that use case. Also skip if you're over 220 lbs — the cushion and base just aren't built for sustained heavier loads.

## FAQ

**Is the Alolo Drafting Chair-907 good for a standing desk?**
Yes, at least initially. The 34-inch max height pairs well with 40–42 inch standing desks. Just be aware the cylinder may lose height retention over time.

**Does the foot ring actually help?**
Yes. It's angled properly for drafting posture and takes real pressure off your legs. This is one of the better-designed foot rings in this price bracket.

**Will it fit a 300 lb person?**
Probably not comfortably. The rated capacity is 250 lbs, but customer feedback suggests the seat cushion compresses significantly well below that. Treat 220 lbs as the realistic limit.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need occasional drafting height, you're under 220 lbs, and you accept this as a 1–2 year budget chair.

**Skip it if** you're a full-time professional, over 220 lbs, or you need a chair that won't fail you mid-project when the cylinder gives out.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The height range and foot ring are genuinely good, but the reliability concerns are real. It's a fair chair at a fair price, with known weaknesses you should walk into with open eyes.
