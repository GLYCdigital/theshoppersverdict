If you've ever sat on a typical folding chair — the metal-framed, nylon-webbed kind that digs into your thighs — you know why most people hide them in the garage. The GOFLAME Folding Dining Chairs Set of 2 tries to fix that with actual padding and a furniture-grade silhouette. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it a permanent dining solution or just a fancy spare chair? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

Two identical chairs, each measuring roughly 21.5" W x 23" D x 34" H when open. The seat height lands at a standard 18 inches, which aligns with most dining tables — no awkward knee-bumping. The upholstery is a bonded leather (PU) over a padded seat and backrest, with a powder-coated steel frame underneath.

As shown in the product image, the design mimics a traditional tufted dining chair rather than screaming "folding furniture." The backrest has a slight curve, and the arms are padded too — a detail you don't expect at this price point. When folded, each chair compresses to about 6 inches deep, which means you can stash both in a coat closet or slide them under a bed.

## Real-World Performance

I'll be direct: these chairs are heavier than you'd expect — about 12 pounds each. That's a tradeoff. The weight gives them stability, so they don't wobble when you lean back, but it also makes them less portable than a camping chair. If you're hauling them to a patio or a relative's house, you'll feel it.

The 300-pound weight capacity is the standout spec here. Most folding chairs cap out around 250 pounds. That extra margin means these handle larger-framed adults without creaking or bowing. The steel frame uses cross-bracing underneath, which distributes weight better than the single-rail designs you see on cheaper foldables.

Assembly is minimal but real. Each chair comes with the legs detached, and you'll need to bolt them on with the included hex wrench. Expect 10–15 minutes per chair. It's not hard, but don't plan on using them the second the box arrives.

## What Buyers Consistent Praise

The overwhelming theme in customer feedback is comfort — specifically, that these don't feel like folding chairs. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"I bought these for Thanksgiving and ended up keeping them at the table permanently. Guests kept asking where I got them."* Another noted, *"The padding is actually thick enough that you can sit for hours without your back hurting."* That's a recurring sentiment across the reviews — people who bought them as temporary seating ended up integrating them into their daily setup.

The fold-flat feature also gets consistent credit, especially from apartment dwellers. *"I live in a 600-square-foot studio. These fold up and go behind my door when I don't need them. Total space saver,"* one reviewer wrote.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the bonded leather. It's a budget material — it looks fine for the first few months, but it's prone to peeling and cracking with heavy use, especially around the armrests where your skin makes constant contact. If you have kids who drag their fingers along the seat edges, expect visible wear faster. Several long-term reviews mention this exact issue at the 8–12 month mark.

The seat width is also snug. At 18.5 inches across, it's fine for average builds, but broader-framed users might feel pinched at the hips. The armrests are fixed, so there's no give there.

One more thing: the legs don't have floor glides. On hardwood or tile, the metal feet can scratch if you drag the chairs. A set of felt pads costs $5 and solves this — worth doing immediately.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Apartment dwellers** who need extra seating but can't dedicate permanent floor space
- **People who host occasional dinners** (holidays, game nights) and want chairs that don't look like folding furniture during the event
- **Anyone with guests over 250 pounds** — the 300-pound rating gives real peace of mind

**Skip these if** you need a permanent everyday dining set. The bonded leather won't hold up to three meals a day for years. For that, spend more on a solid-wood chair with fabric upholstery. These are excellent *supplemental* chairs, not a lifetime purchase.
