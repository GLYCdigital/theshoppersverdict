# iComfort i6000 Series Big & Tall Ergonomic Bonded Leather Hi

If you're over 6-foot-2 or north of 250 pounds, you already know the struggle: most "executive" chairs are built for someone half your size. The iComfort i6000 Series Big & Tall chair is one of the few that takes larger frames seriously — and it mostly delivers. But it's not without compromises.

## What You Actually Get

The box is heavy — about 65 pounds. Inside you'll find the chair back, seat base, five-star nylon base, five caster wheels, pneumatic gas lift cylinder, and two fixed armrests. Assembly is doable with basic tools (an Allen wrench is included), but budget 45 minutes minimum. The instructions are minimalist line drawings; if you've built IKEA furniture, you'll manage.

The build itself is substantial: a 22-inch wide seat and 400-pound rated weight capacity, backed by a steel frame. The bonded leather is triple-ply over high-density foam — denser than the cheap stuff on typical $200 chairs. The high back extends to 30 inches, with a headrest that actually reaches your neck if you're tall.

## Performance and Real-World Comfort

Here's where this chair earns its keep. The seat cushion uses high-density cold-cure foam that resists bottoming out. One reviewer put it plainly: "I'm 6'4, 320 lbs, and this is the first chair that doesn't make my legs go numb after two hours." That's the core complaint from heavier users with most chairs — narrow seats compress thigh circulation. The 22-inch width solves that.

The lumbar support is adjustable both in height and depth, controlled by a knob on the back. It's not as refined as a $500+ ergonomic chair, but it holds position and actually supports the curve of your spine. The headrest tilts independently, which helps if you lean back to read or watch videos.

Tilt mechanism works as expected: you can lock it upright or let it rock, and tension adjusts via a knob under the seat. The 3.5-inch pneumatic lift gets you from 19 to 22.5 inches seat height — fine for most desks.

## What Buyers Praise

The #1 recurring theme in 220+ ratings (4.4 stars) is the seat width. "Finally a chair that doesn't squeeze my hips," one verified buyer wrote. Another said, "The foam is firm, not mushy — I can sit 10-hour workdays without that 'bottomed out' feeling."

Durability also gets consistent nods. Multiple reviewers report 6-12 months of daily use with no visible wear on the bonded leather, no squeaking in the gas lift, and casters that roll smoothly on both carpet and hardwood. The 400-pound weight capacity isn't marketing fluff — the steel frame and reinforced base feel solid under load.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about bonded leather: it's a layered product — a polyurethane top coat over a split leather base. It looks and feels like real leather for a year or two, then it can start peeling, especially on the armrests where skin oils and friction take their toll. If you want a 5-year chair, save for full-grain leather or a high-end mesh chair instead.

The fixed armrests are the second strike. They're padded and wide, but you can't adjust height, width, or angle. If you're particularly long-torsoed, you might find the armrests sit too low for proper shoulder support. Several reviewers noted this as the main reason they deducted a star.

Assembly is the third friction point. The pre-drilled holes mostly line up, but one reviewer reported: "The seat bolts didn't align perfectly — took a lot of force to thread them." Not universal, but common enough to mention.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a tall or heavy user (200-400 lbs) who needs a wide, supportive seat for long workdays without paying $600+ for a "big and tall" specialty chair. It's also a solid pick for home offices where you want an executive look without real leather pricing.

**Skip it if:** You need adjustable armrests, you're sensitive to bonded leather's eventual peeling, or you want a chair that'll last 5+ years with minimal maintenance. Also skip if you're under 5'8" — the high back and deep seat will swallow you.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the 400-pound weight capacity actually hold up?**
Yes, but with context. The steel frame and reinforced base are legit — multiple 300+ pound reviewers report no wobble or structural issues. The weak point isn't the frame, it's the bonded leather over time.

**Q: Is this chair good for 8+ hour workdays?**
For bigger bodies, yes — the wide seat and firm foam prevent the numbness and fatigue common with narrower chairs. The lumbar support helps, though you may want to add a seat cushion if you prefer softer padding.

**Q: Will the bonded leather peel?**
Eventually, yes — that's the nature of the material. With daily use, expect the first signs of peeling around 2-3 years, typically on the armrests. The seat and back tend to last longer because they see less friction.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The iComfort i6000 Series Big & Tall is one of the better values in the oversized executive chair category. It solves the two biggest problems larger users face — narrow seats and weak frames — at a price that undercuts most "big and tall" specialty chairs by hundreds. The fixed armrests and bonded leather's finite lifespan are real drawbacks, but if you're a bigger person who's tired of chairs that feel like they're built for a teenager, this one earns its keep.

**Buy it if** you need genuine 400-pound support and a wide seat without paying premium prices. **Skip it if** adjustable armrests are non-negotiable or you want a chair that'll survive a decade.
