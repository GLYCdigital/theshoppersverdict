The package includes the cabinet body (in three sections), two doors, one drawer bank, the back panel, all hardware, and an instruction manual. ## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The cabinet uses laminated composite wood with a wood-grain finish that looks more expensive than it is. The doors open and close smoothly on European-style hinges, and the drawers glide on metal tracks rather than the flimsy plastic runners you often find at this price point.

The top surface is a genuine highlight. It's thick enough to hold a coffee bar setup, a microwave, or decorative pieces without flexing. If you're using it as a buffet for serving food, the height — right around 31 inches — aligns well with standard dining tables.

Storage capacity is generous. The three upper cabinets are deep enough for dinnerware stacks, and the two drawers work well for linens, cutlery, or remote controls if you're using it as an entertainment console.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has two notable weaknesses.

The **back panel is thin** — around 3mm composite board. It does its job structurally once installed, but you need to handle it carefully during assembly. One buyer warned, "The back panel split at a corner when I tightened the screws too much. Go easy on them."

The **hardware feels budget-grade**. The handles are lightweight metal that looks fine but doesn't have much heft. If you're opening these doors dozens of times daily, plan on upgrading the handles after a year or two. It's a minor swap — standard spacing — but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you need functional, attractive storage for a dining room or living space and don't want to spend furniture-store prices. It's ideal for apartment dwellers, first-time homeowners, or anyone furnishing a space on a budget.
