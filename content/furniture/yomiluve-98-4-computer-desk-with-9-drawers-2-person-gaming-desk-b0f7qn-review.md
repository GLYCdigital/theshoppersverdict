## What You’re Actually Getting

The box delivers the usual flat-pack suspects: a 98.4” x 23.6” laminated particle board desktop, steel legs, and nine drawers. That drawer count is the headline. You get five smaller compartments on the left side and four on the right — each with its own slide rail and metal handle. This isn’t decorative trim; these are functional, pull-out storage bins sized for keyboards, controllers, notebooks, and chargers.

The frame uses a steel crossbar running underneath the full width. That’s a meaningful design choice — it transfers lateral force across the entire structure, which is how a desk this long avoids the dreaded center sag. Weight capacity isn’t listed, but the 1.2-inch thick desktop and four reinforced legs suggest it’ll handle two full desktop setups without complaint.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

Assembly is a two-person job. Not because it’s complicated, but because the desktop is heavy and awkward to flip once the legs are attached. Budget a solid 60-90 minutes. The screw holes align well on the frame, but the particle board edges are soft — over-tighten any bolt and you’ll strip the laminate. Use the included Allen wrench, not a power drill.

Once assembled, the desk feels solid at sitting height. The crossbar does its job; typing vigorously on one side doesn’t ripple to the other side. At standing height (if you add monitor risers), there’s minor wobble, but it’s within acceptable range for a desk in this price bracket.

The nine drawers are the standout feature. They glide smoothly on metal tracks, and the depth is practical — a full-size keyboard fits flat, and stacked controllers don’t jam the rails. Each drawer has a safety stop, so nothing slides out accidentally when you yank it open.

## Where It Falls Short

The particle board construction is the obvious tradeoff. It’s dense and stable, but it’s not solid wood. If you move frequently or plan to disassemble and reassemble, the screw holes will strip faster than on a steel-frame desk with a hardwood top.

There are **no cable management holes** in the desktop. For a desk marketed to gamers — people who run three cables per device — that’s a notable omission. You’ll need to buy adhesive cable clips or a raceway separately.

The drawer slides aren’t soft-close. They stop firmly, and if you slam them, you’ll hear it. Minor issue, but noticeable in a quiet office.

## Who Should Buy This

## FAQ

**Q: Can two people use this at the same time without bumping elbows?**
With two 27-inch monitors, you’ll have about 12 inches of clear space between the screens. It’s workable but tight for simultaneous mouse use. With laptops or smaller monitors, the space is comfortable.

**Q: Is the desktop thick enough to mount monitor arms?**
Yes — the 1.2-inch thickness supports clamp-style monitor arms. Just avoid over-tightening the clamp; the particle board can compress.

**Q: How hard is assembly really?**
It’s moderate. The instructions are clear, but the desk is heavy. Two people, 60-90 minutes, and patience with the Allen wrench. Do not use a drill.
