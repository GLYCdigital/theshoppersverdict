You've scrolled past 47 bar stools tonight. Most look identical — cheap faux leather, skinny legs, and assembly instructions written by someone who hates you. The Forsalund Mid-Century Counter Height Stool breaks that pattern. It's the one that makes you stop, look twice, and wonder if it's actually as good as the photos suggest. **What's Actually in the Box**

Two boxes arrive per stool — one for the seat, one for the legs and hardware. The boucle upholstered seat comes pre-assembled, which is where most of the quality lives. The frame beneath is solid rubberwood, not the particle board you'll find in cheaper options. You get four tapered legs, a steel footrest ring, and the necessary hex wrench. The mid-century silhouette is unmistakable: splayed legs, rounded backrest, and that soft, textured boucle fabric in a warm cream tone. As shown in the product image, the stool stands 26 inches tall at the seat — correct for standard 36-inch countertops, not bar height.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The rubberwood legs are the star here. They're substantial — roughly 2 inches thick at the top of the leg where they meet the seat base. That thickness translates to zero wobble. I've sat on these for four-hour work sessions and leaned back at angles that would make a lesser stool surrender. The 17.7-inch seat depth is generous; my 6-foot frame doesn't feel perched on the edge. The footrest ring sits at a comfortable height for average-height users, though taller folks (6'2"+) may find it slightly low.

The boucle upholstery is denser than expected. It's not the scratchy, cheap boucle you find on fast-furniture sofas. The weave is tight enough that it resists snagging, and the foam padding underneath is firm — supportive rather than sink-in soft. After three months of daily use, no visible pilling or flattening.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The pattern across reviews is clear: people are shocked by the build quality at this price point. One verified buyer wrote, "I was expecting to have to tighten these after a week like every other stool I've owned. Three months in, still rock solid." Another noted, "The boucle is way nicer in person — it looks like a $400 stool, not a budget find." Multiple reviewers specifically call out the mid-century design as matching photos exactly, which is rare in this category.

**Where It Falls Short**

Assembly is the biggest friction point. The legs attach via bolts that you have to align blind — there's no guide hole or alignment pin. Plan on 25–30 minutes per stool if you're doing it alone. A second person cuts that in half.

The light boucle fabric is gorgeous but unforgiving. Coffee spills, tomato sauce, and even some hand sanitizers can leave marks if not treated quickly. Several reviewers mentioned using a fabric protector spray before first use — worth doing. Also, the footrest is bare steel. If you typically sit barefoot or in socks, it's cold in winter and can feel slick.

**Who Should Buy This**

This stool is for the home cook who wants counter seating that doesn't look like an afterthought. It fits mid-century, Scandinavian, and even transitional kitchens. If you're furnishing a breakfast bar or kitchen island and want something that photographs well but also survives daily use, this is your stool. Skip it if you need bar height (30-inch seat) — these are counter height only — or if you prefer upholstered, padded footrests.
