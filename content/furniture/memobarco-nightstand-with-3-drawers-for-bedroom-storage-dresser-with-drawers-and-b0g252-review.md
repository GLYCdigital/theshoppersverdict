## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the nightstand body, three drawer boxes, a back panel, and the hardware kit — cam locks, dowels, and metal drawer slides. The dimensions are standard for a bedroom side table: roughly 23.6 inches wide, 15.7 inches deep, and 24.4 inches tall. That's tall enough to sit level with most mattress tops, and the footprint fits neatly beside a queen or king bed without crowding the walkway.

The build is MDF with a wood-grain veneer finish, not solid timber. That matters for expectations — this isn't heirloom furniture, but it's a significant step up from the flimsy particleboard you'll find at big-box stores. The drawer fronts and frame get a matte protective coating, which handles daily use without showing every smudge.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The standout feature is the full-extension drawer slides. On nightstands in this price range, you usually get three-quarter extension at best, which means the back of the drawer stays hidden. Here, all three drawers pull fully out, so you can actually see what's buried behind your phone chargers and reading glasses. That's a quality-of-life detail that makes a real difference when you're rummaging at 11 PM.

Assembly is straightforward, which buyers consistently confirm. One reviewer noted, "Put together in about 25 minutes with a screwdriver. The cam locks lined up perfectly, no stripped holes." The instructions are illustrated rather than text-heavy, and the parts are labeled clearly. You won't need power tools — just the included Allen wrench and a Phillips screwdriver.

The drawers glide smoothly and close without slamming. Weight capacity is adequate for typical nightstand duties — a lamp, books, a water glass, maybe a tablet. Don't plan on storing heavy tools or stacks of hardcover encyclopedias in here; this is bedroom furniture, not a workshop bench.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has two honest weaknesses. First, some buyers report a slight shade difference between the drawer fronts and the frame in certain lighting. It's not dramatic — more like a subtle tonal shift — but if you're obsessive about color consistency, you might notice it. The photos on Amazon show a uniform dark walnut, but veneer over MDF can vary slightly between panels.

Second, the back panel is thin fiberboard — roughly 3mm. It does its job keeping dust out, but it won't stand up to serious impact. If you're the type to shove furniture hard against a wall, measure your baseboard clearance first. The panel can bow if it's pressed tight against a protruding trim.

One buyer flagged this: "The back is flimsier than I'd like, but it's hidden against the wall so I don't care." That sums up the sentiment — it's a minor compromise, not a dealbreaker.
