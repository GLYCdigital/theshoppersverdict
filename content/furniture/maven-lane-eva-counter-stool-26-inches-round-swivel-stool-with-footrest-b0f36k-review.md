**The Maven Lane Eva Counter Stool is the rare budget-friendly stool that doesn’t feel budget-built.** With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it’s clearly winning over shoppers who want swivel convenience without the $300+ price tag of designer brands. But is it right for your kitchen? Let’s dig into the details.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the stool base, seat, a footrest ring, and the hardware — nothing fancy, no tools included beyond the necessary hex wrench. Assembly is straightforward but not tool-free. You’ll spend roughly 15–20 minutes attaching the seat to the base and securing the footrest. The 26-inch height is fixed, so this is designed for standard 36-inch countertops, not bar-height islands (which typically need 30-inch stools).

As shown in the product image, the round seat is upholstered rather than wood or metal — a deliberate choice for comfort over the cold, hard tops many competitors use at this price. The swivel mechanism is the real selling point here: it rotates a full 360 degrees with minimal resistance, so you can pivot from counter to conversation without scooting the whole stool.

## Performance and Build Quality

The steel base is solid. At roughly 15 pounds, it’s heavy enough to stay planted when you lean forward, but light enough to slide across tile with a gentle nudge. The footrest is welded, not bolted on — that’s a durability win at this price, where many stools use thin crossbars that bend under repeated use.

The upholstery is a mid-weight fabric that feels durable, though it’s not stain-resistant. Multiple buyers note it’s comfortable for 2–3 hour sitting sessions, which is better than most counter stools I’ve tested that start digging into your thighs by the 45-minute mark.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star rating holds up when you read the actual reviews. One shopper wrote, *“The swivel is so smooth I forget it’s not a $500 stool. No squeaking, no wobble, even on our uneven kitchen floor.”* Another noted, *“Finally a stool where my feet actually reach the footrest. I’m 5’8” and this fits perfectly.”*

Assembly complaints are the most common negative, but they’re mostly about user error — several reviewers mention cross-threading bolts because they tightened without aligning properly. Take your time, and it’s a non-issue.

## Where It Falls Short

The fixed height is the biggest limitation. If you have a 34-inch counter or a raised bar island, this stool won’t work — there’s no adjustment mechanism, and you can’t swap the legs. Measure carefully before buying.

The fabric also shows wear faster than leather or vinyl. Pet owners and households with kids should expect to vacuum it weekly and treat spills immediately. In lighter colors like beige or gray, lint and dust are visible from across the room. Darker shades are more forgiving.

Finally, the round seat is 18 inches in diameter — fine for most adults, but if you’re plus-size or prefer a seat with more surface area, this will feel snug after an hour.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have standard 36-inch countertops, want swivel convenience without breaking the bank, and prefer a fabric seat over cold metal or wood. It’s ideal for apartment dwellers, breakfast bars, and anyone who entertains and needs flexible seating options.

**Skip it if:** You have non-standard counter heights, need a stool that stacks for storage, or have heavy daily traffic with messy kids or pets — the fabric won’t survive that abuse as well as a vinyl or wood seat would.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the swivel lock in place?**
A: No, it rotates freely 360 degrees. There’s no locking mechanism, so it will spin if bumped. That’s typical for this style, but worth knowing if you have toddlers.

**Q: What’s the weight capacity?**
A: The manufacturer doesn’t list an official limit, but buyer feedback and the steel construction suggest it comfortably supports up to 250–275 pounds. Heavier users report it feels stable, though the seat might feel narrow.

**Q: Can I use this as a desk chair?**
A: Technically yes, but the 26-inch height is too tall for standard 30-inch desks. It works for standing-height desks or drafting tables, but not conventional office setups.
