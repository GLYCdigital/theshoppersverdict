If you've shopped for TV stands lately, you know the struggle: everything under 30 inches tall forces you to mount the TV or crane your neck. The JXQTLINGMU Farmhouse TV Stand breaks that pattern at a genuine 32 inches — high enough that your eyes land naturally on the center of the screen from a standard sofa. That alone makes it worth a look for anyone with a 65- to 80-inch television.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the stand in five main pieces: two side cabinets, a center bridge section, and the top panel. Each side has a barn-door cabinet with an adjustable shelf inside, plus an open cubby above. The center section is open shelving — two tiers of display space that can hold a soundbar, cable box, or decorative items. As shown in the product image, the two-tone design pairs a dark espresso frame with lighter wood-grain panels, giving it that modern farmhouse look without drifting into full rustic kitsch.

Hardware includes cam locks, dowels, and a center support bar. That support bar is the real story here: it runs underneath the top panel to prevent the dreaded middle sag when you plop a 70-inch TV on it. The top is rated to hold up to 220 pounds, which is generous for this class of furniture.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The frame is laminated MDF with a melamine finish. That's standard at this price point, but it means you're getting the farmhouse aesthetic without paying solid-wood prices. The barn doors slide on plastic rollers — smooth enough in practice, though they're not silent. You'll hear a soft rumble when opening them, which isn't a dealbreaker but worth knowing if you're sensitive to noise.

The 32-inch height is the standout feature. Most competitors hover around 24–28 inches, which forces a 65-inch TV's center point well above eye level unless you wall-mount. With this stand, you can set the TV directly on top and maintain proper viewing posture. The 80-inch width capacity means you have room to spare for a 70-inch model with a few inches of breathing space on each side.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 220 ratings at 4.4 stars skew heavily toward positive, and a few themes come up repeatedly. ## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest complaint. Plan for 1.5 to 2 hours with two people. The cam-lock system is functional but not intuitive, and the instruction manual is mostly exploded diagrams with minimal text. If you're not handy with furniture assembly, budget extra time or recruit a patient friend. A handful of reviewers reported misaligned pre-drilled holes — not widespread, but enough to mention.

The finish is also more delicate than the photos suggest. The wood-grain panels are printed, not textured, and the surface scratches if you slide heavy components across it. Use felt pads under your electronics and a coaster under drinks. One reviewer noted, *"The top scratches easier than I'd like — a small nick appeared when I moved my soundbar."*

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a 65- to 80-inch TV, want proper eye-level viewing without wall-mounting, and need enclosed storage for consoles or media. It's also a strong pick for farmhouse-style living rooms where the 32-inch height adds presence rather than looking stubby.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight timeline and dread assembly, or you need a stand that can take heavy daily abuse from kids and pets. The MDF surface won't survive a direct hit from a flying toy. Also skip if you prefer a low-profile look — 32 inches is tall for a TV stand, and it will feel imposing in smaller rooms.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this hold a 75-inch TV?**
A: Yes, comfortably. The top is rated for 220 pounds, and most 75-inch TVs weigh between 60–90 pounds. The center support bar prevents sagging across the full width.

**Q: Can I mount a soundbar on the center shelf?**
A: The center open shelf is roughly 16 inches deep, which accommodates most soundbars. You'll need to place it on the shelf rather than mounting it to the frame, since there's no dedicated mounting bracket.

**Q: How tall is the interior of the barn-door cabinets?**
A: With the shelf at its lowest position, you get about 14 inches of clearance. At the highest, roughly 8 inches. Adjust the shelf before assembling the cabinet — it's much harder to change afterward.
