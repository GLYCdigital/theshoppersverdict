The Yegosh Vanity Desk is the kind of product that makes you squint at the price tag and wonder what the catch is. At a glance, it promises a lot: a mirrored vanity with storage, built-in charging, and a footprint that fits tight bedrooms. After digging through 215 ratings and weighing the real-world feedback, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the desk top, three drawers with metal glides, a framed mirror that mounts to the back edge, and a small power strip with two USB ports and one USB-C port recessed into the desktop surface. As shown in the product image, the setup is clean and modern — the mirror attaches without visible brackets, and the drawers sit flush when closed.

The desktop measures roughly 39.5 inches wide and 19.5 inches deep — enough for a 24-inch monitor plus a lamp, but not a sprawling double-monitor setup. The engineered wood is 5/8-inch thick, which is noticeably sturdier than the fiberboard you find on comparably priced vanities.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

Here's where the Yegosh earns its 4.3-star average. The drawer glides are genuinely smooth — no wobble, no sticking, even after months of daily use. The charging ports are positioned on the right side of the desk, which works well for right-handed users but might annoy lefties who want their phone closer to their dominant hand.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical about the price, but the drawers feel solid and the USB ports actually charge my phone fast — not the slow trickle you get on cheap furniture."*

The mirror surface is clear and distortion-free, but it's fixed in place. You cannot tilt it or adjust its height. For a 5'8" reviewer, the reflection cuts off at the shoulders when sitting upright — perfectly fine for makeup application. But if you're over 5'10" or under 5'2", you'll be hunching or craning.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest friction point. Multiple reviewers mention the 60–90 minute build time, and the included hex wrench strips if you apply too much torque. Use your own Allen key set and hand-tighten — the pre-drilled holes align well, but the metal inserts can spin if you force them.

The mirror also attracts dust like a magnet, and the surface scratches if you drag heavy items across it. Use a felt pad under your laptop or jewelry organizer.

A second common complaint: the power cord from the charging station exits through a notch in the back panel, which means the desk must sit flush against the wall — you can't run the cord sideways if your outlet is on an adjacent wall.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters and small-apartment dwellers** — the 39.5-inch width fits where a full dresser won't, and it's light enough to move solo.
- **Makeup enthusiasts** — three drawers with a 3-inch depth hold palettes, brushes, and a hair dryer. The top drawer has a divided insert for smaller items.
- **Students or remote workers** — the USB-C port charges a modern phone or tablet at full speed, and the desktop clears a laptop with room to spare.

## Who Should Skip It

- **Tall or short users** — the fixed mirror height is the dealbreaker. You'll either look at your chin or the ceiling.
- **Anyone who hates assembly** — this is not a 15-minute setup. Budget a full evening.
- **Gamers or dual-monitor users** — 39.5 inches isn't enough horizontal space for two screens plus peripherals.

## FAQ

**Does the mirror detach?**
No. It mounts to the back edge with four bolts and cannot be removed without leaving visible holes. If you want a vanity without a mirror, look elsewhere.

**Can the USB ports charge a tablet?**
Yes, the USB-C port outputs 15W, which charges an iPad or modern smartphone at a reasonable pace. The two USB-A ports are standard 5V/2.1A.

**How much weight can the drawers hold?**
Realistically, about 10–12 pounds per drawer. The glides handle the weight fine, but the drawer bottoms are thin — don't stack heavy bottles in a single layer.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a compact, good-looking vanity with functional storage and you're under 5'10" or over 5'2". The charging ports and smooth drawers justify the price.

**Skip it if** you're tall, hate assembly, or need a mirror you can adjust. Those three factors will drive you crazy within a month.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good budget vanity with one design flaw (the fixed mirror) that will rule it out for a meaningful slice of shoppers. Everyone else gets solid value.
