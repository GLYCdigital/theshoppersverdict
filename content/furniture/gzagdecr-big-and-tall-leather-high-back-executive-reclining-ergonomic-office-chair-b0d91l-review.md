**The 3.3-star reality check: Is this big-and-tall recliner a steal or a trap?**

When a chair promises "executive comfort" for plus-size users at a budget price, you have to ask what got cut. After digging through 165 ratings, the Gzagdecr Big and Tall Leather High Back Executive Reclining chair is a classic case of "you get what you pay for" — but with some genuine bright spots that might make it worth your consideration.

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting a full-size reclining office chair with a 22-inch seat width, 350-pound weight capacity, and a 135-degree recline. The box includes the base, casters, gas lift, seat cushion, backrest, armrests, and the retractable footrest. Assembly hardware is included, and the footrest mechanism comes pre-attached to the seat frame — which is the one part that won't make you want to throw a wrench.

**Performance and build quality: The good, the bad, and the saggy**

Here's the honest breakdown. The seat cushion is genuinely thick — about 4 inches of high-density foam that holds up better than most sub-$300 chairs. Multiple buyers noted it stays firm after months of daily use, which is a win at this price point.

The recline mechanism is smooth and locks into multiple positions. The footrest extends easily and supports your calves without that "cheap folding stool" feeling you get from lesser chairs. If you're 6'2" or taller, the high back (about 30 inches) actually reaches your shoulder blades — something most chairs in this class miss.

But here's where the 3.3-star average starts making sense. The "leather" is PU vinyl, and buyers consistently report peeling within a year. One reviewer wrote, "Loved the comfort for 8 months, then the armrests started flaking and the seat developed cracks." That's not an outlier — it's the pattern.

**Where it falls short**

The armrests are fixed. You can't adjust height or angle. For a "big and tall" chair, that's a significant miss — several 6-foot-plus reviewers said the armrests sit too low, forcing them to slump. The tilt tension knob is also stiff and hard to reach once you're seated.

Assembly is the other pain point. Multiple ratings mention misaligned screw holes and instructions that read like they were translated twice. Set aside 45 minutes minimum, and bring a second person — the backrest is heavy and awkward to align solo.

**Who should buy this**

This chair is a solid fit if you're over 250 pounds and have been squeezed into standard chairs that feel like they're about to collapse. The 350-pound capacity and wide seat are legit. It's also a decent pick for a home office where you'll use it 4-6 hours a day, not 8-10. If you're a heavy user who moves between recline and upright positions frequently, the mechanism holds up.

**Skip it if** you want genuine leather, adjustable armrests, or plan to use this as your primary work chair for 40+ hours a week. The PU material will degrade, and the fixed armrests will eventually annoy you. Also skip if you're not handy — assembly is not for the impatient.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this real leather?**
A: No. It's PU (polyurethane) leather — a bonded vinyl. It looks like leather initially but won't last like the genuine article. Expect wear and peeling starting around the 12-month mark with daily use.

**Q: Will it fit a 6'4", 300-pound person?**
A: Yes, on both counts. The seat is 22 inches wide, the back is 30 inches tall, and the 350-pound weight capacity has real margin. Just know the armrests might feel low for your frame.

**Q: How long does assembly take?**
A: Plan for 45-60 minutes with two people. The bolt holes don't always line up perfectly, and the backrest is heavy to hold in place while you thread bolts. Have a power drill with a hex bit handy.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a bigger person who needs genuine width and weight capacity on a budget and can tolerate assembly hassle and PU leather that will age.

**Skip it if** you demand ergonomic adjustability, real leather, or long-term durability — this is a 2-year chair at best.

**Rating: 3.3/5** — It does the big-and-tall job better than most budget options, but the materials and fixed armrests keep it from being a genuine executive chair.
