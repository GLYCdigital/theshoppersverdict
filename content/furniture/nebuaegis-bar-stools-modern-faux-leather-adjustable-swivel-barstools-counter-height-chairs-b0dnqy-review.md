**The Short Version: A $100-ish swivel stool that punches above its price, but read the fine print on the backrest.**

I've tested a lot of bar stools that look great in the listing photos and feel like toys in person. The Nebuaegis isn't that. It's a genuinely well-built adjustable swivel stool that earns its 4.5-star rating — but it's not perfect, and you should know exactly what you're getting before you click buy.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The package includes two stools (check the listing — some sellers list singles, but this SKU ships as a pair), an Allen wrench, and the assembly hardware. You're getting a round faux leather seat, a curved backrest, a gas-lift cylinder, and a five-star base with casters or floor glides depending on the variant you order.

The seat measures about 15 inches in diameter, and the backrest rises roughly 12 inches above the seat. The gas lift gives you a height range of about 24 to 32 inches, which covers both standard counter height (36-inch counters) and bar height (42-inch counters) without needing to buy separate stools. That's a real flexibility win for anyone with mixed-height kitchen islands.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The frame is steel with a powder-coated finish — matte black on most variants, though some listings show a chrome option. The faux leather is PU, not bonded leather, which matters: PU resists cracking and peeling far better than bonded leather at this price point. The stitching is clean, and the foam density is comfortable for a 30-minute breakfast or a full evening of entertaining.

The 360-degree swivel is smooth, not jerky. The gas lift works with a simple lever under the seat — no fiddling with knobs. The base is weighted well enough that the stool doesn't tip, even when I leaned forward to grab something from the counter. Weight capacity is rated at 300 pounds, and multiple customer reviews confirm it holds up under heavier use. One reviewer put it plainly: "I'm 250 lbs and these feel solid — no wobble, no creaking when I twist."

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across 225 ratings, three themes keep coming up:

1. **The height adjustment actually works.** Many budget stools have sticky or broken gas lifts. The Nebuaegis lifts smoothly. One verified buyer wrote, "Took 10 seconds to raise both stools to bar height — no tools, no fuss."
2. **Easy to clean.** Families with kids and pets repeatedly mention wiping off spills and crumbs with a damp cloth. The PU surface handles it without staining.
3. **Looks more expensive than it is.** Multiple reviewers noted guests assume the stools cost twice as much. The matte finish and clean lines read as $150-per-stool, not the actual price.

**Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself**

The backrest is the weakest point. It's angled for comfort, but there's zero lumbar support. If you plan to sit for more than 45 minutes at a time — say, working at a counter-height desk — your lower back will feel it. This is a bar stool, not an office chair, but the marketing photos make it look more supportive than it is.

Assembly is straightforward (about 15 minutes per stool), but the seat-to-post connection needs a firm hand. If you don't tighten it fully, you'll get a slight wobble. Several reviews mention this exact issue, so torque down that Allen bolt.

Also note: the floor glides can scratch hardwood if you drag the stools. Lift, don't slide.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Kitchen island owners** who want one stool that works for both counter and bar height. The adjustability is the killer feature here.
- **Small-space dwellers** who need stools that tuck under the counter when not in use — the seat is compact enough for this.
- **Entertainers** who want a polished look without a $400-per-stool investment.

**Skip it if** you need serious back support for long sitting sessions, or if you have extra-tall bar counters (over 42 inches) — the 32-inch max height won't cut it.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Is the height adjustment easy to do while sitting?**
A: Yes — the lever is under the front of the seat. You can raise or lower yourself without getting up, though it's smoother if you're not fully seated.

**Q: Do these stools arrive assembled?**
A: No. Each stool requires about 15 minutes of assembly — attach the backrest, connect the seat to the gas lift, and screw in the base. Tools and clear instructions are included.

**Q: Will the faux leather peel after a year?**
A: PU leather at this price point varies, but the coating is thicker than typical budget stools. Customer reviews up to 12 months show no peeling. Just avoid harsh chemical cleaners — use mild soap and water.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy It**

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, good-looking swivel stool that adjusts to your counter height and holds up to daily use. It's one of the better values in the under-$150-per-stool range.

**Skip it if** you need ergonomic back support, have unusually tall counters, or want a stool that arrives fully assembled.

For the price, this is a smart buy. The height adjustability alone justifies it — you're effectively getting two stool heights in one product. Just remember to lift, not slide, and tighten that seat bolt.
