If you’re shopping for counter-height seating, you’ve likely hit the same wall I have: most backless stools look identical, feel flimsy, and wobble after three months. The ABET 27" Backless Farmhouse stool doesn’t reinvent the wheel — but it does something more valuable. It gets the basics right, at a price that won’t make you wince. After digging through 215 ratings and the spec sheet, here’s what actually matters.

**What You’re Actually Getting**

The box contains two fully finished stools (sold as a pair), each measuring 15.7" wide with a seat height of 27". That puts them squarely in counter-height territory — they’ll pair with a 36" kitchen counter or a 42" bar top with a footrest. The seat is solid acacia wood, not the MDF or pine you often see at this price point, finished with a hand-rubbed oil that highlights the grain. The legs are powder-coated steel in a matte black, with non-slip floor caps already fitted. Assembly is laughably simple: four bolts per stool, no tools required, done in under ten minutes.

**Real-World Performance**

The acacia seat is the headline here. It’s dense, heavy, and doesn’t flex under a 200-pound adult. The hand-rubbed finish resists water rings and cleans up with a damp cloth — important for a kitchen stool. The built-in footrest bar, positioned about 8 inches off the floor, is a thoughtful touch that makes the backless design far more comfortable for shorter people who’d otherwise dangle their feet.

The powder-coated legs feel sturdy, and the non-slip caps keep the stool planted on tile and hardwood. One buyer noted, *“I was skeptical about a backless stool, but the footrest makes all the difference. I can sit for an hour without discomfort.”* Another praised the wood quality: *“The acacia is gorgeous — darker and richer than the photos suggest.”* That matches my read: the finish is more walnut-toned than the lighter farmhouse look in the product image.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let’s be honest about the downsides. There’s zero padding — this is a hard wooden seat. If you plan to sit for hours working or gaming, you’ll feel it. That’s inherent to the farmhouse aesthetic, but it’s worth knowing before you buy. Second, the paint on the legs is durable but not indestructible. One reviewer mentioned, *“The black finish chipped where I dragged the stool across the floor — lift it, don’t drag it.”* Fair enough, but it’s a minor annoyance. Finally, at 27 inches, these won’t work for standard 30-inch dining tables — they’re strictly for counters or bars.

**Who Should Buy These**

Get these if you have a kitchen island or breakfast bar and want stools that look more expensive than they are. They’re ideal for casual dining, quick meals, or kids who need a boost at the counter. Skip them if you need all-day seating comfort, have a low dining table, or prefer upholstered designs. They’re also a solid choice for a rental or Airbnb where durability matters more than cushioned luxury.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: What’s the weight capacity?**  
The frame is rated for 300 pounds. The acacia seat and steel legs handle it easily, though I’d stay under that for safety.

**Q: Do these wobble on uneven floors?**  
The non-slip caps help, but they’re not adjustable. On a slightly uneven floor, you might get a subtle rock. A felt pad under one leg solves it.

**Q: Can I use these outdoors?**  
The wood is sealed, but it’s not weatherproof. Keep them inside or in a covered patio only — rain will damage the finish over time.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want sturdy, good-looking counter stools that assemble instantly and hold up to daily use without breaking the bank. **Skip it if** you need cushioned seating or adjustable height.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, honest value with minor comfort tradeoffs. For the price, this is one of the better backless farmhouse options on Amazon right now.
