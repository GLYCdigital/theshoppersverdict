## The 8-foot picnic table that actually thinks about accessibility

Most "ADA compliant" picnic tables are afterthoughts — a wheelchair user gets to the table and finds the bench brackets digging into their knees. The Global Industrial 8'L Rectangular model is different. It's engineered around the clearance issue from the start, with a 27-inch opening under the tabletop that accommodates standard wheelchair armrests. That's the difference between a table that technically meets code and one that real people can actually use.

## What shows up at your door

You're getting a disassembled steel frame, two expanded metal benches, and a tabletop made of 14-gauge expanded metal — all coated in a bonded thermoplastic finish. The packaging is functional, not fancy. Expect cardboard, foam corner protectors, and a hardware bag that includes carriage bolts, lock washers, and hex nuts.

The dimensions matter here: 96 inches long, 30 inches high at the table surface, and roughly 29 inches wide across the top. The benches sit at a standard 17 inches. Weight is around 185 pounds assembled — this is not a table you'll be moving solo once it's together.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The expanded metal surface is the standout feature. Unlike wood slats that collect rain, warp, and splinter, this table drains instantly and stays cool enough to sit on in July. The thermoplastic coating isn't paint — it's baked on, which means it resists chipping from normal use. As one buyer put it: "We've had this table outside for two seasons and it looks the same as the day we installed it."

The frame is welded steel with a powder-coated finish, and the leg design uses a wide footprint that doesn't rock on uneven ground. Multiple reviewers noted the stability: "No wobble even with six adults leaning on it."

## Where it comes up short

Assembly is the main complaint. The hardware bag includes basic instructions, but the expanded metal edges are sharp before assembly, and you'll want work gloves. Plan for 45–60 minutes with two people. One reviewer noted: "The bolts are metric, so have a set of sockets ready — the included wrench is useless."

The thermoplastic coating also has a softness to it. Dragging a metal chair or grill across the surface will leave scratches. It's not a dealbreaker, but don't expect this to look pristine after a season of heavy commercial use.

## Who should buy this

- **Parks and recreation departments** that need ADA compliance without a special-order wait
- **Churches and community centers** hosting mixed-age groups where accessibility is non-negotiable
- **Property managers** replacing wood tables that rot every few years

Skip it if you want a lightweight portable table for tailgating — this is a permanent installation piece. Also skip if you have one person assembling it alone; the tabletop is heavy and awkward to align without a second set of hands.

## FAQ

**Does the table really accommodate a wheelchair?**
Yes, but test it. The 27-inch clearance works for standard adult wheelchairs. The 30-inch table height is slightly higher than the 28-29 inches some users prefer, so measure your chair's armrest height before committing.

**Can this stay outside year-round?**
Yes. The thermoplastic and powder-coated steel handle snow, rain, and UV exposure without rusting or fading. It's rated for continuous outdoor use.

**How does it compare to a plastic or wood table?**
Plastic tables crack in cold weather and warp in heat. Wood attracts carpenter bees and needs annual sealing. This metal table outlasts both, but it's heavier and harder to move.
