**A daybed with a trundle and charging ports sounds like the ultimate space-saving solution. The reality is more complicated.**

The MU Daybed with Trundle Bed is a twin-over-trundle frame designed for guest rooms, home offices, or kids' spaces. It promises three things: a compact footprint, a second sleeping surface that hides away, and integrated charging. At a 3.4-star average across 170 ratings, it delivers on the concept but stumbles on execution.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the headboard with attached charging station, the main twin frame, the trundle frame on locking casters, wooden slats for both levels, and all necessary hardware. Setup runs 45–90 minutes depending on your patience level. The frame uses solid pine slats — no box spring required — which keeps the profile low and the cost down.

The charging station is the headline feature: two USB ports plus a standard AC outlet are built into the headboard's side panel. For anyone who's ever hunted for an outlet behind a nightstand, this is genuinely thoughtful design. As one verified buyer put it: *"The charging ports are the reason I bought this and they work fine — my phone and tablet both charge overnight without issue."*

**Where it works**

The trundle is the practical star. It glides on casters, tucks completely under the main frame when not in use, and pops out when you need a second bed. The included mattress (sold separately) sits on a slatted base, so airflow is decent and the bed won't sag prematurely.

Space-wise, this is a smart fit for a 10x10 room. The twin footprint means it hugs a wall, and the trundle adds sleeping capacity only when needed. For a guest room that doubles as a home office, this is about as efficient as it gets.

**Where it falls apart**

Here's the honest part. A 3.4-star rating with 170 reviews doesn't happen by accident. The most frequent complaints cluster around hardware and assembly:

- **Missing or stripped screws.** Multiple buyers report hardware bags that don't match the instruction manual. One review noted: *"Two of the bolts were the wrong size and one hole wasn't drilled at all. I spent an extra hour at the hardware store."*
- **Misaligned predrilled holes.** This is the big one. Several customers describe having to re-drill or force screws through off-center holes, which compromises the frame's stability over time.
- **Charging station placement flaw.** The ports sit on the side of the headboard, not the front. If you push the bed flush against the wall — which is exactly what most people do with a daybed — the charging station becomes difficult to reach without reaching around the side awkwardly. You'll need to leave a gap between the bed and wall, which defeats the space-saving purpose.

**Who should buy this**

**Buy it if:** You're furnishing a guest room or kids' space on a budget, you prioritize the trundle function over premium build quality, and you're comfortable with minor DIY fixes during assembly. The charging feature is a bonus, not the sole reason to purchase.

**Skip it if:** You expect furniture to go together flawlessly, you need the charging ports to be front-facing, or you're furnishing a primary bedroom where durability matters long-term. If you're not handy with a drill, the assembly headaches could turn this into a frustrating weekend project.

**Three questions shoppers ask**

**Q: Does this need a box spring?**
No. The slatted base supports a standard twin mattress directly. Just make sure your mattress isn't unusually thick — anything over 10 inches will sit too high.

**Q: Can the trundle mattress stay stored under the main bed?**
Yes, but you'll want a low-profile trundle mattress (6–8 inches). Full-height mattresses won't fit under the frame.

**Q: How much weight can the trundle hold?**
The frame is rated for standard twin use — roughly 200–250 pounds. It's sturdy enough for a guest, but this isn't built for heavy daily use.

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 3.3/5** — The MU Daybed with Trundle nails the concept but fumbles the details. For the price point, you're trading build consistency for features. If the assembly quirks don't scare you, it's a functional space-saver with a genuinely useful charging station. If you're not prepared to troubleshoot hardware issues, spend more on a frame with better quality control and skip the charging gimmick.

Buy it if you want a budget-friendly trundle solution and don't mind a little DIY. Skip it if flawless assembly and front-facing ports are non-negotiable.
