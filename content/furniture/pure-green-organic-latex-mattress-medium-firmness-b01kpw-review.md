If you've been shopping for a natural latex mattress, you've likely hit a wall of confusion: Dunlop vs. Talalay, organic certifications, and price tags that make you blink twice. The Pure Green Organic Latex Mattress cuts through most of that noise with a refreshingly simple construction — but it's not without its tradeoffs. Here's what 220 Amazon reviewers (4.4 stars) and my own research reveal about whether this bed deserves your money.

**What You Actually Get**

The Pure Green arrives compressed and rolled in a box (queen dimensions: 60" x 80" x 9"). Unboxing takes about 15 minutes — you'll want a second person, because this thing is *heavy*. The mattress is a single 9-inch core of 100% natural Talalay latex, wrapped in a quilted organic cotton cover that's removable and washable. No foams, no glues, no flame retardants — just latex and cotton. The medium firmness rating translates to roughly a 5-6 on the standard 1-10 firmness scale, which puts it firmly in "supportive but not brick-like" territory.

**Real-World Performance**

The defining characteristic here is responsiveness. Unlike memory foam, which slowly swallows you, Talalay latex pushes back immediately. You'll feel supported *on* the bed rather than sinking *into* it. Side sleepers under 160 lbs may find the medium firmness slightly too unyielding at the hips and shoulders — several reviewers noted this. But back and stomach sleepers, plus heavier individuals, consistently report excellent spinal alignment and pressure relief.

Temperature regulation is the standout feature. The open-cell latex structure and breathable cotton cover mean air circulates freely. Multiple reviewers who previously slept on memory foam described waking up without the dreaded "sleeping in a heated blanket" sensation. One reviewer put it simply: *"I used to wake up drenched in sweat on my old foam bed. This mattress is dramatically cooler — it's the main reason I'm keeping it."*

Durability appears genuinely impressive. Several reviewers mention owning this mattress for 4-5 years with no sagging, no body impressions, and no loss of support. That aligns with latex's reputation as the longest-lasting mattress material on the market — expect 12-15 years of service life versus the 7-8 typical of polyurethane foam beds.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, motion transfer is mediocre. If you share a bed with a restless sleeper, you will feel their movements — this is inherent to latex's bounce, and it's a dealbreaker for some couples.

Second, the weight is genuinely problematic. A queen weighs roughly 65 pounds, and the lack of handles makes rotating or moving it an awkward two-person ordeal. Factor this in if you live alone or have mobility issues.

Third, the initial smell. Despite using certified organic materials, the mattress emits a noticeable odor (likely from the latex itself and the compressed packaging) that lasts 3-7 days. It's not toxic, but it's present enough that several reviewers were initially concerned.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Hot sleepers** — this is the #1 reason people choose latex over foam
- **Back and stomach sleepers** seeking firm, responsive support
- **Eco-conscious buyers** who want verifiable organic certifications (GOLS, USDA Organic, OEKO-TEX)
- **Long-haul buyers** who'd rather pay more upfront for 12+ years of service than replace a cheap bed every 5 years

**Skip It If**

- You're a side sleeper under 160 lbs — you'll likely need a topper for pressure relief
- You share a bed with a light sleeper who's easily disturbed
- You're on a tight budget — this is a premium-priced mattress, and there's no getting around that

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this mattress actually organic, or is that just marketing?**
A: It's legitimately certified. The latex is GOLS-certified organic (not merely "natural"), and the cotton cover carries USDA Organic certification. Third-party audits back these claims up.

**Q: How firm is "medium firmness" exactly?**
A: On a 1-10 scale with 10 being hardest, this sits around 5.5-6. It's supportive with minimal sinkage — closer to a traditional innerspring than to plush memory foam.

**Q: Will it work on an adjustable base?**
A: Yes. The latex core flexes easily, and the mattress is thin enough (9 inches) to bend without stress. Several reviewers use it on adjustable frames without issue.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a hot sleeper who wants a non-toxic, durable bed and don't mind the heavy lifting or the premium price. This is a "buy once, cry once" investment that will likely outlast two or three cheap foam mattresses.

**Skip it if:** You need motion isolation, you're a lightweight side sleeper, or you can't manage the weight. In those cases, look at hybrid latex-coil beds or softer Dunlop cores instead.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Deducting points for the weight and motion transfer, but this remains one of the best-certified organic latex mattresses available at its price point.
