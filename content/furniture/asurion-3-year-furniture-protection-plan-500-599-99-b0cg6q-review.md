You've just spent over $500 on a sofa, and Amazon is offering a protection plan for roughly 8-10% of that cost. The question isn't whether furniture gets damaged — it's whether this plan actually pays out when it does. With a mediocre 3.3-star average across 165 ratings, the answer is more complicated than a simple yes.

## What You're Actually Buying

This ASURION plan covers furniture priced between $500 and $599.99 for three years. It's not a warranty — it's an accidental damage protection contract. That means it covers stains, rips, tears, broken frames, and malfunctioning mechanisms like recliners or sleeper sofas. It also extends past the manufacturer's standard warranty period, which typically runs 1-2 years.

The claim process is entirely online. You submit photos of the damage, describe what happened, and wait for approval. If approved, you get a reimbursement check or replacement — not a repair visit. That's important to understand upfront.

## Where It Works Well

The strongest selling point is the breadth of coverage. Manufacturer warranties don't cover your kid spilling grape juice on the cushion or your cat shredding the armrest. This plan does. One verified buyer noted:

> "My dog tore a hole in our new sectional within two months. ASURION approved the claim with just photos and a short description. Got a check for the full purchase price within two weeks."

That kind of experience — quick, painless, full reimbursement — is exactly what you hope for. The plan also transfers to a new owner if you sell the furniture, which is a nice perk you won't find with most competitors.

## Where It Falls Short

The 3.3-star rating isn't an accident. The most common complaint across negative reviews is inconsistent claims decisions. Some buyers report denials for what looks like identical damage that other customers got approved. The specific language that trips people up is "normal wear and tear" — which is excluded. But what counts as wear and tear versus accidental damage is subjective.

One disgruntled reviewer wrote:

> "Submitted a claim for a broken recliner mechanism. ASURION said it was 'normal wear and tear' even though the chair was only 8 months old. The manufacturer warranty had expired, so I was stuck."

There's also a hard 30-day window to purchase this plan after your furniture arrives. Miss it, and you're out of luck permanently. And if you're buying this for a cheap piece that already has a 1-year manufacturer warranty, you're paying extra for coverage you might not need.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're purchasing a moderately priced sofa, recliner, or mattress with moving parts or fabric you can't easily clean. Families with young kids or pets are the ideal candidates — statistically, you will have an accident within three years.

**Skip it if:** You're buying a durable leather piece, a solid wood dining table, or anything that won't see heavy daily abuse. Also skip if you're disciplined about following care instructions and your household doesn't have kids or animals.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this cover manufacturer defects after the original warranty expires?**
A: Yes, for the full three years. That's actually the strongest argument for buying it — you're extending defect protection past the typical 1-year manufacturer warranty.

**Q: What if I move and my furniture gets damaged in transit?**
A: Moving damage is covered, but you'll need documentation. Take photos of the damage before you unpack anything, and keep the moving company's paperwork.

**Q: Can I cancel and get a refund if I change my mind?**
A: You have 30 days from purchase to cancel for a full refund. After that, you'll get a prorated refund based on how much time is left in the three-year term.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.3/5** — This is a genuinely useful product for the right buyer, let down by inconsistent claims enforcement. **Buy it if** you have a chaotic household and want peace of mind for a mid-priced purchase. **Skip it if** your furniture lives a quiet life or you're buying something built like a tank.
