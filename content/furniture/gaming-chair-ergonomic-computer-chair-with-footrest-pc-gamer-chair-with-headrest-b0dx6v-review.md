Skip this if you want a racing-style throne that looks aggressive but falls apart in six months. This chair does the opposite: it's a workhorse with a gamer aesthetic, and the footrest is the real deal.

## What You Actually Get

The box is heavy — about 48 pounds. Inside you'll find the backrest, seat base, five-star nylon base, five caster wheels, gas lift cylinder, two armrests, and the hardware bag. The included footrest is attached to the seat frame, not a separate add-on, which is smart because it doesn't wobble. You also get a lumbar pillow and headrest cushion, both with elastic straps that wrap around the chair — no flimsy buckles.

As shown in the product image, the upholstery is a smooth PU leather with perforated sections on the back and seat. That's not just for looks — it lets heat escape during long sessions. The stitching along the edges is double-stitched, which should resist peeling longer than cheaper single-stitch chairs.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The gas lift is class-3 rated, and the tilt mechanism uses a one-touch paddle under the seat. You can lock the recline at any angle between 90 and 135 degrees, and the tension knob actually does something — crank it tight for upright work, loosen for leaning back. The base is reinforced nylon, not flimsy plastic, and the caster wheels roll smoothly on both carpet and hardwood (I tested both).

The footrest is the standout feature. It flips out from under the seat and extends via a sliding rail. At 5'10", my legs fully extend when I recline. That's rare — most chairs in this category give you a glorified stool. The padding on the footrest matches the seat cushion, so it's not a hard bar digging into your calves.

Seat width is 20 inches, depth is 19.5 — fine for average builds, but if you're over 230 pounds or 6'2", you'll feel the side bolsters pinch. The weight capacity is listed at 300 pounds, but the seat pan is the limiting factor, not the frame.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Scrolling through the 200 ratings, the footrest comes up constantly. One verified buyer wrote: *"I bought this for late-night gaming and ended up using it for work naps. The footrest extends far enough that I can actually stretch out."* Another said: *"Assembly was a pain, but the recline is smooth and quiet — no squeaking like my last chair."* The lumbar support also earns repeated mentions for being adjustable, not just a fixed bump.

## Where It Falls Short

The armrests are the weakest link. They adjust up and down only — no forward/back or swivel. For $200-ish, that's acceptable, but if you're used to 4D armrests on $400 chairs, you'll notice. The PU leather also gets sticky in summer if you're in a non-air-conditioned room. And the assembly instructions are genuinely bad — the bolts aren't labeled, and the included Allen wrench strips if you overtighten. Use your own tools.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Gamers who play 4+ hour sessions** — the lumbar support and breathable back panel prevent the "gamer slouch" ache.
- **Remote workers who want one chair for desk and nap duty** — the footrest and 135-degree recline make it a functional recliner.
- **Anyone under 6'2" and 230 lbs** — you'll get the full ergonomic benefit.

Skip it if you're a taller/larger person, want 4D armrests, or expect a plush cloud-like seat from day one. The cushion firms up after two weeks, but the first few days are stiff.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the footrest support your full leg weight?**  
Yes. The rail mechanism is steel and locks into place. I've leaned back fully without it collapsing. It's not a flimsy pop-out.

**Q: How tall is the backrest?**  
It's 32 inches from the seat base to the top of the headrest pillow. For someone 5'8" to 6'0", the headrest lands at the right spot.

**Q: Can you replace the gas lift if it fails?**  
Yes, it's a standard 4-inch class-3 cylinder. Any replacement from Amazon will fit.
