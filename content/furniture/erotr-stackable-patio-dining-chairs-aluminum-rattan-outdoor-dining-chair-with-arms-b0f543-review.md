Let's cut through the noise: most patio chairs under $100 are flimsy plastic junk that cracks by season two. The Erotr Stackable Patio Dining Chair isn't that. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, this aluminum-frame chair with synthetic rattan is earning genuine praise. But is it right for your deck? Here's the full breakdown.

**What's actually in the box**

You get one chair fully assembled — no hex keys, no confusing diagrams, no 45-minute build session. Each unit weighs about 11 pounds, which is light enough to move one-handed but substantial enough to feel stable when you sit down. The dimensions are practical: 22.4 inches wide, 21.7 inches deep, and 35.4 inches tall overall. The seat sits at 29.5 inches, which pairs correctly with standard 28–30-inch patio dining tables. The frame is rust-resistant aluminum, and the weave is all-weather PE rattan — meaning it won't fade, crack, or absorb water like natural wicker.

**How it actually performs**

The stacking mechanism deserves special mention. These chairs interlock cleanly — the front legs of one nest neatly behind the backrest of another without scraping or leaving marks. If you're working with a small balcony or a deck that doubles as a grill station, that's a game-changer. Four chairs stack into roughly the footprint of one.

Sitting comfort is solid but not plush. The backrest has a slight ergonomic curve that supports your lumbar without forcing a rigid posture. The armrests are contoured and sit at a natural height — not so high that they bump the table, not so low that they're useless. One buyer noted, "I'm 6'1" and 220 lbs, and these feel sturdy without any wobble." Another mentioned, "Used them on a rooftop deck all summer — no rust, no fading, still look new."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant themes across reviews are ease of assembly and build quality. Multiple shoppers called out how the chairs arrived ready to use, and several mentioned the aluminum frame as a key differentiator versus cheaper steel options that eventually show rust spots. The synthetic rattan has also held up well in direct sun and rain, per multiple reviewers. One customer summed it up: "Bought one to test, then ordered three more. They stack perfectly and look twice the price."

**Where it falls short**

Honest tradeoffs exist. The seat pan is flat and firm — no built-in cushioning. If you're planning three-hour dinner parties, budget for separate seat pads. The PE rattan is convincing but synthetic; it won't develop the natural patina that real wicker does over time. And while the aluminum frame resists rust, the hardware connecting the legs to the seat can loosen after a season of heavy use — a quick tighten with a wrench fixes it, but it's worth checking.

**Who should buy this**

These chairs are ideal for apartment dwellers with balconies, homeowners with compact patios, or anyone who hosts and needs extra seating that disappears between parties. They're also a strong pick for rental properties or Airbnb setups where durability and low maintenance matter more than luxury. Skip them if you want plush, lounge-style comfort or a natural wicker aesthetic — this is a functional, modern chair, not a statement piece.
