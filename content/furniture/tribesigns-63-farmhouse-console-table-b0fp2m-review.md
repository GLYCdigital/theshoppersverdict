If you're shopping for a console table under $150, you've likely seen this Tribesigns piece flood your search results. With 220 ratings and a 4.4-star average, it's one of the more popular farmhouse-style entries in the category. But popularity isn't the same as quality. Here's what you actually get.

**What's in the box**

The package arrives flat-packed in a single 40-pound box. Inside you'll find the tabletop (pre-drilled with cam lock holes), two X-frame metal leg assemblies, the lower shelf slats, a hardware bag, and a hex wrench. Note: you'll need your own Phillips screwdriver — the included hex key handles the leg bolts, but the cam locks require a standard screwdriver. There's no visible damage to the engineered wood edges in most units, though one reviewer noted, "One corner had a small chip, but it's on the back side so I didn't bother returning it."

**Build quality and real-world use**

The tabletop measures 1.18 inches thick — noticeably beefier than the typical 0.75-inch boards on comparable tables in this price range. The laminated surface does a decent job mimicking oak grain, though up close you'll spot the repeat pattern. The metal X-frame legs are powder-coated black and feel sturdy, not flimsy. Adjustable foot pads are a thoughtful touch; they let you level the table on carpet or slightly uneven hardwood.

The two-tier design gives you a 20-inch clearance on the bottom shelf and 7 inches on the top shelf. That's enough for a pair of woven baskets, a stack of hardcovers, or a row of decorative boxes. The 63-inch width fits most standard entryways and backs up nicely against a sofa.

Assembly is the weak point. Plan for 45 minutes. The cam locks are functional but not forgiving — you need to align the dowels precisely or the shelf will sit crooked. Several reviewers echoed this: "It took me two tries to get the lower shelf to sit flush. Not hard, just annoying." Once assembled, the table is solid. There's no lateral wobble, and the X-frame design distributes weight well — the stated 220-pound capacity is believable if you distribute the load evenly.

**Where it falls short**

The finish is medium oak, which leans warmer and more yellow than the gray-washed farmhouse look many Pinterest boards feature. If you're after that bleached, coastal vibe, this isn't it. Also, the engineered wood top doesn't love moisture. A sweaty glass left overnight will leave a ring. Use coasters or a runner.

Assembly frustration is the other recurring complaint. It's not hard for someone comfortable with IKEA furniture, but it's not a 10-minute job either.

**Who should buy this**

- **First-time apartment dwellers** needing a functional entryway table without spending $400
- **Renters** who want a piece that moves well and doesn't require wall mounting
- **Anyone** needing a TV stand alternative for a 50-inch or smaller television (the 63-inch width and 220-pound capacity handle it)

**Skip it if** you want solid wood, a whitewashed finish, or a tool-free assembly experience.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this hold a 55-inch TV?**  
A: Width-wise, yes — 63 inches is wider than most TV bases. But the depth is only 15.7 inches, which is shallow for a television stand with a sound bar. As a console or entryway table, it's fine.

**Q: Does the shelf fit storage bins?**  
A: The bottom shelf has 20 inches of clearance. Standard 11x11x11 storage cubes fit, but taller woven baskets (over 14 inches) won't slide under the top shelf.

**Q: Is the wood real?**  
A: No, it's P2-grade engineered wood with a laminated oak finish. It looks convincing and holds up well, but it's not solid lumber.

**The Verdict**

The Tribesigns 63" Farmhouse Console Table delivers the farmhouse look at a budget price without feeling cheap. The thick top, sturdy X-frame, and adjustable feet put it above the flimsy alternatives. Assembly is the tradeoff, and the finish runs warmer than photos suggest. If you're okay with a 45-minute build and want a solid, versatile piece for under $150, this is a smart buy.

**Buy it if** you need a durable, good-looking console for an entryway or behind a sofa and don't mind basic assembly.  
**Skip it if** you demand solid wood or a whitewashed finish — look elsewhere.
