**Bottom line:** The ANCTOR 30-inch bathroom vanity is a solid mid-tier pick that gets the fundamentals right — real wood, soft-close drawers, and a quality ceramic top — but cuts corners on installation convenience and drawer depth. It's ideal if you're handy and want a durable vanity under $500, but renters or plumbing novices should think twice.

## What's in the box

You get the vanity cabinet (fully assembled sides with pre-attached drawer slides), a ceramic countertop with integrated sink basin, four drawer fronts, the drawer boxes, a backsplash, and all hardware. The faucet, drain, and P-trap are **not** included — budget another $80–$150 for those. An Allen wrench and basic screws come included, but you'll need your own Phillips screwdriver, drill, and silicone caulk.

## Build quality and real-world use

Here's where the ANCTOR separates itself from the crowded field of 30-inch vanities. The cabinet frame is solid rubberwood — not the "engineered wood" euphemism most brands use for MDF wrapped in a wood-grain photo. The drawer boxes are plywood with dovetail-style joinery, and the soft-close mechanism works on all four drawers, not just the top pair like some cheaper units. Multiple buyers confirmed this holds up:

> "The soft-close drawers actually work — no slamming after six months of daily use. My last vanity's drawers started sticking after three months."

The ceramic countertop is the other standout. It's a genuine one-piece vitreous china basin, not a resin or acrylic composite. It resists staining and scratches, and the integrated sink means no seam where counter meets basin — one less place for mildew to grow. As shown in the product image, the white finish is bright and clean, though it does show water spots if you have hard water.

## Where it falls short

The biggest frustration: **no faucet holes pre-drilled.** The countertop arrives blank, so you either drill through ceramic (risky — it can crack) or hire a plumber to do it. That's an extra $50–$100 most shoppers don't budget for. Several reviews mentioned this exact pain point:

> "Wish I'd known about the faucet drilling before I bought. Ended up paying my handyman $75 to do it right."

Drawer depth is also shallower than expected. The interior measures roughly 14" wide × 12" deep × 4.5" tall — enough for folded washcloths, makeup, and small toiletries, but your standard 12-ounce aerosol can or tall pump bottle won't fit upright. Plan to store those in the cabinet below.

Assembly is manageable but not quick. Expect 60–90 minutes, and definitely recruit a second person to set the ceramic top — it's heavy and unbalanced, and chipping a corner during installation is the most common damage complaint.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a homeowner or weekend DIYer comfortable with basic drilling and assembly. You want a vanity that'll outlast the cheap MDF options at big-box stores, and you value soft-close hardware and a genuine ceramic top.

**Skip it if:** You're renting and need something you can install and remove without damage, you're on a tight timeline (the faucet drilling adds a trip to the hardware store or a plumber), or you have tall bottles you expect to store in drawers.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the vanity come with a faucet?**
No. You'll need to purchase a faucet separately, and since the countertop has no pre-drilled holes, you'll either drill them yourself or hire a plumber. Factor that cost into your budget.

**Q: What's the actual drawer depth?**
Interior drawer depth is about 4.5 inches. Great for flat items like towels, makeup, and small toiletries, but tall bottles won't fit upright.

**Q: Is the cabinet waterproof?**
The frame is rubberwood with a water-resistant lacquer finish, and the drawer boxes are sealed plywood. It handles normal bathroom humidity fine, but don't let standing water pool on the wood — wipe spills promptly.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The ANCTOR 30-inch vanity delivers above-average build quality for the price, with real wood construction and soft-close drawers that genuinely last. But the missing faucet holes and shallow drawers add hidden costs and limit functionality. It's a smart buy for capable DIYers who want longevity over convenience; casual shoppers may find the installation friction not worth the savings.
