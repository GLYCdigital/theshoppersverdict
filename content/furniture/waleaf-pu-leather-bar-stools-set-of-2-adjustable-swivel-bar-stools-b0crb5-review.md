Let’s cut through the listing photos. The Waleaf PU Leather Bar Stools Set of 2 looks like a premium buy at first glance — tufted backrest, espresso-brown upholstery, chrome footrest. But what do you actually get when the box arrives? I’ve dug through 225 ratings (4.5 stars) and the specs to give you the straight story.

**What’s in the box:** Two stools, each arriving as a base, seat column, seat back, and the hardware bag. The instruction sheet is minimal but adequate — you’ll need the included hex wrench and about 20 minutes per stool. The pneumatic gas lift comes pre-installed, which saves the most frustrating part of assembly. One buyer noted, “Took me 15 minutes each, no stripped screws, everything lined up.” That tracks.

**Real-world performance:** The headline feature is the adjustable height — and it works properly. The gas lift raises the seat from roughly 24 to 30 inches, covering standard counter and bar heights. The mechanism is smooth and responsive; you don’t need to yank or struggle. The 360-degree swivel is genuinely quiet, no grinding or catching. I’ll call out the footrest ring specifically: at 16.5 inches from the seat base, it hits the sweet spot for most adults 5'6" to 6'0". Shorter users will find it slightly high, taller users will wish it dropped another inch.

**Build quality:** The frame is steel with a matte black finish that resists fingerprints. The PU leather is stitched cleanly with no loose threads — at this price point, that’s not guaranteed. The seat cushion has decent density; it won’t bottom out after a month, but it’s not plush either. The base is weighted enough to prevent tipping when you lean forward, which is a common failure with lighter stools. One long-term reviewer reported, “Six months of daily use, still no squeaks, swivel still smooth.”

**Where it falls short:** The PU leather is the obvious compromise. It’s not breathable. On a warm day or during a long dinner party, your back and thighs will sweat. That’s physics, not a defect — but it matters. The seat width is 16.5 inches, which is snug for broader frames. A 250+ lb reviewer wrote, “I feel the edge of the seat on my thighs after 30 minutes.” That’s fair criticism. Also, the base bolts need to be torqued down firmly — one reviewer found a slight rock after a week until they re-tightened everything.

**Who should buy this:** The home bartender who wants adjustable seating for both counter and island heights. The small apartment dweller who needs two stools that don’t dominate the room. The buyer who wants the look of leather without paying for it — PU is a fine tradeoff if you’re not sitting for hours on end. Skip it if you’re over 250 lbs, need all-day seating comfort, or expect this to survive a commercial setting. This is residential furniture, and it knows it.

**FAQ:**

*Can these stools be used at a standard 42-inch kitchen island?*  
Yes. The gas lift extends to 30 inches, which clears a 42-inch island comfortably — you’ll have about 12 inches of clearance between seat and counter. For a 36-inch counter, set the height to around 24 inches.

*Is the swivel lockable?*  
No. There’s no locking mechanism — the stool swivels freely at all times. If you need a fixed seat, this isn’t the one.

*How much weight can these hold?*  
Waleaf rates them at 300 lbs. Real-world feedback suggests staying under 250 lbs for long-term comfort, as the seat width becomes the limiting factor before the frame does.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you want a solid, adjustable, good-looking pair of stools for a home bar or island at a fair price. The swivel and lift mechanics are genuinely well-executed.  
**Skip it if** you need breathable seating for long sessions, have a broader build, or expect commercial-grade durability.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Waleaf set delivers on its core promises with few surprises. The PU leather and snug seat are honest limitations, but for the price, these are hard to beat.
