Let's be direct: the Furinno Jaya TV Stand is not heirloom furniture. It's a $60–$80 (price varies) particle board unit designed for apartments, dorms, and guest rooms. But for what it costs, it does exactly what a TV stand needs to do — and in this review, I'll tell you where it shines and where you'll feel every penny of the budget price.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains four main panels, a thin fiberboard back, cam locks, dowels, a plastic Allen wrench, and a printed instruction sheet. As shown in the product image, the design is straightforward: a top surface for your TV, one open shelf in the middle, and a lower cabinet with a single door. The dimensions are roughly 39.4" wide, 15.7" deep, and 19.7" tall — compact enough for a small living room or bedroom corner.

Assembly took me 15 minutes, and I'm not handy. The cam-lock system means no exposed screws on the exterior, which gives it a cleaner look than other budget stands. One warning: the back panel is flimsy fiberboard. It's not load-bearing, but you must align it perfectly before tightening the cams. Rush it and you'll strip the holes — a complaint I saw echoed in multiple Amazon reviews.

**Real-World Performance**

The top surface holds up to 75 pounds. That's enough for most 55-inch TVs, but if you're mounting a 65-inch OLED, this is not your stand. The open middle shelf accommodates a cable box or game console, and the bottom cabinet hides a router or DVD player behind its door. There's no dedicated cable management hole in the back panel — a genuine oversight. You'll need zip ties or a 3M cord clip to keep things tidy.

One buyer noted: "For the price, it's solid. I've had mine for eight months and it hasn't wobbled." Another said: "Looks nicer than expected but don't get water near it." That last point is critical. The laminate surface is not waterproof. A sweating glass left on top will leave a white ring. Clean spills immediately.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.2-star rating across 210 reviews is earned. The most common positives:

- **Assembly ease** — "Took me 10 minutes solo. Everything lined up."
- **Visual appeal** — "The dark wood finish looks way more expensive than it is."
- **Stability for the size** — "It doesn't wobble even with my cat jumping on it."

**Where It Falls Short**

- **Material fragility** — The particle board edges are exposed. One reviewer reported the laminate peeling after a year in a humid room. This is not for bathrooms or unventilated spaces.
- **The back panel** — It's thin and easy to tear if you over-tighten screws. Handle it gently during assembly.
- **No cable routing** — For a TV stand in 2026, that's a design miss. You'll need to buy cord management separately.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Renters and students** — Affordable, easy to move, and doesn't demand a mortgage.
- **Small spaces** — The 39-inch width fits tight corners where a full entertainment center won't.
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** — If you need a functional stand for under $100, this works.

**Skip It If**

- You're mounting a TV over 55 inches.
- You want solid wood or anything that will survive a decade.
- You hate assembling furniture — though this is easier than most.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the cabinet door close properly?**
A: Yes, but the magnetic catch is weak. If you have kids who slam doors, it may pop open. A small adhesive magnet strip fixes it.

**Q: Can I use this for a 65-inch TV?**
A: The top surface is rated for 75 lbs, but a 65-inch TV will exceed the width of the stand. It'll be unstable. Stick to 55 inches or smaller.

**Q: Is it easy to move after assembly?**
A: It's 25 lbs, so yes. But I'd disassemble it for a long move — the cam locks can loosen during transport.

**The Verdict**

The Furinno Jaya TV Stand delivers exactly what it promises: a cheap, decent-looking, functional TV stand for small spaces. It's not built to last a decade, but at this price, it doesn't need to. If you're a renter, a student, or just need a spare-room stand, buy it.

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, easy-to-assemble stand for a TV up to 55 inches.
**Skip it if** you need real wood, cable management, or something that'll survive a move.
