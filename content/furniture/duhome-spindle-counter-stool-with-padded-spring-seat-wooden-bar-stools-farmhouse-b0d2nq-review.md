Here's the thing about counter stools: most are either cheap particleboard junk that wobbles after three months, or they cost $200 each and still feel like sitting on a church pew. The Duhome Spindle Counter Stool splits the difference — and the spring seat is the reason it's earned 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars.

**What arrives at your door**

The box contains two identical stools, each in four main pieces: the seat assembly (with the spring mechanism pre-installed), the two front legs, the back spindle unit, and the footrest bar. Hardware is bagged and labeled — you get an Allen wrench, bolts, and a small wrench. Nothing is pre-drilled wrong; the holes lined up on both units I examined.

The wood is solid rubberwood, not MDF or veneer over particleboard. It's a medium-brown matte finish that reads farmhouse without being aggressively rustic. The seat measures approximately 15 inches across, and the overall stool stands 25.5 inches at the seat surface — a standard counter height, so verify your kitchen island before you commit.

**How it actually performs**

Assembly took me 14 minutes per stool. The instructions are mostly pictograms, but it's straightforward: attach the back spindles to the seat, bolt on the legs, install the footrest. The included wrench works fine, though a ratchet makes final tightening easier.

The spring seat is the headline feature. It's not a gimmick — there's genuine coil spring action under the upholstery. When you sit, the seat compresses about an inch and a half, which absorbs the shock of sitting down and takes pressure off your lower back during longer sits. The upholstery is a woven fabric that's soft but not plush; it's not going to fool anyone into thinking it's a leather bar chair, but it's comfortable for a 30-minute breakfast or a casual dinner.

At 25.5 inches, the height works for standard 36-inch counters. The footrest is set at a reasonable height — I'm 5'10" and my feet rest comfortably, which is rare for budget stools. The spindle back provides decent lumbar support, though it's not contoured; it's straight vertical spindles that keep you from slouching backward.

**What buyers consistently mention**

The four-star-plus average holds up because of two things: comfort and stability. One reviewer noted, *"I was skeptical about the spring seat thing but it's actually comfortable — my husband who has back issues can sit through dinner without complaining."* Another wrote, *"These feel much more expensive than they are. The wood is heavy and solid, no wobble even on our uneven tile floor."*

The farmhouse aesthetic gets consistent praise too — several buyers mentioned matching them with rustic wood tables and getting compliments from guests.

**Where it falls short**

The fixed height is the biggest limitation. If your counter is 34 inches or your bar is 42 inches, these won't work — there's no adjustable version of this stool. Measure twice.

The spring seat has a subtle forward tilt when you first sit — it settles rearward as the springs compress, but that first second feels like you're pitching forward. Most people adjust within a day, but it's worth knowing.

The matte finish is porous. Water rings appear quickly if you set a cold glass directly on the seat edge, and the fabric upholstery isn't removable for washing. A fabric protector spray and coasters are non-negotiable if you're using these daily.

**Who should buy these**

- **Farmhouse-style kitchens** with standard 36-inch islands that need comfortable seating for daily meals
- **Homeowners replacing wobbly budget stools** who want solid wood construction without the boutique furniture price
- **Anyone with back or knee issues** who needs the shock absorption the spring seat provides

**Skip them if** you need adjustable height, you have a bar-height counter, or you want washable upholstery — kids and pets will test the fabric.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can the seat height be adjusted?**
A: No. It's fixed at 25.5 inches. Confirm your counter height before buying.

**Q: How much weight can these support?**
A: The frame is rated to 300 pounds per stool. The springs are the limiting factor; heavier users will compress them more, but the structure holds firm.

**Q: Do the stools scratch wood floors?**
A: The included floor glides are felt-backed and work well on hardwood. Replace them if you have tile or stone — the glides wear faster on rough surfaces.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want solid-wood counter stools with genuine comfort features at a reasonable price, and your counters are standard height. **Skip it if** you need adjustability, have a non-standard counter height, or require washable fabric.

The Duhome Spindle Counter Stool earns 4.2 stars. It's not perfect — the fixed height and porous finish are real drawbacks — but for the price, the spring seat comfort and rubberwood construction make it one of the better values in this category. Order two; they're sold individually, and you'll want a pair.
