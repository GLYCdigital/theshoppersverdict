**Is this $600-$699.99 furniture protection plan worth the extra cash? Here's the honest breakdown.**

You're dropping $600–$700 on a sofa, dining set, or mattress, and Amazon's checkout page is asking if you want the ASURION 3 Year Furniture Protection Plan. At roughly 10% of the purchase price, it's tempting to skip. But before you click "No thanks," here's what 215 buyers who've actually filed claims have to say.

**What you're actually paying for**

This isn't a manufacturer's warranty — it's an extended service contract that kicks in after the furniture maker's coverage expires. For the $600–$699.99 price tier, ASURION covers structural damage (broken frames, sagging springs), accidental stains, rips, and tears that aren't from normal use. The plan runs for three years from the date of delivery, and there's no deductible per claim.

You get a digital certificate emailed to you after purchase. That's it — no physical card or paperwork. Registration requires your original receipt and the delivery date, which you must do within 30 days of the furniture arriving. Miss that window and your coverage is void, no exceptions.

**How claims actually work in practice**

The claim process is entirely online. You submit photos of the damage, a copy of your receipt, and a short description. Most buyers report hearing back within 24–48 hours. If approved, ASURION either sends a technician to repair the item or issues a replacement — it's their choice, not yours.

One buyer noted: *"My recliner's footrest mechanism broke at month 14. Uploaded photos on a Tuesday, approved Thursday, and a repair tech came the following week. No hassle, no phone tag."*

That's the experience you're paying for. But it's not universally smooth. *"Stain on my fabric sofa at month 8. They asked for three different photo angles and a fabric swatch from inside the cushion. Took two weeks to get approval,"* shared another reviewer. The process works, but it's not instant.

**Where this plan falls short**

Let's be blunt about what this doesn't cover because the fine print stings if you don't read it:

- **Normal wear and tear** — that loose armrest or faded cushion? Not covered.
- **Pet damage** — claws, urine, chewing. The single biggest complaint across reviews.
- **Fabric fading** — sun exposure isn't their problem.
- **Moving damage** — if you relocate during the 3-year term, you're on your own.

Also, the replacement value is capped at what you originally paid. If your $650 sofa gets replaced in year 3 and the same model now costs $800, you pay the difference. Several reviewers were surprised by this.

**Who should buy this plan**

This makes sense if you fall into one of these groups:

- **You have kids under 10.** Crayon, juice, and marker stains are the most common approved claims. The $60–$70 cost pays for itself with one solid stain removal.
- **You buy recliners or motion furniture.** The mechanisms are the #1 failure point, and this plan covers structural breakdowns.
- **You don't have renters or homeowners insurance with furniture riders.** If your policy already covers accidental damage, you're double-paying.

**Skip it if:**

- Your furniture is leather. The plan covers tears, but leather repair often gets "unrepairable" judgments, and you'll get a depreciated payout.
- You have pets. The exclusions will likely leave you with an unusable claim.
- You're buying budget furniture that you plan to replace in 2–3 years anyway. The math doesn't work.

**FAQ: Three questions shoppers actually ask**

**Q: Can I cancel and get a refund?**
A: Yes, within 30 days of purchase for a full refund. After that, refunds are prorated based on time remaining, minus a $25 cancellation fee.

**Q: Does the plan transfer if I sell the furniture?**
A: Yes, the coverage transfers to the new owner if you contact ASURION within 30 days of the sale. The 3-year term doesn't reset, though.

**Q: What happens if ASURION can't repair the item?**
A: They'll replace it with a comparable product or refund the original purchase price, whichever is less. You don't get to keep the damaged item.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you're investing in mid-range furniture you plan to keep for years, especially with kids in the house. The structural damage and stain coverage are genuinely useful, and the claim process is faster than most competitors.

**Skip it if** you have pets, buy leather, or tend to replace furniture every few years anyway. The exclusions will likely mean you never file a successful claim.

At roughly 10% of your furniture's price, this plan is a solid safety net — not a money grab — but only if you actually read the exclusions before you need to use them.
