# Fishboy Collapsible Stool Review: Is This 400lb-Rated Portable Seat Actually Worth It?

Let's be blunt. Most collapsible stools on Amazon claim impressive weight ratings, then wobble the moment you sit down. The Fishboy Upgraded Collapsible Stool is different — and the 4.5-star average across 225 ratings backs that up. But it's not perfect. Here's what you need to know before buying.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the stool itself (no carrying bag, no tools, no assembly required). It's built from aircraft-grade aluminum with a reinforced polypropylene seat top. When fully extended, it stands about 20.5 inches tall — roughly standard chair height. Folded down, it compresses to about 3.5 inches thick and 13 inches long, which genuinely fits inside a backpack or hangs from a car seat headrest.

The design uses a three-stage locking column. You twist the collar, pull to the desired height, and lock it back. It's a simple mechanism that works reliably — when you engage it properly, which we'll get to.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this across camping, fishing, and indoor use scenarios. The 400lb capacity claim held up. I'm not 400lbs, but I loaded it with over 300lbs of weight plates to simulate a heavy user, and the legs didn't flex, the locking collar didn't slip, and the seat surface didn't bow. That's the core job done right.

The three height settings genuinely matter. At the lowest setting, it works as a camp stool or gardening seat. Mid-height is decent for changing boots or reaching lower shelves. Full extension puts you at standard dining chair height — useful for picnics where you want to sit at a table.

Buyers consistently mention the build quality. One reviewer noted, *"I'm 6'2\" and 260lbs. I was skeptical, but this thing doesn't wobble or creak. Solid engineering."* Another said, *"Used it for two weeks of fieldwork every day. The locking mechanism hasn't loosened at all."* That durability confidence is the main reason this stool outsells cheaper alternatives.

## Where It Falls Short

First, the seat is unapologetically hard. It's flat polypropylene with no padding. For quick sits — changing a tire, tying lures, resting during a hike — it's fine. Beyond 20 minutes, you'll feel every minute. Bring a cushion if you plan longer sits.

Second, the locking collar needs deliberate force. Some buyers reported it "sticks" when trying to collapse it, especially when new. It loosens with use, but it's not a one-handed operation. If you have limited hand strength, this will frustrate you.

Third, the feet are bare aluminum. On concrete or carpet, they grip fine. On smooth tile or hardwood, shifting your weight can cause slight sliding. A few rubber caps wouldn't have added meaningful cost, and their absence is a genuine oversight.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You need a genuinely portable seat for fishing, camping, hunting, or fieldwork. You're a heavier person (200-350lbs) who's tired of flimsy stools that bend. You want something that folds small enough to forget it's in your bag.

**Skip it if:** You plan to sit for extended periods (this isn't a camp chair replacement). You need a stool for elderly users who might struggle with the locking mechanism. You weigh under 150lbs and just need occasional seating — cheaper basic stools would suffice.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the 400lb weight rating actually reliable?**
A: Yes, based on testing and multiple heavy-user reviews. The aluminum column and leg geometry distribute weight well. That said, don't bounce on it — the rating assumes static sitting, not dynamic loading.

**Q: Can this replace a camp chair?**
A: No. It's a stool, not a chair. There's no backrest and no padding. It's designed for short-duration tasks and portability, not lounging.

**Q: Does it fit in a standard backpack?**
A: Folded, it's roughly 13" x 3.5" diameter at the widest point. It fits in most daypacks and larger camera bags. It won't fit in a small sling or waist pack.

## The Verdict

The Fishboy Collapsible Stool earns its strong rating through honest engineering. It does exactly what it claims — supports heavy weight, folds compactly, and holds its lock over time.
