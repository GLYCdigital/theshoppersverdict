**The QLLY Adjustable Square Table Leg Review: A Solid Workhorse That Requires a Little Effort**

Most table legs are dumb. You buy them, you screw them in, and you're stuck with that height forever. The QLLY adjustable square table leg actually does what the name promises — it adjusts. But the real question is whether the convenience outweighs the setup hassle. After digging through 215 ratings and the specs, here's the honest picture.

**What You Actually Get**

Each leg is a two-piece steel assembly: an outer square tube and an inner sliding tube. The height range spans 24 to 40 inches (60–100cm), giving you roughly 16 inches of travel. The adjustment mechanism is refreshingly simple — a series of holes drilled every inch along the inner tube, secured by bolts you slide through and tighten. No gas springs, no levers, no complex locking mechanisms to fail later.

The mounting plate is a solid 3.15-inch square steel bracket welded to the top of the outer tube. Four bolt holes per plate. The legs themselves are powder-coated matte black and measure 1.65 inches square — substantial enough to feel sturdy under a real tabletop.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The steel gauge is the standout here. These aren't hollow, tinny legs. The 660-pound weight capacity (per set of four) tells you the manufacturer wasn't cutting corners on material thickness. One reviewer noted, *"I was honestly surprised by the weight of these when the box arrived — they're solid metal, not stamped sheet."* That tracks with what you're paying for.

Assembly is straightforward but requires tools. You'll need a drill to mount the plate to your tabletop (pilot holes are not pre-drilled), and the bolts that lock the height are included. The design is forgiving for DIYers — the square shape prevents rotation, and the sliding fit has minimal play when locked down.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The height range flexibility is the most-cited plus. One buyer wrote, *"I built a standing desk for my wife who's 5'2" and one for myself at 6'1" — same legs, just set them at different heights."* That's the core value proposition: one pair of legs serves multiple users or multiple purposes. The industrial look also gets compliments — the matte black finish fits workshop tables, industrial desks, and farmhouse-style furniture equally well.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest friction point is the adjustment process itself. To change height, you must **remove the bolts, lift the tabletop off the legs, slide the inner tube to a new hole, and re-secure everything**. That's a 10-minute job per leg, not a quick crank. If you're planning to adjust height frequently (like a sit-stand desk), this is not the product for you — you'd want a gas-lift or crank system instead.

Also, the mounting plate has no pre-drilled pilot holes for the bolts. The included hardware is self-tapping, but you'll be drilling into your tabletop blind unless you mark carefully. Measure twice, drill once.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **DIY furniture builders** who want a sturdy, height-flexible base for a custom desk, workbench, or craft table
- **Households with multiple users** of different heights sharing one desk
- **Anyone building a one-time project** (e.g., a standing desk) who wants to dial in the perfect ergonomic height and leave it

**Skip It If:**

- You want tool-free, on-the-fly height adjustment (look at crank or electric systems)
- You're mounting to glass or thin particleboard — the 660lb capacity assumes a solid mounting surface

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're building a custom desk or workbench and want the flexibility to adjust height for different users or tasks — this is a sturdy, honestly-priced solution that will outlast cheap alternatives.

**Skip it if** you need quick, tool-free height changes or you're mounting to fragile surfaces.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid construction, genuine utility, but the adjustment process is more assembly than adjustment. It's a buy-and-forget product, not a daily-tweak one.
