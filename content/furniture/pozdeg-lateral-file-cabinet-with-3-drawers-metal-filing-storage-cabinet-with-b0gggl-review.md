Let’s cut through the noise: most lateral file cabinets under $150 are cheaply made with particleboard backs and drawers that wobble. The pozdeg 3-drawer metal cabinet isn’t that — but it’s also not a $400 Steelcase. At a 3.8-star average from 190 ratings, it sits in a frustrating middle ground. Here’s what you’re actually getting.

**What’s in the box**  
The cabinet ships in three flat boxes. You get the welded steel frame (top, sides, and back are one piece — good sign), three separate drawer boxes, three drawer fronts, and a hardware kit with cam locks and hex wrench. The metal thickness is 0.8mm on the body and 0.6mm on drawers — not heavy-gauge commercial grade, but noticeably sturdier than the 0.4mm stuff IKEA uses. There’s no anti-tip bracket in the box, which matters (more on that below).

**Build quality and real-world use**  
The fully welded frame is the standout feature. No screws holding the main body together means no wobble at the joints — something buyers consistently mention. One reviewer noted, *“The frame feels solid — I sat on it while organizing files and it didn’t flex.”* That’s a real test.

The drawers run on ball-bearing slides rated for 50 pounds each. They extend fully, giving you complete access to hanging files. Letter and legal sizes both fit; the drawer interiors are 14.5” deep, so Pendaflex folders slide in without catching. The cam-lock drawer fronts align well if you install them carefully — but that’s the catch.

**Where it falls short**  
Assembly is the main complaint. The drawer fronts arrive detached, and aligning the cams so all three close flush takes trial and error. Multiple reviews describe the same frustration: *“The hardest part was getting the fronts level — I had to loosen and retighten everything twice.”* Budget 45–60 minutes if you’ve done flat-pack furniture before; an hour-plus if you haven’t.

More concerning: there’s no anti-tip interlock. With all three drawers loaded (150 pounds total capacity), opening the top and bottom drawers simultaneously makes the cabinet noticeably front-heavy. It won’t tip if it’s against a wall, but in an open room with kids or pets, you should bolt it to the wall yourself. The thin paint is another nit — the drawer fronts show scuffs from fingernails and file tabs within weeks.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Home-office workers** with a small footprint: the 15.7” depth fits under most desk returns or in a closet corner.  
- **Renters** who need a functional file cabinet but don’t want to invest in commercial-grade furniture they’ll move in 3 years.  
- **People who value steel over laminate** — this is all-metal, no particleboard to swell or warp.  

**Skip it if** you need heavy daily use (like a medical or legal front desk), if you hate assembly, or if you have young kids who might pull drawers open — the lack of interlock is a genuine safety gap.

**FAQ**  

**Q: Can it hold legal-sized hanging files?**  
Yes. The interior width is 14.5” and depth is 14.2”, so legal folders fit with about 0.3” to spare.  

**Q: Does it come with drawer locks?**  
The two bottom drawers have a central locking bar operated by a key. The top drawer is not lockable.  

**Q: How heavy is it assembled?**  
About 52 pounds. The welded frame accounts for most of that — it feels heavier than it looks, which is a plus for stability.  

**The Verdict**  
The pozdeg 3-drawer lateral cabinet gets the basics right: solid all-steel construction, smooth full-extension slides, and a footprint that works in tight spaces. The assembly learning curve and missing anti-tip feature hold it back from a higher rating.  

**Buy it if** you want an affordable all-metal cabinet and don’t mind an hour of setup.  
**Skip it if** you need plug-and-play assembly or have a household with small children.  

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A functional, sturdy value that demands a bit of elbow grease and a wall anchor.
