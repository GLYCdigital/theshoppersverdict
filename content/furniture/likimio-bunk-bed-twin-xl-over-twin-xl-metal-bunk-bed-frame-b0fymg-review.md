**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the top and bottom bunk frames, a straight ladder, full-length guardrails for the upper bunk, and all necessary hardware. There's no headboard, no footboard, no frills — this is a bare-bones utility frame. The LIKIMIO is constructed from heavy-gauge steel tubing with a matte black finish that resists scratches reasonably well. As shown in the product image, the design is clean and industrial — nothing fancy, but it doesn't look cheap either.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The steel frame is genuinely sturdy. Once assembled, there's minimal wobble — a common failure point in sub-$300 bunk beds. The weight rating is generous enough that it handles not just kids but also adults (the manufacturer doesn't specify an exact number, but buyer feedback consistently mentions adults sleeping on both bunks without issue). The ladder attaches at a slight angle, which makes climbing feel more natural than vertical ladders on competing models.

One detail that stands out: the guardrails run the full length of the top bunk. That's a meaningful safety upgrade over frames with rails that stop short of the head or foot. For parents of younger kids, this matters. The bottom bunk sits low enough that even a preschooler can climb in without a stool.

**Where It Falls Short**

Assembly is the biggest complaint. The instructions are adequate but not great, and the included Allen wrench will test your patience. Plan for 60–90 minutes with two people. A drill with a hex bit will save you significant time.

The metal ladder rungs are bare. In winter, they're cold. For kids, consider adding foam ladder covers — it's a $10 fix but worth budgeting for.

The frame offers zero under-bed storage. If you need space for bins or luggage, this isn't the bed for you. Some buyers also noted the top bunk is high enough that a ceiling fan becomes a hazard — measure your room height before committing.
