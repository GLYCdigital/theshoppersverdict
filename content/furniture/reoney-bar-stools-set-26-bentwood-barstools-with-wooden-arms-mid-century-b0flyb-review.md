You know that moment when the bar stools look perfect in the product photos, but in your kitchen they feel like a theme park ride? The REONEY set of two bentwood bar stools avoids that trap — mostly. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, these are the kind of stools that show up on time, look like the listing, and don't fall apart by month three. That's rarer than it should be in this category.

Here's the honest breakdown before you spend your money.

**What you're actually getting**

The set ships as two stools, so you're not paying for a single showpiece. Each one measures 26 inches from floor to seat — a standard height for counter-height tables and most kitchen islands (typically 35-37 inches tall, for reference). The frame is solid rubberwood with a bentwood curved backrest and wooden armrests. The seat is thick enough — roughly 2 inches — to not feel like you're sitting on plywood.

Assembly is straightforward but not tool-free. You're looking at attaching the four legs, the two crossbars (X-brace style), and the seat to the frame. Budget 30-45 minutes for both stools if you're doing it solo. One reviewer noted, *"The holes didn't perfectly align on the second stool, but after some wiggling it came together fine."* That's consistent with the general feedback — nothing that requires returning, but don't expect IKEA-level precision either.

**How they hold up in real use**

The best thing about these stools is the build. The X-brace crossbars aren't just decorative — they give the frame genuine rigidity. I've seen cheaper bentwood stools that sway when you lean back; these don't. The curved armrests are also functional, not just for looks. At 26 inches, you're sitting high enough that the arms actually catch your elbows naturally.

The footrest is positioned well too. On many budget stools, the footrest ends up too low, forcing you to tiptoe. REONEY placed it at a height that works for most adults — you can comfortably rest your feet without your legs going numb.

Here's the catch. The finish is thin. Multiple buyers have mentioned that the wood staining scuffs easily — *"I accidentally dragged a metal bowl across the armrest and it left a mark immediately."* This isn't a dealbreaker if you're careful, but if you have kids or a high-traffic kitchen, you'll notice wear faster than you'd like.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- Stability: *"Rock solid, no wobble even on my slightly uneven tile floor."*

**Where they fall short**

- No floor pads included. You'll be scratching your hardwood unless you buy felt glides separately.
- The wood finish is more prone to scratching than the product photos suggest.
- Assembly alignment can be finicky on one of the two stools — not a return-level issue, but annoying.

**Who should buy these**

- **Kitchen remodelers** who want a mid-century look without paying boutique prices.
- **Homeowners with counter-height islands** (35-37 inches) — these match that height perfectly.
- **Renters** who need something sturdy but portable. At roughly 15 pounds per stool, they're easy to move.

**Skip them if** you have a high-moisture environment (bathroom seating, outdoor covered porch) — the wood isn't sealed well enough for that. Or if you need bar-height stools (30 inches+), these are too short.

**FAQ**

**Q: What's the weight capacity?**  
The manufacturer doesn't list a certified weight limit, but based on the rubberwood frame and X-brace construction, most users report comfortable seating up to 250 pounds. If you're heavier, consider a steel-frame stool instead.

**Q: Do the armrests fit under a standard counter?**  
No — and that's a good thing. The arms are designed to sit alongside your body, not under the counter. Measure your counter overhang; you'll want at least 3 inches of clearance beyond the edge for the arms to fit comfortably.

**Q: Can I stain or repaint them?**  
Technically yes, but you'll need to sand off the existing thin finish first. It's doable but time-consuming. If you're planning to customize, budget an extra hour per stool.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a solid, good-looking set of counter-height stools that won't wobble and won't break the bank. They're not perfect — the finish is thin and you'll need felt pads — but for the price of a set of two, they outperform most comparable options.

**Skip it if:** You're rough on furniture, need bar-height seating, or expect furniture-grade wood finishing. These are excellent value, not heirloom pieces.
