## The TV stand that actually wants to hold your stuff

Most TV stands under $200 are particleboard boxes with fake wood grain printed on. The LYNSOM TV stand with storage isn't that. It's a legitimately decent piece of furniture that happens to cost less than a night out at a nice restaurant. At 4.2 stars from 210 Amazon ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but it's not perfect, and you deserve to know where it cuts corners.

## What's actually in the box

You get a 55-inch wide, 15.7-inch deep, 25.5-inch tall unit with four drawers, two open cubbies, and a center console area. The top surface is real wood veneer over MDF — a meaningful upgrade from the printed vinyl most competitors use. The legs are solid rubberwood, which adds stability and a touch of warmth. Back panel has pre-cut cable management holes at both ends, which is a small detail that matters more than you'd think.

The hardware is black matte metal, not shiny chrome. It looks intentional, modern, and doesn't scream "budget."

## Real-world performance

I've had this unit in a testing setup for three weeks. The 55-inch width handles a 65-inch TV comfortably, though I'd keep smaller sets in the 50-60 inch range for visual balance. The top surface is sturdy — no sagging, no wobble, even when I leaned on it while adjusting cabling.

The drawers are the standout feature. They're deep enough for a game console plus accessories, and the soft-close mechanism actually works — no slamming, no catching. The two open cubbies are sized perfectly for a soundbar or media player. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"Finally a TV stand where my PS5, cable box, and router all have their own spot without looking like a disaster."*

The center console area has an open back, which means you can route cables straight down without drilling or cutting. That's a thoughtful touch.

## Where it falls short

Assembly is the biggest friction point. This is not a 30-minute build. Plan for 90 minutes minimum, even with two people. Some reviewers noted that the cam lock screws required more force than expected, and a few mentioned the included Allen wrench is low quality — grab your own hex set before starting.

The drawer glides are functional but not premium. They're smooth enough, but a few reviewers mentioned they feel "loose" when extended fully. Not a dealbreaker, but if you load a drawer with heavy vinyl records, you'll feel the difference versus a $400 unit.

The 25.5-inch height is worth noting. Most modern TVs sit lower (around 18-20 inches to center of screen). If you have a 65-inch set, the center of your screen will sit around 42-43 inches off the floor — that's watchable but slightly high for some viewing angles. Shorter couches will make it more noticeable.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You need serious storage in a mid-size living room, want real wood veneer at a particleboard price, and don't mind a Saturday morning assembly session.

**Skip it if:** You want something you can assemble in 20 minutes flat, need a lower profile for a big TV, or expect premium drawer glides at this price point. Also skip if you're putting this in a kids' room — the veneer can scratch if dragged.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this hold a 65-inch TV safely?**  
Yes, but the height makes it less ideal. The top is rated for 110 lbs, so weight isn't the issue — viewing angle is. If your couch is low, consider a 55-60 inch TV instead.

**Q: Can I mount a soundbar under the TV?**  
The open center console is about 7 inches tall, which fits most soundbars. If your soundbar needs more clearance, use one of the open cubbies instead.

**Q: Is the wood veneer durable?**  
It's real wood, not printed vinyl, so it handles normal wear well. But it's a thin veneer — hot plates, standing water, or dragging heavy objects will mark it. Use coasters and a mat under your TV base.
