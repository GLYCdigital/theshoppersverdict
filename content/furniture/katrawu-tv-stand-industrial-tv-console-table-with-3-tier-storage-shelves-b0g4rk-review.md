Let me cut through the noise: the Katrawu TV Stand is a $100-ish piece of furniture that looks like it costs twice that in photos. But does it hold up when you're actually sliding a 65-inch TV onto it? Here's what 210 buyers at 4.2 stars are saying — and what they're not.

## What You Actually Get

The box arrives flat-packed and heavy — roughly 45 pounds of particleboard and powder-coated steel. You're getting a 55-inch wide console (about 55" L x 15.7" D x 23.6" H) with three open tiers: one main surface for your TV and two lower shelves for streaming boxes, games, or baskets. The steel frame is the star here — it's thick, welded properly, and gives the whole unit a stability that cheap pressboard stands lack.

The industrial look comes from the combination of dark wood-grain laminate and black metal. It reads "modern farmhouse meets warehouse" — works in lofts, apartments, or anywhere you want a masculine, grounded anchor piece. The back panel has pre-cut cable management holes, which is a thoughtful touch at this price point.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

Here's the honest breakdown: the laminate is matte, not glossy, which hides fingerprints well. The steel frame is rated to support TVs up to 120 pounds — that's a 75-inch TV territory, though most buyers report using 55-65 inch sets comfortably. The surface is deep enough (15.7") to accommodate standard TV feet without overhang.

Assembly is the friction point. Multiple reviewers mention it taking 1.5 to 2 hours, and one frustrated buyer said, "The cam locks and screws look identical until you're staring at step 14 wondering if you mixed them up." Set aside a solid evening, use a power drill with a hex bit, and you'll be fine. The instructions are pictorial and mostly clear, but the hardware labeling could be better.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The sturdiness gets the most love. One verified purchase writes, "I was skeptical at this price, but the steel legs make it feel rock solid. My 65-inch TV sits perfectly centered with zero wobble." Another buyer highlights the storage capacity: "Three tiers is the sweet spot — soundbar on the middle shelf, consoles on the bottom, and the top stays clean for the TV."

The cable management holes earn repeated shoutouts. "Finally a stand where I don't see a nest of wires from the couch," one reviewer notes. The open design also means your remotes actually work — no glass doors blocking IR signals.

## Where It Falls Short

Dust. Open shelving means everything on display gathers a film within days — plan on weekly wiping. The laminate surface scratches white on the dark finish, so be careful sliding heavy equipment. One reviewer noted, "The edge banding on the top shelf started peeling after four months of a soundbar sitting on it."

Also, the 55-inch width is tight for larger TVs. If you're running a 70-inch or bigger, the feet will overhang the edges. And while the steel frame is sturdy, the particleboard shelves have a weight limit — don't load them with books or heavy decor.

## Who Should Buy This
