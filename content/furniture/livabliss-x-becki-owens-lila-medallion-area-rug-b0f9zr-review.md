# Livabliss x Becki Owens Lila Medallion Area Rug: A Designer Look With Real Tradeoffs

You know that moment when you spot a rug in a magazine spread and assume it costs four figures? The Lila Medallion from Livabliss x Becki Owens captures that aesthetic — soft, organic neutrals, a gently distressed medallion center, and that effortlessly layered "California casual" vibe. But at this price point, the question is always: does it hold up, or is it just a pretty picture?

With 185 ratings averaging 3.7 stars, the answer is nuanced. Here's what you need to know before adding it to your cart.

## What You Actually Get

The Lila is a machine-woven polypropylene rug with a low 0.3-inch pile. It arrives folded (not rolled), which means creases — expect to give it 24–48 hours to flatten out, and possibly weigh down the corners. The latex backing provides decent grip on hard floors, so you can skip the rug pad in low-traffic areas.

Available in multiple sizes from 2'x3' runners up to 9'x12', the pattern scales surprisingly well. The medallion design doesn't get lost in larger dimensions, which is a common issue with budget rugs.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the 3.7-star rating starts making sense. The good news: the polypropylene construction is stain-resistant and handles pets well. One verified buyer noted, "I've had this rug for six months with two dogs and a toddler. It still looks new after countless spot cleans."

The less good news: shedding. Multiple reviewers report significant fiber loss during the first few weeks. One buyer wrote, "Lint rolled this rug three times in the first week and still had fuzz." This typically subsides, but it's worth knowing before you place it in a high-visibility area.

The low pile is a double-edged sword. It's practical — chair wheels glide easily, doors swing freely, vacuuming is effortless. But it offers minimal cushioning. If you're looking for a rug to sink your toes into on a cold morning, this isn't it.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The aesthetic is the star. Reviewers repeatedly mention how the rug elevates a room. "The colors are exactly as pictured — a warm greige with subtle cream and taupe tones that tie my whole living room together," one verified purchaser shared. The distressed pattern does an excellent job hiding everyday dirt and pet hair, which is a practical win for busy households.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the shedding, the most significant complaint involves color consistency. Several buyers received rugs that ran lighter or grayer than the listing photos. One reviewer noted, "I ordered the ivory and got something closer to light gray. It still looks nice, but it's not what I picked." If color matching is critical for your space, order a swatch first if available.

The thinness also surprises some buyers. At 0.3 inches, this is closer to a heavy-duty doormat than a plush area rug. It's fine for layering or low-traffic spaces, but don't expect it to anchor a cozy reading nook.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want the designer look without the designer price tag, need a low-profile rug for under furniture, or have pets and kids who will inevitably test its stain resistance.

**Skip it if:** You want a plush, cushioned feel, need exact color matching, or are placing it in a high-traffic hallway where shedding will drive you crazy during the break-in period.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this rug really stop shedding?**
A: Yes, but it takes time. Most buyers report significant shedding for 2–3 weeks, then it stabilizes. Frequent vacuuming and a lint roller speed up the process.

**Q: Can you wash this rug in a machine?**
A: The manufacturer says yes for sizes up to 40 lbs. Several reviewers have successfully machine-washed theirs on a gentle cycle and air-dried. Larger sizes need professional cleaning.

**Q: Will this work on carpet?**
A: It can, but the latex backing may not grip carpet well. Consider a carpet-specific rug pad to prevent slipping and bunching.

## The Verdict

The Lila Medallion delivers exactly what it promises: an affordable, stylish rug that photographs beautifully and wears well in real life. The tradeoffs — shedding, thinness, and color variance — are consistent with its price point. It won't fool a textile expert up close, but from across the room, it looks like a much more expensive piece.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A solid value for style-focused shoppers who prioritize looks over luxury feel. Buy it for the aesthetic, understand the limitations, and you'll be satisfied.
