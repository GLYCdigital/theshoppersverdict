## What actually shows up

The box arrives flat-packed with the usual suspects: cam locks, dowels, Allen wrench, and a manual that's actually legible. You get the main cabinet body, two doors, four tapered legs, and hardware for both wall anchoring and cabinet knobs. The dimensions are generous — roughly 47 inches wide and 31 inches tall — making it a proper buffet, not a glorified nightstand.

The construction pairs a matte MDF frame with hand-woven natural rattan on the door fronts. The wood grain finish looks convincingly natural, and the rattan has that slightly irregular, artisanal quality that mass-produced veneers can't fake.

## Performance and real-world use

This cabinet earns its keep. The two door compartments each hide an adjustable shelf, giving you four distinct storage zones. In practice, that means dinnerware in the left cabinet, bar supplies in the right, and open cubbies for displaying books or a record player. The doors close with a satisfying click — no magnetic catch issues that plague cheap furniture.

The tapered legs give it a mid-century silhouette and raise the piece high enough for a robot vacuum to navigate underneath. Anti-tip straps are included, which is non-negotiable for households with toddlers climbing furniture.

One buyer noted: *"The quality exceeded my expectations for the price. The rattan is tightly woven, not that cheap flimsy stuff you see on some pieces. It's been in my dining room for three months and still looks brand new."* Another echoed: *"Took me about an hour and a half to assemble alone. The doors needed adjustment after a week, but a quick screw tighten fixed it."*

## Where it stumbles

Assembly is the biggest hurdle. The cam lock system isn't difficult, but aligning the doors perfectly requires patience — expect to fiddle with hinge screws. Budget 90 minutes minimum, and recruit a helper for the cabinet top.

The natural rattan is a living material. You'll notice slight color variation across the weave, and some strands may need trimming with scissors after unpacking. That's not a defect; that's the nature of the material. The open cubbies have no backing, so whatever wall sits behind the unit becomes visible through them — a non-issue against a painted wall, but a consideration for wallpaper.

Dust is the other quiet enemy. The woven texture collects it, and cleaning requires a soft brush attachment rather than a quick wipe-down.
