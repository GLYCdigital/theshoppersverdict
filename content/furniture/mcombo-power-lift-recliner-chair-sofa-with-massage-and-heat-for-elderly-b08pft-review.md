If you're shopping for a power lift chair, you've probably noticed the price gap between "medical-grade" brands (think Golden Technologies at $1,200+) and budget options like this MCombo, which typically lands in the $300–$400 range. The question isn't whether it's worth buying — it's whether the savings come with sacrifices you can live with. After digging through 215 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the chair (mostly pre-assembled — you attach the back and base), a wired remote, and a power adapter. Assembly takes about 15 minutes with two people; the back slides into place and locks with two bolts. The dimensions are 39.4" wide by 39.4" deep, with a 20.5" seat height — standard for this category. It's upholstered in PU leather over a steel frame rated for 350 pounds.

**Performance: The lift is the star**

The lift mechanism is the reason you're considering this chair, and it delivers. It raises the seat to a 21-inch height with a smooth, steady motion that takes about 10 seconds. The remote has up/down buttons and a stop function, so you can pause at any position. What buyers consistently praise is how the lift actually works: "My 89-year-old father uses it daily and can get up without help for the first time in two years," one reviewer noted. The motor is quiet — no grinding or hesitation.

The massage function has three intensity levels across two zones (lumbar and thigh). It's a vibration-based system, not a rolling shiatsu massage, so set expectations accordingly. It's pleasant and relaxing, but it won't knead out deep knots. The heat feature is the weakest link — it's a single pad in the lower back that warms to a gentle temperature. If you need real therapeutic heat, this won't cut it.

**Where it falls short**

The PU leather is the biggest compromise. Multiple reviewers report cracking and peeling on the armrests within a year. One warned: "Looks great for 8 months, then the fake leather starts flaking. Buy a throw blanket." If you're in a hot, humid climate, expect faster degradation.

The recline is also a bit unusual. The entire chair tilts backward rather than just the backrest, which means your feet rise with the seat. For taller users (5'8" and up), this feels natural. Shorter users may find their feet don't reach the floor in the upright position, making the lift function less effective. The chair also doesn't fully recline flat — it stops at about 145 degrees, which is fine for watching TV but not for sleeping.

**Who should buy this**

This chair is ideal for seniors who need lift assistance but don't want to spend $1,000+ on a medical-brand chair. It's also a solid choice for anyone with knee or hip issues who wants a comfortable reading chair with bonus massage. Skip it if you need a chair for full-time sleeping, if you're over 6'2" (the seat depth is 20.5", which may be short for long legs), or if you want a chair that looks pristine after two years of daily use.

**FAQ**

*Q: Does the lift work if the power goes out?*
A: No — this is a fully electric chair with no battery backup. Keep it near an outlet and consider a UPS if power outages are common in your area.

*Q: How noisy is the massage function?*
A: It's noticeable but not loud — about the volume of a white noise machine. The lift motor is quieter than the massage vibration.

*Q: Can this chair fit through standard doorways?*
A: The assembled width is 39.4 inches. It will fit through most doorways, but you may need to remove the back (which is designed for easy removal) for narrower openings under 32 inches.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need reliable lift assistance on a budget, you're between 5'4" and 6'0" tall, and you understand the PU leather will wear over time.

**Skip it if:** You need deep therapeutic heat, you want a chair that recline-flats for sleeping, or you expect this to look new after two years of heavy use.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The lift mechanism (the core function) works flawlessly, and the massage is a genuine bonus. The leather durability and limited heat are the tradeoffs you accept for a chair at this price point. For the money, it's a strong value — just protect the armrests and set realistic expectations.
