If you're shopping for a dresser that doesn't swallow your entire bedroom, the **Loomie 7 Drawers Double Dresser** looks like a smart compromise on paper. Seven drawers in a vertical footprint? That's serious storage density. But at 3.7 stars from 185 ratings, the real-world experience is more complicated than the product photos suggest. Here's what you need to know before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box arrives flat-packed — expect a hefty delivery. Inside you'll find the dresser body panels, seven drawer boxes, hardware, and a tool kit. The dimensions are roughly what you'd expect for a "large" chest: about 31 inches wide, 17 inches deep, and 39 inches tall, so it fits standard bedroom spaces without dominating the room. The double-column design means two stacks of drawers (three on the left, four on the right), which is genuinely useful if you and a partner want separate zones.

Assembly will take most people 45–60 minutes. The instructions are clear, parts are labeled well, and you don't need extra tools. That's a genuine plus — many competitors in this price range have confusing builds.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

Here's where the 3.7 rating starts to make sense. The frame uses engineered wood (particle board with a wood-grain finish), which is fine for a budget-to-mid dresser. The finish looks respectable from a few feet away — the white or wood-tone options photograph well and match most bedroom decor.

But the drawer slides are the weak point. The glides are basic metal tracks, not ball-bearing slides, and multiple buyers report them binding or bending when loaded with heavier items like jeans or sweaters. One reviewer noted, *"The drawers stick after a month of normal use. I have to wiggle them to close."* Another described the slides as "the cheapest I've seen on any furniture."

The drawer boxes themselves are decent — they're deep enough for folded clothes, and the bottom panels are reinforced. But don't expect to hang anything inside; these are strictly for folded items.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The storage capacity is the headline. Owners repeatedly mention how much they can fit in seven drawers. *"I moved from a six-drawer dresser and this holds noticeably more,"* one verified purchase said. The vertical design is also praised for fitting into smaller rooms — you get armoire-level storage without the armoire footprint.

The two-column layout gets specific love from couples. *"My husband takes the left side, I take the right. No more fighting over drawer space,"* wrote another reviewer. For shared bedrooms, that's a practical feature you don't see on many dressers at this price.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the drawer slides, there are two consistent complaints. First, the particle board edges are prone to chipping if you bump them during assembly or moving. Second, the dresser feels light — it's sturdy enough when stationary, but it can wobble slightly if you load the top drawers unevenly. Anchoring it to the wall is recommended, especially if you have kids.

One more thing: the "large" in the title refers to storage capacity, not physical size. If you're expecting a massive, imposing dresser, this is more of a medium-footprint piece. Measure your space before ordering.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** who need maximum storage in a small bedroom footprint
- **Couples** who want separate drawer zones without buying two dressers
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who accept that particle board is a tradeoff for the price

**Skip it if** you need heavy-duty furniture that survives moves, or if you regularly pack drawers to the brim — the slides will frustrate you.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need serious storage in a compact footprint and can handle minor assembly quirks. The seven-drawer layout is genuinely useful, and for the price, it delivers solid value.

**Skip it if** you want furniture that will survive multiple moves or heavy daily use. The drawer slides and particle board are budget compromises.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A practical storage solution held back by flimsy hardware. Perfect for a guest room or single person's bedroom, but don't expect heirloom quality.
