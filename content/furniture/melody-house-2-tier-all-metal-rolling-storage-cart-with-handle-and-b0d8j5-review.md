**A $30 metal cart that actually earns its keep — if you know what you're buying**

Let's cut through the noise: the Melody House 2-Tier Rolling Cart is not a piece of fine furniture. It's a welded steel workhorse that's been quietly racking up a 4.6-star average across 229 Amazon ratings, and once you understand what it does well (and where it cuts corners), it's easy to see why.

**What you actually get**

The cart arrives as a flat-pack bundle: two wire mesh shelves, four caster wheels (two with locking brakes), a fixed handle, and the hardware to connect it all. The all-metal construction is the headline here — there's not a single plastic joint or clip in the frame. The shelves are welded directly to the upright posts, which is a significant step up from the friction-fit carts that dominate this price bracket.

Assembly runs about 15 minutes. You'll need a Phillips screwdriver. The instructions claim "tool-free" assembly, which is optimistic — the wheels and handle bolts require actual torque to seat properly. One reviewer noted, "Took me about 20 minutes with a drill. The metal is thick enough that hand-tightening the bolts felt sketchy."

**Real-world performance**

The dimensions are roughly 23 inches long, 13 inches wide, and 31 inches tall. That footprint is the cart's superpower. It slides into the gap between a refrigerator and counter, tucks beside a washer/dryer, or fits under a desk without dominating the room.

The welded frame makes a genuine difference. Load it with a 12-pack of paper towels on the top shelf and a basket of cleaning supplies on the bottom, and there's zero flex. The casters roll smoothly on hardwood and low-pile carpet, and the two locking brakes hold firm on sloped garage floors — a detail shoppers consistently mention. One verified purchase wrote: "I loaded this with paint cans and tools in my garage. It doesn't wobble. The brakes actually stop it from rolling on my sloped driveway."

The wire mesh is rated for roughly 40 pounds per shelf, which is conservative — the welds suggest it could handle more. But here's the tradeoff: the mesh gaps are wide enough that small items fall through. A pen, a paperclip, a single tea bag — all gone. If you're using this for office supplies or spices, budget for a liner or a small tray on each shelf.

**Where it falls short**

The handle is fixed. There's no height adjustment, and at roughly 31 inches, it's comfortable for average-height users but feels low for anyone over 6 feet. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you commit.

The finish is powder-coated, which resists scratching, but it's not rust-proof. If you're planning to use this in a damp laundry room or outdoors, expect surface rust within six months. Keep it indoors and it'll look fine.

**The consensus from 229 ratings**

The praise clusters around three things: stability, size, and value. Repeated phrases in the reviews include "surprisingly sturdy," "fits perfectly in my narrow space," and "great for the price." The criticism is nearly all about the mesh gaps and the misleading assembly claim.

**Who should buy this**

This cart earns its place in three specific scenarios:

- **Kitchen organization** — microwave, toaster, and coffee supplies on one shelf, pantry overflow on the other. The narrow footprint fits where most carts won't.
- **Garage or workshop** — tools, rags, and spray bottles. The welded frame shrugs off abuse.
- **Office or craft room** — printer paper and supplies, provided you add shelf liners.

Skip it if you need a fully enclosed storage solution or if you're over 6-foot-2 and plan to push it around constantly.

**FAQ**

*Is the cart really all metal?*
Yes. The frame, shelves, and handle are all steel. Only the wheel casters and brake mechanisms contain plastic components.

*What's the weight capacity?*
The manufacturer rates each shelf at 40 pounds. The welded frame suggests it could handle more, but stick to the rated limits to avoid stressing the caster mounts.

*Does it fit under a standard desk?*
The cart stands about 31 inches tall, so it fits under most standing desks and standard-height workstations. It won't fit under a low coffee table or most kitchen counters with upper cabinets above.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a sturdy, space-efficient rolling cart for indoor use and you're okay with adding a liner for small items. At this price point, the welded metal construction is genuinely uncommon.

**Skip it if** you need enclosed storage, plan to use it outdoors or in damp environments, or you're significantly above average height.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a practical, honest utility cart that overdelivers on build quality and underdelivers only on the details the marketing glosses over.
