The Faesun Swivel Side Table solves a problem you didn't know you had: reaching your drink without contorting your body. That 360-degree rotation isn't a gimmick — it's the difference between a side table you actually use and one you ignore.

Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 220 ratings and the build specs.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the table base, column, tabletop, and the Allen wrench. No extra tools needed. The pieces feel substantial out of the box — the steel base has real heft, which matters more than you'd think for stability.

The top measures 15.7 inches in diameter. That's enough for a coffee mug, a book, and your phone. It won't hold a laptop and dinner plate simultaneously, but that's not its job. The height lands in the 24–26 inch range depending on how you seat the column — right for most armchairs and sofas.

## Performance and Build Quality

The swivel mechanism is the star. It rotates smoothly on a ball-bearing track, and here's what matters: the base stays planted. You can spin the top with one finger without the whole unit creeping across the floor. That's rare at this price point.

The steel frame supports a surprising amount of weight. One reviewer noted, "I lean on it to get out of my recliner and it doesn't budge." That tracks with the weighted base design.

The finish is matte and modern — fits both industrial and transitional living rooms. As shown in the product image, it pairs well with leather recliners and fabric sofas alike.

## What Buyers Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is convenience. One buyer wrote, "I don't have to twist to reach my coffee anymore. I just spin the top toward me." Another mentioned using it as a laptop stand while working from the couch, spinning it out of the way when done.

The compact footprint gets consistent love from apartment dwellers and anyone with a cramped seating arrangement.

## Where It Falls Short

Two honest complaints surface repeatedly.

First, the tabletop finish isn't as durable as the frame. Scratches show up if you slide ceramic mugs across it. Use coasters. This isn't a "leave the whiskey glass bare" table.

Second, assembly is straightforward but slightly fiddly. The column threads into the base, and if you overtighten, the swivel can bind. Tighten snug, not gorilla. Takes about 15 minutes if you're patient.

One reviewer also noted the top can feel slightly wobbly if you've assembled it incorrectly — that's user error, not a design flaw, but worth mentioning so you double-check the lock ring.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Recliner users** who hate twisting to reach a standard side table
- **Small-space dwellers** who need a table that tucks away when not in use
- **Anyone with a deep sofa** who wants a table that swings over the armrest instead of sitting out of reach

Skip it if you need a surface for heavy objects like printers or large plants — this is a drinks-and-remote table, not a workhorse.

## FAQ

**Q: How much weight can the swivel top hold?**
The manufacturer doesn't specify exactly, but buyers report using it for laptops and leaning on it without issues. Realistic limit is around 15–20 pounds for daily use. Don't sit on it.

**Q: Does the swivel lock in place?**
No locking mechanism. It rotates freely, which is the point. If you need a fixed position, tighten the base ring slightly to increase friction.

**Q: What tools are needed for assembly?**
Just the included Allen wrench. No drill, no screwdriver. It's a three-piece assembly with four screws.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a functional side table that adapts to your seating position rather than forcing you to adapt to it. The swivel genuinely improves daily use, and the build quality justifies the price.

**Skip it if** you need a scratch-proof surface or a table that stays in one locked position permanently.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-designed, practical piece with minor finish durability tradeoffs. The swivel feature earns its keep, and 220 buyers agree it's worth the space.
