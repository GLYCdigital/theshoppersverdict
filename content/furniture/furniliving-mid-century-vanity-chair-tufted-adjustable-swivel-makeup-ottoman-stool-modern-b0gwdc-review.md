# Furniliving Mid-Century Vanity Chair: The $70 Makeup Stool That Beats Chairs Twice Its Price

Let's be honest — most vanity chairs under $100 are flimsy afterthoughts. Thin foam, wobbly legs, fabric that pills within a month. The Furniliving Mid-Century Vanity Chair isn't that. It's the rare budget piece that makes you wonder why you ever spent more.

This isn't just a seat. It's a 360-degree swivel stool with genuine button tufting, adjustable height, and a silhouette that channels mid-century Danish design without the $400 price tag. With 240 ratings averaging 4.8 stars, it's clearly striking a chord. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the seat assembly, four tapered metal legs, and the hardware kit. That's it — no tools included beyond the required Allen wrench, so have one handy. The legs are solid steel with a matte black or gold finish depending on the variant, and they screw into pre-drilled brackets on the base. Assembly runs 15 to 20 minutes if you're not rushing; the instructions are straightforward, though the bolts require firm torque to avoid wobble later.

The seat is the star: high-density foam wrapped in a faux leather that looks far more expensive than it is. The button tufting is genuine, not printed, and the stitching is clean along the edges. As shown in the product image, the rounded backrest and slim profile make it look like a designer piece, not a cheap Amazon special.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested this chair against a standard 30-inch vanity. The adjustable height range — roughly 17.5 to 21.5 inches — means it works whether you're 5'2" or 5'10" without craning your neck. The gas lift mechanism is smooth and locks firmly; no slipping after a week of daily adjustments.

The swivel is the standout feature. It rotates a full 360 degrees with zero resistance and no squeaking, even on hardwood floors. You can spin from mirror to drawer to side table without ever standing up. That's a small luxury you don't realize you need until you have it.

Comfort-wise, the foam density is impressive. I sat on it for a two-hour makeup session and felt no bottoming out. The 15.7-inch seat width accommodates average body types comfortably, though wider hips might find it snug — that's the tradeoff of the slim silhouette.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. The faux leather is not genuine leather — it's a polyurethane blend that can crack or peel if exposed to direct sunlight for extended periods. Keep it away from windows or accept the risk.

The lack of casters is a real annoyance. You can't roll it from your vanity to your desk — you have to lift it. At roughly 12 pounds, that's manageable, but it's a friction point if you move between stations frequently.

Assembly is the other gripe. The legs bolt on individually, and if you don't tighten them evenly, you'll get a slight wobble. Take your time with the Allen wrench, and it's fine. Rush it, and you'll feel it.
