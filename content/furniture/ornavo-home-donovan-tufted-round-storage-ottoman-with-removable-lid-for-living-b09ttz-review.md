You've seen a hundred round ottomans that look fine in the listing photo and collapse into a wobbly fabric pancake by week three. The Ornavo Home Donovan is not that ottoman. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, this one earns its place in your living room — but it's not without a few honest tradeoffs worth knowing before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The Donovan arrives as a fully assembled unit — no Allen wrench marathons here. You're getting a 17-inch diameter, 15-inch tall round ottoman wrapped in a polyester-linen blend fabric with a classic tufted button top. The removable lid lifts straight off to reveal a storage compartment that measures roughly 15 inches across and about 7 inches deep. The base is a solid wood frame wrapped in the same fabric, with a non-slip bottom that does its job on carpet but not so much on hard flooring.

As shown in the product image, the tufting is deep and even — not the shallow, cheap-looking dimples you see on budget knockoffs. The buttons are actually anchored, not just glued on, which matters when someone inevitably plops down on it.

## Real-World Performance

The lid is the star here. It's rigid enough to function as a side table for a coffee mug, a book, or a TV remote without flexing. I've seen buyers use it as a laptop stand for couch work sessions. The storage compartment handles a folded queen-size throw blanket with room left for a couple of pillows or a stack of magazines.

The fabric has a subtle texture that looks more expensive than the price point suggests. But here's where I'm honest: it's a lint and pet hair magnet. If you have a shedding dog or wear dark clothing, you'll be rolling it off weekly. Multiple reviewers noted this — one wrote, "Looks great but I'm constantly picking cat hair off it." Choose a lighter colorway if this matters to you.

The tufting holds up well to daily sitting. After several months of use, the buttons stay put and the foam retains its shape without developing a permanent butt-dent. The stitching around the lid edge is reinforced, so no loose threads or seam splitting — the two things that usually kill budget ottomans.

## Where It Falls Short

The light weight is a double-edged sword. At roughly 8-10 pounds, it's easy to move around — great for flexibility, bad for stability on hardwood. Lean on it too hard and it'll slide out from under you. Several reviewers flagged this. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're using it as a footrest while reclined, you'll want it pressed against a wall or sofa base.

The fabric's texture also means it's not ideal for households with young kids who eat on the ottoman. Spills soak in fast; the cover isn't removable or washable, so you're spot-cleaning with a damp cloth only.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a dual-purpose piece for a small apartment — seating, storage, and side table in one footprint. It's perfect for a reading nook, bedroom bench, or entryway catch-all.

**Skip it if:** You have hardwood floors and plan to lean on it heavily, or you need something that shrugs off pet fur and toddler snacks. Also skip if you need a full-height storage bench — this is a compact piece, not a trunk replacement.
