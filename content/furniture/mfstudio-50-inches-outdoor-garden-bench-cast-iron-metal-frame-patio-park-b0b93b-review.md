# MFSTUDIO 50-Inch Cast Iron Garden Bench: The Real Story After 225 Reviews

Most outdoor benches under $150 are built from thin tubular steel that bends the first time someone heavier than 200 pounds sits down. The MFSTUDIO 50-inch bench takes a different approach: it's cast iron, and that changes everything about how it performs.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the bench disassembled: two cast iron side frames with decorative scrollwork, a seat pan with slatted wood-look metal planks, the curved backrest section, and a hardware kit. There's no welding required, no drilling — just four bolts per side and you're done. Most buyers report assembly in under 10 minutes with the included Allen wrench.

The 50-inch width fits two average adults with room to spare. The seat height sits at a standard 17.5 inches, which works well for most people. As shown in the product image, the backrest has a gentle curve that follows your spine rather than forcing you upright like many park-style benches.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where this bench separates itself from the $80 Amazon specials. The cast iron frame is genuinely heavy — about 35 pounds total — and that weight translates directly to stability. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "I'm 240 pounds and this bench doesn't budge when I sit down. My previous metal bench flexed and creaked. This one is solid as a rock."

The powder-coated finish is the second standout. Multiple reviewers mention leaving it out for two or three full seasons without rust developing. That's rare at this price point. The seat slats are painted metal designed to look like wood, and while they don't have the warmth of real timber, they also won't splinter, rot, or need yearly sealing.

The scrollwork on the armrests and legs isn't just decorative — it adds structural rigidity. This is a bench that feels like it was built to survive decades, not just a couple of summers.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. At 35 pounds, this isn't a bench you'll casually reposition for afternoon sun. It's a set-it-and-forget-it piece.

The slatted seat has gaps that swallow small items. One reviewer noted, "My phone slipped right through the slats and landed in the grass. Annoying but not a dealbreaker."

The standard model has no armrests. If you're buying for an older family member or someone with knee issues, the armrest version exists — spend the extra money. Getting up from a bench without arms is genuinely harder for some people.
