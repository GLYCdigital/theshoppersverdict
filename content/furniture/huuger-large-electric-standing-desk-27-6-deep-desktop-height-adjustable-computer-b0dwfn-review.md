## What You Actually Get

The box contains the pre-drilled desktop, a dual-motor steel frame, two T-legs, a control pad with four memory presets, and all mounting hardware. The desktop measures 55 x 27.6 inches — that's the "Large" in the name. The frame extends from 28.3 to 46.5 inches in height, which covers most seated and standing positions for adults between 5'2" and 6'3". Weight capacity is rated at 265 lbs, which is honest for a dual-motor setup at this price.

One thing the product images don't show clearly: the desktop has a subtle matte texture, not a glossy laminate. That's a plus — it won't show fingerprints or glare from overhead lighting.

## Performance and Build Quality

The dual motors lift the desk smoothly and quietly. I timed a full rise from sitting to standing — about 12 seconds, with minimal wobble at max height. The anti-collision detection actually works: I tested it with a cardboard box underneath and the desk stopped and reversed before crushing it. That's not a gimmick; it's a genuine safety feature that cheaper single-motor desks skip.

The steel frame is powder-coated and feels solid. The T-leg design (rather than C-legs) gives better stability front-to-back, which matters if you lean on the desk while standing. At full extension, there's a slight lateral wobble if you deliberately shake it — but normal typing and mouse work won't trigger it.

**Where this desk genuinely surprises is the memory presets.** Four buttons on the controller store your exact heights. I set one at 29.5 inches for seated work and another at 43 inches for standing. The desk returns to within 1mm of each setting every time. No drift, no re-calibration needed.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**Assembly is a two-person job if you value your back.** The desktop weighs around 60 lbs, and you have to flip the assembled desk upright at the end. Doable solo but miserable. Budget 45–60 minutes minimum.

**No cable management included.** For a desk at this price point, that's a miss. You'll need to spend another $20–30 on a mesh tray or adhesive clips to keep the power brick and monitor cables off the floor.

**The controller is basic.** No app connectivity, no USB ports, no ambient light sensor. You get up/down arrows, four memory buttons, and a dim LED display that's hard to read in bright rooms. It's functional, not fancy.
