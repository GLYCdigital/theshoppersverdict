## What You Actually Get

The box contains the desk top, the Z-shaped steel frame, a full-desk mouse pad, a cable management tray, and all mounting hardware. The 60-inch width is generous — as one buyer put it, "I can fit my 27-inch monitor, a second vertical monitor, and still have room for my keyboard and coffee." The curved front edge isn't just for looks; it brings the center of the desk closer to you, which reduces reaching for your keyboard.

The carbon fiber textured surface is the standout feature. It's not real carbon fiber, but the vinyl laminate feels durable and resists scratches better than the raw MDF you'll find on cheaper desks. The full mouse pad covers the entire playing surface — a rare inclusion at this price point.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The Z-shaped steel frame is rated to hold significantly more than the average gaming setup. Multiple reviewers noted the stability: "No wobble even when I get intense in FPS games." That's genuinely impressive for a desk with a curved design, which typically sacrifices rigidity for aesthetics.

The cable management tray runs the full width underneath and has two openings — front and back. It's wide enough for a power strip and multiple cables. One reviewer mentioned it "saved my setup from looking like a spaghetti monster."

The surface is a fingerprint magnet. The dark carbon fiber texture shows every smudge, dust speck, and cat hair. You'll be wiping it down weekly if you care about aesthetics.

## What Buyers Praise

The consistent thread in positive reviews is the mouse pad. "The full-desk mouse pad is a game changer — no more mouse dropping off the edge mid-game," one verified buyer wrote. Another noted the assembly quality: "Parts fit together precisely, no stripped screws, no misaligned holes."

The ergonomic curve gets consistent appreciation. Multiple reviewers mentioned it reduced shoulder strain during long sessions.

## Where It Falls Short

The mouse pad is only 2mm thick. On a hard desk surface, that's not enough cushioning for marathon gaming sessions. Several reviewers mentioned buying a separate wrist rest within the first month.

Assembly is the biggest gripe. The manual's exploded diagrams are confusing, and the hardware isn't labeled. Expect 45–60 minutes with a second person, especially when aligning the frame to the pre-drilled holes. One reviewer noted, "The instructions look like they were translated three times."

The cable tray, while useful, is a bit shallow. Large power bricks won't fit flat — you'll need to angle them.
