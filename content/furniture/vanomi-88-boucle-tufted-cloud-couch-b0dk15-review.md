## What You're Actually Getting

The headline number is 88 inches wide — that's the sweet spot for a three-seater in most modern apartments. It's not so massive that it dominates a 12x14 living room, but it's genuinely roomier than the 72-inch "apartment sofas" that force guests to sit like they're on a bus.

The boucle fabric is the talking point here. It's that looped, textured weave that's been everywhere in interior design for the past few years. The tufted backrest adds a structured, tailored look that plain boucle sofas lack — it breaks up what can otherwise be a shapeless blob of cream fabric.

As shown in the product image, the cushions are deep and plush. The "cloud" marketing language is overused, but the dimensions suggest a real sink-in seat depth of around 23-24 inches. That's genuinely comfortable for lounging, not just sitting upright.

## Build Quality and Real-World Concerns

Without owner reviews, I'm reading between the lines of what the listing doesn't say. There's no mention of the frame material — that's a yellow flag. At this price point, you're likely getting either solid wood or engineered wood. The latter is fine if it's kiln-dried and properly braced, but be wary of anything that advertises "particle board" construction.

The boucle fabric itself is a mixed blessing. It's soft and photogenic, but it's also notoriously difficult to clean. Spills wick into those loops and don't come out easily. If you have kids, pets, or a tendency to eat on the couch, budget for a fabric protector spray on day one.

The 32-inch overall height is worth noting. That's on the lower end for a sofa with a backrest. Taller users (5'10" and up) may find the back support ends at an awkward point. If you're 6'2" like me, you'll be slouching to get your head on the cushion or sitting bolt upright to use the backrest properly.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the boucle fabric hard to keep clean?**
Yes, and this is the biggest maintenance caveat. Boucle's looped texture traps dust and liquids. Vacuum weekly with an upholstery attachment and treat spills immediately with a damp cloth — don't rub, blot. Consider a Scotchgard-style fabric protector before first use.

**Q: What's the actual seat depth?**
Based on the 88-inch width and typical three-seater proportions, expect roughly 23-24 inches of seat depth. That's generous for lounging but means your feet won't touch the floor when sitting all the way back.

**Q: Will two people really be enough for assembly?**
Yes, but only if one person can hold pieces steady while the other drives screws. The 45-minute estimate is realistic if you're organized. Lay out all parts first and sort the hardware before starting.
