The vertical file cabinet is the least glamorous piece of furniture in any home office. It's a box with drawers. So when the Realspace 17" D Vertical 2-Drawer File Cabinet in Mulled Cherry shows up with a 4.3-star average across 215 ratings, the question isn't whether it's exciting — it's whether it does the job without making you regret the purchase. After digging through the feedback and the specs, here's the honest breakdown.

## What you actually get

Out of the box, you're looking at a laminate-over-engineered-wood cabinet that stands about 28 inches tall and spans 17 inches deep. The mulled cherry finish is the standout feature — several reviewers noted it looks closer to real cherry veneer than most cabinets in this price tier. The metal drawer pulls match the dark bronze-ish tone of the frame, giving it a cohesive look that won't embarrass you next to a nicer desk.

The two drawers are sized for letter and legal hanging files. The 17-inch depth is the spec that matters: it means your hanging folders sit flush without bowing the drawer front. That's a genuine advantage over the 15-inch "compact" cabinets that force you to crimp your files.

## Performance and real-world use

The drawers run on ball-bearing slides with full extension. That means you can pull a drawer all the way out and see everything — no digging blind in the back. A soft-close mechanism catches the drawer near the end and eases it shut. For a cabinet in this range, that's a feature set you'd normally expect to pay more for.

One reviewer summed it up: "I was surprised how solid the drawers feel. No wobble, no sticking, even when fully loaded with old client files." Another noted the cabinet stays stable even with the top drawer open — a common failure point on cheaper vertical models.

Assembly is where the experience gets less rosy. Multiple buyers describe a two-person job that took 45 to 60 minutes, with the drawer slide alignment being the frustrating part. The instructions are mostly diagram-based, which helps if you're visual but leaves you guessing on the order of operations. Have a screwdriver and patience on hand.

## Where it falls short

The back panel is thin fiberboard. It's fine for a cabinet that sits against a wall and never moves, but if you're the type to rearrange your office every six months, expect corner scuffs or worse. One reviewer mentioned their panel cracked after a single move.

The mulled cherry finish, while attractive, is still laminate. It'll resist coffee rings and scratches, but it won't take a refinish. What you see is what you get.

Also worth noting: this is not a lockable cabinet. If you need to secure sensitive documents, you'll need to look elsewhere. The product images don't hide this, but it's easy to miss in the feature list.

## Who should buy this

Three types of shoppers will get the most value here:

- **Home office workers** who need legal-size file storage and want something that doesn't scream "dorm room"
- **Small business owners** with paper-heavy operations who need a second or third cabinet that matches existing furniture
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want soft-close drawers without paying $200+ for a steel alternative

Skip it if you need security features, plan to move frequently, or want something you can assemble solo in 20 minutes.

## FAQ

**Does it hold legal-size hanging files?**
Yes. The 17-inch depth accommodates both letter and legal hanging files without forcing the folders to bend.

**Is the finish real wood?**
No, it's a laminate over engineered wood. That said, the mulled cherry pattern is well-executed and several reviewers commented on how convincing it looks.

**What tools do I need for assembly?**
A Phillips screwdriver is essential. A power drill with a Phillips bit will save you time. The included allen wrench handles the rest.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a solid, good-looking file cabinet that handles legal files and feels more expensive than it is. The soft-close drawers and full-extension slides are legitimately nice touches.

**Skip it if** you need locking drawers, plan to move it around frequently, or expect a zero-frustration assembly process.

At a 4.2 out of 5, this is a well-above-average choice for the category. It's not perfect — the thin back panel and tricky assembly keep it from the top tier — but for the price, you'd be hard-pressed to find a better-looking, better-functioning vertical file cabinet. If your paperwork needs a home and your office needs a furniture upgrade, this one earns its place.
