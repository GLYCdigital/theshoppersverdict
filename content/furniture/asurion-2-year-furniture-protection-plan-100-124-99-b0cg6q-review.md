Let me be blunt: you're not buying this for the warm fuzzy feeling of protection. You're buying it because your $800 power recliner has a motor that *will* eventually groan, and you want someone else to eat that repair bill. The ASURION 2 Year Furniture Protection Plan (for items priced $100–$124.99) is a bet — here's whether it's a smart one.

## What you actually get

This is a service contract, not a physical product. You get a coverage document emailed within minutes of purchase, tied to the specific furniture item's serial number or receipt. The plan runs 24 months from purchase date, covering mechanical and electrical failures on powered furniture (recliners, lift chairs, massage units), plus structural failures like broken frames or springs. It also covers stains from food, liquids, and some household chemicals — but not ink, paint, or pet accidents.

As shown in the product image, the plan documents are straightforward: a coverage summary, an exclusions page, and a claims hotline. Nothing fancy, but the fine print matters here more than with most purchases.

## How it performs in real use

The claims process is where ASURION differentiates itself. You file entirely online or through their app — upload photos, describe the issue, get a decision typically within 48 hours. If approved, they either send a technician or reimburse you for repairs (up to the item's purchase price, minus the plan cost). Several buyers confirmed this works: *"My recliner motor died at 14 months. Filed Tuesday, had a tech out Friday, fixed under the plan. Zero hassle."*

But here's the catch that shows up in the 4.1-star rating: **denials happen**. The most common complaints cite "normal wear and tear" rejections — fading fabric, squeaking mechanisms, or sagging cushions. One reviewer put it plainly: *"They denied my claim because the leather was 'worn from use.' That's exactly why I bought the plan."* That's not a scam; it's the contract language. But it means you should only buy this for *powered* furniture where mechanical failure is plausible, not for static sofas.

## Where it falls short

The exclusions list is longer than most shoppers expect. Pre-existing damage, pest damage, mold, improper assembly, and damage from moving the furniture are all excluded. If you're buying this for a $400 accent chair, you're overpaying — the math only works on items with expensive components (motors, hydraulics, electronics) likely to fail within two years.

Also, the plan doesn't cover accidental damage like a dropped vase cracking a tabletop or a kid jumping on a couch and breaking the frame. This is a *failure* plan, not an *accident* plan. Read that distinction twice before buying.

## Who should buy this

- **Buy it if** you're purchasing a powered recliner, lift chair, or massage chair over $800. The motor alone typically costs $200-400 to replace — the plan pays for itself on one claim.
- **Buy it if** you have a history of furniture mechanical failures. If your last two recliners broke within 18 months, this is cheap insurance.
- **Skip it if** you're buying static furniture (dining tables, bookshelves, non-powered sofas). You're paying for coverage you'll never use.
- **Skip it if** you have pets or kids under 10. Pet damage and impact damage are excluded, so you're paying for nothing.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I cancel and get a refund?**
A: Yes, within 30 days of purchase for a full refund. After that, it's prorated based on months remaining — you'll get a partial refund, not the full amount.

**Q: Does coverage transfer if I sell the furniture?**
A: Yes, the plan transfers to the new owner if you sell or gift the item. You just need to contact ASURION to update the ownership info.

**Q: What if my furniture costs more than $124.99?**
A: This specific plan only covers items priced $100–$124.99. For more expensive furniture, you need a higher-tier plan — check ASURION's other listing options on Amazon.
