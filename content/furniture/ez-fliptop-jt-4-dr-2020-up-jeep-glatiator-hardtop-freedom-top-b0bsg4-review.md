The EZ FlipTop JT isn't the first aftermarket hardtop for the Gladiator, but it's the first one that actually solves the freedom panel hassle. If you've ever wrestled a factory hardtop panel off your Jeep, wedged it into the storage bag, and prayed it didn't scratch the paint, you already know why 245 buyers gave this thing 4.9 stars.

**What you actually get**

The box shows up with the complete fiberglass hardtop — shell, flip-back freedom panels, rear window with defroster, side glass, and all mounting hardware. It's fully assembled out of the box, which is rare in this category. The fiberglass is textured to match the factory Gladiator finish, and the tinted glass looks factory-installed. As one owner put it: "I've had three hardtops over the years and this is the only one people assume came from the dealer."

**The flip mechanism is the whole story**

Here's how it works: instead of lifting panels off and storing them, the two front panels hinge backward and lock flat over the rear roof section. You reach up, release two latches, push the panels back, and you've got open-air driving — all from the driver's seat. Took me about 20 seconds on the first try, and it's genuinely tool-free.

That said, there's a catch. The panels need clearance to flip, and rear seat headrests have to be pushed down first. If you regularly carry tall adults in the back, they'll lose a few inches of headroom while the panels are stowed. It's a minor annoyance for a convertible Jeep, but worth knowing before you buy.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The fiberglass is thick — noticeably heavier than the factory top, which translates to less flex on rough trails. The seals are dense and the latches feel solid, not cheap. Owners consistently report no wind noise increase at highway speeds, which surprised me given the flip mechanism. One long-term review noted: "Six months of daily driving and trail use. Still dead silent on the interstate."

The rear window has a defroster that wires into the factory harness, and the glass is real automotive glass, not plexiglass. The sliding side windows operate smoothly and seal tight in rain.

**Where it falls short**

The install is a two-person job, period. At roughly 90 pounds, you're not balancing this on your head while aligning the mounting points. Most owners report a 2-3 hour install with basic tools, but don't attempt it solo.

The price is also premium — you're paying for the engineering, not a bargain. If you never use your freedom panels, a standard aftermarket hardtop will save you several hundred dollars. And the flip mechanism adds a bit of complexity to what's otherwise a simple roof; there are more moving parts to maintain over time.

**Who should buy this**

The Gladiator owner who actually uses their freedom panels — the one who flips them open every time the weather's decent. If you live in a climate with unpredictable rain, the ability to close the roof in seconds without pulling over is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade. It's also ideal for anyone who's tired of storing bulky panels in the garage.

Skip it if you only take the top off once a year, or if you frequently carry rear passengers who need maximum headroom.

**FAQ**

*Q: Will this fit a 2023 or 2024 Gladiator?*
A: Yes, the EZ FlipTop JT is designed for all 2020 and newer Gladiator models. The mounting points are identical across model years.

*Q: Does it come with a defroster wiring harness?*
A: Yes, the rear window defroster plugs into the factory harness. No cutting or splicing required.

*Q: Can I still use my factory soft top with this?*
A: No. This replaces the soft top entirely. It's a permanent hardtop installation, not a hybrid system.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want open-air driving without the panel-storage dance, and you value a hardtop that looks and performs like factory equipment.

**Skip it if** you're on a budget, rarely use your freedom panels, or need maximum rear-seat headroom with panels stowed.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The flip mechanism is brilliant, the build quality is excellent, and the minor compromises are worth the trade-off for most Gladiator owners.
