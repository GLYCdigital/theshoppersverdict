Most locking cabinets under $100 are a gamble. Either the lock feels like a toy, or the whole thing wobbles the moment you slide a drawer open. The Yizosh Locking Cabinet with 2 Drawers sidesteps both pitfalls — and at 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, buyers agree it's one of the more dependable budget options out there.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the cabinet body, two drawers, a pair of keys, and four bolts. That's it — and that's the point. As shown in the product image, this is a compact vertical unit measuring roughly 18" wide by 17" deep by 27" tall. The all-steel shell is powder-coated in a matte finish that reads more expensive than the price tag suggests.

Assembly is genuinely painless. Pre-drilled holes align perfectly, and you're looking at 15–20 minutes with a Phillips head screwdriver. No cams, no Allen wrenches, no swearing. One reviewer noted, "I put it together while my coffee was still hot — that never happens."

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The headline feature is the locking mechanism, and it's the real deal. This isn't a decorative turn-lock that a paperclip could defeat. It's a proper keyed lock with a steel bolt that seats firmly into the frame. When engaged, the drawers won't budge even with significant force.

The drawers themselves run on ball-bearing slides — a feature you'd expect on cabinets twice the price. They extend fully and support a reasonable load without sagging. The floor of each drawer is reinforced, so stacking files or small tools won't cause flexing.

Security-wise, buyers consistently mention it handles its job. One verified purchase wrote: "I keep my medication and a backup wallet in here. The lock is solid — my curious teenager hasn't cracked it yet." Another praised it for dorm use: "RA-approved, and honestly sturdy enough to survive move-in day."

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest limitation is internal capacity. Each drawer is about 14 inches deep and 15 inches wide. Standard hanging file folders won't fit — you'll need to use it for stacked papers or loose items, not organized filing systems. If you need letter-size file storage, look elsewhere.

The keys are basic stamped metal. They work fine, but there's no serial number or replacement program. Lose both keys and you're drilling out the lock. Make a spare and keep it somewhere safe.

Finally, the matte finish, while attractive, is a fingerprint magnet. Darker colorways especially show smudges after regular use. A microfiber cloth lives near this cabinet if you care about appearances.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Dorm residents** needing to secure valuables, meds, or expensive electronics from roommates and visitors
- **Home office users** who want lockable storage for sensitive documents, checks, or a backup wallet without a massive filing cabinet footprint
- **Apartment dwellers** with limited floor space who need a secure catch-all for tools, hobby supplies, or personal items

## FAQ

**Q: Can I mount this to the wall to prevent tipping?**
A: There are no pre-drilled mounting holes on the back panel, but the unit is bottom-heavy and stable. If you're placing it on carpet or have small children, anchoring it with your own hardware is a smart precaution.

**Q: Does the lock come with more than two keys?**
A: Two keys are included. No additional spares ship with the unit, and there's no key replacement program, so have a copy made at a hardware store.

**Q: Will it fit under a desk?**
A: At 27 inches tall, it's designed for floor placement beside a desk, not underneath. For under-desk storage, you'd want something shorter.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a compact, genuinely secure locking cabinet at a fair price. The solid steel build, smooth ball-bearing drawers, and functional lock make it a dependable pick for dorms, offices, or anywhere you need to keep things private.

**Skip it if** you're looking for full legal-size file storage or need a piece that mounts directly to a wall. For those needs, you'll want a dedicated filing cabinet or a wall-mounted lockbox.

At 4.6/5, the Yizosh earns its rating. It's not flashy, but it does exactly what it promises — and that's rarer than it should be in budget furniture.
