VINGLI’s four-pack of outdoor dining chairs is trying to solve a very specific problem: how do you furnish a patio for a full dinner party without spending furniture-store money? At roughly $60–$70 per chair (check current pricing — it fluctuates), these stackable mesh chairs undercut most big-box competitors by 30–40%. But the real question isn't the price. It's whether they'll survive a second summer.

**What you actually get**

The box contains four chairs, a set of Allen keys, and screws already partially threaded into the frame. The design is simple: a powder-coated steel tube frame, breathable PVC mesh seat and back, and plastic floor glides on the legs. Each chair measures about 22.5" wide, 33" tall, and 22" deep — a standard footprint that fits most 28–30" deep patio tables. The seat height sits at 17.5", which pairs well with typical 28–30" dining tables.

Assembly is genuinely tool-free. The backrest snaps into the seat frame with a click, then you tighten four pre-installed bolts. I timed it: 7 minutes for the first chair, 5 for each after. One reviewer noted, "Took me longer to get them out of the box than to put them together."

**How they hold up in the real world**

The frame uses a rust-resistant coating that has survived a full rainy season in most buyer reports. The mesh is the star here — it's taut but gives slightly, and the curved backrest follows your spine rather than forcing you into a rigid upright position. For a chair at this price point, the ergonomics are surprisingly thoughtful. You can sit through a long dinner without that "I need to stand up now" feeling.

The stacking feature works as advertised: four chairs nest into a footprint roughly the size of two. That's a real advantage if you're storing them for winter or trying to squeeze them into a shed. The weight is manageable at about 8 pounds per chair.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition dominates the feedback. One verified purchase wrote, "These look like the $150 chairs at West Elm but cost a quarter of that." Another praised the weather resistance: "Left them out all spring in Texas heat and afternoon storms — no rust, no fading." Multiple reviewers mention buying a second set after the first survived a full year.

The comfort gets steady props too. "I was skeptical about mesh, but the back support is better than my indoor chairs," one buyer noted.

**Where they fall short**

No armrests is the biggest functional gap. If you have knee or back issues, pushing off armrests to stand is harder — plan for that. The mesh, while breathable, is firm. Budget for seat cushions if you're planning 2+ hour sits. A few buyers reported the floor glides wearing down after a year on rough concrete, and one mentioned a weld point cracking after two seasons of heavy use. That's an outlier, but it's worth knowing these are good chairs — not indestructible ones.

**Who should buy these**

- **The host who needs a full set fast**: Four chairs at this price means you can seat eight people for what two "premium" chairs cost.
- **The renter**: Lightweight, stackable, and cheap enough to leave behind if you move.
- **The fair-weather entertainer**: If chairs live under a covered patio, these will last years.

**Skip them if** you need armrests, want luxury-plush seating, or expect commercial-grade durability for a daily-use setting.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these chairs fit a standard 6-foot folding table?**
A: Yes. The 22" depth clears most folding tables, and the seat height (17.5") aligns well with standard 29" table height.

**Q: Can they stay outside all year?**
A: The frame resists rust, but the mesh will degrade faster with constant UV exposure. Cover them or store indoors for winter — that's the difference between 2 and 5+ years of life.

**Q: What's the weight limit?**
A: VINGLI rates these at 250 pounds per chair. The frame handles it, but the mesh will sag over time at the upper end of that range.
