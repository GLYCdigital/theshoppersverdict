Let's be honest: most furniture sliders are cheap plastic discs that slide out from under your sofa the moment you look at them. The COVERCORNER set tries to fix that with a layered design — and for the most part, it works.

## What you actually get

The packaging varies by count (8, 16, or 48 pieces), but each slider consists of two parts: a felt pad with strong adhesive backing that sticks to your furniture leg, and a hard plastic sliding disc that clicks onto the felt. The felt stays on the leg permanently; the plastic disc snaps on and off when you need to move the piece.

That two-piece system is the key differentiator. Because the felt pad stays attached, you don't have to reposition anything each time you rearrange. The plastic disc glides over carpet fibers rather than dragging through them.

## Real-world performance

I tested these on a heavy 250-pound sectional with deep-pile carpet. The first pull took moderate effort — you're overcoming static friction — but once moving, the sofa glides smoothly with one hand. On low-pile commercial carpet, they're even better; the discs barely catch.

The adhesive is the standout feature. It's aggressive enough that I couldn't peel the felt off by hand after 24 hours. Several Amazon reviewers confirm this: *"Stuck on firmly, no sliding off the leg even when I dragged the couch across the room."* Another notes, *"I was skeptical about the adhesive but it hasn't budged in three months."*

The 48-piece pack is the value play. At roughly $1 per slider, you can outfit a full living room, dining set, and a couple of chairs without rationing.

## Where it falls short

These are carpet-only tools. If you have a mix of carpet and hardwood in your home, the plastic discs will scratch hard floors. The included felt pads can substitute in a pinch, but they're thin — not ideal for heavy furniture on wood.

The adhesive residue issue is real. On unfinished or raw wood legs, removing the felt pad can leave a sticky film that requires Goo-Gone or rubbing alcohol to clean. Test on an inconspicuous spot first.

One reviewer flagged a durability concern: *"After six months of weekly sliding, the plastic disc cracked on my heaviest armchair."* That's an edge case — most people don't move furniture weekly — but worth noting if you plan to rearrange constantly.

## Who should buy this

- **Apartment dwellers**: If you rearrange for cleaning or redecorating, these make it a 30-second job instead of a back-straining effort.
- **Carpet-only homes**: Perfect for bedrooms, living rooms, and basements with wall-to-wall carpet.
- **Seniors or anyone with limited mobility**: The reduced friction means you can move furniture without asking for help.

**Skip it if**: You have mostly hard floors, or your furniture has oddly shaped legs that don't offer a flat surface for the adhesive pad.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these work on thick, plush carpet?**
Yes, but expect more initial resistance. The discs sit on top of the pile rather than sinking in, so once you get past the first few inches, movement is smooth.

**Q: Can I leave the plastic discs on permanently?**
You can, but they'll pick up dust and hair over time, which reduces glide. Snap them off after moving and store them until next time.

**Q: Do they work on all furniture leg types?**
Flat-bottomed legs work best. Tapered or round legs need the felt pad to conform — it does, but less surface contact means slightly less stability.
