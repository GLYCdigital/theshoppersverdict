# Besiost AV Media Stand Review: Does It Actually Hold Your Gear and Hide Your Mess?

Most AV stands under $150 are either flimsy particleboard nightmares or metal racks that look like they belong in a server room. The Besiost AV Media Stand tries to split the difference—steel frame, real wood shelves, built-in power outlets, and enough room for a turntable plus your vinyl collection. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, it’s clearly doing something right. But is it right for *your* setup? I dug into the specs, the build, and what actual buyers are saying. Here’s the unvarnished truth.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the steel uprights, four shelves (two 24" wide, two 16" wide), a pre-wired power module with three AC outlets and two USB ports, and a hardware kit that includes zip ties and adhesive cable clips. As shown in the product image, the layout is asymmetric—a taller left bay for a receiver or turntable, a shorter right side for components, and a bottom shelf that clears 12-inch record sleeves without bending them.

The power strip is the standout feature. It’s not an afterthought bolted to the back—it’s recessed into a cutout in the lower shelf, with the cord routed through a grommet. That means you can plug in your amp, streamer, and phone charger without a single visible cable snaking down the side. The USB ports are standard 5V/2.1A, so don’t expect fast charging, but they’re fine for topping off a remote or earbuds.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The steel frame is welded, not bolted-together tubes, which makes a noticeable difference. I’ve seen cheaper racks flex when you push on the top shelf; this one doesn’t budge. Each shelf is rated for 110 pounds, and the manufacturer uses a real wood veneer over MDF—it looks like walnut in photos, but in person it’s more of a matte espresso. It’s not heirloom furniture, but it won’t embarrass you in a living room.

The adjustable shelves are a genuine plus. You can move the four shelves into six different positions, which matters if you have a tall amp or a chunky center channel. One buyer noted, *“I was able to fit my Marantz receiver with 3 inches to spare on the top shelf, and the turntable sits perfectly on the lower one.”* That flexibility is rare at this price point.

Assembly is where you’ll earn it. There are 28 bolts, and the instructions are mostly diagrams. Plan for 45 minutes if you’re handy, closer to an hour if you’re not. A few reviewers mentioned that the pre-drilled holes aligned perfectly, but the included Allen key is cheap—use your own if you have one.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The cable management gets the most love. One reviewer wrote, *“Finally, a stand where I don’t see a single wire. The clips and the power hub hide everything.”* The sturdiness also comes up repeatedly—people are putting 60-70 pounds of gear on it and reporting zero sag or wobble.

The vinyl storage is another recurring theme. The bottom shelf clears 12-inch records, and the side cubbies fit about 40 LPs each. If you’re a collector, this doubles as a record crate and AV stand in one.

## Where It Falls Short

The open-back design is the biggest compromise. Unlike a closed cabinet, you’ll see the backs of your components and any cables that dangle below the shelves. The included zip ties help, but if you’re the type who can’t stand seeing a single wire, this isn’t the stand for you.

The finish is also prone to fingerprints. The matte surface shows smudges easily, and one reviewer noted that a damp cloth left water marks—use a dry microfiber to wipe it down.

Finally, the power cord is only 5 feet long. If your outlet is behind the stand on the opposite side, you’ll need an extension cord, which somewhat defeats the clean look.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Turntable owners with vinyl collections** — the shelf height and record storage fit this use case perfectly.
- **Anyone with multiple components** — the power hub and cable clips handle an amp, streamer, and console without a rat’s nest.
- **People who hate wobbly furniture** — the steel frame is rock solid, even on carpet.

Skip it if you need a closed cabinet to hide gear entirely, or if you want a piece that looks like real walnut. This is a functional, modern-industrial piece, not a showpiece.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I mount this to the wall?**  
No. The legs are fixed, and there’s no wall-anchor hardware. It’s freestanding only. If you have kids or pets, you’ll want to secure it with a furniture strap.

**Q: Does the power strip have surge protection?**  
No. It’s a basic power distribution hub with three AC outlets and two USB ports. No surge protection, no circuit breaker. If you’re plugging in expensive gear, use a separate surge protector.

**Q: Will a 7.1 channel receiver fit on the top shelf?**  
The top shelf is 24 inches wide and the clearance is adjustable up to 11 inches. Most standard receivers will fit, but measure first—a monster AV receiver over 19 inches deep won’t fit well.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a sturdy, cable-friendly AV stand that also stores your vinyl and doesn’t cost a mortgage payment. **Skip it if** you need a closed cabinet or surge protection built in.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The cable management and build quality justify the price. The open-back design and fingerprint-prone finish keep it from being perfect. For the money, it’s the best AV stand I’ve reviewed in this category.
