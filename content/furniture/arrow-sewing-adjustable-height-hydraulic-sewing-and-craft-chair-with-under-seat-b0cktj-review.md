**The sewing chair that finally gets the height right**  

If you’ve ever hunched over a sewing machine for three hours, you know the problem: standard office chairs are either too low or too high, and your neck pays the price. The Arrow Sewing Adjustable Height Hydraulic Chair tackles exactly that with a pneumatic lift that spans 20.5 to 27 inches—a range that covers most sewing tables, cutting mats, and even sit-stand desks. At 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, it’s clearly doing something right. But is it the right chair for your craft room? Let’s dig in.

**What’s in the box**  

You get the seat base with five dual-wheel casters, the gas lift cylinder, the seat pan with attached backrest, and an under-seat storage tray. No tools beyond the included (and admittedly flimsy) Allen wrench. The upholstery is a low-pile fabric that feels durable and doesn’t snag on fabric scraps—a thoughtful touch for sewing rooms. The seat measures 18.5 inches wide, which is generous for average builds but not oversized.

**Real-world performance**  

The gas lift is the star here. Pull the lever under the seat and you get smooth, stepless height adjustment—no annoying preset clicks. I tested it with a standard 30-inch sewing table and a 36-inch cutting counter; both felt comfortable without switching chairs. The seat cushion is firm, not plush, which is actually a plus for posture—you won’t sink into it and lose your alignment. The backrest tilts slightly but doesn’t recline, which is fine for forward-focused craft work.

The under-seat tray is more useful than I expected. It slides out with a gentle pull and holds a rotary cutter, seam ripper, and a few spools without interfering with legroom. One buyer noted, “I keep my snips and thread snips in the tray, and it’s saved me from standing up every five minutes.” That’s the use case, and it works.

**Where it falls short**  

The casters are the weak point. They’re hard plastic—fine for carpet or smooth concrete, but on hardwood or laminate they can leave scuff marks. Several reviewers mentioned swapping them for rubber wheel casters (a $10 upgrade). Also, the seat height adjustment lever sits low on the right side; if you’re tall, you might brush it accidentally when reaching for the tray. It’s a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

Assembly is straightforward, but the included wrench will strip if you over-torque. Use your own Allen key set and you’ll be done in 15 minutes.

**Who should buy this**  

This chair is ideal for quilters, garment sewists, and crafters who spend hours at a cutting table or machine. It’s also a solid pick for scrapbookers or jewelry makers who want a compact, height-adjustable seat. Skip it if you need a fully reclining chair for breaks, or if you weigh over 250 lbs—the gas lift is rated to that limit and I wouldn’t push it.

**FAQ**  

*Does the backrest adjust up or down?*  
No, the backrest height is fixed, but it’s positioned well for average torsos (5’4” to 5’10”). Taller users may find it hits mid-back instead of lumbar.

*Can I replace the casters?*  
Yes, they’re standard 1.9-inch stems. Rubber casters from Amazon fit perfectly and protect hard floors.

*Is the seat comfortable for 6+ hour sessions?*  
The firm foam is supportive, but there’s no lumbar curve. Add a small back pillow if you have lower-back issues. Most users report 3-4 hours of comfort without complaints.

**The verdict**  

The Arrow Sewing Adjustable Height Hydraulic Chair earns a solid 4.6/5. It solves the height problem that plagues most craft chairs, the under-seat storage is genuinely handy, and the build quality justifies the price for daily use. The casters and fixed backrest are the only real compromises.

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, height-adjustable chair that keeps you aligned at both machine and cutting table. **Skip it if** you need lumbar support or have a fragile floor that demands premium wheels from day one.
