Let's be blunt: a recliner under $350 with massage and heat usually means you're getting a flimsy cardboard throne that collapses by Christmas. The Consofa Wingback Recliner Chair with Massage and Heat Tufted isn't that — but it's not a La-Z-Boy either. Here's what 215 Amazon buyers (4.3 stars) are actually getting for their money.

**What's in the box**

You get the chair body, the backrest, and a wired remote. That's it. No assembly tools included — you'll need a Phillips head screwdriver. The PU leather is bonded over high-density foam, and the frame is a mix of plywood and metal. At 65 pounds, it's light enough to drag across a room but heavy enough to feel substantial.

**Real-world performance**

The push-back recline is the standout feature. You lean back, and the chair follows — no levers, no handles, no fighting a stiff spring. One buyer put it well: *"I was skeptical about push-back, but it glides smoother than my old lever recliner. My husband falls asleep in it every night."* The recline locks into three positions, and the footrest extends fully for napping.

The heat function is legitimately useful. It targets the lumbar area and gets noticeably warm within two minutes — not just a subtle tingle. The massage, though, is where expectations need adjusting. It's a vibration motor, not a shiatsu roller. It's strong enough to soothe a sore back after a long day, but it's not subtle. Multiple reviews describe the same experience: *"The massage is a bit loud — you'll want the TV volume up."*

**Build quality and materials**

The tufted wingback design looks more expensive than the price tag suggests. The PU leather has a matte finish that doesn't scream "faux," and the stitching is clean throughout. The seat cushion is firm — not plush — which is actually good for lumbar support but takes a week to break in. One recurring complaint: the backrest attachment bolts are tricky to align. Plan for 20-30 minutes with a second person holding the backrest steady.

**Where it falls short**

The massage motor is the biggest compromise. It's more of a deep rumble than a therapeutic knead, and it's audible from across the room. There's also no timer auto-shutoff — you'll need to remember to turn it off manually. The lack of any USB port or cup holder is a miss at this price, especially since competing chairs in this range often include them. And if you're over 6'2" or over 250 pounds, the 42-inch seat depth will feel cramped — this is a standard- sized chair, not a big-and-tall model.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers** who want a functional recliner without the bulk of a full power lift chair
- **Back-pain sufferers** who want targeted heat therapy while watching TV or reading
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want a recliner with massage features without dropping $600+
- **Not for** tall or heavy users, or anyone expecting a silent, spa-quality massage

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the heat function work independently from the massage?**
Yes. The wired remote has separate buttons for heat and vibration. You can run heat-only, massage-only, or both simultaneously.

**Q: How far does it recline?**
The push-back mechanism allows roughly 135 degrees of recline — almost flat but not fully horizontal. The footrest extends about 15 inches from the base.

**Q: Is the PU leather going to peel after a year?**
At this price point, bonded leather is always a risk. However, the coating on this chair is thicker than typical budget PU. Several reviewers report 6+ months of daily use with no cracking or peeling. Just avoid direct sunlight and don't use harsh chemical cleaners.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely comfortable recliner with working heat, don't mind a loud-ish vibration massage, and aren't expecting luxury materials at a budget price.

**Skip it if** you need a silent massage, you're a larger person, or you require cup holders and charging ports in your relaxation setup.

At 4.2/5 stars, this is a solid value for what it delivers — a comfortable, functional recliner that does exactly what it promises. Just know the massage is a bonus, not the main event.
