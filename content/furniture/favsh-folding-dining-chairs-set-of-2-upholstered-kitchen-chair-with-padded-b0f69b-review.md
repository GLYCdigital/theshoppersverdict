If you've ever wrestled with a flimsy folding chair that wobbles, slides, or digs into your back after twenty minutes, you know the category's reputation. The FAVSH Folding Dining Chairs Set of 2 tries to break that mold—and for the most part, it succeeds.

**What's actually in the box**

You get two fully assembled chairs. No tools, no Allen wrench, no "some assembly required" frustration. Each chair measures roughly 21.5 inches wide, 23 inches deep, and 33 inches tall, with a seat height around 18 inches—standard dining height that works with most 28–30 inch tables. The frame is powder-coated steel with cross-bracing underneath, and the upholstery is a faux leather covering over what feels like a legitimate 2-inch foam pad on the seat and a thinner but present pad on the backrest.

**How they hold up in real use**

I've tested these in a cramped apartment kitchen and a suburban dining room. The first thing you notice is the stability. The rubber floor caps grip tile and hardwood without sliding, and the cross-brace design eliminates the lateral wobble that plagues cheaper folding chairs. At 13.5 pounds each, they're solid enough that you don't feel every shift in weight.

The padding is the real differentiator. Most folding chairs in this price range give you a token cushion that compresses to nothing in a week. The FAVSH chairs actually support a 200-pound adult for a full meal without numbness. The backrest is upright—not reclined—so they're better for eating than lounging, but that's what a dining chair should be.

The folding mechanism is smooth. A simple pull on the seat lifts it flat, and they lean against a wall at about 6 inches deep. Two chairs fit side-by-side in a typical hall closet. That's the whole point of these, and they execute it well.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.8-star average across 240 ratings isn't inflated. The recurring themes in customer feedback are comfort and stability. One verified buyer wrote, "I was skeptical about folding chairs, but these feel like real dining chairs. My mother-in-law sat for three hours without complaining about her back." Another noted, "The padding is actually thick. I've paid more for non-folding chairs with less cushion." Multiple reviewers mention using them as permanent dining chairs in small apartments, not just as overflow seating.

**Where they fall short**

The faux leather is the biggest compromise. In a non-climate-controlled room or during summer months, it gets tacky against bare skin. If you're wearing shorts, you'll feel it. The lack of armrests also limits long-term lounging comfort—these are task chairs, not recliners. And at 13.5 pounds each, they're not the lightweight camping-chair alternative some buyers expect. You're trading portability for stability, which is a fair trade but worth knowing upfront.

The seat height is fixed at 18 inches. If you have a bar-height counter or a table over 31 inches, these won't work without a booster.

**Who should buy these**

- **Small apartment dwellers** who need real dining chairs but can't dedicate permanent floor space. These fold flat for storage and come out for meals.
- **Hosts and entertainers** who need four to six extra seats that don't look like an afterthought. The upholstered design blends with permanent furniture better than plastic or metal folding chairs.
- **Renters** who move frequently. They're sturdy enough to survive multiple relocations and don't require disassembly.

Skip them if you need chairs for a formal dining room where aesthetics are paramount—the faux leather and visible folding mechanism give away their hybrid nature. Also skip if you want armrests or a reclined seating position.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these support heavier individuals?**
A: The steel frame is rated for roughly 300 pounds per chair. The cross-bracing distributes weight well, but the 18-inch seat width is snug for larger body types. Test one before committing.

**Q: How do they clean?**
A: Wipe with a damp cloth and mild soap. The faux leather is sealed, so spills bead up rather than soaking in. Avoid abrasive cleaners—they'll dull the finish.

**Q: Do they scratch hardwood floors?**
A: No. The rubber floor caps are effective. I dragged one across oak flooring and it left no marks. The caps are also replaceable if they wear out.
