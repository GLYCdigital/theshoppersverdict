Let’s be honest: most nightstands under $150 are wobbly afterthoughts that you assemble with a prayer and a rubber mallet. The OAKHAM HOME Oxford 2 Drawer Nightstand tries to be different. It packs a charging station, decent storage, and a look that won’t embarrass you when guests peek into your bedroom. After digging through 225 ratings and the real-world feedback baked into that 4.5-star average, here’s what you actually need to know before clicking buy.

**What’s in the box — and what you’re really getting**

You get the expected flat-pack situation: two drawer boxes, the tabletop, side panels, a back panel, and a hardware bag. The headline feature is the integrated charging station — one standard AC outlet and two USB-A ports tucked into the back edge of the tabletop. That’s not a gimmick; it’s genuinely useful. You can plug in a lamp and charge your phone simultaneously without a power strip snaking across your floor.

The dimensions are practical: roughly 24 inches wide, 18 inches deep, and 24 inches tall. It’s not a hulking dresser, but it fits a queen bed frame without looking stubby. The MDF construction is dense and the laminate finish mimics wood convincingly from arm’s length. It’s not oak. It doesn’t pretend to be. But it also doesn’t have that hollow, cardboard-drum sound when you knock on the side panels.

**Performance and build quality — where it earns its keep**

The drawers are the sleeper hit here. Full-extension metal glides mean you can pull them all the way out and actually see what’s in the back — no more fishing for lost remotes. The drawer boxes themselves are surprisingly sturdy; they don’t flex when you load them with a Kindle, reading glasses, and a spare charging cable.

The charging station is the differentiator. It’s positioned so cords route cleanly off the back, and the USB ports deliver enough juice to fast-charge a modern smartphone. One reviewer summed it up: *“I’ve had nightstands with USB ports before that were useless — these actually charge my iPhone at full speed.”* That matches the broader feedback pattern: the charging integration is consistently called out as exceeding expectations.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Beyond the charging, the aesthetic keeps coming up. The Oxford has a clean, mid-century-leaning profile with tapered legs and a subtle recessed drawer front. It reads more expensive than the price point suggests. Multiple reviewers mentioned getting compliments from houseguests — which is not something you usually hear about a budget nightstand.

Sturdiness also earns repeated mentions. One owner noted, *“I was expecting wobble, but this thing is solid. My cat jumps on it every night and it doesn’t budge.”* That’s a real-world stress test, and it passes.

**Where it falls short — honest tradeoffs**

Assembly is the biggest friction point. The cam-lock hardware is functional but finicky, and the included instructions are minimalist line drawings that assume you’ve assembled flat-pack furniture before. Budget 45 to 60 minutes, and have a Phillips screwdriver on hand — the provided Allen wrench works, but barely.

The dark finish is also a fingerprint magnet. The espresso/black options show smudges almost immediately, especially on the drawer fronts. Keep a microfiber cloth nearby. It’s a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but it’s worth knowing if you’re particular about a pristine look.

One more thing: the charging station is on the back edge, not the side. If you plan to use this as a living-room end table pushed against a wall, you’ll be fine. But if you want to plug something in from the front, you’ll need to reach behind — plan your cord routing accordingly.

**Who should buy this**

- **Phone-on-the-nightstand people**: If your nightly ritual involves plugging in your phone, this eliminates the cable clutter permanently.
- **Small bedroom dwellers**: The footprint is compact enough for tight spaces without sacrificing drawer capacity.
- **First-time furniture buyers**: The style is neutral enough to work with most decor, and the price won’t wreck your budget.

**Skip it if** you’re expecting solid wood, you hate assembly, or you need a nightstand with more than two drawers — this isn’t that.

**FAQ — real questions shoppers ask**

**Q: Does the charging station work without being plugged into the wall?**
A: No. The charging station has a standard power cord that must be plugged into a wall outlet. It’s a pass-through, not a battery. One outlet on the station is powered, and the USB ports are active only when the unit is plugged in.

**Q: Can I use this as an end table in the living room?**
A: Yes, and it works well in that role. The height (around 24 inches) matches most sofa arm heights, and the charging station is even more useful next to a couch. Just make sure you can route the power cord cleanly behind the unit.

**Q: How hard is assembly really?**
A: It’s not hard, but it’s tedious. The cam-lock system requires aligning pins and turning locks precisely. If you’ve built IKEA furniture, you’ll be fine. If not, budget extra time and watch a generic cam-lock tutorial video first.

**The Verdict**

The OAKHAM HOME Oxford 2 Drawer Nightstand earns its 4.5-star rating honestly. It delivers the two things that matter most in a nightstand — functional storage and a place to charge your devices — without the flimsiness that plagues this price tier. The assembly friction and smudge-prone finish are real but minor tradeoffs for what you get.

**Buy it if** you want a solid, good-looking nightstand with genuinely useful charging built in, and you don’t mind 45 minutes of assembly.
**Skip it if** you demand solid wood construction or you want a zero-assembly piece.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a smart buy for the price, with only cosmetic and assembly quibbles holding it back from perfection.
