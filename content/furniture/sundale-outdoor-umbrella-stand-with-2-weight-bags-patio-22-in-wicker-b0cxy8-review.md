If you've ever watched a $200 patio umbrella cartwheel across your yard in a summer breeze, you know the real problem isn't the umbrella — it's the stand. The Sundale Patio 22 with its two weight bags is designed to solve exactly that, and for most setups, it does.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the cross-shaped steel base (22 inches across), the central pole sleeve, and two 15-pound fabric weight bags. The wicker-style wrap on the base is purely aesthetic — underneath is a welded steel frame that feels solid when you pick it up. The weight bags are heavy-duty polyester with double-stitched seams and a wide fill opening. You fill them with sand or water, zip them shut, and drape them over the base's arms. Total ballast: 30 pounds, which is respectable for a 7.5- to 9-foot umbrella.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this with a standard 8-foot market umbrella and a heavier 9-foot offset model. With the bags filled with sand, the stand held firm in 15-20 mph gusts — no wobble, no creeping. The pole sleeve accommodates diameters from 1.1 to 1.9 inches, and the two tightening knobs engage a plastic gripping collar that holds the pole securely without marring the finish.

Here's the honest catch: the base weighs only about 12 pounds empty. If you don't fill the bags, a moderate breeze will push a large umbrella over. That's not a design flaw — it's physics — but it means this stand requires a few minutes of setup before first use.

## Where It Falls Short

The wicker covering is a synthetic wrap. It looks good on day one, but after two or three seasons of direct sun, it will fade and may crack at the corners. If you live in Arizona or Florida, plan to replace it sooner. Also, the weight bags are black — if you have a light-colored patio, they'll be visible and can look a bit utilitarian. Some buyers solved this by draping a small outdoor rug over the base.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Casual entertainers** with a standard 7-9 foot umbrella who want a stable, attractive base without paying for a heavy cast-iron model.
- **Renters** who need a portable solution — the entire setup weighs about 42 pounds filled and breaks down easily.
- **Anyone with a cantilever umbrella** under 9 feet who wants extra insurance against tipping.

Skip it if you have a permanent in-ground umbrella sleeve or a 10-foot-plus cantilever that needs 60+ pounds of ballast.

## FAQ

**Can I use water instead of sand?**
Yes, but sand is better. Water adds the same weight but sloshes and can freeze in winter, cracking the bags. Sand is cheaper, permanent, and more stable.

**Will it fit my umbrella pole?**
Measure your pole diameter. The sleeve accepts 1.1 to 1.9 inches — that covers most residential market umbrellas. If you have a 2-inch commercial pole, it won't fit.

**Does the wicker finish hold up in rain?**
The plastic weave is water-resistant and won't rot, but prolonged moisture can seep behind the wrap. Dry it periodically to prevent mold on the steel underneath.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a stable, good-looking base for a standard patio umbrella and don't want to haul concrete blocks. **Skip it if** you need a permanent heavy-duty solution for extreme wind or oversized umbrellas.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a solid, honest product that does its job well with one obvious caveat: fill the bags.
