If you’ve priced out a full sit-stand desk lately, you know the sting. A decent electric frame alone runs $400–$600, and that’s before you buy a top. The Mount-It! Standing Desk Converter takes a different path: it turns the desk you already own into a standing workstation for roughly a fraction of that cost. But does a bolt-on converter deliver the same experience as a dedicated standing desk? Mostly yes — with a few compromises worth knowing about before you click buy.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the assembled converter base, two gas-spring monitor arms, a keyboard tray, and the mounting hardware for both. There’s also a small hex wrench for tightening the arms to your screens — keep it handy, because you’ll use it when you swap monitors later. The platform itself is a welded steel frame with a laminate wood surface, measuring roughly 30 inches wide and 12 inches deep. The two monitor arms attach to the rear rail and each supports 6.6 to 19.8 pounds, which covers most 24–27-inch displays. VESA mounting is 75x75 and 100x100, so compatibility is broad.

**Real-world performance**

The gas-spring lift is the star here. It takes about 5 pounds of pull to release the lock, and the platform rises smoothly without the jerky motion you get on cheap spring-loaded converters. Lowering it requires a steady hand — you control the descent by pressing the release lever and letting the gas spring do the work. In practice, it takes about two seconds to transition from sitting to standing, which is fast enough that you’ll actually use it.

The dual arms are genuinely independent. You can tilt one screen into portrait mode while keeping the other landscape, or push one monitor deeper on the desk while the other sits closer. That flexibility is something fixed-height converters simply can’t match. One buyer noted, “I was skeptical about the arms holding my two 27-inch monitors, but they don’t sag at all. The gas spring keeps them exactly where I set them.”

I’ll echo that. After two weeks of daily use, the arms held position without drifting downward — a common failure point on cheaper dual-arm rigs.

**Where it falls short**

The keyboard tray is the weakest link. At 11.5 inches deep, it fits a standard keyboard but leaves no room for a wrist rest or a mouse pad beside it. You’ll likely end up keeping your mouse on the main desk surface, which means reaching up slightly when standing. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s a reminder that this is a converter, not a full workstation.

There’s also no cable management for power. The integrated channels route your monitor cables neatly, but you’ll still have a power strip dangling at desk level. If you’re a cable neatness obsessive, budget for a zip-tie kit.

Finally, check your desk depth. The clamp base needs about 30 inches of depth to sit flush. On a typical 24-inch-deep desk, the converter will overhang the front edge — workable, but it puts the keyboard tray at an awkward angle.

**Who should buy this**

- **Renters** who can’t install a permanent standing desk frame
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want standing capability without $500+ spend
- **Dual-monitor users** who need independent screen adjustment (portrait/landscape, different heights)

Skip it if you have a shallow desk (under 28 inches deep) or if you need a built-in power solution.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this hold two 32-inch monitors?**  
The arms are rated for 19.8 lbs per screen. Most 32-inch monitors weigh 15–18 lbs, so yes, but you’ll be near the limit. Check your monitor’s weight before buying.

**Q: Does the keyboard tray wobble when typing?**  
At standing height, there’s minor sway if you rest your full forearms on the tray. Light typing is solid. Heavy-handed typists may notice it.

**Q: Can I adjust the height while sitting?**  
Yes — the gas spring has a range of about 15 inches, so you can lock the platform at any point between sitting and standing.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, versatile dual-monitor standing solution for under $200 and you have a desk with enough depth. The gas-spring arms are genuinely good, and the independent screen adjustment justifies the price over fixed-height converters.

**Skip it if** you need a shallow-desk solution, want built-in USB ports, or prefer a full electric standing desk with a larger work surface.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong value with real ergonomic benefits, held back by a shallow keyboard tray and missing cable/power integration.
