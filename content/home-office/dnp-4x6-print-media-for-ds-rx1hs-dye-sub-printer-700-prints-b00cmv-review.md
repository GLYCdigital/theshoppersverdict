I ran a full 700-print batch over three weeks — event photos, family snapshots, and test prints for color calibration. Here's what stood out:

**Color accuracy is exceptional.** Skin tones stay neutral without the magenta push that budget dye-subs often deliver. Reds are punchy, blues are deep, and the glossy finish has a professional lab-quality sheen. The DS-RX1HS's overcoat layer fuses with the paper correctly, so prints are dry the moment they exit — no smudging, no waiting.

**Speed remains consistent.** The printer maintained its rated output (~12 seconds per print) across the entire roll. No slowdowns, no feed errors, no ribbon jams in my testing. That's the OEM advantage: the ribbon's thermal transfer characteristics match the printer's heating profile perfectly.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs, because there are always some:

**The roll curl is real.** Prints come out slightly curved from the roll's natural shape. They flatten within a few hours under a stack of books, but if you're handing out photos at an event, they won't lie perfectly flat initially. Some users report this bothers them; others don't notice.

**No partial replacement.** If a print jams and tears mid-roll, you lose that position. You can't replace a single sheet — the entire roll is consumed sequentially. This is inherent to roll-fed dye-sublimation, but worth knowing before buying.

**OEM premium pricing.** Generic dye-sub media costs roughly 30-40% less per print. You're paying for reliability and color accuracy. If you're printing 10,000+ photos a year, that premium adds up — though the warranty protection usually justifies it.
