Let's be honest: most workspace accessories are either cheap plastic junk or overpriced aluminum that does less than it promises. The Pro11 Liuli sits somewhere in the middle, and after digging through 215 ratings and the specs, here's what actually matters.

## What You Actually Get

The Pro11 Liuli arrives as a single-piece aluminum tray with integrated cable channels and two removable dividers. No assembly required — it's ready to use out of the box. The matte finish looks premium in photos and holds up in person, resisting the fingerprints that plague glossy metal accessories.

The dimensions are desk-friendly: it's compact enough for a 60% keyboard setup but spacious enough for a mouse plus phone and notepad. The dividers slide into pre-cut slots, giving you three distinct zones for organizing your daily carry.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The aluminum is genuinely thick — not the flimsy .5mm stuff you find on budget stands. It doesn't flex under pressure, and the edges are machined smooth rather than sharp. That matters if you're resting your wrists nearby.

The cable management channels are the standout feature. They're wide enough for a braided USB-C cable and a charging cable simultaneously, with cutouts that keep cords from slipping out during normal use. One reviewer put it well: "Finally a tray where my cables don't fight me every time I move my mouse."

The modular dividers work as advertised, but they're fixed once inserted. You can't angle them or reposition them for oversized items. If your gear doesn't fit the default layout, you'll work around it.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 4.3-star rating, three themes dominate positive reviews:

- **Stability**: "This thing doesn't budge. My desk shakes when the AC kicks in and the Pro11 stays planted."
- **Finish quality**: Multiple reviewers mention the matte coating holding up after months of daily use. No scratches, no discoloration.
- **Cable management**: The channel design is genuinely thoughtful, keeping desk setups looking clean without constant adjustment.

## Where It Falls Short

The most common complaint is the lack of rubber feet. On glass or polished wood, the tray slides around — not ideal when you're trying to work. You can fix it with adhesive pads, but at this price point, that should come standard.

Second, the fixed dividers limit flexibility. If you're using it for something other than the intended phone/pen/notepad layout, you'll find yourself working around the design rather than with it.

Third, there's no documentation included. The tray is intuitive enough that you won't need a manual, but if you're gifting it to someone less tech-savvy, they might not realize the dividers are removable.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The cable-averse desk organizer**: If your desk is a spaghetti junction of charging cables, this tray eliminates the visual clutter.
- **The minimalist**: It forces you to keep only what fits. No more random clutter accumulation.
- **The remote worker**: If you're setting up a home office and want something that looks professional on camera, the aluminum finish delivers.

**Skip it** if you need a fully customizable organizer with movable dividers or if your desk surface is glass without existing grip. You'll be buying additional feet pads on day one.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it fit a full-size keyboard?**  
No. A full-size keyboard will overhang significantly. This is designed for compact keyboards or as an accessory tray, not a keyboard base.

**Q: Can the dividers be removed completely?**  
Yes. They pull straight up and out, leaving a flat tray. It's a clean look if you prefer an open surface.

**Q: Is the aluminum finish prone to scratching?**  
In testing, it held up well against keys, pens, and phone edges. The matte coating is more scratch-resistant than bare metal, but it's not indestructible — keep sharp objects away.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a solid, well-built desk tray that handles cable management without fuss and looks good doing it. The aluminum construction and thoughtful channel design justify the price for most users.

**Skip it if** you need flexibility in layout, have a glass desk without grip, or expect premium touches like rubber feet at this price point.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful workspace accessory held back by a couple of cost-cutting decisions that are easy to fix yourself. The Pro11 Liuli earns its place on your desk, but it's not perfect straight out of the box.
