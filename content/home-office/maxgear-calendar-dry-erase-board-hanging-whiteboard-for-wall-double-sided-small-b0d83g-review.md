The wall calendar is dead. Long live the wall calendar. The MaxGear Calendar Dry Erase Board is the modern reincarnation — a double-sided hanging whiteboard that solves the one problem every family command center or home office hits: not enough space. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right planning tool for *your* wall? Let's dig past the star rating.

**What you actually get**

Unboxing reveals a 17 x 23-inch aluminum-framed board with a dry erase surface on both sides. One side features a pre-printed monthly calendar grid; the flip side is completely blank. The package includes mounting hardware (screws and anchors), a plastic marker tray, and — depending on the variant you select — either a couple of markers and an eraser or just the bare essentials. Double-check the listing's included items before ordering; the "with accessories" bundle costs a few dollars more and is worth it if you don't already own dry erase supplies.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The aluminum frame is the standout feature here. It's rigid, lightweight, and gives the board a clean, professional look that doesn't scream "dorm room." The surface handles standard dry erase markers well — buyers consistently report clean erasing with minimal ghosting, even after weeks of daily use. One verified purchaser noted, *"I've had cheaper boards that stained within a month. This one still looks brand new after three months of heavy kid-and-work scheduling."* That tracks with the general feedback pattern.

The magnetic backing is a subtle but appreciated touch. You can pin important papers, receipts, or to-do lists directly to the board, which expands its utility beyond just a writing surface. The included mounting hardware is functional, though the plastic marker tray feels like the weakest link in the build. A few reviewers mentioned theirs arrived slightly warped or needed gentle bending back into shape. It works, but it's the one component that betrays the budget price point.

**Where it falls short**

The 17 x 23-inch footprint is genuinely small. If you're planning a full month with multiple family members' schedules — appointments, school events, work deadlines — the calendar grid boxes get tight fast. You'll be writing in abbreviations and micro-script by mid-month. This is a board for *essential* scheduling, not comprehensive life management. If you need serious calendar real estate, look at the 24 x 36-inch version or a dedicated wall calendar system.

The double-sided design is clever but has a practical quirk: you can only use one side at a time when wall-mounted. Flipping requires unmounting, which means the second side is effectively a backup surface or a board you rotate seasonally. It's a nice-to-have, not a game-changer.

**Who should buy this**

This board is ideal for three specific scenarios:

- **Small home offices** where wall space is precious and you need a monthly overview plus a scratch surface
- **Family command centers** in kitchens or mudrooms — the magnetic surface and compact size fit tight wall gaps between cabinets
- **Dorm rooms or apartments** where you can't drill into walls aggressively; the lightweight design hangs securely with basic anchors

Skip it if you're a detailed planner who needs to see two weeks at a glance with room to write actual sentences. This board rewards brevity.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Does the magnetic backing hold heavy items?** Light papers and photos, yes. Don't expect it to hold a calculator or scissors. Think fridge-magnet strength, not tool-wall strength.

**Can I use any dry erase marker, or does it require specific ones?** Standard dry erase markers work fine. Avoid permanent markers, and use a proper dry erase eraser or soft cloth — paper towels can leave lint residue over time.

**How does it mount?** Two keyhole brackets on the back. Screws and wall anchors are included. It's a five-minute install with a drill or screwdriver.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need a compact, durable, good-looking calendar board for essential scheduling in a small space, and you value the magnetic surface and clean-erasing aluminum frame.

**Skip it if:** You need serious planning real estate, prefer a giant single-surface board, or want a premium marker tray included.

The MaxGear Calendar Dry Erase Board earns its 4.6 stars with honest, functional design. It's not the biggest or the fanciest, but for the price, it's a genuinely well-built tool that does exactly what it promises — and it looks good doing it. **4.6/5**
