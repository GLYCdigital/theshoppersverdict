If you're still paying $12–15 a month to rent a modem from your cable company, this Motorola combo is worth a hard look. But before you click buy, understand exactly what you're getting — because this isn't the modem that'll max out your gigabit fiber-to-cable upgrade.

**What's in the box**

The Motorola cable modem/router combo ships with the unit itself (a tall, vented tower about 9 inches high), a power adapter, and an Ethernet cable. No coaxial cable included — you'll need to reuse the one from your ISP or grab one for a few bucks. Setup is genuinely plug-and-play: screw in the coax, plug in power, connect to your computer, and activate through your provider's app or website. Most buyers report full activation in under 15 minutes.

**Performance and real-world use**

This is a DOCSIS 3.0 modem with a built-in dual-band AC1900 WiFi router. Translation: it handles cable plans up to roughly 600 Mbps. If your internet package is 300 Mbps or below, you'll never notice a difference. If you're paying for gigabit, this unit will bottleneck you — and that's the biggest catch.

The 4 Gigabit LAN ports are a genuine plus for office setups. Plug in a desktop, a printer, a NAS drive, and still have one spare. The vertical design also helps with heat dissipation — a common failure point on flat combo units.

WiFi performance is where opinions split. In a 1,200 sq ft apartment, coverage is solid and speeds hold up. But multiple reviewers with larger homes report the 5GHz band struggles past one wall. One buyer noted, "Works fine in the living room but I lose signal in my home office two rooms over." If you need whole-home coverage beyond 1,800 sq ft, plan on adding a separate access point — which somewhat defeats the purpose of an all-in-one.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The cost savings get mentioned in nearly every positive review. One verified purchaser wrote, "My ISP wanted $14 a month for rental. This paid for itself in 7 months and I own it forever." Another added, "Reliable for streaming and video calls — zero drops in 6 months."

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the range limitations, there are two recurring complaints. First, the 3.9-star rating isn't an accident — roughly 20% of reviewers hit setup headaches with specific ISPs, particularly Spectrum. Second, the lack of DOCSIS 3.1 means you'll replace this when you eventually upgrade your internet plan. It's not a buy-once-for-a-decade device.

**Who should buy this**

- **The cord-cutter** on a 200–400 Mbps plan who wants to kill rental fees permanently
- **Home office users** who need reliable wired connections for video calls and file transfers
- **Budget-conscious households** that don't need mesh WiFi or gigabit speeds

**Skip it if** you have a gigabit plan, a home over 2,000 sq ft, or you're not comfortable troubleshooting a finicky activation call with your ISP.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work with my Xfinity or Spectrum plan?**
A: Yes for both, plus Cox and most cable providers. It's not compatible with fiber services like AT&T or Google Fiber.

**Q: Do I still need a separate router?**
A: No — the WiFi router is built in. But if you have a large home, adding a mesh node is recommended.

**Q: What happens if my internet plan exceeds 600 Mbps?**
A: You'll be capped at roughly 600 Mbps. This modem isn't rated for gigabit speeds.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're on a sub-600 Mbps cable plan, want to eliminate rental fees, and have a modest-sized home. **Skip it if** you're on gigabit, need whole-home coverage, or want future-proofing.

This is a solid, honest workhorse for the right buyer. It saves money, does its job, and doesn't pretend to be something it's not. Just know its limits before you buy.
