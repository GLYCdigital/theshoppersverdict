The box contains two cartridges: one PG-275XL black and one CL-276XL tri-color (cyan, magenta, yellow). Both are remanufactured, not new — they're rebuilt from original Canon cartridges with fresh printheads and aftermarket ink. The chip is new and designed to communicate with Canon's firmware.

## Where It Falls Short

The most consistent complaint: the black ink runs slightly warm. On plain documents you won't notice, but if you print grayscale photos or graphics with fine black lines, you'll see a subtle brownish cast next to an OEM print. One reviewer who prints architectural drawings said, *"The blacks are 90% there, but not 100%. If your work demands true neutral blacks, stick with Canon."*

The cartridge shells also feel lighter than OEM — less rigid plastic, slightly thinner walls. One buyer reported a seam on the color cartridge that appeared loose after installation, though it didn't leak or affect performance. It's cosmetic, but it speaks to the lower build tolerances.

Finally, there's the remanufactured caveat: you're buying a rebuilt product. The printhead is reused, so lifespan varies. Most buyers report 300–400 pages without issue, but a small minority (roughly 5% based on review patterns) saw printhead failure before the ink ran out. That's the risk you accept for the price difference.

## Who Should Buy This

**The right buyer:** Home office users printing documents, school assignments, and occasional color photos. If you go through two or more OEM sets per year, this will save you $80–$100 annually with minimal compromise.
