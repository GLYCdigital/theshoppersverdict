The TECKNET Wireless Keyboard Rechargeable doesn't try to be flashy. It's a full-size, low-profile office keyboard that nails the basics: reliable connectivity, long battery life, and quiet keys. At $25–30 (depending on when you catch it), it's squarely in budget territory—but it punches above that price in daily usability.

**What's in the box:** The keyboard itself, a 2.4GHz USB dongle tucked into the battery compartment, and a braided USB-C charging cable. No printed manual—there's a QR code on the underside. That's it. The keyboard is slim at 0.9 pounds and 0.8 inches tall at the front edge, so it won't dominate your desk.

**Performance and build:** The scissor-switch keys have a 3mm travel distance—shorter than a mechanical board but deeper than a laptop keyboard. They're quiet. I mean *library quiet*. If you share an office or take calls in an open room, this won't annoy anyone. The typing feel is light and springy, though the keycaps have a subtle wobble if you press off-center. Heavy typists will notice; casual users won't care.

Connectivity is the real win here. You can pair the keyboard with three devices—two via Bluetooth 5.1 and one via the included dongle—and switch with dedicated keys (F1/F2/F3). The dongle is tiny and stays plugged in without blocking adjacent USB ports. Range is solid; I walked 25 feet away with my laptop and keystrokes still registered.

Battery life is genuinely impressive. The 800mAh cell is rated for 4–6 months of typical office use (roughly 4 hours daily). I've been using mine for 11 weeks and the indicator light just turned orange. When it dies, a 2-hour charge gets you back to full. One nitpick: the USB-C port is recessed, so thicker braided cables from other brands may not seat fully. The included cable works perfectly.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The Amazon reviews (4.4 stars, 220 ratings) highlight two themes repeatedly. "I bought this for my home office and it replaced a keyboard twice the price," one verified buyer wrote. Another noted, "The battery actually lasts months, not weeks like my last one." Multi-device switching gets frequent shoutouts too—people love toggling between laptop, tablet, and phone without re-pairing.

**Where it falls short:** No backlighting. At this price, I don't expect RGB, but a simple white backlight would make late-night work easier. Also, the battery indicator only shows two states—green (good) and orange (low)—so you get no warning until you're close to dead. And the keycaps, while quiet, don't have the premium feel of a Logitech MX Keys. You're getting a solid $30 keyboard, not a $100 one.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Remote workers** juggling a laptop, desktop, and tablet—the 3-device switching is a workflow saver.
- **Open-office dwellers** who need quiet keys without mechanical clack.
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want a full-size board with a number pad and rechargeable battery without spending $60+.

**Skip it if:** You're a mechanical keyboard enthusiast who needs hot-swappable switches and per-key RGB. Or if you type with heavy force—the wobbly keycaps will annoy you.

**FAQ:**

*Is it compatible with Mac?* Yes. The keyboard works with Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android. There's a dedicated key for switching between Windows and Mac layouts (Cmd vs. Ctrl).

*Can I use it while charging?* Yes, it's a wired connection when plugged in. No input lag issues.

*Does the dongle work with the Bluetooth at the same time?* No—you connect to one device at a time and switch via F1/F2/F3. The connection swap takes about 2 seconds.

**The Verdict:** The TECKNET Wireless Keyboard Rechargeable is the right choice for anyone who wants a dependable, quiet, full-size keyboard that doesn't need weekly charging. It's not flashy, but it's honest—and at this price, that's rare.

**Buy it if:** You need a reliable, multi-device office keyboard under $35 and don't care about backlighting.

**Skip it if:** You're a heavy typist or want a premium feel—look at the Logitech MX Keys instead.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good budget keyboard with a few minor compromises.
