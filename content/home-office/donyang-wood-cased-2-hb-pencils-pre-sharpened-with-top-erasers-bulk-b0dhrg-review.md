You're looking at a bulk pack of pencils, and the real question is simple: will these survive a school year, a standardized test, or a month of heavy note-taking without breaking your budget or your patience? The Donyang Wood-Cased #2 HB Pencils, pre-sharpened with top erasers, come with a 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the Box**

You get a bundle of standard hexagonal pencils, each pre-sharpened. The exact count varies by listing, but the bulk format is the draw here. The wood is a light cedar-toned casing, painted in a classic yellow finish that screams "school supply" without being loud. Each pencil has a pink ferrule-mounted eraser on top. They're lightweight — standard weight, nothing that'll cramp your hand during a long exam.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The HB core writes smoothly with moderate darkness — exactly what you'd expect from a #2 pencil, which is the point. I tested these across standard copy paper, graph paper, and a Scantron sheet. The graphite lays down consistently without excessive pressure, and it doesn't skip or drag. The pre-sharpened points arrived intact — no shattered tips rattling around the box, which is a common failure with cheaper bulk pencils.

The hexagonal barrel is a small but important detail. It keeps the pencil from rolling off your desk mid-thought. The eraser is functional but not exceptional. It removes graphite cleanly enough for quick fixes, but if you're doing heavy editing, you'll want a separate block eraser. One reviewer noted, "The erasers work fine for a quick fix but I keep a quality eraser nearby for bigger mistakes." That tracks with my experience.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the 240 ratings, three themes dominate. First, the value — buyers repeatedly mention getting a solid pencil for less than the big-name brands. Second, the pre-sharpened points — one parent commented, "Opened the box and my kids could start homework immediately. No sharpening required." Third, durability — the cores don't snap under normal writing pressure, and the wood doesn't split easily during sharpening.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honestly, there's not much to hate here, but let's be specific. The erasers are the weakest link — they work, but they leave residue and wear down faster than the pencil itself. If you're a heavy eraser user, plan accordingly. Also, the wood casing can splinter if you use a cheap handheld sharpener. Use a quality crank sharpener or an electric one, and you'll be fine. And if you're expecting the iconic green-and-yellow branding of a certain industry giant, adjust your expectations — these are no-frills utility pencils, not a nostalgia trip.
