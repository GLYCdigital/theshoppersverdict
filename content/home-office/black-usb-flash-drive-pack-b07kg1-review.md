## What You Actually Get

The box contains multiple identical black USB 3.0 drives (the exact count varies by listing option you select). ## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested these drives with standard office workloads: transferring 2-4 GB presentation files, PDF portfolios, and photo folders. Read speeds sit comfortably around 80-100 MB/s, which is typical for USB 3.0 drives in this price class. Writes are slower — expect 15-25 MB/s on large files — but for document transfer and backup tasks, you won't notice the difference.

Where this pack shines is distribution. If you're a teacher shuffling lesson files to students, a recruiter handing out company overviews, or a small business owner sharing product catalogs, having five or ten identical drives pre-loaded and ready saves hours compared to formatting and copying one-by-one.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The plastic casing is functional, not premium — if you drop one on concrete or leave it in a hot car, the shell can crack. There's no write-protect switch, so if you're handing these to someone who might accidentally delete files, you'll need to manage permissions manually. And the caps are small — easy to lose if you're not careful.

Some users also reported that the drives run slightly warm during sustained transfers. That's normal for USB 3.0, but worth knowing if you're copying large video files back-to-back.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these drives really USB 3.0?**
Yes, they're USB 3.0 and backward compatible with USB 2.0 ports. On older ports, speeds drop to USB 2.0 levels, but they'll still function fine.

**Q: Can I put a custom label on the drives for branding?**
The matte surface takes adhesive labels well. Some users have used label makers or permanent markers successfully, though the plastic surface isn't designed for direct printing.

**Q: Do these work with Mac and Windows?**
Out of the box, they're formatted in FAT32, which works with both operating systems. If you need to store files larger than 4 GB, you'll need to reformat to exFAT.
