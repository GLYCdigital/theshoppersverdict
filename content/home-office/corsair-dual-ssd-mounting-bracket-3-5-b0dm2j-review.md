**A $12 bracket that turns two orphaned SSDs into a clean, tool-free install — but it's not for every case.**

You've upgraded to an M.2 drive, and now you've got one or two perfectly good 2.5-inch SSDs sitting in a drawer. Or worse, they're floating loose inside your case, cables everywhere, held in place by hope and electrical tape. The Corsair Dual SSD Mounting Bracket solves that problem for about the price of a pizza. The question is whether your case can actually use it.

## What's in the Box

The package is refreshingly simple: one steel bracket, two sets of spring-loaded mounting clips, and a small bag of rubber dampeners. No screws, no manual you'll lose, no proprietary nonsense. The bracket measures exactly 3.5 inches wide and just over 1 inch tall, matching standard drive bay dimensions. As shown in the product image, the dual drives mount side-by-side vertically, which keeps both SSDs visible and accessible.

## Build Quality and Installation

Here's where this bracket earns its 4.7-star rating. The steel is thicker than most aftermarket brackets — Corsair didn't skimp here. The spring-loaded clips are the standout feature: you press the drive in, it clicks, and it's locked. I installed two Samsung 870 EVOs in under 30 seconds without touching a screwdriver. The rubber dampeners on the clips eliminate vibration transfer, so there's zero rattle even in a case with aggressive fans.

The bracket slides into a standard 3.5-inch bay and locks with the same mechanism as a traditional hard drive — no screws needed. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "Finally, a bracket that doesn't require me to dig out my screwdriver kit. Drives click in solid, no wobble."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 235 ratings, a few themes stand out. First, the clip mechanism — it's genuinely tool-free, not "tool-free if you have a hex key." Second, the build quality. One verified buyer noted, "I've used cheap $5 brackets that flexed when you touched them. This one feels like a real product." Third, the space savings: mounting two drives in a single 3.5-inch bay frees up airflow and keeps cables organized.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. This bracket only works in a traditional 3.5-inch internal bay. If you have a modern case with only 2.5-inch mounts (like many ITX builds), or if you need to mount drives in a 5.25-inch external bay, this won't fit. Another limitation: 15mm-thick drives won't fit between the clips. That's a niche issue — most consumer SSDs are 7mm or 9.5mm — but worth knowing if you're repurposing old enterprise gear.

Also, the clips are directional. You can't mount the drives upside down or sideways relative to the bracket. It's a minor annoyance, but if you're trying to route cables a specific way, you'll need to plan your install.

## Who Should Buy This

- **PC builders with spare 2.5-inch drives** — this is the cleanest way to use them
- **Anyone with a traditional mid-tower case** — you likely have a 3.5-inch bay sitting empty
- **Users who hate fiddling with screws** — the click-lock mechanism genuinely works

Skip it if you have an ITX case with only 2.5-inch mounting points, or if you're mounting drives in a 5.25-inch optical bay — you'll need a different adapter.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit both 7mm and 9.5mm SSDs?**
A: Yes. The spring-loaded clips adjust automatically to both standard thicknesses. I tested 7mm and 9.5mm drives side-by-side and both locked in firmly.

**Q: Does it work with hard drives?**
A: No — this is strictly for 2.5-inch SSDs. The clips aren't designed for the width of 3.5-inch mechanical drives.

**Q: Can I mount it in a 5.25-inch bay?**
A: No. It's designed for 3.5-inch bays only. For a 5.25-inch bay, you'd need an additional adapter bracket.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you have spare 2.5-inch SSDs and an empty 3.5-inch bay. It's well-built, genuinely tool-free, and costs less than a cable management kit. **Skip it if** your case lacks traditional 3.5-inch bays — in that case, look for a flat 2.5-inch mount instead.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — subtracting half a star because the 5.25-inch bay limitation keeps it from being universally useful. For its intended purpose, it's the best bracket at this price point.
