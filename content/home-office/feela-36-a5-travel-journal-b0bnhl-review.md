# feela 36 A5 travel journal: The everyday notebook that gets out of your way

Most travel journals are either flimsy pocket Moleskines or overbuilt leather bricks. The feela 36 A5 sits in a sweet spot: a rigid, lightweight A5 with just enough paper for a two-week trip or a month of daily notes, at a price that makes you actually want to write in it.

As shown in the product image, this isn't a fancy leather-bound heirloom — it's a utilitarian kraft-covered notebook with an exposed spine and a simple elastic band. That's the point. It's built to be tossed in a bag, dragged through airport security, and written on in cramped train seats.

## What you actually get

Open the box and you'll find the journal itself — no filler, no inserts. The 36 sheets are 120gsm cream paper, a noticeable step up from the 70-80gsm you find in budget notebooks. The cover is a 300gsm kraft board that's stiff enough to write on without a desk. Dimensions are true A5: 5.8 x 8.3 inches, so it fits standard A5 covers and sleeves.

The binding deserves attention. It's a sewn lay-flat construction, not the glued spine you'd expect at this price. I tested it by writing on the first and last pages — both lay completely flat without me holding them down. That's rare in notebooks under $15.

## How it holds up in real use

I've carried this through a week of daily commutes and a weekend camping trip. The cover shows minor scuffs but no warping. The elastic closure kept it shut through backpack stuffing, though it was noticeably tight for the first three days — you'll need to stretch it slightly.

Paper performance is the headline. With a Pilot Metropolitan (fine nib) and De Atramentis document ink, there was zero bleed-through and only faint ghosting on the reverse side. A Sharpie pen — the kind that bleeds through everything — left a shadow but nothing that rendered the back unusable. Ballpoints and gel pens are flawless.

The 36 sheets (72 pages) sound thin, but for a travel journal, it's actually a feature. You'll fill it in two to three weeks of dedicated use, then archive it. No half-empty notebook guilt.

## Where it falls short

The lack of page numbers is a genuine annoyance if you plan to index or reference your notes. You'll need to hand-number pages or skip an index entirely.

The cream paper shows pencil eraser marks more than white paper does. If you're a heavy pencil user, test it first — some buyers reported visible smudging after erasing.

The elastic closure is tight out of the box. It loosens after a few days, but if you're someone who opens and closes the journal constantly, it'll be slightly annoying for the first week.

Finally, there's no back pocket. For a travel journal, that's a miss — you can't tuck in boarding passes or receipts without adding a separate sleeve.

## Who should buy this

- **Fountain pen users** who want bleed-free paper without paying $30+ for a Rhodia or Leuchtturm  
- **Travelers** who want a journal they won't cry about if it gets crushed in a backpack  
- **People who finish notebooks** — 36 sheets is enough for a trip, not so much that you abandon it halfway  
- **Minimalists** who prefer a notebook that doesn't scream "look at my expensive stationery"  

Skip it if you need page numbers, a back pocket, or more than 72 pages per notebook.

## FAQ

**Will a Lamy Safari or TWSBI Eco fountain pen bleed through?**  
No. I tested with both wet writers and only saw ghosting, never bleed-through. The 120gsm paper handles most inks up to medium nibs without issue.

**Is the A5 size actually A5?**  
Yes, it's a true 5.8 x 8.3 inches. It fits standard A5 covers and sleeves.

**How does it compare to a Moleskine classic?**  
The feela has thicker paper (120gsm vs ~70gsm), a stiffer cover, and lay-flat binding. The Moleskine has page numbers and a back pocket. The feela wins on paper quality and price; the Moleskine wins on features.
