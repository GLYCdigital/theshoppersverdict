Let's be direct: HP's OEM 962XL cartridges are expensive — often $50+ for a two-pack. The Jetingo 962XL Combo Pack promises the same page yield at half the price. Does it deliver? After digging through 210 Amazon ratings and testing patterns, here's the honest breakdown.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The combo pack includes one black (962XL) and one tri-color cartridge — cyan, magenta, yellow in a single unit, matching HP's standard configuration. These are remanufactured cartridges, not new OEM stock. Jetingo uses recycled HP shells with third-party ink and fresh chips. You get two cartridges total, no printhead cleaning tools or gloves included. For the price point, that's acceptable.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The headline claim is page yield matching OEM specs: roughly 2,000 pages for black, 1,600 for color. That's ambitious. In practical terms, expect slightly less — most remanufactured cartridges fall 10-15% short of OEM claims due to ink formulation differences. If you're a high-volume home office user printing 500 pages monthly, you'll get a solid two months from the black cartridge.

Print quality for text documents is genuinely good. Black text is crisp, dense, and dry — no smudging on standard copy paper. The ink handles well on both matte and glossy photo papers, though color reproduction leans warm. Reds appear slightly orange, and neutral grays carry a faint magenta tint. For everyday documents, charts, and school assignments, this is a non-issue. For professional photography or client-facing color-critical work, you'll notice the difference.

Installation is straightforward. The chips on these cartridges generally bypass HP's authentication checks, so you won't hit the dreaded "non-genuine cartridge" error that plagues many third-party options. That said, HP regularly pushes firmware updates that can block third-party cartridges. If you're on a recent firmware version, check recent reviews before purchasing. One buyer noted: "Worked perfectly on my HP OfficeJet Pro 9015e — no error messages, prints look great." Another warned: "After an HP update, my printer refused to recognize them. Had to buy OEM."

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The value proposition is the clearest win. At roughly half the cost of OEM, the savings add up fast for regular printers. Multiple reviewers highlight the convenience of having both cartridges in one order — no separate shipping, no waiting. One detailed reviewer wrote: "Printed 1,800 pages of black text before the low ink warning. Colors held up well for my kid's school projects." Reliability appears solid for most users, with the 4.2 average rating suggesting more satisfied customers than frustrated ones.

**Where It Falls Short**

The ink level reporting is the most common complaint. Several buyers report the printer showing full ink that suddenly drops to empty with no gradual warning. This makes planning replacements harder — you can't trust the printer's meter. The color warmth we mentioned also appears in customer feedback: "Colors are a bit off compared to HP's own ink — noticeable on photos, fine for documents."

There's also the firmware risk. HP doesn't want third-party cartridges in its printers, and it actively works to block them. If you're not comfortable potentially losing cartridge functionality after a firmware update, this is a real consideration.
