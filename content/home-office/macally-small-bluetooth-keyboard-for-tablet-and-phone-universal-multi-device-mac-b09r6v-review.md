Most compact keyboards make you choose between portability and usability. The Macally Small Bluetooth Keyboard tries to sidestep that tradeoff by shrinking the chassis while keeping the keys full-size. For the most part, it works — with a few compromises you should know about before buying.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the keyboard itself, a Micro-USB charging cable, and a thin quick-start guide. That's it. No carrying case, no USB dongle — this is strictly Bluetooth (5.1, for the spec-minded). At roughly 10.9 x 4.7 x 0.6 inches and 7.2 ounces, it slides into a bag sleeve without adding noticeable bulk. The rounded corners and matte black finish look more expensive than the price suggests, though the plastic shell does pick up fingerprints quickly.

## Real-World Performance

Pairing is straightforward: flip the power switch, hold the Bluetooth button for three seconds, and it shows up as "Macally" on your device. The standout feature here is three-device pairing with dedicated keys (Bluetooth 1, 2, 3 on the top row). Tap once to switch from your iPad to your iPhone to your Windows laptop — it takes about two seconds and doesn't drop the connection on the other devices. This is the reason to buy this over a cheaper single-device keyboard.

The typing feel is genuinely good for a sub-$40 keyboard. Scissor-switch mechanisms with 1.5mm of travel give you tactile feedback without the mushy dead zone you get on many foldable keyboards. The key pitch — the distance between key centers — is 18mm, which matches full-size desktop keyboards. I typed a 2,000-word document on it without the cramped-hand fatigue I expected from a keyboard this small. The layout does require a learning curve, though: the right Shift key is narrower than standard, and the arrow keys are compressed into an inverted-T that takes some getting used to.

Battery life is solid — Macally claims 90 hours per charge, and real-world usage backs that up. I charged it once every three weeks with daily use of a few hours. The auto-sleep after 10 minutes of inactivity is aggressive but predictable, and waking it requires a key press that occasionally registers as a stray character.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects genuine enthusiasm for the multi-device switching. One reviewer noted, "I switch between my iPad and Android phone constantly, and this keyboard handles it flawlessly — no re-pairing, no frustration." Another praised the portability: "Fits in my purse's side pocket and I barely notice it's there. The keys are surprisingly comfortable for a keyboard this size." The battery life also gets consistent shout-outs, with multiple reviewers reporting several weeks between charges.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of backlighting is the biggest practical limitation. If you work in dim environments — a dark office, a hotel room, an evening flight — you'll be hunting for keys. This is a deliberate cost-saving choice, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

The Micro-USB port is another dated decision. In 2026, most of your devices charge via USB-C, so you'll need to keep a legacy cable around. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's an unnecessary inconvenience.

Finally, the plastic build flexes noticeably when you type hard. It won't break — the chassis is sturdy enough for daily use — but it feels less premium than aluminum alternatives that cost twice as much.

## Who Should Buy This

- **iPad/tablet users** who also own a phone and want one keyboard to rule them all
- **Travelers** who need a lightweight, packable keyboard for occasional typing bursts
- **Students** who jot notes on a tablet but don't want to carry a full laptop

Skip it if you're a heavy night-owl typist who needs backlighting, or if you prefer the solid feel of an aluminum keyboard and don't mind paying double.

## FAQ

**Does this work with iPhones and Android phones?**
Yes. It pairs with iOS, Android, Windows, and macOS devices. The function row maps correctly to each OS — volume keys work on both iPhone and Android, though some media keys are OS-dependent.

**Can I use it while charging?**
Technically yes, but it's awkward — the Micro-USB port is on the front edge, so a cable juts out and interferes with your wrist position. Better to charge overnight.

**How do I switch between devices?**
Press the Bluetooth 1, 2, or 3 key on the top row. Each corresponds to a previously paired device. There's no visual indicator of which device is active, so you'll need to look at your screen to confirm.
