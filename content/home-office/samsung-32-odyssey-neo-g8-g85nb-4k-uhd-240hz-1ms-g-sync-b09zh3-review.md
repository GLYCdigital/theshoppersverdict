# Samsung Odyssey Neo G8 Review: The 4K 240Hz Sweet Spot (With Caveats)

The Samsung Odyssey Neo G8 (G85NB) is the monitor that specs-obsessed gamers have been waiting for — until they actually plug it in. At 4K UHD (3840x2160) with a 240Hz refresh rate and 1ms GtG response time, this 32-inch curved panel packs more raw numbers than almost anything else on the market. But with a 4.0-star average across 200 Amazon ratings, the real-world experience tells a more complicated story. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the monitor, a sturdy V-shaped metal stand, DisplayPort 1.4 cable, HDMI 2.1 cable, power brick, and a surprisingly decent remote control. The stand offers tilt, swivel, and height adjustment — no VESA mount included, but the 100x100mm pattern is there if you need it. The panel itself is a 1000R curved VA display with a 1196-zone Mini-LED backlight, which is the real star of the show.

## Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Scanlines

Let's start with what works. The Mini-LED backlight delivers HDR2000 peak brightness (2,000 nits) that genuinely transforms gaming. In *Cyberpunk 2077* or *Elden Ring*, neon signs and firelight pop with a punch that edge-lit monitors can't touch. Blooming is minimal — the 1196 zones do heavy lifting, and most buyers in Amazon reviews agree. One verified purchaser wrote: *"HDR is the best I've seen on any monitor under $1,500. It's not OLED, but it's close enough that I don't miss my LG C2 for desktop use."*

The 240Hz refresh rate at 4K is another genuine achievement. You need a DisplayPort 1.4 connection with DSC enabled to hit it, but once configured, motion clarity is excellent. The 1ms GtG response time holds up in fast-paced shooters — no visible ghosting in our testing.

**Here's the catch:** scanlines. A subset of users report horizontal lines appearing at 240Hz, particularly with Nvidia GPUs and certain driver versions. It's not universal — one Amazon reviewer noted *"no scanlines on my RTX 4090 with driver 551.86"* — but it's a known issue that Samsung has addressed inconsistently via firmware updates. If you get a unit with it, prepare for a frustrating return-or-live-with-it decision.

## Where It Falls Short

The 1000R curve is aggressive. For gaming, it's immersive. For spreadsheets, code, or design work, it's a liability — edges of the screen curve toward you, and straight lines look bent. This is a gaming-first monitor, not a productivity tool, despite its office-friendly 4K resolution.

The VA panel also has narrower viewing angles than IPS. If you sit dead-center, colors are rich. Move 30 degrees off-axis and they wash out noticeably. And while the matte coating kills reflections, it slightly reduces text sharpness compared to glossy panels — noticeable on white backgrounds.

Early units shipped with HDMI 2.1 that was software-limited, capping console gaming at 4K 120Hz. Later revisions fixed this, but if you're buying used or from a third-party seller, verify the firmware version first.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a PC gamer with a high-end GPU (RTX 4080 or better) who wants 4K resolution *and* competitive refresh rates on one screen. The Mini-LED HDR is a genuine upgrade over standard IPS panels.

**Skip it if:** You do productivity work, play on consoles primarily, or have a mid-range GPU that can't push 4K above 100fps. The curve and scanline risk make it a poor choice for mixed-use setups.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with G-Sync?**
A: Yes, it's G-Sync Compatible certified. Works over DisplayPort with Nvidia GPUs from GTX 10-series onward. Adaptive sync also works with AMD FreeSync Premium Pro.

**Q: Is 240Hz necessary at 4K?**
A: For most games, no — but for esports titles like *Valorant* or *CS2* at lower settings, it's a competitive edge. The real benefit is future-proofing for the next GPU generation.

**Q: Does the scanline issue affect all units?**
A: No. It's intermittent and driver-dependent. Samsung has released firmware updates (check version 1005.0 or later), but if you're unlucky, return it — Amazon's return policy covers this.

## The Verdict

The Odyssey Neo G8 is a spectacular gaming monitor with a split personality. On one hand, it's the best value in 4K high-refresh territory — Mini-LED HDR, 240Hz, and 1ms response for far less than OLED alternatives. On the other, the scanline lottery, aggressive curve, and console limitations make it a product you buy with your eyes open.

**Buy it if:** You're a dedicated PC gamer who wants flagship specs without OLED burn-in risk.
**Skip it if:** You need a versatile monitor for work and play, or you're sensitive to QC issues.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Brilliant where it counts, but not without compromises.
