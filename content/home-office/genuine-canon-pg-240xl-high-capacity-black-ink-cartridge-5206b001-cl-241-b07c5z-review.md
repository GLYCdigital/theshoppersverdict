Ink cartridges are the quiet budget-killers of home printing. You buy a $50 printer, then spend $60 a year keeping it fed. The Canon PG-240XL exists to soften that blow — but does the "XL" label actually deliver, or is it just marketing? After digging into specs, customer experiences, and the math, here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

You get one sealed Canon PG-240XL high-capacity black ink cartridge. That's it — no instructions, no software disc, no USB cable. Pop it out of the foil pouch, pull the orange protective tab, and snap it into a compatible Pixma printer. Installation takes about 30 seconds. The cartridge uses Canon's pigment-based black ink, which differs from the dye-based color inks in your printer — it's formulated to resist water smearing and dry faster on plain paper.

**Performance and real-world use**

The headline spec is 600 pages per cartridge, versus 180 pages for the standard PG-240. That's a 3.3x jump in capacity for roughly 1.6x the price — the math favors the XL if you print regularly. In real-world testing, buyers report getting close to that 600-page figure with standard text documents at default quality settings. If you print mostly photos or graphics, expect less — those ink-hungry jobs drain any cartridge faster.

Print quality is what you'd expect from genuine Canon pigment ink: sharp, dark black text with no feathering on standard copy paper. One Amazon reviewer noted, "Text comes out bold and crisp, and it doesn't smear when I highlight with a marker." That's the pigment advantage — dye-based inks soak into paper fibers and bleed, while pigment sits on top.

**Who should buy this**

If you're a student printing lecture notes, a home office worker churning out contracts, or a parent printing school forms, the PG-240XL is the right call.
