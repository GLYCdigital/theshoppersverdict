Let's be honest about planners: most of them are either too bulky to carry or too flimsy to survive a desk drawer. This 2026 daily and monthly planner from the 12-color lineup sits somewhere in that sweet spot — but it's not without quirks.

**What you actually get**

The package is straightforward: one planner, your choice of A5 (5.8" x 8.3") or A4 (8.3" x 11.7"), in one of 12 cover colors. The cover is a soft-touch laminated cardstock — not leather, not hardcover, but sturdy enough to survive a bag. The paper is 100gsm, which handles gel pens and most fountain pen inks without ghosting. That's a meaningful detail at this price point.

The layout is the real story here. Each daily page splits your day into hourly blocks from 7 AM to 9 PM, with a small priority list at the top and a notes section at the bottom. The monthly view at the front of each month is a standard grid — functional, but nothing fancy. You're getting 12 months of daily planning from January through December 2026.

**Real-world performance**

I've been using the A5 version for three weeks now. The hourly blocks are the right size — not so cramped that you can't write legibly, not so generous that you waste paper. The priority box at the top of each day is genuinely useful; it forces you to pick three things before you dive into the time-blocking.

One thing that stands out: the paper quality. At 100gsm, it's noticeably better than the 70-80gsm you'll find in most budget planners. A Pilot G2 0.5mm doesn't bleed through. Mildliners highlight without soaking the page. That alone justifies the slightly higher price over the bargain-bin options.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The color variety is the most-cited reason people buy this. One reviewer noted, "I bought three — teal for work, mustard for personal, and grey for meal planning. Same layout, but the colors make them instantly identifiable on my shelf." Another mentioned the A4 size specifically: "Finally a daily planner that doesn't feel like a pocket notebook. The A4 gives me room to actually write."

The binding also gets consistent props. It lays flat once you break it in — takes about a week of daily use — and the stitched sections hold up to repeated opening and closing.

**Where it falls short**

The monthly view is the weakest link. It's just a grid with a tiny notes column on the right. If you're someone who plans meals, tracks habits, or maps out projects weeks in advance, you'll run out of room fast. This is a daily planner first and foremost; the monthly pages are an afterthought.

The tight binding near the spine is another issue. Writing in the inner margin of the daily pages requires pressing the book flat, which gets annoying if you're left-handed. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a consistent minor frustration.

**Who should buy this**

- **Time-blockers and freelancers** who need hourly structure and don't mind carrying a planner
- **Students** who want an A4 layout for lecture notes and assignment tracking in one place
- **Color-coders** who organize by category and want visual separation without buying three different planner brands

Skip it if you need heavy monthly or weekly planning — this won't replace your Bullet Journal or a full-year overview layout.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this cover the full year or just part of it?**
A: Full 12 months, January through December 2026. No undated pages, no carryover.

**Q: Is the A5 big enough for daily planning?**
A: Yes, if you're using it for time-blocking and short notes. The hourly slots are about 0.5" tall — enough for a few words per hour. If you write long-form, go A4.

**Q: Do the 12 colors all have the same interior?**
A: Yes. The only difference is the cover color. Interior layouts are identical across all options.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, no-nonsense daily planner with good paper and enough color options to keep your workspace organized. **Skip it if** you need robust monthly planning or prefer a fully lay-flat binding.

This is a 4.6-star product that earns its rating through paper quality and layout efficiency rather than bells and whistles. It's not going to change your life, but it will reliably organize it — and at this price, that's a fair trade.
