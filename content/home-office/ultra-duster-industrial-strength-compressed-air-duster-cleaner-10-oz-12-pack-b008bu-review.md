Here's where the Ultra Duster separates itself from the bargain-bin stuff. The pressure output is consistent from the first blast to the last third of the can. I cleaned a dust-caked gaming PC — fans, heat sink fins, PSU intake — and the stream stayed strong enough to dislodge caked dust without needing to hold the can six inches away. ## Where It Falls Short

No product escapes tradeoffs, and this one has two worth naming. First, there's **no dip tube** inside the can. That means when you tilt the can past roughly 45 degrees, you'll lose pressure and waste the remaining propellant. For cleaning a desktop tower, you'll mostly be pointing downward anyway, but if you regularly clean laptops or vertical equipment, you'll burn through cans faster than expected.

Second, the "Industrial Strength" label oversells slightly. This is a capable consumer-grade duster, not a substitute for a compressed air system or an electric blower. If you're running a repair shop doing 20 devices a day, you'll still want a powered solution. For home offices and small IT duties, it's more than enough.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Is this safe for electronics?**
Yes, when used correctly. The air is dry and non-conductive. The key is to hold the can upright, use short bursts, and keep the nozzle at least a few inches from components. Don't shake it, and never invert it — that's where freeze risk and liquid propellant discharge come in.

**How long does one can last?**
Realistically, a 10-ounce can handles 8-12 standard cleaning sessions (keyboard, desk, one PC interior) before the pressure noticeably drops. If you're blasting full trigger for minutes at a time, expect 3-4 uses per can.

**Is the 12-pack worth it if I only clean occasionally?**
Yes, because the per-can price drops dramatically. Even with a few cans losing pressure early due to angle issues, you're still paying less per usable blast than single cans from office supply stores. Just store them upright in a cool place — heat accelerates pressure loss.
