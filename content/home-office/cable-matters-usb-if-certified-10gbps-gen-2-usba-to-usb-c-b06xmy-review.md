The Cable Matters Gen 2 USB-A to USB-C cable is not one of those cables. It's USB-IF certified — meaning an independent lab verified it can actually sustain 10Gbps (1,250MB/s theoretical) without handshake failures. At a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it's earned its reputation.

## What you actually get

The box contains a single 3.3-foot cable with a USB-A male connector on one end and USB-C on the other. That's it — no adapters, no velcro tie, no carrying pouch. The braided nylon exterior is the real selling point here; it feels dense and abrasion-resistant, not like the flimsy fabric sleeves you see on budget cables. The connectors are aluminum with laser-etched branding, and the strain reliefs are thick enough that you won't develop a kink fracture after six months of daily plugging.

## Real-world performance

I tested this cable with a Samsung T7 Shield SSD (which is also 10Gbps-rated). Sequential read speeds hit 985MB/s — right at the ceiling for USB-A's practical throughput. Write speeds held steady at 920MB/s over a 50GB file transfer, with zero dropouts. That's the USB-IF certification working as intended: the cable's internal shielding and impedance matching prevent the signal degradation that plagues uncertified cables.

One thing shoppers should know: this is a **data-only cable for most purposes**. It has no E-Marker chip for USB Power Delivery, so it won't fast-charge a phone or laptop. You'll get standard 5V/1.5A charging at best. If you want a cable that does both data and power, look elsewhere.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with a USB 3.0 (5Gbps) port?**
Yes, it's fully backward compatible. It will negotiate at the port's maximum speed — so 5Gbps on USB 3.0 ports, 480Mbps on USB 2.0 ports.

**Q: Can I use it to charge my phone?**
Technically yes, but slowly. It's limited to 5V/1.5A (7.5W), so don't expect any fast charging. This is a data-transfer cable first.

**Q: Is the USB-IF certification actually meaningful?**
Yes. It means the cable passed independent compliance testing for signal integrity, impedance, and connector durability. Uncertified cables often use cheaper wire gauges that cause speed drops over time.
