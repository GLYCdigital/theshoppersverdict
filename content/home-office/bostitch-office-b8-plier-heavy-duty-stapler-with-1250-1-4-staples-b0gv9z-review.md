Most staplers are a compromise. The cheap plastic ones bend on page five. The heavy-duty desk models need two hands and a grunt. The Bostitch B8 plier stapler sits in a third category — a tool that uses leverage instead of brute force. After testing it against thick booklets, card stock, and the occasional stubborn receipt, here's the honest picture.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the stapler itself and a full box of 1/4-inch staples — 1,250 of them. That's a meaningful inclusion, not a token sample pack. The stapler body is all steel with a textured grip that runs the length of the handle. It's a plier-style design, meaning you squeeze from the sides rather than press down from the top. This changes the physics entirely: your grip strength does the work, not your palm.

The staple capacity is a full strip of 210 staples, which is standard for this class. The 1/4-inch leg length handles up to about 20 sheets of standard paper without buckling.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The plier mechanism is the headline feature, and it earns the billing. Squeezing the handles together gives you far more force than a standard desktop stapler. I stapled a 16-page report without needing to brace the stapler against a table — it just worked. The pivot point sits low, so the staple exits cleanly and clinches flat against the paper.

The jam-release lever is genuinely useful. When a staple misfires (and it will, eventually), you flip the lever, open the channel, and clear it in seconds. No screwdrivers, no prying, no swearing. This is a design detail that separates a good stapler from a frustrating one.

One thing to note: the first staple load takes a firm push. The strip needs to seat fully into the channel or the stapler won't fire. A few Amazon reviewers mentioned this as a moment of confusion. One wrote, "Thought it was broken until I pushed the staples in harder — after that, flawless." That matches my experience.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects a pattern. Buyers repeatedly highlight the grip strength advantage. One reviewer summed it up: "I have arthritis and this is the only stapler I can use for more than a few pages." Another praised the build: "Feels like it'll outlast me. Solid metal, no creaks, no flex."

The included staples also earned frequent mention. Several reviewers noted they'd been using the same staple box for months, which speaks to both the capacity and the stapler's efficiency.

## Where It Falls Short

There's no built-in staple remover. If you make a mistake, you need a separate tool. For a $20-ish stapler, this isn't a dealbreaker, but it's a missing convenience.

The plier shape also doesn't store flat. It takes up more drawer space than a standard stapler, and the handles stick out awkwardly. If drawer organization is your thing, budget for that.

Loading the staples requires a deliberate push to seat the strip correctly. It's not difficult, but it's not as forgiving as a spring-loaded channel on a standard stapler. First-time users may briefly think they've broken it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Office workers who staple thick documents regularly** — the grip leverage saves your hands over a full day
- **People with hand or wrist weakness** — arthritis, carpal tunnel, or just small hands; this design reduces strain significantly
- **Anyone tired of cheap staplers that jam** — the all-steel build and jam-release lever are worth the upgrade
- **Crafters and DIYers** — works well on card stock, fabric layers, and light upholstery projects

Skip it if you only staple a few sheets a week and want something that stores flat in a drawer. A basic desktop stapler will serve you fine.

## FAQ

**Q: How many pages can it staple?**
The 1/4-inch staples handle up to about 20 sheets of standard 20-lb paper. Thicker paper or card stock reduces that to 10-12 sheets. It won't do booklets or thick reports beyond that.

**Q: Does it use standard staples?**
No. It requires 1/4-inch (6mm) staples specifically — the ones included in the box. Standard 26/6 staples won't fit. Replacement boxes are widely available and inexpensive.

**Q: Is it actually easier on your hands?**
Yes, noticeably. The plier grip distributes force across your palm and fingers rather than concentrating it on your thumb. Multiple reviewers with arthritis confirmed this difference.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you staple more than a few pages at a time, have any hand strain issues, or just want a stapler that won't die in a year. The Bostitch B8 is a legitimate tool, not a desk ornament.

**Skip it if** your stapling needs are minimal and you prioritize a slim profile in your drawer.
