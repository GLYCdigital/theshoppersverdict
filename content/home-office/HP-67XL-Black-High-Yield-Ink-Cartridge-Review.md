Let's cut through the ink cartridge noise: the HP 67XL is the most-purchased high-yield cartridge in HP's budget lineup, with 53,000+ ratings averaging 4.6 stars. But at $35.89, you're paying a premium for OEM ink. Is it worth it? Depends entirely on how you print.

**What's in the box**

You get one sealed HP 67XL black ink cartridge. That's it. No install guide beyond the printed insert, no USB cable, no cleaning kit. The cartridge itself follows HP's standard design — a compact, rectangular unit with the tri-color and black versions looking nearly identical, so double-check the label before snapping it in. It's compatible with most HP DeskJet 2700/4100 series, ENVY 6000/7000 series, and Tango printers. If you own one of these, installation takes under a minute: peel the orange tab, push until it clicks, run a nozzle check.

**Performance and real-world use**

The "XL" designation means roughly 600 pages at 5% coverage — double the standard 67's 300-page rating. That math matters: at $35.89, you're paying about 6 cents per page. The standard 67 costs $25.89 for half the yield, which comes to nearly 8.6 cents per page. The XL is the better deal if you print regularly.

Print quality is where HP earns its reputation. Text comes out sharp and dark, with no streaking or banding on standard copy paper. Drying time is quick — you won't smear pages stacking out of the tray. For occasional photo printing, it's fine but not exceptional; this is a text-and-documents cartridge.

Here's the catch I keep coming back to: HP's ink subscription model (Instant Ink) can undercut this cartridge's price significantly for high-volume users. If you print more than 50 pages monthly, the math on subscription plans often beats buying individual cartridges.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme across positive reviews is reliability. One verified buyer noted: *"I've used HP cartridges for years and this one has never failed me. No clogs, no streaks, no 'low ink' warnings that appear after three pages."* Another long-term user echoed that: *"I print about 400 pages a month for my small business. This cartridge lasts me six weeks consistently. The cheap knockoffs last two weeks and clog my printhead."*

That's the real value proposition — consistency. Remanufactured cartridges save money upfront but introduce variables: printhead quality, ink formulation, and failure rates.

**Where it falls short**

The price stings. At $35.89, this is not a budget option. Third-party alternatives sell for $12-18 and offer similar page yields. If you're a light printer (under 100 pages monthly), the standard HP 67 at $25.89 is more cost-effective.

There's also no visible ink-level indicator on the cartridge itself. You'll rely on HP's software or printer display to gauge remaining ink — and the low-ink warning often appears right before the cartridge runs dry, not days ahead. Several reviewers mentioned this frustration: *"The printer told me I had low ink, and two pages later it was completely dead. I had to stop mid-print to run to the store."*

**Who should buy this**

- **Home office workers** printing 100-300 pages monthly of documents and forms — the XL's yield and reliability justify the cost
- **Students** who need consistent printing during term without mid-semester cartridge failures
- **Anyone who's been burned by third-party cartridges** — the peace of mind is worth the premium

**Skip it if** you're a light printer (under 50 pages monthly — get the standard 67), or you're price-sensitive and willing to gamble on remanufactured options.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work in my HP DeskJet 2755e?**
A: Yes, the 67XL is compatible with most HP DeskJet 2700-series, ENVY 6000/7000-series, and Tango printers. Check HP's compatibility page with your exact model number before ordering — some newer printers require different cartridges.

**Q: How many pages will I actually get?**
A: HP's 600-page rating assumes 5% page coverage (standard text documents). Heavy graphics or dense text will reduce yield to 400-450 pages. Light printing with mostly blank pages can stretch it past 700.

**Q: Is the XL worth the extra $10 over the standard 67?**
A: Yes, if you print over 100 pages monthly. The XL's cost-per-page is about 6 cents versus 8.6 cents for the standard. At 200 pages monthly, you save roughly $5 per cartridge cycle.

**The Verdict: 3.5/5 — Buy it if reliability matters more than price**

**Buy it if:** You print regularly, value consistent print quality, and don't want to troubleshoot third-party cartridge failures. The 600-page yield and OEM reliability justify the premium for moderate-to-heavy users.

**Skip it if:** You're a light printer, on a tight budget, or open to HP's Instant Ink subscription — which can significantly undercut this cartridge's cost-per-page for high-volume printing.

The HP 67XL is the safe, dependable choice. It's not the smartest financial decision on the shelf, but it's the one that won't waste your time. For most home office and student users, that trade-off is worth it.
