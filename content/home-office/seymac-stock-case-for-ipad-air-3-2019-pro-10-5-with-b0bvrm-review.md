If you're still rocking an iPad Air 3 (2019) or the 10.5-inch iPad Pro, you know the struggle: new cases are getting harder to find, and the ones that exist are either flimsy folios or overpriced relics. The SEYMAC stock case sits in a sweet spot — it's a rugged, drop-rated shell with a built-in screen protector, priced like a budget accessory but built like something that'll survive a construction site.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the case itself — a one-piece hybrid design with a hard polycarbonate back plate fused to a TPU bumper. There's no separate screen protector to install; it's molded into the front frame. The kickstand folds out from the back and locks into two angles: roughly 45 degrees for typing and 70 degrees for viewing. It also has an Apple Pencil holder — a silicone loop on the right edge, not a slot, so it won't interfere with charging the Pencil via the magnetic connector.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this with an iPad Air 3, and the fit is exact. All cutouts line up: the camera lens is fully exposed (no distortion), the Lightning port has enough clearance for third-party cables, and the volume buttons are covered but still clicky — a common failure point on cheap cases, but SEYMAC got the tactile response right.

The built-in screen protector is the standout feature. It sits flush against the glass with no air gap, so the Apple Pencil still registers pressure correctly. Touch sensitivity is unchanged. The tradeoff is glare — if you work near a window, you'll see your reflection during video calls. Matte screen protectors exist for a reason; this one is glossy.

Drop protection is credible. The corners have air-cushion pockets, and the TPU bumper extends past the screen edge by about 2mm, so face-down drops won't crack the display. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: *"Dropped it on concrete from waist height and the iPad didn't even flinch. The case has a scuff, but that's what it's for."* That sentiment shows up repeatedly in the 229 ratings — this thing takes hits.

**Where it falls short**

The kickstand is genuinely stiff. Out of the box, you'll need two hands to pop it open and adjust the angle. It does loosen with use — after about a week of daily folding, it operates with one hand — but if you need to switch between typing and viewing modes quickly, budget for that initial break-in period.

Bulk is the other real issue. This is a protective case, not a sleek folio. It adds about 0.6 inches of thickness and a noticeable amount of weight. If you're used to a slim magnetic cover, this will feel like a brick in your bag. That's the price of the drop rating and the integrated screen protector.

One more thing: the Pencil loop is tight. If you use a thicker grip sleeve on your Apple Pencil, it won't slide in. Bare Pencil only.

**Who should buy this**

- **Field workers and students** who toss their iPad into a backpack alongside textbooks, tools, or gym gear — this case shrugs off that abuse.
- **Anyone with a 10.5-inch iPad Pro or Air 3** who's tired of hunting for compatible cases. SEYMAC explicitly designed for both, and the fit confirms it.
- **Sellers, warehouse staff, or anyone using an iPad as a POS terminal** — the kickstand holds firm on flat surfaces, and the screen protector means no separate film to bubble up during a transaction.

**Who should skip it**

- **Designers and artists** who need the thinnest possible profile and a matte screen surface for drawing. This case will annoy you.
- **People who primarily use their iPad for media** — the stiff kickstand and glossy screen are dealbreakers in a dimly lit living room.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this case work with the iPad Pro 10.5 (2017) as well as the Air 3 (2019)?**
A: Yes. The two models share identical chassis dimensions. The cutouts for the camera, speakers, and buttons are exactly the same. If you have a 2017 Pro 10.5, this will fit perfectly.

**Q: Can you remove the built-in screen protector and use your own?**
A: No. It's molded into the front frame. If you want a tempered glass protector instead, skip this case and buy a shell-style model without a built-in film.

**Q: Does the Apple Pencil charge wirelessly through the case?**
A: Yes, for the Pencil 2 (which works with the Air 3 and Pro 10.5). The right edge has an open groove where the magnetic charging connector sits, so the Pencil snaps on and charges normally. The silicone loop is positioned to avoid blocking that magnet.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you want one-and-done protection for an older iPad that's still doing daily work. The built-in screen protector, solid drop rating, and precise fit make it a no-brainer at this price point.

**Skip it if** you prioritize thinness or need a matte screen surface for drawing. The bulk and glare will frustrate you more than the protection helps.

For a case that costs less than a single screen protector replacement, the SEYMAC delivers exactly what it promises: protection you won't think about until you drop the tablet — and then you'll be glad you bought it.
