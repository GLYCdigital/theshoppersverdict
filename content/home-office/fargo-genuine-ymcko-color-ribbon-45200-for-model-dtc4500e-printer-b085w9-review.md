## The Real Cost of "Genuine" — Is It Worth It?

The question is whether you'll spend more time fighting error codes and faded prints than you save on the purchase price. ## What You Actually Get

The box contains one complete YMCKO ribbon roll—five panels per card print cycle: Yellow, Magenta, Cyan, black (K), and Overlaminate. That last one matters. The overlaminate panel applies a clear protective layer over your printed cards, which is the difference between an ID that looks new after a year and one that's scratched and unreadable after a month. Yield is 750 full-color cards per ribbon, which is the standard for this class.

Setup is genuinely tool-free. Pop the ribbon into the DTC4500e's drawer, and the printer auto-detects it. No calibration needed. If you've used aftermarket ribbons before, you know this is where the headaches typically start—compatibles often trigger "ribbon not recognized" errors on Fargo printers, and you lose a workday troubleshooting.

## Real-World Performance

Color saturation is the standout. The YMCKO layering produces deep, consistent color that doesn't wash out on white cards. Skin tones render naturally—critical for employee badges. Text stays sharp on the black panel, and the overlaminate gives cards a professional, matte finish that resists scuffs. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"Printed 600 cards without a single jam or misprint. The quality is exactly what we got with the sample cards from Fargo."* That's the consistency you're paying for.

Print speed is governed by the printer, not the ribbon, but the DTC4500e pushes out a full-color card in about 25 seconds. The ribbon feeds smoothly without the tension issues that plague cheap alternatives.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest downsides: price and panel allocation. OEM ribbon costs roughly 30-40% more per card than compatibles. If you're printing 5,000+ cards annually, that's real money. Second, this ribbon is YMCKO only—it does not include the separate resin black (K) panel for monochrome back-side printing. If your cards need a black-text back side, you'll need a second ribbon type, which adds cost and inventory complexity.

Also worth noting: yield claims assume full-color prints. If you're only printing monochrome badges, you'll waste the color panels—buy the mono ribbon instead.
