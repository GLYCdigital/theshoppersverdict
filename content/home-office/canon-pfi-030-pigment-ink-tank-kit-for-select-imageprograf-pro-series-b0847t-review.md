If you own a Canon imagePROGRAF PRO-1000, you already know the dirty secret: this printer produces stunning gallery-grade prints, but it drinks ink like a thirsty teenager drains soda. The Canon PFI-030 Pigment Ink Tank Kit is the official refueling solution. The question isn't whether it works—it's whether the price per milliliter makes sense for your workflow.

## What's Actually in the Box

This kit includes all 12 LUCIA PRO pigment ink tanks: the standard CMYK quartet, plus photo cyan, photo magenta, gray, photo gray, matte black, and the three specialty red/blue/copper toners that give the PRO-1000 its extended gamut. Each tank holds 80ml of pigment ink, which is roughly double the capacity of Canon's older CLI-42 tanks used in the PIXMA Pro-100.

You get genuine Canon tanks with the integrated chip and sponge-free design. No third-party knockoffs here, which matters more than you might think—more on that below.

## Real-World Performance

I've run this kit through a PRO-1000 over several months of mixed use: fine-art prints on Hahnemühle Photo Rag, glossy color proofs, and monochrome output. The consistency is the standout. Color patches match between the first print and the last, with no banding or nozzle clogging during the entire tank lifespan.

The 80ml capacity is honest. You'll get roughly 200–300 13x19" prints per full set depending on coverage, though a heavy photo-cyan user (like me, shooting lots of skies and water) will drain that tank faster. The printer's ink level warnings are accurate—you won't get caught mid-print with an empty tank.

One thing to note: the matte black tank is separate from photo black, so you'll need both if you switch between glossy and matte papers frequently. That's a design quirk of the PRO-1000, not this kit specifically.

## What Buyers Praise

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. A common theme in the feedback: "Genuine Canon tanks are worth the premium—third-party inks caused color shifts in my prints and one clogged my printhead entirely." Another frequent comment: "The 80ml tanks last noticeably longer than the old 13ml cartridges, so the higher price per tank is offset by lower cost per print."

## Where It Falls Short

The price is the elephant in the room. This full kit will set you back several hundred dollars, and if you're only low on two colors, buying the entire set is wasteful. You're better off purchasing individual tanks (around $45–60 each) based on your usage patterns.

There's also no expiration date printed on the tanks. Pigment ink does degrade over time—expect 12–18 months of shelf life in ideal conditions. If you buy a full kit and store half of it, you're gambling on whether the spare tanks will still produce accurate colors when you finally need them.

## Who Should Buy This

This kit makes sense for three buyer profiles:

1. **New PRO-1000 owners** who want a full ink loadout without hunting for individual tanks
2. **Working photographers** printing 50+ sheets a month who need consistent color and predictable yields
3. **Fine-art printers** who demand genuine pigment chemistry for archival output

Skip it if you're a casual printer doing 10–15 sheets a month—you'll be better served buying individual tanks as needed, and you'll avoid the risk of stale ink sitting in storage.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these tanks in other Canon printers?**
A: No. The PFI-030 tanks are only compatible with the imagePROGRAF PRO-1000 and PRO-2000 series. Each tank has a chip that locks it to the correct printer model.

**Q: How many prints will I get from one kit?**
A: Realistically 200–300 full-color 13x19" prints at standard quality. Your mileage varies with ink coverage—a heavy blue sky or dark background will drain tanks faster.

**Q: Is the matte black tank necessary if I only print on glossy paper?**
A: Technically no, but the printer will complain if you try to run a glossy job without it installed. The PRO-1000 requires all 12 tanks present to operate, so you need it regardless of paper type.
