Let’s be honest: most to-do pads are just glorified scratch paper with a line drawn down the middle. The **To Do List Notepads** from Priority Notes actually try to do something smarter. Each sheet is split into three zones — a main checklist area, a dedicated “Priorities” box at the top, and a side column for notes or deadlines. That structure matters more than you’d think. It forces you to separate the urgent from the merely busy, which is half the battle in daily planning.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes **three pads**, each with **50 sheets** (150 total). That’s roughly five months of daily planning if you use one sheet per weekday. Each sheet measures **8.5 x 11 inches** — standard letter size, so they fit clipboards and most desk organizers without modification. The top has a date line, the middle is a generous checklist grid, and there’s a small “Notes” strip along the right edge. The backing is a **thick chipboard** — noticeably sturdier than the flimsy cardboard you get on most grocery-store notepads. It holds its shape on a clipboard or against a desk without curling at the corners.

## Real-World Performance

The paper is **smooth and takes ballpoint and gel ink well**, but here’s the honest tradeoff: it’s not heavy stock. If you press hard with a gel pen or use a wet rollerball, you’ll see faint ghosting on the sheet underneath. It’s not bleed-through — you can still read the page below — but it’s visible. If that bothers you, stick to ballpoints or fine-liners.

The **perforation is genuinely good**. Sheets tear off cleanly along the top binding with a single smooth pull. No jagged edges, no half-torn strips. I tested this on three separate pads, and every sheet separated exactly where it should. The binding itself is **glued and stitched** — it survived being flipped, folded back, and shoved into a work bag without pages coming loose.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest structural complaint is **limited layout variety**. You get one format — checklist-centric with a priority box. If you prefer time-blocking, hourly scheduling, or a vertical weekly view, this isn’t it. There’s also no **undated option**, so if you skip a day, you’re either wasting a sheet or crossing out the date. And as mentioned, the paper weight is **middle-of-the-road** — fine for most pens, but not for heavy ink users or fountain pens.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The busy professional**: If you juggle multiple projects and need to triage daily, the priority box is a game-changer.
- **The clipboard crowd**: Tradespeople, nurses, teachers — anyone who works standing or moving and needs a stable writing surface.
- **The “app fatigue” sufferer**: If digital planners haven’t stuck, the physical act of writing on a structured sheet often does.

**Skip it if** you need hourly time-blocking, if you’re a fountain pen user who requires thick paper, or if you prefer undated, flexible layouts.
