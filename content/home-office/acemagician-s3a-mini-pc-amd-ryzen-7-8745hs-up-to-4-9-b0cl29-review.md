The ACEMAGICIAN S3A is the kind of mini PC that makes you question why anyone still buys a tower. At roughly the size of a thick paperback, it packs an AMD Ryzen 7 8745HS — an 8-core, 16-thread processor that boosts to 4.9GHz — and pairs it with 32GB of DDR5 RAM and a 1TB NVMe SSD. That's not a casual office machine; that's a legitimate workstation in a box smaller than your router.

**What's in the Box**

You get the S3A unit itself, a 65W USB-C power adapter, an HDMI cable, a VESA mounting bracket with screws, and a quick-start guide. The aluminum chassis feels premium — not the cheap plastic you sometimes see at this price point. Port selection is generous: dual HDMI 2.0 outputs, dual USB4 (which, confusingly, are actually USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 — see below), four USB-A 3.2 ports, a 2.5GbE Ethernet jack, and a 3.5mm audio combo jack. Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.2 round out connectivity.

**Real-World Performance**

I ran this thing through a typical heavy office day: 30+ browser tabs, a 4K video call, spreadsheet crunching, and light photo editing in Photoshop. The S3A didn't flinch. The Ryzen 7 8745HS is a 35W part, but AMD's efficiency means it punches well above its wattage. Cinebench R23 scores hover around 15,000 multi-core — that's Ryzen 7 5800X territory, roughly double what you'd get from a comparable Intel N100 or older i5 mini PC.

The 780M integrated graphics are the story here. It's genuinely impressive for an iGPU — capable of 1080p gaming at medium settings in esports titles like CS2 or Valorant. But if you're eyeing Cyberpunk 2077 or Starfield, you'll be disappointed. This is a productivity machine first, a casual gaming box second.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 204 ratings (4.1 stars), the recurring theme is surprise at how much power fits in this footprint. One verified buyer wrote, *"Replaced my 5-year-old i7 desktop with this. Everything loads instantly, and I forgot it was even on my desk — it's that small."* Another noted, *"32GB RAM out of the box means I don't have to think about upgrades for years."* The VESA mount gets frequent shoutouts — it disappears behind a monitor, turning any display into an all-in-one.

**Where It Falls Short**

The dual USB4 ports are the biggest letdown. They're marketed as USB4, but they lack Thunderbolt certification and don't support the full 40Gbps bandwidth — they run at 20Gbps. That means no eGPU enclosures, no high-speed external storage beyond USB 3.2 speeds. For a machine this capable, the I/O bottleneck is frustrating.

Fan noise is the second issue. Under sustained load — say, a video export or a long compile — the fan becomes audible. It's not jet-engine loud, but in a dead-silent office, you'll notice it. Idle and light browsing are whisper-quiet, though.

Finally, there's no SD card slot. Minor, but photographers will need a dongle.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Remote workers and hybrid professionals** who want a clutter-free desk with serious multitasking muscle.
- **Students** running engineering software, data analysis, or heavy research — the 32GB RAM future-proofs you.
- **Home theater enthusiasts** who want a compact, quiet PC for 4K streaming and light emulation.

**Skip it if** you're a gamer needing discrete graphics, or you rely on Thunderbolt accessories (external GPUs, high-speed RAID arrays). Also skip if you're on a tight budget — the S3A sits at a premium vs. N100-based mini PCs, though the performance gap justifies it.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this handle 4K video editing?**
A: Yes, for light-to-moderate editing in DaVinci Resolve or Premiere Pro. The 8745HS and 32GB RAM handle 4K timelines smoothly. Heavy effects stacks or 8K footage will strain the 780M.

**Q: Does it support dual monitors?**
A: Yes — two HDMI 2.0 ports drive dual 4K@60Hz displays simultaneously. The USB4 ports can also carry display output if you have USB-C monitors.

**Q: Is the RAM upgradable?**
A: The 32GB is soldered (LPDDR5X), so no upgrades. The M.2 SSD is replaceable if you need more storage.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you want desktop-class performance in a package that fits in your palm, and you don't need Thunderbolt or discrete graphics. The S3A is a smart investment for anyone tired of tower PCs hogging their workspace.

**Skip it if** gaming or external GPU expansion is on your roadmap, or you need absolute silence under load. For everyone else, this is one of the best-value mini PCs in its class.
