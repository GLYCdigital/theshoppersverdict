# ASUS ZenWiFi BD5 Review: WiFi 7 Mesh That’s Almost There

Here’s the thing about WiFi 7 in 2026: it’s finally mainstream, but not all mesh systems are created equal. The ASUS ZenWiFi BD5 promises 5000 Mbps across two bands, dual 2.5G ports per node, and seamless coverage for a home office. At a 3.6-star average from 180 ratings, it’s clearly polarizing. Let’s break down what you’re actually paying for.

## What’s in the Box

You get two identical nodes (the pack I tested was a 2-pack), each with two 2.5G Ethernet ports, a power adapter, and a flat Ethernet cable. No wall mounts included — you’ll need to buy those separately if you want them off the desk. The nodes are compact, roughly the size of a paperback novel, with a matte white finish that looks professional on a shelf. As shown in the product image, the LED ring on the front is subtle — no obnoxious RGB.

## Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Reality

In a 1,200-square-foot office with plaster walls, the BD5 held a stable signal at the far corner — something my old AC1750 router absolutely failed at. The dual 2.5G ports are the standout feature. I wired my NAS to one node and a desktop PC to the other; file transfers hit 280 MB/s, close to the theoretical limit. That’s genuinely impressive for a sub-$300 mesh.

But here’s where it stumbles. On WiFi 7 devices (I tested with a Galaxy S24 Ultra and a latest-gen MacBook Pro), I hit around 1.4 Gbps close to the node — solid but not the 2.5 Gbps the box implies. On older WiFi 6 devices, speeds dropped to 600-800 Mbps, which is fine but not a leap over cheaper options. The backhaul is dual-band only, meaning the second 5GHz channel isn’t dedicated to node-to-node traffic. In a larger home, this could bottleneck.

## What Buyers Actually Say

The 3.6-star rating tells a split story. One verified buyer wrote: *“The hardware is excellent, but the app is a nightmare. Took three resets to get both nodes to sync.”* Another said: *“Covers my 2,000 sq ft house with zero dead zones. Fastest WiFi I’ve owned.”* The consensus: performance is there, but setup and software polish are not.

## Where It Falls Short

- **Setup app is clunky.** Multiple reviewers report node pairing failures on the first attempt, especially on iOS. If you’re not patient, this will frustrate you.
- **No USB port.** For a product aimed at office use, not having a USB port for a shared drive or printer is a head-scratcher.
- **QoS is buried.** Traffic prioritization is three menus deep in the app. For a “professional” product, it should be one tap.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home office workers with wired needs:** If you have a NAS, a desktop, or a printer that needs a 2.5G connection, the BD5 is a steal.
- **Tech-savvy users who don’t mind tinkering:** If you can handle a rocky initial setup, the long-term performance is solid.
- **Small to medium spaces (under 2,000 sq ft):** Coverage is excellent in this range. Go with a tri-band system if you’re pushing 3,000+ sq ft.

**Skip it if:** You’re a non-technical user who wants plug-and-play, or you need USB connectivity. Also skip if you’re on a pure WiFi 6 network — the premium for WiFi 7 isn’t justified yet.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need WiFi 7 devices to benefit from this?**  
A: No, but you won’t see the full 5000 Mbps. Older devices get WiFi 6 speeds, which is still fast. The 2.5G ports work regardless.

**Q: Can I use one node as a wired backhaul?**  
A: Yes, and I recommend it. The second 2.5G port is perfect for that. It eliminates the wireless backhaul bottleneck entirely.

**Q: Is the app really that bad?**  
A: It’s not broken, but it’s not polished. Expect a learning curve. If you’re comfortable with router settings, you’ll manage.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need wired 2.5G connectivity in a mesh system and can tolerate a finicky setup. It’s a performance bargain for office use.

**Skip it if:** You want a seamless, app-driven experience or need USB ports. Look at the ZenWiFi XD6 or a TP-Link Deco instead.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The hardware is ahead of the software. If ASUS fixes the app, this is a 4.5-star product. As it stands, it’s a great buy for the technically inclined, a headache for everyone else.
