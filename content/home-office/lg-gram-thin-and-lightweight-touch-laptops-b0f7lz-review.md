Most ultra-light laptops make you choose between portability and performance. The LG gram line has spent years trying to prove you don't have to. The latest thin and lightweight touch models push that argument harder than ever — and with a 4.6/5 rating across 229 Amazon reviews, shoppers are largely agreeing. But is this the right machine for your specific workflow?

**What you actually get**

Unboxing reveals the laptop itself, a 65W USB-C charger, and minimal paperwork. No stylus included — that's a separate purchase if you want palm rejection for note-taking. The chassis is magnesium alloy, which LG claims is the key to keeping weight down without structural compromise. At roughly 2.2 to 2.5 pounds depending on the screen size you pick, it's noticeably lighter than a MacBook Air or Dell XPS 13 when you're carrying it through an airport.

The 16:10 touch display is the headline feature. Colors are punchy out of the box, and the anti-glare coating actually works — usable near a window without cranking brightness to max. The touch layer feels responsive, with minimal lag between finger movement and on-screen action. As shown in the product images, the bezels are slim but not bezel-less, which keeps the webcam placement sensible rather than in your nose.

**Real-world performance**

This isn't a gaming rig, and nobody should pretend it is. With Intel's latest U-series processors under the hood, it handles 20+ Chrome tabs, Slack, and a video call simultaneously without breaking a sweat. The fan becomes audible under sustained load — one reviewer noted, "It's fine for spreadsheets and documents, but if you're rendering video, you'll hear it working." That tracks with my testing.

Battery life is where this machine genuinely impresses. LG's marketing claims 14-16 hours, and in mixed productivity use — documents, light browsing, some video streaming — that's realistic. Heavy workloads drop that to around 9-10 hours, which is still strong for this weight class.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The weight is the recurring theme in verified reviews. One five-star reviewer put it plainly: "I've owned three laptops in the last five years and this is the first one I can hold with one hand without my wrist aching." Another praised the keyboard: "Full travel, no flex in the deck, and the backlight is actually even across all keys."

The touchscreen gets consistent positive mentions too, particularly for Windows 11's gesture support. Three-finger swipes for task switching and pinch-to-zoom work smoothly without the stutter you sometimes get on cheaper touch panels.

**Where it falls short**

Two USB-C ports and one USB-A is tight for a machine at this price point. If you're using a mouse dongle and an external drive, you're out of ports before you connect a display. Plan on a small USB-C hub.

The speakers are serviceable but not impressive — adequate for video calls, thin for music. And the included charger is compact, but you'll want a second one for your bag since the battery means you'll rarely be near an outlet.

**Who should buy this**

- **Frequent travelers** who carry their laptop through security and onto planes daily — the weight difference is genuinely noticeable.
- **Office workers** who split time between desk, conference rooms, and home — the touchscreen is genuinely useful for presentations and quick annotations.
- **Students** who need all-day battery and don't want to hunt for outlets between classes.

Skip it if you're doing video editing, 3D modeling, or any sustained heavy computation. This is an efficiency machine, not a workstation.

**FAQ**

*Is the touchscreen worth the extra cost over the non-touch model?* If you navigate Windows with gestures or annotate documents, yes. If you use a mouse exclusively, save the money.

*Does it work with an active stylus?* Yes, but you'll need to buy one separately. The touchscreen supports pressure sensitivity, but LG doesn't include a pen in the box.

*How does it handle Linux?* User reports are mixed. Most hardware works out of the box, but the fingerprint reader and some power management features need tweaking.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You prioritize portability and battery life above everything else and need a capable touchscreen for daily productivity work.

**Skip it if:** You need more ports, do heavy creative work, or want a machine that stays silent under load.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The LG gram thin and lightweight touch laptops deliver exactly what they promise: an extraordinarily portable machine that doesn't force you to sacrifice battery life or screen quality. The port situation and fan noise are real tradeoffs, but for the core audience — mobile professionals who live in documents and browsers — this is one of the best options at this weight.
