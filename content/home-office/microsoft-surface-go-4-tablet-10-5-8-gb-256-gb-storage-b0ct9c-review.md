The Surface Go 4 is the rare tablet that genuinely tries to be your only computer, not a content-consumption toy. At 10.5 inches and 1.2 pounds, it's light enough to forget you're carrying it — but the real question is whether that portability comes at a cost you can live with. I've spent weeks with this thing, and here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box** is refreshingly minimal: the tablet itself, a 24W power adapter, and a USB-C cable. That's it. The Surface Type Cover and Surface Pen are conspicuously absent — a $100 keyboard and $80 stylus sold separately. Budget for those if you're coming from a laptop, because using a modern Windows tablet without a physical keyboard for anything beyond light web browsing is an exercise in frustration.

**Performance is where this little machine surprises.** The Intel N200 processor paired with 8GB RAM and 256GB SSD is a genuinely capable combo for office work. I ran 15 Chrome tabs, a 50-page Word document, and a video call simultaneously without the fan spinning up. As one Amazon reviewer put it: "It's shockingly snappy for the size. I expected a toy; I got a tool." The 256GB storage is double the base model's 128GB, and worth the upgrade if you store files locally rather than in the cloud.

The **10.5-inch PixelSense display** is bright and crisp at 1920x1280 resolution, with good color accuracy for a device in this class. But here's the honest tradeoff: split-screen productivity is tight. Two apps side-by-side means each gets roughly 5 inches of width — workable for email plus a browser, but cramped for comparing documents. This is a single-window-focused device, not a multitasking monster.

**Build quality feels premium.** The magnesium alloy body has that satisfying, solid Surface heft without being heavy. The kickstand is sturdy at any angle, from 15 degrees for writing to nearly flat for presentation mode. The 3:2 aspect ratio is noticeably better than 16:9 for document viewing — you see more vertical content without scrolling.

**Battery life is the biggest gap between marketing and reality.** Microsoft claims 12.5 hours; real-world mixed use gets you 8-9 hours. That's still a full workday, just not a full workday plus commute. One reviewer noted: "Battery drains faster than expected when using Chrome. Edge gets closer to the advertised numbers." If you're a Chrome loyalist, plan for a mid-afternoon charge.

**Where it falls short:** The single USB-C port means you'll need a dongle for HDMI output or external storage. The front-facing 5MP camera is fine for Zoom, but the rear 8MP camera is forgettable — no one should buy this for photography. And while the N200 handles everyday tasks well, video editing or large data analysis will make it sweat.

**Who should buy this?** The Surface Go 4 shines for three specific buyers: students who need full Windows for school software but don't want a 3-pound laptop; field workers (medical, real estate, retail) who need a durable, light device for data entry and client meetings; and travelers who want a real computer, not a tablet-with-keyboard-workaround. It's also a solid secondary device for professionals who already have a desktop workstation.

**Skip it if** you need serious processing power, plan to do heavy multitasking on a single screen, or expect to work a full day away from an outlet with heavy usage. The iPad Air and base MacBook Air are better for battery life and raw performance — though they cost significantly more and don't run full Windows.

**Three questions shoppers actually ask:**

*Can this replace my laptop?* Yes, for light-to-moderate office work, web research, email, and media consumption. No for video editing, programming, or large-scale data work. The Type Cover is essential — don't skip it.

*Is 8GB RAM enough?* Yes, for this processor class. The N200 isn't fast enough to benefit from 16GB in most workloads. You'll hit the processor ceiling before the RAM ceiling.

*Does it run all Windows software?* Yes, unlike the older Surface Pro X with ARM. This is x64 architecture, so legacy applications, custom business software, and even older games run natively.

**The Verdict:** The Surface Go 4 is the best ultra-portable Windows tablet you can buy under $700. It nails the fundamentals — build, display, and enough performance for real work — while making compromises you can live with if you know them upfront.

**Buy it if** you need full Windows in the lightest possible package and can work within the single-screen, single-port constraints.
**Skip it if** battery life is your top priority or you need to multitask across multiple apps simultaneously.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely capable tool that's held back by Microsoft's decision to sell the keyboard separately and its optimistic battery claims. The hardware itself earns the score; just budget for the accessories and lower your battery expectations.
