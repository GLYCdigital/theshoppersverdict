## The "Stick PC" That Actually Works — Mostly

Look, I've tested a dozen of these HDMI-stick computers over the years. Most are e-waste with a Windows logo slapped on. This one is different — but not perfect. Here's the honest breakdown for anyone considering a palm-sized PC for their desk.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the stick itself (about the size of a USB-C hub — roughly 4.5 inches long, 2 inches wide, 0.8 inches thick), a 12V/2A power adapter, an HDMI extension cable (thankfully — the stick's HDMI port is tight against some monitors), and a basic user manual. No keyboard, no mouse. The aluminum-and-plastic body has a small LED indicator and a full-size USB 3.0 port on the side, plus a microSD card slot for expansion.

## Real-World Performance: The J4125 Reality Check

The Intel Celeron J4125 is a 4-core, 4-thread processor clocked at 2.0GHz (bursts to 2.7GHz). In 2026, that's entry-level. But here's the thing: with 8GB of RAM and 256GB of storage, this stick handles what most people actually do at a desk.

I ran it through a typical office day: 8-10 Chrome tabs (Gmail, Google Docs, Slack, a couple of news sites), a Word document, and Spotify streaming. It held up. There's occasional hesitation when switching tabs, but nothing that'll make you throw it against the wall. The 8GB RAM is the saving grace — 4GB sticks are practically unusable with Windows 11's background processes eating 3GB alone.

The 256GB eMMC storage is the weak link. It's not a true SSD — it's flash memory soldered to the board. Booting takes about 25 seconds from cold, and copying a 2GB file runs at roughly 140MB/s. That's fine for documents and spreadsheets. It's not fine for video editing or large database work.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs:

1. **Thermal throttling under sustained load.** The passive cooler (no fan) means the J4125 gets hot. Running a YouTube 4K video for 30 minutes, the CPU clocks drop from 2.7GHz to around 2.2GHz. Not catastrophic, but noticeable.

2. **No DisplayPort, no VGA.** HDMI only. If your monitor is older than 2015, you'll need an adapter.

3. **The power adapter is bulky.** It's a 12V brick, not a USB-C power delivery adapter. Slightly annoying for travel.

4. **Windows 11 on 8GB RAM is tight.** It works, but don't expect to run Photoshop and Chrome simultaneously. You'll be in swap territory.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home office workers** who need a second computer for email, web browsing, and document editing — without the clutter of a tower.
- **Digital signage users** who want a cheap, silent Windows box to drive a display.
- **Students** who need a portable PC that fits in a backpack pocket and can plug into dorm monitors.
- **IT admins** looking for a low-cost thin client for remote desktop sessions.

**Skip it if** you're a gamer, video editor, or anyone who runs multiple heavy applications simultaneously. Also skip if you need to connect to Ethernet — there's no RJ45 port, though you can grab a USB-to-Ethernet adapter for about $10.

## FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Ask

**Q: Can this run Windows 11 Pro?**
A: Yes, but it ships with Windows 11 Home. If you need Pro for Remote Desktop or BitLocker, you'll need to upgrade via Settings (about $99) or buy a Pro license key separately.

**Q: Does it support 4K output?**
A: The Intel UHD Graphics 600 supports 4K at 30Hz over HDMI 1.4. It's fine for video playback, but the desktop will feel slightly laggy at 4K. For daily work, stick to 1080p.

**Q: How much RAM does Windows 11 actually use at idle?**
A: About 3.2GB. That leaves roughly 4.8GB for your apps. It's workable, but close the apps you're not using. And definitely disable startup programs in Task Manager.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a genuinely silent, low-power Windows PC for office tasks, web work, or as a media center — and you value the compact form factor. The 8GB RAM and 256GB storage are the right baseline for 2026.

**Skip it if** your workflow demands real processing power or you're tempted by cheaper 4GB models. Those are false economy.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — solid for its niche, held back by dated storage and thermal limits. For around the price of a mid-range keyboard, it's a legitimate little workhorse that disappears behind your monitor. Just manage your expectations about what a 15-watt stick PC can do.
