The HP 49X is the cartridge that keeps the LaserJet 1320 alive. If you're still running one of these workhorse printers — and plenty of small offices are, because they refuse to die — this high-yield toner is the difference between a sensible printing setup and a money pit.

**What you actually get**

The box contains a single black toner cartridge, model Q5949X, designed specifically for the LaserJet 1320, 1320n, 1320tn, and 1320nw series. HP rates it for 10,000 pages at 5% coverage. That's roughly double the standard 49A cartridge's 5,000-page yield. The cartridge itself weighs about 2.5 pounds — it's a substantial piece of hardware, not a flimsy plastic shell. As shown in the product image, the cartridge has the standard HP form factor with the integrated drum and the orange protective tab you'll need to remove before installation.

**Performance and real-world use**

Installation is the usual HP affair: pull the tab, slide it in, run a couple of test pages. The smart chip registers immediately, so you won't get the "genuine HP toner" warning that plagues third-party cartridges. Print quality is consistent — solid black density, no streaking, no ghosting on standard office documents. I've seen these cartridges push well past their rated yield in low-coverage environments, which is good news if you're mostly printing text-heavy contracts and invoices.

Here's what surprised me: the fade-out curve. Most toners start going gray in the last 10-15% of their life. The 49X holds density remarkably well until the cartridge is genuinely done. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I've been using these for years in my 1320 and they always produce crisp pages right up until the toner level hits zero." That matches my experience — you get what you pay for.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme in the 215 ratings (4.3 average) is reliability. Buyers mention "no jams, no smudging, no compatibility issues" repeatedly. Another common thread: the page yield is genuine. One reviewer said, "I print about 300 pages a month and this cartridge lasts me almost three years." That's the math that makes the higher upfront price tolerable.

**Where it falls short**

The cost is the obvious sticking point. You're paying a significant premium over the standard 49A cartridge, and if you're only printing a few hundred pages a month, that high-yield premium is wasted. The high-yield cartridge only makes financial sense if you're actually running close to the 10,000-page rating. Additionally, HP doesn't include a prepaid return label for recycling in the box. You'll need to hunt down a local office supply store that accepts toner cartridges or pay for shipping to HP's recycling program yourself.

**Who should buy this**

This cartridge is for the small office, the law firm, the medical billing office — anywhere the LaserJet 1320 is still churning out documents daily. If you're printing 500+ pages a month, the 49X will save you money per page and cut your cartridge-swap frequency in half. If you print a hundred pages a month, buy the standard 49A and save your cash.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this cartridge work with the LaserJet 1320nw?**
A: Yes. The Q5949X is compatible with the entire 1320 series: 1320, 1320n, 1320tn, and 1320nw. Same cartridge, same installation.

**Q: How do I know when to replace it?**
A: The printer will alert you when toner is low, and the HP smart chip tracks actual usage. When the cartridge is empty, the printer will stop and prompt you to install a new one — no guessing.

**Q: Is there a cheaper generic alternative?**
A: Yes, but expect more frequent print-quality issues, potential chip errors, and the risk of the printer refusing to recognize the cartridge. With a printer this old, the genuine cartridge saves you troubleshooting time.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're running a 1320 series printer in a high-volume environment and want consistent quality without the headache of third-party cartridge failures.

**Skip it if:** Your print volume is low — the standard 49A is a better value at a lower upfront cost.
