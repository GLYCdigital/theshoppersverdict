**The Criss Cross Chair with Flip-Up Arms** is the answer to a question you probably didn't know you had: What if your office chair let you sit the way you actually sit at home — legs folded, posture relaxed, but still getting work done?

Priced at $99.99 with a 4.5-star average across 38 Amazon ratings, this chair occupies a niche that's been underserved for years. The traditional ergonomic chair market is saturated with options that force you into a rigid 90-90-90 position. This one says: sit however feels natural.

---

## What you actually get

The box contains the chair fully assembled — no Allen wrench archaeology required. You get the padded seat base, backrest, and the signature flip-up arms. The upholstery is a breathable mesh-fabric blend that doesn't trap heat, which matters more than you'd think during summer work-from-home sessions.

The flip-up arms are the headline feature. They pivot upward a full 90 degrees, locking into place when you need them and flipping out of the way when you don't. This serves two practical functions: you can sit cross-legged without the armrests digging into your thighs, and you can slide the chair completely under a low desk without repositioning the arms.

---

## Real-world performance

I spent two weeks using this as my primary work chair at a low standing desk (set to 27 inches). The 14.5-inch seat height paired well with that setup. The high-density foam seat is firm but forgiving — one Amazon reviewer noted, "I've had this chair for 6 months and it still feels as supportive as day one. No sagging, no flattening."

The non-slip rubber base does exactly what it claims. On hardwood floors, the chair stayed put even during active typing sessions. No creeping, no sliding.

The backrest offers a gentle recline that follows your natural spine curve when sitting cross-legged. But here's the honest tradeoff: there's no lumbar support. If you're 6 feet or taller, you'll feel pressure on your lower back after about two hours. Multiple reviews flag this. One buyer wrote, "Love the concept but my back started hurting after 3-hour gaming sessions. I added a small lumbar pillow and it solved the issue."

---

## Where it falls short

The most significant limitation is the height. This is a floor chair, period. If you're working at a standard 30-inch desk, you'll be looking up at your monitor at an awkward angle. You need either a low desk, a coffee table setup, or a standing desk cranked down to its minimum height.

The flip-up arms are sturdy but not infinitely adjustable. They lock fully up or fully down — there's no intermediate position. And while the mesh fabric breathes well, it's not removable for washing, so protect it from spills.

## Who should buy this

This chair is a fit for specific buyers:

- **Cross-legged sitters** who've never felt comfortable in traditional office chairs
- **Meditators or floor-desk workers** with a low table setup
- **Gamers** using floor-level TV setups who want arm support that gets out of the way
- **Small-space dwellers** who need a chair that tucks completely under furniture

Skip it if you need a tall office chair, require built-in lumbar support, or work at a standard-height desk.

## FAQ

**Can I use this at a regular office desk?**
Not comfortably. The 14.5-inch seat height works best with desks at 26–28 inches. Standard 30-inch desks will force you to reach up, causing shoulder strain.

**Are the flip-up arms durable?**
Yes. The hinge mechanism uses a metal locking pin, and reviewers report no looseness or failure after months of daily use. They flip up and down with a satisfying click.

**Is it suitable for heavy users?**
The frame supports up to 250 pounds per the manufacturer. Reviews from users in the 200–240 pound range report no creaking or instability.
