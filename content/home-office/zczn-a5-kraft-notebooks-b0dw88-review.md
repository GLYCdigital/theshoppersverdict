Let's cut through the noise: the ZCZN A5 Kraft Notebook has 235 Amazon ratings sitting at 4.7 stars, and after digging into the reviews and the specs, most of that praise is earned. But it's not perfect. Here's what you need to know before you add it to your cart.

**What you actually get**

The package includes a set of three A5 notebooks, each running 120 pages (60 sheets). The kraft cardstock cover gives them that utilitarian, no-nonsense look — think Moleskine's rugged cousin, not a fancy boutique journal. Each notebook has an elastic closure, a ribbon bookmark, and a back pocket for loose receipts or business cards. The elastic band and pen loop are functional, but they're the weakest physical components — more on that below.

**How it performs in real use**

The 80gsm paper is the headline feature here. For context, that's the same weight as Leuchtturm1917's paper and significantly thicker than Moleskine's 70gsm. I tested this with a Pilot Metropolitan fountain pen (fine nib) and a Sharpie gel pen. The fountain pen had zero bleed-through — just light ghosting on the reverse side, which is normal for this paper weight. The gel pen didn't ghost at all. If you're a heavy ink user or a fountain pen enthusiast, this is a legitimate budget option that performs like notebooks twice the price.

The lay-flat binding is another genuine win. You can open the notebook fully flat and write across the entire spread without wrestling the spine. For left-handed writers, this matters — you won't feel like you're fighting the book's natural bend. The paper has a slight tooth to it, so it's not slick like Rhodia, but it's pleasant to write on with both ballpoint and rollerball pens.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the Amazon reviews, three themes keep coming up. First, durability: multiple reviewers mention tossing these in bags and backpacks for months with the covers staying intact. One reviewer wrote, "I've carried this in my work bag every day for three months and the cover still looks new — no bent corners." Second, the paper quality gets consistent shoutouts, especially from fountain pen users who are notoriously picky about bleed-through. Third, the value: at this price point for three notebooks, you're paying roughly half of what a single comparable notebook costs from bigger brands.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the flaws. There are no page numbers or an index — if you use notebooks for reference material or a bullet journal, you'll need to number pages yourself. That's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

The elastic closure is the bigger concern. A few long-term reviewers noted it stretches out after a few months of heavy use. It won't snap, but it loses tension, meaning the notebook doesn't stay securely closed in a bag. The back pocket is also a bit shallow — a standard business card fits, but anything larger tends to slide out.

And one more thing: the kraft cover, while durable, does show scuffs and marks over time. Some people love that "lived-in" look; if you want a pristine notebook cover, this isn't it.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students** taking lecture notes who want durable notebooks that survive a semester in a backpack
- **Fountain pen users** on a budget who refuse to compromise on paper quality
- **Bullet journalers** who don't mind hand-numbering pages and want multiple notebooks for different projects
- **Professionals** who prefer a rugged, no-frills notebook for daily notes over something that looks pretty on a desk

**Skip it if** you need numbered pages out of the box, want a hardcover for writing on your lap without a desk, or you're looking for a notebook with a smooth, coated paper finish.

**FAQ**

**Does the 80gsm paper work with alcohol-based markers?**
No. Alcohol markers will bleed through. This paper is designed for pen and light water-based ink, not heavy marker use. If you're an illustrator, look elsewhere.

**How does it compare to Moleskine?**
Better paper (80gsm vs 70gsm means less ghosting), more durable cover, and significantly cheaper. The Moleskine wins on brand recognition and has a slightly more refined feel, but functionally this is a better value.

**Is the A5 size actually standard?**
Yes — 5.8 x 8.3 inches, which fits standard A5 covers and sleeves. If you use a notebook cover or planner system, this will slot right in.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a rugged, fountain-pen-friendly notebook that won't fall apart in your bag and costs less than half of what premium brands charge. This is the best value A5 notebook in its price range.

**Skip it if** you need numbered pages, prefer a hardcover for writing on the go, or you're a heavy marker user who needs bleed-proof paper.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The paper quality and durability punch far above the price point. The missing page numbers and the eventual elastic wear keep it from a perfect score, but for most notebook users, this is a no-brainer purchase.
