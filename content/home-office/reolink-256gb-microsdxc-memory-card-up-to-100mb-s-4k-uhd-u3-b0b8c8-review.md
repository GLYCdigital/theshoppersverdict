The REOLINK 256GB microSDXC card is built specifically to avoid those failures — and at 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it's clearly doing something right.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package is minimal: just the microSDXC card itself. No SD adapter, no plastic case, no USB reader. That's worth noting — if you plan to pull footage and view it on a laptop with a standard SD slot, you'll need to supply your own adapter. The card itself is a standard microSDXC form factor, so it fits any dash cam, security camera, drone, or smartphone that accepts microSD.

## Performance Where It Counts

The headline spec is "up to 100MB/s" read speed, but that's not what matters for continuous recording. What matters is sustained write performance, and this card holds a U3 and V30 rating. In plain English: it's certified to maintain at least 30MB/s sequential write speed, which is the floor for smooth 4K UHD video. In real-world use with security cameras that write small clips repeatedly (the workload that kills budget cards), the REOLINK holds up without dropping frames or corrupting segments.

One thing that stands out from customer feedback: repeated mentions of reliability in continuous-recording scenarios. One buyer noted, "Been running this in my Reolink doorbell for 6 months, 24/7 recording, not a single corrupted file." Another user with a dash cam confirmed it handles "summer heat in the car without throttling." That's the durability story that matters — thermal tolerance and write endurance are what separate a good surveillance card from a disposable one.

## Where Buyers Are Consistently Impressed

Across the 229 ratings, a clear pattern emerges: people who bought this for Reolink cameras (obviously) but also for Wyze, Ring, and Eufy devices report smooth 4K recording and easy setup. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the limitations. The 100MB/s read speed means transferring large video files to your computer takes noticeably longer than with premium cards that push 170MB/s+. If you're regularly pulling hours of footage for review, you'll feel that difference. The lack of an SD adapter is a minor annoyance but a real one for laptop users. And while the card is rated for endurance, REOLINK doesn't publish the same detailed TBW (terabytes written) specs that SanDisk provides — so you're trusting the brand's track record rather than hard numbers.

## Who Should Buy This

This card is a no-brainer if you're building out a Reolink camera system — it's literally what the hardware was designed to work with. It's also a strong pick for anyone running a dash cam or home security camera that records 4K continuously and needs reliable 256GB capacity without paying premium-brand prices. If you're a photographer shooting burst RAW on a mirrorless camera, skip this — you want the faster read/write of a V60 or V90 card. And if you need to swap footage to a laptop constantly, factor in the cost of a microSD-to-SD adapter.
