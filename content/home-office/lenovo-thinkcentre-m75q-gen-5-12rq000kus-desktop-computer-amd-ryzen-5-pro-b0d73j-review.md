The Lenovo ThinkCentre M75q Gen 5 is the desktop equivalent of that coworker who never says much but gets everything done. It sits there, quiet and unassuming, roughly the size of a hardcover novel (about 7 inches square and 1.4 inches thick), and runs circles around towers twice its price. At 5.0 stars from 250 Amazon buyers, the consensus is clear — but let's dig into whether it's right for *your* setup.

## What's Actually in the Box

Lenovo ships this with the M75q Gen 5 unit itself (model 12RQ000KUS), a 135W external power brick, a VESA mounting bracket with screws, and a standard US power cord. No keyboard, no mouse, no monitor. You're bringing your own peripherals. The VESA bracket is a genuinely thoughtful inclusion — it bolts to the back of most monitors, turning the whole PC into a zero-footprint setup. One buyer noted: *"Tucked it behind my monitor and honestly forgot it was there for a week."* That's the experience Lenovo is selling.

## Performance: Small Box, Real Muscle

The heart here is the AMD Ryzen 5 Pro 8500G processor — a 6-core, 12-thread chip with Zen 4 architecture. In plain terms: this thing handles 20+ browser tabs, a spreadsheet with 50,000 rows, video calls, and background antivirus scans simultaneously without breaking a sweat. The integrated Radeon 740M graphics are fine for 1080p video and light photo editing, but they are not gaming silicon. If your workflow involves 4K video timelines or CAD rendering, look elsewhere.

The 16GB of DDR5 RAM (upgradeable to 64GB) and 512GB M.2 NVMe SSD round out a config that feels responsive from cold boot — typically under 15 seconds to a usable desktop. Wi-Fi 6E and Bluetooth 5.3 are built in, and the port selection is generous for a 1-liter chassis: two USB-C, four USB-A, HDMI, DisplayPort, and a wired gigabit Ethernet jack.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Across the 250 ratings, three themes dominate. First, the silence — one reviewer wrote: *"I expected a jet engine in a tiny box. It's genuinely whisper-quiet at idle."* Second, the performance-to-size ratio: *"My old OptiPlex tower is now a doorstop. This runs circles around it."* Third, the build quality — the metal top cover and reinforced chassis feel like business-grade hardware, because it is. This is a commercial ThinkCentre, not a consumer plastic box.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The integrated graphics are the ceiling — no expansion slot, no discrete GPU option, ever. If you buy this expecting to game or render, you'll be disappointed. The single populated M.2 slot means upgrades require replacing the existing SSD, not adding a second. And there's a 2.5-inch SATA bay inside, but Lenovo doesn't include the cabling kit, so adding a second drive means a separate purchase. Finally, under sustained load — think 4K video encoding or heavy compile jobs — the fan becomes audible. Not obnoxious, but not silent.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're setting up a home office, a small business workstation, or a clean desk environment where space matters. It's also ideal for IT managers deploying fleets — the VESA mount, tool-less access, and commercial-grade reliability (3-year warranty is standard) make it a no-brainer. **Skip it if** you need GPU power, plan to run heavy local AI workloads, or want a single box that also plays games at 1440p.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Can I upgrade the RAM myself?** Yes. The bottom panel slides off with one screw, and both SODIMM slots are accessible. 10 minutes, zero special tools.

**Does it support dual monitors?** Yes — HDMI and DisplayPort are both on the rear, and the USB-C ports can drive additional displays with the right adapters.

**Is this better than a mini PC from a brand like Beelink or Minisforum?** For reliability and support, yes. Lenovo's commercial warranty, BIOS updates, and build quality justify the premium. For raw specs per dollar, the no-name brands sometimes win on paper — but you're gambling on longevity.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Lenovo ThinkCentre M75q Gen 5 is the smart buy for anyone who wants desktop-class performance without the desk-hogging tower. It's not flashy, it won't game, and it demands you bring your own peripherals — but it's quiet, fast, upgradeable, and backed by a company that actually answers the phone. For the office, the home workspace, or the tidy desk enthusiast, this is the mini PC to beat.

**Buy it if** you value space, silence, and reliability over raw graphics power. **Skip it if** your work demands a discrete GPU. Everyone else: this is the one.
