Let's be blunt: most budget highlighters are garbage. They bleed through paper, dry out in a month, or the color looks like someone sneezed on the page. The Duchong Highlighter Markers 8-pack isn't that. At roughly a dollar a marker, it punches well above its price class — with a couple of caveats you need to know before buying.

**What's in the box**

You get eight markers in assorted colors: yellow, pink, orange, green, blue, purple, and two shades I'd call "mint" and "peach" — not the standard rainbow. Each marker has a dual tip: a chisel end for broad highlighting and a fine point for underlining or margin notes. That's a feature usually reserved for $3+ markers from Stabilo or Sharpie. The body is hexagonal, which stops them rolling off your desk — a small thing, but it tells you someone thought about daily use.

**Real-world performance**

Here's where Duchong surprises. The ink is water-based but dries fast — about three seconds on standard copier paper. I tested it on 80gsm printer paper with a Pilot G2 gel pen and a standard ballpoint. No smearing on either. Let it dry fully and you can even run a mild highlighter over fresh ink without pulling the line apart.

The chisel tip holds its edge well over a month of daily note-taking. The fine tip is genuinely useful for annotating textbooks or color-coding a planner. Color payoff is strong on the neon shades — the green and blue are vivid enough to make your notes pop. The pastel yellow and pink are lighter, more "gentle highlight" than "screaming attention." That's fine for reading, less ideal for exam cramming.

One thing that impressed me: no bleed-through on 80gsm paper. Even the brightest green didn't ghost to the next page. That's rare at this price point.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 235 ratings, the phrase "better than expected" shows up repeatedly. One verified buyer wrote: "I bought these for my nursing school study guides and they've lasted two full semesters. The colors are actually distinct — not like those cheap packs where everything looks orange." Another noted: "The fine tip is the reason I bought these. I can underline in my legal textbooks without highlighting entire sentences."

The bulk value is the other consistent theme. Multiple reviewers mention buying two or three packs to keep one at home, one at the office, and one in a go-bag.

**Where it falls short**

The caps. This is the recurring complaint. They fit snugly when new but loosen within a few weeks. One reviewer said they lost three caps in a single week just from carrying the markers loose in a backpack pocket. If you're a toss-everything-in-a-drawer person, buy a pencil case or accept that you'll lose caps.

The other issue is longevity if you're careless. Leave one uncapped overnight and it's noticeably drier by morning. A week of that and it's dead. Premium markers survive that kind of neglect; these don't. Also, the pastel shades are under-pigmented compared to the neons — if you're a color-coder who needs every shade equally bold, look elsewhere.

**Who should buy these**

- **Students** who burn through highlighters and want bulk color variety without the premium price
- **Office workers** who need a desk set plus a travel set — buy two packs and still spend less than one premium brand
- **Planner addicts** who want the fine tip for precise color-coding without buying separate pens

**Skip these if** you need archival-grade ink that survives decades, or if you're a heavy user who can't remember to cap markers between uses.

**FAQ**

**Do these work with fountain pen ink?**
Mostly no. Water-based fountain pen ink can smear if you highlight over it while wet. Wait 10-15 seconds after writing and you're usually fine, but pigment-based fountain inks are safer.

**Are the colors actually different from each other?**
Yes — five of the eight are clearly distinct. The peach and pink are close, and the mint is a lighter cousin of the green. Don't expect eight wildly different shades.

**Can I refill them?**
No, they're disposable. But at roughly a dollar per marker, replacement cost is minimal.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want functional, dual-tip highlighters that look and perform like markers twice the price. The value is undeniable, the colors are genuinely useful, and the fine tip is a legit productivity feature.

**Skip it if** you're rough on your supplies, need equal pigment across all shades, or demand archival quality. For everyone else — this is the smart buy.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the flimsy caps and uneven pastel pigment, but earns every point back on value and performance.
