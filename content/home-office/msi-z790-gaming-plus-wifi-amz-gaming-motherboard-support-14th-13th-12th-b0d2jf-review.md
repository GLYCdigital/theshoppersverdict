Let's cut through the spec-sheet noise. The MSI Z790 Gaming Plus WiFi-AMZ is the board you buy when you want 14th-gen Intel performance without paying the "flagship tax." At 4.1 stars across 204 Amazon ratings, it's not perfect — but it might be the right kind of imperfect for your build.

**What's actually in the box**

MSI keeps the bundle lean: the board, two SATA cables, a Wi-Fi antenna, M.2 screws, and the usual I/O shield (pre-attached, thank goodness). No USB drive with drivers — you'll download those from MSI's site. The board itself is a standard ATX footprint (12 x 9.6 inches), so it fits most mid-tower cases without drama.

**Performance where it counts**

The 14+1+1 Duet Rail VRM design is the star here. I've seen reviews from builders running i7-14700K and i9-14900K chips with modest air coolers, and the board holds stable clocks during Cinebench runs. One Amazon reviewer noted: *"Upgraded from a B660 board and immediately noticed better stability in CPU-heavy titles like Star Citizen."* That tracks — the VRM thermal solution includes extended heatsinks that actually do their job.

DDR5 support goes up to 7800MHz+ with overclocking, but here's the practical takeaway: most buyers will land at 6000-6400MT/s kits, and the board handles those with XMP enabled without drama. Memory context restore works reliably — boot times stay around 15-20 seconds, not the 60-second agonies of early DDR5 boards.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The M.2 implementation gets consistent love. Three PCIe 4.0 slots with Shield Frozr heatsinks mean your Gen4 NVMe drives won't thermal throttle during long transfers. One reviewer summed it up: *"Installed two 2TB Gen4 drives and both run 10°C cooler than my old board with bare slots."*

The Wi-Fi 6E + 2.5G LAN combo is genuinely useful. The included antenna has a magnetic base that sticks to your case, and signal strength is solid through one wall. Bluetooth 5.3 pairs with controllers instantly — no driver hunting.

**Where it falls short**

The single PCIe 5.0 x16 slot is the biggest limitation. If you're planning a dual-GPU setup or want a high-bandwidth capture card alongside your graphics card, this board forces you to choose. It's also worth noting that M.2 slot 3 shares bandwidth with the PCIe slot — installing a drive there drops the GPU slot to x8. For most gamers, that's a non-issue (x8 vs x16 costs 2-3% FPS at most), but it's worth knowing.

The Realtek ALC897 audio codec is the other notable weakness. It's fine for gaming headsets, but if you've got a decent pair of open-back headphones or studio monitors, you'll notice the lack of clarity compared to boards with better codecs. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"Audio is serviceable but nothing special — I use a USB DAC anyway."*

**Who should buy this**

- **Intel 14th-gen upgraders** with a 12th or 13th-gen chip — the BIOS flashback feature saves you from needing a compatible CPU first
- **Mid-range builders** pairing this with a 14600K or 14700K and a single high-end GPU
- **Wi-Fi dependent users** who need solid wireless without buying a separate adapter

**Skip it if** you're chasing maximum audio quality, need multiple PCIe 5.0 slots, or you're on a tight budget — there are cheaper B760 boards that handle 90% of what this does, minus the overclocking headroom.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this board support Intel 12th-gen CPUs out of the box?**
A: Yes, with a caveat. The board natively supports 12th, 13th, and 14th-gen chips, but a BIOS update may be needed for full 14th-gen features. Use BIOS flashback (the USB port marked on the rear I/O) to update without a CPU installed.

**Q: Will my existing DDR4 RAM work?**
A: No. This is a DDR5-only board. If you're coming from a DDR4 system, factor in the cost of new RAM — roughly $80-120 for a solid 32GB kit.

**Q: How many fans can I connect?**
A: Six fan headers total — one CPU fan, one pump header, and four system fan headers. All support PWM control via MSI Center software. For a typical mid-tower with 3-4 case fans plus a CPU cooler, you're covered without needing a fan hub.

**The Verdict**

The MSI Z790 Gaming Plus WiFi-AMZ is a solid mid-range board that does exactly what it promises — stable overclocking, fast storage, and reliable connectivity. It's not flashy, and the audio codec is a dated choice, but for the price, it's tough to beat.

**Buy it if** you're building a 14th-gen Intel rig with a single GPU and want overclocking headroom without paying flagship prices.

**Skip it if** you need premium audio or plan to run multiple PCIe 5.0 devices.
