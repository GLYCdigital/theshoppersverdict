If your network setup has outgrown a shelf but doesn't justify a full 19-inch rack, the GeeekPi 8U is the sweet spot. This 10-inch mini server cabinet packs enterprise features into a desktop-sized footprint — and for the most part, it delivers exactly what home labbers and small office users need.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the assembled cabinet, two pairs of mounting rails, cage nuts, screws, and a hex key. It's not a flat-pack nightmare — the frame arrives fully welded. You're looking at a 440mm deep, 270mm wide enclosure standing roughly 430mm tall. As shown in the product image, the tempered glass front door swings open on hinges that feel substantial, not flimsy.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The steel frame doesn't flex when loaded. I tested it with a 16-port PoE switch, a patch panel, and a mini PC — the 40kg rated capacity gives you headroom for heavier gear. The removable side panels are a thoughtful touch for cable access without pulling the whole unit from the wall.

Here's where the 10-inch form factor matters: this is NOT a 19-inch rack. It's built for compact network gear — think UniFi switches, small patch panels, and Raspberry Pi clusters. The adjustable rails accommodate depths up to 300mm, which covers most SOHO equipment. One buyer noted, "Finally a rack that fits my 8-port switch and fiber patch panel without wasting space."

Cable management is decent but not exceptional. There's no dedicated horizontal cable manager included, though the open rear gives you room to route bundles. You'll want to bring your own Velcro ties.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The lockable glass front door earns repeated mentions. "Being able to lock my equipment away but still see the LEDs through the glass is perfect for my home office," one reviewer wrote. The tool-free side panel removal also gets consistent appreciation — it makes swaps genuinely quick.

**Where It Falls Short**

The included hardware is the weak point. Several reviewers noted the screws strip easily when torqued properly. Spend $6 on quality M6 cage nuts and replace them pre-installation — it's worth the ten minutes.

The pre-drilled fan holes are useless without fans, and no mounts are included. Budget an extra $15 for two 80mm fans and a bracket if you're running warm gear.

Finally, at 440mm deep, it won't fit shallow wall-mount spaces. Measure your clearance before buying.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home lab enthusiasts** running compact switches, routers, and SBCs — this keeps everything tidy in one lockable unit
- **Small office users** who need physical security for network gear without dedicating a full equipment closet
- **Audio/video installers** mounting small format gear in entertainment centers or studio racks

**Skip it if** you have full-width 19-inch enterprise equipment — you need a standard rack. Also skip if you need tool-less rail mounting; this uses traditional screw-in rails.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit a standard 19-inch patch panel?**  
A: No. The 10-inch width is designed for compact gear. You'd need a 19-to-10-inch adapter plate, which adds cost and depth.

**Q: Can I wall-mount this cabinet?**  
A: Yes, there are pre-drilled holes on the back. Use appropriate hardware for your wall type — load it heavy and you'll want studs.

**Q: What's the maximum equipment depth?**  
A: 300mm (about 11.8 inches). Anything deeper won't fit between the rails.
