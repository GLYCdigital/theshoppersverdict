**The 140-page math that makes this bulk buy actually worth it**

Most one-subject notebooks give you 80 pages. Trail maker doubles that to 140 pages (70 sheets) per notebook. That's the headline feature here, and it changes how you shop. You're not paying for twice the notebooks — you're paying for fewer replacements mid-semester. When you're buying in bulk for a classroom, an office, or a household of students, that page count difference is the difference between restocking in October or coasting to finals.

**What you actually get**

This is a straightforward multipack of one-subject notebooks. Each measures the standard 8 x 10.5 inches — slightly smaller than letter size, which means they slide into binders and backpacks without hanging out awkwardly. The college ruling sits at 9/32-inch spacing, the tighter line pitch that fits more notes per page than wide rule. The covers are cardstock in standard colors (the exact palette varies by pack), and the binding is a simple glued spine with perforated pages for clean tearing.

**Real-world performance**

The paper handles ballpoint and standard gel pens well. Ink dries quickly, and there's no bleed-through on normal writing pressure. The college ruling is crisp and consistent — uniform line spacing across all notebooks in the pack, which matters when you're grading or reviewing notes and don't want visual inconsistency. The perforation tears cleanly along the line without ragged edges.

The tradeoff: this is lightweight paper. If you're a heavy-handed writer or prefer Pilot G2s and similar wet-ink pens, you'll see ghosting on the reverse side. It's not bleed-through — you can still read both sides — but it's visible. And the cardstock covers are exactly that: cardstock. They're not chipboard. Toss one loose in a crowded bag and the corners will curl.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the paper weight and flexible covers, there's one more thing to flag: the binding. It's glued, not sewn. That means if you're aggressive about folding notebooks back on themselves to write one-handed, the spine will eventually crack and pages can detach. For typical note-taking — notebook lying flat on a desk — it's fine. For students who write in cramped lecture halls holding notebooks at odd angles, it's a risk.
