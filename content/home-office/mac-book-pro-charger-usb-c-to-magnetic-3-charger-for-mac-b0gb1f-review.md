**Is this the MagSafe 3 charger Apple should have shipped?** 

Let’s be blunt: paying $99 for Apple's official 96W USB-C to MagSafe 3 charger feels like a tax on people who lost their cable. This third-party alternative—a braided USB-C to MagSafe 3 cable that works with your existing power brick—has earned a solid 4.4-star average across 220 ratings. After digging through the feedback and examining the specs, here's what you actually need to know before clicking buy.

**What you get in the box**

This is a cable-only product. It's a 2-meter (6.6-foot) braided nylon cable with USB-C on one end and the magnetic MagSafe 3 connector on the other. You'll need your own USB-C Power Delivery (PD) charger rated at 60W or higher. The packaging is minimal—cable, a velcro strap, and a thin manual. Some buyers were caught off guard by the lack of a brick, so set your expectations now.

**Real-world performance and build quality**

The connector snaps onto your MacBook Pro with a satisfying click. The alignment is forgiving—you don't need to line it up perfectly, and the magnets pull it into place. That's the core value proposition: if someone trips over the cable, it detaches cleanly instead of ripping your laptop off the desk or damaging the USB-C port. The braided sheath is a genuine upgrade over Apple's smooth rubber, which tends to crack and fray after a year of daily abuse.

Charging speed is where you need to pay attention. On a MacBook Pro 14-inch with a 96W USB-C brick, this cable delivers full speed—you'll hit 0-50% in about 30 minutes. However, if you own a 16-inch MacBook Pro, this cable will not deliver the maximum 140W over MagSafe. It tops out around 96W, which means slightly slower top-end charging. For most users this is irrelevant, but power users should know the limitation.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the recurring theme. One verified buyer wrote: "I already had a 65W GaN charger for my phone. This cable let me ditch Apple's overpriced brick entirely. Works flawlessly." Another reviewer highlighted durability: "My cat chewed through my original Apple cable in three weeks. This braided one has survived six months of the same cat."

Connectivity reliability also gets consistent praise. Multiple reviewers note the magnetic connection stays secure even with a laptop on their lap or in bed—no random disconnects that plagued cheaper magnetic cables.

**Where it falls short**

The magnet is slightly weaker than the genuine Apple MagSafe 3 connector. Most users won't notice, but if you're using your laptop on an uneven surface like a blanket, the connector can detach more easily than the original. A handful of reviewers also mentioned the LED indicator on the connector is dimmer than Apple's—it's visible, but you'll squint in bright rooms.

The biggest frustration is the charging speed ceiling. If you're on a 16-inch MacBook Pro, this cable won't unlock the full 140W potential. That's a real compromise for a premium machine.

**Who should buy this**

- **The budget-conscious professional** who already owns a USB-C PD charger and refuses to pay Apple's markup
- **The accident-prone** who wants the magnetic breakaway safety without the premium price tag
- **Anyone replacing a frayed cable** — the braided build will outlast the original

**Skip it if** you have a 16-inch MacBook Pro and demand maximum charging speed, or if you expect a complete charging solution out of the box.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Will this work with a standard USB-C phone charger?**  
A: Only if your charger outputs at least 60W. Lower-wattage chargers will trickle-charge or not charge at all while the laptop is in use.

**Q: Does it work with the MacBook Air M2/M3?**  
A: Yes, perfectly. The Air only draws 45-67W anyway, so the 96W ceiling is irrelevant.

**Q: Is data transfer supported?**  
A: No. This is charge-only. You cannot use it to connect external displays or transfer files.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, reliable MagSafe 3 cable at a fraction of Apple's price and already own a suitable USB-C charger. **Skip it if** you need 140W charging or want a complete, brick-included solution.

This cable does exactly what it promises: it replaces a $99 Apple accessory with a $25 alternative that's arguably better built. The magnet quibble and power ceiling are acceptable tradeoffs for the price. It earns a solid **4.2/5** from me.
