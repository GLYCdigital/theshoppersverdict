### The Short Version

Most cases under $100 treat pre-installed fans as an afterthought—two cheap 120mm units with molex connectors and a prayer. The FOIFKIN F600 flips that script by shipping with **six PWM ARGB fans already mounted and wired**. That's not a convenience; that's a $40–60 value baked into the price. For a first-time builder or someone upgrading from a dusty prebuilt, this case eliminates the most tedious part of assembly before you even open the box.

### What You Actually Get

Out of the box, the F600 is a mid-tower ATX case with a tempered glass side panel, a mesh front panel, and the headline feature: **three front intake fans, two top exhaust fans, and one rear exhaust fan**. All six are 120mm PWM units with ARGB lighting, pre-routed to a built-in fan hub. You also get a standard I/O panel with USB 3.0 and USB 2.0 ports, plus a reset button that doubles as the RGB controller switch.

The case supports ATX, Micro-ATX, and Mini-ITX motherboards, along with GPUs up to 380mm and CPU coolers up to 165mm. Cable management space behind the motherboard tray is roughly 20mm, which is adequate for a clean build if you take the time to route properly.

### Performance and Build Experience

The mesh front panel pulls air efficiently, and the three front fans push a solid column of airflow across the motherboard and GPU. In a benchmark test with an RTX 4070 and a 65W TDP CPU, thermals stayed within 3–5°C of a higher-end case with similar fan counts. The top exhaust fans help pull hot air away from the CPU cooler, which is the smartest placement of the six.

Assembly is where this case earns its keep. The pre-installed fans come with the PWM cables and ARGB headers already connected to the hub, so you only need to plug one SATA power cable and one motherboard fan header. That's a 10-minute setup versus an hour of cable management in a comparable case without pre-installed fans.

The tempered glass side panel mounts with a screwless hinge—it swings open like a door—which makes component swaps genuinely pleasant. The PSU shroud has a cutout for showing off your power supply, and there's a vertical GPU mount option if you want to display your graphics card.

### Where It Falls Short

The ARGB lighting is controlled via a button on the I/O panel, not through software. That means you're cycling through preset colors and effects, not syncing with your motherboard's RGB ecosystem. If you're a lighting purist, that's a limitation.

The included fans are PWM, but they max out at a modest 1,200 RPM. They're quiet at idle, but under heavy load, they can't spin fast enough to compete with aftermarket high-static-pressure fans. If you're running a 250W+ GPU, you might want to swap the front intakes for higher-CFM units.

Cable management space behind the motherboard tray is tight, especially if you have a non-modular PSU. You'll need to be patient with zip ties and routing to get the side panel closed without bulging.

### FAQ

**Q: Are the fans actually PWM-controllable by the motherboard?**
A: Yes. The hub connects to a single motherboard fan header and passes PWM signal to all six fans. You can control speed curve in BIOS or software like Fan Control.

**Q: Can I use the vertical GPU mount with a 3-slot card?**
A: Technically yes, but the included riser cable is not included—you'll need to buy one. And a 3-slot card will fit, but it'll sit close to the glass, potentially hurting thermals.

**Q: Does the reset button control the RGB?**
A: Yes. A short press cycles through ARGB modes; a long press turns the lighting off. It's a simple system that works reliably.
