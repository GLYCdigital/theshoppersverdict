Let me be blunt: when you see "75 pack" and a price that works out to pocket change per folder, you expect flimsy junk that falls apart by mid-semester. The RAZCC two-pocket folders with prongs aren't that. But they're also not premium archival-grade material. Here's what you're actually getting.

**What's in the box**

Seventy-five folders. That's it — no inserts, no labels, no surprises. Each folder is letter-size (roughly 9.5" x 11.75") with two full-length interior pockets and a metal prong fastener in the center. The colors are assorted, which sounds fun until you realize you can't request specific hues for color-coded systems. If you need 25 red folders for one project and 50 blue for another, you're out of luck.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The material is standard 11-point paper stock — think your typical office supply folder, not cardstock. It's sturdy enough to survive a semester of abuse in a backpack or a year in a filing cabinet. The pockets are full-depth, so a complete stack of letter-size documents sits flush without peeking out the top or sides. That matters more than you'd think; cheaper folders often have shallow pockets that let papers slip out.

The prongs are the standout feature here. They're metal, which is rare at this price point. Push the tabs apart, slide in up to roughly 30 sheets of standard 20-pound paper, and fold the prongs flat. They hold tight — no paper tearing at the punch holes, no prongs popping open unexpectedly. One buyer noted, *"I've bought the cheap plastic-prong folders before and they snap within a week. These metal ones have survived two full semesters."* That matches what I'd expect from the construction.

**Where it falls short**

The paper stock is the weak point. It's fine for filing, organizing, or handing out paperwork. It is not fine for folders you'll pull in and out of a briefcase daily or stuff into overstuffed bags. Corners will bend, edges will fray. For heavy daily use, spend more on poly or heavier-weight options.

The prong placement also limits you. They're at the top edge, which means documents load vertically. If you're left-handed or prefer side-opening folders for desk work, this design will annoy you. It's a minor thing, but it's worth knowing before you commit to 75 of them.

And yes, the random colors. If you don't care about color coding, ignore this. If you do, buy single-color packs instead.

**Who should buy these**

- **Teachers**: This is the sweet spot. You need dozens of folders for student work, parent communications, and lesson plans. 75 is a full school year's supply at a fraction of the cost of buying 10-packs.
- **Office managers**: Stock the supply closet without blowing the budget. These are perfect for daily filing where folders get used and replaced.
- **Students**: Workable for a single semester, especially if you're on a tight budget. Just don't expect them to survive four years.
- **Event organizers**: Handouts, registration packets, name tags — these are cheap enough to treat as disposable.

**Skip these if** you need archival-grade protection for important documents, if you're building a color-coded system, or if your folders will see heavy daily handling in a briefcase. This is a bulk utility product, not a premium one.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will the prongs damage documents?**
A: No. The metal prongs fold flat against the paper and don't tear punched holes if you load 30 sheets or fewer. Overstuff them and you'll get stress marks around the holes.

**Q: Are these heavy-duty enough for daily work use?**
A: Borderline. They'll hold up to regular filing and retrieval. They won't survive being tossed into a work bag daily for months. Treat them as semi-disposable workhorses.

**Q: Do the pockets hold 8.5" x 11" paper without sticking out?**
A: Yes. The pockets are full-length, so letter-size documents sit completely inside. Legal-size paper will overhang.

**The verdict**

The RAZCC 75-pack is a strong value for anyone who burns through folders in bulk. The metal prongs and full-depth pockets punch above the price point, and 75 units means you won't reorder mid-year. The tradeoff is thinner paper and no color control.

**Buy it if** you need a semester's or a fiscal year's worth of folders at the lowest cost per unit, and you're okay with utilitarian build quality.

**Skip it if** you need color-coded organization, archival durability, or folders that survive constant bag travel.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does exactly what it promises at a price that makes the minor flaws easy to forgive.
