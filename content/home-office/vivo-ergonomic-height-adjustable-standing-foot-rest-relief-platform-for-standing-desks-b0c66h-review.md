## VIVO Ergonomic Height Adjustable Standing Foot Rest Review: The Fix for Aching Legs at a Standing Desk?  

You know that burn in your calves 40 minutes into a standing shift? The shifting weight, the subtle bounce, the eventual slump onto one hip? A foot rest won’t make standing *fun*, but the VIVO Ergonomic Height Adjustable Standing Foot Rest Relief P targets the root cause: static posture. With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it’s clearly helping a lot of people. But is it the right fix for *your* setup? Let’s break it down.

### What You Actually Get

The box contains the steel platform, a top pad, the base, and six bolts with a hex wrench. No surprises. Assembly is straightforward but not tool-free — you’ll spend about 10 minutes bolting the two-piece frame together. The top surface measures roughly 17.5” x 11.5”, which is enough for both feet side-by-side or one foot angled.

The headline feature is the height adjustment: 12 preset positions ranging from 3.5” up to 17.5”. That’s a wide range — short users can get a proper 90-degree knee bend, while taller folks can use it as a true leg prop, not just a foot wedge. The locking pin slides into pre-drilled holes, so there’s no guesswork about leveling.

### Real-World Performance

I’ve tested this under a standard 29” standing desk and a 40” counter-height setup. At the lower settings, it behaves like a classic footrest — you alternate feet, shift weight, and feel the pressure leave your lower back. Crank it to the middle range (around 10-12”), and it becomes a leg support for a high stool or a leaning post for one leg. The rubber top pad grips socks and bare feet well; it’s not grippy enough to catch a shoe sole awkwardly, but it won’t slide around either.

The steel frame is the standout. It’s rated for 110 lbs, and it feels like it. I leaned a good portion of my weight on it while reaching for a monitor and it didn’t flex or tip. The base has four rubber feet that stay put on hardwood and carpet. One buyer described it as “sturdier than I expected for the price,” which matches my experience.

### What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in reviews is the height range. One verified purchase wrote: “I’m 5’2” and finally my feet rest flat instead of dangling. The 17-inch max height means I can actually use it with my tall husband’s desk.” Another noted the stability: “No wobble, even when I shift my weight aggressively. Feels like furniture, not a gadget.”

The non-slip surface gets consistent shout-outs too. Multiple reviewers mention that the pad doesn’t peel or wear after months of daily use, unlike cheaper foam alternatives.

### Where It Falls Short

Two real tradeoffs. First, there’s **no tilt adjustment**. The surface is flat, period. If you want an angled rocker to stretch your ankles or a wedge to elevate heels, this isn’t it. You get vertical height only. Second, the **assembly is slightly fiddly**. The bolts are small and you need to align the frame pieces perfectly before threading. It’s a one-time annoyance, but a few reviews mention stripped threads if over-tightened — go easy with the wrench.

Also note: the top pad is removable and washable, but it’s thin (about 0.5”). If you have sensitive heels or wear hard-soled shoes, you might want a gel pad on top. That’s a minor quibble, not a dealbreaker.

### Who Should Buy This

- **Short users at fixed-height desks**: If your feet dangle or you’re constantly on your toes, this gets you to a proper 90-degree angle.
- **Standing desk converts**: Anyone who alternates between sitting and standing will find the wide range useful for both positions.
- **Leaning workers**: If you prop one foot up while reading or typing, the high settings (12-17”) work as a solid leg rest.

**Skip it if** you need an angled foot rocker for ankle mobility, or if you want something you can kick under a low desk and forget — this is a visible, structured piece of gear.

### FAQ

**Q: Will this fit under a standard 29-30 inch desk?**  
A: Yes, at the lowest setting (3.5”) it slides under most desks easily. The base is about 2” thick, so clearance is fine.

**Q: Can I use it with shoes on?**  
A: Yes. The rubber top grips both bare feet and shoe soles. It’s slightly less grippy with hard-soled dress shoes, but it won’t slip off the platform.

**Q: Is the height adjustment easy to change during the day?**  
A: Yes — pull the lock pin, slide the top to a new hole, release. Takes about 3 seconds. No tools needed once assembled.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a stable, height-adjustable footrest for a standing desk and you’re okay with a flat surface. The 12-position range and 110-lb steel frame make it more versatile than most $30-40 alternatives.

**Skip it if** you specifically want tilt or rocking motion, or if you want zero assembly. This is the right tool for static foot placement, not dynamic ankle movement.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It earns the score honestly. The build quality and height range are excellent; the lack of tilt and minor assembly friction keep it from being perfect. For the price, it’s a solid upgrade over a cardboard box or a stack of books.
