## Real-World Performance

I ran this through a week of heavy use with a Dell Precision 5570. The adapter held a steady 20V/4.5A output during a 4K video render that pegged the CPU at 100% for 45 minutes. The brick got warm — noticeable to the touch but far from alarming. No throttling, no power drops, no flicker on the laptop's charge indicator.

The 6-foot USB-C cable is thicker than most — Dell uses 20AWG wire for the power lines, which is why it doesn't get warm even at full draw. The strain relief at both ends is properly molded, not just a heat-shrink tube over a weak joint. This is where cheap third-party adapters fail within months; the OEM design holds up.

One thing to note: this is a *charger*, not a dock. It won't drive external displays or transfer data. If you need those features, look at a USB-C hub with power delivery instead.

## Where It Falls Short

The non-detachable USB-C cable is my biggest gripe. If the cable gets chewed by a pet, pinched in a drawer, or develops an internal break, you're buying a whole new adapter. Dell could have made this cable detachable like the AC cord — they chose not to.

The lack of a USB-A passthrough port is also a miss. Competing adapters from Anker and Baseus offer a 5V/2A USB-A port for charging a phone simultaneously. This Dell unit gives you nothing extra.

And yes, you'll pay the OEM tax. This adapter typically runs $15–25 more than a comparable third-party unit. For a genuine OEM part with proper thermal management and a 2-year warranty, that's defensible — but it's still a premium.
