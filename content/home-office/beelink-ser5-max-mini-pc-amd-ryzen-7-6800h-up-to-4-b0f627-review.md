The Beelink SER5 MAX is the mini PC that makes you question why you ever bought a tower. With AMD's Ryzen 7 6800H and the Radeon 680M integrated graphics, this 4.5-star compact desktop punches well above its size class. At roughly 4.6 inches square and 1.6 inches tall, it's roughly the footprint of a paperback novel — but it delivers performance that rivals desktops twice the price.

**What's Actually in the Box**

Beelink ships the SER5 MAX with the unit itself, a 19V/4.74A power adapter (90W total), an HDMI cable, and a VESA mounting plate with screws. You're getting the bare essentials — no keyboard, no mouse, no frills. The aluminum alloy chassis feels premium and acts as a heatsink, helping dissipate the 45W TDP of the Ryzen 7. Around back you get dual HDMI 2.0 ports, dual 2.5G Ethernet jacks, two USB 3.2 Gen 2 ports, and a USB-C port that supports DisplayPort alt mode. Front-facing, there's a USB-C, a USB 3.2, and a 3.5mm audio jack.

**Performance That Surprises**

The 8-core/16-thread Ryzen 7 6800H boosts up to 4.7GHz, and in Cinebench R23 multi-core tests, owners report scores around 12,500–13,000 — that's solidly in mid-range desktop territory. But the real story is the Radeon 680M iGPU with 12 compute units. Unlike most mini PCs that struggle with anything beyond basic video playback, this one handles esports titles like Overwatch 2, Valorant, and Rocket League at 1080p with 60+ fps on medium-high settings. Even heavier games like Cyberpunk 2077 run at playable 30–40 fps with FSR enabled. For an office machine, this is overkill in the best way.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The networking setup gets frequent shoutouts. As one verified buyer put it: "The dual 2.5G LAN ports are a game-changer for my home lab — I run a NAS and a VM server simultaneously without bottlenecking." Another noted the thermals: "Idle temps sit around 40°C, and even under stress it stays under 85°C. The fan is quiet during normal office work." Multiple reviewers also highlight the upgradeability — the bottom panel pops off with no tools, and you can swap the M.2 SSD and two SODIMM RAM slots in minutes.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The fan is audible under load — not obnoxious, but you'll hear it during video encoding or gaming. The included 512GB SSD (a PCIe 3.0 drive) fills up fast, especially if you install modern games that routinely hit 100GB each. There's a second M.2 slot for expansion, but you'll need to budget for it. Also, the cooling solution uses liquid metal thermal paste, which is great for thermals but means you shouldn't disassemble the CPU heatsink unless you know what you're doing. And while the USB-C port supports video output, it's not Thunderbolt — don't expect eGPU support.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Remote workers and hybrid employees** who need a quiet, powerful desktop that vanishes on a desk
- **Home lab enthusiasts** who want dual 2.5G networking for NAS, Plex, or Proxmox setups
- **Casual gamers** who play esports titles and want one small machine for both work and play
- **Anyone downsizing** from a bulky tower who doesn't want to sacrifice performance

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can it drive three monitors?**  
A: Yes. Dual HDMI plus the USB-C port (with DP alt mode) gives you three simultaneous 4K displays at 60Hz. Great for trading setups or productivity workflows.

**Q: Does it support Windows 11?**  
A: It ships with Windows 11 Pro pre-installed. TPM 2.0 is enabled by default, and the Ryzen 7 6800H is fully supported.

**Q: How does it handle 4K video playback?**  
A: The Radeon 680M has dedicated video decode engines for H.265, VP9, and AV1. 4K YouTube or local files play flawlessly with negligible CPU usage.

**The Verdict**

The Beelink SER5 MAX is one of the best value mini PCs available right now. The Ryzen 7 6800H provides desktop-class compute, the integrated graphics surprise everyone who dismisses them, and the dual 2.5G networking puts it ahead of most competitors. The fan noise under load and the modest 512GB SSD are the only real compromises — and both are manageable.

**Buy it if** you want a small, powerful, upgradeable desktop that handles office work, light gaming, and home server duties without breaking a sweat.

**Skip it if** you need silent operation under sustained load, require Thunderbolt for eGPU expansion, or must have more than 512GB of storage out of the box.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A superb compact desktop that earns its place on any desk.
