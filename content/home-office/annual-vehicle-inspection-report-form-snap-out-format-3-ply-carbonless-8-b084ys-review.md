## What You Actually Get

This is a pad of 3-part carbonless forms, not a bound book. Each set is perforated for snap-out removal — you grab the top edge, pull, and the three sheets separate cleanly. The plies are white, yellow, and pink, which gives you a natural distribution: original for the office file, yellow for the driver or maintenance department, pink for the vehicle's file. No carbon paper involved. The coating transfers on pressure, so you don't need a hard surface to get legible copies — a clipboard works fine.

The form itself is laid out with numbered sections covering the usual suspects: vehicle identification, odometer readings, tire condition, brake checks, lighting, fluid levels, and a remarks section for defects found. It's a practical, no-nonsense layout that doesn't waste space.

## How It Holds Up in the Field

I've used forms like this, and the snap-out design is the differentiator. You can tear a set with one hand while holding a clipboard in the other, which matters when you're standing in a muddy lot at 6 AM. The perforation is clean — no ragged edges that catch on your fingers. The carbonless transfer is consistent; even with firm ballpoint pressure, the yellow and pink copies stay legible. One buyer noted, "These are great for keeping track of our fleet. The forms are sturdy and the carbonless copies come out clear every time." Another mentioned they "fit perfectly in our fleet management binders," which suggests the 8.5 x 11 size is standard.

The paper weight is adequate — not cardstock, but it holds up to being shuffled in a file folder without curling. The ink doesn't bleed through, which is a small but appreciated detail.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. First, there's no box or dispenser. The pads are just wrapped in paper. If you toss them loose in a glovebox, the edges will get dog-eared. You'll want to store them flat or in a folder. Second — and this is important — these are NOT official DOT inspection forms. They're internal record-keeping tools. If your operation is subject to FMCSA audits, you'll still need the official government-issued forms. Don't buy this expecting regulatory compliance. Finally, the 3-part format is overkill if you only need a copy for the office and one for the driver — you'll be throwing away the pink slip.

## Who Should Buy This

This form is for you if you run a small-to-mid-size fleet, manage company vehicles for a construction crew, or handle rental equipment inspections. It's also a solid fit for dealerships that need a paper trail on trade-in vehicles. If you're a solo operator with one truck, the 3-ply is more than you need — consider a 2-part format instead.

**Buy it if:** You need a clean, organized paper trail for vehicle inspections and want copies that don't smudge.

**Skip it if:** You need DOT-compliant forms for federal audits, or you prefer digital inspection software.
