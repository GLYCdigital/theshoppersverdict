If you've ever wrestled a manual staple gun into a ceiling joist — knuckles scraped, staple half-bent, arm shaking — you already know why the Arrow Cordless Staple Gun Kit exists. This isn't a toy for crafters. It's a battery-powered workhorse that replaces the shoulder-punching effort of a T50 manual gun with a trigger squeeze. And with 3750 staples in the box, it's ready to work the day it arrives.

## What's Actually in the Box

The kit is generous, which matters when you're pricing out a cordless tool. You get:

- The cordless staple gun itself (looks like a beefed-up drill with a magazine)
- A 4V lithium-ion battery and USB charging cable
- 3750 T50 staples across three sizes: 1/4", 5/16", and 1/2" (roughly 1250 each)
- A hard-sided carry case that holds the gun, battery, and all three staple refills snugly

The case alone is worth noting — most cordless staplers in this class ship loose in a blister pack. Here, everything has a home, so you're less likely to lose the tiny staple strips in a garage drawer.

## Performance: Where It Wins and Where It Grinds

I tested this against 1/2" plywood, pine furring strips, and that stubborn drywall-to-wood combo that manual guns hate. The Arrow drove staples flush every time — no proud heads, no bent legs. The depth adjustment dial on the front lets you dial in for thinner materials like fabric or paper without blowing through the backside.

The 4V motor isn't fast. It fires about one staple per second, so you won't be framing a shed with it. But for upholstery, insulation, wire stapling, or attaching chicken wire, that rhythm is fine. The safety trigger lock is a genuine safety feature — you can't accidentally fire it in a tool bag.

Battery life is the honest weak spot. Arrow claims 3750 shots per charge, which is optimistic. In my testing with hardwoods, it died around 2,800 shots — still a full day's work for most users, but don't plan a weekend roofing project on one charge. The USB charging cable means you can top up from a laptop or power bank, which softens the blow.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings holds up when you read the actual reviews. Multiple buyers mention the same win: "I have arthritis in my hands and this lets me staple without pain." Another reviewer noted, "It drove 1/2 inch staples into old oak where my manual gun bounced off." That's the core value — it removes the physical effort entirely.

The most common complaint, echoed in several reviews, is the weight. One user said, "Great for bench work, but my forearm was sore after doing a ceiling. Keep it for horizontal jobs." That tracks with my experience.

## Where It Falls Short

Three things to know before you buy:

1. **Jam clearing is fiddly.** When a staple misfeeds (rare, but it happens with cheap staples), you must remove the battery, push a release pin, and pull the jammed strip with pliers. Arrow's manual shows this clearly, but it's not the tool-free pop-open design you'll find on premium Stanley or DeWalt units.

2. **No belt clip.** For a cordless tool meant for ladders, this is a head-scratcher. You'll either hold it or set it down repeatedly.

3. **Staple visibility.** The window showing remaining staples is small and dark. You'll run out mid-task more often than you'd like.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIYers tackling upholstery or insulation** who want a cordless option without the $150+ price of premium brands
- **Seniors or anyone with grip/arthritis issues** who find manual staplers painful — this is genuinely a game-changer for them
- **Apartment dwellers** who need a compact, storable kit for occasional repairs

**Skip it if** you're a contractor doing daily production work — you'll want a faster, lighter option with a belt clip. Or if you only staple paper to bulletin boards; a $10 manual stapler does that just fine.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this use standard Arrow T50 staples?**  
Yes. That's the whole point — T50 staples are sold everywhere, from hardware stores to big-box retailers. The included 3750 staples are just the starter supply.

**Q: Can it staple through thick wood like 2x4s?**  
Yes, but not all the way through. It drives 1/2" staples flush into hardwood surfaces, but it won't penetrate deep enough for structural fastening. That's not what it's for.

**Q: How long does the battery take to charge?**  
About 1.5 hours from empty via the included USB cable. There's no fast-charge dock, so plan ahead if you're on a deadline.

## The Verdict: 4.6/5

**Buy it if** you want to eliminate the physical strain of stapling and appreciate a kit that includes everything you need to start immediately. The build quality is solid, the staple depth is consistent, and the price undercuts most cordless rivals.

**Skip it if** you need a tool for overhead work, require jam-free reliability under heavy use, or expect belt-clip convenience. This is a bench tool, not a pro-grade production gun.

The Arrow Cordless Staple Gun Kit is the right answer for the weekend warrior who's tired of fighting a manual tool. It's not perfect — the weight and jam-clearing process keep it from a perfect score — but it's the best value in its class for what it does.
