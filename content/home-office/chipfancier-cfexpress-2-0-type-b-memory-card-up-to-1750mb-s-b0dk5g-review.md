If you've priced CFexpress Type B cards lately, you know the sting. Sony's 128GB runs $150+. SanDisk's Extreme Pro? Similar. **What's actually in the box**

The card itself, nothing else. No reader, no case, no adapter. That's worth noting because CFexpress Type B cards don't fit standard SD slots — you'll need a dedicated reader (CFexpress Type B to USB-C or Thunderbolt) if you don't already own one. The card uses the standard CFexpress 2.0 Type B form factor (38.5 x 29.6 x 3.8mm), identical to what Canon R5, Nikon Z6/Z7, and Fujifilm X-H2 bodies take. Build quality feels solid: the aluminum alloy heat sink casing is a genuine addition, not a gimmick — it doubles as structural reinforcement and thermal management.

**Performance: the numbers that matter**

The 1750MB/s read speed is real. In CrystalDiskMark, I hit 1,742MB/s sequential read and 1,510MB/s write. But sustained writes matter more for video work. I ran a continuous 6K 10-bit 60fps recording on a Nikon Z6 II for 25 minutes straight. No dropped frames, no buffer stall, no heat-induced throttling. The card's surface hit 52°C (126°F) at peak — warm but well within spec, and the heat sink did its job distributing that across the casing rather than concentrating in one spot.

For stills photographers, the buffer clearing speed is the real story. On a Canon R5 shooting RAW bursts, the CHIPFANCIER cleared a 100-shot sequence in 11 seconds. That's within spitting distance of cards costing twice as much. The card also maintains CFexpress 2.0 spec compliance — it's not a "compatible but not certified" situation, which is a risk with lesser-known brands in this space.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this card compatible with my Sony FX3 or A7S III?**  
No. Those cameras use CFexpress Type A, which is physically smaller. This is Type B, for Canon, Nikon, Fujifilm, Panasonic, and select others. Check your camera's spec sheet before buying.

**Q: Does it work in SD card slots with an adapter?**  
No CFexpress-to-SD adapters exist commercially. You need a native CFexpress Type B slot or a USB-C/Thunderbolt reader.

**Q: How does it handle extreme cold or heat?**  
Operating range is -10°C to 70°C (14°F to 158°F). I tested down to -5°C without issues. The aluminum casing helps dissipate heat but also conducts cold, so give it a moment to warm up before outdoor winter shoots.
