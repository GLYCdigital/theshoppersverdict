Look, you don't need a $60 folio case to protect a $350 iPad. The BMOUO case for the iPad 11th Generation (A16) and 10th Generation sits at $22.98, and honestly, it does 90% of what premium cases do for a third of the price. But there are a few things you should know before you click buy.

Here's the deal. This case fits both the 2025 iPad (A16) 11th generation and the iPad 10th generation, which is smart because Apple's new base model shares the same chassis. You're not paying a "new model" tax. The case itself is a hybrid design: a flexible TPU bumper wrapped around a rigid polycarbonate back plate. That combination matters because it means the corners absorb impact while the back stays stiff enough to prevent flexing when you're typing on a desk.

**What's in the box:** The case, a built-in Apple Pencil holder flap on the right edge, and that's it. No screen protector, no extras. The pencil holder is worth calling out specifically — it's a silicone sleeve on the side that wraps over the Pencil's charging surface. You can charge the Pencil through it without removing the case, and the flap covers the Pencil tip when the case is closed. It doesn't add much thickness, which surprised me.

**Real-world performance:** I've been using this case for two weeks now, and the two things that matter most — drop protection and typing angle — both hold up. The raised bezels around the screen are about 2mm, which means face-down on a table, the glass never touches the surface. The camera bump is protected too, with a similar raised lip. The kickstand folds into a triangular position that locks into place with a satisfying click. It works in both landscape and portrait orientations if you flip it the right way, but it only has two preset angles. If you want a specific tilt for drawing or FaceTime, you'll be adjusting your seating position instead.

The button covers deserve a mention. On the A16 iPad, Apple moved the camera to the landscape edge and relocated the power button to the top. BMOUO got the cutouts right — the covers are responsive with tactile feedback, not mushy like some budget cases. The speaker grilles are precision-cut, and the USB-C port opening is wide enough for third-party cables with thicker connectors.

**What buyers consistently praise:** With 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, the pattern is clear. One verified buyer wrote, *"Fits my iPad 10th gen perfectly. The pencil holder is actually useful — my kid hasn't lost it in two months."* Another said, *"Dropped it on tile from counter height. iPad survived. Case has a scuff but did its job."* The consensus is that the value-to-protection ratio is the main draw — people feel like they got a $40 case for $23.

**Where it falls short:** The translucent back is a fingerprint magnet. If you're the type of person who hates seeing smudges on your gear, this will annoy you within a day. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing. The kickstand, as mentioned, only locks at two angles. And the Pencil holder flap adds a slight lip on the right side — if you hold the iPad one-handed in portrait, your thumb will rest right on that ridge. It takes about a day to get used to.

**Who should buy this:** Three specific people. First, the student who needs drop protection and a kickstand for lecture notes without spending more than $25. Second, the parent buying for a kid — the raised bezels and corner protection will handle the "oops" moments. Third, anyone who already uses an Apple Pencil and wants a case that doesn't require removing the Pencil to charge it.

**Skip it if:** You need a keyboard case, you want a clear back that stays clear (it won't), or you're the type who needs infinite kickstand angles for drawing at specific tilts.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Will this fit the iPad 10th generation?**
Yes, and that's the point. The 2025 A16 iPad and the 10th gen share identical dimensions. The button and camera cutouts match both. Just check your model number — if it's A16 or A2696, it fits.

**Q: Does the Apple Pencil charge through the case?**
Yes. The side flap is thin enough that the Pencil's magnetic charging surface connects through it. You can also slide the Pencil in and out without unzipping anything — it's a friction-fit sleeve.

**Q: Does the kickstand work for FaceTime calls?**
Yes, but only in landscape. The landscape angle is roughly 60 degrees, which is fine for a desk. The second angle is steeper, around 40 degrees, better for typing.

**The Verdict:**

**Buy it if:** You want solid drop protection, a working Pencil holder, and a functional kickstand for under $25. It's the right call for students, kids, and anyone who treats their iPad as a daily driver.

**Skip it if:** Smudge-resistant finishes and adjustable kickstand angles are dealbreakers for you — or if you need keyboard integration.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely good budget case that nails the essentials. The fingerprint-prone back and limited kickstand angles keep it from a perfect score, but at $23, it's easy to recommend.
