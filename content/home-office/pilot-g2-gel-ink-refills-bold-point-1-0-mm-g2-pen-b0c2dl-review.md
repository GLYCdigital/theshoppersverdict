## What's in the package

You're getting a pack of refill cartridges — typically 2 to 6 per pack depending on the listing option you choose. Each refill is a complete drop-in replacement: the twist-off cap, the ink reservoir, and the metal tip assembly come as one unit. There's no fiddly spring or collar to transfer — you just unscrew your existing G2 barrel, pull out the empty cartridge, and push the new one in until it clicks. Takes about five seconds.

The refills measure roughly 4.7 inches in length with a 1.0mm tungsten carbide ball tip. That's the bold point, and it's a meaningful upgrade from the standard 0.7mm if you prefer a more assertive line on the page.

## Performance and real-world use

The bold 1.0mm tip lays down a line that's thick, dark, and remarkably consistent. The gel ink is pigment-based, not dye-based, which means it's water-resistant once dry and doesn't fade over time. On standard copy paper, the ink dries in about 2-3 seconds — acceptable but not instant.

The flow is where these refills shine. The gel formula is engineered to start writing immediately, even after the pen has sat capped in a drawer for weeks. No skipping, no hard starts, no dragging. The 1.0mm tip glides with a smoothness that feels closer to a fountain pen than a ballpoint.

One thing worth noting: the bold point does use more ink per word, which is physics — a wider line consumes more gel. But Pilot compensates with a larger ink reservoir in these refills. You'll get roughly 1,800 meters of writing distance from a single bold refill, which is substantial.

## Where it falls short

Be honest about the tradeoffs. First, dry time. On glossy or coated paper — think presentation covers, photo paper, or most business cards — the bold 1.0mm gel takes noticeably longer to set. Left-handed writers who drag their hand across the page will smudge. That's a real limitation, not a nitpick.

Second, color selection is limited. Most multipacks offer black and blue, with red occasionally available. If you want purple, teal, or the full G2 color spectrum, you'll need to drop down to the 0.7mm refills.

Third, the bold point isn't for everyone. If you write small, take notes in tight margins, or fill out forms with limited space, the 1.0mm line will feel oversized. This is a preference issue, but it's worth knowing before you buy a six-pack.

## FAQ

**Do these refills fit every Pilot G2 pen?**
Yes. Pilot uses a standardized refill across the entire G2 line — metal barrels, plastic barrels, the G2 Pro, and even the G2 Limited. If it says G2 on the side, these refills will fit.

**How long does one refill actually last?**
Pilot rates the bold 1.0mm at approximately 1,800 meters of writing. In practical terms, that's roughly 30-40 pages of dense note-taking, or 2-3 months of daily use for an average writer.

**Is the bold 1.0mm good for left-handed writers?**
Only if you write slowly or use a hand-over-hand grip. The gel formula takes 2-3 seconds to dry on standard paper and longer on coated stock. If you're a lefty who drags your palm, stick with the 0.5mm or use quick-dry ink alternatives.
