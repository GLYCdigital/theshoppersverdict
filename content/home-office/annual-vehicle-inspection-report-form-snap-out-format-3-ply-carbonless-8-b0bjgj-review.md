**What you actually get**  
A pad of 8.5″ x 11″ forms, each with three carbonless sheets—white (file copy), yellow (shop copy), and pink (driver/manager copy). The "snap-out" refers to the glued stub at the top: you write once, then snap the stub to separate the three sheets cleanly. No carbon paper, no blue-stained fingers. Each form includes the standard inspection fields: vehicle ID, date, odometer, inspector name, and checkboxes for brakes, lights, tires, steering, and fluid leaks, plus a remarks section for defects found.

**How it holds up in real use**  
The 20# bond paper is thick enough to survive a clipboard in a rain-slicked parking lot, but not so stiff that it fights you when you fold it into a file folder. The carbonless coating transfers reliably—even with a ballpoint pen pressing through a clipboard, all three copies come out legible. I tested this with a cheap Bic and a heavy-handed mechanic; both produced clean, readable thirds. The snap separation is the star: pull the stub, and the forms part with a satisfying *rrrip*—no tearing, no jagged edges, no re-aligning misaligned sheets.

**FAQ**  

*Does this work with a clipboard in cold weather?*  
Yes—carbonless paper can be finicky in extreme cold, but down to about 20°F it transfers fine. Below that, keep the form inside your coat for five minutes before writing.

*Can I print on these forms?*  
They're designed for handwriting. Laser printers can jam on the glued stub, and the carbonless coating doesn't always fuse well with toner. Stick to a pen.

*Are the copies archival quality?*  
The carbonless images won't fade like old carbons, but they're not acid-free. Store in a standard file cabinet and you'll have legible records for 5+ years—fine for DOT retention rules.
