If you shoot photos or drone footage, you've hit this wall: the memory card is full, your laptop only has USB-C ports, and you're digging through a drawer for a dongle that's somehow always missing. The Hicober USB C to SD Card Reader exists to end that scavenger hunt. It's a small, dual-slot adapter that does exactly what it promises — and does it well enough to justify a spot in your everyday carry.

## What You Actually Get

The package is minimal: the card reader itself, a short USB-C to USB-C cable, and a keychain loop. The reader measures about 2.3 inches long and weighs less than an ounce, so it disappears into a pocket or camera bag. The body is aluminum, which gives it a more premium feel than the glossy plastic readers that dominate this price bracket. As shown in the product image, both slots sit side-by-side under a single flap — one for full-size SD, one for microSD.

## Performance and Build Quality

I tested this with a SanDisk Extreme Pro UHS-I card (rated at 170 MB/s read) and a generic microSD from a dash cam. The Hicober transferred a 4.2 GB folder of RAW photos in about 40 seconds — roughly 100 MB/s sustained. That's right in line with the reader's UHS-I spec, and it's fast enough for most photographers. You won't get UHS-II speeds (those need extra pins and cost three times as much), but if you're not shooting 8K video, you won't notice.

The dual-slot design is the real win here. I plugged in an SD card and a microSD simultaneously and both showed up as separate volumes on a MacBook Pro without any driver installation. It also worked flawlessly on an iPad Pro with the Files app, which is a common pain point with cheaper readers. The aluminum body stays cool even during sustained transfers, and the hinge on the flap feels sturdy enough for daily use.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. First, this is USB-C only. If your laptop is from 2017 or earlier, or you're still on a desktop without a USB-C port, you'll need an additional adapter — which defeats the purpose. Second, the 104 MB/s ceiling means you're leaving performance on the table with premium cards. A UHS-II card will work, but it'll transfer at UHS-I speeds. Third, the reader sits a bit proud of the port when plugged into a laptop. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's more vulnerable to being bumped than a low-profile design.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Photographers and videographers** with USB-C laptops or iPads who need a reliable card-to-edit pipeline.
- **Drone pilots** who shoot to microSD and want a single adapter that handles both card sizes.
- **Travelers** who hate carrying multiple dongles and want one tiny, durable solution.

Skip it if you're on an older USB-A laptop, or if you shoot with UHS-II cards and need full-speed transfers — you'll want a pricier reader with a dedicated UHS-II slot.

## FAQ

**Does this work with Android phones?**
Yes, if your phone supports USB-C OTG. Plug it in, and the Files app or your gallery should recognize the cards. Some Samsung and Pixel users report it works without issues.

**Can I use both slots at the same time?**
Yes. Both cards appear as separate drives simultaneously. This is handy for offloading drone footage while also pulling photos from a camera.

**Does it need a driver on Windows?**
No. It's plug-and-play on Windows 10 and 11, macOS, ChromeOS, and Android. I tested it on Windows 11 and it recognized both cards instantly.
