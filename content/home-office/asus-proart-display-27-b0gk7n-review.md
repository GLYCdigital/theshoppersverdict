If you edit photos or video for a living, you already know the struggle: you color-correct on a monitor, export the file, and it looks completely different on your client's screen. The ASUS ProArt Display 27" (model PA279CV) is designed to kill that problem dead. It's a 4K IPS panel that ships factory-calibrated to Delta E < 2, which is the kind of color accuracy that used to cost $1,000+ just a few years ago.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the monitor, a full-height stand, power adapter, HDMI cable, DisplayPort cable, USB-C cable, and a factory calibration report. That last piece matters — it's not a generic "this is calibrated" sticker. It's an individual test report for your specific unit showing the measured Delta E values. That's a level of quality control you typically only see on monitors twice this price.

The panel itself is a 27-inch 4K (3840×2160) IPS display with a 60Hz refresh rate. It covers 100% of sRGB and 100% of Rec.709 — the color space used for HDTV broadcast and most web content. It also hits 85% DCI-P3, which is the cinema standard, so you're covered for video projects too.

## Real-World Performance

The first thing you notice is the uniformity. ASUS uses a "uniformity compensation" feature that evens out brightness and color across the panel. On cheaper monitors, you'll see corner vignetting or subtle color shifts. Here, it's clean edge-to-edge. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"Colors are accurate and consistent. I use it for editing wedding photos and it matches my print output almost perfectly."*

The USB-C port is a standout feature. One cable carries video, data, and up to 65W of power delivery. If you have a MacBook or a modern Windows ultrabook, you plug in one cable and your laptop is both charged and connected. The monitor also functions as a USB hub with four downstream ports, so you can connect a keyboard, mouse, and external drive without a separate dock.

For workspace productivity, the 4K resolution at 27 inches gives you roughly the same pixel density as a Retina display. Text is razor-sharp, and you can comfortably fit two full documents side-by-side without squinting. The ProArt preset modes — sRGB, Rec.709, DCI-P3, and a dedicated "Darkroom" mode — are genuinely useful, not gimmicks.

## Where It Falls Short

The 60Hz refresh rate is the biggest compromise. If you're a gamer or you just enjoy buttery-smooth scrolling, you'll notice it. This is strictly a work monitor, and it doesn't pretend otherwise.

There are no built-in speakers. Some users won't care — most office setups have headphones or external speakers. But if you expected a do-everything monitor, you'll need to budget for audio.

The stand is also a desk hog. It's fully adjustable — height, tilt, swivel, and pivot — which is excellent for ergonomics. But the base is deep, and if you have a shallow desk, you'll feel it. One reviewer noted: *"The stand is massive. I had to rearrange my whole desk setup to make it fit."*

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a photographer, videographer, graphic designer, or anyone who needs accurate color for client work. The factory calibration alone justifies the price — you'd spend $150+ for a separate calibration tool to get the same result on a cheaper monitor.

**Skip it if:** You're a gamer, you only browse the web and check email, or your desk space is tight. A standard 4K office monitor with speakers will serve you fine for half the cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the USB-C port compatible with MacBooks?**
A: Yes. It delivers up to 65W of power and supports DisplayPort Alt Mode, so a single cable handles video, data, and charging for most MacBooks and Windows ultrabooks. Check your laptop's power requirements — if it needs more than 65W, you'll still need the wall charger.

**Q: Does it work with a standard HDMI cable for a PC?**
A: Absolutely. The monitor has two HDMI 2.0 ports and one DisplayPort 1.2. You'll get full 4K at 60Hz with any of them. The USB-C port is just the most convenient option if your device supports it.

**Q: How accurate is the color out of the box really?**
A: Very accurate. The included calibration report shows your specific unit's measured Delta E values — typically under 1.5, which is excellent. Most users report no need for manual calibration for sRGB work. For critical print work, a hardware calibration tool is still a good investment, but it's not required for day-to-day editing.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The ASUS ProArt Display 27" delivers genuinely professional-grade color accuracy at a price that undercuts most of its competition. The build quality is solid, the ergonomics are excellent, and the USB-C connectivity makes it a genuinely modern workspace investment.

**Buy it if** you need reliable, accurate color for professional work and want a monitor that won't betray you mid-project.

**Skip it if** you're primarily gaming, need built-in audio, or you're shopping on a tight budget and don't need 4K resolution.
