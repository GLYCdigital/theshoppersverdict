Let me be blunt: most "mini" PC cases make you choose between small size and real hardware. The Cooler Master NR200 refuses to make that trade. At just 18 liters, this thing fits a full-length RTX 4090 and a tower CPU cooler that would look at home in a mid-tower. That's not marketing talk — the 330mm GPU clearance and 155mm cooler height limit are printed right in the spec sheet.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the NR200 chassis, two 120mm SickleFlow fans pre-installed (top and rear), a riser cable if you grab the PCIe 4.0 version, and the standard screw kit. No surprises, no junk. The steel-and-plastic build feels solid — nothing flexes when you press on the panels, and the tool-less side panels pop off with a satisfying click. The mesh top and back panels are fine enough to see through, which tells you airflow was the priority here.

**How it performs in the real world**

I built a Ryzen 7 7800X3D system with a 240mm AIO mounted on the side bracket. The dual-chamber layout — motherboard on one side, PSU and drives on the other — keeps hot air separated. CPU temps stayed under 75°C under sustained load, and the GPU ran cool with the mesh side panel feeding it fresh air. The pre-installed fans move respectable air, though they're not the quietest at full tilt. Expect to hear them under gaming load, but nothing offensive.

The layout is genuinely clever. The PSU mounts on the front, the GPU sits vertically in the main chamber, and the drive cages tuck behind the motherboard tray. Cable management is forgiving for an SFF case — there are Velcro straps and routing holes in the right places. First-time SFF builders will appreciate that they don't need custom cables or a degree in cable origami.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't accidental. A common thread: "Fits way more than I expected for the size." Another reviewer noted, "Build quality is excellent for the price — the steel panels feel premium." Multiple buyers mention the tool-less design making component swaps painless, which is rare in this category.

**Where it falls short**

This is a Mini-ITX-only case. If you have a Micro-ATX motherboard lying around, it won't fit — no adapters, no workarounds. The stock fans are serviceable but not great; plan on replacing them if noise matters to you. And while GPU clearance is generous, 3-slot cards require careful planning — check your card's exact dimensions before ordering. The PCIe 4.0 riser cable version costs a bit more, but it's worth it if your GPU supports 4.0.

**Who should buy this**

- **The SFF enthusiast** who wants a compact build without sacrificing a high-end GPU
- **The first-time small-form-factor builder** who wants a case that doesn't punish mistakes
- **The LAN party regular** who needs something that fits in a backpack or carry-on

**Skip it if** you're on a budget and have an ATX motherboard — you'd need to replace the board, RAM, and possibly PSU, which adds up fast.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the NR200 fit a 360mm AIO?**
No. The side bracket supports up to 240mm radiators. For 360mm cooling, look at the NR200P Max, which comes with a 280mm AIO pre-installed.

**Q: Can I mount the GPU horizontally instead of vertically?**
Yes, the NR200 supports horizontal GPU mounting with a riser cable — the PCIe 4.0 version includes it. The standard version supports vertical mounting natively.

**Q: What PSU size do I need?**
SFX or SFX-L only. Standard ATX PSUs won't fit. Most builders grab a 650W-750W SFX unit, which is plenty for a single-GPU build.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely small case that doesn't compromise on performance or build quality. The NR200 is the rare SFF case that both beginners and veterans can love.

**Skip it if** you need ATX compatibility, want silent operation out of the box, or can't be bothered to verify GPU dimensions before purchasing.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — One of the best Mini-ITX cases available, held back only by the stock fans and the Mini-ITX-only limitation.
