**The wishacc Mobile Tablet Stand with Wheels solves a real problem: your tablet is stuck on a desk, and you're stuck at a desk. This rolling floor stand frees both.**

If you've ever tried to follow a recipe while your iPad sits propped against a spice jar, or paced around a workshop with a tablet tucked under your arm, you already know why a mobile stand makes sense. At $56.99, this isn't a budget impulse buy — it's a legitimate workspace tool. Here's what 215 Amazon reviewers (averaging 4.3 stars) and my own research tell me about whether it's worth your money.

**What's in the box**

You get the usual flat-pack situation: a weighted circular base, four caster wheels (two with brakes), three aluminum pole sections, a tablet cradle, and the hardware to join them. The cradle is the standout piece — it's a spring-loaded clamp that opens wide enough for a 12.9-inch iPad Pro but cinches down to hold a 4.7-inch phone. Rubber pads line the jaws, so you won't scratch your device's frame. As shown in the product image, the whole assembly stands about 5.4 feet tall at full extension.

**Real-world performance**

The height adjustment is where this stand earns its keep. A friction-lock collar lets you position the tablet anywhere from roughly 15 inches off the floor to over 5 feet. I've seen reviewers use it for everything from bed-bound streaming to standing desk setups. One buyer wrote: *"I use this in my kitchen for recipes and in my office for video calls. It rolls between rooms easily and the height adjustment is smooth."* That flexibility is the core value proposition, and it delivers.

The wheels deserve specific credit. They're 2-inch casters with a 360-degree swivel, and two have brakes that actually hold. On carpet, the stand rolls without snagging; on hardwood, it glides quietly. The base is weighted with a steel plate, which keeps the center of gravity low enough for normal use.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be direct about the flaws. Assembly is fiddlier than it should be — the pole sections use a friction-fit connection that requires serious torque to tighten. You'll need the included hex key, and you'll probably curse it once or twice. Multiple reviewers mention this. One wrote: *"Took 20 minutes to assemble and I had to re-tighten after a week of use."* Plan for that.

The bigger issue is stability at full extension. If you're using a large tablet and you tap the screen aggressively — think typing on a virtual keyboard — the stand wobbles. The base is weighted, but it's not a sandbag. At lower heights, it's rock solid; at maximum height, it's acceptable but not confidence-inspiring. Also, the cradle is fixed in orientation. To switch between portrait and landscape, you loosen the clamp and physically rotate the tablet. There's no 360-degree swivel joint on the cradle itself.

**Who should buy this**

This stand is ideal for three specific people: the home cook who wants recipes at eye level without counter clutter, the chronic video-call participant who wants to stand during meetings, and the hobbyist or maker who needs a tablet visible while working with their hands. It's also genuinely useful for someone who's bed-bound or recovering from surgery and needs a tablet positioned without holding it.

Skip it if you need a rock-solid mount for heavy stylus work or drawing — the wobble at full extension will frustrate you. Also skip it if you need a cradle that swivels; this one doesn't.

**FAQ**

**Will it hold an iPad Pro 12.9 with a thick case?** Yes, up to about 0.8 inches of thickness. The spring clamp has enough travel, but the rubber pads compress, so a bulky OtterBox-style case might be snug.

**Can I use it with a Kindle or small phone?** Yes, the cradle closes down to 4.7 inches. A phone is usable, though the stand is oversized for that purpose.

**Does it work on carpet?** The wheels roll on low-pile carpet fine. On thick, plush carpet, the brakes struggle to hold — you'll want a hard surface for the locking wheels to bite.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, mobile tablet station for under $60 and can tolerate minor assembly fuss. **Skip it if** you need rock-solid stability for precision work or a cradle that rotates independently.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid value with honest limitations. The wishacc stand does its core job well, and the mobility is genuinely useful. Just don't expect a professional-grade mount at this price point.
