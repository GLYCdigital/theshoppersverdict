## The $60 ITX Case That Actually Gets Airflow Right

Most sub-$70 ITX cases are either cramped ovens or flex-ATX nightmares. The Jonsbo C6 Mesh breaks that pattern — it's a compact 17-liter tower that fits standard ATX power supplies, full-length GPUs, and tower coolers without suffocating your components. After 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the consensus is clear: this is one of the best budget ITX cases you can buy in 2026.

## What You Actually Get

The C6 Mesh ships as a bare chassis — no fans included. You get the case, a hardware bag with screws and standoffs, and a quick-start manual. That's it. The case itself measures roughly 14.2 x 7.9 x 13.8 inches (L x W x H) and weighs about 8.6 pounds. The front, both side panels, and top are all fine steel mesh, which is the whole point here.

## Real-World Performance and Build Quality

The mesh design isn't cosmetic — it's functional. Air enters from three sides and exits through the top, creating natural convection. With two 120mm intake fans on the side and one exhaust on top, my Ryzen 5 5600 build idles at 35°C and peaks around 68°C under load. That's genuinely impressive for a 17-liter case.

Build quality is solid for the price. The steel panels are 0.8mm thick — not premium, but they don't flex or rattle. The mesh is fine enough to block most dust while still moving air freely. The front I/O includes USB 3.0, USB-C, and audio jacks, which is generous at this price point.

**The catch:** The PSU mounts at the front of the case. This frees up the bottom for GPU clearance (up to 355mm), but it means your power supply intake faces the mesh front — and it'll pull dust directly in. Cable management requires patience, especially with a non-modular ATX PSU. Several reviewers noted this exact frustration.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Finally a small case that doesn't cook my GPU. My 3060 Ti runs 10 degrees cooler than it did in my old mid-tower."* That sentiment echoes across the ratings — airflow is the star feature here.

Other recurring praise points:
- **Tool-less side panels** that actually work (magnets hold them securely)
- **GPU clearance** that handles even chunky 3-slot cards
- **Clean aesthetics** — the mesh looks premium, not cheap
- **Price-to-feature ratio** — nobody expects USB-C at this price point

## Where It Falls Short

**No included fans.** This is the biggest gotcha. You'll need to spend an extra $20-30 on decent 120mm fans, which pushes the real cost closer to $80-90. A pair of Arctic P12s or Noctua redux fans will do the job.

**PSU cable routing is tight.** The front PSU mount means your 24-pin and PCIe cables have to cross over the motherboard tray. A modular or semi-modular PSU is almost mandatory. One reviewer wrote: *"Great case, but if you have a non-modular PSU, good luck. I had to buy a modular unit just to make it work."*

**Dust management.** All that mesh means all that dust. Without filters, you'll be cleaning this case every couple of weeks. Magnetic dust filters (available separately) are a worthwhile $10 investment.

## Who Should Buy This

- **First-time ITX builders** who want a standard ATX PSU compatibility to save money
- **Gamers with high-TDP GPUs** who need real airflow in a small footprint
- **Anyone downsizing from a mid-tower** who wants a smaller footprint without buying SFX components

**Skip it if** you need radiator support beyond a 240mm at the side, want a case with included fans, or can't tolerate weekly dusting.

## FAQ

**Q: Will my ATX power supply fit?**
Yes, up to 150mm depth. Anything longer will interfere with GPU clearance. Check your PSU dimensions before buying.

**Q: What GPU sizes fit?**
Up to 355mm length and 3-slot thickness. Most modern cards fit, but verify your specific model's dimensions.

**Q: Does it come with dust filters?**
No. The mesh is the only filtration. Consider aftermarket magnetic filters for the side and front intakes.

## The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy It

**Buy it if** you want a compact, well-ventilated ITX case that doesn't require expensive SFX components, and you're okay adding your own fans.

**Skip it if** you're on a strict $60 budget (fans push the real cost higher), need liquid cooling options, or hate cleaning dust.

The Jonsbo C6 Mesh is the rare budget case that punches above its weight class. It's not perfect — the PSU placement and dust management are real tradeoffs — but at this price, with this airflow, it's the smart choice for budget ITX builds. Pair it with a modular PSU and a couple of good fans, and you've got a compact system that stays cool under pressure.
