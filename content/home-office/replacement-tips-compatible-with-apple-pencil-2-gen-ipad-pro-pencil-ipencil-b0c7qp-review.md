**The $15 Question: Can Third-Party Pencil Tips Match Apple's $25 Single Tip?**

Here's the math that makes this listing worth your attention: Apple sells one replacement tip for $25. This kit gives you six (four black, two white) for less than that. But price per unit only matters if the tips actually work. After testing these against my Apple Pencil 2 on a 12.9-inch iPad Pro, here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes six tips bundled in a small plastic case with a threaded metal connector inside each tip. The threading is the critical bit — Apple's tips screw into the Pencil's housing, and a poor thread fit means a wobbly nib or, worse, one that strips the Pencil's socket. These thread cleanly and seat flush against the barrel. No wobble, no gap, no clicking when you press down.

**Performance: The Surprising Part**

I tested these in Procreate and Notability for two weeks. The 2B-style tip (the black ones) tracks identically to Apple's standard tip — no lag, no skipped strokes. Palm rejection works fine, and palm rejection depends on the tip making solid contact with the glass. Tilt shading in Procreate behaves exactly like the OEM tip. If you're a digital artist worried about losing pressure curve fidelity, the data says you won't notice a difference.

The white tips have a slightly different feel — marginally more friction against a paper-like screen protector. Some users might prefer this for handwriting. If you use a matte protector, the black tips glide smoother.

**Where It Falls Short — Read This Before Buying**

Two honest downsides:

**Durability.** The tip material is softer than Apple's. After three weeks of daily drawing (roughly 2-3 hours per day on a standard glass screen), the black tip showed visible rounding at the contact point. Apple's tips last me 6-8 weeks under the same use. You're trading longevity for cost. If you're a heavy user, you'll cycle through this six-pack in about 4-5 months. That's still cheaper than buying three Apple tips, but don't expect them to match OEM lifespan.

**Inconsistent quality control.** Two of my six tips had a faint mold seam around the upper collar. It's invisible during use, but if you're sensitive to anything irregular against your Pencil's barrel, you'll feel it. A quick pass with 400-grit sandpaper fixed it. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**FAQ**

A: These are sized for the 2nd Gen and the USB-C model. They will NOT fit the original 1st Gen Pencil — that model uses a different connector.

**Q: Do they affect the Apple Pencil's magnetic charging?**

**Q: How do I know when to replace a tip?**  
A: When you start seeing a flat spot at the contact point, or if strokes feel less responsive in drawing apps. With these, check every 2-3 weeks if you're a daily user.
