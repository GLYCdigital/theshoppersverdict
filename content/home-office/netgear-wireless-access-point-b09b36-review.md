If your office Wi-Fi dies every time the marketing team hops on a video call, you don't need another router — you need a dedicated access point. The NETGEAR WAX610 (this is the model behind the B09B36 listing) is aimed squarely at small-to-mid businesses that have outgrown their all-in-one router. After digging through 204 verified buyer ratings and the spec sheet, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the access point itself, a mounting bracket, and ceiling/wall mounting hardware. No power adapter — and that's a feature, not a bug. This AP supports PoE+ (802.3at), so a single Ethernet cable from a PoE switch powers it and carries data. If you don't have a PoE switch, you'll need to buy a separate injector (about $20-25). As shown in the product image, it's a compact white square, roughly 9.5 inches across, designed to sit flush on a drop ceiling. It's not ugly, but it's not invisible either — plan for it to be visible.

**Real-world performance**

This is a Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) dual-band access point with 4x4 MU-MIMO on both 2.4GHz and 5GHz. The spec sheet claims up to 3.6Gbps aggregate throughput, but real-world numbers are more modest. Buyers consistently report strong coverage in spaces of 2,000–3,500 square feet when ceiling-mounted. One verified reviewer wrote: "Replaced two old routers with one of these in our 2,400 sq ft office. Dead zones in the back conference room are gone. Streaming and video calls are flawless." Another noted: "The 5GHz range surprised me — I get solid signal two floors down in the warehouse."

The radio engineering is solid. Beamforming and band steering actually work — devices get pushed to 5GHz when appropriate, which keeps the 2.4GHz band clear for IoT devices and older laptops. For a 20-40 person office with typical cloud-based workloads (email, Slack, video conferencing, shared drives), this AP handles it without breaking a sweat.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- **Reliability after initial setup.** Multiple reviewers mention setting it once and forgetting it for months. One wrote: "It's been running for 6 months without a single reboot. Our old router needed weekly resets."
- **MU-MIMO actually helps.** In mixed-device environments, simultaneous streaming and browsing didn't degrade like with consumer routers.
- **VLAN support.** If you want to segment guest traffic or IoT devices, the WAX610 supports up to 8 SSIDs with VLAN tagging. IT-savvy buyers loved this.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. First, **setup isn't plug-and-play.** You'll need the NETGEAR Insight app or a web browser to configure it. If you're not comfortable with IP addresses and SSID configuration, budget 30-60 minutes and maybe a YouTube tutorial. Second, **no Wi-Fi 6E.** If you're buying in 2026, the 6GHz band is becoming standard on premium devices. This AP caps out at Wi-Fi 6 on 2.4/5GHz. It's still fast, but it's not future-proof. Third, **the plastic enclosure** feels less durable than Ubiquiti's metal housings at similar price points. It's fine for an office ceiling, but don't drop it during install.

Also note: this is an access point, **not a router.** You still need a router/firewall upstream (your existing office router works, just disable its Wi-Fi). Some buyers missed this and expected a full replacement.

**Who should buy this**

- **Small business owners** (10-40 employees) with an existing router who need reliable whole-office coverage
- **IT generalists** managing a multi-AP deployment via NETGEAR Insight (the cloud dashboard is genuinely useful)
- **Home users with large properties** who want pro-grade coverage and don't mind a slightly technical setup

**Skip it if** you want a single-box router+AP solution, need Wi-Fi 6E, or you're a renter who can't mount hardware to ceilings.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this without a PoE switch?**
A: Yes, but you'll need to buy a PoE+ injector separately (around $20-25). The AP itself doesn't ship with a power adapter.

**Q: Does it work with non-NETGEAR routers?**
A: Yes. As long as your router provides DHCP and an Ethernet connection, the WAX610 will work. Just disable your router's built-in Wi-Fi to avoid interference.

**Q: Can I manage multiple units from one dashboard?**
A: Yes, via NETGEAR Insight (subscription required for advanced features). Free local web management works for a single AP.

**The Verdict**

The NETGEAR WAX610 is a dependable, no-nonsense business access point that solves dead-zone problems without breaking the bank. It's not the flashiest or most future-proof option — Wi-Fi 6E support would make it a slam dunk — but for most small offices, it delivers rock-solid coverage and the reliability you can't get from consumer routers.

**Buy it if** you need dependable whole-office Wi-Fi and have basic networking knowledge.

**Skip it if** you want the latest Wi-Fi 6E standard or a true plug-and-play setup.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid workhorse with minor caveats.
