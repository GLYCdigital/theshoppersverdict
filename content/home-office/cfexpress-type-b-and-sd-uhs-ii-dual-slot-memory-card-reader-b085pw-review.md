If you shoot on a flagship mirrorless camera—think Canon R5, Nikon Z8, or Sony A1—you already know the pain of waiting on a cheap reader that chokes on CFexpress cards. This dual-slot unit from a brand that's been quietly making reliable card readers for years addresses exactly that. At $84.99, it's not cheap, but it's also not the $150+ pro-grade docks. Here's where it actually lands.

## What You Get

The package is minimal: the reader itself, a USB-C to USB-C cable, and a USB-C to USB-A adapter. That adapter is smart—many older workstations still lack native USB-C ports. The housing is a single piece of brushed aluminum measuring roughly 4.3 x 2.2 x 0.6 inches. It's heavier than plastic readers, which is good—it stays put on a desk when you're yanking cards in and out. The CFexpress slot uses a spring-loaded eject mechanism; the SD slot is push-push. Both feel solid, not flimsy.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this with a ProGrade Digital CFexpress 2.0 card (128GB) and a SanDisk Extreme Pro SD UHS-II. On a MacBook Pro with Thunderbolt 3, sustained writes to the CFexpress slot held around 940MB/s. That's essentially saturating USB 3.2 Gen 2's 10Gbps ceiling. More importantly, after a 20-minute offload of 4K 10-bit footage, the case was warm but not hot—no thermal throttling. That's the difference between this and cheaper readers that slow to a crawl after five minutes.

The SD slot hit 280MB/s read, which is UHS-II max territory. The dual-slot independence is the real feature here: I offloaded a 64GB SD card and a 128GB CFexpress card simultaneously, and both transferred at full speed. No shared-controller bottleneck.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews isn't accidental. Recurring themes in verified feedback:

- **Reliability**: Multiple reviewers note it's their second or third reader after cheaper ones died. One long-term user wrote, *"I've had this for eight months of daily shoots. It just works every single time."*
- **Speed consistency**: A wedding photographer specifically mentioned, *"The sustained write speed is what sold me. My old reader would drop to 200MB/s after a minute. This doesn't."*
- **Build feel**: Several mention the solid aluminum construction as a differentiator from the plastic mass-market options.

## Where It Falls Short

The included USB-C cable is annoyingly short at 1.2 feet. If your tower sits under a desk, you'll need to buy a 3-foot cable—$10 extra you didn't plan on. Also, the SD slot tops out at UHS-II. It won't support the newer SD Express standard, so if you're planning on buying those cards in the next couple years, this won't handle them at full speed.

One more thing: there's no SD card lock or cover for the slots when not in use. Dust can accumulate in the CFexpress slot over time. Keep it in a drawer or case when traveling.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a working photographer/videographer who offloads CFexpress cards daily and values sustained speed over saving $30. **Skip it if** you only shoot SD cards—you're paying a premium for a slot you won't use. Also skip if you need SD Express support; wait for a newer model.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with iPad Pro or Android tablets?**
A: Yes, with USB-C devices—but power draw can be an issue. Some iPad users report the reader needs external power for CFexpress cards. SD-only works fine.

**Q: Can I use both slots at the same time on a USB-A port?**
A: Yes, but you'll drop to USB 3.0 speeds (around 400MB/s maximum) since the USB-A adapter is 5Gbps. Fine for SD, slow for CFexpress.

**Q: Does it support CFexpress Type A cards?**
A: No. This is Type B only. For Type A, you need a different reader. Make sure you know which format your camera uses.
