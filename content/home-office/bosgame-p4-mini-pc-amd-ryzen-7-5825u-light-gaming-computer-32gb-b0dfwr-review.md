Let's be direct: most mini PCs under $300 are compromises. You get a weak Celeron that chokes on a dozen browser tabs, or you pay triple for something with a discrete GPU you don't need. The BOSGAME P4 sits in a smarter spot — an 8-core Ryzen 7 5825U with 32GB RAM that's aimed squarely at office work, media centers, and light gaming. After spending a week with it, here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the P4 unit itself (measuring roughly 5 x 5 x 1.6 inches), a 19V power adapter, an HDMI cable, a VESA mounting plate with screws, and a basic manual. No keyboard or mouse — which is fine, because you likely have those already. The front panel offers two USB 3.2 ports, a USB-C (data-only, unfortunately), and a combo audio jack. Around back: two HDMI 2.0 ports, two 2.5G Ethernet jacks, and two more USB 3.2 ports. That dual 2.5G LAN config is rare at this price and genuinely useful for network-attached storage setups or router/firewall experiments.

**Performance: the 5825U punches above its class**

This is a Zen 3 architecture chip — the same family that made the Ryzen 5000 series so well-regarded. With 8 cores and 16 threads running up to 4.5GHz, the P4 handles spreadsheets, video conferencing, coding, and 4K video playback without breaking a sweat. The 32GB of RAM (not 16GB like many competitors) means you won't hit memory ceilings with dozens of Chrome tabs, Slack, and a VM or two running simultaneously.

For light gaming, the integrated Vega 8 graphics are honestly better than you'd expect. Titles like *League of Legends*, *CS:GO*, and *Rocket League* run smoothly at 1080p with medium-to-high settings. Older games like *Portal 2* or *Skyrim* are no problem at all. But if you're eyeing *Cyberpunk 2077* or *Starfield*, pump the brakes — you'll be at 720p with everything turned down, and it still won't be pretty. This is a "light gaming" machine, and the description is accurate.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scanning through the 215 Amazon ratings at 4.3 stars, a few themes jump out. One verified buyer wrote: *"I replaced a 5-year-old desktop tower with this and honestly can't tell the difference except my desk is cleaner."* Another noted: *"Setup took five minutes. It's been running 24/7 for two weeks as a Plex server and it's completely silent."* The noise profile deserves emphasis — under sustained load, the fan is audible but not intrusive, and at idle it's virtually silent.

**Where it falls short**

The plastic top shell feels a bit flimsy compared to the aluminum unibody designs from Beelink or Minisforum at similar price points. It won't fall apart, but it doesn't inspire "premium" confidence either. The single USB-C port is also a letdown — it's data-only, no DisplayPort Alt Mode and no Thunderbolt, so you can't run a single-cable setup to a monitor or hook up an external GPU later. That's a meaningful limitation if you're planning to future-proof.

**Who should buy this**

- **Remote workers and students** who need reliable, quiet computing without a tower taking up desk space
- **Homelab tinkerers** who want the dual 2.5G Ethernet for a compact router, NAS, or virtualization host
- **Casual gamers** whose library is mostly esports titles or older AAA games
- **Skip it if** you're a serious gamer needing 60fps at 1440p in modern titles — you need a dedicated GPU, not this

**FAQ**

**Q: Can it handle dual 4K monitors?**
A: Yes. The two HDMI 2.0 ports support 4K at 60Hz each. Just note that running demanding 3D content on both simultaneously will strain the integrated GPU, but standard office work and video playback are fine.

**Q: Is the RAM upgradeable?**
A: It uses two SODIMM slots, so yes — you can swap the 32GB (2x16GB) for up to 64GB if needed. But for 95% of users, 32GB is already overkill.

**Q: Will this work as a Plex media server with transcoding?**
A: Absolutely. The Vega GPU supports hardware-accelerated transcoding, and the 8-core CPU handles multiple simultaneous streams without issues. Several reviewers specifically mention using it this way.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a quiet, capable desktop replacement for office work, streaming, and light gaming without spending $600+ on a full tower. The 32GB RAM and dual 2.5G Ethernet give it versatility that most competitors lack at this price.

**Skip it if** your gaming ambitions extend beyond esports titles, or if you need a premium metal chassis and future-proof USB4 connectivity.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — An excellent value for its intended audience, held back only by a plasticky build and the missing high-speed USB-C. For the money, it's one of the smartest mini PC buys right now.
