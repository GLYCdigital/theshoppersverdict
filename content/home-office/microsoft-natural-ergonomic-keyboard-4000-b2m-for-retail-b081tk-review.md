Some keyboards promise ergonomic relief but deliver a gimmick. The Microsoft Natural Ergonomic Keyboard 4000 b2m isn't one of them. This is a 20-year-old design that's still selling because it solves a real problem: the unnatural, shoulder-cramping position most flat keyboards force you into.

The split-key layout is the headline feature. The keyboard is physically divided into two angled halves, which rotates your forearms outward and lets your shoulders relax. It's a noticeable difference within minutes of typing — your hands naturally fall into a handshake position rather than being twisted inward. The 7-degree reverse tilt (adjustable via fold-out legs) further straightens your wrists, reducing the sharp angle that contributes to repetitive strain.

**What you actually get:** The keyboard itself, a massive cushioned palm rest that clicks into the front edge, and a PS/2-to-USB adapter for legacy systems. No software disc — the drivers are baked into Windows. The palm rest is the standout accessory here. It's covered in a soft fabric-like material with generous foam padding that runs the full width of the board. Your wrists sink into it rather than resting on hard plastic, which makes a real difference during marathon writing sessions.

**Build quality and real-world use:** The keys use a standard rubber dome mechanism with a pronounced, soft landing. They're quiet — good for open offices — but the actuation feels mushy compared to mechanical switches. If you're a fast typist, the high travel distance (about 4mm) can slow you down slightly. I measured the footprint at roughly 18.5 inches wide by 9.5 inches deep with the palm rest attached. That's substantial. You'll need a desk tray or pull-out drawer with real depth.

The extra features punch above their weight. A vertical zoom slider sits between the arrow keys — handy for documents and maps. Six customizable hotkeys (launch calculator, email, etc.) plus F-key shortcuts are programmable through Microsoft's Mouse and Keyboard Center software. One quirk: the spacebar is split into two halves. Your right thumb hits the main portion, but the left half is dead weight unless you remap it. Some users love it; others accidentally hit the dead zone.

**What buyers consistently praise:** Comfort dominates the feedback. One Amazon reviewer wrote, "My wrist pain disappeared within a week of switching." Another noted, "The split design took two days to get used to, but now flat keyboards feel wrong." Durability gets frequent mentions too — several users report 5+ years of daily use without key failure.

**Where it falls short:** The key feel is the biggest compromise. If you're used to crisp mechanical switches or modern low-profile laptops, this will feel sluggish initially. The bulk is real — it won't fit on shallow keyboard trays. And at this price point, you're paying for the ergonomic layout, not premium materials; the plastic shell shows fingerprints and the letters on keycaps can wear off after heavy use (a common complaint in the reviews).

**Who should buy this:** 
- Office workers typing 6+ hours daily with wrist or shoulder discomfort
- Anyone with broad shoulders who feels cramped on standard keyboards
- IT departments standardizing ergonomic setups on a budget

**Skip it if:** You're a gamer needing fast, tactile switches, or you have limited desk depth. Consider a compact mechanical split keyboard like the Kinesis Freestyle instead.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Does it work with Mac?**  
A: Yes, plug-and-play via USB. The Windows key functions as Command. Microsoft's configuration software is Windows-only, but basic typing works fine on macOS.

**Q: Is the split design hard to adapt to?**  
A: Expect 2-5 days of adjustment. Your muscle memory fights the new hand position initially, but most users report full speed within a week.

**Q: Can I replace the palm rest?**  
A: Yes, replacement rests are available separately. The fabric does wear down after 2-3 years of daily use.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you're experiencing wrist or shoulder strain from typing and want a proven, affordable fix. The comfort payoff justifies the learning curve.  
**Skip it if** you prioritize fast, tactile keystrokes or have limited desk space.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A functional, well-built ergonomic tool held back by dated key feel and its substantial footprint. For the price, it's the most cost-effective way to test if split-key typing helps you.
