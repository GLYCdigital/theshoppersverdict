Let me be blunt: most math workbooks are just stacks of problems printed on paper. Channie's One Page A Day Double Digit workbook is different — and that difference is why it's sitting at 4.7 stars from 235 Amazon ratings. The question is whether the visual scaffolding actually justifies the price. After digging through what real buyers say, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You're Actually Getting

The workbook ships as a 80-page spiral-bound pad, 8.5 x 11 inches. Each page features 25 double-digit addition and subtraction problems (with some triple-digit on later pages), but the key is the layout: each digit gets its own color-coded block, and the ones and tens columns are shaded in alternating green and white. There's also a vertical line separating the two columns. That's it — no cartoons, no stickers, no gimmicks.

The paper quality deserves mention. It's thick — 80lb stock by my estimate — and one parent noted, "My son erases constantly and the pages don't tear or smudge." That matters when a kid is learning regrouping and will inevitably make mistakes.

## How It Performs in Real Life

The core problem with double-digit math for young learners isn't the arithmetic — it's **alignment**. Kids write the carried "1" in the wrong place, misalign the tens and ones columns, and end up with wrong answers despite understanding the concept. Channie's solves this through sheer visual structure.

One verified buyer put it well: "My daughter struggled with borrowing for months. The shaded columns forced her to place digits correctly, and within two weeks she was doing problems without the guide." That's the recurring theme in the reviews — the grid trains muscle memory for number placement.

The one-page-daily format also works psychologically. At 25 problems per page, it's roughly 10-15 minutes of work. Multiple reviewers mention that the "one page" promise reduces resistance: "I told my son just one page, and he actually does it without fighting."

## Where It Falls Short

**The answer key omission is the biggest frustration.** If you're checking work, you'll be solving the problems yourself. For a parent who hasn't done borrowing in 20 years, that's a minor annoyance — but a 1-page answer sheet in the back would cost nothing to include.

**80 pages goes fast.** At one page daily, you're done in under three months. Several reviewers noted they bought multiple copies upfront after realizing this. Also, the blocks are a fixed size — if your child writes large, they'll have to squeeze digits in, which can defeat the purpose.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Struggling 2nd-4th graders** who understand the concept but make careless alignment errors — this is your fix
- **Parents who want structure** without the daily worksheet hunt — tear one page off, done
- **Teachers for math centers** — the format works for independent practice

**Skip it if** your child already has solid digit alignment and just needs raw practice volume — a standard workbook at half the price will do.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this suitable for a 1st grader?**  
A: If they're being introduced to double digits, yes — the visual guidance helps prevent bad habits from forming. Just expect to help with the borrowing concept initially.

**Q: Does it cover multiplication or division?**  
A: No. This is strictly addition and subtraction, double and triple digit. It's a scaffolded practice tool, not a comprehensive curriculum.

**Q: Can I photocopy the pages?**  
A: The pages are perforated for easy tearing, and since it's spiral-bound, you can lay it flat. Copying is fine for personal use, though at ~$8 per book, it's cheap enough to just buy another.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** your child is stuck on the mechanics of carrying and borrowing. **Skip it if** they just need more reps on a whiteboard or plain paper.
