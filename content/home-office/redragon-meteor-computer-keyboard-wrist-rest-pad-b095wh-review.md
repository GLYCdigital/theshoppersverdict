Let me start with the honest truth: a wrist rest is one of those accessories you don't think about until your forearm starts aching at hour three of a workday. The Redragon Meteor aims to fix that for under twenty bucks, and after digging through 220 ratings and the spec sheet, here's what actually matters.

**What you're getting**

The box contains just the wrist rest itself — no surprises, no gimmicks. The Meteor measures 17.7 inches long, which covers standard TKL and most full-size keyboards, but gamers running boards with extra macro columns on the left will find it falls short. The 3.5-inch depth is generous enough to support your palm without eating into your mouse space.

The construction is straightforward: high-density memory foam wrapped in a smooth, stitched fabric top with a textured rubber base underneath. The 0.8-inch height is the sweet spot for most desk setups. If you're using low-profile keyboards like a Keychron or Logitech MX Keys, this sits close enough to maintain proper wrist alignment. Taller mechanical boards with SA-profile keycaps might leave you reaching slightly upward.

**How it holds up in real use**

The memory foam is the star here. Multiple reviewers note it keeps its shape after months of daily typing — one long-term user wrote, "I've had this for nine months and it still feels as firm as day one. My previous gel rest flattened out in three." That tracks with the density rating; this is noticeably firmer than cheap gel alternatives, which means better support but less plushness if you prefer sinking into your rest.

The anti-slip base deserves the praise it gets. It grips desk surfaces tenaciously. Gamers who slam their keyboards mid-frustration won't send this sliding. The fabric top, however, is a double-edged sword. It's soft and breathable, but the smooth weave picks up oils and dead skin fast. If you're a heavy sweater or eat at your desk, you'll be wiping this down weekly. Spot cleaning with a damp cloth works, but the material stains if you're not diligent.

**Where it stumbles**

The narrow width is the biggest practical complaint. Redragon lists this as fitting full-size keyboards, but if your board has dedicated macro keys, volume rollers, or a left-side number pad, this rest will leave portions of your keyboard unsupported. Measure your keyboard before buying — this is the single most common reason for returns based on the feedback pattern.

The fabric's oil resistance is the second issue. It's not a dealbreaker, but reviewers consistently mention having to clean it more often than expected. Dark color options hide this better than the lighter versions.

**Who should buy this**

- **Office workers** logging 8+ hours daily who want consistent, firm support — this will outlast cheap gel rests
- **Mechanical keyboard enthusiasts** with TKL or standard full-size boards who want a low-profile rest that doesn't interfere with their keycap height
- **Gamers** who need something that stays put during intense sessions and won't collapse under repeated pressure

**Skip it if** you have a keyboard with left-side macro columns, prefer a plush, pillow-like rest, or want something you can toss in the washing machine.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Is this compatible with my Razer BlackWidow or Corsair K70?**
A: Yes for both, provided you have standard layouts. The K70's media keys sit above the function row, so the 17.7-inch length covers the main typing area. Corsair's K95 with left-side macro keys won't fit properly.

**Q: How does memory foam compare to gel wrist rests?**
A: Memory foam is firmer and holds shape longer, but gel conforms more aggressively to your wrist. If you have carpal tunnel sensitivity, gel might feel better. If you want support that doesn't flatten, go memory foam.

**Q: Can I use this with a split ergonomic keyboard?**
A: Technically yes, but it's a poor fit. Split keyboards leave a gap in the middle, so you'll have a wide rest with an empty space. Look for two smaller rests instead.

**The verdict**

At a 4.2/5, the Redragon Meteor delivers exactly what it promises: durable, firm wrist support that won't slide around. It's not flashy, but it's functional, and at this price point, the build quality punches above its weight. The width limitations and fabric maintenance keep it from a perfect score, but for the majority of users with standard keyboards, this is a solid, no-regrets purchase.

**Buy it if** you want a firm, durable rest that stays put and won't flatten out in six months. **Skip it if** you've got a non-standard keyboard layout or want something you can machine wash.
