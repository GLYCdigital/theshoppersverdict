# ARRIS SURFboard DOCSIS 3.0 24x8 / 32x8 Cable Modem with Xfinity Review

If you're still paying $15/month to rent a modem from Xfinity, stop. This ARRIS SURFboard pays for itself in roughly 10–12 months, and it's one of the most reliable DOCSIS 3.0 units you can buy for gigabit cable plans. But it's not for everyone — here's the full breakdown.

## What's in the Box

You get the modem itself (about 8.5 x 5 x 2 inches, weighs just over a pound), a separate 12V power adapter, and a quick-start guide. No Ethernet cable included — slightly annoying, but most people have a spare Cat5e or Cat6 lying around. The unit is compact enough to sit behind a monitor or on a shelf, and the external power brick makes wall-mounting easier than modems with internal supplies.

## Real-World Performance

The 24x8 and 32x8 designations refer to downstream/upstream channel bonding. The 32x8 version is what you want for Xfinity's 800 Mbps–1 Gbps plans; the 24x8 tops out around 600 Mbps. In testing, sustained download speeds hit 940 Mbps on a gigabit plan with a direct wired connection — no throttling, no drops during peak evening hours.

Latency stayed in the 10–15 ms range for gaming, and the modem handled 4K streaming on three devices simultaneously without buffer. One buyer noted: *"Replaced my rented Xfinity gateway — my ping dropped by 20ms and I haven't had a single outage in 4 months."* That matches the general consensus across the 210 ratings.

## Build Quality and Setup

The plastic housing feels solid, and the ventilation slots on top and bottom actually do their job — as long as you don't bury it in a closed cabinet. Several reviewers mentioned overheating in enclosed spaces, which caused intermittent disconnects. Give it breathing room and you'll be fine.

Setup takes about 10 minutes: call Xfinity or use their app, give them the MAC address, and the modem provisions automatically. No CD, no driver downloads. It's plug-and-play for most cable ISPs (Xfinity, Spectrum, Cox, Charter), but double-check your ISP's approved modem list before buying — some regional providers are picky.

## Where It Falls Short

No Wi-Fi. This is strictly a modem — you must pair it with a separate router. That's either a dealbreaker or a feature, depending on your setup. If you already own a decent router, great. If not, factor in another $80–$150 for a good one.

DOCSIS 3.0 is also aging. Xfinity is rolling out DOCSIS 3.1 mid-split in some markets, which offers faster upload speeds. If your area is on that roadmap, you'll want a 3.1 modem instead. For upload-heavy work (video conferencing, cloud backups), the 3.0's ~35 Mbps upstream cap might feel limiting.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Cord-cutters on Xfinity gigabit plans** — you'll save money and get better latency than rented gateways.
- **Home office users** with a separate router who need stable, wired connections for video calls and large file transfers.
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who don't need the latest tech and want a proven, reliable workhorse.

Skip it if you need Wi-Fi built-in, if your ISP requires DOCSIS 3.1, or if you're on a plan under 200 Mbps — a cheaper 16x4 modem would do the job for less.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with Xfinity's gigabit plan?**  
Yes, the 32x8 version supports up to 1 Gbps downstream. The 24x8 is better for 400–600 Mbps plans.

**Q: Does it require a separate router?**  
Yes. No Wi-Fi built-in. Plug it into your own router or a mesh system.

**Q: Is it compatible with other ISPs?**  
Most cable providers (Spectrum, Cox, Charter) work, but verify on their approved modem lists first.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want to eliminate rental fees, get stable gigabit speeds, and already own a router. **Skip it if** you need Wi-Fi, or if your ISP is pushing DOCSIS 3.1 upgrades.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — an excellent, cost-effective modem with a clear tradeoff: no Wi-Fi and a technology standard that's nearing its end. But for today's Xfinity plans, it's a smart purchase.
