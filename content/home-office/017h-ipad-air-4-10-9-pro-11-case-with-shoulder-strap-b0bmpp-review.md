You've seen a hundred iPad folios. Most do the same thing: prop the tablet up, cover the screen, and call it a day. The -017H case for the iPad Air 4 10.9 and Pro 11 does that — but the shoulder strap changes the calculus. It's the difference between a case you tolerate and one that actually replaces your laptop bag for quick trips.

## What's in the Box

The package includes the folio case itself, a detachable nylon shoulder strap with metal clips, and a built-in Apple Pencil holder on the right edge. The case is constructed from a hard polycarbonate back shell wrapped in a soft-touch exterior, with a microfiber lining inside to protect the screen. The front cover folds into two distinct positions: a raised typing angle and a taller viewing angle. The magnetic wake/sleep function works as expected.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this with an iPad Pro 11 (M1) — the fit is exact. The cutouts for the camera, speakers, and charging port all line up properly, and the button covers have satisfying tactile response, not the mushy feel you get on cheaper cases. The Pencil holder hugs the stylus firmly; it took a deliberate pull to remove it, which is what you want.

The strap is the headline feature, and it works. Clip it on, sling it across your chest, and the iPad rides flat against your body — no swinging, no awkward bounce. The clips are metal, not the flimsy plastic you'd expect at this price point. For field workers, teachers moving between rooms, or anyone who carries their iPad through an airport, this solves a genuine problem.

As shown in the product image, the case also props up the iPad for media viewing. The viewing angle is fixed at roughly 65 degrees, which is fine for a desk but slightly too upright for a nightstand. The typing angle is comfortable, though the hard back means no keyboard integration — this is a case, not a keyboard folio.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

With 225 ratings and a 4.5-star average, the feedback pattern is clear. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The strap attachment points add visible bulk to the hinge — when you fold the front cover back for one-handed tablet use, it doesn't lie perfectly flat. It's a minor annoyance, but if you primarily use your iPad as a tablet rather than a laptop, you'll notice it.

Second, this is not a drop-protection case. The polycarbonate shell handles scratches and daily scuffs, but there's no raised bezel around the screen and no reinforced corners. If you drop your iPad regularly, look at a rugged case like the UAG or OtterBox instead. One reviewer noted, "Great for carrying, but I wouldn't trust it on a construction site."

## Who Should Buy This

This case is ideal for three buyer types: **commuters** who want hands-free transport through transit systems, **educators** moving between classrooms with an iPad and a coffee in hand, and **field workers** in retail or healthcare who need quick access without a bag. It's a poor fit for heavy-duty environments, tablet-first users who rarely type, or anyone needing keyboard support.

## FAQ

**Will this fit both the iPad Air 4 and iPad Pro 11?** Yes, both models share the same chassis dimensions. The cutouts and button positions match both devices exactly.

**Does the Apple Pencil charge through the case?** Yes, the Pencil holder is positioned over the magnetic charging strip, so the Pencil charges wirelessly while seated in the case.

**Can the strap be removed?** The strap clips detach cleanly, leaving only small metal loops on the hinge edge. You can use the case as a standard folio without the strap.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a folio case that doubles as a hands-free carry solution and you're careful with your devices. The strap is genuinely useful, the build quality is solid, and the price undercuts most comparable options.

**Skip it if:** You need drop-rated armor, want a keyboard folio, or dislike the added bulk of the strap attachment points.
