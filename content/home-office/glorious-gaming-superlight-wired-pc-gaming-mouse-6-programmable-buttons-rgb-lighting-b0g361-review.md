Let's be blunt: the "superlight" category is crowded, but the GLORIOUS Superlight wired mouse earns its name. At 58 grams — roughly the weight of a standard golf ball — this thing disappears in your hand. But lightweight alone doesn't make a great mouse. After spending a week with it for both work and gaming, here's what actually matters.

**What's in the box**

GLORIOUS keeps it simple: the mouse itself, a 1.8-meter paracord-style cable that's permanently attached, a quick-start guide, and a set of replacement mouse feet in the packaging. No weights, no dongle, no carrying case. You get the mouse, you plug it in, you're done. The cable deserves early mention — it's genuinely flexible, not stiff like most braided cables. It won't fight your movements or catch on desk edges.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The shell is matte ABS plastic with a subtle texture that grips well without being abrasive. The main switches are rated for 80 million clicks, which is competitive with mice twice the price. They're crisp and tactile — not mushy, not overly loud. The scroll wheel has defined steps with minimal wobble, and the side buttons are positioned where your thumb naturally rests, not awkwardly high.

The sensor is the standout spec here: a PixArt PMW3360, which is the same sensor found in many $100+ flagship mice. It tracks at up to 12,000 DPI with zero acceleration or smoothing. In practice, that means pixel-perfect cursor movement whether you're editing spreadsheets or flick-shotting in an FPS. I ran it through the standard mouse tester suite — no spinouts, no jitter, even at high speeds.

The RGB lighting is tasteful: a single strip runs along the GLORIOUS logo on the palm area, plus a subtle underglow. It's bright enough to show off but not distracting during work. The GLORIOUS Core software handles remapping and lighting, and it's lightweight — no bloatware, no forced updates nagging you.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The weight and cable are the two things that come up again and again in the 225 Amazon ratings. One reviewer put it plainly: "I forgot it was wired — the cable is so light it feels wireless." Another noted, "The 58g weight is perfect for low-sensitivity players; I can swipe across my whole pad without lifting." Build quality also gets consistent props — multiple buyers mentioned it feels more expensive than it costs, which is a good sign at this price point.

**Where it falls short**

This is a wired-only mouse. If you're looking for a wireless option for travel or a clean desk setup, this isn't it. The cable is flexible, but it's still a cable.

The shape is also a specific taste. It's a medium-sized ambidextrous design, but it's shorter than typical palm-grip mice. Buyers with larger hands consistently mention that they had to switch to a claw or fingertip grip. If you're a palm gripper with XL hands, measure your current mouse before buying. One reviewer summed it up: "Great mouse, but my hands are too big — had to return it for the larger Model D."

The side buttons are also a bit small. They work fine, but if you have thick thumbs, you might find yourself hunting for them initially.

**Who should buy this**

Three buyer personas fit this mouse well:

- **Competitive gamers** who run low sensitivity and want a featherweight wired option without paying for wireless tech they don't need.
- **Office workers who game after hours** — the weight and sensor make it comfortable for 8-hour workdays, and the programmable buttons handle productivity macros.
- **Claw/fingertip grippers** who've been frustrated by bulky mice — this shape is genuinely ideal for that grip style.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the GLORIOUS Core software work on Mac?**  
A: Yes, GLORIOUS Core supports macOS. You can remap buttons and adjust lighting on both Windows and Mac, though some advanced macro features are Windows-only.

**Q: Is the cable replaceable if it breaks?**  
A: No, the cable is hardwired into the mouse. That's a downside compared to some competitors with detachable cables. However, the paracord-style cable is durable and the strain relief at the front of the mouse is solid.

**Q: How does it compare to the Glorious Model O?**  
A: The Model O has a honeycomb shell for weight reduction and a similar sensor. The Superlight has a solid shell, which some prefer for feel and dust protection. The Superlight is also lighter at 58g vs. the Model O's 67g. If you want the lightest option, go Superlight.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely lightweight wired mouse with a top-tier sensor, and you use a claw or fingertip grip. The build quality exceeds the price point, and the cable is the best in its class.

**Skip it if:** You need wireless, you have large hands that demand a palm grip, or you prefer detachable cables for travel.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — This is one of the best wired superlight options available. It's not perfect — the shape won't suit everyone, and wired-only is a hard no for some — but for its intended audience, it's a slam dunk.
