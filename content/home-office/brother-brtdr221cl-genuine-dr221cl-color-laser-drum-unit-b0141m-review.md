Let's cut through the confusion right now: the Brother DR221CL drum unit is *not* a toner cartridge. It's the imaging drum that works alongside your toner. Skip this part and your prints will come out streaky, faded, or with that annoying vertical line down the middle. And if you're staring at a "Replace Drum" message on your HL-3140CW, HL-3150CDW, HL-3170CDW, MFC-9130CW, MFC-9330CDW, or MFC-9340CDW, this is the part you need.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains one DR221CL drum assembly — a single unit that handles all four colors (CMYK). That's a key detail: unlike some competing designs that use separate drums per color, Brother consolidates them into one cartridge-style component. It's rated for approximately 18,000 pages, which is solid for a color laser in this class. The unit arrives sealed in OEM protective wrapping with an orange protective sheet covering the drum surface. Installation is genuinely tool-free: open the front cover, pull out the old drum, slide this one in, close the cover. About three minutes of your life.

## Real-World Performance

I've run this drum in an MFC-9330CDW for several weeks of mixed printing — mostly text documents with occasional full-color graphics. Print quality is indistinguishable from a factory-new machine: crisp black text down to 6-point font, smooth color gradients with no banding, and no ghosting on duplex prints. The drum's organic photoconductor layer holds charge consistently across the page, which is where cheap compatibles often fail.

One thing worth noting: the drum counter reset. After installation, you must navigate to Machine Info → Reset Parts Life → Drum, then hold down the "Stop" or "OK" button (model-dependent) for several seconds. The machine will prompt you — don't skip this, or the printer will still think the drum is dead and refuse to print.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the price. A compatible DR221CL drum runs roughly $30-45; the genuine Brother unit typically costs $80-120 depending on the seller. That's a significant premium. For low-volume home users printing a few hundred pages a month, a quality compatible (like the well-reviewed LD Products version) may be fine. But here's the tradeoff: compatibles sometimes arrive with damaged drum surfaces, and Brother firmware updates have occasionally caused issues with third-party parts.

The second downside: no reset chip. Some aftermarket drums include a chip that auto-resets the counter; Brother's genuine unit doesn't. It's a minor annoyance, but it's a step you'll have to do manually.

A: No. It's specifically compatible with the HL-3140CW, HL-3150CDW, HL-3170CDW, MFC-9130CW, MFC-9330CDW, and MFC-9340CDW. Check your model number before ordering.
