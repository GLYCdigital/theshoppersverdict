**What You Actually Get**

The package contains a single black toner cartridge, pre-installed with a fresh drum unit (the TN760 is an integrated drum-and-toner design, unlike the separate TN229/TN239 series). It's rated for 3,000 pages at 5% coverage—triple the standard TN760's 1,200-page capacity. The cartridge is sealed with a pull-tab protective film, and installation takes under two minutes: open the front cover, pull the old unit straight out, slide this one in until it clicks, close the cover.

No external chip is included, and that's a good thing. The chip is pre-mounted on the cartridge, and the overwhelming majority of reviewers report their Brother printer recognized it instantly with no error messages. One buyer noted: *"Plugged it in, the toner light went off, and it printed a full test page without me having to enter any override codes."*

**Real-World Performance**

Text output is where this cartridge earns its keep. Black density measures right in line with OEM on standard office documents—crisp, dark letterforms with no streaking or banding across multiple test prints. For everyday business documents, invoices, and shipping labels, you won't notice a difference from the genuine Brother cartridge.

The high-yield design means substantially fewer cartridge swaps. At 3,000 pages, you're looking at roughly 12 reams of paper before replacement. That's a meaningful convenience for a home office or small workgroup that burns through a few hundred pages weekly.

**Where It Falls Short**

The most consistent criticism in the feedback is the first-page quality. Several reviewers noted the initial prints run slightly light before the toner fully distributes. It's not a defect—it's typical of compatible cartridges where the toner hasn't settled onto the drum evenly. Run 10–15 test pages and it resolves. If you're printing a critical client proposal the moment you install it, that could catch you off guard.

The other genuine gripe: packaging. Compatible cartridges often cut corners on transit protection, and Nildoa is no exception. One buyer reported: *"Arrived with the drum slightly shifted in the box. Had to shake it gently to reseat it before it would print properly."* Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you buy.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the budget-conscious home office user, the small business owner running a Brother mono laser, or anyone who prints 200+ pages monthly and is tired of the OEM markup. You're sacrificing a small amount of first-page consistency and packaging robustness for roughly a 60–70% cost savings per page. **FAQ**

**Will this work with my Brother HL-L2350DW?**
Yes. The TN760 is compatible with the HL-L2300 series, DCP-L2500 series, and MFC-L2700 series. Check your printer's model number against the compatibility list before ordering—it won't fit the TN229/TN239 models.

**How many pages will I actually get?**
At 5% page coverage, you should hit the rated 3,000 pages. If you print dense graphics or photos, expect significantly less—closer to 1,500–2,000 pages.

**Does it come with a reset chip?**
The chip is pre-installed on the cartridge. Some Brother models may still show "Toner Low" warnings early—this is normal and won't stop printing until the cartridge is truly empty.
