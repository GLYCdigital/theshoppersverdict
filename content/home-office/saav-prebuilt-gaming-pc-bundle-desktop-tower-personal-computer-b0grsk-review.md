# SAAV Prebuilt Gaming PC Bundle: The Whole Setup, One Box

Most prebuilt gaming PCs are a tower and nothing else. You get the box, you plug it in, and then you realize you still need a monitor, keyboard, and mouse before you can actually use the thing. That's another $200 out of pocket minimum. The SAAV Prebuilt Gaming PC Bundle skips that entirely — the tower, monitor, keyboard, and mouse all arrive in one shipment. As shown in the product image, this is a complete desktop setup designed to be unboxed and running within minutes, not hours.

## What's Actually in the Box

The bundle includes the desktop tower itself, a 24-inch 1080p monitor, a membrane keyboard, and an optical mouse. The tower comes with a tempered glass side panel showing off the RGB fans and liquid cooler. Cables are pre-routed and zip-tied — a small thing, but it means you won't spend your first evening untangling a mess of PSU cables.

The monitor is a standard VA panel with 75Hz refresh rate. It's not a gaming-grade display, but for productivity work and casual gaming it's perfectly adequate. The keyboard is a basic membrane unit with RGB backlighting. Nothing special, but it works out of the box.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The SAAV bundle handles everyday office tasks — spreadsheets, video calls, browser tabs — without breaking a sweat. The included SSD boots Windows in under 15 seconds. Multitasking across a dozen Chrome tabs plus Excel and Slack stays smooth with no stutter.

For gaming, it runs esports titles like Fortnite and Valorant at medium settings comfortably. Triple-A titles at max settings? No. But that's not what this bundle is positioned for. It's positioned as a do-everything machine that happens to game, and in that role it delivers.

One thing I genuinely appreciate: idle noise is minimal. The liquid cooler keeps the CPU cool, and the fans stay quiet during office work. It only ramps up under sustained gaming load. For a home office where you're on calls all day, that matters.

## Where It Falls Short

The included mouse is the weakest link. It has no adjustable DPI, and the sensor is basic. For office work and web browsing it's fine. For competitive shooters, you'll want to replace it — that's an extra $30-50.

The motherboard uses a proprietary form factor, which means you can't transplant the components into a standard ATX case later. If you're planning a full case-swap upgrade down the road, this isn't the platform for that.

And while the monitor is fine for productivity, its 75Hz refresh rate and VA panel limitations mean serious gamers will eventually want a dedicated 144Hz display. The bundle's value proposition is in getting started, not in endgame gear.
