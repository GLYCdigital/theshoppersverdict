**The SP Silicon Power A2 MicroSD card has one job: hold your files without drama. After 225 Amazon reviews averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly doing that job well. But "no drama" doesn't mean "no tradeoffs." Here's what you actually need to know before you click buy.**

## What's in the box

You get the microSD card itself, a full-size SD adapter with a physical write-protect switch (rare at this price point), and a plastic storage case. No USB reader, no branding fluff. The card surface is marked with the A2 symbol and speed class — check the product image to confirm your specific capacity's ratings, because they vary slightly across the lineup.

## Real-world performance

The A2 rating is the headline feature. That means 4000 random read IOPS and 2000 random write IOPS — the spec that matters for running apps directly from the card. In practical terms, a Nintendo Switch loads games noticeably faster with this card than with older U3 cards. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Swapped this into my Steam Deck and load times dropped compared to the stock card. Zero complaints."*

For 4K video, the sustained write speed holds up for typical 10-15 minute clips. Action camera users should note that prolonged recording (30+ minutes) causes thermal throttling — the card slows down to protect itself. It won't corrupt files, but you'll see dropped frames if you're pushing continuous 4K60.

## What buyers consistently praise

- **Price-to-performance ratio.** Multiple reviewers called it "the best value A2 card" they've found, often costing 20-30% less than SanDisk Extreme equivalents.
- **Reliability in drones and dashcams.** The shockproof, waterproof, and X-ray-proof ratings (standard for the category) held up in real-world use. A dashcam reviewer noted: *"Three months of daily 128GB loop recording, zero corruption."*
- **The included adapter.** Sounds minor, but reviewers appreciated not having to buy a separate SD adapter for DSLR use.

## Where it falls short

The thermal throttling is the biggest honest caveat. If you're a professional videographer shooting continuous 4K, spend more for a card with better thermal management. Also, the included adapter is plastic and feels flimsy compared to SanDisk's metal versions — handle it gently when swapping between devices.

There's no memory card reader included, so if your laptop only has USB-A ports, you'll need to buy a reader separately or transfer via a device that has a built-in slot. That's an extra cost people don't factor in.

## Who should buy this

- **Nintendo Switch or Steam Deck owners** who want faster game load times without paying premium prices
- **Drone and action camera users** shooting 4K in short bursts (under 20 minutes)
- **Budget-conscious smartphone users** needing expandable storage for photos, music, and offline maps
- **Dashcam owners** who want a reliable card for continuous loop recording

**Skip it if** you're a professional videographer shooting long 4K sessions, or if you need the absolute fastest sustained write speeds for RAW photo bursts — look at the SanDisk Extreme Pro or Samsung Pro Plus instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this card compatible with the Nintendo Switch?**
Yes. The A2 rating and U3 speed class meet the Switch's requirements, and it's a popular choice among owners. Format it in the Switch before first use.

**Q: Will this work in a Raspberry Pi?**
Yes, but for Pi OS installations, consider the A1 version instead. The A2 rating offers no benefit for sequential reads in this use case, and you'll save money.

**Q: How long is the warranty?**
Silicon Power offers a 5-year limited warranty. It's not lifetime like some competitors, but for the price, it's acceptable.
