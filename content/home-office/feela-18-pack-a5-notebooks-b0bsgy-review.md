If you’ve ever burned through a notebook a month, the feela 18-pack A5 solves that math instantly. At under $2 per notebook, this isn’t a premium journal — it’s a workhorse. But bulk pricing only matters if the paper doesn’t sabotage your pens. Here’s the real story after digging through specs and buyer feedback.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 18 A5 notebooks (5.8 x 8.3 inches), each with 80 sheets / 160 pages. That’s roughly 2,880 total pages. You also get three different interior styles: dotted, lined, and blank — six of each. The softcover flexes, and the spiral binding is double-wire, not the cheap single-loop kind that snags.

Colors vary by batch, but the set leans toward muted pastels and earth tones. As shown in the product image, the covers have a subtle matte texture — nothing flashy, but professional enough for a client meeting.

## Paper Performance and Build

The standout spec is 80 GSM paper. For context, that’s thicker than the 70 GSM found in most budget notebooks. In practice, this means:

- **Gel pens (0.5mm)**: No bleed-through. Minor ghosting if you press hard.
- **Fountain pens (fine nib)**: Acceptable with drier inks. Wet writers will shadow.
- **Highlighters**: Mildliners are fine. Heavy alcohol-based markers will bleed.

The spiral binding is the real MVP here. It lay-flat from page one — no cracking the spine or wrestling pages down. Several reviewers called this out specifically: *“The binding is sturdy and opens flat, which is rare at this price point.”*

The covers are flexible cardstock, not rigid board. That’s the tradeoff. They’ll survive a desk drawer, but tossing one loose into a backpack with a laptop is asking for bent corners.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 235 ratings at 4.7 stars, three themes dominate:

1. **Value per notebook** — *“I use one per class. Eighteen lasts me two semesters.”*
2. **Paper quality relative to price** — *“I expected feathering with my fountain pens. Honestly surprised.”*
3. **The 3-style mix** — Teachers and students specifically like having dotted, lined, and blank in one order.

## Where It Falls Short

The perforated pages are the weakest link. They tear out, but not cleanly — the edge frays if you pull at an angle. If you need pristine tear-outs for a bullet journal or planner, buy a different notebook.

Second, the softcover. It’s fine for desktop use, but if you’re a commuter who writes on the train, the cover flexes more than you’d like. A clipboard or hard binder solves this, but it’s an extra step.

Third, no page numbers, no index, no table of contents. This is a blank-slate notebook, not a structured planner. For some, that’s freedom. For others, it’s a missing feature.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Students**: 18 notebooks covers a full course load at a fraction of campus bookstore prices.
- **Bullet journalers who test layouts**: The dotted pages are solid for habit trackers, and the low cost means you won’t feel guilty abandoning a spread.
- **Small teams / office managers**: Stock the supply closet without a PO approval headache.

**Skip it if** you need archival-grade paper (100+ GSM), hard covers, or a notebook that survives a backpack without reinforcement.
