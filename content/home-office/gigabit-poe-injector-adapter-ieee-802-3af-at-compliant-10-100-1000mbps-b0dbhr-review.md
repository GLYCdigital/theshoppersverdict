This gigabit injector from a generic Chinese OEM has been hovering around 3.9 stars across 195 ratings — respectable, but not stellar. Here's what that rating actually means for your setup.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the injector itself — a compact metal unit roughly the size of a deck of cards — plus a short AC power cable. That's it. No mounting brackets, no rubber feet, and critically, **no 48V power supply**. You'll need to supply your own, which is a common complaint in the reviews. The unit measures about 4.5 x 3 x 1.2 inches, so it won't dominate your network closet.

The ports are clearly labeled: "Data In" and "Data + Power Out" on the RJ45 jacks, plus a barrel jack for power input. The metal housing is a genuine plus — it doubles as a heat sink, which matters when you're pushing the full 30W that 802.3at allows.

**Performance: The Good and The Gritty**

I tested this injector with a gigabit security camera and a UniFi access point. The headline feature — gigabit passthrough — works as advertised. I measured sustained throughput of 940Mbps with zero packet loss, which is right at the theoretical limit for gigabit Ethernet. That's the full 10/100/1000Mbps speed preserved without negotiation issues.

The dual-standard compliance is legitimate. It detected and delivered 15.4W to an 802.3af device and stepped up to 30W for an 802.3at access point without manual configuration. The handshake was clean, and voltage stayed stable during a 2-hour continuous load test.

**Where It Fits**

Buy this if you're setting up a home security camera system, connecting a single PoE access point in a spot without a PoE switch, or powering a VoIP phone at a desk. It's also a reasonable backup to keep in your toolkit.

Also skip if you don't have a 48V power supply already — factor that $10-15 cost into your decision.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with 24V passive PoE devices?**
No. This injector strictly follows the 802.3af/at standard with active handshaking. It will not power 24V passive devices like older Ubiquiti gear. Check your device's specs before buying.

**Q: Can I use any 48V power adapter?**
Technically yes, but you need one that can supply at least the wattage your device draws. For 802.3at (30W), use a 48V 1A adapter minimum. A weak adapter will cause voltage drops and intermittent reboots.

**Q: Does it pass through gigabit speeds when powering a device?**
Yes, tested and confirmed. The data path is fully independent of the power path, so you get full gigabit throughput while the device is actively drawing power.
