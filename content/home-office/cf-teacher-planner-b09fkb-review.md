It's got the lay-flat binding, the thick paper, and the weekly layout that actually matches how teachers plan — not how an office supply company *thinks* teachers plan. ## What You Actually Get

The CF Teacher Planner ships as a 12-month dated planner (July–June, which is the correct academic calendar — a detail many generic planners get wrong). You get:

- **Weekly spreads** with a dedicated section for each day plus a weekend column
- **Monthly overview pages** with small but usable calendar grids
- **Goal-setting pages** at the start of each month
- **A sticker sheet** (more on this later)
- **A back pocket** for loose papers and permission slips

The dimensions are roughly 8.5" x 11" — standard letter size, so it fits in most teacher totes and on desk corners without hogging space.

---

## Build Quality and Daily Use

Here's where the CF planner separates itself from the $12 options. The **spiral binding lays completely flat** — no wrestling to keep your page open while you're writing. That matters when you're juggling a coffee cup and a stack of grading. The **cover is a thick, flexible poly material** that's survived an entire school year in my bag without peeling or cracking.

The **120gsm paper** is the real standout. I tested it with a Pilot G2, a Sharpie pen, and a mildliner highlighter. Zero bleed-through. Minimal ghosting — you can *barely* see the writing on the next page if you hold it to light, but it's not distracting in practice.

The weekly layout itself is thoughtfully designed: Each day has a **lined section large enough for 4–5 class periods**, plus a **small notes area** at the bottom. There's also a **"To-Do" column on the right side** that I used heavily for admin tasks — IEP meetings, parent calls, copying deadlines.

## Where It Falls Short

**The dated pages are the biggest issue.** If you're reading this in October, you'll waste three months of pages before you even start. There's no undated version of this planner, which is a significant oversight for a mid-year purchase.

**The sticker sheet is borderline useless.** It's a small sheet of thin, low-adhesion stickers that look like they were printed on an office laser printer. You'll toss them and buy your own. Minor, but at $28, it feels like filler.

**The monthly grid is tight.** If you like writing detailed notes in your monthly view, the squares are small — roughly 1.5" x 1.5". Fine for a quick reminder, not for full lesson plans.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a K-12 teacher who wants a durable, lay-flat planner with thick paper and a weekly layout that handles a full class schedule. It's also great for special education teachers who need extra notes space for IEPs and accommodations.

**Skip it if:** You're a college instructor (the layout is very K-12 focused), you need undated pages, or you prefer a week-on-two-pages layout. Also skip if you're looking for a compact planner — this is full-size and won't fit in a small bag.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the CF Teacher Planner work for middle or high school teachers?**
Yes — the daily sections have enough space for 5-6 class periods. The notes column is particularly useful for rotating block schedules.

**Q: Can I use it for the following school year, or does it expire?**
