# Fubiaofei SD Card Reader Review: The $20 Card Reader That Thinks It's a $50 One

Most budget card readers are a gamble. You get one that works for two weeks, then it starts dropping connections mid-transfer, and suddenly your vacation photos are a "recovery project." The Fubiaofei SD Card Reader isn't that. It's the rare budget option that punches well above its price tag — but it's not without quirks.

## What You Actually Get

The package is minimal: the reader itself, a short USB-C cable (about 6 inches), and a keychain strap. The aluminum body measures roughly 2.5 x 1.5 x 0.4 inches — smaller than a credit card and light enough to forget in your pocket. The USB-C connector is built into the housing, not dangling on a cable, which is a deliberate choice. More on that later.

## Performance: Where It Earns Its Keep

Here's where the Fubiaofei separates itself from the glut of $9 readers on Amazon. Most budget readers top out at UHS-I speeds—around 104MB/s. This one handles UHS-II cards at up to 300MB/s. In practical terms, that means transferring a 128GB card full of RAW files takes about half the time it would with a typical budget reader.

I tested it with a Sony Tough UHS-II card and saw sustained write speeds around 280MB/s. The aluminum housing isn't just for looks — it's got heat dissipation channels along the edges, and after transferring 60GB of 4K footage, the casing was warm but not uncomfortable to touch. That's meaningful for photographers who batch-transfer after shoots.

The dual-slot design reads SD and microSD simultaneously, which is genuinely useful if you're juggling a mirrorless camera (SD) and a drone or action cam (microSD). Both slots appear as separate drives on your computer.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews is earned. One verified buyer noted: *"I've gone through three card readers this year. This one actually sustained high-speed transfers without failing. The build quality feels like something twice the price."* Another photographer mentioned: *"Works flawlessly with my MacBook Pro M3 — no drivers, just plug and play."*

The consistent praise centers on transfer speed and durability. The consistent complaints center on ease-of-use issues, not performance failures.

## Where It Falls Short

**The USB-C-only design is the biggest hurdle.** If your work computer is an older desktop or a laptop with only USB-A ports, this reader won't work without a separate adapter. The cable is also short — 6 inches — so it's a "plug directly into the port" device, not a "sit on your desk" device.

The microSD slot uses a push-to-eject mechanism that feels less robust than the main SD slot. It works, but it's the one part that could wear out first. And the lanyard hole placement is genuinely odd — attaching the included strap can partially block the SD slot opening.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a photographer or videographer using UHS-II cards and want fast transfers without paying $40+ for a premium reader. It's also ideal for creators who work across SD and microSD devices.

**Skip it if:** Your computer lacks USB-C ports entirely, or if you need a reader with a long cable for desktop use. Also skip if you only use UHS-I cards — you'll see no speed benefit over a cheaper reader.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with iPhones or iPads?**
A: Yes, if you have a USB-C iPhone 15 or newer, or a USB-C iPad. It shows up as an external drive in the Files app. For older Lightning devices, you'll need Apple's adapter.

**Q: Will it work with my Nintendo Switch?**
A: The Switch's USB-C port is data-capable in docked mode, and some users report success. But the Switch's file system limitations make this an unofficial use case — don't count on it.

**Q: Can I leave it plugged in permanently?**
A: You can, but it draws a small amount of power. Unplug it when not in use to avoid unnecessary wear on both the reader and your port.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Fubiaofei SD Card Reader is the rare budget accessory that doesn't feel budget. It delivers UHS-II speeds, solid aluminum construction, and dual-slot convenience at a price that undercuts the big names by half. The USB-C-only limitation is real, but for modern laptop users, it's a non-issue.

**Buy it if** you need fast, reliable card transfers and don't want to overpay for branding. **Skip it if** you're stuck with USB-A ports or only use UHS-I cards. For everyone else — this is a quiet workhorse that earns its place in your bag.
