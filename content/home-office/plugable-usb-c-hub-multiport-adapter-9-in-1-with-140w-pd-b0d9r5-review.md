# The 9-in-1 USB-C Hub That Actually Delivers on Its Specs

Most USB-C hubs lie about their specs. I've tested dozens that claim "4K60" but stutter at 30Hz, or advertise "100W PD" but throttle your laptop the moment you plug in a second device. The Plugable 9-in-1 isn't one of those hubs. It's the rare adapter that does what the box says — and a few things better than expected.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the hub itself with a permanently attached USB-C cable, a quick-start guide, and nothing else. No power brick (you supply your own USB-C charger), no carrying case. The housing is matte black aluminum — it looks and feels like a premium piece of gear, matching the finish of a MacBook Pro or a ThinkPad X1. It's compact enough to slip into a laptop sleeve's accessory pocket, though the fixed cable means you can't swap in a longer one if you need more reach.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the Plugable separates itself from the $20 Amazon-brand hubs. The 140W PD 3.1 pass-through is the headline feature, and it works. I ran a 16-inch MacBook Pro M3 Max at full compile load while transferring a 40GB video file through the 10Gbps USB ports — the laptop stayed at 100% charge. That's not something most hubs can say. The two 10Gbps USB-A ports are genuinely fast; moving large files is noticeably quicker than the 5Gbps ports on cheaper alternatives.

The HDMI port outputs true 4K at 60Hz. I connected it to a 43-inch 4K monitor and ran it for six hours straight — zero flicker, no random disconnects. The Gigabit Ethernet port is solid too, giving me full line-speed throughput without the flakiness that plagues cheaper USB Ethernet adapters. The 3.5mm audio jack works, though you'll want a decent pair of headphones; it's a standard DAC, not an audiophile-grade one.

## Where It Falls Short

The fixed six-inch cable is the biggest annoyance. If your laptop's USB-C port is on the right side, you'll have the hub dangling awkwardly or need to route it around the machine. It's a small thing, but it's a daily friction point. The hub also runs warm under sustained load — not alarming, but if you're doing heavy transfers while charging, the aluminum body gets noticeably warm to the touch. That's physics, not a defect, but it's worth knowing.

More frustrating is the absence of an SD card slot. For a 9-in-1 at this price, photographers and content creators will miss it. You're getting 2× USB-A, 1× USB-C data, HDMI, Ethernet, audio, and PD — that's eight functions. The ninth is... well, you're paying for the 140W capability, which is fair, but don't buy this expecting card reader support.

## Who Should Buy This

This hub is for the power user who needs their laptop to stay fully charged while running a full desk setup. If you've got a 14- or 16-inch MacBook Pro, a Dell XPS, or a ThinkPad and you're running an external monitor, Ethernet, and peripherals simultaneously, this is the hub that won't bottleneck you. It's also ideal for anyone who regularly moves large files — video editors, developers, or designers working with big assets.

Skip it if you're on a budget, only need basic connectivity, or absolutely require an SD card slot. A $30 hub will handle a mouse, keyboard, and HDMI — but it won't charge your laptop at full speed or move files at 10Gbps.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this hub work with a 65W laptop charger?**
A: Yes. The 140W rating is the maximum pass-through, not a requirement. A 65W charger will work fine — your laptop just won't get more than 65W. The hub passes through whatever your charger provides, minus a small overhead for the hub's own operation.

**Q: Will it work with a USB-C phone or tablet?**
A: It'll work with devices that support USB-C alt mode, like an iPad Pro or a Samsung Galaxy Tab. You'll get video output and data, but don't expect the full 140W PD — the hub negotiates with whatever the connected device supports.

**Q: Is the Ethernet port gigabit or fast Ethernet?**
A: It's full Gigabit (1000Mbps). I tested it at sustained speeds and it held line-rate without drops. It's powered by a Realtek chipset, which has solid driver support on Windows, macOS, and Linux.
