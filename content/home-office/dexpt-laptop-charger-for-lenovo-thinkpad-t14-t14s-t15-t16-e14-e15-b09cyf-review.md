Losing a Lenovo ThinkPad charger is a $60 mistake. The OEM 65W brick costs that much, and the knockoffs on Amazon are a lottery — some work, some buzz, some die in a month. The Dexpt charger at $15.99 sits in the middle, and after digging through 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, here's what you need to know before you buy.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the USB-C wall adapter itself (the block) plus a detachable 6-foot USB-C to USB-C cable. The connector on the laptop end is right-angled, which matters more than you'd think — it hugs the back of a T14s or T16 so you can push the laptop flush against a wall or use it on your lap without a stiff cable jabbing your arm.

The brick is compact: roughly 2.5 x 1.5 x 1 inches, with folding prongs. It's noticeably lighter than Lenovo's stock charger — about half the weight, which makes it a genuine travel candidate. The cable detaches, so if you fray a cord, you replace just the cable, not the whole unit.

**Performance and real-world use**

This is a 65W USB-C Power Delivery charger. That's the correct wattage for the T14, T14s, T15, and T16 (both AMD and Intel versions), plus the E14/E15 if you need a spare. It hits the full 65W profile, not a crippled 45W that trickle-charges while you work.

In practice, buyers report it charges a drained T14 to about 50% in 45 minutes to an hour while the laptop is in use. That's consistent with the OEM charger's behavior. Heat is controlled — the brick gets warm, not hot, under sustained load. No one in the reviews reports buzzing, coil whine, or the charger cutting out mid-session.

One thing to note: this is a single-port charger. There's no USB-A port for your phone, so if you're used to charging your earbuds or phone from your laptop brick, you'll need a second adapter. That's a tradeoff for the small size.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme in reviews is value and reliability. One verified buyer writes, "I was skeptical of a $16 charger for a $1,500 laptop, but it charges exactly like the original and runs cooler." Another notes the detachable cable saved them: "My dog chewed the cord, and I just bought a replacement cable instead of a whole new charger."

Travelers specifically call out the folding prongs and light weight. Multiple reviewers mention it packs flat and handles international voltage (100-240V) without issue.

**Where it falls short**

Honestly, the flaws are minor but real. The folding prongs are reported as slightly wiggly — not loose enough to fail, but you'll notice the play when you plug it in. A few reviewers mentioned the right-angle connector can be awkward if your ThinkPad's USB-C port is on the left side and you prefer the cable to run right — check your port orientation before you commit.

The lack of a second port is the biggest functional gap. If you regularly need to charge a phone and laptop from one outlet, this isn't the charger for you.

**Who should buy this**

- **Travelers** who want a lighter, more packable alternative to the OEM brick
- **Home-office users** who need a second charger to leave at the desk and don't want to pay Lenovo prices
- **Anyone who's killed a cable** — the detachable design means cheaper future fixes

**Skip it if** you need multi-device charging from one brick, or if warranty paranoia means you won't trust anything with "Dexpt" on the label for your $2,000 ThinkPad.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, full-power 65W charger for your ThinkPad at a third of the OEM price. **Skip it if** you need a multi-port hub or demand OEM-branded components. This is a solid, honest replacement that does exactly what it claims. 4.6/5.
