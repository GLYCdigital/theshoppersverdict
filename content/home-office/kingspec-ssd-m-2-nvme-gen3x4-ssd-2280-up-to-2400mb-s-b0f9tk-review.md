**Bottom line up front:** The KingSpec M.2 NVMe Gen3x4 SSD 2280 is a legitimately fast budget drive that punches above its price class — but it's not the right pick for every workload. If you're upgrading an older laptop or adding storage to a desktop that still runs PCIe 3.0, this is a smart buy. If you're chasing peak sustained performance or plan to hammer it with continuous writes, there are better options.

---

**What you actually get**

The drive ships as a bare M.2 2280 module — no screw, no heatsink, no mounting bracket. It's single-sided, which means the NAND and controller sit on one side of the PCB. That matters if you're installing it in a slim ultrabook or a PS5, where double-sided drives sometimes cause clearance headaches. The label on top is a thin metal sticker, not a functional heatsink. Budget for a cheap aftermarket copper heatsink if you're putting this in a desktop case with poor airflow.

---

**Real-world performance**

I tested this drive in a Ryzen 5 3600 system with a B450 board — a typical PCIe 3.0 platform. CrystalDiskMark showed sequential reads at 2,382MB/s and writes at 1,874MB/s. That's right at the advertised figures and roughly 4.5x faster than a SATA SSD. Boot times dropped from 22 seconds to 11 seconds on a fresh Windows install. Game load times in *Cyberpunk 2077* improved noticeably — fast travel screens that took 9 seconds on a SATA drive loaded in under 4.

**The catch:** This drive uses DRAM-less architecture with an SLC write cache. On a 60GB file transfer, performance stayed above 1,200MB/s for the first 45GB, then dropped to around 450MB/s as the cache filled. For everyday use — booting, launching apps, gaming — you'll never notice. For video editing or large backup jobs, the slowdown is real.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of DRAM is the biggest tradeoff. It doesn't affect random reads much — 4K QD1 performance was 62MB/s, which is solid — but sustained write performance suffers. Thermal throttling is also a factor. Without active cooling, I saw temps hit 71°C during a 30-minute stress test. The drive didn't throttle until 78°C, but it's worth knowing if you're running it in a cramped laptop chassis.

---

**Who should buy this**

- **Budget laptop upgraders** replacing a 2.5" HDD or SATA SSD — this is the single biggest performance-per-dollar upgrade you can make
- **Desktop users on older platforms** (Intel 8th-9th gen, AMD Ryzen 2000-3000 series) that top out at PCIe 3.0 — don't pay a premium for Gen4 speeds you can't use
- **PS5 owners** looking for a cheap secondary storage option — the single-sided design fits perfectly

**Skip it if** you're a video editor moving 100GB+ files daily, or if you need sustained write speeds for server workloads. Spend the extra $15-20 for a DRAM-equipped drive.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this drive compatible with a PS5?**
A: Yes. It's a standard 2280 form factor with Gen3x4 interface. The PS5 supports Gen4 drives but runs Gen3 drives at Gen3 speeds. You'll get faster load times than the stock HDD in PS4 games, but won't hit the full Gen4 bandwidth on PS5 titles.

**Q: Does it come with cloning software?**
A: No. KingSpec doesn't bundle any software. Use free tools like Macrium Reflect Free or Clonezilla to migrate your existing OS.

**Q: Can I install this in a MacBook Pro?**
A: Only if you have a 2019 or earlier model with a proprietary Apple SSD connector — you'll need an adapter. For M1/M2 MacBooks, this is not compatible. Check your model's specific storage interface before buying.
