If you've ever bought "neon" cardstock that turned out to be a washed-out pastel, the Astrobrights Mega Collection in Neon Pink will be a revelation. This is the real deal — a shade that practically glows under office lighting. But this isn't just about the color. It's about whether 320 sheets of 65 lb cover stock actually earn their place in your workspace. After testing, the answer is a qualified yes.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains 320 sheets of 65 lb cover weight cardstock (176 gsm), which is the sweet spot for projects that need structure without being board-stiff. Each sheet measures the standard 8.5 x 11 inches. As shown in the product image, the color is uniformly saturated — no white edges, no streaking, no light patches. The paper has a slightly textured felt finish that takes ink well but doesn't have that glossy coating that makes pens skip.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this through a Brother laser printer, an HP inkjet, and a Cricut cutting machine. The laser output was crisp with zero curling — even at full-page coverage. Inkjet ink dried quickly without smudging, though the neon color does slightly alter how lighter ink colors appear on top. The Cricut cut cleanly on standard cardstock settings; the paper holds creases well for card-making and folds without cracking along the scored line.

The 65 lb weight is substantial enough for DIY wedding invitations, classroom flashcards, or signage that needs to stand up in a holder. It's not heavy enough for business cards you'd hand out — for that, you'd want 110 lb index stock — but for everything else in the cardstock category, this performs admirably.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. One recurring theme: "The color is actually neon — it's almost blindingly bright, which is exactly what I wanted." Another buyer noted, "I've bought cheaper cardstock before and ended up wasting half of it on printer jams. This fed through my printer without a single issue."

The most common complaint isn't about quality but quantity — several reviewers mentioned they didn't realize they were getting 320 sheets and were pleasantly surprised by the bulk value.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires acknowledging two tradeoffs. First, this neon pink is *aggressively* bright. It's fantastic for posters, labels, or accent pieces, but printing a multi-page document on this will strain eyes. Don't buy it for readability — buy it for visibility.

Second, the slight paper texture means if you're doing extremely fine detail work with a die cutter (anything under half an inch), edges can fray slightly. For standard shapes, letters, and tags, it's fine. For microscopic intricate cuts, you'll want a smoother finish.

## FAQ

**Will this work in my home printer?**
Yes, for both inkjet and laser printers. The 65 lb weight is well within standard paper path specifications. It won't jam unless your printer is already prone to jamming on thicker stock.

**Is this cardstock acid-free and archival safe?**
Astrobrights uses acid-free paper in this line, making it safe for scrapbooks and projects meant to last. It's not explicitly labeled as archival, so for museum-grade storage, use protective sleeves.

**How does it compare to the 8.5 x 11 Astrobrights regular weight?**
The regular weight is 24 lb (90 gsm) — much thinner, more like standard copy paper. This 65 lb cover weight is roughly 2.5 times thicker. For projects that need to stand up or be handled repeatedly, the cardstock is the right choice.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need an intense, reliable neon cardstock in bulk for projects where visibility matters more than subtlety. The per-sheet cost is roughly half of what you'd pay at a big-box craft store, and the quality is consistent from the first sheet to the 320th.

**Skip it if** you need muted colors, want heavyweight 110 lb stock, or expect to print long text documents on this. This is a specialty paper for a specific job, and it nails that job.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Half a star deducted for the texture limitation on fine cuts and the fact that the neon intensity limits its use cases. But for what it's designed to do, it's the best in its class.
