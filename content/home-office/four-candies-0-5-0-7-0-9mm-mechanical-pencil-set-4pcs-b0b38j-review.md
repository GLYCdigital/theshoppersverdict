**Three lead gauges. Four pencils. One honest take on whether this set earns a spot in your daily carry.**

Most mechanical pencil sets force you to pick a lane — fine drafting or bold sketching. The Four Candies Meta set sidesteps that compromise by giving you all three common lead sizes in one kit: 0.5mm for precise writing, 0.7mm for everyday notes, and 0.9mm for heavier lines and shading work. That's genuinely useful, not a gimmick.

**What's actually in the box**

You get four pencils total: one 0.5mm, one 0.7mm, and two 0.9mm. The extra 0.9mm makes sense — it's the lead size most people burn through fastest when sketching or taking margin notes. Each pencil has a metal clip, knurled grip section, and a twist-off cap that hides extra lead storage inside. The body is aluminum with a matte metallic finish, and the whole set weighs in light enough for pocket carry without feeling flimsy in hand.

**Build quality and real-world handling**

The knurled grip is the standout feature here. It's aggressive enough to lock your fingers in place but not so rough that it chews up your skin during a three-hour exam or long journaling session. The clips are spring-loaded and actually functional — they slide over notebook covers and shirt pockets without bending out of shape.

During testing, the 0.5mm pencil delivered consistent, crisp lines with minimal lead breakage — the internal clutch holds the lead firmly. The 0.7mm is the sweet spot for general use, offering a good balance between durability and fine detail. The 0.9mm handles shading and bold strokes well, though the advance mechanism feels marginally less precise than the thinner siblings. It's a minor quibble, not a dealbreaker.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 229 ratings (4.6 stars), the recurring themes are the grip comfort and the convenience of having multiple lead sizes. One verified buyer noted, "The knurled grip is perfect — I have sweaty hands and these don't slip at all." **Where it falls short**

Honest downsides? No integrated eraser — every pencil in this set requires you to carry a separate one. At this price point, that's a noticeable omission, especially for students. The metallic finish is a fingerprint magnet; if you're obsessive about aesthetics, you'll be wiping these down regularly. And while the 0.9mm is fine for general use, serious artists may find the lead advance notchy compared to dedicated drafting pencils like the Rotring 800.

**Who should buy this**

This set is ideal for:
- **Students** juggling math homework (0.5mm), essay writing (0.7mm), and diagram sketching (0.9mm) without buying three separate pencils
- **Professionals** in engineering, architecture, or design who need quick lead-size switching during site visits or client meetings
- **Journalers and bullet journalers** who want a reliable, comfortable pencil that won't cramp their hand during long entries

Skip it if you only ever use one lead size — you'll get better value from a single premium pencil at a similar price.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the lead storage in the cap hold all sizes?**
A: Yes, each cap holds two spare leads of its corresponding size. The 0.9mm pencils store 0.9mm lead, the 0.7mm stores 0.7mm, and so on. You can't mix sizes.

**Q: Are these refillable with standard leads?**
A: Absolutely. They take any standard mechanical pencil lead — Pentel, Pilot, Uni, or generic brands all work. No proprietary refills here.

**Q: How does the 0.9mm handle for shading?**
A: It's decent for mid-tone shading and bold lines, but it's not a replacement for a proper sketching pencil. The fixed sleeve means you can't expose a long lead for broad strokes, which limits its versatility for artists.
