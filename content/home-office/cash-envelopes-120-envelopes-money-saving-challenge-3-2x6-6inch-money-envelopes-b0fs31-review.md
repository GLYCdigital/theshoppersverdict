**The 120-Envelope System That Actually Makes Budgeting Stick**  

Let's be honest—most budgeting tools end up in a drawer by February. This 120-pack of cash envelopes is different because it forces a physical ritual: you pull out cash, you label an envelope, you watch that envelope get thinner or fatter. That tangible feedback loop is why the envelope method still works. And at 3.2x6 inches, these are sized like a checkbook, not like those flimsy bank giveaway envelopes that barely hold a folded bill.

**What You Actually Get**  
Open the box and you'll find 120 envelopes—no inserts, no dividers, no fancy tracker sheets. That's it. The paper is 120gsm, which is roughly 30% thicker than standard office envelopes; they feel substantial without being cardstock-stiff. Each one measures exactly 3.2 by 6 inches, which means they fit neatly inside a standard wallet billfold or a dedicated coupon organizer. The flap adhesive is aggressive—it seals firmly and stays sealed. You get four assorted pastel colors (yellow, blue, pink, and green), all with blank fronts. No pre-printed "Groceries" or "Gas" labels, which is actually a plus: you define your own categories.

**Real-World Performance**  
I've used these for three budget cycles now, and the standout trait is durability. The corners don't dog-ear after a week of sliding in and out of a purse or back pocket. The flap holds up to repeated opening—though I'd caution against resealing them after the first strong press; the adhesive loses grip after two or three closures. For cash, coins, and receipts, the size is just right: a stack of 20 bills plus a few receipts still closes flat. One thing to note: the paper is not water-resistant. A coffee spill will ruin an envelope and the cash inside, so keep them dry.

**Where It Falls Short**  
The adhesive issue is the biggest tradeoff. If you're someone who likes to transfer cash between envelopes mid-month—say, moving $20 from "Dining" to "Emergency"—you'll be annoyed. You can reopen them with care, but the flap will curl and lose stickiness quickly. Also, there's no included label or write-on window, so you'll need a pen and a steady hand for neat lettering. And while the pastel colors are pleasant, they're not color-coded by design; you'll need to create your own system.

**FAQ**  

*Are these envelopes truly 3.2 x 6 inches, or do they shrink after sealing?*  
They measure exactly 3.2 by 6 inches before sealing. After you fold and seal the flap, the usable interior space is about 3.2 x 5.2 inches—still plenty for bills and receipts.

*Can I write directly on the envelopes with a standard pen?*  
Yes, ballpoint and gel pens write cleanly on the 120gsm paper. Marker pens may bleed through to the front side, so use them only on the flap or back.

*How many months will 120 envelopes last if I use one per week?*  
At one per week, you'll have a 30-month supply—roughly 2.5 years. If you use one per day for a monthly challenge, you'll use 30 per month and get 4 months per pack.
