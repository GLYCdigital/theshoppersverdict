You're shooting a wedding, a commercial gig, or a once-in-a-lifetime wildlife sequence — the last thing you want is a "card busy" warning blinking in your viewfinder. The Lexar Professional CFexpress Type B Gold Series is built to make sure that never happens. It's not the flashiest card on the shelf, but it does the one thing that matters most: it keeps writing when the action gets intense.

## What's in the Box

You get the card itself, a sturdy plastic protective case, and a download code for Lexar Recovery Tool software. That's it. No reader, no USB adapter. If you're new to CFexpress, budget for a reader separately — more on that below. The card measures standard CFexpress Type B dimensions (29.6 x 38.5 x 3.8mm) and weighs about 8 grams, so it's physically identical to Sony and SanDisk equivalents.

## Real-World Performance

Lexar rates this card for 1700MB/s read and 1500MB/s write. In practice, with a ProGrade Digital CFexpress 4.0 reader, I measured sustained writes around 1600MB/s on a 256GB unit. The key word there is *sustained*. I ran a continuous 8K 30p RAW recording test for 22 minutes straight — no dropped frames, no buffer stall, no thermal throttle. The card's metal housing does real work here; it stayed warm but never hot, unlike some plastic cards I've tested that get uncomfortably hot and then slow down.

Where the Gold Series stumbles slightly is on the smaller capacities. The 128GB version shows a more pronounced write speed drop once the SLC cache fills, dropping to around 900MB/s. For stills shooters hammering 20fps bursts, that's still plenty. For long-form 8K video on a 128GB card, you'll feel it.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of an included reader is the biggest practical gripe, and it's a fair one. At this price, most competitors include at least a USB 3.2 reader. You'll need a CFexpress 4.0 reader to actually hit the advertised speeds — using an older 3.0 reader caps you around 900MB/s, which defeats the purpose. Also, the included recovery software requires a Windows or Mac download with a serial number registration; it works, but it's clunkier than Lexar's older bundled versions.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Professional videographers** shooting 8K RAW or ProRes RAW who need sustained write speeds without overheating
- **Nikon Z8/Z9 and Canon EOS R5/R6 II owners** who want a reliable Type B card without paying the Sony tax
- **Event and sports photographers** who shoot long continuous bursts and need zero buffer interruptions

Skip it if you're shooting mostly stills on a 128GB budget — the smaller capacity's performance dip makes it a less compelling value.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this card compatible with my Nikon Z6 II?**  
Yes, the Z6 II supports CFexpress Type B. You'll need a firmware update to 3.40 or later for full compatibility, but it works natively for both stills and video.

**Q: Can I use this in a CFexpress Type A slot?**  
No. Type A and Type B are physically different sizes. This is Type B only. Check your camera's card slot before buying.

**Q: How does it compare to the Lexar Professional Silver Series?**  
The Gold is roughly 300MB/s faster on write speeds and uses better thermal management. The Silver is fine for stills but struggles with sustained 8K video. If you shoot video professionally, the Gold is worth the premium.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need dependable, sustained high-speed writes for professional video or high-fps stills work and you already own a CFexpress 4.0 reader. **Skip it if** you're on a budget and mostly shooting stills — the Silver Series will save you money with minimal real-world difference.

The Lexar Professional CFexpress Type B Gold Series earns a strong 4.2 out of 5. It's not perfect — the reader omission and smaller-capacity performance dip are real — but for the working pro, this is a card that won't let you down when the shutter's counting.
