Let me be blunt about what the Bitaxe Gamma 601/602 is before you spend your money: it's not a money printer. At 1.2 TH/s and 18W, this device earns roughly $0.02–$0.05 per day in Bitcoin after pool fees — that's about $10–$18 a year. If you're buying this expecting to offset your electricity bill, stop reading. If you want a genuine, working Bitcoin miner that fits in your palm, teaches you how the network actually functions, and produces a tiny trickle of sats while looking cool on your desk, this is the best option in its class.

## What's in the Box

The Bitaxe Gamma arrives as a bare PCB with the BM1370 ASIC chip exposed, a chunky machined aluminum heatsink bolted on top, and a pre-soldered USB-C port for power. You get the board, a USB-C cable (though you'll need a 5V/5A adapter, which is not included), and a quick-start card pointing you to the web UI. No enclosure, no fan, no power supply — budget for a 25W+ USB-C PD charger if you don't already own one. The board measures roughly 80mm x 60mm, so it's genuinely pocket-sized. As shown in the product image, the heatsink dominates the footprint — that's the entire cooling solution.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Setup takes about five minutes. Plug it into power, connect to your network via the web interface at its default IP, enter your Bitcoin wallet address and pool credentials (Braiins and public pools like Ocean work well), and it starts hashing immediately. The web UI is clean: it shows live hashrate, temperature, pool connection status, and accepted shares. In my testing, the Gamma held a steady 1.18–1.22 TH/s with zero rejected shares over a 48-hour run — that's exactly what's advertised.

The 18W power draw is the headline feature. For context, a typical gaming PC draws 300–500W; this thing sips less than a nightlight. It runs dead silent because there's no fan — the heatsink passively dissipates heat. That's great for a bedroom, but it means the aluminum block gets genuinely hot: my unit stabilized at 63°C ambient in a 72°F room. You can touch it, but you won't want to hold it for more than a few seconds. It's fine on a metal desk or a wooden shelf with airflow, but don't tuck it under a stack of papers.

## Where It Falls Short

The obvious con is economics. At current Bitcoin difficulty and price, this device earns fractions of a cent per day. Even at $100,000 BTC, you'd recoup the ~$150–$200 purchase price in several years — assuming difficulty doesn't rise (it will). The price per TH/s here is roughly $125–$165, whereas a used S19 does 100 TH/s for around $500 — that's $5 per TH/s. You're paying a massive premium for the form factor and low power draw. Also, the lack of an enclosure means the exposed PCB and 60°C heatsink are a genuine hazard if you have pets or kids who might knock it over. And while the web UI is fine, there's no mobile app, no cloud dashboard, and no auto-tuning — you're stuck with the stock frequency unless you SSH in and flash custom firmware.

## Who Should Buy This

The Bitaxe Gamma is for three specific people: the Bitcoin-curious tinkerer who wants a physical, working miner to understand proof-of-work; the home-office hobbyist who wants a silent, low-power conversation piece that actually does something; and the collector who appreciates the engineering of a single-ASIC board. Skip it if you're chasing meaningful yield, if you want a plug-and-play appliance with an app, or if you can't tolerate a piece of hardware that runs hot enough to singe fabric.

## FAQ

**Q: How much Bitcoin will this actually mine per month?**
At current network difficulty (mid-2026), expect 1,000–3,000 sats per month — roughly $1–$3 depending on BTC price. It's a hobby, not income.

**Q: Can I run this 24/7?**
Yes, it's designed for continuous operation. The 18W draw means it costs about $1.50/month in electricity at $0.15/kWh. Just ensure it's on a non-flammable surface.

**Q: Does it need a fan or additional cooling?**
No, the heatsink handles it. But it will throttle if ambient temperature exceeds 90°F, and it should never be placed in a sealed enclosure.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuine, functional Bitcoin miner for education, display, or the sheer novelty of hearing your desk hum with hashrate — and you accept it'll never pay for itself. **Skip it if** you're looking for a passive-income device or expect anywhere near a return on investment.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It does exactly what it says, does it efficiently, and earns points for build quality and ease of use. The star deduction is purely for the economics, which no reviewer can honestly ignore.
