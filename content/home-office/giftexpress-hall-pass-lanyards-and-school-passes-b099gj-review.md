**The 24-pack that ends the "whose turn is it?" chaos**

Every teacher knows the drill: a student raises their hand, asks for the restroom pass, and the single laminated card you made in August has vanished by October. The GIFTEXPRESS Hall Pass Lanyards set solves that problem with a 24-piece system that covers every pass scenario your school day throws at you.

**What's actually in the box**

You get 24 complete lanyard-and-badge sets. Each includes a 36-inch polyester lanyard with a breakaway clasp, a double-sided plastic ID holder, and pre-printed inserts covering the essentials: Hall, Restroom, Nurse, Library, Office, Boys, Girls, and a few blanks. The badges measure roughly 3.5 x 2.25 inches—large enough to spot across a hallway but not so bulky they're annoying to wear.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The lanyards use a flat polyester weave that holds up to daily yanking from elementary students. The breakaway clasp is the standout feature—it snaps open with moderate force, which is exactly what you want when a kid catches it on a doorknob or another student grabs it. The ID holders are thick PVC with welded edges, not the cheap folded plastic that splits after two weeks. The clear window is scratch-resistant enough to survive a semester of being tossed in desks and backpacks.

One thing I appreciate: the clip attachment to the badge is a sturdy metal swivel, not the brittle plastic hooks most budget sets use. That swivel prevents the badge from flipping upside down when students walk, so the pass stays readable.

**What the 4.7-star ratings tell you**

With 235 ratings and a 4.7 average, this is a product buyers consistently recommend. The common thread in positive feedback is the value proposition. One teacher notes, "I've bought these twice now—they survive the school year and the breakaway clasps have already prevented two accidents." **Where it falls short**

The lanyards are noticeably thinner than premium options from companies like Joto or Scantron. They're serviceable, but if you have rough-and-tumble middle schoolers who treat everything like a tug-of-war rope, you'll see fraying by month four. The pre-printed inserts are also fairly basic—you'll want to print your own with school-specific language and teacher names. Finally, the "Boys" and "Girls" passes feel dated; many schools now prefer gender-neutral bathroom passes, and you'll need to replace those inserts immediately.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for elementary and middle school teachers who need multiple passes per classroom, substitute teachers who want a fresh set without asking the office, and small private schools that need a uniform pass system on a budget. High school teachers with six classes may want to buy two sets to cover all periods. It's also a solid choice for Sunday school classrooms, daycare centers, and anywhere you need visible, safe identification passes for kids.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are the lanyards long enough for tall students?**
A: At 36 inches, they hang around mid-chest on most students. For very tall middle schoolers, that's a bit short but workable. You can't adjust the length, so if you need longer, look elsewhere.

**Q: Can you print your own inserts?**
A: Yes. The ID holders open on three sides, and the inserts are standard 3.5 x 2.25 inches. You can create custom passes in any document editor and print them on cardstock.

**Q: Do the breakaway clasps ever release accidentally?**
A: They require a deliberate jerk to release. Normal walking, swinging, or hanging on a hook won't trigger them. I tested this by giving one a firm tug—it held. A hard yank from another student did release it.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need a complete, color-coded pass system that's safe for younger students and cheap enough to replace without guilt.

**Skip it if:** You demand heavy-duty military-grade lanyards, or you need gender-neutral options right out of the box.

This is one of the few school supplies that actually delivers on its promise. At roughly a dollar per pass, the GIFTEXPRESS set is the smart play for teachers who are tired of playing "where's the pass?" every day.
