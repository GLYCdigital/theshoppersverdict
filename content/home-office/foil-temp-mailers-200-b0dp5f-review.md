## Honest Shortfalls

Here's where the rose-tinted glasses come off. The paper exterior is not water-resistant. If your package sits in rain or on a damp porch, the foil layer won't help — moisture soaks through and can compromise the thermal barrier. That's a real limitation for outdoor shipment scenarios.

Also, the foil layer is visible at the seams and edges, which looks a bit unfinished. If you're shipping to retail customers who care about unboxing aesthetics, plan to use an outer poly mailer. That's an extra cost and an extra step.

Finally, the 200-count box is bulk. There's no resealable inner bag — once you open the outer carton, you're storing loose mailers. They're flat, not gusseted, so they don't take up much space, but organization purists will want a container.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these be reused?**
Technically yes, but the self-seal adhesive is single-use. You'd need tape to reseal, and the foil layer loses effectiveness after creasing. Treat them as single-ship.

**Q: Do they work for cold shipping without a gel pack?**
No. The foil only slows temperature change — it doesn't create cold. You still need a cold source (gel pack, dry ice for frozen goods) inside. These extend the cold window, they don't replace it.

**Q: What's the maximum weight they can hold?**
The structure handles up to 5-6 lbs safely, but the bubble layer becomes less effective above that. For heavier items, you're better off with a rigid box.
