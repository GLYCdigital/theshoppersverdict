**The 16-inch screen is the whole story here.** That's not a criticism — it's the reason you're looking at this HP in the first place. The 1920x1200 resolution with a 16:10 aspect ratio gives you roughly 11% more vertical workspace than a standard 16:9 panel. For anyone living in spreadsheets, code editors, or long documents, that extra height matters more than pixel count. The touch layer works reliably, though the glossy finish means you'll be wiping smudges off the screen regularly.

**What you actually get:** The laptop itself, a 65W USB-C charger, and the standard paperwork. No stylus in the box despite the touchscreen, and no sleeve. The chassis is all-plastic but feels denser than the price point suggests — the hinge is stiff enough to hold the screen at any angle without wobble, though opening it one-handed takes deliberate effort.

**Performance is where this gets complicated.** Under typical office loads — 15 browser tabs, Slack, a video call, and a spreadsheet open simultaneously — the system stays responsive. The Fast Charge marketing holds up in practice; multiple reviewers note hitting 50% charge in under an hour. But the battery life itself is middling. Real-world reports consistently land between 5 and 6 hours of mixed use, which is underwhelming for a modern laptop. The power brick is compact, but you'll be reaching for it before the workday ends.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The screen size and touch responsiveness get the most love. One verified buyer wrote, *"The 16:10 ratio is a game changer for my law practice — I can see a full contract page and my notes simultaneously."* Another noted, *"Charging is genuinely fast. I plug in during lunch and get through the afternoon."* Build quality also earns respect, with several long-term owners reporting no hinge issues or keyboard problems after six months of daily use.

**Where it falls short:** The 720p webcam is the biggest compromise. On video calls, you'll look soft and slightly washed out — acceptable for internal meetings, rough for client-facing work. The speakers are serviceable but lack bass, and they get harsh at high volume. A handful of reviewers report the touchscreen occasionally registers phantom touches during charging, though this seems tied to specific third-party chargers rather than the included adapter. The keyboard is comfortable for long typing sessions, but the lack of a backlight on the base model is a genuine oversight for anyone working in dim lighting.

**Who should buy this:** 
- **The spreadsheet warrior** who needs maximum vertical screen space without jumping to a 17-inch monster
- **The touch enthusiast** who genuinely uses touch gestures and wants a larger canvas
- **The frequent traveler** who values quick top-ups over marathon battery endurance

**Skip it if:** You need all-day battery, do serious photo/video editing (color accuracy is decent but not professional-grade), or spend hours on video calls where webcam quality matters.

**FAQ:**

**Is the 2K display sharp enough for text?** Yes. At 16 inches, 1920x1200 gives you about 145 PPI — noticeably crisper than standard 1080p laptops. Text rendering is clean, though it won't match a 4K panel for fine detail.

**Can this handle light gaming?** Integrated graphics only. It'll run older titles or esports games at low settings, but this is not a gaming machine. Stick to work tasks and you'll be happy.

**Does the touchscreen work with a stylus?** Yes, but there's no active pen support — you'll need a capacitive stylus, which lacks pressure sensitivity. Fine for navigation, not for drawing.

**The Verdict: 3.7/5 — Buy it if** you prioritize a large, touch-friendly display and don't mind average battery life. **Skip it if** you need a webcam that flatters you or all-day endurance. This is a solid workhorse, not a flagship — and priced accordingly, it delivers reasonable value for its strengths.
