# 67XL Ink Cartridges Black/Color Combo Pack: The Value Play That Actually Works

Let's be direct: OEM HP 67XL ink is a ripoff. You're paying premium prices for ink that costs pennies to manufacture, and HP's proprietary chip games make third-party options a gamble. This combo pack from a compatible manufacturer sits in that gamble zone — and surprisingly, most buyers are winning.

**What's in the box:** Two cartridges — one black (rated ~300 pages) and one tri-color cyan/magenta/yellow (rated ~315 pages). The XL designation means these are the high-yield versions, not the standard 67s that choke out after 120-150 pages. You get exactly what's pictured: two sealed cartridges with protective packaging, no messy installation guides needed.

## Build and Installation

These cartridges follow the standard HP 67 form factor — they slot into the same carriage as OEM units. The chip contacts were clean on my units, and installation took under two minutes. Here's the thing shoppers worry about: the "genuine HP cartridge" error screen. On my HP Envy 6055e, the printer recognized them immediately with zero warnings. That said, one Amazon reviewer noted, "Worked fine on my DeskJet 2755 but took three tries to seat properly — the latch didn't click on the first attempt."

The protective tape situation deserves a mention. The orange pull-tab is where some buyers hit trouble. One verified purchaser wrote: "The tape on the color cartridge ripped halfway out and I had to fish it out with tweezers. Messy but no permanent damage." If you buy these, pull the tab slowly and at an angle, not straight up.

## Print Quality: The Honest Breakdown

Black text is where these shine. Crisp, deep, and consistent across a 50-page document test — no streaking, no gray fade. For everyday printing — school assignments, shipping labels, invoices — this is indistinguishable from OEM.

Color is where you need to manage expectations. The tri-color cartridge produces accurate blues and greens, but reds come out slightly muted and skin tones lean warm. A reviewer who prints photos regularly said: "Fine for homework and charts. Don't print family photos with these — faces look orange-tinted." If your workflow is documents with occasional color accents, you'll be satisfied. If you're a scrapbooker or print marketing materials, stick with OEM.

Finally, there's no recycling program — these end up in your trash, not a mail-back envelope. Environmentally conscious buyers should factor that in.
