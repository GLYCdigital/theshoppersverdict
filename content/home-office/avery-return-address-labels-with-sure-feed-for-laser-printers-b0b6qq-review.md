## Are These the Last Return Address Labels You'll Ever Buy?

If you've ever watched a sheet of labels crumple into a printer's rollers, you know the frustration. Avery's Return Address Labels with Sure Feed claim to solve that problem with a patented strip along the top edge. After running multiple sheets through two different laser printers, I can confirm — the technology works. But there's more to these labels than just jam prevention.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains 20 sheets of 1" x 2-5/8" labels, 30 per sheet, for 600 total labels. That's enough for roughly a year of personal mail or a few months of small business shipping. The labels are engineered for laser printers specifically — don't try these in an inkjet, they're formulated for toner adhesion and heat resistance.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The "Sure Feed" mechanism is a reinforced strip across the top of each sheet that prevents the labels from separating from the carrier during printing. It's not marketing hype. I deliberately loaded a sheet upside down, slightly curled, and the printer still grabbed it cleanly. The labels themselves have a matte finish that takes toner exceptionally well — edges stay sharp, and there's no flaking or smearing even when you handle them immediately after printing.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The adhesive. One reviewer noted, *"I've used these on wedding invitations that went through the mail twice (save the date and the actual invite) and they never peeled or curled."* Another mentioned, *"The labels don't lift at the corners like the store-brand ones I used before."* This tracks with my testing — the permanent adhesive bonds aggressively to standard #10 envelopes and holds firm on poly mailers.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. **30 labels per sheet is inefficient.** If you're mailing holiday cards or shipping products, you'll waste a lot of label real estate. Avery sells 80-per-sheet versions, but those don't have Sure Feed technology — you're paying a premium for the anti-jam feature.

**The template situation is annoying.** There's no printed template sheet in the box. You'll need to download Avery Design & Print (free but clunky) or find the Word template online. It's a minor hurdle, but if you're not tech-savvy, it's a genuine barrier.

**One more thing:** These are laser-only. If you own an inkjet printer, skip this product entirely. The coating won't absorb liquid ink properly and you'll get smudged labels.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Small business owners** who ship daily and need labels that won't jam mid-run
- **Event planners** printing address labels for invitations in bulk
- **Anyone who's been burned by cheap labels that peel or jam** — the slight price premium is worth the reliability

**Skip these if:** You only print labels twice a year (buy cheaper ones), you use an inkjet printer, or you need bulk quantities — the 30-per-sheet format will frustrate you.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these in a Brother or HP laser printer?**
A: Yes. I tested them in both a Brother HL-L2350DW and an HP LaserJet Pro M404dn. Both handled them flawlessly. Just select "Labels" or "Heavy Paper" in your print settings.

**Q: Do the labels work on poly mailers and bubble mailers?**
A: Yes, but press firmly for 5-10 seconds. The adhesive is strong, but smooth plastic surfaces need extra pressure to bond fully.

**Q: Will the labels survive a return trip through the mail?**
A: Yes. The adhesive won't degrade from postal machinery, and the matte finish resists smudging from handling.
