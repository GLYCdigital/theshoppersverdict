**What's in the box:** The laptop, a 65W USB-C power adapter, and the standard Lenovo paperwork. No stylus, no sleeve, no USB-A to Ethernet dongle — what you see is what you get. The aluminum top cover feels substantial, and the 16-inch display sits in a chassis that Lenovo claims is 15-inch sized. It's genuinely portable for the screen size at 4.1 pounds, but it's not a featherweight.

**Performance and real-world use:** The AMD Ryzen 7 PRO processor handles 20+ browser tabs, video calls, and spreadsheets without breaking a sweat. The 16GB of RAM is adequate for most office work, though power users will want the 32GB option. The keyboard is the star — deep 1.5mm travel, responsive, and spill-resistant. If you type all day, this is one of the few laptops that won't leave your fingers tired. The 1080p webcam is serviceable but not great; expect slightly soft video in dim lighting. The 16:10 display is crisp and readable, but at 300 nits, it washes out in bright rooms. One buyer noted, *"The screen is great indoors but useless near a window — I had to close the blinds."*

**What buyers consistently praise:** The build quality gets near-universal approval. Multiple reviewers mention the MIL-SPEC 810H certification — it's survived drops and spills in real-world testing. The port selection is also a winner: two USB-C (one with Thunderbolt 4), two USB-A, HDMI 2.1, and a full-size Ethernet jack. That's rare in 2026 laptops. The fingerprint reader on the power button is fast and reliable.

**Where it falls short:** Battery life is the biggest complaint. Lenovo advertises up to 12 hours, but real-world usage lands at 6-8 hours with screen brightness above 50%. One reviewer summed it up: *"I get through the morning, but I'm hunting for an outlet by 2 PM."* The palm rest attracts fingerprints like a magnet — you'll be wiping it down daily. And at this price point, the 300-nit display and 1080p webcam feel like cost-cutting in the wrong places. Some buyers also reported the fan spinning up audibly during video calls, which is distracting in quiet offices.

**Who should buy this:** The T16 Gen 4 is for the corporate road warrior who prioritizes typing comfort, durability, and port selection over battery life and screen brightness. It's ideal for:
- **Accountants and analysts** who live in spreadsheets and need a reliable keyboard
- **IT professionals** who need Ethernet and multiple ports without dongles
- **Field workers** who need a laptop that survives real-world abuse

**Skip it if:** You're a student who needs all-day battery for lectures, a creative professional who needs accurate color and high brightness, or anyone who works in a bright, sunlit space.
