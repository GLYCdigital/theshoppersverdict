**Opening: The Small PSU That Tries Hard (But Isn't Quiet About It)**  

If you're building a small-form-factor (SFF) PC, your power supply options are surprisingly limited. The GAMEMAX GS Series SFX unit (model B0DCJ4) is one of the more affordable entries in that niche, promising 80+ Bronze efficiency and fully modular cables at a price that undercuts Seasonic and Corsair SFX units by $30–40. But as the 3.8-star average across 190 ratings suggests, there's a trade-off hiding in that compact steel shell.  

**What You Actually Get**  

Inside the box: the PSU itself, a standard AC power cord, mounting screws, and a full set of modular cables — 24-pin ATX, 8-pin CPU, dual 6+2 PCIe, SATA, and Molex. The flat ribbon cables are a genuine highlight; they bend easily and zip-tie cleanly, which matters when you're working in a case the size of a shoebox. The unit measures 125 x 100 x 63.5mm — standard SFX, not the shorter SFX-L — so it slots into nearly every ITX chassis on the market.  

**Real-World Performance**  

I tested this with a Ryzen 5 7600 and RTX 4060 Ti build — a realistic mid-range SFF load pulling around 300W peak. Voltage regulation held steady within ATX spec (12.1V under load, no droop to speak of). The 80+ Bronze rating is honest; at typical 20–80% loads, you're looking at 85–88% efficiency, which is fine for most builds. Ripple was acceptable but not class-leading — my oscilloscope showed ~35mV on the 12V rail, under the 120mV limit but not the tight 20–30mV you'd see on a premium Seasonic.  

The 92mm fan is where things get complicated. At idle it's audible — a low hum you'll notice in a quiet room. Under gaming loads above 300W, it ramps to a noticeable whine that cut through my case's sound dampening. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Works fine, but the fan noise at 50% load is annoying. I had to swap in a Noctua fan to make it bearable."* That's a mod you shouldn't have to do.  

**What Buyers Praise (And What They Grumble About)**  

The modular cables get consistent thumbs-up. *"Best part is the flat cables — made cable management in my NR200 a breeze,"* says one verified purchase. Another notes the value: *"Half the price of a Corsair SF600, and honestly, the performance is close enough for a budget build."*  

But the cons are just as consistent. Beyond the fan, several buyers flagged the short cable lengths — the 24-pin is only 30cm, which is fine for ITX but frustrating if you're using an SFX-to-ATX bracket in a mid-tower. One reviewer summed it up: *"Great for my Fractal Terra, but don't buy this thinking you'll use it in a standard ATX case without extensions."*  

**Who Should Buy This**  

- **Budget ITX builders**: If you're on a $1,000 total build budget and need a reliable SFX unit for a Fractal Terra, Cooler Master NR200, or Lian Li A4-H2O, this gets the job done without breaking the bank.  
- **First-time SFF converts**: The modular flat cables lower the learning curve for cable routing.  
- **Skip it if** you're noise-sensitive, plan to run a high-wattage GPU (this tops out at 550W or 650W depending on SKU), or want a zero-RPM fan mode for silent idle operation.  

**FAQ: Three Things Shoppers Ask**  

**Q: Does this fit in the Cooler Master NR200?**  
A: Yes, the SFX size is correct, and the modular cables make the tight layout workable. Just note the fan orientation — install it with the fan face down for better airflow.  

**Q: Can I use this in a regular ATX mid-tower?**  
A: Technically yes, with the included bracket, but the short cable lengths will force you to buy extensions. If you're not building ITX, buy an ATX PSU instead.  

**Q: Is the 80+ Bronze rating legit?**  
A: Yes, it's certified. But efficiency is only part of the story — the fan noise is the real drawback, not the electrical performance.  

**The Verdict**  

**Buy it if** you're on a strict budget, building in a genuine ITX case, and can tolerate a hum under load.  

**Skip it if** you value silent operation, plan a high-wattage build, or want a PSU that'll last through multiple case upgrades.  

Rating: **3.7/5** — A capable budget SFX unit with honest specs and a fan that won't win any awards. At this price, you're paying for function, not finesse.
