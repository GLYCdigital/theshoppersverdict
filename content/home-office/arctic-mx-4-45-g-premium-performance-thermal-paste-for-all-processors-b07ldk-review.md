## What You're Actually Getting

Open the box and you'll find a large, sturdy syringe containing 45 grams of MX-4 paste. That's roughly 10-15 full CPU applications depending on how generously you apply it. The syringe design is practical—narrow enough to reach tight spots, with a plunger that gives you precise control over the amount dispensed.

The paste itself has a dense, viscous consistency with a grayish-silver color. It's not runny like some pastes, which means it won't drip or migrate off your CPU die over time. No curing time required either—it works at peak performance immediately after application.

## Real-World Performance

MX-4 is a carbon-based paste, not silver or ceramic based. That means it delivers excellent thermal conductivity—rated at 8.5 W/mK—without the electrical conductivity risks that come with silver-based pastes. In practical terms, you're looking at temperature improvements of 5-10°C over stock paste, and it holds its own against premium pastes costing twice as much per gram.

The consistency is forgiving for first-time builders. It spreads smoothly and evenly, and while you'll get best results with the pea method or spread method, MX-4 is forgiving enough that you won't destroy your thermals with a slightly imperfect application.

One note for those using it on laptops: the thicker consistency is actually a plus here. It resists the "pump-out effect" that can cause thinner pastes to degrade faster in laptops' high-pressure thermal modules.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the downsides. First, 45 grams is a LOT of paste. If you're building a single PC and never plan on repasting anything else, you'll have 90% of this tube sitting in a drawer for years. The paste does have a shelf life of roughly 5 years when stored properly, but that's a lot of product to work through.

Second, while performance is excellent, it's not the absolute top-tier anymore. Enthusiast pastes like Thermal Grizzly Kryonaut or Thermalright TFX can outperform MX-4 by 2-3°C in extreme overclocking scenarios. For 99% of users (even gamers and casual overclockers), this difference is negligible, but hardcore enthusiasts chasing every last degree might want something more aggressive.

Third, the syringe tip can be a bit stiff for precise, tiny applications—like repasting a GPU die. It's manageable, but a finer tip would be more forgiving.

## Three Questions Buyers Ask

**Q: Does this expire? How long does it last in the tube?**
A: ARCTIC states a shelf life of about 5 years when stored at room temperature with the cap sealed. Once opened, keep the cap tight and avoid extreme temperatures. It'll stay effective for years of occasional use.

**Q: Is it electrically conductive?**
A: No. MX-4 is non-conductive and non-corrosive, making it safe even if you accidentally get some on motherboard components. This is a major reason it's recommended for beginners.

**Q: How many applications do you get from 45 grams?**
A: Realistically, 10-15 full CPU applications using the standard pea method. If you're applying with a spreader or being generous, expect closer to 8-10 applications.
