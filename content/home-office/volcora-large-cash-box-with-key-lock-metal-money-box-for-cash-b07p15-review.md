Let's cut through it: most cash boxes under $30 are tin cans with a latch that a butter knife could defeat. The Volcara Large Cash Box isn't that. It's the box you buy when you're tired of trusting your day's take to a zippered pouch or a flimsy plastic container.

**What you actually get**

The Volcara is a 10.2" x 8.3" x 3.5" steel box — large enough for a full day's register drawer but compact enough to slide under a seat. Out of the box: the cash box itself, two sets of keys (two per lock — so four total), and a removable plastic tray with dividers that you can reconfigure. The tray sits on top of the main cash compartment, giving you two layers of organization. Coins go in the tray's slotted sections; bills, checks, and receipts go underneath.

The build is where this thing earns its keep. The body is heavy-gauge steel — not the flimsy 0.4mm stuff you see on cheaper boxes — and the corners are reinforced with welded seams. The lid doesn't wobble or flex when you press on it. That matters because a cash box's only job is to be annoying to break into.

**The locking system is the real story**

Most boxes in this price range use a single cam lock. The Volcara uses two — one on the front latch, one on the side — and both must be disengaged to open the lid. That's a meaningful difference. It means a thief doesn't just have to pick one lock; they have to defeat two, and the side lock makes prying the lid significantly harder because there's leverage on both axes.

Customers consistently mention this in reviews. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've had cheap cash boxes before where the lock was basically a toy. This one feels solid — the keys are real, the locks click into place, and I don't worry about the drawer when I step away."* Another noted: *"The double lock is overkill for my flea market table, but I actually like it. Peace of mind is worth the extra second it takes to open."*

**Where it falls short**

The keys are stamped metal — thin, easy to bend, and not laser-cut. If you lose both sets, there's no master key or lock override; you're drilling through the steel. Write down the key code on the box's underside and order spares from Volcara now, not later.

There are also no pre-drilled mounting holes on the base. This is a portable box, not a fixed safe. If you're using it in a public-facing setting (food truck, farmers market, garage sale), it can be grabbed and carried off. The carrying handle is sturdy, which is good for you — but it's also good for a thief. Pair this with a cable lock or just don't leave it unattended.

The paint finish is durable but not scratch-proof. It'll show scuffs if you're throwing it in and out of a vehicle. Cosmetic only, but worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

- **Small business owners** running a weekend stall, food truck, or pop-up shop who need something that survives daily abuse
- **Event organizers** handling cash at ticket tables or merch booths — the dual locks mean one person can't be alone with the money

**FAQs shoppers actually ask**

**Q: Can the tray be removed completely?**  
Yes. It lifts out, leaving a single large compartment for bulk cash or envelopes. The dividers also slide, so you can size the slots for your coin mix.

**Q: How many bills fit in the main compartment?**  
Realistically, about 300-400 folded bills (ones, fives, tens) plus a register's worth of coins in the tray. If you're doing more than that, you need a bank drop, not a bigger box.

**Q: Are the locks pick-resistant?**  
No. These are standard pin tumbler locks. They're deterrents, not bank-grade security. What makes them effective is the dual-lock design and the steel body — it's more effort than most casual thieves are willing to invest.
