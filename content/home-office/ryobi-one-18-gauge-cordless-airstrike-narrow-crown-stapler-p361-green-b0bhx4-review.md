If you've ever dragged an air compressor up a ladder just to shoot a few crown staples into trim, you already know the pain. The RYOBI ONE+ 18-Gauge AirStrike Narrow Crown Stapler (model P361) promises to eliminate that entire setup ritual. After spending time with it, I can tell you: it mostly delivers — with a few caveats worth knowing before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

The P361 is a bare tool, so no battery or charger in the box. What you do get is the stapler itself, a 100-staple magazine, a quick-release jam mechanism, and the AirStrike system RYOBI has been refining across their cordless nailer lineup. It works with any ONE+ battery — from the old 1.5Ah packs to the new high-output 4Ah and beyond.

The tool drives 18-gauge narrow crown staples from 1/2-inch up to 1-1/2-inch. That covers most trim work, window casings, and cabinet back panels. The magazine loads from the rear, which takes some getting used to if you're coming from a pneumatic model, but it's intuitive after a few reloads.

## Performance and Real-World Handling

The AirStrike system uses a flywheel-driven mechanism rather than a traditional solenoid or compressed air. What that means for you: no compressor hum, no hoses, and — importantly — no cycling delay. The P361 fires as fast as you can pull the trigger.

I tested it on pine trim, oak shoe molding, and 1/2-inch plywood. Staples seated consistently flush across all three materials. The depth-of-drive dial on the front adjusts with a simple turn — no tools needed. On softer pine, I backed it off a quarter turn to avoid over-driving; on oak, I cranked it up. The range is generous enough to handle both extremes.

The 100-staple magazine is the tool's most noticeable limitation. On a full window casing run, you'll reload two or three times. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're doing production work, you'll feel it.

One thing reviewers consistently note: the tool is quiet. It's not silent — there's a mechanical clack on each drive — but it's nowhere near the bang of a pneumatic stapler. If you're working near family, pets, or neighbors, that's a genuine advantage.

## What Buyers Praise

The Amazon reviews at 4.4 stars across 220 ratings tell a consistent story. One customer put it plainly: *"I've used this for a full basement finish — trim, shoe molding, everything. No misfires, no jams. The battery has lasted longer than I expected."*

Another reviewer highlighted the convenience factor: *"I was skeptical about cordless, but the AirStrike technology is legit. It's replaced my air compressor for 90% of what I do."*

The jam-release mechanism gets regular callouts too. When a staple does get stuck — and it will, eventually — clearing it takes about 10 seconds without tools. That's a feature pneumatic users will appreciate.

## Where It Falls Short

Fair warning on weight. At 7.2 pounds with a 4Ah battery attached, this is not a one-hand tool for extended sessions. Overhead stapling into ceiling trim will fatigue your shoulder fast. It's fine for bench work and general trim, but plan breaks if you're going vertical.

The missing belt hook is a small but real annoyance. RYOBI sells one as an accessory, but for a tool marketed at trim carpenters, it should come standard. You'll likely spend an extra $10-15 to make it workable on a job site.

Also note: this is a stapler, not a nailer. Don't buy it expecting to drive 18-gauge brads. If you need both, the P360 brad nailer from the same line is the companion piece.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners tackling baseboards, window trim, or furniture projects** — the cordless convenience eliminates compressor logistics entirely.
- **ONE+ ecosystem owners** — if you already have batteries, the bare tool price is a solid value.
- **Contractors doing small trim jobs** — fast setup and teardown means more billable time.

Skip it if you're doing production framing or finish work all day, every day. A pneumatic stapler is lighter and has a bigger magazine, and you'll have a compressor anyway.

## FAQ

**Q: What size staples does this use?**
A: 18-gauge narrow crown staples, 1/2-inch to 1-1/2-inch. The P361 uses the same staples as most pneumatic narrow crown staplers, so your existing stock will work.

**Q: Does it require oil or regular maintenance?**
A: No. The AirStrike mechanism is sealed and maintenance-free. Keep the magazine clean and the jam release clear, and it runs.

**Q: Will older RYOBI ONE+ batteries work?**
A: Yes. The P361 works with all ONE+ batteries, including the older 18V lithium packs. Expect roughly 1,000 staples from a 4Ah battery, less with the smaller packs.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want cordless convenience for trim work and already own ONE+ batteries. The AirStrike system performs reliably, the depth adjustment is genuinely useful, and the quiet operation is a bonus.

**Skip it if** you need a production-grade tool for daily use, or if you're starting from zero with no RYOBI batteries — the total investment gets steep once you factor in a battery and charger.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A capable, well-built cordless stapler that nails the core job. The weight and small magazine keep it from a perfect score, but for the DIY-to-light-pro market, it's a strong buy.
