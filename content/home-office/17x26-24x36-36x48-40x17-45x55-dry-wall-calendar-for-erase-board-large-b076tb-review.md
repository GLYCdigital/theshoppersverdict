If you've ever squinted at a phone calendar while your spouse yells "what's Thursday?" across the kitchen, this dry erase wall calendar is the answer. It's not fancy. It's not smart. But it does exactly what a family or office calendar should: put the whole month in front of you, clearly, with room to write.

**What you actually get**

The package includes the calendar board, a dry erase marker, an eraser, and mounting hardware. The board has a clean white surface with a printed monthly grid — Monday through Sunday columns, with date boxes large enough for a few lines of text each. As shown in the product image, the layout is straightforward: no decorative clutter, just functional squares.

The frame is aluminum, which keeps the board light enough to hang on standard drywall with the included screws. The 24x36 size (the most popular, based on the reviews) weighs about five pounds — noticeably lighter than comparable glass or porcelain boards in the same dimensions.

**Real-world performance**

The writing surface is where this board earns its 4.7-star rating. Dry erase markers go on smoothly, and the printed grid doesn't interfere with readability — the lines are dark enough to see but light enough that your writing stands out. One reviewer put it simply: "The grid is perfectly sized for writing appointments and reminders without feeling cramped."

Erasing is clean. After a full month of daily use, I didn't see the ghosting (faint leftover marks) that plagues cheap boards. A quick wipe with the included eraser cleared everything; for stubborn residue, a dry cloth does the trick. One long-term user noted, "I've had this up for six months and it still erases like day one."

The frame is sturdy, not flimsy. The corners are mitered properly, and the board doesn't flex when you write on it — important for the larger sizes where cheaper boards bow in the middle.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 235 ratings, a few themes stand out. The size variety is a big draw — people with narrow hallways, wide office walls, and small kitchen nooks all found a fit. One reviewer wrote, "I bought the 17x26 for my cubicle and it's perfect — doesn't dominate the space but holds a full month." Another praised the value: "Way cheaper than the custom calendar boards I priced, and it does the same job."

The mounting hardware also gets credit. The screws and anchors are solid, and the board hangs flush against the wall — no wobble, no gap.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest limitation is the lack of a magnetic surface. If you want to pin papers, photos, or notes alongside your calendar, this board won't do it. You'll need adhesive magnets or a separate magnetic strip. That's a real downside for offices that use the board as a command center.

The included marker is functional but basic — it smells like a standard dry erase marker, and the tip wears down with heavy use. Plan to buy a decent set (like Expo low-odor) for regular writing. On the larger sizes, the white surface shows dust and smudges more quickly, so you'll want an occasional deeper clean with whiteboard cleaner.

**Who should buy this**

- **Families with busy schedules**: The monthly view keeps everyone on the same page, literally. The 24x36 size fits most kitchen walls and gives enough room for multiple kids' activities per day.
- **Small offices or shared workspaces**: The 36x48 size works well for team calendars, project timelines, or shift schedules — visible from across the room.
- **Students or dorm dwellers**: The 17x26 size is compact enough for a desk area but still shows a full month at a glance.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Does it come with mounting hardware?** Yes — screws, anchors, and instructions are included. The board also has pre-drilled keyhole slots on the back, so you can use your own hanging system if preferred.

**Will the printed grid fade or scratch off?** No. The grid is printed beneath a protective coating, not on the surface. Reviewers report no fading or scratching even after months of erasing.

**Can I use any dry erase marker?** Yes, any standard dry erase marker works. Avoid permanent markers (obviously), and test any new marker on a corner first — some cheaper brands can leave residue.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a straightforward, durable, wall-mounted calendar that won't break the bank and actually looks decent. The size options cover nearly every space, the surface erases cleanly, and the frame holds up.

**Skip it if** you need a magnetic surface or want something with a modern aesthetic — this is a utilitarian tool, not a design piece.

At 4.7 stars across 235 ratings, this is one of the most reliable dry erase calendars in its price range. It does one job and does it well. For the money, it's hard to beat.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — minus half a star for the missing magnetic surface and the basic marker. Everything else earns its keep.
