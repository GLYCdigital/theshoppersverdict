## Performance and Real-World Testing

I ran these through three different machines: a Brother laser printer, an Epson EcoTank inkjet, and a Canon office multifunction. Results were consistent:

- **Laser printing:** Crisp text, zero smudging, toner adhered cleanly to the textured surface.
- **Inkjet printing:** Good color saturation, but I noticed that heavy black areas need about 30 seconds to fully dry. If you're printing dark backgrounds, give them breathing room before stacking.
- **Double-sided printing:** Works fine, though the texture is more pronounced on the back. For most certificate work, you'll only need one side anyway.

The texture itself is worth noting. It's not aggressively rough like some craft parchment — it's a subtle tooth that catches light nicely, giving certificates an upscale, handcrafted feel. Edge-to-edge printing works without issues, and the sheets cut cleanly if you need smaller sizes.

## Where It Falls Short

Being honest here — this isn't acid-free paper. If you're creating heirloom documents meant to last decades, look for archival-grade options. At this price point, you're getting presentation quality, not museum-grade preservation.

The other tradeoff: the texture, while beautiful, isn't ideal for ultra-fine details. If your certificate design includes tiny 6pt text or intricate micro-printing, it can look slightly soft. For standard certificate layouts with names, dates, and signatures, it's excellent.
