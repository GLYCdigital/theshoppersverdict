**What's Actually in the Box**

You get two sealed cartridges: one PG-240XL black (listed at 400 pages) and one CL-241XL color (300 pages). The packaging is basic but functional—each cartridge sits in a vacuum-sealed foil pouch with a protective clip over the print head. Installation is genuinely tool-free: snap out the old ones, push these in until they click, run the printer's alignment cycle. Most users report zero chip errors on Canon models from the TS series, the MG series, and the older MX line.

**Performance: Where It Surprises and Where It Stumbles**

The black cartridge uses dye-based ink, not pigment. That's the biggest technical compromise. For everyday documents, it's crisp and dark—comparable to OEM. But if you're someone who highlights printed text with a marker, expect smudging. That's a dye-ink trait, and TECKKIN doesn't hide it, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Color output is genuinely better than I expected at this price point. One verified buyer noted, *"I print a lot of school handouts with graphics. The colors are vibrant, not washed out like the last generic brand I tried."* Skin tones in photos lean slightly warm, but for anything short of professional photo printing, it's acceptable.

Page yield is the real headline. Multiple reviewers report hitting the 400-page estimate on the black cartridge—or exceeding it. One user wrote, *"I got 430 pages before the low-ink warning. For a third of the OEM price, that's a no-brainer."* Your mileage varies with print density, but the XL rating appears honest.

**The Consistent Praise**

Across the ratings, three patterns emerge: price, compatibility, and yield. **Where It Falls Short — Straight Talk**

The dye-based black is the biggest tradeoff. If you print contracts, shipping labels, or anything requiring water-resistance, this isn't your cartridge. Also, quality control isn't perfect at this price tier. A handful of reviews report a dead cartridge on arrival or a slow leak in the color unit. It's maybe 4–5% of buyers, but it's real. TECKKIN's return policy covers it, but you're out time.

Also—no expiration date on the packaging. Ink degrades. If you're buying a six-month supply, the last cartridges may produce faint prints.
