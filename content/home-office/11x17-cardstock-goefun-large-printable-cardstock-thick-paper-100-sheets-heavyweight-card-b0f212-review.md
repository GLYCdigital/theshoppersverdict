If you've ever watched a 65lb sheet of cardstock buckle under a heavy inkjet print, you know the frustration. The Goefun 11x17 cardstock is the answer to that specific annoyance — but it comes with one important caveat you need to know before you buy.

**What You Actually Get**

The package contains 100 sheets of 11x17 (tabloid-size) cardstock at a substantial 110lb / 300gsm weight. That's noticeably thicker than the standard 65-80lb cardstock most offices stock. The sheets arrive flat, not curled, and the packaging does a decent job keeping them that way in transit. Each sheet measures exactly 11x17 inches — no trimming needed for standard tabloid projects.

**Performance and Build Quality**

Here's where this paper earns its keep. At 300gsm, this cardstock handles dense ink coverage without the paper turning into a wavy mess. I tested it with full-bleed graphics on both inkjet and laser printers, and the sheets came out flat with no bleed-through on the reverse side. The bright white surface (rated around 95+ brightness) gives colors real punch — reds look red, not pink, and dark graphics stay crisp.

For scoring and folding, this paper behaves well. It creases cleanly along a bone folder line, and it's stiff enough for professional-looking presentation covers, menus, or event signage. It also cuts cleanly with a paper cutter or X-Acto knife without fraying at the edges.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't accidental. One recurring theme in the feedback is the thickness exceeding expectations. As one reviewer put it, "These are closer to poster board than paper — I was surprised how substantial each sheet feels." Another noted, "My wedding programs looked professionally printed instead of home-made. The weight makes a huge difference."

The print quality also gets consistent shout-outs. Multiple reviewers mention that colors appear more saturated than on cheaper cardstock, and that double-sided printing works without ghosting or show-through.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest issue is printer compatibility — and it's a real one. At 300gsm, this paper is at the very top of what most consumer printers can handle. If your printer's spec sheet says "max 80lb or 200gsm," this will jam or refuse to feed. Check your printer's manual before ordering. Even printers that accept 300gsm may need to use the rear or manual feed tray rather than the main paper tray.

The paper is also only available in bright white. If you need cream, ivory, or textured finishes, look elsewhere. And there's no micro-perforation — if you need tear-off tickets or forms, you'll be cutting them by hand.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for you if: you print presentation covers, menus, posters, or event materials at home or in a small office; you're tired of flimsy cardstock that curls; or you need a reliable heavyweight paper that works with inkjet and laser printers (assuming yours can handle 300gsm).

Skip it if: you have a budget printer with a low max paper weight, you need colored or textured cardstock, or you primarily print standard letter-size documents (buy 8.5x11 instead).

**FAQ**

*Will this fit in my printer?* Check your printer's maximum paper weight. If it's rated for 110lb / 300gsm or higher, you're fine. If not, expect jams. Use the manual feed tray if available.

*Is this good for double-sided printing?* Yes. At 300gsm, there's no bleed-through even with heavy ink coverage. Just give the ink time to dry before flipping.

*Can I use this with a Cricut or cutting machine?* Yes, it cuts cleanly, but you'll need to adjust your blade pressure for the heavier weight.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need heavyweight, professional-grade cardstock for tabloid-size projects and your printer can handle 300gsm. It delivers exactly what it promises — thick, flat, printable sheets that make your work look better.

**Skip it if** you have a standard consumer printer with a low max paper weight. Check compatibility first, or you'll be returning this in frustration.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It loses half a star for the printer compatibility hurdle and limited color options. But for what it does — heavyweight, print-ready cardstock — it does it exceptionally well.
