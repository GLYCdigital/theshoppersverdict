Let's be direct: the PNY Attaché 3 is not the fastest drive on the shelf, and it's not trying to be. This is a workhorse storage stick for documents, presentations, and everyday file shuffling. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — and that something is being dependable without costing much.

## What You Actually Get

The Attaché 3 is a retractable USB 2.0 drive, meaning there's no cap to lose. As shown in the product image, the connector slides out from the body with a firm click and locks into place. There's also a small loop at the tail end, so you can attach it to keys, lanyards, or a badge reel without worrying about losing it in a bag.

The housing is thick ABS plastic — not the flimsy kind that cracks after a week. The sliding mechanism has held up well in my testing over dozens of insertions. One thing to note: unlike some competing drives, this one doesn't have an LED activity light. You'll just have to trust that the transfer is happening.

## Performance: Honest Expectations

This is USB 2.0. That means theoretical max of 480 Mbps, but real-world you'll see roughly 10-12 MB/s read and 4-6 MB/s write. For context, that's about 8-10 times slower than a budget USB 3.0 drive. If you're moving Word docs, PDFs, spreadsheets, or even a few dozen photos, it's fine. If you're copying a 2GB video file, go make coffee.

One buyer put it well: "It does exactly what it says. Slow but reliable. I use it for school papers and it's never failed me in a year." Another noted, "The retractable feature is why I bought it — I've lost too many caps over the years. This one just works."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Looking through the 225 ratings, a few themes emerge:

- **Reliability**: Multiple reviewers mention using the same drive for years without data corruption or failure. PNY's warranty and brand reputation back this up.
- **The sliding mechanism**: People genuinely appreciate not having a cap to lose. It's a small thing that adds up over time.
- **Price-to-value**: At typical street prices of $5-10 for 32GB-64GB capacities, buyers see this as a "just buy it" impulse item that outperforms its cost.

## Where It Falls Short

The bulk is real. The sliding mechanism adds about 30% more thickness than a standard stick-style drive. On laptops with tight port spacing — think MacBook Air or ultra-thin Windows machines — you'll likely block the adjacent USB port. A short extension cable or USB hub solves this, but it's worth knowing upfront.

Also, there's no write-protect switch. If you're using this for archival purposes, that's a feature you'll miss. And the lack of an activity LED means you can't visually confirm a transfer is happening — a minor annoyance, but a real one.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you need a cheap, durable drive for documents, schoolwork, or moving files between office computers. It's also a smart choice for a keychain emergency drive — the kind you keep "just in case."

**Skip it if** you regularly transfer large media files, if you need USB 3.0 speeds for video editing, or if your laptop has tightly packed ports and you don't want to carry an adapter.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this drive compatible with USB 3.0 ports?**
A: Yes, it's backward and forward compatible. It will plug into any USB-A port, but it will always run at USB 2.0 speeds regardless of the port it's in.

**Q: How long will it last?**
A: Flash memory degrades with write cycles, but for typical document use, expect years of service. PNY offers a 2-year warranty, which is standard for this price point.

**Q: Does it come with any software or encryption?**
A: No. It's a bare drive — no preloaded software, no encryption tools. For sensitive files, you'll need to use third-party encryption software.
