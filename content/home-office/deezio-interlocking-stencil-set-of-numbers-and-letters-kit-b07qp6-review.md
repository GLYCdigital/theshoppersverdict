If you've ever fought with flimsy cardboard stencils that buckle mid-letter, you know the frustration. The Deezio Interlocking Stencil Set takes a different approach — these are flexible, plastic templates that link together like puzzle pieces. After working with them across several projects, here's what you need to know before buying.

**What You Actually Get**

The kit includes uppercase A-Z and numbers 0-9, with multiple font sizes included. The pieces are roughly 4-6 inches across depending on the character, cut from translucent plastic that's thin enough to bend but thick enough to hold its shape. There's no storage case — a real oversight for a set with this many small pieces. You'll want a zip pouch or binder sleeve to keep them organized.

**How It Performs**

The interlocking mechanism is the standout feature. Each stencil has tabs that slot into adjacent pieces, letting you build continuous words or long numbers without eyeballing spacing. For signage like "WELCOME" or "2026," this is genuinely useful — the letters stay aligned and connected as one unit.

The flexibility does double duty. You can wrap these around curved surfaces — water bottles, mason jars, even slightly rounded notebook spines — and they conform without cracking. That's the main reason crafters pick these over rigid acrylic alternatives.

The cutouts are clean with no rough edges, and paint or ink doesn't bleed underneath when you use a light touch with a stencil brush. One buyer noted, *"I've used these on canvas totes and wood signs — crisp lines every time, no smudging."* Another mentioned, *"The letters link together so I can spell my daughter's full name without measuring."*

**Where It Falls Short**

The smaller stencil sizes are genuinely annoying to work with. They're thin enough that they want to curl up at the edges, especially if you're not pressing down evenly. You'll need painter's tape for any precision work — holding them flat with one hand while painting with the other doesn't work well.

Also, while the interlocking is clever, the connection points aren't perfectly seamless. You may see tiny gaps where tabs meet, which matters if you're doing fine detail work. For bold, chunky lettering it's invisible; for delicate calligraphy-style projects, it's noticeable.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Bullet journalers and planners** who want consistent headers without hand-drawing every letter
- **Crafters working on mixed media** — the flexibility onto curved surfaces is a genuine advantage
- **Teachers or parents** making classroom signs, labels, or educational materials
- **Anyone doing occasional home DIY** like stenciling a crate or labeling storage bins

Skip it if you only need one-off stencils for a single project — disposable cardboard works fine for that. Also skip if you're doing ultra-fine detail work; the interlocking seams will frustrate you.

**FAQ**

**Are these washable and reusable?**
Yes. Clean them with warm soapy water after use. Avoid acetone-based cleaners, which can cloud the plastic.

**Can I use them with a spray adhesive?**
You can, but it's overkill. The flexible plastic grips most surfaces well enough on its own. Use repositionable adhesive spray for vertical surfaces.

**Do they work with fabric paint?**
Yes, but use a stencil brush with minimal paint. Load the brush and dab off excess before applying — that prevents paint creeping under the edges.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reusable, flexible lettering system for varied projects — the interlocking design and bendable material make it genuinely better than cheap alternatives for curved surfaces and multi-letter words.

**Skip it if** you need museum-grade precision or want a tidy storage solution out of the box.

At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, the Deezio set earns its reputation. It's not perfect — the smaller pieces are fiddly and the lack of a case is annoying — but for the price, it's a solid addition to any crafter's toolkit. **4.2/5**
