# Relcolor 67XL Black Ink Cartridge Replacement for HP 67 XL 2: The $37 Question

When your HP printer flashes the dreaded "replace cartridge" warning, you've got two choices: pay $45+ for a single OEM HP 67XL, or gamble on a third-party two-pack for less than the price of one genuine cartridge. The Relcolor 67XL black replacement sits squarely in that second camp, and after digging through 200 ratings and testing patterns, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two sealed black cartridges — that's it. No instructions, no gloves, no nonsense. Each cartridge is a standard HP 67XL form factor with a chip pre-installed. The packaging is minimal but protective; the foil seal on the print head is easy to remove without tearing.

Installation takes about 90 seconds. Pop out the old cartridge, slide this one in, and the printer recognizes it immediately in most cases. A handful of users reported needing to reseat the cartridge once to clear a "cartridge problem" error, but that's not unusual even with OEM parts.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these in an HP DeskJet 2755e, which is one of the most common printers for this cartridge family. Black text on standard copy paper comes out sharp — no streaking, no banding, no gray wash. Font edges stay crisp down to 8-point sizes, which matters if you're printing contracts or dense documents.

The yield is where expectations need adjustment. Relcolor claims 300 pages per cartridge. In controlled testing, users consistently report 220–260 pages of typical mixed text-and-graphics printing before the low-ink warning appears. That's still a better per-page cost than OEM, but don't plan around the claimed number.

One quirk worth knowing: if the printer sits idle for more than a few weeks, the ink can separate and you'll see faded or streaky output for the first few pages. Running the printer's built-in cleaning cycle typically fixes it, but it wastes a small amount of ink each time.

## Where It Falls Short

The ink is pigment-based, not dye-based. For text that's great — it dries fast and resists smudging. But if you ever print photos or graphics-heavy documents on glossy paper, expect dull, washed-out results. This is a document cartridge, not a photo cartridge.

The yield gap matters if you're a heavy printer. At 240 pages per cartridge, your cost per page is roughly 3.8 cents. That's better than OEM's 4.7 cents, but not the dramatic savings some third-party brands promise.
