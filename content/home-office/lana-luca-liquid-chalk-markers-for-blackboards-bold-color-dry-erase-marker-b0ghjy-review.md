**The Short Version:** If you run a café, teach in a classroom, or just like your weekly meal planner to actually look good, LANA & LUCA’s liquid chalk markers are a solid buy. They deliver bold, opaque color, write smoothly, and erase without leaving that annoying ghost residue.

**What You Actually Get**

The set includes 8 vibrant markers — white, red, blue, green, yellow, orange, pink, and purple — each with a fine 3mm bullet tip. That tip is the real story here: it's narrow enough for neat handwriting and menu lettering, but you can angle it for thicker strokes when you want to fill in a header or draw a border. The ink is a liquid chalk formula, not the dusty stick kind, so you get that crisp, almost paint-pen look on blackboards and glass.

There's no pump-action priming needed. You just shake them with the cap on, press the tip down on scrap paper until ink flows, and you're writing. Each marker holds enough ink for a couple of months of regular weekly menu updates, based on typical usage.

---

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

This is where LANA & LUCA earns its 4.4-star rating. The ink is genuinely pigmented — not that watery, translucent stuff you get from cheaper sets. On a black chalkboard, yellow and orange pop like a neon sign. White is opaque enough to cover dark surfaces in one pass.

Erasing is straightforward: a damp cloth or paper towel wipes it clean from non-porous surfaces like glass, acrylic, and sealed chalkboards. One buyer noted, *"I've tried three other brands and these are the first that didn't leave a faint white haze after erasing."* That tracks with the formula — it sits on the surface rather than soaking in.

The fine tip also holds up well. After a month of daily use, the nibs haven't frayed or pushed back into the barrel, which is a common failure point on cheaper markers.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides.

First, the caps. If you leave a marker uncapped while you answer the phone or help a customer, the tip dries out fast. You can sometimes revive it with a drop of water, but it's not guaranteed. Keep the caps on when not actively writing.

Second, on rough or unsealed chalkboard surfaces, the ink flow runs dry noticeably faster. The liquid formula needs a smooth surface to glide. If your board is old and chalk-scarred, you'll be pressing harder and going through markers quicker than expected.

Third, the yellow and orange markers, while vivid, require a bit more shaking between uses to keep the pigment suspended. Skip the shake and you'll get a few streaky strokes before it evens out.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these work on regular whiteboards?**
Yes. They write smoothly on whiteboard surfaces and erase the same way. Just be aware the ink is more pigmented than standard dry-erase, so you'll want to wipe within a few days to avoid any lingering tint.

**Q: How do you activate a new marker?**
Shake vigorously with the cap on for 10-15 seconds. Then press the tip down on a scrap piece of paper until the ink starts flowing. It usually takes 30-60 seconds on the first use.

**Q: Can you refill them?**
No. These are disposable markers. When the ink runs out, you recycle the barrel. At this price point, replacement cost is low enough that refillability isn't a dealbreaker.
