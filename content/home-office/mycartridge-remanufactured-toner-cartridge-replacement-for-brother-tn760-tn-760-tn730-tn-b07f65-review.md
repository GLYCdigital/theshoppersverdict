**The $20 Question: Can a Remanufactured Toner Actually Keep Up?**

Let's cut through the marketing. Remanufactured toner cartridges are a gamble — some save you real money, others leak toner all over your office carpet and leave you questioning every life choice that led to that moment. The myCartridge TN760 replacement sits firmly in the first category, with a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings backing that up. But it's not without quirks.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get one remanufactured cartridge — no retail packaging fanfare, just a sealed foil bag inside a plain box. The cartridge is a recycled Brother TN760 shell that's been cleaned, inspected, and refilled with third-party toner. A fresh chip is installed to communicate with your printer. Notably, there's no instruction sheet beyond basic installation steps, and no return label for recycling your used cartridge. If eco-credentials matter to you, that's a miss.

**Print Quality and Real-World Performance**

I ran this cartridge through a Brother HL-L2360DW over three weeks, pushing roughly 1,500 pages of mixed content — dense text documents, spreadsheet hell, and occasional grayscale graphics. The output is genuinely impressive for a remanufactured product. Text stays crisp down to 6-point font with no smudging or ghosting. Solid black fills come out uniform without streaking. I did notice slightly lighter density on 50% gray fills compared to OEM toner — noticeable side-by-side, but not something the average office document will reveal.

One buyer noted, "Prints look identical to the Brother brand cartridges I was using before, for almost half the price." Another mentioned, "No error messages, no leaks, no drama. Just works." That matches my experience.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the yield claim. myCartridge advertises 3,000 pages per cartridge based on 5% coverage. In real mixed office use with 10-20% coverage, I'd estimate you'll get 2,300–2,500 pages. That's still respectable, but budget accordingly.

The bigger concern is chip compatibility. A handful of recent reviews (2025-2026 manufacturing batches) report intermittent "Toner Life End" errors on printers with updated firmware. Brother has been tightening third-party cartridge detection. If your printer auto-updates firmware, you might hit this wall. The workaround is disabling firmware updates in your Brother printer settings — a step that shouldn't be necessary but sometimes is.

**Who Should Buy This**

This cartridge is ideal for home offices and small businesses running Brother HL-L2300D, L2320D, L2360DW, or MFC-L2700DW models. If you print 500+ pages monthly and want to cut supply costs without sacrificing quality, this delivers. It's also a solid choice for students or anyone with a high-volume Brother printer who's tired of paying OEM prices.

Skip it if you can't tolerate any compatibility risk, need the full 3,000-page yield, or want a manufacturer that takes back your empties.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many pages will I actually get?**
At standard 5% coverage, expect closer to 2,500 pages. With dense documents, that drops to around 2,000. Buy two if you're a high-volume printer.

**Q: Does it come with a warranty?**
myCartridge offers a 12-month warranty covering defects and leaks. Customer service response times are generally reasonable, though some users report slow resolution on chip-related issues.
