## The Workhorse Black Cartridge That Just Works

If you own an HP Color LaserJet Pro M252, MFP M277, or the M274n, you already know the printer's strength: dependable color output in a compact chassis. But the printer is only as good as the toner you feed it. After testing the HP 201A black cartridge (CF400A) across a mixed workload of text documents, shipping labels, and grayscale presentations, here's the honest story.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get a single, sealed black toner cartridge rated for 1,400 pages. That's the standard yield — no high-capacity XL option exists for the 201A series, which is worth knowing before you buy. The box includes basic installation instructions, though you likely won't need them. The cartridge comes with a pull-tab seal and a plastic protective cover that snaps off cleanly. No drum unit to replace, no waste toner bottle to manage — it's a straightforward swap.

## Real-World Performance and Build Quality

I ran this cartridge through a 600-page test cycle: dense legal documents, email printouts, and a batch of grayscale graphics. The output stayed consistently sharp — no banding, no toner dust on the page edges, no gray cast on white backgrounds. The JetIntelligence chip communicated properly with the printer, showing accurate remaining toner levels from day one.

Text at 10-point font remains legible down to the serifs. If you're printing contracts or client-facing materials, this cartridge delivers the kind of professional finish that cheap refurbs often struggle with. The cartridge body itself feels robust — the plastic housing has no flex, and the drum surface is well-protected during handling.

One thing I appreciated: the cartridge's low-toner warning appeared only when genuinely low, not prematurely. On a compatible cartridge I tested previously, the printer nagged me for 200 pages before actually running dry. No such games here.

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't random. The consistent themes in customer feedback are reliability and hassle-free operation. ## Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself

The price per page is the elephant in the room. At roughly 4–5 cents per page for black-only output, this cartridge costs about double what a reputable compatible brand charges. For a home office printing 50 pages a week, the annual difference is maybe $30–40. For a busy law practice printing 300 pages daily? That's real money.

The 1,400-page yield is also modest. If you're a high-volume user, you'll be swapping cartridges every few weeks. That's not a flaw in execution — it's a limitation of the platform. There's no XL variant, so you can't buy your way to a longer interval.

Finally, genuine HP cartridges are notorious for regional locking. If you bought your printer overseas or use a grey-market unit, verify compatibility before purchasing. This isn't an HP-specific complaint, but it's a real gotcha for international users.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a professional whose prints must be flawless — attorneys, accountants, medical offices, or anyone printing client-facing documents. The reliability premium is worth it when a streaked contract costs you credibility. Also buy if you've been burned by compatible cartridges that leak or throw chip errors.

**Skip it if:** You're a budget-conscious home user printing school forms and casual documents. A high-rated compatible brand will save you meaningful money with acceptable quality. Also skip if you print high volume — the cost per page will hurt, and you'd be better served by a printer with XL cartridge options.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this cartridge work in my HP Color LaserJet Pro MFP M277dw?**
Yes. The 201A series (CF400A–CF403A) is cross-compatible with the M252, M274n, M277, and their variants. Double-check your exact model number, but these are the standard consumables for that family.

**Q: How many pages will I actually get?**
HP rates it at 1,400 pages at 5% coverage. That's the ISO standard, and in my testing, it's accurate. If you print dense graphics or full-page images, expect closer to 800–1,000 pages.

**Q: Is the cartridge recyclable?**
Yes. HP's Planet Partners program takes back genuine cartridges for free. You print a prepaid shipping label from HP's website, box it up, and drop it off. No need to toss it in the trash.
