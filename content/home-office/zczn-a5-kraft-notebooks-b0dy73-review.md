Let’s cut through the noise: the ZCZN A5 Kraft Notebooks are not flashy, not branded, and not going to win awards for design. But if you burn through notebooks like I do, these are the quiet workhorses that keep your ideas flowing without bleeding your wallet dry. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, they’ve clearly earned their keep.

**What you actually get**

The set includes six A5 notebooks (5.8 x 8.3 inches), each running 96 pages of 100gsm paper. That’s a total of 576 pages for roughly the price of two premium single notebooks. The kraft cardstock covers are minimal—just a blank tan front with a small label area—and the stitched binding means these lie flat when opened. As shown in the product image, they arrive as a clean stack, ready to be deployed across your desk, bag, or nightstand.

**Real-world performance**

The headline feature is the paper. At 100gsm, it’s noticeably thicker than the 70-80gsm you find in most budget notebooks. I tested it with a Pilot G2 gel pen, a Lamy Safari fountain pen with medium nib, and a sharpie—no bleed-through on any of them. There’s minor ghosting with the fountain pen (you can faintly see the writing on the other side), but nothing that compromises readability. The paper has a slight tooth to it—not glossy, not rough—which makes it pleasant for both ballpoint and fountain pen users.

The lay-flat binding is genuinely useful. I’ve used these for journaling, meeting notes, and even sketching, and the pages stay open without needing a paperweight. The stitch binding is sturdier than the glued spines you’ll find on cheaper alternatives—after three months of daily use, none of my notebooks have shed a page.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scanning through the reviews, a few themes stand out. One buyer wrote, "Finally a notebook that doesn't bleed with my fountain pens. The paper quality is honestly better than notebooks twice the price." Another noted, "I bought these for my team of five. Everyone loves them. The size is perfect for daily notes, and they're durable enough to survive a backpack." The paper quality and value-for-money are the two most frequently mentioned positives, with several reviewers specifically calling out the 100gsm thickness as the reason they repurchased.

**Where it falls short**

The kraft covers are a double-edged sword. They look good and feel natural, but they scuff easily—if you toss one loose into a bag with keys or a laptop charger, expect visible wear within a week. A cover or sleeve solves this, but it’s an extra purchase.

There are no page numbers, no table of contents (TOC) section, and no perforated pages. For bullet journalers or anyone who needs to reference past notes, you’ll need to DIY an index. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s a feature you’ll notice missing if you’re used to structured notebooks like Moleskine or Leuchtturm.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students and note-takers**: The paper quality handles highlighters and gel pens without smearing, and the 6-pack means you’re covered for a full semester.
- **Fountain pen users on a budget**: This is one of the cheapest notebooks you’ll find that doesn’t feather or bleed with wet inks.
- **Journalers who go through books fast**: At this price, you won’t feel guilty about filling one up in three weeks.
- **Skip if**: You need a notebook with pre-printed structure (page numbers, TOC, index) or you plan to carry it unprotected in a rough bag.
