You're getting five 8.5x11-inch spiral-free workbooks: two math books, one alphabet book, one number book, and one drawing book. ## Performance: Where This Shines and Where It Stumbles

The grooved design is the star. As shown in the product image, each letter, number, or shape has a recessed channel that physically steers the pen. For a 5-year-old learning proper stroke order, this is transformative — their hand learns the motion before their brain fully understands it. The fading ink works reliably; we tested it, and pages were blank again in under 20 minutes.

The math books are a smart inclusion. They cover numbers 1-100 with addition and subtraction practice, which extends the set's usefulness well beyond preschool. The drawing book is simpler — basic shapes and patterns — but it's a nice warm-up for younger kids.

**Where it falls short:** The pen quality is the weak link. After about two weeks of daily use, the tip starts to flatten, which affects groove contact. You'll need to buy replacement pens (sold separately). Also, without spiral binding, pages don't stay open on their own — you'll need a paperweight or a parent's hand. And if your kid is left-handed, note the grooves are optimized for right-handed writing; it's usable but less intuitive.

## 3 Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: How long does the ink really take to disappear?**
A: 10-20 minutes depending on humidity and how hard you press. Cooler, drier rooms speed it up. It's not instant, so plan for about 15 minutes between sessions.

**Q: Can you use regular pens with these books?**
A: No. The ink needs to be erasable/fading. Standard pens will permanently mark the grooves. Stick with the included pens or buy the brand's replacements.

**Q: Are the grooves deep enough for a 3-year-old?**
