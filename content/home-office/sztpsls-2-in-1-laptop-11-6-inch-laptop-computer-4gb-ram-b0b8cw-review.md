The SZTPSLS 2-in-1 sits in an awkward spot. It's priced like a budget tablet, but it runs full Windows 11 Home. That's both its biggest selling point and its biggest trap. At 3.6 stars across 180 ratings, buyers are split — some love the portability, others hit a wall with performance. Here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

You get the 11.6-inch tablet, a detachable keyboard, a USB-C charger, and a basic user manual. No stylus included, which is worth noting since the touchscreen begs for one. The keyboard connects via pogo pins and folds into a stand for laptop mode. It's a clean setup, though the hinges feel plasticky.

**Performance and build: the honest breakdown**

The 4GB RAM and Intel Celeron-class processor are the ceiling here. For a single browser with 5-6 tabs, a word processor, and maybe Spotify, it handles the load. Push it further — video streaming while typing, or multiple Office docs — and you'll see lag. One reviewer wrote, "It's fine for my daughter's homework, but I wouldn't want to do my day job on this." That's the consensus.

The 64GB eMMC storage (the listing says 1TB but that's a typo in the truncated title — actual specs show 64-128GB) fills up quickly. Windows 11 itself eats nearly half of it. You'll need a microSD card or cloud storage from day one.

Build quality is acceptable for the price. The aluminum back feels sturdier than most budget laptops, but the keyboard flexes under pressure. The 1366x768 display is sharp enough for documents but lacks brightness outdoors — 250 nits tops.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Portability is the star. At 2.5 pounds, it disappears into a bag. One Amazon review said, "Took it on a two-week trip across Europe — used it for flights, hotel research, and journaling. Never felt heavy." The touchscreen in tablet mode also gets consistent praise for reading and casual browsing.

Battery life, however, is the most common complaint. The 7.4V/4000mAh battery delivers 4-5 hours of mixed use. That's enough for half a workday, not the "all-day" claim in the listing.

**Where it falls short**

The 4GB RAM is the dealbreaker for power users. Even light multitasking will cause hiccups. The keyboard is cramped — the keys are smaller than standard and the travel is shallow. If you write long documents, your fingers will feel it. Also, the single USB-C port means you'll need a hub for peripherals.

**Who should buy this**

This is for students who need a basic note-taking device, travelers who want a lightweight secondary machine, or anyone on a tight budget who needs full Windows compatibility for school portals or legacy software. It's not for professionals running heavy apps, video editors, or anyone who needs to multitask aggressively.

**FAQ**

*Can it run Microsoft Office?* Yes, but stick to the web versions or Office Online. The desktop apps work but will slow down with large files.

*Does the keyboard detach completely?* Yes, it's fully detachable for pure tablet mode.

*Can I upgrade the RAM?* No. It's soldered. What you buy is what you get.

**The Verdict**

The SZTPSLS 2-in-1 earns 3.7 stars because it does one thing well: it's a budget-friendly, portable Windows machine for light work. It fails as a primary computer.

**Buy it if** you need an affordable secondary device for browsing, email, and note-taking, and you understand the performance limits.

**Skip it if** you need to run demanding software, multitask heavily, or expect all-day battery. Spend more for a machine with 8GB RAM minimum.

For the price point, it's a fair trade-off — just know exactly what you're getting into before you click buy.
