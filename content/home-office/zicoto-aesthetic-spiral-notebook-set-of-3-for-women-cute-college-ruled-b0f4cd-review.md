The market is flooded with "aesthetic" notebooks that are all cover and no substance. The ZICOTO set of three, in the Cute C design, actually delivers on both fronts—and at a price point that makes buying three feel like a no-brainer. But let's dig past the pretty exterior and see what you're really getting.

## What's in the Package

You get three spiral notebooks, each measuring roughly 8.5 x 11 inches—standard letter size, which matters if you're pulling pages out for binders. Each has 60 sheets of college-ruled paper, which is 20-30 fewer pages than you'll find in a typical Mead or Five Star. The covers are a flexible cardstock with a soft-touch laminate that feels almost like a matte phone case. The spiral is double-wound metal, not the flimsy single-coil you see on cheap imports.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this set separates itself from the $8 three-pack at Target. The paper weight is noticeably heavier than standard notebook stock. I tested with a Pilot G2 gel pen and a Zebra Mildliner highlighter—the two most common culprits for bleed-through—and the ink sat on the surface without ghosting to the next page. That's a genuine win for anyone who writes with anything beyond a basic ballpoint.

The spiral binding lies flat when opened, which sounds basic but is shockingly rare at this price. The coils are aligned properly, so pages turn without catching or snagging. The college ruling (8.7mm spacing) is tight enough for dense note-taking but not cramped.

One thing worth noting: the covers are more flexible than rigid. They'll survive a backpack commute, but don't expect them to hold up if you're shoving them into an overstuffed tote without protection.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.8-star average across 240 ratings holds up when you read the actual feedback. One verified purchaser wrote, "I bought these for my bullet journaling side projects and the paper is way better than I expected for the price." Another mentioned, "The design is even prettier in person—the floral pattern isn't as busy as the photos suggest."

The most common complaint is the page count. Multiple reviewers noted they wish each notebook had more sheets. There were also a couple of mentions of the covers picking up minor creases at the corners after a few weeks of daily use—nothing structural, just cosmetic wear.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the biggest limitation is the 60-sheet count. If you're a student taking notes across multiple classes, each notebook will last you roughly three to four weeks. That's fine for a set of three, but you'll be reordering sooner than you'd like.

The "Cute C" design is distinctly floral and feminine. If that's not your aesthetic, ZICOTO makes other variants in this same series—the geometric and minimalist patterns might suit you better. Also, the pages are not perforated. If you need to tear out assignments cleanly, you'll be fighting the spiral binding.

## Who Should Buy This

- **College students** who want notebooks that look good in the front row but don't fall apart by mid-semester
- **Planners and journalers** who use gel pens or highlighters and are tired of bleed-through
- **Gift buyers** looking for a practical-but-pretty set for a coworker, teacher, or friend who loves stationery

Skip this if you need heavy-duty pages for fountain pens (the paper is good, but not Tomoe River good) or if you need 100+ pages per notebook.

## FAQ

**Do these notebooks work with fountain pens?** They handle fine-tipped fountain pens without feathering, but you'll see some show-through with wetter inks. For ballpoint, gel, and rollerball, you're completely safe.

**Are the pages perforated?** No. If you need tear-out sheets, look elsewhere or be prepared to cut carefully along the spiral.

**What's the difference between the design variants?** The "Cute C" features a floral print in muted tones. Other variants in the series offer different patterns—check the product listing for the full range.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely pretty notebook set that performs above its price class for everyday writing. **Skip it if** you need maximum page count or require perforated pages.

The ZICOTO set earns its 4.6/5 rating. It's not perfect—the page count and non-perforated pages are legitimate tradeoffs—but for the price, you're getting paper quality and binding durability that usually costs twice as much. If the floral design speaks to you, this is a solid purchase. If not, check the other variants in the series before you commit.
