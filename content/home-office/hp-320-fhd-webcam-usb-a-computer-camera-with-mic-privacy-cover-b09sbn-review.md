**Bottom line: The HP 320 FHD Webcam is the sensible, no-drama choice for anyone who needs better video than a laptop's built-in camera without emptying their wallet.**

Most laptop webcams make you look like you're broadcasting from a coal mine. The HP 320 FHD fixes that for under $50, and it brings a few thoughtful touches that justify the price tag. After digging through the specs, customer feedback, and real-world performance data, here's the full picture.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the webcam itself, a detachable USB-A cable (about 5 feet long), and a quick-start guide. No mounting bracket drama — the camera clips directly onto your monitor or laptop lid with a spring-loaded hinge that grips firmly. The unit is compact at roughly 3.5 inches wide and weighs next to nothing, so it won't tip over flimsy laptop screens.

The standout design feature is the integrated privacy shutter. It's a physical sliding door built into the lens barrel — you rotate it to cover the glass. No tacky stick-on cover, no lost piece. That's the kind of detail that shows HP actually thought about how people use these things.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 1080p sensor delivers clean, sharp video at 30fps. In natural daylight, colors look accurate without that oversaturated "webcam look." Low-light performance is genuinely surprising for this price point — the automatic light correction kicks in and brightens your face without turning the background into a grainy mess. It's not magic, but it's noticeably better than most built-in laptop cameras.

The fixed 78° field of view frames your head and shoulders nicely. You won't get that trendy "coffee shop in the background" wide shot, but for professional calls, that's honestly a feature, not a bug.

The dual built-in mics pick up your voice clearly in a quiet room. In a noisy environment, they'll pick up background chatter too — that's expected at this price. The camera's auto white balance handles mixed lighting reasonably well, though you'll still want a light source in front of you for best results.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. The fixed field of view means you'll need to adjust if you want to show a whiteboard or include a second person. The microphone is acceptable for meetings but won't flatter your voice if you're recording content. And while the clip works well, the camera doesn't swivel — you're adjusting the whole unit to change angle.

One reviewer summed it up: *"Great for the price, but if you need a wide angle for group calls, look elsewhere."* That's fair. This is a one-person camera.
