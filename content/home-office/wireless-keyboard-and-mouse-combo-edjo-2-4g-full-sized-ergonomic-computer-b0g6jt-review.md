Let me be blunt: most sub-$30 keyboard and mouse combos are landfill fodder. The EDJO 2.4G Full-Sized Ergo set isn't that, but it's also not the endgame setup. After digging through 210 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

You get the full-sized keyboard with integrated palm rest, a contoured wireless mouse, the 2.4G USB receiver, and two AA batteries (one per device). The keyboard measures roughly 17.5 inches wide—no compact sacrifice here. The mouse is smaller than the keyboard suggests, about 4.5 inches long, which matters if you've got bear paws.

**Performance and build quality**

The 2.4G connection is the standout feature. It's plug-and-play: pop the dongle in, and both devices pair instantly with zero pairing menus. The 10-meter range claim holds up in real use—I've seen buyers mention walking across a small office while the connection stays solid. Battery life is respectable; EDJO rates the keyboard for up to 12 months and the mouse for 6 months on a single pair of AAs, which aligns with what long-term reviewers report. There's also a power switch on both devices to preserve juice when idle.

The typing experience is where opinions split. The scissor-switch keys have a short travel distance and a soft landing—quiet enough for shared spaces, but if you're coming from a mechanical board, they'll feel like typing on a marshmallow. One verified buyer said, "Keys are a little mushy but fine for spreadsheets and emails." That's the honest consensus. The ergonomic wrist rest is a fixed plastic curve, not a removable gel pad, but it does angle your wrists into a more neutral position than a flat board.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The full-sized layout is the most-cited win. People who need the number pad for data entry or accounting specifically call it out. One reviewer noted, "Finally a combo that doesn't shrink the arrow keys to fit Bluetooth." The wireless reliability also gets frequent mentions—no dropped inputs during typing marathons. The quiet keys are another recurring plus for anyone sharing an office or working near a sleeping baby.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of Bluetooth is the biggest limitation. If you're on a laptop that lives in a dock, or you want to switch between a desktop and a tablet, you're out of luck—the dongle is the only connection path. That also means you burn a USB-A port permanently. The mouse is genuinely small; buyers with larger hands report their palm drags on the desk. And the build is all-plastic with a glossy finish that picks up fingerprints, though it's sturdy enough for daily use.

**Who should buy this**

- **The spreadsheet warrior**: If you crunch numbers all day and need a reliable number pad, this is a solid pick.
- **The budget-conscious office worker**: It's a functional, no-frills setup that won't embarrass you in a professional setting.
- **The silent-typing enthusiast**: If your colleagues complain about clacking, these quiet keys are a diplomatic solution.

**Skip it if** you're a gamer needing high polling rates, you want multi-device Bluetooth switching, or you have XL hands.

**FAQ**

**Is the mouse comfortable for small to medium hands?** Yes—most buyers with average hand sizes report it fits fine. It's specifically the large-hand crowd that struggles.

**Can I use the dongle with multiple computers?** Only one at a time. You'd need to unplug and re-plug to switch devices manually.

**Does the keyboard have media keys?** It has a few function shortcuts (volume, mute, play/pause) via the Fn key, but no dedicated media row.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, full-sized wireless combo for office work without spending a fortune. **Skip it if** Bluetooth connectivity or a mechanical typing feel are dealbreakers.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid, honest gear that delivers on its core promise, held back by the missing Bluetooth and small mouse. For the price, it's easy to recommend to anyone who just needs a functional setup that works.
