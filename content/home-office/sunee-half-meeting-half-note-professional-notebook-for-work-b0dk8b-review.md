# SUNEE Half Meeting Half Note Review: The Notebook That Finally Separates Notes From Tasks

Most meeting notebooks fail because they're just blank pages. You scribble discussion points, action items, and random thoughts in a continuous stream, then spend ten minutes after each meeting trying to decode what's actually a task versus context. The SUNEE Half Meeting Half Note solves that problem with a dead-simple idea: split the page in half, use the left for notes, the right for to-dos.

Here's the honest breakdown after digging through specs and buyer feedback.

## What You Actually Get

The notebook is A5-sized (roughly 5.8 x 8.3 inches), which hits the sweet spot for portability without feeling cramped. It's 160 pages of 100gsm paper — noticeably thicker than the 70-80gsm you find in most office-supply notebooks. The binding is thread-sewn and lays flat, which is non-negotiable for a notebook you'll reference while typing.

The cover is a semi-rigid leatherette that survives backpack abuse without curling. There's an inner pocket on the back cover for loose receipts or business cards, and an elastic closure band. Nothing revolutionary, but everything functional.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The defining feature is the page layout. Every page has a vertical divider — left side for notes, right side for action items. If you've ever tried the Cornell method or Bullet Journaling, this is a simplified, more rigid version of both. It forces structure without requiring you to learn a system.

The paper is the standout. At 100gsm, it handles fountain pens, rollerballs, and even mild markers without ghosting. Several buyers mentioned they use it for watercolor notes, which is a stretch, but it's genuinely bleed-resistant for standard office writing. One reviewer noted, "I use a Pilot G2 and a Lamy fountain pen — zero bleed-through on either." That's consistent with what I'd expect from this paper weight.

The lay-flat binding isn't marketing hype. It stays open at any angle, which matters more than people think during long meetings.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't inflated. The recurring themes in customer feedback are:

- **The layout saves time.** A reviewer wrote: "I no longer re-read whole pages looking for action items. The right column is my task list, period."
- **Paper quality surprises people.** "Expected standard office notebook paper. Got something close to a premium journal."
- **Durability.** Multiple reviewers mention the cover and binding holding up after months of daily carry.

## Where It Falls Short

The fixed 50/50 split is the biggest limitation. If you're in a meeting where the discussion is mostly informational with few action items, the right column sits empty while you run out of space on the left. Some users adapt by using the right column for follow-up questions or deadlines, but you can't adjust the ratio.

There's also no index or page numbers. The Amazon listing doesn't advertise those features, but for a notebook marketed as "professional," the omission is noticeable. You'll need your own tabs or sticky flags if you want to retrieve past meeting notes efficiently.

The cover picks up fingerprints and scuffs easily. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're particular about aesthetics, expect it to show wear.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Project managers and consultants** who live in back-to-back meetings and need a consistent capture system.
- **Students** taking lecture notes that include both concepts and assigned tasks.
- **Anyone who's tried bullet journaling** and found it too flexible — this gives you the structure without the learning curve.

Skip it if you prefer free-form note-taking, or if you need one notebook for multiple purposes (meetings, journaling, sketching). This is a single-purpose tool.

## FAQ

**Does it fit in a standard bag?**
Yes. The A5 size fits in most laptop bags, purses, and even larger coat pockets. It's roughly the same footprint as a Kindle.

**Can I remove pages cleanly?**
No. The pages are sewn in, not perforated. If you need to tear out pages regularly, look elsewhere.

**Is it fountain-pen friendly?**
Yes. The 100gsm paper is the strongest feature here. I'd still avoid heavy marker use, but standard fountain pen ink and gel pens won't bleed through.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want your meeting notes to translate into action items without a second pass. The layout is a genuine productivity upgrade, and the paper quality exceeds the price point.

**Skip it if:** You need a flexible notebook for multiple uses, or you're not willing to adapt to a rigid two-column structure.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The fixed layout and missing index hold it back from perfection, but for its intended purpose, it's one of the best meeting notebooks available at this price.
