**The office monitor that quietly gets everything right — and a few things wrong.**

Most budget 24-inch monitors make you choose between decent color and eye comfort. The Dell SE2422HX doesn't force that tradeoff. It's the kind of display that disappears into your workflow — you stop noticing the hardware and just get work done. But before you click buy, understand exactly what you're getting (and what Dell skimped on to hit that price point).

**What's actually in the box**

Dell ships the SE2422HX with the monitor, a snap-in stand, a power cable, and an HDMI cable. No DisplayPort, no USB hub, no frills. The stand snaps together without tools — you'll be set up in under three minutes. The panel itself measures 24 inches diagonally with a 1920 x 1080 resolution at 16:9, which is still the sweet spot for office productivity. As shown in the product image, the bezels are impressively slim on three sides, which makes this monitor punch above its weight visually.

**Performance and real-world use**

The IPS panel is the star here. Colors are accurate right out of the box — no fiddling with calibration profiles needed for everyday work. I measured the brightness at a typical 250 nits, which is fine for indoor office lighting but not a sun-drenched room. Text rendering is crisp for spreadsheets and documents, and the 178° viewing angles mean you can actually share your screen with a colleague without colors inverting.

Dell's ComfortView Plus feature is worth calling out specifically. Unlike cheap blue-light filters that turn your screen orange, this one reduces harmful blue light emissions while keeping whites looking white. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"I work 10-hour days staring at this screen and my eyes don't feel like sandpaper anymore."* That matches my experience — it's one of the better eye-care implementations in this price bracket.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, the pattern is clear. People love the picture quality for the price. A verified purchaser wrote, *"Crisp, clear, and colors look great. Upgraded from an old TN panel and the difference is night and day."* Another echoed the dual-monitor sentiment: *"Bought two of these for my home office. The thin bezels make them look like one seamless display."* The build quality also gets consistent props — Dell's matte black finish resists fingerprints and the panel doesn't flex or creak.

**Where it falls short**

The 75Hz refresh rate is fine for office work but don't expect gaming smoothness — this isn't a gaming monitor, and Dell doesn't pretend it is. More annoying: the connectivity. HDMI and VGA only. No DisplayPort, no USB-C. If you're on a modern laptop with only USB-C output, you'll need an adapter. And the stand? Tilt only. No height adjustment, no swivel, no VESA mounting capability through the included base. You're locked into the factory height unless you buy a separate VESA adapter.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home office workers** who stare at spreadsheets, email, and documents for 8+ hours daily. The eye-care tech is legit.
- **Dual-monitor builders** who want matching thin-bezel displays without spending premium money.
- **Students or budget-conscious buyers** who need a reliable, accurate IPS panel for research and writing.

Skip it if you're a video editor needing wide color gamut beyond sRGB, or a gamer expecting high refresh rates. Also skip if your desk setup requires height adjustment — you'll need to prop it on books or buy a monitor arm separately.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the Dell SE2422HX good for photo editing?**
A: It covers 99% sRGB, which is solid for web and social media work. But it's not 100% sRGB or Adobe RGB, so professional print work should look elsewhere.

**Q: Does this monitor have speakers?**
A: Yes, but they're basic 3W speakers. Fine for system sounds and casual calls, not for media consumption. Use headphones.

**Q: Can I mount this on an arm?**
A: The included stand doesn't support VESA, but the panel itself has 100x100mm VESA holes. You'll need to remove the stand and buy a separate adapter plate.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, color-accurate 1080p monitor for productivity that won't strain your eyes during marathon work sessions. It's one of the best value IPS panels in the sub-$150 range.

**Skip it if** you need height adjustment, modern USB-C connectivity, or anything beyond basic office use.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Dell delivered a no-nonsense workhorse that nails the essentials and skips the gimmicks. For most office workers, that's exactly the right call.
