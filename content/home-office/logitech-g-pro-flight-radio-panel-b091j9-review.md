If you fly Microsoft Flight Simulator or X-Plane and you’re still reaching for your mouse to click tiny radio dials, you’re doing it wrong. The Logitech G Pro Flight Radio Panel fixes that pain point with a dedicated, cockpit-grade hardware interface for managing COM and NAV frequencies. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it’s clearly doing something right. Here’s my honest breakdown after digging deep into what owners actually say.

## What you actually get

The box contains the radio panel itself, a USB cable, and a quick-start guide. No software CD, no bloatware — plug the USB into your PC, and it's recognized instantly by Microsoft Flight Simulator 2020/2024 and X-Plane 11/12. The unit measures roughly 8.5 x 4.5 x 1.5 inches and has a weighted metal base that gives it a reassuring heft. As one reviewer put it, “It feels like a real cockpit instrument, not a toy.” The front face features two large rotary knobs for frequency selection, three smaller knobs for standby/active swap and barometer adjustment, and a backlit LCD display showing both active and standby frequencies.

## Real-world performance

The standout feature is the dual concentric knobs — one for the whole number, one for decimals. This lets you dial in frequencies quickly without cycling through every digit. The LCD screens are bright and readable in daylight, and the backlighting works well in darker sim rooms. Setup takes under two minutes: plug in, launch the sim, and the panel auto-maps to COM1/COM2 and NAV1/NAV2.

But there’s a learning curve. The knobs are smooth-turning with no detents, which means precise inputs require deliberate, controlled rotations. One buyer noted, “I had to slow down my knob twisting to avoid overshooting frequencies — but once I got used to it, it became second nature.” The barometer knob on the right side is a nice touch for altitude-sensitive flying, though it’s not assignable to all aircraft profiles.

## What buyers consistently praise

Reviewers across the board highlight three things: build quality, instant sim integration, and the immersion factor. One top review states, “This panel transformed my sim setup — I no longer fumble with mouse clicks during approach.” Another praises the “crisp, bright displays that are easy to read without leaning in.” The weighted base gets frequent mentions, with owners confirming it doesn’t slide around on a desk even during aggressive knob twisting.

## Where it falls short

No product is perfect, and this panel has two notable quirks. First, if you want dual COM/NAV functionality (running two panels), you’ll need to connect both via USB and manually configure them in your sim’s settings — it’s not a seamless plug-and-play for multi-panel setups. Second, the Logitech Gaming Software utility is Windows-only, so Mac users are stuck with default sim mappings and can’t customize button assignments. A few buyers also mentioned that the knobs feel slightly loose compared to the yoke’s machined controls, though this doesn’t affect functionality.

## Who should buy this

- **MSFS/X-Plane enthusiasts** who fly airliners or GA aircraft and want faster frequency management
- **Sim pit builders** looking for an affordable, realistic radio stack component
- **Streamers or YouTubers** who want tactile controls visible on camera

Skip it if you only fly casual, arcade-style sims, or if you’re on macOS and want deep customization.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024?**  
A: Yes, it’s plug-and-play with MSFS 2024, just as with 2020. No additional drivers needed.

**Q: Can I use two panels for dual COM/NAV?**  
A: Yes, but you’ll need to connect both via USB and assign them manually in the sim’s controller settings. It’s a bit fiddly but works.

**Q: Is the backlighting adjustable?**  
A: No, it’s a fixed brightness — bright enough for daylight, not blinding in the dark.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you’re serious about flight sim realism and want to eliminate mouse-clicking during critical flight phases. **Skip it if** you fly casually or need Mac support.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-built, immersive peripheral with minor quirks that don't outweigh its practical value. At $150-180 street price, it’s a worthwhile upgrade for dedicated simmers.
