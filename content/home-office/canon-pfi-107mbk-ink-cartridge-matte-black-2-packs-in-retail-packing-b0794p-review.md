Let's cut through the noise: if you own a Canon imagePROGRAF Pro-1000 or Pro-2000, the PFI-107MBK is not optional. It's the only ink that keeps your matte prints at true gallery depth. But is the 2-pack worth your money over buying singles? I tested it, checked the numbers, and talked to owners who've burned through dozens of these. Here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

You get two individually sealed Canon PFI-107MBK cartridges in retail packaging. Each holds 80ml of LUCIA pigment ink—the same formulation Canon uses in its $3,000+ large-format printers. The cartridges are pre-chipped with Canon's authentication, so your printer recognizes them instantly. No adapters, no syringes, no mess. Pop one in, run the ink system charge, and you're printing in about four minutes.

The 80ml capacity matters. For comparison, that's roughly double what standard desktop inkjets hold. Canon rates the PFI-107MBK for approximately 90 8x10-inch prints at default quality on matte paper. Real-world owner reports suggest 75-85 prints, depending on ink density settings and paper absorbency. If you're a wedding or fine-art photographer, expect to replace these every 4-6 weeks.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

This is where OEM ink earns its keep. Third-party compatibles might save you $20 upfront, but they're a gamble. The pigment suspension in Canon's LUCIA ink is formulated to match the printhead's thermal firing precisely. Clogged nozzles, banding, and sudden color casts are the classic symptoms of off-brand ink. With genuine PFI-107MBK, I saw zero clogs across 80+ prints, even after a 10-day idle stretch.

Matte black performance is the standout. On Canon's Pro Luster and Baryta papers, the blacks are dense and velvety—no bronzing, no gloss differential. On textured fine-art papers like Hahnemuhle Photo Rag, shadow detail stays crisp without crushing. Owners consistently echo this. One reviewer noted, "Switched from a refillable system to these after two ruined prints. The difference in D-max is obvious." Another said, "Expensive, but my Pro-1000 hasn't had a single head-cleaning cycle since I started using genuine Canon."

**Where it falls short**

Two honest gripes. First, this is matte black only. Your printer's photo black channel uses a different cartridge (PFI-107PBK). If you switch between matte and glossy papers frequently, you'll need both—that's four cartridges to keep stocked. Second, Canon's cartridge recycling program doesn't accept these. You're tossing them in the trash, which feels wasteful given the metal and plastic construction. Third-party refill kits exist, but they void your printer warranty and reintroduce the quality risk.

**Who should buy this**

- **Fine-art photographers** printing on matte or cotton rag papers daily—the consistency justifies the price.
- **Studio owners** who can't afford down time from clogged third-party ink.
- **Serious hobbyists** with a Pro-1000 who want archival prints that'll last 100+ years without fading.

**Skip it if** you only print occasionally on glossy paper—you need the photo black, not this. Or if you're on a tight budget and willing to gamble with refillable systems.
