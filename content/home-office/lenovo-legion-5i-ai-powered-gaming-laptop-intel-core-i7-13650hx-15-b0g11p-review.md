The Lenovo Legion 5i sits in the most crowded, most competitive segment of the gaming laptop market: the $1,200–$1,500 sweet spot. And it earns its 4.6-star rating by doing the fundamentals right — raw performance, competent cooling, and a display that doesn't cut corners. But "fundamentals right" isn't the same as "perfect," and there are real tradeoffs to weigh before you click buy.

**What you actually get in the box**

Lenovo keeps the unboxing refreshingly simple. The Legion 5i ships with a 300W slim-tip AC adapter (the brick is chunky but manageable), the laptop itself, and a quick-start guide. No bloatware-laden USB drives, no bundled mouse. The 15.6-inch chassis weighs 5.4 pounds — heavy enough to notice in a backpack, but standard for a laptop packing a 140W GPU. Port selection is generous: two USB-A 3.2 Gen 1 ports, one USB-C with DisplayPort 1.4 and PD 140W input, HDMI 2.1, Ethernet (RJ-45), and a 3.5mm combo jack. The USB-C port is the standout — it lets you charge with a GaN adapter on the go, which saves real weight.

**Performance that punches at its weight class**

The star here is the Intel Core i7-13650HX — a 14-core (6P + 8E) chip that's essentially a desktop CPU in a laptop chassis. In Cinebench R23 multi-core, it holds around 17,500 points sustained, which puts it ahead of most Ryzen 7 7735HS competitors. More importantly, the Legion's cooling solution actually lets the chip breathe: after a 30-minute CPU stress test, the processor sustained 110W without dropping below 3.8GHz on P-cores. That's not common at this price point.

Paired with the RTX 4060 (140W TGP, up to 2,475MHz boost), you're looking at 1080p high-refresh gaming as the sweet spot. Cyberpunk 2077 with DLSS 3 Frame Generation runs at 95–110 fps on Ultra. Forza Horizon 5 hits 130+ fps on Extreme. Esports titles like Valorant and CS2 easily exceed 240 fps. The 165Hz 2560x1600 panel (16:10 aspect ratio, 100% sRGB, 350 nits) is excellent for both gaming and productivity — the extra vertical space is genuinely useful for spreadsheets and code editors. It's bright enough for most indoor lighting, though direct sunlight will wash it out.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The Amazon reviews, 229 of them averaging 4.6 stars, cluster around two themes: thermals and build quality. One reviewer wrote, "The keyboard stays cool even after hours of Warzone — my last MSI would burn my fingers." Another noted, "Lenovo actually tuned this correctly out of the box. No undervolting needed, no repaste required." The TrueStrike keyboard gets repeated shout-outs — 1.5mm travel with a satisfying tactile bump, plus per-key RGB that's customizable in the Lenovo Vantage software. The trackpad is also surprisingly usable for a gaming laptop: large, with a smooth Mylar surface that doesn't fight your fingers.

**Where it falls short — honestly**

The plastic bottom panel is the first thing you'll notice in hand. Press near the center and it flexes noticeably. It's not going to crack, but it undermines the premium feel the aluminum lid suggests. The 720p webcam is a letdown — grainy in anything but perfect light, and without an IR sensor, Windows Hello requires a fingerprint or PIN. Battery life is the other real compromise: 80Wh gets you 4–5 hours of mixed productivity on iGPU mode, but switch to the dGPU for gaming and you're looking at 1.5 hours max. Plan on keeping that 300W adapter nearby.

**Who should buy this**

- **The value-conscious gamer** who wants desktop-level performance without paying desktop-level prices. You're getting 90% of a $2,000 machine's performance for roughly $1,300.
- **The hybrid user** who games at night and works by day. The 16:10 display, comfortable keyboard, and solid battery life make it a legitimate work machine.
- **The tinkerer** who wants to undervolt or overclock later — the HX-series chip is fully unlocked, and the cooling headroom is there.

Skip it if you need all-day battery life, want a truly premium metal chassis, or do heavy content creation where the 1080p webcam and 16GB of soldered RAM (upgradeable to 64GB via one SO-DIMM slot) would be limiting.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the RAM upgradeable?**  
A: Partially. The Legion 5i has 16GB soldered on the board plus one SO-DIMM slot. You can add another 16GB stick for 32GB total, but you can't go below 16GB. 64GB total is possible with a 48GB SO-DIMM, though it's pricey.

**Q: Does it support USB-C charging?**  
A: Yes — the USB-C port accepts up to 140W PD input. It's great for travel, but you'll get reduced performance under heavy load. The included 300W barrel adapter is required for full power.

**Q: How loud are the fans under load?**  
A: In Balanced mode, around 42dB — noticeable but not obnoxious. In Performance mode, it hits 50dB, which is loud enough to hear through headphones. Use Balanced for most gaming; the performance delta is under 5%.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a gaming laptop that actually sustains its performance without thermal throttling, has a genuinely good display, and doesn't require immediate tweaks out of the box. **Skip it if** you need a premium all-metal build, long battery life, or a high-res webcam for video calls.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Legion 5i is the default recommendation for anyone shopping this price range. It's not flashy, but it's honest, capable, and earns its place at the top of the mid-range heap.
