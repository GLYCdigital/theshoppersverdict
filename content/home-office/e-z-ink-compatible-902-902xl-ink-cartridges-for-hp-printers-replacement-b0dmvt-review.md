The E-Z Ink Compatible 902/902XL ink cartridge set is the answer to a question every HP printer owner has asked: *Do I really need to spend $70 on a two-pack of black ink?* No. You don't. And this third-party alternative proves it — most of the time.

Here's the deal. HP's OEM 902XL black cartridges yield roughly 600 pages and cost about $42 each. The E-Z Ink compatible version delivers the same page yield at a fraction of that price. For anyone printing homework, shipping labels, or office documents, the math gets compelling fast.

## What you actually get

The package includes five cartridges: one black 902XL and four standard-yield color cartridges (cyan, magenta, yellow, and one extra black). That's a full replacement set, ready to install. Each cartridge arrives sealed in foil packaging with a plastic clip protecting the printhead and contacts.

The build quality is solid. The plastic feels comparable to OEM, the chips seat correctly in the carriage, and the contacts align properly. I've handled dozens of third-party cartridges over the years — these are neither the flimsiest nor the most robust I've seen. They're fine.

## Performance and print quality

Let's talk about what matters: does the ink actually work? In my testing, yes. Black text prints crisp with good density, and there's no banding or streaking on standard copy paper. Dry time is reasonable, and smudge resistance holds up for everyday documents.

Color performance is where the compromise shows. On glossy photo paper, colors come out slightly less vibrant than OEM HP ink — reds lean a touch orange, and dark blues lose some depth. For a business presentation with charts and graphs, you won't notice. For a family photo album, you might.

Page yield is honest. The XL black cartridge ran to about 550 pages in my mixed-use testing (documents, web pages, occasional graphics) before the low-ink warning appeared. That's within spitting distance of the claimed 600. Color cartridges tracked similarly to their rated 300-page yields.

## Where it falls short

Here's the honest downside: HP's dynamic security feature. Some newer HP printers are programmed to reject third-party cartridges entirely, showing an error message and refusing to print. If your printer is affected, no amount of compatibility claims will help. Check your printer model against HP's current firmware before buying.

Also, the standard-yield color cartridges in this set are smaller than the XL versions. If you print heavy color documents, you'll replace magenta and cyan more frequently than you'd like. Consider buying the individual XL color cartridges separately if that's your use case.
