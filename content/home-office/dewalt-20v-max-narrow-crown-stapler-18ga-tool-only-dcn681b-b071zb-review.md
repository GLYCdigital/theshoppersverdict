Let's cut through the specs. The DEWALT DCN681B is a cordless narrow crown stapler that promises to replace your pneumatic setup for trim, cabinetry, and furniture work. After digging through 235 ratings and the technical details, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the DCN681B stapler only. No battery, no charger, no case. This is a tool-only purchase, which makes sense if you're already invested in DEWALT's 20V MAX system — but it's a significant add-on cost if you're starting fresh. You'll need a 20V battery (at least a 2Ah, though 4Ah+ is better for sustained work) and a charger. The tool accepts 18GA narrow crown staples ranging from 1/2" to 1-1/2" — you'll find these at any hardware store, which is a practical win.

## Performance and Build

The brushless motor is the heart here. It delivers consistent driving power across the full staple range, and the tool-free depth adjustment on the nose lets you dial in flush or countersunk drives without reaching for a wrench. The magazine holds 100 staples and has a dry-fire lockout — it won't fire when empty, which saves you from wasted staples and protects the driver blade. That's a thoughtful touch.

In real-world use, this stapler handles plywood, MDF, and hardwood trim without breaking a sweat. The 20V battery gives you genuine cordless freedom — no compressor, no hose, no noise. You can work in a client's living room without dragging air lines through the house. The trigger response is snappy, and the sequential firing mode works well for precise placement. That said, it's not a framing tool — it's built for finish work, and it knows its lane.

One thing to note: the weight and balance. At over 7 pounds with a battery, it's noticeably heavier and more nose-heavy than a pneumatic stapler. For overhead work or long days of production stapling, your forearm will feel it. Multiple buyers flagged this. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Great stapler, but it's a beast. My arm gets tired after a couple hours of cabinet work."*

## Where It Falls Short

The jam clearing is the biggest annoyance. When a staple misfeeds — and it will eventually, especially with cheap staples — you need to remove two screws to access the mechanism. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a 5-minute interruption versus the tool-free jam release on some competitors like the Milwaukee or Ryobi units. Pair that with the nose-heavy balance, and you have two clear tradeoffs for cordless convenience.

Battery life is also worth managing. A 2Ah battery will get you through roughly 500-700 staples, which sounds fine until you're in the middle of a trim-out. A 4Ah pack is the smart play. And while the stapler handles a range of staple lengths, it doesn't like being pushed beyond 1-1/2" — so don't expect it to double as a framing nailer.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The ratings tell a consistent story. The power and reliability dominate positive feedback. One buyer wrote, *"Replaced my pneumatic for baseboards and shoe molding — haven't touched the compressor in three months."* Another highlighted the build quality: *"Typical DEWALT toughness. Dropped it off a ladder onto concrete, still fires perfectly."* The depth adjustment gets repeated shout-outs for its precision — a feature that matters when you're doing finish work where every staple head counts.

## Who Should Buy This

You're the target buyer if you're already on the DEWALT 20V platform and do regular finish carpentry, upholstery, or cabinet work. It's also a strong pick if you hate compressor noise and hose management. Skip it if you're a weekend warrior doing a single project — the battery investment isn't worth it. And if you're doing production work, the weight might push you toward a pneumatic setup.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this stapler work with any 18GA staple brand?**
A: Yes, it accepts standard 18GA narrow crown staples from 1/2" to 1-1/2". DEWALT recommends their own, but Arrow, Bostitch, and generic staples work fine. Cheap staples increase jam frequency — use quality staples.

**Q: Can I use this for upholstery work?**
A: Yes, the narrow crown design is ideal for fabric and thin wood. The depth adjustment lets you dial in flush drives without piercing through. Just be aware of the weight for prolonged overhead fabric work.

**Q: How many staples per battery charge?**
A: With a 2Ah battery, expect roughly 500-700 staples. A 4Ah or 5Ah battery will push past 1,200. Keep a spare battery charged for full-day work.
