If your SSD dies tomorrow, your photos, work files, and tax documents die with it. That’s the cold truth about solid-state drives — when they fail, they often fail completely, with no warning and no chance to spin up for one last backup. The Rescue 2-Year Data Recovery Plan is designed for that exact moment. Here’s what it actually does, and whether it’s worth the money.

## What You’re Actually Buying

This isn’t software. It’s a service contract. You pay once, register the plan, and for two years you’re covered for up to one data recovery attempt on a single SSD. The box contains your plan activation code and instructions — nothing else. You’re buying peace of mind, not a product you can hold.

The coverage is broad: accidental deletion, formatted drives, corrupted partitions, virus damage, power surges, and physical failures like a dead controller chip. That last part matters because SSDs don’t have moving parts — when they go, it’s usually the controller or NAND flash failing, which requires lab-level equipment to recover.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Here’s the honest part. Data recovery from SSDs is harder than from traditional hard drives. The TRIM command, which is standard on modern SSDs, actively erases deleted data blocks. If your drive fails and TRIM was enabled, recovery success drops significantly. Rescue’s own literature acknowledges this — they’ll attempt recovery, but they don’t guarantee success on SSDs.

That said, when it works, it works well. Buyers report turnaround times of 1–3 weeks from shipping the drive to receiving recovered files. One reviewer noted: *“Sent in my dead Samsung 970 EVO, got back a flash drive with my financial records and family photos. Took 11 days total.”* Another said: *“They recovered 90% of my thesis after my laptop took a power surge. Worth every penny.”*

The service uses a secure lab with cleanroom facilities for physical recoveries, and you get a detailed report of what was recovered and what wasn’t. For logical failures — accidental deletion, corrupted filesystems — success rates are much higher, closer to 90% in most user reports.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The flat-fee model is the standout feature. You pay once, and there are no surprise charges. One customer summarized it well: *“No per-gigabyte fees, no 'we need a bigger cleanroom' upsell. The price quoted was the price paid.”* That’s rare in the data recovery industry.

Buyers also appreciate the brand-agnostic coverage. Whether you’re running a Crucial, Samsung, WD, or Sabrent SSD, this plan covers it. You don’t need to match the plan to your drive’s manufacturer.

## Where It Falls Short

**Registration is on you.** If you buy this and never activate it online, you’re not covered. Several negative reviews stemmed from buyers who assumed the plan auto-activated on purchase. It doesn’t. Set a calendar reminder.

**The 2-year clock starts at purchase, not first use.** If you buy this as a spare plan for a drive you’re installing next year, you’re burning coverage time.

**SSD recovery isn’t guaranteed.** This is the big one. Unlike HDDs, where platters can often be read with specialized equipment, NAND flash recovery is more complex. If the drive’s controller is fried, recovery might require chip-off procedures that aren’t always successful. The plan doesn’t promise a recovery — it promises an *attempt*.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this cover accidental deletion after the file is already in the trash bin?**  
Yes. Logical recovery covers deleted files, even after emptying the recycle bin — as long as the SSD hasn’t run TRIM on those blocks already.

**Q: Can I transfer the plan to a new SSD if I upgrade?**  
No. The plan is tied to the specific drive you registered at activation. If you replace the SSD, you need a new plan.

**Q: How long does the actual recovery take?**  
Most buyers report 7–14 days from shipping the drive to receiving recovered data. Express options exist for an extra fee.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you store irreplaceable data on a single SSD and don’t have a bulletproof backup routine. It’s a cheap safety net for a catastrophic failure.

**Skip it if** you already run automated cloud backups or have a second local drive with mirrored data.
