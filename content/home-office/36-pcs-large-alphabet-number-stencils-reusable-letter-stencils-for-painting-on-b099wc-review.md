**What's in the box**

The set delivers exactly what the title promises: 36 individual stencils. You get the full alphabet (26 letters), numbers 0-9, plus a few bonus punctuation marks — ampersand, question mark, exclamation point, and a couple of common symbols. Each stencil is a 6x6-inch sheet of flexible mylar with roughly 2-inch-tall characters. That's a workable size for poster board, wooden signs, canvas totes, or even larger journal pages.

The individual-sheet format is actually smarter than the multi-character strips many competitors sell. You can position each letter independently, which matters when you're spacing out a word on a curved surface or need precise alignment.

**Real-world performance**

The mylar material is the star here. It's thin enough to cut clean lines but flexible enough to conform to rounded objects. I've seen buyers use these on stainless steel tumblers, ceramic mugs, and even mildly curved wooden plaques — the stencil bends without creasing or pulling away from the surface. One reviewer noted, "I used these on curved wine glasses and the paint stayed crisp with zero bleed-through."

Cleanup is straightforward. Acrylic paint wipes off with warm soapy water, and the material doesn't warp from solvent-based cleaners. The stencils are also reusable — I've seen reports of 10+ uses per stencil with proper care.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The completeness of the set gets mentioned repeatedly. No running to the craft store mid-project because you're missing a "Q" or a "7." One verified purchase said, "Finally a set with all the letters and numbers. My last set was missing three letters and I didn't notice until I was halfway through a sign."

The size is another consistent win. At 2 inches, the characters are readable from across a room — perfect for garage sale signage, classroom bulletin boards, or warehouse labeling. But they're not so large that you're wrestling with unwieldy sheets.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the limitations. The mylar is on the thinner side — roughly 0.2mm. If you're using spray adhesive to hold it in place, use a light mist. Too much tack and the edges curl when you peel it off, which can smear wet paint. A few reviewers mentioned this exact issue.

Also, this is strictly uppercase and numbers. No lowercase, no script, no decorative fonts. If your project needs elegant cursive or mixed-case text, look elsewhere. For basic block lettering, this set nails it.

**Who should buy this**

This set is ideal for three specific buyers: DIY crafters making custom signs or gifts, teachers setting up classroom displays, and small business owners labeling products or creating window signage. If you're a professional sign painter or need fine-detail work, you'll want sturdier laser-cut brass stencils — but you'll also pay five times as much.

**FAQ**

**Can these stencils handle spray paint?**
Yes, but tape them down securely. The thin edges can lift with overspray pressure. Painter's tape works better than spray adhesive here.

**Are the letters one continuous piece or individual cutouts?**
The letters are cut as individual shapes within each 6x6 sheet. The entire sheet is one stencil with one character per sheet — so you get 36 separate stencils, not one big multi-letter sheet.

**Will paint bleed under the edges?**
With a dry brush or light sponge application, no. With a heavily loaded brush, you'll get minor bleed. The mylar edge is crisp, but thin materials always have slightly less contact pressure than thick acrylic stencils.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a complete, budget-friendly lettering set for hobby projects, classroom use, or occasional signage. The 4.5-star rating from 225 buyers isn't accidental — this delivers consistent results at a price that won't make you wince.

**Skip it if** you need lowercase letters, work with heavy-bodied paints regularly, or plan to use these professionally every single day. For those cases, invest in thicker stencils.
