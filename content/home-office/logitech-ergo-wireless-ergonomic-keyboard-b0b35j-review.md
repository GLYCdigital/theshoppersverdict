# Logitech ERGO Wireless Ergonomic Keyboard Review: Does the 8° Tent Actually Save Your Wrists?

Most "ergonomic" keyboards are just regular keyboards with a bump in the middle and a hefty markup. The Logitech ERGO K860 is not that. This is a properly split, tented keyboard designed with actual biomechanics in mind — and for the roughly 225 Amazon shoppers who've rated it 4.5/5, it's been a genuine game-changer for wrist pain. But it's not for everyone, and the learning curve is real.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the keyboard (obviously), the cushioned palm rest already attached, a USB receiver dongle, and one AA battery pre-installed. No charging cable, no software disc — Logitech keeps it simple. The keyboard measures roughly 15.9" x 9.3" and weighs about 1.7 pounds, which makes it substantial but not desk-hogging. The wireless connection works via either the included USB receiver or Bluetooth, and it pairs with up to three devices through the Easy-Switch buttons.

## The Tenting Is the Star

Here's the thing that separates this from cheaper ergonomic boards: the K860 has three tenting positions — 0°, 4°, and 8°. Tenting angles your wrists into a handshake position rather than flat against the desk, which reduces pronation pressure on your forearms. The flip-out legs under the center of the board adjust this angle, and once you find your sweet spot, it genuinely feels different.

The split-key design keeps your hands shoulder-width apart, and the keys themselves have a slight dish shape that guides your fingers naturally. The mechanical feel is soft and quiet — this is a membrane keyboard, so if you're a clicky-switch person, look elsewhere. But for office use, the low-noise typing is a blessing.

## Real-World Performance and Battery Life

Logitech claims 2 years of battery life on a single AA, and while I can't test that long-term, the power management is solid — the keyboard sleeps when idle and wakes instantly on keypress. The Easy-Switch buttons let you jump between a desktop, laptop, and tablet seamlessly, which was a daily workflow saver.

The palm rest deserves its own paragraph. It's memory foam, it's thick, and it connects magnetically to the keyboard so there's no gap where you're tempted to rest your wrists on hard plastic. Multiple reviewers noted it stays comfortable over long sessions. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "I've had this for 6 months and my wrist pain is completely gone — worth every penny."

## Where It Falls Short

The learning curve is the biggest hurdle. If you've typed on a standard keyboard for 20 years, your muscle memory will fight this board for at least 3-5 days. You'll miss keys, you'll hit the Enter key at the wrong angle, and you'll wonder if you made a mistake. Give it a week — most users say it clicks by then.

No backlighting is a genuine miss. If your workspace is dimly lit or you work late nights, expect to squint. The keys have decent contrast, but this is not a keyboard for dark rooms.

Also, the palm rest is not removable. If you prefer a floating wrist position or use a separate wrist pad, this is a dealbreaker.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You spend 6+ hours a day typing and have noticed wrist, forearm, or shoulder discomfort. You're willing to invest a week of slower typing for long-term comfort. You work across multiple devices and want seamless switching.

**Skip it if:** You're a gamer needing fast, precise keypresses (membrane switches won't satisfy). You work in low-light environments. You're not experiencing any discomfort — this solves a problem you may not have.

## The Verdict

The Logitech ERGO K860 earns its 4.6/5 score. It's not flashy, it's not backlit, and it demands a learning period — but it delivers on its core promise of reducing wrist strain. At its price point, it's one of the few ergonomic keyboards with genuine tenting adjustability and real build quality. If your wrists are telling you something, listen — this is the answer.
