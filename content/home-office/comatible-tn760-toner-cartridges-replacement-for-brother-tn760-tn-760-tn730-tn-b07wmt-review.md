## Print Quality: The Real Test

Black text at 600 DPI comes out crisp and dark — I couldn't distinguish output from my last OEM cartridge in a blind comparison. Business documents, invoices, and text-heavy PDFs render cleanly with no streaking or fading. At 2,400 DPI, fine print stays sharp.

## Where It Falls Short — Read This Before Buying

**The chip situation.** This cartridge doesn't include a reset chip. Once your printer's toner sensor reads low, you'll get a persistent "Replace Toner" message. You can dismiss it via the printer menu, but it returns until you physically replace the cartridge. If you're the type who ignores warnings, this is fine. If you want a set-and-forget experience, this is the tradeoff.

**Ink density variance.** On the last 300 pages, I noticed slightly lighter output than the first 2,700. Not enough to compromise readability, but if you demand uniform density across an entire cartridge life, OEM remains the benchmark.

**No warranty coverage.** Brother's warranty technically excludes damage from third-party supplies. In practice, toner cartridges rarely cause printer damage, but know the risk before you buy.
