If you own a Brother HL-L2350DW, MFC-L2710DW, or any of the dozen printers that take the TN730/TN770 series, you already know the dirty secret: the standard-yield cartridge runs dry faster than you expect, and buying singles means paying the "convenience tax" every few months. This 2-pack of genuine TN770 Super High Yield cartridges is the fix — but it's not the cheapest fix. Here's the honest math.

## What's in the box

Two sealed TN770 cartridges, each rated for 6,000 pages at 5% coverage. That's triple the 2,600-page yield of the standard TN730. The package is plain retail — no tools, no gloves, no instructions beyond the QR code pointing to Brother's install video. Installation is genuinely tool-free: peel the orange tape, rock the cartridge into the drum slot, close the front cover. The chip is pre-installed, so there's zero firmware fiddling or "toner low" override tricks.

## Real-world performance

I've run these through a Brother MFC-L2710DW over several months. Print quality is consistent — crisp text down to 6-point fonts, no streaking, no gray banding on dense graphics. Brother's genuine toner also handles 20-pound multipurpose paper without smudging, which third-party cartridges often struggle with due to inconsistent particle size.

The yield claim holds up under normal office use. At roughly 1,200 pages per month of mixed text and light graphics, each cartridge lasted about five months. That aligns with Brother's 6,000-page rating. One note: this is a toner cartridge, not a drum. The drum unit (DR730) is separate and typically lasts 12,000 pages — plan to replace it every second or third toner swap.

## Where it falls short

The honest downsides:

- **Upfront cost.** You'll pay roughly $70–90 for this 2-pack. A generic two-pack runs $25–35. If you print fewer than 500 pages a month, the standard-yield TN730 single at ~$45 will get you 2,600 pages — and you won't tie up cash in inventory.
- **No expiration date on the box.** Toner does degrade. If you buy this and let it sit in a humid garage for 18 months, expect clumping and streaky prints. Store it in a climate-controlled space.
- **5% coverage rating is the asterisk.** Heavy graphics or dense legal documents will cut yield by 30–40%. That's true of all OEM cartridges, but worth remembering when budgeting.

## Who should buy this

- **Home-office users printing 800+ pages monthly** — this is the sweet spot. You'll swap cartridges half as often as standard-yield.
- **Small businesses with Brother laser printers** — the 2-pack means one less reorder cycle per quarter, and genuine toner keeps your print quality client-ready.
- **Anyone burned by third-party cartridges** — if you've cleaned toner dust off a drum or dealt with faded prints, the premium is worth it.

Skip it if you're a light printer (under 200 pages a month) or on a tight budget — the standard TN730 single will serve you fine.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my Brother HL-L2350DW?**
Yes. The TN770 fits all Brother printers that accept TN730-series cartridges, including HL-L23xx, HL-L28xx, MFC-L27xx, and MFC-L28xx models. Check your manual or Brother's compatibility page to confirm.

**Q: What's the actual cost per page?**
At a $75 street price for the 2-pack, you're looking at roughly 0.6 cents per page per cartridge (12,000 total pages). That's excellent for an OEM product — most standard-yield cartridges run 1.5–2 cents per page.

**Q: Is the chip reusable if I refill?**
No. The chip is one-time use and locks after installation. Refilling genuine cartridges requires a chip resetter, which voids any warranty and risks leaks. If you want refillable, buy a third-party cartridge designed for it — but accept the quality risk.
