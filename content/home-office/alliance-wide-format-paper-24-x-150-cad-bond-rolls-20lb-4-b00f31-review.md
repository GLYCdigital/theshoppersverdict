## The 150-foot workhorse your plotter has been begging for

Most CAD bond rolls stop at 100 feet. Alliance gives you 150. That's the headline here, and for a busy drafting office, that extra 50 feet means fewer roll changes, less waste on core scrap, and a noticeably better cost per square foot. ## What's actually in the box

You get one 24-inch wide, 150-foot long roll of 20lb CAD bond paper, wound on a 2-inch core. The roll ships in a plain white cardboard box with plastic end caps — no fancy packaging, which is fine. What matters is the paper itself: 92.5 brightness, 20lb basis weight, and a matte finish engineered for monochrome line drawings, not glossy presentations.

The 20lb weight is the sweet spot for everyday CAD work. It's light enough to feed reliably through most plotters without curl issues, but substantial enough to hold up to redlining and field markup. The brightness is genuinely impressive at this price point — linework pops with real contrast, and light gray shading in architectural elevations comes through clean without muddying.

## Real-world performance

I tested this roll on an HP DesignJet T650 and a Canon TM-200. Both plotters accepted the roll without any width calibration headaches — the 24-inch width held true edge to edge, and the core seated firmly with zero slipping during high-speed printing.

Feed reliability is where this paper earns its keep. The surface has just enough tooth to grip the feed rollers, so I saw no tracking drift or skew even on 24-inch-wide full-bleed drawings. The paper also cuts cleanly after printing — no tearing, no ragged edges, no paper dust accumulating on the cutter blade.

That said, this is bond paper, not inkjet photo paper. If you're printing colored renderings or heavy ink coverage, expect some ink absorption variance and a slight cockling effect on large sections. For its intended purpose — black-and-white CAD drawings, engineering schematics, floor plans — it performs exactly as specified.

## Where it falls short

Honesty requires flagging two limitations. First, at 20lb, the paper is somewhat translucent. If you're printing double-sided or overlaying sheets on a light table, dense hatching will show through. Second, this is uncoated bond — freshly printed sheets will smudge if you handle them immediately. Give prints 30-60 seconds to dry before stacking, especially in humid environments.

There's also the packaging consideration: the roll comes without a plastic sleeve inside the box. If you store it exposed for extended periods, dust can accumulate on the surface. Keep it in the box when not in use and you'll be fine.
