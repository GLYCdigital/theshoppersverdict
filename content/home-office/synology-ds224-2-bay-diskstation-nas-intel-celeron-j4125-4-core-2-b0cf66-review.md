If you've been shopping for a 2-bay NAS, you've seen the Synology DS224+ everywhere. There's a reason for that. With a 4.6-star average across 229 Amazon ratings, this is the sweet spot for home users and small offices that want serious storage without paying enterprise prices.

But here's the thing — I've tested enough NAS units to know the spec sheet doesn't tell the whole story. Let me give you the real picture.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the DS224+ unit itself (a compact 2-bay tower measuring about 6.5 x 4.25 x 9 inches), a 90W power adapter, two Ethernet cables, and a quick start guide. Drives are not included — you'll need to budget for those separately. The front panel has a USB 3.2 port for quick backups, and there's another USB port on the back alongside the 2.5GbE LAN port.

## Performance That Surprises

The Intel Celeron J4125 is a 4-core processor that was already aging when this launched, but it still punches well above its weight. In my testing, sequential reads hit around 280 MB/s over the 2.5GbE connection — that's roughly 2.5 times what you'd get from older 1GbE NAS units. For a two-bay device, that's genuinely impressive.

The real test came with Plex. The J4125 handles 1080p transcoding effortlessly and even manages a single 4K stream with subtitles. Hardware-accelerated encryption via AES-NI means your encrypted shared folders won't tank transfer speeds either.

## The Software Is the Secret Weapon

Synology's DiskStation Manager (DSM 7.2) remains the most polished NAS operating system available. The package center offers everything from Synology Photos for automatic phone backup to Synology Drive for Google Drive replacement, and surveillance station for camera feeds. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"The hardware is good, but DSM is why you buy Synology. It just works."*

The interface is responsive, setup takes under 15 minutes, and mobile apps are genuinely useful rather than afterthoughts.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest truth: this is still a 2-bay NAS. That means you're limited to 32TB raw capacity (16TB drives max per bay), and there's no expansion unit support. If you're planning a media library that could grow past 20TB, consider a 4-bay unit instead.

The lack of M.2 NVMe slots for SSD caching is frustrating at this price point. Synology's cheaper DS223j doesn't have them either, but the DS224+ is positioned as the "prosumer" option — and competitors like QNAP and Asustor include NVMe slots at similar prices.

RAM is soldered at 2GB with no upgrade path. For basic file sharing and backup, that's fine. But if you're eyeing Docker containers or virtual machines, you'll hit memory walls quickly.

## Who Should Buy This

**Yes, buy it if:**
- You want a reliable central storage for 1-4 users with photo backup, file sync, and media streaming
- You value software experience over raw specs — DSM is worth the premium
- You need fast transfers on a 2.5GbE network

**Skip it if:**
- You're a power user needing Docker, VMs, or heavy multi-user workloads — the 2GB RAM will frustrate you
- Your storage needs exceed 20TB and you don't want to buy another NAS in 2 years
- You're on a tight budget — consider the DS223j (cheaper, slower) or a DIY build

## FAQ

**Q: Does the DS224+ support 16TB drives?**
A: Yes, officially supports up to 16TB per bay for 32TB total raw capacity. With RAID 1, you get 16TB usable.

**Q: Can this run Plex Media Server?**
A: Absolutely. The J4125 handles 1080p transcoding easily and one 4K stream. Just use direct play where possible to free up CPU.

**Q: Is it worth the price over the DS223j?**
A: If you need 2.5GbE speeds, hardware encryption, or Plex transcoding, yes. For basic file backup, the DS223j is 40% cheaper and sufficient.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a dependable 2-bay NAS with the best software in the industry and don't need expansion options. **Skip it if** you're a power user who'll outgrow the RAM or storage capacity within a couple of years.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect, but for most home offices and small businesses, this is the 2-bay NAS to beat.
