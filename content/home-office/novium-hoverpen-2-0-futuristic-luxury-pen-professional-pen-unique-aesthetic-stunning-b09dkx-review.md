Look, you don't buy the novium Hoverpen 2.0 because you need another pen. You buy it because you want something on your desk that makes people stop and ask, "Wait, is that thing floating?" And it does exactly that — when it works.

Here's the honest breakdown before you drop serious money on a writing instrument that's also a science project.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the pen, the magnetic base, a USB-C cable, and a spare refill. The pen itself is machined aluminum with a brushed finish — the product images don't exaggerate the build quality. It's 5.7 inches long and weighs about 1.4 ounces, which puts it in the same territory as a premium rollerball like a Lamy or a high-end Parker. The base is a solid disc that's heavier than it looks, which is good — you don't want the whole thing tipping over when you grab the pen.

Assembly is plug-and-play: connect the USB-C cable, place the pen in the holder, and it rises into the hover position. The floating gap is about 1cm, and the pen slowly rotates — a small detail, but it's what makes this feel alive rather than just "suspended."

**Performance: The Float and the Write**

The magnetic levitation is the star. It works reliably once you get the hang of it — the pen needs to be roughly centered before it locks into the hover. Off-center placement means it wobbles or drops. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's not as seamless as the marketing suggests. One Amazon reviewer put it well: "It takes a little practice to get it to float perfectly, but once you do, it's mesmerizing. I've had it for three months and I still catch myself staring at it during calls."

When it comes to actual writing, the Hoverpen 2.0 doesn't embarrass itself. The rollerball refill writes smoothly with consistent ink flow, and the pen's weight gives it a confident feel in hand. The cap is magnetic and clicks on securely. It's not going to replace a dedicated writing instrument for long sessions, but for signing documents or jotting notes, it's genuinely usable. That's more than you can say for most gimmicky desk toys.

**Where It Falls Short — Be Real With Yourself**

The biggest catch: the base needs constant power. Unplug it, and the pen drops. That's obvious, but the implication is that this is a permanent desk fixture, not something you move around. If your workspace is dynamic, this becomes an anchor rather than an accessory.

Also, the price. At $100+, you're paying a hefty premium for the levitation mechanism. If you're looking for a pure writing instrument, you can get a far better writer for half the price. This is a statement piece that also writes — not a writer that also floats.

Some buyers have noted the pen's finish is a fingerprint magnet, and the aluminum is prone to micro-scratches if you're rough with it. Nothing major, but it's not a "throw it in your bag" kind of pen.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The executive with a corner office:** This is a status symbol that actually does something. It signals "I appreciate design" without being tacky.
- **The desk-shelf curator:** If your workspace is a carefully styled environment, this fits perfectly alongside a mechanical keyboard and a monitor riser.
- **The gift-giver:** This is one of those rare gifts that elicits a genuine "whoa" reaction. The unboxing experience is solid, and the floating effect never gets old.

**Skip It If:**

- You're buying for the writing experience alone. Buy a Pilot Metropolitan or a Lamy Safari instead.
- You have a cluttered desk. The pen needs a clear, stable space to float properly.
- You're on a budget. This is a luxury item, not a tool.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the pen need batteries?**
A: No — the pen itself is passive. Only the base needs power via the included USB-C cable. The pen has no electronics.

**Q: Is the refill standard?**
A: It uses a standard rollerball refill, and the box includes a spare. Replacement refills are easy to find on Amazon.

**Q: Can you write with it while it's floating?**
A: No — you have to lift it off the base to write. The floating is purely for display when the pen is docked.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a desk centerpiece that's genuinely functional and you're okay paying for the spectacle. This is a 4.6/5 product because it delivers exactly what it promises — a futuristic, high-quality pen that floats — and does so with solid build quality.

**Skip it if:** You need a workhorse pen or you're sensitive to paying luxury prices for non-essential features.

The novium Hoverpen 2.0 is a rare case where the gimmick is actually well-executed. It's not for everyone, but for the right person, it's worth every penny.
