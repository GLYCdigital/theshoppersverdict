The Lenovo V15 Gen 4 sits in a crowded field of sub-$500 business laptops, a segment where manufacturers usually cut corners you discover three months in. After digging through 220 ratings and examining the spec sheet closely, here's the real story: this is a machine that gets the fundamentals right and skips the frills — which might be exactly what you need.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the laptop itself, a 65W barrel charger, and the standard paperwork. No USB-C charging, no sleeve, no surprises. The 15.6-inch FHD display is the headline feature at this price point — 1920x1080 resolution with decent brightness for indoor use. But here's the critical catch: Lenovo ships this model with both IPS and TN panel variants depending on configuration. The product listing photos show the IPS version, but you should verify the exact SKU before ordering. TN panels on these budget machines are genuinely hard to look at for eight-hour workdays.

**Performance and real-world use**

The Intel Core i5-1334U (on the config most buyers report receiving) handles everyday productivity without drama. Ten browser tabs, a spreadsheet with heavy formulas, and a video call running simultaneously? It manages. It won't edit 4K video or compile code efficiently, but that's not what this machine is for. The 8GB of RAM included is the weak point — Windows 11 eats about half of that at idle. Plan on opening the bottom panel and adding another 8GB stick immediately; the dual SODIMM slots make this a five-minute job.

Battery life lands around 6-7 hours of mixed office work, which is average for this class. The 45Wh battery isn't exceptional, but it'll get you through a day of classes or client meetings if you're not pushing the processor.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The keyboard gets genuine love across reviews. One buyer put it plainly: "The number pad is why I bought this over a smaller laptop, and it hasn't disappointed. Typing feels crisp with good travel." The port selection also earns repeated mentions — you get two USB-A ports, one USB-C, HDMI, and an Ethernet jack. That's a complete set for office use without dongles.

**Where it falls short**

The build quality is the obvious compromise. The plastic lid flexes noticeably when you grab it from a corner, and the screen wobbles if you type aggressively on an unstable surface. Several reviewers noted the trackpad has a hollow feel when clicking. The 250-nit brightness (on the IPS panels) is workable indoors but struggles near windows or under harsh office lighting. And the speakers? They're serviceable for calls but tinny for music — use headphones.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students on a budget**: The number pad and battery life make this a solid lecture-hall companion.
- **Office workers doing document-heavy work**: Email, Word, Excel, web apps — this handles it all without complaint.
- **Small business owners**: The Ethernet port and upgradeable RAM mean this can serve as a dependable daily driver.

**Skip it if**: You need a laptop for photo editing, you present frequently (the screen wobble is noticeable), or you want something you'll be proud to pull out in a client meeting.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I upgrade the RAM and storage?**
A: Yes. There are two DDR4 SODIMM slots (up to 32GB total) and a standard M.2 2242 slot. The bottom panel comes off with a Phillips screwdriver — no prying tools needed.

**Q: Does this support USB-C charging?**
A: No. The USB-C port is data-only. You'll need the included barrel charger or a compatible Lenovo adapter.

**Q: Is the display good enough for photo editing?**
A: No. Color accuracy is mediocre (around 45% sRGB coverage on the IPS panels). This is a productivity display, not a creative one.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, upgradeable workhorse for everyday tasks and you're comfortable adding RAM yourself. The V15 Gen 4 delivers solid performance, a genuinely useful keyboard, and full port coverage at a price that won't make you cringe.

**Skip it if** you want premium build quality, accurate colors, or something that'll turn heads. This is a tool, not a statement.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's not exciting, but it's honest. For the money, that's worth more than flashy specs that fail after six months.
