Let's be direct about what this is: a wireless keyboard and mouse combo built specifically for iPad users who type more than they tap. The 4.4-star average across 220 ratings tells a consistent story — people who need a physical typing setup for their iPad generally like this one, with a few caveats worth knowing before you click buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the keyboard, the mouse, a USB-C charging cable for the keyboard, and a separate USB-C cable for the mouse. Both devices charge via USB-C, which is good — you're not hunting for micro-USB cables in 2026. The keyboard pairs through Bluetooth 5.1, and the mouse uses a 2.4GHz USB receiver that's stored in the mouse's battery compartment. The keyboard connects to two devices simultaneously; the mouse connects to one. That's a limitation worth noting if you juggle multiple machines.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The keyboard uses scissor-switch keys with about 1.5mm of travel. That's noticeably better than the cheap membrane keyboards that dominate this price tier. Typing feels crisp, with a satisfying tactile bump that doesn't bottom out harshly. The keycaps are labeled with both standard characters and iPadOS-specific shortcuts — brightness controls, volume, home screen, and a dedicated search key that pulls up Spotlight. These work natively with iPadOS, no configuration required.

As shown in the product image, the layout is a full 78-key arrangement with a number pad. That's a double-edged sword. If you're doing spreadsheet work or data entry, the numpad is a genuine productivity win. But it makes this a desktop keyboard — this isn't something you'll want to balance on your lap on the couch.

The mouse is where things get more complicated. It's a standard optical mouse, not a trackpad, which means you're getting click-and-scroll functionality but losing iPadOS gesture controls. Swiping between apps, accessing the dock, and the four-finger multitasking gestures simply aren't available. Several buyers noted this exact frustration. One reviewer put it plainly: "The keyboard is great, but I still find myself reaching for the screen constantly because the mouse can't do the gestures."

Battery life is genuinely good. The keyboard claims 90 days on a charge, and the mouse claims 120 days. Real-world reports from buyers suggest these numbers are roughly accurate with moderate daily use — several reviewers mentioned going over two months without charging. The keyboard auto-sleeps after 10 minutes of inactivity, which helps.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Bluetooth pairing experience is the most common positive thread in the reviews. It connects almost instantly to iPads, and the dual-device switching on the keyboard works without the finicky re-pairing dance that plagues cheaper combos. One buyer wrote: "Switching between my iPad and MacBook is seamless. Press Fn+1 or Fn+2 and it just works."

Typing comfort is the second pillar of praise. Multiple reviewers note the keys are quiet — noticeably quieter than laptop keyboards — which makes this viable for shared workspaces or open offices. The low-profile design reduces wrist strain compared to typing directly on the iPad's glass screen.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of backlighting is the most common complaint in the ratings. If you work in dim lighting or before sunrise, you'll be hunting for keys. This is a genuine oversight for a keyboard in this category.

The mouse's build quality is the second issue. It's lightweight plastic that creaks slightly under firm grip at the seam where the two halves meet. It doesn't feel fragile, but it doesn't feel premium either. If you're coming from a high-end Logitech mouse, you'll notice the difference immediately.

## Who Should Buy This

This combo makes sense for three specific buyer types. First: students who take notes on an iPad and need a reliable typing setup for their dorm or library desk. Second: office workers who use an iPad as their primary device and need a comfortable full-size keyboard for long writing sessions. Third: anyone who wants a budget-friendly alternative to Apple's Magic Keyboard, which costs several times more and doesn't include a mouse.

Skip it if you need a trackpad for gestures, if you primarily use your iPad on the couch without a desk, or if you're a night owl who types in the dark.

## FAQ

**Will this work with a regular iPad, not just iPad Pro?**
Yes. It connects via standard Bluetooth and works with any iPad running iPadOS 13.4 or later. That covers iPad, iPad Air, iPad Mini, and iPad Pro models.

**Can I use the mouse with a Mac or Windows PC?**
The mouse uses a 2.4GHz USB receiver, so it works with any computer with a USB-A port. The keyboard connects via Bluetooth to Macs and PCs as well, though the iPad-specific shortcut keys won't function outside iPadOS.

**Does the keyboard have a built-in stand for the iPad?**
No. This is just the keyboard and mouse. You'll need a separate stand or case to prop up your iPad. Some buyers found this surprising, so it's worth knowing upfront.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You type extensively on your iPad and want a comfortable, full-size keyboard with a usable mouse at a reasonable price. The typing experience genuinely holds up, and the dedicated iPadOS shortcuts are a real productivity boost.

**Skip it if:** You need gesture controls, work in dark environments, or expect a premium-feeling mouse. This is a functional workhorse, not a luxury accessory.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, practical typing solution for iPad users who value function over flash. The keyboard carries the product; the mouse is adequate but unremarkable. At this price point, it delivers real value for desk-based iPad work.
