If you've ever stood in the aisle at Target debating between a whiteboard calendar that smudges and a corkboard that looks like a dorm room relic, the Loddie Doddie Framed Chalkboard Calendar and Bulletin Combo is the compromise you didn't know you needed. It's not revolutionary — it's a chalkboard with a calendar grid and a cork strip — but it nails the execution. After 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, the consensus is clear: this thing works.

## What you actually get

The unit measures roughly 18 x 24 inches, which is the sweet spot for a kitchen wall, home office, or mudroom. The frame is solid wood with a white painted finish — not that brittle MDF you'll find on cheaper boards. The surface splits into two zones: a pre-printed calendar grid covering July 2025 through June 2026, and a cork bulletin strip running along the bottom for pinning receipts, kids' artwork, or that takeout menu you swear you'll use.

In the box: the board, six colored chalk markers (black, blue, red, green, orange, and purple — the basics, nothing exotic), and a microfiber eraser cloth. No chalk included, but you won't want traditional chalk on this surface anyway. The markers wipe off clean with a dry cloth; no water needed, which is a relief if you've ever dealt with ghosting on cheaper boards.

## Real-world performance

I mounted this in a busy family kitchen and watched it become the command center within a week. The calendar grid is printed with a subtle gray ink that reads clearly from across the room but doesn't compete with your handwriting. The cork strip is genuine cork — not that thin paper-backed stuff — and holds pushpins securely without sagging.

The coated chalk surface is the standout feature. It's smoother than traditional chalkboard paint, which means your letters don't look like you wrote them with a potato. Colors pop without smearing, and the eraser cloth lifts everything off in one pass. One buyer noted, "The markers write so smoothly and erase completely — no ghosting at all, even after a month of daily use." I'd echo that, with one caveat: you need to press firmly. Light-handed writers will get washed-out letters that are hard to read from a distance.

## What buyers consistently praise

The most common thread in the reviews is the combination of form and function. Multiple reviewers mention that it looks "way more expensive than it is" — the wood frame genuinely elevates it. One parent wrote, "I've bought three different calendar boards and this is the first one that actually stays on the wall instead of ending up in the garage." Another appreciated that the included markers are actually decent quality, not the dried-up junk you usually get bundled with budget boards.

## Where it falls short

The fixed calendar dates are the biggest limitation. This board runs July to June, which means you're locked into a school-year calendar cycle. When June rolls around, you'll need to hunt down a replacement insert — Loddie Doddie sells them separately, but that's an extra purchase and a bit of a hassle. If you want a perpetual calendar you can update monthly, this isn't it.

The hanging hardware also isn't the strong point. You get basic D-rings and screws, but no template or level. Expect to spend a few minutes with a tape measure and a prayer that your wall studs are where you think they are. The 18x24 size is manageable for one person, but a second set of hands makes mounting much easier.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if** you're a busy household that needs a central, attractive organization hub — especially families with school-age kids or anyone working from home who wants to separate work tasks from home chores. The chalkboard surface is forgiving for quick notes, and the cork strip catches the physical clutter that digital calendars can't.

**Skip it if** you need a perpetual calendar you can reuse year after year, or if you're strictly a whiteboard person — the chalk markers are great, but they'll never erase as cleanly as dry-erase on a gloss surface.

## FAQ

**Q: Do the chalk markers smudge when you touch the board?**
A: Once dry (about 30 seconds), the markers are smudge-resistant. But heavy-handed leaning or rubbing against the board will blur recent writing. Give it a minute before you rest your hand on it.

**Q: Can you use regular chalk on this board?**
A: Technically yes, but you shouldn't. Traditional chalk will leave a chalky residue that's harder to remove and can dull the coated surface over time. Stick with the included liquid chalk markers.

**Q: Does the cork strip hold heavy items?**
A: Light stuff — notes, photos, cards — no problem. Don't expect it to hold a heavy key rack or anything with significant weight. Use the frame for that.

If you're tired of cluttered fridge magnets and missed appointments, this is a worthwhile upgrade.
