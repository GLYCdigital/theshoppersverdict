# Lenovo ThinkCentre M75q Gen 5 Tiny Desktop with AMD Ryzen 7

Most people think a powerful desktop has to take up half your desk. The Lenovo ThinkCentre M75q Gen 5 flips that assumption on its head — this is a full-fledged Ryzen 7 workstation that fits in the palm of your hand. Literally. At just 7 inches square and barely over 1 liter in volume, it's smaller than a hardcover novel. But don't let the size fool you.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the M75q unit itself, a Lenovo proprietary 135W power brick, a VESA mount bracket with screws, and a vertical stand. No keyboard, mouse, or monitor — this is a bare-bones business machine, and that's fine. The VESA mount is the real gift here: attach it to the back of any monitor and the PC essentially disappears.

The unit I'm evaluating ships with an AMD Ryzen 7 PRO 8700GE processor, 32GB DDR5 RAM, and a 512GB NVMe SSD. The Ryzen 7 PRO chip is the business-tier variant with 8 cores and 16 threads, and it boosts up to 4.9GHz. This isn't a low-power compromise — it's a genuinely fast chip that trades a little clock speed for efficiency.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this through a typical office workload: 30+ browser tabs, Slack, Excel with multi-megabyte spreadsheets, video calls, and light photo editing. It didn't break a sweat. The Ryzen 7 has enough headroom that you'll never feel a stutter in normal productivity work. Heavy multitaskers will appreciate the 32GB of RAM — if you're looking at a base config with 8GB or 16GB, budget for an upgrade.

The integrated Radeon 780M graphics are surprisingly capable. It'll handle 4K video playback flawlessly and even light photo editing in Photoshop. I'd skip this machine for serious gaming or 4K video rendering — there's no discrete GPU option, and that's the one hard limitation.

One thing that impressed me: thermals. The fan is nearly silent during normal use, and even under sustained load it stays whisper-quiet. Lenovo's thermal design here is genuinely excellent.

## Ports and Connectivity

Front: two USB-A 3.2 Gen 2 ports, one USB-C 3.2 Gen 2, and a headphone jack. Rear: two USB-A 3.2 Gen 2, two USB-A 2.0, two HDMI ports, one DisplayPort, one USB-C with DisplayPort Alt Mode, and a 2.5GbE Ethernet port. That's enough for three 4K monitors simultaneously — a rarity at this price point.

Wireless is solid too: Wi-Fi 6E and Bluetooth 5.3. The 2.5GbE port is a nice business-class touch that most consumer desktops skip.

## What Buyers Are Saying

With a 5.0 average across 250 ratings, owners are genuinely happy. One reviewer noted: "I've had it for two months running dual monitors and it's completely silent. My old tower sounded like a jet engine." Another wrote: "Blazing fast for office work. I didn't expect this level of performance from something this small." The consistent themes: quiet operation, compact size, and speed.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. First, RAM is soldered on the base configuration — you cannot upgrade it later. Order the config you need upfront, or you're stuck. Second, the proprietary power brick is annoying; if you lose it, replacements cost $40+. Finally, no discrete GPU means this isn't a gaming machine, period.

For raw value, you're paying a premium for the tiny form factor. A similarly-specced mini tower from Lenovo would cost less — you're paying for the engineering that shrinks it down.

## Who Should Buy This

This is perfect for: office workers with cramped desks, IT managers deploying fleets of VESA-mounted units, anyone running a home office who wants a clean look, and business users who need dual or triple 4K monitors. Skip it if you're a gamer, video editor, or need upgradeability down the road.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it handle three monitors?**
A: Yes — two HDMI plus one USB-C with DisplayPort Alt Mode. All three support 4K at 60Hz.

**Q: Is the RAM upgradeable?**
A: On the base config, no — it's soldered. Order your target RAM upfront. Some configs have one SODIMM slot, but don't count on it.

**Q: How does it compare to a Mac Mini?**
A: The M75q is roughly comparable in CPU performance to the base M4 Mac Mini, but it supports more external displays and runs Windows natively.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need serious office performance in a footprint smaller than a laptop, want silent operation, or need multi-monitor support without a tower.

**Skip it if:** Gaming, video rendering, or future upgradeability are priorities.

This is one of the best tiny desktops available today. It's not cheap, but it earns the premium with exceptional engineering, genuine performance, and a form factor that transforms how your workspace looks. At 4.6/5, this is a strong recommendation for anyone who values desk space as much as computing power.
