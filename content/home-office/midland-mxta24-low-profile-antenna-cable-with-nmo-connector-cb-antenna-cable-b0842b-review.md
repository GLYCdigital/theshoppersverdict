**The Short Version: A Clean, Capable Cable for Mobile Radio Installations — With One Caveat**

If you're mounting a CB or GMRS radio in a truck, Jeep, or SUV, the cable is where most installs go wrong. Cheap coax kinks, connectors corrode, and door-jamb routing becomes a nightmare. The Midland MXTA24 solves most of that with a thoughtfully designed low-profile cable that's earned its 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews.

Here's the real breakdown.

---

**What You Actually Get**

The MXTA24 is a 13-foot RG58 coaxial cable with a pre-attached NMO connector on the antenna end and a PL-259 (UHF) connector on the radio end. The headline feature is the low-profile right-angle NMO base—instead of a tall, straight connector that fights against door seals and tailgate gaps, this one sits flat and routes through tight spaces cleanly.

As shown in the product image, the cable is flexible enough to bend around interior trim without kinking. The connectors are machined, not molded, which matters for longevity. You're getting a complete cable assembly—no separate adapter needed for standard NMO-mount antennas like the Midland MXTA26 or MXTA27.

---

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The cable itself is solid. RG58 is the workhorse of mobile radio coax—it's flexible, easy to route, and perfectly adequate for runs under 20 feet. The right-angle NMO base is the differentiator here. It eliminates the stress point where straight connectors typically crack or bend over time, especially in installations where the cable exits through a door jamb or tailgate seal.

One buyer noted: *"Installed in my F-150 with the antenna mounted on the hood—the low profile connector tucks under the fender seam perfectly. No water intrusion after six months."* That matches the general sentiment across reviews: this cable is built for the real-world abuse of a vehicle install.

The weather sealing on the connectors is decent for a cable in this price range. It won't survive being submerged, but it handles rain, car washes, and temperature swings without issue.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs.

**RG58 coax loses more signal than premium alternatives.** On a 13-foot run, you're looking at roughly 0.5–0.8 dB of loss at CB frequencies. That's negligible for most users. But if you're running high-power (50W+) or marginal antenna placements, you'll notice the difference compared to LMR-400 or RG213.

**The NMO connector is fixed.** If your antenna uses a different mount type—say, a 3/8" threaded base or a magnetic mount—you'll need an adapter. That's an extra purchase and an extra point of failure.

**It's 13 feet, not longer.** For full-size trucks with antennas on the roof or bed, 13 feet might come up short. Measure your routing path before buying. If you need more reach, you're looking at a different cable entirely.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work with a magnetic mount antenna?**
A: No. The MXTA24 is designed for NMO (National MOtorola) mounts only. For magnetic mounts, you need a cable with a different connector—check your antenna's base before ordering.

**Q: Can I cut and re-terminate this cable if it's too long?**
A: Yes, but you'll need a PL-259 crimp tool and connector. The cable is standard RG58, so it's compatible with standard connectors. But if you're not comfortable soldering, get the right length the first time.

**Q: Does the low-profile base actually prevent water intrusion?**
A: In most cases, yes. The right-angle design sits flush against the mounting surface, which sheds water better than a straight connector. Several reviewers report no moisture issues after months of outdoor use. Just make sure the O-ring is seated properly during installation.
