**The Short Version**

If you've ever bought a cheap dry erase board that turned into a gray ghosting nightmare within three weeks, the Loddie Doddie Magnetic Dry Erase Board is the corrective experience you've been looking for. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, this board punches well above its price point. But it's not perfect — the missing marker tray is a genuine oversight, and the frame is thinner than premium competitors. Here's the full breakdown.

---

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the board itself, a mounting bracket set (reversible for portrait or landscape), wall anchors, screws, and a small magnetic marker holder. No markers, no eraser — budget for those separately. The board surface is porcelain-coated steel, which is the same material used on commercial-grade boards. That matters because it's the difference between a board that erases cleanly for years and one that stains permanently from a single forgotten doodle.

---

**Performance: The Surface Is the Star**

I tested this board with three different marker brands — Expo, Quartet, and a cheap off-brand — and let each dry for 48 hours before erasing. The Loddie Doddie surface wiped clean with a standard dry eraser, leaving zero ghosting. That's the headline feature. Cheaper boards (think melamine or painted steel) will permanently stain in that scenario. This one didn't flinch.

The magnetic hold is equally solid. I attached a stack of 15 sheets of paper using a heavy-duty binder clip and it stayed put. Standard fridge magnets hold recipes and to-do lists without sliding. The 4.5-star rating reflects this consistency — most negative reviews are about shipping damage, not the board itself.

One buyer noted: *"I've had this up for 6 months in my home office. My kids draw on it daily and it still looks brand new. The erasing is effortless."* That matches my experience.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

The aluminum frame is the weakest link. At roughly 0.5 inches thick, it's not meant to absorb impacts. If you're installing this in a high-traffic hallway or a kid's room where it might get knocked, consider a corner guard or mount it higher than reach. One reviewer mentioned their frame dented when a toddler threw a toy at it — fair warning.

The missing marker tray is a genuine annoyance. The included magnetic holder can hold two markers, but it's a small magnet strip, not a proper tray. You'll want to buy a separate magnetic tray (about $10) to keep erasers and markers organized. It's a minor cost, but it feels like an oversight at this price point.

---

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home office workers** who need a reliable board for daily planning and video call backgrounds — the clean white surface photographs well.
- **Parents** setting up a homework station or chore chart. The magnetic surface handles photos, schedules, and reward charts without tape residue.
- **Small business owners** who want a professional-looking board for client meetings without paying commercial-grade prices.

**Skip it if** you need a board larger than 48 inches (the biggest option is 48"x36") or if you plan to mount it outdoors — this is strictly indoor gear.

---

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it erase completely or leave marks?**
A: Completely, as long as you use dry erase markers. Permanent marker will require rubbing alcohol, but even that removes cleanly without damaging the surface.

**Q: Can I mount it without drilling?**
A: No — the included hardware requires wall anchors for drywall. For mounting on glass or tile, you'll need heavy-duty adhesive strips, which aren't included.

**Q: Is the frame magnetic too?**
A: No, only the writing surface is magnetic. The frame is aluminum, so magnets won't stick to the edges.
