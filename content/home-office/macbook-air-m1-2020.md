The 2020 MacBook Air with the M1 chip isn't just a good laptop — it's the gadget that proved Apple's ARM gamble was a slam dunk. Years later, it's still one of the best-value ultraportables money can buy.

### The Good

The M1 chip turned this from a politely warm performer into a genuinely fast machine. Browsing, writing, light coding, media consumption — it handles all of it without breaking a sweat. And because there's no fan, it does so in complete silence. That alone is worth the price of admission for anyone who works in quiet spaces.

Battery life is genuinely all-day. You can leave the charger at home, work through a full shift, and still have juice left for Netflix on the commute back. The Retina display (2560×1600) is sharp enough that you won't feel the need to plug into an external monitor for most tasks. Touch ID works instantly and makes logging in feel archaic by comparison.

### The Not-So-Good

The two-port limit is the most practical annoyance. With one occupied by charging, you're down to one Thunderbolt port for everything else. A dongle is basically mandatory.

The 8GB RAM in the base config is fine for casual use, but power users hitting heavy multitasking or virtualization will feel the wall. Speaking of virtualization — Windows via Parallels works, but performance takes a noticeable hit compared to Intel Macs running Boot Camp. If native Windows is a requirement, look elsewhere.

Finally, the 720p webcam was mediocre in 2020 and it hasn't improved with age. If video calls matter to you, budget for an external webcam.

### Bottom Line

The M1 MacBook Air is still the gold standard for budget-friendly Apple laptops. It's fast, silent, and the battery won't quit. The port situation and RAM ceiling are real limitations, but for most people — students, writers, casual creators — this is more than enough machine at a price that's hard to beat.

**⭐ 4.6/5** — An instant classic that everyone got right.
