**The $60 Question: Can a Budget Type Cover Match Microsoft's $160 Original?**

Most Surface Pro owners eventually face this dilemma: do you shell out for the official Microsoft Type Cover, or gamble on a third-party alternative that costs half as much? The **Zoof Type Cover** is one of the most visible contenders on Amazon, sitting at 3.9 stars from 195 ratings. After testing it across a week of real work — emails, spreadsheets, and a 3,000-word article — here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the Box**

The package is refreshingly simple: the keyboard cover itself and a slim quick-start guide. No charging cable needed, because the cover draws power directly from the Surface Pro's magnetic connector — no Bluetooth pairing, no batteries. It's compatible with Surface Pro 7+, Pro 7, Pro 6, Pro 5, and Pro 4, which covers most of the last five years of Microsoft's lineup. If you're on a Pro 8 or newer, this won't fit — the magnetic strip alignment is different.

**Build Quality and Typing Feel**

The hinge is the first thing you'll notice. It snaps onto the Surface's bottom edge with a satisfying click and holds tight — no wobble when typing on a desk. The key travel is about 1.3mm, which is shallower than the official cover's 1.5mm but noticeably deeper than typing on a tablet screen. Keys have a tactile bump that's easy to find without looking down.

The backlit keys are genuinely useful, with three brightness levels toggled via the F1 key. This is a feature the official cover charges a premium for, so it's a welcome surprise here. The layout is standard QWERTY with a full function row — something the official Surface Type Cover actually omits (it only has F1–F12 shortcuts, not dedicated Esc or function keys).

**Where It Falls Short**

The trackpad is the weakest link. It's about 4.2 inches diagonally, which is usable but cramped compared to the official cover's 4.5-inch pad. The click requires real pressure — you'll find yourself jabbing at it rather than tapping naturally. Worse, palm rejection is inconsistent. When typing, my palms occasionally triggered random cursor jumps mid-sentence. It's manageable if you keep your wrists elevated, but it's a constant annoyance.

The fabric surface is another issue. The dark gray material shows fingerprints and smudges after just a few days of use. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're someone who eats at your desk, this will look grimy quickly. There's also no pen loop and no USB passthrough — two features that come standard on the official cover.

**Real-World Performance**

Battery life is a non-issue since it draws from the Surface's own battery. The keys register input reliably, with no missed keystrokes or ghosting during my testing. The magnetic hinge holds the cover at a stable angle for typing on your lap — though it's not quite as rigid as the official cover, which uses a more robust friction hinge.

One thing buyer reviews consistently note is the price-to-value ratio. One verified buyer wrote, "For $60, this is a solid backup or travel keyboard. It's not the official cover, but it does 90% of the job at half the price." Another reviewer warned, "The trackpad click is mushy — be prepared to use a mouse for precise work."

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Budget-conscious students** who need a functional keyboard for note-taking and essays but can't justify $160 for the official cover.
- **Second-home users** who want a spare cover for a secondary Surface Pro or a travel setup.
- **Function-key fans** who actually use Esc, F1–F12, or dedicated media keys — this has them all, which the official cover doesn't.

**Who Should Skip It**

- **Professionals who do precision trackpad work** (graphic design, video editing, heavy spreadsheet navigation). The stiff click and spotty palm rejection will frustrate you.
- **Surface Pro 8/9/11 owners** — this physically won't fit. Check compatibility before ordering.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it work with Surface Pro 7+ and Pro 7?**  
Yes. It's designed for the 12.3-inch Pro models from Pro 4 through Pro 7+. It will not fit Pro 8 or newer, which use a different connector.

**Q: Does the backlight have adjustable brightness?**  
Three levels, toggled via the F1 key. It's not as granular as the official cover's auto-brightness, but it's more than adequate.

**Q: Can I use it as a standalone Bluetooth keyboard?**  
No. It connects exclusively via the Surface Pro's magnetic pogo pins. No Bluetooth, no USB — it's permanently tethered to the device.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a functional typing solution on a budget and can live with a mediocre trackpad. **Skip it if** precise cursor control is essential to your workflow — you'll end up buying the official cover anyway.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A solid backup keyboard with real compromises. It's not the official cover, but it's not trying to be. It's trying to save you $100, and for most casual users, that's a fair trade.
