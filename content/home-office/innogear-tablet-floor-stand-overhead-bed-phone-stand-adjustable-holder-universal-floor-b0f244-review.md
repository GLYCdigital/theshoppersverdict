Let’s be honest: most tablet stands are glorified lap trays. The InnoGear Tablet Floor Stand takes a different approach — it’s a floor-mounted gooseneck that arcs over your head, letting you watch movies, follow recipes, or video-call while lying flat. That’s a genuinely different use case, and it’s executed well enough to earn a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings. But it’s not without tradeoffs.

## What you actually get

The box contains the assembled floor base, a two-section gooseneck arm, a universal spring-loaded clamp, and the phone/tablet holder itself. Assembly takes under five minutes — the arm screws into the base, the clamp attaches to the arm’s end, and you’re done. There’s no charging cable, no remote, no mounting hardware for walls or desks. This is a pure floor-standing unit, and it’s designed to stay put.

The base measures roughly 12 inches in diameter and is weighted, which is critical for the overhead use case. The arm extends to about 30 inches of reach, with the gooseneck allowing 360-degree articulation. The clamp opens wide enough for tablets up to 12.9 inches (the iPad Pro size) and closes down to hold a phone in a thin case.

## Performance and real-world use

The headline feature is the overhead positioning. You can bend the arm so the tablet hangs directly above your face, roughly 18 to 24 inches away — perfect for hands-free viewing while lying in bed. The clamp holds devices firmly; one reviewer noted, *“I’ve got a 12.9-inch iPad Pro in a heavy case, and it hasn’t slipped once in three weeks of nightly use.”* Another said, *“The base is heavy enough that I don’t worry about it tipping, even when I bump it getting out of bed.”*

The gooseneck holds its position well during normal use. Adjusting it takes deliberate force, which is good — you don’t want it sagging mid-episode. But it’s worth noting the arm has limits. If you bend it sharply at the same joint repeatedly, it can weaken over time. One long-term reviewer mentioned, *“After about four months, the arm started drooping slightly at the first bend. I had to reposition it every couple of days.”* That’s the tradeoff for the flexibility.

The universal clamp has rubber padding, so it won’t scratch your devices. It’s also positioned to avoid covering the power or volume buttons on most phones — a detail cheaper stands miss.

## What buyers consistently praise

- **Stability:** The weighted base gets near-universal positive mentions. It doesn’t wobble or tip.
- **Versatility:** Works for phones and tablets, and the overhead position is a genuine selling point.
- **Ease of adjustment:** The gooseneck bends smoothly without needing tools.

## Where it falls short

The biggest complaint is the lack of a clamp option. The floor base requires about a square foot of clear space, which is fine in a bedroom but annoying in a cramped apartment or if you want to attach it to a couch arm. There’s also no integrated cable management — the charging cord dangles from the device down the arm. You can zip-tie it, but that’s a DIY fix, not a design feature.

The droop issue mentioned above is real for heavy tablets if you’re aggressive with adjustments. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it means the stand isn’t a “set it and forget it forever” product.

## Who should buy this

- **Bedroom streamers** who want to watch hands-free while lying flat — this is the primary use case and it nails it.
- **Home chefs** following recipes on a tablet above the counter (though you’ll need the base on the counter, which takes space).
- **Chronic phone users** who want to reduce neck strain from looking down at a screen for hours.

Skip it if you need a clamp-on mount, have no floor space, or want something you can travel with. This is a stationary piece of gear.

## FAQ

**Will it hold a 12.9-inch iPad Pro with a case?**
Yes. The clamp opens wide enough, and the weighted base keeps it stable. Just don’t expect to adjust it with one hand — it takes a firm grip.

**Can I use it while sitting on a couch?**
Technically yes, but the base needs to sit beside or behind you. The overhead position is easier to achieve from a bed or recliner than from a standard sofa.

**Does it work with a phone in a PopSocket?**
No. The clamp grips the flat edges of the device, so anything protruding from the back will prevent a secure hold. Remove the PopSocket first.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a stable, adjustable overhead stand for bed or floor use and don’t mind the footprint. **Skip it if** you need a clamp mount, have limited space, or plan to move it between rooms frequently.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, specialized tool that does exactly what it promises, with minor durability caveats that won’t bother most users.
