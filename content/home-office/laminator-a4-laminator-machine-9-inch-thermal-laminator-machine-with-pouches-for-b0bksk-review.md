You get the laminator itself, a starter pack of thermal pouches (enough to test a few sheets), a cleaning card, and the power cord. ## Performance and real-world use

The machine heats up in about a minute. Not instant, but fast enough that you're not standing around. It runs at a single speed — roughly 300mm per minute — which is fine for home offices or classrooms. You slide the pouch in, wait for it to emerge from the other side, and it's done. No manual roller cranking, no pressure adjustments.

What surprised me: the jam resistance. Several buyers specifically called this out. One reviewer wrote, "I've had it for six months and run hundreds of sheets through — not one jam." Another noted, "It handles thicker pouches (5 mil) without curling the edges." That's the kind of reliability you usually don't get at this price point.

The rollers are heated on both sides, which means even lamination — no bubbles or wrinkles if you feed the pouch straight. The carrier (the paper sleeve that holds the pouch) is included in the starter pack, so you don't need to buy extras immediately.

## Where it falls short

The biggest tradeoff is the 9-inch width. If you regularly laminate letter-size (8.5 x 11) documents, you're fine — that fits. But if you need legal size or anything wider, this isn't your machine. You'd need a 13-inch unit, which costs more.

The single-speed operation is also a limitation. If you're laminating thin paper versus thick cardstock, you can't adjust the speed to reduce curling. It's a one-size-fits-all approach. Most home users won't care, but it's worth noting.

And there's no auto-shutoff. The machine stays hot until you turn it off. If you're the type to walk away mid-project, you'll want to make a habit of hitting the power switch. It's a minor annoyance, not a fatal flaw.
