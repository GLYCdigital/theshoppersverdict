**Opening Hook**

Most budget keyboard cases for the 10.2” iPad are a compromise — you get keys, but they feel like typing on a cereal box, and the trackpad is an afterthought. The AHYWTAN case for iPad 9/8/7th Gen doesn’t follow that script. It’s a full-featured contender that undercuts Apple’s Magic Keyboard by hundreds of dollars while delivering a genuinely usable typing experience. But “usable” doesn’t mean “flawless.” Here’s the real story after digging through specs, user feedback, and the 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes the keyboard case, a USB-C charging cable, and a basic user manual. No wall adapter — you’ll use your iPad charger. The case itself is a two-piece design: a hard back shell that snaps onto the iPad and a magnetic folio cover that folds into a typing stand. The keyboard is a full QWERTY layout with a top row of iPadOS shortcut keys (home, brightness, volume, play/pause, screenshot). The trackpad sits below the spacebar, and the whole unit charges via USB-C — roughly 2 hours for a full charge that lasts about 30 days at 2 hours of daily use, per the manufacturer.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The keys have about 1.2mm of travel — not laptop-level, but significantly better than the membrane-feel budget cases. They’re scissor-switch style with a satisfying tactile bump. The backlight is the standout feature: seven color modes (white, blue, green, red, purple, cyan, and a rainbow cycle) plus three brightness levels. It’s not per-key RGB, but for a sub-$60 case, it’s impressive.

The trackpad is where this gets interesting. It connects via Bluetooth, and the unit supports multi-touch gestures — two-finger scroll, three-finger swipe up for app switcher, and tap-to-click. One reviewer noted, *“I was skeptical about a trackpad on a budget case, but it works well with iPadOS — no lag, and I didn’t have to fiddle with accessibility settings.”* That tracks with my analysis: the case uses a standard HID profile, so it pairs cleanly without the janky third-party drivers some competitors require.

Build quality is solid for the price. The back shell is a rigid polycarbonate with a soft-touch finish that resists fingerprints. The hinge is a friction-based mechanism — it holds angles from 15 to 150 degrees, but it’s not as stiff as the Logitech Combo Touch. If you’re typing on a lap, the case will wobble slightly. On a desk, it’s stable.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Beyond the trackpad, the recurring theme in positive reviews is value. One buyer wrote, *“I was about to spend $300 on Apple’s keyboard. This does 90% of the job for a fraction of the price.”* Another praised the battery: *“I’ve used it daily for two weeks and haven’t charged it once.”* The Pencil holder also gets consistent love — a magnetic strip on the right edge holds the Apple Pencil 1st gen (which charges via the Lightning port on the iPad) without adding bulk.

**Where It Falls Short**

The weight is the biggest compromise. At 1.2 pounds, this case makes your iPad heavier than a MacBook Air. One-handed reading in bed becomes a forearm workout. Also, the trackpad is small — about 3.5” x 2” — and palm rejection is imperfect. If you rest your palms on the deck while typing, you’ll occasionally trigger accidental cursor jumps.

There’s no auto-wake/sleep function. You’ll need to press the power button or rely on the magnetic flap’s cover detection — which only works if you close it fully. And the keyboard doesn’t detach from the case, so you can’t use the iPad as a bare tablet without unclipping the whole thing (which takes about 10 seconds, but still).

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Students on a budget** who need a laptop-like setup for note-taking and essay writing. The backlight is a lifesaver in lecture halls.
- **Travelers** who want one case for typing, media, and light productivity without carrying a separate keyboard.
- **Home users** who dock their iPad on a desk and want a stable typing platform.

**Skip it if** you’re a heavy lap-typer (the hinge wobble will annoy you), or if you prioritize portability over functionality — this case is chunky.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the trackpad work with all iPadOS versions?**
A: Yes, it works with iPadOS 14 and later. Gestures like three-finger swipes require iPadOS 15 or newer. No app or driver installation needed.

**Q: Can I use the keyboard while the case is charging?**
A: Yes, but it’s wired-only during charging — Bluetooth disconnects while plugged in. You can still type via the USB-C connection.

**Q: Will this fit an iPad with a screen protector?**
A: Yes, but thicker glass protectors (0.3mm+) can cause the case to fit snugly. The back shell has a bit of give, but tempered glass with rounded edges is safer than flat-edge protectors.

**The Verdict**

The AHYWTAN keyboard case delivers 80% of the Magic Keyboard experience at 20% of the price. It’s not perfect — the weight and trackpad size are real compromises — but for the price, it’s the best Bluetooth keyboard case option for the 10.2” iPad.

**Buy it if** you want a backlit keyboard, a functional trackpad, and solid build quality without spending $300+.

**Skip it if** you need a detachable keyboard, lap-friendly stability, or a featherweight case.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-executed budget option with honest limitations.
