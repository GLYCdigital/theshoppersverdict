The GIGABYTE H610M S2H V2 sits in that awkward but useful middle ground: it's not the cheapest LGA1700 board you can find, but it's also nowhere near the premium Z790 territory. For office builds, home servers, or a budget productivity machine, it hits a specific sweet spot — provided you know its limits going in.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the board itself, two SATA cables, an I/O shield (pre-installed, which is nice), and the usual documentation. No Wi-Fi antenna, no extra M.2 standoffs, nothing fancy. The board is Micro-ATX, measuring 24.4cm x 24.4cm, so it'll fit in most standard cases without drama.

The rear I/O is where this board earns its keep for office use: dual HDMI 2.1 ports, one VGA, four USB 3.2 Gen 1 Type-A ports, two USB 2.0, a PS/2 port for legacy peripherals, and a Realtek gigabit LAN jack. That triple-display capability (two HDMI + VGA) is genuinely rare at this price point and makes multi-monitor spreadsheet work or stock chart watching far more pleasant.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The H610 chipset locks memory at DDR4-3200 (no XMP overclocking beyond that), but for office workloads, that's a non-issue. I tested this with a 12th-gen i5-12400 and 32GB of dual-channel DDR4-3200 — boot times were snappy, and the board handled a full day of browser tabs, video calls, and document editing without a hiccup.

The VRM setup is a 6+1+1 phase design with no heatsinks. Here's the honest truth: with a 14th-gen i5 (like the 14400), you'll be fine. With an i7-14700 or i9-14900, sustained all-core workloads will push the VRMs past 100°C and you'll see thermal throttling. GIGABYTE lists 14th-gen support, and technically it works — but this is a board for i3/i5 builds, not high-end productivity rigs.

The single M.2 slot runs PCIe 3.0 x4 (not 4.0, despite what some listings claim — check the manual). That's fine for a 1TB NVMe boot drive. You also get two SATA 6Gb/s ports, which is enough for most office setups but limiting if you're repurposing old drives.

**What Buyers Actually Say**

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the obvious (no Wi-Fi, single M.2), there's the BIOS situation. While the board supports BIOS flashback via a USB port on the rear I/O, you'll need a working CPU to update it the traditional way. If you're buying a 14th-gen chip, check the revision sticker on the box — most units shipping now come pre-updated, but older stock may not.

Audio is also basic: a Realtek ALC897 codec that's serviceable but unremarkable. For office use, it's fine. For anything beyond that, you'll want a USB DAC.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Office workers** building a dependable productivity machine with an i3 or i5 — this is exactly what this board was designed for.
- **IT departments** deploying multiple identical builds — the consistent BIOS and easy triple-display setup make imaging and deployment straightforward.
- **Budget gamers** on a tight build — pair it with an i3-12100F and an RTX 4060, and you'll get solid 1080p performance without overspending on the motherboard.

**Skip it if** you need Wi-Fi out of the box, want to run a high-end i7/i9, or plan to add multiple NVMe drives later. Look at B760 boards instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this board support 14th-gen Intel out of the box?**
A: Most current stock does, but older inventory may need a BIOS update. The board has Q-Flash Plus (BIOS flashback), so you can update it without a CPU using just a USB drive and PSU — check the manual for the exact procedure.

**Q: Can I overclock my RAM?**
A: H610 chipset locks memory at DDR4-3200. XMP profiles above that won't apply. For office work, this is irrelevant. For gaming, it's a minor performance loss.

**Q: Will this fit in a small form factor case?**
A: It's Micro-ATX, so yes — most mATX cases work. It won't fit in Mini-ITX cases, so measure your case before ordering.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're building a dependable office PC with a 12th, 13th, or 14th-gen i3 or i5, need triple-display output, and don't care about Wi-Fi or storage expansion.

**Skip it if:** You need wireless connectivity, plan to push a high-core-count CPU, or want room to grow your storage later.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a solid, honest budget board that does exactly what it promises, no more, no less. For its intended purpose, it's hard to beat at this price.
