If you've ever wrestled with a cheap flip calendar that slides down your whiteboard every time you flip a card, you already know why this set from the Eucalyptus line is worth a look. The 4.2-star average across 210 ratings tells a consistent story: this is a solid, attractive daily calendar system with one or two real limitations. Let's get into the details.

**What you actually get**

The box contains a metal holder with two hanging loops, a set of monthly headers, numbered date cards (1–31), day-of-the-week cards, year cards through 2030, and a selection of special occasion cards (holidays, birthdays, field trips). All cards are 3x3 inches, which means you can swap in any standard magnetic or pocket-chart cards if you want to customize. The eucalyptus print is a soft sage green with thin stems and leaves — subtle enough for a classroom, calm enough for a home office.

**Performance and build quality**

The holder is the star here. It's made of coated steel with a clean finish, and the magnets are genuinely strong. Multiple reviewers noted it "stays put on the whiteboard even with daily use," which is the single most common failure point in this product category. The cards themselves are printed on decent weight cardstock with clear, readable text. The flip mechanism is a simple two-ring binder design — nothing fancy, but it works reliably. You flip the date card over each morning; the ring holds the stack firmly without binding.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The aesthetic is the standout feature. One teacher wrote, "I get compliments on this calendar every single day — it looks like something from a boutique, not a supply catalog." Another reviewer mentioned the versatility: "I use it for my preschool circle time and also have one in my home office for tracking deadlines. Works for both." The fact that the cards are interchangeable across the set also gets frequent mention — you can mix and match the special event cards without buying a separate set.

**Where it falls short**

The cards are the weak point. At cardstock thickness with no lamination, they're vulnerable to bending at the corners, especially if kids are the ones flipping them. A few reviewers reported needing to replace cards within a few months of heavy use. The holder also lacks any mounting hardware for wall installation — it's designed for magnetic surfaces only. If you want it on a bulletin board or wall, you'll need to supply your own adhesive strips or hooks.

**Who should buy this**

- **Classroom teachers** (Pre-K through elementary) who want a calendar that looks polished and survives daily use — just consider laminating the cards yourself.
- **Homeschool parents** who need an organized, visually calm calendar for a dedicated school space.
- **Home office workers** who want a physical, low-tech date tracker that doesn't scream "cubicle supply."

Skip it if you need a wall-mounted calendar with included hardware, or if your students are rough enough to destroy un-laminated cardstock within a semester.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this on a regular wall, not a magnetic surface?**
A: Not directly. The holder uses magnets, so it needs a steel surface. You can add your own adhesive magnets or hook-and-loop tape to the back — several reviewers did exactly that.

**Q: Does it include cards for all 12 months?**
A: Yes — 12 monthly headers, 31 date cards, 7 day cards, and year cards through 2030. Plus a sheet of special event cards you punch out yourself.

**Q: Is the eucalyptus design too "feminine" for a general office?**
A: It's a muted sage green with light line art — reads as botanical and neutral, not flowery. Most users describe it as "calming" and "modern."

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-designed, attractive flip calendar that nails the basics: strong magnets, clean print, and a holder that won't slide off your board. The un-laminated cards are the only real compromise, and that's fixable with a $5 laminator.

**Buy it if** you want a durable, good-looking calendar for a magnetic surface and don't mind a little DIY card protection.
**Skip it if** you need wall-mount hardware included, or you're buying for a very young classroom where cards will get grabbed and twisted daily.
