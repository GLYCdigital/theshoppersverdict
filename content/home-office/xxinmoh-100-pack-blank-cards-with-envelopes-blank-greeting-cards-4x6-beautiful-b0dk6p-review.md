If you've ever priced blank cards individually at a craft store, you know the pain: $4 for a single card and envelope feels like a scam. The XXINMOH 100-pack changes that math entirely. At roughly 15 cents per complete card-and-envelope set, this is the kind of bulk buy that makes you wonder why you ever paid retail. But value alone doesn't make a product worth your money — the paper quality, the envelope fit, and the printability do. Here's the honest breakdown after looking at what 240 buyers are saying.

**What you actually get**

The box contains 100 blank white cards measuring 4x6 inches (the standard A6 size) plus 100 matching envelopes. Each card is folded, so you're getting real greeting card construction — not flimsy flat stock. The paper weight is a legit 300gsm, which is thicker than most store-bought greeting cards. As one reviewer put it, "I was shocked at the quality for the price. These feel like cards I'd buy at Papyrus." The envelopes are peel-and-seal, which means no damp sponge or licking required.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

Here's where this pack earns its 4.8-star rating. The 300gsm cardstock handles wet media surprisingly well. Watercolor washes dry without the paper buckling into a mess, and alcohol markers don't bleed through to the back — something that even expensive mixed-media paper sometimes fails at. The cards score and fold cleanly, with no cracking along the crease line.

The envelopes are where you'll notice the cost-cutting. They're a standard 80gsm paper, perfectly fine for mailing, but they don't match the heft of the cards themselves. If you're planning to stuff these with thick photos or multiple insert sheets, upgrade to a padded mailer. For a standard card and a check or gift card, they're more than adequate. One buyer noted, "The envelopes are a bit thin but they've all survived mailing without issues."

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 240 ratings, three themes keep appearing. First: the quantity is real. "I counted. There are exactly 100 of each," wrote one skeptical buyer. Second: the paper quality surprises people. Multiple reviewers mention using them for calligraphy, watercolor, and even acrylic paint pouring. Third: the value proposition. When you're making wedding invitations, thank-you notes for a large event, or classroom parent gifts, the cost per card matters.

**Where it falls short**

The packaging is genuinely weak. The cards come in a thin cardboard tuck box that will not survive being stacked under anything heavy. If you're buying these for a wedding project that spans months, plan to transfer them to a plastic storage container immediately. Also, the cards are pure white — no cream option, no off-white. If you're aiming for a vintage or antique aesthetic, you'll need to source elsewhere or accept the bright white finish.

**Who should buy this**

This pack is ideal for three specific buyer types. **Wedding and event planners** — making your own invitations or place cards at this price point is unbeatable. **Teachers and crafters** — classroom thank-you notes, holiday cards for students, or bulk DIY projects. **Watercolor and calligraphy artists** — the paper genuinely holds up to wet media, which is rare at this price.

Skip it if you need pre-printed designs, want colored or textured paper, or if you're planning to mail heavy items. This is a blank canvas, not a finished product.

**FAQ**

**Do these work in a standard home printer?** Yes, but feed them one at a time through the manual feed slot. The 300gsm cardstock is on the heavier end of what most home printers handle. Don't try to run all 100 through at once.

**Are the envelopes self-sealing?** Yes, they have a peel-and-seal adhesive strip. Just remove the paper cover and press firmly.

**Will markers bleed through?** Alcohol markers like Copic or Sharpie won't bleed through the cardstock. Water-based markers and watercolor are safe. The one thing I'd avoid is heavy acrylic paint without a primer layer.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're planning a bulk card project, you work with wet media, or you want to stop overpaying for craft store blanks. At this price per unit, the quality is genuinely impressive.

**Skip it if:** You need decorative designs, colored paper, or heavy-duty envelopes for mailing thick contents.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The paper quality and value are exceptional. The thin envelopes and weak packaging keep it from a perfect score, but for the price, this is the best bulk blank card option I've seen.
