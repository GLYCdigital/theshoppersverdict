**What you actually get**

The package is utilitarian: a single 16GB module in a static-dissipative bag. No screws, no manual, no thermal pads. The stick itself is a standard 260-pin SO-DIMM measuring 69.6mm x 30mm — the universal laptop form factor. It's dual-rank (2Rx8), which matters more than most people realize. Dual-rank memory can deliver 5-10% better performance in memory-intensive tasks because the memory controller can interleave between ranks. The module runs at 2666MHz with JEDEC standard timings of 19-19-19-43 at 1.2V.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work in my MacBook?**
A: No. MacBooks use soldered RAM after 2015 (for most models). This is for Windows/Linux laptops with accessible SO-DIMM slots. Check your manual or use CPU-Z to see if your RAM is upgradeable.

**Q: Can I mix this with my existing 8GB stick?**
A: You can, and it'll run in flex mode (8GB dual-channel + 8GB single-channel). You'll get 16GB total but lose some dual-channel benefit. For most users, this is fine. For optimal performance, buy two identical 16GB sticks.

**Q: Is 2666MHz outdated in 2026?**
A: For office work and most creative apps, no. DDR5 platforms are mainstream now, but if your laptop is DDR4-based (Intel 12th-gen and earlier, AMD Ryzen 5000 and earlier), 2666MHz is the maximum many of those chips officially support anyway. Buying 3200MHz would just downclock.
