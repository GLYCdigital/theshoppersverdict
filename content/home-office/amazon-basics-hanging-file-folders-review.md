The Amazon Basics Hanging File Folders are the default choice for home offices, small businesses, and anyone who's ever stared at a drawer full of crumpled papers. Here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

This is a box of 25 letter-size hanging folders. Each one measures 12.75 x 9.5 inches — standard fit for any file drawer that takes Pendaflex-style folders. The paper stock is a manila tone, and the tabs are the 1/5-cut style with repositionable inserts. You get a mixed set of tab positions, which is smart — it lets you stagger labels so you can actually read them without flipping through everything.

The box itself is plain, but that's fine. You're not buying the box.

**Build Quality: The Honest Breakdown**

Here's where I need to be straight with you. The paper density is noticeably lighter than premium brands like Pendaflex. When you pull one out, it has a slight flex to it that cheaper folders often show. That said, the critical components — the metal hooks and the reinforced top rod — are genuinely well-made. The rods run the full length of the folder, and the hooks are welded, not just crimped. That matters because crimped hooks are the first thing to fail on budget folders.

In real-world testing, these held up fine under a full load of documents. The bottom of the folder is scored, so it expands as you add paper — you can get a solid inch of files in each one before it starts to feel crowded. The tabs are reinforced with an 11-point strip, which is a meaningful detail. That's where cheap folders fail first, and this design handles repeated grabbing and pulling without tearing.

**What 126,000 Buyers Say**

The consensus in the reviews is remarkably consistent. Buyers repeatedly mention that these are "just as good as the expensive brands" and "perfect for the price." One reviewer I found put it well: "I've been buying these for years and they've never let me down. The hooks don't bend, the folders don't tear, and they slide smoothly in my drawer." Another noted, "I was skeptical because of the price, but they're honestly indistinguishable from the ones I used to buy at the office supply store."

The most common praise centers on the value proposition: you're getting 25 folders for roughly $13, which works out to about 52 cents each. That's a significant savings over the $1-per-folder you'd pay for premium brands at Staples or Office Depot.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let me be clear about the tradeoffs. First, the thinner paper stock means these won't survive years of heavy daily handling in a high-traffic office. If you're a paralegal pulling files 20 times a day, spend the extra money on heavier folders. Second, the hooks can be snug on some drawer rails. A few reviewers mentioned needing to give them a firm push to get them seated properly. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you fill a drawer and discover one won't slide.

Also note: these are letter-size only. If you need legal-size folders, this isn't the product.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these compatible with any file cabinet?**
A: They fit standard letter-size drawers with hanging rails. The hooks are designed for the common 2-inch rails found in most cabinets, but they may sit tight on some brands. If you have an unusual drawer system, measure your rails first.

**Q: Can I reuse the tab labels?**
A: Yes. The labels are insertable and come blank, so you can write on them, insert them, and replace them whenever you reorganize. The inserts are repositionable — slide them out and swap new ones in.

**Q: How many sheets does each folder hold comfortably?**
A: With the scored bottom, you can fit roughly 100-150 sheets before the folder starts to strain. The hooks and rod will handle the weight fine, but the paper stock will show wear sooner if you consistently pack them full.
