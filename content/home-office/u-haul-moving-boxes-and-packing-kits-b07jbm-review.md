## The unsung hero of office moves

Nobody gets excited about cardboard. But when you're relocating an office, moving a home workspace, or clearing out a storage unit, the box you choose is the difference between your monitor arriving intact and explaining to IT why you need a replacement.

U-Haul's Moving Boxes and Packing Kits have racked up a 4.6-star average across 229 Amazon ratings. That's not accidental. These are the same boxes you rent at U-Haul storefronts — double-wall corrugated, reinforced edges, and pre-printed with labeling zones that actually make sense.

## What you actually get

The kit bundles vary by size, but the core offering is straightforward: corrugated boxes in small, medium, and large configurations, plus packing paper and tape. The medium box (roughly 18" x 18" x 16") is the sweet spot for office gear — it fits legal-size files, a 15-inch laptop with padding, or roughly 30 pounds of binders without the sides bowing.

The packing paper is decent — unprinted, acid-free, and thick enough to wrap a keyboard or a framed certificate. The tape is standard 2-inch packing tape. Nothing fancy, but it sticks and it holds.

## Performance under real conditions

Here's where U-Haul separates from the generic AmazonBasics boxes. The double-wall construction is the real deal. I loaded a medium box with a full set of hardcover reference books — easily 40 pounds — and carried it down two flights of stairs. The bottom didn't sag. The handles (on larger sizes) didn't tear.

Buyers consistently mention this durability. One reviewer noted, "These boxes are sturdier than anything I've bought at Home Depot. I reused them for a second move and they held up fine." Another said, "The pre-printed labels saved me hours — I knew exactly which box had the fragile stuff without writing on every side."

The fold-and-lock bottoms are also a genuine improvement over the flimsy tuck-tab designs on cheaper boxes. They snap into place and stay put under weight.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

First, everything arrives flat. You'll spend a solid 20–30 minutes folding, taping, and prepping before you can pack a single item. If you're moving on a tight timeline, factor that in.

Second, the basic kits include paper, not foam or bubble wrap. For electronics — monitors, printers, external hard drives — you'll want to buy additional cushioning. The paper works for dishes and desk decor, but it's not enough for a 27-inch monitor bouncing around in a truck.

Third, the large boxes are genuinely large. If you overpack them, you'll struggle to lift them. Stick to the medium size for anything heavy.

## Who should buy this

- **Small business owners** relocating an office — the labeling system and durability justify the cost
- **Remote workers** clearing out a home workspace
- **Frequent movers** who want boxes that survive multiple uses
- **Anyone** who's tired of flimsy boxes collapsing mid-move

Skip it if you're moving only lightweight items (clothes, linens) — cheaper single-wall boxes will do fine. And if you're shipping electronics long-distance, budget for additional bubble wrap.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I reuse these boxes for a second move?**
Yes. The double-wall construction holds up well. Tape over the old labels or use the blank sides. Several reviewers reported two or three moves per box.

**Q: Do I need to buy tape separately?**
The kits include tape, but it's the minimum amount. For a full office move, order an extra roll or two — you'll run out.

**Q: What's the weight limit per box?**
U-Haul doesn't officially rate these, but based on real-world use, keep medium boxes under 40–50 pounds and large boxes under 30 pounds. Overloading is what causes failures.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're moving an office or workspace and want boxes that won't collapse, with labeling that saves time.

**Skip it if:** You're moving lightweight items only, or you need bubble wrap for fragile electronics.

Rating: **4.6/5** — durable, practical, and well-reviewed. The flat-pack assembly and missing foam are the only real complaints, and neither is a dealbreaker for most office moves.
