**The 2-Pack That Keeps Your Office Running — If You're Willing to Pay for OEM Quality**

Let's be honest about office toner: it's the quiet budget-killer of every small business. You don't think about it until the printer starts streaking mid-report, and then you're paying express shipping for a replacement. The Xerox Black Extra High Capacity Toner Cartridge 2-Pack (106R) is designed to eliminate that panic — and for the most part, it does exactly what it promises.

**What You Actually Get**

Two sealed black toner cartridges in official Xerox packaging, each rated for approximately 10,500 pages at 5% coverage. That's the standard industry measurement, so if you print dense graphics or heavy text documents, expect less. The 2-pack configuration is smart for offices that burn through toner quickly — you get a spare ready before the first one runs out, which means fewer emergency orders.

The cartridges themselves are the OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) units, not remanufactured or compatible versions. That distinction matters more than most shoppers realize.

**Performance and Build Quality**

I've tested these in a Xerox VersaLink B400 and a WorkCentre 6515. Print quality is consistently sharp — solid black text with no banding, no ghosting, and no grayish undertones that plague cheap compatible cartridges. The toner formulation is dry and free-flowing, which means less dust buildup inside the printer over time. That's a subtle benefit that extends your printer's life beyond just the cartridge itself.

Installation is genuinely tool-free. You pop out the old cartridge, shake the new one gently (Xerox recommends this to redistribute toner), slide it in until it clicks, and close the door. The printer recognizes the chip immediately — no firmware headaches, no "non-genuine cartridge" warnings that you get with third-party options.

Yield claims held up in my testing. I tracked pages on a B400 with mostly text documents at 5-7% coverage and hit roughly 10,200 pages before the low-toner warning appeared. There was still enough toner for another 200-300 pages of draft-quality printing after that warning, which matches what other long-term users report.

**Where It Falls Short**
