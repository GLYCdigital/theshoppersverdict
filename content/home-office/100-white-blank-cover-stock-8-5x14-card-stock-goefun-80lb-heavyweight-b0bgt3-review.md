**Is this the legal-size card stock your printer has been begging for? Probably — if you know what 80lb actually means.**

Let's cut through the listing jargon. Goefun sells a 100-sheet pack of 8.5x14 white card stock at 80lb cover weight. That translates to about 218gsm — thicker than standard copy paper (20lb bond), but not the near-cardboard stiffness of 110lb or 12pt stock. It's the sweet spot for projects that need structure without fighting your printer's paper path.

**What's in the box:** You get a shrink-wrapped stack of 100 sheets, legal size (8.5x14 inches). No extras, no sample pack, no nonsense. The paper arrives flat with clean, square-cut edges — I found zero bent corners or curled sheets in my pack, which is more than I can say for some budget brands.

**Real-world performance:** I ran this through a Brother laser printer, a Canon inkjet, and a Cricut cutting machine. Laser printing produced sharp, smudge-free text at 600dpi — no fusing issues, no ghosting. Inkjet results were equally good; the bright white surface (brightness is listed around 98+) makes colors pop without that grayish tint you get with cheaper pulp blends. The Cricut cut cleanly through it at medium pressure with no tearing — this is a solid choice for die-cut tags, labels, or layered paper crafts.

One thing that impressed me: the surface is smooth and coated enough that pen ink doesn't feather. If you're using this for hand-addressed envelopes or calligraphy place cards, the writing experience is far better than uncoated cardstock.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The ratings speak to reliability. One reviewer noted, "Printed perfectly in my HP LaserJet — no jams, no curling, just clean results every time." Another mentioned using it for table numbers at a wedding: "Sturdy enough to stand in a holder but thin enough to cut with scissors when I was in a rush." The consistency of the 100-sheet count also gets frequent mentions — no shorted packs, no damaged sheets.

**Where it falls short:** Be honest about what "80lb" gets you. If you want a paper that stands upright on its own as a place card or holds a business card's crisp fold, this is borderline. It's more flexible than heavier stocks — fine for flyers, certificates, and menu inserts, but it will flop if you expect it to behave like 110lb. Also, if you're printing full-bleed images with heavy ink saturation, the edges curl slightly as the moisture evaporates. It's fixable with a flat book on top for an hour, but it's worth knowing.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Event planners** printing table numbers, menus, or place cards on a budget
- **Teachers or homeschool parents** making flashcards, charts, or sturdy worksheets
- **Crafters** who need a reliable, cuttable base for Cricut or Silhouette projects
- **Small business owners** creating DIY flyers or tent cards without paying print shop prices

**Skip it if:** You need true cover-stock stiffness for packaging, or you're a fine artist needing archival, acid-free paper for prints — this isn't that.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Can I run this through a standard home printer?**
A: Yes, if your printer accepts legal-size paper (8.5x14). Most laser and inkjet printers with a rear or adjustable paper tray handle it fine. It's 218gsm, which is well within the range of most home printers — just don't try to feed it through a cheap all-in-one's automatic document feeder.

**Q: Is it double-sided?**
A: It's printable on both sides, but it's not guaranteed for duplex printing in every printer. The paper is bright white on both sides with no visible watermark, but if you need perfect no-bleed-through on heavy ink, test a sheet first.

**Q: Can I cut it into different sizes?**
A: Absolutely. Each sheet cuts cleanly into two 8.5x5.5 half-sheets, three 4.25x7 strips (perfect for table tents), or custom sizes with a guillotine cutter or Cricut. It's scored well with a bone folder too.
