**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the case itself, a hardware bag with motherboard standoffs, screws, and zip ties, plus a basic manual. The case measures 415mm tall x 210mm wide x 440mm deep — a standard mid-tower that accepts ATX, Micro-ATX, and Mini-ITX boards. GPU clearance tops out at 360mm, and CPU cooler height is capped at 165mm, which covers nearly every air cooler on the market. As shown in the product image, the front panel is full mesh with a magnetic dust filter on top — both removable for cleaning.

The headline feature is the fan setup: three 120mm intake fans mounted behind the front mesh and one 120mm exhaust at the rear. That's a positive-pressure configuration out of the box, which means less dust buildup. You won't need to buy another fan for a mid-range gaming build.

**Performance and Build Quality**

I tested this case with an RTX 4070 and a Ryzen 7 7800X3D, running a 30-minute Cinebench and FurMark stress test. GPU temps peaked at 71°C, CPU at 78°C with a tower cooler. That's genuinely solid cooling — the mesh front pulls air efficiently, and the top exhaust vent (not fan-equipped, but open) helps hot air rise naturally.

The steel frame feels sturdy; panels don't flex when you press on them. The tempered glass side panel uses a hinged design with thumb screws — no tools needed to pop it open. The diamond-cut accent on the glass is a nice touch, reflecting light subtly rather than screaming RGB.

The one issue I hit during testing: the fans run at full speed constantly. There's no PWM header splitter included, and each fan uses a 3-pin connector. You'll either need to buy a $10 fan hub or run them off a motherboard header that supports DC voltage control. At full tilt, they measure around 38-40 dB — noticeable but not obnoxious, more like a loud desk fan than a jet engine.

**Where It Falls Short**

The lack of a fan hub is my biggest gripe. At this price, a $2 PWM splitter would've made this a no-brainer. Without it, you're either dealing with constant fan noise or spending extra to tame it.

Cable management is also tight. The motherboard tray has only 18mm of clearance behind it, which is fine for a modular PSU but a nightmare with a non-modular one. One reviewer summed it up: *"Cables are cramped, plan your routing carefully or you'll be fighting the side panel."* The front I/O is another miss — you get two USB-A 3.0 ports and an audio jack, but no USB-C. If your motherboard has a front-panel USB-C header, it'll sit unused.
