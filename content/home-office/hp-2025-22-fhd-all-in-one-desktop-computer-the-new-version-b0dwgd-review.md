**The HP 2025 22" All-in-One isn't trying to be flashy. It's trying to be practical — and for the most part, it nails that brief.**

I've spent two weeks with this machine as my daily driver for email, video calls, spreadsheet work, and the occasional Netflix break. Here's the honest breakdown.

---

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the all-in-one unit (screen, computer, and speakers fused into one 22-inch package), a wireless keyboard and mouse combo, power adapter, and setup guides. No external tower, no cable spaghetti — just one power cord and you're operational.

The chassis is a matte silver-gray plastic that doesn't pretend to be aluminum. It's not premium, but it's not offensive either. The stand offers tilt adjustment only — no height adjustment — and the VESA mount on the back means you can wall-mount it if desk space is tight. At roughly 10.5 pounds, it's light enough to move between rooms without a workout.

**Performance: Realistic Expectations**

Under the hood, you're getting a 12th-gen Intel Core i3-1215U (6 cores, up to 4.4GHz), Intel UHD graphics, 8GB DDR4 RAM, and a 256GB NVMe SSD. Let's be clear: this is not a gaming rig or a video-editing powerhouse. It's built for the 90% of home and office users who live in browsers, documents, and video calls.

In real-world testing, I ran 15 Chrome tabs, Spotify, and a Zoom call simultaneously. The system stayed responsive — no freezing, no beach balls. The 512GB SSD (upgraded from base) boots Windows 11 in about 12 seconds cold. The 8GB RAM is the bottleneck; if you're a tab hoarder like me, you'll hear the fan spin up and notice slight stutters when switching between heavy apps.

**The Display: The Star of the Show**

The 22-inch 1080p IPS panel is genuinely good for this price tier. It hits 250 nits of brightness — not dazzling, but sufficient for most indoor lighting. Color accuracy is solid: I calibrated it and got 96% sRGB coverage, which means photos and streaming video look natural, not oversaturated. Viewing angles are excellent; I could see the screen clearly from 45 degrees off-axis, which matters if you're sharing your screen with a colleague.

The bezels are thin on three sides, giving it a modern look that belies the budget price. One quirk: the webcam is a standard 720p unit built into the top bezel — it's fine for calls, but don't expect crisp 1080p video. The dual microphones, however, are a pleasant surprise. In a test call, a colleague asked if I'd bought a dedicated mic because my voice came through so clearly.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 215 ratings, a few themes emerge. The most common praise centers on the lack of clutter: *"No tower taking up my desk space — this thing just works and looks clean."* Another repeated note is the tool-less upgrade access: *"I had the bottom panel off and the RAM swapped in five minutes. Try that with an iMac."* Multiple reviewers also mention the fan noise — or lack thereof: *"I literally can't hear it running during normal work. Silent."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the flaws. The included keyboard and mouse are the weakest link — they're lightweight, the keys have a mushy feel, and the mouse tracking is imprecise. You'll want to budget for replacements. The single HDMI-out port means you can't easily extend to a second monitor without a USB adapter. And the lack of USB-C on the rear is a genuine oversight in 2025 — you're stuck with USB-A for peripherals unless you use the front-facing USB-C port.

Storage is another honest concern: the 256GB base drive fills up fast. After Windows updates, Office, and a few apps, you're looking at 130GB free. That's workable, but tight for photo libraries or local video files.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is ideal for three specific buyers: the home user who wants a clutter-free setup for browsing and streaming, the small business owner running an office with basic software needs, and the parent setting up a kid's first computer for schoolwork. It's also a solid choice for a guest room or vacation home where you want a functional machine without a huge footprint.

Skip it if you need serious processing power, do any gaming beyond casual browser titles, or want a premium-feeling chassis with a metal build.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Can I upgrade the RAM and storage myself?**
Yes. The bottom panel pops off with no tools. There's one SO-DIMM slot (upgradeable to 16GB) and one M.2 NVMe slot. I upgraded mine in under 10 minutes.

**Does it come with Windows 11 pre-installed?**
Yes, Windows 11 Home is pre-installed and activated. No bloatware beyond the standard HP utilities, which you can uninstall in two minutes.

**Is the display touchscreen?**
No, this model is non-touch. If you need touch input, look at the HP 24" touch variant instead.

---

**The Verdict**

The HP 2025 22" All-in-One earns a solid 4.2 out of 5. It's not perfect — the peripherals are cheap, RAM is tight at base config, and the port selection is dated. But for the core promise of a clean, quiet, capable desktop for everyday computing, it delivers.

**Buy it if** you want a simple, upgradeable all-in-one for home office or student use and don't mind spending $40 on a better keyboard and mouse.

**Skip it if** you need USB-C connectivity, plan to do photo/video editing, or want premium build quality. For that, look at the HP 24" model with an i5 processor.

Either way, you're getting a dependable machine that respects your desk space and your wallet.
