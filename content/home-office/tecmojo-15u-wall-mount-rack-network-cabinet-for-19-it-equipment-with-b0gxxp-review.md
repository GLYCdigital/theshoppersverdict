If you've ever tried to bolt a 15U rack to a wall and realized halfway through that the cabinet weighs more than your entire network closet, you know the drill. The Tecmojo 15U Wall Mount Rack is trying to be the answer to that headache — and for the most part, it delivers. But let's get into what you're actually paying for.

## What you get in the box

The cabinet arrives mostly assembled. You're looking at a 15U, 19-inch rack that's 29.5 inches tall, about 23.6 inches wide, and 15 inches deep — deep enough for most switches, patch panels, and even some shallow 1U servers. The front is a tempered glass door with a key lock, and the side panels swing out on hinges. You also get the standard mounting hardware: cage nuts, screws, and a hex key for the lock.

What's notably absent is any kind of cable management. No finger ducts, no horizontal organizers, no Velcro strips. For a cabinet at this price point, that's a miss — you'll spend another $20–30 on management accessories if you want clean runs.

## Build quality and real-world use

The steel frame feels solid. The powder coat is even, the glass door closes with a satisfying click, and the hinged side panels actually work — they swing open about 90 degrees, giving you genuine access to the rear of your gear. That's a big deal because most wall-mount racks in this class make you fish cables through fixed panels.

One thing to know: this is a swing-out design, not a full swing-frame. The entire cabinet doesn't pivot away from the wall like some pricier units. Instead, the sides open and the front door lifts off. That's fine for most home labs and small offices, but if you need to work on the back of your equipment frequently while it's mounted, you'll be working through the side openings — not ideal but workable.

Heat is handled via vented top and bottom panels. There are pre-drilled holes for optional fans, and you can see the slots in the product images. Passive ventilation is adequate for a few switches; if you're stacking 10+U of active gear, add a fan or two.

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings isn't random. The recurring themes in feedback:

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the negatives.

**Wall stud spacing is the big one.** The mounting bracket uses a fixed hole pattern that aligns with standard 16-inch on-center studs. If your walls are 24-inch on-center — common in garages, basements, and newer builds — you'll need to mount a plywood backer board first. That's an extra step and an extra trip to the hardware store.

**The cage nuts are fiddly.** The rails are stamped steel, not tapped, so you'll need to install cage nuts manually. They fit snug, but getting them seated properly across all 15U takes patience. If you're used to pre-threaded rails, this will feel like a downgrade.

**Depth is 15 inches, not 20+.** That's fine for patch panels and most switches, but if you're planning to mount a deeper enterprise switch or a UPS, measure first. This is not a full-depth rack.

## Who should buy this

- **Home lab builders** with a dedicated network closet or utility room — the size is right for a switch, patch panel, and a small server or two.
- **Small office setups** that need a neat, lockable enclosure for network gear without dedicating floor space to a full 42U cabinet.
- **AV installers** mounting equipment in conference rooms or retail spaces where aesthetics matter — the glass door looks clean and professional.

**Skip it if** you need full-depth server support, if your walls have non-standard stud spacing and you don't want to build a backer, or if you expect cable management to come included.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this rack support standard 19-inch equipment?**
A: Yes. The mounting rails are standard 19-inch spaced, compatible with virtually all rack-mount switches, patch panels, and 1U servers. Just check the depth — the cabinet is 15 inches deep.

**Q: Can I mount this to drywall without studs?**
A: You shouldn't. The cabinet weighs roughly 35–40 pounds empty, and loaded with gear it's heavier. Use the included bracket with proper lag bolts into studs, or install a plywood backer first. Drywall anchors are not sufficient.

**Q: Does the glass door lock securely?**
A: Yes, it uses a keyed lock with a hex-style key. It's not a high-security solution — anyone with the key or a determined thief with a screwdriver can get in — but it keeps casual access out, which is what most small offices need.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely usable wall-mount rack with real rear access, solid steel construction, and a clean, professional look — and you're prepared to handle your own cable management and wall-mounting prep.

**Skip it if** you need full-depth capacity, have non-standard stud spacing you don't want to work around, or expect a complete all-in-one solution with management accessories included.

The Tecmojo 15U hits a sweet spot for home labs and small offices. It's not perfect — the cage nuts and fixed bracket pattern will test your patience — but at this size and price, it's one of the better options available. The swing-out sides alone justify the purchase for anyone who's ever crawled behind a wall-mounted rack with a flashlight.
