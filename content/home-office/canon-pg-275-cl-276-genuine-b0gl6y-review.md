Let's be direct: you're here because your PIXMA just flashed a low-ink warning, and you're staring at a $60+ decision. The Canon PG-275/CL-276 combo is the OEM (original equipment manufacturer) ink for a huge swath of Canon's current PIXMA lineup — TR7020, TS3522, MG3620, and a dozen others. Here's the honest breakdown of whether genuine is worth it or if you should roll the dice on remanufactured.

**What you actually get:** Two sealed cartridges — the PG-275 black (rated for 200 pages) and the CL-276 color (rated for 180 pages, based on ISO/IEC 24712 test documents with 5% coverage). This is the standard-yield set. There's no XL high-capacity variant for these models, which is a critical limitation I'll get to in a moment. Installation is genuinely tool-free: remove the orange protective tape, snap each cartridge down into its slot until it clicks, and run a quick alignment. Most users report being done in under two minutes.

**The real-world performance story:** Print quality from genuine Canon ink is noticeably better than third-party refills — particularly on the color side. The CL-276 delivers accurate skin tones and smooth gradients in photo prints on glossy paper, and text from the PG-275 is sharp with no feathering on standard copy paper. One 4.6-star reviewer put it plainly: *"I've tried the cheap refill kits — they leak and the colors look washed out. These are more expensive but the prints look like they came from a store."* That tracks with my testing. The pigment-based black ink dries fast and resists smudging when you highlight text — a real issue with dye-based remanufactured blacks.

**Where the genuine ink frustrates:** The economics are ugly. At typical street pricing, you're looking at roughly 9 cents per black page and 19 cents per color page. If you print 50 pages a week, that's $4-8 per month just in ink — which over a year approaches the cost of a new printer. The CL-276's 180-page yield is the real weak point. Color cartridge replacement frequency catches people off guard. One verified buyer noted: *"The black lasted me three months, but I swapped the color twice in that same period. It's frustrating but the print quality keeps me coming back."* Also worth knowing: remanufactured cartridges for this model run about 40% cheaper, but they void your Canon warranty and carry a real risk of printhead clogging — a repair that costs more than the ink you saved.

**Who should buy this:** The genuine combo is the right call for three specific groups — home users printing under 100 pages monthly (you'll replace cartridges 2-3 times a year, which is tolerable), anyone printing photos or documents for professional or academic purposes where color accuracy matters, and people who've been burned by third-party leaks or compatibility errors. If you're a student churning out 500+ pages per semester, or a small office doing daily volume, you need to look at a tank-based printer (Canon MegaTank or Epson EcoTank) — the per-page cost drops to under a penny, and you'll recoup the higher upfront cost within 6 months.

**FAQ — three questions shoppers actually ask:**

**Q: Can I use only the black cartridge and skip the color one?**
A: Yes, most PIXMA models will print in black-only mode if you leave the color slot empty, but you'll lose color printing entirely and some printers refuse to scan or fax until both are installed. You can't just pop in a used color cartridge from another printer — Canon's chip locks it to the first device.

**Q: How do I know if this fits my printer?**
A: Check your printer's model number against Canon's compatibility list. This cartridge family covers most TR7000, TS3000/5000/6000, and MG3600 series. If your printer takes PG-240/CL-241 or PG-245/CL-246, this won't fit — those are physically different cartridges.

**Q: Will Amazon's "Subscribe & Save" lower the cost meaningfully?**
A: You'll save about 5% on recurring delivery, but that doesn't change the fundamental cost-per-page math. It's convenience savings, not a strategic price cut.

**The Verdict:**
**Buy it if** you print under 100 pages a month, need dependable color output, or value warranty protection and zero-clog reliability over saving a few dollars per cartridge. **Skip it if** you're a high-volume printer — the per-page cost will bleed you dry, and a tank printer is the smarter long-term investment.
