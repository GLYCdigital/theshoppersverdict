**The Verdict: A Serious Ergonomic Upgrade—If Your Desk Has the Space**

Most under-desk trays are cheap plastic planks that force your wrists into a bend. The HUANUO 360 Adjustable model is a different animal. It’s a gas-spring, multi-plane rig that genuinely mimics a height-adjustable standing desk for your upper body. At a 4.3-star average from 215 ratings, it’s clear buyers appreciate the mechanism—but it’s not a universal fit.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the steel mounting clamp, the articulated gas-spring arm, the wooden tray (roughly 23.5 x 10 inches), and a small gel wrist rest and mouse pad. The clamp is the star here: it’s a C-clamp design rated for desktops up to 3.2 inches thick, which covers most solid-wood and butcher-block tops that cheap particle-board desks can’t handle.

**Build Quality & Real-World Use**

The mechanism is where HUANUO spent the money. Unlike friction-lock trays that slip after a week, this uses a gas spring for vertical lift. You press a lever, and the tray rises or lowers with the weight of your hands on it. The 360° pivot is genuinely smooth—you can angle the keyboard toward you if you recline, or push it flat for typing. The lock knobs are metal, not plastic, so they don’t strip.

One reviewer noted: *"I was skeptical about the clamp holding my 2-inch solid oak desk. It’s rock solid—no bounce when I type aggressively."* That matches the engineering. However, the tray’s underside has a central support bar. If you rest your palms hard on the front edge, you’ll feel a slight flex in the center—not a failure, just a reminder it’s a 23-inch span, not a solid slab.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

- **The height range.** The gas spring offers a travel of about 4.5 inches, which is double what most fixed trays offer. Users with tall chairs or thick seat cushions report finally getting their elbows at a 90-degree angle.
- **The clamp’s grip.** Multiple reviews mention it holding firmly on surfaces where suction mounts failed.
- **The swivel.** The horizontal rotation locks at any point, so you can angle your keyboard for gaming or shift it out of the way when writing by hand.

**Where It Falls Short**

The clearance issue is the big one. The clamp and arm assembly needs roughly 2.5 inches of open space beneath your desktop. If you have drawers, a monitor stand base, or a cable management trough in that zone, this won’t fit. Measure before you buy—this is the #1 reason for returns, per the review comments.

The bundled accessories are also weak. The mouse pad is a thin 7.5 x 12.5 inches—fine for a basic mouse, but if you play FPS games at low sensitivity, your sweeps will hit the tray’s edge. And the gel wrist rest is a thin strip that doesn’t match the tray’s depth; most users will toss it.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The tall desk worker:** If your fixed-height desk forces you to hunch, this tray lowers your typing surface to a proper neutral position.
- **The sit-stand desk user** who wants to keep their keyboard at elbow height while the desk moves—the gas spring adjusts on the fly.
- **The multi-tasker** who wants to slide the keyboard away completely to write or draw on the desk surface.

**Skip It If:**

- Your desk has a lower crossbar or drawer within 2.5 inches of the underside.
- You want a quick, tool-free install. This needs a screwdriver and patience for the initial bolt alignment.
- You’re on a strict budget—this costs more than simple sliding trays because the mechanism is genuinely better.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work on a glass desk?**
A: No. The clamp applies point pressure that can crack tempered glass. Stick to wood, metal, or thick laminate.

**Q: Can I use a heavy mechanical keyboard?**
A: Yes, the rated capacity is around 22 lbs. A full-size mechanical keyboard with a steel plate plus a mouse is fine. The gas spring holds position without sagging.

**Q: Does the tray wobble when I type?**
A: Minimal lateral wobble if the clamp is tight. There’s slight vertical flex if you push down hard on the front edge, but it’s not distracting during normal typing.

**The Verdict: Buy It If You Need Real Height Adjustment**

**Rating: 4.2/5** — This is the best under-desk tray I’ve tested under $150. The gas spring mechanism is the differentiator; it feels premium and holds position. The clearance requirement is the only hard blocker.

**Buy it if:** You have a thick desk, a proper ergonomic chair, and you’re tired of wrist pain from a fixed keyboard surface.

**Skip it if:** Your desk has a crossbar in the way, or you just need a cheap way to hide your keyboard. This is an ergonomic tool, not a storage drawer.
