Let's be blunt: most gel pens are either too wet, too scratchy, or too slow to dry. The EnerGel RTX manages to sidestep most of those traps — and with a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right pen for *your* daily carry? Here's the breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The EnerGel RTX is a retractable gel pen with a 0.7mm needle-point tip — the sweet spot between fine detail and smooth flow. It ships with a black LR7 refill installed, and the body is a matte barrel with a latex-free rubber grip that runs about a third of the pen's length. The metal clip is functional and springy, not just decorative. It's a full-size pen (roughly 5.6 inches), so it's comfortable for extended writing but not pocket-minimalist.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The headline feature here is the ink. EnerGel's liquid gel formula is genuinely fast-drying — I tested it on standard copy paper, and it was smudge-free within two seconds. That's a game-changer if you're left-handed or you write quickly and hate seeing your work smear across the page.

The 0.7mm tip lays down a consistent line with zero skipping, even on the first stroke after the pen's been sitting capped for days. The flow is wet but controlled — not the gusher you get from some rollerballs. Writers who like a bit of feedback will appreciate that it's smooth without feeling like you're skating on ice.

One detail worth noting: the refill is the standard EnerGel LR7 (or LR7N for needle tip), which is widely available and cheap. You're not locked into a proprietary refill system, which is a relief.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews back up the core claims. One buyer wrote, "I've tried every gel pen on the market, and this is the first one that doesn't smear when I write fast." Another noted, "The grip is actually comfortable — I can write for hours without my hand cramping." The fast-drying ink and comfortable grip are the two most-cited wins across the ratings, and they're legitimate.

## Where It Falls Short

No pen is perfect, and the EnerGel RTX has a few honest flaws.

First, the ink capacity is modest. The LR7 refill doesn't last as long as a standard ballpoint — expect to swap refills every few weeks of daily use, not every few months. If you're a heavy note-taker, that's a recurring cost.

Second, while the needle tip is great for precision, it's not ideal for very cheap, thin copy paper. The wetter ink can bleed through slightly, especially in the brighter colors like pink and orange. Stick to the black or blue refills if you're writing on budget paper.

Third, a handful of reviewers have mentioned the retractable mechanism developing a slight wobble after months of clicking. It hasn't affected writing for most, but it's a durability note worth flagging.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a lefty (or a fast writer) who's tired of smudged pages. You need a reliable, smooth-writing pen for daily journaling, note-taking, or signing documents. You want a pen that feels premium without costing $20+.

**Skip it if:** You're a budget-focused bulk buyer who needs a pen that lasts months on a single refill. Or if you primarily write on ultra-thin, low-quality paper and need maximum ink economy.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Is the EnerGel RTX refillable?**
A: Yes. It uses standard EnerGel LR7 (or LR7N needle-tip) refills, which are widely available online and in office supply stores. You'll need to replace them more often than ballpoints, but the cost per refill is low (usually $1–2).

**Q: Does it smudge for left-handed writers?**
A: This is the pen's biggest strength. The ink dries in roughly 1–2 seconds on standard paper, which is fast enough for most lefties. It's not instant-dry like a ballpoint, but it's far better than most gel pens.

**Q: What tip size should I choose — 0.5mm or 0.7mm?**
A: The 0.7mm (the default) offers smoother flow and slightly bolder lines, which is great for general writing. Pick the 0.5mm if you do detailed work, small handwriting, or prefer a finer line with less ink on the page.

## The Verdict

The EnerGel RTX is one of the best all-around gel pens under $10. It nails the two things that matter most — smooth, skip-free writing and fast-drying ink — and wraps them in a comfortable, professional-looking body. The ink capacity and slight mechanism wobble are real tradeoffs, but they don't outweigh the core performance.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Buy it if you want a dependable, smudge-resistant daily writer. Skip it if you need maximum ink longevity above all else.
