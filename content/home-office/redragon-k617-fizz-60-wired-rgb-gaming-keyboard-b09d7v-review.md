## The 60% Keyboard That Actually Gets It Right (Mostly)

The compact keyboard market is flooded with cheap 60% boards that rattle, flex, and die within six months. The Redragon K617 Fizz breaks that pattern — mostly. With a 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews, it's clearly earning its keep on desks. But "mostly" is doing heavy lifting here, and you deserve to know exactly where it shines and where it cuts corners before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the keyboard itself, a keycap puller, a switch puller, and a braided USB-C cable. That's it — no fancy carrying case, no spare switches. The keycap and switch pullers are genuinely useful because this board is **hot-swappable**, meaning you can yank out the stock linear switches and drop in your own without touching a soldering iron. That's a feature normally reserved for $100+ custom boards.

The chassis is where Redragon surprised me. It's aluminum, not plastic. At this price, that's uncommon. The board weighs in at about 1.5 pounds, giving it a solid, planted feel on a desk. No flex when you type aggressively, no hollow ping when you bottom out. The white keycaps are double-shot ABS, which means the legends won't wear off — a common failure point on budget boards.

## Real-World Performance

Out of the box, the K617 uses Redragon's own linear switches, rated for 50 million keystrokes. They're smooth enough for gaming but slightly scratchy compared to Gateron or Cherry switches. If you're a heavy typist, you'll notice the difference within an hour. The fix is simple: swap them. The hot-swap sockets accept 3-pin and 5-pin switches, so your options are wide open.

The RGB is genuinely impressive for the price. As shown in the product image, the board features per-key backlighting plus a side underglow strip. There are 18 preset modes — wave, ripple, reactive, breathing, and more — all adjustable via function-key shortcuts. No software required, which is a blessing and a curse. You get what you need, but you can't fine-tune per-key lighting colors.

Latency feels responsive in fast-paced games. I tested it with shooters and rhythm games; no noticeable input delay. The polling rate is 1000Hz, matching boards that cost three times as much.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Reviewers consistently highlight the same things. One buyer wrote, "The aluminum frame makes this feel like a $100 keyboard." Another noted, "Hot-swappable at this price is insane — I put in some Gateron Browns and it's a completely different board." The RGB also gets frequent shout-outs: "The side lighting is what sold me. It looks way more expensive than it is."

## Where It Falls Short

The 60% layout is the biggest hurdle. No arrow keys, no F-row, no dedicated function keys. You'll need to hold the Fn key for everything from volume control to arrow navigation. If you're coming from a full-size board, plan on a week of frustrated fumbling. Some buyers never adapt; one reviewer admitted, "I returned it because I couldn't live without arrow keys for spreadsheet work."

The non-detachable cable is another annoyance. It's braided and durable, but if you want to pack this for travel or reposition your setup, you're stuck. Redragon cut this corner to hit the price point.

Finally, the stock switches are the weak link. They're not bad, but they're not great. If you're fine with a decent linear feel, you'll save money by keeping them. If you're picky, factor $15–$20 for replacement switches into your budget.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Budget gamers** who want a compact, responsive board with RGB that punches above its price
- **Keyboard tinkerers** who want to experiment with hot-swap switches without committing to a $150 custom build
- **Minimalist desk setups** — this board frees up serious mouse space

Skip it if you're a spreadsheet warrior or someone who needs arrow keys for daily work. Also skip if you want wireless — this is wired only.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the Redragon K617 Fizz compatible with Mac?**
Yes, it works via USB plug-and-play on macOS. The function key shortcuts for media and volume work, though the Windows key acts as a Command key. No software needed.

**Q: Can I use it with a PS5 or Xbox?**
It works on PS5 and PS4 via USB for gaming. Xbox consoles are more limited — the keyboard works for typing but not all games support it. PC is the primary target.

**Q: Are the switches loud?**
The stock linear switches are quiet-ish — no click, but you'll hear a soft thock when bottoming out. If you need to type in a shared space, this is acceptable but not silent. O-ring dampeners can further reduce noise.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a compact, hot-swappable keyboard with premium build quality at a budget price. It's the best entry point into customizable mechanical boards under $50.

**Skip it if** you can't live without arrow keys, need wireless, or expect flawless stock switches. This is a great foundation, not a perfect finished product.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The aluminum frame, hot-swap sockets, and RGB make this a steal. The layout and stock switches keep it from being a slam dunk, but for gamers and tinkerers, it's hard to beat at this price.
