# DJiEX Gaming Monitor Review: Surprisingly Capable at $85

Let's be straight about what a sub-$90 monitor usually gets you: a blurry TN panel with washed-out colors and a refresh rate your grandpa's CRT could beat. The DJiEX Gaming Monitor at $84.99 isn't that. It's genuinely decent — with a few honest caveats you need to know before buying.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 24-inch IPS panel, a metal stand base, an HDMI cable, and a power adapter. No DisplayPort cable, no VGA cable — just HDMI. The stand snaps in without tools, which is nice, but it only tilts. No height adjustment, no swivel, no VESA mount holes on the included stand. If you need ergonomic positioning, budget for a monitor arm or a stack of books.

The panel itself is 1920x1080 with a 100Hz refresh rate. That's not 144Hz or 240Hz, but at this price, 100Hz is a legitimate selling point. It also packs in speakers — they're basic, tinny, and fine for YouTube but not for music. You're buying this for the screen, not the audio.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this through color calibration, gaming sessions, and standard office work. The IPS panel delivers viewing angles that crush any budget TN display. Colors are punchy but run cool out of the box — skin tones look slightly blue until you dig into the on-screen display (OSD) and adjust the color temperature. It's a 10-minute fix, but it's worth noting if you're photo editing.

Gaming at 100Hz feels noticeably smoother than 60Hz. I tested with fast-paced shooters, and motion blur isn't an issue. Response time is listed at 5ms — not competitive-tier, but fine for casual and mid-level play. The FreeSync compatibility works over HDMI, though you need to enable it in the OSD first, and it only functions within the 48-100Hz range.

One pleasant surprise: the anti-glare coating is effective. Even in a bright room, reflections stay manageable. Text is crisp at 1080p on a 24-inch panel — no scaling headaches like you'd get on a 27-inch 1080p display.

## What Buyers Are Saying

With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, the consensus matches my testing. One verified buyer wrote, "Honestly shocked at the quality for the price. My old monitor was 60Hz and this thing is butter smooth." Another noted, "Got this for my kids' homework station and it works perfectly with their older desktop."

The recurring praise centers on value and build quality. Multiple reviewers mention the thin bezels making it look like a much pricier monitor. The metal stand base earned specific credit for stability — no wobble on desks that get bumped.

## Where It Falls Short

The OSD menu is clunky. Navigating settings uses a single joystick, but the menu structure is unintuitive, requiring multiple clicks to find basic settings like brightness or input source. It's a minor annoyance you'll deal with daily.

The built-in speakers are genuinely weak — muffled at higher volumes with no bass. If you need audio, buy a cheap speaker bar.

And the lack of VESA mounting is the biggest miss. The stand works, but if you want to mount this on an arm for a cleaner setup, you're out of luck without a third-party adapter.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're building a budget gaming rig, setting up a home office with a secondary display, or outfitting a child's study space. It's also a solid choice for a dorm room where space and money are tight.

**Skip it if:** You need color-accurate output for photo/video work, want a 144Hz+ refresh rate for competitive esports, or require ergonomic adjustments for all-day desk work.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this monitor work with a MacBook?**
A: Yes, but you'll need a USB-C to HDMI adapter since the monitor only has HDMI and VGA inputs. MacBook users report no issues with resolution detection.

**Q: Is the 100Hz refresh rate worth it over a standard 60Hz?**
A: For gaming, yes. The difference is immediately noticeable in smoothness. For office work, you won't care. The price difference between 60Hz and 100Hz monitors is small enough to make the faster panel the smarter buy.

**Q: Can I mount this on a wall or monitor arm?**
A: Not out of the box. There's no VESA mounting pattern on the included stand. You'll need a third-party VESA adapter bracket that clips onto the stand's pole, which works but adds height to the setup.

## The Verdict

The DJiEX Gaming Monitor is exactly what it claims to be: a budget-friendly 1080p display that punches above its price point for gaming and general use. It's not perfect — the speakers are weak, the OSD is clunky, and the stand lacks ergonomic adjustments. But at $84.99, those tradeoffs are acceptable.

**Buy it if** you want a solid 100Hz gaming panel without spending $200+. **Skip it if** color-critical work or ergonomic flexibility are non-negotiable.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good budget monitor with honest limitations.
