Opening a fresh cartridge and getting that first perfect page is satisfying — until you realize you're already 120 pages closer to the next $30 purchase. That's the tradeoff with the EPSON 202 Claria Standard Capacity Black Cartridge (T202120-S). It's the genuine-ink route for a reason, but it's also not the cheapest way to feed a hungry printer.

**What you actually get**

The box contains a single standard-capacity black cartridge using EPSON's Claria dye-based ink formulation. It's designed for a specific family of Expression and WorkForce printers — the "202" designation covers models like the XP-4200, XP-5200, and WF-2800 series, among others. The cartridge uses a microchip that communicates directly with your printer, which means no compatibility surprises and no voided warranty. That's the genuine-ink advantage, and it matters.

**Real-world performance**

Claria ink has a reputation, and it's deserved. Black text comes out dense and sharp, with clean edges even at smaller font sizes. On standard multipurpose paper, drying time is fast enough that you won't smear pages when stacking them. The dye-based formula also means colors stay vibrant if you're printing photos alongside documents, though this cartridge is strictly black.

The page yield is the honest sore spot. EPSON rates this at approximately 120 pages, which is fine for light home use — a few assignments, some shipping labels, the occasional recipe. But if you're printing 20 pages a day, you'll be shopping for cartridges every single week. That's not a flaw in the ink itself; it's just the standard-capacity reality.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.3-star average across 215 ratings tells a consistent story. One verified buyer noted, "Prints perfectly every time, no streaks, no smudges." Another mentioned the ease of installation: "Took 30 seconds to swap, printer recognized it immediately." The frustration points are predictable — "Wish it lasted longer" appears repeatedly, and several reviewers flagged the price-per-page ratio versus third-party options.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the cost per page is roughly double what you'd pay with a high-yield XL cartridge or a reputable third-party brand. If EPSON offers a 202XL for your printer model, buy that instead — the math works out dramatically better over time. Second, the standard capacity means you're replacing it more frequently, which isn't just a cost issue; it's also an inconvenience. Third, the packaging has no obvious tamper-evident seal, so there's a small but real risk of receiving a counterfeit if you buy from an unauthorized seller. Stick with the listing you're reading or a trusted retailer.

**Who should buy this**

- **The occasional home printer**: You print a few pages a week. The low upfront cost and guaranteed compatibility make this a no-brainer.
- **The warranty-conscious buyer**: You don't want to risk ink-related issues with a third-party cartridge. Genuine EPSON keeps every claim valid.
- **The photo enthusiast**: If you also print photos with your Expression printer, Claria ink maintains consistent color profiles across all cartridges.

**Skip it if** you're a high-volume office user or you're comfortable with third-party inks. The XL version or a compatible brand will save you real money.

**FAQ**

*Will this work with my EPSON XP-4100?*  
No. The 202 cartridge fits specific XP-4200, XP-5200, and WorkForce WF-2800 series models. Check your printer's manual or EPSON's compatibility page before ordering.

*How many pages will I actually get?*  
The 120-page ISO rating assumes 5% coverage per page. Real-world yield varies — dense documents, graphics, or heavy printing will reduce that number.

*Is it worth paying more for genuine EPSON ink?*  
If you've had clogged printheads or faded output from third-party cartridges, yes. Genuine ink maintains print quality and protects your printer's internals. If you've had good luck with compatibles, you're paying a premium for peace of mind.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want genuine, reliable black ink without compatibility headaches and your print volume is light to moderate.

**Skip it if** you print heavily — the cost per page will frustrate you. Look for a high-yield XL option or a reputable third-party alternative.
