Let's cut through the noise about third-party ink. The OEGGOINK PG-260XL CL-261XL combo pack is one of those products that makes you question why Canon charges $70+ for OEM cartridges. At roughly half the price, this two-pack delivers the same stated page yields — 300 pages for the black PG-260XL and 220 for the tri-color CL-261XL. But as with any third-party ink, the question isn't whether it's cheaper. It's whether it's actually good.

**What's in the package**

You get two cartridges: one PG-260XL black and one CL-261XL tri-color (cyan, magenta, yellow). Both arrive in sealed foil packaging with a protective clip over the printhead and a visible ink window on the color unit — a nice touch for checking remaining levels. The cartridges are physically identical to Canon's OEM units, so installation takes about 30 seconds. No tape to peel, no contact pads to clean, just snap them in and run the printer's alignment cycle.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these in a Canon PIXMA TR8620 over three weeks, printing a mix of black-and-white text documents, color presentations, and 4×6 photo prints. The chipset registered immediately — no "incompatible cartridge" errors, which is the single biggest failure point for third-party ink. The printer recognized full ink levels from the start.

Black text output is crisp with good edge definition down to 8-point font. There's a slight warm cast to the black — more of a charcoal brown than pure neutral black — that becomes visible on bright white paper but disappears on standard multipurpose stock. Color documents are where this set shines. My test chart showed strong cyan and magenta output with accurate skin tones in photos. The tri-color cartridge handles gradient transitions smoothly without visible banding.

Photo quality on Canon glossy paper is surprisingly good for the price point. Colors are vibrant, with punchy blues and natural greens. Black-and-white photos show that warm tint more prominently, so if you print mostly monochrome photos, the OEM Canon cartridge remains the better choice.

**What buyers consistently say**

Scanning through the 210 ratings, the dominant theme is value. One verified buyer noted, *"I was skeptical about third-party ink but these worked flawlessly in my MG3620. **Where it falls short**

The warm black tint is the most consistent complaint across reviews. Users printing tax documents or grayscale reports noticed it immediately. Page yield variance is the second issue — some users report getting 250 pages from the black cartridge rather than the advertised 300, depending on print density and printer model. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's not the dead-on 300 pages Canon advertises either.

The tri-color cartridge also runs out unevenly — yellow tends to deplete first if you print presentations with yellow backgrounds. That's a limitation of all tri-color cartridges, not specific to OEGGOINK, but worth knowing before you buy.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home users printing school projects, coupons, and occasional photos** — the cost savings justify the slight color compromise
- **Small offices with moderate volume (under 500 pages/month)** — the per-page cost drops to roughly 3 cents for black
- **Budget-conscious Canon PIXMA owners** who don't want to pay OEM prices for a printer that cost less than $100

**Skip it if** you're a photographer printing gallery-quality black-and-white prints, or if print quality consistency across every batch matters more than saving $30. For those cases, stick with Canon OEM.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these void my Canon printer warranty?**
A: In the US, the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act prevents manufacturers from voiding warranties solely for using third-party consumables — unless they can prove the ink caused the damage. That said, Canon's warranty does exclude damage from non-genuine ink. In practice, printhead clogging is the main risk, and OEGGOINK's ink formulation appears well-matched to Canon's printhead thermal requirements.

**Q: Do I need to reset anything after installing?**
A: No. The chips on these cartridges communicate with the printer automatically. You may need to run the alignment cycle once, but that's standard for any new cartridge.

**Q: How do these compare to Canon's XL cartridges?**
A: Same page yield specs, roughly 45–55% lower cost. Print quality is 90–95% of OEM — the main difference is the warm black tint. For color printing, you'll struggle to tell them apart.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need reliable, affordable ink for everyday printing and can tolerate a slightly warm black tone. The chipset compatibility alone puts this ahead of most third-party options.

**Skip it if** you're a photo purist or print mostly black-and-white documents where neutral black matters.

For the price, this is a solid 4.2-star product — genuinely good value with honest limitations. It won't replace OEM for everyone, but for most home and small-office users, it's the smarter buy.
