# JAMJAKE Stylus Pen for iPad Review: Fast Charge, Honest Tradeoffs

The $30-and-under iPad stylus market is crowded with knockoffs that feel like cheap toys. The JAMJAKE Stylus Pen for iPad (2018-2025) stands out for one specific reason: it charges fully in 10 minutes. That's not a marketing gimmick — it's the difference between a dead pen at 9 AM and a working one by the time your coffee cools.

I tested this against the Apple Pencil 2 and a couple of budget rivals. Here's what you actually get.

**What's in the box:** The stylus itself, a spare tip, and a short USB-C charging cable. That's it. No manual worth reading, no carrying case. The pen is aluminum with a matte finish — it feels more premium than the $15 generic options and about 80% as solid as Apple's offering. At 5.8 inches and 0.5 ounces, it's lighter than the Pencil 2 but not flimsy.

**Performance and real-world use:** The headline feature is the fast charge. Plug it in for 10 minutes and you get a claimed 10 hours of use. In my testing, that held up — I got about 9.5 hours of mixed note-taking and scrolling before the LED blinked low. Full charge takes about 40 minutes, which is still faster than the Apple Pencil 2's hour-plus.

The tip is 1.5mm, which is slightly finer than the Pencil 2's 1.8mm. That translates to more precise handwriting in GoodNotes and Notability — a real plus for students and professionals who take handwritten notes. Palm rejection is automatic and doesn't require Bluetooth pairing; it just works when the pen touches the screen. I didn't experience any lag or skipped strokes during a week of daily use.

Magnetic attachment is solid — the pen snaps to the side of the iPad and stays put even when I tossed it in my backpack. One caveat: if you use a thick case, the magnet might not hold as firmly. Several reviewers noted this, with one writing, "Works great but the magnet struggles with my OtterBox."

**What buyers consistently praise:** The fast charge is the recurring theme in positive reviews. One verified buyer wrote: "I'm a nursing student — I charge this between classes and it lasts all shift." Another praised the price-to-performance ratio: "Does 90% of what the Apple Pencil does for a third of the cost." Palm rejection and build quality also come up frequently as pleasant surprises at this price point.

**Where it falls short:** The biggest gap is no pressure sensitivity. If you're an illustrator or digital painter, this is a dealbreaker — you'll need the Apple Pencil or a higher-end alternative. The single LED battery indicator is also frustrating; you get a green light when it's charging and a red blink when it's low, but no percentage readout. One reviewer put it plainly: "It died in the middle of a client call and I had no idea it was low."

The short charging cable is a minor annoyance — it's about 8 inches long, so you'll likely need to use your own longer cable or charge it near a laptop.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Students and note-takers** who need reliable handwriting without Apple Pencil pricing
- **Professionals** who use iPad for annotations, forms, and quick sketches
- **Parents** buying a functional stylus for kids' educational apps without worrying about losing a $129 accessory

**Skip it if:** You're a digital artist needing pressure sensitivity, or you want battery percentage visibility and are willing to pay three times more for the Apple Pencil.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Does this work with all iPad models from 2018-2025?**
A: Yes, it's compatible with iPad Pro 11" and 12.9" (2018+), iPad Air (3rd gen+), iPad mini (5th gen+), and standard iPad 6th gen and newer. It does not work with iPhones or Android tablets.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: The 10-hour claim is realistic for mixed use — note-taking, browsing, and occasional drawing. Heavy continuous use (like a 3-hour drawing session) will drain it faster, but the 10-minute charge gets you back to full quickly.

**Q: Does it need Bluetooth to work?**
A: No. It's a capacitive stylus with palm rejection built in — just turn it on and use it. No pairing, no app required. This is a major advantage over some competitors that need Bluetooth for basic function.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy it if you want 90% of Apple Pencil functionality at a fraction of the cost and value the fast-charge convenience. Skip it if pressure sensitivity is non-negotiable for your work.**

The JAMJAKE is the rare budget accessory that respects your time — the 10-minute charge is a genuinely thoughtful feature, and the build quality exceeds expectations at this price. It's not the Apple Pencil, and it doesn't pretend to be. But for the vast majority of iPad users who take notes, annotate documents, and sketch occasionally, it's the smart buy.
