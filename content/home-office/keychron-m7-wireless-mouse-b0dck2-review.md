The Keychron M7 isn't trying to be another generic wireless office mouse. It's a lightweight (67g) ambidextrous performer that straddles the line between productivity tool and budget gaming peripheral. After digging through 215 Amazon ratings and the spec sheet, here's what actually matters if you're thinking about buying one.

**What you get in the box**

Keychron keeps it minimal: the M7 mouse, a 2.4GHz USB-C dongle, a USB-C to USB-A charging cable (braided, decent quality), and a quick-start guide. No carrying case, no extra feet. The dongle stores magnetically inside the mouse body — a small touch that prevents the classic "lost the dongle" problem. The mouse itself measures about 4.9 x 2.5 x 1.5 inches, which puts it in the medium-small range. If you've got larger hands, you'll likely need a claw or fingertip grip; palm-grippers should look elsewhere.

**Performance and real-world use**

The headline feature is the 4000Hz polling rate in 2.4GHz mode. For context, most office mice run at 125-1000Hz. At 4000Hz, cursor movement feels immediate — no perceptible lag when you're flying across a 4K monitor or doing precise spreadsheet selections. The PixArt PAW3395 sensor is genuinely high-end, tracking up to 26,000 DPI with zero acceleration issues. It's overkill for email, but if you occasionally jump into a game or do photo editing, it handles everything smoothly.

Bluetooth mode caps at 1000Hz, which is fine for everyday work. Battery life is where things get complicated. Keychron claims 70 hours, but that's at 1000Hz. At 4000Hz, real-world reports put it closer to 25-30 hours. One reviewer noted, "I got about two and a half days of full workdays before the low-battery warning popped up." If you're a heavy user, you'll be charging weekly. The USB-C port is front-mounted, so you can keep working while it charges — a small but appreciated design choice.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The hot-swappable Gateron switches are a standout. You can pull the switches out with the included tool and swap in different click feel — heavier, lighter, clickier — without any soldering. That's rare at this price point. Multiple reviewers called this out. One wrote: "Being able to change the click feel is genius. I put in silent switches and now I can use it in open offices without annoying anyone."

Build quality earns consistent praise too. The magnesium alloy shell feels rigid, no creaking or flex when you grip it hard. The PTFE feet glide smoothly on both cloth and hard surfaces. The scroll wheel has defined detents without being stiff — good for long document scrolling.

**Where it falls short**

The software situation is the biggest frustration. Keychron's configuration app works, but it's basic. There's no onboard memory, so if you remap buttons or adjust DPI steps, those settings only work while the software runs in the background. Close the app and you're back to defaults. That's a genuine annoyance for anyone who wants a set-and-forget setup.

The 67g weight is a double-edged sword. It's featherlight for gaming, but some office users find it too light for precise work. One reviewer complained: "It moves too easily. The cable drag on my wired mouse actually helped me make finer cursor adjustments." If you prefer some heft, this isn't the mouse for you.

**Who should buy this**

The M7 fits three buyer profiles: (1) the hybrid worker who games after hours and wants one mouse for both, (2) the tinkerer who enjoys customizing click feel and DPI settings, and (3) anyone with small-to-medium hands who's tired of oversized ergonomic bricks. Skip it if you need a heavy, palm-filling mouse, rely on custom button profiles, or want a true multi-week battery life without charging.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Can I use it while charging?* Yes. The USB-C cable works as a wired connection, so you can keep working while the battery tops up.

*Does it work with Mac and Linux?* Yes, for basic functions. The software is Windows and macOS only, but standard buttons and scrolling work on Linux via Bluetooth or the dongle.

*Are the switches really swappable?* Yes. Gateron optical switches pop out with the included puller tool. Third-party compatible switches are available, though stick with known brands to avoid fit issues.

**The verdict**

The Keychron M7 is a solid 4.2-star product — a capable, well-built mouse that delivers genuine performance where it counts. The polling rate, sensor, and hot-swappable switches justify the price. But the weak software and battery tradeoffs keep it from being a slam dunk.

**Buy it if** you want a lightweight, high-performance mouse for work and play without paying flagship prices.

**Skip it if** you need all-day battery endurance or deep customization without software running in the background.
