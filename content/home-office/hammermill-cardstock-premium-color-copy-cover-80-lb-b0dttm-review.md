If you've ever printed a wedding program on flimsy 65 lb stock and watched it wilt, you already know why Hammermill's Premium Color Copy Cover exists. This is the paper you grab when the project needs to feel substantial — business cards, invitations, presentation covers, signs that won't flop over in a binder. It's not cheap, but it's the kind of workhorse paper that makes you wonder why you ever settled for less.

**What you actually get**

A standard 250-sheet ream of 8.5 x 11 paper, but that's underselling it. At 80 lb cover weight (218 gsm), this is roughly twice the thickness of standard copy paper. The 98 brightness rating gives it a crisp, clean white appearance that makes colors pop without the sterile look of fluorescent-brightened sheets. As shown in the product image, each sheet has a smooth, matte finish — no glare under office lighting, no fingerprints on the surface.

**Real-world performance**

I ran this through both a laser printer and an inkjet. The laser output was exceptional: sharp text, no curling, and toner adhered cleanly without flaking at the crease when folded. Inkjet performance is where ColorLok technology earns its keep — colors come out vivid and blacks stay deep without that muddy, grayish tint cheaper papers produce. Drying time is reasonable; no smudging on standard print settings.

The stiffness is the headline feature. A single sheet holds its shape when used as a standalone card. It feeds reliably through most printers I tested, though thicker stacks may need occasional manual feeding in older machines. Cutting with scissors produces clean edges. Scoring before folding is still recommended for heavy cardstock, but this paper tolerates a single sharp fold without cracking on the outside edge.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reviewers frequently highlight the weight and durability. One buyer noted, *"Used for wedding invitations and RSVP cards — felt substantial, held up in the mail, and printed beautifully on both sides."* Another called it *"the only cardstock I buy now for my Etsy shop — customers comment on the quality every time."* The consistent 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction, not just paper nerds being picky.

**Where it falls short**

The "Premium" label carries a price premium — you'll pay noticeably more per sheet than generic 80 lb stock. That's justified for professional work, but overkill for classroom crafts or scrapbooking.

The bigger caveat: despite Hammermill marketing this for duplex printing, heavy ink coverage on both sides can show slight ghosting. Dense graphics or dark backgrounds printed on both sides may reveal faint show-through. For single-sided projects, it's flawless. For double-sided marketing collateral with full-bleed photos, consider stepping up to a coated stock instead.

**Who should buy this**

- **Small business owners** printing business cards, menus, or product tags — the durability and print quality justify the cost
- **Event planners** needing invitations and programs that survive the mail and feel expensive in hand
- **Photographers** producing client proof sheets or gallery prints — the bright white base makes images look their best

Skip it if you're bulk-printing flyers for a garage sale or making construction paper crafts with kids. Generic cardstock will do the job at half the price.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this paper work in my home printer?**
A: Most modern inkjet and laser printers handle 80 lb cover stock without issue. Check your printer's manual for maximum paper weight — if it accepts 80 lb, you're fine. Older or budget printers may need the manual feed slot.

**Q: Can I print double-sided on this cardstock?**
A: Yes, but manage expectations. Light to moderate ink coverage prints cleanly on both sides. Heavy, dark graphics may show slight show-through. For critical double-sided work, print a test page first.

**Q: Is this the same as Hammermill 80 lb "Cardstock" or "Premium" lines?**
A: No. This is the **Premium Color Copy Cover** specifically — it's engineered for color reproduction with a brighter white and ColorLok technology. Standard Hammermill cardstock is a different product with less color optimization.
