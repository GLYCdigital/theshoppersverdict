**Bottom line up front:** The HP Series 5 527sh is the office monitor that most budget shoppers should buy before they overpay for a "premium" 60Hz panel. It delivers a 100Hz refresh rate, accurate IPS colors, and an ergonomic stand at a price that undercuts most 27-inch competitors. It's not a creative professional's dream — the 1080p resolution is the obvious compromise — but for productivity, hybrid work, and casual use, it's a genuinely smart purchase.

## What you actually get

The box includes the monitor, the height-adjustable stand base, a DisplayPort cable, and an HDMI cable. No USB-C, no USB hub pass-through, no frills. The stand attaches via a standard 100x100mm VESA mount if you want to swap it for an arm later. The panel itself is slim — about 0.8 inches at the thinnest point — though the stand footprint is deeper than I'd like at roughly 9.5 inches front-to-back.

## Real-world performance

The headline feature is the 100Hz refresh rate. In a 27-inch office panel, this matters more than you'd think. Scrolling through long documents, moving between browser tabs, and even dragging windows around feels noticeably more fluid than the 60Hz standard. If you're coming from a laptop that runs at 120Hz, you won't feel a downgrade. If you're coming from a 60Hz desktop, the difference is immediate and pleasant.

Color accuracy is solid for the class. The IPS panel hits roughly 99% sRGB coverage out of the box, which means spreadsheet charts and website colors look correct without calibration. Contrast sits at a typical 1000:1 for IPS — blacks are grayish in a dark room, but that's the IPS tradeoff across the board at this price.

The 100Hz refresh also gives you a slight gaming edge, though this isn't a gaming monitor. No FreeSync Premium, no 1ms response time — it's a 5ms panel with standard adaptive sync support that'll handle light esports titles fine but isn't chasing competitive frames.

## What buyers consistently praise

The ergonomic stand is the sleeper feature. Multiple reviewers note it's the reason they chose this over cheaper alternatives. One buyer said, *"I was going to buy a separate monitor arm, but the stand that came with this is actually good — height adjustment is smooth and it stays put."* Another reviewer highlighted the refresh rate: *"Scrolling through code all day at 100Hz is genuinely easier on the eyes than my old 60Hz monitor."* The 3-sided micro-edge bezel design also gets consistent mentions for looking more premium than the price suggests.

## Where it falls short

Let's be direct about the 1080p resolution. On a 27-inch screen, you're getting about 81 pixels per inch. Text is readable and fine for most work, but if you're coming from a 1440p monitor or a high-DPI laptop, you will notice slightly softer fonts and less screen real estate. A 27-inch 1440p panel costs $100-150 more, so this is a deliberate budget tradeoff, not a defect.

The built-in speakers are genuinely poor. At 2 watts per channel, they're fine for system sounds but will frustrate you if you try to watch a video or join a call without headphones. Plan on external speakers or a headset.

Connectivity is limited: one HDMI 2.0 port, one DisplayPort 1.2, and a headphone jack. No USB-C, no USB hub, no daisy-chaining. If you're on a MacBook or a modern ultrabook, you'll need an adapter.

## Who should buy this

- **Hybrid office workers** who sit at a desk for 8+ hours and want a comfortable, adjustable monitor with smooth scrolling.
- **Budget-conscious upgraders** moving from a 24-inch 60Hz panel who want a bigger screen without the 1440p price premium.
- **Students or home office setups** running a desktop or laptop that already has the right cables.
- **Anyone who needs a second monitor** for productivity and prioritizes ergonomics over pixel density.

Skip it if you're a photo/video editor needing accurate color at 1440p, or if you're a serious gamer needing high refresh with low latency.

## FAQ

**Is 1080p on 27 inches too blurry?**
For most users, no. Text is slightly less crisp than 1440p, but it's perfectly usable. If you work with lots of dense text or small UI elements, consider the 1440p upgrade. For general web, office apps, and video, you'll adapt within a day.

**Does the 100Hz refresh rate work over HDMI?**
Yes, but you'll get the full 100Hz only over DisplayPort or HDMI. Check your laptop or GPU's HDMI version — some older ports cap at 60Hz. The included DisplayPort cable is your safest bet for 100Hz.

**Can I mount this on a monitor arm?**
Yes. It uses a standard 100x100mm VESA mount. The included stand is good, but the arm is an easy upgrade if you need more desk space.
