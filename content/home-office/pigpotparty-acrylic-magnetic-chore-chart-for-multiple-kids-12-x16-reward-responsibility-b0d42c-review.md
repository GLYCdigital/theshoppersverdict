The PigPotParty Acrylic Magnetic Chore Chart isn't the first chore chart you'll buy — it's the one you buy after the paper printable fell apart and the whiteboard version got scratched beyond recognition. At 12x16 inches, it's a solid upgrade that actually looks intentional on a kitchen wall or home office command center, unlike the flimsy laminated charts that curl at the corners.

**What's actually in the box**

The package includes the acrylic board itself, 5 dry erase markers with eraser caps, and a substantial bag of magnets. You're looking at over 60 pieces total — color-coded task magnets for different kids, reward magnets, and blank ones you can write on. The board is genuinely thick, not the thin acrylic that flexes when you press a marker to it. There are pre-drilled corner holes with screws and anchors included, though you'll want to be careful mounting it on drywall.

**Real-world performance**

Here's where this chart separates itself from the cheap stuff. The acrylic surface erases completely clean — no ghosting, no residue, no gray haze building up after a week of daily checkmarks. The magnetic backing holds task magnets firmly; a 4-year-old can slap a "Make Bed" magnet on without knocking the whole row out of alignment. The layout has space for multiple kids side by side, with columns for each child and rows for tasks — the design is intuitive enough that kids can navigate it without constant parental translation.

One detail that matters: the board is clear acrylic, which means it mounts over whatever wall color you have and blends in. It doesn't scream "kiddie product" the way bright plastic charts do. Parents in home offices have noted it works double-duty as a family command center.

**Where it falls short**

The mounting hardware is the weak point. The screws and anchors work, but the chart hangs flush against the wall with no standoff — if you load it with heavy magnets, the bottom edge can tilt forward slightly over time. Plan to mount it with the screws snug, not just finger-tight.

The included markers are serviceable but thin-tip and prone to drying if kids leave caps loose. Budget for replacement dry erase markers — it's a minor recurring cost, not a dealbreaker.
