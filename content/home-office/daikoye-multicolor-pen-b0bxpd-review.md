Let’s be honest: most multicolor pens are gimmicks. They’re either built like a toy, write like a dried-out marker, or both. The DAIKOYE Multicolor Pen, however, is trying something different. With eight retractable colors packed into a single aluminum barrel, it’s aiming to be the only pen you need on your desk. Does it pull that off? Mostly, yes — with a few caveats worth knowing before you click "add to cart."

## What You Actually Get

The pen ships in a simple box with the pen itself and a spare ink refill set — that’s eight additional cartridges covering the same color lineup. The body is machined aluminum with a knurled grip section, which gives it a premium feel that belies its price point. As shown in the product image, the color indicators sit in a window near the clip, showing you exactly which shade is active before you write.

Each of the 0.7mm ballpoint tips is retractable via a side-mounted push button. Click once for black, again for blue, red, green, orange, purple, pink, and teal. The mechanism cycles through all eight, and the window indicator flips to match. It weighs about 1.2 ounces — not featherweight, but substantial enough to feel deliberate in hand.

## Build Quality and Real-World Writing

The aluminum body is the standout feature here. It’s not painted plastic pretending to be metal; it’s genuinely cold-to-the-touch aluminum that resists scratches and fingerprints. The knurled grip is aggressive enough to keep your fingers planted during a three-hour study session without feeling like sandpaper.

Writing performance is solid for a ballpoint. The 0.7mm tip lays down consistent, skip-free lines on standard copy paper. I tested it on cheap printer paper, a Moleskine notebook, and a glossy planner — no bleed-through and only faint ghosting on the thinnest stock. Dry time is quick, so lefties won’t smear their work.

The color range is genuinely useful. The black and blue handle daily notes; red and green work for grading or marking up documents; teal and purple add visual hierarchy to bullet journals without needing a separate set of pens.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 220 Amazon ratings, the most repeated compliments center on the color selection and build. One reviewer writes, *"I've bought multicolor pens before and they always feel cheap. This one actually feels like a real pen."* Another notes, *"The colors are vibrant and the mechanism hasn't jammed in three months of daily use."* That durability point matters — cheap multicolor pens often seize up or skip colors within weeks.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest issue is the barrel thickness. At roughly 12mm in diameter, this is a chunky pen. If you have smaller hands or prefer slim writing instruments, you’ll find it fatiguing after extended writing. It’s better suited for quick notes and annotation than marathon journaling.

The color-click mechanism also demands a firm, deliberate press. Push too softly and you might cycle past your intended color, forcing an extra click to loop back around. It’s a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you’re someone who flicks pens absentmindedly.

One other note: the ink is standard ballpoint quality. It’s reliable and waterproof, but if you’re expecting the smoothness of a gel pen or the saturation of a rollerball, you’ll be underwhelmed. This is a workhorse, not a luxury writer.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Students and note-takers** who color-code lecture notes and want one tool instead of five
- **Professionals** who annotate documents and need red, blue, and green at hand without clutter
- **Bullet journalers** who want variety without carrying a pen case the size of a lunchbox

Skip it if you need ultra-smooth gel writing, prefer slim pens, or only ever use black ink.

A: The pen comes with a full set of eight spare cartridges in the box. Replacement refills are available on Amazon, though they’re often sold as multi-packs. A single cartridge lasts roughly 800 meters of writing, so you won’t need to replace them often.

**Q: Does the color indicator window actually match the ink?**
A: Yes, it does. The mechanism uses a rotating collar that lines up the correct color chip with the window. In my testing, the indicator was accurate every time — a feature cheaper pens often get wrong.

**Q: Will it fit in a standard pencil case or pocket?**
A: The pen is about 5.7 inches long with a pocket clip, so it fits in most shirt pockets and standard pencil cases. The clip is metal and spring-loaded, so it won’t snap off after a month of clipping to a notebook.
