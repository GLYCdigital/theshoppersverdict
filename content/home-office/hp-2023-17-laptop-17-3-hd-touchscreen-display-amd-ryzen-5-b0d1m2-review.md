Big laptops are a niche. Most people want 14 or 15 inches because they're portable enough to carry. The HP 2023 17-inch with an AMD Ryzen 5 processor is not for those people. This is for someone who wants a desktop replacement that occasionally leaves the house. And for that buyer, it delivers where it matters.

Let me be clear about what you're getting. The box contains the laptop itself, a 45W AC adapter, and the standard HP documentation — no stylus, no sleeve, no surprises. The star is the 17.3-inch HD+ touchscreen with a 1600x900 resolution. That's the first thing you need to understand.

**The display is the whole story here.** At 17.3 inches, 1600x900 means you're getting roughly 106 pixels per inch. Compare that to a 15.6-inch 1080p panel at 141 PPI, and the difference is obvious. Text has a slight fuzziness, especially if you're coming from a newer laptop. But the touch response is excellent — accurate, no lag, and the hinge holds firm when you're tapping the screen. For spreadsheets, documents, and side-by-side browser windows, the physical size wins. One Amazon buyer summed it up: "I can actually see my Excel columns without squinting, and having two windows open side by side is a game changer."

**Performance is where the Ryzen 5 earns its keep.** The AMD Ryzen 5 7530U is a 6-core, 12-thread chip that handles everyday office workloads with ease. I ran 15 Chrome tabs, a Spotify stream, and a 1080p YouTube video simultaneously — no stuttering, no thermal throttling, and the fans stayed quiet. It's paired with 8GB of RAM (soldered, so upgrade now or never) and a 256GB NVMe SSD. Boot times are snappy at around 10 seconds. For light photo editing in something like Canva or GIMP, it's fine. For 4K video editing or serious gaming, look elsewhere.

**The keyboard and build are a mixed bag.** The full-size keyboard with a dedicated number pad is genuinely comfortable — key travel is decent, and the layout is logical for number crunching. The trackpad is responsive and supports Windows Precision drivers. But the chassis is all plastic, and there's noticeable flex around the keyboard deck if you press firmly. It's not creaky, but it doesn't feel like a $700 machine. It feels like a $500 machine that got a bigger screen and a better processor.

**What buyers consistently praise:**

- Battery life: Most users report 7-8 hours of mixed office use. That's solid for a 17-inch panel.
- Touchscreen utility: "I use the touchscreen more than I expected on a laptop this size. Scrolling and zooming feels natural."
- Value for money: Multiple reviewers note they compared it against Dell and Lenovo equivalents and got more screen for less cash.

**Where it falls short — honestly:**

The 72% NTSC color gamut is the biggest compromise. Colors look muted, and if you do any visual work, you'll notice it immediately. Also, the 8GB soldered RAM is a real limitation — you can't add more later. And the 256GB SSD fills up fast; a 64GB microSD card in the slot is almost mandatory for media files.

One more thing: the webcam is 720p and mediocre, but it has a physical privacy shutter, which I appreciate. The single USB-C port is also a drag — you'll want a hub or dongle for anything beyond a mouse.

**Who should buy this:**

- Office workers who primarily work at a desk and want a big screen without buying a separate monitor
- Students who need a number pad for accounting or engineering coursework
- Anyone with vision issues who benefits from larger text and icons
- Budget-conscious buyers who want a 17-inch touchscreen without paying premium prices

**Skip it if:**

- You travel frequently — this thing is heavy at nearly 5 pounds, and the charger adds bulk
- You're a creative professional — the color accuracy isn't there
- You need more than 8GB RAM for future-proofing

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I upgrade the RAM or storage?**
A: RAM is soldered — 8GB is your ceiling. The SSD is replaceable, but it's a 2280 M.2 drive, so you'll need to clone or reinstall Windows.

**Q: Is this good for gaming?**
A: No. The integrated Radeon graphics handle older titles at low settings, but don't expect to play anything released in the last few years.

**Q: Does the touchscreen support a stylus?**
A: It's a standard capacitive touchscreen — any passive stylus works, but there's no active pen support, so no pressure sensitivity.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if:** You want the largest touchscreen your money can buy, and you value screen real estate over portability and pixel density.

**Skip it if:** You need accurate colors, plan to keep it for more than three years, or carry your laptop daily.

The HP 2023 17-inch is a solid office workhorse that knows exactly what it is: a big-screen productivity machine at a reasonable price. It's not flashy, it's not perfect, but for the right buyer, it's a genuinely smart purchase.
