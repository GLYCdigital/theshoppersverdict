## Does the Astrobrights Mega Collection actually live up to the hype?

With a 4.9-star average across 245 ratings, this cardstock pack has clearly won over a lot of buyers. But here's the thing about paper products: specs on the box don't tell you how it feels in your hands, how it handles ink, or whether those colors look as electric in person as they do on screen. I've put a full pack through real-world testing — printing, cutting, folding, and general abuse — and here's what you need to know before adding it to your cart.

## What's Actually in the Box

The "Mega Collection" in the Brilliant assortment gives you 320 sheets total — 64 sheets each of five colors: **Raspberry, Gamma Green, Martian Green, Lunar Blue, and Solar Yellow**. All are 8.5" x 11", standard US letter size. The paper is rated at 65 lb cover weight, which puts it in that sweet spot between flimsy printer paper and rigid cardstock.

You're getting a resealable poly bag, which sounds minor until you've had a half-used pack of paper spilling across your craft room. It's a small detail that shows Astrobrights actually thought about how people store this stuff.

## Real-World Performance: Printing, Cutting, Folding

Here's where this cardstock earns its reputation. I ran sheets through a standard inkjet printer with double-sided printing, and the colors held up on both sides with zero bleed-through. Text was crisp, and even full-bleed color images didn't warp or buckle the paper — something cheaper 65 lb stocks often do.

The fold test matters more than most people realize. Fold a sheet in half and it creases cleanly without cracking along the fold line. That's the difference between a birthday card that looks handmade and one that looks homemade. This paper nails it.

Cutting is where you need to adjust expectations. If you're using a Cricut or Silhouette for intricate cuts, this will work — but it's not a heavy-duty craft board. Thin detailed cuts can tear if your blade is dull. For straight cuts with a paper cutter or scissors, it's perfectly fine.

As one verified buyer put it: *"The colors are so much more vivid in person than they look on screen. I've bought cheaper cardstock before and always regretted the washed-out results. This is the real deal."*

Another reviewer echoed that sentiment: *"Perfect for my son's school projects. The paper is thick enough that markers don't bleed through, but it still goes through our printer without jamming."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. First, you're locked into just five colors with this specific assortment. If you need a wider palette, you're buying multiple packs — and the colors don't necessarily coordinate across different Astrobrights collections.

Second, despite being called "cardstock," this is closer to a heavy printer paper. It's 65 lb, but true cardstock typically starts at 80 lb and goes up from there. For heavy embossing, letterpress, or architectural models that need rigidity, this isn't the right choice. It's more of a premium presentation paper than a craft-grade board.

Third, the colors are neon-bright. That's the point of the Brilliant line, but if you're looking for muted, sophisticated tones for wedding invitations or professional materials, these will scream at your guests. Choose the "Sublime" or "Vintage" assortments instead.

## Who Should Buy This

This pack is ideal for:
- **Teachers** creating classroom materials that need to pop
- **Small business owners** making flyers, signs, or menu inserts
- **Parents** tackling school projects that require durability and visual appeal
- **DIY crafters** who need a reliable everyday colored stock for cards and scrapbooking

Skip it if you need archival-grade board, subtle color palettes, or a true heavyweight cardstock for professional print work.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Will this work in a laser printer?**
Yes, but test a single sheet first. The coating handles heat fine, but some laser printers run hot enough to slightly curl 65 lb paper. Most users report no issues.

**Q: Is this paper double-sided?**
Yes — both sides are colored, and the color is consistent on both faces. You can use either side without noticing a difference.

**Q: How does it compare to Astrobrights 24 lb printer paper?**
Night and day. The 24 lb is flimsy office paper that's good for memos. This is 65 lb cover stock — nearly three times the thickness. It has a substantial feel and stands up to handling.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, vibrant colored cardstock that prints beautifully, folds cleanly, and won't break the bank. The 4.9-star rating is earned — this is consistently good paper.

**Skip it if** you need true heavyweight cardstock for embossing or intricate die-cutting, or if you want a wider color selection in a single pack.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The color payoff and print quality are genuinely excellent. The limited color count and the "more like heavy paper than cardstock" reality keep it from a perfect score, but for everyday creative projects, this is the pack I'd reach for first.
