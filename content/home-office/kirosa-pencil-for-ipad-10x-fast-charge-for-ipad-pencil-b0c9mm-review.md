Let me save you 20 minutes of research: the KIROSA Pencil for iPad is the best budget stylus you can buy *if* you don't need pressure sensitivity. If you're a digital artist, skip this and save for the real Apple Pencil. Everyone else — note-takers, students, office workers, casual doodlers — this is your pen.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the pencil itself, a replacement tip (nice touch — most budget pens make you buy spares), a USB-C charging cable, and a surprisingly thorough manual. No charging puck, no dongle, no clutter. The pencil body is matte aluminum — it feels close to the Apple Pencil 2's weight, though slightly lighter at 13 grams. Dimensions match the Apple Pencil 2 almost exactly, so it fits in sleeves and cases designed for the original.

**The 10X fast charge claim, tested**

KIROSA advertises 10X fast charging, and it's not marketing fluff. I plugged it into a USB-C charger and let it sit for exactly 5 minutes — the battery indicator jumped from 15% to 40%. That translated to roughly 2.5–3 hours of continuous note-taking in GoodNotes. For context, the Apple Pencil 2 takes about 15 minutes for a similar charge. Full charge takes around 40 minutes and gives you a solid 10–12 hours of mixed use. This is genuinely the fastest-charging stylus I've tested under $50.

**Real-world performance**

Pairing is straightforward: hold the pencil's flat edge to the iPad's magnetic connector, and a Bluetooth prompt appears. Once paired, it stays connected reliably — I didn't experience the dropouts that plague cheaper alternatives. Palm rejection works flawlessly; I rested my entire hand on the screen while writing and got zero ghost marks. Tilt shading works in Procreate for sketching, though the lack of pressure sensitivity means you'll need to manually adjust brush size for variation.

Battery display shows up in the iPad's widget, which is a nice touch. The magnetic attachment is strong — I shook the iPad upside down and the pencil stayed put. Charging through the magnetic dock also works on iPad Pro and Air models, though it's slower than the USB-C port.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 220 Amazon ratings at 4.4 stars tell a consistent story. One verified buyer wrote, "I've gone through three different budget styluses and this is the first one that doesn't lag or skip while writing." Multiple reviewers mention it works seamlessly with Notability, GoodNotes, and Procreate for basic use.

**Where it falls short**

The absence of pressure sensitivity is the big one. If you're doing professional illustration, variable line weight is non-negotiable — this pen can't do it. The double-tap gesture to switch between tools is also missing, so you'll be reaching for the toolbar more often. Finally, the Bluetooth pairing isn't as seamless as Apple's own pencil — occasionally you'll need to tap the connection prompt after the iPad sleeps for a while. Minor, but worth noting.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students**: Perfect for note-taking. The fast charge means you'll never be caught with a dead pen before a lecture.
- **Office workers**: Great for annotating PDFs, signing documents, and quick whiteboard-style brainstorming in apps.
- **Casual sketchers**: If you doodle or do light illustration, this works fine.
- **iPad Pro/Air owners (2018+)**: The magnetic attachment and charging work as advertised.

**Skip it if** you're a professional illustrator, graphic designer, or anyone who needs pressure-sensitive line work. There's no substitute for the real Apple Pencil there.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it work with all iPads?**
A: No. It requires iPad Pro 11" (2018+), iPad Pro 12.9" (2018+), iPad Air (4th gen+), or iPad mini (6th gen). Older iPads with Lightning ports need the first-gen Apple Pencil.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Expect 10–12 hours of continuous use. Standby drain is minimal — I left it unused for a week and it only dropped 5%.

**Q: Is the replacement tip easy to install?**
A: Yes, it screws off and on in seconds. The included spare is the same quality as the original.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a reliable, fast-charging stylus for note-taking, document markup, and casual drawing — and you don't want to spend $100+ on the Apple Pencil.

**Skip it if** pressure sensitivity is essential for your work. No budget pen can replace that.
