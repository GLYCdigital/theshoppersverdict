## The Digital Lock That Actually Keeps Keys Secure

If you're still using a flimsy lockbox or a drawer stuffed with unlabeled keys, the AdirOffice 40 Keys Cabinet is the upgrade you didn't know you needed. This isn't a decorative piece — it's a serious security tool for anyone managing multiple properties, a small business, or a facility with shared access. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the steel cabinet itself (roughly 16 x 13 x 4 inches), two physical override keys, four AA batteries, and mounting hardware. The cabinet holds 40 keys on numbered hooks inside, with a small shelf at the bottom for larger items like fobs or access cards. The front door is a solid steel panel with a digital keypad and a key slot hidden behind a sliding cover.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The steel body feels genuinely sturdy — this isn't a hollow tin box. The powder-coated finish resists fingerprints and minor scuffs, which matters when it's mounted in a hallway or behind a front desk. The door closes with a satisfying thunk, and the latch engages cleanly.

Setting the PIN code takes about 30 seconds: open the door, press the program button inside, enter your code, confirm, done. You can use 4-8 digits, which is flexible enough for a simple code or something more complex. The override keys are a lifesaver if the batteries die or you forget the code — and trust me, you'll want them stored somewhere safe.

One thing that surprised me: the key hooks are welded in place, not glued. That means no wobbling when you yank a key off, and the numbered labels are clearly printed and easy to read even in dim light.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The overwhelming theme in customer feedback is *peace of mind*. One reviewer put it well: "Finally stopped losing keys in the office. The digital lock means I don't have to hunt down a physical key to get to the keys." Another noted, "I manage 12 rental units and this has cut my key-related chaos in half."

People also consistently mention how easy it is to reprogram when staff changes. A property manager wrote: "When we fired a contractor, I just changed the code. No more re-keying everything."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the drawbacks. The digital keypad is the weakest link — it's functional but feels a bit cheap. The buttons are stiff and the backlight is dim, so you'll fumble in a dark closet. Not a dealbreaker, but noticeable.

The lack of pre-drilled wall-mounting holes is a genuine annoyance. You'll need to mark, drill, and use the included anchors yourself. If you're mounting on drywall, that's fine — but if you're dealing with concrete or brick, budget extra time and a masonry bit.

Battery life is also shorter than I'd like. With regular use (5-10 opens a day), you're looking at 4-6 months before the low-battery warning flashes. It's not terrible, but it means keeping spares nearby is non-negotiable.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Property managers** juggling keys for multiple units — the 40-hook capacity is ideal
- **Small business owners** needing to control access to supply closets or equipment rooms

## FAQ

**Q: Can the PIN code be changed if an employee leaves?**
A: Yes. Open the door, press the program button inside, enter the current code, then the new code. Takes under a minute. No tools needed.

**Q: Does it come with a mounting template?**
A: No template included. You'll need to measure and mark the hole positions yourself. The included anchors work for drywall, but you'll need your own hardware for concrete.

**Q: What happens if the batteries die completely?**
A: The override key slot lets you open the door mechanically. There's also a 9V battery terminal on the outside for emergency power, so you can key in the code even with dead internal batteries.
