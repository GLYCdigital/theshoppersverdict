**Are these the cartridges that finally make your HP Envy affordable to run? Let's find out.**

If you own an HP Envy 5540 or 7640, you already know the dirty secret: the printer itself is cheap, but the ink will bleed you dry. A genuine HP 62XL black cartridge runs about $45 at retail. The color? Another $50. That's nearly a hundred bucks every time you refill. Skyink's 62XL replacement set promises the same page yields at a fraction of the price. Here's what you actually get.

You also get a simple instruction sheet and a pair of rubber gloves — a nice touch if you're clumsy with ink like I am. The cartridges ship in sealed foil pouches with plastic protective caps on the print heads.

As shown in the product image, the cartridges are clearly labeled and shaped identically to the OEM HP 62XL units. The chip contacts are protected by an orange tab that needs to be pulled before installation.

**Performance and real-world use**

I tested these in an HP Envy 7640, printing a mix of text documents, color graphs, and photo prints on standard copy paper. Installation was straightforward — the printer recognized both cartridges immediately with no error messages or "non-genuine" warnings. That's the biggest fear with third-party ink, and Skyink handles it well.

Text quality is sharp and dark, with no smudging or streaking on plain paper. Black density is comparable to OEM — maybe 95% of the way there. For everyday documents, you won't notice a difference.

Color output is where things get interesting. Bright colors like blues and greens look vibrant and accurate. But skin tones and reds run slightly warm — a bit more orange than true red. One Amazon reviewer put it this way: *"Photos come out looking like they have a slight Instagram filter on them. Fine for school projects, but I wouldn't print family portraits with these."* That's fair. For documents and charts, you'll never notice. For photo printing, you'll want to stick with genuine HP cartridges.

**Where it falls short**

The color accuracy issue is the main tradeoff. If you print photos regularly, this is not your cartridge. The warm cast is consistent across units, not a defect — it's a characteristic of the ink formulation.

The protective tab removal is another minor annoyance. A few reviewers reported that pulling the tab at an angle caused slight ink leakage around the print head. It's a small percentage of units, but worth mentioning. Pull the tab straight off, slowly, and you'll be fine.

Battery life of the chips is also a potential concern. Skyink's chips are designed to report ink levels, but some users noted that the printer shows "low ink" warnings earlier than with OEM cartridges — sometimes with 15-20% of ink remaining. The printer will still function, but you'll see the warning light sooner than you'd like.
