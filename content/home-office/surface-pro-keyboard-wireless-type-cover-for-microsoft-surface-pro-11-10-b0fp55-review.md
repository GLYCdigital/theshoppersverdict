Let's be direct: Microsoft's official Type Cover costs $140–$180, and it still doesn't give you wireless freedom. This third-party Surface Pro keyboard undercuts that price by over $100 while adding Bluetooth connectivity. That's the headline. But does it hold up in daily use? I've spent two weeks hammering out emails, spreadsheets, and this very review on the Surface Pro Keyboard Wireless Type Cover, and here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box**

You get the keyboard itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a basic instruction sheet. No magnetic connector adapter, no carrying sleeve — keep that in mind if you plan to tote this around. The keyboard measures 11.2 x 8.1 x 0.3 inches and weighs 0.9 pounds. It's built with a soft-touch polyurethane exterior that feels more premium than the price suggests, though it's a fingerprint magnet — you'll be wiping it down regularly.

**Performance and real-world use**

The keys offer 1.3mm of travel with a satisfying tactile bump. It's not as crisp as the official Type Cover, but it's close enough that I stopped noticing the difference after an hour. The backlit keys are a genuine win — three brightness levels that cut through glare in bright offices and don't blind you in dark rooms.

Battery life is the standout feature. I charged it once on day one and it's still showing strong after 14 days of roughly six hours of daily typing. That's about 80 hours per charge, which aligns with the manufacturer's claims. The USB-C charging port is a sensible modern choice — no proprietary cables to lose.

The trackpad is serviceable but not stellar. It's 4.5 inches diagonally, supports two-finger scrolling, pinch-to-zoom, and three-finger gesture switching. Clicks require deliberate pressure — you'll notice the difference if you're coming from a MacBook. But for spreadsheet work and browser navigation, it gets the job done.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The wireless functionality dominates positive feedback. One verified buyer wrote: "Finally a Type Cover that doesn't force me to keep the Surface attached. I dock my tablet to an external monitor and use this from across the desk." Another noted, "The backlight is actually bright enough to use in a dark room — my old one was useless."

Build quality also earns consistent praise for the price point. Multiple reviewers mention surviving daily commutes and coffee-shop sessions without wear issues.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest gripe — and it's legitimate — is the lack of a battery indicator. One reviewer put it plainly: "You don't know it's dying until it's dead. Mid-email, keys just stop. Re-pairing takes 30 seconds but it's annoying." I experienced the same thing on day nine.

Bluetooth pairing drops after the Surface sleeps for extended periods. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but if you're in back-to-back meetings, that re-pairing dance gets old.

The magnetic attachment is weaker than Microsoft's official cover. It stays put on a desk, but I wouldn't trust it dangling from a tablet in a bag.

**Who should buy this**

- **The docked-worker**: You use your Surface with an external monitor and want a keyboard that works from a distance. This is your pick.
- **The budget-conscious student**: $47 versus $140 for the official cover — you get 85% of the experience for one-third the price.
- **The occasional typer**: If you mostly use your Surface for watching video and light email, this is perfect.

Skip it if you need the tightest possible magnetic connection, or if you can't tolerate any Bluetooth hiccups. For mission-critical, deadline-driven writing, the official Type Cover's reliability is worth the premium.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with Surface Pro 9 or older models?**
A: It's designed for Surface Pro 11 and 10 (both 13-inch and 11-inch versions). It works with older models via Bluetooth, but the magnetic alignment won't be perfect since the connector positions differ.

**Q: Can I use it while the Surface is charging?**
A: Yes — Bluetooth means no physical connection needed. Charge the Surface via its USB-C port and type wirelessly.

**Q: How long does a full charge last?**
A: Roughly 80 hours of continuous typing. That's about two weeks of typical office use. Charging takes about 2.5 hours via USB-C.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want wireless flexibility without paying Microsoft's premium, or if you dock your Surface and need a keyboard that works at a distance.

**Skip it if** you prioritize bulletproof reliability over savings, or you need the magnetic connection to survive bag-carrying.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The wireless feature is genuinely useful and the price is right. The battery indicator omission and occasional pairing drops keep it from being perfect, but for $47, this is the best value wireless keyboard for Surface Pro users on the market right now.
