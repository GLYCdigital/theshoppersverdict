**The budget laptop that finally ditched the ancient N4120 — but is the N150 enough for real work? Let's dig in.**

Most sub-$400 laptops are recycled 2019 hardware with new stickers. The HP 2025 Student Business Laptop with the Intel N150 processor is different — and that's both good and complicated. This chip, codenamed Twin Lake, replaces the tired N4120 that plagued budget laptops for half a decade. But "better than a 2019 chip" is a low bar. Here's what you're actually getting.

**What's in the box:** The laptop itself, a 45W AC adapter (barrel plug, not USB-C — noted), and the usual paperwork. No stylus, no sleeve, no surprises. The chassis is dark gray plastic with a subtle brushed texture that resists fingerprints better than most in this tier. At roughly 3.5 pounds and 0.7 inches thick, it's portable enough for a backpack but not an ultrabook featherweight.

**Performance where it counts:** The N150 is a 4-core, 4-thread Alder Lake-N chip with Intel UHD graphics. In real terms, this means:
- 8-10 browser tabs with moderate extensions: fine
- Google Docs with 50+ pages plus Spotify: no stutter
- Light photo editing in GIMP or Photoshop Elements: usable, not fun
- 1080p YouTube or Netflix: smooth, no dropped frames

One buyer put it plainly: *"My old laptop couldn't handle Zoom and a spreadsheet at the same time. This one does, plus Slack. That's all I needed."* That sums up the target user perfectly. The N150's single-core performance is roughly on par with a Core i3-8100 from 2018 — not exciting, but entirely workable for document-heavy student or office workflows.

**Build quality and keyboard:** The hinge feels solid — no wobble when typing on a lap. The keyboard has about 1.5mm of travel, which is genuinely comfortable for long writing sessions. It's backlit, which is rare at this price. The trackpad is a bit small (about 4.1 x 2.6 inches) and the surface is smooth but not glass — expect occasional finger drag on humid days. The 720p webcam is mediocre but includes a physical privacy shutter, a thoughtful touch.

**Battery life reality check:** HP claims up to 11 hours. Real-world use with screen at 70% brightness, mixed Wi-Fi browsing and document work: expect 7-8 hours. That's still a full class day, but not the all-day-and-then-some endurance of M-series MacBooks. With the included 45W charger, you'll hit 50% in about 45 minutes.

**What buyers consistently praise:**
- The N150's responsiveness compared to older Celeron/Pentium models
- The backlit keyboard — a genuine surprise at this price
- The port selection: full-size HDMI, USB-C, two USB-A, and a microSD slot
- Quiet operation — the fan is essentially inaudible during normal use

**Where it falls short:**
The 8GB RAM is soldered and non-upgradeable. If you're a tab-hoarder or plan to run Windows 11 plus anything memory-hungry, get the 16GB version or look elsewhere. Also, the eMMC storage is a real bottleneck — boot times are okay (~15-20 seconds), but copying a 5GB folder will feel glacial compared to laptops with NVMe drives.

**Who should buy this:**
- **The college student** doing essays, spreadsheets, Canvas, and Netflix — this is plenty
- **The office worker** who needs a secondary travel laptop for email, Teams, and VPN work
- **The parent** buying a first laptop for a middle/high schooler — durable enough and cheap enough to replace

**Skip it if:** You edit video, compile code, or run virtual machines. The N150 simply isn't that machine — look for a used ThinkPad with a Ryzen 5 or an M1 MacBook Air.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Can I upgrade the RAM or storage later?**  
RAM is soldered — no upgrade possible. Storage is eMMC, also not replaceable. Buy the configuration you need upfront.

**Q: Will this run Windows 11 smoothly?**  
Yes, with the caveat that you should expect occasional lag when launching heavy apps for the first time. Once running, it's stable.

**Q: Is it good for Zoom/Teams video calls?**  
Yes. The webcam is mediocre quality, but the N150 handles video encoding without bogging down — a major improvement over the N4120.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you need a reliable, affordable laptop for everyday work and school — the N150 is a genuine step up in this class.  
**Skip it if** you need upgradeability, heavy processing power, or fast storage — this isn't your machine.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, honest budget laptop that nails the basics and skips the gimmicks. Just know its limits going in.
