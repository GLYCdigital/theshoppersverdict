Let's cut the chase: a 27-inch 165Hz monitor for $109.99 shouldn't exist. Yet here's the Z-Edge, sitting at a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, and it mostly delivers. But "mostly" is doing heavy lifting — this isn't the monitor for everyone.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the 27-inch panel, a metal stand base that screws in without tools, a power adapter, and an HDMI cable. That's it. No DisplayPort cable included, which is annoying since you need DP to hit the full 165Hz refresh rate. HDMI caps you at 144Hz. Budget monitors love this trick — plan on dropping $8 for a decent DP cable.

The panel itself is a VA (Vertical Alignment) display with a 1920x1080 resolution. At 27 inches, that's roughly 81 pixels per inch. Text won't be razor-sharp, but it's perfectly usable for daily work. The 2mm side bezels are the star of the show visually — in the product image, it looks like a $300 monitor, and in person, it mostly holds up.

**Real-world performance**

The 165Hz refresh rate is the headline, and it's legit. Desktop scrolling feels buttery, and casual games like Rocket League or Fortnite run noticeably smoother than a standard 60Hz panel. Response time is rated at 1ms MPRT, but that's marketing math. In practice, the VA panel shows visible ghosting in dark scenes and fast camera pans. If you're a competitive CS2 or Valorant player, this isn't your monitor. Period.

Color accuracy is decent out of the box — nothing calibrated, but skin tones aren't weird and whites don't lean yellow. The 300-nit brightness is fine for indoor use, but next to a sunny window, you'll be squinting. One thing buyers consistently mention is the anti-glare coating doing its job well, even in bright rooms.

**What buyers are saying**

The 4.4-star average holds up when you read the actual feedback. One verified purchaser wrote: *"Bought this for my son's first gaming setup. For the price, the refresh rate is unreal. We had to buy a DisplayPort cable separately, but once we did, it was smooth as butter."* Another noted: *"Great for work and light gaming. The bezels are stupid thin for the price."*

The complaints cluster around three things: the speakers (universally panned), the stand (no height adjustment), and the ghosting. One reviewer summed it up: *"Fine for the money, but don't expect a $400 experience."*

**Where it falls short**

The speakers are genuinely bad. They're fine for system sounds and YouTube, but music lacks any bass and dialogue sounds hollow. Buy a cheap soundbar or use headphones. The stand only tilts — no height, no swivel. If you're particular about ergonomics, factor in $25 for a monitor arm. And the VA panel's viewing angles are narrow; colors shift if you're not dead center.

**Who should buy this**

- **Budget gamers on a casual level**: If you play single-player titles, MMOs, or co-op games, the 165Hz refresh is a genuine upgrade.
- **Hybrid work-from-home users**: 27 inches of screen real estate for spreadsheets and documents, plus gaming after hours.
- **Parents building a first setup**: It's cheap enough to replace, good enough to impress.

**Skip it if**: You're a competitive FPS player, you need accurate colors for photo editing, or you can't live without ergonomic adjustability.

**FAQ**

*Can the Z-Edge run at 165Hz on any graphics card?* No. You need a GPU with DisplayPort 1.2 or higher, and a DisplayPort cable. HDMI only reaches 144Hz.

*Does it support FreeSync?* Yes, FreeSync works over both HDMI and DisplayPort, though it's not G-Sync certified. NVIDIA users can enable G-Sync compatibility through the control panel with mixed results.

*Is the 1080p resolution sharp enough at 27 inches?* For gaming, yes. For heavy text work, you'll notice slightly soft text compared to 1440p. It's a tradeoff you accept at this price.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if**: You want a big, fast monitor under $110 and understand its limitations. It's genuinely impressive for the price.

**Skip it if**: You need color accuracy, competitive-grade response times, or a fully adjustable stand. Those features cost double.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Z-Edge delivers exceptional value for casual gaming and daily work, but the VA panel ghosting and weak stand keep it from being a universal recommendation. For the price, though, it's hard to argue with the math.
