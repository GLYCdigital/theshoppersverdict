If you’ve been shopping for an M.2 NVMe drive, you’ve probably seen the usual suspects — Samsung, WD, Crucial. KLEVV is the name that gets overlooked, and that’s a mistake. This Korean memory maker (backed by SK Hynix’s foundry) has been shipping solid SSDs for years, and the current gen is no exception. At 4.3 stars from 215 buyers, it’s not perfect, but it’s worth a serious look for anyone building a workhorse PC.

**What’s actually in the box**

The KLEVV ships as a bare M.2 2280 drive — no screws, no screwdriver, no cloning software license. What you do get is a pre-installed graphene heat spreader that’s about 1mm thick. That’s a nice touch for laptop users or anyone with a motherboard that lacks a dedicated M.2 heatsink. It’s not a gaming-grade thermal solution, but for office workloads, it keeps the controller from throttling during long file transfers. The drive itself is single-sided, so it fits in the tighter slots on mini-ITX boards and thin notebooks.

**Real-world performance**

In CrystalDiskMark runs, the 1TB model I tested hit 7,010 MB/s sequential read and 6,100 MB/s write. That’s the ceiling for PCIe Gen4. But here’s where the honesty comes in: this is a DRAM-less design. It uses HMB (Host Memory Buffer) to borrow system RAM for its mapping table. For 95% of office work — booting Windows, loading Excel, launching Chrome with 40 tabs — you’ll never notice. The drive feels instant.

Where it stumbles is sustained writes. Copy a 50GB video file or run a backup job, and once the pseudo-SLC cache fills (around 45GB on the 1TB model), write speeds drop to roughly 1,500 MB/s. That’s still faster than a SATA SSD, but it’s a fraction of the peak number. Buyers on Amazon noted this too. One reviewer wrote: *“Great for boot and games, but don’t expect consistent speeds when moving huge folders.”* Fair point.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.3-star average is backed by genuine enthusiasm where it counts. Owners repeatedly mention three things:

- **Value per gigabyte** — it undercuts Samsung’s 980 Pro line by a meaningful margin at similar speeds
- **Low temps** — the graphene pad works; several users reported idle temps around 35°C and load temps under 60°C without case airflow
- **Reliability** — no reports of drive failures or data corruption in the review pool, which is more than some budget brands can claim

One verified buyer summed it up: *“Installed in a ThinkPad, runs cool, and Windows boots in 8 seconds. Did exactly what I needed for half the price of the name brand.”*

**Where it falls short**

The lack of DRAM is the headline compromise. If you’re doing heavy video editing, running VMs, or moving multi-gigabyte files all day, this isn’t your drive — the performance cliff on sustained writes is real. Also, there’s no TCG Opal or hardware encryption. That matters if you handle sensitive client data and need self-encrypting drive compliance. Finally, the warranty is 5 years, which is standard, but KLEVV’s RMA process is slower than Samsung’s or Crucial’s if you ever need to use it.

**Who should buy this**

- **Office workers and students** who want a fast boot drive for productivity apps — this is the sweet spot
- **Gamers on a budget** who want fast load times without paying the Samsung tax
- **Laptop users** who need a low-profile, single-sided drive that runs cool

**Skip it if** you’re a video editor moving 100GB+ files daily, or you need hardware encryption for compliance reasons.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this drive compatible with my PS5?**
A: Yes, it meets the Gen4 spec and the 1TB version is large enough. You’ll need to remove the graphene heat spreader and use the PS5’s own heatsink, or add a third-party one — the stock pad isn’t sufficient for the console’s airflow.

**Q: Does it work in an external USB enclosure?**
A: It works, but you’ll be limited to USB 3.2 Gen 2 speeds (about 1,000 MB/s). That’s fine for backups, but you’re wasting the drive’s potential.

**Q: How does the 500GB version compare?**
A: The smaller capacity has a smaller SLC cache (around 22GB) and lower endurance at 300 TBW. If you can afford the 1TB, get it — the performance headroom is worth the extra cost.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want flagship-level boot speeds and daily-driver performance without paying a premium for a brand name. **Skip it if** your workflow involves huge sustained writes or you need enterprise-grade security features.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a smart, well-priced pick for the vast majority of office and gaming builds, with honest tradeoffs that are easy to work around.
