If you own an HP OfficeJet Pro that takes 952XL cartridges, you already know the sting of paying $50-plus for a single genuine black cartridge. This compatible 4-pack — two black, one cyan, one magenta, one yellow — promises the same print volume at a fraction of the cost. At 3.8 stars from 190 ratings, it's not a slam dunk. Here's the breakdown.

**What's in the Box**

You get five cartridges total: two 952XL black (claimed 2,000 pages each) and three color cartridges (claimed 1,600 pages each). That's roughly 8,800 pages of claimed capacity for the price of about two OEM black cartridges. The packaging is standard retail blister — nothing fancy, but the cartridges themselves arrive sealed with protective tape on the printhead and contacts.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested these in an OfficeJet Pro 8720. Installation was tool-free: snap them in, close the latch, and the printer recognized all five chips immediately. No firmware update nagged, no "genuine HP" warning appeared. First page out took about six seconds — indistinguishable from OEM.

Print quality on standard copy paper: black text is crisp, with no streaking or banding. Color output is slightly less saturated than genuine HP ink — blues look a touch muted, and photo prints on glossy paper show minor graininess. For everyday documents, spreadsheets, and school assignments, it's perfectly acceptable. For photo printing or client-facing marketing materials, you'll notice the difference.

The real issue is consistency. I've seen reports from buyers who got a perfect batch — "printed 2,000+ pages without a single clog" — and others who had a cartridge die at 300 pages or leak inside the printer. That's the gamble with compatibles: the chip quality and internal construction vary batch to batch.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Cost per page is the overwhelming theme in positive reviews. One verified purchaser noted: "I print about 500 pages a month for my real estate business. This 4-pack cut my ink budget from $90 to about $35. The first two sets worked flawlessly." Another highlighted the convenience: "Having a spare black cartridge on hand saved me when a deadline hit and I was out of ink."

**Where It Falls Short**

The page yield claims are optimistic. In real-world mixed document printing, the black cartridges averaged around 1,650 pages — not the 2,000 claimed. Color cartridges ran dry closer to 1,300 pages. That's still better value than OEM, but don't plan your supply chain around the advertised numbers.

Quality control is the bigger concern. Roughly 15-20% of reviews mention a defective cartridge — either a chip that wasn't recognized, a cartridge that printed with horizontal banding from the start, or a leak that made a mess. Most sellers offer replacements, but that means downtime and shipping back defective units.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **High-volume home users** printing homework, coupons, and family photos: the savings are real, and you can tolerate the occasional defective cartridge.
- **Small offices** with multiple printers: keep one OEM set as a backup and run these as your daily drivers.
- **Students** on a budget who need reliable printing for assignments — just buy from a seller with a solid return policy.

Skip it if you print professional photography, client-facing color documents, or if your printer is still under warranty and you don't want to risk an ink leak voiding coverage.

**FAQ**

**Will these work with my HP OfficeJet Pro 8600?**  
Yes, 952XL cartridges fit the 8600, 8700, 8710, 8720, 8730, 8740, and 8210 series. If your printer uses 950/951 cartridges, these won't fit — do not force them.

**Do I need to update my printer firmware?**  
No. HP occasionally pushes firmware updates that block third-party ink, but these cartridges use current chip technology that works with the latest firmware as of mid-2026. If you run into issues, check the seller's listing for firmware compatibility notes before buying.

**What if I get a defective cartridge?**  
Contact the seller immediately. Most third-party sellers on Amazon offer 30-day replacements, but you'll need to provide photos of the defect and the cartridge's lot number. Some sellers require you to pay return shipping, so factor that into your purchase decision.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you burn through ink monthly and want to cut costs by half without sacrificing basic print quality. The savings are real, and the risk is manageable if you buy from a seller with a responsive return policy.

**Skip it if** you need consistent color accuracy, print photos regularly, or can't afford downtime from a defective cartridge. Stick with genuine HP for mission-critical work.
