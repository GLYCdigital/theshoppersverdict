If you've ever run a dash cam power cable down your A-pillar, under the dash, and into the cigarette lighter, you know the pain: visible wires, loose trim clips, and that one rattle that never goes away. The Dongar 12-pin Type A adapter eliminates that entire hassle by pulling power straight from your rearview mirror's existing harness. It's the cleanest install you'll do this year — provided your vehicle is on the compatibility list.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the Dongar adapter itself: a short, molded plastic dongle that plugs inline between your vehicle's mirror wiring and the mirror. There's a pigtail that exits the dongle with a standard 5V/2A USB-A port (or a hardwired mini/micro-USB cable, depending on which variant you order). No tools, no instructions booklet worth reading, no fuse taps. It's a 30-second install: unplug mirror harness, plug in Dongar, plug mirror back in, connect your dash cam. That's it.

**Real-world performance**

The adapter is rated for 5V/2A output — that's 10 watts, which covers essentially every mainstream dash cam (think Garmin, VIOFO, Thinkware, and the budget Nexar units). In testing, voltage stays stable even with a camera pulling continuous recording in hot weather; no brownouts, no random restarts. The ignition-switched power means the camera powers down with the car, so you won't come back to a dead battery after a weekend parked.

Build quality is solid: the connector seats firmly, the plastic housing is compact enough to tuck behind the mirror trim, and the pins align correctly without forcing. One buyer noted, "I was skeptical about tapping into the mirror harness, but it took two minutes and my Garmin has been rock solid for six months." Another said, "No more cable running down the windshield — this is how it should come from the factory."

**Where it falls short**

Compatibility is the big one. This is the 12-pin Type A version, which fits a specific set of vehicles — mostly Toyota Tacoma, 4Runner, Tundra, and some Subaru models. If your vehicle isn't listed, it won't fit, and you'll be returning it. Double-check the manufacturer's compatibility chart before ordering; don't assume "12-pin" means universal.

The pigtail is also short — roughly 12 inches. If your dash cam mounts low on the windshield or offset to the passenger side, you might need a small USB extension cable (any quality 1-ft cable works). And there's no USB-C output on this version; you'll need the Type B/C variant if your camera uses USB-C natively.

**Who should buy this**

- **Toyota/Subaru owners** with a factory auto-dimming mirror — this is the single best $20-ish upgrade for a clean dash cam install.
- **Anyone who's tried fuse tapping** and wants to avoid messing with the fuse box entirely.
- **Daily commuters** who want a permanent, set-and-forget dash cam setup without visible wires.

Skip it if you have a vehicle outside the compatibility list, or if you need parking-mode recording (this adapter only provides ignition-switched power, not constant-on).

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work with my 2023 Tacoma SR5?**  
A: If it has the auto-dimming rearview mirror with a 12-pin connector, yes. The Type A fits most Tacoma trims from 2016 onward, but verify your mirror connector visually before buying.

**Q: Does it drain my car battery when parked?**  
A: No. It's ignition-switched, so power cuts when the car turns off. No parasitic drain.

**Q: Can I use it with a radar detector instead of a dash cam?**  
A: Yes, as long as the detector draws under 2A and uses a standard USB or the included pigtail connector.
