The ZCZN Colorful Blank Notebook set solves a specific problem: you need multiple notebooks for different purposes, and you want to tell them apart at a glance. Twelve colors, blank pages, and a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings. The question isn't whether these are good notebooks — it's whether they're the right ones for how you work.

## What You Actually Get

Twelve notebooks, each with a different solid-color cover — think classic memo-book styling in shades ranging from muted pastels to deeper jewel tones. Each measures 5.7 x 8.5 inches, which sits between a pocket Moleskine and a full-size composition book. That's a sweet spot for carrying in a tote or backpack without dominating the bag.

Inside, you get 60 sheets of blank 80gsm paper. That's lighter than premium brands like Rhodia (90gsm) but heavier than bargain-basement filler. The pages are genuinely blank — no dots, no lines, no grid. If you're a sketcher, mind-mapper, or someone who hates ruling lines, that's the point.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The binding deserves specific praise. These notebooks are thread-sewn, not glued, which means they lay completely flat when open. That's rare at this price point. You can write right up to the inner margin without the page curling up on you — a detail that matters when you're taking notes in a meeting or sketching on a train.

The paper handles ink better than expected. Multiple Amazon reviewers specifically mention fountain pens working without bleed-through. One writes, "I was skeptical at $2 per notebook, but the paper is surprisingly thick. My Pilot Metropolitan doesn't ghost through." Another confirms, "Gel pens and highlighters both work — mild ghosting on the back but nothing that ruins the next page."

The covers are the main compromise. They're 300gsm cardstock — flexible, not rigid like a hardcover. That keeps the set affordable and the notebooks lightweight, but they won't survive being crammed in a backpack pocket without getting bent corners. If you're a desk-drawer organizer, fine. If you're a field-worker who tosses notebooks around, budget for a sleeve.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The color coding is the headline feature. With twelve distinct hues, you can assign one per subject, project, or client without ever mixing them up. Reviewers repeatedly mention this: "I teach five classes and run two side projects. Seven colors down, five to go," and "Finally stopped buying black notebooks and losing track of which is which."

The value story is strong too. At roughly $2 per notebook, these undercut most single-brand alternatives by half or more. For teachers, students, or anyone who burns through notebooks seasonally, the economics work.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. Sixty sheets per notebook is thin. If you journal daily or take heavy lecture notes, one of these lasts three to four weeks, not a semester. You're buying the set for variety, not longevity per book.

The soft cover, as mentioned, is the other weakness. One reviewer noted: "The corners of my red one are already dog-eared after two weeks in my work bag. I need to start carrying a hard case." It's fixable, but it's an extra cost to factor in.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a teacher, student, or project manager juggling multiple subjects and want instant visual organization. You prefer blank pages for sketching, diagrams, or freeform notes. You want to test whether a color-coded system works for you without spending $8-12 per notebook on premium brands.

**Skip it if:** You need hardcover durability for fieldwork or heavy bag abuse. You write dense, long-form content daily — 60 sheets will frustrate you. You specifically need ruled or dot-grid paper, because this is blank-only.

## FAQ

**Do these work with fountain pens?**
Yes, based on multiple buyer reports. The 80gsm paper handles moderate ink flow without bleed-through, though heavy writers may see ghosting on the reverse side.

**Are the colors exactly as pictured?**
Close enough. They're saturated and distinct, but pastels are slightly deeper in person than on screen. No two shades are confusingly similar — which is the whole point.

**Can I tear pages out cleanly?**
The pages are sewn-bound, not perforated. Tearing one out will leave a ragged edge. If you need clean removal, these aren't the right pick.

## The Verdict
