**The 62XL is the cartridge everyone loves to hate — until they try a cheap knockoff and watch their printhead clog.** This genuine HP option sits at 4.1 stars from 204 Amazon ratings, and honestly, that rating reflects reality: it's not exciting, it's not cheap, but it works exactly as promised. If you own an HP Envy 5000 series, OfficeJet 5200, or the Tango printer, this is the cartridge that keeps your machine alive.

**What you actually get:** The box contains one black 62XL and one tri-color 62XL cartridge. The "XL" designation means roughly 600 pages for black and 415 pages for color per HP's ISO testing — double the standard 62 cartridge. The cartridges are sealed with the standard orange pull-tab, and installation takes about 30 seconds. No packaging waste beyond the cardboard and foil seal, which is more than most third-party options offer.

**Real-world performance:** I've run these through a full lifecycle in an Envy 6055. Text documents come out crisp with deep, saturated black — no gray cast, no streaking on plain paper. Color prints are where the 62XL justifies its existence: photo prints on glossy paper show accurate skin tones and smooth gradients, something remanufactured inks frequently butcher. The printhead cleaning cycle runs cleanly, and the chip reads instantly on first install. No firmware drama, no "genuine HP ink" warnings after a software update.

**What buyers consistently say:** The consensus in those 204 reviews is reliability. One buyer notes, *"I've tried cheap brands twice and both ruined my printer. These just work — I've replaced the black twice and never had a clog."* Another mentions, *"The XL lasts about two months of heavy printing for my small business."* The complaints that do appear center on price and the tri-color design — both valid points we'll address below.

**Where it falls short:** The tri-color cartridge is the biggest design flaw. When yellow runs dry, the entire cartridge is dead even if cyan and magenta are half-full. That's wasted ink and wasted money. The price per page is also notably higher than remanufactured options — roughly 6-8 cents per page versus 3-4 cents for third-party. But here's the tradeoff: third-party cartridges have a documented track record of leaking, clogging, and confusing the printer's firmware. You're paying a premium for peace of mind.

**Who should buy this:** The home office worker who prints 20-50 pages a week and needs consistent output without babysitting their printer. The small business owner who can't afford a printer failure mid-printing job. The photo enthusiast who prints family memories and expects accurate color. If you print rarely — a few pages a month — the standard 62 cartridge makes more sense, as the XL ink can dry out before you use it.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Will these work in my HP Envy 6000 series?**
A: Yes, the 62/62XL is compatible with Envy 5000, 6000, 7000 series, OfficeJet 5200 series, and the Tango. Check your printer's spec sheet — if it lists "HP 62" ink, these fit.

**Q: How do I know if I'm getting a genuine HP cartridge?**
A: Buy from Amazon directly or a fulfilled-by-Amazon seller. Counterfeit HP cartridges exist — check the packaging hologram and the cartridge's copper contacts. A genuine cartridge has a clean, uniform chip.

**Q: Can I use just black 62XL and a third-party color cartridge?**
A: Technically yes, but I don't recommend it. The printer relies on consistent ink chemistry across all cartridges for printhead calibration. Mixing OEM and third-party often causes banding or color shifts.
