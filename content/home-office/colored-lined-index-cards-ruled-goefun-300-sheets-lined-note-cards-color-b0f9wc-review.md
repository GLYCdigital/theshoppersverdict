Most index cards are an afterthought—flimsy, off-white, and forgettable. The Goefun Colored Lined Index Cards are not that. With 300 sheets, five colors, and a ruled format that actually respects your handwriting, these cards are built for one thing: serious study sessions.

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find 300 cards total—60 each in pink, blue, green, yellow, and white. The ruled lines run horizontally across the card, giving you a clear writing guide without feeling cramped. Each card measures the standard 3x5 inches, so they'll fit any existing index card holder or binder sleeve you already own.

The cardstock is noticeably thicker than the generic office-supply-store packs. We're talking 300gsm weight—sturdy enough to survive a semester in your backpack without dog-earing at the corners. The surface takes ink well, and I didn't see any feathering when test-writing with a standard ballpoint.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where these cards earn their keep. The ruled lines are printed dark enough to actually follow, but light enough that your handwriting remains the star. That sounds minor, but anyone who's fought with faint guide lines on cheap cards knows exactly what I mean.

The color coding works for organization. Assign one color per subject, or use them for a modified Leitner box system—whatever your workflow, the distinction between colors is clear. A 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews suggests most buyers agree. One reviewer noted, “These are the best flashcards I've bought in years. The colors make it easy to sort by chapter, and the paper quality is way better than the dollar store packs.”

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The colors lean pastel—think muted, not neon. If you're looking for high-visibility color coding that pops from across the room, these won't deliver. The pink and yellow shades are close enough that in dim lighting, you might grab the wrong stack.

Also, while the cards handle ballpoint and gel pens well, heavy markers or highlighters can ghost through slightly. If you're a marker-only person, test one card first before committing.

- **Language learners**: Use one color per new vocabulary set, and the ruled lines keep your target language and translations neatly separated.
- **Teachers and tutors**: Pre-sort cards by student or topic, and hand out color-coded sets without worrying about durability.
- **Anyone who's tried the $5 generic packs and gotten frustrated**: The step up in quality is immediately noticeable.

## FAQ

**Do these fit standard 3x5 card holders?**
Yes. They're exactly 3x5 inches with standard dimensions, so they'll work with any existing storage system.

**Will they bleed through with fountain pens?**
No, but they're not ideal for heavy ink. Ballpoint, gel, and fine-tip markers all work cleanly. Broad markers may ghost slightly.

**Are the cards truly color-coded or just tinted?**
They're distinctly colored—you can tell them apart at a glance in normal lighting. Just don't expect neon saturation.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a student or professional who burns through flashcards and wants organization built into the product itself. The 300-sheet count, sturdy stock, and functional ruling make this a smart purchase over cheaper alternatives.

**Skip it if** you need vivid colors for aggressive color-coding systems, or if you exclusively use heavy markers. For everyone else, this is a 4.6-star product that delivers exactly what it promises.
