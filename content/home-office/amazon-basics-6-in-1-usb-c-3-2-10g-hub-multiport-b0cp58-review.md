The Amazon Basics 6-in-1 USB-C hub sits in an odd spot: it's not the cheapest dongle on Amazon, nor the most premium. But after digging through 210 ratings and the spec sheet, this one earns its keep for a specific kind of buyer. If you've been burned by $15 no-name hubs that drop connections mid-presentation, this is the safety net you've been looking for.

**What you actually get**

The hub is a compact aluminum wedge, roughly the size of a deck of cards, with a permanently attached 8-inch USB-C cable. On the sides you'll find two USB-A 3.2 Gen 2 ports (10Gbps), one USB-C data port (also 10Gbps), an HDMI 2.0 port capable of 4K at 60Hz, an SD/TF card reader, and a gigabit Ethernet port. Notable absence: no separate power button, no DisplayPort, and no 3.5mm audio jack. For a 6-in-1, the port selection is sensible — you're getting the daily essentials without gimmicks.

**Real-world performance**

The headline feature here is the 10Gbps transfer speed, and it's not just spec-sheet theater. Multiple verified buyers report hitting 800-900 MB/s when moving large files to an external NVMe SSD. One reviewer noted, *"Copied a 20GB video project in under 30 seconds — my old hub took three minutes."* That's the difference this makes in actual workflow.

HDMI output holds up at 4K60 with stable refresh rates, though you'll want a high-quality HDMI cable — a few buyers mentioned flicker with cheap cables. The Ethernet port is rock-solid for video calls; no dropped connections reported in the feedback I read.

The 100W pass-through charging is a genuine differentiator. It keeps a 16-inch MacBook Pro fed while driving an external display and two USB peripherals simultaneously. That's the full desk setup working from one port.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- Build quality: The aluminum housing feels dense and premium, not hollow like cheaper hubs.
- Heat management during normal use: It runs warm, not hot, when doing everyday tasks like browsing and light file transfers.
- Plug-and-play reliability across Mac, Windows, and even Steam Deck — no driver installation drama.

**Where it falls short**

The heat issue is real under load. Sustained 4K output plus a large file transfer made the casing uncomfortably warm to touch — one buyer said it *"got hot enough to worry me, though it never throttled."* It won't fail, but it's worth knowing if you plan to run it hard for hours.

The 8-inch integrated cable is both a pro and con. It's convenient for travel, but it forces the hub to dangle awkwardly from a laptop on a stand. Also, the SD card reader is UHS-I only, so professional photographers moving raw files will bottleneck at around 100 MB/s.

**Who should buy this**

- **The hybrid worker**: You split time between a laptop and a desk setup with external monitor, Ethernet, and peripherals. This does it all from one port.
- **The traveler**: Compact enough for a tech pouch, with every port you'd need in a hotel room or airport lounge.
- **The MacBook owner**: If you're on an M1/M2/M3 Mac with only two USB-C ports, this expands your capability without the Apple tax.

**Skip it if** you need 8K output, dual external monitors, or 2.5G+ Ethernet. This is a solid daily driver, not a workstation powerhouse.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this hub charge my laptop while also transferring data?**
Yes, at up to 100W pass-through. Your laptop gets power while the other ports operate at full speed simultaneously.

**Q: Does it work with iPad Pro or Steam Deck?**
Yes. Buyers report it functions with iPadOS 17+ and Steam Deck in desktop mode, though the Steam Deck may need external power for the hub to run at full capacity.

**Q: Is the 10Gbps speed real or marketing hype?**
Real, but only if your devices support it. You need a 10Gbps USB-C port on your laptop and a 10Gbps cable/peripheral to see those speeds. Most modern laptops from the last two years have it.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, well-built hub that handles everyday office demands with room to spare for occasional heavy transfers. **Skip it if** you're a power user needing dual display output or faster Ethernet.

At 4.2/5 stars, this is a quietly excellent choice for the mainstream buyer — not the cheapest, not the fastest, but the one that just works. For the price of a decent dinner, it solves the modern laptop's biggest annoyance: not enough ports.
