## What's actually in the box

You get 25 individually packaged HID 1346 ProxKey III fobs. Each is a standard 125kHz proximity credential — the industry workhorse frequency, not the newer (and pricier) 13.56MHz smart card tech. They measure about 1.6" x 1.1" x 0.15" and weigh next to nothing. The classic HID blue-and-white casing is intact, with the HID logo and "ProxKey III" branding molded into the plastic.

These arrive **blank and unprogrammed**. That's the point — you encode them with your own facility's credentials using whatever programming hardware your access control system already uses. No proprietary lock-in, no activation fees.

## Real-world performance and build

The ProxKey III has been HID's entry-level fob for over two decades, and there's a reason it hasn't been retired. The ABS plastic shell is tough. I've seen these things survive years on janitorial keyrings, get run through washing machines, and take daily abuse from maintenance crews. The internal coil and chip are potted — sealed against moisture and minor physical shock.

Read range is typical for 125kHz proximity: about 2–4 inches depending on your reader and mounting surface. If you're expecting tap-and-go like modern smart cards, you'll be disappointed. This is older tech — you have to physically present the fob to the reader. But for door access, it's reliable, and the read is nearly instantaneous.

## Where it falls short

Honest tradeoffs, because there are always some:

- **No keyring hole.** The 1346 form factor is a solid rectangle with no pre-drilled attachment point. If you want these on lanyards or keychains, you'll need to drill a small hole — doable but annoying, and it voids any warranty.
- **No visual indicator.** There's no LED or beeper on the fob itself. You won't get feedback that the read worked unless your reader has its own light/sound.
- **The 25-pack math.** If you only need 5 fobs for a small office, this bundle is still cheaper per unit than buying singles, but you're tying up $50+ in inventory you won't touch for months.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these fobs programmable with any HID system?**
A: Yes, they're compatible with any HID proximity reader operating at 125kHz. Programming requires a compatible encoder or your access control software's credential management feature. If you're unsure, your security vendor can confirm compatibility in minutes.

**Q: What's the difference between this and an HID ProxCard II?**
A: The ProxKey III is a fob (keychain-sized), while the ProxCard II is a credit-card-sized credential. Both use the same 125kHz proximity technology and work with the same readers. The fob is more durable and easier to carry; the card is easier to badge with a photo.

**Q: Can I clone or copy these fobs?**
A: 125kHz proximity fobs are generally cloneable with specialized hardware, which is a known security limitation. For higher-security applications, you'd want HID's iCLASS or Seos smart-card line. For standard office door access, the ProxKey III is the accepted industry standard.
