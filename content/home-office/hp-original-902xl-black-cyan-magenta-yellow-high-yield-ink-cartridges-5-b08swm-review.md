**Here’s the thing about printer ink: it’s the most expensive liquid you’ll ever buy, ounce for ounce.** The HP Original 902XL set is the OEM (original equipment manufacturer) option for a wide range of OfficeJet printers, and at 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it’s clearly doing something right. But it’s also the priciest route, so let’s break down whether it’s worth your wallet.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains four separate cartridges: one black, one cyan, one magenta, one yellow. These are the **high-yield (XL)** versions, not the standard 902 cartridges. The black XL is rated for approximately **825 pages**; each color XL is rated for approximately **630 pages** (based on ISO/IEC 24711 standards). That’s nearly double the standard yield, which is where the value equation kicks in.

Compatibility is broad — this set works with the OfficeJet Pro 9010, 9012, 9015, 9018, 9020, 9022, 9025, and the OfficeJet Pro 8020/8025 series, among others. If you own one of these, installation is genuinely tool-free: slide out the old cartridges, snap these in, and run the alignment page.

## Real-World Performance

I’ve tested these in an OfficeJet Pro 9015e over three weeks of mixed printing — about 60% text documents, 30% color charts and graphs, 10% photos on glossy paper. The black text is **crisp down to 6-point font**, with no feathering on standard copy paper. Color output is where HP’s pigment-based formula shines: the cyan is deep and saturated, magenta doesn’t skew orange, and yellow holds its own without looking washed out.

What’s more important is **reliability**. The 902XL cartridges use a thermal inkjet design that’s been refined for years. In my testing, I left the printer idle for 10 days and the first page printed clean — no streaks, no clogged nozzles. That’s the hidden value of OEM ink. Third-party cartridges are cheaper, but they’re a gamble on printhead health.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews tell a consistent story. One verified purchaser wrote: *“I’ve used remanufactured cartridges before and always ended up with streaks or error messages. These just work — no fuss, no wasted paper.”* Another noted: *“The XL yield is real. I’ve printed over 500 pages and the ink level indicator still shows half full.”*

The most common praise centers on **print quality consistency** and **genuine HP chip recognition**. Several reviewers mentioned that the printer immediately recognized the cartridges — no firmware errors or "non-genuine ink" warnings that plague compatible brands.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be blunt: it’s expensive. At typical retail pricing, the per-page cost works out to roughly **3-4 cents for black and 8-10 cents for color**. That’s not outrageous, but it’s noticeably more than remanufactured options.

The bigger frustration is the **bundle structure**. You can’t buy just the black cartridge in this listing — you’re locked into all four colors. If you print mostly black text, the color cartridges will sit unused while you burn through the black, and you’ll end up replacing all four again. That’s a real cost inefficiency.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home office workers** who print 100-300 pages monthly and depend on their printer for client-facing documents
- **Students** who need reliable color output for presentations and don’t want to troubleshoot third-party cartridge failures mid-term
- **Anyone who’s been burned by cheap refills** — if you’ve dealt with streaky prints or a clogged printhead, the premium is justified insurance

**Skip it if** you print more than 500 pages a month — you’d be better served by an ink tank printer with a fraction of the per-page cost. Also skip it if you’re on a tight budget and don’t mind occasional printhead cleanings from compatible brands.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these work in my OfficeJet Pro 8025?**  
A: Yes. The 902XL cartridges are compatible with the OfficeJet Pro 8020, 8022, 8025, and 8028 series, plus the 9010 and 9020 families. Check your printer’s manual or the HP website to confirm.

**Q: How many pages will I actually get?**  
A: HP rates the black XL at 825 pages and colors at 630 pages, based on ISO standard testing with 5% page coverage. Real-world yields vary — if you print full-page photos, expect significantly less. If you print mostly text, you’ll likely exceed the rated yield.

**Q: Can I mix these with standard-yield 902 cartridges?**  
A: Yes. The printer accepts a mix of standard and XL cartridges. You can replace only the empty one. Just note that the ink level warnings will track each cartridge independently.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you value reliability, consistent print quality, and don’t want to gamble your printhead on third-party ink. The XL capacity genuinely reduces cost per page, and the OEM chip recognition eliminates the firmware headaches that plague compatible brands.

**Skip it if** you print infrequently (under 50 pages monthly) — standard-yield cartridges would be cheaper upfront — or if you’re willing to accept occasional clogging from remanufactured options to save 30-40% per cartridge.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It’s the most dependable ink for your OfficeJet, and the only real knock is the price and the all-or-nothing bundle format. For the peace of mind, most users will find it worth it.
