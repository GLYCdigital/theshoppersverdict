The Brother MFCL2710DW isn't flashy. It's a boxy, gray workhorse that sits on your desk and does exactly one thing well: printing crisp, sharp black-and-white documents without drama. If that's all you need, this is one of the most solid buys in the sub-$200 laser printer category. But let's be clear about what you're getting — and what you're not.

**What's actually in the box**

Brother keeps it simple. You get the printer itself, a starter toner cartridge (rated for 700 pages — not the full 1,200), a drum unit that's already pre-installed, and a quick setup guide. No USB cable is included, so if you plan to connect via USB instead of Wi-Fi, factor that into your order. The wireless setup itself is straightforward — Brother's own mobile app walks you through it in under five minutes in most cases.

**Real-world performance**

This is where the MFCL2710DW earns its keep. Print speed is rated at 32 pages per minute, and in practice, the first page hits the output tray in about 7 seconds. That's genuinely fast for a home-office machine. Text comes out razor-sharp — 2,400 x 600 dpi resolution, and it shows. Even small fonts at 8-point remain legible, which is critical if you print contracts or legal documents.

The 35-page automatic document feeder is a quiet lifesaver. Stack a multi-page PDF, hit scan, and walk away. It handles the whole batch without jamming — something budget all-in-ones often struggle with. Duplex printing is automatic and genuinely works, not the "flip it yourself" fake duplexing you see on cheaper units.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The overwhelming consensus across 225 Amazon ratings is reliability. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've had this printer for over a year and it has never jammed once. I'm at about 5,000 pages and the drum is still going strong."* Another noted, *"Setup took me under 10 minutes. It just works."* That's the recurring theme — people aren't raving about features, they're relieved it doesn't break.

**Where it falls short**

The 250-sheet paper tray is the biggest practical limitation. If you print more than a few dozen pages daily, you'll be refilling it constantly. The starter toner also runs out fast — expect 700 pages, not the 1,200 you'd get from a full cartridge. Budget for the high-yield TN760 toner (about 3,000 pages) from day one.

There's no color output, obviously. And while the scanner is fine for documents, don't expect photo-quality scans — it's a flatbed for paperwork, not a photo digitizer. Mobile printing works via Brother's app, but the app's interface feels dated compared to HP or Epson equivalents.

**Who should buy this**

This printer is for the home office worker, the student, or the small business owner who prints documents, invoices, and forms — not photos, not color marketing materials. If you need to print 20-50 pages a day, occasionally scan a contract, and want something that won't make you scream at 7 AM before a deadline, this is your machine. If you print color presentations or want a sleek device that looks good in your living room, look elsewhere.

**FAQ**

**Does the MFCL2710DW work with Mac and Chromebook?**
Yes. Brother provides drivers for both macOS and Chrome OS. Wireless printing from iPhones and Androids works via AirPrint and the Brother Mobile Connect app.

**Can I use third-party toner cartridges?**
Technically yes, but Brother's warranty covers the printer for life — not the drum. Using remanufactured toner can void that coverage. The genuine TN760 high-yield cartridge is the safer long-term bet.

**Is the drum unit replaceable?**
Yes, and that's a major cost advantage. The drum (DR730) lasts about 12,000 pages and costs around $60. That's far cheaper than replacing an entire print head on an inkjet.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, fast monochrome laser that handles duplex printing and multi-page scanning without fuss. It's the kind of purchase you'll forget about — because it just keeps working.

**Skip it if** you need color, print more than 100 pages daily, or want a printer that looks like it belongs in a design studio rather than a utility closet.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A no-nonsense workhorse with minor paper-tray and toner-cost caveats. The best budget monochrome all-in-one for small offices.
