You can print 1,000 business cards for the cost of a lunch. That's the pitch with this 100-pack of blank cardstock. And honestly, it mostly delivers. But the devil is in the perforation and the paper weight — so let's get specific.

**What you actually get**

100 sheets of 12pt cardstock, each pre-perforated with ten standard 2" x 3.5" business cards. That's 1,000 cards total. The paper has a smooth matte finish — not glossy, not textured, just clean and professional. It's marketed for both inkjet and laser printers, which is rare in this category. Most budget cardstock picks one or the other.

**Real-world performance**

I ran these through a mid-tier HP inkjet and a Brother laser. The inkjet side was the pleasant surprise. Colors sat on top of the coating without bleeding into the fibers, and text down to 8pt stayed sharp. Drying time is reasonable — about 30 seconds before you can safely stack sheets.

The laser test was the one I watched closely. Laser printers run hot, and cheap cardstock often curls or melts its coating. This paper took the heat without warping. No curl at the edges, no toner flaking. That's genuinely better than the Avery and Neenah options at double the price.

The perforations are where this product lives or dies. The tear is clean along the straight edges — a light fold and pull separates the card cleanly. The corner nicks tear evenly too, which is where most budget cardstock fails. I saw one review from a buyer who said, *"The perforation quality is the best I've tried, and I've been through three other brands this year."* That matches my experience.

**Where it falls short**

No design templates or software included. You're getting paper, not a workflow. If you don't already have a design tool or template file, you'll spend time setting up the 10-up layout yourself. The product listing mentions compatibility with standard template formats, but there's no QR code or download link in the box.

Perforation consistency varies slightly between batches. Most sheets tear cleanly, but a handful from my 100-sheet pack had a rougher edge on one side — not enough to ruin the card, but enough to notice if you're handing out premium-feeling cards. A few Amazon reviewers noted the same, with one saying, *"About 10% of my sheets had uneven perforation — the cards still separated but the edge wasn't perfectly smooth."*

**Who should buy this**

- **Freelancers and solopreneurs** who need 200-500 cards for a conference or client meeting without paying $50+ for rush printing.
- **Small business owners** who want to iterate on designs without committing to a 5,000-card print run.
- **Students or job seekers** who need professional-looking cards for networking events on a tight budget.

**Skip it if** you need premium thick cards (this is 12pt, not 16pt linen or cotton), or if you expect a turnkey template experience.

**FAQ**

**Will this work in my printer?**
If your printer can handle 110 lb cardstock (most inkjets and lasers can), yes. Check your printer's paper thickness spec before buying. If it maxes out at 80 lb, this will jam.

**Can I print double-sided?**
Yes, but the perforations on the back side won't align perfectly with the front. Print both sides before separating — it's easier to align that way.

**Do I need special software to design cards?**
No. Canva's free tier has business card templates, and you can set up a custom 2" x 3.5" document in Word or Google Docs. Just add 0.125" bleed if you're printing edge-to-edge color.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need affordable, quality cards in small batches and don't mind setting up your own design. **Skip it if** you want premium thickness or a seamless template experience. At 4.2/5, this is the best value-per-card option in the blank cardstock category — just know the perforation consistency isn't perfect.
