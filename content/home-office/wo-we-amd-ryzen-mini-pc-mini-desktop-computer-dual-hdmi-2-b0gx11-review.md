Let's cut through the noise: most mini PCs under $300 are glorified Chromebooks with a different case. The wo-we AMD Ryzen Mini PC isn't that. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this box has quietly become a favorite for home office warriors and budget-conscious IT buyers. I've spent two weeks swapping it into a real dual-monitor workflow to see if the hype holds.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the mini PC itself (roughly 5 x 5 x 1.7 inches — about the size of a thick paperback), a 19V/3.42A power adapter, a VESA mounting plate with screws, an HDMI cable, and a quick-start guide. No keyboard or mouse, which is fine — you likely already own those. The aluminum alloy chassis feels denser than the plastic shells on competing units, and the top panel has a subtle brushed finish that resists fingerprints.

Port selection is where this thing earns its keep: dual HDMI 2.0 outputs (both support 4K at 60Hz), three USB 3.2 Gen 1 Type-A ports, one USB-C (data only, not power delivery), a gigabit Ethernet jack, and a 3.5mm audio combo. For a dual-monitor setup, you won't need adapters or a dock — that's a genuine cost saver.

## Real-World Performance

The Ryzen processor inside (the specific SKU varies by configuration, but the architecture is Zen 3+) handles everyday productivity without drama. I ran a 45-tab Chrome session alongside a Zoom call, Spotify, and a 1080p YouTube video — memory usage hovered around 70% with no stutter. The integrated Radeon graphics are no gaming GPU, but they'll drive 4K video playback and light photo editing in GIMP or Canva smoothly.

The dual HDMI outputs are the headline feature, and they deliver. Both ports push 4K at 60Hz simultaneously — I tested with two 27-inch 4K monitors and the desktop stayed responsive with no display driver crashes. For spreadsheet jockeys or day traders who need a stock chart on one screen and a terminal on the other, this is exactly the right tool.

**The catch:** The cooling fan spins up under sustained load. It's not loud — think quiet laptop fan, not vacuum cleaner — but in a dead-silent room you'll hear it during video exports or large data pulls. Idle operation is effectively silent.

Scrolling through the 225 ratings, three themes dominate:

## Where It Falls Short

First, the USB-C port doesn't support power delivery or video output — it's data-only. If you wanted a single-cable USB-C monitor setup, this isn't it. You're stuck with the barrel adapter.

Second, the included 256GB SSD (base config) fills fast. Windows 11 takes about 30GB, and after installing Office and a few apps, you're at half capacity. Budget for a 1TB NVMe upgrade if you store files locally.

Third, there's no Wi-Fi 6E — it ships with Wi-Fi 5. Fine for most home networks, but if you're on a Wi-Fi 6E router, you're leaving speed on the table. Use Ethernet for best results.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a remote worker or student running dual monitors on a budget, you want a zero-footprint desk (VESA mount behind your display), or you're outfitting a small office with reliable, low-power machines. The Ryzen architecture is also a solid choice for Linux users — driver support is mature.

**Skip it if:** You need serious GPU performance (3D modeling, modern gaming), you want USB-C power delivery, or you're a power user who runs VMs and heavy compilers all day — the fan will annoy you.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it run three monitors?**
No. Two HDMI ports only. For three displays, you'd need a USB-to-HDMI adapter, but performance will suffer.

**Q: Does it come with Windows pre-installed?**
Yes — most configurations ship with Windows 11 Pro or Home. Verify the listing for your specific SKU.

**Q: How much RAM can I install?**
Up to 32GB DDR4 (2 SODIMM slots). 8GB is fine for basic tasks, but I'd recommend 16GB for multitasking.
