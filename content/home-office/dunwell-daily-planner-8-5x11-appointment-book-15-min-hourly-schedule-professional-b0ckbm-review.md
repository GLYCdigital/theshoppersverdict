If you're a professional whose day runs on 15-minute increments — a therapist, attorney, medical scheduler, or project manager — you already know the frustration of trying to squeeze a 9:15 appointment into a planner built for hourly blocks. The Dunwell Daily Planner, 8.5x11 Appointment Book with 15-minute hourly scheduling exists specifically to solve that problem. And for the most part, it nails it.

## What You Actually Get

This is a daily-page planner, not a weekly spread. Each dated page gives you a full day with time slots running from early morning through evening in 15-minute increments — that's 96 individual scheduling blocks per day. The 8.5x11-inch format means each slot gets real vertical space, roughly a quarter-inch tall, which is enough to write a patient name, procedure code, or meeting location legibly without resorting to shorthand hieroglyphics.

The binding is a twin-wire spiral that lays completely flat when open. The cover is a flexible, textured cardstock that feels durable enough to survive a year in a work bag. The back cover includes a pocket for loose notes, business cards, or receipts. There's also a year-at-a-glance page at the front and a notes section in the back.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The standout feature here is the paper. Dunwell specs this at 120gsm — that's noticeably heavier than the 70-80gsm you'll find in most mass-market planners. What that means in practice: if you use a fountain pen, gel pen, or even a heavy-handed ballpoint, there's no ghosting and no bleed-through to the next page. That's a rare quality at this price point.

The 15-minute layout is the real draw. As shown in the product image, the time column runs down the left edge with clear separators between each quarter-hour. The grid lines are printed in a light gray that stays visible without competing with your handwriting. Each day page also includes a small "top three priorities" box at the top — a thoughtful addition for people who want to distinguish between appointments and actual tasks.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews reflects a consistent pattern. One reviewer noted, *"Finally a planner that understands therapy scheduling. Multiple reviewers in healthcare and legal fields specifically cited the 15-minute increments as the reason they bought this over competitors. The lay-flat binding also gets repeated mentions — people appreciate not having to hold pages down while writing.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, this is a large, desk-bound planner. At 8.5x11 inches, it won't fit in a standard purse or a briefcase's interior slip pocket. It's meant to live on a desk and travel in a tote bag or backpack.

Second, there are no monthly overview pages. You get the year-at-a-glance at the front and then it's straight into daily spreads. If you need to see your whole month at a glance for capacity planning, you'll need a separate calendar or the companion monthly version.

Third, some users have noted the date range is January through December — if you buy mid-year, you'll have a stack of unused pages before you reach the current date. There's no undated option.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a therapist, counselor, medical scheduler, attorney, or consultant who lives in 15-30 minute blocks. You want a permanent, dated record of your day. You use nicer pens and care about paper quality. You work primarily from a desk or carry a large bag.

**Skip it if:** You need month-at-a-glance planning, you want a portable planner for on-the-go scheduling, or you only need hourly blocks and want a smaller format.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this planner work for a full year, and when does it start?**
A: Yes, it's a dated daily planner covering January through December. There's no mid-year start option, so if you purchase in July, you'll flip through six months of unused pages.

**Q: Can I use it for both appointments and to-do lists?**
A: Yes — each daily page has a "top three priorities" box at the top and the time slots themselves can double as task blocks. However, there's no dedicated long-form notes section on each day page.

**Q: How does the paper handle highlighters?**
A: The 120gsm paper resists bleed-through well, but highlighter ink can still ghost slightly on the reverse side. If you're heavy-handed with highlighters, test on a corner page first.
