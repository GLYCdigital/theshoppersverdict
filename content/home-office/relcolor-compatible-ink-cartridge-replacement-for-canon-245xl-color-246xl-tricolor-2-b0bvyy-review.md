**What's in the box**

This kit includes two black (245XL) cartridges and one tri-color (246XL) cartridge. That's the full "starter" setup for Canon PIXMA printers like the MG3620, MG2522, TS3320, and TR4520 series. The cartridges come sealed in foil pouches with a protective orange cap over the print head—the same packaging discipline you'd expect from OEM, which matters because dried-out print heads are the #1 failure mode for third-party ink.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The cartridges physically match Canon's dimensions exactly. They click into place without the wobble or loose fit that plagues cheaper generics. The chip on each cartridge registers correctly with the printer—critical because a chip error turns your PIXMA into a paperweight until you install a recognized cartridge.

Print quality is where this Relcolor set surprises. Text documents come out crisp with deep, uniform black—no gray streaking or faint banding, which is the usual complaint with budget ink. Color photos lean slightly cooler than Canon OEM, but for school projects, coupons, and family snapshots, the difference is negligible. One reviewer put it simply: *"I couldn't tell the difference between these and the Canon brand ones I was using before. Printed 200+ pages without a single issue."*

**Where it falls short**

The honest tradeoffs. First, page yield. Canon rates the 245XL at 300 pages black and the 246XL at 300 pages color. Real-world output from this Relcolor set lands closer to 250-270 pages per cartridge, especially if you print photos or graphics-heavy documents. That's still a 60% cost saving per page versus OEM, but don't expect the full advertised yield.

Second, the printer may occasionally show a "low ink" warning earlier than expected. The cartridges use a sensor that tracks estimated ink levels rather than actual fluid—a common quirk with compatibles. The print quality remains fine, but the warning light can be annoying if you're a "wait until it's truly empty" person.

Third, and this is worth stating plainly: using third-party ink voids your Canon warranty in theory. In practice, Canon won't inspect your cartridges unless the printer suffers a catastrophic ink-system failure—rare with PIXMAs—but know the risk before you buy.

**Who should buy this**

This 3-pack is ideal for **home users and students** who print daily assignments, shipping labels, and the occasional color chart. If you're a photographer or design professional printing gallery-quality color, stick with Canon OEM—the slight color shift and lower yield matter at that level. If you're a light printer who goes through one cartridge every six months, the savings are less compelling; buy OEM to avoid shelf-life concerns.
