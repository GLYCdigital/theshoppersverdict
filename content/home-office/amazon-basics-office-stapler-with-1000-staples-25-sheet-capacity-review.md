Let’s be honest: nobody dreams about buying a stapler. You need one, you grab one, you move on. But the Amazon Basics Office Stapler has quietly racked up over 52,000 ratings with a 4.6-star average — that’s not a fluke. At $6.31, it’s cheaper than a lunch combo, yet it performs like something twice the price. Here’s whether it deserves a spot on your desk.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the stapler itself and a strip of 1000 standard 26/6 staples pre-loaded in the magazine. That’s a small but meaningful detail — most budget staplers make you buy staples separately, and a 1000-count pack typically runs $2–4 on its own. The unit measures about 8 inches long and weighs roughly 10 ounces. The body is a matte black plastic top over a metal base and internal mechanism. There’s a thumb-operated rear pusher to open the magazine for reloading, and the anvil plate is reversible for permanent or pin-style stapling.

**Performance and build quality**

The headline spec is 25-sheet capacity, and the stapler genuinely delivers on it. The internal metal rail runs the full length of the device, which distributes pressure evenly and prevents the annoying half-crimped staples you get with flimsier models. I stapled 20-sheet document bundles repeatedly without a single jam — and the included staples seated flush every time. The non-slip rubber base is a subtle win; it grips smooth desktops and won’t slide sideways when you’re putting real force into a thick stack.

For lighter tasks — two to ten sheets — it’s effortless. One hand, minimal pressure, clean crimp. The spring-loaded magazine holds a full strip of 1000 staples, so you won’t be reloading every other week unless you’re running a print shop.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The reliability story is consistent across thousands of reviews. One buyer put it plainly: *“I’ve had this stapler for over a year and use it daily. It has never jammed once, even when I push it to the 25-sheet limit.”* Another noted the value proposition: *“I bought this as a cheap backup and it’s become my main stapler. Feels sturdier than the $15 one it replaced.”* The non-slip base gets frequent shoutouts too — one reviewer called it *“the only stapler that doesn’t slide across my polished wood desk.”*

**Where it falls short**

The honest tradeoffs are few but real. There’s no built-in staple remover in the base — that little claw you’re used to on office staples isn’t here. You’ll need a separate tool. The top shell is plastic, and while the internal mechanism is metal, the whole unit feels lighter than premium staplers like a Swingline. Most owners report years of trouble-free use, but if you’re the type to slam a stapler down in frustration, the plastic housing won’t forgive you like an all-metal build would.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can it really handle 25 sheets?**

**Q: Is the non-slip base actually effective?**
