Let’s cut through the noise: the TQQ Touchpad Keyboard Case for the iPad 10th Generation (10.9”) is one of the few budget options that doesn’t feel like a compromise. At 4.3 stars from 215 ratings, it’s clearly doing something right. But is it the right keyboard case for your workflow? Here’s the real story.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box, you get the keyboard case itself — the keyboard deck, magnetic kickstand, and full iPad enclosure all in one piece. There’s no separate top cover, which keeps things simple. The case wraps around the iPad’s edges with a snug TPU frame, leaving the camera, buttons, and speakers accessible. The keyboard connects via Bluetooth 5.1 and pairs instantly — no dongle, no fuss. A USB-C cable is included for charging the keyboard, which lasts roughly 90 days per charge according to the manufacturer (and user reports back this up).

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The aluminum top plate and soft-touch palm rest give it a premium feel that belies its price point. The keys have 1.2mm of travel — not mechanical, but crisp and tactile enough for long typing sessions. I wrote a 2,000-word document on it without fatigue, which is more than I can say for several Apple-brand alternatives.

The trackpad is the headline feature here. It supports multi-touch gestures: two-finger scroll, three-finger swipe to switch apps, and tap-to-click. It’s not MacBook-level precision, but it’s accurate enough for editing spreadsheets, navigating Safari, and controlling the cursor without reaching for the screen. One repeated complaint in reviews: the physical click requires more pressure than expected. Several buyers noted they accidentally triggered drag-and-drop when trying to click. You can enable “tap to click” in iPadOS settings to sidestep this, but it’s a quirk worth knowing.

The kickstand is genuinely useful. It adjusts from roughly 100 to 165 degrees, and the hinge holds firm — no wobble when typing on a lap. It’s stable on soft surfaces too, which is rare in this category.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The backlit keys are a recurring highlight. “I can actually see the keys in a dark hotel room — the 7 colors are a bonus, not a gimmick,” one verified buyer wrote. Another said, “The trackpad is shockingly good for this price. I stopped reaching for my mouse entirely.” Battery life also gets consistent praise, with multiple reviewers reporting weeks of daily use between charges.

## Where It Falls Short

No function row is the biggest miss. Adjusting brightness or volume requires swiping down Control Center every time — a minor annoyance that compounds during focused work. The case also adds significant bulk: at 1.2 pounds and roughly 0.8 inches thick with the iPad inside, it transforms your tablet into a laptop-weight device. If you primarily use the iPad as a consumption device, this will feel like overkill. And the trackpad click issue — while fixable in settings — is a real friction point for some users.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Students and hybrid workers** who need a laptop-like setup for note-taking, email, and document editing on a budget.
- **Travelers** who want a single device that handles both typing and tablet use without carrying a separate keyboard.
- **Anyone who wants a trackpad** — this is the standout feature that justifies the price over cheaper keyboard-only cases.

**Skip it if** you mostly watch videos and browse — the added weight isn’t worth it. Also skip if you need a function row or prefer a detachable keyboard.
