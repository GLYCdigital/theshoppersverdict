**If you've got shoeboxes of old photos gathering dust, the Epson FastFoto FF-680W is the closest thing to a time machine you can plug into a wall.** I've tested dozens of scanners, and this one solves the single biggest pain point of digitizing family archives: speed. It's not perfect — nothing at this price point is — but it's the only consumer scanner I've used that makes digitizing 1,000 photos feel like a weekend project instead of a month-long slog.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the scanner itself, a 100V-240V power adapter, a USB cable, a setup guide, and a software CD (yes, in 2026, it still ships with a disc — but you can download the drivers from Epson's site instead). The unit measures 11.9 x 6.4 x 5.2 inches and weighs 6.6 pounds, so it's compact enough to live on a shelf between uses. The input tray holds up to 30 photos, and the output tray catches them face-down after scanning.

## Performance: The Numbers That Matter

Epson claims one photo per second at 300 DPI. In my testing, that's accurate for standard 4x6 prints. A full 30-photo stack takes about 35 seconds from start to finish. Documents scan even faster — a 10-page stack of letter-size paper finished in 15 seconds.

The key feature here is the **automatic photo feeder**. Unlike flatbed scanners where you lift the lid, position the photo, scan, and repeat, the FF-680W pulls each photo through a roller mechanism. It handles mixed sizes — 3x5, 4x6, 5x7, even polaroids — without you needing to adjust guides between scans. The bundled Epson ScanSmart software automatically detects photo edges, rotates them correctly, and applies color restoration to faded prints.

I ran a stack of 1980s Kodak prints with yellowed edges and faded faces through it. The color restoration feature brought back skin tones that were nearly orange in the originals. It's not magic — heavily damaged photos will still show their age — but for typical "kept in a shoebox for 30 years" condition, results are genuinely impressive.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews at 4.5 stars (225 ratings) point to the same strengths I found. One verified buyer wrote: *"Scanned 2,000 photos over a weekend. My wife cried when she saw the restored colors on her grandparents' wedding photos."* Another noted: *"The Wi-Fi setup took 10 minutes and I scan directly to my iPad now. No computer needed."*

That wireless capability is a differentiator. You can scan directly to a phone or tablet via the Epson Smart Panel app, which is genuinely useful if your family archives live on a shared tablet. The scanner also supports scanning to cloud services like Google Drive and Dropbox without a PC.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**The feed mechanism is the weak point.** Multiple reviewers report the rollers eventually wear out or pick up dust, causing jams after 6-12 months of heavy use. Replacement roller kits cost around $40 and require opening the scanner with a screwdriver — not difficult, but it's an added cost and hassle you should budget for.

**The 36-inch scan limit is a real constraint.** If you have panoramic photos longer than that — common in the 1970s and 1980s — you'll need to scan them in sections and stitch them together. Doable, but annoying.

**No automatic date detection.** The software won't read handwritten dates on photo backs or EXIF data (old prints don't have it). You'll be manually renaming files or sorting into folders if chronology matters to you. For a $600 scanner, this feels like a missed opportunity.

**It's not a document powerhouse.** While it scans documents up to 8.5 x 36 inches, it lacks duplex (two-sided) scanning for documents. You'll flip pages manually for double-sided paperwork. That's fine for occasional use, but if you're digitizing a filing cabinet, a dedicated document scanner does this better.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You have more than 500 physical photos you've been meaning to digitize
- You value speed over pixel-perfect reproduction (this isn't a professional archival scanner)
- You want a single device that handles both photos and occasional documents
- You're willing to do basic maintenance (cleaning rollers) to extend its life

**Skip it if:**
- You have fewer than 200 photos — a $150 flatbed scanner is more cost-effective
- You need professional-grade archival quality at 1200+ DPI for museum-level reproduction
- Your primary need is high-volume document scanning with duplex capability
- You're on a tight budget — this is a premium tool for a specific job

## FAQ

**Q: Does the FF-680W scan photos with text on the back (like dated stamps)?**
A: No. It's a single-sided scanner. You'd need to flip each photo and scan the back separately if you want to preserve that information. Most users skip this and rely on the software's auto-organization tools.

**Q: Can I use it without installing software on my computer?**
A: Yes, but with limits. The scanner supports Wi-Fi Direct, so you can scan to a phone or tablet via the Epson Smart Panel app without a PC. For full features (color restoration, edge detection, batch editing), you'll want the desktop software installed on a Mac or Windows machine.

**Q: How does it handle curled or damaged photos?**
A: Poorly. The feeder requires relatively flat photos. Curled, creased, or torn photos will jam or scan with shadows. You'll need to manually flatten these or use the flatbed mode (which this scanner doesn't have — it's feeder-only). For damaged photos, consider a separate flatbed scanner or a professional service.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're drowning in old photos and want this project done this year, not in five years. **Skip it if** you're a casual user with a small collection or need professional archival reproduction.

The Epson FastFoto FF-680W earns its 4.5-star rating. It's the best consumer photo scanner on the market for speed and ease of use, with software that genuinely improves old prints. The feed mechanism durability and lack of duplex scanning are real downsides, but for its primary purpose — digitizing family photo collections quickly — nothing else comes close.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Recommended for anyone with a serious photo-digitization project ahead of them.
