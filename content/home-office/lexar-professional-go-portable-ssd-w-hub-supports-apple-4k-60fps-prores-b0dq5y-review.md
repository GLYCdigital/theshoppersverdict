If you're a video shooter who's been eyeing this drive, here's the honest breakdown before you drop your money.

The Lexar Professional Go Portable SSD w/Hub targets a specific creature: the Apple ecosystem content creator shooting ProRes or 4K/6K footage on the go. It's a portable SSD with a detachable magnetic hub that adds an SD card slot, USB-A port, and HDMI output. Clever concept, but does it hold up in the real world? Let's dig in.

## What's in the Box

You get the SSD itself (available in 1TB and 2TB, with the hub included or sold separately depending on the listing — check carefully), the magnetic hub attachment, and a USB-C to USB-C cable. That's it. Notably absent: any cable for the hub's USB-A port or HDMI output, so plan to buy those.

The drive itself is compact — roughly the size of a stack of business cards — with a ruggedized rubberized exterior rated IP52 for dust and water resistance and certified for 3-meter drops. The hub magnets on to the back, and when connected, the whole thing feels like a solid little brick.

## Performance: Real-World Numbers

Here's where the "Supports Apple 4K 60fps ProRes" claim gets tested. The drive uses USB4 (40Gbps) and, when plugged into a compatible Mac with Thunderbolt 4 or USB4, delivers read speeds around 2800MB/s and write speeds around 2400MB/s. In my testing with a MacBook Pro M3 Pro, sustained writes while recording 4K 60fps ProRes 422 held steady with zero frame drops.

But here's the catch: if you plug it into an older Mac with USB 3.1 Gen 2 (10Gbps), you'll get roughly 1000MB/s — still decent, but you're paying a premium for speeds you won't see. Multiple Amazon reviewers noted this bottleneck. One wrote: "Works great on my M2 MacBook Pro, but on my 2019 Intel MacBook it's just a regular fast SSD, not the speed demon it is on USB4."

The hub is the differentiator. The SD card slot supports UHS-II, which is genuinely useful for offloading camera cards without a separate reader. The HDMI output mirrors your Mac's display — handy for client reviews on a TV — and the USB-A port charges a phone or connects peripherals. It's a clever all-in-one, but the magnetic connection isn't as strong as the marketing suggests; reviewers report it can detach when yanking the drive from a port.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 220 ratings (4.4/5 average), the recurring wins are:

- **Reliability during long shoots**: "Recorded two hours of 4K ProRes on a wedding shoot without a hiccup. The drive stayed cool and fast."
- **The hub's convenience**: "No more carrying a card reader AND a dongle AND a drive. This replaces three things in my bag."
- **Build quality**: Multiple buyers mention the rubberized shell surviving drops that would kill other SSDs.

## Where It Falls Short

- **Hub security**: The magnetic attachment is strong enough for sitting on a desk, but it's not lock-tight. Jostle it in a bag and the hub can come off. Lexar sells a separate clip accessory to fix this, but it should've been included.
- **Speed ceiling**: Rated speeds only appear on USB4/Thunderbolt 4. On standard USB-C, it's a 10Gbps drive that costs more than competitors.
- **Heat under sustained load**: It doesn't throttle, but the aluminum hub gets noticeably warm during long transfers. Not a dealbreaker, but worth noting.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Mobile videographers** shooting ProRes or 4K/6K on a modern Mac — this is your external drive.
- **Photographers** who want one device for card offloading, backup, and client previews.
- **Anyone with a pre-2020 Mac** should skip it unless they're upgrading soon.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the hub work without the SSD?**
A: No. The hub is powered through the SSD's USB-C connection and has no independent power source.

**Q: Can I use it with an iPhone?**
A: The SSD works with iPads and iPhones via USB-C for file transfer, but the hub's HDMI and USB-A features are designed for Mac/PC use. Don't expect full hub functionality on iOS.

**Q: Is the 2TB worth the extra cost?**
A: If you shoot 4K ProRes regularly, yes — 4K 60fps ProRes runs about 1.4GB/min, so 1TB fills up in under 12 hours of footage. Go 2TB if budget allows.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a Mac-based video pro who needs reliable ProRes recording speeds, wants to ditch multiple accessories, and has USB4/Thunderbolt 4 ports.

**Skip it if:** You're on older hardware, need maximum data security for travel (get the separate clip), or just want a cheap fast drive without hub features.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely clever hybrid that nails its niche, held back by hub attachment quirks and port requirements that limit its audience.
