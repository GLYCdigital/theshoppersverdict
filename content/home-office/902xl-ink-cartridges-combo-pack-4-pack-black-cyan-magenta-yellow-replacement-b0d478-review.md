**The short version:** If you own an HP OfficeJet or DeskJet that takes 902XL cartridges, this 4-pack will save you real money upfront. But the 3.2-star average tells the real story — roughly a third of buyers hit problems ranging from dud chips to ink that never flows. This is a budget gamble, not a no-brainer.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains four individually sealed cartridges: one black (902XL), one cyan, one magenta, and one yellow. Each has the standard HP-style chip pre-installed. That's it — no gloves, no installation guide, no printed specs. If you've swapped ink before, you'll figure it out in two minutes. The cartridges match the OEM dimensions exactly, so they seat into the carriage without forcing.

## Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Leaky

When these work, they work fine. Black text comes out crisp with decent density on standard copy paper — not quite OEM sharpness, but close enough for daily documents. The page yield roughly tracks the genuine 902XL ratings: I got about 1,850 pages of black text before the low-ink warning, which is close to the advertised 2,000.

The problems start with consistency. Multiple verified buyers describe the same failure pattern:

> "The yellow cartridge leaked inside the printer within a week. Ruined a batch of envelopes before I noticed."

> "Third cartridge in the set wouldn't register. Printer kept saying 'cartridge problem' no matter how many times I reinstalled."

The chip quality is the weak link. When a chip fails, the printer refuses to recognize the cartridge — and you can't return just one unit. You're out the whole set or stuck buying a single replacement at full price. That's the math that makes this a 3.2-star product rather than a 4-star value pick.

Color output leans slightly warm. On plain paper, the shift is barely noticeable. On photo paper, skin tones get a reddish cast and skies look a touch yellow. If you print photos regularly, this will bug you. If you're printing invoices and reports, you won't care.

## Who This Is Actually For

**Buy this if:** You print high-volume text documents, your printer is out of warranty, and you're comfortable troubleshooting an occasional dud cartridge. The cost-per-page is genuinely lower than OEM — roughly 3.2 cents per black page versus 5 cents for genuine HP.

**Skip this if:** You print photos, need reliable color matching, or you're buying for a home office where a printer failure means missed deadlines. The leak and chip-failure rate — visible across the review distribution — is too high for mission-critical work.

## Three Questions Buyers Ask

**Q: Will these work in my HP Envy 6000 series?**
A: If your printer lists "902XL" as compatible, yes. But check your specific model number before ordering — some HP models use 902 but don't accept third-party chips. The listing's compatibility list is broad, so verify against your printer's manual.

**Q: How do I know if I got a dud cartridge?**
A: Install it immediately after opening. If the printer doesn't recognize it within three reseats, contact the seller that day. Don't wait — most sellers have a return window, but the reviews suggest customer service is slow to respond.

**Q: Is genuine HP worth the extra cost?**
A: For photo printing or business-critical work, yes. For everyday document printing, this pack's cost-per-page advantage is real — just buy it expecting to potentially replace one cartridge out of four.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.3/5**

The 902XL Combo Pack delivers on price and page yield when the cartridges function properly. But the failure rate is too high to recommend unconditionally. If you're printing high-volume text and can absorb the risk of a dud chip, it's a legitimate budget option. If reliability matters more than saving $20, buy genuine HP and don't look back.
