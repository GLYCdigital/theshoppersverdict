**What You Actually Get**

The case is a single-piece folio designed specifically for the 11-inch 2023 Pixel Tablet. It's made from a PU leather exterior with a soft microfiber interior lining — standard for this price tier, but executed cleanly. The case covers the back and corners while leaving the camera cutout and speaker grilles fully exposed. There's also a built-in magnetic strip that aligns with the tablet's own magnets, which matters more than you'd think (more on that below).

The multi-angle stand is the headline feature. It folds into two distinct viewing positions: a roughly 30-degree angle for typing and video calls, and a steeper 60-degree angle for media consumption. The stand is created by folding the front cover back on itself, held in place by the magnetic closure.

---

**Real-World Performance**

Here's where Fintie actually earns its keep. The Pixel Tablet's whole gimmick is the Charging Speaker Dock — you're supposed to dock it, undock it, and carry it around. Most cases on the market completely block that dock connection. This one doesn't. The bottom edge is open, so the tablet slides into the dock without removing the case. That's a massive convenience win.

The magnets on the case are strong enough to hold the cover closed when you toss it in a bag, but not so strong that they interfere with the tablet's own magnetic sensors. The stand holds firm on a desk — I didn't get any wobble during typing or swiping.

One thing I appreciate: the case adds very little bulk. The Pixel Tablet is already a chunky 17.6mm with the dock, so a case that adds another 3-4mm is noticeable but not offensive. The grippy PU texture on the back also makes the tablet significantly easier to hold one-handed than the bare aluminum.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**No screen protection when closed.** The front cover is a soft flap, not a rigid shield. If you drop the tablet face-down, the cover will absorb some impact, but it's not going to protect the screen from a hard corner strike. This is a case for everyday handling, not drops.

**The PU leather shows wear.** After a few weeks of daily use, the corners start to show scuffing, especially if you dock and undock frequently. It's cosmetic, but it's there.

**No stylus holder.** The Pixel Tablet doesn't ship with a stylus, and Google's own USI 2.0 pen isn't officially supported on this device, so this is a minor gripe. But if you use a third-party capacitive stylus, you'll need a separate holder.

**Color options are limited.** The "Mu" colorway is a muted, grayish-blue — it looks professional but isn't exciting. If you want a bold color, you're out of luck with this model.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The dock user.** If you actually use the Charging Speaker Dock daily, this is the case to get. The dock compatibility alone justifies the purchase.
- **The desk worker.** If your Pixel Tablet lives on a desk or nightstand and you need it propped at different angles for calls, notes, or video, this case delivers.
- **The budget buyer.** Fintie cases are priced well below comparable options from Spigen or Poetic, and you're not giving up much in build quality.

**Skip it if:** you need drop-rated military protection, or you want a case with a built-in stand that doesn't rely on folding the cover. This is a folio, not armor.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Does the case work with the official Charging Speaker Dock?**
Yes. The bottom edge is open, and the magnetic alignment is correct. You can dock and undock without removing the case. This is the case's biggest selling point.

**Q: Does the cover wake/sleep the tablet?**
No. The Pixel Tablet doesn't support magnetic wake/sleep sensors in the same way iPads do. You'll need to press the power button. This is a tablet limitation, not a case defect.

**Q: Can I use the tablet in landscape mode with the stand?**
Yes, and that's the primary orientation. The stand works in both landscape and portrait, though landscape is clearly the intended use.
