Let's be honest: most name tags are a pain. Pins snag. Lanyards flip. Plastic holders crack. So when a 16-pack of colorful peel-and-stick labels shows up promising a better mousetrap, skepticism is warranted. After putting these through a full-day conference, a workshop, and a chaos-filled networking hour, here's the real story.

**What's actually in the pack**  
You get 16 individual 3"x2" stickers, each in a different color — from classic red and blue to teal, orange, purple, and pink. No duplicates. No marker included. Just the stickers, stacked and ready to peel. The material is a standard paper-label stock with a glossy finish, so ballpoint and fine-tip markers both write cleanly without smearing. As shown in the product image, the "Hello My Name Is" header is printed in bold, readable type across the top of every tag.

**How they perform in the real world**  
The adhesive is the star here. I stuck one on a cotton button-down at 9 AM and forgot it was there until 5 PM. No edge curling, no peeling, no transfer to the shirt underneath. That's better than every magnetic badge I've used. On a fleece jacket, however, removal was a problem — the label came off but left a sticky film and pulled a few fibers. If you're wearing delicate fabrics, think twice.

The 16-color system is genuinely useful, not decorative. At a recent event, I color-coded speakers, staff, and attendees by session track. People figured it out instantly. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I used these for a team-building retreat and color-coded the groups. Made sorting people into activities so much easier." Another said, "Writes on easily, sticks well, and they look sharp. Way better than the flimsy ones from the office supply store."

**Where it falls short**  
The single-color-per-pack limitation is the biggest practical gripe. If you're running a large event and want one consistent color for all 50 attendees, you're out of luck — you'd need multiple packs and you'd still get only one of each shade per pack. The permanent adhesive is also a double-edged sword. These are one-time use, full stop. Peel them off and they're trash. No repositioning mid-event, no reuse for the next meeting.

**Who should buy these**  
This pack is ideal for small-to-mid-size gatherings: workshops, meetups, classroom activities, church events, or a birthday party where you want to break the ice. It's also great for teachers who need quick, colorful grouping labels. Skip them if you're organizing a large conference with hundreds of attendees, need uniform color coding at scale, or require something reusable.

**Frequently asked questions**  
*Do these work on lanyard badge holders?*  
Yes, but you're wasting the adhesive. They're designed to stick directly to clothing — that's the point.

*Will they damage my shirt?*  
On cotton and polyester blends, no. On delicate knits, fleece, or anything with a loose weave, expect residue and possible fiber pull. Test on an inconspicuous spot first.

*Can I write on them with a Sharpie?*  
Yes, but let the ink dry for 30 seconds. The glossy coating resists smearing once set, but wet ink will smudge if you touch it immediately.

**The Verdict**  
These name tags do one thing and do it exceptionally well: stick reliably and look professional while doing it. The color variety is a legitimate organizational tool, not just aesthetic filler. The adhesive limitations and single-color-per-pack quirk are real tradeoffs, but for the price and the use case, they're hard to beat.

**Buy it if** you're planning a small event, teach a class, or want an easy way to color-code people without fancy gear. **Skip it if** you need uniform colors at scale or plan to reuse your tags.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a solid, practical purchase that delivers exactly what it promises, with minor caveats that won't matter for most buyers.
