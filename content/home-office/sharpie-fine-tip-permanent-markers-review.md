## Sharpie Fine Point Permanent Markers Review

Let's get one thing straight: not having Sharpie Permanent Markers near or in your work area is like having a house without toilet paper. That's not my line — that's from one of the 44,828 reviews backing this icon with a 4.8-star average. The Sharpie Fine Point is less a writing instrument and more a cultural institution. At $8.99 for a 12-pack, it's also one of the easiest purchasing decisions you'll make all year.

## The Good

**Legendary versatility.** Sharpie writes on everything. Cardboard, plastic, metal, glass, fabric, CD-Rs, rubber, wood, sneakers, your kid's "I'll just use a Sharpie" arts and crafts project gone rogue. The ink chemistry is formulated to bond with non-porous surfaces in a way that cheaper markers simply can't match. That "WATERPROOF, DEPENDABLE & PERFECT" review isn't hyperbole — it's the product description.

**The black is properly black.** Not charcoal, not dark grey — actual deep, permanent black. The fine tip (0.8mm) gives you enough control for neat labels on shipping boxes and filing folders, while still laying down a bold line that you can read from across a warehouse. Multiple reviewers note they're "always reliable," and consistent ink flow is something you don't appreciate until you pick up a marker that doesn't deliver it.

**Build quality is timeless.** Sharpie hasn't messed with a winning formula. The barrel, the cap, the snap, the nib — it's been the same for decades because it works. The cap clicks securely and the marker doesn't dry out between uses as long as you store it properly. For $8.99 per dozen, you're buying tools that outlast their expected lifespan.

**Value is undeniable.** At 75 cents per marker, you can afford to lose a few under your desk, give them to colleagues who "borrow" them, and still have a full set in your drawer. Compared to specialty markers that cost $3-$5 each for similar performance, the Sharpie 12-pack is an absurdly efficient purchase.

## The Bad

**Bleed-through is real.** Sharpie ink is designed to penetrate and bond, which means it goes straight through thin paper. If you're writing on printer paper, notebook pages, or anything less than 80gsm, expect ghosting on the other side. For cardboard boxes and shipping labels, it's perfect. For bullet journals and fine stationery — bring a backup.

**The smell hasn't aged well.** That distinctive Sharpie aroma is nostalgia for some and a headache trigger for others. The ink is alcohol-based and the fumes are potent, especially in enclosed spaces. If you're doing extended marking sessions in a small room, crack a window or switch to a low-odor alternative.

**Cap discipline is mandatory.** Leave the cap off for more than an hour, and you're done. The marker will dry out. This isn't a Sharpie-specific flaw — it's physics — but if you're the type to absentmindedly set down an uncapped marker, you'll lose some to neglect.

## The Verdict

The Sharpie Fine Point Permanent Marker is the default for a reason. With nearly 45,000 reviews averaging 4.8 stars, the consensus is overwhelming: these are the gold standard for permanent markers. The ink is bold, the tip is precise, the value is excellent, and the versatility is unmatched. Yes, the fumes are strong, paper bleed is a thing, and you need to cap them promptly. None of that changes the fact that every desk, every toolbox, and every shipping station needs a set.

At 75 cents per marker, you have no excuse not to buy them. Just don't run with them.

**Verdict: 4.8/5** — An essential tool. Buy the 12-pack and never borrow a marker again.
