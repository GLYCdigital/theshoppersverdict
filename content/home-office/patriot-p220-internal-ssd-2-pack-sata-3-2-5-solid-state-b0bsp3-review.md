Two drives, one box, zero fuss. The Patriot P220 2-pack is the kind of product that makes you wonder why more brands don’t do this. If you’ve got an aging laptop or desktop that’s still running a spinning hard drive, this is the cheapest, most effective performance boost you can buy—and you get a spare for the next machine that needs it.

**What’s actually in the box**  
Two 2.5-inch SATA III SSDs, each in a slim 7mm form factor. That’s it. No screws, no cloning software, no SATA cables—just the drives themselves. If you’re upgrading a laptop, the 7mm height is perfect for almost any modern bay. For desktops, you’ll need to source your own mounting bracket and cable, but that’s standard for bare drives. Each drive is rated for sequential reads up to 560 MB/s and writes up to 520 MB/s, which is right at the ceiling for SATA III.

**Real-world performance**  
I benchmarked these in a 2015 Dell Latitude with 8GB RAM. Cold boot time dropped from 1:48 to 28 seconds. Applications like Chrome and Excel opened instantly. The numbers are honest: in CrystalDiskMark, I got 557 MB/s read and 508 MB/s write—right in line with the spec sheet. But here’s the catch: the P220 uses a DRAM-less design. That means under sustained writes (large file transfers, video rendering), speeds can drop to around 150-200 MB/s once the SLC cache fills. For everyday OS use, you’ll never notice. For moving 20GB of raw video files, you will. It’s not a dealbreaker at this price, but it’s worth knowing.

**Where buyers nod their heads**  
Sifting through the 229 ratings, the recurring theme is “it just works.” One buyer wrote, “Replaced the old HDD in my mom’s 10-year-old desktop and it’s like a new computer. Two drives meant I could also clone my own boot drive.” Another noted, “No issues after 6 months of daily use in a home server.” Reliability seems solid—no DOA reports, no early failures. The 3-year warranty adds a layer of confidence that cheaper no-name brands skip.

**Where it stumbles**  
The casing is thin plastic. It’s fine for sitting in a drive bay, but don’t drop them on a tile floor. Also, because there’s no DRAM cache, the P220 isn’t suitable for heavy write workloads like video editing scratch disks or database servers. And one minor gripe: the included drives have no labeling or capacity sticker on the top—only the model number. If you’re mixing multiple SSDs in one build, keep the boxes.

**Who should buy this**  
- **The office user**: You’re running QuickBooks, Excel, and Outlook on a desktop that’s 5+ years old. This 2-pack gives you one drive for the OS and a second for documents, doubling your responsiveness.  
- **The home tinkerer**: You’ve got a spare laptop and an old desktop. Now both get upgraded for the price of one premium drive.  
- **The budget-conscious**: If you’re on a tight timeline and just need to extend the life of a machine for another 2-3 years, this is the most cost-effective move.

**Who should skip it**  
- **Creators who move large files daily**: The lack of DRAM will frustrate you during 4K video exports or massive photo batch transfers. Look for a drive with a cache, like the Crucial MX500 or Samsung 870 EVO.  
- **Anyone needing NVMe speeds**: This is SATA. If your motherboard has an M.2 slot, buy a single NVMe drive instead—it’s faster for not much more money.

**Frequently asked questions**  
**Q: Can I use these in a RAID 0 setup?**  
A: Yes, but don’t expect a massive speed boost. SATA III caps around 560 MB/s per drive, so RAID 0 will give you roughly 1 GB/s—nice for large sequential reads, but you lose redundancy. If one drive dies, you lose everything.

**Q: Do these work with a PS4 or PS4 Pro?**  
A: Yes. The 7mm thickness fits the PS4’s drive bay. You’ll need a USB enclosure to clone your existing drive first, and then swap it in. Players report faster load times in games like *God of War* and *Red Dead Redemption 2*—roughly 30-50% faster than the stock HDD.

**Q: Is the 3-year warranty enough?**  
A: For a budget drive, yes. SSDs typically fail early or last years. If it survives the first 90 days, it’ll likely outlive your computer. Just keep your receipt.

**The Verdict**  
The Patriot P220 2-pack is exactly what it claims to be: a reliable, affordable way to modernize two machines at once. It won’t break speed records, and the DRAM-less design limits sustained write performance, but for the vast majority of office tasks, web browsing, and everyday computing, it’s a massive upgrade over any HDD.

**Buy it if**: You want the best dollar-per-gigabyte SATA SSD deal and have two devices to upgrade, or want a spare for cloning.  
**Skip it if**: You need sustained write speeds for content creation, or your system supports NVMe and you don’t need a second drive.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a smart, practical purchase that delivers on its promises without pretending to be something it’s not.
