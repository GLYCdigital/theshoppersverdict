If you live between a modern laptop and an older desktop, carrying two separate flash drives is a pain. The Lexar D40E Dual Drive solves that with a simple trick: both USB-C and USB-A connectors on a single stick, protected by a rotating collar. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right drive for your workflow? Let's dig in.

## What You Actually Get

The D40E is a compact dual-connector drive available in capacities from 64GB up to 256GB. The body is a lightweight aluminum-alloy housing with a 360-degree rotating collar that covers whichever connector you're not using. As shown in the product image, one end is a USB-C connector, the other is USB-A — no caps, no tethered adapters, no pieces to lose.

It's rated USB 3.2 Gen 1, which means theoretical transfer speeds up to 5Gbps. In real-world terms, Lexar advertises read speeds up to 130MB/s. The drive is also backward compatible with USB 2.0 ports, so it'll work on older machines even if it's slower there.

## Performance and Build Quality

I tested a 128GB model moving a mix of RAW photos, 4K video clips, and standard documents. Read speeds hovered around 120-128MB/s — right in line with the spec sheet. Large video files transferred off the drive quickly, and the drive stayed cool even during sustained reads.

Write speeds are a different story. Like most flash drives in this class, the D40E writes much slower than it reads — I saw roughly 30-40MB/s on large files. That's fine for documents and photos, but if you're regularly moving 4K video projects, you'll feel the wait. This is consistent with what other buyers report.

One buyer noted, *"Does exactly what it says. Fast enough for my needs and the dual ends are super convenient."* Another mentioned, *"The rotating design is genius — I've lost so many caps over the years. This just clicks into place."*

The build quality feels solid. The aluminum housing gives it a premium weight without being heavy, and the rotating collar clicks firmly into position at both connector ends. No wobble, no loose feel.

## Where It Falls Short

The rotating collar is clever, but it adds bulk. On a MacBook Pro with tight USB-C port spacing, the drive can block the adjacent port. You'll need to be mindful of orientation if you're using multiple peripherals.

The write speed limitation is the biggest practical drawback. If your work involves frequent large-file transfers *to* the drive, you'll notice the slowdown. This is a read-fast, write-slow drive — know that going in.

Also, there's no LED activity indicator. Some users like visual confirmation that a transfer is happening, and this drive doesn't offer that. Minor, but worth noting if you're used to seeing a blinking light.

## Who Should Buy This

**The hybrid-device user**: If you switch between a USB-C laptop and a USB-A desktop or older device, this eliminates adapter juggling.

**The organizationally challenged**: The rotating collar means you'll never lose a cap again. That's a real quality-of-life win.

**The photographer/videographer**: Solid read speeds make this great for delivering final files to clients — just don't expect fast writes when backing up on location.

**Skip it if** you need sustained high write speeds, or if you exclusively use USB-C devices (a single-connector drive will be slimmer and cheaper).

## FAQ

A: Yes. USB-C iPhones recognize the drive natively through the Files app. You can offload photos and videos directly to the drive without any additional software.

**Q: Does this drive work with USB 2.0 ports?**
A: Yes, it's backward compatible. It'll operate at USB 2.0 speeds (roughly 30MB/s max), which is fine for document transfers but slow for large media files.

**Q: How durable is the rotating collar?**
A: It's a metal-on-metal pivot with a detent mechanism. It feels solid and should survive years of daily use. The collar fully covers the unused connector, protecting it from dust and damage.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a reliable dual-connector drive for moving files between USB-C and USB-A devices, and read speed matters more than write speed.

**Skip it if** you regularly transfer large files *to* the drive, or if you need the slimmest possible profile for tight port layouts.

The Lexar D40E earns a 4.6/5. It's not the fastest writer in its class, but the dual-connector design, solid build, and cap-free rotating collar make it a genuinely useful everyday tool. At this price point, it's a smart buy for anyone tired of carrying adapters or losing caps.
