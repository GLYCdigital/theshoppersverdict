**Bottom line: The GIGABYTE Z790 UD AC is the sensible middle child of Intel's LGA 1700 lineup — not flashy, not cheap-feeling, just a well-rounded DDR5 board that gets out of your way.**

Let's be honest about what this board is: it's the "UD" (Ultra Durable) tier, which means GIGABYTE stripped some RGB and PCIe 5.0 extras to hit a more accessible price point. But don't confuse that with lower quality. The PCB is a thick 6-layer design, and the VRM heatsinks have actual mass to them — not the flimsy stamped aluminum you see on budget boards.

## What's in the box

You get the board, two SATA cables, a Wi-Fi antenna kit that mounts on your rear I/O, M.2 screw packs, and a decent printed manual. No flashy extras, no SATA cable rainbow — just what you need. The antenna kit is worth calling out specifically: it's the magnetic base type that lets you position antennas on your desk or case, which genuinely improved my Wi-Fi signal from 2 bars to full strength in a room away from the router.

## Real-world performance

I paired this with an i5-13600K and 32GB of DDR5-6000. Out of the box, XMP profiles loaded without drama. The 12+1+1 phase VRM (60A per phase) is overkill for a 13600K and comfortable for an i7-13700K. During a 30-minute Cinebench R23 run, VRM temps peaked at 61°C with standard airflow in a Fractal case — no throttling, no drama.

The triple M.2 slots are the standout feature here. All three run at PCIe 4.0 x4, and each has its own heatsink. That's genuinely rare at this price point. I loaded a 2TB Gen4 drive in the top slot with the heatsink attached, and sustained write speeds stayed consistent through a 100GB file transfer — no thermal dip.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.3-star rating (215 reviews) tells a consistent story. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"Replaced an older Z690 board with this and the Wi-Fi 6 is a huge upgrade. Setup took 15 minutes and everything posted first try."* Another noted the M.2 layout: *"Finally a board where all three M.2 slots get heatsinks. My 980 Pro runs 10 degrees cooler than on my old board."*

The DDR5 support gets regular shoutouts too. People are running 6400MT/s kits with XMP without stability issues, which isn't a given on budget Z790 boards.

## Where it falls short

The audio codec is the weakest link. The ALC897 is fine for casual use, but if you're running decent headphones or desktop speakers, you'll hear the difference — it lacks the warmth and separation of higher-end codecs. Plan on a USB DAC if audio matters to you.

The 2.5GbE Realtek LAN has a known quirk: on fresh Windows 11 installs, you may need to manually update the driver from GIGABYTE's site because the Windows Update version can drop connection under load. It's a 10-minute fix, but it's a real friction point.

And yes, there's no PCIe 5.0 M.2 slot. If you're building for future-proofing with next-gen SSDs, this isn't your board. The GPU slot is PCIe 5.0 x16, so you're covered there, but storage tops out at Gen4.

## Who should buy this

**You, if:** You're building a mid-range to upper-mid-range Intel system — i5-13600K through i7-13700K — and want DDR5 without paying flagship prices. You're using Gen4 SSDs, you need reliable Wi-Fi 6 built in, and you don't care about RGB or PCIe 5.0 storage.

**Not you, if:** You need PCIe 5.0 M.2, you're an audiophile who refuses external DACs, or you're planning a 13900K/14900K with heavy overclocking — this board will handle stock but the VRM isn't built for extreme OC sessions.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this board support Intel 14th gen CPUs out of the box?**  
Yes. The Z790 chipset natively supports 12th, 13th, and 14th gen LGA 1700 processors. If you're on an older BIOS, update it first, but most retail units ship current enough.

**Q: How many fan headers does it have?**  
Six total — one CPU, one AIO pump, and four chassis. That's enough for a standard build with three case fans plus a 360mm AIO, though you may need a splitter for more than three case fans.

**Q: Does it come with Wi-Fi antennas in the box?**  
Yes, the external magnetic antenna kit is included. It's a meaningful upgrade over the stubby PCB antennas on cheaper boards.

## The Verdict

The GIGABYTE Z790 UD AC earns its 4.2/5 rating by nailing the fundamentals: solid VRMs, triple M.2 with heatsinks, reliable Wi-Fi 6, and clean DDR5 support. It's not a showpiece, and it has real gaps (audio codec, no Gen5 M.2), but for the vast majority of builders, it's the smart value pick.

**Buy it if** you want a dependable DDR5 foundation for a 12th-14th gen Intel build without paying for features you'll never use.  
**Skip it if** PCIe 5.0 storage or premium onboard audio are non-negotiables.
