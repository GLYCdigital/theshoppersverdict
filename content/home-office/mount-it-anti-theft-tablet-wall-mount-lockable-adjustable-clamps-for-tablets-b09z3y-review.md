The Mount-It! Anti-Theft Tablet Wall Mount isn't the cheapest way to put a tablet on a wall. It's not the sleekest either. But if you're securing a $400 iPad to a retail counter, a clinic waiting room, or a workshop wall, that lockable steel arm is exactly what you need — and what most cheap mounts skip entirely.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the steel wall plate, the locking cradle assembly, two barrel keys, mounting screws with drywall anchors, and a hex wrench. The cradle opens wide enough (7.5 inches) to accept tablets like the iPad Pro 11 or Galaxy Tab S8, and closes down to 4.7 inches for mini tablets or phones in thick cases. The rubber padding on the clamps is genuinely soft — no scratches on aluminum or glass.

As shown in the product image, the mount sits flush against the wall when locked, which means the tablet reads as a fixed kiosk rather than an obvious snatch-and-run target.

**Real-world performance**

The tilt and swivel are the standout features here. You get 90 degrees of tilt and 360 degrees of rotation, which matters more than you'd think. In a retail setting, you can flip the screen from landscape to portrait depending on the POS software. At home, you can angle it toward the kitchen counter while cooking or swing it flat against the wall when not in use.

The locking arm clicks into place with a satisfying mechanical snap. To remove the tablet, you insert the barrel key and twist — it takes about two seconds, but a thief without the key isn't getting it off without tools and time. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "Feels solid. The lock gives me peace of mind in our lobby. No one has even tried to mess with it." Another noted, "The swivel is smooth and holds position — no drooping like my last mount."

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The locking cradle frame is thick. If your tablet has physical buttons on the sides — like older iPads with the mute switch or newer ones with volume rockers — the frame can cover them. You'll still access the screen and charging port, but you might need to unlock the mount to change volume. Plan for that.

Second, the included hardware works fine for drywall and wood studs, but there's no masonry anchor. Mounting this on concrete, brick, or tile requires a trip to the hardware store. For an "anti-theft" product, you'd think they'd include the right bits for a dental office or hotel lobby wall. They don't.

Finally, the mount is rated for tablets up to 11.6 inches, so the largest iPad Pro (12.9 inches) won't fit. Check your tablet's dimensions before ordering.

**Who should buy this**

- **Retail and restaurant owners** running counter-side ordering or inventory apps — the lock is the whole point.
- **Clinics and dental offices** with patient check-in tablets that need to stay put.
- **Home users** with kids who think every screen is a toy — the lock means you control when it comes off.
- **Anyone with a tablet in a shared workspace** who's tired of finding it on the floor.

Skip it if you just want a simple, minimal wall mount for a tablet that never leaves your house. A basic $20 mount does that job without the lock bulk.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my iPad Air 5th generation?**
A: Yes. The iPad Air measures about 7 inches wide in landscape — well within the 4.7 to 7.5-inch clamp range. The depth is fine for most cases, though the thickest OtterBox-style cases might be tight.

**Q: Can I charge the tablet while it's locked in?**
A: Yes. The cradle leaves the bottom edge open, so you can route a charging cable through. Just make sure your cable's connector doesn't add too much height that it blocks the clamp.

**Q: Is it truly theft-proof?**
A: Nothing is fully theft-proof, but this mount stops opportunistic theft. The locking arm is steel, and removing the mount from the wall requires unscrewing it from behind the cradle — which you can't do while it's locked. For a retail counter, that's enough.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need to secure a tablet in a public or semi-public space and want the peace of mind of a physical key lock. The build quality justifies the price, and the swivel/tilt adjustability makes it genuinely usable, not just a security device.

**Skip it if** your tablet is larger than 11.6 inches, or if you need masonry hardware out of the box. For everyone else — this is the lockable mount that actually works.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Security done right, with minor installation and fitment caveats.
