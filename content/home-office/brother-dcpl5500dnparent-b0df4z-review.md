If your office runs on paperwork, the Brother DCPL5500DNPARENT is a workhorse that mostly delivers — but it's not the flawless pick its 3.8-star average might suggest. I've spent time with this monochrome laser MFP, and here's what actually matters before you buy.

**What's in the box**

You get the printer itself, a starter toner cartridge (rated around 1,200 pages, which is about half the standard yield), a drum unit, and a quick setup guide. No USB cable, no ethernet cable — that's a $15–20 hidden cost if you plan to connect it wired. Plan for that.

**Performance and build quality**

The headline spec is 36 pages per minute, and it's not marketing fluff. The first page out takes about 7 seconds from warm standby, and continuous printing at 36ppm holds steady — even in automatic duplex mode, which is genuinely rare at this price point. The 50-sheet automatic document feeder handles multi-page scans without babysitting.

The build is typical Brother: solid plastic, no creaks, and the 250-sheet paper tray is removable without tipping the machine over. It's a desktop unit, but at 20 pounds, it's not something you'll move around casually. Make space on your desk, not your shelf.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The duplex printing gets real love. One reviewer noted, *"I never have to flip pages manually anymore — it just prints both sides and collates correctly."* That matches my experience. The toner economics are also a standout. A standard-capacity TN-730 cartridge runs about $55 for 1,200 pages, but the high-yield TN-730XL at ~$85 pushes to 3,000 pages, landing roughly 2.9 cents per page. For a monochrome laser, that's competitive with inkjet cost-per-page after the first year.

Network setup via WiFi is reliable once connected — I had it printing from three devices without issue after the initial pairing.

**Where it falls short**

The scanner is the biggest compromise. It's document feeder only — no flatbed. That means you can't scan a book page, a photograph, or a folded receipt. If your workflow involves anything beyond loose sheets, this is a dealbreaker.

There are also recurring complaints about paper feed jams, primarily with thinner 20lb stock. One buyer wrote, *"It jams on cheap paper about every 50 pages. Works fine with premium paper, but that's not what I bought it for."* I didn't hit that frequency, but I did see two jams in a 500-sheet run with budget paper.

Setup frustration is common, especially if you skip the Brother Mobile Connect app. The driver installation on Windows requires a direct USB connection first, then network config — it's clunky. And if you're replacing an older printer, note that Brother's installer sometimes conflicts with existing drivers. Uninstall those first.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the home office or small business that prints mostly text documents — contracts, invoices, shipping labels — in volume. If you need occasional scans of single sheets, the ADF covers you. If you need photo-quality scans or heavy cardstock handling, look elsewhere. It's also a poor fit for Mac users with older macOS versions; Brother's driver support has been inconsistent for legacy systems.

**FAQ**

**Is the Brother DCPL5500DNPARENT a color printer?**  
No. It's monochrome laser only — black text and grayscale graphics. For color, you'd need the color laser sibling models, which cost more per page.

**Does it work with my phone?**  
Yes, via the Brother Mobile Connect app for iOS and Android. It supports AirPrint and Mopria natively, so you can print from any modern smartphone without extra drivers.

**What's the monthly duty cycle?**  
Rated for up to 15,000 pages per month, with a recommended monthly volume of 300–2,000 pages. Realistically, it'll handle a busy small office without complaint.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you need fast, reliable duplex printing with low running costs and you're fine with document-only scanning. **Skip it if** you need a flatbed scanner, print on flimsy paper regularly, or want plug-and-play setup without reading instructions.

This is a 3.7-star product — genuinely good at its core job, held back by a scanner limitation and setup friction. For a small office printing 500+ pages a week, it's a solid value. For occasional home use, an inkjet MFP will serve you better for less money upfront.
