## What's in the Box

You get two cartridges: one black (XL) and one tri-color (cyan/magenta/yellow, XL). Both are packaged in sealed foil pouches with the standard protective orange caps. The chip contacts are covered with a clear plastic tab you peel off before installation. There's no instruction manual — you don't need one; they pop into the carriage exactly like OEM carts.

## Performance and Build Quality

Let's talk about what actually matters: does the ink work?

**Print quality** is the first thing I checked. On plain copy paper, black text comes out crisp with minimal feathering — comparable to OEM 63XL output. The tri-color cartridge produces well-saturated reds and blues in test prints. Skin tones in photos lean slightly warm, but that's common among third-party inks and easily corrected with printer driver settings.

**Page yield** is where this set shines. The black cartridge is rated for 480 pages, the color for 330 pages. In real-world testing, users report getting close to those numbers with standard 5% coverage documents. One verified buyer noted, *"I've printed over 400 pages of text before the black started looking faded — way better than the starter cartridges that came with my printer."* That matches the XL claim.

**Chip reliability** was a concern I had going in. Third-party cartridges often fail to register or trigger false "low ink" warnings. In the 215 Amazon ratings (4.3/5 average), the vast majority report seamless installation. A few users did mention needing to clean the chip contacts with a dry cloth or reseat the cartridge once. That's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

## Where It Falls Short

**The black cartridge can run dry faster than expected** when you print photo-heavy documents or high-density graphics. The 480-page estimate assumes standard text coverage. If you're printing presentation slides with dark backgrounds, expect maybe 60-70% of that. That's true for most XL cartridges, but the gap between claim and reality here is slightly wider than OEM.

**No expiration date on the packaging** is a legitimate gripe. If you buy these in bulk and store them for six months or more, the ink can settle or thicken, leading to clogged printheads and streaky output. Use them within a few months of purchase.

**Color cartridge balance** — the tri-color cart runs out of one color (usually cyan) before the others, which is standard for single-cartridge color systems. You'll replace the whole color cartridge even if two colors remain. That's a design limitation of the HP 63 platform, not this specific product.

## FAQ

**Will these work with my HP Envy 5000 series?**
Yes, if your printer accepts HP 63 or 63XL cartridges. Check your printer model against HP's compatibility list — this set covers the Deskjet 1110–3750 range, Envy 4500–5000 series, and OfficeJet 3830–4650 series.

**How do I know when the ink is actually low?**
The chip tracks ink levels and will display a warning on your printer's status screen or the HP Smart app. Some users report the warning appears earlier than actual depletion — you may have ~15% ink remaining when the alert triggers. That's a feature, not a bug; it prevents printhead damage.

**Will this void my printer warranty?**
No. In the US, the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act prevents manufacturers from voiding warranties solely due to third-party consumables. HP must prove the cartridge caused the damage to deny coverage.
