The SAMSUNG 860 PRO is the last of a dying breed: a consumer SATA SSD built with MLC NAND. Most drives on shelves today use TLC or QLC flash, which is cheaper but less durable. This drive uses 2-bit MLC, giving it roughly double the program/erase cycles of standard consumer SSDs. For a 2.5-inch SATA III drive, that endurance advantage is the entire story.

## What You Actually Get

Inside the retail box: the 2.5-inch drive itself, a paper quick-start guide, and — importantly — no mounting screws or SATA cable. The 860 PRO measures 100mm x 69.85mm x 6.8mm and weighs just 56 grams. It's compatible with any standard 2.5-inch bay or a 3.5-inch adapter bracket. If you're upgrading a laptop, verify your model accepts 7mm-thick drives — this one fits, but some ultra-thin laptops require 5mm drives.

The drive uses Samsung's MJX controller paired with 2GB of LPDDR4 cache on the 1TB model I tested. The 512GB version has 512MB of cache. That cache matters — it's what keeps random read/write performance consistent under load.

## Performance in the Real World

SATA III caps out at 560 MB/s, and the 860 PRO hits 556 MB/s sequential reads and 530 MB/s writes in CrystalDiskMark. Those numbers match the spec sheet. But here's where the 860 PRO separates from cheaper drives: sustained writes. When I copied a 40GB folder, the drive maintained 500+ MB/s throughout. Most TLC drives — including the 870 EVO — drop to 150-200 MB/s once their SLC cache fills. The 860 PRO doesn't have that problem because MLC NAND doesn't require cache trickery.

Random 4K performance is where you'll feel it in daily use. The drive delivers 98K IOPS read and 88K IOPS write, which translates to snappier application launches and faster boot times. Windows 11 boots in about 12 seconds on a clean install. Game load times improve, but don't expect miracles — SATA is still SATA.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews reflects a pattern. One verified buyer wrote: "I've been running this drive 24/7 in a NAS for three years with zero issues. SMART data still shows 98% life remaining." Another noted: "Installed in a 2012 MacBook Pro — it's faster than the original drive ever was, and the TRIM support works perfectly."

Reliability is the recurring theme. The 600 TBW endurance rating on the 1TB model means you could write 300GB daily for five years and still stay under warranty. That's why this drive shows up in surveillance systems, network-attached storage, and other write-heavy workloads.

## Where It Falls Short

The 860 PRO is not fast by modern standards. If you have an M.2 NVMe slot available, a PCIe Gen4 drive delivers 7,000 MB/s — twelve times faster. The 860 PRO exists for older systems, SATA-only laptops, or mission-critical storage where endurance outweighs speed.

Also, this drive is discontinued. Samsung officially replaced it with the 870 EVO series. You can still find the 860 PRO new from third-party sellers, but prices fluctuate and stock is inconsistent. The 870 EVO costs less and performs nearly identically — you're paying a premium here specifically for MLC durability. If you don't need that endurance, you're overpaying.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You run a home server, security camera NVR, or any system that writes constantly. The MLC endurance means it will outlast your hardware. Also ideal if you're maxing out an older laptop or desktop that only has SATA ports.

**Skip it if:** You have an M.2 slot available, want maximum speed, or just need a cheap boot drive for everyday use. The 870 EVO or a budget NVMe drive will serve you better for less money.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the 860 PRO compatible with my older motherboard?**
A: Yes, if it has SATA II or SATA III ports. The drive works with both, though SATA II will cap speeds at ~280 MB/s. Windows 7, 8, 10, and 11 all support it natively.

**Q: How long will this drive actually last?**
A: The 1TB model is rated for 600 TBW (terabytes written). For a typical user writing 20GB daily, that's over 80 years. Even heavy users writing 100GB daily would need 16 years to hit the limit.

**Q: Does it require any special drivers or software?**
A: No drivers needed. Samsung Magician software is recommended but optional — it handles firmware updates, secure erase, and lets you check drive health. The drive works plug-and-play on any OS.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The SAMSUNG 860 PRO is a specialist tool in a generalist market. It's not the fastest SATA drive, and it's not the cheapest. But for anyone who prioritizes data integrity and sustained write performance, it remains one of the most reliable 2.5-inch SSDs ever made. The 5-year warranty and 600 TBW endurance rating back that claim. If your workload involves constant writes — or you just want a drive you'll never think about again — this is it. For everyone else, the 870 EVO offers 90% of the experience at 70% of the price.
