Let me save you some money and frustration: the 2024 Surface Laptop is the first Windows laptop that actually feels like it was designed for how people work in 2026, not how they worked in 2016. But it's not perfect, and the base config will bite you if you're not careful.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the laptop itself, a 39W Surface Connect charger, and a quick-start guide. That's it. No USB-C cables, no dongles, no stylus (the touchscreen works with fingers, but the Surface Slim Pen is a $130 add-on). The 13.8-inch model weighs 2.96 pounds and measures 0.72 inches thick — meaningfully lighter than the 14-inch MacBook Pro and comparable to the MacBook Air.

The build quality is genuinely excellent. The anodized aluminum chassis has zero flex in the keyboard deck, and the hinge opens one-handed without lifting the base. This is the most MacBook-like Windows laptop I've tested, and that's a compliment.

**Performance and battery: the Snapdragon story**

The Snapdragon X Elite (X1E-80-100 in this config) is the headline. In Cinebench 2024 multi-core, it scores about 780 — roughly 15% behind the M3 MacBook Air but ahead of most Intel Core Ultra 7 laptops from the same generation. What matters more: I ran a 14-hour workday of Chrome (18 tabs), Slack, Excel, and Spotify, and the battery went from 100% to 23%. Real-world, expect 15-20 hours depending on brightness. That's a full work week for most people without touching a charger.

The catch is compatibility. Native ARM64 apps (Edge, Chrome, Office, Zoom, Slack) run flawlessly. But if you rely on niche x64 utilities — I tested a tax prep app and an older VPN client — they run through Microsoft's Prism emulation, which works but can be 20-40% slower. Check your critical apps support ARM64 before ordering.

**Display and trackpad**

The 13.8-inch PixelSense display runs at 2304x1536 (3:2 aspect ratio). You get about 20% more vertical workspace than a 16:9 panel, which is huge for document editing and web browsing. Peak brightness hits 600 nits, and color accuracy is solid at 99% sRGB, 86% AdobeRGB. It's not OLED, so blacks aren't infinite, but for office work it's excellent.

The haptic trackpad deserves special mention. It doesn't physically move — it vibrates — but the feedback is convincing and consistent whether you click near the top or bottom edge. This is the best Windows trackpad I've used, period.

**What buyers are saying**

Amazon reviewers at 4.4/5 stars largely mirror my experience. One verified buyer wrote: "Battery life is unreal. I flew LA to Tokyo and worked almost the entire flight without charging." Another noted: "The display is the reason to buy this. Reading documents for 8 hours doesn't strain my eyes." The complaints cluster around two issues: the lack of USB-A ports and the base 12GB RAM feeling tight with many Chrome tabs open.

**Where it falls short**

The 12GB RAM base model is the trap. Windows 11 with Copilot+ features eats 5-6GB at idle, leaving you ~6GB for everything else. I saw memory pressure with 25+ Chrome tabs, OneNote, and Teams running simultaneously. Spend the extra $200 for 16GB — it's soldered, so you cannot upgrade later.

The single USB-C port is also limiting. You get one USB-C (USB4/Thunderbolt 4), one USB-A, and Surface Connect. Plug in a monitor and you're out of ports. The Surface Connect charger is proprietary, and the included 39W brick is slower than modern USB-C PD chargers.

**Who should buy this**

- **The 9-to-5 professional** who lives in Office, browser, and video calls — this is your machine
- **Frequent travelers** who need all-day battery and a sub-3-pound weight
- **Mac-curious Windows users** who want premium build without switching ecosystems

**Skip it if** you need Windows-only legacy software, if you want a 2-in-1 with a detachable screen (get the Surface Pro instead), or if you're on a tight budget — this starts near $1,000 and climbs quickly with RAM/storage upgrades.

**FAQ**

*Does the touchscreen work with the Surface Pen?* Yes, but the Pen is sold separately (~$130). Palm rejection is good, and latency is about 20ms — fine for note-taking, not great for digital art.

*Can I upgrade the RAM or SSD later?* No. Both are soldered/LPDDR5X and the SSD is a proprietary form factor. Buy the config you need upfront.

*Is it worth upgrading from a Surface Laptop 5?* If battery life matters to you, absolutely — you'll get roughly double the runtime. Otherwise, the performance gain is modest for daily office tasks.

**The Verdict**

The 2024 Surface Laptop is the best Windows ultrabook for most office workers. The battery is class-leading, the display is genuinely better for productivity than 16:9 competitors, and the build quality rivals Apple. The ARM compatibility caveats and port limitations are real, but for the typical user — browser, Office, email, video calls — this is a 4.2/5 purchase that will serve you for 4-5 years.

**Buy it if** you want a premium Windows laptop with MacBook-beating battery life and you've verified your essential apps run native on ARM64.

**Skip it if** you need legacy x64 software reliability, multiple USB-A peripherals, or you want the cheapest possible Windows laptop.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Recommended with the caveat to skip the 12GB base model.
