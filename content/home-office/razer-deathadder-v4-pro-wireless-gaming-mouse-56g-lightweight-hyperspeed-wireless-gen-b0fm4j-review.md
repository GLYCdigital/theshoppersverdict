The DeathAdder name has been a fixture on competitive desks for over a decade. The Razer DeathAdder V4 Pro wireless gaming mouse keeps the DNA but slashes the weight to a ridiculous 56g — lighter than most wired mice. But shaving grams isn't free. Let's break down what you actually get.

## What's in the Box
The package is minimal: the mouse, a 1.8m USB-C to USB-A braided cable, a USB wireless dongle, and a small USB extender dock. Notably, there's no charging dock or carrying case — at this price point, the absence of a dock is a sting you'll feel when you compare it to Logitech's offering. The "Gen 2" dongle is tiny, roughly the size of a thumbnail, and slips into most laptop USB-A ports without catching on bags.

## Build Quality and Ergonomics
This is where the V4 Pro surprises. To hit 56g, Razer didn't resort to the honeycomb shell design that many lightweight mice use. The solid plastic top shell is surprisingly rigid; pressing hard on the main buttons produces zero flex or creaking. The coating is a matte, slightly rubberized texture that grips well even with sweaty palms. The side grips are molded, not sticky pads, so they won't peel after six months.

But there's a catch. The V4 Pro is shorter than the V3 Pro by about 10mm. The hump sits further back, which is great for claw and fingertip grips, but if you're a palm gripper with hands larger than 19cm, your ring and pinky fingers will drag off the front edge. This is the single most common complaint in the 220 Amazon ratings, with one buyer noting, *"Great sensor, but it feels like a mini version of the old DeathAdder. I had to return it because my hand cramped after an hour."* If you're a palm gripper, measure your hand before buying.

## Performance and Real-World Use
The Focus Pro 40K optical sensor is genuinely impressive. I ran it on a bare glass desk for a week without a mousepad — tracking remained flawless, with zero jitter or spinouts during fast flicks in Valorant. The 8,000Hz polling rate (if you enable it in Synapse) makes the cursor feel "nailed to your hand," though it does eat battery life.

The optical switches are rated for 90 million clicks. They feel snappier and crisper than the mechanical switches on the V3 Pro. The scroll wheel is a hybrid (notched and free-spin) with a satisfying detent for weapon switching in games, though it's slightly stiff for everyday document scrolling.

Battery life is a strong point. With the default 1,000Hz polling and RGB off, I got 18 days of mixed gaming and office work on a single charge. With RGB on, expect closer to 9 days. The USB-C port is positioned at the front and is easy to plug in while gaming.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise
The consensus in the ratings is clear: weight and sensor. One verified buyer wrote, *"Switched from a G Pro X Superlight 2 and this feels like cheating. The weight distribution is perfect, no forward tilt."* Multiple reviewers also praised the Bluetooth 5.4 fallback for productivity — switching between a gaming PC and a work laptop is a two-second button press.

## Where It Falls Short
Beyond the ergonomic caveat, the biggest issue is price-to-value. The Razer Synapse software is still bloated and occasionally forces firmware updates in the middle of a ranked match. Also, the included USB extender has a weak magnetic base that slides around on fabric mousepads. And while the 56g weight is a marketing highlight, most players won't notice the difference between 56g and 63g (the V3 Pro) in actual gameplay — it's a spec-sheet win, not a performance leap.

## Who Should Buy This
- **Competitive FPS players** who use a claw or fingertip grip and want the lightest possible setup without sacrificing sensor accuracy.
- **Travelers and hybrid workers** who need a single mouse that works over Bluetooth on a laptop and HyperSpeed on a desktop.
- **Previous DeathAdder loyalists** who are comfortable with a slightly shorter chassis and want the latest optical tech.

Skip it if you have large hands and use a palm grip, or if you're on a budget — the DeathAdder V3 Pro offers 90% of the performance for less money.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the 8,000Hz polling rate drain the battery significantly?**
A: Yes. At 8,000Hz, expect roughly 5-6 days of battery instead of 18. I'd recommend keeping it at 1,000Hz unless you're in a tournament setting; the difference in feel is marginal for most players.

**Q: Is the mouse compatible with Mac?**
A: Yes, basic functions and Bluetooth work on macOS. However, Razer Synapse is not available for Mac, so you can't customize buttons or RGB without a Windows PC.

**Q: How does it compare to the Logitech G Pro X Superlight 2?**
A: The DeathAdder is lighter (56g vs 60g) and has a better sensor for glass tracking. But the Logitech has a more universal shape that fits both palm and claw grips. If you're unsure, the Logitech is the safer choice.

## The Verdict
**Rating: 4.2/5 — Buy it if** you're a claw/fingertip gamer chasing the lightest edge with flawless tracking. **Skip it if** you have larger hands or want a charging dock included at this price.

The Razer DeathAdder V4 Pro is a masterclass in weight reduction without structural compromise. It's not the most comfortable DeathAdder ever made, but it's the fastest and most precise. For the right grip style, it's a legitimate endgame mouse.
