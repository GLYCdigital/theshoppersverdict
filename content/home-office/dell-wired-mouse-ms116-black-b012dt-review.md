Let's be honest: a $15 wired mouse shouldn't be exciting. But the Dell MS116 has quietly become one of the most reliable office peripherals on Amazon, holding a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings. The question isn't whether it works — it's whether this budget workhorse deserves a spot on your desk.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the mouse itself and a 5.9-foot USB cord. That's it. No software CD, no driver disk, no manual you'll throw away. The MS116 uses standard HID protocol, meaning it's recognized instantly by Windows, macOS, Chrome OS, and Linux. Plug it in and you're moving your cursor within three seconds.

The design is classic Dell: matte black sides with a glossy top panel, measuring 4.5 x 2.6 x 1.5 inches and weighing just 3.2 ounces. It's an ambidextrous shape — no sculpted right-hand ergonomics here — with a rubberized scroll wheel that provides tactile, notched feedback. The optical sensor tracks at 1000 DPI, which is modest but perfectly adequate for office work, spreadsheets, and web browsing.

## Real-World Performance

I've spent two weeks with the MS116 as my daily driver, and here's what matters: it tracks flawlessly on surfaces that give other budget mice fits. Glossy IKEA desk? No problem. Dark wood grain? Handled. A cloth mousepad? Obviously fine. The optical sensor doesn't skip or stutter, and the left and right clicks are quiet with a satisfying, non-mushy actuation.

The cord is braided slightly and resists kinking, though it's not the most flexible cable I've used. The 5.9-foot length is adequate for most setups, but if your tower sits under a desk with the USB port facing backward, you'll want a USB extension — that's a genuine limitation.

One thing I appreciate: there's no LED lighting, no DPI toggle buttons, no gamer nonsense. It's a tool, and it behaves like one. The 1000 DPI resolution is fine for 1080p and even 1440p monitors. You won't be pixel-sniping in Photoshop, but for document work and email, it's smooth and precise.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The Amazon reviews consistently highlight three things. First, reliability: "I've bought three of these for different computers and they all work flawlessly," notes one verified purchaser. Second, the plug-and-play simplicity — multiple reviewers mention using them on laptops, desktops, and even a Raspberry Pi without any configuration. Third, the value proposition: "For this price, I expected cheap plastic. It actually feels solid and durable."

## Where It Falls Short

The glossy top panel is the main cosmetic culprit. After a few days, it collects fingerprints and fine scratches that show under direct light. Not a functional issue, but if you're particular about aesthetics, it'll annoy you.

The lack of side buttons is the bigger functional miss. If you're used to thumb-button forward/back navigation in browsers or file explorers, you'll feel its absence immediately. The MS116 is strictly a two-button-plus-scroll experience.

And it's worth repeating: that 5.9-foot cord. For laptops where the port is on the left or right side, it's fine. For desktop towers on the floor, you might be stretching it — literally.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You need a dependable, no-driver mouse for an office workstation, a school computer, a secondary setup, or a spare to keep in a drawer. You don't care about aesthetics and want something that will work for years without complaint. You're outfitting multiple machines and want uniformity at a low cost per unit.

**Skip it if:** You need side buttons, you have a USB port that's hard to reach, or you want a mouse that looks premium on a modern desk. Also skip it if you do precision work like graphic design or CAD — the 1000 DPI sensor isn't built for that.

## FAQ

**Is the Dell MS116 compatible with Mac?** Yes. It's a standard HID device, so it works with macOS, Windows, Linux, and Chrome OS without any drivers. The scroll wheel works natively on Macs including gesture support.

**How durable is it?** Dell rates it for 10 million clicks. Real-world reports suggest it holds up well — several Amazon reviewers mention using theirs for 2-3 years without issues. The cord is the most likely failure point, not the switches.

**Does it work on a glass desk?** No. Like most optical mice, clear or mirror-finished glass surfaces will defeat the sensor. It works on opaque, non-reflective surfaces including glossy painted wood and laminate.

## The Verdict

The Dell MS116 is the office equivalent of a Toyota Corolla — unremarkable, unpretentious, and utterly dependable. At its price point, it does exactly what a wired mouse should do: connect instantly, track accurately, and keep working without drama. It won't impress anyone, but it won't let you down either. For budget-conscious office workers who don't need extra buttons and don't care about RGB lighting, this is a sensible, honest purchase.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Knocking half a star for the fingerprint-magnet finish and the absence of side buttons at a time when even budget mice often include them. But for pure reliability per dollar, few options beat it.
