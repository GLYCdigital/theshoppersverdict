Let's be honest: you probably didn't wake up today wondering if a $4 notebook is worth your attention. But the Mead Composition Notebook has quietly become the default choice for students, journalers, and note-takers for a reason — and it's not just nostalgia for the black-and-white marble cover.

**What you're actually getting**

This is a 7-1/2" x 9-3/4" notebook with 100 sheets of wide-ruled paper. That's 200 pages, which sounds modest until you realize most "premium" notebooks at twice the price give you 80 sheets. The wide ruling sits at 8.7mm line spacing — noticeably more generous than the 7.1mm college rule. If you write large, have younger kids doing homework, or simply don't want your handwriting cramped into tight lines, this is the practical choice.

The sewn binding is the sleeper feature here. Unlike glued or spiral-bound notebooks, the pages lay flat when you open to any spread. That matters when you're writing near the margin or copying notes from a textbook propped next to it. And because the binding is stitched, you won't get that infuriating moment where pages start shedding after a month of daily use.

**How it holds up in real use**

The 15 lb paper is thicker than the standard 10 lb stuff you'll find in bargain notebooks. It handles ballpoint and rollerball pens without bleed-through. Fountain pen users will see some ghosting — faint shadows of writing on the back of the page — but nothing that makes the reverse side unusable. Gel pens and highlighters are safe.

One buyer described it as "the only notebook that survives my backpack" — and that tracks. The flexible cover is the weakest physical point, but it bends rather than cracks, which is what you want from something that'll be shoved between textbooks.

**Where it falls short**

The sewing is a double-edged sword. You cannot tear out pages. If you're someone who rips out notes to hand in assignments or reorganize your binder, this will frustrate you. There are no perforations, and attempting a clean tear will just give you a ragged edge.

The cover is also just cardstock. It's not waterproof, and the corners will dog-ear if you're careless. For the price, that's acceptable — but don't expect the rigid, wipe-clean cover of a hardbound notebook.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students (middle school through college)** who need a durable, page-count-rich notebook that won't fall apart by finals week
- **Journalers and bullet journalers** who prefer wide spacing and want a cheap everyday carry without emotional attachment
- **Writers** doing longhand drafts who need lots of pages and don't mind the utilitarian look

Skip it if you're a page-ripper, if you need college-rule spacing for dense technical notes, or if you want a leather-wrapped aesthetic piece for your desk.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the paper fountain pen friendly?**  
It's usable but not perfect. You'll get ghosting with wet nibs, and heavy ink can bleed through. For a $4 notebook, it's surprisingly good — just don't expect Tomoe River quality.

**Q: Does it lay flat?**  
Yes, thanks to the sewn binding. That's a genuine advantage over spiral notebooks, which have a coil in your way, and glued notebooks, which pop shut.

**Q: How many pages are there really?**  
100 sheets, 200 pages. That's double what many "premium" notebooks offer at two to three times the cost.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a reliable workhorse notebook with generous line spacing and real durability. **Skip it if** you need removable pages or a rigid cover.
