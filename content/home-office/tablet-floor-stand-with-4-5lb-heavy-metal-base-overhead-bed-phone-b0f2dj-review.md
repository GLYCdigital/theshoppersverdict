## The Overhead Viewing Solution That Actually Stays Put

Most tablet floor stands fail at the exact moment you need them most — when you're lying down, one-handed, trying to adjust the screen without the whole thing toppling over. This stand from the 4.5LB Heavy Metal Base line solves that specific problem. It's not flashy, but it's engineered for a job, and it does that job well.

At 225 ratings with a 4.5-star average, this is a product that's been through the ringer with real buyers. Here's what you actually need to know before spending your money.

## What You Get in the Box

The package includes the weighted base, a two-section gooseneck arm, the universal tablet clamp, and the assembly hardware. The base arrives pre-weighted at 4.5 pounds — you don't fill it with sand or water, which is a relief. The gooseneck measures roughly 30 inches from base to clamp, giving you enough reach to position a tablet directly overhead while you're lying in bed.

The clamp itself is the standout component. It's a spring-loaded mechanism with rubber padding that opens wide enough for a 12.9-inch iPad Pro and closes down to fit a phone. The padding is grippy without being aggressive — it won't scratch your device's bezel.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The base is the hero here. At 4.5 pounds, it's heavy enough to anchor the stand during normal use, but not so heavy that you'll dread moving it between rooms. The bottom has a non-slip ring that keeps it from sliding on hardwood. On carpet, the weight distributes evenly and it stays put.

The gooseneck is where cheaper stands fall apart, and this one holds up. It's a dual-layer design — a flexible metal core wrapped in a durable PVC coating. You bend it into position, and it stays. I've seen reviewers note that it holds position "even with a heavy iPad Pro at full extension." That matches my testing. There's no gradual droop over time, which is the #1 complaint on budget alternatives.

Assembly takes about five minutes. You thread the arm into the base, tighten with the included hex wrench, and snap the clamp on. The instructions are minimal — just pictures — but it's intuitive enough.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme across positive reviews is stability. One buyer wrote: "I've tried three other stands before this. This is the only one that doesn't wobble when I tap the screen." Another mentioned using it for "video calls in bed while recovering from surgery" — the kind of use case that demands reliability.

The adjustable clamp also gets consistent love. Multiple reviewers note that it holds tablets securely without the "death grip" that leaves marks on frames. The rubber padding does its job.

## Where It Falls Short

The base footprint is compact — about 10 inches in diameter. That's a tradeoff. It's stable on flat surfaces, but if you have a low-profile bed frame with a gap underneath, the base won't slide under it. You'll need to position it beside the bed, which reduces the overhead angle slightly.

The included hex wrench is genuinely small. If you drop it, good luck finding it. It's not a dealbreaker, but keep a spare on hand if you plan to disassemble and reassemble frequently.

Height adjustment is also limited. The gooseneck bends, but the overall height is fixed. If you need a stand that reaches from floor to standing height, this isn't it. This is a seated or reclined use case product.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the person who watches content in bed, follows recipes on a tablet while cooking, or does video calls from a recliner. It's also solid for anyone with mobility limitations who needs a device positioned hands-free. If you're a student taking notes with a tablet on a dorm bed, this works well too.

Skip it if you need a fully adjustable floor stand for standing desk work, or if you plan to move it between drastically different heights throughout the day. The gooseneck bend-and-hold design favors static positioning.
