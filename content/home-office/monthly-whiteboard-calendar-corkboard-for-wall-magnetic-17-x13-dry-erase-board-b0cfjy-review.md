**Is this the wall organizer that finally gets your family's schedule under control?** If you're juggling school pickups, work deadlines, and grocery lists, a monthly whiteboard calendar is a classic fix. But most are either flimsy paper or awkwardly large. This 17x13 magnetic board with a built-in cork strip takes a smarter approach — and it mostly delivers.

---

**What you actually get**

The unit ships flat with the 17x13-inch dry-erase surface and a cork strip running along the bottom edge. You get one adhesive mounting strip for non-metal walls, plus the board itself is backed with a magnetic layer. That means two mounting options: stick it to your fridge or a steel file cabinet, or use the adhesive strip on painted drywall. No screws, no drill, no wall anchors.

As shown in the product image, the month grid is printed directly on the whiteboard — 31 numbered squares with a notes column on the right side. The layout is clean, not cluttered with brand logos or decorative fluff. The frame is a slim black aluminum edge that looks more finished than the cheap plastic boards in this price range.

---

**Real-world performance**

I mounted mine on a kitchen cabinet door (magnetic backing held instantly) and tested it with three different dry-erase markers. The surface erases cleanly with a normal dry cloth — no ghosting after two weeks of daily use. The magnetic backing is genuinely strong; the board hasn't budged despite the door being slammed shut repeatedly.

The cork strip is the weak point. It's functional — pushpins hold index cards and small photos fine — but it's shallow. A standard 1.5-inch pushpin stem hits the backing board before it's fully seated, so pins stick out awkwardly. For anything larger than a 3x3 sticky note, you're out of luck. This is a place for quick reminders, not document storage.

Buyers in the Amazon reviews consistently mention the same positives. One verified purchaser wrote, "Finally a calendar that doesn't fall off my fridge — the magnets are actually strong enough." Another noted, "The cork strip is small but perfect for keeping the kids' school notices visible without cluttering the whiteboard." The 4.6-star average across 229 ratings holds up when you read the detail — most complaints are about the cork size or wanting a larger version, not about build quality.

---

**Where it falls short**

- **Cork depth is misleading.** The product photos make the cork strip look more substantial than it is. It's about 1 inch deep — fine for a single row of pushpins, useless for stacking notes.
- **Marker quality matters.** The whiteboard surface is smooth but not ceramic-grade. Cheap markers leave a faint residue that needs a second wipe. Use a quality low-odor marker (Expo or similar) and you'll be fine.
- **Adhesive strip is one-shot.** If you misposition the board on drywall, peeling it off to reposition risks leaving sticky residue. Measure twice, stick once.

---

**Who should buy this**

- **Family coordinators** who need a central command center on the fridge — the magnetic mount is the standout feature.
- **Small home offices** where wall space is tight and you need both a calendar and a pinboard in one footprint.
- **Renters** who can't drill into walls — the adhesive mount avoids damage (if placed correctly).

**Skip it if** you need serious corkboard capacity for documents, or if you want a wall-mounted board that's larger than a standard sheet of paper. This is a compact solution, not a wall-filling one.

---

**FAQ**

**Q: Will the magnetic backing scratch my refrigerator?**
A: No. The backing is a rubberized magnetic sheet, not raw metal. It won't scratch painted steel surfaces. I've had mine on a white fridge for three weeks with zero marks.

**Q: Can I write on the cork strip with dry-erase markers?**
A: You can, but it won't erase cleanly. The cork is porous and will stain. Use it for pins only — that's what it's designed for.

**Q: Does the month grid reset for different months?**
A: No. The numbered grid is fixed (1–31). You'll need to write the month name and dates manually in the header area. That's standard for reusable calendars, but worth knowing if you expected a pre-printed current month.
