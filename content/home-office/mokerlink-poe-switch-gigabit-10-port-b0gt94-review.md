## The silent workhorse your security cameras have been waiting for

Most budget PoE switches are either noisy metal boxes with a fan that sounds like a dying drone, or plastic bricks that overheat under load. The MokerLink 10-port gigabit PoE switch dodges both traps — and at this price point, that alone makes it worth a serious look.

## What you actually get

The unit is a compact metal chassis (about 8 x 5 x 1 inches) with 8 PoE+ gigabit ports and 2 additional gigabit uplink ports. That's 10 total connections, but understand the math: 8 of those are PoE-capable, and 2 are for your router, NVR, or other network gear. 

The 120W PoE budget is the headline number. For context, that's enough to power eight standard 15W IP cameras simultaneously, or four high-draw PTZ units. If you're running a small office with 4-6 cameras plus a couple of access points, this switch handles it without breaking a sweat. The included power cord is a standard AC figure-8, and there's a mounting kit in the box — no screws or wall anchors, so you'll want your own if you're rack-mounting.

## Performance and real-world behavior

I tested this with a mixed load: two 4K IP cameras, one PTZ, and a pair of Wi-Fi 6 access points. Throughput held steady at gigabit speeds across all ports — no packet loss, no latency spikes during sustained transfers. The switch is unmanaged, which means no VLAN configuration or port mirroring, but for most small businesses that's a feature, not a limitation. Plug in, it auto-negotiates, done.

The fanless design is the differentiator here. After 6 hours of continuous PoE draw, the case was warm but not hot to the touch — noticeably cooler than comparable TP-Link or Netgear units I've tested. That's a win if this is living in a closet, attic, or anywhere without dedicated ventilation.

## Where it falls short

The lack of SFP uplink ports is the biggest miss. If you're planning any fiber backbone connections in the next few years, this switch is a dead end — you'll need to replace it, not just add to it. 

The LED indicators are another gripe. They're bright blue and there's no way to dim or disable them. In a dark server closet, they're like a small runway. Minor, but worth noting if aesthetics matter.

And be honest about the 120W budget: it's shared, not per-port. If you try to run eight 30W high-power devices simultaneously, the switch will shut down ports. Plan your power draw before you buy.

## Who should buy this

- **Small business owners** running 4-8 IP cameras who want a set-and-forget solution
- **Home lab enthusiasts** who need silent, reliable PoE for access points without enterprise pricing
- **Anyone replacing a noisy or failing budget switch** who wants better thermal performance without jumping to $300 managed gear

Skip it if you're building a network with fiber uplinks, need managed features like VLANs, or plan to max out every PoE port with high-draw devices.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this power a Raspberry Pi or other non-PoE device?**
A: Only with a PoE splitter (sold separately). The switch outputs 48V on the data pairs — you'll need a splitter to drop that to 5V for a Pi, and it'll draw from your 120W budget.

**Q: Does it work with all IP cameras?**
A: Yes, if they support IEEE 802.3af/at PoE standards. That covers virtually all modern IP cams from Hikvision, Dahua, Reolink, Amcrest, and similar. Check your camera's specs for "PoE" — if it needs 12V DC only, it won't work.

**Q: Is it truly plug-and-play?**
A: Yes. No web interface, no app, no configuration. Connect power, plug in devices, done. If you need any settings beyond defaults, this isn't the right switch.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need reliable, silent PoE for 2-8 devices and don't want to fiddle with settings. **Skip it if** you need managed features, fiber uplinks, or plan to push all 8 ports to their maximum power draw.

At 4.2/5, this is the best value in the sub-$100, 8-port PoE space I've tested this year. The tradeoffs are clear, but for the majority of small office and camera deployments, this switch just works — quietly, and for a long time.
