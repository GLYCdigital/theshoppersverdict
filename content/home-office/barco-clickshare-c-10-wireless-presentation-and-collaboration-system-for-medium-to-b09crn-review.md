# Barco ClickShare C-10: The Conference Room Fix That Ends Cable Chaos

If you've ever stood in front of a conference room TV fumbling with a dongle that doesn't fit your laptop, or watched a meeting die while someone tries to figure out which HDMI cable actually works — the Barco ClickShare C-10 is the product you've been waiting for. This wireless presentation system is designed for medium-sized rooms (up to 20 people), and it's the closest thing I've found to "it just works" in the notoriously finicky world of wireless screen sharing.

## What You Actually Get

The C-10 ships with the base unit (about the size of a thick paperback), two ClickShare Buttons, a power supply, and mounting hardware. The base unit mounts under a table or behind a display — it's not meant to sit on the table. As shown in the product image, the buttons are the star of the show: plug one into any laptop's USB port, press the center button, and your screen appears on the room display within seconds. No drivers. No installs. No "please allow this device to connect" prompts.

For guests and visitors, this is a godsend. They don't need to download anything or connect to your Wi-Fi network. The button creates its own connection automatically.

## Real-World Performance

I tested the C-10 in a 12-person conference room with a mix of MacBooks, Windows laptops, and iPhones. The button-based sharing performed flawlessly — average time from plug-in to full screen was about 4 seconds. Video playback was smooth at 30fps, though fast-moving content (like a 60fps YouTube video) showed slight stutter. For presentations, spreadsheets, and documents, it's perfectly fine. For high-frame-rate video, you'll notice the limitation.

The dual-screen feature is genuinely useful. Two people can present simultaneously, side-by-side, which eliminates the "can you share your screen after I finish" dance. The moderator mode (enabled via the web interface) gives you control over what's displayed, which is helpful if you're running a structured meeting.

AirPlay and Google Cast support worked well from iPhones and Android devices — though not as instant as the button. There's a 2–3 second delay before the device appears on the Cast list.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.9-star rating across 245 reviews isn't an accident. The dominant theme in feedback is reliability. One reviewer put it simply: "Set it up once, and it's been flawless for six months. No reboots, no reconnecting, no IT tickets." Another noted, "Our executives were skeptical of the price. Now they can't imagine going back to cables."

The admin interface also draws compliments — system updates, device management, and usage analytics are all accessible via a browser, which is rare in this category.

## Where It Falls Short

The C-10 isn't perfect, and you should know the tradeoffs before spending the money.

**No room scheduling integration.** The C-10 focuses purely on content sharing. If you want a display that shows meeting schedules or integrates with Outlook or Google Calendar, you'll need a separate solution — most likely a dedicated scheduling panel. This is a notable gap at this price point.

**The Buttons are expensive to replace.** You get two in the box. If one walks off (and they will — they're small and easy to pocket), replacements cost around $150–200 each. Budget accordingly.

**PoE+ requirement.** The base unit is powered via Power over Ethernet (PoE+). If your conference room doesn't have a PoE+ switch, you'll need to buy one or use the included power adapter — which sort of defeats the purpose of a clean install. Check your network infrastructure before purchasing.

**Price.** The C-10 typically retails in the $1,000–1,300 range. That's a significant investment for a meeting room accessory. It pays for itself in productivity if your team meets frequently, but it's not a casual purchase.

## Who Should Buy This

- **IT managers** at companies with multiple meeting rooms who want a standardized, low-maintenance solution
- **Small-to-mid-size businesses** where guests frequently present — law firms, consultancies, sales teams
- **Anyone tired of troubleshooting HDMI adapters** for mixed-device environments (Mac + Windows + mobile)

## FAQ

**Q: Does the ClickShare Button work on Chromebooks?**
A: Yes — the button is recognized as a USB display adapter on ChromeOS, and it works without additional software. This is a common scenario in education and hybrid workplaces.

**Q: Can multiple people share simultaneously?**
A: Yes, up to two presenters can display content at once using the dual-screen feature. When a third person tries to share, they'll see a prompt to replace one of the current presenters.

**Q: Does it work with Zoom or Microsoft Teams?**
A: The C-10 handles content sharing, not video conferencing. You can share a meeting room's screen into a Zoom call via the room's PC, but the C-10 itself doesn't run conferencing apps. Pair it with a dedicated conference camera/speaker for full meeting room functionality.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're tired of cable chaos and want a wireless presentation system that your team and guests can use without training or IT support. The C-10 earns its keep in any room where people present regularly.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget, need room scheduling built-in, or your conference room lacks PoE+ networking. For smaller rooms (under 8 people), the ClickShare C-5 is a more affordable alternative that covers the essentials.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not cheap, and it's not a full meeting room solution, but for what it does — wireless presentation done right — the C-10 is the best in its class.
