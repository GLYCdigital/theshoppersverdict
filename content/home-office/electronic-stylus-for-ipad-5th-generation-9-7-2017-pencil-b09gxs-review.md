**The honest truth about this stylus: it's the best option that exists for your specific iPad.**

If you own a 5th generation iPad (the 9.7-inch from 2017), you already know the painful reality: Apple's Pencil *doesn't work* with your tablet. It's a hardware limitation, not a setting issue. So when you see a stylus explicitly designed for your model, you're right to be skeptical — most of the cheap options on Amazon are universal capacitive pens that feel like drawing with a sausage.

This one is different. It pairs via Bluetooth to your specific iPad model and actually delivers palm rejection, which is the feature that makes it feel like a real stylus rather than a finger replacement.

**What you get in the box**

The package includes the stylus itself, a USB-C charging cable, and two replacement nibs. Notably, the pen has a flat edge that snaps magnetically to the side of your iPad — it won't stay attached as firmly as the Pencil does on newer models, but it's convenient for short-term storage. The body is aluminum with a matte finish, which gives it a more premium feel than the glossy plastic alternatives in the same price range.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this across Notability, GoodNotes, and Procreate. In note-taking apps, it's genuinely good. Latency is acceptable — not Apple Pencil level, but completely usable for handwriting. The palm rejection works as advertised: I could rest my whole hand on the screen while writing, which is the dealbreaker feature for most people.

Battery life is solid. A 30-minute charge via USB-C gets you through a full workday of note-taking. I'll note that the LED indicator is small but visible enough to check your remaining charge at a glance.

One limitation you need to understand: there's no pressure sensitivity. If you're sketching, you'll get consistent line weight regardless of how hard you press. For handwriting, this doesn't matter. For illustration, it's a dealbreaker.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Looking through the 215 ratings, the pattern is clear. One buyer wrote: "Finally a stylus that works with my old iPad — I didn't want to upgrade my whole tablet just to use a pen." Another noted: "Palm rejection works better than I expected. I can write naturally without lifting my hand."

The common thread is relief — people who felt stuck with an older iPad finally have a competent stylus option.

**Where it falls short**

The Bluetooth connection is the weakest link. After the pen sits idle for 20-30 minutes, it sleeps, and waking it requires pressing the button and waiting 2-3 seconds for reconnection. It's not a huge deal, but it's noticeably less seamless than Apple's own implementation.

The tip wear is another honest concern. Several reviewers reported going through the included replacement nibs within 4-5 months of daily use. Plan to buy a pack of replacements when you order this.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students with an older iPad** who need to take handwritten notes in class — this is your best option short of buying a new tablet
- **Professionals** who primarily use their iPad for PDF annotation, forms, or quick notes
- **Budget-conscious users** who refuse to pay $99+ for a stylus on a device that's now several generations old

**Skip it if** you're a digital artist who needs pressure sensitivity, or if you have an iPad Pro or newer iPad that supports the actual Apple Pencil — in that case, spend the extra money.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with the iPad 6th generation (2018)?**
A: Yes, it's compatible with both the 5th and 6th gen 9.7-inch iPads. These models share the same hardware for stylus support.

**Q: Does it charge wirelessly like the Apple Pencil?**
A: No. It uses USB-C wired charging. You'll get about 10 hours of use from a 30-minute charge.

**Q: Will the palm rejection work in every app?**
A: No. It works in apps that support Apple's stylus APIs, like Notability, GoodNotes, Procreate, and Notes. In apps without that support, you'll get basic touch functionality without palm rejection.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You own a 5th or 6th gen iPad and want a functional stylus with palm rejection without upgrading your tablet. This is the best-priced option that actually works with your hardware.

**Skip it if:** You need pressure sensitivity for art, or you plan to upgrade your iPad soon — in that case, save your money and buy the real Apple Pencil when you get a compatible device.
