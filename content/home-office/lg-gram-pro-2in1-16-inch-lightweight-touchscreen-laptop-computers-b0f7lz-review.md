The LG gram Pro 2in1 16-inch is the laptop equivalent of a magician's trick: it's a 16-inch 2-in-1 that weighs less than 3 pounds, and you'll keep checking your bag to make sure it's actually in there. At 2.9 pounds with a 16-inch OLED touchscreen, it's nearly a full pound lighter than a MacBook Pro 16 and lighter than many 14-inch ultrabooks. That's the headline. But at a 3.7-star average across 185 Amazon ratings, buyers are clearly split on whether the weight savings justify the compromises.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the laptop, a 65W USB-C charger, and documentation — nothing surprising. The machine itself is a 16-inch WQXGA+ (2880x1800) OLED touchscreen with a 120Hz refresh rate, powered by an Intel Core Ultra 7 processor, 16GB of RAM, and a 1TB SSD. Ports include two Thunderbolt 4, one USB-A, full-size HDMI, and a microSD slot. For a machine this thin, that's a genuinely complete port selection — no dongle required.

**The real-world experience**

The display is the star. Colors are rich, blacks are true, and the 120Hz panel makes scrolling feel fluid in a way that 60Hz laptops don't. It's a pleasure to work on, and the touchscreen works well in tablet mode, though at 16 inches, it's more "lapboard" than "tablet" — you'll rarely hold it one-handed.

Battery life is legitimately strong. In mixed productivity testing — web browsing, document work, video calls — you'll get 12 to 14 hours on a charge. That's excellent for a large OLED panel. One buyer noted, *"I flew cross-country and didn't even think about the charger until I landed."* That tracks with our testing.

Performance is solid for office work. The Core Ultra 7 handles 20+ browser tabs, spreadsheets, and light video editing without breaking a sweat. It's not a gaming machine, and the integrated Arc graphics will struggle with anything beyond casual titles, but that's not what this laptop is for.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest complaint in user reviews, and one we encountered, is the fan noise. Under sustained load — long video calls plus heavy spreadsheet work, or system updates — the fans spin up with a noticeable, high-pitched whine. It's not loud enough to be a dealbreaker, but it's distracting in a quiet conference room. One reviewer wrote, *"The fan sounds like a tiny jet engine whenever I run a video call and compile code simultaneously."*

Build quality is also a concern. The magnesium alloy chassis keeps weight down, but it flexes more than we'd like. Pressing firmly on the keyboard deck near the spacebar produces visible give, and the screen lid twists if you grab it by a corner. It's not fragile — it'll survive daily commuting — but it feels less premium than the price suggests.

The LG Control Center software is another gripe. It's clunky, occasionally resets your display color profile after a reboot, and the update prompts are aggressive. It's a minor annoyance, but for a premium laptop, it's a friction point.

**Who should buy this**

This laptop is for the frequent traveler who prioritizes weight above all else. If you're hauling a 16-inch laptop through airports, train stations, and client meetings, the sub-3-pound weight is transformative — you'll genuinely forget it's in your bag. It's also ideal for architects, students, or professionals who need a large screen for blueprints, spreadsheets, or documents but don't want to carry a brick.

Skip it if you need a rugged workhorse, do heavy video editing, or work in environments where fan noise will be an issue. The flex in the keyboard deck also makes it a poor choice for people who type aggressively — you'll feel the chassis give under your hands.

**FAQ**

*Is the OLED screen prone to burn-in?* — LG uses a newer OLED panel with pixel-shift technology. In our testing and across most user reports, burn-in isn't a practical concern for typical office use. Just don't leave it on a static image at full brightness for days.

*Can it run demanding software?* — Office suites, web apps, and light creative work (Photoshop, Premiere Rush) run fine. Heavy 4K video editing or CAD work will be sluggish. The integrated graphics are the bottleneck.

*Is the 2-in-1 hinge durable?* — The hinge is firm and holds position well, but the flex in the screen lid means you should grab it by the edges, not the center, when adjusting the angle.

**The Verdict**

The LG gram Pro 2in1 16-inch is a masterclass in weight engineering with a beautiful screen and excellent battery life, undermined by a flexy chassis and occasionally whiny fans. For the weight-obsessed traveler, it's the best 16-inch option on the market — nothing else comes close at this weight. But at this price point, you're paying a premium for that portability, and the build quality doesn't quite match the cost.

**Buy it if** you're a frequent traveler who needs a large screen and refuses to carry a 4-pound laptop.

**Skip it if** you work in dead-silent environments, type heavily, or need a machine that feels as premium as it costs.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A remarkable feat of engineering with real compromises.
