Let's cut through the noise. The Redragon M656 is a budget wireless mouse that tries to be both a gaming peripheral and an office workhorse. At 4.2 stars from 210 Amazon ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right mouse for your desk? Here's the honest breakdown.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The M656 arrives with the mouse, a 2.4GHz USB receiver, and a quick-start guide. That's it. No batteries — which is the first thing you'll need to grab. The receiver tucks neatly into a compartment underneath the mouse, which is a thoughtful touch for anyone who travels with their gear. The mouse itself is ambidextrous in shape but has left-side thumb buttons, so it's really built for right-handers.

**Build and Real-World Performance**

The M656 weighs in at around 105 grams — light enough for fast flicks in games, substantial enough to feel planted during a long spreadsheet session. The shell is a matte ABS plastic with textured side grips. The scroll wheel has defined, tactile steps that register cleanly on each notch, and the left and right buttons use a quiet switch that won't annoy your office neighbors.

Wireless performance is where this mouse surprises. The 2.4GHz connection is stable — I didn't experience any dropped inputs or lag during testing, even with a Bluetooth keyboard running simultaneously. The advertised 10-meter range is optimistic; you'll get a solid 3-4 meters before signal degrades, which is plenty for a desk setup.

The 7200 DPI optical sensor is overkill for most tasks, but that's the point. You can cycle through five DPI presets (1200, 1600, 2400, 3200, 7200) using the button just below the scroll wheel. No driver installation needed — plug in the receiver and it works. That's a genuine convenience for anyone who's tired of bloated mouse software.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The recurring theme in positive reviews is value for money. One verified buyer wrote: "For the price, this mouse is a steal. Wireless, RGB, adjustable DPI, and it feels solid in the hand." Another noted: "I've had $80 mice that felt cheaper than this." The RGB lighting — which cycles through colors by default — is subtle enough for an office but flashy enough for gaming. The ergonomic curve draws consistent compliments from users with medium to large hands.

**Where It Falls Short**

Battery life is the biggest gripe. With the RGB lighting on, you'll get roughly a week of 8-hour workdays before the LED indicator starts flashing. You can turn the lighting off via the bottom switch, which extends battery life to around two weeks. Two AAA batteries aren't expensive, but it's a recurring cost that adds up.

The side grips are another weak point. They're rubberized and collect dust and dead skin quickly. On a used unit, they look grimy within a month. Some reviewers reported the grips becoming slick after extended use. The thumb buttons also feel slightly mushy compared to the crisp main clicks — serviceable, but not premium.

**Who Should Buy This**

This mouse makes sense for three types of people:

- **Budget-conscious gamers** who want a wireless option with good sensor performance without spending $60+
- **Office workers** who want one mouse for both work and casual gaming, with the convenience of wireless
- **Anyone tired of cable clutter** who needs a reliable, no-software-required pointing device

Skip it if you need rechargeable batteries, prefer a truly silent mouse, or want a premium feel — the plastic construction will remind you this is a budget product.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it work with Mac?**
A: Yes, it's plug-and-play on macOS. The DPI button works, but the side buttons need third-party software like BetterTouchTool to customize.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Expect 5-7 days of full workday use with RGB on. Turning off the lighting gets you to about two weeks. Keep spare AAAs handy.

**Q: Is it good for FPS gaming?**
A: The 7200 DPI sensor handles fast movements fine, but the 125Hz polling rate is lower than many wired gaming mice. It's fine for casual play; competitive players should look elsewhere.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you want a dependable wireless mouse that handles both spreadsheets and Sunday gaming sessions without breaking the bank. The M656 delivers 90% of what a $50 mouse offers at a fraction of the price.

**Skip it if** you're a competitive gamer needing high polling rates, or you hate buying batteries. Also, if you have small hands, the M656's full-size profile might feel bulky.

The Redragon M656 is proof that you don't need to spend big for a solid wireless mouse. Just budget for batteries.
