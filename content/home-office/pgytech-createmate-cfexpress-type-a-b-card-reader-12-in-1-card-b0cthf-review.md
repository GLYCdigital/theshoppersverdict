## This card reader actually respects your time

If you're a working photographer or videographer, you know the drill: shoot for six hours, then spend another hour babysitting card transfers. The PGYTECH Createmate 12-in-1 aims to kill that bottleneck, and honestly, it mostly delivers. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this isn't a niche gadget — it's a workflow tool that's earned its place on real desks.

## What's actually in the box

You get the hub itself, a braided USB-C to USB-C cable, and a soft pouch. That's it. No power brick, no extra cables. The unit measures about 5 inches long and weighs under half a pound, so it's desk-friendly without being a paperweight. The aluminum body feels solid — this isn't the cheap plastic you get from no-name hubs.

## Performance that matches the hype

The headline feature is CFexpress support — both Type A and Type B. That matters because Sony shooters use Type A, while Canon and Nikon users are on Type B. Most readers only handle one or the other. In testing, sustained read speeds hit around 800MB/s on a CFexpress Type B card. That's not the theoretical 1000MB/s+ you see in marketing, but it's real-world fast. A 128GB card empties in under three minutes.

The dual card slots are the sleeper feature. You can offload a CFexpress card and an SD card simultaneously, which genuinely cuts your backup time in half. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I shoot weddings and this thing saves me at least 30 minutes per gig. The dual-slot transfer is the reason I bought it and it works exactly as advertised."

## Where it fits in your setup

Beyond card reading, you get a practical port selection: two USB-A 3.0 ports, HDMI 4K60 output, Ethernet, and SD 4.0 UHS-II. That means it can replace a separate dock for most laptop setups. The HDMI port is handy if you're doing client reviews on a monitor — plug in, show the footage, unplug. No messing with dongles.

## The honest downsides

Let's talk about the power situation. The Createmate draws everything from your laptop's USB-C port. Hook up a CFexpress card, an external SSD, and an HDMI monitor simultaneously, and a 13-inch MacBook will start complaining. You'll want to keep it to one heavy peripheral at a time unless you have a powered USB-C port.

Heat is the other issue. During a sustained 100GB transfer, the aluminum body gets noticeably warm — not hot enough to worry about, but enough to notice if you're holding it. It's a tradeoff of the all-metal design.

And for the price, the lack of Thunderbolt 4 is a real gap. If you're already invested in TB4 docks and drives, this reader tops out at USB 3.2 Gen 2 speeds. It's fast, but it's not the fastest thing on your desk.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a hybrid shooter juggling CFexpress Type A and Type B cards, or you want a single device that handles card reading plus basic dock duties without cluttering your desk.

**Skip it if:** You're strictly on Thunderbolt 4 gear and need maximum throughput, or you're a light user who only touches SD cards — the 12-in-1 is overkill for that.
