If your office PC needs a processor that handles spreadsheets, video calls, and the occasional light photo edit without demanding a $300 graphics card, the AMD Ryzen 5 8500G is quietly one of the smartest buys at $149. This isn't a flashy flagship — it's a workhorse with an integrated GPU that actually works. But it comes with a few quirks you should know before you click buy.

## What's actually in the box

The 8500G ships as a bare CPU — no cooler included, which is a notable change from older AMD bundles. You'll need to budget $25–35 for a budget AM5 cooler like the Thermalright Assassin X. The processor itself is a 6-core, 12-thread chip built on TSMC's 4nm process, running at a 4.0GHz base clock and boosting up to 5.0GHz. It fits AMD's AM5 socket, so you'll need a B650 or A620 motherboard and DDR5 RAM — no skimping on older DDR4 memory here.

## Performance: where it shines and where it stumbles

The real star is the integrated Radeon 740M graphics. With 4 compute units clocked at 2.8GHz, this iGPU comfortably drives dual 4K monitors for office work and breezes through 1080p video playback. It can even handle esports titles like League of Legends or CS2 at playable frame rates — something the competing Intel Core i5-13400 simply can't match without a discrete GPU.

CPU performance for office workloads is solid. The 8500G scores around 13,800 in Cinebench R23 multi-core, putting it ahead of the Ryzen 5 5600X. Spreadsheet crunching, document rendering, and video conferencing all feel snappy. The 2MB L2 and 16MB L3 cache are sufficient for productivity apps.

Here's the catch: the 8500G uses a hybrid design with two "Zen 4c" efficiency cores that run at lower clocks (3.2GHz max vs. 5.0GHz on the performance cores). In lightly threaded tasks, Windows scheduling occasionally pushes work to these slower cores, causing brief hitches. It's barely noticeable in daily use — one Amazon reviewer noted, "I've had zero issues with stuttering in Excel or Chrome, but I did notice a tiny lag spike once when rendering a large PDF." If your workflow involves heavy multi-threaded rendering, the Ryzen 5 7600 is a better $20-$30 upgrade.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. The most common compliments center on value and simplicity:

- "Upgraded from a 5-year-old Intel i5. This thing is silent and fast. Boot times dropped from 45 seconds to 12."

## Where it falls short

Beyond the missing cooler and hybrid-core scheduling quirks, there's a bigger limitation: the 8500G only provides 8 PCIe 4.0 lanes for the GPU slot. If you plan to add a discrete graphics card later, you'll see a 5–10% performance penalty compared to a Ryzen 5 7600's 16 lanes. For pure office use this is irrelevant, but it's a genuine constraint for anyone eyeing future gaming upgrades.

## Who should buy this

- **Office workers building a compact, quiet PC** — the 65W TDP means a small form factor case with a basic cooler works fine
- **Budget-conscious upgraders** on the AM5 platform who want a current-gen socket with room to grow
- **Casual gamers** who play esports titles at 1080p and don't want a separate GPU

Skip it if you're doing heavy video editing, 3D rendering, or plan to pair it with a high-end GPU — the lane limitation and efficiency core quirks will frustrate you.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this CPU work with DDR4 RAM?**  
A: No. The 8500G requires DDR5 memory and an AM5 motherboard. Budget for 16GB of DDR5-5600 or faster.

**Q: Can I run dual 4K monitors with the integrated graphics?**  
A: Yes, the Radeon 740M supports up to four displays at 4K60 via the motherboard's DisplayPort and HDMI outputs.

**Q: Is the included box cooler sufficient?**  
A: There is no included cooler. Buy a budget tower cooler — a stock AMD Wraith Stealth from older CPUs won't fit the AM5 socket.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a capable, power-efficient office processor with good integrated graphics and an upgrade path to future Ryzen CPUs.

**Skip it if** you're a power user who needs maximum multi-threaded performance, or you're building a gaming rig with a discrete GPU — spend the extra $30 on the Ryzen 5 7600 instead.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The 8500G nails the office PC sweet spot at $149, with only the missing cooler and hybrid-core quirks keeping it from a perfect score.
