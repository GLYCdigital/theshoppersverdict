Let's cut the chase: if you own a Brother laser or inkjet that takes the LC-75 cartridge, you've likely stared at the shelf full of third-party knockoffs and wondered if saving eight bucks is worth the gamble. The Brother LC752PKS two-pack of genuine LC-75BK black cartridges answers that question with a resounding "probably, unless you're feeling lucky."

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting two individually sealed LC-75 black ink cartridges, each rated for approximately 300 pages at 5% coverage. That's ISO/IEC 24712 standard yield, not the inflated numbers some off-brands claim. The retail packaging includes the standard installation guide—nothing fancy, but you don't need a manual to pop these into most Brother models. Just remove the orange protective cap, slide it in until it clicks, and run a quick nozzle check.

**Real-world performance**

The standout feature here is consistency. Genuine Brother ink uses a pigment-based formula that dries fast and resists smudging on plain copy paper. In my testing across standard office documents, text came out razor-sharp at 600 DPI with no banding or streaking—even at the edges where cheaper carts often fade. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I've used knockoffs that leaked and ruined a printhead. These just work, every time." That's the core value proposition: reliability over raw cost savings.

The cartridges also play nicely with Brother's ink monitoring system. The printer accurately tracks remaining ink, giving you a genuine low-ink warning rather than the "surprise, it's empty" moment that plagues compatibles. This matters more than you'd think—I've seen aftermarket carts report 70% full right before dying mid-print job.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the math. At roughly $25–30 for the two-pack, you're paying around 4–5 cents per page. Compatible cartridges run 1–2 cents per page. If you're printing 500+ pages monthly, that difference adds up to real money over a year.

The lack of a visible ink level indicator on the cartridge itself is also mildly annoying—you're dependent entirely on the driver software. And while the 300-page yield is accurate for standard documents, heavy graphics or photo printing will drain a cartridge in far fewer pages. That's not a defect; it's just physics.

**Who should buy this**

This two-pack is ideal for the home office worker or student who prints 50–150 pages monthly and values zero-fuss reliability. If you've ever dealt with a clogged printhead from cheap ink, the peace of mind is worth the premium. It's also the right call if your printer is still under warranty—using non-genuine ink can void coverage in some cases.

Skip it if you're a high-volume printer chasing maximum cost efficiency. Look into Brother's high-yield XL cartridges or a solid compatible brand with good reviews. Also skip if your printer is older and you're not worried about warranty implications.
