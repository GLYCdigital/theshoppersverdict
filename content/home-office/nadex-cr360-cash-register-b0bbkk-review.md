The Nadex CR360 sits in that awkward middle ground of cash registers: too capable to be a toy, too simple to run a full retail operation. That's exactly where most small businesses live, so it's worth a serious look.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the register unit, a thermal receipt printer (built-in, not a separate brick), a 4-position cash drawer with removable till, and a paper roll. The keyboard is full-size with a two-line LCD display — the seller's display faces the customer, the operator's faces you. The layout is standard for the category: department keys up top, numeric pad below, and a dedicated set of function keys for void, refund, and subtotal.

**Real-world performance**

Setup takes about 30 minutes if you read the manual, closer to an hour if you don't. The CR360 supports up to 40 departments, which you program with the SET key and a series of menu prompts. It's not plug-and-play — you'll want to block out time to assign tax rates and department names before your first shift.

Once programmed, daily operation is genuinely solid. The thermal printer is quiet and fast, spitting out a clear receipt in about two seconds. The cash drawer pops open automatically when you complete a sale, which sounds minor but it's the kind of thing that keeps your line moving. The display is bright enough for a well-lit storefront, and the keys have a tactile click that gives you confidence you've actually pressed them.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 210 ratings at 4.2 stars tell a consistent story. One reviewer noted, "I've had this for 8 months in my food truck and it hasn't missed a beat," while another mentioned the training curve: "Took my high school staff about a day to get comfortable, but now they run it without looking." The department-based tracking is a recurring theme — owners of small bakeries, barber shops, and concession stands all mention that the 40-department limit is more than enough for their inventory.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of battery backup is the biggest practical flaw. If your power flickers mid-transaction, you lose that sale and have to re-key it. For a food truck or outdoor market stall, this could be a dealbreaker. The 8-clerk limit is also restrictive if you run multiple shifts with different staff — you'll be overwriting employee IDs rather than tracking individual performance. And the programming interface, while functional, is dated. If you're used to app-based POS systems, expect a learning curve.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the business owner who wants a dedicated, no-subscription register without the monthly fees of iPad-based POS systems. Perfect for a single-location coffee shop, a retail boutique, a food truck, or a service counter. It's also a strong choice for a classroom or training environment where you want to teach basic cash handling without the complexity of a full POS.

Skip it if you need cloud reporting, inventory management, or integration with e-commerce. Those require a smarter system with ongoing costs.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the Nadex CR360 track inventory?**
A: No. It tracks sales by department and total counts per item, but there's no stock-level tracking or low-stock alerts. You'll need a separate inventory system or a spreadsheet.

**Q: Can I connect a barcode scanner?**
A: Yes, the CR360 has a PS/2 port for a wired scanner. It's plug-and-play with most standard models, but there's no Bluetooth support.

**Q: Does it print a report at the end of the day?**
A: Yes, you can print a Z-report (daily summary) by department, by clerk, and overall. It's a thermal printout, so no export to Excel without manually re-entering data.

**The Verdict**

The Nadex CR360 is a reliable, no-nonsense cash register that does exactly what it promises — for a very specific type of buyer. It won't grow with a scaling business, and the lack of battery backup is a genuine vulnerability. But for the price point and the simple, durable design, it's the kind of machine that just works.

**Buy it if** you run a small, single-location business that needs dependable, no-subscription cash handling.

**Skip it if** you need cloud sync, robust reporting, or have more than 8 employees per shift.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid, honest hardware with clear limitations. Good value for the right use case.
