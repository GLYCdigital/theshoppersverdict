### What You're Actually Getting

The box contains 100 blank, fold-over card bases and 100 matching white envelopes, all sized 4x6 inches. That's a full 200 pieces for $14.99 — roughly 7.5 cents per complete card-and-envelope unit. The cards are made from a heavier cardstock (typically 200 GSM or similar), which is the first thing that separates this from flimsy printer paper. The envelopes are standard gummed-seal style, not the peel-and-stick variety, which matters if you're bulk-mailing.

### Real-World Performance

I've seen these used for everything from wedding RSVP inserts to real estate thank-you notes, and the build quality holds up. The cardstock takes ink well — both gel pens and fountain pens — without excessive bleed-through. For anyone using them as **insert cards** for larger invitation suites, the 4x6 size fits perfectly inside a standard A6 envelope or folded into a 5x7 invitation.

The **blank interior** is the key selling point. It's not lined, not printed, not perforated. That means calligraphers can draw their own borders, crafters can stamp, and businesses can run them through a laser printer without fighting pre-printed designs.

**Buyers consistently praise three things:**

- **The value per unit** — one reviewer noted, "Half the price of the craft store and you get twice as many."
- **The envelope quality** — several people specifically called out that the envelopes are "sturdier than expected" and don't tear when inserting a card at slight angles.
- **The consistent sizing** — no warping, no cuts that are off by a millimeter, which matters when you're stuffing a hundred at a time.

### Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs.

**The cards are not pure white.** They have a warm, slightly cream tone. If you're matching them against bright white invitation paper, the mismatch will be visible. If that's a dealbreaker, look elsewhere.

**The gummed envelopes require moisture.** You'll need a damp sponge or a lick-and-stick approach. For bulk mailings of 100+ pieces, this is a minor annoyance. Peel-and-seal would be a welcome upgrade, but at this price point, it's a reasonable compromise.

**The paper is good, not great.** It will handle a fountain pen nib and most gel pens, but heavy watercolor or alcohol markers will likely ghost through. This is a card for writing, not for painting.

### Who Should Buy This

This set is ideal for three specific buyers:

1. **The small business owner** who sends regular thank-you notes or appointment reminders and needs a reliable, bulk supply without a craft store markup.
2. **The DIY wedding planner** creating insert cards, place cards, or favor tags — the 100-count coverage is perfect for a 100-guest wedding.
3. **The craft hobbyist** who draws, stamps, or letterpresses their own cards and wants a blank canvas that won't buckle under ink.

Skip it if you need pure white paper, peel-and-seal envelopes, or heavy watercolor paper.

### FAQ

**Q: Can these cards be printed on with a standard home printer?**
A: Yes, but feed them one at a time through the manual tray. They're 200 GSM cardstock, which is within most laser and inkjet printer tolerances. Don't stack them in the main paper tray.

**Q: What size envelope do I need for these cards?**
A: The envelopes are included, but if you need to reorder or replace them, look for **A6 envelopes** (4.75 x 6.5 inches) — they fit a 4x6 card with a comfortable 1/8 inch margin on each side.

**Q: Are the envelopes self-sealing?**
A: No. They use a traditional gummed adhesive that requires moisture. A damp sponge or the edge of a wet paper towel works best.
