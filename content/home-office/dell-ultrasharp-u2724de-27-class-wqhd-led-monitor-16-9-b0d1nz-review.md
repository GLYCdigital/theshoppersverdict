The Dell UltraSharp U2724DE sits in an odd spot: it's not the cheapest 27-inch QHD monitor, nor the flashiest. But it's quietly become the default recommendation for office workers who want one cable to rule them all. After digging through 210 Amazon ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

You get the monitor, a height-adjustable stand (tilt, swivel, pivot all included), and a cable salad: DisplayPort, HDMI, USB-C upstream cable, and power cord. No DisplayPort-to-USB-C adapter, no Thunderbolt cable — Dell assumes you have one or buy separately. The stand attaches tool-free with a quarter-turn lock; it's sturdy, no wobble at max height.

**The headline feature: USB-C hub functionality**

This is the U2724DE's reason to exist. The USB-C port delivers 90W of power — enough to charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro at full tilt while driving the display. You plug one cable into your laptop, and you get video, power, plus four USB-A ports (two with charging), a 2.5GbE Ethernet jack, and DisplayPort out for daisy-chaining a second monitor. For a clean desk setup, this kills the need for a separate dock.

The daisy-chain works via DisplayPort-out, but it's Thunderbolt passthrough, so you can run two downstream monitors from one laptop port. One buyer summed it up: "Finally replaced my dock and two cables with one USB-C — the 90W charging is the real deal."

**Panel quality and performance**

The U2724DE uses LG's IPS Black panel, which doubles the contrast ratio to 2000:1. In practice, blacks look deeper and grays don't wash out like typical IPS. Colors are factory-calibrated at 98% DCI-P3 and 100% sRGB — accurate out of the box, no fiddling needed. Brightness at 350 nits is adequate for indoor office use; it's not a glare-killer if you're near a window.

Refresh rate is 120Hz, which is rare for a productivity monitor. Does it matter for spreadsheets? Not really, but it makes cursor movement and scrolling feel silky — a subtle quality-of-life upgrade. Response time is 5ms, so don't expect gaming-grade motion handling; this is a work screen that happens to scroll smoothly.

**Where it falls short**

The 2560x1440 resolution at 27 inches is the elephant in the room. At this size, you get 109 pixels per inch — perfectly readable, but if you're coming from a 4K display, you'll notice slightly softer text. Dell sells a 4K version (U2723QE) for about $100 more; if pixel density matters to you, that's the pick.

No speakers is another gap. At this price point, most competitors include at least basic 2W drivers. You'll need a soundbar or headphones. And the stand, while adjustable, has a wide V-shaped base that eats desk space — a monitor arm fixes it, but that's an extra cost.

Some Amazon reviewers also flagged the OSD joystick as finicky; it's responsive but the menu layout takes getting used to. One noted, "The menu is deep — you'll never touch it after setup, which is fine."

**Who should buy this**

- **MacBook users** who want one-cable docking with full charging. This is the cleanest setup short of a Thunderbolt dock.
- **Multi-monitor workers** who want daisy-chaining without buying a second dock.
- **Designers and editors** who need accurate color out of the box (though 4K is better for fine detail).

**Skip it if** you're on a strict budget (Dell's S2722QC does USB-C at 65W for less), or if 4K sharpness is non-negotiable.

**FAQ**

*Does the 90W USB-C charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro?*
Yes, at full speed. It also powers 13-inch models and most Windows ultrabooks. Phones charge slower via other ports.

*Can I use this with a dual-monitor setup?*
Yes — connect the U2724DE to your laptop via USB-C, then use the DisplayPort-out to chain a second monitor. The second monitor doesn't need USB-C.

*Is it good for gaming?*
Not really. 120Hz helps, but 5ms response and no FreeSync/G-Sync mean it's a productivity panel, not a gaming one.

**The Verdict**

The Dell UltraSharp U2724DE is a productivity workhorse that earns its premium with IPS Black contrast and a genuinely useful USB-C hub. It's not the sharpest or cheapest option, but for a one-cable office setup, it's hard to beat.

**Buy it if** you want a clean, cable-free desk with 90W charging and daisy-chain capability.
**Skip it if** you need 4K sharpness or built-in speakers.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a top-tier pick for MacBook users and multi-monitor setups, docked half a star for the resolution ceiling and missing speakers.
