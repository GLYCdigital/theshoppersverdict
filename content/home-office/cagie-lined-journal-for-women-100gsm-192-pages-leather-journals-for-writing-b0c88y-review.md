**Is the CAGIE Lined Journal just another pretty notebook, or does it actually hold up to daily writing?** With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, this 192-page, 100GSM leather-look journal has clearly impressed a lot of shoppers. I tested it against the claims — here's what you need to know before adding it to your cart.

## What You Actually Get

The CAGIE journal is a medium-format notebook (roughly A5-ish, though not exact) with a stiff PU leather cover, 192 pages of lined 100GSM paper, a sewn binding, an elastic closure, a ribbon bookmark, and an interior pocket in the back. As shown in the product image, the cover has a subtle textured grain that looks more expensive than the price tag suggests. The pen loop is attached to the spine — a small detail, but one that keeps it out of the way when writing on a flat surface.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The first thing I noticed is the lay-flat binding. This is where many journals in this price range fail — pages curl up, the spine cracks, or you have to press down awkwardly. The CAGIE opens flat from the first page to the last. No fighting it. That's a genuine quality indicator for a sewn binding, not a glued one.

The paper is the real story here. At 100GSM, it's thicker than the 70–80GSM found in most standard notebooks. I tested it with a Pilot G2 gel pen, a fountain pen with wet ink, and a mildliner highlighter. The fountain pen showed slight ghosting (you can faintly see the writing on the back of the page), but no bleed-through. The highlighter didn't soak through either. If you're a gel pen or rollerball user, you'll have zero issues. Fountain pen users should be fine with finer nibs or drier inks.

The lined ruling is a standard 7mm spacing — comfortable for most handwriting sizes, not cramped, not too loose. The lines are a subtle gray, so your writing stands out without the page feeling visually noisy.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 240 ratings, a few themes come up repeatedly. One verified buyer wrote: *"The paper is thick enough that my gel pens don't bleed through at all. I've bought three more for gifts."* Another noted: *"I'm a teacher and this journal has survived my purse for two months without any cover wear."* The durability of the PU cover and the paper quality are the two most-cited positives — and both held true in my testing.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The paper is bright white, not cream. If you're used to warm-toned pages (like those in Moleskine or Leuchtturm), this will feel starkly white. That's a preference issue, not a defect, but it matters for people who write at night under warm lighting.

There are no page numbers. For a journal this size, that's a missed feature — you'll be flipping to find entries unless you number pages yourself. The elastic closure is also on the thinner side. It works fine, but it's not the robust band you'd find on a $30+ notebook.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Bullet journalers and daily writers** who want a reliable, thick-paper notebook without paying premium prices
- **Gift-givers** — the packaging is presentable, and the leather-look cover reads as more expensive than it is
- **Students or professionals** who need a durable notebook that survives bag-carrying

**Skip it if** you're a dedicated fountain pen user who needs 120GSM+ paper, or if you require page numbers and an index for structured journaling.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Ask

**Q: Will my Sharpie or alcohol marker bleed through?**
A: Yes. This is 100GSM paper, not marker paper. Use it with pens, gel pens, rollerballs, or dry highlighters. Alcohol markers will ghost and likely bleed.

**Q: Is it truly leather?**
A: No. It's a PU (polyurethane) leather-look cover. It feels substantial and looks premium, but it's not genuine leather. For the price, that's expected.

**Q: How does the size compare to a standard A5?**
A: It's very close to A5 (roughly 5.5" x 8.3"), but not exact. If you use A5 inserts or planners, don't expect a perfect fit in accessories.
