**Are you tired of dropping $19 on a two-pack of Apple's official pencil tips every few months?** This upgraded set from a third-party seller promises compatibility across the entire Apple Pencil lineup — Pro, 2nd gen, 1st gen, and USB-C — for a fraction of the cost. But does saving money mean sacrificing the feel that makes the Apple Pencil the gold standard for iPad input? I've spent three weeks hammering these tips through daily note-taking, Procreate sketching, and PDF markup to find out.

## What's in the Box

The package contains four replacement tips, which is already double what Apple sells in their standard pack. The tips are visibly identical to OEM versions at a glance — same cylindrical shape, same metallic connector threading, same dimensions. You'll need to screw them in with the same clockwise twist that seats official tips. No adapter or tool is needed, which keeps things simple.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where these tips either earn their keep or fall flat. I tested them on an iPad Pro with a 2nd gen Pencil, then swapped to a friend's iPad Air with the new Pencil Pro. The threading mates cleanly with both — no wobble, no loosening during vigorous sketching. Palm rejection and tilt sensitivity worked flawlessly in Procreate and Notability; I couldn't detect any lag or missed strokes compared to the original tip.

The material is where things get interesting. These tips use a slightly denser polymer than Apple's, which translates to less friction on the glass. For note-takers, that's actually a pleasant improvement — the pen glides more smoothly, reducing hand fatigue during long writing sessions. For digital artists who rely on the drag of the nib for texture, you'll notice the difference immediately. It's not bad, just different — think writing with a gel pen instead of a ballpoint.

Durability is the real win. After three weeks of daily use, the tip shows minimal flat-spotting. Apple's official tips typically start developing a worn edge around the two-week mark with similar usage. Multiple reviewers in the 220-ratings pool echo this, with one noting: "I've been using these for two months now and the tip still looks new. The Apple ones would've been swapped by now." Another commented: "Works exactly like the original. I can't tell the difference unless I look really closely at the tip."

## Where It Falls Short

The smoother surface is the main compromise. If you're a heavy Procreate user who relies on texture for shading, you'll need to adjust your technique or add a paper-like screen protector to compensate. Additionally, the tips arrive loose in a simple plastic bag — no storage case. Given how easy it is to drop a tiny tip down a drain or lose one in a bag, that's a minor annoyance.

Compatibility is claimed across all four Pencil models, but I'd note that the 1st gen Pencil has a slightly different connector. It works, but I'd recommend double-checking the fit if you're using an older iPad with the original Pencil.
