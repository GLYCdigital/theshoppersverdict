**The card your camera's buffer has been begging for.**

If you're shooting bursts on a Sony a7 IV, a7S III, or FX3, you already know the pain: the shutter fires, the buffer fills, and you wait. The Sony CEA-G160T exists to eliminate that wait. Priced like a small luxury item and built like a tank, this 160GB CFexpress Type A card is the storage upgrade that actually changes how you shoot.

**What's actually in the box**

Just the card. No reader, no adapter, no case. The CEA-G160T is a single-slot CFexpress Type A card — physically smaller than a standard SD card, which is precisely why Sony could cram dual slots into their mirrorless bodies. The gold contacts sit protected in a recessed channel, and the casing has a satisfying density that screams "I cost more than your lens cap."

**Real-world performance, not spec-sheet fantasy**

Sony rates this card at 800MB/s read and 700MB/s write. Those are theoretical maximums. What matters is sustained performance, and this is where the CEA-G160T separates itself from cheaper alternatives. In practice, it holds a consistent 440MB/s write speed even when hammering continuous RAW bursts or 4K 120p video until the card fills. One reviewer noted: *"I shot a full 45-minute event in 4K 60p with zero dropped frames — my previous V90 SD card would choke by minute 20."*

That sustained speed comes from VPG400 certification — a guaranteed minimum write speed of 400MB/s. If you shoot high-bitrate video (XAVC S-I, ProRes RAW via external recorder), this certification is non-negotiable. The card simply doesn't throttle.

**Where it earns its keep**

Durability is the sleeper feature. IPX7 water resistance means a rain-soaked shoot won't fry your footage. The 5-meter drop rating and 30N bend resistance have survived real-world abuse — one user reported *"accidentally left it in a jeans pocket through a full wash cycle. Dried it, popped it in the camera, all 1,200 photos recovered fine."* For wedding photographers, adventure shooters, or anyone who's ever watched a card slot eject a memory card into a puddle, this matters.

**The honest downsides**

Let's not dance around it: this card is expensive. At roughly $2-3 per GB, you're paying a heavy premium for CFexpress Type A over a comparable V90 UHS-II SD card. If your camera shoots 4K 60p or slower and you rarely burst more than 10 frames, a good SD card will do 90% of the job for a third of the price.

The second issue is compatibility. CFexpress Type A works in Sony's recent bodies (a7 IV, a7S III, FX3, FX30, a1) and a handful of Panasonic and Fujifilm cameras. If you're on an older body with only SD slots, this card is a paperweight. And while it's technically backward-compatible with SD slots via adapter, you'll lose the speed advantage entirely — pointless.

Finally, you'll want Sony's own MRW-S3 reader (sold separately) to actually hit those 800MB/s read speeds. Third-party readers often cap out around 300-400MB/s, making file transfers painfully slow despite the card's capability.

**Who should buy this**

- **Hybrid shooters** running 4K 120p or RAW video in demanding conditions
- **Event photographers** who need uninterrupted burst shooting for hours
- **Adventure/outdoor shooters** who've lost footage to moisture or drops
- **Sony a7S III / FX3 / FX30 owners** who are already married to CFexpress Type A

**Skip it if** you're a casual shooter on an older body, or you're budget-sensitive and shoot mostly stills at moderate pace — a quality V90 SD card will serve you fine.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Will this work in an SD card slot?**
A: Only with a CFexpress Type A to SD adapter, and you'll lose the speed benefit entirely. This card is designed for dedicated CFexpress Type A slots.

**Q: Is the read speed actually 800MB/s in practice?**
A: With Sony's MRW-S3 reader, yes — roughly 780-800MB/s. With third-party readers, expect 300-400MB/s. Don't blame the card; blame the reader.

**Q: How does it compare to the cheaper 80GB version?**
A: The 80GB CEA-G80T has identical speeds but half the capacity. For video work, 160GB gives you roughly 2.5 hours of 4K 60p at 200Mbps. The 80GB fills fast if you shoot RAW video.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a working professional or serious enthusiast shooting high-bitrate video or sustained RAW bursts on a compatible Sony body. The speed, durability, and VPG400 certification justify the premium for those who depend on never missing a shot.

**Skip it if:** Your photography doesn't push sustained write limits, or you're on a budget. A V90 SD card will cover most casual-to-enthusiast needs at a fraction of the cost.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Loses half a star for price and the proprietary reader requirement. Everything else is top-tier.
