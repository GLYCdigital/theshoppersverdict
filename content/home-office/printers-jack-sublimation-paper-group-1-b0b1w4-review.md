## Is This the Budget Sublimation Paper That Actually Delivers?

Sublimation printing is unforgiving. Cheap paper bleeds, curls, or wastes expensive ink. The Printers Jack Group 1 paper sits in that crowded middle tier — priced below the big names, promising professional results. After digging through 215 ratings and testing feedback, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 110gsm paper — noticeably thicker than standard copy paper, which matters. Thin sublimation paper curls under heat and humidity; this holds its shape better during printing and transfer. As shown in the product image, the sheets have a distinct coating on the print side; don't confuse the sides or you'll waste a sheet.

You're getting straightforward loose sheets, not a roll. That's fine for mugs, t-shirts, and small panels. If you're doing wall art or continuous lengths, look elsewhere.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The coating is the star here. It grabs ink fast and holds color density well. Multiple buyers noted that prints come out "comparable to my previous brand at nearly half the cost." One reviewer put it plainly: "Great colors, no fading, and the white areas stayed clean — no pink tint like my last paper."

The paper also handles high-ink saturation without buckling. That's the failure point for cheap papers — they ripple when you lay down heavy cyan or magenta, and the printhead catches the bump. No such complaints here.

## The Honest Shortfalls

Let's not pretend this is flawless.

**Ghosting on dark fabrics.** Several buyers mentioned seeing faint outlines on black polyester when press times crept past 60 seconds. This is likely the coating off-gassing under prolonged heat. Stick to 45–55 seconds at 400°F and you're fine; push it and you'll see artifacts.

**Feed jams.** The 110gsm weight is a double-edged sword. In some Epson EcoTank models with tight rollers, buyers reported occasional misfeeds. One user said, "Works great in my Sawgrass, but my Epson 2720 eats it sideways about 1 in 20 sheets." If you're batch-printing hundreds of sheets unattended, budget for occasional babysitting.

**Ink consumption.** This paper is thirsty. It holds color well because it absorbs aggressively. You'll use noticeably more ink per print than with a lighter, less absorbent paper. That's a hidden cost if you're running high volumes.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You're a small-batch maker doing mugs, shirts, and coasters
- You want near-professional color payoff without paying premium prices
- You're using a Sawgrass printer (best compatibility reports)
- You're okay babysitting your printer during long runs

**Skip it if:**
- You need roll paper for continuous printing
- You're doing unattended high-volume production (the jams will frustrate you)
- You're pressing dark fabrics regularly and can't control press time precisely

## FAQ

**Q: Does this paper work with a regular inkjet printer?**
A: No. You need a dedicated sublimation printer (Sawgrass, Epson EcoTank with sublimation ink). Standard ink won't transfer.

**Q: Can I use it for mugs?**
A: Yes. Multiple buyers confirm it works well with mug presses. Just trim the paper to size and use heat-resistant tape.

**Q: Is the coating on both sides?**
A: No. Only one side is coated. Print on the coated side — it's slightly shinier. Printing on the wrong side wastes the sheet.

The Printers Jack Group 1 paper hits a solid sweet spot for hobbyists and small shops. It delivers vibrant color, resists curling, and plays nice with the two major printer ecosystems. The jams and ghosting issues are real but manageable — they're tradeoffs for the price.
