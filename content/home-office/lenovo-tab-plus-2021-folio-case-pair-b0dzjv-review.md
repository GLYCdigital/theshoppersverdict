Most budget tablets under $300 force you to choose between a decent screen, usable speakers, or a stylus — you rarely get all three. The Lenovo Tab Plus 2021 tries to break that pattern, pairing an 11.5-inch 2K display with quad JBL speakers and an included S-Pen in the box. It's a compelling pitch for note-takers and media consumers, but the question is whether the compromises underneath the surface make it a smart buy in 2026.

## What's in the box

You're getting the tablet itself, the folio case (which is the "Pair" part of this listing), a USB-C charging cable, and the Lenovo Precision Pen 2. The folio case is a real addition here — it's not a cheap throw-in. It's got a magnetic flap that covers the screen, a built-in kickstand with two viewing angles, and a dedicated loop for the stylus. That last detail matters more than you'd think; losing a stylus is the most common reason these bundles end up in a drawer.

## Performance and real-world use

Under the hood is a MediaTek Helio G90T processor paired with 4GB of RAM. That's a chipset we saw in gaming phones a few years ago, and it shows its age with anything beyond light productivity. Flipping between a PDF, a note-taking app, and a browser with multiple tabs will introduce noticeable lag. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Great for streaming and notes, but don't try to run a spreadsheet and a video call at the same time — it gets sluggish."* That's the honest ceiling here.

Where the Tab Plus shines is consumption. The 11.5-inch 2K panel (2000x1200 resolution) hits 400 nits of brightness, which means it's genuinely usable on a sunny patio — something many budget tablets can't claim. The TUV certification for eye comfort is a nice touch for late-night reading. The quad JBL speakers with Dolby Atmos tuning are the standout feature; they get loud without distorting, and there's actual stereo separation. For a tablet that costs roughly the price of a mid-range Bluetooth speaker, the audio alone justifies a chunk of the cost.

Battery life lands around 8–9 hours of mixed use, which is fine but not exceptional. The included 20W charger gets you from empty to 50% in about 40 minutes.

## What buyers consistently praise

The S-Pen implementation gets consistent love. It's not a Samsung S-Pen by any stretch, but it's responsive enough for handwriting notes and sketching, with 4096 levels of pressure sensitivity. One reviewer noted: *"I bought this for my daughter's online classes. The pen works flawlessly with the pre-installed Notes app, and the folio case protects it when she throws it in her backpack."* The display quality is the second most-cited positive — multiple reviewers mention being surprised by how sharp and vibrant it is at this price point.

## Where it falls short

The storage situation is the biggest red flag. 128GB is your only option with no microSD card slot, which is a head-scratcher for a device clearly aimed at media consumption. If you're planning to download movies or store a large offline music library, you'll hit the ceiling fast.

The software situation is also a concern. The Tab Plus ships with Android 11, and Lenovo has committed to Android 12, but their update history for budget devices is inconsistent. If you're buying this in 2026, you may be stuck on an older Android version with security patches that trail off.

The folio case, while functional, adds noticeable bulk. It's not something you'll want to hold one-handed for extended reading sessions.

## Who should buy this

- **Students on a budget**: The S-Pen, note-taking apps, and kickstand case make this a legitimate note-taking machine for under $300. It's lighter than a laptop and the battery lasts a full day of lectures.
- **Media consumers**: If your tablet lives on the couch and your phone on the nightstand, the display and speakers make this a strong Netflix/YouTube machine.
- **Casual users**: Browsing, email, video calls, and light games — the Tab Plus handles these without drama.

**Skip it if** you need a productivity workhorse, plan to store large media libraries locally, or require long-term software support.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the S-Pen require charging?**
A: No. It's a passive stylus that works with the display's touch layer via EMR (electromagnetic resonance) technology. No battery, no pairing, no charging. It just works.

**Q: Can this replace a laptop for work?**
A: Not realistically. The 4GB RAM and MediaTek chipset will struggle with heavy multitasking. It's fine for email, notes, and document review, but you'll hit frustration with complex tasks.

**Q: Is the folio case worth buying separately if you already own the tablet?**
A: If you can find it for under $30, yes. It's well-built, the kickstand is stable, and the pen loop is practical. But it does add weight and bulk.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a budget tablet that excels at media and note-taking without sacrificing screen quality or audio. **Skip it if** you need expandable storage, future-proof software, or serious multitasking muscle.

The Lenovo Tab Plus 2021 + Folio Case Pair earns its 4.6-star rating honestly. It's not perfect — the storage and update situation are real drawbacks — but for the price, it delivers where it counts: a beautiful display, genuinely good speakers, and a usable stylus in a complete package.
