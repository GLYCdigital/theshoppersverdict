## The $150 Office Workhorse That Actually Delivers

Most budget monitors under $200 feel like they were designed by accountants — functional, boring, and built to a price. The HP 24mh breaks that mold with an IPS panel that punches above its weight class. After spending a week with this display on a mixed workload of spreadsheets, video calls, and light photo editing, here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

HP keeps it refreshingly simple: the monitor, a stand that clicks in without tools, an HDMI cable, a VGA cable (yes, really), and a power brick. No DisplayPort cable included, which is mildly annoying since that's the only way to hit the full 75Hz refresh rate. The stand assembly takes about 30 seconds — it's a simple push-and-click mechanism that feels sturdy once locked in.

## The Panel: Where This Monitor Earns Its Keep

The 23.8-inch IPS display is the star here. HP rates it at 99% sRGB coverage, and in real-world use, colors look vibrant without the oversaturation you see on cheaper TN panels. Viewing angles are genuinely excellent — I could read text from a 45-degree angle without color shifting, which matters if you're sharing your screen during meetings.

The 75Hz refresh rate over DisplayPort or HDMI is the sleeper feature. It doesn't make this a gaming monitor, but scrolling through long documents and spreadsheets feels noticeably smoother than the 60Hz standard. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"I didn't think I'd notice the difference from my old monitor, but the smoother scrolling makes working feel less fatiguing."*

Brightness sits at a respectable 250 nits. It's fine for most indoor office lighting, but you'll want to close blinds if your desk faces a window. The anti-glare coating does a decent job cutting reflections, though it's not magic.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

With 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, the pattern is clear. Owners repeatedly mention three things: the color accuracy, the slim bezels, and the value. One verified buyer noted, *"I was skeptical about the price, but the colors match my MacBook Pro better than monitors costing twice as much."* Another highlighted the connectivity: *"Having HDMI, DisplayPort, and VGA in one budget monitor is rare — I can switch between my work laptop and desktop without unplugging anything."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the flaws. The built-in speakers are weak — 2W per channel with no bass response. They're fine for system notifications or a quick YouTube video, but you'll want headphones for anything serious. The stand only offers tilt adjustment. No height, no swivel, no pivot. If you're particular about ergonomics, budget another $25 for a VESA arm (the monitor is VESA-compatible, which helps).

The 1080p resolution at 24 inches is also worth a reality check. It's perfectly sharp for most users, but if you spend all day staring at dense text or spreadsheets, a 27-inch 1440p panel might be worth the extra $100.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Remote workers** who need a reliable second monitor with solid color for video calls and document work
- **Students** on a budget who want a display that won't look washed out during late-night study sessions
- **Small office setups** where one monitor serves multiple purposes — spreadsheets, presentations, occasional Netflix breaks

Skip it if you're a serious photo editor needing 100% Adobe RGB, or a gamer chasing high refresh rates.

## FAQ

**Does the HP 24mh work with Macs?**
Yes, via HDMI or DisplayPort. Colors render well, though you'll want to use the monitor's "HP Enhance" picture mode for the best Mac-specific color matching.

**Can I mount this on a monitor arm?**
Yes, it has a standard 100x100mm VESA mount. You'll need to remove the included stand base, which takes about a minute.

**Is 75Hz noticeable for non-gaming use?**
Surprisingly, yes. Scrolling, animations, and even moving windows between screens feel smoother. It's subtle but makes daily work feel less choppy.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a color-accurate, versatile IPS monitor under $200 for office work, school, or as a secondary display. The 75Hz refresh and full connectivity suite make it a smart long-term investment.

**Skip it if** you need height adjustment, want speakers with actual audio quality, or require higher resolution for dense text work.

At 4.6/5, the HP 24mh earns its rating. It's not flashy, but it's the rare budget monitor that doesn't force you to compromise on the things that matter most: color, clarity, and connectivity.
