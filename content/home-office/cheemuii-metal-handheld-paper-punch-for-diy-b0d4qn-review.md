Some tools you buy, use twice, and lose in a drawer. The CheeMuii Metal Handheld Paper Punch is not one of them. This is the kind of tool that earns a permanent spot on your desk—or in your craft bag—because it does one thing exceptionally well: punching a clean, precise hole through paper and light materials without complaint.

## What You Actually Get

The CheeMuii is a single-hole punch with an all-metal body and a cushioned, non-slip grip. The punch head is hardened steel, which matters more than you'd think—cheaper punches use soft metal that dulls quickly and starts tearing paper instead of cutting it. The unit measures about 5 inches long and weighs enough to feel substantial without being bulky. There's also a small built-in compartment that catches punched circles, so you're not constantly brushing paper dots off your workspace. It's a thoughtful touch that most budget punches skip entirely.

## Real-World Performance

I put this punch through the paces with standard printer paper, 65 lb cardstock, and even some thin craft leather. The result: crisp, clean holes every time. The cutting action is smooth and requires less hand pressure than you'd expect—the leverage design does the heavy lifting. On cardstock, the holes came out perfectly round with no jagged edges or half-torn fibers. That's the mark of a properly sharpened punch head.

The grip deserves special mention. It's rubberized and contoured, which makes a real difference during extended use. If you're making a handmade journal with 40+ holes, your hand won't cramp halfway through. That's not something you can say about most metal punches in this price range.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. This is a single-hole punch, which means any multi-hole project—binders, planners, multi-ring notebooks—requires careful measuring and manual alignment. If you're making a 6-ring planner insert, you're looking at six separate punches per page. It's doable, but it's tedious.

The punch also has its limits on stack thickness. It handles about 10 sheets of standard paper comfortably. Push it beyond that and you'll get incomplete cuts or the punch will jam. For thicker stacks, you need a heavy-duty two-hole punch or a binding machine.

The waste compartment is small—it holds maybe 30-40 punches before needing emptying. Minor annoyance, but worth noting if you're doing a big project.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a card maker, scrapbooker, or journal enthusiast who needs a reliable single-hole punch for everyday use. You appreciate tools that feel solid and won't fall apart after a few months. You want something that handles cardstock and thicker materials without a fight.

**Skip it if:** You need multi-hole punching for binders or planners—get a proper multi-hole punch instead. You only punch standard paper occasionally and don't want to spend money on a tool you'll rarely use. You need to punch through stacks thicker than 10 sheets regularly.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this punch handle leather or thin plastic?**
A: Yes, thin leather (2-3 oz) and most craft plastics work fine. Avoid thick leather or rigid materials like acrylic—you'll damage the punch.

**Q: Does the hole size work for standard binders?**
A: The punch produces a standard 6mm (about 1/4 inch) hole. That fits standard binder rings, but remember it's single-hole only—you'll need to measure and align each hole yourself.

**Q: How does this compare to plastic punches?**
A: The all-metal construction is the key difference. Plastic punches flex, allowing the cutting edge to misalign and tear paper. The CheeMuii stays rigid, giving you consistent clean cuts. It's also more durable—plastic punches crack at the hinge point over time.
