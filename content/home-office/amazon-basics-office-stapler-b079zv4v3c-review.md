Let's be honest: most staplers under $10 are disposable junk. The Amazon Basics Office Stapler isn't. With 52,636 ratings and a 4.6-star average, this $6.31 tool has earned its place in thousands of home offices and cubicles. But is it actually good, or just cheap enough that nobody complains? ## What You Actually Get

The box contains the stapler itself and a full strip of 1000 staples (24/6 size, the standard for office use). That's it — no filler, no fancy packaging. The stapler measures about 8.4 x 2.4 x 2.3 inches and weighs roughly 11 ounces. As shown in the product image, it's a classic half-strip design: metal head and base, plastic body with a textured grip, and a sliding rear door for loading.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

Here's where the Amazon Basics stapler surprises. The metal head and base aren't just decorative — they give the stapler enough weight to sit firmly on your desk while you staple. The soft-grip handle is comfortable for extended use, and the spring mechanism returns smoothly without that cheap "clack" you get from bargain-bin models.

I tested the 25-sheet claim on standard 20-lb printer paper. It goes through 20 sheets with zero effort. At 25, there's slight resistance but the staple still seats flush. Beyond that, you're pushing it — don't expect 30+ sheets, no matter what the listing implies. One reviewer put it plainly: *"It handles a normal school or office workload. I've stapled 15-20 pages multiple times without issue."*

The 1000 included staples are standard quality, not the flimsy kind that bend on thick stacks. When you run out, any 24/6 staples work — including the Amazon Basics refill packs that cost about $7 for 5000.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme across thousands of reviews is value. *"For the price, this is unbeatable. I bought three — one for home, one for the office, one for my bag,"* wrote one verified buyer. Another noted: *"I've had expensive staplers that jammed more than this one. It just works."* The included staples get frequent shout-outs too — most budget staplers make you buy a box immediately.

## Where It Falls Short

Two honest downsides matter. First, there's no built-in staple remover. The base is flat metal with no notch for prying out mistakes — a feature found on many $15+ models. You'll need a separate remover or a fingernail for errors.

Second, the staple pusher is finicky about loading. If you try to shove in a second strip before the first is fully spent, it can jam. The fix is easy (open the back, reset the spring), but it's an annoyance you won't hit with higher-end staplers like the Swingline Optima.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You need a reliable stapler for home, school, or light-to-moderate office use. You're setting up a desk on a budget. You want a spare for your bag or a second location. You don't staple more than 25 pages at once.

**Skip it if:** You regularly staple 30-50 page documents (get a heavy-duty model). You want a built-in staple remover. You prefer a full-strip stapler that holds 200 staples instead of 100. You need something that looks premium on an executive desk.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it jam easily?**
A: No, provided you load staples correctly. The spring mechanism is reliable. Most reported jams come from overloading or mixing staple brands mid-strip.

**Q: What staple size does it use?**
A: Standard 24/6 (also called No. 6) staples. The included 1000-staple strip is this size, and they're widely available at any office supply store.

**Q: Can it really handle 25 sheets?**
A: Yes, on standard 20-lb paper. You'll feel resistance at the top end, but staples seat properly. Thicker cardstock or 24-lb paper reduces capacity to about 15-18 sheets.
