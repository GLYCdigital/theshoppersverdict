Let’s be blunt: external hard drives are boring. You buy one, plug it in, and forget it exists until the day your laptop dies and you thank past-you for making a smart call. The WD My Passport is the best-selling boring drive on Amazon for a reason — but with 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it’s worth asking whether it still earns that spot in 2026.

Here’s the full picture.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the drive itself, a USB 3.0 cable (about 18 inches), and WD’s backup software pre-loaded. No power adapter needed — it’s bus-powered, meaning one cable handles both data and juice. The drive weighs 4.8 ounces and measures roughly 4.2 x 2.9 x 0.8 inches — smaller than a smartphone, thick enough to feel solid in hand.

## Real-World Performance

The My Passport spins at 5400 RPM, which is standard for portable HDDs. Sequential read/write speeds top out around 130 MB/s on USB 3.0. That’s roughly three to five times faster than USB 2.0, but nowhere near an SSD. What this means practically: transferring a 10 GB video folder takes about 80 seconds. A 500 GB photo library takes a few hours — set it and walk away.

The standout feature here is **hardware encryption**. Unlike software-based locks, the AES-256 encryption is built into the drive’s controller. Even if someone pulls the drive apart and reads the platters directly, your data stays scrambled. For freelancers with client files or anyone storing tax documents, that’s a genuine security layer most drives in this price range simply don’t offer.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The backup software is the surprise MVP. WD’s utility schedules automatic backups to run when the drive is connected, and it handles incremental backups — only new or changed files get copied, so it stays fast even after months of use. One verified buyer put it this way:

> *"Plugged it in, set up automatic backup in about four minutes, and haven't thought about it since. My entire 2TB photo collection is safe and I didn't have to learn anything."*

Another common thread in reviews is durability. WD drop-tests these drives to 6.5 feet, and multiple reviewers mention surviving accidental floor drops with no data loss. One wrote: *"Knocked it off my desk twice. Both times it kept working like nothing happened."* That aligns with the 4.6-star average — failures do happen, but they’re the exception, not the rule.

## Where It Falls Short

**The USB-C problem is real.** The drive ships with a USB 3.0 Type-A cable. If you own a MacBook Air, a modern ultrabook, or any 2024+ Windows laptop, you’ll need a USB-C adapter or a separate cable. That’s an extra purchase and an easy thing to forget when you’re packing for a trip. The drive itself is USB-C compatible at the port level, but WD cheaps out on the included cable.

**Speed expectations matter.** If you’re a video editor working with 4K footage directly off the drive, you will be frustrated. This is a backup and archival drive, not a working drive. The 130 MB/s ceiling means scrubbing timelines will stutter. For that workflow, step up to an SSD — you’ll pay roughly double per gigabyte but the experience is night and day.

**The plastic shell** feels thin compared to the rubber-armored LaCie Rugged or the aluminum Samsung T7. It’s fine for a desk drawer, but if you’re a field photographer tossing gear into a backpack daily, consider tougher options.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You need a reliable, large-capacity backup drive for documents, photos, music, and general file hoarding. You value hardware encryption. You want a set-and-forget solution from a brand with the best warranty support in the HDD business (WD’s 3-year limited warranty is genuinely solid).

**Skip it if:** You edit video directly off external storage, you’re all-in on USB-C and refuse to carry adapters, or you need something that survives being thrown into a muddy worksite bag. Also skip if you’re buying for a Mac without a USB-A port — factor the adapter cost into your budget.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the WD My Passport work with Mac and Windows?**
A: Yes, but with a catch. The drive ships formatted for Windows (NTFS). Macs can read it but not write to it without reformatting. If you’re on macOS, reformat to exFAT in Disk Utility — takes two minutes, works with both systems, and you lose nothing since the drive is empty out of the box.

**Q: How long does the 2TB version last?**
A: Realistically, 3-5 years of daily use is the common lifespan for 2.5-inch portable HDDs. WD’s warranty covers 3 years. The biggest killer is physical drops while spinning — always eject the drive before unplugging. Heat is the second killer, so avoid leaving it in a hot car.

**Q: Is the encryption worth using?**
A: Yes, but only if you set it up. WD Discovery software lets you enable hardware encryption with a password on first use. If you skip that step, the drive works like a normal unencrypted HDD. Once enabled, you’ll need the password every time you connect — don’t lose it, because recovery without the password is impossible by design.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The WD My Passport is the default choice for good reason. It’s not the fastest, not the toughest, and not the most modern — but it delivers exactly what most people need: reliable, encrypted, large-capacity backup storage at a fair price, backed by a brand that’s been doing this for 35 years.

**Buy it if** you want dependable backup without overthinking it. **Skip it if** your workflow demands SSD speeds or you refuse to carry a USB-C adapter.

For 90% of home and office users, this drive quietly does its job — and that’s the highest compliment a backup drive can earn.
