**The ESR Magnetic Case for iPad Air 5 (2022) is a study in tradeoffs.** It nails the magnetic attachment system that Apple's own folio cases charge a premium for, but it skimps on edge protection and long-term magnet durability. At a 4.4-star average from 220 ratings, most buyers are satisfied — but "most" isn't "all," and the complaints are worth hearing before you drop your money.

**What you actually get:** The case ships with two pieces — a polycarbonate back shell that snaps onto the iPad Air 5, and a fabric-covered front flap that connects magnetically. The back shell has precise cutouts for the volume buttons, USB-C port, and camera bump. The front flap folds into a triangle for two viewing angles: roughly 60 degrees for typing and 30 degrees for media. The Apple Pencil 2 attaches to the right edge and charges wirelessly through the case material — no need to remove it. As shown in the product image, the Pencil sits flush against the side with the magnetic strip aligned correctly.

**Performance in the real world:** The magnet strength is the headline feature, and it mostly delivers. The front cover snaps on with a satisfying click and stays put in a bag. In landscape orientation, the iPad Air 5 holds firm at both kickstand angles — I tested the 60-degree setting for a two-hour FaceTime call with no sagging. Portrait orientation works too, which is rare in this category. The back shell fits snugly without being impossible to remove; the corners are open (not fully wrapped), which keeps the case light but means drops on the corners put the iPad's aluminum frame at risk.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The magnetic ecosystem is the top compliment. One verified purchaser wrote, *"The magnets are surprisingly strong for a $30 case — my iPad stays put on the fridge when I'm following recipes in the kitchen."* Another noted, *"The kickstand is actually stable, unlike the flimsy ones on cheaper cases. I use it on my desk every day for work."* The Pencil charging passthrough also gets frequent shoutouts — it works consistently, which isn't true for every magnetic case at this price point.

**Where it falls short:** The biggest structural flaw is the detached front cover. When you fold it back for kickstand mode, the screen is completely exposed — no lip, no raised bezel, nothing. If you drop the iPad face-down while using the kickstand on a desk, you're looking at a cracked screen. The second issue is magnet degradation. One long-term reviewer reported, *"After 4 months, the front cover doesn't snap on as securely. It still works, but you can feel the difference."* This tracks with the physical reality — neodymium magnets lose strength with repeated stress cycles.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Office workers** who dock their iPad Air 5 on a desk for video calls and note-taking — the kickstand angles are genuinely useful
- **Kitchen users** who want to mount the iPad on a fridge or metal surface for recipes
- **People who hate folio cases** that cover the screen when using the iPad as a tablet

**Who should skip it:**
- **Clumsy users** who drop devices — the open corners and exposed screen are a liability
- **Travelers** who need edge protection in a backpack — this case prioritizes form over protection
- **Those who want a one-piece case** — the two-piece design means the front cover can get lost

**FAQ:**

*Q: Does the case work with a screen protector?*  
A: Yes, but keep it thin. Tempered glass under the front cover adds pressure that can weaken the magnetic snap. A 0.3mm film protector works best.

*Q: Will the magnets interfere with the iPad's internal compass or Apple Pencil?*  
A: No. The magnets are positioned along the edges, away from the internal sensors. The Pencil pairing and charging work normally.

*Q: Can you use the iPad in portrait mode with the kickstand?*  
A: Yes, but only at the steeper 60-degree angle. The 30-degree angle is too shallow for portrait stability.

**The Verdict:** Buy it if you want a magnetic case that delivers Apple-quality attachment at a mid-range price, and you're willing to accept the lack of edge protection. Skip it if you need drop protection or plan to use the kickstand in exposed environments. At 4.2/5, it's a solid office companion — just don't mistake it for a rugged case.
