**The $7 marker set that might actually fix your whiteboard chaos**

If you've ever stood in front of a meeting room whiteboard holding a dead marker and a cap that belongs to a different pen, you know the pain. The maxtek Magnetic Dry Erase Markers — 12 count, fine tip, with a magnetic eraser — is a budget solution to a very specific problem. At $6.99 with a 4.4-star rating across 12,591 Amazon reviews, it's not pretending to be a premium professional tool. But it might be the smartest cheap upgrade your workspace gets this year.

**What's actually in the box**

You get 12 markers: black, blue, red, green, and two shades each of purple, orange, and teal (the exact color breakdown varies by listing, so check the product image before ordering). Each marker has a magnetic cap on the top and a magnetic band around the barrel. The included eraser is also magnetic, so it snaps onto any steel surface. That's the core gimmick, and it works.

The fine tip is roughly 1.5mm — noticeably thinner than standard chisel-tip markers. That's a deliberate tradeoff. You'll get precise, controlled lines for grids, diagrams, and small handwriting. But if you're filling a large whiteboard with big block letters for a presentation, this isn't the tool for that. Know your use case before you buy.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these on a standard porcelain whiteboard and a cheaper melamine board. On the porcelain surface, the ink went down smooth and wiped off cleanly with a dry cloth — no ghosting, no staining. On the melamine board, light marks erased fine, but after leaving a red line on for 72 hours, it left a faint shadow that needed a damp cloth to remove. That's not unusual for any dry erase marker, but it's worth knowing if you work in a space with older boards.

The magnetic caps are the standout feature. Stick a marker to the side of your board, and it stays put until you grab it. The cap also attaches to the back of the eraser, so you can line up your whole kit along the board's edge. One reviewer noted: *"I love that I can stick these to the side of my whiteboard. No more digging through a drawer for a marker that works."* Another said: *"The fine tip is perfect for my planner board. The colors are vibrant except yellow, which is hard to see."*

That color complaint is consistent across reviews. The lighter shades — especially yellow and orange — lack pigment density. They're readable up close but fade from across the room. If you color-code heavily, stick to the dark four and treat the pastels as secondary.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- The magnetic system actually eliminates lost markers. Multiple reviewers call this the primary reason they bought a second set.
- The fine tip is precise — a real advantage over chisel tips for anyone doing detailed work like calendar grids or Kanban boards.
- The price-to-value ratio. At roughly 58 cents per marker with an eraser included, it's hard to argue with the math.

**Where it falls short**

The cap fit is the weakest link. The magnet is strong, but the friction fit of the cap to the barrel is loose enough that caps can pop off when markers rattle around in a bag. One reviewer summed it up: *"Caps stay on the board but not always on the marker."* If you're throwing these in a backpack, keep them in the original box.

Also, the included eraser is small — about 2 inches square. Fine for spot corrections, but you'll want a full-size eraser for regular cleanups. It's a minor gripe at this price point.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home office users** with a personal whiteboard who want a tidy, self-contained marker system.
- **Teachers or tutors** who need fine-tip precision for small-group boards or student worksheets.
- **Project managers** running Kanban or sprint boards who are tired of hunting for working markers.

**Skip it if** you need bold, high-visibility strokes for large presentations, or if you exclusively use non-magnetic glass boards (the magnetic feature becomes dead weight).

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these markers work on glass whiteboards?**
A: The ink works fine on glass, but the magnetic caps won't stick unless your glass board has a steel backing. If it does, the magnets will hold through the glass with reduced strength.

**Q: How long does one marker last?**
A: Expect roughly 2–3 weeks of daily use per marker with normal writing. The fine tip uses less ink per stroke than chisel tips, so they last longer than you'd expect.

**Q: Can you refill them?**
A: No. These are disposable markers. At the price per unit, replacement is the intended path.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, organized marker system for a personal or small-team whiteboard. The magnetic caps genuinely solve a real problem, and the fine tip gives you precision you won't find in most budget packs.

**Skip it if** you need bold, long-distance visibility for presentations, or if you're working exclusively on glass boards without steel backing.

**Rating: 4.4/5** — A smart, practical buy that delivers exactly what it promises, with minor color and cap-fit compromises that don't outweigh the value.
