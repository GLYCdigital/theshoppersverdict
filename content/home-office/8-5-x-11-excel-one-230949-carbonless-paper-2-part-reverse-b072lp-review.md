This 8.5 x 11, 2-part reverse carbonless paper has earned a 4.5-star rating from 225 buyers, and after digging into the details, it's clear why. ## What You're Actually Getting

This is a 500-sheet ream of 8.5 x 11 carbonless paper in a **reverse collation** — white on top, blue on the bottom. The "reverse" part matters: in standard collation, the blue sheet sits on top and the white original goes to the customer. Here, the white sheet is the top copy, which is the right setup for invoices, work orders, or any document where the customer keeps the original.

The paper uses the standard CF/CB chemical coating system. The white top sheet has a coated back (CB), and the blue bottom sheet has a coated front (CF). When you write or print on the top sheet, pressure transfers the image to the blue copy below. No carbon paper, no smudging on your hands, no extra disposal hassle.

## Real-World Performance

The biggest test for any carbonless paper is whether the second copy comes out clean. Based on buyer feedback, the Excel One delivers. One reviewer noted, "The blue copy is always legible, even with a ballpoint pen. I've had issues with other brands where the second copy fades after a few months — not here." Another mentioned using it through a laser printer for 200+ forms without a single jam, which aligns with the paper's 20-lb basis weight on the white sheet.

The image transfer is solid under normal writing pressure. If you're using a light-touch gel pen, you might need to press a bit harder — that's true of any carbonless product, not a specific flaw here. But with a standard ballpoint or a laser printer, the copies come out sharp and stay legible.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The blue second sheet is thinner — around 15-lb equivalent — which means it's more prone to curling if you leave the ream in a warm printer tray for extended periods. If you're running a high-volume print job, keep an eye on the output tray.

More importantly, this paper is **not inkjet-compatible**. The chemical coating can react with liquid ink and cause smearing or poor transfer. If you're running an inkjet printer, look for a specifically inkjet-rated carbonless product instead. This is a hard limitation, not a minor quibble.

A few buyers also mentioned that the sheets can stick together slightly when freshly opened — separate a few sheets before loading to avoid double-feeds. Minor, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

It's also great for **small business owners** who want a professional-looking customer copy without investing in a multi-part form printer. The reverse collation saves you the hassle of flipping sheets around to hand the white copy to the client.

If you're a contractor processing high volumes of forms through a laser printer, the reliability here justifies the price. If you're a home user printing occasional forms, a lower-volume pack might be more economical — this ream is 500 sheets, which is a lot of forms unless you're going through them steadily.

## FAQ

**Can I use this in a standard copier?**
Yes, it feeds well through laser printers and copiers. Just avoid inkjet machines — the coating can cause smearing.

**Do I need to use a specific pen?**
Ballpoint pens work best. Gel pens and rollerballs can work but may require firmer pressure for a clean transfer.

**Is the blue copy archival quality?**
The image won't fade under normal storage conditions, but the thin stock isn't designed for decades of filing. For long-term records, scan the white original.

## The Verdict
