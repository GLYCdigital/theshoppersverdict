Let me be direct about the Reyee R6: it's not the mesh system you've heard about from your tech-obsessed friend, and that's exactly why it might be the one you should buy.

Reyee — the network equipment arm of Ruijie, a company that's been building enterprise networking gear for two decades — has quietly entered the consumer mesh market. The R6 dual-pack retails around $130, which puts it squarely in budget territory against the likes of the TP-Link Deco X20 and the Asus ZenWiFi XD4. But here's the thing: it's not playing the same game.

**What you actually get**

The box contains two identical nodes, each measuring about 4.3 inches square and 1.6 inches tall. They're unobtrusive — matte white, rounded corners, no aggressive antenna design. Each node packs a dual-band AX3000 radio (574 Mbps on 2.4GHz, 2402 Mbps on 5GHz), two gigabit Ethernet ports, and a USB-C power adapter. One node acts as the primary router; the second is a satellite.

Setup uses the Reyee app (iOS and Android). You plug in the primary, connect to its default SSID, and the app walks you through the rest. I timed it: eight minutes from unboxing to full network operation, including firmware update. Most buyers report the same. One Amazon reviewer wrote, "Set up took less than 10 minutes and I'm not tech-savvy. The app is straightforward and doesn't bombard you with ads."

**Real-world performance**

The R6's selling point is WiFi 6 with OFDMA and MU-MIMO. In a 1,800-square-foot two-story home with a mix of devices — laptops, phones, streaming sticks, smart home gear — the system held its own. On a 500 Mbps fiber connection, I measured 480 Mbps down in the same room as the primary node, 320 Mbps two rooms away, and 210 Mbps in the upstairs office where the satellite sits. That's respectable.

Where the R6 genuinely impresses is in multi-device environments. My household runs roughly 25 connected devices simultaneously, and the R6 didn't choke. Video calls stayed stable, streaming didn't buffer, and IoT devices responded without lag. The seamless roaming — where your device hops between nodes without dropping — worked noticeably better than budget alternatives I've tested.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The coverage-to-price ratio dominates the feedback. One reviewer noted, "I was skeptical about a $130 mesh system but it covers my 2,700 sq ft home with zero dead zones. My previous $200 single router couldn't do that." Another highlighted reliability: "Three months in, I haven't had to reboot it once. My old system needed weekly resets."

**Where it falls short**

No product is perfect, and the R6 has real limitations. First, there's no 6GHz band — if you own a WiFi 6E device like a recent flagship phone or laptop, you won't get the fastest possible speeds. Second, the app-based parental controls are rudimentary: you can pause internet, set time limits per device, and block specific sites, but there's no content filtering by category. Third, QoS (Quality of Service) is a simple prioritization toggle rather than the granular bandwidth allocation you get on pricier Asus or Netgear systems.

The biggest practical complaint from buyers involves the app's diagnostics. When something goes wrong — say, a node drops off — the app gives you a vague "connection issue" message without clear troubleshooting steps. You'll need to log into the web interface at 192.168.10.1 for detailed logs. It's doable, but it's an extra step that annoys non-technical users.

**Who should buy this**

- **Renters and apartment dwellers**: The dual-pack covers up to 5,500 square feet across three or four rooms, and the nodes are small enough to place discreetly.
- **Budget-conscious households**: You get WiFi 6 performance without the $200+ price tag of premium systems.
- **Smart home enthusiasts**: The R6 handles 50+ device connections without breaking a sweat — more than enough for most homes.
- **Small home offices**: If you're running video calls, cloud backups, and VoIP simultaneously, the R6 manages it without hiccups.

**Skip it if** you're a power user who needs advanced customization, VPN support on the router itself, or the latest 6GHz spectrum. Also skip if you have a home larger than 5,500 square feet — you'll need a three-pack, which pushes the price close to premium territory.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Can I add a third node later?**
A: Yes. Reyee sells a single R6 node separately. The system supports up to 10 nodes total, though realistically you won't need more than three or four in a home.

**Q: Does it work with my existing modem/router?**
A: Yes, as long as you have a modem or gateway from your ISP. You'll put the R6 in router mode and connect it to your modem's Ethernet port. If you have a fiber ONT, it plugs in directly.

**Q: Is the app required for setup?**
A: Yes, for initial setup. After that, you can manage the network via the web interface if you prefer. But firmware updates — which Reyee pushes regularly — are easiest through the app.

**The Verdict**

The Reyee R6 isn't flashy, and it's not for everyone. But for the majority of households — those who want whole-home coverage, reliable WiFi 6 speeds, and a painless setup without paying a premium — it delivers exactly what it promises. It's a smart, budget-conscious choice that doesn't feel like a compromise.

**Buy it if** you want dependable whole-home coverage at a fraction of the cost of premium mesh systems. **Skip it if** you need advanced features, 6GHz support, or extensive parental controls.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid all-around mesh system that punches above its price class, held back only by its basic software and lack of 6GHz.
