## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains 12 legal-size hanging folders made from translucent polypropylene. Each folder measures roughly 14.75 x 9.5 inches with a 2-inch gusseted bottom that expands. The hanging rails are rigid plastic, not the flimsy wire you'll find on bargain folders. The pack includes 24 repositionable tab labels — two per folder — plus a set of A-Z and 1-31 insert tabs for custom indexing.

As shown in the product image, the material is semi-transparent, giving you a quick visual inventory without pulling each folder. The reinforced top rail is noticeably stiffer than standard folders, which matters when you're sliding them across a crowded drawer rail.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the "extra capacity" claim gets tested. I loaded one folder with a year's worth of legal documents — roughly 200 sheets — plus several bound reports. The gusseted bottom expanded cleanly, and the folder hung straight without the bottom sagging into the drawer. Standard manila hanging folders start bowing around 100 sheets. The NORDUN held its shape at double that.

The polypropylene material is genuinely tough. It doesn't tear at the stress points where the gusset meets the side walls — a common failure mode on cheaper plastic folders. Spilled coffee? Wipe it off. The material is waterproof, which is a real advantage over paper folders in humid environments or if you're filing near a printer.

One buyer noted, "These are the first hanging folders I've bought that don't warp after six months of daily use in a busy law office." Another mentioned the clear plastic made it easy to spot misfiled documents without opening every folder.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the drawbacks. The extra capacity comes from a wider, deeper gusset — that means these folders take up more horizontal space in your drawer. If you have a tight-fit file cabinet or a drawer with less than 12.5 inches of interior clearance, these won't slide smoothly. Measure before you buy.

The adhesive tab labels are a minor frustration. They stick firmly on first application, but if you peel and reposition them, the adhesive loses grip. For permanent filing systems, that's fine. For people who reorganize frequently, it's annoying.

Finally, at roughly $25–30 for a 12-pack depending on current pricing, these cost 2–3x more than basic manila hanging folders. The durability justifies the premium for heavy users, but if you're filing a handful of documents quarterly, the value isn't there.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy if:** You're a legal professional, accountant, medical office, or anyone handling high-volume paperwork that needs frequent access. The durability and capacity pay for themselves in less drawer-jamming and fewer replacements. Also ideal for home offices that store important documents long-term — the waterproof, tear-resistant material protects better than paper.

**Skip if:** You have a compact or older file cabinet with tight tolerances, you file rarely, or you need to constantly rearrange your filing system with movable tabs. Standard manila folders at half the price will serve you fine.
