## What You Actually Get

The package includes six identical clear tents. No inserts, no paper, no assembly tools — just the holders themselves. Each one is made from 0.8mm PET plastic, which is noticeably sturdier than the 0.5mm stuff you'll find in cheaper alternatives. The dimensions are generous: a standard letter-size sheet (8.5" x 11") folded in half drops right in with about an inch of breathing room on each side. If you print your own name cards, you won't be fighting with trimming or folding gymnastics.

The design is straightforward: a flat base that flares outward slightly, with the two clear panels meeting at a gentle peak. The rounded corners aren't just aesthetic — they prevent the sharp edges that plague cheaper acrylic holders, which means no paper cuts when you're grabbing them mid-meeting.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Set one up on a desk, and it holds its ground. The weighted base keeps it stable even when someone's gesturing enthusiastically during a whiteboard session. Dropping a standard business card inside works fine, though the tent looks best with a half-sheet insert that fills more of the visible space.

I tested these across a week of daily use — conference room, home office, a client meeting. The plastic held up without cracking or warping, even after being packed and unpacked from a laptop bag. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. The insert doesn't lock in place — it just sits between the two panels. A firm bump can shift your name card off-center, and you'll be reaching over to nudge it back mid-conversation. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

The PET surface also scratches if you're careless with cleaning. Use a microfiber cloth; paper towels with any grit will leave hairline marks that catch the light. And if you're hoping to stick these to a metal filing cabinet or a whiteboard, forget it — there's no magnetic backing. These are strictly tabletop tools.
