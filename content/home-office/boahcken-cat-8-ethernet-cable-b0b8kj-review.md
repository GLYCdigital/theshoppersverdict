Most ethernet cables are an afterthought. You grab whatever's in the drawer, plug it in, and hope the Wi-Fi extender does the heavy lifting. The Boahcken Cat 8 changes that calculus—but not for everyone. After testing this cable in a home office setup with a 2.5Gbps fiber connection, I can tell you exactly where it shines and where it's overkill.

## What's actually in the box

You get the cable, nothing more. No velcro ties, no carrying pouch, no documentation. That's fine—ethernet cables don't need accessories. What matters is the build. The flat ribbon design measures roughly 6mm thick, and the gold-plated RJ45 connectors feature a snagless boot that clicks into place with satisfying certainty. The cable is available in lengths from 3 to 100 feet, so measure your run before ordering.

## Real-world performance

Here's the thing about Cat 8: it's rated for 40Gbps up to 30 meters. Most home users don't have 40Gbps equipment. But that headroom means this cable will never be your bottleneck. In testing, I ran sustained transfers between two NAS units—one with 10Gbps networking—and the Boahcken maintained full line speed without drops or retransmissions. The shielding (S/FTP, foil plus braided) kept interference at bay even when I ran it parallel to power cables for six feet.

One buyer noted, "I was skeptical about the flat design, but it handles my 10Gbps connection flawlessly. No packet loss after three months of daily use." That matches my experience.

## Where it falls short

The flat form factor is a double-edged sword. It's brilliant for running under a rug or along a wall—no tripping hazard, no visible bulge. But if you need to make a tight 90-degree turn right at the wall plate, the cable resists. It wants to lie flat, not bend sideways. In a rack or behind a desk with limited clearance, you'll fight it.

Also, the stiffness. This is a heavily shielded cable—that's why it performs so well—but it doesn't drape like a Cat 6. You can't casually coil it and tuck it away. Plan your path before you commit.

## Who should buy this

- **Home office workers with gigabit or faster internet**: If you're paying for speeds above 1Gbps, this cable ensures you actually get them.
- **Gamers and streamers**: Low latency, zero jitter, and the flat design runs cleanly to your console or PC.
- **Anyone running cable along floors or baseboards**: The flat profile is genuinely better than round cables for discreet routing.

## Skip it if

You only need a short, flexible patch cable between devices on the same desk. A Cat 6 or Cat 6a will do the job for a fraction of the price. And if you're doing in-wall installation, check local code—some jurisdictions require plenum-rated cable, which this isn't.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Cat 8 backward compatible with my old router?**
A: Yes. It works with any device that has an RJ45 port, from 10/100Mbps legacy gear to 10Gbps+ networking. It just runs at whatever speed your equipment supports.

**Q: Does the flat cable affect performance?**
A: No. The flat design is purely about routing convenience. The internal twisted pairs and shielding meet Cat 8 specs—performance is identical to round cables.

**Q: How durable is the connector?**
A: The gold-plated pins resist corrosion, and the snagless boot protects the retention clip. I've plugged and unplugged mine dozens of times with no loosening.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a future-proof, high-performance cable that routes cleanly and won't become your network's weak link. **Skip it if** you need maximum flexibility in tight spaces or you're on a budget with sub-gigabit internet.
