The MSI MPG Z790 Carbon MAX WiFi II sits in an odd spot. It's not the flagship Godlike, but it's not budget either. At this price point, you're paying for the stuff that actually matters when you're building a serious Intel rig: power delivery, connectivity, and storage headroom. Let's cut through the marketing.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the board, three SATA cables, two M.2 screws and standoffs, a Wi-Fi antenna that actually mounts externally (not the cheap stick-on kind), and the usual I/O shield — which is pre-installed this generation. There's also a USB drive with drivers, but honestly, just download the latest from MSI's site. The box also includes a quick-start poster that's more useful than the full manual for the first hour.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The Carbon MAX WiFi II is a heavy board — the 8-layer PCB and massive VRM heatsinks make it feel substantial. That's not fluff. The 19-phase VRM (105A smart power stages) is the real headline. On a 14900K with an all-core Cinebench load, VRM temps stayed around 62°C under an open bench with ambient at 24°C. That's headroom for overclocking or running a hot chip without worrying about throttling.

The PCIe 5.0 x16 slot is reinforced with steel armor, and the latch mechanism works well. The five M.2 slots are the standout feature for anyone building a storage-heavy workstation. The primary slot runs off the CPU at full Gen5 x4 speeds — with a Gen5 drive like the Crucial T705, you'll see sequential reads north of 12GB/s. The other four slots share the chipset lanes, but for gaming or general productivity, you won't notice the difference.

The Wi-Fi 7 module (BE1750) is not a paper spec. On a 6GHz network, I pulled sustained 2.1Gbps transfers to a NAS. The pre-installed antenna with magnetic base is a genuine upgrade over previous generations.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews reflects real satisfaction. One verified buyer noted: *"The BIOS is the best I've used since my old Crosshair days. The memory try-it feature got my 7200MHz kit stable in minutes."* That's a recurring theme — the BIOS memory overclock presets are genuinely helpful. Another reviewer mentioned the board's aesthetics: *"The RGB is subtle, not tacky. The carbon pattern on the heatsinks looks premium in a glass case."*

## Where It Falls Short

The Q-Release button for the GPU is the biggest frustration. It's positioned right next to the RAM slots, and with a large graphics card installed, you'll be fishing with a screwdriver. Multiple buyers have mentioned this. One said: *"I had to remove my GPU fan shroud just to reach the release. Design oversight."*

The SATA situation is also worth noting. MSI placed four of the six ports vertically along the board edge. In a case with a bottom-mounted PSU shroud, you'll need right-angle cables. Two ports are side-mounted, which helps, but it's still a minor annoyance.

BIOS flashback works, but the manual's diagram is genuinely confusing. The port is labeled clearly on the rear I/O, but the button placement on the board itself — near the bottom edge — isn't intuitive. Watch a YouTube video before you need it.

## Who Should Buy This

This board is for the builder who wants premium features without paying Godlike-level prices. If you're pairing it with a 14th-gen i7 or i9, or a 13th-gen that you plan to run at high power limits, the VRM headroom is worth it. The Wi-Fi 7 and five M.2 slots make it a strong choice for content creators who need fast storage and reliable networking. If you're on a 12600K with a single SSD and don't care about Wi-Fi, you're paying for features you'll never use — get a cheaper Z790.

## FAQ

**Does this support DDR4?** No. It's DDR5 only. If you're upgrading from a DDR4 system, factor in new RAM costs.

**Will it fit a 14900KS?** Yes, with proper cooling. The VRM can handle it, but you need a 360mm AIO or custom loop.

**Can I run three Gen5 drives?** No. Only the first M.2 slot runs at Gen5 x4 off the CPU. The other four are Gen4 x4 via the chipset.

## The Verdict: 4.2/5

**Buy it if:** You're building a high-end Intel system with a 14th-gen i7/i9, want Wi-Fi 7 today, and need multiple fast M.2 slots without stepping up to flagship pricing.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget, don't need Wi-Fi 7, or your build is more mainstream — a B760 or cheaper Z790 will serve you fine.

The Carbon MAX WiFi II is a solid, capable board with a few frustrating quirks. The VRM and storage options are exceptional; the Q-Release button and SATA layout are the tradeoffs. At this price, it's a fair deal for the right builder.
