The Xerox B415DN is a serious black-and-white workhorse hiding in a box that often confuses the people who buy it. At 3.7 stars from 185 ratings, this machine earns its middling score honestly — it prints like a champ but can make you want to throw a router out a window during setup. Let's break down who should tolerate the quirks and who should walk away.

**What's in the box**

You get the B415DN unit itself — a substantial 38-pound desk anchor measuring roughly 17.5 x 15.7 x 16.5 inches. Included: a starter toner cartridge (good for about 6,000 pages, less than the 10K standard yield), a setup guide that assumes more technical comfort than most buyers have, and a power cord. No USB cable, no Ethernet cable — plan to buy one or go wireless. The 3.9-inch color touchscreen is the control center, and the 50-sheet automatic document feeder (DADF) sits on top, which is the feature that actually justifies the price for many offices.

**Performance: where it shines**

Print speed is the headline. Xerox rates it at 50 pages per minute, and while real-world duplex printing slows things down, single-sided jobs fly. First page out lands around 5 seconds. Text is crisp at 1200 x 1200 dpi — this is a true office-grade print engine, not a home printer with a fancy badge. The DADF genuinely works: drop in a 20-page two-sided contract, hit copy, and it flips and scans both sides automatically without you touching the glass. That alone saves 10+ minutes a day in busy offices.

Buyers consistently praise the output quality. One reviewer wrote, *"Print quality is excellent — sharp text, fast, and the duplex scanning is a lifesaver for our HR paperwork."* Another noted, *"This replaced an HP that died after two years. The Xerox feels built to last."*

**Where it falls short**

Setup is the big one. Multiple reviewers report the same story: wireless connection drops within hours or days, the printer vanishes from the network, and the touchscreen setup wizard loops endlessly. One frustrated buyer said, *"I spent three hours trying to get this on Wi-Fi. Finally plugged in an Ethernet cable and it worked instantly."* If you're not comfortable running a cable or digging into router settings, budget for that friction.

The touchscreen itself is another sore point. It's responsive enough for basic tasks, but navigating advanced settings feels like using a 2012 smartphone. Menus lag, and options like scan-to-email require manual SMTP configuration — a genuinely annoying process for small offices without IT support.

Toner costs are reasonable per page but the upfront sting is real: a standard cartridge runs around $120, high-yield closer to $300. You'll pay less per page over time, but the initial investment catches some buyers off guard.

**Who should buy this**

The B415DN fits three specific scenarios: a small office that prints 3,000+ pages monthly, a workgroup sharing one reliable black-and-white machine, or anyone who needs automatic duplex scanning without paying $800+ for a color MFP. It's a poor fit for home offices that print 200 pages a month — you'll pay for speed you don't need and deal with setup headaches for minimal benefit.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Is the wireless reliable?** Mixed. Many buyers report success after initial setup, but a significant minority deal with recurring drops. If you have a modern router and some networking patience, it's workable. Otherwise, hardwire it.

**Does it work with macOS?** Yes, AirPrint is supported, but a few Mac users report needing to download the full driver package rather than relying on AirPrint alone for scan functions.

**What's the monthly duty cycle?** Rated for up to 150,000 pages monthly, with a recommended 15,000-page workload. That's genuine office capacity, not marketing fluff.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need fast, sharp black-and-white printing and automatic duplex scanning, and you're willing to hardwire it to skip the Wi-Fi drama.

**Skip it if:** You want plug-and-play wireless, a slick interface, or scan-to-email without IT tinkering. Consider the Brother MFC-L5710DW or HP LaserJet Pro series for friendlier setups, even if you sacrifice some speed.

The B415DN is a professional print engine wrapped in frustrating connectivity. If you can get past the setup, it'll outwork most rivals in its class. If you can't, you'll join the ranks of 3-star reviewers who wanted to love it but couldn't get it online.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — Powerful and fast, but the wireless quirks and dated interface keep it from greatness.
