## What You're Actually Getting

The package contains a stack of standard catalog-size envelopes — roughly 2.5 x 4.25 inches, which is the industry-standard size for coin rolls and small cash bundles. The open-end design means the opening is on the short side, so you slide contents in lengthwise. This matters more than you'd think. The typical coin envelope has the opening on the long edge, which forces you to fold bills or angle coins awkwardly. This design eliminates that entirely.

The gummed seal is the star here. It's a traditional moisture-activated adhesive — not the peel-and-stick strips you see on cheaper envelopes. You wet it with a sponge or your tongue, press, and it bonds. When done right, it creates a stronger seal than any pressure-sensitive adhesive I've tested. The paper itself is a medium-weight kraft stock that holds its shape even when packed with a full roll of quarters.

## Where It Falls Short

The gummed seal has a learning curve. If you over-wet the adhesive, the flap curls and sticks unevenly, leaving you with a wrinkled envelope that looks sloppy. You need a light touch — barely damp, then press firmly. This isn't a dealbreaker, but if you're expecting peel-and-stick convenience, adjust your expectations.

There's also no pre-printed label area or window. If you're using these for systematic organization — say, a coin collection or a monthly budget system — you'll need a pen or a label maker. It's a minor friction point, but worth knowing before you buy.
