The ASUS TUF Gaming 27” sits in that sweet spot most shoppers are hunting for: 1440p resolution, a 165Hz refresh rate, and a sub-$350 price tag (when it's on sale). With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right monitor for *your* desk? Let's break down what you're actually getting.

## What's in the box

The monitor ships with the panel, a sturdy metal stand, DisplayPort cable, HDMI cable, power cord, and a quick-start guide. Nothing flashy — no calibration report, no RGB lighting gimmicks. The stand is a genuine highlight: it offers height adjustment, tilt, swivel, and VESA 100x100 mounting compatibility. You won't need to drop another $50 on a monitor arm unless you want the extra desk space.

## Performance and build quality

The 27-inch IPS panel delivers 2560x1440 resolution with a 165Hz refresh rate and 1ms response time (MPRT). In real-world use, that means sharp text for spreadsheets and documents, and fluid motion when you switch over to gaming or video. The matte anti-glare coating is well-executed — it cuts reflections without adding that grainy "sparkle" effect cheaper panels suffer from.

Build quality is typical TUF: chunky, durable plastic with a brushed-metal look on the back. It's not a design statement, but it feels like it'll survive a few moves. The on-screen display (OSD) is controlled by a joystick on the back — once you get used to it, it's actually more intuitive than fiddling with four separate buttons.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 1440p/165Hz combination is the headline feature across Amazon reviews. One verified buyer noted: *"Upgraded from a 1080p 60Hz panel and the difference is night and day. Text is so much crisper and games feel incredibly smooth."* Another reviewer called the stand *"surprisingly premium for this price point — solid, stable, and adjusts exactly how I need it."*

The connectivity suite also gets regular shoutouts: DisplayPort 1.4, two HDMI 2.0 ports, and a USB 3.0 hub with two downstream ports. That's enough to run a desktop and a laptop without constantly swapping cables.

## Where it falls short

The built-in speakers are the most common complaint — they're tinny and lack any bass. ASUS clearly expects you to use headphones or external speakers, and you should. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth planning around.

Color accuracy is another caveat. Out of the box, the monitor covers 100% of sRGB, but it's not factory-calibrated. If you're doing color-critical work like photo editing, you'll need to adjust settings or get a calibration tool. For everyday office use, web browsing, and gaming, it's perfectly fine.

A few buyers also noted the OSD menu can be clunky to navigate at first, though the joystick mitigates that after a couple of days.

## Who should buy this

- **Office workers who game after hours**: You get crisp text for work and smooth gameplay for fun, without needing two separate monitors.
- **Budget-conscious gamers**: If you want 1440p at high refresh rates without paying premium prices, this is a solid pick.
- **Anyone tired of flimsy stands**: The included stand is genuinely good — no need to budget for an aftermarket arm.

Skip it if you're a professional photo editor needing factory-calibrated accuracy, or if you absolutely need built-in speakers that don't sound like a phone speaker.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this monitor G-Sync or FreeSync compatible?**
A: It supports both Adaptive-Sync and G-Sync Compatible via DisplayPort. NVIDIA users can enable G-Sync in the control panel; AMD users get FreeSync automatically over both DisplayPort and HDMI.

**Q: Can it run 4K content?**
A: It's a 1440p panel, so it will downscale 4K content. It looks good, but you won't get the full 4K detail. If 4K is a must-have, look elsewhere.

**Q: Does it include a DisplayPort cable?**
A: Yes, both DisplayPort and HDMI cables are included in the box. That's a small but appreciated touch.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a versatile 1440p monitor that handles office work and gaming equally well, with a solid stand and full connectivity — all without breaking the bank.

**Skip it if:** You need factory-calibrated colors for professional creative work, or if you refuse to use external speakers.

This is a 4.2/5 monitor. It doesn't reinvent the category, but it nails the fundamentals that matter most: sharp image, smooth motion, solid build, and a stand that actually works. At its typical street price, it's one of the better value picks in the 27-inch 1440p space.
