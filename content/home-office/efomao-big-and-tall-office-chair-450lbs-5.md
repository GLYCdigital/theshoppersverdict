If you're over 250 pounds, you already know the struggle: most "big and tall" chairs are just standard chairs with longer gas lifts and a marketing badge. The Efomao Big and Tall Office Chair actually changes the geometry. The seat pan measures 22.5 inches across — that's 3–4 inches wider than a typical office chair — and the backrest rises to 30 inches. At $244, it's undercutting competitors like the Flash Furniture XT series by $50–70 while claiming a 500-pound capacity.

## What you actually get

The box contains the chair (backrest, seat base, armrests), a five-star aluminum base, five 3-inch dual-wheel casters, a Class-4 gas lift, and the hardware kit. There's no assembly manual in print — you'll scan a QR code for the PDF. The parts are individually wrapped and labeled, which helps, but set aside a full hour.

The upholstery is bonded leather over high-density foam. The foam density matters here: at 45 pounds per cubic foot, it's noticeably firmer than the cheap 30-pound foam in sub-$200 chairs. You won't bottom out the seat even at 300+ pounds.

## Real-world performance

I've been testing this chair for three weeks, including 10-hour workdays. The 3D armrests are the standout feature — they adjust in height, angle, and width independently. That last one is rare at this price. You can actually get the armrests wide enough that your shoulders don't hunch.

The lumbar support is a separate pad that adjusts vertically about 4 inches and has a tension dial. It's not as refined as the built-in systems on $500+ chairs, but it's genuinely adjustable — not just a fixed bump like most budget options.

The tilt mechanism has a multi-position lock and a tension knob. The tension range is wide enough that a 150-pound person and a 350-pound person can both find a comfortable recline resistance. That's not typical at this price.

One thing buyers consistently praise: the stability. As one verified purchaser put it, *"I'm 6'4", 340 lbs, and this is the first chair that doesn't wobble when I lean back. The base is solid and the casters roll smoothly on carpet."* Another noted, *"The seat depth is actually enough for my thighs — I don't feel like I'm sitting on a stool."*

## Where it falls short

The bonded leather is the biggest long-term concern. Multiple reviewers report cracking at the seat edge within a year. This isn't an Efomao-specific flaw — it's the nature of bonded leather at this price. If you want something that lasts 5+ years, budget another $100–150 for a chair with fabric or mesh upholstery.

The fixed headrest is a real issue for shorter users. It's positioned for someone around 6 feet tall. If you're under 5'10", it'll push into your upper back or shoulders, which is worse than no headrest at all. You can't remove it — you'd have to leave the backrest bolts loose, which compromises stability.

The assembly experience is mediocre. The included Allen wrench is soft metal, and several buyers report stripping bolts. Use your own tools. Also, the armrest width adjustment requires a screwdriver and can loosen over time — check them monthly.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're 250–400 pounds, need a wide seat and genuine 500-pound capacity, and you're okay with replacing the upholstery in 18 months. It's also a solid choice for tall users (6'2" and up) who need the extended backrest height.

**Skip it if:** You're under 5'10" (the headrest will annoy you), you want a chair that looks new after 3 years, or you need a fabric/mesh option for hot climates. Bonded leather doesn't breathe.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the 500-pound capacity feel legit?**
A: Yes. The steel frame is welded, not riveted, and the base is aluminum instead of plastic. At 340 pounds, I never felt flex or heard creaking. The Class-4 gas lift has held height without sinking over three weeks.

**Q: Is the seat comfortable for 8+ hour days?**
A: The foam is firm — that's actually good for long sits. You won't get the "sinking into a cloud" feel, but you also won't get tailbone pain. The seat depth is 20.5 inches, which works for most heights. The lack of breathability is the bigger issue in summer.

**Q: Will it fit under a standard desk?**
A: The armrests adjust down to 26 inches from the floor, which clears most desks. The seat height range is 19.5–23 inches. If your desk has a lower drawer or modesty panel, measure first — this is a bulky chair.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Efomao Big and Tall delivers where it matters: real capacity, real adjustability, and real stability. You're making a tradeoff between durability (the leather will age poorly) and price. If you treat this as a 2-year chair with heavy-duty bones, it's an excellent value. If you need a decade-long purchase, spend more on a fabric model.

**Buy it if** you're a larger or taller user who's tired of chairs that wobble and squeak. **Skip it if** you're average height, want breathable upholstery, or expect showroom condition after two years.
