**Bottom line up front:** The MIKIBY AJAZZ AK980 isn't just another wireless keyboard — it's a productivity tool that puts system feedback where you can actually see it. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, it's earning its keep. But "smart" doesn't mean "perfect," and there are a few gotchas worth knowing before you click buy.

## What's actually in the box

You get the keyboard itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a quick-start guide. That's it. No dongle, no extra keycaps, no wrist rest. The AK980 is a 98-key layout — full arrow keys and number pad but shaved down from the full 104-key size. It's a solid 1.3 pounds, so it's desk-bound rather than travel-friendly.

The headline feature is the small LCD screen tucked between the Escape key and the function row. It displays battery percentage, connection status (which of the three paired devices you're on), and a clock. Next to it sits a machined aluminum rotary knob that defaults to volume but can be reprogrammed for zoom, scrolling, or media skip.

## How it performs in real use

The gasket-mounted design with pre-lubed switches gives a soft, cushioned typing feel that's noticeably quieter than a standard mechanical board. The linear switches (you'll choose between red and brown at purchase) bottom out at around 3.5mm travel. For long writing sessions, the AK980 holds up — no fatigue, no harsh clack.

Battery life is the sleeper hit here. With the display backlight set to auto-off and RGB off, the 3000mAh cell pushes past two weeks of 8-hour days. With RGB blazing, you're looking at 3-4 days max. The USB-C charging hits full in about 3 hours, and you can type while it charges.

The rotary knob is the unsung hero. It's stepped and precise — you feel each detent when adjusting volume, and there's zero wobble. The display updates instantly when you switch between connected devices, showing which laptop, tablet, or phone you're now typing to.

The praise is consistent across the 229 ratings. ## Where it falls short

The ABS keycaps are the weakest link. At this price point, competitors like Keychron include double-shot PBT caps that resist oil and wear. You'll see shine develop on frequently used keys — WASD if you game, or the spacebar and Enter if you type all day.

Bluetooth-only connectivity is the second issue. The AK980 pairs via Bluetooth 5.1 only. There's no 2.4GHz dongle option. For office work, this is a non-issue. For competitive gaming, the ~2-5ms additional latency over a dongle connection could matter if you're twitch-sensitive.

The smart display, while handy, is limited. It's monochrome and only shows preset icons — you can't upload custom graphics or get per-app stats. It's informational, not customizable.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if** you're an office worker, writer, or hybrid worker juggling multiple devices (laptop, desktop, tablet) and want one board that switches between them without fuss. The display eliminates the "wait, is this connected?" guessing game.

**Skip it if** you're a competitive gamer needing the lowest possible latency, or if you're a keycap enthusiast who'll immediately want to swap caps — you'll spend extra to replace the ABS set.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use the knob for anything besides volume?**
A: Yes. The included software lets you remap it for zoom, scroll, media control, or custom macro functions. It works across Windows and macOS.

**Q: Does the display work without software installed?**
A: Yes, for battery, clock, and connection status. Advanced features like knob remapping require the AJAZZ software, which is Windows-focused. Mac users get basic functionality.

**Q: How many devices can it pair with?**
A: Three devices via Bluetooth, toggled with Fn + Q/W/E. The display shows which device you're on. Switching takes about one second.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The AK980 nails the productivity keyboard formula: great typing feel, useful smart display, and a knob that solves a real problem. The ABS keycaps and Bluetooth-only connection keep it from being perfect, but for desk-bound office work and multi-device setups, it's one of the smartest buys under the $100 mark. Buy it if you value workflow efficiency over gaming latency. Skip it if you're a keycap purist or need dongle connectivity.
