Let's cut the chase: you're looking at these because you need a marker that writes small, writes permanent, and doesn't bleed into illegibility. The Sharpie Ultra Fine Point in black does exactly that — and it's been doing it for decades for a reason.

**What you actually get**

Five identical black markers, each with a 0.2mm ultra fine tip. The barrel is the classic Sharpie shape — hexagonal, which keeps them from rolling as much as round markers (though not entirely, see cons). Each marker is roughly 5.5 inches long and lightweight. There's no clip, no retractable mechanism, just a standard pull-off cap with the classic Sharpie snap. The ink is the same alcohol-based permanent formula you know, just delivered through a much narrower tip.

**Real-world performance**

The defining feature here is line precision. The 0.2mm tip produces hairline strokes that stay crisp on virtually any surface. I've used these to label microscope slides, write on SD card labels, mark electrical wires, and address small shipping boxes. On glossy paper, plastic storage bins, and even glass jars, the ink bites in immediately and dries in under 10 seconds. No smudging, no dragging, no skipping.

One thing that surprised me: how well the ultra fine tip handles pressure variation. Light touch gives you a delicate hairline; moderate pressure gives a slightly bolder line without the tip splaying or bending. That control matters when you're doing detailed work.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The ultra fine tip is the draw, but it's also the weakness. Under heavy, constant use — think marking hundreds of items daily — the tip will mushroom and soften within a couple of weeks. The ink capacity is also smaller than a standard Sharpie, so if you're doing broad strokes or filling large areas, this is the wrong tool.

The missing cap clip is a genuine annoyance. These roll. They slide off slanted desks. If you carry markers in a pocket or bag, they'll migrate. Buy a small pencil case or use the box they come in.

And the black-only limitation is real if you're color-coding. For that, look at the multi-color ultra fine pack — but expect to pay more per marker.

**Who should buy this**

This 5-pack is a smart buy for:
- **Lab techs and researchers** who need permanent, precise labeling on vials and slides
- **Electricians and AV installers** marking wires and connectors

**FAQ**

*Do these bleed through standard printer paper?*  
At 0.2mm, bleed-through is minimal. On 20lb copy paper, you'll see faint ghosting at worst. On thicker cardstock or notebook paper, no issue.

*Are they truly permanent on plastic?*  
Yes, on most plastics — polypropylene, vinyl, and polyethylene included. The ink resists water and most solvents. It will fade over months of direct UV exposure, but that's true of all permanent markers.

*How long does one marker last?*  
With moderate daily use (labeling 20-30 items), expect 4-6 weeks per marker. Heavy industrial use will cut that in half.
