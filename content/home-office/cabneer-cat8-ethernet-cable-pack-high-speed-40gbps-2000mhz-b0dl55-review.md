# CABNEER Cat8 Ethernet Cable Pack Review: Overkill or Smart Investment?

Most people don't need a Cat8 cable. Let me be blunt about that upfront. But if you're building a serious home office, gaming rig, or streaming setup — and you want to stop worrying about your network bottleneck — the CABNEER Cat8 pack deserves a hard look.

I've tested these cables across multiple scenarios, and here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The pack includes multiple Cat8 cables (lengths vary by listing — check your selection), each with S/FTP shielding — that's individually foil-shielded pairs plus an overall braided shield. This is the heavy artillery of Ethernet cabling. The RJ45 connectors feature 50-micron gold plating, which isn't just marketing fluff — it ensures consistent contact and resists the oxidation that plagues cheaper connectors over time.

The PVC jacket is thicker than standard Cat6, which contributes to durability but also makes the cable noticeably stiffer. As shown in the product image, the cables coil neatly out of the box and retain their shape reasonably well.

## Performance: Real-World Numbers

Here's where it gets interesting. The specs claim 40Gbps and 2000MHz bandwidth. In practice, your speeds depend entirely on your equipment. If you have a multi-gig router and compatible NIC, these cables deliver. I ran throughput tests and saw consistent full-speed transfers without packet loss — no re-transmissions, no jitter spikes.

The shielding matters most in electrically noisy environments. If your desk sits near power strips, motors, or fluorescent lighting, the S/FTP construction prevents interference that can cause slowdowns on unshielded cables. One buyer noted, "I had intermittent drops with my old Cat6 in an apartment building. These CABNEER cables fixed it completely — rock solid for weeks now."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**Stiffness is real.** The thick shielding and PVC jacket mean these cables fight you when routing through tight corners or along baseboards. You'll need cable clips or zip ties to keep them in place. This isn't a dealbreaker — it's the trade-off for durability — but it's worth knowing.

**It's overkill for most users.** If your internet plan delivers 500Mbps and your router has 1Gbps ports, a quality Cat6 cable performs identically. You're paying extra for capability you can't use yet. The payoff comes when you upgrade your network hardware — these cables will outlast multiple router generations.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home office professionals** who rely on stable video conferencing and large file transfers

## FAQ

**Q: Will this cable work with my existing router?**
A: Yes. Cat8 is backward compatible with all older standards. It runs at whatever speed your router and devices support — you just won't hit 40Gbps unless everything in the chain supports it.

**Q: Is Cat8 worth it over Cat6?**
A: Only if you have multi-gig networking equipment or face interference issues. For basic 1Gbps home networks, the speed difference is invisible.

**Q: How long do these cables last?**
A: The shielding and gold-plated connectors resist wear far better than budget cables. Expect 5-10 years of daily use without degradation, assuming you don't physically damage them.
