Let's cut through the noise: the Dell P2722H is not the flashiest monitor you can buy. It won't wow you with 4K resolution or a 165Hz refresh rate. But if you spend eight hours a day staring at spreadsheets, email, and browser tabs, this is arguably the smartest office display Dell sells under $300. Here's why.

## What's Actually in the Box

Dell keeps it straightforward: the P2722H panel, a height-adjustable stand that attaches tool-free via a quick-release button, power cable, and a HDMI cable. You also get a DisplayPort cable and a USB-C upstream cable — the latter is critical because it's what activates the monitor's USB hub function. No DisplayPort-to-HDMI adapters needed, no surprises.

## Build Quality and Ergonomics: Where Dell Earns Its Reputation

The stand is the star here. It offers 150mm height adjustment, tilt, swivel, and a 90-degree pivot to portrait mode. As shown in the product image, the thin bezels on three sides make this a strong candidate for multi-monitor setups — you won't have thick plastic borders interrupting your workflow. The base is solid steel, not flimsy plastic, and the whole assembly feels like it could survive an office move without issue.

The panel itself uses IPS technology with a 178-degree viewing angle. Colors stay consistent even when you're not sitting dead-center, which matters more than most shoppers realize when you're sharing your screen with a colleague.

## Real-World Performance: The Honest Breakdown

Here's the tradeoff you need to understand before buying: this is a 1080p panel stretched across 27 inches. At typical viewing distance, individual pixels are visible if you look closely. Text isn't as razor-sharp as a 1440p or 4K display. If you're coming from a 24-inch 1080p monitor, you'll notice the difference immediately — everything looks slightly softer.

That said, for standard office work — documents, email, web browsing, video calls — it's perfectly usable. The 8ms response time is fine for productivity but you'll see ghosting in fast-moving content. This is not a gaming monitor, and Dell doesn't pretend otherwise.

What genuinely impressed me is the color accuracy out of the box. Dell rates this at 99% sRGB coverage, and in practice, colors look natural without the oversaturated punch that budget monitors often have. You won't want to do professional photo editing on it, but for presentations and basic design work, it's more than adequate.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars tell a consistent story. One verified buyer noted, "The USB hub is the reason I bought this — I plug in my mouse, keyboard, and webcam, and my laptop connects with a single USB-C cable." Another long-term owner mentioned, "Had mine for 18 months, used 10 hours a day, not a single dead pixel or flicker."

The ergonomic stand gets frequent shoutouts too. Multiple reviewers mention that the height adjustment range is generous enough to work with a standing desk setup, which is a legitimate differentiator at this price.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the pixel density issue, the lack of built-in speakers is annoying for a monitor positioned as an office workhorse. Most budget monitors include at least basic speakers — Dell's omission forces you to buy separate audio or use your laptop's speakers.

The USB hub is also only USB 3.2 Gen 1 (5Gbps), not the faster Gen 2 speeds. For peripherals like mice and keyboards, it's fine. If you wanted to use it as a docking station for external SSDs, you'll hit speed limits.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Office workers with a laptop**: The USB-C upstream plus USB hub means one cable handles video, power delivery (up to 65W), and peripherals
- **Multi-monitor users**: Slim bezels and consistent color reproduction make these pair well
- **Anyone with neck or eye strain**: The adjustable stand and ComfortView Plus low-blue-light mode are genuinely beneficial for all-day use

**Skip it if**: You're a gamer needing high refresh rates, a photo/video editor needing 1440p or 4K resolution, or someone who wants a monitor with speakers built in.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I charge my laptop through the USB-C connection?**
A: Yes, the USB-C upstream port delivers up to 65W of power delivery. Most ultrabooks and thin-and-light laptops will charge fine. Heavier workstations may need their own power adapter.

**Q: Does this work with MacBooks?**
A: Yes, but with a caveat. MacBooks support USB-C video output natively, so the single-cable setup works. However, the 1080p resolution on 27 inches is not ideal for macOS — Apple's scaling options make text look slightly fuzzy. It works, but a 1440p display is better for Mac users.

**Q: Is the stand really tool-free?**
A: Yes. The panel snaps onto the stand arm with a click, and the base attaches with a captive thumbscrew. You can assemble it in under two minutes without any tools.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if**: You need a reliable, ergonomically excellent office display with a functional USB hub and don't require razor-sharp pixel density.

**Skip it if**: You value text sharpness over everything else — in that case, spend a bit more for a 1440p panel.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A near-perfect office monitor that loses points only for its 1080p resolution on a 27-inch panel and missing speakers. For everything else, it's the gold standard for productivity displays.
