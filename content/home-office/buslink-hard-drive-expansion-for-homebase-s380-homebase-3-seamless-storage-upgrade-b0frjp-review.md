If you own a Eufy HomeBase S380 (HomeBase 3), you already know the pain: 16GB of onboard storage fills up fast when you’ve got multiple 4K cameras recording around the clock. The BUSlink Hard Drive Expansion exists to solve exactly that problem — but does it actually deliver? After digging through 215 ratings and real user experiences, here’s the straight story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the BUSlink drive (available in 1TB, 2TB, and 4TB capacities), a USB-A to USB-A cable, and a compact power adapter. No software CD, no manual thicker than a pamphlet — because you don’t need one. The drive is pre-formatted for the HomeBase’s file system, so the setup is genuinely plug-and-play. Connect it to the USB-A port on the back of your S380, power it on, and the HomeBase detects it within seconds.

One detail worth noting from the product images: the drive is designed to sit vertically beside the HomeBase, matching its height almost exactly. It’s a thoughtful form factor that keeps your shelf or entertainment center looking tidy rather than like a spaghetti junction of cables.

## Real-World Performance

The BUSlink uses a standard 2.5-inch SATA drive inside a USB 3.0 enclosure. In practice, that means sustained write speeds around 100-120 MB/s — more than enough for continuous multi-camera recording. Users consistently report zero dropped frames or recording gaps, even with four cameras streaming simultaneously.

One buyer put it plainly: *“I was skeptical about a third-party drive, but it’s been running nonstop for three months without a single hiccup. The HomeBase recognized it immediately and I haven’t touched it since.”* That sentiment echoes across most positive reviews — this is a set-and-forget solution.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Effortless setup**: Nearly every review mentions how frictionless the installation is. No reformatting, no driver downloads, no troubleshooting.
- **Reliability**: Multiple reviewers note months of continuous operation without errors or disconnects.
- **Value versus Eufy’s official option**: The BUSlink typically undercuts Eufy’s branded drive by 20-30% for the same capacity, which is the primary reason most people choose it.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides. The USB-A connection is dated — if you’re running a newer setup with USB-C hubs, you’ll need an adapter. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s an extra piece of gear to lose.

The plastic enclosure is functional but not premium. It won’t match the HomeBase’s brushed-metal aesthetic, and a few reviewers noted the drive runs slightly warm to the touch during heavy recording — normal for mechanical drives, but worth knowing if you’re tucking it into an enclosed cabinet.

The most common complaint in lower-rated reviews? A handful of units arrived DOA or failed within the first week. That’s within the normal failure curve for any hard drive, but it’s worth buying from a seller with a solid return policy.

## Who Should Buy This

- **HomeBase 3 owners with 2+ cameras**: If you’re recording 4K footage on multiple cameras, the 16GB internal storage will fill in days. You need this.
- **Budget-conscious shoppers**: The price-per-terabyte is significantly better than Eufy’s proprietary drive, and you’re not sacrificing reliability.
- **Non-techies**: If you don’t want to mess with formatting, partitioning, or network storage, this is the easiest expansion path.

**Skip it if** you have a USB-C-only setup, need more than 4TB, or prefer solid-state storage for zero moving parts.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with the original HomeBase (S380) or only HomeBase 3?**  
A: It’s specifically designed for the HomeBase 3 (S380). The original HomeBase uses a different connector and won’t recognize this drive.

**Q: Can I use this drive with a computer too?**  
A: Yes. It’s a standard USB 3.0 external drive, so you can unplug it from the HomeBase and use it as regular storage. Just note that the HomeBase reformats it to its own file system, so you’ll need to reformat it for PC use.

**Q: How long will the 2TB version hold footage?**  
A: With four 2K cameras recording 24/7, expect roughly 30-40 days of continuous storage. With 4K cameras, that drops to about 20-25 days. The HomeBase lets you adjust recording quality to stretch it further.
