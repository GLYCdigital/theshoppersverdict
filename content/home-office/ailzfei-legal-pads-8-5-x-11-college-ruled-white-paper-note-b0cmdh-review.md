## What you actually get

The package includes multiple 8.5 x 11 inch legal pads — check the listing for the exact count, but most buyers report receiving 4 pads. Each pad has 50 sheets of 20-pound bright white paper with college rule (9/32 inch spacing). The pages are three-hole punched on the left margin, which is unusual for legal pads and appreciated if you archive notes in binders. The cardboard backing is genuinely stiff — it doesn’t curl when you write on the last sheet.

One detail that stands out from the product images: the perforation runs the full length of each sheet, not just a few inches at the top. That means clean tearing from any point, not just at the start of a page.

## How it performs in real use

The paper is the headline feature. At 20-pound weight, it’s noticeably thicker than the 16-pound stock most budget pads use. Gel pens write smoothly without catching, and fountain pen users report minimal ghosting and zero bleed-through — even with wetter inks. One reviewer noted, “I use a Lamy Safari with Noodler’s ink and the paper takes it like a champ. No feathering, no show-through on the back.”

The ruling is crisp and uniform. Horizontal lines are light enough to recede when you write but visible enough to keep rows straight. The margin line is standard — not overbearing.

The perforation deserves its own mention. It tears cleanly every time, leaving a straight edge without fringe. This is a minor feature that cheap pads consistently botch, so it’s worth calling out that AILZFEI got it right.

## Where it falls short

Honest tradeoffs exist. At 50 sheets per pad, heavy note-takers (students, journalists, therapists) will finish a pad in 5–7 days. That’s a deliberate design choice for portability, but you’ll repurchase faster than with a 100-sheet pad.

There’s also no wide-rule option. If you write large or need more space per line, college rule at 9/32 inch might feel cramped. And the white paper is the only color offered — no canary yellow or pastel options if you prefer color-coding by subject.

Finally, the pads don’t come with a cover. The cardboard backing is sturdy, but the front sheet is exposed, so if you toss these loose in a bag, the top page will get scuffed. This is standard for the category, but worth knowing.
