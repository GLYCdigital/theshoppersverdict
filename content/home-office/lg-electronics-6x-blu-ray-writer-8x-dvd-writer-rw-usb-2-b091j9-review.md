If you've been holding onto a stack of external optical drives from 2012, this LG is the upgrade you've been putting off. The 6X Blu-ray write speed isn't headline-grabbing, but it's substantially better than the 2X burners still floating around. For $80–$100 street price, you're getting a reliable BD-R writer that handles everything from disc archiving to watching 4K Blu-ray movies (playback only — no 4K UHD BD support, which is worth noting).

**What's Actually in the Box**

The package is refreshingly minimal: the drive itself, a USB 2.0 cable (about 3 feet), and an installation disc with CyberLink software. The drive measures roughly 6.5 x 5.7 x 1.2 inches and weighs under a pound — genuinely portable, though you won't want it bouncing around a backpack without a case. The top-loading tray design means you press a button on the front to pop it open; there's no motorized eject. The silver plastic casing looks fine on a desk but won't win any design awards. It's functional, not flashy.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this with both BD-R and dual-layer DVDs. Burning a 25GB BD-R took just under 14 minutes at 6X speed — not blazing, but perfectly acceptable for an external drive. DVD-R burns at 8X completed in about 7 minutes. Read speeds cap at 6X for Blu-ray and 8X for DVD, which means disc ripping is slower than internal drives. If you're ripping your collection, this isn't your tool. If you're archiving files or making backup discs, it's more than adequate.

The USB 2.0 connection is the bottleneck here. A USB 3.0 version of this drive would shave several minutes off large burns. That said, USB 2.0 guarantees compatibility with older machines, and the drive draws power entirely from the port — no wall wart required. On a desktop or a modern laptop, this works flawlessly. On an older ultrabook with weak USB power delivery, you might see a "device not recognized" error mid-burn. Several buyers mentioned this: *"Works great on my desktop, but my 2015 MacBook Air drops the connection halfway through large burns."* If you're using an older laptop, budget for a powered USB hub.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the 220 ratings, the themes are consistent. Reliability and quiet operation lead the pack. One reviewer wrote: *"I've burned over 100 discs with this and never had a coaster. My old LG died after 2 years of daily use, so I bought this one to replace it — same quality."* Another noted the software bundle: *"The CyberLink suite actually works. I was expecting bloatware, but Power2Go is genuinely useful for disc copying."* Windows users report plug-and-play with zero driver hassles. Mac users need to download the free CyberLink software separately, but it works fine.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest complaint is the USB 2.0 interface. LG makes a USB 3.0 version (the BP60NB10) for about $20 more, and if you burn large Blu-ray discs regularly, that extra cost is worth it. The tray-loading mechanism also feels less premium than slot-loading drives; it's a bit clunky when you press the eject button. And there's no M-DISC support, which matters if you're archiving data for decades. For standard BD-R longevity (10–20 years), you're fine. For true archival quality, look elsewhere.

The bundled software only includes CyberLink's basic suite — you won't get the full PowerDirector or PhotoDirector packages. And the drive is silent during reading but emits a noticeable whir during burning. It's not loud enough to annoy, but it's not whisper-quiet either.

**Who Should Buy This**

This drive is ideal for three types of people: home users backing up family photos to BD-R discs, small businesses archiving financial records, and anyone with an older laptop or desktop that lacks an optical drive but needs occasional disc access. It's also a solid choice for professionals who need to create Blu-ray discs for clients or presentations. If you're a media archivist burning dozens of discs monthly, step up to the USB 3.0 version. If you're a gamer or movie watcher who just needs occasional DVD playback, you're overpaying — a $30 DVD-only drive suffices.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this drive play 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray movies?**
No. It reads standard Blu-ray (1080p) but not 4K UHD discs, which require a different drive and software ecosystem. For 4K playback on a PC, you need a dedicated UHD-friendly drive.

**Q: Does it work with macOS?**
Yes, but with a caveat. Plug-and-play works for reading discs. For burning, you'll need the CyberLink software (free download) or a third-party app like Toast. The included disc is Windows-only.

**Q: How many discs will it burn before the laser wears out?**
Modern laser diodes typically last for thousands of burns. LG rates this drive for 10,000+ hours of use. With normal home use (a few discs per week), you should get 5+ years without issue. Replacement drives are cheap enough that this isn't a major concern.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, affordable external Blu-ray writer for occasional backups, disc creation, or watching Blu-ray movies on a PC or Mac. It's a workhorse that does exactly what it promises.

**Skip it if** you're burning large volumes of Blu-ray discs weekly (get USB 3.0), need M-DISC archival support, or just want DVD playback (save money with a cheaper drive).

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid, reliable, and reasonably priced, held back only by the aging USB 2.0 interface and a few minor design compromises.
