**Does 25% cotton actually matter for your business documents? I tested this Southworth paper against standard office stock to find out.**

Here's the honest truth: most paper is paper. But when you're sending a proposal to a potential client or handing over a final contract, the feel of that sheet says something about you before a single word is read. Southworth has built a reputation on this exact premise — that premium paper creates a premium impression. The question is whether that impression justifies the price.

---

**What you actually get**

The box contains 100 sheets of 20-pound, 25% cotton paper in a bright white shade. The finish is "linen" — a subtle, textured weave pattern that's visible under light and adds grip to the sheet. Standard US Letter size (8.5" x 11"), compatible with any printer that handles 20lb bond.

It's worth noting the difference between this SKU and Southworth's other offerings. The P403C you're looking at has a linen finish without a watermark. If you want the classic "registered watermark" (the little Southworth emblem visible when you hold the sheet to light), you need the P403R variant. That distinction matters to some buyers and is completely irrelevant to others.

---

**Performance: where this paper earns its keep**

I ran this through both a laser printer and an inkjet with full-bleed color graphics. The cotton content makes a real difference in how ink sits on the surface. Colors appeared slightly more saturated than on standard 24lb multipurpose paper, and there was zero bleed-through — even with heavy black text on both sides. Dry time on inkjet was noticeably faster than standard paper, which is a practical win if you're running a batch of resumes.

The texture is the headline feature, though. It's not smooth like standard bond. It has a toothy, substantial feel that signals "this document was printed on purpose." For legal documents, certificates, or anything that needs to feel official, this is exactly the right tool.

**What 235 buyers consistently say**

The 4.7-star average holds up in the reviews. **Where it falls short — be honest with yourself**

First, the 20lb weight. If you're coming from 24lb cotton resume paper, this will feel thinner than you expect. It's still premium, but it's not the heavyweight cardstock-adjacent feel some buyers assume from the price.

Second, the cost. You're paying a significant premium per sheet. If your documents are destined for a recycling bin within 48 hours, this is wasted money. Standard 24lb bond will do the job for internal memos.

Third — and this is the one that trips people up — the lack of watermark on this specific SKU. If you specifically want that registered watermark for authenticity, double-check you're ordering the P403R, not this P403C.

**FAQ**

**Is 100 sheets enough, or should I order multiple boxes?**
For resumes and client letters, 100 sheets goes further than you think — you're not printing 50-page proposals on this. One box typically lasts 3-6 months for most users. If you're running a high-volume practice, order 2-3 boxes upfront since pricing per box doesn't drop significantly with quantity.

**Will it work in my printer?**
Yes — if your printer handles standard 20lb paper, it will handle this. It's slightly stiffer than standard bond, and some reviewers report it feeds better than standard paper because of the added texture. No special handling required.

**Can I use it for both laser and inkjet?**
Yes, it's rated for both. It actually performs better on inkjet than standard paper due to faster drying. Just be aware that heavy watercolor washes or large ink coverage will still cause some waviness — that's true of any paper.
