**How they perform**

The Gelly Roll's reputation comes down to one thing: the ink. It's heavily pigmented, almost like paint flowing through a pen. The white Gelly Roll, in particular, is the gold standard for drawing on dark or kraft paper — it actually shows up, opaque and bright. The colors are rich and saturated, not washed out like some budget gel pens.

The flow is consistent from the first stroke. No skipping, no hard starts, no shaking the pen like a thermometer. I wrote several pages of notes and didn't hit a single dry patch. That reliability is what keeps people coming back.

But here's the tradeoff: gel ink dries slowly. On coated or glossy paper, you're waiting several seconds before you can touch the page without smearing. If you're left-handed, this will test your patience. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "Love the colors, but I have to be careful not to drag my hand across the page." That's not a flaw in the pen — it's physics. But it's a real consideration.

**Where it falls short**

The 0.5mm tip is the biggest point of contention. If you prefer bold, thick lines, these will feel delicate. On textured or rough paper, the fine tip can catch and feel scratchy. This isn't a pen for signing documents or writing on cheap printer paper — it's a pen for quality notebooks.

And while the case is nice, it's not a hard-shell protector. Toss it in a backpack loose and you risk cracked barrels. Use the included case — it's there for a reason.

**FAQ**

**Do these pens bleed through notebook paper?**
On standard 80gsm journal paper, you'll see ghosting — faint marks on the back of the page. On thicker 120gsm+ paper, no bleed-through. Use a quality notebook with these.

**How long do they last?**
Each pen has a standard gel ink reservoir. For daily journaling, expect a few weeks per pen. The set's value is in the color range, not the longevity of any single pen.

**Are these refillable?**
No. When a pen runs dry, it's done. That's the tradeoff for the price point.
