Let’s be blunt: most budget iPad keyboard cases are flimsy plastic trays with mushy keys that make you want to throw the whole thing across the room. The APHBZGE iPad 9.7'' Keyboard Case isn't that. It's not perfect, but it punches well above its price class in ways that matter for daily typing.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the folio case with a detachable Bluetooth keyboard, a micro-USB charging cable (yes, micro-USB — not USB-C, so don't lose it), and a basic instruction sheet. The keyboard attaches via strong magnetic pogo pins, so it snaps in place with a satisfying click. As shown in the product image, the keyboard sits flush against the case when closed, protecting both the screen and keys during transport.

The case itself is a two-piece design: a silicone-gripped back shell that snaps onto the iPad and a front cover that folds into a typing stand. The hinge mechanism is stiffer than average, which is actually good — it holds the tablet at your preferred angle without flopping backward mid-sentence.

## Performance and Daily Use

Typing is where this case surprises. The keys have genuine scissor-switch mechanics with decent travel — roughly 1.2mm of depth, which is closer to a laptop feel than the shallow membrane keys found on many competitors. There's a noticeable tactile bump on each press, and the layout includes full-size letter keys plus a top row of iOS shortcuts (home, brightness, volume, media controls).

Connectivity is straightforward: pair once via Bluetooth 3.0, then it auto-connects when you slide the iPad in. No app required — it's plug-and-play with iPadOS. Battery life is genuinely impressive; the 500mAh cell lasts about 60 hours of continuous typing on a single charge, which for most users translates to 3-4 weeks between charges.

The auto wake/sleep function works via magnetic sensors built into the case. Close the lid, the iPad sleeps; open it, it wakes. I tested this over several days and it never missed once — which is more than I can say for cases costing twice as much.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the weight is the biggest tradeoff. At 1.4 pounds, this case nearly doubles the heft of your iPad. If you primarily want a screen protector with occasional typing, this is overkill. The keyboard also goes into a deep sleep after about 15 minutes of idle time; you'll need to press any key to wake it, which takes 2-3 seconds. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

The micro-USB charging port is dated, and the case won't fit the newer iPad 10.2" or iPad Pro models — it's specifically for the 9.7" generation (iPad 5th/6th gen, iPad Air 2). If you're on a newer device, skip this.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a student or writer using a 9.7-inch iPad for notes, emails, or drafting documents. You want a real keyboard experience without spending $100+. The backlighting makes it viable for late-night work.

**Skip it if:** Your iPad is primarily for media consumption and you rarely type more than a quick search. The bulk and weight aren't worth it. Also skip if you're on anything other than the 9.7" form factor.
