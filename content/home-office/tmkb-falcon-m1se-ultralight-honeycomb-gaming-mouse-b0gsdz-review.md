The TMKB Falcon M1SE is the kind of mouse that makes you question why you've been lugging around a 100-gram brick for years. At 58 grams, it's lighter than most wireless earbuds cases, and that weight difference becomes immediately obvious the moment you pick it up. But is ultralight worth the tradeoffs? After spending time with it, here's the honest picture.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the mouse itself, a braided USB-C cable, a 2.4GHz USB receiver, and a quick-start guide. There's no carrying case, no extra skates, no grip tape — just the essentials. The honeycomb shell is visible immediately, with hexagonal cutouts across the main buttons and palm area. The RGB lighting around the base and scroll wheel adds a gaming aesthetic, though it's tasteful enough to pass in an office if you turn it off.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The PAW3395 sensor is the headline feature here. This is the same sensor found in flagships costing twice as much, and it shows. Tracking is flawless at any DPI setting, with zero acceleration or smoothing artifacts. I ran it through spreadsheets, design software, and a few rounds of competitive shooters — the cursor never skipped, never drifted, never felt laggy.

The tri-mode connectivity is genuinely useful. Bluetooth for the laptop, 2.4GHz for the desktop, and wired for charging or low-latency gaming. Switching between devices takes about two seconds via the button on the bottom. Battery life sits around 70 hours with RGB off, which got me through roughly two work weeks on a single charge.

The clicks use optical switches rated for 80 million actuations. They're crisp and tactile, with a satisfying snap that doesn't feel mushy. The scroll wheel has defined detents — good for scrolling through long documents without overshooting.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The 225 Amazon ratings average 4.5 stars, and the recurring theme in positive reviews is the weight-to-performance ratio. One buyer put it simply: *"I forgot I was holding a mouse. That's how light this thing is."* Another noted, *"The sensor is absurdly accurate for the price. My aim improved almost immediately in FPS games."* Multiple reviewers also mention the build quality — despite the honeycomb cutouts, there's no creaking or flex in the shell.

## Where It Falls Short

The honeycomb design has a real downside: it collects debris. Dust, skin flakes, crumbs — they all find their way into those hexagonal holes. Plan on blowing it out with compressed air weekly if you eat at your desk. One reviewer warned, *"This thing is a crumb trap. I learned that the hard way with a bagel."*

The software situation is also limited. The companion app works only on Windows, so Mac users are stuck with default DPI presets and can't remap buttons or customize RGB. That's a genuine limitation for a mixed-platform workspace.

The shape is medium-sized and ambidextrous-leaning, which means it's comfortable for most hand sizes but doesn't fill a large palm the way ergonomic mice do. If you have bigger hands, this will feel small.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Office workers with repetitive strain concerns**: The 58g weight genuinely reduces wrist and forearm fatigue over a full day of clicking and scrolling.
- **Gamers on a budget**: You're getting flagship sensor performance for a fraction of the price of premium gaming mice.
- **Travelers and hybrid workers**: The tri-mode connectivity and lightweight build make it easy to carry between home and office.

## Skip It If

- You eat at your desk and don't want to maintain a honeycomb shell.
- You're on macOS and need full customization.
- You prefer a heavier, more substantial mouse feel.

## FAQ

**Is the honeycomb design uncomfortable over long sessions?** No — the cutouts don't create pressure points. The shell is smooth and the weight reduction actually helps with comfort. Just keep it clean.

**Does it work with macOS?** Yes for basic tracking and clicking, but you can't customize DPI presets, remap buttons, or control RGB lighting without the Windows software.

**What's the battery life on Bluetooth?** Around 70 hours with RGB off, roughly 40 hours with RGB on. It charges via USB-C in about two hours.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want flagship-level sensor performance without the flagship price, and you're willing to spend two minutes a week cleaning the shell. It's an exceptional value for both office productivity and competitive gaming.

**Skip it if** you're a Mac user who needs full customization, or you have a strong preference for heavier ergonomic mice.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The weight, sensor, and tri-mode connectivity make this a standout. The dust issue and software limitations keep it from perfection, but at this price point, it's hard to argue with the value.
