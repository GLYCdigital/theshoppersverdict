## What You Actually Get

The box contains 100 white boards, each measuring a true 22x28 inches. That matters more than you'd think. Several budget brands advertise 22x28 but ship boards that run a quarter-inch short. These come in at full size, which means they'll fit standard 22x28 display frames and tri-fold presentation boards without awkward overhang.

The surface is a smooth, bright white with a slight sheen. It's not the matte, toothy surface you get on art-grade illustration board, but it's a step above basic construction paper. Markers glide without feathering, and acrylic paint sits on top without soaking through. One reviewer noted, "Used these for a science fair — the kids painted planets and glued on foam shapes. ## Real-World Performance

Let's talk about the elephant in the room: thickness. These boards measure about 1/16 inch — thinner than the heavyweight boards you'll find at office supply stores. For standard use — school projects, signage, event displays — that's totally fine. They stand upright in a presentation easel without drooping. But if you're planning to mount heavy materials or build a 3D structure, you'll want something sturdier.

Cutting is where these boards shine. The paper core is dense enough that you can score it with a craft knife and get clean, straight breaks. A rotary cutter works even better. Just don't expect to fold them — there are no pre-scored lines, and creasing them creates a permanent white crack.

## Where It Falls Short

The thinness is the real tradeoff. If you're a teacher who needs boards that survive being handled by dozens of students, or if you're creating displays that need to stand for weeks, these will show wear faster than heavyweight options. The white surface is also prone to scuffing — a black mark from a binder clip or fingernail will leave a visible gray smudge.

Also worth noting: these are uncoated. If you're using wet media like watercolor or wet glue, the boards will buckle slightly. For dry applications — markers, colored pencils, printed graphics — they're flawless.
