Let's be blunt: if you're still shooting on V90 SD cards in a camera that supports CFexpress Type A, you're leaving performance on the table. This card class is the bridge between pro workflows and compact bodies, and the CFexpress Type A 4.0 slot sits right in that sweet spot. With a 4.6 average across 229 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep. But the real question isn't whether it's fast — it's whether *your* workflow needs that speed.

## What You Actually Get

The card itself is deceptively small. As shown in the product image, it's the same physical dimensions as a standard SD card — which is the entire point of the Type A format. No adapter nonsense, no fiddly CFexpress Type B bulk. It drops straight into Sony's A7 IV, A7S III, A1, and FX3/FX6 bodies, plus select other mirrorless cameras that adopted the standard.

You're getting a single card, not a multi-pack. No reader included — that's expected at this tier, but worth noting if you're coming from SD cards that often ship with cheap USB adapters.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the CFexpress Type A 4.0 separates itself from the SD crowd. In sustained write tests, this card holds its rated speeds without the dramatic throttling you see on high-end SD cards after 30–60 seconds of continuous 4K 120p or 6K recording. One buyer noted, *"I shoot wedding ceremonies straight through, 40+ minutes at a time, and this card never dropped a single frame. My V90s would start coughing around minute 12."* That's the real-world difference.

Read speeds are equally solid — pulling 100GB of footage off the card into Lightroom or Premiere happens noticeably faster than SD. If you're tethered to a card reader on location, that time savings adds up quickly.

The catch: this card runs warm. Under heavy sustained writes, it gets genuinely hot to the touch. It's within spec, but if you're swapping cards rapidly in the field, give it a few seconds before stuffing it in a pocket or card case. Several reviewers flagged this exact issue.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 229 ratings cluster around two themes: reliability and sustained speed. Beyond the wedding videographer above, another reviewer summed it up: *"Bought two of these for my FX3. Zero corrupted files in six months of daily use. Worth every penny."* Durability seems legit — multiple reports of cards surviving drops, airport scanners, and general abuse without failure.

## Where It Falls Short

The price is the elephant in the room. CFexpress Type A 4.0 cards cost roughly double what a comparable V90 SD card runs. For photographers who primarily shoot stills — even fast burst work — the buffer on modern Sony bodies often makes the speed advantage marginal. You're paying for video headroom you might never use.

Also, that heat. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real physical characteristic you'll notice. And if you're using third-party card readers, check compatibility before buying — some older readers don't handle the Type A form factor correctly.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You shoot video on a Sony mirrorless — especially 4K 60p or higher, long interviews, events, or documentaries where dropping a single frame is unacceptable. Also buy if you're a hybrid shooter who hates waiting on card transfers.

**Skip it if:** You're a pure stills photographer shooting single frames or short bursts. V90 SD cards will serve you fine, and you'll save serious money. Also skip if your camera only has SD slots — this won't fit.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work in my Sony A7 III?**  
No. The A7 III only has SD slots. This card requires a camera with a CFexpress Type A slot — think A7 IV, A7S III, A1, FX3, or FX6.

**Q: Does it come with a card reader?**  
No, single card only. You'll need a CFexpress Type A reader — the Sony MRW-G2 or third-party options like ProGrade or Angelbird work well.

**Q: Is it backward compatible with SD slots?**  
No. The physical shape is the same, but the pin layout and interface are completely different. It will not fit in a standard SD slot.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You demand sustained write speeds for video or buffer-free burst shooting, and you're already invested in a CFexpress Type A camera body.

**Skip it if:** Your needs max out at V90 SD performance — you won't see enough real-world benefit to justify the cost.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Fast, reliable, and worth the premium for video shooters. The heat and price keep it from a perfect score, but for the right user, this is the card to beat.
