**Review Body:**

Nobody *wants* to think about toner. You just want the print to come out right the first time, without a magenta streak running through your client proposal. That's exactly the promise of Brother's genuine TN221 set — but at roughly $0.06–$0.09 per page, it's not the cheapest way to feed your HL-3140CW or MFC-9330CDW.

**What's Actually in the Box**

This is a full CMYK set: one TN221BK black cartridge and three color cartridges (TN221C cyan, TN221M magenta, TN221Y yellow). Brother rates the black for approximately 1,400 pages and each color for 1,000 pages, based on 5% coverage. All four are factory-sealed with the orange protective tab intact — I've seen knockoff sets arrive with broken seals or loose toner inside the packaging, which is never an issue here.

**Real-World Performance**

The standout trait is consistency. Because these are genuine Brother cartridges, the printer's color calibration algorithms work exactly as designed. Print a full-page color brochure and you'll get smooth gradients with no visible banding. Text stays razor-sharp, even at 8-point font sizes. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"I've tried the cheap compatibles and always ended up with streaky color prints. These just work — every single time."* Another noted the chip integration: *"The printer actually shows accurate toner levels now, which is something I never got with third-party cartridges."*

That chip communication matters more than people realize. Compatible cartridges often show "toner empty" prematurely or fail to report levels at all, leading to wasted paper and mid-print failures. Brother's genuine chips are read correctly by the printer firmware — no guesswork, no resets, no shaking cartridges to squeeze out another 20 pages.

**The Honest Tradeoffs**

Here's where I'll push back. If you print mostly black-and-white documents, this set is wasteful — you're paying for three color cartridges you're barely using. You'd be better served buying the TN221BK individually. And the cost per page is genuinely steep compared to compatibles. A set of remanufactured TN221 cartridges can cost 40% less. The tradeoff is quality and reliability — you're paying a premium for zero surprises.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Small business owners** who print client-facing materials and can't afford a streaky batch of 200 flyers
- **Home office users** with a Brother color laser who print photos or marketing collateral monthly
- **Anyone who's been burned by compatibles** — if you've dealt with leaking toner or persistent drum errors, the extra cost is insurance

**Skip it if** you print mostly text documents, or if your volume is high enough (500+ pages/month) that the per-page cost actually hurts. That's when a third-party high-yield option starts making sense.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these work in my Brother HL-3150CDW?**  
Yes. The TN221 series is compatible with the HL-3140CW, HL-3150CDW, HL-3170CDW, MFC-9130CW, MFC-9330CDW, and MFC-9340CDW, among others. Double-check your model number against Brother's compatibility list if you're unsure.

**Q: How many pages will I really get?**  
The 1,400-page black rating assumes 5% page coverage — roughly 15-20 lines of text per page. If you print dense graphics or photos, expect closer to 800-900 pages. Colors at 1,000 pages will drop to 600-700 with heavy image coverage.

**Q: Is it worth paying extra for genuine over compatible?**  
If you print anything where color accuracy matters — client materials, product photos, presentations — yes. The consistent color output and accurate chip reporting are worth the premium. If you're just printing internal drafts and memos, a reputable compatible brand will save you money without much visible difference.

**The Verdict**

**⭐ 4.6/5 — Buy it if** you need reliable, consistent color output and value your time over saving a few dollars per cartridge. It's the safe, smart choice for professionals who can't afford a botched print run. **Skip it if** you're a high-volume text printer or on a tight budget — the per-page cost will sting over time.

One final note: buy the full set only if you actually use color regularly. If you're replacing a single empty cartridge, buy that one individually. The set makes financial sense when you need at least two colors, not when you just need black.
