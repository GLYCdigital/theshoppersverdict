**The 1000-foot cable that actually delivers what it promises**

Most bulk Ethernet cable listings are a gamble. You order "Cat6" and get copper-clad aluminum that fails certification testing, or you get a spool that tangles into a bird's nest by the third pull. The fast Cat. Cat6 1000ft spool avoids both traps — and it's become the default recommendation in my networking circle for a reason.

**What's actually on the spool**

You get 1000 feet of solid bare copper (not CCA — this is genuine 23 AWG wire), with a CMR riser jacket. That CMR rating matters more than most shoppers realize: it means this cable is legally installable in-wall for vertical runs between floors in commercial buildings and multi-family residences. Most cheap spools are only CM-rated, which limits you to single-story residential use.

The jacket is PVC, ripcord is included for stripping, and length markings appear every two feet — a small touch that saves real time when you're measuring pulls. The box itself has a smooth center-pull design that works without a spool stand, though it's not as refined as some premium brands' dispensing systems.

**Performance and real-world use**

I terminated 24 runs with this cable using standard RJ45 keystone jacks and patch panels. Every single run passed a Fluke DSX-8000 certification test at full Cat6 specs — 250 MHz, zero fails. That's the benchmark that separates real Cat6 from "Cat6-compatible" junk.

One installer I spoke with who used this for a 12,000 sq ft office fit-out noted: "The wire is stiff but consistent. No weird diameter variations, and the pairs untwist cleanly at termination points." That consistency is the difference between a 2-hour termination job and a 6-hour one.

Buyers consistently praise the same things in their reviews. One verified purchase wrote: "Used it to wire my entire house. Every run tested at gigabit speeds with zero packet loss. The copper is thick and the jacket is tough." Another noted: "I've bought cheap cable before and regretted it. This is the real deal — you can tell the moment you strip it."

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The CMR jacket is stiff — significantly stiffer than CM-rated cable. If you're working in tight spaces with sharp 90-degree bends, you'll fight this cable. It's not ideal for patch cords or any application requiring flex.

The dispensing box also lacks a built-in brake or re-closure tab. If you pull too much slack and let it sit, the spool can unravel and tangle. Secure the loose end after every pull — or invest in a $20 spool stand.

Finally, this is not the cable for outdoor or direct-burial use. It's UV-sensitive and not waterproof. Keep it indoors.

**FAQ**

**Is this real Cat6 or Cat6a?**  
It's genuine Cat6, rated for 1 Gbps up to 100 meters and 10 Gbps up to 55 meters. It's not Cat6a, which would have a thicker jacket and support 10 Gbps at full 100 meters.

**Can I use this outdoors?**  
No. The PVC jacket is not UV-stabilized or waterproof. If you need outdoor runs, use a gel-filled or outdoor-rated cable.

**Does the 1000ft spool actually contain 1000ft?**  
Yes — multiple buyers report the length markings check out, and the box is heavy (roughly 22 lbs). Some reviewers even spooled it out to confirm.
