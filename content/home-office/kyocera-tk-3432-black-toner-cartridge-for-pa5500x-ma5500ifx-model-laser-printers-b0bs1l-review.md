If your office runs a KYOCERA PA5500x or MA5500if, you already know these workhorses are built for volume. The TK-3432 is the OEM black toner that keeps them humming — and at 5.0 stars across 250 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But at roughly $90–$110 per cartridge, is it worth the premium over third-party options? Here's what I found after digging into the specs, the feedback, and the real-world tradeoffs.

## What You're Actually Getting

The TK-3432 is a single black toner cartridge rated for **12,000 pages** at 5% coverage — that's the ISO standard, so expect closer to 8,000–9,000 if you print dense documents or graphics-heavy reports. It's a genuine OEM part, meaning the toner formulation is matched specifically to the PA5500x's drum unit and fuser assembly. The cartridge itself is compact and lightweight, roughly the size of a large paperback, and installs in under 30 seconds. There's no chip reset dance or alignment procedure — it clicks in, the printer recognizes it, and you're printing.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The first thing you'll notice is the consistency. Genuine KYOCERA toner is engineered for their proprietary drum coating, and it shows. Text comes out crisp at 8-point fonts, with no feathering or grey cast on standard 20-lb copy paper. Users consistently report clean output from the first page to the last. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"Prints are sharp and black, no smudging even on cheap paper."* Another noted, *"I've run three of these through my MA5500if — zero jams, zero leaks, just solid output."*

That reliability matters. Compatible cartridges can save you 30–40% upfront, but they also bring a higher risk of toner dust inside the printer, uneven fusing, or premature drum wear. With the TK-3432, the cartridge itself is designed to be the only consumable you touch — the drum unit is separate and rated for 100,000+ pages. In my view, the OEM cartridge is cheap insurance for a machine that costs $700+.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: **the price stings.** At roughly $90–$110, this cartridge costs about 0.8–1.2 cents per page. Compatible options from brands like LD Products or Armor run closer to 0.5–0.7 cents per page. If you're printing 2,000 pages a month, that's a $6–10 monthly difference — not huge, but it adds up over a year.

Second, there's no visible toner-level gauge on the cartridge itself. You'll need to check via the printer's driver or the front panel display. Most users won't mind, but it's a minor annoyance if you're used to third-party cartridges with a clear window.

Finally, **yield can vary more than the rating suggests** if you print at higher resolutions or use the "toner save" mode inconsistently. The 12,000-page claim assumes 5% coverage — standard for text documents, but if you're printing marketing materials, plan for 7,000–8,000 pages.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Small to mid-size offices** running a PA5500x or MA5500if as a shared workgroup printer — the reliability is worth the premium.
- **Print shops or legal/medical practices** where document quality and uptime are non-negotiable.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work in the PA5500x AND the MA5500if?**
Yes — both models use the same TK-3432 cartridge. Just verify your firmware is current, and it'll be recognized instantly.

**Q: How do I know when it's nearly empty?**
The printer driver shows a toner gauge, and the front panel displays a warning at roughly 10% remaining. You'll get a "Toner Low" alert before it's critical — you won't be caught mid-print.

**Q: Can I use a compatible cartridge instead and save money?**
You can, but you're accepting risk. Compatibles sometimes produce lighter prints or inconsistent fusion, and the savings narrow if you account for reprints. For a business-critical printer, I'd stick with OEM.
