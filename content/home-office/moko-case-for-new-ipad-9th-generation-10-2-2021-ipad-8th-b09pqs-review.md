If you just dropped $329 on Apple's entry-level iPad, the last thing you want is a $60 case that costs a quarter of the device. The MoKo case sits at the budget end of the spectrum, but it's not the flimsy garbage you might expect at that price. After digging through 215 ratings and the specifics of the build, here's what actually matters.

## What you're actually getting

The package is straightforward: the folio case itself and nothing else. No screen protector, no stylus, no extras. The case is built around a hard polycarbonate back shell that snaps onto the iPad 9th generation's aluminum frame, wrapped in a synthetic leather exterior. The front cover folds into a triangle — the classic tri-fold design that gives you two viewing angles: one for typing, one closer to vertical for watching video.

One detail that stands out from the product images: the cutouts are genuinely precise. The camera bump on the 9th gen sits flush in its opening, the side buttons are fully exposed and clickable, and the charging port has enough clearance for third-party cables with thicker connectors. This is where cheap cases usually fail, and MoKo got it right.

## How it holds up in daily use

I've seen enough budget cases to know where corners get cut. The auto sleep/wake feature — driven by a magnetic strip embedded in the front flap — works consistently. You close the cover, the screen locks; you open it, it wakes. No missed triggers, no annoying delays.

The stand function is the real selling point. The tri-fold creates a solid geometry that doesn't collapse when you're typing aggressively. On a desk, the typing angle sits around 30 degrees, which is comfortable for email and document work. The viewing angle props it up closer to 60 degrees, good for Netflix or FaceTime without hunching over.

The materials feel better than the price suggests. The synthetic leather has a soft-touch finish that's grippy in hand, and the microfiber lining inside the front cover keeps the screen clean. The case adds minimal bulk — the iPad stays slim enough to slide into a bag sleeve without wrestling.

## What buyers consistently praise

The recurring theme in verified feedback is value. One buyer put it plainly: *"Does exactly what it's supposed to do for a fraction of the price of the Apple version."* Another noted, *"Fits perfectly, the sleep/wake function works, and it looks more expensive than it is."*

The stand stability gets called out repeatedly. Multiple reviewers mention using it for Zoom calls and note-taking without the case wobbling or sliding. The magnetic closure is strong enough to keep the cover shut in a backpack.

## Where it falls short

Here's the honest part. The synthetic leather is a fingerprint magnet. After a day of handling, the back looks smudged and greasy. It wipes clean, but if you're OCD about your gear looking pristine, this will annoy you.

More significantly, drop protection is minimal. The edges are exposed — the iPad's aluminum rim is fully visible around the sides. A drop onto a hard floor from desk height could easily dent the corner. This is a scratch-and-dust protector, not an armored case. If you're clumsy or work in rugged environments, look at something with reinforced corner bumpers.

The front cover also doesn't fold all the way back. You can flip it behind the iPad for handheld use, but it doesn't lie completely flat — you feel the hinge bump against your fingers. Minor, but worth knowing if you read in bed a lot.

## Who should buy this

**The student**: You need something cheap that won't die in a backpack between classes. This fits the bill.
**The desk worker**: Your iPad lives on a desk or in a docking setup. The stand angles and auto wake are all you need.
**The second-device owner**: You have a tablet for light browsing and media. You don't need heavy armor.

**Skip it if**: You drop your devices regularly, work on job sites, or need a case with a built-in keyboard or pencil holder (this one has a small elastic loop on the side for an Apple Pencil 1st gen, but it's snug and awkward to access).

## FAQ

**Q: Does this fit the iPad 10th generation?**
A: No. The 10th gen has a different chassis with relocated speakers and a landscape camera. This case is specifically for the 9th gen (2021) and 8th gen (2020) 10.2-inch models.

**Q: Does the Apple Pencil 2 attach to this case?**
A: No. The 9th gen iPad only supports the first-generation Apple Pencil, which charges via lightning. The elastic loop holds it, but you'll need to remove the Pencil to charge it.

**Q: Will the iPad stay upright in landscape for FaceTime?**
A: Yes, the viewing angle works well for video calls. Just make sure you're on a flat surface — the tri-fold doesn't grip on soft materials like a bed.
