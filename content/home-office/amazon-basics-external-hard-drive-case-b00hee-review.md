**Review Body**

You’ve got a $100 portable SSD and you’re tossing it loose into a backpack next to keys, pens, and a water bottle. That’s how drives die. The Amazon Basics External Hard Drive Case is the $10 insurance policy that prevents that specific, avoidable tragedy. It’s not flashy, but it does exactly what it promises — and with a 4.8-star average from 240 ratings, it’s clearly working for a lot of people.

**What you actually get**

The case measures roughly 5 x 3.7 x 1 inches — sized specifically for 2.5-inch portable drives, not the larger 3.5-inch desktop units. Inside, you get a semi-rigid EVA shell (that’s the molded foam-like plastic that holds its shape) lined with a soft interior. There’s an elastic strap across the main compartment to hold your drive in place, plus a mesh pocket on the lid for cables or a USB adapter. A standard zipper runs around three sides, and there’s a small webbing loop on the end if you want to clip it to a bag.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The EVA shell is the star here. It’s firm enough that you can feel genuine protection when you squeeze it — your drive isn’t going to bend or flex inside. The interior padding is soft and non-abrasive, so you won’t scratch a brushed-aluminum SSD. The zipper is surprisingly smooth for the price point; no catching or snagging, even when the case is packed full.

In practice, this thing disappears into a backpack or briefcase. It’s thin enough to slide into a laptop sleeve pocket, and light enough that you’ll forget it’s there — until you need it. The elastic strap holds a standard 2.5-inch drive firmly; there’s no rattling or shifting when you carry it by the handle.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The ratings tell a clear story. One reviewer put it simply: “Fits my Samsung T7 perfectly, and the quality is way better than I expected for the price.” Another noted, “I’ve bought three of these for different drives. They’re all still holding up after two years of daily commuting.” The recurring theme is that this case punches above its price tag — people expect flimsy at this price point and get something that genuinely feels protective.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. The mesh pocket is tight — if your USB cable has a thick molded connector head, it’s a squeeze. Stick to thin, basic cables or you’ll be zipping the case shut over a bulging pocket. The exterior finish also picks up scuffs and scratches fairly easily; the black matte texture shows every scrape against a laptop charger. It’s cosmetic, not structural, but if you’re particular about gear looking clean, this will annoy you. And a minor gripe: there’s no carabiner or belt loop, so you’re relying on that webbing loop if you want to attach it to anything.

**Who should buy this**

- **Commuting professionals** who carry a portable SSD between home and office — this is the exact use case it nails.
- **Students** with a backup drive for thesis work or portfolios; the price makes it a no-brainer.
- **Photographers/videographers** who need lightweight protection for on-location media backups.
- **Not for** anyone with a 3.5-inch desktop drive — it won’t fit, period. And if you need waterproof or crushproof protection for extreme travel, look at a hard Pelican-style case instead.

**FAQ**

**Will this fit a Samsung T7 or SanDisk Extreme SSD?**
Yes. Both are 2.5-inch form factor drives, and they fit with room to spare. The elastic strap holds them snugly without pressing on the connector ports.

**Can I fit a USB-C cable and a small adapter inside?**
A standard USB-C cable, yes. A thick braided cable or a large hub, no — the mesh pocket is about 4 inches wide and shallow. Keep it to one slim cable.

**Is the case actually drop-proof?**
No case is drop-proof — that’s a marketing myth. But the EVA shell absorbs minor impacts and protects against compression in a bag. It’s designed for everyday bumps, not throwing your bag off a roof.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you own any 2.5-inch external drive and want cheap, reliable protection that fits in a bag without adding bulk. At this price, buying a second one for a backup drive is basically free.

**Skip it if** you need heavy-duty protection, carry multiple thick cables, or expect a premium feel — this is a functional tool, not a luxury accessory.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The best sub-$15 drive case you can buy. It protects your gear, fits universally, and the minor flaws (scuff-prone exterior, tight cable pocket) are easily forgiven at this price point.
