# Paper Mate EverStrong #2 Pencils Review: Do Reinforced Leads Actually Work?

Pencils break. It's the universal frustration every student, teacher, and desk-jockey knows. You're mid-sentence on a Scantron or deep into margin notes, and *snap* — the tip shears off. Paper Mate's EverStrong line claims to fix that with reinforced lead. After testing a full box through real-world abuse, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

This listing ships a 72-count box of #2 HB pencils. Each one measures a standard 7.5 inches with a classic hexagonal barrel — no ergonomic gimmicks, no grip padding. The latex-free eraser is firmly crimped in a metal ferrule. What matters is the lead: Paper Mate bonds the graphite core to the wood casing during manufacturing, which is their answer to the breakage problem.

## Performance and Build Quality

I put these through three torture tests: dropping a pencil tip-first onto a tile floor from desk height, pressing hard enough to dent notebook paper, and using a cheap handheld sharpener repeatedly. The results were surprising.

The drop test? Zero breaks across five attempts. The heavy-pressure writing? The lead wore down faster than standard pencils, but it didn't snap clean in half like typical #2 cores do. Instead, it crumbled slightly — which is preferable to losing the whole point.

Writing feel is smooth with a consistent dark line. These are true HB hardness — not the hard, faint cores you find in bargain brands. The wood sharpens cleanly when your blade is fresh. One annoyance: the wood grain can be inconsistent, so some pencils sharpen unevenly with duller sharpeners, leaving jagged edges that catch on paper.

The eraser deserves a mention. It's not pink, it's the white rubber compound, and it lifts graphite without smudging or ripping pages. That's genuinely better than most pencils in this price range.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't inflated. The dominant theme in customer feedback is durability validation. One verified buyer wrote: *"My son drops these constantly at school. We've gone through three boxes of other brands this semester. These are the first pencils that survived more than a week."* Another reviewer noted: *"The lead doesn't break when you sharpen them, which sounds silly, but it's the most annoying part of cheap pencils."*

Multiple teachers in the review thread specifically call out using these for standardized testing where snapped leads cost students time. That's a strong real-world endorsement.

## Where It Falls Short

The reinforced lead has a tradeoff: it's slightly more brittle when you apply lateral pressure, like when you angle the pencil for shading. You can't bear down sideways the way you would with a standard pencil. Also, the 72-count box has no pre-sharpened option in most listings. If you're buying for a classroom, budget time to sharpen dozens of pencils before the first day.

Some reviewers mention the paint coating chips off with heavy use, exposing raw wood. Cosmetic, but noticeable if appearance matters to your kid.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Parents of elementary/middle schoolers**: The break-resistance pays for itself in fewer replacement boxes
- **Test-takers and Scantron users**: No mid-exam tip failures is worth the slightly higher per-pencil cost
- **Teachers buying classroom supplies in bulk**: The 72-count box is practical, but factor in sharpening time

Skip these if you're a mechanical pencil purist or need a premium feel for daily journaling — the hexagonal barrel is utilitarian, not luxurious.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these really break-resistant, or is it marketing?**
A: Independent testing confirms the reinforced core survives drops and vertical pressure better than standard pencils. Lateral pressure still risks breakage, but the claim holds up for the most common failure scenarios.

**Q: Do these work in electric sharpeners?**
A: Yes, but the reinforced core can produce slightly more dust than regular pencils. Clean the sharpener after every few dozen uses.

**Q: How do they compare price-wise to Ticonderoga or Dixon?**
A: Per-pencil cost is similar to mid-range brands, but the durability means you'll likely buy fewer boxes over a school year.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're tired of replacing broken pencils weekly, need reliable performance for standardized tests, or manage classroom supplies for active kids.

**Skip it if** you need pre-sharpened pencils, prefer a premium writing experience, or only use pencils for occasional notes where breakage isn't an issue.

The Paper Mate EverStrong pencils deliver exactly what the name promises. The reinforced lead genuinely reduces breakage, the writing quality is solid, and the eraser outperforms the category standard. It's not a revolutionary product, but it's a practical upgrade that saves money and frustration over a school year.
