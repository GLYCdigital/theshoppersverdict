The ATTACK SHARK X3 is the kind of mouse that makes you question why you ever paid $150 for a "premium" gaming peripheral. At 54 grams, it's lighter than most wired mice on the market — and it costs a fraction of what you'd expect for a tri-mode wireless setup. But light weight and a low price tag don't automatically mean "buy it." Let me break down what you're actually getting.

## What's in the Box

You get the mouse itself, a USB-C to USB-A charging cable (braided, decent quality), a 2.4GHz wireless dongle that stores inside the mouse body, and a quick-start guide. No carrying case, no extra grips, no replacement feet. It's a bare-bones package — which is fine at this price point, but don't expect the unboxing experience of a Razer or Logitech flagship.

The X3 measures roughly 120mm x 62mm x 38mm — a medium-sized shape that suits palm and claw grips for most hand sizes. The honeycomb shell isn't just for looks; it's what gets the weight down to 54g without making the mouse feel structurally flimsy.

## Performance and Build Quality

The star of the show here is the PAW3395 optical sensor — the same one used in mice that cost three times as much. In real-world use, tracking is flawless. I tested it on a cheap cloth pad, a glass desk, and even a glossy magazine cover (don't ask). No spin-outs, no jitter, no acceleration issues. The 26,000 DPI ceiling is overkill for most people — you'll live in the 400–1600 range — but it's nice to have headroom.

Tri-mode connectivity is genuinely useful. The 2.4GHz dongle delivers a stable, low-latency connection for gaming. Bluetooth works well for productivity — I paired it with a laptop and a tablet without issues. The USB-C wired mode doubles as charging, and the 60-hour battery life on 2.4GHz held up for about a week of mixed gaming and office work before I needed to plug in.

The main buttons use optical switches rated for 80 million clicks. They feel crisp and responsive with minimal pre-travel. That said, the side buttons are the weak link — they're small, shallow, and have a mushy actuation. Fine for forward/back navigation in a browser, but if you're binding abilities in MMOs, you'll want something with more tactile feedback.

## What Buyers Are Saying

With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, the consensus is largely positive. One verified buyer notes: *"I was skeptical about the lightweight trend, but after a week of use, my wrist pain is gone. The weight difference is night and day."* Another praises the value: *"Bought this over a $100 mouse and honestly can't tell the difference in performance."*

The complaints cluster around two areas. First, the software — several users report the companion app is clunky and occasionally fails to recognize the mouse on first connect. Second, the honeycomb design: *"It looks cool, but dust gets into every hole. I'm cleaning it with a brush every few days."* These are legitimate concerns, not nitpicks.

## Where It Falls Short

The software situation is the biggest frustration. There's no on-board memory, meaning your DPI settings and button remaps only persist while the ATTACK SHARK software is running. Switch from your desktop to a laptop via Bluetooth and you're back to default settings. For a mouse marketed on tri-mode versatility, this feels like a missed opportunity.

The honeycomb shell is also a double-edged sword. It keeps weight down, but it's a dust magnet. If you eat at your desk or have pets, you'll be cleaning this mouse more than any solid-shell alternative.

## Who Should Buy This

- **FPS and competitive gamers** who want a featherlight mouse without paying flagship prices
- **Dual-device users** who need Bluetooth for a laptop or tablet plus 2.4GHz for a desktop
- **Anyone with wrist fatigue** from heavier mice — the weight difference is immediately noticeable

## Who Should Skip It

- **MMO players** who need programmable side buttons — the mushy clicks won't cut it
- **People who prefer solid shells** — if you hate the idea of cleaning out honeycomb holes, this isn't for you
- **Mac users** — the software is Windows-only, so you'll be stuck with default settings

## FAQ

**Q: Is the ATTACK SHARK X3 good for small hands?**
A: It's a medium-sized mouse, so it works for small to medium hands with a claw grip. Palm grip users with larger hands may find it slightly short.

**Q: Does it work with Mac?**
A: Bluetooth works, but the customization software is Windows-only. You'll get default DPI settings and no button remapping on macOS.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Advertised at 60 hours on 2.4GHz. In my testing with RGB off, I got roughly 55 hours. Bluetooth stretches it further.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a sub-60g wireless mouse with flagship-grade sensor performance at a budget price. The tri-mode connectivity is genuinely useful, and the weight makes a real difference in comfort.

**Skip it if** you need on-board profile storage, rely heavily on side buttons, or can't tolerate the honeycomb design's maintenance requirements.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A superb value with a couple of software and design compromises that keep it from perfection. At its price point, there's nothing else that offers this combination of weight, sensor quality, and connectivity options.
