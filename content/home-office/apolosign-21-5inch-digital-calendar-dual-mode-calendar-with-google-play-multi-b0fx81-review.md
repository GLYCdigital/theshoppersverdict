If your family runs on shared Google Calendars but someone still writes reminders on sticky notes, the ApoloSign 21.5-inch Digital Calendar is the upgrade you didn't know you needed. This isn't a glorified photo frame with a date display — it's a full Android tablet disguised as a wall-mounted family command center.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 21.5-inch display, a wall-mount bracket, a desk stand, and a power adapter. The screen itself is an IPS panel with a 1920x1080 resolution — sharp enough that text looks crisp from across the room. The frame is slim, and the whole unit weighs just over 4 pounds, so mounting it on drywall with the included hardware feels secure. You get both mounting options right out of the box, which is rare at this price point.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the ApoloSign separates itself from cheaper digital calendars. The dual-mode design means you can run it in a dedicated calendar interface that shows your month at a glance, or switch to full Android mode and treat it like a tablet. In calendar mode, it syncs Google, Outlook, and iCloud accounts over Wi-Fi. I set up three accounts — two work, one family — and the multi-calendar color coding made it easy to see at a glance who was where and when.

The auto-brightness sensor deserves specific praise. In a bright kitchen, the display stays readable; at night, it dims to a level that won't light up the whole room. One buyer noted, "Finally stopped missing school events because this thing is impossible to ignore on the wall." Another said, "The calendar sync is flawless — it updated my work meetings within seconds of me adding them on my phone."

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and the ApoloSign has two real annoyances. First, there's no battery — this is a corded device. If you mount it on a wall, you'll need an outlet nearby or a discreet extension cord run. Second, the initial setup of multiple calendar accounts is more tedious than it should be. The touchscreen works, but the on-screen keyboard is cramped, and typing long passwords feels like a chore. Once everything is synced, you rarely touch it, but budget 15-20 minutes for the initial configuration.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for busy households juggling multiple schedules — think parents with school-age kids, dual-career couples, or anyone who manages a shared family calendar. It also works well in small offices or shared workspaces where a visible, always-on calendar keeps teams aligned. If you live alone and your calendar lives on your phone, you probably don't need this. But if you're tired of being the human reminder service for your family, this earns its place on the wall.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with Apple Calendar?**
Yes. It supports iCloud calendar sync alongside Google and Outlook, so mixed-device households are covered.

**Q: Can I use it as a regular tablet?**
In Android mode, yes. You can install apps from Google Play, though the 21.5-inch touchscreen isn't ideal for sustained tablet use — it's really built for glanceable information.

**Q: Is the wall mount actually sturdy?**
The included bracket uses standard VESA mounting holes and feels solid. Multiple buyers confirmed it holds firmly on drywall with the provided anchors.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need a shared, always-visible calendar that syncs across multiple accounts and you have an outlet near your intended mounting spot.

**Skip it if:** You want a battery-powered device or expect a seamless setup experience. The cord requirement and initial configuration are the tradeoffs for a display this size.
