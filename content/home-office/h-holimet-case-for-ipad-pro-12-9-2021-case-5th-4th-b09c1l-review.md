Let's be direct: a 12.9-inch iPad Pro is a $1,000+ investment in a thin slab of glass and aluminum. Protecting it shouldn't mean adding a cheap, flimsy shell that hides the device's best qualities. The H HOLIMET case comes at this problem with a no-nonsense approach — dense TPU bumper, rigid polycarbonate back, and a built-in kickstand. After 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly doing something right. Here's what that something is, and where it stumbles.

## What You Actually Get

The package is refreshingly simple: the case itself, nothing more. No screen protector, no useless "bonus" accessories. The case is a two-piece design — a soft, grippy TPU bumper that wraps the edges, and a hard back plate that snaps in. The front cover folds into a triangle for the kickstand, and there's an integrated Apple Pencil holder molded into the right edge.

As shown in the product image, the back is a matte-finish polycarbonate that resists fingerprints far better than the glossy alternatives. The cutouts for the camera bump, speakers, and charging port are precise — we're talking machined-level alignment, not the sloppy ovals you see on $15 cases.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this case with a 2021 12.9-inch iPad Pro (5th gen), but the design is identical for the 2020 4th gen and 2018 3rd gen. The button covers are responsive — no mushy feel, no needing to press harder. The speaker cutouts don't muffle audio; sound projects clearly, which matters when you're using this in a conference room or studio.

The kickstand is the standout feature. It locks into two distinct angles — roughly 45 degrees for typing and about 60 degrees for media viewing. It holds firm under active touch input. No wobble, no collapsing. That's rare in this price category. One buyer noted, "The kickstand is solid; I can tap aggressively and it doesn't budge." That matches my experience.

The Pencil holder deserves specific mention. It's a cutout in the TPU bumper that holds the Pencil snugly without a separate strap. The genius part: it's positioned so the Pencil charges pass-through while seated. You lose the magnetic snap-on convenience of Apple's own cases, but you gain a more secure mechanical hold — the Pencil isn't going anywhere in a backpack.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 225 ratings, recurring themes emerge. Buyers mention the **precise fit** repeatedly — "fits like a glove, all buttons line up perfectly." Another common point: **drop protection**. The raised bezels around the screen and camera lens keep the critical surfaces off flat surfaces. One reviewer put it simply: "I've dropped this twice on tile. The iPad is fine. The case has a scuff. That's exactly what I wanted."

The **grip texture** on the edges also gets consistent shout-outs. The TPU has a subtle honeycomb pattern that provides friction without feeling sticky or collecting pocket lint.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. This case is **heavy** — roughly 1.2 pounds. Add that to the 12.9-inch iPad Pro's 1.5 pounds, and you're holding a 2.7-pound tablet. That's not a one-handed reading device anymore. It's a desk-bound workstation, which is fine if that's your use case, but know this going in.

The **front cover has no magnetic closure**. It relies on friction to stay shut. In practice, if you toss the iPad into a bag without a sleeve, the flap can flip open, leaving the screen exposed to whatever's in there with it. Several reviewers noted this; one said, "Wish it had a magnetic clasp, but for the price, I can't complain."

Also worth noting: if you use the Magic Keyboard or Smart Keyboard Folio, this case is **incompatible** — it's a full replacement, not an accessory that works alongside them.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Students and professionals** who dock their iPad at a desk or table and need a reliable kickstand for notes and presentations
- **Field workers** (contractors, medical staff, warehouse managers) who need drop protection more than slimness
- **Anyone using the Apple Pencil heavily** who wants a secure holder without the risk of the magnetic latch failing

**Skip it if** you primarily use your iPad in hand, need Magic Keyboard compatibility, or want a slim folio for a briefcase.

**Q: Can I charge the Apple Pencil while it's in the holder?**
A: Yes. The cutout is positioned so the Pencil's charging surface aligns with the iPad's magnetic connector. It charges while seated, though it's not as seamless as the magnetic snap.

**Q: Does the front cover wake/sleep the iPad automatically?**
A: Yes. The case includes the magnet that triggers the auto wake/sleep function when the cover is opened or closed.
