The LG 26WQ500-B is the monitor that makes you question why you ever tolerated dual monitors in the first place. At $250, this 25-inch ultrawide (don't let the "26" in the name fool you — it's a 25-inch panel, a quirk LG hasn't bothered to explain) delivers the productivity boost of two side-by-side displays in a single, seamless screen. After 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the consensus is clear: this is a serious workhorse. But it's not without compromises.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box, you get the monitor, a stand that clips on without tools, a power brick, and an HDMI cable. Notably absent: a USB-C cable. That's a real oversight because the USB-C port is this monitor's best feature — it carries display signal and delivers 65W of power to your laptop. You'll need to buy a cable separately, which feels like LG nickel-and-diming you after dropping $250.

The panel itself is a 2560x1080 IPS display with a 100Hz refresh rate. The 21:9 aspect ratio is the star here. In the product image, you can see how it stretches across a desk — that's the appeal. Two document windows fit comfortably side-by-side, and spreadsheet warriors will appreciate the extra columns visible without horizontal scrolling.

## Real-World Performance

The 100Hz refresh rate is the sleeper feature on an office monitor. It's not for gaming — though it handles light games fine — it's for the way your cursor glides and how scrolling doesn't stutter. Once you've worked on a 100Hz panel for a week, going back to 60Hz feels like wading through molasses. That said, 2560x1080 on a 25-inch screen gives you roughly 92 PPI, which is fine but not sharp. Text has a slight softness compared to 1440p panels. You'll adapt quickly, but if you're coming from a high-DPI laptop screen, the difference is immediate.

Color accuracy is respectable out of the box for an IPS panel. It covers 99% of sRGB, which matters if you do any photo editing or design work. It won't replace a calibrated professional monitor, but for everyday office tasks, it's more than adequate.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The praise in the Amazon reviews is consistent. One buyer noted, "The ultrawide format has completely replaced my dual monitor setup. I forgot how annoying bezels were." Another highlighted the USB-C convenience: "One cable charges my MacBook and drives the display. My desk has never been cleaner."

The criticisms are equally consistent. Several reviewers mention the flimsy stand and limited adjustability. One put it bluntly: "The stand is the weakest link. It wobbles slightly when I type aggressively." Another flagged the missing USB-C cable: "LG includes an HDMI cable but not USB-C. That's a $15 cable they should've included."

## Where It Falls Short

The stand is genuinely the weakest part of this package. Tilt-only adjustment means you can't raise the panel to eye level unless you buy a VESA mount (the monitor supports 100x100mm). Factor that cost into your decision. The built-in speakers are also a waste of space — they sound like a laptop from 2012. Plan for headphones or external speakers.

The resolution is the other tradeoff. At this size, 2560x1080 works, but you're getting about 40% fewer pixels than a standard 27-inch 1440p monitor. Side-by-side windows work great, but you won't fit three full windows comfortably, and fine text rendering isn't the strong suit.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a multitasker drowning in browser tabs, a spreadsheet analyst comparing data across windows, or a writer who needs reference material and a document open simultaneously. If you have a USB-C laptop and want a clean one-cable desk, this is a strong candidate.

**Skip it if:** You're a photo editor needing pixel-perfect sharpness, a gamer wanting high refresh rates at higher resolutions, or someone who refuses to use a monitor arm — the stand's limitations will frustrate you daily.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with MacBooks?**
A: Yes, via USB-C. The 65W power delivery handles most MacBooks, though the 16-inch MacBook Pro may need its own charger under heavy load.

**Q: Can I game on this monitor?**
A: Casual games run fine at 100Hz with FreeSync support. Competitive gamers should look elsewhere — the 1080p vertical resolution and 5ms response time aren't competitive specs.

**Q: Is one ultrawide better than two monitors?**
A: For most office work, yes. No bezel, easier window snapping, and fewer cables. The tradeoff is less total screen space than two 24-inch monitors side-by-side.

## The Verdict

The LG 26WQ500-B earns its 4.5-star rating through sheer practicality. It solves a real problem — desktop clutter and bezel fatigue — at a price that undercuts dual-monitor setups. The USB-C charging and 100Hz refresh are features you'll appreciate daily. But budget for a monitor arm and a USB-C cable, and know that the resolution is a compromise, not a feature.

**Buy it if** you want a seamless ultrawide workspace without breaking $300. **Skip it if** the stand limitations or 1080p resolution will drive you nuts. For everyone else, this is a smart, capable work monitor that delivers where it matters.
