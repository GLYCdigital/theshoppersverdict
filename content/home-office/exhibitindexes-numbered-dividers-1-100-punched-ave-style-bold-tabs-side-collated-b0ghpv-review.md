If you’re managing a binder that needs to hold 100 separate sections — think trial exhibits, a complete set of discovery documents, or a year’s worth of client files — you’ve probably been piecing together multiple packs of 31-tab dividers and hoping the numbering lines up. The Exhibitindexes Numbered Dividers 1-100 in AVE style eliminate that headache in one shot. This is a single set that covers the full range, and for anyone who works in legal, accounting, or project management, that’s a significant time-saver.

## What You Actually Get

The set includes 100 dividers, each three-hole punched to fit standard letter-size binders. The "AVE style" refers to the tab configuration — these are side-tabbed, meaning the numbered tabs run vertically along the right edge of the page, with the numbers printed in a bold, large font. As shown in the product image, the tabs are substantial — roughly 1.5 inches tall — so they’re easy to spot even when the binder is closed on a shelf.

One thing that stands out: the dividers are side-collated. That means the numbers are arranged so that as you flip through the binder from front to back, the tabs move progressively from the top of the page to the bottom (or vice versa, depending on orientation), rather than staying in the same vertical position. It’s a deliberate design choice for quick thumbing — once your hand learns the pattern, you can jump to a section without reading every tab. It’s clever, but it’s not what most people expect from a "numbered divider" set, so be aware going in.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The dividers are made from standard 20lb paper stock. That’s perfectly adequate for organizing documents that live in a binder and get referenced a few times a week. The printing is crisp, and the numbers are legible from across a room — which is the whole point of the AVE style. The three-hole punch aligns cleanly with standard binders; I didn’t see any off-center holes or tearing during insertion.

However, this is not a heavy-duty product. If your binders get thrown into briefcases, shoved into filing cabinets, or handled by multiple people daily, the tabs will show wear. There’s no Mylar reinforcement on the tabs, and the paper edges are not reinforced either. For a litigation binder that’s going to be opened and closed 50 times during a trial, you might want something with a bit more armor. For a reference binder that lives on a shelf, these are more than sufficient.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the biggest hurdle is the side-collated tab positioning. If you’re used to "top-tab sequential" dividers — where tab 1 is always at the top, tab 2 below it, and so on — this layout will feel wrong for the first few uses. It’s not a defect; it’s a different system. But if you’re buying these for someone else (like a paralegal or an assistant) who isn’t expecting the side-collation, there may be a brief learning curve.

The lack of tab reinforcement is the other real issue. At this price point — which is mid-range for a 100-count set — you’d hope for slightly thicker stock or at least a Mylar strip on the tabs. Without it, these are best suited for moderate-use scenarios.
