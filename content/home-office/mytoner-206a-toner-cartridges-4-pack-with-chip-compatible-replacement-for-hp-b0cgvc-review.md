Let's be blunt: buying toner for an HP LaserJet Pro is usually a gut-check moment. OEM cartridges run $80+ each, and the generic market is a minefield of leaky drums and flaky chips. The MYTONER 206A 4-pack sits right in the middle of that tension — it's a third-party option that promises OEM-matching yield at a fraction of the cost. After digging through 225 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

**What's actually in the box**

You get four sealed black toner cartridges, each with a copper-colored chip already installed on the side. The packaging is utilitarian — no foam padding theatrics, just sturdy cardboard and plastic caps on the imaging drums. Each cartridge is rated for 2,400 pages at 5% coverage, which matches the HP 206A OEM spec exactly. That's roughly 9,600 total pages across the set. The chips are the big deal here: they're pre-programmed to communicate with the printer's toner-level monitoring, so you won't get the dreaded "genuine HP toner" warning that some generics trigger.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these in an HP LaserJet Pro M255dw — one of the most common printers this cartridge fits. Installation took under two minutes: pull the orange tab, line up the rails, click. The printer recognized all four without a firmware fight. Text output at 600 DPI is sharp — no jagged edges on 10-point fonts, no gray ghosting on double-sided prints. Solid black fills on charts and headers came out dense and consistent.

Where these fall slightly short is grayscale photo printing. A test print of a landscape image showed slightly lighter shadow detail compared to an OEM cartridge side-by-side. It's not a dealbreaker for everyday business documents, but if you're printing presentation graphics with heavy gradients, you'll notice the difference. One buyer put it plainly: "Text is perfect, but my photo-heavy flyers look washed out compared to the HP originals." That tracks with my testing.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The overwhelming theme across positive reviews is reliability without the price tag. One verified purchase said: "I've been burned by cheap toner before — leaking cartridges, printer errors. These just worked. 2,000+ pages in and no issues." Another noted the yield holds up: "I'm a teacher printing worksheets daily. Four months in, still on the first cartridge." The chip compatibility gets repeated shout-outs — no error messages, no wasted time on HP support chat.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest practical gripe: you can't buy a single cartridge. If your black toner runs out but the other three colors are fine (or vice versa), you're stuck buying the full set. That's a $50+ up-front commitment even if you only need one color. Also, the cartridges are slightly noisier during printing — a faint whir during rotation that OEMs have dampened. It's not annoying, but it's there. And while the chips work out of the box, HP firmware updates are a moving target; a future update could theoretically disrupt compatibility, though I saw no reports of that so far.

A: The 2,400-page rating is at standard 5% page coverage — that's about 250-300 words of text per page. If you print dense graphics or full-bleed images, expect closer to 1,500-1,800 pages per cartridge. For typical office documents, the rating holds up.

**Q: Do these leak or cause printer damage?**
