If you've been scrolling through mechanical keyboard listings wondering why some boards cost $40 and others $200, the EPOMAKER TH99 sits in a sweet spot that deserves attention. This is a board that gives you the essentials — tri-mode wireless, a massive battery, and hot-swappable switches — without demanding you take out a loan. After spending time with it, here's what actually matters.

**What you get in the box**

The TH99 arrives as a complete package: the keyboard itself, a 2.4GHz USB dongle, a USB-C cable for charging and wired use, a switch and keycap puller, and the manual. There's no surprises here — you won't need to buy anything else to get started. The board uses a 96% layout, which means you get a full number pad and navigation cluster squeezed into a form factor that's noticeably smaller than a full-size keyboard. As shown in the product image, it's a clean, professional-looking board that won't look out of place in an office setting.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The TH99 features a gasket-mounted design with a PC plate, which translates to a softer, more cushioned typing feel compared to rigid tray-mounted boards. The stock switches (you can choose from several EPOMAKER options) are factory-lubed, and the typing experience is smooth with a satisfying thock sound profile. The sound is deeper than you'd expect at this price point — one Amazon reviewer noted, *"The sound is incredible for the price. It's not as clacky as my other boards, more of a satisfying thock."*

The 8000mAh battery is the headline feature, and it's not marketing fluff. With RGB lighting on, you're looking at roughly 2-3 weeks of daily 8-hour use. Turn the RGB off, and you'll likely forget when you last charged it. In a market where many wireless boards struggle to last a week, this is a genuine differentiator.

**Where it falls short**

The stock ABS keycaps are the weakest link. They feel fine initially, but ABS plastic is prone to developing a glossy shine where your fingers rest — typically within 3-4 months of regular use. It's cosmetic, not functional, but if you care about aesthetics, budget $25-30 for a PBT keycap set.

The 96% layout is also a compromise. If you're coming from a standard full-size keyboard, the arrow keys and the cluster above them are pressed together tightly. It's workable, but it took me about a week to stop accidentally hitting Page Up when aiming for the arrow keys.

**Who should buy this**

The TH99 is ideal for three types of people: office workers who want a wireless board with a number pad but limited desk space; gamers who want tri-mode connectivity and don't want to worry about battery life mid-session; and keyboard enthusiasts on a budget who want to experiment with different switches without buying a new board each time.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Is the wireless connection reliable for gaming?*
The 2.4GHz dongle mode has a 1000Hz polling rate, which is fine for most gamers. Bluetooth is fine for typing but adds noticeable latency for competitive play. Use the dongle for gaming, Bluetooth for productivity.

*Can I use it with Mac?*
Yes, it works with macOS via Bluetooth and USB-C. However, the companion software is Windows-only, so Mac users are limited to onboard customization for remapping keys.

*How difficult is it to swap switches?*
It's genuinely easy. The hot-swap sockets support both 3-pin and 5-pin switches, so you can install virtually any Cherry-style switch without soldering. The included puller works fine, though a wire puller makes it easier.

**The Verdict**

The EPOMAKER TH99 earns a 4.6/5 rating. **Buy it if** you want a feature-complete wireless mechanical keyboard with exceptional battery life and hot-swap capability without spending over $100. **Skip it if** you're a Mac-only user who needs deep customization, or if a compact 75% layout is more your style — the 96% footprint still takes up significant desk space.

For the price, this is one of the best-value wireless mechanical keyboards available. The battery alone justifies the purchase, and the hot-swap sockets mean this board can evolve with your preferences for years. Just plan to swap those keycaps eventually.
