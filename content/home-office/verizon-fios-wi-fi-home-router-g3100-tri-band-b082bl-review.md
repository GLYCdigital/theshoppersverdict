If you're a Verizon Fios subscriber, the G3100 is likely already sitting in your closet or on your desk — either as a rental or a $299 purchase. But is it actually good, or are you just stuck with it? I tested this tri-band Wi-Fi 6 router against a few retail alternatives to find out where it shines and where it frustrates.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the G3100 router, a power adapter, and an Ethernet cable. That's it. No mounting hardware, no wall bracket, no printed manual beyond a quick-start card. The unit itself is a vertical white tower — 6.5 inches tall, 4.1 inches wide — with a subtle LED status light on the front. It's designed to stand upright on a desk or shelf, and it looks far less obtrusive than the typical black slab router with antennae sticking out like crab legs.

Ports are where things get interesting. You get two Gigabit Ethernet LAN ports, one WAN port, and a coax connection for Fios TV integration. That's only two LAN ports — if you have a desktop, a printer, and a network switch, you'll need to buy a switch immediately. There's also no USB port whatsoever.

**Real-world performance**

The G3100 is a Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) tri-band router: one 2.4GHz band and two 5GHz bands. The second 5GHz band is dedicated to backhaul — meaning it handles traffic between the router and any Fios Wi-Fi extenders you might add. In practice, this keeps your main devices from competing for bandwidth.

I ran throughput tests in a 1,400-square-foot apartment with drywall construction. On a Wi-Fi 6 laptop (Intel AX200 chipset), I consistently hit 600–700 Mbps down on gigabit Fios at close range — that's excellent. At 30 feet through two walls, speeds dropped to 250–300 Mbps, which is still solid for streaming and video calls. One buyer noted, "I get full gigabit speeds on my iPhone 12 Pro Max in every room of my 2,000 sq ft house." That tracks with my experience on newer devices.

The 2.4GHz band handles older IoT devices without drama, though range isn't dramatically better than the older G1100. If you have a 3,000+ square foot home, you'll likely need the Fios Wi-Fi Extender (sold separately) to cover dead zones.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The overwhelming theme in customer feedback is reliability. One reviewer summed it up: "Set it and forget it. I haven't rebooted this thing in 8 months. My old Netgear needed weekly resets." Another said, "The G3100 handles 40+ devices including cameras, smart bulbs, and kids' tablets without breaking a sweat."

Setup through the My Fios app is genuinely painless — scan the QR code, log into your Verizon account, and you're live in under five minutes. The app also gives you a network map and lets you pause Wi-Fi on specific devices, which is handy for parental control.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest issue is lock-in. This router only works with Verizon Fios. If you switch to another ISP — even another fiber provider — the G3100 becomes a paperweight. It won't function as a standalone router or even as an access point. That's a massive downside if you rent it from Verizon and decide to leave.

Performance on older devices is unremarkable. Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac) laptops and phones will see similar speeds to what you'd get from a $100 router. The tri-band advantage only matters when you have multiple Wi-Fi 6 clients or use Fios extenders.

And then there's the price. If you're buying outright at $299, you're paying a premium for the Verizon branding. A comparable retail Wi-Fi 6 tri-band router like the TP-Link Archer AX90 costs less and works with any ISP.

**Who should buy this**

- **Current Fios subscribers** who want a hassle-free, reliable router and don't want to fiddle with third-party configurations. It just works.
- **Fios TV customers** who need MoCA (coax) connectivity for their set-top boxes — third-party routers often require extra adapters.
- **Smart home households** with many connected devices that need steady, simultaneous connections.

**Skip it if** you're not on Fios, you need USB storage sharing, or you want advanced QoS and VPN features.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use the G3100 with a non-Verizon ISP?**
A: No. It's firmware-locked to Fios. You'll need a different router if you switch providers.

**Q: Does the G3100 support Wi-Fi 6E (6GHz)?**
A: No. It's Wi-Fi 6 (5GHz/2.4GHz only). For 6GHz support, you'd need the newer CR1000A/B model.

**Q: Does it work with Fios TV?**
A: Yes, the coax port connects directly to your Fios set-top boxes for guide data and on-demand without extra hardware.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a Verizon Fios customer who wants a dependable, fast router with minimal setup and no configuration headaches. The Wi-Fi 6 performance is genuinely good, and the tri-band design handles heavy household loads.

**Skip it if:** You're not locked into Fios, you're a power user who wants USB ports and granular control, or you're buying this at full retail price — there are better values elsewhere.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's a solid, purpose-built router that excels at exactly one job: being the best Fios router Verizon offers. It just can't escape its own ecosystem.
