Let's cut through the listing copy. The Ejet 952XL 4-pack is a third-party ink option for HP printers that take the standard 952 cartridge family — think OfficeJet Pro 8700, 8720, 8740, and the 8210/8216 series. The selling point is obvious: you're paying roughly half of what HP charges for OEM 952XL cartridges, and the "high yield" label promises roughly 2,000 pages per black cartridge and 1,600 per color. But with a 3.4-star average across 170 ratings, the real question is whether the savings justify the gamble.

**What you actually get**

The box contains four cartridges: one black, one cyan, one magenta, one yellow. Each is individually sealed and ships with a plastic clip that you'll need to remove before installation. The chips are pre-installed, which means no fiddling with reset tools or tape tricks. Physical dimensions match the OEM 952XL cartridges exactly — they click into place the same way, and the printhead contacts align properly. As shown in the product image, the cartridges look nearly identical to HP's own, right down to the labeling style — which is fine for compatibility but worth knowing if you're concerned about warranty implications.

**Real-world performance**

Here's where the 3.4 rating starts making sense. In standard draft mode, the black cartridge genuinely pushes past 1,800 pages before hitting empty. That's honest performance. Text documents look crisp — black text is deep and sharp, with no smudging or feathering on plain copy paper. For a home office cranking out contracts and invoices, this works.

But the color cartridges are a different story. On glossy photo paper, you'll notice a yellow-green cast in skin tones and muted blues. It's not a dealbreaker for charts and graphs, but if you print family photos or marketing materials, you will see the difference. One reviewer put it plainly: "Text is fine, but my color prints look washed out compared to HP cartridges. Fine for everyday, not for presentations."

The bigger issue is quality control. While most buyers report smooth installation — "They installed without any warnings on my HP 8720, and the ink level indicators work correctly" — a significant minority describe leaking cartridges, chips that fail to register, or printers that refuse to recognize the cartridges entirely after a firmware update. HP has been aggressive with firmware patches that block third-party ink, and that's a risk no discount can fully offset.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of individual cartridges in the 4-pack is a real pain point. If your cyan runs dry and the other three are still half-full, you're buying a whole new set — or hunting for a single compatible cartridge elsewhere. That erodes the cost advantage if you print unevenly across colors.

Ink level reporting is also inconsistent. Some users report the printer shows "low ink" after just a few hundred pages, even though the cartridge keeps printing for weeks. It's not a fatal flaw, but it undermines confidence and forces you to keep printing on faith.

**Who should buy this**

This is a budget play for high-volume document printing. If you're printing primarily black-and-white text for a home office, small business, or student use, the savings are real and the performance is adequate. It's also a reasonable choice if you're comfortable with the occasional cartridge swap and don't need precise color reproduction.

Skip it if you print photos, if your HP printer has received recent firmware updates (check HP's support page before ordering), or if you're the type of user who wants zero troubleshooting — OEM cartridges are still the reliable path.

**FAQ**

*Will these void my HP printer warranty?* Under US law, using third-party cartridges cannot void your printer warranty unless HP can prove the cartridge caused the damage. In practice, HP may refuse service for printhead issues, so weigh that risk.

*Do they work with HP Instant Ink?* No. Instant Ink requires HP-branded cartridges with embedded tracking chips. These will not work with that program.

*How do they compare to refilled cartridges?* These are new-build cartridges with fresh printheads, which makes them generally more reliable than refilled options. You're less likely to get clogs or streaky output.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you print mostly black-and-white documents, want to cut ink costs by half, and can tolerate occasional quality-control roulette.

**Skip it if** you need accurate color, rely on HP firmware updates, or can't afford downtime from a bad cartridge.

**Rating: 3.3/5** — The cost savings are undeniable, but the QC inconsistency and color limitations keep this from being a slam dunk. For high-volume text printing on a budget, it's a calculated risk worth taking. For anything else, stick with OEM.
