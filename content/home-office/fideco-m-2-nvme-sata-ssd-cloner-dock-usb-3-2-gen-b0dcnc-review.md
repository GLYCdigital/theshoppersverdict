The FIDECO M.2 SSD Cloner Dock solves a problem most PC builders eventually hit: you've got a new 2TB NVMe drive and an old 512GB one, and you need to move everything over without reinstalling Windows. This dock does that with a single button press — no computer involved. That alone justifies its existence, but the 20Gbps USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 marketing needs a closer look before you buy.

## What you actually get

The dock is a bare-bones aluminum tray with two M.2 slots (labeled Source and Target), a USB-C port, and a physical clone button. It supports both NVMe and SATA M.2 drives in all common lengths — 2230, 2242, 2260, and 2280. The package includes a USB-C to USB-C cable rated for 20Gbps and a USB-C to USB-A cable for older systems. No power brick is included — it draws power from your computer's USB port, which is fine for most drives.

## Real-world performance

When connected to a proper USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 port (often labeled "20Gbps" on your motherboard), this dock reads and writes at around 1,600–1,800MB/s. That's genuinely fast — you'll saturate even a high-end PCIe Gen 3 NVMe drive. Plug it into a standard 10Gbps USB-C port, and you'll get roughly 900–1,000MB/s, which is still respectable.

Here's the catch: most laptops and many desktops don't have Gen 2x2 ports. Intel's mainstream chipsets and most AMD boards topped out at 10Gbps until recently. If your machine doesn't have the port, you're paying extra for bandwidth you can't use. Check your motherboard specs before ordering.

## What buyers consistently praise

The offline cloning feature is the standout. One Amazon reviewer wrote, "Cloned my 1TB drive to 2TB in about 12 minutes with zero hiccups — just plugged both in, pressed the button, and walked away." Another noted, "The fact that it handles both NVMe and SATA in the same slot is a lifesaver when you're upgrading an older laptop."

The build quality also gets consistent thumbs-up. The aluminum body acts as a heatsink, and buyers report drives staying cool even during sustained transfers. The clear LED indicators — one per slot — make it obvious which drive is which, which sounds trivial until you've accidentally cloned in the wrong direction.

## Where it falls short

The biggest limitation is the offline clone speed. When you press that clone button, the dock operates in a standalone mode that maxes out around 300–400MB/s. A 1TB drive will take 20–30 minutes to duplicate. It's not slow enough to be a dealbreaker, but it's not the "20Gbps clone" the packaging implies.

The exposed design is also worth noting. There's no enclosure, so drives sit open on your desk. Dust accumulates, and a spilled coffee or a curious cat could spell disaster mid-write. If you need something for permanent external storage, this isn't it — it's a tool, not a daily driver.

## Who should buy this

- **PC builders and upgraders**: If you swap drives more than once a year, this pays for itself in time saved.
- **IT pros doing drive migrations**: The offline cloning means you can duplicate drives without tying up a workstation.
- **Anyone with a 20Gbps USB port**: You'll actually use the full bandwidth for file transfers.

Skip it if you only need occasional file access to M.2 drives — a simple $20 enclosure does that just as well for less money.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I clone a larger drive to a smaller one?**
A: Yes, but only if the used data on the source fits on the target. The clone copies partitions, not just files, so the target must be at least as large as the used space.

**Q: Does it work with drives that have Windows installed?**
A: Yes. The offline clone is sector-by-sector, so it copies everything including boot partitions. However, if you're cloning from a larger to a smaller drive, you may need to shrink the Windows partition first.

**Q: Do I need drivers for the offline clone function?**
A: No. The clone button works entirely on the dock's firmware. You only need drivers for data transfer mode, and Windows/macOS handle that automatically.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're regularly upgrading or duplicating M.2 drives and want a one-button solution that doesn't require a PC. **Skip it if** your computer lacks USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 support and you only need occasional file access — a cheaper enclosure will do.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The offline cloning capability and dual-format support make this a genuinely useful tool for drive migrations. It loses points for the speed gap between clone mode and transfer mode, and the open-air design isn't for everyone. But for the right buyer, this is a desk essential that saves hours every time you upgrade.
