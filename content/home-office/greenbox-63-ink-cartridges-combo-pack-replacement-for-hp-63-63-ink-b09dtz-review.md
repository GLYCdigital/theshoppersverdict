**The $20 question: Can a third-party ink cartridge actually save you money without wrecking your HP printer?**

I've tested dozens of compatible cartridges over the years, and most fall into two camps: the cheap ones that leak or throw error codes, and the overpriced ones that barely beat OEM pricing. The GREENBOX 63 combo pack sits in a third, more interesting category — it's the option that just *works*, with enough quirks to keep you honest about what you're buying.

**What's in the box**

You get two black (63XL-equivalent) cartridges and one tri-color cartridge, all sealed in foil packs with protective orange caps. The packaging is functional, not fancy, but the cartridges themselves feel solid — no rattling internals, no loose contacts. Each unit weighs in at roughly 45 grams, and the chip contacts are cleanly seated. **Real-world performance**

I ran a 40-page mixed document test — text, charts, and a handful of color photos. Black text came out crisp at 12-point and smaller, with no smudging even on cheap multipurpose paper. The 63 tri-color cartridge uses a dye-based formula that handles standard office documents well, but don't expect gallery-grade photo prints — that's not what this is for.

One thing I noticed: the black ink runs slightly warm. If you're printing pure grayscale photos, you'll see a faint brown cast in the shadows. For everyday text and mixed documents, it's a non-issue.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this void my HP printer warranty?**
A: In the US, no. The Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act protects you from being penalized for using third-party consumables, unless HP can prove the cartridge caused the damage. In practice, GREENBOX's chip design is clean and shouldn't trigger any issues.

**Q: Do I need to change any printer settings?**
A: No. The printer recognizes these as standard 63XL cartridges. Just install and print.

**Q: How long do they last if I don't print often?**
A: The cartridges are sealed until you open them, so shelf life is solid. Once installed, expect about 6-8 months before the ink starts drying out in the print head — typical for any cartridge.
