You’ve got Starlink’s rectangular dish. Your Wi-Fi drops mid-video-call. You wonder if a $25 piece of plastic is the fix. Here’s the honest answer.

The Starlink Ethernet Adapter is a small, unassuming accessory that solves a big problem: the Gen 2 router has no ethernet port. It’s the only official way to get a wired connection from that dish. And for anyone running a home office, gaming setup, or smart home hub that demands stability, it’s less of a luxury and more of a necessity.

## What you actually get

The box contains the adapter itself — a small wedge-shaped unit with a proprietary connector on one end and a single RJ45 gigabit ethernet port on the other. There’s no cable included, so budget for a standard Cat5e or Cat6 patch cord. The housing is the same matte white plastic as the router, and it clicks in place with a firm, reassuring snap. It’s not a flimsy third-party knockoff; it feels like a genuine Starlink component, because it is.

## Real-world performance

Setup is genuinely plug-and-play. You pop the adapter onto the bottom of the router, plug your ethernet cable into it, and the router automatically detects it. No app settings, no reboots, no firmware gymnastics. Within thirty seconds, you’ve got a hardwired connection. That’s it.

In practice, the adapter simply passes through the same connection your dish receives. If your Starlink signal is solid, you’ll get full gigabit-capable speeds — though real-world throughput depends on your plan and local congestion. The real win is latency consistency. Wi-Fi introduces jitter, especially when the microwave runs or a neighbor’s network overlaps your channel. A wired connection eliminates that variance, which matters for VoIP calls, competitive gaming, or VPN-heavy workflows.

## Where it falls short

Let’s be blunt about the limitations. First, you only get one port. If you need to wire up a desktop, a printer, and a network-attached storage drive, you’ll need to buy a separate gigabit switch. That’s an extra purchase and an extra cable dangling off your router.

Second, this adapter is exclusively for the rectangular Gen 2 dish. If you have the newer Gen 3 router or the older round dish, this won’t fit. Starlink’s proprietary connector design means you can’t MacGyver a standard USB ethernet adapter — you’re locked into their ecosystem.

Third, there’s no passthrough power. The adapter sits between the router and its power source, but it doesn’t offer any additional ports or power delivery. It’s a single-purpose tool, and it knows it.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with the round Starlink dish?**
No. This is specifically for the rectangular Gen 2 dish. The round dish and Gen 3 router have different connectors.

**Q: Do I need to configure anything in the Starlink app?**
No. Plug it in, connect your cable, and it works immediately. The router auto-detects the adapter.

**Q: Can I connect multiple devices to this adapter?**
Only one directly. To connect more, you’ll need a network switch plugged into the adapter’s single ethernet port.
