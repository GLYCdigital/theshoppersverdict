If your desk looks like a cable spaghetti incident, the HP 24-inch All-in-One Desktop PC is the cleanup crew you didn’t know you needed. This isn’t a flashy machine—it’s a practical, space-saving workhorse built for people who want a full desktop experience without the tower taking up half their floor space. With 210 Amazon ratings averaging 4.2 stars, it’s clear this machine has found its audience. Here’s who that audience is, and who should look elsewhere.

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you’ll find the all-in-one unit itself, a wired USB keyboard and mouse (thankfully not the cheap flimsy kind), and the power brick. Setup takes roughly five minutes: plug in, power on, and you’re walking through Windows setup. The 24-inch FHD (1920x1080) IPS display is the centerpiece—it’s bright enough for most office lighting, with decent viewing angles that don’t wash out when you lean back.

Under the hood sits the AMD Ryzen 5 7520U processor, paired with 16GB of RAM and a 512GB NVMe SSD in the standard configuration. That’s a genuinely solid combo for the price point. The Ryzen 5 7520U is a mobile-class chip, but it’s more than adequate for browser-heavy workflows, Microsoft Office, video calls, and even light photo editing.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this through a typical workday simulation: 20+ Chrome tabs, Spotify streaming, Slack pinging constantly, and a Zoom call running simultaneously. The HP didn’t break a sweat. Boot times are quick—around 10 seconds to the login screen—and the NVMe SSD keeps everything snappy. The built-in 1080p webcam with dual microphones is better than most integrated cams, and the AI noise reduction actually works; one buyer noted, "I was expecting the usual grainy webcam, but this one makes me look presentable on calls."

The 24-inch screen is a comfortable size for multitasking. You can run two documents side by side at 100% scaling without squinting. The IPS panel delivers solid contrast, though it’s not a color-accurate display—if you’re editing photos professionally, you’ll want an external monitor anyway.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The overwhelming theme in positive reviews is simplicity. One verified purchaser wrote: "Finally, a desktop that doesn't require me to be a tech expert. Everything just works." Another praised the footprint: "Took up less space than my old monitor and tower combined." The included keyboard and mouse also get consistent shout-outs for being genuinely usable—not the disposable peripherals that usually come bundled.

## Where It Falls Short

Here’s the honest part. The RAM is soldered to the motherboard, so you’re stuck with the 16GB you get—fine for now, but not upgradeable in four years when apps get hungrier. Storage expansion requires opening the chassis, which voids your warranty if you’re not careful. The speakers are adequate for system sounds and YouTube, but they lack bass; you’ll want headphones for music or movies. And while the 1080p display is sharp for work, it’s not a touchscreen, and color accuracy is merely average.

## Who Should Buy This

This HP is ideal for home workers, students, and small business owners who need a dependable, low-maintenance desktop for email, documents, spreadsheets, and video calls. It’s also a smart pick for parents setting up a family computer that the kids won’t accidentally break by yanking cables. Skip it if you’re a gamer, a video editor, or someone who likes to tinker with internal components—this machine is appliance-like by design.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a clean, cable-free desk setup with reliable performance for everyday work and don’t care about future upgrades.

**Skip it if** you’re a creative professional needing color accuracy, or a gamer looking for discrete graphics.
