The Apple Pencil costs $129. This stylus runs about a quarter of that and claims compatibility with every iPad from 2018 through 2025. After testing it against a first-gen Apple Pencil on an iPad Air 5, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the pen, a USB-C charging cable, and a spare tip. No manual needed — it pairs automatically when you attach it magnetically to the iPad's side. The body is matte aluminum, slightly lighter than Apple's version at 13 grams. The flat edge prevents rolling off desks, a small detail that matters when you're sketching at a coffee shop.

**Performance where it counts**

Latency is the first thing you'll notice — or rather, won't. Writing in Notability feels immediate, with no perceptible lag between tip and screen. Palm rejection works correctly; resting your hand on the display while writing doesn't trigger stray marks. I tested this for an hour of note-taking and didn't get a single phantom line.

Tilt support functions as advertised. Shading in Procreate with the side of the tip produces natural-looking strokes, which surprised me at this price point. The 1.5mm tip diameter is slightly finer than Apple's, so handwriting looks a bit crisper.

Battery life is the standout feature. The 30-minute USB-C charge delivers roughly 10 hours of continuous use. In practice, I charged it Monday morning, used it for daily note-taking and sketching, and didn't need to recharge until Thursday. The LED indicator on the barrel shows green above 30%, red below — simple and effective.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects real satisfaction. One verified buyer wrote: "Bought this as a backup to my Apple Pencil and honestly use it more now. The battery lasts longer and it feels the same in hand." Another noted: "Works flawlessly with my 2022 iPad Pro. I was skeptical about the price difference but there's no practical downside for note-taking."

**Where it falls short — be honest with yourself**

If you're a digital artist, stop reading. This pen has no pressure sensitivity. The Apple Pencil detects how hard you press and varies line weight accordingly. This stylus can't. In Procreate, you'll need to manually adjust brush opacity or size, which kills creative flow. The reviews from artists reflect this — several note they returned it specifically for this reason.

The magnetic attachment also doesn't charge the pen. Apple Pencil 2 charges wirelessly when snapped to the iPad. This one requires the USB-C cable, which means carrying a cable if you travel. The double-tap gesture to switch between tools is also missing — you'll be reaching for the toolbar constantly.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students** taking lecture notes in Notability or GoodNotes who want reliable palm rejection without the Apple tax
- **Office workers** who annotate PDFs and sign documents daily
- **Casual sketchers** who don't need pressure sensitivity for doodles or diagrams
- **Anyone replacing a lost Apple Pencil** on a budget

**Skip it if** you're a professional illustrator, digital painter, or anyone whose work depends on pressure-sensitive strokes. Save for the real thing.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work with my iPad model?**
A: If your iPad is from 2018 or newer and supports the Apple Pencil (either generation), this works. That covers iPad Pro 2018+, iPad Air 4th gen+, iPad mini 6th gen+, and base iPad 6th gen+. Check your model's Apple Pencil compatibility to be sure.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: About 10 hours of active use. The 30-minute fast charge is accurate — I timed it at 28 minutes from empty to full.

**Q: Does it need Bluetooth pairing?**
A: No. It connects automatically when you attach it to the iPad's magnetic connector. No settings, no pairing menus.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a reliable iPad stylus for note-taking, PDF annotation, or casual sketching and don't want to spend Apple Pencil money. At roughly $25, it delivers 90% of the core functionality for 20% of the price.

**Skip it if** pressure sensitivity is non-negotiable for your work. This is a note-taking tool, not an artist's instrument.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — an exceptional value that nails the essentials, held back only by the missing pressure sensitivity and wireless charging.
