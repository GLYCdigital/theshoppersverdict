**What you actually get**

The listing offers both A4 (8.3" x 11.7") and A5 (5.8" x 8.3") versions. That's smart — the A5 fits in a tote or backpack, while the A4 lives on your desk as a command center. The "4 colors" refers to the paper itself, not the cover. You're choosing between different paper tints (typically white, cream, and other shades) to reduce glare or match your aesthetic. It's a small touch, but it matters if you're staring at these pages for hours.

Inside, you get the standard layout: monthly spreads, weekly pages, and note sections. It's undated, which is either a feature or a flaw depending on your style. If you're someone who abandons planners in February, undated means you can pick it back up in July without wasting pages. If you need to see the whole year at a glance, you'll be writing in numbers yourself.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The paper is the star here. Buyers repeatedly mention it handles wet ink without bleeding through. One reviewer noted, "My Pilot G2 doesn't ghost at all — that's rare at this price point." That aligns with the 4.6-star average across 229 ratings. The binding lays flat when open, which sounds minor until you've fought a spiral-bound planner that snaps shut on your hand.

Here's the honest downside: the cover is flexible cardstock, not a rigid hardcover. It's lighter and easier to carry, but don't expect it to survive a year stuffed in a backpack with a laptop. I'd recommend a cover sleeve if you're rough on your gear. Also, there's no built-in pen loop — a small omission that annoys more people than you'd think.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The color options are the headline feature, and they deliver. Multiple reviews mention being able to coordinate planners across home and office without looking mismatched. The size variety also gets consistent love — families buying both A4 for the desk and A5 for the purse. One parent wrote, "Finally found a planner my daughter and I can share layout-wise, just different sizes."

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the flexible cover and missing pen loop, the undated format will disappoint anyone who wants a rigid 2026 calendar structure. You're trading pre-printed dates for flexibility, and that's a real tradeoff. Also, the paper colors are subtle — don't expect vibrant pastels. These are professional tints, not Instagram-worthy hues.
