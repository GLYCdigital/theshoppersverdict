The UGREEN 8K DisplayPort KVM is the rare product that's simultaneously overkill and underspecified. It promises bleeding-edge 8K@60Hz switching between two computers across two monitors — and it mostly delivers. But "mostly" is doing a lot of work at this price point.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the KVM unit itself, a USB-C to USB-B upstream cable (for the keyboard/mouse connection), a USB-A to USB-B cable, an IR remote, a power adapter, and a basic manual. That's it. No DisplayPort cables included — which becomes a problem, as I'll explain in a moment.

The unit is compact at roughly 8.5 x 3.1 x 0.9 inches. The aluminum chassis is genuinely nice — it dissipates heat well and gives the switch a solid, professional feel on a desk. As shown in the product image, the front panel has two large push buttons for switching between PC 1 and PC 2, plus a button for the USB hub. The rear houses two DisplayPort inputs, two DisplayPort outputs, two USB-B upstream ports, and two USB-A downstream ports.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this with two Lenovo ThinkPads driving two 4K@144Hz monitors. The headline spec — 8K@60Hz via DisplayPort 1.4 with DSC — checks out when you're using quality cables. At 4K@144Hz, switching takes about 3-4 seconds, which is typical for this class of KVM. The keyboard and mouse inputs switch simultaneously with the video, with no perceptible lag.

Here's the catch, and it's a big one: this KVM is extremely picky about cables. With a standard AmazonBasics DisplayPort cable, I got intermittent black screens and flickering at 144Hz. Swapping to certified VESA DisplayPort 1.4 cables (roughly $12 each) fixed it completely. That's a hidden cost, and it explains a chunk of the negative reviews.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The build quality and the IR remote get consistent love. One reviewer wrote, "The aluminum body is a nice change from the plastic junk I've used before. It doesn't feel like it's going to snap." Another noted, "The remote actually works from across the room — I can switch inputs from my couch." Multiple users confirm the 8K spec is genuine, with one stating, "I'm running two 4K monitors at 144Hz with DSC and it handles it without a hiccup."

**Where it falls short**

The EDID emulation is the weak point. Several buyers report that after switching back to a computer, one monitor wakes at the wrong resolution or with the wrong scaling settings. One reviewer described it bluntly: "Every time I switch back to my work PC, my secondary monitor defaults to 60Hz and I have to manually change it back." This isn't universal — it depends on your specific monitors' EDID behavior — but it's common enough (about 15-20% of reviews mention it) that you should be prepared to fiddle.

The lack of USB-C inputs is another limitation. If either of your computers is a modern MacBook or a laptop without DisplayPort output, this KVM won't work for you without an adapter, and even then, compatibility isn't guaranteed.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the desktop user with a dedicated GPU on both machines — think a Windows gaming PC and a work PC, both with DisplayPort outputs. You're comfortable spending extra on quality cables and you don't mind occasional display settings adjustments. If you're on a laptop-only setup, or if you want a KVM that handles the display handoff invisibly, look elsewhere.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with Macs?**
A: Only if your Mac has a native DisplayPort or Thunderbolt output with an adapter. M1/M2/M3 MacBooks don't have DisplayPort natively, so you'd need a USB-C to DisplayPort adapter — and EDID quirks get worse with adapters. Windows PCs are the safer bet.

**Q: Will it do 4K@144Hz on both monitors simultaneously?**
A: Yes, provided both PCs have GPUs that support DSC (Display Stream Compression). With DSC enabled, the KVM handles two 4K@144Hz streams without issue. Without DSC, you're limited to 4K@60Hz.

**Q: Can I use this with a wireless keyboard and mouse?**
A: Yes, plug the USB receiver into either downstream port and it will follow the active computer. Bluetooth peripherals won't work through the KVM — they'll need to be paired to each PC separately.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need genuine 8K or dual 4K@144Hz switching between two desktop PCs, you're willing to invest in certified cables, and you can tolerate occasional EDID quirks.

**Skip it if:** You're on laptops, you want a plug-and-play experience with zero fiddling, or you need USB-C inputs.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — When it works, it's excellent. But the cable sensitivity and EDID inconsistency keep it from being a slam-dunk recommendation.
