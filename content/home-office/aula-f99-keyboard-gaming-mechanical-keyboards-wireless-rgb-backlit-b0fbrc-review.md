Let's cut through the noise: the Aula F99 is a 99-key wireless mechanical keyboard that's been quietly dominating budget-tier discussions, and with a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it's earning its reputation. But is it actually good, or just cheap enough that buyers forgive its flaws? I've spent three weeks with this board as my daily driver for both office work and evening gaming sessions. Here's the unfiltered truth.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the F99 keyboard itself, a coiled USB-C cable, a 2.4GHz wireless dongle, a keycap/switch puller, and a basic manual. Notably absent: spare switches. That's fine for most buyers, but if you're new to mechanical keyboards, know that the hot-swap sockets accept standard 3-pin and 5-pin switches — so the aftermarket is your friend when you want to experiment.

**Build Quality and Typing Feel**

The F99 uses a gasket-mounted design where the PCB sits sandwiched between silicone gaskets rather than screwed directly into the case. Combined with three layers of foam inside the chassis, this produces a typing experience that's genuinely soft and quiet — the sound is a muted "thock" rather than the hollow "clack" you'd expect at this price point. The switches (I got the linear red variant) are pre-lubed and smooth out of the box, with no noticeable spring ping or scratchiness. Stabilizers on the spacebar and shift keys come pre-lubed too, which eliminates the rattle that plagues many sub-$100 boards.

Battery life is a standout. The 4000mAh cell with RGB disabled lasted me three full weeks of 8-hour workdays plus evening gaming. With RGB on full brightness, expect closer to a week — still respectable. Charging via USB-C takes about three hours to top up.

**What Buyers Are Saying**

The consensus on Amazon mirrors my experience. One verified buyer notes: *"The gasket mount makes this feel like a $150 keyboard. I honestly didn't expect this quality at this price."* Another common thread in positive reviews is the connectivity reliability: *"Bluetooth pairs instantly, and I've had zero dropped connections across two devices."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Here's where you need to pay attention. The keycaps are ABS plastic, not the more durable PBT. That means after 4-6 months of heavy use, your WASD keys and spacebar will develop a visible glossy sheen from finger oils. It's cosmetic, not functional, but it bothers some people.

The companion software is also a weak point. It's Windows-only, and it's clunky. You can remap keys and adjust RGB per-key, but the interface feels dated and the settings occasionally fail to save after reboot. Mac users get no software support at all — basic typing works, but no customization.

Finally, the 2.4GHz dongle has no onboard storage slot. If you plan to carry this keyboard between home and office, you'll need a dedicated spot for that tiny receiver or you *will* lose it.

**Who Should Buy This**

The F99 is ideal for three buyer profiles: office workers who want a quiet, comfortable typing experience without paying premium prices; gamers who need a reliable wireless board with low-latency 2.4GHz mode and hot-swap capability; and keyboard tinkerers who want a solid base platform for switch and keycap experimentation.

Skip it if you're a Mac-only user who needs full software control, or if you demand PBT keycaps out of the box — you'll be replacing them eventually.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the 2.4GHz connection good for gaming?**
A: Yes. I measured no perceptible input lag in fast-paced shooters. The polling rate feels comparable to wired. Just remember to switch to wired mode when the battery drops below 20% — wireless performance degrades slightly at low charge.

**Q: Can I use this with a Mac?**
A: It works via Bluetooth and USB-C for basic typing. Function row shortcuts for brightness and volume work. But no software support means no remapping or RGB control on macOS.

**Q: How loud is it compared to a standard membrane keyboard?**
A: Quieter. The gasket mount and foam dampen sound significantly. Office colleagues won't complain. If you want truly silent operation, swap in silent tactile switches like Gateron Silent Browns.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a premium-feeling wireless mechanical keyboard with gasket mount, hot-swap switches, and all-day battery — without paying premium prices. **Skip it if** you're a Mac power user needing software control, or if ABS keycaps are a dealbreaker for you.

The Aula F99 delivers 90% of what a $150+ board offers at roughly half the price. The ABS keycaps and limited software are honest compromises, but they don't undermine the core experience. At 4.6/5, this is one of the best value mechanical keyboards available right now.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Highly recommended for budget-conscious shoppers who refuse to compromise on typing feel.
