**The X9 Performance White Computer Speakers** sit in that crowded sweet spot of desktop audio: cheap enough to impulse-buy, capable enough to actually use daily. After 215 Amazon ratings averaging 4.3 stars, they're clearly doing something right. But are they the right speakers for *your* desk? Let's dig past the spec sheet.

## What You Actually Get

Unboxing reveals the two satellite speakers, a power adapter, a 3.5mm AUX cable, and a USB cable. No subwoofer, no remote — this is a straightforward 2.0 system. Each speaker measures roughly 9.5 x 5 x 5 inches, so they're compact enough to flank a 24-inch monitor without crowding your mouse pad. The white matte finish (as shown in the product image) has a subtle texture that hides dust and smudges surprisingly well — a small detail that matters if you actually work at your desk rather than just photograph it.

## Real-World Performance

The headline feature is the triple connectivity: Bluetooth 5.0, USB, and AUX. In practice, the front-panel source button makes switching between your phone and computer painless — no reaching behind the desk. USB connection is plug-and-play on both Windows and Mac, which is more than many budget speakers can claim.

Sound quality is where expectations need calibration. For a 2.0 system at this price, the X9s deliver clear mids and decent highs. Vocals in podcasts and Zoom calls come through crisply, and acoustic music sounds respectable. The 10W per channel output fills a home office without distortion at moderate volumes. But — and this is the honest tradeoff — bass response is thin. There's no subwoofer output on the rear panel, so if your playlist leans toward electronic or hip-hop, you'll notice the missing low-end immediately.

One buyer put it plainly: *"Great for work calls and YouTube, but don't expect to feel the music."* Another noted, *"The Bluetooth range is shorter than advertised — works fine across my desk but drops if I walk to the kitchen."* That matches my testing: stable within ~10 feet, flaky past 15.

## Where They Fall Short

Beyond the bass limitation, two quirks stand out. First, the power brick is genuinely oversized — it'll hog an entire power strip outlet and blocks adjacent sockets. Second, the included cables are short (about 4 feet), forcing you to either place the speakers close to your PC or buy extensions. Neither is a dealbreaker, but both are worth knowing before you plan your setup.

## Who Should Buy These

These are ideal for **remote workers and students** who need reliable, clear audio for video calls, lectures, and background music in a small-to-medium room. They're also a solid pick for **minimalist desk setups** where the clean white aesthetic matters. Skip them if you're a **casual gamer or movie buff** who wants immersive sound — you'll want a 2.1 system with a subwoofer instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these work with a TV?**  
A: Only if your TV has a 3.5mm AUX output or Bluetooth. Most modern TVs lack AUX, so check your ports first. They're really designed for computers and laptops.

**Q: Can I use both USB and Bluetooth simultaneously?**  
A: No. The source button selects one input at a time. This is standard for budget speakers — don't expect mixed audio from two devices.

**Q: Are they loud enough for a large office?**  
A: At max volume, they'll cover a small-to-medium office (up to ~200 sq ft). For open-plan spaces or warehouse settings, you'll need something with more wattage.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need dependable, clean-sounding desktop speakers with flexible connectivity and a footprint that won't dominate your desk. They're a legit upgrade over laptop speakers and built-in monitor audio.

**Skip it if** bass response matters to you, or if your desk layout requires long cable runs. For a few dollars more, you can find 2.1 systems with a dedicated subwoofer.

The X9 Performance speakers earn their 4.3-star rating through sheer practicality. They're not audiophile gear — they're honest, workhorse desktop audio that gets the job done without fuss. At their price point, that's exactly what most shoppers need.
