**The USB-C hub market is a swamp of identical aluminum bricks.** This one stands out for one specific reason: the cable detaches magnetically from the hub body. That sounds like a gimmick until you realize how much it changes daily use.

## What You Actually Get

The hub itself is a compact aluminum block roughly the size of a credit card but thicker. Ports include:

- 1x HDMI (4K@60Hz)
- 2x USB-A 3.0 (5Gbps)
- 1x USB-C PD pass-through (up to 100W)
- 1x SD card slot
- 1x microSD slot
- 1x 3.5mm audio jack

The USB-C cable is detachable — about 6 inches long, braided, and it clicks into place with magnets. When connected to a MacBook, the hub body can sit flat on your desk while the cable snakes up to the port. That's easier on your MacBook's USB-C port than a rigid stick-style hub, which puts constant leverage on the connector.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this with a 2023 MacBook Pro M3, an iPad Pro M2, and an iPhone 15 Pro Max. The HDMI port pushed a 4K desktop at 60Hz to an external monitor without flicker or dropped frames over a two-hour session. That's rare at this price — most hubs in this range cap out at 4K@30Hz, which feels laggy for cursor movement and miserable for video editing.

Data transfer over the USB-A ports hit around 450MB/s with a Samsung T7 SSD using the included USB-C cable. The SD card slot was slower — about 80MB/s read. If you're dumping 64GB of RAW files from a Sony A7IV, that adds minutes to your workflow. Keep your dedicated card reader for that job.

The 100W PD pass-through worked as advertised. My MacBook Pro charged at full speed while the hub was pushing video to an external display. No heat issues — the aluminum shell stayed warm, not hot, after an hour of simultaneous charging and video output.

**The standout feature:** the magnetic cable separation. On an iPad Pro, you can dock the hub to the USB-C port, then attach the hub body to a magnetic stand or just let it hang. The cable pops off cleanly when you need to move. It's a small thing that makes the hub feel more like a tool than a dongle.

## Where It Falls Short

The SD card speed is the biggest compromise. If you're a photographer who regularly ingests RAW files, this hub will bottleneck you. It's fine for casual SD card reads — phone backups, drone footage, point-and-shoot JPEGs — but not for professional workflows.

The magnetic connection is a double-edged sword. It's strong enough for desk use, but if you toss the hub into a bag with the cable attached, a sharp tug can separate them. You won't lose the cable (it's short and stays with the hub), but you'll need to re-seat it.

The audio jack is a nice addition but outputs at a basic quality level. Fine for headphones, but don't expect to drive studio monitors through it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **MacBook owners** who want a hub that doesn't stress their ports and delivers reliable 4K@60Hz video
- **iPad Pro users** who want a detachable hub for desk setups without cable clutter
- **Travelers** who need one compact hub for multiple devices (MacBook, iPad, iPhone 15/16)

**Skip it if:** you're a professional photographer needing fast SD reads, or you need more than two USB-A ports simultaneously.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this hub work with the iPhone 15/16 series?**
Yes. It connects via USB-C and supports external display output to HDMI. You can also hook up a USB keyboard or storage drive. Note that iPhone video output tops out at 4K@60Hz, which this hub supports.

**Q: Can I charge my MacBook while using the hub?**
Yes. The USB-C PD pass-through supports up to 100W. Your MacBook will charge at full speed while the hub outputs video and data simultaneously.

**Q: Will this work with my Windows laptop?**
Yes, as long as your laptop has a USB-C port that supports DisplayPort Alt Mode. Some budget Windows laptops only support data over USB-C, so check your specs first.
