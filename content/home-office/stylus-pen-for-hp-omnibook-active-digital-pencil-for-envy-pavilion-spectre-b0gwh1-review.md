If you've priced HP's official rechargeable pen lately, you know the sting. This third-party active stylus for the Omnibook and Envy P line undercuts it significantly while claiming the same core specs. After testing it across note-taking, diagramming, and light sketching, here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the pen itself, one spare nib, and a short USB-C charging cable. No manual beyond a tiny quick-start card — which is fine, because pairing takes about three seconds. The barrel is aluminum with a matte finish, measuring 5.8 inches long and weighing 0.4 ounces. It feels balanced in hand, slightly lighter than a standard ballpoint but not cheap-light. The single side button sits where your thumb naturally rests and is configurable in Windows settings for right-click or eraser functions.

**Performance where it counts**

The 4096 levels of pressure sensitivity are the headline spec, and they deliver. In OneNote, my handwriting came out clean with consistent line weight — no skipping on fast strokes. Palm rejection worked flawlessly throughout testing; I rested my entire hand on the screen while writing for an hour straight and got zero phantom marks. Latency is imperceptible in everyday use, though you'll notice it slightly in fast sketching apps if you're pixel-peeping.

Battery life is genuinely impressive. A 30-minute USB-C charge gave me a full workday of intermittent note-taking — roughly eight hours — and the LED indicator turns red at 20 percent so you're not caught dead mid-meeting. The pen auto-sleeps after five minutes idle, which preserves juice but means a slight wake-up delay when you grab it again.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.3-star average across 215 ratings holds up. One reviewer wrote, "I was skeptical about a non-HP pen but this has been flawless for my Omnibook X360 — handwriting recognition in OneNote is spot-on." Another noted, "The battery life is the real winner. I charged it once on Monday and it lasted until Friday with daily use." Multiple reviewers confirm the palm rejection is the standout feature — several mention it works *better* than the OEM pen they replaced.

**Where it falls short**

No tilt support is the biggest limitation. If you're a digital artist who shades with angled strokes, this pen won't do it — lines stay uniform regardless of angle. That's a hard dealbreaker for illustrators but irrelevant for note-takers and annotators.

The magnetic attachment is weaker than OEM pens. It holds the pen to the side of your Omnibook for desk movement, but a bag with other laptops or cables will knock it loose. Also, the replacement nibs are a proprietary fit — you'll order them online rather than grabbing a pack at a local electronics store.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students and professionals** who take handwritten notes in OneNote, GoodNotes, or Nebo — this is your pen.
- **Office workers** who annotate PDFs, sign documents, or mark up presentations on an Omnibook or Envy P.
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who want near-OEM performance at a fraction of the price.

**Skip it if** you're a professional illustrator needing tilt shading, or if you need a pen that stays magnetically attached through rough travel.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this pen work with HP Pavilion and Spectre models?**
A: It works with any HP laptop or 2-in-1 that supports active stylus input via Microsoft Pen Protocol (MPP). That covers most Omnibook, Envy, Pavilion, and Spectre models from 2020 onward. Check your device specs for "active pen support" before buying.

**Q: How do I pair it?**
A: It's Bluetooth-paired once through Windows Settings > Devices. After that, it auto-connects. The side button functions require the Bluetooth connection; pressure sensitivity and writing work even without it.

**Q: Does it need a screen protector?**
A: No. The fine tip works directly on glass. A matte screen protector improves paper-like friction, but the standard tip glides smoothly on bare screens without scratching.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a reliable, long-lasting active stylus for everyday note-taking and document work on an HP 2-in-1. **Skip it if** tilt-sensitive drawing is non-negotiable or you need a rugged magnetic attachment.

This pen hits the sweet spot for 90 percent of users — solid build, excellent battery, and pressure sensitivity that punches above its price class. The missing tilt support is a real but narrow tradeoff. For the money, it's the best value active stylus for the Omnibook and Envy P line right now.
