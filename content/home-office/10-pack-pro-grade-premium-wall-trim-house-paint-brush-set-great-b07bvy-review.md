You're about to paint a room. You could grab that crusty brush from the garage — the one with paint fused into the ferrule — or you could spend $20ish on a ten-pack that actually gets the job done. This set from Pro-Grade sits squarely in the "just buy it" category for most DIYers, but it's not without quirks. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

Ten brushes in four sizes: one 1-inch, four 2-inch, two 3-inch, and three 4-inch. The mix leans heavily toward trim work — that's smart, because cutting in and edging are where most people struggle. The synthetic bristles are flagged as "pro-grade," which in practice means they're a medium-stiff nylon blend. Handles are unfinished beechwood, comfortable enough for a full afternoon of painting. As shown in the product image, the brushes arrive with a cardboard sleeve protecting the bristles — nice touch that prevents splaying in transit.

**Real-World Performance**

Here's where it gets interesting. The bristles are stiff. Not unusable stiff, but noticeably rigid on the first pass. One reviewer noted, "After a quick warm-water rinse and a few strokes on cardboard, they softened right up." That's the move — don't skip it. Once broken in, they lay down paint evenly with minimal streaking. The 2-inch brushes are the workhorses; they handle window trim, baseboards, and door frames with good control. The 4-inch brushes cover broad sections surprisingly well, though they're not a substitute for a roller on large walls.

Paint pickup is solid — a full dip loads enough for a clean 12-inch stroke before reloading. The bristles hold their shape through washing, which matters more than initial performance. Multiple reviewers mention completing entire rooms with one brush and seeing zero shedding. That's the durability metric that counts.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The value proposition is the headline. "I've paid more for a single brush at the hardware store," one verified buyer wrote. "This set covers every size I need for a whole house repaint." Another reviewer highlighted the lack of shedding: "Used the 2-inch for all my trim — not a single bristle came loose." The handle comfort gets consistent love too, with several people noting they painted for hours without hand cramps.

**Where It Falls Short**

The stiffness issue is real. If you're someone who grabs a brush and expects perfection immediately, you'll be frustrated. Plan a five-minute prep session. The brushes also don't come with any cleaning tool — a minor annoyance since dried paint will ruin them quickly. And while the wooden handles are comfortable, they're unfinished, so they'll absorb paint splatters and look rough after a few uses. Cosmetic, but worth knowing.

One other thing: the bristle tips aren't as fine as high-end specialty brushes. For razor-sharp lines on detailed crown molding, you'll want a dedicated angled sash brush. For everything else, this set handles it.

**Who Should Buy This**

Three buyer personas fit this set well:

1. **The weekend warrior** — painting a bedroom or two, needs multiple sizes without buying singles at $8+ each
2. **The rental flipper** — touching up walls between tenants, wants disposable-but-durable brushes that won't break the bank
3. **The first-time homeowner** — building a basic tool kit, this covers all painting bases for less than a single pro brush

Skip it if you're a professional painter who needs precision-cut bristles for fine detail work. You'll notice the difference.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these brushes washable and reusable?**
A: Yes, with proper care. Rinse immediately after use with warm water and mild soap. The synthetic bristles hold up through multiple wash cycles — several reviewers report using the same brush for multiple rooms and even multiple projects.

**Q: What kind of paint works best with these?**
A: Latex and acrylic paints work best. The synthetic bristles are water-based paint compatible. Avoid oil-based paints — they'll be harder to clean out and will shorten the brush's lifespan.

**Q: Do the brushes shed bristles during painting?**
A: After the initial break-in, no. That's one of the most consistent positives in the reviews. A few loose bristles on the first use is normal; if shedding continues after that, you got a defective unit.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a DIYer tackling a painting project and want a full range of sizes without paying retail prices per brush. The value is undeniable — ten brushes for roughly the cost of two singles at a hardware store.

**Skip it if** you're a professional needing fine-tipped precision brushes for detailed work, or if you hate any prep time before painting.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the break-in stiffness and lack of cleaning tools. Everything else delivers exactly what's promised.
