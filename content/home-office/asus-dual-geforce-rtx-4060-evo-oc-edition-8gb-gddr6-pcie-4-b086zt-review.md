If you're building a 1080p gaming rig or upgrading an aging GTX 1060 or RTX 2060 system, the ASUS Dual GeForce RTX 4060 EVO OC Edition is the card that makes the most sense on paper right now. It's also the card that makes the most sense in practice — with one significant caveat.

I've spent two weeks hammering this GPU through benchmarks, ray-traced titles, and long productivity sessions. Here's what you actually need to know before spending your money.

## What's in the Box and What You're Actually Getting

The retail package is refreshingly simple: the card itself, a quick-start guide, and a support bracket that you'll likely never use. No RGB hub, no extra cables, no bloatware USB drive. ASUS kept it clean.

The card measures 267mm long and occupies 2.5 slots — compact enough for the vast majority of mid-tower cases. The dual axial-tech fans spin in opposite directions to reduce turbulence, and they completely stop below 50°C. In an office or a quiet living room, this card is dead silent at idle.

Under the hood, you're getting the AD107 chip with 3,072 CUDA cores, 8GB of GDDR6 on a 128-bit bus, and a boost clock of 2,505MHz in OC mode. The 8-pin power connector is the standout practical feature — it draws just 115W under gaming load, which means any 550W power supply from the last decade will handle it without breaking a sweat.

## Performance and Real-World Use

At 1080p, this card is an absolute monster. Cyberpunk 2077 with Ray Tracing: Ultra and DLSS 3.5 hits 90-100fps consistently. Call of Duty: Warzone runs at 140-160fps with competitive settings. Even demanding sim titles like Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 stay above 60fps with DLSS Frame Generation enabled.

The DLSS 3 Frame Generation technology is the real differentiator here. Games that struggle to hit 60fps natively can easily double their frame rates with Frame Gen enabled, and the visual artifacts are minimal in most titles. This is the primary reason to choose the RTX 4060 over a similarly-priced Radeon RX 7600.

However, the 8GB VRAM is the elephant in the room. At 1080p with high texture settings, most 2025-2026 releases consume 7-8GB. Games like Ratchet & Clank: Rift Apart and Hogwarts Legacy can exceed 8GB at max settings, causing stutters and texture pop-in. At 1440p, you'll need to dial textures down to High or Medium in the most demanding titles.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.7/5 rating at 235 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. One verified buyer noted: "Upgraded from a 2060 and the difference is night and day. Runs cool and quiet even after hours of gaming." ## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the VRAM limitation, there are two honest criticisms worth mentioning. First, the OC mode is practically meaningless — the factory overclock adds just 30MHz over reference clocks, which translates to a 1-2% real-world performance gain. You're paying for the name, not the performance.

Second, the card's 128-bit memory bus means bandwidth is limited. In memory-heavy workloads like 4K video editing or AI image generation, it falls behind cards with wider buses. This is strictly a gaming and light-creation card, not a workstation GPU.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're gaming at 1080p, upgrading from a GTX 10-series or RTX 20-series card, have a 500-650W power supply, or want whisper-quiet operation in a bedroom or office PC.

**Skip it if:** You play at 1440p or 4K, do heavy 3D rendering or video work, or want to keep your next GPU for 5+ years without upgrading. In those cases, stretch to a 4070 or look at the used market for 3080-class cards.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this card support DLSS 3 Frame Generation?**
A: Yes. It fully supports DLSS 3, including Frame Generation, which is the main feature that makes it competitive with more expensive cards.

**Q: Will it fit in my small form factor case?**
A: It's 267mm long and 2.5 slots thick. Check your case's GPU clearance — most mid-towers and some mini-ITX cases will accommodate it. The dual fans are more compact than triple-fan designs.

**Q: Is 8GB VRAM enough for gaming in 2026?**
A: For 1080p, yes — with the caveat that you'll need to drop textures to High (not Ultra) in the most demanding 2025-2026 titles. For 1440p, it's borderline. If you plan to play at 1440p, consider the 12GB RTX 4070 instead.
