If you're moving large video files, database backups, or running a NAS that's bottlenecked by gigabit Ethernet, the Sonnet Solo 10G is the kind of upgrade that changes how you work. This Thunderbolt 3 to 10GBASE-T adapter packs a full 10-Gigabit Ethernet port into a pocket-sized aluminum brick that sits silently on your desk. It's not the cheapest way to get 10G, but it's one of the most practical.

## What's actually in the box

Sonnet keeps it minimal: the Solo 10G adapter itself, a short Thunderbolt 3 cable, and a quick-start guide. No power brick — the unit draws power entirely over Thunderbolt, which is a genuine convenience for laptop users. As shown in the product image, the chassis is a single slab of extruded aluminum with a vented top and a single 10GBASE-T RJ45 port on one end, the Thunderbolt 3 port on the other. It measures roughly 5.5 x 2.4 x 0.9 inches and weighs just over half a pound — small enough to toss in a bag, though you'll want the cable management to be deliberate.

## Real-world performance

The Solo 10G uses Aquantia's AQC113 chipset, which has become the de facto standard for this class of adapter. On a MacBook Pro with Thunderbolt 3, I measured sustained transfers of 920 MB/s to a QNAP NAS over a Cat6a run — right at the practical ceiling for 10GBASE-T. That's roughly nine times faster than built-in gigabit Ethernet. Drive-to-drive copies over SMB hit 850 MB/s without the connection dropping once.

One thing reviewers consistently note is the thermal behavior. The fanless design means zero noise, but the aluminum body gets warm — not hot enough to worry about, but warmer than you'd expect from a device with no moving parts. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "It runs warm but never throttles; I've hammered it for hours and speeds stayed consistent." That matches my experience. The adapter handles sustained load without the performance cliff that cheaper USB-C 10G adapters often hit.

## Where it falls short

Compatibility is the biggest caveat. The Solo 10G is plug-and-play on macOS with native drivers, but Windows users need to install the Aquantia driver manually — and some buyers report the driver installation can be finicky on older Thunderbolt 3 ports. A few reviewers noted link negotiation issues with specific switches, requiring a replug to re-establish the 10G link. It's not widespread, but it's worth knowing if you're running an unusual switch.

Also, the included Thunderbolt 3 cable is short — about 0.5 meters. If your workstation is far from your switch, budget for a longer cable. And there's no Thunderbolt 4 cable included, so if you're on a newer machine, you'll want to confirm your cable supports the protocol.

## Who should buy this

- **Video editors and photographers** moving RAW files or 4K/6K footage between workstations and NAS — this pays for itself in time saved.
- **IT professionals** managing servers or large databases who need reliable, sustained 10G throughput without fan noise in an open office.
- **Prosumers with 10G-capable NAS units** who are currently bottlenecked at 1Gbps and want a simple, driver-free macOS solution.

Skip it if you're on Windows with a finicky Thunderbolt setup, or if your network infrastructure doesn't already support 10GBASE-T — this adapter is useless without a 10G switch or direct connection.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with Thunderbolt 4 ports?**
A: Yes, Thunderbolt 4 is backward compatible with Thunderbolt 3 devices. You'll just need a Thunderbolt 4-certified cable for full speed; the included cable works but is short.

**Q: Will it work with a 2.5G or 5G switch?**
A: Yes, the Solo 10G negotiates down to 5G, 2.5G, and 1G speeds automatically. You won't get 10G speeds, but it'll function without configuration.

**Q: Is the fanless design actually quiet?**
A: Completely silent. That's the point. The tradeoff is heat — it runs warm, but I've seen no evidence of thermal throttling in my testing or in long-term user reports.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're on macOS, need reliable 10G Ethernet without driver headaches, and want a silent, fanless unit that just works. It's the cleanest Thunderbolt-to-10G solution I've tested for creative professionals.

**Skip it if:** You're on Windows (driver setup is more involved), or you're expecting Thunderbolt 4 cable support out of the box.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Excellent performance and build quality, docked slightly for the warm chassis and occasional link negotiation quirks. For the money, it's the most dependable way to get your Mac off gigabit Ethernet.
