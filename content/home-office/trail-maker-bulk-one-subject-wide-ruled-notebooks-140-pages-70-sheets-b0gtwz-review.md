**The 140-Page Notebook That Makes You Question Why You Settled for Less**

Most one-subject notebooks cap out around 60-80 pages. **What You Actually Get**

The bulk pack ships with 5 notebooks, each measuring 8" x 10.5" — slightly larger than standard letter-size notebooks, giving you more writing surface per page. Each notebook contains 70 sheets (140 pages) of wide-ruled paper with 8.7mm line spacing. That's the classic spacing teachers and students expect, not the cramped 7mm "wide" you find on some budget brands.

The covers are 5mm polypropylene — flexible but tough. They're water-resistant, which is a practical upgrade from the thin cardboard most notebooks use. The twin-wire binding lies flat when open, which matters if you're writing near the spine or photocopying pages.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The paper is 50lb/75gsm. That's standard for school-grade notebooks, not premium. Ballpoint pens and standard gel pens (0.5mm-0.7mm) write cleanly with minimal bleed-through. But if you're a heavy-handed writer using 1.0mm gel pens or Sharpie pens, expect ghosting on the back of pages. Markers and highlighters? Don't even try — they'll bleed through.

The perforations are the standout feature. They tear out cleanly along a straight line, leaving a neat edge rather than the ragged fringe you get on cheaper notebooks. Multiple buyers mentioned this specifically, with one noting, "The pages tear out perfectly every time — no jagged edges, no half-torn sheets."

The wire binding is functional but not flawless. Several reviewers noted the coils can catch on backpack zippers or bag liners during transit. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you're tossing these loose into a bag.

**Where It Falls Short**

- **No cover reinforcement** — The polypropylene is tough, but there's no closure band or elastic. Pages can get dog-eared if you're rough on your stuff.
- **Bulk pricing** — You're buying 5 at once. If you only need a single notebook, this isn't the listing for you — though per-unit cost makes it hard to beat for teachers or students.
