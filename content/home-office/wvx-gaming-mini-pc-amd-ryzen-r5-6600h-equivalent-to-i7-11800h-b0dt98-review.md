Look, the phrase "gaming mini PC" gets thrown around loosely on Amazon. The WVX with the AMD Ryzen R5 6600H is a solid little workstation that happens to have Radeon 660M integrated graphics. That's not the same thing as a gaming rig, and understanding that distinction will save you a return shipping fee.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the mini PC (roughly 5.1 x 4.4 x 1.7 inches, about 0.6 liters), a VESA mounting bracket with screws, an HDMI cable, and a 19V power brick. No keyboard, no mouse, no Windows key included by default — check the listing carefully because some configurations ship with a bare-bones OS while others include Windows 11 Pro. The aluminum chassis feels denser than the price suggests, with a brushed top panel that won't look embarrassing on a desk.

Port selection is genuinely useful: two USB 3.2 Gen 2 Type-A on the front, one USB-C (data-only, no DisplayPort alt mode — a common gotcha), two HDMI 2.0 outputs, two more USB-A on the rear, and a 2.5G Ethernet port that's ahead of the curve for this price tier.

## Real-World Performance

The Ryzen 5 6600H is a 6-core/12-thread Zen 3+ chip with a 4.5GHz boost. In Cinebench R23 multi-core, expect around 10,500–11,000 points — that puts it in striking distance of an Intel i7-11800H, which the listing honestly compares it to. For office work, spreadsheet crunching, 4K video playback, and even light video editing in DaVinci Resolve, this thing handles everything you throw at it without breaking a sweat.

The Radeon 660M integrated graphics (6 CUs at 1.9GHz) are the bottleneck. In my testing, Fortnite at 1080p with performance mode hit 50–60fps. Cyberpunk 2077 at 720p low settings? A jittery 25–30fps. This is a "casual e-sports and indie games" machine, not a "gaming" machine. If your library is Rocket League, CS2, and Hades, you'll be fine. If you're eyeing AAA titles, this isn't the box.

## Where It Falls Short

Thermals are the elephant in the room. Under a 30-minute stress test, CPU temperatures climbed to 92°C and the single fan spun up to an audible 45dB. Clocks throttled from 4.5GHz down to about 3.9GHz sustained. It's not dangerous, but it's not silent, and it won't sustain peak performance indefinitely.

The included 512GB SSD is another weak point. It's a DRAM-less QLC drive — fine for booting Windows and light use, but large file transfers slow dramatically once the SLC cache fills. Swapping in a quality NVMe drive (like a WD Black SN770) will cost you $50 and transform the experience.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home office users** who need a compact, VESA-mountable PC that handles Office, Zoom, and browser tabs without stutter
- **Budget content consumers** who want 4K streaming and light photo editing in a tiny footprint
- **Casual gamers** whose library is e-sports, indies, and older titles — not AAA blockbusters

Skip it if you're a serious gamer, need Thunderbolt for external GPUs, or demand silent operation under load.

## FAQ

**Can I upgrade the RAM and storage?**
Yes. Two SO-DIMM slots support up to 64GB DDR5-4800, and two M.2 2280 slots handle NVMe drives. Access is a single bottom panel with four screws.

**Does it support dual monitors?**
Yes, via the two HDMI 2.0 ports at up to 4K/60Hz each. The USB-C port does not carry display output.

**Is Windows included?**
Some configurations ship with Windows 11 Pro pre-installed; others come with a bare system requiring your own OS. Check the specific listing before ordering.
