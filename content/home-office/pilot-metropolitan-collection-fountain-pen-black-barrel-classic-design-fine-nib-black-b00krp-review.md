The $30 fountain pen that outperforms pens costing three times as much. That's the reputation the Pilot Metropolitan has earned, and after putting the black barrel Classic Design through weeks of daily use, I understand why. This isn't just a gateway drug into fountain pens—it's a legitimate daily writer that belongs on any desk, regardless of your experience level.

## What's in the box

Pilot ships the Metropolitan with everything you need to start writing immediately: the pen itself, one black ink cartridge pre-installed, a spare cartridge, and a piston-style squeeze converter for bottled ink. The brushed black lacquer over brass barrel looks understated and professional—no tacky branding, just a subtle "PILOT" engraving near the clip. At 5.4 inches capped and 0.9 ounces, it's compact enough for a shirt pocket but substantial enough to feel like a real instrument.

## Performance and build quality

The Metropolitan's secret is its construction. Most pens at this price point use lightweight plastic or aluminum. Pilot uses solid brass with a lacquer finish, which gives it a heft that feels deliberate and expensive. The snap cap closes with a satisfying click and posts securely on the back—no wobble, no risk of it sliding off mid-sentence.

The steel nib (available in fine or medium) is where the real magic happens. Pilot's nibs are notoriously smooth, and the Metropolitan's is no exception. It writes with a consistent, reliable line that starts instantly—even after leaving it uncapped for a minute or two, which is rare for fountain pens in this class. The fine nib produces a line roughly equivalent to a 0.5mm gel pen, making it ideal for everyday note-taking and journaling.

One thing to know: the nib runs dry initially. Expect to write a few pages before the ink flow loosens up to its full potential. This isn't a defect—it's Pilot's feed system settling in. Give it time.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects genuine enthusiasm. One reviewer noted, "This pen writes smoother than my Lamy Safari and my TWSBI Eco, which both cost more." Another called it "the best value in fountain pens, period." The consistent themes: the weight feels premium, the nib is smooth, and it converts from cartridge to converter without mess.

## Where it falls short

The weight is the double-edged sword. At 0.9 ounces, it's noticeably heavier than plastic pens like the Lamy Safari (0.6 ounces). For quick notes or short writing sessions, that heft feels great. But marathon journaling sessions will fatigue your hand faster than a lighter pen would. I found myself switching to a lighter pen for anything over three pages.

The fine nib's dry start is another minor annoyance. If you're used to the wet, gushing flow of a German nib, the Metropolitan will feel restrained at first. It evens out, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

## Who should buy this

- **Fountain pen beginners**: The Metropolitan is the standard recommendation for a reason. It's affordable, reliable, and forgiving of mistakes.
- **Office workers**: The understated black barrel won't draw attention, and the smooth nib makes long meetings more bearable.
- **Journalers**: The fine nib works well on standard notebook paper without excessive feathering or bleed-through.
- **Anyone who wants to test the fountain pen waters** without committing to a $100+ pen.

Skip it if you need a feather-light pen for marathon writing sessions or prefer a wet, gushing line style.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it come with an ink converter?**
A: Yes—a squeeze-style converter is included in the box, along with two black ink cartridges. You can use bottled ink immediately without buying anything extra.

**Q: Is the fine nib really fine?**
A: Yes, it's comparable to a 0.5mm gel pen. If you prefer a bolder line, order the medium nib version. The fine is ideal for small handwriting and everyday notes.

**Q: Can I use standard Pilot cartridges with this pen?**
A: Yes, it uses Pilot's proprietary cartridge format. They're widely available and come in a variety of colors. Just avoid international standard cartridges—they won't fit.
