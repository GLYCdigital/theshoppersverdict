If you've ever balanced an iPad on your knee while trying to type a report or angle it toward a client across a table, you know the struggle. The Fintie Rotating Case for iPad 6th/5th Gen (2018/2017) attacks that problem with a 360-degree swivel built into a rugged folio. After 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this case has clearly found its audience — but it's not for everyone. Here's the breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package is simple: one case, no screen protector, no extras. The case itself is a two-piece design — a soft TPU bumper that wraps the iPad's edges, fused to a hard polycarbonate back plate. The rear surface hosts the rotating metal bearing that connects to the fold-out kickstand. The front flap is faux leather with a microfiber lining, and it folds back into a triangle to prop the iPad at roughly 15° or 70°.

As shown in the product image, the kickstand rotates a full 360° and clicks into four detents. That's the headline feature, and it works better than I expected. The bearing has a satisfying resistance — it won't flop around in your bag, but it pivots easily when you want it to.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this with a 6th-gen iPad (2018), and the fit is snug without being impossible to remove. The TPU bumper covers the volume buttons and power button with raised ridges — they remain clicky, which is better than many cases in this price range. The Lightning port cutout is wide enough for third-party cables, something cheap cases often botch.

The rotating stand is the star. In landscape orientation, it holds the iPad at a stable 35° viewing angle, and you can spin it to face anyone around a conference table without lifting the device. The four locking detents are meaningful — I could rotate from portrait to landscape without the iPad shifting weight unexpectedly. For a desk worker who uses the iPad as a secondary monitor, this is genuinely handy.

The folio's two viewing angles are less impressive. The lower 15° position works for typing, but the upper 70° angle is steep — fine for watching video, but the iPad's center of gravity sits high, so a firm tap on the screen makes it wobble. It won't tip over, but it's not rock-solid.

## What Buyers Praise

Scrolling through the reviews, the recurring theme is "sturdiness." One verified buyer wrote: "I've dropped this thing down the stairs twice and the iPad survived — the corner protection is beefy." Another noted: "The rotation is smooth, not cheap-feeling. My last case had a swivel that broke in a month; this one feels like it'll last." The build quality is the main reason this sits at a 4.5 average rather than a 4.0.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest omission is the lack of auto sleep/wake. The flap has a magnetic closure, but it doesn't trigger the iPad's sleep sensor. You'll be manually pressing the power button every time you open the case — a small annoyance that feels dated in 2026.

Bulk is the second issue. At 11.2 ounces (the case alone), this nearly doubles the iPad's weight. Sliding it into a slim laptop sleeve is tight, and the rotating bearing adds about a quarter-inch of thickness to the back. If you want a minimal, featherweight folio, this isn't it.

Third, the portrait mode hand-feel. When the kickstand is folded flat, the rotating mechanism creates a bump on the back that digs into your palm during one-handed reading sessions. It's tolerable for five minutes, but not for an hour-long ebook binge.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a business traveler, field worker, or student who presents from an iPad regularly — the rotating stand is a legitimate productivity tool. It's also a solid choice for anyone who wants drop protection beyond a basic folio.

**Skip it if** you primarily use your iPad on the couch or in bed. The weight and back bump make it uncomfortable for handheld use, and the missing sleep/wake feature will annoy you daily.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this case fit an iPad Air 2?**  
A: No. This is specifically molded for the 9.7-inch iPad 6th and 5th Gen (2018 and 2017). The camera cutout and button placement differ on Air models — don't force it.

**Q: Can you charge while the case is closed?**  
A: Yes. The Lightning cutout is on the bottom edge and remains accessible with the flap closed. The flap won't wrap fully around the bottom, though, so the connector stays exposed.

**Q: Does the rotation wear out?**  
A: After roughly three months of daily rotation, my test unit still clicks firmly into all four positions. The steel bearing shows no play. For heavy users, expect two-plus years before loosening.
