## What's Actually in the Box

You get 100 white PVC CR80 cards, standard credit-card size (3.375" x 2.125", 0.03" thick). They're blank on both sides with a matte finish designed for direct-to-card printing (Zebra, Fargo, or Magicard units). Each card contains an internal 26-bit Weigand proximity chip — no magnetic stripe, no smart chip, just the standard 125kHz proximity format that's been the industry workhorse for decades.

The "blank" designation means two things: no pre-printed graphics and no pre-encoded facility codes. You're responsible for both.

## Performance: The Chip Inside Matters

The 125kHz proximity chips in these cards are compatible with virtually every major access control reader that accepts 26-bit Weigand — HID, Paxton, Honeywell, and most generic Chinese readers. Buyers consistently confirm this:

> "Worked perfectly with our Paxton Net2 system. Encoded them with our existing programmer and they read instantly at every door."

Another verified buyer noted:

> "Half the price of the 'brand name' cards and I honestly can't tell the difference in read range or reliability."

Read range is typically 2–4 inches depending on your reader, which is standard for proximity cards. The chip itself is solid — no reports of premature failure or dead cards in the review data.

## Build Quality and Printing

The PVC construction is rigid enough for daily use but not indestructible. The matte surface takes direct card printing cleanly — colors come out crisp and the finish doesn't smear or peel during lamination. One caveat: the white surface is a fingerprint magnet and shows edge scuffs quickly. If your team keeps cards loose in pockets with keys, expect visible wear within a few months. Card sleeves or lanyard holders mitigate this.

For printing, these cards work with both monochrome and full-color direct card printers. Buyers report no feeding issues or jams, which is notable since cheap cards often have rough edges that snag printer rollers.

## Where It Falls Short

**The encoding requirement is the biggest gotcha.** If you don't already own a 26-bit proximity card programmer (like an HID 6010 or a generic USB writer), factor that cost into your budget. You cannot just wave these at your reader and expect them to work — they're genuinely blank.

Also, the 26-bit format is the *lowest* security tier in proximity access control. If your facility requires high-security 37-bit or MIFARE DESFire, these won't work. This is a non-issue for most small businesses, but worth stating plainly.

Finally, no bulk discount at higher quantities. The per-card price holds at 100, 200, or 500. If you need 500+, shop around for volume pricing elsewhere.

## FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I print on both sides?**
Yes. The matte laminate accepts direct printing on both sides. Just flip the card in your printer's duplex mode.

**Q: Will these work with HID readers?**
Yes, if your HID reader is configured for 26-bit Weigand (most are by default). They won't work with HID's newer iCLASS SE or Signo readers that require SEOS or MIFARE.

**Q: Do I need a special programmer?**
Any standard 125kHz prox card programmer that supports 26-bit format will work. The HID 6010B is the most common, but generic USB writers from Amazon at $40–$80 also handle these fine.
