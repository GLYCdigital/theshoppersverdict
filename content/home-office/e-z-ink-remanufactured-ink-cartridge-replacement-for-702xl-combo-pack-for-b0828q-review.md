**Where It Falls Short**

Not everything is rosy. The most consistent complaint involves ink level monitoring. Several buyers reported the printer showing full ink levels for weeks, then jumping to empty without warning. One frustrated review stated: *"The chip said 100% full, then suddenly my print jobs came out faded. Replaced it and the new one showed 100% again. The readouts are basically fiction."* This is a known limitation of remanufactured chips — they often can't accurately track remaining ink.

Color accuracy is another tradeoff. The cyan leans slightly cooler and more vivid than HP's OEM formulation. For business graphics and charts, this is fine — arguably even punchier. But if you're printing photographs, expect a subtle shift in color balance that requires profile adjustment.

Finally, there's no expiration or production date printed on the packaging. If you buy in bulk and store cartridges for six-plus months, you risk dried print heads or clogged nozzles.

**FAQ**

**Will this void my HP printer warranty?**
No. In the U.S., the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act prevents manufacturers from voiding warranties solely for using third-party consumables. HP must prove the cartridge caused the damage, which is rare.

**Does this work with HP Instant Ink?**
No. If you're enrolled in HP's Instant Ink subscription, you must use HP-branded cartridges. This cartridge is for standard, non-subscription printing only.

**How long does installation take?**
Under two minutes. Remove the orange protective cap, insert into the cyan slot, close the door. Most printers recognize it immediately with no setup prompts.
