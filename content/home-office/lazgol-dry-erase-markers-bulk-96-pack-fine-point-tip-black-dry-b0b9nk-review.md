Let me be blunt: 96 black dry erase markers is a lot of commitment. But if you're running a classroom, a busy office, or a church that burns through whiteboard pens like they're candy, this LAZGOL bulk pack might be the smartest office supply purchase you make this year.

## What You're Actually Getting

Open the box and you'll find 96 fine-point markers with black ink, packaged in a simple cardboard sleeve. No fancy dispenser, no color variety — just a brick of workhorse markers. The barrels are standard-length matte plastic with a color-coded cap and tip indicator, so you can spot them in a drawer at a glance. The fine point is genuinely fine — roughly 1.5mm — which makes it ideal for detailed notes, calendar grids, and small text on meeting room boards.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these on three surfaces: a porcelain whiteboard, a glass partition, and a cheap melamine board from a big-box store. On the porcelain and glass, the ink flowed smoothly from the first stroke. No dry starts, no skipping, no squeaking. The low-odor formula is a genuine relief — you can stand at the board for an hour without that chemical headache cheap markers cause.

The fine point holds its shape. After writing an entire meeting agenda and leaving it for 48 hours, the ink wiped off with a dry microfiber cloth leaving zero ghosting. That's the test that separates decent markers from good ones, and LAZGOL passes.

On the melamine board, results were less impressive — the ink soaked into the surface slightly and required a damp cloth plus some elbow grease to fully remove. If your workspace uses those budget boards, you'll want to test one marker first before committing to the full 96.

## The Honest Tradeoffs

The fine point is excellent for precision work, but if you're doing large-format brainstorming on a big whiteboard, you'll want a chisel tip for readability across a room.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these work on glass surfaces?**
A: Yes, they write smoothly on glass and erase cleanly with a dry cloth. Just avoid using them on glass that gets wet — the ink can smear before it dries.

**Q: How long does one marker last?**
A: Under normal use — writing a few pages of notes daily — expect 2-3 weeks per marker. Heavy classroom use shortens that to about a week. Either way, 96 markers is months of supply.

**Q: Are they really low-odor?**
A: Yes, the alcohol-based formula has a mild scent that dissipates quickly. You won't smell them across a room, but they're not completely odorless. If you're extremely sensitive to marker fumes, test one before committing.
