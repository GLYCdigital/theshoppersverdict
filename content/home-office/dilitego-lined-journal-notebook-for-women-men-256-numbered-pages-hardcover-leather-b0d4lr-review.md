Let me be blunt: most journals in this price range are either pretty but useless (paper so thin your pen bleeds through four pages) or functional but ugly. The dilitego Lined Journal actually tries to be both, and for the most part, it pulls it off.

**What you actually get**

Out of the box: a hardcover journal with a faux-leather exterior, 256 numbered pages, an elastic closure, one ribbon bookmark, and a back pocket for loose notes. The dimensions are roughly 8.5 x 5.7 inches — slightly larger than a standard A5, so it fits in most bags but won't disappear in a purse. The cover has a subtle texture that resists fingerprints, which is more than I can say for my last three journals.

**The paper is the real story**

The 100gsm paper is the standout feature. I tested it with a Pilot G2, a Lamy Safari fountain pen with medium nib, and a Sharpie pen. Zero bleed-through. Zero ghosting. That's uncommon even in journals twice this price. The lined pages are 7mm apart — comfortable for most handwriting sizes, though if you write large, you might find it cramped.

**Numbered pages done right**

The numbering isn't just decorative. There's a two-page index at the front, so you can actually use this as a working notebook. For bullet journalers, this is huge — you can create your own key and reference it. For writers, it means you can finally say "see page 142" without guessing. The binding lays completely flat, which makes writing near the spine painless.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 240 ratings, the pattern is clear: people love the paper quality and the structure. One reviewer wrote, "I've bought Moleskine and Leuchtturm and this holds up better for half the price. The pages don't bleed and the cover doesn't scuff." Another noted, "Finally a journal that doesn't fall apart in my bag after two weeks." The faux-leather cover holds up well to daily abuse — one reviewer mentioned dropping it in a puddle and the pages survived intact.

**Where it falls short**

The elastic band is the main complaint. It's snug when the book is new, but as you add pages, it gets tighter — one reviewer said it "stretched the cover slightly" after filling half the journal. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

The single ribbon bookmark is an odd choice given the indexed pages. You'll want to mark both your index and your current page, and one ribbon forces you to choose. Minor, but annoying.

And despite the product images suggesting a pen loop on the spine, there isn't one. You'll need a clip or a separate pen holder.

**Who should buy this**

- **Bullet journalers** who want structure without buying a dedicated planner. The index and numbering replace the need for a separate system.
- **Students** taking lecture notes that need to be referenced later. The paper handles highlighters without bleeding.
- **Writers** who need a durable daily journal that won't fall apart in a bag.
- **Gift buyers** — the cover looks more expensive than it is, and the packaging is presentable.

**Skip it if** you need dotted or grid pages (this is lined only), you prefer a pocket size, or you're a fountain pen user who writes with broad nibs — the 100gsm paper is good, but it's not Tomoe River.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, functional journal with excellent paper at a fair price. The numbered pages and index make it genuinely useful, not just decorative.

**Skip it if** you need dotted pages, a pen loop, or you're a die-hard Moleskine loyalist who can't switch.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect (the elastic band and missing pen loop are real annoyances), but for the price, this is one of the best-value journals on Amazon right now. The 4.8-star average from 240 buyers holds up under scrutiny.
