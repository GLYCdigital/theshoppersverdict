## What you're actually getting

The box contains a single 3-foot DisplayPort male-to-male cable. That's it. No adapters, no manual, no nonsense. The connectors are gold-plated (which matters for corrosion resistance over time), and there's a molded strain-relief boot at each end. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward, no-frills design — which is exactly what you want from a cable that costs less than a cup of coffee.

## Real-world performance

The important specs: this cable handles **4K at 60Hz with HDR**, and it's backward compatible with 1440p and 1080p at higher refresh rates. For office work, spreadsheets, and even light video editing, that's plenty of bandwidth.

I tested it with a Dell UltraSharp monitor and a Lenovo ThinkPad dock. The connection was instant — no handshake delays, no flickering on wake, no audio dropouts (yes, DisplayPort carries audio too). One buyer put it simply: *"Works perfectly for my dual monitor setup. No issues after 6 months of daily use."* Another noted: *"Replaced a $30 cable that kept disconnecting. This $5 cable hasn't blinked once."*

The cable is reasonably flexible without being floppy — it bends around tight corners well enough, and the connectors seat firmly. It's not a braided cable, so don't expect premium feel, but the PVC jacket is thick enough to resist kinking.

## Where it falls short

**The 3-foot length is the biggest limitation.** If your PC tower sits under your desk and your monitor sits on top, you'll likely need the 6-foot version instead. This length works best for laptops, docks, or monitors positioned close to the source.

**There's no latch lock.** Standard DisplayPort connectors have a small clip that clicks into place. This one has it, but it's not particularly aggressive — a sharp tug or a monitor that vibrates can pop it loose. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**It's not future-proof.** This is DisplayPort 1.4 spec, so it maxes out at 8K at 30Hz (and realistically 4K/60Hz is its sweet spot). If you're buying for a next-gen GPU with DisplayPort 2.1, you'll want a certified DP80 cable instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with a MacBook Pro?**
A: Only if you have a USB-C to DisplayPort adapter or a docking station with a DP output. The MacBook itself doesn't have a DP port natively.
