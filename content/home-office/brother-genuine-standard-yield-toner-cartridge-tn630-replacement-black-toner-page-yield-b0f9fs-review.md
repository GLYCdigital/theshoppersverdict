If you own a Brother HL-L2300D, DCP-L2520DW, or any printer in that family, you already know the brand's reputation for reliability. The TN630 is the standard-yield cartridge that keeps those workhorses running. At 4.7 stars from 235 Amazon ratings, buyers are clearly satisfied — but is this cartridge worth the OEM premium over third-party alternatives? Let's break down what you're actually paying for.

**What's in the box**

You get a sealed Brother Genuine TN630 toner cartridge rated for approximately 1,200 pages at 5% coverage. That's the standard yield — not the high-capacity TN660 (which bumps you to 2,600 pages). The cartridge arrives in Brother's tamper-evident packaging with the familiar orange protective seal. Installation is genuinely tool-free: open the front cover, pull out the old unit, slide this one in until it clicks. About 30 seconds of work. The printer's internal sensor will recognize the genuine chip immediately, so you won't get "unauthorized toner" warnings.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The TN630 uses Brother's proprietary toner formulation with a precision-machined drum and magnetic roller. In practice, that means sharp, consistent text down to 6-point fonts with no streaking or banding. The black density is uniform across the page — no gray gradients or faded edges that plague cheap refurbs. One thing buyers consistently mention: no compatibility headaches. As one reviewer put it, *"Installed in seconds and my printer recognized it instantly. No chips to reset, no error messages, just printing."* Another noted, *"I've used cheap cartridges before and ended up with toner all over my hands. This one was clean from start to finish."*

The toner sensor is worth calling out. Because it's an OEM chip, your low-toner alerts are accurate — you'll get a warning when you genuinely have ~200 pages left, not a premature "replace now" nag that third-party chips often trigger.

**Where it falls short**

The honest downsides: 1,200 pages is a modest yield. If you print a lot of text-heavy documents, you'll be swapping cartridges every couple of months. The cost per page runs roughly $0.04–$0.06 depending on your purchase price — noticeably higher than compatible cartridges that can get you to $0.02–$0.03 per page. And while Brother's OEM quality is excellent, some budget-conscious buyers find the premium hard to justify for simple everyday printing.

The other tradeoff: no reset button on the cartridge itself. If you ever want to refill it (which we don't recommend), you'll need a separate reset gear. Stick with genuine replacements.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home office users** printing 200–400 pages monthly — the standard yield matches your needs without wasting money on a high-yield cartridge that would sit for months.
- **Small business owners** who charge clients for printing — reliability matters more than saving a few cents per page when a jam or smear costs you an hour.
- **Anyone frustrated by compatibility headaches** with third-party cartridges. The TN630 just works, every time.

**Who should skip it**

- **High-volume printers** — buy the TN660 high-yield instead and save on cost per page.
- **Ultra-budget printers** who don't mind occasional quality dips — third-party options exist at half the price, though you risk voiding your printer warranty.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Will this cartridge work with my Brother HL-L2350DW?**  
A: Yes. The TN630 is compatible with the HL-L2300D, HL-L2305W, HL-L2310D, HL-L2315DW, HL-L2320D, HL-L2325DW, HL-L2340DW, HL-L2350DW, DCP-L2520DW, DCP-L2540DW, MFC-L2700DW, and MFC-L2720DW. Double-check your model number, but it's a broad compatibility range.

**Q: How many pages will I actually get?**  
A: Brother rates it at 1,200 pages at 5% coverage (roughly a standard text page). If you print mostly graphics or photos, expect less. If you print light text, you may exceed it slightly.

**Q: Is the genuine cartridge worth the extra cost over compatible brands?**  
A: If you value consistent quality and zero compatibility issues, yes. The extra $10–$15 buys you OEM reliability and doesn't void your printer warranty. If you're printing drafts and don't care about slight quality variations, save the money.

**The Verdict**

The Brother TN630 is the right cartridge for the right user. It delivers dependable, crisp output with zero installation drama — exactly what you expect from a genuine OEM part. The 4.7-star rating is earned. But don't ignore the economics: at 1,200 pages, it's not for heavy printers, and the per-page cost is higher than compatibles.

**Buy it if** you own a compatible Brother printer, value hassle-free installation, and print under 400 pages monthly.  
**Skip it if** you're a high-volume printer or on a tight budget — get the TN660 or evaluate third-party options.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A reliable, well-engineered cartridge with one caveat: the standard yield will make you reorder sooner than you might like.
