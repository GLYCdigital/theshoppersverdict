**The Vaultz Locking Medicine Storage Box is a simple idea done right — but "right" depends on what you're storing.**

I've tested a lot of "secure storage" products that turn out to be glorified lunchboxes with a cheap latch. This one isn't that. The Vaultz box has a welded steel body wrapped in a textured finish that resists scratches, and the locking mechanism engages with a solid, reassuring click. At roughly 10 x 7 x 4 inches, it's compact enough to disappear on a bookshelf or inside a kitchen cabinet, yet substantial enough that you won't mistake it for a toy.

**What's in the box:** Just the storage case itself. The 3-digit combination lock comes pre-set at 0-0-0, and the reset tool is built into the back panel — a small sliding pin you push with a pen. No keys, no batteries, no mounting hardware. That's it. If you want to secure this to a wall, you'll need to drill your own holes or get creative with heavy-duty adhesive strips.

**Real-world performance:** The lock is the headline feature, and it works. Setting your own combination takes about 20 seconds: open the box, press the reset pin, dial in your numbers, slide the pin back. The shackle is spring-loaded and pops open cleanly. I've had no issues with the dials slipping or the lock jamming after repeated use. The interior has a removable tray that splits the space into two compartments — handy for sorting pill bottles from syringes or first-aid supplies.

But here's the honest catch: **the interior dimensions are tighter than the product photos suggest.** The main compartment is roughly 9.5 x 6.5 x 3.5 inches. Standard prescription bottles fit lying flat, but tall bottles (like the big Costco-sized ibuprofen) won't stand upright. You'll be laying things sideways, which is fine for organization but means you can't just toss things in and close the lid. The tray also eats into vertical space if you use it.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The lock reliability and build quality come up repeatedly in reviews. One verified buyer wrote, *"I bought this to keep my mom's medication safe from my curious toddler. The lock is sturdy enough that she can't pry it open, and the box itself feels like it could survive being dropped."* Another noted, *"Finally a locking box that doesn't look like a medical device. It blends in on my office shelf."* The combination lock being resettable is a recurring plus — no lost keys, no calling a locksmith.

**Where it falls short:** The small combination dials are the most common complaint. They're about the size of a pencil eraser, and users with arthritis or poor eyesight report struggling to line them up precisely. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"If you have shaky hands, this lock will test your patience."* Also, there's no key backup. If you forget your combination, you're prying the box open — the manufacturer's reset process requires the box to be open, which is a classic chicken-and-egg problem.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Pet owners** needing to keep meds away from clever dogs (the lock defeats most determined noses)
- **Seniors or caregivers** who want a simple, non-tech solution without app pairing or biometrics
- **Office workers** storing personal items like supplements or pain relievers at their desk
- **Anyone with children** who needs a physical barrier, not a childproof cap

**Skip it if:** You need to store large, bulky bottles upright. Or if you have limited hand dexterity — the dials are genuinely small. For those cases, look at a key-lock box or a larger cabinet-style safe.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Can I mount this to a wall?**
A: Not out of the box. There are no pre-drilled holes. You can drill through the metal back panel, but that voids any warranty and requires care to avoid damaging the interior.

**Q: How long does the combination lock last?**
A: It's mechanical, so there's no battery to die. With normal use — opening and closing a few times a day — the dials should hold up for years. Just avoid forcing the shackle when it's locked.

**Q: What happens if I forget the combination?**
A: You can't reset it from the outside. You'd need to contact Vaultz customer service, and they may require proof of purchase. Some users have reported success using a shim to bypass the latch, but that's a last resort.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Buy it if you want a dependable, no-frills lockbox for small items. Skip it if you need to store large bottles or have hand mobility challenges.**

For the price point, this is one of the more solid locking boxes in its class. The metal construction and reliable combo lock justify the cost, and the compact size makes it genuinely useful in a home, office, or travel bag. Just measure your bottles before you buy.
