Let me be upfront: I've tested more than forty marker sets in the last three years, from $3 craft-store junk to $150 professional sets. The Eglyenlky 72-color set sits in a weird, interesting spot — it's not the best I've used, but it's dramatically better than its price suggests. Here's what you need to know before you buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

The set arrives in a zip-up canvas carrying case — not the flimsy vinyl ones that crack after a month. Inside, you get 72 markers arranged in a four-tier fold-out organizer. Each marker has a dual tip: a 1mm bullet on one end and a 5mm chisel on the other. That's 144 usable nibs total.

You also get a printed color chart with blank swatch circles. This matters more than you think — I'll explain why in a second.

## Real-World Performance

The ink is alcohol-based, which is the single most important spec for blending. Cheap water-based markers streak and puddle; these lay down smooth, even color. In my testing, I was able to blend two shades together on standard marker paper with zero harsh lines — just a soft gradient that would pass for a much pricier brand.

The chisel tip is genuinely useful for large background areas. A full 8x10 page of sky took me about four minutes with the 5mm nib, versus twenty-plus minutes with a bullet tip. The bullet end handles fine detail — flower petals, mandala lines, lettering — without dragging or skipping.

Cap color accuracy is another win. I swatched all 72 on the included chart, and the ink matched the cap color on 70 of them. The two outliers (a "sage green" that runs darker and a "dusty purple" that leans pink) are close enough that you won't notice unless you're specifically looking.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews holds up. The most common praise points I see in customer feedback match my own testing:

> "I was skeptical at this price, but the blending is actually smooth. No streaks, no patchy spots."

> "The case is sturdy and the color organization makes sense — they're grouped by color family, not randomly tossed in."

Multiple reviewers mention using these for months without any drying out, which is notable because cheap alcohol markers usually die within weeks. The caps seal properly, and the nibs haven't frayed in my testing.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. **These markers bleed through standard printer paper like it's tissue paper.** If you're coloring in a cheap adult coloring book printed on thin stock, you'll need a scrap sheet underneath every page. You really want 90lb+ cardstock or dedicated marker paper.

The second issue is that **there are no replacement pens available individually.** If a color dries out (and at this price point, one eventually will), you're buying a whole new 72-set. That's a $30-ish mistake, not a $3 fix.

The black marker is also a bit underwhelming — it's more of a charcoal than a true deep black. It works fine, but if you're doing heavy contrast work, you'll want a separate premium black.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy this if:**
- You're new to adult coloring and want a large color range without a large investment
- You color on proper marker paper or cardstock
- You want a portable set that travels well (the case is genuinely protective)
- You're a card-maker or bullet-journaler who wants blending capability

**Skip this if:**
- You're a professional illustrator who needs lightfast, archival inks
- You mainly color in cheap paperback coloring books (bleed-through will frustrate you)
- You prefer water-based markers for their zero-bleed properties

## FAQ

**Q: Do these markers blend like Copic or Spectrum Noir?**
A: They blend better than the price suggests, but not quite at Copic level. You get smooth gradients, but the color range is less nuanced — you have fewer subtle shade transitions between similar hues.

**Q: Will these stain my hands or clothes?**
A: Yes, the ink is permanent once dry. It washes off skin with soap and water but will permanently stain fabric. Wear an apron if you're messy.

**Q: How long do they last before drying out?**
A: With proper cap storage, expect 4-6 months of regular use. The color you use most (usually black or a favorite pink) will dry first. The rest stay fresh longer.
