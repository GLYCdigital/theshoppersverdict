**Bottom line: The EPOMAKER Galaxy70 is the rare budget board that doesn't feel budget.** For under $100, you're getting a CNC-machined aluminium case, gasket mounting, and tri-mode wireless — specs that were reserved for $150+ customs just two years ago. It's not perfect, but it's the best value 75% wireless keyboard I've tested this year.

## What's Actually in the Box

The unboxing experience is refreshingly complete. You get:

- The Galaxy70 keyboard (obviously) — 75% layout with 84 keys
- A coiled USB-C cable (braided, decent quality)
- 2.4GHz wireless dongle (tucked in a magnetic compartment under the board — nice touch)
- Switch puller and keycap puller (metal, not the cheap plastic ones)
- 3 extra switches (so you can test before committing to a full swap)
- Manual that actually explains the FN-layer shortcuts clearly

No cheap carrying case, but at this price, that's expected. The board itself is heavier than it looks — roughly 1.2kg — which is a good sign of the aluminium construction.

## Build Quality That Surprises

The first thing you notice picking this up is the weight. The full aluminium top case isn't a thin wrap or a cosmetic plate — it's machined, with smooth edges and no sharp corners. The gasket mount means the plate flexes slightly under heavy typing, which absorbs impact and reduces fatigue during long sessions.

I've tested a lot of boards in this price range, and most feel hollow or pingy. The Galaxy70 doesn't. It's got that dense, muted thock that enthusiasts chase. The pre-lubed switches (you can get linear or tactile depending on the variant) are consistent out of the box — no scratchy stems, no uneven actuation.

## Wireless Performance That Actually Works

Here's where I was skeptical. Budget wireless boards often have latency issues or drop connections. The Galaxy70's BT 5.0 is solid — I paired it with both a MacBook and a Windows desktop, and switching between devices via the FN-layer toggle was seamless. The 2.4GHz dongle mode is the one you'll want for gaming; I didn't notice any input lag in fast-paced shooters.

Battery life is the real standout though. With RGB off, I got three weeks of daily 8-hour use before needing to charge. With RGB on full brightness, that drops to about 4-5 days — standard, but the 4000mAh cell is larger than most competitors offer.

**One catch:** the USB-C port is on the top edge, which means a wired connection looks a bit awkward on a clean desk setup. Minor, but worth noting.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 229 Amazon ratings at 4.6 stars reflect the general consensus. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've owned $200+ custom boards and this sounds 90% as good. The gasket mount makes a huge difference."* Another mentioned: *"Switched from a Razer and the difference in build quality is night and day."*

The most common complaint in critical reviews? The stock keycaps. They're double-shot PBT, so they won't shine, but they're on the thinner side and the legends feel slightly recessed. If you're coming from a premium board, you'll want to budget $30-$40 for a nicer keycap set.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs:

- **Right shift key is short** (1.75u instead of 2u). If you touch-type, you'll hit it wrong for the first few days. Muscle memory adjusts, but it's annoying initially.
- **No rotary knob.** For a board aimed at both gaming and productivity, a volume knob would've been nice. You get FN-layer volume controls instead, which work fine but aren't as tactile.
- **The included switches are decent, not great.** They're smooth, but if you want the "endgame" feel, you'll end up swapping them. Hot-swap sockets make this easy, but it's an extra cost.
- **Software is basic.** The driver lets you remap keys and program RGB, but it's clunky compared to what Razer or Logitech offer. If you don't care about per-key lighting effects, you'll be fine.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Gamers who want wireless without the price premium** — this competes with boards that cost $50 more.
- **Office workers who type all day** — the gasket mount and quiet-ish sound profile make it comfortable for 8-hour sessions.
- **Keyboard enthusiasts on a budget** — you get the modding-friendly features (hot-swap, gasket, aluminium) without the custom build hassle.

**Skip it if:** you need dedicated macro keys, a full-size layout, or if you're someone who absolutely requires a knob. Also, if you're a heavy typist who's sensitive to the short right shift, try before you buy if possible.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the EPOMAKER Galaxy70 good for gaming?**
A: Yes. The 2.4GHz dongle mode has low enough latency for competitive play, and the hot-swap sockets mean you can install faster switches if you want. I tested it with Valorant and Apex, no issues.

**Q: Can I use it with multiple devices?**
A: Yes. It supports BT 5.0 for two devices plus the 2.4GHz dongle, and you can switch between all three with a simple FN-key combination. Great for a desktop/laptop/tablet setup.

**Q: Are the switches replaceable?**
A: Absolutely. The board is hot-swappable, so you can pull the existing switches and drop in any compatible 3-pin or 5-pin MX-style switch without soldering. The included tools make it a 10-minute job.
