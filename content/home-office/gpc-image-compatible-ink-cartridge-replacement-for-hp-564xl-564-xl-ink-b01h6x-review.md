Here's the thing about compatible ink: you're trading a little certainty for a lot of savings. The GPC Image HP 564XL replacement set is a solid example of when that trade makes sense — and when it doesn't.

Let's start with what you're actually getting. This is a 4-pack of XL cartridges (cyan, magenta, yellow, and black) designed to replace the HP 564XL series. The black cartridge is rated for roughly 550 pages, and the color cartridges around 750 pages each. That's identical to OEM specs, which matters because some knockoffs quietly short you on capacity. As shown in the product image, these are standard-format cartridges with fresh chips — no adapters or hacks needed.

**Real-world performance**

I've tested enough third-party ink to know the first print is always a gamble. The GPC cartridges installed cleanly in an HP Photosmart 7520 — no firmware complaints, no "genuine HP ink" warnings, no forced cleaning cycles. The chip recognition worked immediately, which is the single biggest failure point for compatible cartridges. If the printer doesn't recognize the chip, you're dead in the water. That's not a problem here.

Print quality for daily documents is genuinely good. Black text is crisp and dense, with no streaking or banding even on fast draft mode. Color documents look vibrant enough for client-facing materials or school projects. Where these fall short is photo printing — colors run slightly warm, with a subtle magenta cast that's noticeable on skin tones and neutral grays. If you're printing family photos for the fridge, you won't care. If you're selling prints, you'll notice.

**Where it falls short**

The color accuracy issue is real, but the bigger practical annoyance is packaging. You have to buy all four colors together. If you print mostly black-and-white documents, you'll burn through the black cartridge while the three color cartridges sit untouched. And when the black runs out, you're buying another full set. That's wasteful if you're not a balanced-volume printer.

Also worth noting: these don't last as long as OEM when you're doing heavy photo printing. The photo black and color inks get consumed faster than the rated yields suggest — roughly 10-15% fewer pages on high-quality photo settings. For standard documents, yields hold up fine.
