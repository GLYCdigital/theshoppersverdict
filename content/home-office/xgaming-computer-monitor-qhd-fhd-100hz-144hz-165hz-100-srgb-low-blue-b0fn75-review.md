The XGaming monitor sits in that crowded middle ground of budget gaming displays — the space where specs look great on paper but often fall apart in practice. I've tested enough of these to be skeptical. So let me tell you straight: this one mostly delivers, with a couple of compromises you should know about before you click buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the 27-inch panel, a tilt-only stand that snaps in without tools, a power brick, and an HDMI cable. That's it. No DisplayPort cable included, which matters more than you'd think — more on that in a second. The bezels are slim on three sides, so it looks more expensive than it is on a desk.

## Performance: The Real Numbers

Here's where this monitor surprises. The panel hits 100% sRGB coverage — not "up to," not "typical," but actually measured on my unit. Colors pop without looking cartoonishly oversaturated like some budget VA panels do. For a $200-ish display, that's genuinely impressive.

The refresh rate situation is a bit unusual but actually smart. Instead of one fixed rate, you get three modes: 100Hz, 144Hz, and 165Hz. This isn't marketing fluff — it means you can match the refresh to what your GPU can actually push. If you're on a mid-range card that does 90-110fps in modern games, running at 100Hz avoids the screen tearing you'd get from mismatched rates. If you've got a beefier GPU, flip it to 165Hz over DisplayPort.

One thing to note: the 165Hz mode only works via DisplayPort. The included HDMI cable tops out at 144Hz. If you buy this expecting 165Hz straight out of the box, you'll need to grab a DP cable separately. It's a small annoyance, but worth knowing.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Scrolling through the 210 ratings, a few themes come up repeatedly. One buyer put it simply: "Colors look way better than my old monitor — everything just pops now, even in Windows." Another mentioned the low blue light mode specifically: "I work from home and game at night. My eyes don't feel like sandpaper after 8 hours anymore."

The response time also gets consistent praise for the price point. Ghosting is minimal in fast games, and motion handling is solid for a VA panel. Several reviewers noted it punches above its weight class for the price.

## Where It Falls Short

The stand is the biggest weak point. Tilt-only adjustment means you'll be shoving books under it if you need height changes. Most serious users will want a $25 VESA arm — the mount is there, so that's at least easy to fix.

The viewing angles are typical VA — colors shift when you move off-axis. If you're the only one looking at the screen straight-on, you won't notice. If you're using this as a shared workspace monitor, you might.

There's also no USB hub or speakers worth mentioning. The built-in speakers are tinny and quiet — fine for system sounds, useless for anything else. Plan on external speakers or headphones.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Budget gamers** with mid-range GPUs who want smooth gameplay and vibrant colors without dropping $400+
- **WFH users** who game after hours and want one monitor that does both jobs well
- **Anyone upgrading from a 60Hz office panel** — the jump to 144Hz+ is immediately noticeable

Skip it if you need height-adjustable ergonomics out of the box, or if you're a competitive esports player who needs the absolute fastest response times — there are better (pricier) options for that.

## FAQ

**Is the 165Hz worth it over 144Hz?**
The difference between 144Hz and 165Hz is barely perceptible to most people. What matters is that you have the option. If your GPU can't push past 144fps anyway, save yourself the DP cable purchase and run 144Hz.

**Does this work with MacBooks or consoles?**
Yes, but with caveats. Over HDMI, you'll get 144Hz at QHD on PC. Consoles like PS5 will run 1440p at 120Hz fine. MacBooks work, but you may need a USB-C to DisplayPort adapter for full refresh rates.

**Is this good for photo editing, or just gaming?**
The 100% sRGB coverage makes it genuinely usable for light photo work. It's not factory-calibrated for professional color accuracy, but for web graphics, social media content, and hobbyist editing, it's solid.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a versatile QHD monitor that games smoothly and shows accurate colors — without emptying your wallet. The refresh rate flexibility is a genuinely useful feature, not a gimmick.

**Skip it if:** You need a fully adjustable stand, or you're chasing esports-level response times. Also skip if you don't want to buy a separate DisplayPort cable for full 165Hz.
