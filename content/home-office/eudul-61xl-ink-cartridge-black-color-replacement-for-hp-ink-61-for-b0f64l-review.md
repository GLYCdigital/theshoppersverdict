Let me start with something that'll save you money: if you're paying full retail for OEM HP 61 cartridges, you're overspending. The Eudul 61XL replacement set targets the exact same HP Deskjet, Envy, and OfficeJet printers that use the standard 61 ink system, but at a fraction of the price. ## What's in the Box

The kit includes one black (XL) and one tri-color (cyan/magenta/yellow) cartridge, both in XL capacity. Eudul rates the black at 600 pages and the tri-color at 440 pages — notably higher than HP's own 480/330 official ratings for 61XL. The cartridges come sealed in foil packs with a pull-tab that exposes the print head. Installation is genuinely plug-and-play: snap them into the carriage, close the latch, and the printer recognizes them immediately. No tape to peel inside the cartridge, no rubber bands to remove, no firmware prompts.

## Build Quality and Print Performance

The cartridges are physically lighter than OEM — the plastic shell feels marginally thinner — but that doesn't affect function. The key spec here is the automatic chip recognition. Cheap compatibles often trigger "Incompatible Cartridge" errors on HP printers, which is the #1 complaint across generic brands. Eudul avoids this with a pre-programmed chip that the printer reads as genuine. This matters if you've been burned by off-brand ink before.

Print quality for everyday documents is solid. Black text comes out crisp and dark on standard copy paper, with no streaking or banding in my test prints. Color documents are vibrant enough for school projects, presentations, and family photos at standard quality settings. The tri-color cartridge uses dye-based ink, which gives good color saturation on glossy photo paper.

However, there's a consistent observation across buyer feedback about color accuracy. One reviewer noted, "Colors look good but not exactly like the HP originals — reds are a bit orange." Another said, "Fine for homework and coupons, but I wouldn't trust it for professional client proofs." If color-critical work is your primary use, budget for OEM. For everything else, this is fine.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The value proposition is the star here. Multiple verified purchasers highlight the same math: "Two Eudul sets cost less than one HP 61XL combo pack, and I get more pages." Page yield complaints are nearly nonexistent for standard document printing — most users report hitting or exceeding the claimed 600 black pages with typical 5% coverage documents.

The chip reliability deserves specific mention. I found zero reports of printer lockouts or error messages in the review data, which is remarkable for a compatible cartridge. Users running older HP Deskjet models (the 1110, 2130, and 3630 series appear frequently) report seamless compatibility.

## Where It Falls Short

The warm color cast is the most consistent criticism. If you print family photos, expect skin tones to lean slightly orange. It's not egregious, but it's noticeable side-by-side with OEM output. This is a dye-based ink characteristic, not a defect — but it's worth knowing.

Photo-heavy printing will also gut the page yield. A buyer who printed mostly 4x6 photos reported, "Got maybe 150 photos before the color cartridge died." That's expected with high-density printing, but Eudul doesn't disclose this caveat clearly on the listing.

The packaging is minimal — no resealable bags, just foil packs. If you buy a multi-pack and don't install the second set within a month, the ink can dry and clog the print head. Install promptly or store in a ziplock bag.

**Q: Will HP warranty be voided?**
