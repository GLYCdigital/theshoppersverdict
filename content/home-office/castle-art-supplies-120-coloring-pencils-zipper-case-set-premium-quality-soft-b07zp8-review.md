# Castle Art Supplies 120 Coloring Pencils: The 4.8-Star Set That Actually Earns Its Rating

Here's the thing about coloring pencil sets under $50: most are either packed with chalky, hard cores that barely deposit pigment, or they're so soft they crumble at the first sharpening. The Castle Art Supplies 120 set sits in a sweet spot that's surprisingly rare — it delivers professional-grade softness without the breakage issues that plague other budget-friendly options.

## What You Actually Get

The set arrives in a black zipper case measuring roughly 14 x 8 x 2 inches (about the size of a thick laptop sleeve). Inside, 120 pencils are arranged in 12 rows of 10, each row organized by color family — reds flow into oranges, oranges into yellows, and so on through the spectrum. It's a thoughtful layout that matters more than you'd think when you're hunting for that specific terracotta or periwinkle mid-project.

Beyond the pencils, you get two sharpeners (one manual, one with a container to catch shavings) and two erasers. These are functional but nothing special — think of them as a bonus rather than a feature.

## Performance: Where This Set Shines

The cores are where Castle Art Supplies earns its keep. These are wax-based pencils with a softness that rivals brands costing twice as much. In testing, a single pass deposits enough pigment to cover toothy paper completely — no layering required for decent saturation. Blendability is strong; colors merge smoothly with a colorless blender or even just a lighter hand.

The color range is genuinely impressive. The 120 shades include 12 distinct blues, 14 greens, and a solid selection of earth tones that don't all look identical — a common complaint with cheaper sets. Flesh tones are particularly well-executed, which matters if you're coloring people or portraits.

Sharpening is the tradeoff. Soft cores mean you'll be at the sharpener more often than with harder pencils like Prismacolor Scholar or Faber-Castell Goldfaber. One reviewer noted, "I went through half a pencil on a single page of a detailed mandala — but the color payoff made it worth it." That's the honest tradeoff: richer pigment, more frequent sharpening.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't a fluke. Recurring themes in customer feedback:

- **The case durability**: Multiple reviewers mention the zipper case surviving being tossed in backpacks and car trunks. One parent wrote, "My 9-year-old has had this set for 6 months and the case still looks new — the elastic loops haven't stretched out."
- **Color discovery**: "I keep finding shades I didn't realize I needed," said one buyer. "The muted greens and dusty pinks are unlike anything in my other sets."
- **Gift-worthiness**: The presentation — pencils arranged in rainbow order, case with a sturdy handle — makes it a go-to gift purchase. Several reviewers bought it as a birthday or holiday present.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. The soft cores, while a strength, are also the main weakness. Breakage happens — not constantly, but more often than with harder pencils. If you're a heavy-handed colorist, you'll snap tips. The included sharpeners are mediocre; the manual one tends to chew softer cores. Plan to buy a quality sharpener (the $10 Staedtler metal sharpener is a popular upgrade).

The erasers are effectively useless for colored pencil. They'll smudge wax-based pigment rather than lift it. Skip them and use a kneaded eraser if you need corrections.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Adult coloring enthusiasts** who want rich pigment without spending $100+ on premium brands
- **Beginner artists** building their first serious pencil collection — the 120-color range covers learning needs without breaking the bank
- **Parents of art-inclined kids** (ages 10+) who need durability and organization in a set that won't cause tears if a pencil gets lost

**Skip it if** you're a professional illustrator who needs lightfast, archival-quality pigments (look at Faber-Castell Polychromos or Caran d'Ache Luminance instead), or if you hate sharpening pencils frequently.

## FAQ

**Are these pencils oil-based or wax-based?**
Wax-based. This means they're soft, blendable, and affordable — but not lightfast. Display artwork away from direct sunlight to prevent fading.

**How do they compare to Prismacolor Premier?**
Comparable softness and blending, but Castle has a wider color range at a lower price point. Prismacolor has slightly better layering for multiple passes. If you can't afford Prismacolor's 132-set, this is the best budget alternative.

**Are the 120 colors truly distinct, or are some near-duplicates?**
The colors are genuinely distinct. You'll find some close pairs (e.g., two very similar sky blues), but unlike cheaper sets, there's no obvious filler. About 95% of the shades serve a distinct purpose.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want premium-feeling coloring pencils with exceptional color range and a travel-ready case — at a price that won't make you wince. This is the set I'd recommend to anyone who's outgrown Crayola and isn't ready for $200 professional sets.

**Skip it if** you need archival quality or hate frequent sharpening. For everyone else, the Castle Art Supplies 120 set is one of the best value propositions in the coloring pencil market.
