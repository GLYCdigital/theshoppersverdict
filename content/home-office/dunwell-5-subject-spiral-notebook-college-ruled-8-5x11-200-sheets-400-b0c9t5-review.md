The Dunwell 5-Subject Spiral Notebook is the academic equivalent of a Swiss Army knife: it does one thing — hold a semester's worth of notes — but it does it exceptionally well. With 200 college-ruled sheets per notebook (that's 400 pages total), this isn't a throwaway pad. It's a serious tool for students, note-takers, and anyone who's tired of juggling five separate notebooks.

## What You Actually Get

The package is straightforward: one 8.5x11-inch spiral notebook with five color-coded subject dividers, 40 sheets per section. The covers are a flexible poly material that resists water spills and coffee drips. Inside, the college-ruled paper (9/32-inch spacing) is slightly thicker than your average notebook stock — you can feel the difference when you flip pages. The spiral binding is double-wound steel, not the flimsy single-coil you see on cheap pads.

## Performance in the Real World

This is where the Dunwell earns its keep. The paper handles ink better than most notebooks in this price range. I tested it with a Pilot G2 gel pen and a Zebra Mildliner highlighter — no bleed-through, minimal ghosting. Ballpoint pens glide without catching on the paper fibers. If you're left-handed, the spiral sits on the right side in standard orientation, but you can flip it over and write from the back without the coil digging into your wrist.

The lay-flat design is the standout feature. Open it to any page, and it stays flat without you pinning it down with an elbow. That matters when you're copying diagrams or writing in a cramped lecture hall. The reinforced tape along the spine prevents pages from tearing out during aggressive page-flipping — a common failure point on lesser notebooks.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides? It's heavy. At roughly 1.5 pounds, this notebook will weigh down a backpack. If you're a minimalist who carries only a tablet and one notebook, this is overkill. The divider tabs are sturdy but permanent — you can't rearrange subjects mid-semester without ripping them out. And the flexible cover bends in tight backpacks; it's not the rigid cardboard you get from premium brands like Moleskine.

One more thing: the perforation is there, but it's not the cleanest tear. If you need to remove pages frequently, expect a slightly jagged edge. It's fine for turning in assignments, but not for creating polished handouts.

## Who Should Buy This

- **College students** taking multiple lecture courses — one notebook covers all five classes
- **Professionals** who attend frequent meetings and need chronological, separated notes
- **Writers and journalers** who want substantial page counts without buying new notebooks monthly
- **Left-handed writers** who need lay-flat pages and don't want coil interference

Skip it if you need a slim notebook for a single class, prefer hardcover protection, or want removable/rearrangeable dividers.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Does the paper hold up to fountain pens?**
A: It handles medium and fine nibs well, but broad nibs may ghost slightly. For gel, ballpoint, and rollerball pens, it's excellent. Skip heavy ink layering or alcohol-based markers.

**Q: How many pages per subject section?**
A: 40 sheets (80 pages) per section, with 5 sections total. That's roughly one section per class per semester for most courses.

**Q: Can you remove the spiral binding and use the pages separately?**
A: Yes, but the spiral unwinds permanently. If you need to split it, do it at the end of the semester — the pages tear out cleanly along the perforation, but the binding won't reattach.
