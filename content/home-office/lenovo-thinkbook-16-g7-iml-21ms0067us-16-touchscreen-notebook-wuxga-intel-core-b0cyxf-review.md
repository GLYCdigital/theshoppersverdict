The 16-inch business laptop is a tricky category. Most are either overpriced Ultrabooks with soldered RAM or bulky plastic bricks. The ThinkBook 16 G7 IML sits somewhere in the middle — and for the right buyer, that's exactly where you want to be.

Let me walk you through what $700-ish actually gets you here, because the spec sheet tells a more interesting story than the price suggests.

## What's in the Box

Lenovo keeps it business-boring: the laptop, a 65W USB-C power adapter, and a quick-start guide. No stylus, no sleeve, no bloatware theatrics. The machine itself is a matte graphite affair — 16 inches, about 4.1 pounds, and 0.78 inches thick. It's not a featherweight, but it's not a boat anchor either. The aluminum lid feels premium, though the bottom panel is plastic.

## Performance and Build Quality

The star here is the Intel Core Ultra 5 125U processor paired with 16GB of DDR5 RAM. For productivity work — think 20 browser tabs, Excel models, Slack, and a video call running simultaneously — this thing doesn't break a sweat. The integrated Arc graphics handle 4K video playback smoothly, but don't expect to game on it. One buyer put it plainly: "It's not a gaming laptop, and I didn't buy it for that. For my CPA work, it's lightning fast."

The 16-inch WUXGA (1920x1200) touchscreen is genuinely good. At 300 nits with an anti-glare finish, you can actually work near a window without cranking brightness to max. The 16:10 aspect ratio gives you extra vertical space — a small thing that makes a big difference when you're scrolling through spreadsheets all day.

The keyboard deserves special mention. Full-size with a dedicated number pad and 1.5mm travel, it's a data-entry dream. One reviewer who types all day noted: "I've owned ThinkPads for years. This keyboard is 90% as good, at half the price." That's high praise.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reading through the 200 ratings, three themes keep coming up:

1. **Value for money** — "Got this for $650 on sale. The build quality rivals machines costing $300 more."
2. **Display quality** — "The touchscreen is responsive and colors look accurate. Editing photos in Lightroom is surprisingly pleasant."
3. **Upgradability** — "I popped in an extra 16GB stick and a second NVMe drive in 10 minutes. Try doing that on a MacBook."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs.

**Battery life is the biggest compromise.** The 45Wh battery is undersized for a 16-inch panel. Real-world use gets you 6-7 hours — enough for a workday if you're near a charger, not enough for cross-country flights. One reviewer summed it up: "I get about 5.5 hours with screen at 70% and WiFi on. It's fine, but I expected more."

**The plastic base flexes.** Press firmly near the palm rest and you'll feel it give slightly. It's not creaky or broken — just not as rigid as an all-aluminum chassis.

**Speakers are mediocre.** They're downward-firing and get tinny above 50% volume. Fine for YouTube, rough for music.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Accountants, analysts, and office workers** who need a number pad and don't want to carry an external one
- **IT managers** buying fleets — the tool-free access panel makes RAM/SSD upgrades a 2-minute job
- **Students** in business or engineering programs who need a big screen for multi-window work

**Skip it if** you're a frequent flyer needing all-day battery, or a creative professional requiring a discrete GPU.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I upgrade the RAM and storage myself?**
A: Yes. One SODIMM slot is free (up to 32GB additional), and there's a second M.2 2242 slot. The bottom panel opens with standard Phillips screws — no prying tools needed.

**Q: Does the touchscreen work with a stylus?**
A: It supports basic capacitive touch, but there's no active pen support. If you need handwriting or precise drawing, look elsewhere.

**Q: Is the 16-inch screen too big to carry daily?**
A: It's 4.1 pounds and fits in most 16-inch laptop sleeves. It's noticeably wider than a 14-inch, so check your bag — but it's manageable for daily commuting.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Buy it if you need a large, bright touchscreen with a comfortable keyboard and upgradable internals at a mid-tier price. Skip it if battery endurance tops your priority list, or you need discrete graphics.

This is a smart, practical work machine that respects your money. It won't win beauty contests, but it'll still be running strong in four years — and you can upgrade it along the way. That's rarer than it should be in 2026.
