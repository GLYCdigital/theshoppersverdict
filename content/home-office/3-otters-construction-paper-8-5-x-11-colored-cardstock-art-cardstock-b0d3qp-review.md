## The 50-Color Cardstock That Actually Earns Its Keep

Most construction paper packs give you eight or twelve colors and call it a day. The 3 Otters Construction Paper 8.5 x 11" pack skips that nonsense entirely—you get **50 different shades** in one box. That's not a typo. Fifty. For teachers, homeschool parents, or anyone who's ever had a kid demand "the specific purple from last week," this alone justifies the purchase.

But variety means nothing if the paper disintegrates under a glue stick. So let's talk about whether it holds up.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains 50 sheets of 65lb cardstock in true letter size (8.5 x 11 inches). Each sheet measures approximately 0.008 inches thick—noticeably sturdier than standard 20lb printer paper and most bargain construction paper that runs closer to 40-50lb.

You're getting:
- 50 unique colors spanning the full spectrum
- Acid-free, lignin-free paper (important for archival projects)
- Sheets that work with inkjet and laser printers
- Enough opacity that markers and paint don't bleed through to the next sheet

As shown in the product image, the color range runs from deep burgundies and navy blues to bright neons and soft pastels. There's no duplication—each shade is genuinely distinct.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this paper three ways: Cricut cutting, marker drawing, and old-fashioned folding.

**Cutting:** The 65lb weight feeds through cutting machines cleanly. Intricate designs hold their shape without tearing at stress points. Standard scissors also glide through without the ragged edges you get from flimsier paper.

**Markers and paint:** Here's where this cardstock separates itself. Regular construction paper turns into a soggy mess the moment water touches it. This 3 Otters paper handles light watercolor washes and alcohol markers with minimal buckling. Heavy wet-on-wet applications will still cause slight warping—that's physics—but it dries flat enough to use in layered projects.

**Folding:** Clean creases hold well. The one caveat: some darker colors (especially the deep reds and purples) can show faint white stress lines when you fold them back and forth multiple times. For origami or greeting cards with repeated folds, this is worth knowing.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws.

**The edges.** The sheets are cut to size, but the edges arrive slightly rough—not enough to affect use, but noticeable if you're making precise geometric projects where clean edges matter.

**No storage.** The paper ships in a simple plastic wrap. Once opened, you're managing 50 loose sheets. If you don't have a paper organizer or large envelope, expect some chaos.

**Folding stress marks.** As mentioned, darker colors show faint white lines on repeated folds. This won't matter for most projects, but it's a real limitation for card-makers who fold heavily.
