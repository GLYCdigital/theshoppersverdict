Let's be blunt: most third-party styluses are cheap plastic disappointments that make you question why you didn't just pay Apple's premium. This one is different. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, this fast-charging stylus has carved out real credibility among iPad owners who refuse to overpay for the Apple Pencil.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the stylus itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a spare tip. No bulky charging dock, no proprietary dongle. The pen is 166mm long and weighs 13.5 grams — nearly identical to the Apple Pencil 2 in hand. The matte aluminum barrel prevents the slippery feel that plagues cheaper glossy models. As shown in the product image, the flat edge design lets it snap magnetically to your iPad side, just like the real thing.

## Performance Where It Matters

The headline feature is the fast charge. Ten minutes plugged into USB-C gives you around 30 hours of writing time. That's not marketing math — that's genuinely usable. Full charge takes about 40 minutes and lasts through a full work week of heavy note-taking. Battery percentage shows in your iPad's Bluetooth widget, so there's no guessing game.

Palm rejection is the other critical test. In my testing across GoodNotes and Notability, the pen correctly ignored resting palms while tracking the tip precisely. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I take 4+ hours of lecture notes daily and haven't had a single accidental mark on the page." That's the experience you want, and it's consistent with what most buyers report.

Tilt sensitivity works for shading in drawing apps, and latency feels responsive — roughly on par with the Apple Pencil 2 for everyday writing. The replaceable tip is a thoughtful touch; you won't need to toss the whole pen when the nib wears down.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. There is **no pressure sensitivity**. If your workflow involves variable stroke weight in Procreate or Photoshop, you'll feel the limitation immediately. This is a note-taking and sketching tool, not a professional illustration device. Buyers who need pressure curves should stick with the genuine Apple Pencil.

Bluetooth pairing is another quirk. First-time setup requires going into your iPad's Bluetooth settings and tapping the device. It's a one-time annoyance, but some users report the connection dropping after iPadOS updates, forcing a re-pair. One reviewer mentioned, "After iOS 18.2 update, I had to re-pair the pen. Took 30 seconds, but it's worth knowing."

## Who Should Buy This

Three buyer types will get real value here:

- **Students** taking handwritten notes daily — the battery life and palm rejection make it a reliable study companion
- **Professionals** in meetings who need quick annotations on documents without Apple's premium price tag
- **Casual sketchers** who want tilt functionality and decent latency without committing to professional drawing

Skipping this? Digital artists who need pressure sensitivity, and anyone who wants zero-friction setup without touching Bluetooth settings.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with all iPad models?**
A: It works with iPad Pro 11"/12.9" (2018+), iPad Air 4th gen and newer, iPad Mini 6th gen, and standard iPads from 2018 onward. It won't work with the 1st-gen Apple Pencil's original iPad models — check compatibility before ordering.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Around 30 hours of continuous use from a 10-minute fast charge. Full charge extends that to roughly 40 hours. The pen auto-sleeps after 5 minutes idle to conserve power.

**Q: Does it need an app to function?**
A: No. It's a standard Bluetooth stylus — works natively with iPadOS. You'll see battery status in the Bluetooth menu, but there's no companion app required.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want 90% of the Apple Pencil 2 experience at a fraction of the cost, especially for note-taking, annotations, and everyday tasks.

**Skip it if** you're a digital artist requiring pressure sensitivity or you want a completely hands-off setup with zero Bluetooth pairing.
