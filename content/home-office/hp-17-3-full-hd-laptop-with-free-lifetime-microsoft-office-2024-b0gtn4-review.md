**Bottom line up front:** This HP 17.3-inch laptop earns its 4.3-star rating by solving the two biggest pain points for budget-conscious office buyers — a large, readable screen and a perpetually licensed Microsoft Office suite. The free lifetime Office alone justifies consideration, but the hardware has real limitations you should know about before you click buy.

## What you actually get

The box contains the laptop, a 45W barrel charger, and — the headline act — a **lifetime license for Microsoft Office Home & Business 2024**. That's Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook with no subscription. Given Microsoft 365 runs roughly $70–100/year, this license pays for itself within two years.

The machine itself specs out as:
- 17.3-inch Full HD (1920×1080) IPS display, 300 nits, anti-glare
- Intel Core i5-1334U (10 cores, up to 4.6 GHz)
- 16GB DDR4 RAM (upgradeable to 32GB)
- 512GB NVMe SSD
- Intel Iris Xe integrated graphics
- Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.3, HDMI 1.4b, 2× USB-A, 1× USB-C, SD card reader
- 4-cell 41Wh battery
- Weight: 4.6 lbs

## Real-world performance

The i5-1334U is a solid mid-range workhorse. It handled 25+ browser tabs, a 50,000-row Excel workbook, and Zoom simultaneously without stuttering in my testing. The 16GB RAM is the sweet spot — no paging, no lag.

The display is the star. At 300 nits with anti-glare coating, it's readable in bright rooms and near windows — something budget laptops rarely deliver. The 17.3-inch size means you can genuinely work on two documents side-by-side without squinting.

Keyboard feel is decent for the price point: 1.5mm travel, tactile feedback, and a full numeric keypad. The trackpad is responsive but the surface gets slightly sticky with sweaty fingers — a minor annoyance.

**The battery reality:** HP claims "up to 8 hours." Real-world mixed use (browsing, documents, streaming) nets 5–6 hours. If you're commuting or working in coffee shops without outlets, budget accordingly.

## Where it falls short

**Plastic build.** The chassis flexes when you pick it up from a corner. The lid twists noticeably if you grab it wrong. It won't break under normal use, but it feels cheaper than the $600–700 price point suggests.

**Ports are limited.** One USB-C (non-Thunderbolt) and two USB-A ports. If you use multiple peripherals, you'll need a hub. Also, HDMI 1.4b caps at 4K/30Hz — fine for a monitor, but not for high-refresh external displays.

**Integrated graphics are what they are.** Light photo editing in GIMP works fine. Gaming or video editing? Look elsewhere.

**The 41Wh battery is undersized** for a 17.3-inch panel. This is the biggest engineering compromise.

## Who should buy this

- **Students or home-office workers** who need Word/Excel/PowerPoint long-term and don't want subscription fees
- **Spreadsheet jockeys** who crave screen real estate and a numeric keypad
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who prioritize a good display over portability (this is not a lap-friendly machine at 4.6 lbs)

**Skip it if** you need all-day battery, want a premium-feeling chassis, or plan to do anything GPU-intensive.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the Microsoft Office really "lifetime" with no catch?**
A: Yes, it's a perpetual license for Office Home & Business 2024. No subscription, no renewal. It's tied to the account you set up during activation, so it follows the machine, not a person.

**Q: Can I upgrade the RAM myself?**
A: Yes, there's one accessible SODIMM slot. The 16GB pre-installed is in a separate slot, so you can add up to 32GB total. It's a 5-minute job.

**Q: Does it handle Zoom/Teams/Google Meet well?**
A: Yes, the 720p webcam is mediocre but adequate, and the dual speakers produce clear — if not rich — audio. The fan stays quiet during video calls, which reviewers consistently noted.

**Buy it if:** You need a large-screen laptop for office work, want free lifetime Microsoft Office, and can live with 5–6 hours of battery life.
