**Opening: Is this the centerpiece your Dark Master needs?**

Most hero models in the Age of Sigmar range are either too static or too fiddly to build. The Slaves to Darkness Daemon Prince—Games Workshop’s 2023 update to a classic—strikes a rare balance. It’s a towering, snarling centerpiece that reads as genuinely menacing from across the table, yet the build is forgiving enough for a first-time large model. But is it worth the shelf space and hobby time? Here’s what 235 buyers and my own assembly time reveal.

**What’s in the box**

You get one 54-piece plastic sprue (no glue, no paints included). The kit builds a single Daemon Prince on foot or with a 60mm flying base, with three head options (daemonic, horned, and a bare-faced variant), two weapon choices (a daemonic axe or a sword), and a trophy rack you can attach to the backpack. Also included: a 60mm round base and a separate 32mm base for the optional "familiar" mini—a small, clawed beast that adds a nice narrative touch. As shown in the product image, the model stands roughly 4 inches tall, comparable to a Primaris Lieutenant but broader and more imposing.

**Build quality and assembly notes**

The plastic is the standard Games Workshop grey, with crisp detail and almost no flash. Mold lines are minimal but present on the wing edges—a quick scrape with a hobby knife fixes it. Key joints (torso-to-legs, arm-to-shoulder) fit snugly; I needed no gap-filler except where the wing meets the upper back, which was a touch loose. The instructions have one vague step: the wing orientation. The manual shows a side view, but it’s easy to mount the wing upside-down. Dry-fit twice before gluing. Plastic cement (not included) is required—this is not a push-fit kit.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The modularity is the standout feature. One reviewer put it: “I built the axe and horned head, then my buddy built the sword and bare head—they look like completely different models.” Another noted the pose: “Finally a Daemon Prince that doesn’t look like he’s doing a squat. The lunge pose is aggressive but balanced.” The trophy rack, which includes severed helmets and a small shield, was called “a paint-perfect canvas for freehand details.” With 4.7 stars across 235 ratings, the consensus is clear: this kit delivers on value and presence.

**Where it falls short**

Honest downsides: The wings are large and somewhat fragile at the attachment point—if you transport the model without foam, expect a snap risk. Also, there is no magnetization guidance; if you want to swap heads or weapons after gluing, you’ll need to source 3mm magnets and plan your own drilling. Finally, the bare-faced head option looks slightly less “daemonic” than the other two—if you want pure menace, stick with the horned variant. One buyer noted: “The instructions don’t tell you which head matches which weapon for lore accuracy, but it doesn’t matter—build what you like.”

**Who should buy this**

- **Hobbyist painters** who want a large canvas for skin tones, glazing, and OSL—the bare chest and large wing membranes are ideal for blending.
- **Slaves to Darkness players** who need a durable, customizable centerpiece for matched play or narrative campaigns.
- **Gift-givers** buying for a Warhammer fan who already has paints and tools—this is a satisfying weekend project.

Skip it if you’re a beginner who’s never built a multi-part kit (start with a push-fit squad first) or if you want a purely tabletop-optimized model with no visual drama—there are cheaper, simpler options.

**FAQ**

*Q: Is this the same as the older Daemon Prince kit?*  
No. This is a 2023 redesign—taller, more detailed, and with more head/weapon options than the 2012 version.

*Q: Can I build it as a winged or foot model?*  
Yes, but the wings are not optional on the sprue—you choose either the flying base (with a clear plastic stem) or the standard base, and the wings attach in both cases. There is no “no-wing” option.

*Q: Does it come with a 40mm or 60mm base?*  
60mm base for the Prince, plus a separate 32mm base for the familiar. Tournament-legal for current matched play.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, imposing centerpiece that rewards careful painting and customizing. **Skip it if** you’re on a strict budget (there are cheaper hero models) or if fragile wing joints scare you.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The modularity and sculpt quality push it near-perfect; the wing fragility and vague instructions cost it half a star. A worthy addition to any Chaos collection.
