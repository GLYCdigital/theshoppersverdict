I've tested this stick in a Ryzen 5 3600 build and an older Intel i7-6700 system. At stock speeds, it runs at 2133MHz or 2400MHz depending on your CPU's default memory controller. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, there's zero RGB. If you're building a glass-panel showcase rig, this stick will look like a plain black bar next to your fancy fans and AIO. That's fine for office builds, frustrating for enthusiasts.

Second, overclocking headroom is limited. Most buyers report stable XMP profiles, but pushing beyond rated speeds — say, 3200MHz to 3600MHz — often requires manual voltage tweaks that don't always stick. The memory ICs are budget-tier, and they behave accordingly.

Third, this is DDR4. If you're planning a new build in 2026, DDR5 prices have dropped enough that investing in a DDR4 platform makes less sense unless you're upgrading an existing system. The value proposition narrows every quarter.

## Who Should Buy This

This RAM is perfect for three specific buyers:

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my motherboard?**
A: If your board supports DDR4 (not DDR5), it will almost certainly work. Check your motherboard's QVL list if you're being cautious, but the broad compatibility testing means most standard builds will POST immediately.

**Q: Can I mix this with other RAM brands?**
A: Technically yes, but I don't recommend it. Mixing different kits — even same speed and capacity — can cause instability. Buy a matched pair or kit for dual-channel reliability.

**Q: Is the heat spreader necessary?**
A: Not for daily use. DDR4 runs cool enough that the spreader is mostly cosmetic. It does help if you're running small form factor cases with poor airflow, but don't pay extra for it.
