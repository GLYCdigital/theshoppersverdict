If you've been hunting for a full-size mechanical keyboard that plays nice with both Mac and Windows without requiring a degree in firmware flashing, the Keychron C2 is the unassuming workhorse you've probably seen in every "best budget keyboard" roundup. But does it actually deliver, or is it just riding the Keychron name? I've spent weeks with this board, and here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the C2 itself (a hefty 104-key behemoth), a USB-C to USB-A cable, and — this is the sleeper hit — **both Mac and Windows keycap sets**. That means the Command/Option keys and the Windows/Alt keys are all included, plus the little switch on the side that toggles between operating systems. You're not buying extra keycaps or living with mismatched modifiers.

The board itself is a full-size layout: number pad, arrow keys, function row, the whole deal. It's 17.5 inches wide and weighs about 2 pounds — it's not going anywhere on your desk. The frame is a mix of ABS plastic with an aluminum top plate, which gives it a solid, non-flexy feel that belies its sub-$100 price tag.

## Real-World Performance

The C2 comes in two flavors: Gateron switches (Red, Blue, or Brown) or the hot-swappable version. **Get the hot-swappable one.** It's a $10-15 upcharge and it means you can pull out the switches with the included tool and drop in any Cherry, Kailh, or Gateron switch you want. If you buy the fixed-switch version and decide you hate the clicky Blues, you're buying a new keyboard.

Typing feel is solid. The Gateron Reds I tested are smooth and linear, with a satisfying bottom-out that isn't harsh. The stabilizers on the spacebar and enter key are decently tuned — there's a slight rattle on the spacebar, but nothing that'll drive you crazy. For a $75-ish keyboard, this punches well above its weight class.

The wired connection is genuinely plug-and-play. No software required, no driver installs, no "detect your device" loops. It registers on both macOS and Windows instantly. If you're someone who hates RGB software bloat, this is a breath of fresh air.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Scrolling through the 220 Amazon ratings at 4.4 stars, a few themes dominate. People also consistently mention the value. For the price, you're getting hot-swappable switches, dual-OS keycaps, and a solid aluminum top plate. That's a combination that was hard to find below $100 until this board hit the market.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. **The ABS keycaps are the weakest link.** After a few months of daily use, they'll start to develop a glossy, oily sheen on the most-used keys (W, A, S, D, spacebar, Enter). It's cosmetic, not functional, but if you're picky about aesthetics, budget $30-40 for a PBT keycap set down the road.

The wired-only design is also a limitation. If your desk setup is cable-averse or you want to switch between your MacBook and desktop without plugging in, this isn't the board for you. The C2 is firmly a "set it on your desk and leave it" keyboard.

And one more thing: the white backlighting (on the RGB version) is just that — white. There's no per-key color customization. If you want rainbow wave effects, this isn't your board. It's a lit keyboard, not a light show.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You're a Mac user who's tired of Windows-centric keyboards with wrong modifier keys
- You want a full-size layout with a numpad for spreadsheets, data entry, or accounting work
- You're new to mechanical keyboards and want to experiment with different switch types later
- You want a zero-fuss, no-software keyboard for a home office or cubicle

**Skip it if:**
- You need wireless connectivity for a clean desk or multi-device use
- You're a gamer who wants per-key RGB and macro programming
- You're a keycap enthusiast who'll immediately want to swap to PBT caps (just budget for it)

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with iPad or tablets?**
A: Yes, if your iPad has USB-C and supports keyboard input (iPadOS 16+ handles this well). But it's wired, so you'll need a USB-C hub or adapter. It's not a travel keyboard — this is a desk-bound board.

**Q: Can I use it on Windows without the Mac keycaps?**
A: Absolutely. Flip the switch to Windows mode and swap the keycaps. The included Windows keycaps are in the box, and the board defaults to Windows-style layout out of the box.

**Q: Is the hot-swappable version worth the extra money?**
A: Yes. Even if you never swap a switch, the hot-swap sockets are more durable and the board is easier to clean and repair. If a switch dies, you replace a $1 switch instead of a $75 keyboard.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Keychron C2 is the best full-size wired keyboard under $100 for Mac and Windows users who want reliability without fuss. It's not flashy, it's not wireless, and the keycaps will wear out eventually. But for a no-nonsense daily driver that feels more expensive than it is, this is a genuinely smart buy.

**Buy it if** you want a sturdy, full-size mechanical that works flawlessly with your Mac or PC and won't break the bank. **Skip it if** you need wireless or per-key RGB — those are dealbreakers you can't fix with a software update. For everyone else, the C2 is the quiet MVP your desk has been waiting for.
