**The office default that actually earns its reputation.**

Walk into any conference room in America, and you'll find these. The EXPO chisel-tip 8-pack is the quiet standard of whiteboard markers — not because it's the cheapest or the flashiest, but because it works exactly like it should. At 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, the numbers back the ubiquity. But is this the pack you should grab for your home office, classroom, or team? Let's break it down.

**What's actually in the box**

Eight markers: black, blue, red, green, and four more in the classic spectrum (typically orange, purple, brown, and a second blue or black depending on the batch). The chisel tip is the headline feature — you get a broad stroke for filling in diagrams or headers, then flip the angle for crisp, narrow lines for annotations or small text. One marker doing two jobs means fewer pens cluttering your board tray.

The low-odor claim isn't marketing fluff. These genuinely don't hit your nose like older solvent-based markers. If you've used cheap generics, the difference is immediate — no headache after an hour of brainstorming.

**Performance and build quality**

The ink flows consistently from the first stroke to the last. It dries fast enough that you won't smudge your work if you're writing quickly, but it stays wet long enough to correct small errors with a fingertip. Erasing is where EXPO separates from the pack: even after leaving a board untouched for a weekend, a dry cloth or standard eraser clears it without ghosting or staining. One buyer put it simply: *"These erase cleaner than any marker I've tried — no residue, no faint outlines left behind."*

The chisel tip holds its shape through moderate use. I've hammered a single black marker through two weeks of daily meeting notes, and it only started rounding after serious pressure was applied. That said, fine-line work loses its precision as the tip wears — plan for that if you're doing detailed flowchart work daily.

**Where it falls short**

The honest tradeoffs: The 8-count pack is a starter, not a sustainable system. Black and blue are the workhorses in any office, so you'll burn through those two while the orange and purple sit nearly untouched. You can't buy singles in this lineup; you're repurchasing the whole kit. For a busy team, that's a hidden cost.

Second, the colors are vivid but not archival. Red fades slightly on boards under harsh fluorescent light over several weeks — not a problem for normal use, but worth knowing if you leave permanent reference diagrams up.

Yes. EXPO low-odor markers work on glass, porcelain, and melamine surfaces. They erase cleanly from glass with a dry cloth — just avoid leaving them on for weeks without cleaning.

**Are they really low odor enough for a small office?**
