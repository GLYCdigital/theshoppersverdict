## What You Actually Get

The Marsrock board ships flat with the frame pre-assembled—no fiddly corners to snap together. Inside the box you'll find the board itself, a plastic marker tray, and a basic mounting kit (screws, drywall anchors, and brackets). The board comes in multiple sizes, but the US FT model refers to the standard US measurements (2x3, 3x4, 4x6 feet are the most common options). The surface is a painted steel sheet, which explains the strong magnetic hold—this isn't one of those flimsy cork-backed boards that sag after six months.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The aluminum frame is the standout feature here. It's thicker than what you'll find on comparably priced boards from AmazonBasics or ArtCreativity, and the rounded corners mean no sharp edges to catch on clothing or scratch drywall during installation. The magnetic surface is genuinely functional—one reviewer noted, "I can pin up 15 pages of printouts without any of them sliding down, even after a week." That's the kind of real-world testing that matters.

For dry-erase performance, the coating handles standard Expo markers well. Ghosting (the faint residue left behind after erasing) is minimal if you don't leave markers on for days at a time. A 4.6-star average with this many ratings suggests most buyers are satisfied, and the complaints that do exist are mostly about installation, not the board itself.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the weak points. The included mounting hardware is functional but basic—the plastic anchors are fine for drywall, but if you're mounting into concrete, brick, or metal studs, you'll need to buy your own hardware. The marker tray is also plastic, and several reviewers mentioned it cracking when they over-tightened the screws. It's a $5 fix to replace, but it's a minor annoyance on a product that otherwise feels premium.

Another tradeoff: the board's surface, while resistant to ghosting, isn't as slick as high-end porcelain boards. If you're a heavy user who writes and erases dozens of times daily, you may notice slightly more drag on the marker. For most home office or classroom use, this won't matter.

## Who Should Buy This

This board is ideal for:
- **Home office users** who need a reliable, good-looking board for planning and brainstorming
- **Small business owners** setting up a conference room on a budget
- **Teachers and tutors** who want a durable board that can handle daily classroom abuse
- **Parents** who need a family planning board that can survive kids and magnets

Skip it if you need a board for heavy industrial use, if you're installing into unusual wall materials, or if you're looking for a glass board aesthetic.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Does it come with markers and an eraser?**
A: No. The box includes the board, tray, and mounting hardware only. You'll need to buy dry-erase markers and an eraser separately. Most standard-size Expo markers work well.

**Q: How hard is the installation?**
A: It's a two-person job for boards 4 feet and larger. The frame is pre-assembled, so you're just marking stud locations and screwing in brackets. Count on 30-45 minutes for a clean install. The included anchors work fine for drywall; buy better anchors for other surfaces.

**Q: Will the magnetic surface hold heavy items?**
A: Yes—up to about 2 pounds per magnet. Multiple magnets distribute weight well. One buyer mentioned using it to hold a small whiteboard tray with supplies, and it held fine.
