## The $45 Question: Can a Third-Party TN760 Match Brother?

If you own a Brother monochrome laser like the HL-L2350DW or MFC-L2710DW, you already know the dirty secret: OEM toner is a racket. A genuine TN760 runs $80–$90. This TN760 replacement for Brother TN76 sells for roughly half that, promising the same 3,000-page yield. That's the kind of math that makes office managers sit up straight.

I tested this cartridge in a DCP-L2550DW over 200 pages of mixed documents. Here's the unfiltered truth.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the toner cartridge itself — no drum unit, no waste toner box, no extras. That's expected since Brother separates drum and toner. The cartridge feels solid, weighs about 1.5 pounds, and the green locking lever clicks into place without forcing. Installation took me 90 seconds, including pulling the orange protective seal.

The cartridge is labeled for TN760, TN730, and TN76 compatibility. That's the full range of Brother's current monochrome lineup — HL-L2300 through MFC-L2900 series. Check your printer model against that list before buying.

## Real-World Performance: Where It Shines

Print quality is the make-or-break for any toner. This one delivers. Text at 12-point Arial came out razor-sharp with zero feathering on standard copy paper. Even at 600 DPI, I couldn't distinguish output from a genuine Brother cartridge in a blind test. The black density is uniform — no light streaks down the middle or ghosting on the back of pages.

The 3,000-page yield is the headline feature. That's the TN760 high-yield spec, not the 1,200-page TN730 standard. If you print 30 pages a day, that's roughly three months between swaps. Several Amazon reviewers confirm hitting near the rated page count. One shopper wrote, *"I've printed over 2,500 pages and it's still going strong — the OEM never lasted this long for me."* That sentiment echoes across the 215 ratings.

Drum compatibility is another win. Brother drums (like the DR2300) are rated for 12,000 pages — this toner won't prematurely wear the drum because it uses the same physical dimensions and toner formula as OEM.

## Where It Falls Short

The chip situation is the biggest gripe. This cartridge doesn't have a reset chip. When the toner runs low, Brother printers show a "Toner Low" warning that can't be ignored through the driver on some models. You'll need to either shake the cartridge to redistribute remaining toner or override the alert manually. It's a minor annoyance, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Packaging is also thinner than OEM. A few reviewers reported light toner dust in the box — cosmetic, not functional, but messy if you're installing over a carpet. If that matters to you, open it over a trash can.

Grayscale gradients are slightly lighter in the mid-tones on photo or graphic-heavy prints. For pure text documents, you'll never notice. For printing charts with subtle shading, the OEM has a slight edge.
