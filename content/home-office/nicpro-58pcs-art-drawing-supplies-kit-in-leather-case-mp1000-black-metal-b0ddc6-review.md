# Nicpro 58PCS Art Drawing Supplies Kit in Leather Case, MP100: A Serious Kit for Serious Sketching

Most art kits under $50 are either a joke or a trap. You get 200 pieces of something that looks like a crayon factory threw up into a plastic box, and the "sketching pencils" are harder than a calculus exam. The Nicpro 58PCS kit is not that. It's the rare set that respects the craft — and the buyer.

## What You Actually Get

Open the leather case and you'll find a thoughtfully organized layout. The 24 colored pencils are the standout — they lay down pigment smoothly without the waxy skip you get from cheap sets. The 12 graphite pencils run from 6H (hard, light) to 8B (soft, dark), which covers the full range you need for realistic shading. There are also 3 charcoal pencils (though no loose charcoal), 3 blending stumps, a metal sharpener that doesn't chew up the wood, and a kneaded eraser that actually lifts graphite instead of smearing it.

The case itself is the surprise. It's rigid, not a floppy sleeve. The strap-and-loop system holds everything in place — I shook it upside down and nothing moved. The brass clasp feels solid, and the leather is thick enough that you won't worry about the pencils poking through.

## Real-World Performance

I've been sketching for 15 years, and I put this kit through a week of daily use — portrait studies, value scales, and color practice. The 8B pencil is properly soft; it deposits dense, dark strokes without needing to press hard. The H pencils are crisp for line work. The colored pencils are the real find: they're oil-based, so they blend well and don't break mid-stroke like cheaper wax cores.

One buyer put it plainly: "I was shocked at the quality for the price. The case alone is worth it." Another noted, "The pencils are buttery smooth. My 10-year-old uses it for school art, and I've started borrowing it for my own sketches."

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. There's no pastel or chalk — if you want to work in soft pastels, this kit won't cover you. The leather smell is strong out of the box; it dissipates after a week but can linger if you store it in a closed drawer. And while the sharpener works, it's not a crank-style — you'll need a few extra seconds per pencil. For the price, none of these are dealbreakers, but they're worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Art students** who need a portable, organized kit for classes and sketch sessions
- **Professionals** who want a travel backup that doesn't feel like a downgrade
- **Parents** buying for a serious young artist — this will last through high school
- **Skeptics** who've been burned by cheap kits and want something that won't disappoint

Skip it if you need pastels, or if you prefer loose pencils in a roll-up case — this is a fixed-layout organizer, not a free-form bag.

## FAQs

**Is the case real leather?**
Yes, it's genuine leather — not bonded or vinyl. It has a natural grain and will develop a patina over time.

**Can I add more pencils to the case?**
The straps are sized for the included 58 pieces. You can swap pencils in and out, but there's no room for significant additions.

**How does this compare to the Derwent or Faber-Castell kits?**
Those brands have slightly better individual pencil quality, but at 2-3x the price and often with fewer pieces. The Nicpro is the best value for the money.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a complete, well-built drawing kit that won't fall apart or frustrate you. **Skip it if** you're a pastel artist or you prefer building your own set piece by piece.
