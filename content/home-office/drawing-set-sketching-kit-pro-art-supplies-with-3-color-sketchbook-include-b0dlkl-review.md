# Drawing Set Sketching Kit Review: Is This 72-Piece Pro Art Set Worth Your Money?

If you're tired of buying art supplies piecemeal—a pencil here, an eraser there, and still missing the right tool when inspiration hits—this all-in-one sketching kit is designed to end that cycle. With a 4.7-star average from 235 ratings, it's clearly earning fans. But is it genuinely "pro" quality, or just another beginner's gift set? I've dug into the specs, the build quality, and what actual buyers are saying to give you the straight answer.

## What's Actually in the Box

This kit is comprehensive. You're getting 72 pieces total, which breaks down to:

- **12 graphite pencils** spanning 6B to 6H—that's the full range of hardness for everything from light construction lines to dense shadow work
- **3-color sketchbook** (the product name references this)—a spiral-bound pad with 60 sheets that handles both dry media and light washes
- **Charcoal sticks and pencils** in various hardnesses
- **Watercolor pencils** for adding color when the mood strikes
- **Blending stumps, tortillons, and erasers** including a kneaded eraser and precision eraser pen
- **Sharpener, sandpaper block, and cutting knife** for point maintenance

As shown in the product image, everything nests into a fold-out carrying case with elastic loops—a thoughtful layout that keeps each tool visible and accessible.

## Real-World Performance

The graphite pencils are the standout. The 6B glides with buttery smoothness, laying down dense, velvety marks that don't require excessive pressure. The harder grades (H and up) hold their points decently and produce clean, consistent lines without scratching the paper. For hatching, cross-hatching, and gradient work, this range covers everything you'd need for a semester of drawing classes or a year of hobby sketching.

The included sketchbook deserves specific mention. The 3-color aspect means the pad has three different paper tints (typically white, cream, and light gray), which lets you experiment with tonal contrast without buying separate pads. Paper weight is around 110gsm—not heavy enough for wet watercolor washes, but perfectly suited for pencil, charcoal, and light watercolor pencil activation.

One buyer noted, *"I've bought individual pencils that cost more than this entire set, and honestly, the quality is comparable. The 2B and 4B are my daily drivers now."* That sentiment echoes across the reviews—value per dollar is exceptional.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The charcoal sticks are the weakest link. Several buyers reported arriving pieces already broken or crumbling during use. If you primarily work in charcoal, consider this a backup kit rather than your primary setup.

The blending stumps are shorter than standard versions—about 4 inches versus the 6-inch industry norm. For small-format work (A5 or postcard size), they're fine. For larger pieces, you'll want longer tortillons for better leverage and control.

The carrying case, while organized, isn't built for heavy travel. The fabric is lightweight, and the zipper feels like it might not survive a semester in a backpack. It's fine for home use or studio storage, but don't expect a hard-shell case at this price point.
