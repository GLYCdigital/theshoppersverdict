If you're kiosk-mounting an iPad for a retail counter, a clinic check-in, or a warehouse work cell, you've probably learned the hard way that a $30 adhesive dock won't survive a week. The Mount-It! Anti-Theft Tablet Wall Mount (model B0DZ33GM6Q) approaches this differently — it treats your tablet like something worth stealing, because it is.

What you actually get in the box: the steel cradle assembly, a wall plate, a set of security screws with a hex wrench, the locking key, and a thin foam pad for the back of your tablet. No VESA adapter is included, but the backplate has standard 75x75mm holes, so it works with most articulating arms if you want to move it off the wall later. That's a thoughtful design choice for a product in this price tier.

**Build and daily use**

The cradle is all steel, powder-coated in a matte black that doesn't show fingerprints. The locking mechanism is a real keyed cam lock, not the flimsy spring-loaded clip you see on cheaper mounts. Once closed, the front lip covers the iPad's edges completely — you can't wedge a screwdriver under the bezel to pop it out. The backplate is thick enough that it won't flex when someone leans on the tablet while tapping through a checkout flow.

One detail that stands out in the 215 Amazon ratings: buyers repeatedly mention the home button cutout. The cradle is designed so the physical home button remains fully accessible, which matters for older iPads that still use Touch ID. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I was worried I'd have to use assistive touch to get home, but the button works perfectly through the cutout."* The charge port is also open, so you can plug in a cable without unlocking the mount. You just won't have a tidy channel to hide that cable — plan for a surface-mounted raceway or a hole drilled behind the plate.

**Where it falls short**

The included key is the only one you get. If it walks off, you're prying the lock open or ordering a replacement from Mount-It's support. Not a dealbreaker, but for a product whose entire purpose is security, a spare key in the box would cost them ten cents. Also, the rubber grips inside the cradle are soft but thin — if you crank down the tightening screws too aggressively, they'll leave faint compression marks on the iPad's aluminum edge. Snug is enough; don't torque it.

**Who should buy this**

This is a solid fit for three specific scenarios. First, small retail or food-service operations mounting an iPad for orders or self-checkout — the lock deters opportunistic theft. Second, clinics or dental offices using iPads for patient intake forms in shared waiting rooms; the all-steel build survives disinfectant wipes that degrade plastic mounts. Third, workshops or garages where a tablet is mounted near a workbench and needs to stay put against vibration.

It's less ideal if you need frequent angle adjustments — the wall plate is fixed, and while you can pair it with a VESA arm, that adds cost and complexity. It's also overkill if your tablet never leaves your sight and theft isn't a real concern; a simple friction holder would save you money.

**Real buyer feedback**

The 4.3-star average holds up across the ratings. The consistent praise centers on the lock quality and the sturdy cradle. As one verified buyer noted: *"Fits the iPad Pro 11 like a glove. The lock is smooth and the whole thing feels like it belongs in a commercial setting, not a hobby project."* The criticisms cluster around two things: the key situation and the lack of cable management. A few reviewers also mentioned the foam pad is a bit thin — if your iPad has a case, you'll need to remove it or double up the padding, because the cradle is sized for a bare tablet.

**FAQ**

*Will this fit an iPad with a thick protective case?* No. The cradle is machined to spec for bare iPads. You'll need to remove any case, including slim ones, or the front lip won't close flush.

*Can I lock it without the key?* No — the cam lock requires the key to engage and disengage. There's no padlock loop or tool-free option.

*Does it work with the iPad Pro 12.9?* The listing covers the Pro 11 and Air, but for the 12.9 you'll need to check the exact dimensions against the mount's stated range — the larger Pro can be tight in this cradle.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a genuinely theft-resistant, all-metal mount for a busy public or shared space and you don't mind routing your own cable. **Skip it if** you want tool-free angle adjustment or you expect to swap tablets frequently — the lock adds friction to every change.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It does exactly what it promises, with minor annoyances around the key and cable routing that don't undermine its core job. For the price, this is one of the more secure iPad wall mounts you'll find under $60.
