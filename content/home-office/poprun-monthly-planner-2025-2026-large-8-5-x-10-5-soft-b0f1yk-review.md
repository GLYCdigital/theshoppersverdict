If you've ever tried to cram a week's worth of appointments into a tiny A5 grid, you know the frustration. The POPRUN Monthly Planner 2025-2026 takes the opposite approach: it's big, it's unapologetically spacious, and it gives your schedule room to breathe. But size alone doesn't make a great planner — let's dig into whether this one earns a spot on your desk.

## What You Actually Get

The package is straightforward: one softcover planner measuring a full 8.5" x 10.5" with a durable, slightly textured polyurethane cover that resists scuffs. It runs from July 2025 through June 2026 — a deliberate choice that aligns with academic calendars and Q3-to-Q2 fiscal planning. Inside you'll find 12 monthly spreads, each with a generous grid for daily notes, plus a year-at-a-glance overview, a contacts section, and a few lined pages in the back for miscellaneous jottings. The binding is sewn, not glued, which matters more than most people think — it lays flat on your desk without you having to wrestle it open.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The headline feature here is the size, and it delivers. Each day gets a cell roughly 1.5 inches tall and 2 inches wide — enough space to write multiple appointments, deadlines, and a short to-do list without your handwriting shrinking to microscopic levels. For project managers, teachers, or anyone juggling multiple schedules, this is the difference between a usable planner and a decorative one.

The 100gsm paper is thicker than the 80gsm you'll find in most budget planners, which means standard gel pens and ballpoints won't bleed through. That said, if you're a fountain pen user, be warned: wet inks can ghost slightly on the reverse side. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you commit.

One thing that surprised me positively: the monthly grid includes a small notes column on each spread's right side. It's a minor addition, but it's genuinely useful for capturing stray thoughts without cluttering your date cells.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. First, there's no weekly spread — if you need to break down tasks day-by-day at a granular level, this won't serve you. Second, the paper, while decent, isn't premium. If you're used to Tomoe River or similar high-end stock, you'll notice the difference. Third, the cover, while durable, is matte and prone to showing fingerprints over time — minor, but noticeable.
