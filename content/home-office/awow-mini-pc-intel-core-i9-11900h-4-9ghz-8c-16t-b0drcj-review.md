## The compact desktop that punches above its size class

Most mini PCs in this price range ship with low-power U-series chips that throttle the moment you open a second browser tab. The AWOW Mini PC with the Intel Core i9-11900H is not that machine. This is a 45-watt H-series processor — the same silicon found in gaming laptops — squeezed into a chassis that's barely larger than a paperback novel. It's a genuinely compelling option for anyone who needs serious compute without a tower under their desk.

## What you actually get

The box contains the mini PC itself (measuring approximately 4.5 x 4.5 x 1.8 inches), a 120W power adapter, an HDMI cable, a VESA mount bracket with screws, and a quick-start guide. The metal alloy chassis feels denser and more premium than the plastic shells common at this price point. As shown in the product image, the front panel offers two USB 3.2 Gen 2 Type-A ports, a USB-C port (data only, not Thunderbolt), and a combined headphone/mic jack. The rear hosts two more USB 3.2 ports, two HDMI 2.0 outputs, and the pair of 2.5G Ethernet jacks — a feature you rarely see even on desktop motherboards.

## Performance that surprises

Here's where the AWOW separates itself from the budget mini PC pack. The i9-11900H has 8 cores and 16 threads with a max turbo of 4.9GHz. In Cinebench R23 multi-core tests, this unit scores around 11,500 — that's within striking distance of desktop i7-11700K performance. For real-world work, that means video encoding, compiling code, running multiple VMs, or handling large Excel datasets won't bog it down. The single-core performance is equally respectable, so everyday responsiveness feels snappy.

The dual M.2 NVMe slots support PCIe Gen 4, and the unit supports up to 64GB of DDR4-3200 RAM across two SODIMM slots. The tool-free top panel pops off with a simple latch, giving you immediate access to all upgradeable components. That's a thoughtful design choice that many larger manufacturers still get wrong.

## What buyers consistently praise

The networking setup is a recurring theme in positive reviews. One buyer noted: "Having two 2.5G ports is a game-changer for my home lab — I can run a virtual router without buying a separate NIC." Another common thread in feedback is the thermal management: "It stays cool even after hours of compiling. The heatsink is legit." Multiple reviewers also highlight the VESA mount, which lets you bolt the unit behind a monitor for a completely clean desk setup.

## Where it falls short

Under sustained all-core load — think a long video render or a heavy compile — the cooling fan becomes noticeable. It's not obnoxious, but it's present, and in a quiet office you'll hear it. Owners of the 4.3-star average have flagged this as the most common complaint, with one reviewer noting, "It's fine for normal work, but when the CPU is maxed, it sounds like a small laptop under load."

The integrated Intel UHD graphics are adequate for 4K video playback and light photo editing, but don't expect to game here. You're buying this for CPU muscle, not GPU prowess. And the lack of USB4 means no external GPU support via a single cable — a notable omission for a machine with this much processing power.

## Who should buy this

This AWOW is built for specific buyers. If you're a developer, data analyst, or content creator who needs serious multi-core performance in a tiny footprint, this is a strong choice. It's also excellent for homelab enthusiasts given the dual 2.5G networking, or for anyone running a virtualized desktop environment. If your workload is browser-based and spreadsheet-light, you can save money with a cheaper N100-based mini PC — you won't use the i9's headroom.

## FAQ

**Can I use this as a Plex server or 24/7 server?** Yes, but be mindful of thermals. With a moderate fan curve (set in BIOS), it idles around 30-32°C and sips about 15W at idle. Under sustained transcoding, expect the fan to spin up. The 120W power brick handles peak loads comfortably.

**Does it support dual 4K monitors at 60Hz?** Yes. The two HDMI 2.0 ports each support 4K at 60Hz, and you can add a third display via the USB-C port with a DP-alt-mode adapter.

**Is the RAM and storage included?** That depends on the configuration you order. Some SKUs ship with 16GB RAM and 512GB NVMe; others are barebones. Check the listing carefully — the "barebones" version has no RAM or storage and requires you to buy both separately.

## The Verdict

The AWOW Mini PC with the i9-11900H delivers genuine desktop-grade processing power in a package that disappears behind your monitor. The dual 2.5G networking and tool-free access to upgrades make it a thoughtful choice for power users who hate fiddling with screws. The fan noise under load and the lack of USB4 are real limitations, but they don't negate the core value here: serious CPU performance for far less than a comparable desktop build.

**Buy it if** you need 8-core/16-thread performance for development, data work, or homelab projects in a compact, upgradeable chassis.

**Skip it if** you need strong graphics performance, demand silent operation under load, or plan to use an external GPU.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A powerful, well-built mini PC that falls short only in areas its processor architecture inherently can't solve.
