**The 9.7-inch iPad from 2018 is old. That's exactly why this $33 case makes sense.**

Most accessory makers abandoned the 6th Gen iPad years ago. BLUTLOTUS didn't. And for anyone still rocking Apple's entry-level tablet from 2018 — the one with the A10 Fusion chip and first-gen Apple Pencil support — this keyboard case is one of the few affordable ways to turn it into a mini laptop. But "affordable" doesn't automatically mean "good." I spent a week typing on this thing to find out.

**What's in the box**

The package includes the keyboard deck, a silicone protective back shell, and a magnetic folio cover that folds into a stand. The keyboard attaches to the case via pogo pins on the bottom edge and magnets along the spine. Total weight lands around 1.1 pounds — heavy enough to notice but reasonable for a full keyboard.

The keyboard itself is powered by a built-in 180mAh battery. BLUTLOTUS claims 60 hours of typing between charges. In my testing, I got roughly 50 hours of mixed use before the low-battery warning popped up. Close enough. Charging is via USB-C, which is a small blessing — you're not hunting for a micro-USB cable in 2026.

**Typing experience: better than it has any right to be**

Here's where this case surprises. The scissor-switch keys have about 1.5mm of travel — shallow by mechanical standards, but deeper than most budget Bluetooth keyboards at this price. The layout is full-width, so your hands don't cramp from scrunching together. Key spacing matches a standard laptop deck.

The one real annoyance: no backlighting. In a dimly lit room, you're typing blind. That's a tradeoff I'd accept at $32.99, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Connectivity is Bluetooth 4.0, and pairing takes about three seconds. The keyboard auto-sleeps after 10 minutes of inactivity and wakes instantly when you tap a key. I didn't experience any dropped connections or input lag during my testing.

**Build quality and the case itself**

The back shell is a soft-touch silicone that grips the iPad's aluminum body without scratching it. Cutouts align perfectly with the camera, speakers, and Lightning port — I tested all of them. The folio cover magnets are strong enough to hold the lid closed in a bag but not so strong that you struggle to open it.

The stand hinge lets you adjust viewing angles from about 15 to 120 degrees. At the lowest angle, the keyboard tucks flat against the back, giving you a bare-tablet mode for reading. It's a genuinely useful feature that many competitors skip.

**What buyers are saying**

The 204 ratings average 4.1 stars. Most praise centers on value and compatibility. One verified buyer wrote, *"I bought this for my daughter's school iPad and it's held up through a full semester. The keyboard is way better than I expected for the price."* Another noted, *"Finally a case that fits the older 9.7 iPad without leaving gaps."*

Negative reviews cluster around two themes: the lack of a trackpad and occasional Bluetooth pairing hiccups after the iPad updates iOS. One reviewer complained, *"After updating to iPadOS 17, the keyboard sometimes needs to be toggled off and on in Bluetooth settings."* I didn't hit this bug, but it's a known quirk with older Bluetooth stacks on newer OS versions.

**Where it falls short**

The missing trackpad is the biggest functional gap. If you're used to a Magic Keyboard or Logitech Combo Touch, you'll miss cursor control. This case forces you to touch the screen for everything beyond typing.

Also, this fits *only* the 2018 6th Gen and the 2017 5th Gen 9.7-inch iPads. If you've upgraded to the 10.2-inch model that Apple released in 2019, this won't fit. Double-check your model number — it's A1893 or A1954 — before ordering.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students on a budget** who need a lightweight typing solution for notes and essays
- **Owners of the 2018 iPad** who refuse to upgrade but want modern functionality
- **Travelers** who want one case that protects and types without adding bulk

**Skip it if** you need a trackpad, work in dark environments frequently, or own a newer iPad model.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the keyboard work with iPadOS shortcuts?**
A: Yes. Command-Tab for app switching, Command-H for home, and arrow keys for navigation all work. The keyboard includes a dedicated home button, too.

**Q: Can you use the iPad while the keyboard is detached?**
A: Absolutely. The case works as a standard folio without the keyboard attached. The keyboard shuts off when detached, so it won't drain its battery.

**Q: Does it support the Apple Pencil?**
A: The case doesn't have a dedicated Pencil holder, but the 1st-gen Pencil charges via the iPad's Lightning port, which remains accessible.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you own a 2018 6th Gen iPad and want a functional typing case without spending $100+. **Skip it if** you need a trackpad or plan to upgrade your iPad soon.
