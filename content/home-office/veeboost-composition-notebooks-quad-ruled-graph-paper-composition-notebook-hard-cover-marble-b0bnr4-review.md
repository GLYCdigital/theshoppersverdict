If you've ever bought a "graph paper" notebook only to discover the grid is actually a faint dot matrix or a loose approximation of squares, you know the frustration. The VEEBOOST Composition Notebooks deliver the real thing: crisp 5mm quad ruling that stays uniform from page one through page 80. That alone puts it ahead of half the graph notebooks on Amazon.

**What you actually get**

The package is straightforward: a single hardcover composition notebook measuring 9.75 x 7.5 inches — the standard size that fits in most bags and on standard desk surfaces. The marble pattern on the cover is a classic, but the texture is what surprised me. It's a laminated hardcover that doesn't pick up fingerprints the way glossy notebooks do. The sewn binding is the real selling point here. Unlike the glued spines that crack and shed pages after a month, this one held up through daily use in my testing.

Inside, you get 80 sheets (160 pages) of 24 lb paper. That's heavier than the 20 lb standard you'll find in most composition books. The grid is a true 5mm square — not the 4mm or 6mm that some brands pass off as "quad ruled." The lines are printed in a light gray that scans cleanly if you digitize your notes, which math and engineering students will appreciate.

**Performance and real-world use**

I tested this notebook with a variety of writing tools: fine-tip pens, standard ballpoints, gel pens, and a mechanical pencil. The 24 lb paper handles ballpoint and pencil flawlessly — no bleed-through, no feathering. Gel pens are where it gets interesting. A standard 0.5mm gel pen writes cleanly with only minor ghosting on the reverse side. It's visible but not distracting, and nothing bleeds through to the next page. If you're a heavy-handed writer or prefer alcohol markers, this isn't the notebook for you — that's asking too much of any paper at this price point.

The binding deserves specific praise. When you open the notebook fully, it stays open without you holding it down. That's rare in composition books, where pages often flip back and close on you mid-equation. The cover also bends slightly without cracking, which matters if you shove it in a backpack.

**Where it falls short**

The paper weight is the main tradeoff. At 24 lb, it's better than most, but it's not fountain-pen friendly. If you use a wet nib with a free-flowing ink, expect feathering and see-through. The light gray grid is also a double-edged sword — it photographs and scans beautifully, but in a dimly lit lecture hall, the lines can be tough to see. And while the hardcover is durable, it adds weight. This isn't the lightest notebook for students who carry three or four at once.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for three specific buyers: students taking math, physics, or engineering courses who need precise graph paper for diagrams; professionals who keep handwritten project notes and need clean scans; and anyone who's tired of notebooks that fall apart mid-semester. If you're a bullet journaler who prefers dot grids, or a fountain pen enthusiast, look elsewhere.

*How many pages does it actually have?* 80 sheets, 160 pages total. The product listing is accurate on this.

*Does the hardcover lay flat when writing?* Yes, the sewn binding allows it to stay open flat without cracking the spine. That's a significant upgrade over glued alternatives.

**The Verdict**
