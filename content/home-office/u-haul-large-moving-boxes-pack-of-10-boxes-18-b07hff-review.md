Moving boxes are the most boring purchase you'll make all year — until the bottom of a cheap one gives out on a staircase and your cast-iron skillet becomes a bowling ball. That's why I've tested a lot of corrugated cardboard, and the U-Haul Large Moving Boxes (pack of 10) hold up better than most.

**What you actually get**

Ten 18" x 18" x 24" boxes, flat-packed. Each one is made from 200 lb. test, double-wall corrugated cardboard — that's the same spec U-Haul uses in their rental stores, not the flimsy single-wall stuff that collapses under a stack of hardcovers. The "Large" designation means roughly 4.5 cubic feet per box, which is the sweet spot for books, kitchen gear, and tools. They're not the giant wardrobe boxes (those are 24" x 24" x 45"), but for general household goods, these are the size you'll actually fill.

**Build quality and real-world use**

Here's the difference between these and the cheaper alternatives: the double-wall construction. When you fold one open, you can feel the rigidity. I loaded one with about 45 pounds of hardcover books and stacked three more boxes on top — zero bowing on the sidewalls after 48 hours. The bottom flaps overlap cleanly and don't gap, which means you're less likely to have small items escape through the seams.

The pre-printed labeling zones are a small but genuinely useful touch. Each box has dedicated spaces for "Fragile," "Books," "Kitchen," and "This End Up." You don't have to hunt for a blank spot on a box already covered in tape, and the markings stay legible — one reviewer noted, "The labels made unpacking so much easier. I knew exactly which boxes had the fragile stuff without opening every single one."

Assembly takes about 90 seconds per box. The creases are pre-scored accurately, so they fold crisply. You'll still need 2-inch packing tape — the boxes don't come with any — and I'd recommend reinforcing the bottom with an extra strip after folding.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews isn't an accident. Recurring themes in customer feedback:

- **Strength:** Multiple reviewers mention using them for heavy items without issues. One wrote, "I've moved 6 times in 5 years and these are the only boxes that never split at the seams."
- **Consistency:** U-Haul's manufacturing tolerances are tight — boxes in the same pack are genuinely identical, so they stack evenly. No wobbly towers.
- **Value:** At roughly $2 per box when you buy the pack, you're paying a small premium over generic brands, but the durability means you can reuse them for a second move — which drops the effective cost.

**Where it falls short**

The hand-holds are the weakest point. They're die-cut into the double-wall, which makes them stronger than a single-wall box, but there are no plastic handle inserts. With 40+ pounds in the box, those cutouts will dig into your fingers after a short carry. If you're moving a three-bedroom house, buy a set of box-handle grips or plan to use a dolly for the heavy loads.

Assembly is also on you. These arrive flat, and folding ten boxes before you start packing is a solid 15 minutes of busywork. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're in a time crunch, consider buying one assembled at a U-Haul store to test the size before committing to the pack.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY movers** packing a one-bedroom apartment or small house — the 18x18x24 size is perfect for dishes, books, and tools.
- **Frequent movers** who want boxes that survive a second or third move without replacing them.
- **Anyone shipping items** via freight or UPS — these meet most carrier requirements for double-wall boxes.

**Skip it if** you're moving a studio apartment and only need a few boxes (buy singles), or if you're packing only light items like clothing and linens (save money with single-wall boxes).

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these boxes come with tape or handles?**
A: No. You get ten flat boxes only. You'll need 2-inch packing tape, and if you're moving heavy items, add handle grips or use a dolly.

**Q: Can I use these for shipping via UPS or FedEx?**
A: Yes, for most items under 50 pounds. Double-wall construction meets standard carrier requirements. Just reinforce the seams with tape and use proper cushioning.

**Q: How much weight can these actually hold?**
A: The 200 lb. test rating refers to the bursting strength of the corrugated material, not the safe load. In practice, 40–50 pounds per box is comfortable. Beyond that, the bottom flaps start to stress and the hand-holds become a problem.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're moving and want boxes that won't fail mid-carry. The double-wall construction, clean assembly, and labeling system justify the slight premium over store-brand options. **Skip it if** you're only packing lightweight items or need just a couple of boxes — in those cases, cheaper alternatives make more sense.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Strong, dependable moving boxes with a minor ergonomic flaw. For the price of a fast-food lunch per box, you're buying peace of mind that your grandmother's china won't become a driveway mosaic.
