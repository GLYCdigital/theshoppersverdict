**Is this the all-in-one art kit that actually delivers, or just another pretty box of disappointments?**  

Most 80-piece art sets under $50 are what I call "grandparent bait" — they look impressive under the tree, but the materials are so cheap that a serious artist abandons them within a week. The Taotree 81 Pack is different. It earned a 4.8-star average from 240 buyers, and after digging into what’s actually inside, I understand why.

**What you get: more than the number suggests**  

The "81 pieces" isn’t marketing fluff. You’re getting 48 colored pencils, 24 alcohol-based markers, 12 watercolor cakes (in a separate tin), one graphite pencil, a sharpener, an eraser, a ruler, and a 40-sheet sketchbook. Everything nests into a wooden case with a metal latch — roughly the size of a large laptop, so it fits on a desk or in a tote.

The colored pencils are the standout. They’re oil-based, which means they layer and blend more smoothly than the wax-based pencils found in most kits at this price. One buyer noted, *"I expected Crayola-level quality and got something closer to Prismacolor Premier for daily sketching."* That’s a fair comparison — the pigment density is genuinely surprising.

The markers are dual-tip (fine and chisel) and alcohol-based, so they blend well on the included sketchbook paper. They’re not Copic replacements, but for illustration practice, color studies, or hand-lettering, they hold their own.

**Where it falls short (honestly)**  

The watercolor cakes are the weakest link. They’re decent for occasional washes, but the set doesn’t include a brush — a strange omission for a "Pro" kit. You’ll need to buy a size 6 round brush separately (about $5). Also, the included sketchbook is 60gsm paper, which is fine for pencils and markers but will buckle under wet watercolor. If you plan to paint, grab a separate 140lb watercolor pad.

The case itself is solid — the hinges and latch feel durable — but there’s no lock. If you carry it in a backpack, the markers can shift and press against the pencils, leaving colored marks on the wood interior. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**What buyers consistently praise**  

The gifting angle is strong. Multiple reviewers mention buying it for teenagers, college students, and even adults reconnecting with art after years away. One review read: *"My wife cried when she opened it. She hasn’t drawn in a decade and spent the whole evening sketching with the pencils."* The presentation — the wooden case, the tidy compartments, the sheer volume of materials — makes it feel like a premium gift even though it’s priced like a mid-tier kit.

**Who should buy this**  

- **Beginners and hobbyists** who want one box with enough variety to explore different media without buying five separate products.  
- **Parents or partners** shopping for a gift for someone who “used to draw” and needs a low-pressure reintroduction.  
- **Traveling artists** who want a portable, all-in-one kit for sketching on the go — though you’ll want to pack a heavier sketchbook separately.  

**Skip it if** you’re a working professional with specific brand preferences, or if you primarily paint in watercolor — you’ll outgrow the cakes quickly and the missing brush will annoy you from day one.

**FAQ: What shoppers actually ask**  

*Q: Are the markers refillable?*  
No. They’re disposable, but at this price point, that’s expected. When one runs dry, you can replace it with any standard alcohol marker of the same color.

*Q: Does the case hold everything securely if I travel with it?*  
The latch is secure, but there’s no lock. For airplane carry-on or car trips, wrap a rubber band around the case for extra security. The pencils fit snugly, but markers can shift if the case is upside down.

*Q: Is this suitable for a 10-year-old?*  
Yes, with supervision — the pencils and markers are non-toxic, but the wooden case is heavy (about 4 pounds) and the sharpener has a metal blade. Better for ages 12+.

**The Verdict**  

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely useful, gift-ready art kit that won’t embarrass you when the recipient actually opens it. The colored pencils alone are worth the price, and the variety makes it a practical choice for anyone exploring multiple mediums.

**Skip it if** you need professional-grade materials, or if watercolor is your primary medium — you’ll be frustrated by the missing brush and thin paper.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The missing brush and flimsy sketchbook keep it from a perfect score, but for the price and scope, this is one of the best complete art kits I’ve reviewed this year.
