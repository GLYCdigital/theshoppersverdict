## What You Actually Get

The package contains a single staple remover — no frills, no carrying case, no spare parts. What you get is a compact tool roughly 4 inches long with a two-ended design:

- **End 1 (pry tip):** A flat, angled blade that slides under the staple's crown to lift it
- **End 2 (puller claws):** A forked gripper that grabs the staple's legs from the back side and pulls them out completely

The body is plastic with a rubberized grip section, and there's a small magnet embedded near the puller end. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward, no-nonsense design — nothing fancy, but nothing that screams "cheap" either.

## How It Actually Performs

I tested this on three scenarios: single-page documents, 10-page reports, and 25-page stapled packets.

**Single pages: Excellent.** The pry tip slides under the staple cleanly, lifts it in one motion, and the staple pops out without tearing the paper. This is the tool's sweet spot — quick, clean, and effortless.

**10-page reports: Very good.** The pry tip still works, but you need a bit more leverage. The puller claws come into play here — they grip the staple legs and extract them fully, which is a game-changer if you're removing staples to photocopy or scan documents. No more half-removed staples snagging on the copier glass.

**25-page packets: Struggles.** The claws can't get a solid grip on deeply clinched staples from heavy-duty staplers. You'll end up using the pry tip more aggressively, which risks slightly tearing the top page. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real limitation.

The ergonomic grip is genuinely comfortable — I removed 40+ staples in one sitting without hand fatigue, which I can't say for the classic metal "duck-bill" style removers. The magnet, however, is underwhelming. It's supposed to catch staples as they're pulled out, but in practice, the staples still fly off. Plan on sweeping up after a big unstapling session.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the magnet and heavy-stack limitations, there's one more thing to note: this isn't a precision tool. If you're removing staples from delicate documents (old photographs, archival papers, vintage books), the pry tip is too aggressive. For that use case, you'd want a specialized flat-head remover with a thinner profile.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this damage my documents?**
A: On standard 20-lb paper, no — the pry tip lifts staples cleanly without tearing. On thin or fragile paper (like receipts or tissue paper), you risk tearing. Test on a scrap page first.

**Q: Can it handle staples from heavy-duty staplers?**
A: Not reliably. The puller claws struggle with thick, tightly clinched staples from 50+ page capacity staplers. Stick to standard staples for best results.

**Q: Is the magnet strong enough to catch staples?**
A: No. It catches the occasional staple but most still fly off. Keep a magnet or your foot handy.
