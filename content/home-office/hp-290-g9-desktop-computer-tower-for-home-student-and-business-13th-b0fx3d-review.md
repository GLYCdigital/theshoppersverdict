Most desktop towers under $500 make you choose between "cheap" and "actually usable." The HP 290 G9 tries to split the difference, and based on 210 Amazon ratings averaging 4.2 stars, it's mostly succeeding. But "mostly" is doing a lot of work here — let me tell you exactly where this machine shines and where it'll frustrate you.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the tower itself, a basic USB keyboard and mouse, and the power cable. No monitor, no speakers. The chassis is a traditional mini-tower measuring roughly 6.5 x 13.3 x 12 inches — compact enough to slide under a desk but with enough internal space for real upgrades. The front panel gives you two USB-A ports, a headphone jack, and an optical drive on most configurations, which is increasingly rare and genuinely useful if you still burn CDs or watch DVDs.

**Performance where it counts**

The star here is the 13th-gen Intel Core i5 processor. In everyday tasks — web browsing with a dozen tabs open, Microsoft Office, streaming video, video calls — this thing doesn't break a sweat. Boot times are quick, and the included 8GB of DDR4 RAM handles typical student or small-business workloads fine. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"Set it up for my grandmother and she's been using it daily for video calls and email without a single hiccup."*

The 512GB NVMe SSD is the other smart choice HP made. It's noticeably faster than the older SATA drives still found in many budget towers, and you'll feel the difference every time you open a large file or restart.

**Where the 290 G9 falls short**

Let's be blunt: the integrated Intel UHD graphics are not for gaming. If you're hoping to play anything beyond older titles or light esports at low settings, look elsewhere. This is a work machine, not a play machine.

The bigger gotcha is the configuration lottery. Some units ship with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth built in; others require you to add a USB adapter. Multiple buyers have noted this discrepancy. One frustrated review stated: *"Product description said Wi-Fi but my unit didn't have it. Had to buy a $15 USB adapter."* Check the specific listing you're ordering from and confirm wireless connectivity before you click buy.

The included keyboard and mouse are functional but flimsy. Plan to spend $30–40 on a decent wireless set if you'll be typing more than an hour a day.

**Upgradeability is the hidden value**

Here's what makes this tower worth considering long-term: you can pop the side panel off without tools. RAM slots are accessible, there's a free 3.5-inch drive bay, and the power supply can handle a modest GPU upgrade later. This isn't a sealed laptop-style box — it's a real desktop that can grow with you. Adding another 8GB of RAM costs about $20 and will noticeably improve multitasking.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students** who need a reliable machine for research, essays, and Zoom classes without breaking their budget
- **Home office workers** running standard business software who want a desktop that won't feel obsolete in three years
- **Small business owners** needing dependable machines for accounting, inventory, or customer management
- **Families** wanting a shared computer for homework and streaming

Skip it if you're a gamer, a video editor, or someone who needs maximum performance in a tiny form factor — look at mini-PCs or gaming towers instead.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Can I add more RAM later?* Yes. The 290 G9 has two SODIMM slots (not standard desktop RAM), and adding another 8GB stick takes about five minutes. Just make sure you buy laptop-style SODIMM memory.

*Does it come with Windows pre-installed?* Most configurations ship with Windows 11 Home ready to go. You'll just need to complete the setup wizard on first boot.

*Is this quiet enough for an office?* Yes, the fan noise is minimal under normal use. It's not silent, but you won't notice it during a video call or while working.

**The verdict**

The HP 290 G9 doesn't try to impress anyone — it just works. The processor is genuinely modern, the SSD is fast, and the tool-less case makes future upgrades painless. The integrated graphics and configuration inconsistencies are real drawbacks, but for the price point, this is a solid, honest desktop for everyday computing.

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, upgradeable workhorse for school, home, or small business without spending premium money.

**Skip it if** you need gaming performance, want Wi-Fi guaranteed out of the box, or expect a premium-feeling keyboard and mouse included.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good budget desktop with a couple of asterisks worth knowing about before you buy.
