Let’s get one thing straight: the Logitech K480 is not a mechanical keyboard, and it’s not trying to be. It’s a $40-ish Bluetooth board that solves a very specific, very real problem — juggling a laptop, tablet, and phone without swapping peripherals. After 220 Amazon ratings holding a 4.4 average, the consensus is clear: this thing works. But it’s not without quirks. Here’s the full breakdown.

**What’s in the Box**

You get the keyboard itself, a 2-year AA battery pre-installed, and a Logitech Unifying receiver dongle (though most users will just use Bluetooth). No USB-C cable, no carrying case. The K480 measures roughly 11.7 x 7.9 x 1.6 inches and weighs 1.5 pounds — heavier than it looks, which is actually a plus. The rubberized base grips desks firmly, and the top has a molded slot that props up a phone and a tablet at the same time. That cradle is the K480’s party trick. As one verified buyer put it, *"I keep my iPad in the slot and my iPhone leaning next to it. Everything’s in one line of sight. It’s replaced my laptop stand for quick notes."*

**Performance and Build Quality**

The K480 pairs over Bluetooth 3.0 with a twist: a dial on the top-left corner lets you flip between three paired devices instantly. No re-pairing, no menus. Turn the dial, start typing. In testing, switching latency was under a second. The key layout is laptop-style scissor switches with 1.8mm of travel — shallow but tactile, with a crisp bottom-out. The spacebar and enter key feel solid, not mushy. However, the keys are *loud*. Every keystroke produces a distinct clack. In a quiet home office, you’ll hear it. In a shared cubicle, your neighbor will hear it too.

Battery life is rated at 24 months from a single AA. That’s respectable, but the K480 lacks any backlighting, and the Bluetooth connection doesn’t support multi-device syncing like Logitech’s newer MX series. It’s a workhorse, not a showhorse.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

- **The cradle**: Repeatedly cited as the reason people buy this over a standard keyboard. *"I can type on my iPad and glance at my phone for messages without picking anything up,"* one reviewer noted.
- **Multi-device simplicity**: The dial is physical and obvious — no software needed. Works with Windows, macOS, iPadOS, Android, and even ChromeOS.
- **Build heft**: Several reviews mention the weight as a feature, not a flaw. It stays put on a lap desk or a cluttered desk.

**Where It Falls Short**

- **No backlighting**: If you work in dim lighting, this is a dealbreaker. The white-on-gray keycaps are low-contrast.
- **Key noise**: The clack is real. Logitech’s quieter K380 is a better pick if you need silence.
- **Bluetooth 3.0 limitations**: Pairing sometimes drops after long idle periods; you’ll press a key and wait a second for the connection to wake. Not a dealbreaker, but noticeable.
- **The cradle angle**: A 12.9-inch iPad Pro in the slot can feel top-heavy. Smaller tablets and phones are fine.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The multi-device worker**: You answer texts on your phone, reference docs on a tablet, and type on a laptop — all day. The K480 centralizes that chaos.
- **The desk minimalist**: One keyboard for everything, no dongles, no software, no charging cables.
- **The budget-conscious**: At its typical street price, it undercuts most multi-device keyboards with a built-in stand.

**Skip It If**

- You need silent keys for a shared workspace.
- You require backlighting for late-night sessions.
- You prefer a slim, travel-friendly board — this one is bulky.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I connect the K480 to a Windows PC and iPad simultaneously?**
A: Yes, up to three devices at once. Pair each via Bluetooth, then use the dial to switch. It remembers all three pairings, so no re-syncing.

**Q: Does the cradle hold a Kindle or an e-reader?**
A: The slot is about 0.8 inches deep and widens slightly. A 6-inch Kindle fits, but a 7-inch tablet with a thick case may be snug.

**Q: Is the K480 compatible with USB-C-only laptops?**
A: Yes, via Bluetooth. The Unifying receiver is USB-A, but you don’t need it if your laptop has Bluetooth. Some users pair via the dongle on a desktop and Bluetooth on a laptop.

**The Verdict**

The Logitech K480 is a purpose-built tool that nails its primary job: instant, reliable switching between three devices with a one-of-a-kind cradle. It’s not the quietest keyboard, and it’s not the prettiest, but it’s one of the most practical for anyone living across multiple screens. The 4.4-star average holds up — this is a 4.2/5 product that earns its rating through pure utility.

**Buy it if** you want a single keyboard to rule your phone, tablet, and laptop without fumbling with settings.

**Skip it if** you need silent, backlit keys or a keyboard you can toss in a backpack daily.
