## Is this planner actually worth your desk space?

Most to-do notebooks fall into two camps: cheap spiral pads that fall apart, or $40 "productivity systems" that require a PhD to understand. The ZICOTO Beautiful To Do List Notebook sits in a rare middle ground — it's a no-nonsense daily planner with genuinely premium touches, and at 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, buyers largely agree. But it's not perfect. Here's the real breakdown.

## What you actually get

The notebook measures a compact 8.5 x 6 inches — smaller than a standard sheet of paper, which makes it feel more like a journal than a desk pad. You get 160 pages (80 days of planning) with a lay-flat spiral binding that actually works. The cover is a soft-touch matte finish that resists fingerprints, though it's not waterproof, so keep it away from coffee mugs.

The layout is where this shines. Each day gets a two-page spread: the left page has 12 numbered task lines with checkboxes, plus a priority section for your top three. The right page offers a blank notes area with a subtle dot grid and a small "wins of the day" box. It's flexible enough for work tasks, meal planning, or hybrid use.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The 100gsm paper is the standout feature. Most planners in this price range use 70-80gsm stock that ghosts with a standard ballpoint. I tested this with a Pilot G2 gel pen, a Sharpie pen, and a mildliner highlighter — zero bleed-through, minimal ghosting even with the highlighter. That's genuinely impressive at this price point.

The spiral binding deserves specific praise. It's a twin-wire design that lays completely flat on a desk, and the coils are coated so they don't snag on sleeves or catch on bag liners. One buyer noted, "I've bought three of these now because I keep giving them away. The binding holds up after months of daily use." Another mentioned, "The paper quality shocked me — my fountain pen doesn't bleed at all, which I never expected from a notebook under $20."

## Where it falls short

No product is flawless, and this one has two honest weaknesses. First, there's no closure band or elastic strap. If you toss this in a backpack, pages will curl and the cover can bend. Second, the daily format is rigid — each day has exactly 12 task lines, so heavy days will feel cramped and light days feel wasteful. There's no weekly or monthly overview, so if you need long-term planning, this isn't your tool.

One more consideration: at 80 days, you'll need to reorder roughly every 2-3 months with daily use. That's fine if you love it, but it's a recurring cost to factor in.

## Who should buy this

- **The task-focused professional** — you live by checkboxes and want a clean, dedicated space for daily work items without flipping through calendar pages.
- **The journaling hybrid** — you like structure but also want room for freeform notes, and the dot-grid right page delivers that balance.
- **The gift-giver** — this makes a genuinely thoughtful, practical gift for students, teachers, or anyone who swears by paper planning.

Skip it if you need weekly or monthly views, or if you're rough on your gear and need a durable cover with a closure mechanism.
