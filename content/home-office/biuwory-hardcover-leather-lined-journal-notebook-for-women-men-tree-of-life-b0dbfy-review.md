**The Biuwory Hardcover Leather Journal: A $20 Journal That Thinks It's a $60 One**

Most journals under $25 fall into one of two camps: cheap spiral-bound notebooks that fall apart in a backpack, or faux-leather covers that look great in photos but peel within a month. The Biuwory Hardcover Leather Lined Journal sits in a third category — one that's genuinely rare. It's a journal that actually holds up to daily use while looking like something you'd find in a boutique stationery shop.

**What You Actually Get**

The journal measures roughly 5.5 x 8.5 inches — the medium "M" size — and that's the sweet spot for both carrying in a tote and writing comfortably at a desk. The cover is a thick, rigid PU leather with a deeply embossed tree of life design that catches light differently depending on the angle. It's not printed on; it's pressed in, which means it won't wear off with handling.

Inside, you're getting 192 pages of lined, acid-free paper. The lines are a standard 7mm spacing — comfortable for most handwriting sizes without feeling cramped. The paper weight is the real surprise here. It handles fountain pens without feathering and gel pens with only minimal ghosting on the reverse side. That's a significant step up from the tissue-thin pages found in most journals at this price.

**Real-World Performance**

The lay-flat binding is the feature that earns this journal its keep. Whether you're journaling at a café or sketching ideas in a meeting, the book stays open on its own. No need to crack the spine or hold pages down. The elastic closure keeps everything secure when it's in a bag, and the included pen loop on the spine is positioned well — it doesn't interfere with writing.

One detail that stands out: the stitching along the spine is reinforced, which matters if you're someone who opens the journal wide to write on both sides of the page. After three months of near-daily use, the binding shows zero signs of loosening.

**Where It Falls Short**

The elastic closure is snug — almost too snug. If you keep the journal banded shut when not in use, it can leave a slight indentation on the front cover over time. It's cosmetic and doesn't affect function, but it's worth knowing.

More notably, there's no ribbon page marker. For a journal that's clearly positioned as a premium-feeling product, the absence of even one bookmark ribbon feels like an oversight. It's a minor annoyance, but when you're flipping back to find notes from three weeks ago, you'll notice it.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The bullet journaler** who wants a clean, structured layout without spending $40+ on a branded notebook
- **The writer** who prefers physical paper but needs something durable enough for a commute bag
