If you've ever watched a greeting card flop over like a wet noodle or had a DIY project bleed ink straight through the page, you know the difference between "paper" and "cardstock." This 110 lb option sits firmly in the latter camp. With a 4.6 average from 229 raters, it's clearly doing something right — but let's talk about whether it's the right thick paper for your specific use case.

## What You Actually Get

The package delivers 100 sheets of 8.5x11 inch cardstock, weighing in at a solid 110 lb (about 300 gsm). That's the sweet spot for most crafters and office workers — heavy enough to feel substantial for business cards or invitations, but not so thick that it jams your home printer. The sheets have a smooth, uncoated finish that takes ink well without the glossy glare that makes writing with pens feel slippery.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this in both an inkjet and a laser printer. Inkjet results were sharp with no feathering on text, and colors stayed vibrant without soaking through to the other side. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I've been through a dozen brands of cardstock for my Etsy shop, and this is the first that doesn't curl at the edges after a full-bleed print." That matches my experience — curl resistance is genuinely above average here.

Cutting is where this paper really shines. A paper trimmer slices through it cleanly, and even intricate die-cut shapes come out with smooth edges. There's minimal dust or paper lint, which means less cleanup and fewer stray fibers stuck to your glued projects. For double-sided printing, the opacity is solid — you'd have to hold it up to strong light to see any ghosting.

Scanning through the 229 ratings, a few themes pop up repeatedly. Crafters love the versatility: "Perfect for card making, scrapbooking, and even my son's school projects. ## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. This paper is not acid-free or archival quality. For memory books or documents you want to last decades, you'll want a lignin-free option. One reviewer mentioned, "I didn't realize it wasn't archival until I noticed slight yellowing on cards I'd stored for two years." That's a fair tradeoff for the price point, but it matters if you're making heirloom-quality items.

Also, you're limited to standard letter size. Scrapbookers using 12x12 layouts will need to look elsewhere. And while it handles most home printers fine, some high-volume commercial printers with tight paper paths might struggle with the 110 lb thickness.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for **home crafters** making cards, invitations, or party favors. It's also great for **small business owners** printing business cards or product tags without outsourcing. **Teachers** preparing classroom materials will appreciate the durability — laminating becomes optional for most projects. Skip it if you need archival-grade paper, oversized sheets, or ultra-heavy 140 lb+ stock for specialty projects like box templates.

## FAQ

**Will this work in my home printer?**
Yes, for most inkjet and laser printers. The 110 lb weight is within standard printer specifications. If your printer has a rear paper feed, use that for best results. Avoid the bypass tray on some models, which can bend heavier stock.

**Can I write on it with regular pens?**
Absolutely. The uncoated surface takes ballpoint, gel, and even fountain pens well. Marker ink won't bleed through, though alcohol-based markers may ghost slightly on the back.

**Is it double-sided printable?**
Yes. The opacity prevents show-through with standard text and graphics. For heavy full-bleed coverage, print on the same side you'll display — that's true for any cardstock, not just this one.
