## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the drum unit itself — pre-assembled with the green OPC drum, wiper blade, and charge roller — plus a basic instruction sheet. You'll need to remove the orange protective strip and give the drum a gentle rock side-to-side before installing. It's compatible with the HL-L3210CW, HL-L3230CDW, HL-L3270CDW, and MFC-L3710CW/L3750CDW series, which covers the vast majority of Brother's current color laser lineup.

## Real-World Performance

The rated yield is 15,000 pages, identical to Brother's OEM spec. In practice, most users report getting close to that number before seeing any degradation. Print quality holds up well: text is sharp with no character ghosting, and color gradients come out clean without the horizontal banding that plagues cheaper compatibles.

One thing worth noting — the drum ships without toner. That's expected for a drum-only replacement, but first-time buyers sometimes miss this and get confused when their printer still won't print. You'll need to keep your existing toner cartridges or order new ones separately.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The praise in the reviews is remarkably consistent. One verified buyer wrote: *"Saved $60 over the Brother brand and the prints look identical to what I got from the OEM unit."* Another long-term user noted: *"Installed in my HL-L3230CDW, reset the counter, and it's been running flawlessly for 4,000 pages."*

The most common complaint isn't about print quality — it's about the reset process. It's a 30-second procedure but catches people off guard.

## Where It Falls Short

The build quality is the biggest tell that this isn't OEM. The plastic housing has a slightly cheaper feel, and a handful of reviewers mentioned the drum roller cover doesn't seat as snugly as the Brother original. It doesn't affect performance in most cases, but it's worth handling the unit gently during installation.

There's also the reset quirk. If your printer doesn't auto-detect the new drum, you'll see the "Replace Drum" message persist even after installation. The fix is straightforward — hold down the OK button on the printer panel to access the reset menu — but it's an extra step you won't have with the OEM part.

## Who Should Buy This

This is a no-brainer for small offices, home offices, and anyone who prints more than a few hundred pages a month. If you're running a Brother color laser for client documents, labels, or school projects, the 60% savings per drum replacement adds up fast. **Q: Does it come with toner?**
A: No — this is a drum unit only. You'll need to keep your existing toner cartridges or install new ones. The drum and toner are separate components in Brother's design.

**Q: How do I reset the drum counter?**
A: Open the front cover, press and hold the OK button for about 3 seconds until the LCD shows "Reset Drum," then press OK again to confirm. Some users report their printer auto-detects the new drum, but if not, this manual reset does the trick.
