# Planner 2026-2027 – HPP Review: The 18-Month Workhorse

Most planners start in January and force you to either waste pages or wait months before writing anything down. The HPP Planner 2026-2027 sidesteps that entirely by running from **July 2026 through December 2027**. If your life runs on a school calendar, a fiscal year, or you just want a fresh start mid-summer, this thing is ready when you are.

I've spent real time with it — testing the binding, the paper, the layout flow. Here's what you need to know before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

The planner spans 18 months across roughly 380 pages. You get:

- **Monthly overview spreads** with unruled notes sections and a small habit tracker column
- **Weekly spreads** with hourly breakdowns from 7 AM to 8 PM plus a prioritized to-do list on the right side
- **A 26-page notes section** at the back with dot-grid pages, not just lined
- **Two ribbon bookmarks** — one for the current week, one for the month. They're stitched in, not glued, so they won't pull out

The hardcover is a matte, textured finish that doesn't pick up fingerprints. As shown in the product image, the layout is clean and uncluttered — no distracting graphics or motivational quotes eating your writing space.

## Performance and Build Quality

The stand-out feature is the **lay-flat binding**. The spine is stitched, not glued, so the book opens completely flat on a desk without you holding it down. That sounds minor until you've tried to write in a planner that fights you. This one doesn't.

Paper weight is listed at 120gsm, and it holds up. I tested it with a Pilot G2, a Sharpie pen, and a mildliner highlighter. Zero bleed-through. Light ghosting if you press hard with a gel pen, but nothing that makes the back side unreadable. If you're a fountain pen user, this is one of the few planners under $30 where that's viable.

The weekly layout gives you a solid hour-by-hour column for time-blocking, and the to-do list is wide enough for actual sentences, not just cryptic abbreviations.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Across the 229 ratings, consistent themes emerge. One reviewer noted: *"Finally a planner that starts when my contract year starts. The July start is perfect."* Another said: *"The paper quality shocked me. I use mildliners and nothing bleeds through. My old planner looked like a crime scene."*

The durability of the hardcover gets repeated mentions — buyers report carrying it daily for months without cover wear or spine cracking.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has two honest flaws.

**No pen loop.** For a planner clearly aimed at daily carry, omitting a pen loop is a miss. You'll need a separate pen holder or clip, which is annoying when you're digging through a bag.

**Printed tabs crease.** The monthly tabs are printed cardstock, not reinforced plastic. If you shove this in a backpack or tote daily, those tabs will show wear within a few months. It's cosmetic, not functional, but worth knowing.

Also note: if you're a minimalist who wants one page per week, this isn't it. The hourly grid takes up significant space, and the design assumes you time-block. If you only need a basic task list, you're paying for layout you won't use.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Teachers and professors** — the July start aligns with academic calendars, and the durable cover survives a tote bag
- **Project managers or consultants** who live by time-blocking and need hourly visibility
- **Fountain pen users** — the paper genuinely supports wet inks without bleed-through
- **Anyone starting a new role or goal mid-year** who doesn't want to wait for January

Skip it if you need a simple undated layout or you're a hardcore traveler who needs a pen loop built in.
