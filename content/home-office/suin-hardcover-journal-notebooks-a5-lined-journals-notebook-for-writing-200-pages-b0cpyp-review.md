The SUIN A5 hardcover journal is the kind of notebook that makes you wonder why you ever settled for flimsy spiral binds. With 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right notebook for *your* workflow? Let's break it down.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes one A5 notebook (5.8 x 8.3 inches) with 200 numbered pages — that's 100 sheets of 100gsm paper. The cover is a textured faux leather that looks more expensive than the price tag suggests. There's an elastic closure band, a ribbon bookmark, and an inner back pocket for loose notes or receipts. The stitched binding is the headline feature here, and it shows — this thing lays completely flat the moment you open it, no breaking-in required.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The paper is the star of the show. At 100gsm, it's noticeably thicker than the 70-80gsm you'll find in most budget notebooks. I tested it with a Pilot G2 gel pen, a Lamy Safari fountain pen, and a Sharpie for good measure. The gel and fountain pen ink dried clean with zero bleed-through. There was slight ghosting with the Sharpie, but that's expected — and honestly, it's better than most notebooks at this price point. The pages have a subtle cream tint that's easier on the eyes than stark white.

The cover has a nice give to it — firm enough to write on without a desk, but not so rigid that it cracks at the spine. The stitched binding is the real durability win here. One reviewer noted, *"I've been using this daily for six months and the spine shows zero signs of wear. My old Moleskine fell apart after three."* That tracks with the construction quality.

**Where It Falls Short**

The elastic band is the weakest link. It's functional, but it doesn't have the tension of premium brands — if the notebook is jammed in a bag with other items, the band can slip off. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

The line spacing is also worth noting. At 6mm, you get about 20 lines per page. For people who write small and pack a lot of information per page, this feels wasteful. You'll burn through the 200 pages faster than you might like. If you prefer wider spacing for readability, you'll love it. If you're a dense note-taker, you may want to look elsewhere.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Journalers and diary keepers**: The lay-flat design and thick paper make it perfect for long writing sessions on the couch or at a desk.
- **Fountain pen enthusiasts**: This is a budget-friendly option that won't feather or bleed with most ink types.
- **Students and professionals**: The durable cover and stitched binding will survive a semester or a year of daily use without falling apart.

**Skip it if** you need dot-grid or graph pages (this is lined only), or if you prefer ultra-thin notebooks that slip into a pocket.

**Real Buyer Feedback**

The 4.7-star rating holds up. The most common praise centers on the paper quality and the lay-flat binding. One customer wrote, *"I've bought three more since the first one. The paper takes fountain pen ink beautifully."* The most frequent complaint is the elastic band tension — a recurring theme in reviews.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the paper bleed with fountain pens?**
A: No. The 100gsm paper handles most fountain pen inks without bleed-through. Heavier inks or flex nibs may show slight ghosting on the reverse side, but it's minimal.

**Q: How many pages does it have?**
A: 200 numbered pages (100 sheets). The pages are numbered, which is a nice bonus for indexing or table of contents.

**Q: Is it truly lay-flat?**
A: Yes. The stitched binding opens to 180 degrees and stays open without any pressure. This is the standout feature.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, fountain-pen-friendly journal that lays flat and looks professional — without spending $30+ on a premium brand.

**Skip it if** you need tight line spacing for dense notes, or if a weak elastic band would drive you crazy.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A solid, dependable notebook that punches above its weight class. The lay-flat binding and paper quality alone justify the price. The elastic band and line spacing are minor tradeoffs, but they don't diminish what is otherwise an excellent everyday writing companion.
