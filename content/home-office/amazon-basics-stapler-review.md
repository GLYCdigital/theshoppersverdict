## Build and Design

The Amazon Basics stapler is **all plastic** — the body, the mechanism, even the spring-loaded staple pusher. The only metal parts are the staple-forming plate and the striking arm. This is not a premium product, and it doesn't pretend to be.

But the plastic is decent. The body has a matte texture that resists fingerprints and doesn't look cheap. The top-loading magazine is straightforward — push the release button, slide the magazine open, drop in a strip of staples, and close.

The **non-slip base** is a real feature, not a marketing bullet point. The bottom has a rubberized strip that keeps the stapler in place even on smooth desks. You don't chase the stapler around as you press down.

One reviewer who bought the 3-pack for their home and school needs summed up the value proposition: *"This 3-pack of Amazon Basics Desktop Staplers is an excellent product that offers essential functionality and fantastic value. The staplers are lightweight, easy to use, and reliably handle their 25-sheet capacity."*

## Staple Performance

The stapler reliably penetrates **20-25 sheets of standard copy paper**. It'll do 30 if you push hard, but you're at the limit. The staple legs curl properly and lie flat — no bent staples or half-formed crimps.

**On thicker stacks:** Multi-page reports and stapled booklets require more pressure. The plastic mechanism flexes under heavy load, which is the limiting factor. If you regularly staple 30+ pages, the stapler will work but you'll feel the strain.

**On thin stacks:** 2-5 sheets staple cleanly with minimal effort. This is where the stapler excels — quick, one-handed operation for small documents.

**Staple compatibility:** Uses standard 24/6 staples (the most common size). The included 1000-staple strip will last a casual user months.

## Longevity Expectations

Let's be realistic: this is a $2 stapler. If you get a year of regular use, you've gotten your money's worth. If you get five years, you've beaten the system.

The most common failure mode is the **plastic magazine spring** losing tension over time. Eventually, staples won't feed reliably. When that happens, throw it away and grab the next one from the 3-pack.

The second most common issue is **jamming from overfilling**. The magazine holds a full strip of staples, but if you try to cram in a second strip while one is already partially loaded, the mechanism can bind. Solution: use one strip at a time.
