**The $10 stapler that 52,000 people bought — is it actually good?**

Let’s be honest: a stapler is not a sexy purchase. You’re not shopping for a status symbol. You need something that punctures paper, doesn’t jam, and doesn’t fall apart in six months. The Amazon Basics Office Stapler has over 52,000 ratings and a 4.6-star average. That's a lot of people who’ve shoved paper through this thing. Here’s the real story after digging through the feedback.

## What you actually get

The box contains the stapler itself — that’s it. No spare staples, no case. It’s a full-strip stapler, meaning it takes a standard 210-staple strip (26/6 size). The body is a mix of metal and plastic: the base and the main mechanism are metal, while the top cover is plastic with a matte black finish. It weighs about 9.6 ounces, so it has enough heft to stay put on your desk, but it’s light enough to toss in a drawer.

The standout physical feature is the integrated staple remover tucked into the back of the base. It’s not an afterthought — it’s a solid little claw that actually works. You’ll use it more than you think.

## How it performs

I’ll cut to the chase: this stapler does what it’s supposed to do, and it does it well. The manufacturer claims a 20-sheet capacity, and that’s accurate with standard 20-pound paper. It punches through cleanly with a satisfying *thunk* — not a weak *click*. The spring-loaded mechanism is smooth, and the base doesn’t slide around on your desk, thanks to rubberized feet on the bottom.

One customer put it this way: *"I've had this for two years and it has never jammed once. My old Swingline jammed every other week."* That’s the experience echoed across hundreds of reviews — reliability is the headline here.

It also handles cardstock and folded documents without complaint. If you’re stapling a 10-page report with a cardstock cover, this stapler won’t flinch.

## What buyers consistently praise

- **Reliability:** The number one theme in reviews is "no jams." That’s the whole job, and it nails it.
- **The staple remover:** People genuinely love this. One reviewer noted, *"The built-in remover is actually better than the standalone one I bought separately."*
- **Value:** At this price point, buyers repeatedly say it outperforms expectations. *"I expected to replace this in a year. It's been three years and it's still going strong,"* wrote another customer.

## Where it falls short

Let’s be fair — this isn’t a premium stapler, and there are tradeoffs.

**The plastic top cover is the weak point.** The metal base feels solid, but the plastic shell on top can crack if you drop it on a hard floor. A couple of reviews mention this exact failure after a fall. It won’t affect function immediately, but it’s a durability concern.

**No adjustable throat depth.** The distance from the staple to the edge of the paper is fixed at about 2.5 inches. If you’re trying to staple the center of a booklet or a thick folded document, you’ll have to angle it awkwardly. A professional-grade stapler with an adjustable anvil will do this better.

**Staple size limitation.** It only takes standard 26/6 staples. That’s fine for 95% of office work, but if you regularly staple 30+ pages, you need a heavy-duty model. This isn’t that.

## Who should buy this

- **Home office workers** who need a dependable stapler for occasional use — this is perfect.
- **Students** who need something cheap that survives a backpack. The metal base helps here.
- **Small business owners** who want a backup stapler for the drawer, not a centerpiece.

**Skip it if** you’re a legal assistant stapling 50-page briefs daily, or if you need a stapler with a deep throat for booklet binding. You’re in heavy-duty territory, and this isn’t the tool for that.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it jam with standard staples?**
A: No. The mechanism is simple and forgiving. Use quality staples (like Bostitch or Swingline) and you’ll rarely see a jam. Cheap no-name staples are the most common cause of jams in any stapler.

**Q: Can I use it for tacking (temporary stapling)?**
A: No. This model doesn’t have a rotating anvil for pinning or temporary stapling. You get a permanent clinch, and that’s it.

**Q: How do I reload it?**
A: Push the plastic top cover forward and it pops open. Drop in a full strip of 26/6 staples, close it, and you’re done. Takes about five seconds.
