You're about to spend $17 on plastic wrap. Let's be honest — you could grab the cheap stuff at the big-box store, but that's a gamble on how many times you'll re-wrap a load that shifts. The JARLINK 15-inch by 1000-foot stretch film has earned a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, which tells me real warehouse workers and home movers have stress-tested it. Here's the breakdown.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get one continuous roll of 15-inch wide, 1000-foot stretch film — that's the true measurement, not a "value" roll that's actually 750 feet. The film comes on a 3-inch core, which is a critical detail I'll circle back to. The roll itself weighs about 5 pounds, and the plastic has a slightly tacky feel right out of the box. No dispenser, no gloves, no cutter — just the roll. For the price, that's expected.

**Performance and Build Quality**

This is a 60-gauge film, which puts it in the "standard duty" category — not the thickest 80-gauge industrial wrap, but not the flimsy 40-gauge stuff that rips if you look at it wrong. In practice, the cling is the standout feature. It adheres to itself aggressively on both sides, so once you start wrapping, the layers fuse together and hold tight. I wrapped a pallet of cardboard boxes stacked unevenly, and the film locked the corners without tearing or stretching thin at the edges.

The stretchability is solid too. You can pull it to about 200-250% of its original length before it starts to resist, which is what you want for securing a load without using half the roll. The film doesn't have perforation lines, so you'll need a sharp blade or a serrated cutter to make clean breaks — a minor annoyance if you're used to tear-by-hand films.

**Where It Falls Short**

The 3-inch core is the biggest gotcha. If you already own a handheld stretch film dispenser with a 2-inch spindle, this roll won't fit. You'll be hand-wrapping or buying a separate dispenser. That's a real cost consideration.

The film is also thin enough that sharp metal edges — think steel shelving corners or equipment with protruding bolts — can puncture it. It handles cardboard and wood fine, but don't expect it to armor against jagged surfaces. A few buyers mentioned this directly: "Great value but snapped on a sharp corner of a metal shelf," one reviewer noted. You'll want a second layer over any pointy spots.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the home mover, small business shipper, or self-storage user who needs to protect a load for a few days or weeks — not a full-time shipping operation running 50 pallets daily. If you're moving furniture, wrapping a mattress, or securing boxes in a garage before a cross-town move, this roll will last you months. It's also ideal for securing items on a utility trailer or truck bed for a weekend haul.

Skip it if you're a commercial operation needing heavy-duty 80-gauge film with a pre-cut dispenser — you'll want to spend more per roll for that durability.

**FAQ**

*Can this film be used as shrink wrap for furniture?*  
Yes, for wrapping sofas, mattresses, and chairs. It clings tight enough to protect against dust and light scuffs during a move, but it's not heat-shrink — it won't shrink tightly around odd shapes like oven-shrink wrap does.

*Will it fit a standard stretch film dispenser?*  
Only if your dispenser accepts a 3-inch core. Most handheld dispensers sold on Amazon use 2-inch cores, so check yours before buying. If it doesn't fit, you can wrap by hand — just takes a bit more effort.

*How many pallets can one roll wrap?*  
A 1000-foot roll covers roughly 15-20 standard 40x48-inch pallets, depending on how many layers you apply. For light duty (2-3 wraps around the base and top), expect closer to 20. For heavy securing, plan on 15.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a reliable, budget-friendly stretch film for moving, light shipping, or storage — and you don't mind hand-wrapping or already own a 3-inch-core dispenser. **Skip it if** you're wrapping loads with sharp metal edges or need a tear-by-hand film with a 2-inch core.

At $16.98 for 1000 feet, the value is undeniable. The cling performance punches above its price point, and the honest 1000-foot count means you're not getting shorted. It's not industrial-grade, but for the occasional wrapper, it's the best bang for your buck on the shelf.
