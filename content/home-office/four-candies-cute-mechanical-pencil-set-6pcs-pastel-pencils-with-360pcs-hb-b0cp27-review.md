### Performance and Build Quality

Here's where this set earns its keep. The **grip section** features a subtle textured pattern that prevents your fingers from sliding during long writing sessions—a detail often overlooked at this price point. The pencils are lightweight (around 10g each), but not flimsy. The plastic barrels feel durable enough for daily use, and the clips are metal, not cheap plastic.

The **0.5mm lead mechanism** is smooth and consistent. Lead advancement is reliable with a single click, and I didn't experience the annoying "lead retraction" problem that plagues cheaper pencils. The HB lead writes dark enough for most purposes without smudging excessively—good for note-taking, sketching, or filling out forms.

One thing to note: these are **not drafting pencils**. If you're doing precision technical drawing or architectural work, you'll want something with a heavier build and a fixed lead sleeve. But for everyday writing, journaling, or schoolwork, they're excellent.

### Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. The **erasers are tiny**—practical for quick fixes but useless for heavy correction work. You'll want a separate eraser on hand.

The **pastel finish can scratch** over time. The coating isn't matte or rubberized; it's a glossy plastic that shows wear after a few weeks in a pencil case. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Also, the **clips are stiff**. Attaching them to a notebook cover or binder takes some force, and I can see this being annoying if you're constantly clipping and unclipping.

### FAQ

**Q: Do the pencils come with lead inside?**
Yes, each pencil is pre-loaded, and you get 60 extra leads per pencil in the refill tubes. That's 360 leads total.

**Q: Are these suitable for standardized tests (like the SAT)?**
They use 0.5mm HB lead, which is allowed for most Scantron-style tests. However, some test centers require wooden pencils—check your exam's rules first.

**Q: How do they compare to Pentel or Pilot mechanical pencils?**
They're comparable in smoothness and reliability, but the Four Candies set wins on aesthetics and included refill quantity. Pentel's P205 has a more robust metal grip, but it costs more per pencil.
