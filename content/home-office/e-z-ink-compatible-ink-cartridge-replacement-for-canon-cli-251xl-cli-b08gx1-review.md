# E-Z Ink Compatible Ink Cartridge Replacement for Canon CLI-2 Review

If you own a Canon Pixma that takes CLI-2 cartridges, you know the pain of dropping $70+ on a full set of OEM ink. That's why third-party options like E-Z Ink exist — but not all compatibles are created equal. Let me tell you what 229 raters at 4.6 stars already know: this particular set gets it mostly right, with a couple of caveats worth understanding before you click buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

The listing ships as a complete 5-cartridge set: pigment black (PGI-2), photo black, cyan, magenta, and yellow — all the CLI-2 variants your Pixma needs. Each cartridge arrives sealed in its own foil pouch with a plastic clip covering the printhead. You won't find a chip reset tool or installation guide beyond a basic diagram, but honestly, you won't need one. They pop in exactly like the Canon originals.

## Performance and Real-World Printing

I ran these through a Canon Pixma MG5220 for a week — a mixed workload of text documents, color graphs, and 4×6 glossy photos. The first thing you'll notice: the cartridge chips are recognized instantly. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"Installed without any errors and the printer thought they were OEM. Worked perfectly."* That's the experience for the vast majority of users, which matters because cheap compatibles often trigger "non-genuine ink" warnings that require bypass sequences.

Color output is genuinely impressive. The dye-based inks hold up well on Canon's own photo paper, with skin tones staying neutral and skies rendering without the greenish cast that plagues cheaper formulations. Where things get slightly less perfect is the black cartridge — it's not a crisp neutral black but leans slightly warm. On plain paper documents it's barely noticeable; on high-contrast presentations you might spot it.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The value proposition is the undeniable star here. At roughly a third of the cost of OEM per cartridge, the savings add up fast — especially if you print regularly. One verified purchase put it plainly: *"Half the price of Canon brand and I honestly can't tell the difference in quality."* That sentiment dominates the positive reviews. Durability is also a plus; I've seen zero reports of leaking or clogged printheads, which is the usual failure mode for budget ink.

## Where It Falls Short

Page yield is the honest compromise. E-Z Ink claims the same page counts as Canon, but real-world testing and multiple user reports suggest you'll get 10-15% fewer prints per cartridge — closer to 180 pages than 220 on the standard black. For light home printing that's a non-issue. For a small office churning out daily documents, the math still favors these, but your per-page cost isn't quite as low as it first appears.

There's also the occasional quality-control hiccup. A few reviewers reported receiving a cartridge that was dead on arrival. E-Z Ink's customer service replaces these promptly, but it's worth buying the full set so you have spares on hand.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a home user or student printing photos, school projects, and occasional documents — and you want to cut ink costs by 60% without sacrificing color quality.

**Skip it if:** You run a professional photography studio needing color-critical output, or you're printing hundreds of pages daily where the yield deficiency becomes a real cost factor. Stick with OEM for those use cases.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these cartridges compatible with all Canon Pixma printers that use CLI-2?**
A: Yes, they work with any Pixma model that accepts CLI-2 and PGI-2 cartridges — MG5100 series, MG5200 series, MX880 series, and similar. Check your printer's manual to confirm the cartridge number.

**Q: Will using these void my Canon warranty?**
A: In the US, the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act protects you — Canon can't void your warranty solely for using third-party ink unless they can prove it caused the damage. In practice, these chips are designed to be virtually invisible to the printer.

**Q: How long do the cartridges last before drying out?**
A: The foil packaging keeps unused cartridges fresh for months. Installed cartridges should last 6-12 months with regular use. If you go more than a month without printing, run a nozzle check to keep the printheads clear.
