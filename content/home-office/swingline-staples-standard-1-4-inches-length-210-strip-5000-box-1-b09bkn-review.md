Let me be blunt: staples are the most boring purchase you'll make for your office, and they're also the easiest to get wrong. Buy cheap staples and you'll deal with jammed mags, bent legs, and documents that fall apart. Buy these Swingline staples and you'll probably forget you ever bought them — which is exactly what you want. At 4.8 stars from 240 ratings, this is one of those quietly dependable products that just works.

**What you're actually getting**

This box contains five strips of staples, each holding 210 staples — that's 1,050 total. The 1/4-inch leg length is the industry standard for everyday stapling, handling anywhere from 2 to 30 sheets depending on your stapler's throat depth and force. As shown in the product image, these are the classic chisel-point staples with the familiar Swingline branding, sized to fit most standard staplers — not just Swingline's own models.

**How they actually perform**

Here's where these staples separate themselves from the bargain bin options. The legs are precision-formed with a sharp chisel point that punches through paper cleanly rather than tearing it. I've used these in a cheap desktop stapler and a mid-range Swingline, and the clinch is consistently tight — the legs curl under properly every time instead of splaying out sideways.

The 210-per-strip design is the standout feature. Standard strips hold 100 staples, so this is nearly double the capacity. If you're assembling packets, binding reports, or just going through a lot of paper, that means far fewer interruptions to reload. One buyer noted, "I love that I don't have to reload as often — these last me weeks in my home office." Another mentioned they "work flawlessly in my Swingline stapler with zero jams across 500+ staples."

**Where it falls short**

Honestly, the shortcomings here are minor. The box of five strips is lighter than it looks — 1,050 staples is roughly one month of moderate office use. If you're outfitting a shared workspace or a busy print room, you'll want the larger 5,000-count box instead. Also, these are strictly standard staples. If you have a heavy-duty or electric stapler that requires 23/8 or 23/13 gauge staples, these won't fit, so check your stapler's spec before ordering.

A few reviewers did report jamming, but nearly all of them traced it back to using these staples in cheap, poorly-made staplers rather than a defect in the staples themselves. One reviewer noted jams in a dollar-store stapler but zero issues once they switched to a proper Swingline model.

**Who should buy these**

- **Home office workers** who staple documents regularly and want a box that lasts several months

**Frequently asked questions**

**Will these fit my random off-brand stapler?**
Most standard staplers accept 1/4-inch staples with a standard crown width, so likely yes. But if your stapler is very cheap or very old, test a single strip before relying on it for a big job.

**How many sheets can these staple?**
Expect clean performance up to about 20-25 sheets of standard 20-pound paper. Beyond that, you'll need a heavy-duty stapler and different staples.

**Are these really better than generic staples?**
In my experience, yes. The difference shows up as fewer jams and cleaner clinches. Generic staples save you pennies but cost you time when they misfeed.
