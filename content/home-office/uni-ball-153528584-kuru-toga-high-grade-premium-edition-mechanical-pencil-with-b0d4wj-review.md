The Kuru Toga is the mechanical pencil that obsessed stationery nerds have been evangelizing for a decade. The High Grade Premium Edition is uni-ball's attempt to dress that clever lead-rotating engine in something worthy of a desk where money is no object. After 235 Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars, it's clear uni-ball mostly pulled it off — but "mostly" comes with caveats.

Let's get the obvious question out of the way: what do you actually get for the premium? The box contains the pencil itself (0.5mm, as shown in the product image with its distinctive brass grip), a pack of HB lead, and a small eraser under the push-button cap. No fancy case, no extra leads, no refill erasers. Just the pencil and bare essentials. That's fine — you're paying for the engineering, not the packaging.

**The Kuru Toga engine is the whole story.** Every time the lead touches paper and lifts off, a gear rotates the lead 20 degrees. This means the lead wears evenly on all sides instead of forming a flat chisel edge like every other mechanical pencil you've ever used. The result: consistent, crisp 0.5mm lines from the first word to the last paragraph. No rotating the pencil in your fingers, no line width drift mid-sentence. If you've ever been annoyed by that sudden thick "blob" stroke when your lead flattens, this is the fix.

What sets the High Grade apart from cheaper Kuru Toga models (like the plastic-bodied Standard or the aluminum Roulette) is the solid brass grip section. It gives the pencil a satisfying 20-gram heft that sits low in your hand, pulling the center of gravity down toward your fingertips. For long writing sessions — think essay writing, journaling, or architectural drafting — this makes a real difference in control. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: "The brass grip makes my handwriting noticeably neater. It's heavier than I expected, and that's a good thing."

The retractable lead sleeve is another thoughtful touch. Push the cap down and the sleeve slides back into the body, protecting both the lead and your shirt pocket. It also prevents the sleeve from scratching your desk surface when you set the pencil down. Small detail, but one that shows uni-ball understands how real people actually carry these things.

**Where it falls short:** The price. This is a $20+ pencil when the Roulette — which has the exact same internal mechanism — sells for half that. You're paying for the brass and the matte finish, not better performance. The knurled brass grip is also a genuine love-it-or-hate-it proposition. It provides excellent purchase, but after two hours of continuous writing, some users report it feeling almost abrasive on their fingers. I'd recommend trying one in person at a stationery store before committing, if that's an option.

The lead advance mechanism is also slightly stiff initially. A handful of reviewers noted it takes about a week of regular use before the click-feel loosens up to that satisfying, crisp "snick" the Kuru Toga is known for. It's not a dealbreaker, but don't panic if your first few clicks feel resistant.

**Who should buy this:** Students who write pages of notes daily and want consistent line quality. Engineers, architects, and designers who need precision line work. Anyone who's tried a standard Kuru Toga and wants a heavier, more premium-feeling version. It's also a genuinely great gift for someone who appreciates quality writing instruments.

**Who should skip it:** If you're a casual writer who goes through a pencil every few months, the $10 Roulette will serve you identically. If you hate heavy pens or have arthritis in your hands, the brass weight may fatigue you faster than a plastic body would.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Does this use standard 0.5mm lead?**
Yes, any standard 0.5mm lead works. Uni-ball's own NanoDia lead is excellent, but Pentel Ain Stein and Pilot Neox are equally compatible.

**Q: Is the grip really brass or just brass-colored?**
It's solid brass. That's why the pencil has genuine heft. You can feel the temperature differential when you first pick it up — it's noticeably cooler than plastic-bodied pencils.

**Q: Will the Kuru Toga mechanism work with 2B or softer lead?**
Yes, the rotation works with any lead hardness. Softer leads (2B, 4B) will show the consistent line benefit even more clearly, though you'll need to click more frequently to advance fresh lead.

**The Verdict:** The uni-ball Kuru Toga High Grade is an excellent pencil trapped in a slightly-overpriced body. The mechanism is genuinely innovative and delivers on its promise of consistent line width. The brass grip transforms the writing experience in a way that justifies the upgrade from plastic models — if you write a lot.

**Buy it if** you're a daily writer who wants a heavy, precise, premium-feeling instrument that actually improves your handwriting consistency.

**Skip it if** you're budget-conscious or already own a Kuru Toga Roulette. The mechanism is identical; you'd be paying for cosmetic upgrades only.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — minus half a star for the price premium and the polarizing grip texture. It earns those 4.7 Amazon stars honestly, but it's not for everyone.
