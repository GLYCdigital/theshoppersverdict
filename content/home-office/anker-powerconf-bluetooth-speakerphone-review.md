# Anker PowerConf Bluetooth Speakerphone Review: Your WFH Conference Room

The shift to remote and hybrid work made the humble speakerphone relevant again. If you spend your day hopping between Zoom calls, Teams meetings, and Google Meet standups, a quality speakerphone is the difference between sounding professional and sounding like you're calling from a coffee shop. The Anker PowerConf promises pro-grade voice pickup and noise cancellation at a home-office-friendly price. We tested it across a month of daily meetings.

## Design and Build

The PowerConf is a black hockey-puck-shaped device about 4 inches across and an inch tall. It weighs 10.6 ounces — heavy enough to feel solid on the desk, light enough to toss in a bag. The top surface is a soft-touch finish with a subtle fabric texture around the edge where the microphones sit.

Around the side you'll find a USB-C port for charging and wired connectivity, a Bluetooth pairing button, and a power switch. On top, touch-sensitive controls for volume, call answer/end, and Bluetooth are laid out around the perimeter. There's no physical mute button — mute is handled by the touch controls, which means you can't find it by feel. A red LED ring around the device indicates when you're muted, but the ring is hard to see at a glance, especially if the device is positioned to the side of your keyboard.

## Call Quality and Microphone Performance

This is where the PowerConf earns its keep. Anker packed six microphones into that small body, and the result is genuinely impressive voice pickup. Speak normally from 6 feet away and the person on the other end hears you clearly. Walk around a small room while talking and your voice stays centred — no noticeable volume drop until you're about 10 feet out.

Anker's proprietary noise-cancellation algorithm (VoiceRadar) does a solid job of filtering out background noise. We tested with a mechanical keyboard typing at full speed two feet from the device, a desk fan on high, and a phone playing music in the background. On the receiving end, participants reported hearing only the speaker's voice with a faint hint of the fan. Keyboard noise was effectively eliminated. The algorithm isn't quite as aggressive as the high-end conference speakers from Jabra or Poly, but it's close — and those cost twice as much or more.

Speaker volume is adequate for a home office desk. A single person sitting directly in front of the PowerConf hears crisp, clear audio at comfortable levels. For small-group call listening, it fills a huddle room of about 10x10 feet comfortably. Crank it to maximum and there's no distortion, though voices lose a bit of warmth. Music playback is functional but uninspiring — the PowerConf is optimized for speech, and you can hear it.

## Connectivity and Battery

Battery life is a standout feature. Anker rates the PowerConf at 24 hours of talk time on a single charge, and our testing matched that estimate. We went through a full work week of meetings (roughly 4-5 hours of calls per day) without needing to recharge. Charging takes about 4 hours via USB-C.

Bluetooth pairing is straightforward. The device remembers up to 8 paired devices and connects to the most recent one automatically. The Bluetooth range is solid — about 30 feet through walls, which is standard for this class.

You also have the option of using the USB-C cable for a wired connection. This is useful for two reasons: it eliminates any potential Bluetooth audio lag, and it keeps the device charged during long call days. On Windows and macOS, it shows up instantly as a standard audio device.

## Room Coverage

Anker says the PowerConf handles up to 8 people in a meeting room. In practice, 4 to 6 people in a small room works well. When we tested with 8 people distributed around a 12x12 foot conference table, participants at the far end sounded noticeably quieter, and the noise cancellation struggled with overlapping voices. For its size and price bracket, this is expected — the premium Jabra Speak 710 handles larger rooms but costs $200+.

For its intended use case — a single person working from home or a small group huddled around a desk — the coverage is excellent.

## Value Verdict

At $70, the Anker PowerConf Bluetooth Speakerphone sits in a sweet spot. It significantly outperforms budget speakerphones in the $30-50 range, delivering real noise cancellation and wide voice pickup. It doesn't match the $200+ conference speakers in room coverage or build quality, but it doesn't need to — it's built for the home office, not the boardroom.

For anyone working from home who wants to sound professional on calls without wearing a headset all day, the PowerConf is an easy recommendation. The lack of a physical mute button and the flat music playback are genuine annoyances, but the core function — making you sound great on calls — delivers reliably.

**Who should buy the PowerConf?** Remote workers and hybrid employees who take multiple calls daily and want hands-free, professional-sounding audio. Nomads who need a portable speakerphone for calls on the road.

**Who should skip it?** Anyone who needs a single device for both calls and casual music listening. Teams in large conference rooms who need full-room coverage. Those who rely on muscle memory for mute — you'll miss the physical button.
