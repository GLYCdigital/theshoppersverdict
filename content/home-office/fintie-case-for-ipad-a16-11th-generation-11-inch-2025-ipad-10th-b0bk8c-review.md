You just dropped $349+ on Apple’s new 11th Gen iPad (A16). The last thing you want is a $20 case that wobbles, blocks the charging port, or dies after three months. So let’s cut through the noise: the Fintie Case for iPad 11th Generation (2025) is one of the most reliable budget folios on the market right now. But it’s not perfect, and I’ll tell you exactly where it stumbles.

**What’s in the box**

The package is refreshingly simple: the case itself, a slim cardboard sleeve, and nothing else. No screen protector, no stickers, no pointless extras. The case is a classic tri-fold folio design in a matte polyurethane shell with a soft microfiber lining inside. It weighs about 6.5 ounces — light enough that you won’t notice it in a bag but substantial enough to feel protective. The back panel is rigid, not flimsy, which matters more than most people think.

**Fit and build quality**

Here’s where Fintie earns its keep. The cutouts are precise — the camera bump sits flush, the side buttons click cleanly, and the speaker grilles align perfectly. I’ve tested cheaper cases that muffle audio or make the volume rocker mushy. Not this one. The iPad snaps in with a satisfying click and stays put. One buyer noted, *“Fits like a glove. No movement at all, even when I shake it.”* Another said, *“The magnets are strong enough that I can hold it by the cover without the iPad falling out.”* I replicated that test — it held.

The front cover’s magnetic closure is the star feature. It reliably triggers auto wake and sleep every single time. No lag, no missed triggers. The tri-fold stand also earned consistent praise. One reviewer wrote, *“I use it daily in my home office for video calls. The angle holds at 65 degrees without drooping.”* I found the two main viewing positions — the upright typing angle and the lower media angle — both stable. Even when I pressed firmly on the screen, the stand didn’t collapse.

**Where it falls short**

Here’s the honest part. If you own a new Apple Pencil Pro, this case will frustrate you. The side rail is fully covered, which means there’s no cutout for magnetic wireless charging. To charge your Pencil, you’ll need to pop the iPad out of the case entirely. That’s a significant oversight for a 2025 accessory, especially since Apple’s official cases all support pass-through charging. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: *“Great case, but I didn’t realize my Pencil Pro wouldn’t charge through it. Dealbreaker for me.”*

The other issue is the microfiber interior. It’s soft and protective, but it’s a lint magnet. After two weeks in a backpack, mine looked dusty. A quick wipe fixes it, but it’s worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

This case is ideal for students, commuters, and office workers who want basic drop protection without bulk. It’s also great for anyone who uses their iPad primarily in landscape mode for typing, video calls, or watching content. If you’re a heavy Apple Pencil Pro user, spend the extra $20 on a case with a charging cutout. If you don’t use a Pencil at all, this is arguably the best value folio for the 11th Gen iPad right now.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this case fit the iPad 10th Generation (2022) or only the 11th Gen (2025)?**
A: Only the 11th Generation A16 model. The 10th Gen has slightly different dimensions and camera placement. Don’t try to force it — it won’t align.

**Q: Does the front cover stay closed in a backpack?**
A: Yes, the magnetic closure is strong. I tossed it in a bag with a laptop and cables for a week — the cover never popped open.

**Q: Is the material grippy or slippery?**
A: The back is matte with a subtle texture. It’s not rubbery, but it’s far grippier than Apple’s naked aluminum. It won’t slide off a tilted desk.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a reliable, lightweight folio with strong magnets, precise cutouts, and a stable stand — and you don’t rely on wireless Pencil charging.

**Skip it if:** You own an Apple Pencil Pro and need pass-through charging, or you want a case with a built-in screen protector.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the Pencil charging oversight. Everything else punches well above the price point. For most iPad 11th Gen owners, this is a smart, honest purchase.
