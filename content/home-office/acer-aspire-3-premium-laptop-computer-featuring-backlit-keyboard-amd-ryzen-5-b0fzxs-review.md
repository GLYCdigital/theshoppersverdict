The Acer Aspire 3 Premium with a Ryzen 5 and backlit keyboard sits in a crowded, competitive space. It's not the flashiest laptop, not the lightest, and definitely not the one with the best display. But it hits a specific sweet spot — if you need a reliable daily workhorse with plenty of RAM and a keyboard you can actually see in the dark, this deserves a close look. Just know exactly what you're getting before you click buy.

**What's in the box**

You get the laptop itself, a 65W barrel charger (not USB-C, which is a bit dated), and the standard paperwork. No sleeve, no USB-C dock, no extras. The 15.6-inch chassis is what Acer calls "premium" — the lid is aluminum, the deck is plastic but well-textured. At 3.9 pounds, it's portable enough for a commuter bag but not a featherweight. Port selection is decent: two USB-A 3.2, one USB-C (data only, no charging or DisplayPort), HDMI 1.4, and a headphone jack. That's workable, but the USB-C limitation means you can't charge with a phone charger or connect a modern monitor with a single cable.

**Performance and real-world use**

The AMD Ryzen 5 7530U with 16GB of DDR4 RAM is the star here. In daily use — 20+ Chrome tabs, Slack, Spotify, a PDF reader, and occasional light photo editing — this thing doesn't break a sweat. The 512GB NVMe SSD boots in about 12 seconds, and apps open instantly. For office work, spreadsheet wrangling, or writing, this is more than enough. The backlit keyboard is the headline feature, and it's legitimately good — two brightness levels, even key illumination, and decent key travel (1.5mm). Typing for hours is comfortable.

Where it stumbles is under sustained load. Push the Ryzen 5 with video encoding or heavy Excel calculations, and the fan becomes audible — not obnoxious, but noticeable in a quiet room. The 250-nit display is the bigger issue. Indoors, it's fine. Near a window or outdoors, you'll be squinting. Color accuracy is acceptable for spreadsheets and documents but not for photo work.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The reviews are mixed (3.6 stars) but the positive ones cluster around two things. One buyer wrote, "The backlit keyboard is why I bought this, and it doesn't disappoint — typing in a dark living room is actually pleasant now." Another said, "For the price, you get a lot of RAM and a processor that handles everything I throw at it." Performance-per-dollar is the recurring theme.

**Where it falls short**

Battery life is the biggest complaint. Acer claims up to 10 hours, but real-world testing puts it at 6-7 hours with mixed use — 50% brightness, Wi-Fi on, document work. That's fine for a workday if you're near an outlet, but you'll want the charger for a full day out. The display's 250-nit brightness is the second most common gripe. And a handful of reviews mention the plastic deck flexing slightly under pressure near the touchpad — not a dealbreaker, but it undermines the "premium" branding.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the budget-conscious professional or student who prioritizes performance and typing comfort over portability and screen quality. If you need a secondary laptop for spreadsheets, writing, and research, it's a strong pick. If you're a creative professional needing color accuracy, a frequent traveler needing all-day battery, or someone who hates fan noise — keep looking.

**FAQ**

*Q: Can I upgrade the RAM or storage later?*  
A: Yes — both RAM slots and the M.2 SSD are accessible via a single bottom panel. Upgrading to 32GB RAM costs about $60 and takes five minutes.

*Q: Does the USB-C port support video output?*  
A: No — it's data only. You'll need the HDMI port for external displays. That's a limitation for docking setups.

*Q: Is the backlit keyboard adjustable?*  
A: Yes — two brightness levels plus off, controlled via the F9 key. It's bright enough for a dark room without being blinding.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a workhorse laptop with a genuinely usable backlit keyboard, strong performance for daily tasks, and expandable RAM — and you can live with average battery life and a dim screen.

**Skip it if** you need all-day battery, work in bright environments, or demand USB-C charging and display support.

This is a solid 3.7-star product — good value for the right buyer, but with compromises that are dealbreakers for others. At the current price, it's a fair deal, not a steal.
