Let’s get this out of the way: the WRITECH Ombre Gel Pen Set 0171 is not a luxury pen. It’s a 12-pack of gradient-barreled gel pens that costs about what you’d pay for a single premium rollerball. And for most people, that’s exactly the point.

**What you actually get**

The box contains 12 pens, each with a 0.7mm needle-point tip and black ink. The barrels feature a different ombre gradient per pen — think deep purple fading to lavender, teal melting into mint, crimson bleeding into pink. As shown in the product image, the color coding is distinct enough that you can grab the right pen without squinting at the tip.

Each pen uses a standard gel refill. The body is lightweight polypropylene, roughly 5.7 inches long, with a pocket clip that actually stays put. No blister pack, no fancy tin — just the pens in a simple cardboard sleeve. That’s fine. You’re paying for writing performance, not packaging theater.

**Performance and build quality**

I tested these on three surfaces: cheap copy paper, a Moleskine notebook, and glossy cardstock. The 0.7mm tip lays down a crisp, consistent line that dries in about two seconds. No skipping on the first stroke, no hard starts after leaving the cap off for an hour. That’s better than many pens at double the price.

The ink flow is wet but not sloppy. You get that satisfying gel-pen glide without feathering on standard printer paper. On glossy stock, the ink holds its edge — no spreading or blobbing. Line width is true to the 0.7mm spec, making these solid for everyday note-taking, journaling, and signing documents where you want a bit of presence without going full 1.0mm bold.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reading through the 229 Amazon ratings, a few themes come up repeatedly. One reviewer noted, “These write so smoothly — I threw out my old pens after the first day.” Another mentioned the color variety: “The ombre barrels make it easy to tell which pen is which in my pencil case.” Multiple buyers appreciated that the ink doesn’t bleed through standard notebook pages, which is the #1 complaint with cheaper gel pens.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be honest about the downsides. The ink is not waterproof — a spilled coffee or a sweep of highlighter will smear your writing into illegibility. If you need archival-quality ink, look at Sakura Pigma or Pilot G2 with permanent ink.

The grip is smooth plastic with no texture. For a 20-minute writing session it’s fine; for an exam or long journaling marathon, your fingers may slide. If you have sweaty hands, budget for a grip sleeve.

One more thing: the 0.7mm tip is forgiving but not fine. If you write tiny or need precision for technical drawing, you’ll want a 0.5mm or 0.38mm pen instead.

**FAQ**

A: Yes. Give the ink at least 30 seconds to dry before highlighting, and even then, water-based highlighters can cause slight smearing. If you highlight immediately after writing, expect smudges.

**Q: Are the refills replaceable?**
A: Yes, they use standard gel pen refills. You can find compatible refills from major brands, though the fit may vary slightly. Most buyers just buy a new set given the price.

**Q: How long does one pen last?**
A: Roughly 800–1,000 meters of writing, which translates to about 2–3 months of daily note-taking for most users. The ink reservoir is visible, so you can see when you're running low.
