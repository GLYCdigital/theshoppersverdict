If you spend hours in transcription software, digital audio workstations, or FPS games, you already know the problem: your left hand is doing too much. The iKKEGOL Upgraded USB Single Foot Pedal offloads one repetitive action to your foot, and the optical switch inside means it'll outlast the mechanical pedals that die after a year of daily stomping.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the pedal itself, a 5-foot USB-A cable, and a small instruction card. That's it — no software disc, no manual worth reading. The pedal measures roughly 3.5 by 2.5 inches and sits about an inch off the floor. The top surface has a slight textured grip, and the underside is a solid rubber pad that doesn't slide on hardwood or shift on carpet. The cable is braided, which is a nice touch at this price point.

**How it performs in real use**

Plug it into any USB port and it registers as a standard keyboard. No drivers, no configuration utility, no headaches. It works on Windows, macOS, and Linux out of the box — I tested it on all three. The default output is the spacebar, which is perfect for transcription apps like Express Scribe or for push-to-talk in Discord.

The optical switch is the star here. Instead of a mechanical contact that arcs and degrades, an infrared beam is interrupted when you press the pedal. That's why iKKEGOL rates it for 10 million presses — roughly five times what a typical mechanical foot switch handles. The actuation is crisp and immediate, with a satisfying click that's audible but not obnoxious.

To reprogram it, you hold down the pedal, type the key you want it to output, and release. It takes about three seconds and supports single keys, function keys, and modifier combos like Ctrl+Shift+S. It does not support multi-key macros or mouse button emulation — it's strictly one key per pedal.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews is earned. One reviewer noted, "I've gone through three mechanical foot pedals in two years. This one has survived six months of daily transcription with zero issues." Another user in audio production said, "The optical switch feels instant — no lag between my foot and the recording stopping."

The durability claims are the most common thread. Multiple reviewers mention that the pedal feels sturdier than its price suggests, with one calling the build "surprisingly solid for something this light."

**Where it falls short**

The single-pedal limitation is real. If you need play, pause, and rewind, you're buying three of these — and they don't chain together. Each pedal is independent, so you'll need three USB ports and three separate programming steps.

The lack of onboard memory also bites if you move between workstations. Unplug it, plug it into a different computer, and it reverts to spacebar. You'll reprogram it every time. For a dedicated home setup, this is irrelevant. For someone who works in an office and at home, it's a genuine annoyance.

The pedal is also smaller than some competitors. If you wear size 12+ shoes, you might find yourself catching the edge rather than the center. It works, but it requires a bit more precision than a full-width pedal.

**Who should buy this**

- **Transcriptionists** who need a durable, instant foot switch for start/stop control — this is your workhorse.
- **Gamers** using push-to-talk who want to free up their left hand — the optical switch means no double-taps or missed activations.
- **Podcasters and audio editors** doing hands-free recording control on a budget.

Skip it if you need multi-pedal functionality in a single unit, or if you regularly hot-swap between different computers and don't want to reprogram each time.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with Express Scribe?**
A: Yes. It defaults to spacebar, which Express Scribe recognizes natively for start/stop. No configuration needed.

**Q: Can I program it to type a word or phrase?**
A: No. It only outputs a single key or key combination. For text expansion, you'll need third-party software like AutoHotkey.

**Q: Is the USB cable replaceable?**
A: The cable is hardwired into the pedal. If it fails, you're replacing the whole unit — though the braided cable quality makes this unlikely.
