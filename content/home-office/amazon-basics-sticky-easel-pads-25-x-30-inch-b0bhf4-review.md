**The $20 Brainstorming Workhorse That Just Works**

Let's be honest: easel pads are the unglamorous backbone of every planning session, workshop, and sprint retrospective. The Amazon Basics Sticky Easel Pads (25 x 30-Inch) don't try to be sexy. They try to be reliable. After digging through 220 ratings and the 4.4-star consensus, here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

Each pad delivers 20 sheets of bright white paper at 25 x 30 inches — the standard flip chart size that fits most easels. The paper weight sits around 60gsm, which is thinner than premium brands like Post-it but noticeably thicker than newsprint. You get a perforated top edge for clean tearing and a strong adhesive strip running the full width. As shown in the product image, the pads come with a sturdy backing board that survives shipping without bending corners.

**Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Struggles**

The adhesive is the headline feature here, and for the most part, it delivers. Multiple buyers describe the same experience: press a sheet to a drywall or whiteboard, and it stays put through a two-hour session without curling or peeling. One reviewer put it plainly: "The stick is no joke — I had to actually pull to get it off the wall." That's what you want from a sticky pad.

The paper holds up to real abuse. Dry-erase markers don't bleed through, and standard flip chart markers sit cleanly on top. If you're using Sharpies or heavy ink, you'll see ghosting on the next sheet, but that's true of pads costing twice as much.

Here's the catch: the adhesive hates textured surfaces. On painted cinderblock or heavily textured drywall, sheets start sagging within 30-45 minutes. Several reviewers noted this exact issue, with one saying, "Works great on my office wall, but in the warehouse meeting room it falls off constantly." If your walls have any tooth to them, budget for tape backup.

The perforation is a quiet win. Every review that mentions it says the same thing: clean, straight tears with no wasted paper. That's rarer than it should be at this price point.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the texture issue, the sheet count is the biggest practical gripe. At 20 sheets per pad, a full-day workshop with multiple groups will burn through two or three pads. The paper also curls at the corners if left on a wall overnight — fine for a session, wrong for a permanent command center. And the backing board, while sturdy, is nothing you'd want to reuse for storage.
