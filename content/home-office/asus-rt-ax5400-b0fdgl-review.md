The ASUS RT-AX5400 sits in that awkward middle ground of the router market: not the cheapest Wi-Fi 6 option, not the flagship tri-band monster. But here's the thing — for most home offices and small apartments, this is exactly the right amount of router. It's the Goldilocks option for people who want Wi-Fi 6 without paying for features they'll never use.

## What's actually in the box

You get the router itself — a matte black box with four external antennas that look like they mean business — plus a power adapter and a standard RJ-45 Ethernet cable. That's it. No wall-mounting kit, no stand if you want to tuck it vertically. The unit sits flat on a desk or shelf, and at roughly 9.5 x 6.7 x 1.6 inches, it won't dominate your workspace. The four antennas are detachable, which is nice for cable management.

## Real-world performance

The RT-AX5400 is a dual-band AX5400 router: 574 Mbps on 2.4GHz and 4804 Mbps on 5GHz. In practical terms, that means solid Wi-Fi 6 speeds at 80MHz channel width. If you have a recent laptop or phone with Wi-Fi 6, you'll notice the difference immediately — lower latency, better stability when you're moving around your space.

For a home office setup, this handles video conferencing, cloud backups, and a half-dozen connected devices simultaneously without breaking a sweat. The OFDMA and MU-MIMO features actually work here, unlike some budget routers where these are checkbox features that don't function properly.

One buyer noted: "I replaced a three-year-old AC router and my Zoom calls stopped dropping instantly. The 5GHz range is genuinely better than I expected for a router in this price bracket."

## Where it falls short

The missing USB port is the biggest gotcha. If you wanted to plug in an external drive for network storage or share a printer across your office, this router can't do it. That's a deliberate cost-cutting move, and it's fine if you already have a NAS or cloud storage — but it's a hard dealbreaker for some small offices.

The dual-band limitation also shows up in busier environments. If you're in a dense apartment building with dozens of competing 5GHz networks, you'll see more interference than you would on a tri-band unit. The 2.4GHz band helps, but it caps out at 574 Mbps, which is tight if you're moving large files around.

Parental controls are disappointingly shallow. You can block categories and schedule internet access, but per-device granular filtering requires digging into the web interface or using third-party tools. ASUS's AiProtection Pro is genuinely useful — it's free for life, not a trial — but the parental features feel like an afterthought.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a home office worker or small business user who needs reliable Wi-Fi 6 for video calls, cloud apps, and a handful of devices. You want easy setup via the ASUS Router app and don't need USB storage sharing. You're upgrading from an older AC router and want a tangible speed and stability boost without spending flagship money.

**Skip it if:** You need network-attached storage, have a large home with thick walls, or consistently run 20+ devices simultaneously. Also skip if you're a gamer who wants the lowest possible latency — you'll want a tri-band model with 160MHz channel support.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the RT-AX5400 work with my existing modem?**
A: Yes, it works with any standard cable, fiber, or DSL modem that has an Ethernet port. It doesn't have a built-in modem, so you'll need to keep your ISP's equipment or buy a separate modem.

**Q: Is the ASUS Router app required for setup?**
A: No, but it's strongly recommended. The app walks you through setup in about five minutes. You can also access the full web interface at router.asus.com for advanced configuration.

**Q: Will this router work with my ISP's gigabit fiber plan?**
A: Yes, the wired ports are gigabit-capable, and the Wi-Fi 6 speeds are more than enough to saturate a gigabit connection on capable clients. Just note that you'll only get near-gigabit wireless speeds on the 5GHz band with a Wi-Fi 6 device.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The ASUS RT-AX5400 is a smart, honest router that delivers exactly what it promises: fast, reliable Wi-Fi 6 for homes and small offices. It's not flashy, it doesn't have every feature under the sun, and that's precisely why it works. The missing USB port and dual-band limitation are real tradeoffs, but for the target audience — people who need dependable wireless for work and streaming — this is a solid, no-regrets purchase.

If you're on an older Wi-Fi 5 router and your daily work depends on stable connections, this is a meaningful upgrade. Just make sure you don't need USB storage, and you'll be happy with what the RT-AX5400 brings to your desk.
