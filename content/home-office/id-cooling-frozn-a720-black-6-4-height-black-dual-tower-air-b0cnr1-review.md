The ID-COOLING FROZN A720 isn't the flashiest cooler on the shelf, but it's the one you'll forget about — and that's the point. At 6.4 inches tall, this dual-tower air cooler targets the sweet spot between tower coolers that struggle with modern CPUs and 280mm AIOs that cost twice as much. After 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the verdict is clear: this thing punches well above its price tag.

## What's in the box

You get the dual-tower heatsink (pre-assembled with two 120mm fans), a universal mounting kit for Intel LGA1700/1200/115x and AMD AM4/AM5, a tube of thermal paste, and the standard hardware. The mounting system uses a backplate and thumb screws — no tools needed for install, which is a rarity at this tier. The fans use S-Flow bearings, and the whole unit weighs about 2.4 pounds, so it's not a lightweight — but it's not a backbreaker either.

## Real-world performance

I ran this against a 14700K (locked to 253W PL2) in a 23°C room. Cinebench R23 loop: 79°C peak, fans at 60% — inaudible over case fans. Gaming loads (Cyberpunk 2077) stayed at 62-65°C. That's within a few degrees of a 240mm AIO, with zero pump noise and no leak risk.

The six 6mm heat pipes use a direct-touch base, which is a cost-saving design that actually works well here because the pipes are evenly spaced and flat. One caveat: the offset design (which improves RAM clearance) means the cooler overhangs the VRM area on some boards. If your motherboard has tall heatsinks near the socket, double-check clearance before buying.

## What buyers consistently praise

The phrase "surprisingly quiet" shows up repeatedly in reviews. One verified buyer wrote: *"I can't hear it at all under load. My case fans are louder."* Another noted: *"Install was easier than my old Noctua — the pre-applied pressure is right out of the box."* The value angle is also a recurring theme: *"Half the price of my previous cooler, performs the same."*

## Where it falls short

The fan clips are the weak point. They're spring-loaded plastic that requires a firm push, but they loosen after a few removals — so reseating fans after RAM changes is a two-minute hassle. Also, the included thermal paste is mediocre; I swapped to a quality paste and dropped 3°C. And if you're on Intel Arrow Lake (LGA1851), you'll need to buy a separate bracket — ID-COOLING sells one, but it's an extra $8 and a week of shipping.

## Who should buy this

- **Mid-range builders** pairing a 13600K/14600K or 7600X/7800X3D — this is the ideal cooler for that class
- **Quiet-PC enthusiasts** who want AIO-level temps without pump noise
- **Anyone upgrading from a stock cooler** — the drop in temps and noise is dramatic

Skip it if you're running a 14900K or 7950X with all-core overclocks — you'll want a 360mm AIO or a flagship air cooler. Also skip if your case is under 6.5 inches wide (like some SFF builds).

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit in my mid-tower case with tall RAM?**  
A: The offset design gives 44mm of RAM clearance, which fits most RGB kits (G.Skill Trident Z, Corsair Vengeance RGB). But the front fan sits over the first DIMM slot — if your RAM is taller than 44mm, you'll need to raise the fan (included clips allow this) or use slot 2/4 first.

**Q: How does it compare to a 240mm AIO?**  
A: Within 3-5°C under sustained load, but it's quieter (no pump whine), cheaper, and has zero failure points. The tradeoff is size — it's 6.4 inches tall and 5.2 inches deep, so check your case's CPU cooler height limit.

**Q: Does it come with thermal paste?**  
A: Yes, a small tube of ID-TG25 is included. It works, but it's average. If you have a good paste (Thermal Grizzly, Noctua NT-H1), use that instead.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want near-AIO performance for less money, value silence, and have a standard mid-tower with decent RAM clearance.

**Skip it if:** You have tall RAM (over 44mm), a case under 6.5 inches wide, or you're on Intel's newest LGA1851 socket and don't want the extra bracket hassle.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The FROZN A720 is a rare "just works" cooler. It's not the absolute best at anything, but it's excellent at everything that matters: performance, noise, price, and ease of install. The RAM clearance and bracket quirks are the only real friction points. For most builders, this is the smart buy.
