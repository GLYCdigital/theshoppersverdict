Let's be honest about notebook paper: most of it is trash. Thin enough to see your last class's notes through the page, flimsy enough to rip when you flip it. So when a 4.7-star rating shows up on a product literally called "Amazon Basics," I get skeptical. Then I got my hands on a pack, and I get it now.

This is the wide-ruled loose-leaf paper that actually respects your pen.

**What's in the pack**

You're getting 200 sheets of 24-lb paper in five colors — pink, blue, green, yellow, and purple. You also get a 3-hole punch pattern that fits standard binders, plus micro-perforations along the top for clean tear-out. These are 8 x 10.5-inch sheets, slightly shorter than standard 8.5 x 11, which keeps them from curling over the top of a binder ring. Small detail, but it matters.

**The paper quality is the real story**

Most budget paper sits around 20-lb. This is 24-lb, and you feel the difference the second you pick up a sheet. It has a slight tooth — enough grip to keep ballpoint from skating but smooth enough that gel pens lay down clean lines without skipping.

I tested it with a Pilot G2, a fountain pen with Noodler's ink, and a Sharpie (for science). The G2 and fountain pen? Zero bleed-through. The Sharpie ghosted slightly on the reverse side, but that's expected — no loose-leaf paper under 32-lb survives a marker. What impressed me was the lack of feathering. The fibers held up; ink didn't spread into fuzzy edges.

The color saturation is also worth mentioning. These aren't pastel washed-out shades. The pink is a legitimate bubblegum, the blue is a strong periwinkle. When you're color-coding notes by subject, the distinction is actually visible across a room.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 235 ratings, the same themes keep coming up. One reviewer wrote, "These are thicker than the Mead paper I've used for years — my gel pens don't bleed through at all." Another mentioned, "The perforation is perfect. I've torn out maybe 30 sheets and not one has ripped wrong."

The most common praise, though, centers on the color variety for organization. As one teacher put it: "I assign a color per subject for my students. It's made a genuine difference in how they organize their binders."

**Where it falls short**

The 200-sheet count is the biggest drawback. At this thickness, you're paying a slight premium per sheet, and heavy note-takers (especially college students) will go through a pack in a month or two. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Also, wide ruling is 9/32-inch spacing. If you write small — or you're an adult who hasn't taken notes since high school — this feels wasteful. You'll fit maybe half as many words per page as you would on college-ruled. The multicolor line only comes in wide rule, so if you need narrow spacing, this isn't your product.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students with binder systems**: The color-coding + perforation combo is genuinely useful for separating subjects.
- **Gel pen and fountain pen users**: This is one of the few budget papers that won't bleed through.
- **Teachers**: The color variety helps younger students organize, and the durability handles rough handling.

**Skip it if**: You need college-ruled spacing, or you burn through paper at a rate where 200 sheets won't last a month — in that case, the extra thickness isn't worth the cost per sheet.

**FAQ**

A: It's loose-leaf, not printer paper. The 3-hole punch and perforations would jam most printers. Buy this for handwriting, not printing.

**Q: Is the paper acid-free?**
A: Yes, it's acid-free. It won't yellow or become brittle over time, which is important if you're keeping notes long-term.

**Q: How do the colors compare to Amazon's product images?**
A: They're accurate. The pink is slightly deeper than it appears on screen, but the blue, green, and purple match well.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a student, teacher, or anyone who writes with pens that bleed through cheap paper. The 24-lb weight, clean perforations, and true-to-life colors make this the best value in loose-leaf right now.

**Skip it if:** You need college-ruled spacing, or you're a minimalist who just needs plain white paper — you'll pay extra for color you don't want.
