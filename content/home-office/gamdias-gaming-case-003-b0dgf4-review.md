The GAMDIAS Gaming CASE 003 is a mid-tower that tries to do it all: aggressive looks, pre-installed RGB, and enough room for a beefy build — all without demanding flagship money. With 220 ratings holding a 4.4-star average, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right case for *your* build? Let's get into it.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box, you're looking at a steel-and-tempered-glass mid-tower measuring roughly 18.5 x 8.3 x 18.9 inches (470 x 210 x 480mm). It ships with **three 120mm ARGB fans pre-installed** — two in the front, one in the rear — plus a small fan hub to keep the wiring tidy. The tempered glass side panel swings open on a hinge with a tool-less latch, which is a genuinely nice touch at this price point.

The front I/O is practical: two USB 3.0 ports, a USB-C port, and separate mic/headphone jacks. Notably, there's no reset button — just power and RGB control. If you're someone who actually uses reset, that's a minor omission.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 003 supports ATX, Micro-ATX, and Mini-ITX boards, and there's room for a **360mm radiator up top** or a 280mm in front. CPU cooler clearance caps out at 170mm, which fits most air towers like the Noctua NH-D15 or DeepCool AK620 without issue. GPU clearance is listed at 400mm, though realistically you'll want to stay under 380mm if your power cables are thick — the shroud cutout is adequate but not generous.

The biggest performance caveat: the front panel is solid metal with narrow side vents. The three intake fans pull air through those slits, which means thermals are **good, not great**. In a standard setup with a mid-range CPU and GPU, you'll see acceptable temps. But if you're running a 14900K or a 4090, expect to either remove the front panel or add high-static-pressure fans. One buyer noted, *"The case looks amazing, but my GPU runs 5-7°C hotter than my old mesh-front case. I ended up leaving the front panel off during summer."* That's a fair trade-off for the aesthetic, but know it going in.

Cable management is a strong point. There's about 22mm of clearance behind the motherboard tray, with Velcro straps and multiple grommets. The included fan hub mounts cleanly to the backplate, and the pre-routed fan cables make for a tidy first build. One reviewer summed it up: *"First time builder here — cable management was actually easy. The Velcro straps and channels are forgiving."*

## Where It Falls Short

Besides the airflow restriction, the included fans are **3-pin DC, not PWM**. That means you can't fine-tune fan curves through your motherboard without a separate controller. They'll run at a fixed speed, which is fine for noise-neutral builds, but enthusiasts will want to swap them out or add a PWM hub.

The RGB situation is another minor gripe. The fans are controlled via a button on the front I/O that cycles through about 20 presets. There's no ARGB header on the hub, so you **can't sync with your motherboard's software** (ASUS Aura, MSI Mystic Light, etc.). For RGB purists, that's a dealbreaker; for everyone else, the presets are varied enough to keep things interesting.

## Who Should Buy This

- **First-time builders** who want an easy assembly experience with pre-installed fans and forgiving cable management.
- **Aesthetic-focused builders** who want a dark, aggressive look with glass and RGB without paying $150+ for a flagship case.
- **Air-cooled setups** with mid-range components (Ryzen 5/7, Core i5/i7, RTX 4070-class GPUs) that don't need extreme airflow.

Skip it if you're running a high-TDP rig that relies on liquid cooling or if motherboard RGB sync is non-negotiable.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the GAMDIAS CASE 003 come with a GPU riser cable for vertical mounting?**
A: No. The case has two vertical PCIe slots, but you'll need to buy a riser cable separately. It's a 4.0 slot, so make sure you get a compatible cable.

**Q: Can I fit a 360mm radiator in the front AND top?**
A: Not simultaneously. You can fit a 360mm in one location or a 280mm in the front and a 240mm up top, but clearance gets tight with long GPUs. Plan your loop accordingly.

**Q: How loud are the stock fans at full speed?**
A: They're rated around 28 dBA, which is audible but not obnoxious. At fixed speed, they're noticeable in a quiet room. Most buyers find them acceptable, but if you're noise-sensitive, budget for aftermarket fans.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a sharp-looking mid-tower with pre-installed RGB, easy cable management, and room for a high-end air-cooled build — and you don't mind the airflow compromise.

**Skip it if:** You're chasing maximum thermals, need PWM fan control, or want motherboard RGB sync. This is a style-first case, not a performance-first one.
