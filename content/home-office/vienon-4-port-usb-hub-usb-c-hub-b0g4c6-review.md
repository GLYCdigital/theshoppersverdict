# VIENON 4-Port USB Hub USB C Hub Review: Small Hub, Real Utility

Most USB-C hubs try to do everything — HDMI, SD cards, Ethernet, power delivery — and end up mediocre at all of it. The VIENON 4-Port USB Hub takes the opposite approach. It does one thing (expand your USB-A ports) and does it cleanly. For the right buyer, that focus is exactly the point.

## What You Actually Get

The hub is a small aluminum rectangle — roughly the size of a stack of five credit cards — with four USB-A 3.0 ports on one end and a permanently attached USB-C cable on the other. The cable is about four inches long, which is worth noting before you buy. It's designed to sit flush against your laptop, not stretch across a desk.

There's no box of extras, no manual you'll need. It's the hub, period. That's fine.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The aluminum shell is the first thing you'll notice. It feels substantial — not like the cheap plastic hubs that flex when you plug something in. It also dissipates heat well. I ran three external drives off it simultaneously, transferring large video files, and the case stayed warm but never hot. That's more than I can say for several pricier hubs I've tested.

Transfer speeds are capped at USB 3.0 — 5Gbps theoretical, realistically around 350-400MB/s with good drives. That's fine for moving documents, photos, and even moderate video files. It's not for someone who needs to offload 4K RAW footage quickly.

The port spacing deserves specific praise. As shown in the product image, the four ports are evenly spaced with enough room between them that a wide flash drive won't block its neighbor. This sounds minor until you've owned a hub where port 1 makes ports 2-4 useless.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the limitations. There's no USB-C Power Delivery (PD) passthrough. If your laptop charges via USB-C, you can't plug your charger through this hub — you'll need to use a separate port. For many modern ultrabooks with only two USB-C ports, that means juggling.

The fixed cable is another tradeoff. Four inches is fine for a laptop, but if you want to use this with a desktop or position the hub away from your machine, you'll be fighting the cable the whole time.

And while 5Gbps is adequate, it's not current-gen. If you're buying for a machine that supports USB 3.2 Gen 2 (10Gbps), you're leaving speed on the table.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a MacBook, Dell XPS, or similar USB-C-only laptop and need to connect older USB-A peripherals — a mouse, keyboard receiver, flash drives, an external HDD. This is a travel-friendly, no-nonsense solution that costs less than a comparable multi-function hub.

**Skip it if:** You need to charge your laptop through the hub, require 10Gbps speeds for large file transfers, or want a hub with a detachable cable for flexible positioning.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my iPad Pro or Android tablet?**
A: Yes, if your device supports USB-C host mode. iPads run it fine for accessing flash drives. Android phones with USB-C and OTG support will recognize it too, though power draw from multiple devices might exceed what the phone can supply.

**Q: Does it support USB 2.0 devices?**
A: Yes, backward compatible. Older devices will run at their own speeds — a USB 2.0 mouse or keyboard will work without issue.

**Q: Can I use all four ports at once?**
A: Yes, but know the limits. Four bus-powered devices (like flash drives) work fine. If you connect power-hungry gear like external SSDs that draw significant current, you may see dropouts unless your laptop supplies enough power through the USB-C port.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The VIENON 4-Port USB Hub isn't flashy, but it's honest. It solves a specific problem — lacking USB-A ports on a modern laptop — without adding complexity or bulk. The build quality beats the price point, and the plug-and-play reliability is exactly what you want from a utility product.

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, compact way to connect USB-A devices to a USB-C laptop and don't need charging passthrough or blazing speeds.

**Skip it if** your workflow demands 10Gbps transfers or you need a single-cable solution that also charges your laptop. For everyone else, this is a solid, straightforward buy.
