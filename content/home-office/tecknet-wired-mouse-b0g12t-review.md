**TECKNET Wired Mouse Review: The $10 Office Workhorse That Skips the Frills**

Most wired mice under $15 are disposable afterthoughts — the kind you grab in a pinch and replace by spring. The TECKNET Wired Mouse, sitting at a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, claims to be different. After looking closely at what this thing actually offers, I can tell you: it's not revolutionary, but it's smarter than the price tag suggests.

**What You Actually Get**

The box is refreshingly minimal: the mouse itself and a 5-foot braided USB cable. No software CD, no driver disk, no manual thicker than a receipt. Plug it into any USB-A port — Windows, macOS, ChromeOS, even Linux — and it works instantly. The braided cable is a genuine upgrade over the cheap rubber sheaths on most budget mice; it resists kinks and won't split at the stress point after six months.

The shell is matte ABS plastic with a subtle textured grip on the sides. It's not rubberized, so it won't get sticky over time, but it also won't feel premium. The scroll wheel is rubber-coated with defined detents — notchy enough for precise spreadsheet navigation, quiet enough for a library. Weight comes in around 3.5 ounces, light enough for fast movement but heavy enough to feel planted.

**Performance in the Real World**

The optical sensor tops out at 2400 DPI with four adjustable settings (800/1200/1600/2400) controlled by a button behind the scroll wheel. For office work — spreadsheets, documents, web browsing — this is plenty. The 2400 DPI ceiling becomes a limitation only on high-resolution displays like a 27-inch 4K monitor, where you'll be lifting and re-centering constantly.

The clicks are the headline here. TECKNET uses silent switches rated for 10 million presses. They're genuinely quiet — a soft, dampened thud rather than the sharp click-clack of a standard office mouse. If you work in a shared space or take calls while clicking around, this matters more than you'd think.

What buyers consistently praise is the reliability. One reviewer noted, "I've had $40 mice die in a year. This one just keeps working." Another mentioned, "The cable is thick and well-made. No drag or resistance while moving." The ambidextrous shape is a smart choice for a budget device — it accommodates lefties without sacrificing comfort for right-handers.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the limitations. The 5-foot cable is the biggest practical gripe. If your tower sits on the floor under a desk with a cable management tray, you might find yourself an inch short. A 6-foot cable would have been the difference between "works" and "frustrating."

The DPI button placement is also awkward — it's positioned behind the scroll wheel, easy to hit accidentally when reaching for the wheel. You'll occasionally find yourself suddenly at 2400 DPI when you wanted 1200. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

And while the build quality is solid for the price, this is still a budget mouse. The plastic flexes slightly under firm grip, and the side texture won't win any design awards. It's functional, not beautiful.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Office workers** who need a reliable, quiet, zero-fuss mouse for 8-hour days
- **Students** who want something cheap enough to lose in a dorm room but durable enough to last a degree
- **IT departments** buying in bulk — this is the kind of mouse you hand out with a laptop and never hear about again

**Skip it if** you're a gamer (you need higher DPI and faster polling rates), you're on a 4K display, or your PC is positioned far from your desk.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this mouse work with Mac?**
A: Yes. It's plug-and-play on macOS, including the DPI button. No drivers needed.

**Q: Is it actually silent?**
A: Quieter than standard mice, but not silent. You'll hear a soft, muffled click. Good enough for a shared office, not for a recording studio.

**Q: How long is the cable?**
A: 5 feet (1.5 meters). It's braided and durable, but measure your setup before buying.

**The Verdict**

The TECKNET Wired Mouse is proof that you don't need to spend $30 to get a decent daily driver. It's quiet, reliable, and shape-comfortable for most hand sizes. The short cable and capped DPI are real limitations, but for the price, they're acceptable tradeoffs.

**Buy it if** you need a dependable office mouse that won't aggravate coworkers with click noise.

**Skip it if** you're on a 4K monitor, game even casually, or your setup demands a longer cable.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, no-nonsense pick for the budget-conscious office worker.
