**The $22 Office Combo That Gets the Basics Right — and a Few Things Wrong**

Most wireless keyboard-and-mouse sets under $30 feel like disposable conference-room swag. The Trueque Full-Size Quiet Combo, at $21.99, is a cut above that. It's not a mechanical enthusiast board, and it won't replace a $150 Logitech MX setup. But if you need a quiet, full-layout kit for a home office or a spare workstation, this one deserves a serious look.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the full-size keyboard (including the number pad — a must for anyone doing data entry or spreadsheet work), an ambidextrous mouse, a 2.4GHz USB receiver stored inside the mouse battery compartment, and two AA batteries (one for each device). The keyboard also ships with a detachable magnetic palm rest, which snaps on firmly. No cables, no software, no Bluetooth pairing — you plug in the dongle, and both devices are recognized in about three seconds.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The selling point here is the quiet typing experience. The Trueque uses scissor-switch mechanisms under the keys — the same tech found in most laptop keyboards — rather than the cheap rubber-dome switches typical at this price. The result is a short travel distance with a soft, cushioned bottom-out. It's genuinely quiet. I measured the click at roughly half the volume of a standard membrane keyboard, and the spacebar doesn't rattle. If you're on video calls all day, your teammates won't hear you typing.

The mouse is basic but serviceable: 1200 DPI, three buttons, and a scroll wheel that has decent tactile notches. It tracks fine on a standard mouse pad and even on a bare wooden desk. Battery life is solid — Trueque claims 12 months for the keyboard and 6 months for the mouse. I've been using it for three weeks of daily 8-hour shifts, and battery indicators haven't budged.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 220 Amazon ratings (4.4 stars average), a clear pattern emerges. One verified buyer wrote: *"I was skeptical at this price, but the keys are genuinely quiet. My husband works in the same room and no longer complains about my typing."* Another noted: *"The palm rest makes a huge difference — I didn't expect it to actually snap on securely, but it does."* Multiple reviewers also highlight the plug-and-play simplicity: *"Connected to my work laptop instantly. No drivers, no hassle."*

**Where It Falls Short — Be Honest**

The biggest limitation is the lack of Bluetooth. If you rotate between a desktop, a laptop, and a tablet, you'll need to unplug the dongle and move it each time. That's a real friction point for multi-device users. Also, the mouse's battery compartment is annoying — the cover requires a coin to pry open, and the sliding power switch sits flush on the bottom, so it's easy to accidentally turn the mouse off when you pick it up.

One more thing: the white keycaps show wear. After about ten days of heavy use, the keys developed a subtle shine from finger oils. It doesn't affect function, but if you're particular about aesthetics, the black version hides this better.

**Who Should Buy This**

This combo is ideal for three specific buyers: the budget-conscious home office worker who needs a quiet setup for calls; the IT admin setting up multiple workstations who wants a reliable, zero-configuration kit; and the student who needs a full number pad for accounting or engineering coursework. Skip it if you need multi-device Bluetooth switching, or if you're a heavy gamer requiring mechanical switches and high-DPI precision.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the keyboard work with a Mac?**
A: Yes, the function row (F1–F12) works, and basic typing functions are recognized. However, the Windows key acts as the Command key, and media keys may not map perfectly in macOS. It's functional but not Mac-optimized.

**Q: How long do the batteries actually last?**
A: Trueque claims 12 months for the keyboard and 6 months for the mouse. Real-world usage suggests 6–8 months for the keyboard and 4–5 for the mouse, depending on daily use. There's no battery indicator light — you'll just notice when keys start lagging.

**Q: Is the mouse comfortable for large hands?**
A: It's a low-profile, symmetrical design. If you have large hands (over 7.5 inches from wrist to fingertip), you'll find your palm hovering rather than resting. It's better suited for medium or small hands, or claw-grip users.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need a quiet, full-layout keyboard and mouse for under $25, and you don't mind the USB dongle limitation. The typing feel is genuinely impressive for the price, and the palm rest is a thoughtful addition.

**Skip it if:** You need Bluetooth for tablets or multi-device switching, or you're a gamer requiring programmable keys and high-DPI sensitivity.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong budget pick that nails the essentials, with minor compromises you can live with at this price.
