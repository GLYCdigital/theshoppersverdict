If you ship more than a dozen packages a month, you already know the math: boxes cost too much, weigh too much, and eat up storage space. Poly mailers are the sensible alternative, but buying cheap ones online is a gamble — you might get tissue-thin plastic that splits at the seams. The Reli. 10x13 500-pack sits in the middle of the market, and after digging through 220 ratings and the product specs, here's the honest picture.

**What you actually get**

The box contains 500 white poly mailers, each measuring 10x13 inches. That's a true 10x13 interior dimension — enough for a folded hoodie, a small book, or roughly three stacked shirts. The material is 2.2 mil polyethylene, which is thicker than the flimsy 1.5 mil bags you'll find at dollar-store prices but not as stiff as premium 3 mil options. Each mailer has a self-sealing adhesive strip and a perforated tear-away opening strip across the top.

**Real-world performance**

The 2.2 mil thickness is the sweet spot for most e-commerce sellers. It resists punctures from sharp corners on products, yet stays flexible enough to squeeze into tight mailbox slots or drop boxes. The adhesive is the standout feature — it bonds aggressively to itself, not to the contents. Multiple buyers noted that the seal held through USPS sorting machines without popping open. One reviewer put it plainly: "I've shipped over 200 items with these and never had one come back due to seal failure."

The mailers are also lightweight — roughly 10 grams each — which matters when you're paying per ounce on shipping. Compared to a corrugated box of similar capacity, you'll save about 4-6 ounces per shipment, which can drop a package into a lower rate tier.

**Where they fall short**

The white exterior is both a blessing and a curse. It looks clean for presentation, but it's a magnet for scuffs, dirt, and handling marks. If you're shipping to retail customers who expect a pristine unboxing, these will arrive with visible wear unless you double-bag or use a protective outer layer. That's a real tradeoff — dark gray or black mailers hide this far better.

The tear-away strip is functional but not flawless. Several reviewers reported that the perforation can be finicky, occasionally tearing at an angle and requiring a second pull to fully open the bag. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but it slows down your packing line if you're processing high volume.

**Who should buy this**

This 500-pack is clearly aimed at small-to-mid-volume sellers — think Etsy shop owners, eBay resellers, or anyone running a side hustle shipping 10-50 items a week. At roughly 13-15 cents per mailer, you'll save $30-50 over a comparable 100-pack from a premium brand. If you're shipping heavier items (over 5 pounds) or anything with sharp edges, spring for a thicker mailer or add padding — 2.2 mil won't protect a cast-iron skillet.

**Three questions shoppers actually ask**

*Will these fit a USPS Priority Mail flat-rate slot?* No — the 10x13 dimension is slightly larger than the flat-rate envelope opening. Use these for standard First Class or Priority cubic shipping instead.

*Can I write directly on the surface?* Yes, but ballpoint pens can skip on the slick plastic. A permanent marker works best, or use a shipping label — the surface takes adhesive labels cleanly.

*Are they recyclable?* They're LDPE #4 plastic, which most curbside programs won't take. Check your local drop-off — many grocery stores accept this grade.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you're moving moderate volume, want the lowest per-unit cost without sacrificing seal integrity, and don't mind white mailers that show handling scuffs. **Skip it if** you need a premium unboxing experience for retail customers, ship heavy or sharp objects, or prefer a tear strip that opens perfectly every time.

The Reli. 10x13 500-pack earns a 4.2/5. It's not the most refined mailer on the market, but for the price per unit, the durability-to-cost ratio is hard to beat. For most independent sellers, this is the pragmatic choice — and that's a compliment, not a compromise.
