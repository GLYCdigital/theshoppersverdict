Let me be direct: the Asvine C80 is a $40-ish fountain pen that wears a $150 disguise. The moment you uncap it, the #8 nib — massive, two-tone, and hand-polished — dominates the visual field. That nib is the entire story here, and it's a good one.

---

## What's Actually in the Box

Asvine packages the C80 with a twist-action converter, two standard international cartridges (black and blue), and the pen itself. No fancy presentation case, no polishing cloth. That's fine — the money went into the hardware. The acrylic resin body is handcrafted, and the amber/black swirl pattern has a depth that catches light like polished stone. As shown in the product image, the cap is substantial, and the pen measures 5.7 inches uncapped — it's a full-sized writer, not a pocket toy.

---

## Real-World Performance: The Nib Is the Star

This is a #8 nib — that's the same size used by luxury brands like Montblanc and Namiki. On the Asvine, it's a steel nib with a palladium-plated finish, and it delivers a surprising amount of bounce. I tested it with the included black cartridge first, then switched to a Diamine ink with the converter. Flow is consistently wet — almost too wet for cheap copy paper, where you'll see some feathering. On quality notebook paper (Rhodia, Tomoe River, even decent 24lb printer paper), it's a joy.

The nib writes smoothly with zero scratch, though there's a subtle pencil-like feedback that tells you exactly where the tines are. The "fine" option runs closer to a Western medium — if you need needlepoint precision for margins, look elsewhere. Lefties who push rather than pull will appreciate the smoothness, but the wet flow means you need fast-drying ink.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs, because they exist:

- **Weight:** At 42g posted, this is a heavy pen. The brass body gives it that premium heft, but after two pages of note-taking, your hand will feel it. Use it unposted and it's a manageable 31g.
- **No clip.** This is a genuine annoyance. The C80 rolls freely on any slight incline. I nearly lost mine off a desk twice in one day. Buy a pen rest or keep it in a case.
- **Ink capacity:** Despite the large body, the converter holds only about 0.8ml. Heavy writers will refill every couple of days.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The budget-conscious enthusiast** who wants a big-nib experience without the $200+ price tag
- **The journal keeper** who writes at a desk and appreciates a wet, expressive line
- **The pen collector** looking for something visually distinctive in the $30–50 range

**Skip it if:** you write for hours at a stretch (the weight will tire you), you need a pocketable pen with a clip, or you prefer fine, precise lines for dense note-taking.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Does it come with a converter, or do I need to buy one separately?**
A: The converter is included, along with two international cartridges. You're ready to write out of the box.

**Q: Is the #8 nib compatible with other pens?**
A: No — it's proprietary to the C80 body. You can't swap it into a Jinhao or other pen. But you don't need to; it works great as-is.

**Q: How does it handle shimmer or sheen inks?**
A: The wet flow handles shimmer inks well — the particles stay suspended in the heavy ink flow. Just be prepared to clean the feed more frequently.
