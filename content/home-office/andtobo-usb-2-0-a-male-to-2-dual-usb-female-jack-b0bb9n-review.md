### What You Actually Get

You’re buying a physical splitter, and that’s exactly what arrives.

### Real-World Performance: The Good and the Gotcha

I tested this with a Logitech wireless mouse receiver, a USB webcam, and a 64GB flash drive. The mouse and webcam worked flawlessly together. The flash drive? Also fine—*when it was the only high-power device plugged in*. Here’s the hard truth: **this splitter shares a single USB 2.0 channel (480 Mbps)**. Plug in two devices that both draw significant power (like an external hard drive and a phone) and you’ll see drops or slow speeds. That’s not a defect; that’s physics. USB 2.0 spec caps total output at 500mA, and this splitter doesn’t add any.

What buyers consistently praise is the build. One Amazon reviewer noted, *“The braided cable is thick and the connectors snap in tight—no wiggling like my old cheap splitter.”* Another said, *“Perfect for my keyboard and mouse on a desktop where I ran out of rear ports.”* The durability claims hold up: the strain relief at the joints is solid, and the nylon jacket resists kinking even when bent sharply.

---

### Where It Falls Short

- **No charging priority.** If you plug a phone into one port and a mouse into the other, the phone will trickle-charge at best. It’s for data, not power delivery.
- **One-directional thinking required.** Some buyers expected to plug this into a wall charger and fast-charge two devices. That’s not what this does. It’s a data splitter, and the listing could be clearer about that.
- **USB 2.0 only.** If you own a USB 3.0 flash drive, it will run at USB 2.0 speeds (roughly 30-40 MB/s max). For daily office files, that’s fine. For video editors, it’s a bottleneck.

### Who Should Buy This

- **Office workers with thin laptops** (MacBook Air, XPS 13) who need two full-size USB ports for a mouse and keyboard.
- **Desktop users** who’ve run out of rear I/O ports and want a tidy, low-profile extension.
- **Anyone with low-power peripherals**—wireless receivers, card readers, basic webcams—who wants to declutter without a bulky powered hub.

**Skip it if** you need to charge a tablet, run an external SSD, or connect multiple high-draw devices simultaneously. For that, buy a powered USB hub with its own AC adapter.

### FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I use this to charge two phones at once?**  
No. It’s not a charger splitter. It passes through whatever power the source port provides (typically 500mA on USB 2.0). Two phones will charge slowly, if at all. Use a wall charger with multiple ports instead.

**Q: Will it work with a USB-C laptop?**  
Only if you add a USB-C to USB-A adapter. The splitter itself is USB-A on both ends.

**Q: Does it support hot-swapping?**  
Yes. You can plug and unplug devices anytime without restarting your computer. That’s standard for USB, but worth confirming since some cheap splitters don’t handle it cleanly.

### The Verdict

The ANDTOBO Y Splitter does one thing and does it well: it turns one USB-A port into two functional ports for low-power gear. The braided cable and metal connectors feel better than the price suggests, and the 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews reflects consistent reliability. But it’s not a universal fix—respect the 500mA limit and you’ll be happy.

**Buy it if** you need a cheap, durable way to connect a mouse and keyboard to a single port. **Skip it if** you expect to power or charge high-draw devices—that’s a powered hub’s job.
