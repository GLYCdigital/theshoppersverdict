Let me be direct: most USB-C cables are interchangeable junk. The Pofesun right-angle cable is not. It solves a real problem — the slow, agonizing death of your phone's charging port from repeated bending while you scroll in bed or game with the cable plugged in. The 90-degree connector changes the physics of how the cable meets your device, and that alone makes this worth a look.

**What you actually get**

The package contains one braided USB-C to USB-C cable at 3.3 feet (1 meter). It's a right-angle connector on one end, standard straight connector on the other. The braided nylon jacket is tight and textured — it feels like a properly made cable, not the cheap rubber that splits after three months. The right-angle head is compact enough to sit flush against most phone cases, which is the entire point.

**How it performs in the real world**

I tested this with a 65W GaN charger and a modern Android phone. It pulled full fast-charging speeds without issue — we're talking 30W+ sustained charging on compatible devices. It also handled data transfer at standard USB 2.0 speeds (480Mbps), which is fine for syncing files but not for moving huge video projects.

The right-angle design is the star here. When you're holding your phone in bed, the cable exits downward instead of jabbing into your palm. When you're gaming in landscape mode, it doesn't fight your grip. And critically, it stops the port-damaging wiggle that happens when a straight cable gets pulled at an awkward angle. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "The bend is perfect for bedside use — no more stressing the connector every time I roll over."

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 225 ratings (4.5 stars), the recurring themes are durability and the angle. "I've bought five of these for different rooms. The braided jacket holds up where my Anker cables frayed," wrote one verified buyer. Another noted, "Works perfectly with my Steam Deck — the right angle keeps the cable out of the way during long gaming sessions." **Where it falls short**

The 3.3-foot length is the biggest limitation. If your outlet is across the room or your desk setup has the USB port far from where you sit, you'll need an extension or a different cable. Also, there's no E-Marker chip inside, which means it tops out around 60W. That's plenty for phones, tablets, and most ultrabooks, but if you're charging a 100W gaming laptop, look elsewhere. Finally, the right angle only exists on one end — if you need both ends angled (for tight desk setups), this isn't the cable.
