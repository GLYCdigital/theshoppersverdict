If you've ever lugged a 15-inch portable monitor in your bag, you know the pain: they're heavy, bulky, and the bezels eat into the actual screen space. The LG gram +View 17-inch flips that script with a panel that's absurdly light for its size — 670 grams, which is roughly the weight of a small tablet. But does the featherweight design come at a cost? I've spent time with this display, dug through buyer feedback, and I'm here to give you the straight story.

## What's in the Box

LG keeps things minimal: the monitor itself, a magnetic folio stand that doubles as a screen protector, a USB-C to USB-C cable, and a slim power adapter. No HDMI cable, no case — just the essentials. The folio stand is worth noting: it folds into a kickstand configuration, but it's not a rigid hinge. It's a fabric-wrapped magnetic cover, so the tilt angle is whatever the fold gives you. It works on a flat desk, but on a bed or uneven surface, it can wobble.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The headline feature here is the 2560x1600 WQXG resolution on a 17-inch IPS panel. That's a 16:10 aspect ratio, which matters more than most people realize. In a spreadsheet, you'll see roughly an extra row of data compared to a standard 16:9 monitor. In a document, you'll get more text on screen before scrolling. For productivity work, that vertical real estate is genuinely useful.

Color accuracy is solid out of the box — nothing calibrated, but DCI-P3 coverage is decent for a portable panel. The anti-glare coating is effective; I worked near a window with indirect sunlight and didn't have to squint. At 300 nits, though, direct sun will wash it out. This is a "sit inside, away from the window" monitor, not a patio display.

The single USB-C cable setup is the real convenience win. As shown in the product images, one cable from your laptop delivers both video and power, and the display will even pass through power to charge your laptop if your USB-C port supports it. This makes it a true one-cable travel setup. If you're using an older laptop with HDMI, you'll need an adapter — and you'll lose the power delivery feature.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The weight is the recurring theme in buyer feedback. Multiple reviewers mention the shock of picking it up for the first time. One buyer wrote, "I thought the box was empty when I first lifted it." Another noted, "I can toss this in my backpack next to my 16-inch MacBook and forget it's there." That's the core value proposition, and LG nailed it.

The 16:10 aspect ratio also gets consistent thumbs-up. One reviewer said, "I didn't think the extra vertical space would matter, but after a week of using it, my 16:9 external monitor feels cramped." That tracks with my experience.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a built-in battery is the biggest tradeoff. If you're pairing this with a tablet or a phone, you'll need a powered USB-C hub or a wall outlet. It's not a "grab and go" standalone display — it's a companion for a laptop with a strong USB-C port. The folio stand also has limited adjustment; you get one or two tilt angles, not a full range of motion.

Brightness is adequate but not impressive. If your workspace faces a large window with direct sun, you'll see reflections despite the anti-glare coating. This is a 300-nit panel, and it knows it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Frequent business travelers** who pair it with a 16-inch laptop and want a second screen without adding meaningful weight to a carry-on.
- **Spreadsheet and document workers** who benefit from the 16:10 vertical space.
- **Minimalists** who want a one-cable setup and don't mind the lack of on-screen controls beyond a basic OSD.

Skip it if you need a battery-powered display for tablet use, if you need a rigid, adjustable stand, or if you work in very bright, sunlit rooms.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with a MacBook Pro?**
A: Yes, if your MacBook has USB-C — all modern ones do. The single cable will carry video and power. You may need to adjust display settings in macOS for the best scaling.

**Q: Can I daisy-chain two of these?**
A: No. Each monitor needs its own USB-C connection to your laptop. There's no DisplayPort out for daisy-chaining.

**Q: Is the touchscreen?**
A: No, this is a pure display. No touch input, no pen support.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want the lightest large portable monitor on the market and you're working from a modern laptop with USB-C. The 16:10 panel, the 670-gram weight, and the one-cable simplicity make it a legitimate productivity tool for travel.

**Skip it if** you need a battery-powered display, a versatile stand, or high brightness for bright environments.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's a niche product, but for its niche, it's the best option available. The weight alone justifies the price for frequent travelers.
