If you've been hunting for a storage upgrade for a thin-and-light laptop and keep hitting a wall because the drive bay is shorter than standard, this Transcend M.2 2242 PCIe SSD is likely the exact part you've been missing. The 2242 form factor—22mm wide, 42mm long—is the oddball size used in many ultra-portables, mini PCs, and even some gaming handhelds. Most manufacturers only ship 2280 drives, which simply don't fit. This one does.

**What you actually get**

The package is straightforward: the SSD itself, a quick-start guide, and a screw if your laptop's bay doesn't already have one mounted. No cloning software disc, no external enclosure—just the drive. You'll want to download Transcend's SSD Scope utility from their site for firmware updates and health checks. The drive uses a PCIe Gen3 x4 interface, which is the sweet spot for older and mid-range ultrabooks that don't support Gen4 anyway.

**Real-world performance**

In practice, sequential reads hover around 3,500 MB/s and writes around 2,900 MB/s—right at the ceiling for PCIe Gen3. But the more important spec here is the integrated DRAM cache. Budget SSDs often skip DRAM to cut costs, which causes write speeds to tank once the pseudo-SLC cache fills up. This Transcend keeps a dedicated DRAM chip on board, so sustained transfers—say, moving a 50GB video project folder—stay consistently fast instead of dropping to HDD-like levels halfway through.

One buyer who upgraded an older Dell XPS noted: *"Boot times dropped from 45 seconds to about 12. The drive runs cooler than the Samsung it replaced, and I haven't seen any throttling during long gaming sessions."* Another user with a Surface-style tablet wrote: *"Finally a 2242 drive that doesn't feel like a compromise. CrystalDiskMark numbers match the spec sheet almost exactly."*

**Where it falls short**

There's no bundled heatsink, and in a cramped ultra-thin chassis with poor airflow, this drive can run warm under heavy load. It's not dangerous—the controller throttles before damage occurs—but you may see slight speed dips during sustained writes in hot environments. If your laptop has a metal bottom panel that acts as a heat spreader, you'll likely be fine.

Also, this is strictly a 2242 drive. If your laptop takes the more common 2280 length, this won't fit without a bracket adapter, and even then the mounting hole alignment can be tricky. Measure your existing drive before ordering.

**Who should buy this**

- **Ultrabook owners** with a 2242 slot who want to replace a slow eMMC or aging SATA drive.
- **Mini PC and handheld gamers** running out of space on their 2242 boot drive.
- **IT professionals** standardizing on one reliable 2242 SKU for fleet upgrades.

Skip it if your machine takes 2280 drives, or if you need Gen4 speeds for heavy video editing—this tops out at Gen3 bandwidth.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Is this drive compatible with my Lenovo ThinkPad / Dell XPS / Surface?**
Check your laptop's service manual for "M.2 2242" support. Most thin-and-lights from 2018 onward with a 42mm slot will work. If your bay says "2280," it will not fit.

**Q: Does it support NVMe or is it SATA?**
This is NVMe PCIe Gen3 x4. It will not work in a SATA-only M.2 slot, so verify your motherboard or laptop spec before purchasing.

**Q: Can I use it as an external drive?**
Yes, with a USB 3.2 Gen2 NVMe enclosure that accepts 2242 drives. Some enclosures only fit 2280, so check the compatibility list first.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have a 2242 slot and want a reliable, DRAM-cached drive that won't choke on sustained writes. It's a solid performer with a 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews for good reason.

**Skip it if** your laptop takes standard 2280 drives, or you're chasing Gen4-level sequential speeds for professional 4K video work.
