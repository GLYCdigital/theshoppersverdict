## What's Actually on the Spool

You're getting 1000 feet of 24AWG solid bare copper wire, not the copper-clad aluminum (CCA) junk that floods Amazon at lower price points. That distinction matters. CCA conducts poorly, runs hot with PoE, and fails terminations. This cable is the real deal — and several buyers confirmed it with magnet tests and resistance checks.

The cable carries a CMR (Communications Riser) rating, meaning it's approved for vertical runs in residential and commercial buildings. If you're pulling through walls, this is the fire safety rating you need — CMP plenum is for air handling spaces, but CMR covers most home and office runs without the extra cost.

The listing markets it as "CAT6e" — technically that's not an official TIA/EIA category, but the cable tests to Cat6 specs at 550MHz. It's effectively premium Cat6. Don't overthink the label.

## Real-World Performance

Punching down this cable onto keystone jacks is straightforward. The 24AWG solid conductors are thick enough to seat firmly in IDC terminals without splitting, but not so stiff that they're painful to work with. One reviewer noted: "Terminated all 48 drops without a single bad end — that's rare for bulk cable." That's the experience I'd expect from quality solid copper.

For gigabit runs, you'll get full 1000BASE-T performance at the 100-meter limit. If you're setting up 10GBASE-T, keep runs under 55 meters — that's standard for Cat6, not a flaw. PoE is a non-issue here; solid bare copper handles the heat dissipation that PoE+ (802.3at) and even PoE++ (802.3bt) demands without the voltage drop you'd see in CCA.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this spool has two honest drawbacks.

First, coil memory is real. After sitting on the spool, the cable wants to hold its coil shape. Pulling long horizontal runs requires tension and some patience — it's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you start.

Second, there's no dispenser box. A 1000ft spool of solid copper cable weighs around 25 pounds. If you're working alone, you'll want a spool roller or a helper to keep it feeding smoothly. This is standard for bulk cable at this price, but it's a logistics consideration.

## FAQ

**Is this actually CMR riser rated, or just marked as such?**
Yes — the jacket is printed with the CMR rating and the NEC code designation. Several buyers confirmed it passed inspection in residential and commercial builds. It's not plenum rated, so don't use it in HVAC air returns.

**Can I use this for outdoor runs?**
No. This is indoor riser cable. The jacket isn't UV-rated or water-resistant. For outdoor runs, you need direct burial or outdoor-rated cable. Don't save money here — water ingress will destroy the copper.

**Does it support 10GBASE-T?**
Yes, up to 55 meters (180 feet). Beyond that, you're limited to gigabit speeds. For most home and small office runs, that's more than enough headroom.
