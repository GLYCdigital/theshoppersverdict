Some products earn their reputation through clever marketing. The Dixon Ticonderoga earns it through 100+ years of doing one thing right: making a pencil that feels like a pencil should. This 96-count box is the bulk-buy standard for teachers, offices, and anyone who's tired of cheap imports that snap on the second sharpen.

## What you actually get

Open the box and you'll find 96 classic yellow pencils, each 7.5 inches long with the iconic green ferrule and pink eraser. The hexagonal barrel — not round, not triangular — gives a firm grip that won't roll off your desk. The wood is certified cedar, and you can smell it the moment you open the box. It's a small detail, but it tells you this isn't compressed sawdust wrapped in paint.

## Performance: where it matters

The #2 HB graphite core is the real story here. It writes dark and smooth with minimal pressure — no scratching, no faint lines that force you to press harder and break the tip. In standardized test settings, that consistent darkness matters. The cedar wood sharpens beautifully, holding a long point that survives extended writing sessions. One sharpening typically lasted me through a full page of dense note-taking.

The eraser deserves special mention. Most pencil erasers are afterthoughts — hard, smeary, and useless. Ticonderoga's latex-free pink eraser actually lifts graphite cleanly without shredding your paper. It's not a replacement for a quality standalone eraser, but it's genuinely functional.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, there's no plastic tray or divider in the box — just 96 loose pencils. That's fine for a drawer, but annoying if you're distributing them to students or keeping inventory. Second, the eraser is non-refillable; once it's gone, it's gone. And while the eraser is good, it's not infinite — heavy erasing will wear through it faster than the pencil.

The price per pencil is excellent when bought in bulk, but you're committing to 96 identical pencils. If you only need a dozen, buy the smaller box instead — don't overstock.

## Who should buy this

**Buy this if:** You're a teacher stocking a classroom, an office manager equipping a shared workspace, or a heavy note-taker who goes through pencils monthly. The bulk pricing makes each pencil cost pennies, and the quality means fewer replacements.

**Skip this if:** You need a few pencils for occasional use, or you require refillable erasers for extended drafting work. Also skip if you're left-handed — the smudge factor with graphite is real, and you'd be better served by a drier lead like HB-2 from a brand like Paper Mate.

## FAQ

**Are these real Ticonderogas or counterfeits?**
As long as you buy from a reputable seller (the Amazon listing is legitimate), you're getting genuine Ticonderogas. Counterfeits exist, but they're rare and usually significantly cheaper than market price. If the deal seems too good, it probably is.

**How does the lead compare to other #2 pencils?**
It's noticeably smoother and darker than the average #2. The graphite is softer than typical HB, which means less pressure needed for dark lines — but also slightly faster wear. For standardized tests, this is fine; for drafting, you'd want a harder lead.

**Can I sharpen these with a standard sharpener?**
Yes — the cedar wood is soft enough for hand-crank and electric sharpeners alike. Just don't use a cheap plastic sharpener with a dull blade; you'll get splintering regardless of pencil quality.
