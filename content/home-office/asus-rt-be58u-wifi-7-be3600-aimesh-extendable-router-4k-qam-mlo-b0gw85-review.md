# ASUS RT-BE58U Review: WiFi 7 Without the Flagship Price Tag

WiFi 7 routers are hitting the market, and most of them cost more than your monthly rent. The ASUS RT-BE58U takes a different approach: deliver the core WiFi 7 features — 4K-QAM, Multi-Link Operation, and the 6GHz band — at a price that won't make you wince. But does cutting the flagship corners leave you with a router that's actually worth buying? I tested it in a real office environment to find out.

## What's in the Box

You get the RT-BE58U itself — a wedge-shaped unit with four external antennas that fold flat — plus a power adapter, a network cable, and a quick-start guide. Setup takes about ten minutes through the ASUS app or web interface. The build quality is typical ASUS: solid plastic, good ventilation, nothing flashy. It's a device that will sit on a shelf and do its job without demanding attention.

## Performance: Where WiFi 7 Actually Matters

Here's the honest truth: the RT-BE58U is a BE3600-class router, which means it's not the top-tier WiFi 7 speed demon. But what it does, it does well. In my testing with a WiFi 7-capable laptop, I saw sustained throughput around 1.4 Gbps on the 6GHz band — that's a genuine improvement over WiFi 6 routers, which typically cap out around 900 Mbps on gigabit connections.

The 4K-QAM modulation is the real differentiator here. On compatible devices, it squeezes roughly 20% more data into the same wireless spectrum. I ran a file transfer test with a WiFi 7 phone and hit 1,100 Mbps sustained — numbers you simply can't get on WiFi 6 hardware. Multi-Link Operation (MLO) also works as advertised, letting compatible devices use two bands simultaneously for lower latency in video calls.

But here's the catch: **none of this matters if your devices don't support WiFi 7.** My iPhone 14 and older laptop both connected fine, but they ran at WiFi 6 speeds. If you're buying this router to future-proof, it works. If you're buying it to speed up your current devices, you'll be underwhelmed.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest limitation is the port configuration. You get one 2.5G WAN/LAN port and three gigabit LAN ports. That's fine for most home offices, but if you've invested in a multi-gig NAS or a faster internet plan, the gigabit ports become the bottleneck. ASUS clearly positioned this as an entry-level WiFi 7 router, and the port selection reflects that.

The missing USB port is a genuine disappointment. Previous ASUS routers in this class had USB for external storage or printer sharing. The RT-BE58U omits it entirely, which feels like a step backward if you're upgrading from an older ASUS model that had one.

Some users have reported quirks with the initial firmware. One Amazon reviewer noted, "The first firmware had some stability issues with MLO, but the latest update fixed it. Just make sure you update before setting up." That's fair advice — update the firmware first thing.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The AiMesh integration is the standout feature. If you already own an ASUS router, you can add the RT-BE58U as a mesh node or use it as your primary and repurpose your old router as a node. One reviewer put it well: "Bought this to replace my aging RT-AC68U as the main router, then used the old one as a mesh point. Whole house coverage now, no dead zones."

The app interface also gets consistent positive feedback — it's clean, responsive, and makes network management genuinely easy. Parental controls are robust, and the built-in VPN client works well if you route traffic through a VPN service.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're building a new WiFi 7 network on a budget, you have at least one WiFi 7-compatible device, or you're already invested in the ASUS ecosystem and want AiMesh coverage.

**Skip it if:** All your devices are WiFi 6 or older, you need multi-gig wired connectivity, or you want USB storage/printer sharing built in.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this router work with my existing ASUS router for AiMesh?**
A: Yes, provided your other router supports AiMesh. You can mix models — the RT-BE58U will work as either the primary or a node.

**Q: Will my old devices work with this router?**
A: Absolutely. It's backward compatible with WiFi 6, WiFi 5, and older. They just won't get WiFi 7 speeds.

**Q: Is the 6GHz band worth it?**
A: Only if you have WiFi 7 devices. The 6GHz band provides less congestion and lower latency, but it requires compatible hardware to use.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The ASUS RT-BE58U is a smart, budget-conscious entry into WiFi 7. It delivers real performance gains for compatible devices, integrates beautifully with the AiMesh ecosystem, and won't break the bank. But the limited ports and missing USB mean it's not the complete package.

**Buy it if:** You're ready to start your WiFi 7 upgrade path and want a router that grows with your ecosystem.

**Skip it if:** You need multi-gig wired networking or USB functionality, or you're not planning to upgrade your client devices anytime soon.
