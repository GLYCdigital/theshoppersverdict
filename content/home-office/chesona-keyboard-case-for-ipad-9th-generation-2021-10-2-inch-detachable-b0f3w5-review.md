Let's be honest about what the CHESONA Keyboard Case for the iPad 9th Generation actually is: a budget-friendly way to turn a $329 tablet into a laptop-style workhorse. It's not a Magic Keyboard clone, and it doesn't pretend to be. But for the price, it delivers more than most shoppers expect.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes the keyboard case itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a basic instruction sheet. The case is a two-piece design — a silicone back shell that snaps onto your iPad and a detachable keyboard deck that connects magnetically. The keyboard charges via USB-C and connects over Bluetooth. The back shell has a built-in pencil holder, which is a nice touch if you use an Apple Pencil (1st gen, since that's what the 9th Gen supports).

The keyboard deck measures roughly 10.5 inches wide and weighs about 1.1 pounds on its own. Combined with the iPad, you're looking at around 2.3 pounds total. That's heavier than a MacBook Air, so this isn't a featherweight setup.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I tested this case for a week of daily use — email, note-taking, Google Docs, and some light spreadsheet work. The key travel is surprisingly decent for a membrane keyboard. There's a satisfying tactile bump, and the keys are spaced well enough that I wasn't constantly hitting the wrong letters. The backlighting is a standout feature: seven colors, three brightness levels, and it actually illuminates the keys evenly.

The trackpad is where budget cases usually fall apart. Not here. It supports multi-touch gestures — two-finger scrolling, three-finger swipe between apps, and tap-to-click. It's not as smooth as Apple's trackpads, but it's responsive and accurate enough for real work. One caveat: the trackpad surface is small (about 3.5 inches wide), so if you have large hands, you might find yourself hunting for the right spot.

Bluetooth pairing was painless — it connected on the first try and reconnected automatically every time I snapped the keyboard back on. Battery life is rated at 90 days with backlighting off and about 20 days with it on, based on two hours of daily use. I didn't drain it in my testing week, so that claim seems plausible.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews reflects a pattern. One reviewer wrote, "I was skeptical about the trackpad at this price, but it actually works. I've used it for two weeks now and haven't touched my mouse once." Another said, "The backlit keys are the reason I bought this. My previous case had no backlight and I was always squinting in dim rooms."

Multiple reviewers specifically call out the magnetic detach feature as a selling point — you can pop the keyboard off and use the iPad as a tablet without wrestling with a bulky hinge. The 360° rotation also gets consistent praise, with users noting it works well for watching videos in tent mode.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest tradeoff is bulk. This case adds about 0.7 inches to the iPad's thickness and roughly 1.1 pounds. If you're looking for a slim folio, this isn't it. It's a workhorse, not a showpiece.

The lack of a top function row is another annoyance. There's no dedicated key for brightness or volume — you'll need to swipe down Control Center or use the on-screen controls. For a productivity-focused case, this feels like a missed opportunity.

A few reviewers reported occasional Bluetooth dropouts after the iPad went to sleep, requiring a quick reconnect. I didn't experience this myself, but it's worth noting if you're sensitive to connection hiccups.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Students** who need a budget laptop alternative for notes and essays
- **Remote workers** who want a portable second screen setup without spending $300+ on Apple's keyboard
- **Casual users** who want backlit keys and a trackpad without the premium price tag

Skip it if you prioritize portability over functionality, or if you need a keyboard with dedicated function keys for your workflow.

**FAQ**

**Does this case work with the iPad 10th Generation?**  
No. This is specifically molded for the 9th Generation (2021) 10.2-inch iPad. The camera cutout and button placement don't match the 10th Gen.

**Can you use the keyboard while the iPad is charging?**  
Yes, there's a cutout on the side for the Lightning port. The keyboard charges separately via its own USB-C port, so you can charge both simultaneously.

**Will the Apple Pencil charge while in the holder?**  
Yes, the pencil holder is positioned so the Pencil's charging connector aligns with the iPad's Lightning port. You can slide the Pencil in and it will charge.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a functional, backlit keyboard with a working trackpad for your iPad 9th Gen without spending a fortune. It's a solid daily driver for productivity tasks.

**Skip it if** you need a slim, light case, or if you can't tolerate occasional Bluetooth reconnection needed after sleep.
