## Is This the Last Outdoor Cable You'll Buy?

Most outdoor Ethernet cables are either flimsy indoor cables painted black or overpriced "industrial" options that cost three times as much. The Black Cat5e Outdoor Ethernet Cable sits in a practical middle ground — it's built for weather exposure without the boutique price tag. With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's clearly doing something right. Here's what you actually need to know before buying.

## What You Get

The package is straightforward: the cable itself, terminated with RJ45 connectors on both ends. As shown in the product image, the connectors feature molded strain-relief boots that protect the terminations from moisture and tugging. The jacket is matte black, UV-resistant polyethylene — the same material used in many commercial outdoor cables. It's available in standard lengths from 10 to 100 feet, which covers most security camera and outdoor access point installs.

## Real-World Performance

The key spec here is 24 AWG solid bare copper. Many budget cables use copper-clad aluminum (CCA) to cut costs, but CCA degrades signal quality over distance and runs hotter. This one uses solid copper, which means you'll get reliable gigabit speeds up to the Cat5e spec of 100 meters. For typical runs — a security camera 50 feet from your router, or a weatherproof AP mounted on a porch — you won't notice any latency or throughput loss.

The gel-filled jacket is the headline feature. It's genuinely waterproof, not just water-resistant. Rain, sprinklers, and humidity won't wick into the cable. One reviewer noted, "I've had this running along my fence line through two Minnesota winters and it still looks brand new." That matches the general sentiment — the cable holds up to sun exposure and freezing temperatures without the jacket cracking or stiffening to the point of breaking.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the tradeoffs. The connector boots are bulky — fine for open runs, but if you're feeding this through a tight ½-inch conduit, you'll struggle. The cable itself is also not direct-burial rated, so if you're planning to bury it underground, you need to run it through conduit anyway.

A more practical annoyance: the coil comes tightly wound with no spool. Expect to spend a few minutes untangling it without kinking the jacket. One reviewer complained, "The memory from the coil makes it want to stay curled for the first day. Not a dealbreaker, but mildly irritating."

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're installing outdoor security cameras, a Wi-Fi access point under an eave, or running Ethernet between outbuildings where weather exposure is a factor. It's also a solid choice for anyone who's been burned by cheap cables that fail after one season.

**Skip it if** you need direct-burial cable, if your run exceeds 100 meters (you'd need fiber or a powered switch anyway), or if you need a flat cable to run under siding or carpet. And if you only need indoor cable, you're overpaying for the outdoor jacket.

## FAQ

**Is this cable rated for direct burial?**  
No. It's weatherproof for above-ground exposure, but you need conduit for underground runs. The jacket isn't rated for soil contact or groundwater pressure.

**Can I use it for PoE (Power over Ethernet)?**  
Yes. The 24 AWG solid copper conductors handle PoE+ (up to 30W) without issue. It's commonly used for outdoor PoE cameras and access points.

**Does it work with gigabit speeds?**  
Cat5e is rated for gigabit up to 100 meters. In practice, this cable delivers full gigabit throughput at typical residential distances. If you're planning 10-gigabit networking, you need Cat6a or better.
