## What You're Actually Getting

The package includes the laser-engraved name plate itself plus both mounting systems: a wall-mount bracket with screws and a clear acrylic desk stand. As shown in the product image, the plate sits at a slight angle in the desk holder — readable from across the room, not just when someone's standing over your keyboard.

The engraving is the headline feature here. Lasercrafting cuts the text into the material rather than printing on top of it. That means no vinyl letters peeling at the corners six months in, no fading from sunlight hitting your window desk. One reviewer put it plainly: *"The engraving is deep and crisp. Looks way more expensive than it was."* Another noted the font choices actually look professional — not the default Comic Sans disaster you get from some personalization shops.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The plate itself has a solid, substantial feel. It's rigid, not flimsy, and the edges are finished cleanly — no sharp burrs or rough spots that snag your sleeves. The engraving depth is consistent across the entire surface, which is something cheaper laser shops often botch.

Here's where the honest tradeoffs come in. The desk holder is functional but basic — it's a simple acrylic slot, not a weighted base. On a glossy or glass desk, it can slide around if you bump it. Several reviewers mentioned adding a small piece of mounting putty to keep it in place. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you buy.

The wall-mount option is more secure. Screws are included, and the bracket holds the plate flush against the wall. Good for office doors, conference rooms, or cubicle entrances where you want something that won't get knocked over.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the sliding desk stand, there are two limitations worth flagging. First, personalization is text-only. If you wanted your company logo or a custom graphic engraved, this isn't the product for that — you'll need a shop that handles vector artwork. Second, the plate size options matter more than you'd think. The smaller sizes are genuinely compact; measure your desk or door before ordering, because what looks proportionate in the product photo might feel undersized in person.
