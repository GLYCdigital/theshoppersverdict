If you handle client data, legal files, or medical records, you already know the drill: a forgotten laptop bag is a career-ending event. The Apricorn Aegis NVX is built specifically for that paranoid crowd — and it's fast enough that you won't resent carrying it.

**What's in the box**

The Aegis NVX ships with the drive itself, a USB-C to USB-A cable, and a quick-start guide. That's it. No software CD, no bloatware installer, no cloud subscription push. The lack of a USB-C to USB-C cable is a real oversight at this price point — you'll likely need to buy one separately to hit full speed on modern laptops. The drive is available in 500GB, 1TB, and 2TB capacities; the 1TB model reviewed here measures 3.5 x 2.2 x 0.6 inches and weighs 2.9 ounces.

**Performance and real-world use**

This is where the NVX earns its keep. Over USB 3.2 Gen 2, I measured sequential reads around 1,000 MB/s and writes near 950 MB/s using Blackmagic Disk Speed Test — that's roughly double what a SATA-based encrypted drive delivers. Large video files transfer in seconds, not minutes. The NVMe controller inside doesn't throttle during sustained writes either; a 50GB folder copy held steady without dropping to SATA-like speeds.

The hardware encryption is the headline feature. The built-in alphanumeric keypad requires an 8–16 digit PIN before the drive mounts. There's no software involved, which means it works on Windows, macOS, Linux, and even Chromebooks without driver headaches. The drive locks automatically when unplugged, and after 10 failed PIN attempts it performs a cryptographic erase — a feature that makes brute-force attacks pointless.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The Amazon reviews at 4.1 stars across 204 ratings cluster around durability and security. One reviewer noted, "I've dropped this off a workbench onto concrete twice and it still reads perfectly. My old encrypted drive would have shattered." Another said, "Finally, a drive that doesn't require me to install 'SecureDrive Manager' on every client's computer. The PIN pad is the only thing that matters."

**Where it falls short**

The PIN entry is the biggest daily friction point. If you dock and undock your laptop throughout the day, you'll punch in that PIN constantly — it takes about 5 seconds each time, and it's easy to fat-finger. The drive also runs warm under sustained load; not alarming, but noticeable on bare skin.

The IP67 rating is genuine — I submerged it briefly and it survived — but the exposed USB-C port lacks a rubber flap. Apricorn includes a cap, but it's easy to lose. And while the aluminum body feels premium, it's a fingerprint magnet.

**Who should buy this**

- **IT professionals** managing encrypted backups for small businesses — the hardware encryption eliminates software compliance headaches
- **Field workers** (inspectors, surveyors, photojournalists) who need crash-proof storage for on-site data
- **Anyone handling HIPAA or GDPR-regulated data** who needs verifiable hardware encryption without annual software licensing fees

**Skip it if** you just need fast, cheap storage for game captures or media files — the non-encrypted Samsung T7 or Crucial X9 offer similar speeds for less money and smaller footprints.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the Aegis NVX work with an iPad or Android phone?**
A: Yes, but with caveats. The drive draws power from the host device, so older iPads or phones may report "device draws too much power." Newer USB-C devices work fine, but you'll need a USB-C to USB-C cable (sold separately).

**Q: Can I change the PIN if I forget it?**
A: No — the drive performs a cryptographic erase after 10 failed attempts. You'll need to reinitialize the drive with a new encryption key, which wipes all data. This is a security feature, not a bug.

**Q: Is the encryption FIPS 140-2 validated?**
A: Yes, the Aegis NVX is listed on the FIPS 140-2 Level 2 validated modules list, which makes it compliant for federal and defense contractor use.

**The Verdict**

The Apricorn Aegis NVX is the right tool for a specific job: moving sensitive data in hostile environments. It's fast, genuinely rugged, and the hardware encryption is bulletproof — no software to fail, no drivers to fight. The ergonomic friction of PIN entry and the missing USB-C cable are real tradeoffs, but they don't undermine the core mission.

**Buy it if** you need verifiable hardware encryption with real speed and drop-proof durability.

**Skip it if** you just want a fast backup drive and don't need the security theater — you'll pay a premium for features you won't use.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A specialized tool that executes its primary function flawlessly, with minor annoyances that won't matter to its target buyer.
