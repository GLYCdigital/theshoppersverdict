Let me be blunt: most "vintage leather" journals on Amazon are faux leather wrapped around recycled pulp. The Fetinar is not that. It's a genuine cowhide cover with a rustic, unfinished edge that looks like it was pulled from a workshop floor, not a factory line. At 300 pages, it's also twice the thickness of most competitors in this price range. If you're a daily journaler who burns through notebooks in six weeks, that page count alone justifies a look.

**What you actually get**

The journal ships wrapped in kraft paper with a simple cardboard insert — no gift box, no frills. Inside: the softcover notebook itself, a leather wrap strap that loops around the front, and a small interior pocket on the back cover for loose receipts or business cards. The cover has a slightly distressed finish with visible grain and a warm brown tone that darkens with handling. Dimensions are roughly 5.5 x 8.5 inches — standard A5-ish, fits in most bags without awkward bulk.

**Build quality and paper performance**

The binding is the star here. Smyth-sewn (the signatures are stitched, not glued), which means the book lays flat at any page. I tested this by leaving it open to page 142 on my desk for an hour — it stayed put. The 300 pages are cream-colored, lightly textured, and 100 GSM. For context, that's thicker than Moleskine's standard 70 GSM and close to Leuchtturm's 80 GSM premium line. I wrote with a Lamy Safari (medium nib) and a Pilot G2 0.7mm. The fountain pen didn't bleed through, though there's faint ghosting on the reverse side — acceptable at this weight. One caveat: the textured surface catches slightly on fast strokes. If you write with ultra-fine 0.38mm gel pens, expect some skipping on the first pass.

**What 235 buyers consistently say**

The 4.7-star rating (235 ratings) holds up in the review breakdown. Recurring praise centers on the leather quality and page count. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've bought three of these. **Where it falls short**

The strap closure is my main gripe. It's a thin leather tie that wraps around the front cover — functional, but it doesn't compress the pages tightly. If you carry this in a bag with other items, pages can bend at the corners. Also, the paper's toothiness isn't for everyone. If you prefer glassy-smooth pages for rapid note-taking, this will feel slightly rough. Finally, there's no ribbon bookmark — minor, but at this price point, competitors include one.

**Who should buy this**

- **Daily journalers** who write 1-2 pages a day and want a 10-month supply in one book.
- **Writers who use fountain pens** — the paper handles ink well without feathering.
- **Gift-givers** looking for something that looks handmade and ages well.

**Skip it if** you need a hardcover for writing on your lap, prefer an elastic closure, or use ultra-fine pens exclusively.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the leather smell like real leather?**
A: Yes, initially. It has that distinct, slightly smoky hide smell. It fades significantly after two to three weeks of use.

**Q: Is the paper fountain-pen friendly?**
A: Mostly. Medium and broad nibs work great with minimal bleed. Wet inks (like Noodler's) may ghost more noticeably. Avoid flex nibs that lay down heavy lines.

**Q: How many pages are actually usable?**
A: All 300 are numbered and lined. The last 10 pages are a simple index section, and the interior pocket adds storage without reducing page count.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, substantial journal with genuine leather and thick paper that won't quit on you. **Skip it if** you need a hardcover or prefer a smooth, ultra-refined writing surface.

The Fetinar delivers where it matters: build quality and page capacity. It's not perfect — the strap closure and textured paper won't suit everyone — but for the price, you're getting a notebook that punches well above its weight class. **4.6/5 stars.**
