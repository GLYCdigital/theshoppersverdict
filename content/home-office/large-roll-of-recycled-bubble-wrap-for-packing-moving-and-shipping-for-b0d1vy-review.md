### Performance: Where This Roll Earns Its Keep

The recycled material doesn't feel flimsy. Multiple reviewers noted the film has good tensile strength — you can pull it taut around corners without immediate tearing, which is the #1 frustration with cheap bubble wrap. The bubbles hold air well; nobody reported significant popping during transit.

For shipping small businesses, this roll is practical. The sheet width accommodates standard box sizes without excessive trimming, and the recycled content doesn't compromise cushioning. One reviewer noted: *"I've used cheaper rolls that puncture if you look at them wrong. This one survived a cross-country move with zero broken dishes."*

### Where It Falls Short

**No perforations.** This is the most consistent complaint. You'll need scissors handy, and cutting straight lines across a wide roll is annoying. If you're packing solo, this slows you down.

**Size can be overwhelming.** For small jobs — wrapping a few mugs or a single vase — this roll is overkill. You'll have leftover material taking up closet space for years. Check the dimensions carefully before ordering; if you're not moving a whole household, consider a smaller roll.

**Bubble size limitation.** The small bubbles are great for delicate items but less ideal for heavy, oddly-shaped objects that benefit from larger air pockets. If you're packing power tools or heavy kitchen appliances, you'll want a second roll with bigger bubbles.
