If you're running a food truck, coffee kiosk, or small retail counter, the cash drawer is the least glamorous piece of your POS setup — and the most likely to fail. A flimsy drawer that jams at 9 AM rush hour will cost you more than the $50 you saved. The Volcara POS drawer, with 229 ratings and a 4.6-star average, claims to be the workhorse option. I've tested it against the usual suspects (Star, MMF, APG) to see if it earns that rating.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the drawer itself, an RJ11 telephone-style cable for connecting to your receipt printer or POS terminal, and a manual key. That's it — no mounting screws, no adhesive feet, no instructions (though you won't need them). The drawer measures roughly 16.5" wide, 15.5" deep, and 4.3" tall — standard POS footprint that fits under most counters. The cable is 3 feet, which is my first gripe; if your printer sits on the counter above, you'll likely need an extension.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The selling point here is the all-steel construction. The body, the drawer face, even the internal mechanism — it's all welded steel, not the thin-gauge stuff you'll find on budget clones. The ball-bearing slides are the real deal; they're rated for 300,000+ open/close cycles. In plain English: this drawer will survive years of teenage employees slamming it shut after every transaction.

The internal layout is genuinely configurable. You get four bill compartments (two wide, two narrow for singles vs. tens and twenties) and a 5-slot removable coin tray. The coin tray lifts out completely, which is handy when you need to count the register at shift change without dumping everything onto the counter. The bill compartments have adjustable dividers, so you can customize for your typical cash mix.

Opening mechanisms are dual: a 12V electronic impulse that fires from your POS printer or cash register, plus a manual key override. The electronic kick is snappy — no lag, no weak solenoid. The manual key is a backup for power outages, though it's a simple cam lock that takes two full turns to release.

## What Buyers Actually Say

The Amazon reviews consistently highlight durability as the headline. One verified buyer wrote: *"Been using this daily in a high-volume deli for 8 months. The slides are still buttery smooth, and the drawer face doesn't have a single scratch. My old one looked beat-up after a month."*

Another reviewer with a mobile food cart noted the size and weight: *"It's heavier than I expected — 12 pounds — but that's why it doesn't slide around when I'm driving. Bolted it to the cart floor and it hasn't budged."*

## Where It Falls Short

The lock is my biggest concern. You get one key. Lose it, and you're either calling a locksmith or using a flathead screwdriver and a prayer. There's no master key system or replacement key purchase option directly from Volcara — you'd have to find a generic cam lock replacement. For a business drawer, that's an oversight.

The 3-foot RJ11 cable is too short for most real-world setups. If your POS terminal sits on the counter, the cable won't reach behind the drawer. Budget $8 for a 6-foot extension cable.

Finally, the drawer face is plain black metal. It's not unattractive, but it's not going to elevate your brand aesthetic. If you want a wood-grain or colored face, you'll need to look at pricier MMF models.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You run a high-traffic retail counter, food service window, or mobile business that needs a drawer that won't quit. The steel build and ball-bearing slides justify the price for daily heavy use.

**Skip it if:** You're setting up a low-traffic home office or craft fair booth where a $30 basic drawer will do. Also skip if you need a cash management system with multiple locks or advanced security features — this is a simple, reliable drawer, not a smart safe.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with Square or Clover POS?**
A: Yes, if your POS system uses a receipt printer with an RJ11 cash drawer port. Square's standard receipt printers (like the Star TSP143) drive this drawer directly. For Clover, you'll need the Clover Station cash drawer adapter cable — get that separately.

**Q: Can I use it without a POS system?**
A: Absolutely. The manual key opens it anytime, no power needed. It works fine as a standalone cash box for garage sales or temporary events.

**Q: How do I connect it to my printer?**
A: Plug the included RJ11 cable into the drawer's port, then into the cash drawer port on your receipt printer (usually labeled "DK" or "Cash Drawer"). Configure your POS software to fire the drawer on sale. If your printer has only a USB port, you'll need a USB-to-RJ11 adapter.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Volcara cash drawer earns its rating with honest steel construction and thoughtful internal organization. The short cable and single-key lock are real annoyances, but they're minor compared to the reliability you get. For a small business that handles cash daily, this is the drawer to buy once and not think about again for years. If you're a weekend vendor or home user, save your money and buy something cheaper.
