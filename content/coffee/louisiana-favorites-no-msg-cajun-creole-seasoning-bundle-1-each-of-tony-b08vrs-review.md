## What's Actually in the Box

This bundle ships with two 17-ounce containers — one from Tony Chachere's (the "More Spice" variant) and one from Louisiana Fish Fry Products. That's nearly two pounds of seasoning total, which is significant. Most specialty spice blends come in 4-6 ounce jars; you're getting roughly four times that amount here.

The "No MSG" claim checks out. Both blends list salt, spices, and herbs as primary ingredients rather than relying on monosodium glutamate for umami punch. The Tony Chachere's version uses red pepper, black pepper, and garlic powder prominently; the Louisiana version leans on paprika and chili pepper for color and warmth.

## How It Actually Performs

I've tested both blends across eggs, roasted chicken, and rice dishes. The Tony Chachere's "More Spice" lives up to its name — noticeably hotter than the standard version, with a cayenne-forward burn that builds rather than hits instantly. The Louisiana blend is slightly sweeter, with paprika giving it a deeper red color and more mild heat profile.

Both perform exactly as you'd expect in cooking: they dissolve well into wet dishes, create a decent crust on proteins when used as a dry rub, and hold up through longer cooking times without turning bitter. The garlic and onion notes stay present but don't overpower the pepper.

## Where It Falls Short

Salt. Both blends list salt as the first ingredient, and it shows. If you're watching sodium intake or you're used to seasoning with a light hand, these will overpower your food quickly. You'll need to reduce or eliminate added salt in recipes that call for this seasoning.

The second issue is redundancy. This bundle gives you two similar Creole blends rather than complementary ones. If you wanted a blackened seasoning and a gumbo base, you're out of luck. What you get is two variations on the same theme — one hotter, one slightly smokier.

## FAQ

**Is the "More Spice" version actually hotter than regular Tony Chachere's?**
Yes. It's roughly 40-50% hotter per teaspoon, with cayenne as the primary heat source. It's still manageable for most people — think medium salsa heat, not ghost pepper territory.

**How long will these last?**
Each 17-ounce container is roughly 2.5 cups of seasoning. For a household using it 3-4 times weekly, expect 6-8 months per container. Store in a cool, dry place away from steam.

**Can I use these as a dry rub for grilling?**
Absolutely. Both blends work well as a dry rub, though the Tony Chachere's version handles high heat better without the paprika burning. Apply 30-60 minutes before cooking for best results.
