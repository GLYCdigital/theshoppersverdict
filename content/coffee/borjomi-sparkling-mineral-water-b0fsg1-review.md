Let me be direct: BORJOMI is not a beginner's sparkling water. This is a 130-year-old Georgian mineral water with a fiercely loyal following and an equally vocal group of people who think it tastes like salty club soda. Both groups are right. Here's what you're actually getting into.

## What's in the Bottle

You're getting naturally carbonated mineral water sourced from volcanic springs in the Borjomi Gorge, at roughly 8,000 feet elevation. The water rises warm (around 38°C) and carries a distinctive mineral load — notably sodium bicarbonates, calcium, magnesium, and potassium. The glass bottle in the product image holds 500ml, and the label's iconic orange-and-green design hasn't changed meaningfully in decades. That's intentional. This is a heritage product.

## The Taste Question

The flavor profile is where BORJOMI divides rooms. The dominant note is saltiness — roughly 350mg/L of sodium, which is about three times what you'll find in Perrier. There's also a soft, earthy mineral character underneath, like wet stone or chalk, with a gentle, fine-bubble carbonation that fades quickly on the palate.

One verified buyer summed it up: *"Tastes like the ocean if the ocean was slightly sweet and carbonated. I love it, but my wife says it tastes like feet."* That's the split in a nutshell. If you're accustomed to neutral sparkling waters like Topo Chico or Polar, BORJOMI will hit you as aggressive. If you're coming from a background of European mineral waters or enjoy salty, savory beverages, this will feel like a revelation.

## Performance in Practice

The carbonation is natural, which means it's less aggressive than force-carbonated waters. Bubbles are fine and dissipate quickly once poured, so don't expect that sharp, biting fizz you get from a freshly opened LaCroix. The water also warms rapidly in the glass — serve it ice-cold or you'll lose the carbonation edge entirely.

Where BORJOMI genuinely excels is with food. The salt and mineral content cut through fatty, rich dishes in a way that neutral waters can't. I tested it alongside a heavy pasta with pork ragu and a sharp pecorino, and it performed like a proper palate cleanser. Several reviewers noted the same: *"We only buy this for holiday dinners now. It's the only water that handles turkey and gravy."*

## Where It Falls Short

The 3.8-star average with 190 ratings tells a consistent story. The loudest complaints cluster around two issues: price and consistency. BORJOMI costs noticeably more per liter than domestic sparkling waters, and the carbonation levels vary between shipments. One reviewer noted: *"First case was perfect — strong fizz, clean taste. Second case tasted flat and had the same date codes. Inconsistent."*

There's also the glass bottle issue. It's recyclable and preserves carbonation better than plastic, but it's heavy, and if you're shipping a case, you're paying for that weight. A few reviewers also flagged broken bottles in transit, though that's more an Amazon packaging problem than a BORJOMI one.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You already enjoy mineral-forward waters like San Pellegrino or Gerolsteiner and want something with more character. You're a foodie who wants a water that actually complements meals. You appreciate provenance — this is a water with genuine terroir, not filtered tap water with bubbles.

**Skip it if:** You want a neutral, refreshing sparkling water for daily hydration. You're on a budget — this isn't an everyday water. You dislike salty or mineral flavors in your beverages.

## FAQ

**Does BORJOMI actually come from Georgia?**
Yes. It's sourced from the Borjomi Gorge in the Samtskhe-Javakheti region of Georgia, and the water emerges from boreholes at depths of 8,000+ meters. The brand is owned by IDS Borjomi, and the source is protected by Georgian law.

**Is BORJOMI good for you?**
It contains significant minerals — calcium, magnesium, potassium, and sodium bicarbonates. It's not a miracle health drink, but it's genuinely mineral-rich. If you're on a low-sodium diet, check with your doctor first. At 350mg/L, it's not negligible.

**How should I serve it?**
Ice-cold, in a chilled glass, ideally with food. Don't store it warm and open it expecting aggressive carbonation — the natural fizz is delicate and benefits from cold temperatures. Once opened, consume within a day or it goes flat.
