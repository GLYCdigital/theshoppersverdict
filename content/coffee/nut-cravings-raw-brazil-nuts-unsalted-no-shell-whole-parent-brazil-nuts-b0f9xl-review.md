**Opening: The Selenium King, Done Right (Mostly)**

Brazil nuts occupy a weird spot in the nut world. They're not as snackable as almonds, not as indulgent as cashews. But they're the single richest dietary source of selenium on the planet — one or two nuts a day covers your entire RDA. That's why people buy them in bulk. And that's why NUT CRAVINGS' raw, unsalted, shell-free offering deserves a closer look. At 4.3 stars from 215 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right Brazil nut for you? Let's dig in.

**What You Actually Get**

The product image shows a clean, matte-finish stand-up pouch with a clear window. Inside, you're getting whole Brazil nut kernels — no shells, no salt, no oil, no roasting. The 1-pound bag is roughly the size of a small hardcover book, and the zip-lock closure is genuinely functional, not decorative. One thing worth noting: the bag lists a lot code but no explicit "best by" date, which is a minor annoyance for a product that can go rancid if stored improperly. I'd recommend transferring these to an airtight glass jar if you're not going to polish them off within a month.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I tested these three ways: straight out of the bag, chopped over oatmeal, and blended into a homemade nut butter. The texture is the headline. Brazil nuts have a naturally high oil content — that's where the buttery, almost coconut-like richness comes from. NUT CRAVINGS delivers that. The kernels are firm but not rock-hard, with a satisfying snap when you bite into them. They're not overly dry, which is a common failure point for raw nuts that have sat in a warehouse too long.

Flavor-wise, these are genuinely raw. There's no roasted toastiness, no salt to mask imperfections. You taste the nut itself: earthy, slightly sweet, with that distinctive creamy finish. If you're used to roasted, salted nuts, these will taste almost bland at first. That's not a flaw — it's the point. But it's worth knowing before you buy.

The one recurring complaint across Amazon reviews is consistency. Some batches arrive with a noticeable number of broken pieces — not crumbs, but split halves and chips. It doesn't affect taste, but if you're buying these for presentation (think charcuterie boards or gift baskets), you might be disappointed. One reviewer noted, "Great taste but about a quarter of the bag was broken pieces. Fine for baking, not great for snacking straight from the bag."

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the packaging. No visible expiration date is a real oversight for a perishable product. Second, the broken-piece issue. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's inconsistent enough that multiple reviewers mention it. Third, Brazil nuts are naturally high in fat — about 66% fat by weight. That's what makes them delicious, but it also means they can go rancid quickly if you don't store them properly. These are not a "buy once and forget" pantry item.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many Brazil nuts should I eat per day?**
A: Two to three nuts. Brazil nuts contain roughly 68–91 mcg of selenium per nut, and the RDA for adults is 55 mcg. More than 5 nuts daily risks selenium toxicity over time.

**Q: Are these actually raw, or are they pasteurized?**
A: They're raw and unroasted. Some imported nuts are steam-treated for safety, but the flavor profile here is consistent with genuinely raw, unprocessed kernels.

**Q: How should I store them?**
A: Airtight container in a cool, dark place for up to 3 months. For longer storage, freeze them — they'll keep for up to a year without losing texture.
