Iced coffee is a $5-a-day habit for millions of people, and most of them are making it wrong at home — brewing hot coffee, dumping it over ice, and wondering why it tastes watery and bitter. The Keurig K-Iced ($97.75, 4.4 stars from 6,819 ratings) tries to fix that with a dedicated cold brew cycle that extracts at a lower temperature directly over ice. Is it worth the counter space? I've dug through the reviews and specs, and here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

You get the brewer itself in the gray finish, a 60-ounce removable water reservoir, a drip tray that accommodates tall travel mugs (up to 7 inches with the tray removed), and a starter pack of K-Cup pods. The machine measures about 12.5 inches tall, 15 inches deep, and just 5 inches wide — significantly slimmer than the standard K-Elite or K-Supreme models. That narrow profile is a genuine selling point for small kitchens, dorm rooms, or office desks.

**How the cold brew actually works**

The K-Iced's party trick is its "Brew Over Ice" button. Instead of heating water to 192°F like a regular cycle, it uses a lower temperature (around 160°F) and a slower flow rate to extract coffee without melting the ice immediately. The result: you fill a 16-ounce glass with ice, place it on the drip tray, hit the button, and get a properly chilled cup in about 2-3 minutes.

Buyers consistently confirm this works. One reviewer wrote, *"I was skeptical that this would just be hot coffee poured over ice, but it's genuinely cold. The ice barely melts during brewing and the coffee tastes smooth, not bitter."* Another noted, *"My iced coffee from Starbucks tastes identical to this — saves me $4 every single day."* The hot mode is standard Keurig fare: 6, 8, 10, or 12-ounce servings at normal temperature, so this isn't a one-trick pony.

**Where it shines**

The universal K-Cup compatibility is a bigger deal than it sounds. Keurig's pricier K-Iced models sometimes struggle with non-standard pods, but this one accepts everything from Starbucks Pike Place to generic store-brand pods without issue. Multiple reviewers praised this versatility, with one saying, *"I've used 8 different brands of pods, including some off-brand ones, and they all brewed perfectly."*

The 16-ounce cold capacity is also smart — it fills a standard fast-food cup size, not the tiny 8-ounce servings you get from older machines. For hot coffee, the strong brew setting (hold the power button for 3 seconds) gives a slightly more concentrated cup, which helps if you're using lighter roasts.

**Honest downsides**

The cold mode is one-size-fits-all: 16 ounces, period. Want a smaller iced coffee with extra milk? You're out of luck — you'll brew the full cup or waste coffee. There's also no strength control on cold brew, so if a pod tastes weak, you can't compensate except by choosing a darker roast. The 60-ounce reservoir is smaller than the 78-ounce tanks on Keurig's premium models, which means more frequent refills if you're making multiple drinks daily. Several reviewers also mentioned the plastic housing feels less substantial than older Keurigs — it's lighter and slightly hollow-sounding, though no one reported actual durability failures.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for iced-coffee drinkers who want convenience over artisanal precision. If you're the person who stops at Dunkin' or Starbucks every morning, this pays for itself in under a month. It's also great for small households or offices where counter space is tight. Skip it if you're a cold brew purist who wants 12-hour steeped concentrate, or if you prefer espresso-based iced lattes — this does regular drip-style coffee only.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it work with any K-Cup pod for cold brewing?**
A: Yes. The cold cycle works with any standard K-Cup, though darker roasts and cold-brew-specific pods (like the Starbucks Iced Coffee line) produce stronger results.

**Q: How long does cold brew take?**
A: About 2-3 minutes for the full 16 ounces, which is faster than waiting for a pot of hot coffee to cool down.

**Q: Can I use pre-ground coffee with a reusable filter?**
A: Yes, the machine is compatible with the My K-Cup Universal Reusable Filter (sold separately).

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You drink iced coffee regularly, want it fast without bitterness, and appreciate that it also does solid hot coffee in the same footprint.

**Skip it if:** You need brew-size flexibility in cold mode, want a heavy-duty machine that feels premium, or you're strictly a hot-coffee drinker — cheaper Keurig models do that just fine.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The K-Iced nails its core promise at a fair price, with minor compromises in adjustability and build that keep it from perfection.
