**What You Actually Get**

McCormick's Salt Free Roasted Garlic comes in a standard 5.9-ounce plastic shaker bottle. Inside is a fine, tan-colored powder with visible darker flecks — evidence of actual roasting. The aroma hits you immediately upon opening: sweet, caramelized, almost nutty, with none of the sharp bite you get from raw garlic powder. It's the kind of scent that makes you want to sprinkle it on everything before you've even tasted it.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I tested this across three applications: roasted vegetables, pan-seared chicken, and a vinaigrette. The grind is consistently fine — think powdered sugar texture — which means it dissolves quickly in liquids and coats dry surfaces evenly. No clumping, no bitter pockets of concentrated flavor.

On roasted broccoli and carrots, it delivered that deep, almost buttery garlic character you associate with slow-roasted cloves. On chicken, it formed a light crust when seared, though it doesn't brown like garlic salt would. In the vinaigrette, it dissolved completely within a minute, releasing a mellow sweetness that balanced the vinegar nicely.

The salt-free aspect is the headline here, and it earns its keep. For anyone watching sodium — whether for blood pressure, kidney health, or general wellness — this delivers the garlic punch without forcing you to adjust other seasonings. You control the salt entirely.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.8-star average across 240 ratings isn't inflated. Reviewers repeatedly highlight the flavor depth. One buyer wrote, *"I was skeptical that roasted garlic powder could taste this fresh. **Where It Falls Short**

Honest tradeoffs exist. The price per ounce is noticeably higher than standard garlic powder — often 30-40% more. You're paying for the roasting process, and it shows in the flavor, but budget-conscious shoppers should know that upfront.

The sweetness is also a potential surprise. This isn't savory garlic salt. It has a distinct caramelized character that works beautifully on vegetables and proteins but can clash with certain dishes — think traditional Italian red sauces or anything where you want sharp, pungent garlic. If you need that raw bite, keep regular garlic powder on hand too.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I substitute this 1:1 for garlic salt in recipes?**
No. Since it contains no salt, you'll need to add salt separately. Start with half the garlic salt amount, then adjust salt to taste.

**Q: Does it lose flavor when cooked?**
Some. The sweet notes mellow further with heat. For maximum impact, add half during cooking and half at the end.

**Q: How long does it stay fresh?**
Properly sealed, expect 2-3 years. Check for aroma loss — when it stops smelling strongly roasted, it's past its prime.
