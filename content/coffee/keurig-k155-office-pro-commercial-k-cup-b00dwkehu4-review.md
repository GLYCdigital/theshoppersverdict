The Keurig K155 Office Pro is the coffee equivalent of a commercial fridge — it's designed for traffic. The question is whether your traffic justifies the sticker price.

**The big draw is capacity.** With a 90 oz. water reservoir, the K155 brews roughly 35 cups between refills. In a busy office, that means fill it once in the morning and you're done. A single cup brews in under a minute. The construction is noticeably heavier than the $99 home Keurigs — less creak, more heft, more stability when you press down on the pod handle.

**The hot water dispenser is a sleeper feature** that office workers genuinely appreciate. Need instant soup? Hot tea? A quick mug of hot water to warm a teapot? That lever on the side delivers without running a brew cycle. In a workplace kitchen, that's real utility.

**But at $355, the omissions are frustrating.** The most common complaint in reviews is that the K155 tops out at 10 oz. brew sizes. Cheaper Keurig models offer 12 oz. cups. You're paying commercial money for a machine that pours a smaller cup. It's a head-scratcher.

**Durability concerns exist.** While many office reviewers report years of happy service, a meaningful number report units failing after 4-12 months. One four-month failure on a $355 machine is a painful ratio. Hard water seems to be the common factor — descaling maintenance is not optional with this unit.

**Who should buy this?** Medium to large offices with 10+ daily users where speed and volume matter more than per-cup cost. Break rooms, waiting areas, open-plan kitchens. It's also a fit for heavy coffee drinkers at home who want the commercial durability without the consumer price-point compromises.

**Who shouldn't?** Small home kitchens and low-volume users. The K155 is overkill if you make one cup in the morning. You'll pay for capacity you don't use and still be annoyed by the missing 12 oz. setting.

**The bottom line:** A good commercial machine that's overpriced for what it delivers. If your office goes through the coffee equivalent of a water cooler, the K155 earns its place. If you just want a nice cup of coffee, a smaller Keurig or a pour-over cone will serve you better for a lot less money.
