**What you're actually getting**

This is a 16-ounce glass jar of ground sesame paste. That's it. No added soybean oil, no sugar, no salt, no stabilizers — just unhulled sesame seeds ground into a smooth paste. As shown in the product image, the jar is a standard cylindrical glass container with a simple white label, nothing flashy. The ingredient list is exactly one item long, which is precisely what you want from tahini.

**How it performs in the kitchen**

The first thing you'll notice when you crack the jar is the aroma — deeply roasted, nutty, with that characteristic sesame intensity that fills the kitchen. The texture is where this product earns its keep. It pours smoothly at room temperature, which is genuinely unusual for tahini. Many brands produce a thick, almost cement-like paste that requires warming before it becomes workable. Baron's is fluid enough to drizzle straight out of the jar.

In practice, this makes a difference. If you're whisking up a quick lemon-tahini dressing for a weekday salad, you don't want to wrestle with a solidified block. This one incorporates into vinaigrettes with minimal effort. For hummus, it blends cleanly into chickpeas without leaving gritty bits behind. The flavor profile is balanced — it has that pleasant bitterness that authentic tahini should have, without the sharp edge that some brands develop from over-roasting.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring theme in the 229 ratings is the ingredient purity. One reviewer noted, "Finally a tahini that tastes like sesame, not like oil with a hint of flavor." Another mentioned, "I've tried every brand at the grocery store and this is the closest to what I had in Israel." The consistency receives frequent mentions — people specifically call out that it doesn't separate into a rock-hard layer at the bottom like some competitors do.

**Where it falls short**

The oil separation issue is real. While the product is smoother than most, you'll still see a layer of oil on top when you open a fresh jar. You have to stir it thoroughly — and I mean thoroughly, scraping the bottom corners — before first use. Multiple buyers have commented on this, with one saying, "I almost threw it out thinking it was expired. It's not — you just have to mix it well." That's not a flaw in the product itself; it's the nature of pure sesame without emulsifiers. But it's worth knowing before you open it.

There's also no safety seal under the lid. The jar comes with a simple screw cap, so you're trusting the freshness date printed on the label. For a product that can go rancid over time, that's a minor but legitimate concern.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this tahini gluten-free and kosher?**  
A: Yes. It's certified kosher and contains only sesame seeds, which are naturally gluten-free. Always check the label for your specific dietary needs.

**Q: Why is there oil floating on top?**  
A: That's naturally occurring sesame oil separating from the solids. It's a sign of purity — no emulsifiers are preventing separation. Stir it thoroughly before each use.

**Q: How should I store it after opening?**  
A: Refrigeration extends shelf life, but it will thicken significantly. If you use it within a few months, a cool, dark pantry cabinet works fine. Just stir before each use.
