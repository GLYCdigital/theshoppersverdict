If you drink Diet Mountain Dew by the case, this SodaStream syrup is either a money-saving revelation or a fizzy disappointment—depending on how you handle carbonation. I've spent two weeks testing this against canned Dew, and here's the honest breakdown.

## What's in the Box

You get one 440ml bottle of concentrated Diet Mountain Dew syrup. That's it. But here's the math that matters: that single bottle yields 9 liters of finished soda. In can terms, that's about 25 cans' worth. At typical retail, you're looking at roughly $0.25 per 12-ounce glass—versus $1.50 to $2.00 for the same amount in cans. Over a month of daily drinking, the savings add up to real money.

## The Taste Test: Does It Actually Taste Like Mountain Dew?

This is the make-or-break question, and the short answer is: yes, remarkably so. The signature citrus-forward profile is there—that sharp, slightly sweet kick that Dew fans crave. It's sweetened with Splenda (sucralose), not aspartame, which gives it a slightly cleaner finish than regular Diet Dew. One Amazon reviewer nailed it: *"I was skeptical, but honestly can't tell the difference from a can. My wife thought I'd opened a can."*

The flavor intensity is strong out of the bottle, but you'll need to experiment with the ratio. The bottle suggests a 1:7 syrup-to-water mix, but I found 1:6.5 gives a closer match to canned Dew's boldness. Too much syrup and you get a syrupy, almost medicinal aftertaste—too little and it tastes like vaguely citrus-flavored sparkling water.

## The Carbonation Catch

Here's where the honest criticism comes in. SodaStream carbonation is aggressive right out of the machine—those big, sharp bubbles that sting your tongue. But it doesn't hold. Within 10 minutes, that intensity drops noticeably. By 15 minutes, it's approaching flat. Canned Dew holds its fizz for 30+ minutes. This means you can't pour a glass and sip it slowly while working. You need to drink it fast.

One workaround: carbonate your water extra-hard (3-4 presses instead of 2) before adding syrup. The syrup knocks some carbonation out, so over-carbonating compensates. It's a small adjustment, but it makes a real difference.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't inflated. Recurring themes in positive reviews:

- **Taste accuracy**: Multiple reviewers report blind taste tests where family members couldn't distinguish it from canned Dew
- **Convenience**: No more hauling 12-packs from the store; one bottle lasts weeks
- **Cost savings**: Several reviewers calculated their per-glass cost and noted significant monthly savings versus cans

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the carbonation issue, there are two real downsides. First, the syrup is occasionally out of stock on Amazon—one reviewer noted *"love the taste, but it's frustrating when it's unavailable for weeks at a time."* Second, if you're a casual soda drinker (a glass or two a week), the savings are minimal, and the upfront cost of a SodaStream machine (if you don't already own one) won't pay off for over a year.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You drink Diet Dew daily, already own a SodaStream, and want to cut your soda budget without sacrificing taste.

**Skip it if:** You're a once-a-week soda drinker, or you hate the idea of adjusting your drinking pace to beat the fizz clock.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does an opened bottle of syrup last?**  
A: The bottle is good for about 3 months after opening if refrigerated. With daily use, you'll finish it well before then.

**Q: Does it work in any SodaStream model?**  
A: Yes, the syrup works with all SodaStream machines. You just add the syrup after carbonating plain water.

**Q: Is it really sugar-free?**  
A: Yes, sweetened with sucralose (Splenda), zero calories, zero sugar. Taste-wise, it's roughly equivalent to Diet Dew, not regular.

## The Verdict

**4.6/5 — Buy it if you're a daily Diet Dew drinker looking to cut costs.** The taste is genuinely close to the real thing, the savings are real, and the convenience is undeniable. Just be prepared to drink your glass quickly and fine-tune your syrup ratio. Skip it if you're a light soda drinker—the carbonation tradeoff isn't worth it for occasional use. It's not perfect, but for the price per glass, it's hard to beat.
