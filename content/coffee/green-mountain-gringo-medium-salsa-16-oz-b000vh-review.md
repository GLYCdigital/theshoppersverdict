**The 16-ounce jar of Green Mountain Gringo Medium Salsa sits quietly in the refrigerated section, and that’s exactly where it belongs.** This is a fresh-tasting, refrigerated salsa that doesn’t try to be the hottest thing on the shelf — it tries to be the most *honest* thing. And for the most part, it succeeds.

## What You’re Actually Getting

One 16-ounce glass jar (not plastic, which is a plus for flavor retention). The ingredient list is refreshingly short: tomatoes, onions, jalapeños, cilantro, garlic, salt, and lime juice. No xanthan gum. No artificial colors. No high-fructose corn syrup. As shown in the product image, the salsa has a chunky, rustic appearance — this isn’t a pureed smooth blend.

The medium heat level sits right in the middle of the brand’s lineup (they also offer mild, hot, and extra hot). If you’ve had their mild, this is a clear step up in warmth. If you’re a habanero devotee, you’ll find this closer to mild-plus than true medium.

## How It Performs in Real-World Use

The first thing you notice is the roasted flavor. The tomatoes are fire-roasted before blending, which gives a smoky backbone that carries through every bite. It’s the kind of depth you usually only get from fresh-made salsa at a Mexican restaurant — not from a jar.

The consistency is where opinions split. This salsa leans thin and juicy rather than thick and chunky. That means it’s excellent for drizzling over eggs, tacos, or grilled chicken. It’s less ideal for chip dipping — expect drip casualties. If you prefer a salsa that clings to a tortilla chip, you’ll want to drain some of the liquid or pair it with a heartier brand.

The heat builds slowly rather than hitting you upfront. It’s a warm, tingly sensation on the lips that fades within a minute. The jalapeño flavor comes through fresh, not vinegary — a sign they’re using quality peppers.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be direct about the tradeoffs:

**It’s thin.** If you’re a thick-salsa person, this will disappoint. The liquid separates easily when the jar sits — you’ll need to stir it well before each use.

**Sodium is noticeable.** At roughly 260mg per 2-tablespoon serving, it’s not the worst offender on the shelf, but it’s not the lightest either. If you’re watching salt intake, factor that in.

**The 16-ounce jar is a tease.** At this price point, it’s reasonable — but this salsa disappears fast. Plan on buying two jars if you’re hosting.

**It’s not fiery.** The label says medium, and it means it. Don’t expect serious heat.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You appreciate roasted, smoky flavor over raw onion-and-tomato bite
- You want a salsa with ingredients you can pronounce
- You’re serving it over food rather than just dipping chips
- You’re tired of salsa that tastes like tomato sauce with sugar

**Skip it if:**
- You need a thick, chunky dip that stays on a chip
- You want serious heat from a “medium” label
- You’re feeding a crowd on a budget — this jar won’t stretch far

## FAQ

**Q: Is this salsa actually spicy?**
A: Medium here means a gentle warmth. It’s noticeable but not intense. If you like serious heat, go for their Hot or Extra Hot varieties.

**Q: Does it need refrigeration?**
A: Yes, after opening. The jar arrives at room temperature but should go in the fridge once opened. It stays fresh for about two weeks.

**Q: How does it compare to restaurant salsa?**
A: Closer than most. The roasted tomato base and fresh cilantro give it a made-from-scratch character. The texture is thinner than what most restaurants serve, but the flavor profile matches.
