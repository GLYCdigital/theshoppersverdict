Hunt's Tomato Ketchup in the squeeze bottle has quietly built a 4.8-star rating across 240 Amazon reviews, and that kind of consistency doesn't happen by accident. If you're staring at this in your cart wondering whether it's worth switching from your usual brand, here's what you actually need to know.

## What You're Getting

The 20-ounce squeeze bottle arrives ready to use — no shaking required, though you'll want to give it a quick one anyway. The flip-top cap is the real workhorse here. It snaps open with a satisfying click and dispenses ketchup in a controlled stream without the dreaded watery splatter that plagues thinner brands. The bottle itself is squeezable but not flimsy; it holds its shape when you grip it, which means you won't accidentally launch a blob across your plate.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Here's where Hunt's separates itself from the bargain bin options. The ketchup is noticeably thick — it holds its shape on a plate for a solid minute before spreading. That matters when you're dipping fries or building a burger. The flavor profile leans sweet with a clean tomato finish, and there's no metallic aftertaste that plagues cheaper alternatives.

One thing that impressed me: no separation. Even after sitting in the fridge for three weeks, the ketchup comes out the same color and consistency from first squeeze to last. That's not universal in this category — plenty of competitors develop a watery layer on top that requires vigorous shaking to reincorporate.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The Amazon reviews tell a consistent story. One verified buyer put it simply: "This is the only ketchup my kids will eat. We've tried others and they always come back to this." Another long-term customer noted, "Thicker than Heinz, sweeter than store brand. Perfect balance." The recurring theme is that Hunt's hits a middle ground — not too vinegary, not cloyingly sweet — that seems to satisfy picky eaters and adults alike.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. High fructose corn syrup is the second ingredient after tomato concentrate. If you're trying to avoid HFCS, this isn't your bottle — look for the organic version or a brand like Sir Kensington's instead. Also, at 20 ounces, this is smaller than the standard 24-ounce bottles from competitors. For a family that goes through ketchup quickly, you'll be rebuying more often.

A minor gripe: the cap can get gunked up if you're careless about wiping the nozzle. It's a small thing, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Hunt's ketchup thicker than Heinz?**
Yes, noticeably. Hunt's has a denser, more paste-like consistency that holds its shape longer on food. Heinz runs slightly thinner and more vinegary.

**Does this need refrigeration after opening?**
Yes, like all commercial ketchups, refrigerate after opening. It'll maintain quality for about six months.

**Is this gluten-free?**
Yes, Hunt's Tomato Ketchup contains no gluten ingredients and is safe for most celiacs, though it's not certified gluten-free.
