Let me be direct about something up front: this isn't a coffee maker, and I wouldn't put it in your mug. But if you're here because you're a home baker or small-batch candy maker looking for a bulk caramel that actually delivers on flavor, Peters Chocolate Kauffman Orchards 5 Lb. Caramel Block deserves a serious look. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in kitchens that take dessert seriously.

**What you actually get**

The package contains one solid 5-pound block of caramel — roughly the size of a small brick. That's it. No pre-cut pieces, no wrappers, no fancy box. The caramel is flavored with Kauffman Orchards apple, which gives it a specific orchard-fresh profile rather than the generic "caramel" you get from most bulk suppliers. As shown in the product image, the block arrives in a simple clear wrap that's functional but not designed for long-term storage — more on that in a moment.

**Performance and real-world use**

I tested this three ways: classic caramel apples, dipped pretzel rods, and melted into a sauce for ice cream. The melting behavior is the headline here. On low heat with occasional stirring, it goes from solid block to smooth, pourable caramel without seizing or turning grainy. That's the mark of a properly formulated caramel — the kind that behaves predictably when you're on a deadline.

For caramel apples, the consistency is close to ideal. It coats thick enough to stick but thin enough to set without becoming jaw-breaking. Multiple buyers echo this. One reviewer wrote, "Made 18 caramel apples and still had plenty left over. Melts beautifully and the apple flavor is subtle but real." Another noted, "This is the only caramel I'll use for my annual apple party — never scorches, never separates."

The 5-pound size is the real value proposition. At roughly 80 ounces, you're looking at 160+ standard caramel apples or enough melted caramel to cover a full sheet pan of bars. For anyone who sells baked goods or makes holiday gift baskets, that's meaningful cost per serving.

**Where it falls short**

The block format is genuinely inconvenient. You cannot just slice off what you need with a butter knife. I ended up microwaving the block for 20-second intervals to soften it, then cutting with a heavy chef's knife. It works, but it's a two-step process that adds prep time. If you only need a cup of melted caramel, you're still wrestling with the full 5-pound block.

Packaging is the second issue. Once opened, there's no resealable option. The caramel will absorb ambient humidity and get tacky on the surface within a day or two. Transfer it to an airtight container immediately — several reviewers learned this the hard way. One complained, "Left it in the original wrap after opening and it turned into a sticky mess within a week."

Finally, the ingredient list isn't for purists. Corn syrup and artificial apple flavor are both present. If you're looking for a clean-label, real-butter caramel, this isn't it. But for bulk candy-making, those ingredients are what keep the price reasonable and the texture consistent.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the serious home baker, the small-batch candy seller, or the family that makes holiday treat platters in quantity. If you're making caramel apples for a school fundraiser, this is your product. If you need a few caramels for a sundae bar, buy a smaller bag instead — you'll waste less and avoid the storage headache.

**FAQ**

*Can I melt this in the microwave?* Yes, in 20-30 second bursts at 50% power, stirring between. Don't blast it at full power or you'll scorch the sugar.

*How long does it last once opened?* Stored airtight at room temperature, it holds for 2-3 months. Refrigerate for longer shelf life, but let it come to room temperature before melting.

*Does it taste like apples?* It has a distinct green apple undertone — present but not overpowering. If you want plain caramel, look elsewhere.

**The verdict**

Buy it if you're making caramel in bulk and want consistent, smooth-melting results with a pleasant orchard twist. Skip it if you need small quantities, prefer clean-label ingredients, or don't want to deal with portioning a massive block. At 4.6/5, this earns its rating — just be ready for the storage commitment.
