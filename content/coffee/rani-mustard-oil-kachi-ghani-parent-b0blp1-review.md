Some oils are background players. This one is a lead actor. Rani's Kachi Ghani mustard oil doesn't whisper — it announces itself with a sharp, sinus-clearing punch the moment the cap comes off. If you grew up with Indian cooking, that smell is instant nostalgia. If you didn't, it might take a minute to appreciate. Either way, this is an oil with a distinct personality, and that's exactly the point.

**What you actually get**

A standard bottle of golden-yellow oil, cold-pressed from mustard seeds using the traditional kachi ghani method — a wooden press that crushes seeds slowly without heat. This matters because heat extraction degrades the allyl isothiocyanates, the compounds responsible for mustard oil's signature bite and its purported health benefits. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: just mustard oil. No hydrogenated fillers, no palm oil stretching the batch, no artificial coloring to make it look more "premium."

**How it performs in the kitchen**

I tested this oil across three applications: tadka (tempering), shallow frying, and a raw drizzle. For tadka, it excels — the oil carries mustard seeds, curry leaves, and hing beautifully, and the pungency cuts through rich lentil dishes without getting lost. Shallow frying paneer and bhindi produced crispy, non-greasy results with no burnt taste, even at medium-high heat. The smoke point is high enough for most home cooking, though I wouldn't push it to deep-fry temperatures repeatedly.

The raw drizzle is where it gets divisive. A spoonful over dal or pickles delivers that authentic, throat-tingling warmth. But use it like olive oil on a salad and you'll regret it — the bitterness is intense and lingers.

**FAQ**

**Is kachi ghani mustard oil the same as regular mustard oil?**
No. Kachi ghani means cold-pressed at low temperatures, which preserves more of the natural compounds and flavor. Regular mustard oil is often solvent-extracted, which yields more oil but strips some of the character.

**Can I use this for deep frying?**
Yes, but watch your temperature. It handles medium-high heat well, but sustained high heat can degrade the flavor. For occasional deep frying, it works fine.

**Does this oil need to be refrigerated after opening?**
No. Store it in a cool, dark cabinet. It has natural preservative properties from the mustard compounds. Just keep the cap clean and tight.
