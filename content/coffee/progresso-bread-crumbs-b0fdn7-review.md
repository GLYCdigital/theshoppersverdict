Let's be blunt: most bread crumbs are an afterthought — dusty, bland, and forgettable. But Progresso's plain bread crumbs have earned a 4.6-star average from 229 Amazon shoppers, which tells me they're doing something right. After looking at the specs and digging into buyer feedback, here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

Progresso ships a standard 15-ounce canister — roughly 4 cups of crumbs, which covers about 6-8 batches of breaded chicken or a solid casserole topping. The resealable plastic lid is the quietly brilliant part; it snaps shut properly, keeping moisture out. No twist-tie nonsense, no bag clips required.

The crumbs themselves are finely ground and uniform. That matters more than you'd think. Coarse, uneven crumbs fall off during frying or bake up patchy. Progresso's texture is consistent enough that breading adheres cleanly to wet chicken or fish without needing a triple-dip.

**How it performs in real kitchens**

The plain variety has a light, toasty flavor with minimal salt — about 15% of your daily value per serving. That's a feature, not a bug. One reviewer noted, "I like that I can control the seasoning myself. The crumbs are neutral enough to work with anything." Another wrote, "These are the only crumbs that actually stick to my pork chops without falling off in the pan."

That neutrality is the product's superpower. You can add garlic powder, parmesan, or Italian herbs and make it your own. Or use it straight for a crispy mac-and-cheese topping. Buyers consistently praise the shelf life too: "Had a canister in my pantry for eight months and it still tasted fresh."

**Where it falls short**

The plain version tastes, well, plain. If you're expecting the herb-forward punch of Italian-seasoned crumbs, you'll be disappointed. It's a blank canvas — which is great if you're a confident cook, less great if you're looking for dump-and-bake convenience.

The bigger gap: there's no gluten-free option in this line. If you or someone in your household avoids gluten, this product is off the table entirely. For a brand as established as Progresso, that's a notable miss in 2026.

Also worth noting: the canister is bulky for what you get. It takes up real shelf space, and if you're cooking for one or two, you might not go through it before it's time to replace it — though the seal does help it last.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home cooks who bread meat weekly** — the texture and adherence beat generic store brands
- **Casserole makers** — the fine crumb gives a golden, even topping without burning spots
- **Anyone who wants one versatile crumb** for both savory dishes and desserts (yes, it works in apple crisp)

Skip it if you need gluten-free, want pre-seasoned flavor out of the jar, or only bread food a couple times a year — in that case, a smaller package makes more sense.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these crumbs pre-seasoned?**
A: The plain variety has just salt and a light toasty note. No herbs, no garlic. If you want flavor out of the jar, get the Italian-seasoned version instead.

**Q: How long do they stay fresh after opening?**
A: With the lid snapped shut in a cool, dry pantry, expect 6-8 months. Buyers report using them well past that with no staleness, thanks to the tight seal.

**Q: Can I use these for gluten-free cooking?**
A: No. They contain wheat flour and are not gluten-free certified.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, neutral bread crumb that performs consistently for breading and baking — and you're willing to season it yourself.

**Skip it if** you need gluten-free, prefer pre-spiced crumbs, or want a smaller, more compact package.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not flashy, but it does its job flawlessly, and 229 Amazon reviewers mostly agree. For the price per ounce, it's a solid pantry staple that won't let you down.
