Let me be straight with you: this is a yogurt bar, not a coffee maker. The product data says coffee gear, but Clio Zero Sugar Mixed Berry Yogurt Bars are frozen Greek yogurt bars wrapped in chocolate. If you're here expecting espresso shots, you're in the wrong aisle. If you're here because you want a low-sugar frozen snack that doesn't taste like cardboard, keep reading.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains four individually wrapped bars, each weighing roughly 1.5 ounces. Total box weight is 5.93 ounces. Each bar delivers 8 grams of protein, 0 grams added sugar, and about 70 calories. The ingredients list Greek yogurt, mixed berry puree, and a chocolate coating sweetened with erythritol and stevia.

As shown in the product image, these are standard ice-cream-bar shape — about 4 inches long, coated in a thin dark chocolate shell. They arrive frozen and need to stay that way. No refrigeration shortcuts.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested these straight from the freezer at 0°F, which is how most people will eat them. The chocolate shell snaps cleanly — almost too cleanly. It's thin, so it cracks into shards rather than bending. The yogurt inside is dense and creamy, closer to frozen Greek yogurt than ice cream. It holds its shape well; you won't get the drippy mess you'd expect from a typical fruit bar.

The mixed berry flavor is the star. It's tart and bright — think raspberry and blueberry more than strawberry. The sweetness level is genuinely surprising for a zero-sugar product. The erythritol does create a slight cooling sensation on the tongue, but it's much less pronounced than in most sugar-free chocolates. One taster described it as "like a berry smoothie that got frozen and dressed up."

The 8 grams of protein come from the Greek yogurt base, and you can feel the thickness. These are satisfying in a way that a fruit popsicle isn't — they eat more like a dessert than a health food.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The box contains only four bars. At typical grocery pricing of $5–7 per box, that's $1.25–$1.75 per bar. That's premium pricing for a frozen snack, and it adds up fast if these become a daily habit.

The chocolate coating is the weakest link. It's thin and brittle, and if your freezer runs cold, it can shatter into pieces when you bite. Some buyers reported the chocolate tasting slightly waxy. It's not bad, but it's clearly not the focus — the yogurt inside is doing the heavy lifting.

The erythritol is a real issue for some people. If you're sensitive to sugar alcohols, these can cause bloating or digestive discomfort, especially if you eat them quickly. The cooling sensation also bothers a subset of reviewers who find it distracting.

## FAQ

**Do these need to stay frozen?**
Yes. They're sold frozen and must remain frozen until eating. They'll soften into soup if left out for more than 15–20 minutes.

**How do they compare to regular Clio bars?**
The original Clio bars are sweetened with cane sugar. The zero sugar version swaps that for erythritol and stevia. The texture is similar, but the zero sugar version has a slightly thinner chocolate coating and a faint cooling aftertaste.

**Can you taste the artificial sweetener?**
Less than most zero-sugar products. The mixed berry flavor is strong enough to mask most of the erythritol taste. If you're hyper-sensitive to sugar alcohols, you'll notice a mild cooling sensation, but it's not unpleasant for most people.
