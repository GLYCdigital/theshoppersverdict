Let’s be blunt: most protein pastas taste like cardboard dressed up with sauce. Goodles Lucky Penne is different — and that’s why 240 Amazon shoppers have pushed it to a 4.8-star average. I’ve tested this 8 oz box against the usual suspects (Banza, Barilla Protein+, Explore Cuisine), and here’s what you actually need to know before adding it to your cart.

## What’s In the Box

You get one 8 oz box of penne. That’s it — no seasoning packets, no gimmicks. The pasta itself is made from a proprietary blend of wheat, chickpea flour, and a touch of pea protein. The ingredient list is notably clean: no gums, no artificial colors, no weird aftertaste-inducing additives. One serving (2 oz dry) delivers 14g protein, 8g fiber, and 40g whole grains — numbers that beat standard pasta by a mile.

## Real-World Performance

I cooked a full box according to the package directions: 8-9 minutes in salted boiling water. The penne came out with a genuine al dente bite — not the mushy texture that plagues many GF or high-protein pastas. Sauce adhesion is where this thing shines. The bronze-cut surface grips marinara, alfredo, or pesto far better than smooth extruded pastas. In my side-by-side test, the Goodles held a chunky vegetable sauce noticeably better than Banza, which tends to shed sauce into the bottom of the bowl.

One thing to flag: the texture is *slightly* firmer than traditional semolina pasta, even when cooked to the max recommended time. It’s not unpleasant — think fresh pasta from a decent Italian deli — but if your household expects soft, American-style noodles, there may be resistance.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The Amazon reviews are overwhelmingly positive, and the patterns are consistent. One verified buyer wrote: *“I genuinely can’t tell this isn’t regular pasta. My kids ate it without a single complaint — that’s a miracle.”* Another echoed that sentiment: *“Finally a protein pasta that doesn’t taste like a health food. The texture is spot on.”* The most common praise centers on the taste being indistinguishable from “normal” pasta, which is the highest compliment this category can receive.

## Where It Falls Short

The 8 oz box is the biggest practical issue. Standard pasta boxes are 16 oz; this gives you roughly half. If you’re feeding a family of four, you’ll need two boxes — and that adds up quickly. The price per ounce is steeper than conventional pasta, though it’s actually competitive with other protein pastas (typically $0.50-$0.75 per oz vs. Banza’s similar range). 

Also, the nutritional claims, while strong, shouldn’t be mistaken for a complete protein source. It’s not a meal replacement — you still need a protein-rich sauce or side to make it a balanced dinner.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Macro-conscious pasta lovers** who refuse to give up noodles while cutting carbs or boosting protein
- **Parents of picky eaters** who want to sneak extra protein and fiber into a familiar shape
- **Meal preppers** looking for a pasta that reheats well (it does — leftover penne holds its shape better than most)

Skip it if you’re on a tight budget, need a full pound for a big family dinner, or expect a texture identical to traditional semolina pasta.

## FAQ

**Is Goodles Lucky Penne actually healthier than regular pasta?**
Yes, significantly. You’re getting double the protein (14g vs. 7g) and four times the fiber (8g vs. 2g) per serving, with 40g of whole grains. It’s not magic — it’s just a better ratio of the same ingredients.

**Does it taste like chickpea or legume pasta?**
No. That’s the standout feature. The blend masks the beany flavor that plagues most alternative pastas. If I didn’t know better, I’d guess it was a premium semolina penne.

**How long does it take to cook?**
About 8-9 minutes, which is standard for dried pasta — but notably faster than many protein pastas that need 12+ minutes to soften.
