**The 1.15-ounce squeeze pack that thinks it's dessert — and mostly pulls it off.**

Here's the honest question every shopper asks about Justin's Chocolate Hazelnut & Almond Butter: is this just a fancy portable Nutella, or does it actually deliver something different? After digging through the specs and 229 verified ratings, the answer is: it's both, and that's exactly why it works.

**What you actually get**

Each box contains 10 individual 1.15-ounce squeeze pouches. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: dry-roasted almonds, hazelnuts, dry-roasted cocoa, palm fruit oil, and a touch of vanilla. No hydrogenated oils, no artificial flavors, no corn syrup. It's certified gluten-free and non-GMO, which matters if you're feeding kids or managing dietary restrictions.

The pouch design is the quiet hero here. As shown in the product image, the wide-mouth opening tears cleanly and the packet holds its shape when squeezed — no mid-snack blowouts. At roughly the size of a credit card folded in half, it slides into a cycling jersey, hiking pack, or work bag without drama.

**How it actually tastes and performs**

The flavor profile is sophisticated for a squeeze snack. The hazelnut comes through first, followed by a deep cocoa note that's more dark-chocolate than milk-chocolate. The almonds add a subtle savory backbone that keeps it from being cloyingly sweet. It's genuinely satisfying — think melted dark chocolate truffle meets almond butter, not sugary frosting.

Texture-wise, it's deliberately runny. This is a squeeze-and-eat product, not a spread. If you want to put it on toast, you'll need to squeeze it into a bowl first. But for its intended use — fueling up mid-ride, packing in a kid's lunch, or curbing a 3 p.m. craving — the consistency works perfectly.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scanning the reviews, three themes dominate. First, convenience: "Perfect for my son's lunchbox and he doesn't make a mess." Second, taste satisfaction: "Tastes like a treat but doesn't have the sugar crash." Third, clean ingredients — multiple reviewers specifically mention feeling good about giving these to their kids versus standard chocolate spreads.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. At roughly $0.70–$0.90 per pouch depending on the listing, it's pricier than making your own almond butter or buying a jar. The sweetness level is moderate — if your family is used to Nutella's sugar bomb, this will taste almost bitter by comparison. And the palm fruit oil, while sustainably sourced per Justin's, remains a concern for buyers who avoid palm products entirely.

**Who should buy this**

- **Cyclists and runners** who need portable calories with protein (7g per pack) that won't melt in a pocket
- **Parents packing school lunches** who want a treat that's not pure sugar
- **Road-trippers** who want a mess-free snack that doesn't require refrigeration
- **Nutella fans** who want a slightly healthier, more filling alternative

**Skip it if** you want a thick spread for toast, need something sugar-forward, or are strict about palm oil.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this actually healthy or just marketing?**
A: It's a legitimately better-for-you treat. Seven grams of protein, 3g fiber, and no artificial anything. But it's still 190 calories and 16g of fat per pack — treat it as a snack, not a health food.

**Q: How does it compare to Nutella?**
A: Less sweet, more nutty, with real almond protein. If you're weaning off Nutella, this is the logical next step.

**Q: Do the packs leak or burst?**
A: The vast majority of reviewers report no leakage. The seals are solid, and the foil material is sturdy. Store them flat and they'll be fine.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy it if you want a portable, cleaner chocolate-hazelnut fix. Skip it if you demand Nutella-level sweetness or need a thick spread. For everyone in between, this is a genuinely useful, tasty product that earns its rating.**
