Let's be blunt: most mayonnaise is just a white, creamy vehicle for other flavors. Blue Plate is not that. This is the mayo that Southern cooks have sworn by since 1927, and it has a personality — a tangy, slightly sweet zip that makes you notice it. If you're tired of bland, one-note spreads, this jar deserves a spot on your counter.

## What You're Actually Getting

The 30-ounce jar arrives sealed and shelf-stable. The product image shows the classic squat jar with the bold blue label — it looks like something from a diner counter, which is fitting. Inside, the mayo is a bright, almost lemony white. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: soybean oil, egg yolks, vinegar, water, salt, sugar, and natural flavors. No high-fructose corn syrup, no modified food starch, no weird gums. That's it.

## How It Performs in Real Life

I've tested this against Hellmann's, Duke's, and a couple of store brands. Blue Plate is noticeably thicker — it holds its shape when you spoon it out and doesn't slump. Spread it on bread for a BLT and it stays put, no soggy bottom. The flavor is where it wins: there's a sharp vinegar tang up front, then a subtle sweetness that balances it out. It's not as salty as Duke's and not as neutral as Hellmann's. It's the Goldilocks of mayo.

In potato salad, it doesn't turn gloppy. In deviled eggs, it gives the filling a brighter color and a cleaner taste. One reviewer put it simply: "I grew up on this and nothing else compares. It's the only mayo that makes tuna salad taste right." Another said, "I bought this on a whim and now I'm ruined for everything else. It's got actual flavor."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 225 ratings, the recurring theme is nostalgia and flavor. People who grew up in Louisiana, Mississippi, or Alabama almost universally call it "the real deal." ## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. Blue Plate is a regional brand, and that comes with two real drawbacks. First, availability: if you don't live in the Southeast, you're paying shipping costs that can double the price. One reviewer grumbled, "Love the mayo, hate that I have to pay $8 to ship a $5 jar." Second, the jar size: 30 ounces is generous, but it's a squat, wide container that struggles to fit in fridge door shelves. You'll likely need to store it in the main compartment.

Also, if you prefer a lighter, sweeter mayo (think Miracle Whip), this will not convert you. It's a savory, egg-forward spread, and that's a feature, not a bug — but know what you're buying.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The sandwich purist** — someone who believes mayo is a star ingredient, not a filler
- **The Southern transplant** — missing the taste of home and tired of substitutes

Skip it if you're happy with your current brand and don't want to pay shipping, or if you prefer a sweeter, creamier spread.

## FAQ

**Is Blue Plate mayonnaise gluten-free?**
Yes. The ingredients — soybean oil, egg yolks, vinegar, and spices — contain no gluten. It's safe for celiacs, though it's not certified gluten-free.

**How long does it last after opening?**
The jar stays fresh for about 2 months in the fridge. The vinegar acts as a natural preservative. If you see separation, just stir it — that's normal and doesn't mean it's spoiled.

**Is this the same as Duke's?**
No. Duke's is saltier and more vinegar-forward. Blue Plate is slightly sweeter and creamier. They're both Southern classics, but the flavor profiles are distinct. Try both and pick your lane.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a mayo with actual character — tangy, thick, and versatile enough for everything from breakfast sandwiches to dip bases. **Skip it if** you're on a tight budget or don't want to deal with shipping costs outside the South.

Blue Plate earns its 4.5-star rating honestly. It's not perfect — the jar is awkward and the price outside its home region is steep — but for pure flavor and texture, it's a standout. I'd rate it 4.6 out of 5, docking a bit for the availability headache. If you can find it locally, grab two jars. You'll thank me later.
