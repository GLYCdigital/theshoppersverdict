**The $80 Grinder That Punches Way Above Its Price Tag — But Read This Before Buying**

Most sub-$100 burr grinders are a compromise. You get a motor, some metal cones, and a plastic dial that clicks through settings that don't actually change anything. The AYCHIRO Electric Conical Burr Grinder is not that. It's the rare budget grinder that makes you double-check the price tag.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the grinder base with a built-in touchscreen, a 50g detachable bean hopper, a 50g ground coffee container, and a cleaning brush. The body is compact — roughly the footprint of a standard toaster — and the black matte finish looks more expensive than the $79.99 price suggests. The touchscreen panel is the standout feature here; it's a full digital interface, not just a few preset buttons, which is almost unheard of at this price point.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I ran this grinder through its paces across the full spectrum of brew methods. The 30 grind settings range from powdery espresso-fine to chunky French press coarse. The difference between adjacent settings is subtle but real — I could dial in a proper pour-over grind that held its structure through blooming. The conical burrs produce noticeably consistent particle sizes. I measured a batch for pour-over and found minimal fines, certainly better than anything a blade grinder produces at any price.

The touchscreen lets you select grind size, then start/stop grinding with a single tap. There's also a manual pulse mode for precise control. One caveat: the touchscreen requires a deliberate press. It's not sluggish, but if you're used to capacitive phone screens, you'll find yourself tapping twice occasionally.

The detachable hopper and container are genuinely useful. Swapping beans between a light roast for morning pour-over and a dark roast for afternoon espresso takes about 10 seconds. Cleaning is straightforward — the burrs are accessible, and the included brush handles the chaff.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.9-star rating across 28 reviews isn't an accident. One verified buyer noted, *"I've owned three grinders under $100 and this is the first one that doesn't produce dust with my espresso settings."* Another commented on the build quality: *"The touchscreen feels premium. I expected plastic buttons at this price."* Multiple reviewers specifically called out the consistent grind size for French press — a method where blade grinders notoriously fail.

**Where It Falls Short**

The 50g hopper is the biggest practical limitation. That's roughly enough for 5-6 cups of coffee, but if you're grinding for a full 12-cup pot or entertaining, you'll refill mid-batch. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

There's also no timed dosing or weight-based output. For espresso, you'll need a separate scale to dial in consistent shots. The AYCHIRO is a single-dose grinder in spirit — you add beans, grind, and repeat. That's fine for most home users, but espresso enthusiasts may find the lack of programmable dosing limiting.

Finally, the touchscreen is the only control method. If it ever fails, you have no fallback. That's a long-term reliability question that a physical dial doesn't have.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The pour-over or French press brewer** upgrading from a blade grinder — this is a massive step up for the money
- **The single-dose espresso enthusiast** on a budget who doesn't mind weighing each shot
- **The small household** brewing 2-4 cups daily who wants variety without cleaning between bean swaps

**Skip it if** you need to grind 60g+ at once, or you want a grinder that works without a scale for espresso.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this grinder suitable for espresso?**
A: Yes, the finest settings produce powder-fine grounds suitable for pressurized espresso baskets. For non-pressurized portafilters, you'll need a scale and patience to dial in, as there's no timed dosing.

**Q: How loud is it compared to blade grinders?**
A: It's noticeably quieter. Burr grinders run slower and produce a lower-pitched hum. It won't wake the house, but it's not silent either.

**Q: Can I grind directly into a portafilter?**
A: No, the ground coffee container is the only output. You'll need to transfer grounds manually.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want genuine conical burr consistency without spending $150+. The AYCHIRO delivers 90% of the performance of grinders twice its price, with a modern touchscreen interface that's genuinely pleasant to use.

**Skip it if** you need high-volume grinding or want programmable dosing for espresso. The 50g hopper and manual operation make this a single-dose tool, not a set-and-forget machine.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — An exceptional value that nails the fundamentals. The small hopper and lack of dosing hold it back from perfection, but for most home brewers, this is the grinder to beat under $100.
