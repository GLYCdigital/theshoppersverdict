You're looking at a 2-pound bag of fruit slice jelly candies — the kind your grandmother kept in a crystal dish. The question isn't whether these are nostalgic. It's whether they're worth your money at 3.7 stars from 185 Amazon shoppers. Let's dig in.

## What's in the bag

This is a straightforward bulk candy purchase. You get one 2-pound poly bag (no resealable zipper, so plan ahead) filled with assorted orange, lemon, and lime slices. Each piece is sugar-dusted, dome-shaped, and roughly the size of a half-dollar. Expect somewhere between 60 and 70 pieces depending on how the batch was cut. The ingredients list is short: sugar, corn syrup, pectin, citric acid, natural and artificial flavors. No gelatin, so this is vegetarian-friendly.

## Texture and flavor — the real story

Here's where the 3.7 rating makes sense. When these are fresh, the texture is genuinely good — soft, pliable, with a slight chew that gives way without sticking to your teeth. The sugar coating is light, not the thick crust you find on cheaper gummies. The orange and lemon flavors are bright and distinct; the lime is slightly more muted but still recognizable.

But — and this is the catch — freshness is inconsistent. Multiple Amazon reviews mention receiving bags with hard, crystallized pieces. One reviewer noted, "About half the bag was perfect and soft, the other half was tough and chewy like old candy." That's the gamble you're taking. If you're buying for a specific event, order ahead and test a few pieces immediately.

## What buyers consistently praise

The value proposition is strong. At roughly 2 pounds for the price, this is cheaper per ounce than most grocery store fruit slices. Reviewers regularly mention using these for:

- Candy buffets and wedding favors
- Holiday baking (chopped into cookies or brownies)
- Gift bags and party favors
- Retro candy dishes that need filling

One five-star review summed it up: "These taste exactly like the fruit slices from the old-fashioned candy shop. The orange ones are the best." Another buyer noted they arrive well-packaged with minimal breakage — worth mentioning since bulk candy often arrives as a pile of crumbs.

## Where it falls short

**The texture lottery.** This is the biggest complaint across the 185 ratings. You may get a perfect bag, or you may get pieces that are hard and sugary. It's a manufacturing consistency issue, not a shipping damage issue.

**No resealable bag.** For a 2-pound purchase, this is annoying. Unless you have a clip or a container ready, the sugar coating will absorb humidity and clump within days.

**The sugar situation.** If you're watching sugar intake, this is not the candy for you. These are essentially pure sugar with pectin. That's the point, but buyers expecting a softer, more "jelly-like" texture (like the German version) may be disappointed.

## FAQ

**Q: How long do these stay fresh once opened?**
A: In an airtight container at room temperature, expect 3-4 weeks of good texture. After that, the sugar coating will start to crystallize and harden. Don't refrigerate — that accelerates the hardening process.

**Q: Are these the same as the Brach's fruit slices?**
A: Similar concept, slightly different execution. These are softer when fresh and have a more pronounced fruit flavor. Brach's tend to be firmer and more uniform in texture.

**Q: Can I use these for baking?**
A: Yes. Chop them with a lightly oiled knife and fold into cookie dough or brownie batter. They hold their shape better than gummy bears and don't melt completely.
