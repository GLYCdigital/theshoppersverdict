## CASABREWS Espresso Machine 20 Bar Review

The CASABREWS 20 Bar Espresso Machine (B09QGJQV8Q) represents a growing trend in home coffee: good-enough espresso at a fraction of the price of Italian stalwarts. With 5,000 Amazon reviews and a 4.4-star average, it's one of the most popular budget espresso machines on the market.

We put it through two weeks of daily espresso, cappuccinos, and lattes to see how it stacks up.

### First Impressions

The packaging is straightforward — double-boxed with foam inserts. The machine itself is more attractive than the price suggests: a brushed stainless steel body with black accents and a compact footprint that fits under most kitchen cabinets.

The control layout is refreshingly simple:
- **Rotary dial** for brew/steam/off selection
- **Pressure gauge** on the front panel
- **Indicator lights** for power and ready status

No touchscreens, no smartphone app, no IoT nonsense. It's a manual espresso machine that expects you to learn the basics.

### Build Quality

For $149, the build is impressive. The stainless steel body isn't just decorative — it's structural, giving the machine a solid feel that belies its price. The drip tray is plastic but substantial, and the cup-warming surface on top gets genuinely warm after 15 minutes.

Weak points:
- **Plastic tamper:** Functional for day one, but you'll want an upgrade
- **Portafilter basket:** Pressurized (dual-wall) — limits your ability to fine-tune with fresh beans
- **Steam wand tip:** Plastic sleeve over the metal steam tip — removable for better frothing

### Espresso Performance

The 20-bar Italian pump is the headline feature. While marketing "20 bars" is mostly a number game (actual espresso extraction happens at 9 bars), the pump has enough headroom to maintain consistent pressure throughout the shot.

**With pre-ground espresso:** The pressurized baskets work exactly as designed. Even grocery-store pre-ground espresso produces a reasonable shot with visible crema. Fine-tuning is limited, but for convenience, it's hard to beat.

**With fresh beans and a decent grinder:** This is where the CASABREWS surprises. Using freshly-ground beans from our OXO Gen 2 burr grinder (setting 2-3, fine), the machine produced shots with genuine crema, good body, and balanced extraction. Total brew time from dead cold to a pulled shot: about 45 seconds (15 seconds heat-up once the indicator light is green, 25-30 seconds to pull a 1.5 oz double).

The **pressure gauge** is a genuine value-add at this price point. It lets you see if your puck is offering proper resistance. Needle in the "ESPRESSO RANGE" zone? You're good. Needle too low? Grind finer. Needle maxed out? Too fine or over-packed. It's a teaching tool that helps you improve.

### Milk Steaming

The steam wand is a single-hole pannarello wand with a plastic texturing sleeve. For best results, remove the plastic sleeve — it's friction-fit and slides off easily. What remains is a stainless steel single-hole tip that produces surprisingly decent microfoam.

The workflow:
1. Brew your shot
2. Turn the dial to steam
3. Wait about 30-60 seconds for the boiler to reach steam temperature
4. Purge the wand, submerge in milk, and steam
5. Turn the dial off when done

**Quality:** With the sleeve removed and proper technique, we achieved latte-art-ready microfoam within a week of practice. It's not going to rival a $2,000 La Marzocco, but it's absolutely good enough for home cappuccinos and lattes.

**The trade-off:** Single boiler means you wait between brewing and steaming. The 30-60 second transition isn't terrible, but if you're making back-to-back milk drinks, it adds up.

### The Big Question: Does It Make Real Espresso?

Yes — with caveats. The CASABREWS produces genuine espresso with pressure, crema, and body. A well-pulled shot rivals what many chain coffee shops serve. What it won't do is give you the control of a $500+ machine with a non-pressurized basket, PID temperature control, and stepless grind adjustment.

But here's the thing: 90% of home espresso drinkers don't need that level of control. They want a machine that reliably produces a good shot with minimal fuss. The CASABREWS delivers that at $149.

### Who Should Buy It

**Buy the CASABREWS if:**
- You want real espresso at home without spending $300+
- You're learning espresso and want a machine that grows with you
- Counter space is tight
- You value simplicity — no apps, no subscription, just espresso

**Skip it if:**
- You already have a good grinder and want non-pressurized basket control
- You make multiple milk drinks in quick succession
- You need a machine that brews and steams simultaneously

### Maintenance and Longevity

The machine requires basic care: backflush the portafilter with water weekly, descale every 2-3 months depending on water hardness, and clean the steam wand tip after every use. The drip tray and water tank are dishwasher-safe.

Most reported issues involve scale buildup in machines used with hard water without regular descaling. Use filtered or softened water to extend the machine's life. The single-boiler design is simple and relatively easy to maintain.

The CASABREWS 20 Bar Espresso Machine proves you don't need to spend a fortune for decent home espresso. The pressure gauge alone sets it apart from budget competitors, and the steam wand produces genuinely good microfoam with practice. It's not a pro machine, and it doesn't pretend to be — but for $149, it delivers the best espresso we've tested at this price point.
