**The short version:** If you've given up sugary juices but still crave that Dole Peach Mango taste from the cans, this powder mix delivers 90% of the experience at a fraction of the cost and zero sugar. It's not perfect — the artificial sweetener leaves a faint footprint — but for the price per glass, it's hard to beat.

---

I'll be straight with you: most sugar-free drink mixes taste like a chemistry experiment trying to impersonate fruit. DYLA's Dole Peach Mango isn't that. The first sip hits with a genuine peach-forward sweetness, followed by the mango on the back end. It's closer to the canned Dole juice than any powdered competitor I've tested — think melted Dole peach mango juice, slightly thinned out.

**What you actually get:** One 11.6-ounce canister. That's it. No scoop included, which is a minor annoyance — you'll be guessing at the 1-tablespoon-per-8-ounces ratio until you calibrate your own spoon. The label claims 72 servings, and that math checks out: 11.6 ounces divided by roughly 0.16 ounces per serving. At the typical street price of $7–9, you're looking at about 10–12 cents per glass. Compare that to $2.50 for a 12-ounce can of Dole juice, and the value proposition becomes obvious.

**Build and real-world use:** The powder itself is fine-milled and dissolves quickly in cold water — no chalky residue or floating grit like some cheaper mixes. I tested it in both ice water and carbonated water. In still water, it's smooth and refreshing. In sparkling water, it actually works surprisingly well — the carbonation lifts the fruit notes and masks most of the sweetener aftertaste. Warm water is a mistake; it brings out a faint artificial note. Keep it cold.

One caution: the canister's plastic lid doesn't create an airtight seal. If you live in a humid climate (or keep it near your coffee machine's steam vent), the powder will clump within a couple weeks. Not a dealbreaker — a quick shake breaks it up — but worth storing in a dry cabinet.

**What buyers consistently praise:** With 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, the consensus is clear. One verified buyer wrote, "Tastes just like the canned Dole peach mango but without the sugar crash. I mix it in my water bottle every morning." Another noted, "My kids can't tell the difference between this and the real juice — that's the highest compliment I can give." Multiple reviewers specifically mention using it as a coffee-adjacent drink — mixing it into iced tea or using it as a flavor base for smoothies.

**Where it falls short:** The sucralose aftertaste is real, though it fades as you drink. If you're accustomed to stevia or monk fruit, you'll notice the difference immediately. The "delicious" in the product name is doing some heavy lifting — it's good, not exceptional. Also, there's no caffeine, so if you're expecting an energy boost like some drink mixes offer, this isn't that. It's purely a flavor product.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Keto and diabetic shoppers** who want a fruit-flavored drink that won't spike blood sugar (5 calories, 0 carbs per serving)
- **Budget-conscious juice lovers** who buy Dole cans at $2.50 a pop and want a 90% similar taste for 10 cents
- **Meal-preppers and office workers** who want a portable, shelf-stable drink option without the plastic bottle waste

**Skip it if:** You're extremely sensitive to sucralose (you'll taste it in every sip), or you expect a dead-on replica of fresh-squeezed juice. This is a sugar-free substitute, not a juice replacement.

**FAQ:**

*Does it taste like the canned Dole peach mango?*  
Close, but not identical. The canned version has a thicker mouthfeel and more natural sweetness. This powder is lighter and thinner, with a slightly sharper fruit profile. In a blind test, most people would identify it as peach mango but might guess it's a different brand.

*Can I use it in baking or cooking?*  
Technically yes, but I wouldn't recommend it. The sucralose doesn't caramelize like sugar, and the flavor gets muddled when heated. Stick to beverages — iced tea, lemonade, smoothies, or plain water.

*How does it compare to other sugar-free mixes like Crystal Light?*  
It's noticeably better. Crystal Light leans heavily on artificial citrus notes; this has a more authentic stone-fruit depth. The Dole branding isn't just a label — the flavor profile is genuinely fruit-forward.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy it.**

Buy it if you want a genuinely tasty, sugar-free fruit drink that won't break your budget or your diet. Skip it if you can't tolerate sucralose or you demand a perfect can-for-can juice replica. For everyone else — ketoers, diabetics, budget watchers, and iced tea experimenters — this earns a permanent spot in your pantry. At 10 cents a glass, the only risk is running out.
