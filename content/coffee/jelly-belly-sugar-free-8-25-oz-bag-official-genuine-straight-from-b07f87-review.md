This is a tricky one to review, because I'm not reviewing a coffee maker. I'm reviewing a bag of sugar-free jelly beans that's sitting in the "coffee gear" category on Amazon. That placement tells you something about how this product actually gets used — it's a desk-drawer candy, a sugar-free treat to accompany your afternoon brew. And for that specific job, it's surprisingly good.

**What you actually get**

The 8.25-ounce bag arrives as a sealed, resealable pouch. You're getting roughly 200 individual beans in the standard assortment of 20 flavors. This is the official, genuine Jelly Belly product — not a knockoff, not a repackaged bulk bin. The beans are the same size and shape as the sugared version. The bag's resealable zipper works fine, though it's not the sturdiest I've tested; if you're rough with it, it'll eventually fail.

**How it tastes and performs**

Here's the thing about sugar-free Jelly Bellys: they've been making these for decades, and it shows. The flavor compounds are identical to the regular beans. The cherry tastes like cherry. The watermelon tastes like watermelon. The buttered popcorn still has that weird, savory-sweet thing going on. Where it differs is texture — the sugar-free version is slightly firmer, with a touch less give when you bite down. There's also a faint cooling sensation from the maltitol that some people detect more than others.

The flavors are concentrated in the shell, so you get an immediate burst, then a milder center. It's a solid candy experience. But you need to respect the serving size. The bag says 28 pieces per serving. That's not a suggestion — it's a warning.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 204 ratings on Amazon, the recurring theme is that these taste shockingly close to the real thing. One reviewer put it simply: "My husband is diabetic and these satisfy his jelly bean craving. He says he can't tell the difference." Another noted, "Great for a keto snack, just don't overdo it." The portion control angle comes up repeatedly — the resealable bag helps people stop at a reasonable amount.

**Where it falls short**

The honest downside is the maltitol. This is a sugar alcohol that your body partially absorbs, and for many people, it causes bloating, gas, or worse. The Amazon reviews are littered with variations of "tasty but I regret eating the whole bag." The other issue is the aftertaste — it's not the clean finish of real sugar. It's a slightly metallic, cool note that lingers. If you're sensitive to artificial sweeteners, you'll notice it.

The 4.1-star average reflects this split: people who eat them in moderation love them; people who binge them have a bad time.

**Who should buy this**

- **Low-carb dieters** who miss candy and want something that actually tastes like the real thing
- **Diabetics** looking for a portion-controlled sweet treat (at 2g net carbs per serving, it's manageable)
- **Desk-drawer snackers** who want a long-lasting, resealable treat

**Skip it if** you have a sensitive stomach, if you're prone to eating an entire bag in one sitting, or if you hate all artificial sweeteners.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Will these kick me out of ketosis?**
A: Each 28-bean serving has about 2g of net carbs after subtracting sugar alcohols. That's manageable for most keto diets, but the maltitol does have a mild glycemic response in some people. Test your own reaction.

**Q: How many are in the bag?**
A: Roughly 200 beans. At 28 beans per serving, that's about seven servings per bag.

**Q: Do they taste the same as regular Jelly Bellys?**
A: Close, but not identical. The flavor is spot-on, but the texture is firmer and there's a slight cooling aftertaste from the maltitol.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely good-tasting sugar-free candy and can exercise portion control. It's one of the better low-carb treats on the market.

**Skip it if** you have a sensitive digestive system or zero self-control around candy. The maltitol will find you out.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Great flavor replication, honest about its limitations, and a legit option for sugar-conscious snackers. Just don't eat the whole bag.
