## The Stevia That Actually Belongs in Your Coffee

Most liquid stevia products taste like they were engineered in a chemistry lab — thin, metallic, with an artificial sweetness that clings to your tongue. Pure Mountain Botanicals takes a different route. This is a two-ingredient product: organic stevia leaf extract and water. No alcohol, no glycerin, no maltodextrin fillers that spike blood sugar. That simplicity matters when you're adding it to a drink you taste every single morning.

As shown in the product image, you're getting a compact 2-ounce amber glass bottle with a glass dropper. It's small enough to live permanently on your desk or kitchen counter without cluttering the space.

## What's Actually in the Bottle

One bottle yields roughly 400-500 drops. For context, that's about 60-80 cups of coffee at 6 drops per cup. The math works out to pennies per serving — significantly cheaper than sugar, honey, or even generic liquid sweeteners from the grocery store.

The sweetness profile is where this product separates itself. Stevia's biggest weakness has always been the delayed bitterness that shows up after the initial sweet hit. Pure Mountain Botanicals minimizes this. The sweetness comes on fast and clean, with only a faint herbal note in the finish. In coffee, that translates to a smoother experience than most stevia powders I've tested, which tend to leave a chalky residue and artificial aftertaste.

## Where It Falls Short

The dropper is the weak point. It's a standard glass pipette, which means you're at the mercy of gravity and surface tension. One drop might be barely sweet; two drops can tip you into oversweetened territory. This isn't a dealbreaker, but it requires a learning curve. My advice: start with 3 drops in a 12-ounce cup, taste, and adjust from there. You'll find your sweet spot within a week.

A smaller but notable complaint: the glass bottle is fragile. If you travel with it or keep it in a purse, it can crack. This is a kitchen-counter product, not a travel companion.
