Let's cut through the packaging. Brioschi has been making this fizzy digestive since 1901, and it shows. This isn't a novelty Italian import — it's a functional antacid with a century of formulation behind it. The question isn't whether it works; it's whether it works for *you*.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting a box of individual foil packets, each holding 4.5 grams of effervescent powder. Drop one into 4-6 ounces of water and it fizzes into a clear, lemony solution. Unlike tablet-style antacids that can leave chalky sediment at the bottom of your glass, Brioschi dissolves completely. No stirring required beyond a quick swirl.

The active ingredient is sodium bicarbonate — 63.2 grams per 100 grams of powder, to be precise. That's the same base chemistry as baking soda, but processed to pharmaceutical grade and flavored with natural lemon oils.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this after a particularly aggressive bowl of chili con carne, which felt like a fair stress test. The fizz reaction is immediate — drop the packet in water and it's fully dissolved within 15 seconds. The drink itself tastes like lightly sweetened sparkling lemon water with a mineral edge. It's not candy; it's medicine that doesn't punish you for drinking it.

Relief kicked in within about 5 minutes and lasted roughly 2 hours. That's on par with liquid antacids, which is impressive for a portable format. The sodium bicarbonate neutralizes stomach acid directly, not by coating your stomach lining like some competitors.

**Where it shines:** Travel. Toss three packets in your carry-on and you've got relief for a whole flight without TSA flagging a liquid. The packets are flat, lightweight, and don't explode if they get crushed in a bag.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires balance. Brioschi has limitations:

**It's purely an antacid.** If you're dealing with GERD symptoms that include chest pain or regurgitation, this isn't a complete solution. There's no alginate, no H2 blocker, no pain reliever. It neutralizes acid and that's it.

**The lemon flavor is subtle.** If you're expecting a sweet lemonade experience, you'll be disappointed. The mineral taste from the sodium bicarbonate is present. It's pleasant but unmistakably medicinal.

**Sodium content.** Each packet contains roughly 1.2 grams of sodium. If you're on a low-sodium diet or managing hypertension, this isn't your product. That's not a flaw in design — it's inherent to the active ingredient — but it deserves mention.

## FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I take this daily?**
A: Generally yes, but daily use of sodium bicarbonate antacids can mask underlying conditions. If you need it more than twice a week, see a doctor. That's not Brioschi-specific advice — it applies to all antacids.

**Q: How does it compare to Alka-Seltzer?**
A: Brioschi is smoother and faster-dissolving with a cleaner taste. But Alka-Seltzer includes aspirin for pain, which Brioschi doesn't. They're different tools for different jobs.

**Q: Does it need to be refrigerated?**
A: No. The packets are shelf-stable and survive heat well. I tested one that had been in a car glovebox for three weeks — it worked perfectly.
