# Raw Unfiltered Light Clover Honey by Desert Creek, 5 Gallon: The Bulk Honey Honest Review

Most honey on grocery shelves has been heated to 160°F+, filtered until it's crystal-clear, and stripped of the pollen and enzymes that make honey... actually honey. Desert Creek's 5-gallon bucket is the opposite of that — it's raw, unfiltered, and arrives with a layer of beeswax and propolis floating on top. For anyone who uses honey daily or in serious volume, this is a meaningful difference, not a marketing gimmick.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 60-pound (net weight) food-grade bucket of light clover honey. It's not a cute 12-ounce bear bottle. The bucket is substantial — roughly 14 inches tall and 12 inches in diameter. You're getting about 5 gallons of honey, which translates to roughly 400 tablespoons. If you're a daily tea or coffee sweetener, this is a year's supply for one person or a 2-3 month supply for a family that cooks and bakes with honey regularly.

The honey arrives with visible particles — beeswax flecks, pollen grains, and occasionally small bits of propolis. That's the unfiltered part. It's a sign of authenticity, but if you're expecting the crystal-clear golden syrup from the supermarket, you'll be surprised. The consistency is thick and slightly cloudy, with a light amber color that's typical of clover.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Flavor-wise, this is a mild, floral clover profile — sweet without being cloying, with a subtle grassy undertone. It dissolves cleanly in hot coffee and tea without clumping, and it works well in baking where you don't want a strong buckwheat or wildflower flavor competing with other ingredients.

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews reflects genuine user experience. One reviewer noted: *"I've bought this twice now. The flavor is consistently good, and it's the only honey I've found that doesn't taste like it was cut with corn syrup."* Another mentioned: *"Took a while to arrive but the honey is legit. You can see the pollen in it. My kids use it on everything."*

The biggest practical issue: **this honey granulates fast.** Expect crystallization within 1-3 months of opening, especially if your kitchen runs cool. To reliquefy, you'll need to place the sealed bucket in warm (not boiling) water for several hours, stirring occasionally. This is standard for raw honey — it's actually proof it wasn't overheated — but it's an inconvenience many buyers don't anticipate.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this honey need to be refrigerated?**
No. Honey never spoils if sealed properly. Store at room temperature, away from direct sunlight. Refrigeration accelerates crystallization.

**Q: How do I deal with the granulation?**
Place the sealed bucket in a larger container of warm water (100-110°F, not boiling). Leave for 4-8 hours. Stir well. Never microwave honey — it destroys enzymes and can create hot spots.

**Q: Is this safe for infants?**
No. Raw honey of any kind should never be given to children under 12 months due to botulism risk. This is true of all honey, not specific to this product.
