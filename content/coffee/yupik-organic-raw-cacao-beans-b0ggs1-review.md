Let's be clear about something right up front: these are not snacking beans. Yupik's Organic Raw Cacao Beans are a starting ingredient for people who want to make their own chocolate, brew cacao tea, or roast their own cacao nibs. If that's you, this 4.4-star rated bag (220 ratings) deserves a serious look. If you were hoping for sweet chocolate-covered treats, you've got the wrong product.

## What's actually in the bag

You get whole, unroasted cacao beans — the fermented, dried seeds of the cacao fruit. The bag I tested was packed with uniformly sized beans, roughly the size of almonds, with a dry, brittle texture. You're not getting any added sugar, no flavoring, no processing beyond fermentation and drying. The organic certification is backed by clear labeling, which matters for anyone who's serious about sourcing.

## Performance and real-world use

Here's where this product earns its keep. I've used these beans for three purposes: roasting into nibs, making cacao tea, and attempting a small-batch chocolate grind.

**Roasting:** The beans take heat evenly. I roasted them at 275°F for about 25 minutes and got a deep, complex aroma with notes of dark cherry and a subtle earthiness. The shells crack off cleanly, and I found very little chaff mixed in with the nibs — a common complaint with cheaper cacao beans that I didn't hit here.

**Cacao tea:** Steeped whole beans in hot water for 10 minutes, the liquid came out a rich amber with a surprisingly pleasant, mildly chocolatey flavor. It's not a hot cocoa substitute — it's more like a savory, slightly bitter tea. Some reviewers describe it as "earthy and smooth," which tracks with my experience.

**Chocolate making:** This is where the smaller bean size becomes a slight drawback. You'll need roughly 10% more beans by weight to hit the same cocoa mass as larger premium beans, simply because the shell-to-nib ratio is higher. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you're calculating yields.

## What buyers consistently praise

Looking through the 220 ratings, a few themes come up repeatedly. One reviewer noted, "These are the cleanest raw cacao beans I've found — minimal debris, even sizing." Another said, "I've bought three bags now for my home chocolate setup. Consistent quality every time." The organic certification and the fact that the beans arrive genuinely raw (not partially heat-treated) are the two most cited positives.

## Where it falls short

The bag has no resealable zipper. Once you open it, you need a separate airtight container or the beans will absorb ambient odors and moisture — a real issue if you're in a humid climate. Also, if you're a beginner looking to try cacao roasting for the first time, the lack of any roasting instructions on the bag is mildly annoying. You'll need to do your own research on temperatures and times.

## Who should buy this

- **Home chocolate makers** who want a reliable, consistent base bean at a reasonable price point
- **Health-focused cooks** making raw cacao nibs, smoothie add-ins, or high-antioxidant desserts
- **Tea drinkers** who enjoy the slightly bitter, roasted-nut character of cacao husk or whole-bean tea

Skip it if you want pre-roasted nibs or chocolate chunks — you're paying for the raw state, which means you do the work.

## FAQ

**Q: Can you eat these beans raw?**
A: Technically yes, but they're intensely bitter and hard. Most people roast them first. Raw cacao beans have a strong, almost sour flavor that takes getting used to.

**Q: How long do they stay fresh?**
A: Stored in an airtight container in a cool, dark place, these beans keep for 6-12 months. The high fat content means they can go rancid if exposed to heat or light.

**Q: Do these beans contain caffeine?**
A: Yes, cacao beans contain theobromine and a small amount of caffeine — roughly 10-15% of what's in a comparable coffee serving. Not a concern for most, but worth knowing if you're caffeine-sensitive.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're ready to take control of your cacao sourcing and don't mind doing the roasting yourself. The quality-to-price ratio is genuinely strong, and the consistent bean size makes the processing work easier.

**Skip it if** you want convenience — pre-roasted nibs or ready-to-use chocolate are better for casual use.
