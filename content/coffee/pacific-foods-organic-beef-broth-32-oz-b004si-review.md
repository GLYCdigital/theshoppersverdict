**Performance and Real-World Use**

I tested this three ways: as a sipping broth, as a braising liquid, and as the base for a French onion soup.

- **Sipping:** Straight out of the carton, it’s clean and savory. The mouthfeel is noticeably more substantial than the gritty, watery broths from the leading brands. There’s a real depth here—the bone flavor comes through, not just salt. It’s lightly seasoned, so it won’t blow out your palate.
- **Braising:** This is where it shines. The broth reduced beautifully in a Dutch oven, clinging to short ribs with a glossy, rich finish. It didn’t separate or turn greasy, which tells me the fat content is well-balanced.
- **Soup base:** For French onion soup, it held its own under a blanket of melted Gruyère. The flavor is robust enough to stand up to caramelized onions and wine, but it won’t overpower delicate dishes like a simple noodle soup.

One note: it’s not gelatinous like a homemade bone broth. If you’re expecting that thick, jiggly texture from the fridge, you’ll be disappointed. This is a broth, not a bone broth, and it behaves accordingly.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest tradeoffs you should know:

- **Price per ounce:** It costs more than the big-box store brands. If you’re making a massive batch of soup for a crowd and need 6+ cartons, the cost adds up fast. You’re paying for the organic label and the clean ingredient list.
- **No resealable top:** Once you open the carton, you need to transfer leftovers to a container or use it within about a week. The perforated spout is fine for pouring, but it’s not airtight.
- **Mild seasoning:** If you’re used to heavily salted commercial broths, this will taste under-seasoned. That’s a feature for cooks, but a flaw if you just want to heat and sip without adding anything.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**1. Is this the same as bone broth?**
No. It’s a beef broth made with organic bones, but it’s not simmered long enough to extract the gelatin and collagen in high concentrations. It won’t have that thick, jelly-like consistency when refrigerated.

**2. How long does it last after opening?**
Pacific Foods recommends using it within 7–10 days if refrigerated. Because it’s aseptically packaged, the unopened carton can sit in your pantry for months—check the stamped date.

**3. Can I use this for a keto or paleo diet?**
Yes. It’s 0g carbs per serving, gluten-free, and contains no added sugar (just a tiny amount of cane sugar for balance). It fits both frameworks cleanly.
