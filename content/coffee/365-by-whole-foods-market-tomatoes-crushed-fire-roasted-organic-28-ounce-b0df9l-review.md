Some products claim "fire roasted" and deliver a whisper of smoke. This one delivers a campfire punch. The 365 by Whole Foods Market Organic Fire Roasted Crushed Tomatoes have earned their 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews — and after digging into what buyers actually say, the praise is consistent: real char flavor, clean ingredients, and a can size that actually makes sense for cooking.

## What you're actually getting

This is a 28-ounce can of organic crushed tomatoes that have been fire roasted before crushing. That's double the size of the standard 14.5-ounce can most brands sell, which matters if you're making a batch of chili, a big pot of soup, or a Sunday gravy. One can replaces two of the smaller ones, and you're not paying for extra packaging.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: organic tomatoes, organic tomato juice, and sea salt. No citric acid, no calcium chloride, no sugar hiding in there. For shoppers who read labels, that's a genuine win.

## Real-world performance

The standout feature is the smoke. Multiple reviewers note this isn't a subtle background note — it's front and center. One buyer wrote, "The fire roasted flavor is unmistakable and takes my homemade pizza sauce to another level." Another mentioned using it for salsa and getting compliments at a party. That's the kind of feedback that tells you the roasting process is legit, not just marketing.

The texture, however, is where some buyers hit a snag. Several reviewers mention the consistency is thinner than expected for "crushed" tomatoes — closer to a puree. One reviewer noted, "Great flavor, but I had to simmer longer to thicken my sauce." If you're planning a slow-simmered ragu, that's fine. If you need a quick weeknight sauce, budget extra time or add tomato paste.

## Where it falls short

Beyond the thinner texture, the smoke intensity is a double-edged sword. In dishes where tomatoes should play a supporting role — say, a delicate seafood stew or a light vegetable soup — the char can dominate. One reviewer warned, "Use half a can unless you want everything to taste like a barbecue." That's fair. This isn't a neutral pantry staple; it's a flavor-forward ingredient that needs to be deployed thoughtfully.

A few reviewers also noted the price point runs higher than conventional canned tomatoes. You're paying for organic certification and the roasting process. For budget shoppers, that's a real consideration.

## Who should buy this

This can is ideal for three types of cooks: the chili and stew maker who wants depth without adding liquid smoke; the pizza and pasta sauce enthusiast who wants that wood-fired character without firing up an outdoor oven; and the clean-label shopper who refuses to buy tomatoes with additives. If you're making dishes where the tomato flavor is the star — shakshuka, arrabbiata, barbecue sauce — this will elevate your cooking.

Skip it if you need neutral crushed tomatoes for a recipe where other flavors should lead, or if you're cooking for someone sensitive to smoky flavors.

## FAQ

**Is this actually spicy?**
No. Fire roasting adds smokiness, not heat. The tomatoes themselves are mild; you'll add your own chili flakes or peppers for spice.

**Can I substitute this for regular crushed tomatoes in any recipe?**
Technically yes, but expect a smoky flavor shift. If a recipe calls for crushed tomatoes and you want the dish to taste neutral, this isn't a 1:1 swap.

**Does the 28-ounce can need refrigeration after opening?**
Yes, like any canned tomato. Transfer leftovers to a glass container and refrigerate — they'll keep about 5-7 days. You can also freeze portions in ice cube trays for later use.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want real fire-roasted flavor in a clean, organic can and don't mind adjusting simmer time for thickness. **Skip it if** you need neutral tomatoes or a quick-thickening sauce base.
