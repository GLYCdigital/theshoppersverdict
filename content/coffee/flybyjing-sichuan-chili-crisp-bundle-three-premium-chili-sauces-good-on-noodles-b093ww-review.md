**What's Actually in the Box**

The FLYBYJING bundle ships three 8.8-ounce jars: the original Sichuan Chili Crisp, the Mala version (extra numbing), and the Spicy version (extra heat). Each jar is a glass, wide-mouth container with a metal lid — no plastic, no flimsy packaging. The oil is a deep, rust-red color with visible whole Sichuan peppercorns, dried chili flakes, and crispy garlic pieces suspended throughout. As shown in the product image, the texture is chunky and substantial, not a thin flavored oil.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The flavor is legitimately authentic. The base chili crisp delivers that signature fermented broad bean paste depth with a slow-building warmth. The Mala version is the standout — the Sichuan peppercorn oil produces that genuine tongue-numbing tingle that builds after about 15 seconds and lingers pleasantly. It's not gimmicky; it's the real deal.

I tested these three ways: over plain white rice with a fried egg, mixed into cold sesame noodles, and as a dumpling dip. The chili crisp adds crunch and umami that bottled hot sauce simply can't replicate. One tablespoon is enough to transform a bowl of plain rice. Two tablespoons on noodles and you're in business.

The heat levels are honest. The original is mild — accessible to anyone who enjoys a little warmth. The Mala is medium on the Scoville scale but high on the flavor complexity scale. The Spicy version is genuinely hot but not punishing; it's a steady burn, not a fire alarm.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The Amazon reviews at 4.3 stars across 215 ratings repeatedly highlight the same things. One buyer wrote, *"Finally a chili crisp that tastes like what I ate in Chengdu — the numbing is real, not just vinegar and oil."* Another noted, *"The bundle is smart because you can figure out your tolerance without committing to three full-size jars of one heat level."* Multiple reviewers mention the clean ingredient list — no MSG, no preservatives, just oil, chilies, garlic, and spices.

**Where It Falls Short**

The oil separation is the biggest frustration. Every jar needs a vigorous stir before each use — I'm talking 30 seconds of stirring with a chopstick to fully incorporate the sediment. If you skip this, you'll pour out mostly oil and then hit a concentrated glob of chili paste on your food. It's a minor annoyance that becomes major when you're in a hurry.

The Spicy version also underwhelms for true chili heads. If your baseline is habanero or ghost pepper sauces, this won't challenge you. It's hot enough for most people but marketed as "spicy" when it's really just medium-plus.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home cooks** who want to elevate eggs, rice, noodles, and stir-fries without learning complex Sichuan techniques
- **Heat-curious beginners** who want to explore Chinese chili oil without committing to a single flavor profile
- **Gift buyers** looking for a useful, non-gimmicky food gift for someone who loves cooking

Skip it if you're a serious chili extremist seeking maximum heat, or if you're unwilling to deal with the stirring ritual every single time.

**FAQ**

**How long do the jars last after opening?** Refrigerated, about 3-4 months. The oil preserves the chilies, but the garlic loses its crunch after about 6 weeks. Use it regularly.

**Is this gluten-free?** Yes — the base ingredients are oil, chilies, garlic, and Sichuan peppercorns. No soy sauce or wheat derivatives.

**Can I cook with this or is it just a condiment?** Both. It's excellent stirred into stir-fries at the end of cooking, but the crispy texture is best preserved when added after cooking.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want authentic Sichuan flavor with three heat options in one package — this bundle delivers genuine mala numbing, clean ingredients, and enough volume to experiment freely.

**Skip it if** you hate stirring your condiments or you need face-melting heat.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Deducting points for the oil separation annoyance and the misleadingly named "Spicy" variety. The flavor is excellent, the value is fair, and the bundle concept is genuinely useful. Just budget 30 seconds of stirring per use.
