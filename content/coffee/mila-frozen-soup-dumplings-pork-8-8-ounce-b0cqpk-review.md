If you’ve ever paid $12 for six soup dumplings at a dim sum house and wished you could replicate that at home, Mila’s frozen pork soup dumplings are the closest thing I’ve found short of flying to Shanghai. But they’re not perfect — and here’s the honest breakdown before you hit add to cart.

**What’s actually in the box**

The 8.8-ounce package contains roughly 10 frozen dumplings, each about 1.5 inches across. You also get a sheet of parchment liners — a thoughtful inclusion that most competitors skip. No dipping sauce included, so plan to make your own (black vinegar and julienned ginger is the classic move). The dumplings arrive individually frozen, not stuck in a clump, which means you can steam only what you need.

**Build quality and the wrapper test**

The wrapper is the make-or-break for soup dumplings. Too thick and you’re eating pasta; too thin and the broth escapes before it hits your mouth. Mila nails this balance. The dough is rolled to about 2mm — thin enough to see the broth shimmer through but sturdy enough to survive 8 minutes in a bamboo steamer. I tested two batches: one steamed per package instructions (8 minutes over boiling water), one nuked in the microwave (don’t do this — see below). The steamed batch held up perfectly. Every single dumpling had intact soup when I bit into it.

**Flavor profile: where they shine**

The filling is a coarsely ground pork blend with a pronounced ginger kick and a hint of scallion. It’s not as delicate as Din Tai Fung’s — the pork is more forward, less subtly seasoned — but it’s far more flavorful than Trader Joe’s version, which tends toward blandness. The broth is the real star: rich, gelatinous, and deeply savory, it coats your tongue the way a proper xiao long bao should. You’ll get a good 2-3 teaspoons of soup per dumpling.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 215 ratings, the recurring theme is “surprisingly close to restaurant quality.” One reviewer wrote: *“I’ve tried every frozen soup dumpling at my local Asian market and these beat them all. The wrapper doesn’t fall apart and the soup actually tastes like it’s been simmered for hours.”* Another noted: *“The ginger flavor is spot-on — not overpowering, but you know it’s there.”* Multiple customers mentioned the parchment liners as a deciding factor versus other brands.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest issue is value. At roughly $10 for 10 dumplings, you’re paying $1 per piece — that’s restaurant pricing. You can get a 50-ounce bag of frozen soup dumplings at Costco for less per ounce, though the quality isn’t as good. The second issue is preparation sensitivity. If you steam them even 60 seconds too long, the wrappers start to split and you lose the soup. And whatever you do, don’t microwave these — the broth boils over and the dough turns gummy. I tried it so you don’t have to.

**Who should buy this**

- **Weeknight dumpling cravings:** If you want restaurant-quality soup dumplings at home without the 45-minute drive to your nearest dim sum spot, these are your answer.
- **Soup dumpling snobs:** If you already know the difference between a 12-fold and 18-fold pleat, you’ll appreciate the craftsmanship here, even if it’s not perfect.
- **Skip it if:** You’re feeding a family of four — you’ll need three boxes and a second mortgage. Or if you prefer a sweeter, milder filling; Mila’s ginger-forward profile isn’t for everyone.

**FAQ: Three real questions shoppers ask**

**Can I cook these in an air fryer?**  
Technically yes, but you’ll lose the soup. The hot air dries out the wrapper and the broth evaporates. Stick to a steamer basket or a pot with a metal colander — it’s worth the extra 3 minutes.

**Are these gluten-free?**  
No. The wrappers are made with wheat flour. If you’re celiac, skip these entirely.

**How many dumplings are actually in the box?**  
The package says 8.8 ounces, which translates to about 10 dumplings. Some reviewers reported 9, some 11 — it varies slightly by batch, so don’t plan your dinner around an exact count.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** You value taste over bulk, you’re willing to follow steaming instructions precisely, and you want a genuinely impressive appetizer or light meal for one or two people.

**Skip it if:** You’re on a budget, you want a freezer staple for the whole family, or you can’t be bothered with stovetop steaming — this isn’t a microwave meal, and treating it like one will ruin it.

**Rating:** 4.2/5 — Mila delivers exceptional flavor and texture for a frozen product, but the price per dumpling and the finicky preparation keep it from a perfect score. Worth it for the right occasion, but keep your expectations calibrated.
