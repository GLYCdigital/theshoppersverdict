Let me be straight with you: most grocery-store flour tortillas taste like cardboard wrapped in plastic. The Vista Hermosa 8-count, 12-ounce pack is not that. These are the real deal — the kind of tortillas you'd get at a family-run taqueria, not the factory-extruded discs that crack when you look at them wrong.

**What you're actually getting**

One sealed bag, eight tortillas, 12 total ounces. That works out to 1.5 ounces per tortilla, which is noticeably thicker and heavier than the standard 8-inch Mission or Guerrero you'll find on the shelf. As shown in the product image, these are pale, slightly mottled, and look hand-stretched rather than die-cut. That's a good sign.

The ingredient list tells the story: flour, water, lard, salt, and a touch of baking powder. No preservatives, no dough conditioners, no sugar-heavy sweeteners. The lard is the key — it's what gives these the rich, savory depth you can't fake with vegetable shortening.

**Real-world performance**

I've used these for everything from breakfast burritos to enchiladas, and they hold up. The thickness means they don't tear when you load them up with filling, and they char beautifully on a comal or dry skillet — you get those dark, blistered spots that actually taste like something. They're also forgiving in the microwave: 20 seconds wrapped in a damp paper towel restores them to near-fresh pliability.

One consistent theme in the 235 Amazon ratings is texture. Buyers repeatedly mention how soft these stay over time. One reviewer wrote, "I've bought these three times now and they stay flexible even after a week in the fridge — my kids use them for lunch wraps with zero cracking." Another said, "Tastes like my abuela's tortillas, not the rubbery ones from the chain store."

The 4.7-star average is earned — but not perfect.

**Where they fall short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. Eight tortillas for roughly the same price as a 10-count of mainstream brands means you're paying a premium per tortilla. For a family that goes through a pack every two days, that adds up.

The lard is a double-edged sword. It's what makes these taste authentic, but it also means vegetarians are out of luck and the saturated fat content is higher than oil-based competitors. If you're watching that, check the nutrition panel before committing.

Finally, these are not the giant 12-inch burrito size. They're medium — ideal for tacos, quesadillas, and smaller wraps. If you're planning massive Mission-style burritos, you'll need to double up or look elsewhere.

**Who should buy these**

- **Home cooks chasing authenticity** — if you want your homemade tacos to taste like they came from a real taqueria, these are your move.
- **Tortilla snobs** — you'll notice the difference immediately compared to standard supermarket brands.
- **Meal preppers** — the staying power means you can prep wraps on Sunday and eat them Friday without disappointment.

**Skip them if** you're on a tight budget, need vegetarian-friendly tortillas, or you just need a giant tortilla for massive burritos.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these raw or pre-cooked?**
A: Fully cooked and ready to eat. Heat them briefly on a skillet for the best texture, but they're fine straight from the bag.

**Q: How should I store them?**
A: Keep them in the sealed bag at room temperature for up to a week. For longer storage, freeze them with parchment paper between each tortilla — they thaw beautifully.

**Q: Do they contain preservatives?**
A: No. That's why the shelf life is shorter than mass-market brands. Use them within a week of opening.
