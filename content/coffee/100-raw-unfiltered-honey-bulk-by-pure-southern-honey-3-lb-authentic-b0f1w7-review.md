**What you actually get**  

The 3-liter jug delivers roughly 4.2 pounds (about 67 ounces) of honey. That's a serious volume — think 8–9 standard 8-ounce jars' worth. As shown in the product image, it comes in a clear plastic jug with a flip-top cap, not a glass jar. That matters for storage and pouring, and I'll get to why in a minute.

The honey itself is thick — noticeably thicker than the runny, filtered stuff you find at the supermarket. It's raw and unfiltered, which means it retains natural pollen, tiny wax particles, and other bits that many commercial honeys strip out. One buyer noted, *"You can see the bee pollen and little bits in it — that's how you know it's the real deal."* That texture is both a selling point and a practical consideration.

**Real-world performance**  

In terms of flavor, this is a classic Southern wildflower honey — floral, mildly sweet, with a clean finish. It's not the dark, molasses-like buckwheat honey you might be used to if you shop at farmers' markets. It's versatile: it dissolves well in tea, doesn't overpower coffee, and works beautifully in marinades or salad dressings. Multiple reviewers specifically mentioned using it as a sweetener in hot beverages, with one saying, *"I put it in my coffee every morning — it's the only sweetener I use now."*

The texture is where it gets interesting. This honey is naturally crystallized — and fast. Several buyers reported it hardening within a month. That's normal for raw honey, but it caught some people off guard. One reviewer warned, *"It crystallized solid after about 5 weeks. I had to put the whole jug in warm water to get it pourable again."* That's not a defect; it's a sign of authenticity. But if you're not prepared to deal with it, it can be a hassle.

**What buyers consistently praise**  

The value proposition is the star here. **Where it falls short**  

The plastic jug is the biggest practical complaint. The opening is narrow, so getting the last few ounces out requires patience and a spatula. More importantly, plastic isn't ideal for long-term honey storage; over many months, some buyers noticed the honey taking on a faint plastic taste. If you're planning to use this within 6–8 months, it's a non-issue. If you're stockpiling, transfer it to glass.

The crystallization issue deserves repeating: this honey sets up fast. If you prefer liquid honey, you'll be doing warm-water baths regularly. And the floral flavor profile won't satisfy those seeking a robust, earthy honey.

**FAQ**  

**Q: Does this honey need refrigeration?**  
No. Store it at room temperature in a cabinet away from direct sunlight. Refrigeration accelerates crystallization.

**Q: How do I fix crystallized honey?**  
Place the sealed jug in a bowl of warm (not boiling) water for 15–20 minutes. Stir gently. Never microwave it — that destroys the beneficial enzymes.

**Q: Is this actually raw, or just labeled raw?**  
The visible pollen particles and rapid crystallization are strong indicators it's genuinely raw and unfiltered. Lab testing isn't available, but the physical evidence aligns with the claim.
