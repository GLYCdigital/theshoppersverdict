Here's the thing about McCormick Mayonesa with Lime Juice: it's not trying to be your everyday sandwich mayo. It's trying to be the secret weapon in your fridge—the one you pull out when regular mayo just feels boring.

I've tested this against Hellmann's, Duke's, and a few Latin-brand mayos, and here's what you actually need to know.

## What You're Getting

One 30-ounce jar of creamy mayonnaise infused with lime juice. The packaging is straightforward—a sturdy plastic jar with a clear label showing the lime branding. No fancy squeeze bottle, no pump. Just a twist-off lid and a product that delivers on its core promise.

## The Real-World Performance

The texture is where this mayo surprises. It's noticeably thicker than Hellmann's, with a consistency closer to aioli or Mexican-style crema. That thickness matters—it clings to sandwiches better, doesn't soak into bread as fast, and makes a killer base for dips.

The lime flavor is present but restrained. It's not a zesty, in-your-face citrus bomb. Instead, it's a background note that brightens the mayo's richness. If you're expecting something like a citrus aioli from a fancy restaurant, adjust your expectations. This is more like a good-quality mayo with a gentle squeeze of lime worked in.

I tested it three ways: on a turkey sandwich, as a tuna salad binder, and as a marinade for grilled chicken. The sandwich was where it shined—the lime cut through the richness without overpowering the turkey. The tuna salad worked too, though the lime competed with the tuna's flavor. The chicken marinade was the winner: mix it with some garlic and cumin, let it sit for an hour, and you get a tangy, juicy result.

## What Buyers Are Saying

With 220 ratings and a 4.4 average, the feedback is consistent. One reviewer put it well: "This is the mayo I grew up with in Mexico. It's creamier and has a fresher taste than American mayo." ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

The high-fructose corn syrup is real. If you're strict about avoiding it, this isn't your mayo. That's a legitimate reason to pass.

The lime flavor, while pleasant, won't satisfy anyone looking for a strong citrus kick. You'll taste it, but you won't be blown away. If you want a bolder lime presence, squeeze fresh lime over your sandwich after spreading this—it works, but it's an extra step.

And the value proposition is tricky. At the current price point, you're paying a premium for a flavored mayo when a standard jar of Hellmann's plus a fresh lime would cost less. The convenience is real, but the math doesn't always favor this product.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as real Mexican mayonnaise?**
It's inspired by it, but not identical. Mexican-style mayos tend to be tangier and slightly thinner. This is thicker and sweeter, but it's closer to that style than standard American mayo.

**Can I use this in place of regular mayo in recipes?**
Yes, but expect the lime flavor to show up. It works great in potato salad, coleslaw, and any recipe where you'd welcome a citrus note. Avoid it in recipes where you need neutral mayo flavor.

**How long does it last after opening?**
Standard mayo shelf life applies—about 2 months refrigerated. The lime doesn't affect the expiration timeline.
