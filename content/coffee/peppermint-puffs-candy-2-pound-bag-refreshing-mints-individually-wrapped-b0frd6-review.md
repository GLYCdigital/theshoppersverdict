Let’s be clear about something upfront: these are *not* your grandmother’s hard peppermint candies. Peppermint Puffs are soft, chewy little pillows of mint — think marshmallow-adjacent with a serious cooling kick. That distinction matters, because half the disappointment in the reviews comes from people expecting a crunchy candy and getting something entirely different.

The two-pound bag is a bulk purchase, plain and simple. You’re getting a heavy-duty resealable pouch packed with individually wrapped pieces — roughly 200+ candies depending on size variance. As shown in the product image, the puffs are pale white, pillow-shaped, and dusted lightly with powdered sugar to prevent sticking. Each one is twisted in clear plastic wrap, which makes them practical for tossing in a bag or keeping on a desk without a dish.

**Flavor and texture: the real story**

The peppermint here is legit. It uses real peppermint oil, and you taste it immediately — a clean, icy burst that clears your sinuses without the medicinal bite of some cheap mints. The sweetness is moderate; it’s not cloying, and the mint lingers pleasantly for a few minutes after the candy dissolves.

Texture is where things get divisive. The puffs are soft and slightly spongy, dissolving slowly rather than shattering. One reviewer put it well: *“They’re like little mint clouds — but if you leave them in a hot car, they turn into one giant mint blob.”* That’s the tradeoff. In cool conditions, they’re delightful. In heat or humidity, they get tacky and can stick to your teeth. Keep the bag sealed and stored in a pantry, not a glovebox.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The bulk value is the standout. At roughly 2 pounds, this bag outlasts a standard tin of mints by a mile. Reviewers frequently mention using them for office candy bowls, church pews, and road trips. One buyer noted: *“I put these in a dish at my front desk and they disappear — but the bag keeps up for a month.”* The individual wrapping is also a consistent plus; no sticky fingers, and they’re easy to share without touching.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the texture surprise, there are two legitimate complaints. First, the puffs can be inconsistent in size — some are noticeably larger than others, which matters if you’re portioning them out. Second, the powdered sugar coating can leave a faint residue on your fingers, and if you’re someone who eats mints frequently, that gets old. Also, a few reviewers mentioned that the bag’s “resealable” zipper isn’t particularly sturdy; after a few weeks of use, it may not seal fully, so transfer to a jar if you’re storing long-term.

**Who should buy this**

- **Office managers and receptionists** who need a high-volume, individually wrapped candy that won’t melt into a mess on a desk.
- **Peppermint purists** who prefer authentic oil flavor over artificial mint extract.
- **Road trippers** who want a stash that lasts the whole drive without needing a refill.

**Skip it if** you expect hard, crackable candy — these are soft, and that’s not changing. Also skip if you live in a hot, humid climate without climate-controlled storage.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these gluten-free?**  
A: Yes, the ingredients are sugar, corn syrup, peppermint oil, and powdered sugar — no wheat or gluten derivatives. That said, check the label on your specific batch for allergen statements.

**Q: How long does the bag last?**  
A: Realistically, 3–6 weeks with daily use of a few pieces. If you’re handing them out to a crowd, it’ll go faster, but it’s still the best per-piece value in the mint category.

**Q: Can I use them for baking or hot drinks?**  
A: Technically, you can crush them for peppermint bark or stir into hot cocoa, but they’re soft, so they won’t crush into a fine powder like hard candies. You’ll get chunks, not dust.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a big, affordable stash of genuinely minty, individually wrapped candies — and you’re okay with a soft, chewy texture. **Skip it if** you’re set on hard candies or don’t have a cool place to store them.

Rating: 4.2/5 — the flavor and value earn the score; the texture surprise and sticky-in-heat issue cost it a half-star.
