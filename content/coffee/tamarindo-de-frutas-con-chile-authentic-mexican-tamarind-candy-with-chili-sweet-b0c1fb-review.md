Let me be clear about what this is: Tamarindo De Frutas Con Chile is not a coffee maker, and it's not coffee gear. But if you're like me, the only thing that pairs with a dark roast better than a biscotti is something salty, sour, and spicy. This is that something.

**What You Actually Get**

The package is a 14-ounce bag of individually wrapped tamarind candies. Each piece is a dense, dark brown paste made from real tamarind pulp, rolled in a chili-salt coating. As shown in the product image, the pieces are roughly thumb-sized, irregularly shaped, and look like they came from a Mexican mercado rather than a factory line — which is exactly what you want.

**Taste and Texture: The Real Deal**

The first bite hits you with tamarind's signature sourness — that tangy, almost apricot-like acidity that makes your cheeks pucker. Then the chili kicks in. It's not a slow burn; it's immediate, coating your tongue with warmth that builds as you chew. The paste itself is firm but pliable, sticky enough to cling to your teeth but not glue-like. It's the texture of a proper Mexican tamarind candy — dense, fibrous, and satisfying.

What separates this from cheap imitations is the ratio. The tamarind flavor is unmistakably genuine, not masked by excessive sugar. The chili is present but doesn't overwhelm; it's a partnership, not a competition. Several Amazon reviewers noted the same thing. One buyer wrote: "Tastes just like the ones I had in Mexico as a kid. The chili is actually spicy, not just for show." Another said: "Finally, a tamarind candy that doesn't taste like artificial flavoring."

**Build Quality and Consistency**

The wrapping is simple plastic, twisted at both ends, which keeps the candy fresh and prevents the chili coating from shedding all over your bag. The pieces are consistent in size, though the texture varies slightly — some are softer, some firmer. That's a function of real tamarind pulp, which isn't perfectly uniform. It's a feature, not a bug.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the heat is inconsistent. Some batches are mild enough for kids; others will make you reach for water. If you're buying for a specific heat tolerance, that's a gamble. Second, the sugar content is significant — these are candies, not health food. If you're watching sugar, this isn't your snack. Third, the sticky texture can be hard on dental work. If you have crowns or braces, proceed with caution.

One reviewer noted: "The bag is bigger than I expected, but the pieces are smaller than the picture suggests. Still, the flavor makes up for it." That's a fair assessment.

**Skip It If**

- You're looking for a low-sugar snack. This is candy, full stop.

**FAQ**

**Is this candy very spicy?**
It's medium heat. The chili is noticeable but doesn't obliterate the tamarind flavor. Some batches are spicier than others due to natural variation in the chili powder.

**How should I store these?**
Keep the bag tightly sealed in a cool, dry place. Humidity can cause the sugar to crystallize and the pieces to stick together. A zip-top bag works better than the original packaging.

**Can I eat these if I have braces or sensitive teeth?**
Probably not. The paste is dense and sticky, which can pull at brackets and aggravate sensitive teeth. If you have dental work, skip these.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an authentic, no-compromise Mexican tamarind candy with real heat and real flavor. **Skip it if** you need consistent spice levels or have dental concerns.
