You can get about 40 glasses of Arnold Palmer–style refreshment from this single 19-ounce canister. That's the headline. The question is whether the powdered shortcut delivers flavor worth the trade-off versus grabbing bottles at the convenience store.

I've tested this Country Time mix against both bottled half-and-half and homemade versions. Here's what you need to know before adding it to your cart.

## What's in the Canister

The 19-ounce plastic canister contains a fine orange-tinted powder. One scoop (about 2 tablespoons) mixes with 8 ounces of water. The label claims 40 servings per container, and that math checks out—you're getting roughly 10 quarts of finished drink.

Ingredients list sugar as the primary sweetener, followed by instant tea, citric acid, natural flavors, and sucralose. The blend uses both real sugar and artificial sweetener to hit the flavor profile without needing double the powder.

## Real-World Performance

Mixability is where this product earns its keep. Stir it into ice-cold water straight from the tap and the powder dissolves almost immediately—no clumps, no gritty film on top. I tried it with a spoon, a straw, and a shaker bottle; all three worked fine. That's genuinely better than some competing powdered drink mixes I've tested that require warm water or vigorous blending.

The flavor lands somewhere between sweet tea and lemonade. The tea note is present but not assertive—this is not a drink for tea purists. The lemonade side carries more weight, giving it that bright, tangy finish. It's noticeably less syrupy than bottled versions, which makes it more refreshing for day-long sipping.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Amazon reviewers with 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars highlight two things repeatedly: value and convenience. One verified buyer puts it plainly: *"Tastes just like the bottled version but costs half as much. I keep one at work and one at home."* Another notes, *"My kids drink this instead of soda now. Mixes in seconds and no sugar crash complaints."*

## Where It Falls Short

The sucralose aftertaste is real. If you're sensitive to artificial sweeteners, you'll notice it in the first sip. It fades as the drink chills, and the sugar helps mask it, but it's there. Several reviewers mention this exact issue, with one saying, *"I wish they'd just use more sugar and skip the sucralose entirely."*

The instant tea powder also means you won't get the complexity of brewed tea. It's one-dimensional compared to steeping real tea bags and adding your own lemon juice. For a quick fix, that's fine. For a backyard barbecue where people actually taste their drinks, it's a compromise.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Families with kids** who go through gallons of flavored drinks weekly—this replaces bottled purchases at significant savings
- **Campers and RV owners** who want shelf-stable drink options without hauling heavy bottles
- **Office workers** who want a zero-effort cold drink that doesn't require a trip to the break room vending machine

**Skip it if** you're a tea snob who can taste the difference between instant and brewed, or if artificial sweeteners give you headaches.

**Does it need hot water to dissolve?**
