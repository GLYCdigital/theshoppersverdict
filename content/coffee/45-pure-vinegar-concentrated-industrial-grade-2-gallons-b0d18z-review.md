## Where It Falls Short

Let's be blunt about the risks. This is industrial-strength acid. Undiluted, it will chemically burn skin and can damage rubber gaskets, silicone hoses, and aluminum components in your machine. You **must** wear gloves and eye protection when handling the concentrate. You **must** rinse your equipment thoroughly afterward — residual acetic acid at this strength will corrode metal over time.

Also, the lack of any measuring tool means you're guessing the first time. The math isn't hard — 1 cup concentrate to 8 cups water for a standard batch — but beginners may find a pre-mixed descaling solution less intimidating.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I use this directly in my coffee maker without diluting?**
Absolutely not. At 45% concentration, it will damage seals, plastics, and metal components. Always dilute to at least 1:8 before use.

**Q: How do I know the dilution is right?**
For descaling, mix 1 part vinegar to 8 parts water. For light maintenance cleaning, use 1:10. You'll smell it clearly at either ratio.

**Q: Will this void my espresso machine warranty?**
Most manufacturers require descaling but don't specify the product. However, damage from improper use isn't covered. Dilute properly and rinse thoroughly and you're fine.
