Let me be blunt: most low-carb tortillas taste like pressed cardboard with ambition. The Mr. Tortilla Big Big Burrito Wraps are different — but they're not perfect. Here's what you need to know before spending your money.

**What You're Actually Getting**

Each package contains 4 or 8 oversized wraps (depending on the listing you choose), each measuring a full 12-13 inches across. As shown in the product image, these are legitimately burrito-sized — not the sad 8-inch imposters that claim to be "big." The small-batch artisan claim isn't marketing fluff either; the texture suggests real dough handling rather than industrial pressing.

The nutritional profile is the main event: 45 calories per wrap, 5g net carbs, and 9g of fiber. For anyone tracking macros, that's a game-changer. You can build a loaded burrito weighing over a pound and still stay under 10g net carbs for the entire meal.

**Performance and Build Quality**

I've tested these three ways: cold wraps straight from the fridge, warmed on a dry skillet, and microwaved. The results varied significantly.

Cold, these are surprisingly pliable. No cracking, no splitting at the edges when you roll tight. That's rare even among premium tortillas. Warm, they get even more forgiving — I folded a double-bean burrito with rice, cheese, and salsa without a single tear. That said, they're thin. Not fragile-thin, but you'll notice they don't have the substantial chew of a fresh flour tortilla from a taqueria.

The flavor is where things get interesting. The flour version is mild to the point of neutrality — it works as a vehicle but doesn't add anything. The multigrain and sun-dried tomato versions have more character. If you're choosing, the corn-based options taste notably better but are slightly less flexible.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews tells a consistent story. One verified buyer said, "Finally a tortilla that doesn't fall apart when I load it up. I've been keto for two years and these are the first wraps I actually look forward to eating." Another noted, "The size is real. I measured one at 12.5 inches. My old 'big' tortillas look like cocktail napkins next to these."

The most common praise centers on the size-to-carb ratio. Nobody else is making a 13-inch tortilla with 5g net carbs at this quality level.

**Where It Falls Short**

The packaging is the weak link. Multiple reviewers — and I agree — report the resealable bag doesn't seal properly after the first open. Wraps exposed to air develop dry, cracked edges within 2-3 days. You'll want to transfer them to a zip-top bag or use them quickly.

The price is the other issue. At roughly $1.50-2.00 per wrap depending on the listing, these are 3-4x the cost of standard flour tortillas. If you're not strict about carbs, you're paying a premium for a problem you don't have.

**Who Should Buy These**

This product is for two specific buyers:

1. **The serious low-carb eater** — if you're keto or diabetic and miss real burritos, these are worth every penny. The size and flexibility make them a legit substitute, not a compromise.

2. **The meal-prepper** — the 13-inch size is perfect for batch-making frozen breakfast burritos. They hold up to reheating better than most wraps.

**Skip them if** you're only mildly curious about low-carb options, or if you're feeding a family where carbs aren't a concern. Standard tortillas are cheaper and taste more traditional.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these really taste like regular tortillas?**
A: No, and anyone who says otherwise is lying. They're close — much closer than the competition — but there's a subtle textural difference and a slightly earthy aftertaste. Warm them on a dry skillet for 30 seconds per side; it makes a noticeable difference.

**Q: Can I freeze these?**
A: Yes. They freeze well for up to 3 months. Separate with parchment paper between each wrap so they don't stick together.

**Q: What's the actual carb count per wrap?**
A: The label shows 9g total carbs and 4g dietary fiber, putting net carbs at 5g per tortilla. The serving size is one wrap.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're committed to low-carb eating but refuse to give up proper burritos. The size, texture, and macro profile make these the best option I've tested in this category.

**Skip it if:** You're not carb-conscious, or you're hoping for an exact flour tortilla dupe. This isn't that — it's better for what it is, but it's not the same thing.
