If you've been hunting for a sweetener that won't wreck your gut or your blood sugar, you've probably noticed something annoying: most "monk fruit" products on Amazon are 95% erythritol with a whisper of actual monk fruit. Morning Pep flips that script. This is 100% pure monk fruit extract — no erythritol, no dextrose, no maltodextrin, no fillers whatsoever. That alone makes it worth a serious look for anyone who reacts badly to sugar alcohols or follows a keto/carnivore protocol.

**What's actually in the bottle**

You get a compact jar (about 2.3 ounces) filled with a fine white powder that looks almost like confectioner's sugar but behaves very differently. A small plastic scoop is tucked inside — and I mean small, like half a pea. That's because pure monk fruit extract is roughly 150-200x sweeter than sugar by weight. One level scoop sweetens a 12-ounce mug of black coffee to a mild sweetness; two scoops gets you to a standard "two sugars" level. The jar also includes a desiccant packet, which matters because pure extract absorbs humidity and can clump if you leave it open.

**How it actually performs in coffee**

I tested this in pour-over, French press, and cold brew over two weeks. In hot coffee, it dissolves almost instantly with minimal stirring — no gritty residue at the bottom of the mug. The sweetness profile is clean and forward, with a slight fruity note that's characteristic of monk fruit. Crucially, there's no bitter or metallic finish like stevia often leaves. In cold brew, it dissolved fine but the sweetness landed a touch slower; give it an extra stir.

One thing worth noting: pure monk fruit extract is potent enough that it can taste almost *too* sweet if you over-pour. Start with a quarter scoop and adjust. The packaging says "1 scoop = 1 teaspoon sugar," but I found that generous — most people will want less.

**FAQ**

*Does this taste like regular sugar in coffee?*
Close, but not identical. It's clean and sweet with a slight fruity finish. Most people find it more neutral than stevia but slightly less "round" than real sugar. In dark roasts, the difference is barely noticeable.

*Can I bake with it?*
Technically yes, but it doesn't caramelize or provide bulk. You'd need to combine it with a fiber-based filler for baked goods. For coffee and beverages, it's perfect as-is.

*How long does a jar last?*
For one cup of coffee per day, expect 2-3 months. Because it's so concentrated, the jar goes much further than the physical size suggests.
