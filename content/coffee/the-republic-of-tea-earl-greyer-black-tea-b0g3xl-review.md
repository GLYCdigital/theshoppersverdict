Some teas are polite. This one has opinions. The Republic of Tea's Earl Greyer leans into its name — it's a bolder, more caffeinated take on the classic Earl Grey, and it's been earning 4.7 stars from 235 Amazon reviewers for good reason.

**What's in the tin**

You're getting a 50-count tin of whole-leaf black tea bags, each individually wrapped in foil. The packaging matters more than you'd think: the tin seals tightly, and the foil wrappers preserve the bergamot oil that gives Earl Grey its signature citrus punch. If you've ever had a box of Earl Grey go stale in three weeks, you understand why this matters.

The blend itself uses full-leaf Ceylon and Assam black teas — not the fannings and dust you'll find in most grocery-store bags. That's the difference between a tea that tastes like hot water with perfume and one that actually has body.

**Performance and real-world use**

Steep it for 3 minutes and you get a smooth, malty base with a bright bergamot top note. Push it to 4-5 minutes and the caffeine kicks in noticeably — this is marketed as having double the caffeine of regular black tea, and I'll confirm the effect is real. It's not jittery like cheap energy drinks; it's more like the clean alertness you'd expect from a strong cup of coffee, which makes it a legit coffee alternative for the morning crowd.

The flavor holds up to milk and sweetener without collapsing into bitterness. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "Finally an Earl Grey that actually tastes like bergamot instead of a whisper of it. I drink it black and it's smooth, not astringent." Another noted, "I've tried every Earl Grey on Amazon. This is the only one I reorder."

**Where it falls short**

It's not perfect. The bergamot is assertive — if you prefer a subtle, delicate Earl Grey, this will feel aggressive. Steep it too long and the citrus turns slightly medicinal. And you'll pay a premium over Twinings or Bigelow: roughly $12–$15 for 50 bags, depending on the current listing price. For daily drinking, that adds up.

The bags are also untethered — no strings or tags. If you're used to dangling a tag over your mug, you'll either adapt or dig out a spoon. Minor, but worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

- **Coffee drinkers looking to cut back**: The double-caffeine formula makes this a genuinely satisfying swap.
- **Earl Grey purists**: If you want bergamot front and center, this delivers.
- **Gift buyers**: The tin looks sharp on a counter and the quality justifies the price.

**Skip it if** you prefer delicate teas, or if you're on a strict budget and just need something to dunk in the morning.

**FAQ**

*How many times can you resteep the bags?*  
Twice, realistically. The first cup is bold, the second is lighter but still flavorful. The third is weak tea water — toss it.

*Is this actually double-caffeinated?*  
Republic of Tea doesn't publish lab numbers, but the blend uses high-caffeine Assam and the effect is noticeable. If you're sensitive to caffeine, drink it before noon.

*Does it contain real bergamot or artificial flavoring?*  
Real bergamot oil. That's why the aroma is so vivid and why the foil wrappers are necessary.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a bold, citrus-forward Earl Grey that doubles as a coffee replacement. **Skip it if** you're loyal to mild, budget-friendly tea bags.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a premium tea that justifies its price with quality leaves, real bergamot, and a genuinely effective caffeine kick. Just watch your steep time.
