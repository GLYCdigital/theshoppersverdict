Let me be direct: I've reviewed dozens of "premium" imported candies that turned out to be repackaged bulk sweets with fancy labels. The Perle di Sole Italian fruit candies aren't that. These are the real deal — a legitimately Italian product that delivers on its promise of authentic fruit flavor. But they're also not perfect, and the texture debate is real.

## What You're Actually Getting

The 500-gram bag (about 1.1 pounds) contains individually wrapped fruit-flavored candies in a mix of lemon, orange, strawberry, and other Mediterranean fruit varieties. As shown in the product image, each piece has that classic pearlescent sheen that gives the brand its name. The wrappers twist-seal individually, which makes them ideal for tossing in a purse, desk drawer, or glove compartment without creating a sticky mess.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: sugar, glucose syrup, fruit juice concentrates, natural flavors, and citric acid. No artificial colors — the pastel shades come from the actual fruit content. That's a meaningful distinction from mass-market fruit candies that rely on food coloring and synthetic flavoring.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested these over three weeks, keeping a bag on my desk and another in my car. The flavor profile is where Perle di Sole earns its keep. The lemon tastes like lemon zest, not Lemonhead. The strawberry is subtle and floral, closer to a real berry than the candy-shop version most Americans grew up with. That's the Italian approach — restraint over intensity.

The texture, however, is the dividing line. These are chewy-soft candies with a light sugar shell, not hard candies. If you're expecting something you can crunch, you'll be disappointed. The chew is pleasant — think a softer version of a fruit chew — but it does stick to teeth slightly. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "Soft and delicious, but I had to adjust my expectations. These aren't hard candies." Another praised them for being "gentle on my fillings and crowns — I can actually enjoy these."

The 4.4-star average across 220 ratings reflects this split. Most buyers love the authentic taste, but a vocal minority expected a different texture entirely.

## Where It Falls Short

The sugar coating is both a feature and a flaw. In warm conditions — my car hit about 85°F one afternoon — the candies became noticeably tacky, and a few wrappers stuck to the candy itself. Plan to store these in a cool place.

The bag is also not resealable. It's a simple heat-sealed pouch, so if you're not finishing it in a couple of weeks, transfer the candies to an airtight container. The individually wrapped pieces protect them, but the bag itself won't.

Finally, the flavor variety is blended in the bag without a way to identify which is which before unwrapping. If you're picky about flavors, you'll find yourself checking wrappers. Some buyers mentioned wishing the flavors were separated or labeled.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You appreciate authentic European confectionery, want a candy that tastes like actual fruit rather than artificial flavoring, or need individually wrapped sweets that are gentle on dental work. They're also an excellent gift for anyone who's traveled to Italy and misses the real thing.

**Skip these if:** You prefer hard candies, want intense American-style fruit flavors, or need a resealable package for long-term storage.
