**Review Body**

Let’s cut the chase: You already know what Goldfish are. The question is whether the **Old Bay Seasoned** variant is a novelty gag or a legit pantry staple. I’ve tested the 27.3-ounce bulk carton for a week—here’s what you need to know before you add it to your cart.

**What You’re Actually Getting**

This is the big carton—27.3 ounces, which is roughly the size of a small cereal box. Inside, you get the classic fish-shaped crackers, but the bag is sealed with a heavy dose of Old Bay’s signature blend: celery salt, paprika, black pepper, and a hint of cayenne. The smell hits you the second you tear open the bag. It’s not subtle. The seasoning is visibly speckled on every cracker, which is a good sign—many flavored crackers skim on the powder. This one doesn’t.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

If you’ve had standard Goldfish, you know they’re light and airy. The Old Bay version keeps that crunch but adds a serious savory kick. I ate them straight out of the bag, crushed them over clam chowder, and even tried them with cream cheese dip. The cracker holds its structure well; it doesn’t get soggy quickly, and the seasoning doesn’t rub off on your fingers as aggressively as you’d expect. That’s a win for movie-night snacking.

The heat level is moderate—think “zesty,” not “Sriracha.” The celery salt dominates, which is classic Old Bay. If you’re a Maryland native or a seafood-boil enthusiast, you’ll feel right at home. If you’re expecting a mild cheddar-like snack, this will surprise you.

**What Buyers Are Saying**

With 229 ratings and a 4.6-star average, the consensus is overwhelmingly positive. One buyer wrote, *“I bought these for a crab boil and they disappeared in ten minutes. The seasoning is generous, not like other brands that just dust the crackers.”* Another noted, *“Great crunch, strong flavor. My only complaint is I wish they came in a resealable bag—the carton is fine, but you need a clip.”* That matches my experience: the carton is sturdy, but the inner bag isn’t zip-locked. Use a clip or transfer to a container.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honestly, the flaws are minor but real. First, the seasoning distribution isn’t perfectly uniform. The bag I tested had a few clusters of crackers that were noticeably heavier on the spice—good for some, but if you’re serving kids, they might hit a hot pocket. Second, the Old Bay flavor is polarizing. If you don’t like celery salt or paprika-heavy blends, you’ll find this overwhelming. It’s not a “neutral” snack. Third, the 27.3-ounce size is great for parties but hard to store in a standard pantry. It’s a big box.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The Host/Hostess:** If you’re throwing a party, this carton is a crowd-pleaser for adults. Serve it with beer or Bloody Marys.
- **The Seafood Lover:** If you put Old Bay on everything from shrimp to fries, this is a no-brainer.
- **The Snack Adventurer:** If you’re tired of plain cheddar and want a bold flavor shift, this delivers.

**Skip It If:**

- You’re buying for a kids’ lunchbox—the spice level might be too much for young palates.
- You’re a purist who thinks Goldfish should only taste like cheddar or pizza.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these spicy?**
A: They have a mild cayenne kick, but the dominant flavor is celery salt and paprika. Think “flavorful” rather than “hot.” If you’re sensitive to spice, you’ll still be fine.

**Q: Do they taste like actual seafood?**
A: No—there’s no fish flavor. Old Bay is a spice blend, so you get the seasoning profile, not the crab. It works well as a stand-in for a seafood snack without the fishy aftertaste.

**Q: Can I freeze them?**
A: Yes, but I wouldn’t recommend it. The texture can change slightly when thawed. Better to store them in an airtight container at room temperature—they stayed crunchy for me for over a week.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a bold, savory snack that stands out on a snack table or pairs perfectly with a cold beer. The 27.3-ounce value is solid, and the flavor is genuinely good—not a gimmick.

**Skip it if:** You’re looking for a mild, kid-friendly cracker or if you dislike Old Bay’s distinctive celery-salt-forward profile.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It loses a half-star only for the inconsistent seasoning distribution and the lack of a resealable bag. Otherwise, this is one of the best flavored-cracker launches in recent memory.
