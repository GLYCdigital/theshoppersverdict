**Bottom line:** BODYARMOR LYTE Orange Clementine is the smart pick for anyone who wants real hydration without drinking liquid candy. It's not perfect — the Stevia finish is a real thing — but at 20 calories with 500mg of electrolytes, it beats most of what's on the shelf.

## What you're actually getting

Each 16 oz bottle delivers what BODYARMOR calls a "premium sports drink" — coconut water as a base, plus potassium (700mg), sodium (250mg), and magnesium. The electrolyte profile is solid for a low-calorie option. A standard Gatorade Thirst Quencher runs about 160 calories and 36g of sugar per 20 oz bottle. This has 20 calories and 4g of carbohydrates total. That's a massive difference if you're watching intake.

The Orange Clementine flavor is the standout in the LYTE lineup. As shown in the product image, it's a clean, simple bottle — no flashy graphics, just the orange citrus branding. It tastes like a lighter, less acidic orange juice rather than a candy-forward sports beverage. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Tastes like real orange juice, not like a vitamin packet dissolved in water."* That matches my experience.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across 235 ratings at 4.7 stars, three themes dominate:

1. **The clean ingredient list.** People are tired of artificial dyes and sucralose. This uses Stevia and natural flavors, and buyers notice. One reviewer wrote: *"Finally a sports drink my kids can have that doesn't turn their mouths neon orange."*

2. **The flavor balance.** It's sweet but not cloying. The orange-citrus blend has enough tartness to keep it refreshing. Multiple reviewers specifically called out that it doesn't have that "diet drink" flavor profile.

3. **Post-workout recovery.** Several buyers mentioned it helps with cramping and feels less heavy than sugary alternatives. The coconut water base gives it a smoother mouthfeel than standard electrolyte drinks.

## Where it falls short — honestly

The Stevia aftertaste is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. It's not overpowering, but on the finish there's that slightly bitter, herbal note that Stevia brings. If you're sensitive to it, you'll notice it every sip.

Also, the 16 oz bottle size is a real consideration. Most sports drinks come in 20 oz or 32 oz. You'll burn through a 12-pack faster than you expect, and the per-ounce cost is higher than a standard Gatorade. For heavy training sessions where you're sweating for over an hour, 500mg of sodium is on the lower end — you'd want something with more salt, like Gatorade Endurance or LMNT.

Finally, price. It's not cheap, and it's not always in stock on Subscribe & Save. If you're buying single bottles, it adds up.

## Who should buy this

- **The everyday gym-goer** doing 45-60 minute sessions — this is perfect. Enough electrolytes, no sugar crash.
- **The parent** who wants a sports drink for kids that isn't loaded with sugar and dye. The clean label is a genuine win.
- **The low-carb or keto person** who needs electrolytes without the carbs. At 4g carbs, this fits macros easily.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this actually better than Gatorade Zero?**
For taste and clean ingredients, yes. Gatorade Zero uses sucralose and artificial colors. BODYARMOR uses Stevia and natural flavors. Both have similar electrolyte levels, but BODYARMOR's coconut water base gives it a smoother taste. Gatorade Zero is cheaper per ounce, though.

**Q: Can you use this for hangover recovery?**
People do. The potassium and coconut water help with dehydration. It won't fix everything, but it's gentler on your stomach than a heavy sugar drink.

**Q: Is the Orange Clementine flavor the best one?**
It's the most popular, but the Mixed Berry and Peach Mango are close. The Orange Clementine is the most "juice-like" of the lineup. If you prefer tart over sweet, this is the right pick.
