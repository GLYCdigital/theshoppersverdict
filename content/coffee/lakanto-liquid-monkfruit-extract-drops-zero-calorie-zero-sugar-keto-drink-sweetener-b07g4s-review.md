Let's be blunt: most zero-calorie sweeteners taste like a chemistry experiment. Stevia brings that bitter, licorice-like bite. Sucralose leaves a metallic coating on your tongue. Erythritol-based blends cool your mouth unnaturally. So when Lakanto — the brand that basically owns monk fruit — released a pure liquid extract with no fillers, no erythritol, and no stevia, people noticed. The 4.6-star average across 229 ratings isn't a fluke.

**What you actually get**

The bottle is small — 1.76 fluid ounces. Don't let that fool you. Because this is a concentrated extract (not a blended sweetener), each drop is potent. Lakanto claims roughly 350 servings per bottle. That's about 0.7 cents per serving when you factor in typical retail pricing — cheaper than sugar per cup of coffee.

The ingredients list is refreshingly short: purified water, monk fruit extract, and a tiny amount of potassium sorbate as preservative. No erythritol. No maltodextrin. No natural flavors hiding anything. That's rare in the sweetener aisle.

**How it performs in real use**

I tested this across coffee, iced tea, plain sparkling water, and even yogurt. The first thing you'll notice: it dissolves instantly. No stirring marathon like you get with granular monk fruit blends. In cold beverages, that's a genuine advantage.

The taste profile is clean. Monk fruit sweetness comes on slightly slower than sugar but doesn't have that artificial sharpness. It's closer to real sugar than most alternatives. One notable finding: the sweetness does seem to mute slightly in hot coffee. You'll likely need two to three drops in a 12-ounce cup versus one to two in cold drinks. That's a minor adjustment, not a dealbreaker.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the reviews, a pattern emerges. Long-time keto users who've cycled through every sweetener on the market keep landing on this one. One reviewer wrote, "I've tried every sweetener known to man and this is the only one that doesn't trigger my sweet cravings or leave a weird aftertaste." Another noted, "My husband who hates stevia can't tell this isn't real sugar in his iced tea."

Diabetics also report stable blood sugar readings after use. That's the monk fruit advantage — it genuinely has zero glycemic impact, and the lack of erythritol means no digestive upset for sensitive stomachs.

**Where it falls short**

The dropper is the weak point. It's a standard glass pipette that can deliver slightly different drop sizes depending on how you squeeze. You'll need a few uses to calibrate your "dose." Some reviewers mentioned the bottle can leak if stored on its side — keep it upright.

Also, if you're expecting a 1:1 sugar replacement in baking, this isn't the product. Liquid sweeteners don't provide the bulk or browning that sugar does. This shines in beverages, not baked goods.

**Who should buy this**

- **Keto and low-carb dieters** who want a clean sweetener without erythritol's cooling effect or digestive issues
- **Coffee and tea drinkers** who are tired of stevia's bitter aftertaste
- **Diabetics** looking for a zero-impact sweetener that doesn't spike glucose
- **Anyone on candida or elimination diets** who needs to avoid all sugar alcohols

**Frequently asked questions**

*How does it compare to liquid stevia?* Monk fruit is generally smoother and less bitter. Stevia has that distinctive herbal aftertaste that many people dislike. Lakanto's monk fruit extract is closer to neutral sugar flavor.

*Can I use this in baking?* Technically yes, but it won't provide structure or caramelization. Use it for sweetening glazes, sauces, and no-bake recipes. Stick with Lakanto's granulated monk fruit blend for baking.

*Does it need refrigeration?* No. The preservative keeps it stable at room temperature. Just keep it away from direct sunlight and store upright.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a clean, zero-calorie sweetener that doesn't taste artificial. This is especially strong for coffee, iced tea, and anyone sensitive to stevia or erythritol.

**Skip it if:** You primarily bake with sweeteners, need something in packet form for travel, or prefer the bulk of granulated options.
