## What's Actually in the Bag

You're getting Callets — those flat, coin-shaped discs — not chips or chopped chunks. That shape isn't cosmetic. Flat discs melt faster and more uniformly than irregular chunks, which means less time hovering over a double boiler and fewer scorched bits. The bag size varies by seller, but most units arrive sealed and intact, which matters because white chocolate absorbs odors like a sponge.

## Performance: Where It Shines

I tested these for dipping and molding. The melt is smooth and fluid at around 40°C (104°F), and the coating sets with a clean snap if you temper properly. The flavor is genuinely Belgian — sweet, creamy, with a distinct vanilla-forward profile that doesn't taste like the waxy supermarket stuff. As one buyer put it: *"These melt like a dream and taste like actual chocolate, not sugar paste."* Another noted: *"I've bought three bags now for my cookie business. Consistent every time."*

The Callets format also means you can measure by weight without chopping — a small but real convenience if you're portioning for multiple batches.

## Where It Falls Short

First, the elephant in the room: this is not pure white chocolate. It contains vegetable fat alongside cocoa butter, which purists will notice. It's still better than most grocery store white chips, but if you need couverture-grade for a high-end pastry program, look elsewhere.

Second, tempering is non-negotiable. Skip it and your dipped strawberries will bloom into a streaky, soft mess. Several reviewers complained about "soft results" or "grainy texture" — that's almost always a tempering issue, not a product defect. Read the instructions before you blame the chocolate.

Third, if you're in a hot climate, shipping can be rough. A few reviews mentioned melted bags in summer. Not the brand's fault, but worth knowing if you order in July.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a home baker who makes dipped cookies, truffles, or molded candies and wants reliable, good-tasting white chocolate without paying couverture prices. Also great for coffee enthusiasts making chocolate-covered espresso beans or drizzling over homemade biscotti.

**Skip it if:** You need pure cocoa butter white chocolate for professional pastry work, or if you're looking for a snacking chocolate — this is a baking ingredient, not an eating bar.

## FAQ: Real Questions, Direct Answers

**Q: Can I microwave these?**
A: Yes, but in 15-second bursts at 50% power, stirring between each. White chocolate scorches easily, so low and slow wins.

**Q: Do I need to temper these for dipping?**
A: For professional-looking results, yes. For casual drizzling where texture doesn't matter, you can get away with melting and using immediately — but expect softer set.

**Q: How do these compare to Ghirardelli white chips?**
A: Better. The Callets melt more smoothly, taste less artificial, and set harder. Ghirardelli is fine for cookies; these are better for anything where chocolate is the star.
