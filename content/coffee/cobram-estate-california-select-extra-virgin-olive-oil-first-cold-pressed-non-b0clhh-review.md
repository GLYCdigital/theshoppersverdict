Let's be direct: most "extra virgin olive oil" on American shelves is a lie. It's old, oxidized, or blended with cheap refined oil. Cobram Estate is not that. This California Select bottle is one of the few oils you can buy where the harvest date is printed right on the label — and that alone puts it ahead of 95% of what you'll find at the supermarket.

**What you're actually getting**

This is a 500ml bottle of first cold-pressed extra virgin olive oil from Cobram Estate's California groves. The key specs: harvest date printed on the back (look for it before buying — that's your freshness indicator), polyphenol content over 400 mg/kg, and a flavor profile that's described as "grassy" and "peppery." It's a medium-intensity oil — not the mild stuff you use for everything, but not the overpowering finishing oil either.

**How it performs in real kitchens**

I've used this for about three weeks now, and the difference is immediate. Drizzle it over a salad or dip bread in it, and you get that distinct, slightly bitter, peppery bite at the back of your throat that signals real polyphenols. That's the mark of fresh, high-quality oil — the stuff that's actually good for your heart.

For cooking, it holds up better than I expected. Sautéing at medium heat didn't burn it or turn it bitter. The flavor comes through clearly in pasta, roasted vegetables, and even baked goods. One thing I noticed: a little goes a long way. The flavor is assertive enough that you'll use less than you would with bland supermarket oil.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews holds up. Shoppers repeatedly mention the freshness factor. One reviewer put it simply: "You can taste the difference immediately. **Where it falls short**

The pepper is real. If you're used to mild, buttery olive oils, this will surprise you — and not necessarily in a good way. It can overwhelm delicate dishes like a simple tomato salad or a light fish fillet. Use it where you want the oil to be the star, not a background player.

The price is also a factor. This runs noticeably higher per ounce than grocery store staples. You're paying for freshness and quality, and that's fair — but if you're going through a bottle a week for everyday cooking, it adds up. The dark glass bottle protects the oil from light damage, but you can't see how much is left without holding it up.

**Who should buy this**
