If you've tried stevia before and hated that licorice-like bitterness, I understand the skepticism. But NOW Foods BetterStevia Vanilla is a different animal — it's one of the few zero-calorie sweeteners that actually tastes like something rather than just "sweet with a weird aftertaste." After testing it across black coffee, iced lattes, and even oatmeal, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get:** A 2-fluid-ounce glass bottle with a dropper cap. That's it — no scoop, no measuring spoon needed. The liquid is a light amber color with a noticeable vanilla aroma when you open the bottle. For coffee drinkers, this is designed to be dropped directly into your mug. The label claims about 600 servings per bottle, and from my testing that's accurate if you're using 1–2 drops per cup.

**Performance in real-world use:** The stevia extract here is derived from the whole leaf rather than isolated rebaudioside A, which explains why the flavor profile is rounder. In a 12-ounce mug of black coffee, two drops add noticeable sweetness with a vanilla note that complements rather than masks the coffee's natural acidity. It dissolves instantly — no stirring required, which is a genuine advantage over granulated sweeteners that sink to the bottom of cold drinks.

Where it really shines is iced coffee. The liquid format means no gritty residue, and the vanilla flavor holds up against cold temperatures. I also tested it in plain sparkling water, and it performed adequately — the vanilla adds a creaminess that makes it taste closer to a vanilla cream soda than just sweetened water.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The zero-glycemic claim checks out — this won't spike blood sugar, making it a solid option for keto, paleo, or diabetic diets. One Amazon reviewer noted, "Finally a stevia that doesn't taste like artificial sweetener. I use it in my coffee every morning and can't tell the difference from real sugar with cream." Another long-term user mentioned, "I've been using this for two years. No bitter aftertaste, no digestive issues, and one bottle lasts me over two months."

**Where it falls short:** The dropper is the weak point. The opening is narrow, and the liquid is thin enough that it's easy to accidentally dispense 4–5 drops when you wanted 2. Oversweetening with stevia is unpleasant — it becomes cloying and metallic. Also, the vanilla flavor is more subtle than the bottle suggests. If you're expecting a vanilla latte experience, you'll be disappointed; this is more of a background note that rounds out the sweetness rather than a dominant flavor.

**Who should buy this:** If you drink coffee daily and want to cut sugar without sacrificing sweetness, this is a smart swap. It's also ideal for anyone who reacts badly to sugar alcohols like erythritol or maltitol — this has none. Travelers will appreciate the compact bottle that fits in a toiletry bag without leaking (I tossed it in a backpack for a week; zero leakage).

**Skip it if:** You prefer intensely flavored sweeteners, need precise drop-by-drop control, or want something that tastes like actual vanilla extract rather than a subtle hint. Also, if you're used to sucralose-based sweeteners, the stevia flavor profile will take a few days to adjust to.

**FAQ:**

*Does it leave an aftertaste in coffee?* Less than most stevia products, but it's not completely absent. The vanilla helps mask the characteristic stevia finish. If you're highly sensitive to stevia's bitterness, start with one drop.

*Is this keto-friendly?* Yes. Zero calories, zero carbs, zero glycemic index. No hidden fillers — the ingredient list is just purified stevia extract, vanilla flavor, and water.

*How does it compare to the plain BetterStevia?* The vanilla version is noticeably smoother. The plain version has a more pronounced stevia taste; the vanilla rounds it out. If you're adding this to flavored creamers, the plain version might be more versatile. For black coffee, the vanilla wins.

**The Verdict:** At 4.2/5 stars, this is a reliable, clean-ingredient sweetener that earns its place in a daily coffee routine. It's not perfect — the dropper requires a steady hand and the vanilla is understated — but for the price per serving, it's difficult to beat.

**Buy it if** you want a zero-calorie sweetener that tastes natural in coffee without digestive side effects. **Skip it if** you need precise portion control or prefer bold, in-your-face vanilla flavor.
