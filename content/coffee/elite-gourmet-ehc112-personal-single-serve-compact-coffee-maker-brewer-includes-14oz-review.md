**The $35 Coffee Maker That Kills Keurig's Biggest Rip-Off**

Here's the thing about single-serve coffee makers: they're convenient, sure, but they lock you into paying $0.50–$0.80 per cup for years. The Elite Gourmet EHC112 takes a different approach. For $35, it gives you a permanent mesh filter and a 14-ounce thermal travel mug — no pods, no cartridges, no subscription. You grind (or scoop) your own coffee, and you're done.

At 4.3 stars from nearly 9,800 buyers, it's clearly doing something right. Let me tell you what that something is — and where it stumbles.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the brewer base, a reusable mesh filter basket, and the 14-ounce stainless-steel-lined thermal mug with a lid. That's it. No pods, no paper filters, no measuring spoon. The machine measures about 8.5 inches tall and 8 inches wide — small enough to slide under a cabinet or squeeze between a toaster and a blender. As shown in the product image, the black finish is matte and inoffensive; it won't win design awards, but it won't clash with anything either.

**Real-World Performance: What 9,794 Buyers Actually Experienced**

The brewing process is dead simple: scoop grounds into the reusable filter, fill the mug with water to the fill line, pop it on the base, and press the button. The machine heats water to the proper range (about 185–195°F) and drips through in roughly 3–4 minutes. The thermal mug is the real star here — double-wall stainless construction means your coffee stays drinkable-hot for two hours, not the 20 minutes you'd get from a glass carafe. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I brew at 6:30 AM and it's still warm when I finish it at 9."* That's a genuine difference-maker for slow sippers.

The reusable filter is a huge win for anyone tired of buying #4 paper filters or K-Cups. It catches medium-coarse grounds well, and cleanup is just a rinse under the tap. Owners consistently praise this: *"No pods to buy ever again. I've had mine for 8 months and it's paid for itself twice over."*

**Where It Falls Short — Read This Before You Buy**

Let's be honest about the flaws. First, there's no strength control. If you like bold, dark-roast coffee, this machine makes everything taste medium at best. The water-to-coffee ratio is fixed by the mug's fill line, so you can't dial in a stronger cup without overfilling the filter basket — which leads to problem number two.

The mesh filter is fine, but it's not fine enough for espresso-ground coffee. Use powder-fine grounds and you'll get sludge in your cup and a slow, clogged drip. Stick to a standard drip grind and you're fine, but this is a limitation worth knowing.

Third, the travel mug lid is a push-on design that's *mostly* leak-resistant. It won't spill in a car cup holder, but toss it sideways in a backpack and you'll find coffee in your laptop bag. Don't trust it for commuting. One buyer warned: *"The mug is great but the lid is not leakproof. I learned the hard way."*

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Dorm residents or small-apartment dwellers** who want real coffee without the counter-space commitment of a 12-cup machine.
- **Office workers** who want fresh-brewed coffee at their desk without pod expenses.
- **Eco-conscious drinkers** tired of single-use plastic pods and paper filters.
- **Budget shoppers** who'd rather spend $35 once than $35 on K-Cups every few months.

**Skip it if** you need espresso-strength coffee, you insist on programmable auto-brew timers, or you want a carafe for serving guests. This is a solo-machine, full stop.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Can I use paper filters with this?**  
A: Technically yes, but the reusable mesh basket is designed to work without them. If you do add a paper filter, use a #4 cone — but honestly, the mesh does the job and saves you money.

**Q: Will this fit a standard car cup holder?**  
A: The mug's base is slightly tapered, and it fits most standard cup holders. But it's tall — about 7 inches. If you have a shallow holder, it might wobble.

**Q: How many cups does it make per fill?**  
A: One. The 14-ounce mug is the brew chamber, so you're limited to one cup per cycle. It's a personal brewer, not a carafe machine.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want cheap, pod-free, single-cup coffee with a genuinely good thermal mug and no ongoing costs. At $34.99, this is one of the best value plays in the single-serve category.

**Skip it if** you need adjustable brew strength, you're a fine-grind espresso drinker, or you want to brew more than one cup at a time.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid, honest, and affordable. Just manage your expectations on strength and the lid's leak resistance, and you'll get years of cheap, good coffee out of this little machine.
