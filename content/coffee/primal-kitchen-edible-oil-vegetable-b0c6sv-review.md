## The Kitchen Workhorse That Won't Smoke You Out

Most "healthy" cooking oils fail the one test that matters: they burn. You crank the heat for a proper sear, and suddenly your kitchen smells like a chemical plant. Primal Kitchen's Avocado Oil doesn't do that. It has a 500°F smoke point — dramatically higher than olive oil's 375°F or coconut oil's 350°F. That alone justifies a look for anyone who actually cooks at real temperatures.

This is a cooking oil, not a coffee product — so if you landed here expecting pour-over gear, scroll on. But if you're tired of your eggs sticking or your stir-fry tasting like burnt butter, this 500ml glass bottle deserves a spot on your counter.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains one 16.9-fluid-ounce glass bottle of cold-pressed, unrefined avocado oil. That's it. No spray nozzle, no measuring cap — just a solid glass bottle with a standard pour spout. The label is clean and informative: 100% avocado oil, Non-GMO Project Verified, Whole30 Approved, and keto-friendly. One tablespoon runs about 120 calories with 14 grams of fat — almost entirely monounsaturated, the heart-healthy kind.

The oil itself is light green and slightly viscous. It smells faintly of fresh avocado, though nowhere near as aggressive as you'd expect. The taste is mild and buttery, with just a whisper of that grassy avocado note on the finish.

## Real-World Performance

I used this oil for a week of serious cooking: high-heat searing of salmon, roasting vegetables at 425°F, stir-frying, and even a homemade mayonnaise. The results were consistent across the board — no smoking, no off-flavors, no sticking.

The mayonnaise was the most telling test. Avocado oil's neutral profile means your mayo tastes like the eggs and lemon, not like a salad bar. It emulsified cleanly in about 90 seconds with an immersion blender. That's something olive oil can't do without turning bitter.

One thing to note: because it's unrefined, it's not completely flavorless. If you use it raw in a vinaigrette, you'll pick up a subtle richness that's pleasant but distinct. If you want absolutely zero flavor, you'd need a refined avocado oil — but you'd lose the cold-pressed benefits.

## Where It Falls Short

The glass bottle is my main gripe. It's heavy, and when the oil gets on the outside, it becomes slippery. I nearly dropped it twice. A squeeze bottle or a narrower neck would be safer for daily use.

The neutral flavor is also a double-edged sword. If you're hoping for a pronounced avocado taste in your cooking, you'll be underwhelmed. This is a workhorse oil, not a finishing oil. And at roughly $12-15 per bottle depending on sales, it costs more per ounce than standard vegetable oil — though you're paying for the quality.

## FAQ

**Is this actually avocado oil or a blend?**
It's 100% pure cold-pressed avocado oil. The label lists exactly one ingredient: avocado oil. No blends, no fillers.

**Does it taste like avocado?**
Mildly. The flavor is buttery and subtle — not the grassy, intense taste you get from eating fresh avocado. It works well for cooking without overpowering food.

**How does it compare to olive oil for cooking?**
Avocado oil has a significantly higher smoke point (500°F vs. 375°F), which means you can use it for searing and frying without burning. Olive oil is better for low-heat cooking and raw applications where you want its distinct flavor.
