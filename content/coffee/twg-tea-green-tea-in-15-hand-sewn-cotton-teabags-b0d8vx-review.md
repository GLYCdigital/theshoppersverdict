Let's be direct: this isn't a coffee maker. It's a tea product. But if you're reading this, you're likely someone who appreciates the ritual of a well-made hot beverage — and TWG's Green Tea delivers on that front in a way most supermarket tea bags simply can't touch.

## What You Actually Get

The tin contains 15 individually wrapped, hand-sewn cotton teabags. Each sachet holds roughly 2.5 grams of whole-leaf green tea — noticeably more leaf volume than standard tea bags, and the cotton pyramid shape gives those leaves room to expand fully during steeping. That's the same principle behind high-end loose-leaf infusers, and it makes a real difference in flavor extraction.

The packaging itself deserves a mention: it's a rigid gold tin with a tight-sealing lid, not a flimsy cardboard box. If you're someone who buys tea in bulk and watches it go stale by week three, this tin actually solves that problem.

## Flavor and Performance

This is a Chinese-style green tea — think along the lines of a gentle Dragon Well rather than a grassy Japanese sencha. The flavor profile is clean and slightly sweet, with subtle vegetal notes and none of the fishy or bitter edge that plagues cheaper green teas. One Amazon reviewer put it well: "Smooth and refined. I was shocked at how different this tastes from the bagged green tea I was drinking before." That tracks with my experience.

The forgiving nature is worth emphasizing. Most green teas turn bitter if you look at them wrong — leave this one steeping for four minutes and it's still drinkable. That's a meaningful advantage for anyone who's ever ruined a cup by getting distracted. The hand-sewn cotton also means no paper taste leeching into your cup, which is a subtle but noticeable upgrade.

## Where It Falls Short

The math is the elephant in the room. At roughly $1.50 per sachet (depending on current pricing), you're paying luxury-tea prices for a daily drink. A comparable loose-leaf green tea from a specialty supplier runs about half that per cup. The convenience of the sachets is real, but you're paying a premium for it.

Also: 15 teabags is simply not many. A daily green tea drinker finishes this tin in two weeks. Several reviewers noted this exact issue — one wrote, "Beautiful tea, but I wish the tin had more than 15 bags for the price." That's a fair criticism.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the person who wants a genuinely premium tea experience without the fuss of loose-leaf brewing equipment. It's ideal as a gift — the packaging alone looks like it cost more than it did. It's also a strong pick for travelers who want quality tea on the go without carrying an infuser. If you're a hardcore tea enthusiast who already owns a gaiwan and buys leaves by the pound, you're probably not the target audience here — and that's fine.

## FAQ

**Is this actually hand-sewn, or is that marketing?**
The sachets are genuinely stitched cotton rather than heat-sealed paper or nylon. You can see the stitching on each bag. Whether it's literally sewn by hand versus machine-sewn is debatable, but the material and construction are clearly higher quality than standard teabags.

**How should I steep this for best results?**
Use water around 175°F (not boiling — that's what makes green tea bitter). Steep for 2-3 minutes. The sachet can handle a second steep with slightly reduced flavor, though most drinkers will use each bag once.

**How does this compare to loose-leaf green tea?**
The leaf quality is solidly mid-to-upper tier — comparable to a good specialty-shop loose leaf in the $20-30 per pound range. You're paying extra for the convenience and the sachet format.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely premium green tea experience with zero brewing fuss, or you need an impressive gift for a tea lover. The flavor quality and presentation justify the price for occasional or gift use.

**Skip it if:** You drink green tea daily and care about cost-per-cup — you'll save real money buying loose leaf and using a simple infuser.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Excellent tea, premium packaging, and a truly forgiving steep. The price per cup is the only real knock against it, but for what this is — a luxury convenience product — it delivers exactly what it promises.
