# Michel et Augustin Changemaker Chocolate Cookie Squares: A French Treat That Almost Justifies the Hype

Let me be upfront: I've tested dozens of "gourmet" cookies that promise Parisian bakery quality and deliver packaged mediocrity. The Michel et Augustin Changemaker Chocolate Cookie Squares are not those cookies. But they're also not quite the transcendent experience the branding suggests.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 10 individually wrapped cookie squares — roughly 1.5-inch pieces of buttery shortbread studded with dark chocolate chunks. The packaging is cheerful and colorful, with the "Changemaker" label referencing the company's commitment to donating a portion of profits to food-access charities. It's a nice sentiment, but it doesn't change what's in the wrapper.

## The Real-World Performance

Here's where these cookies earn their keep. The texture is genuinely excellent — crisp on the outside, tender inside, with a pronounced butter flavor that reminds me of a high-end shortbread rather than a mass-market cookie. The chocolate chunks are substantial and dark (around 60% cacao), providing a bitter counterpoint that keeps the sweetness in check.

I tested these with coffee, because that's the real question for anyone buying a cookie. They hold up remarkably well — dunk one in a hot mug of black coffee and it softens without disintegrating into sludge. That's a meaningful engineering achievement for a cookie.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews (215 ratings, 4.3 stars) cluster around two themes. First, the texture: one buyer wrote, "These are dangerously good — buttery, not too sweet, and the chocolate is actually dark and rich." Another praised the portion control: "The individual wrapping keeps me from eating the whole box in one sitting, which is both a blessing and a curse."

The second consistent praise point is the coffee pairing. Multiple reviewers specifically mention these as their go-to coffee companion, which tracks with my testing.

## Where It Falls Short

The price is the elephant in the room. At roughly $1.25 per cookie, you're paying a premium that the ingredients don't fully justify. The butter and chocolate are good quality, but they're not $12-per-box quality.

There's also a consistency issue. Several reviewers report receiving boxes where cookies arrived broken or stale. One wrote: "First box was perfect, second box tasted like it had been sitting in a warehouse for months." That's a fulfillment problem, not a product problem, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You're a coffee drinker who wants a genuinely good dunking cookie, or you need an impressive hostess gift that comes in attractive packaging. The portion control also makes them ideal for office snacks or lunchbox treats.

**Skip them if:** You're on a budget, or you expect a soft, chewy American-style cookie. These are firmly in the crisp/biscuit camp, and if that's not your texture preference, you'll be disappointed.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these actually from France?**
A: Yes, Michel et Augustin is a French brand (founded in 2004), and the cookies are made in France. The butter-forward recipe reflects that heritage.

**Q: How do they compare to Pepperidge Farm or Tate's?**
A: They're closer to a high-end shortbread than a standard chocolate chip cookie. More butter, less sugar, and the chocolate is darker and more pronounced. Tate's is crisp but thinner; these have more substance.

**Q: Can you freeze them?**
A: Yes, they freeze well. The individual wrapping makes this practical — just pull one out and let it sit for 10 minutes before eating.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — These are excellent cookies that are slightly overpriced for what they are. If you value texture, butter quality, and coffee compatibility, they're worth the splurge as an occasional treat. If you're looking for an everyday cookie, you can do better for less money.

Buy them for the quality, not the "Changemaker" story. The cookies speak for themselves — they just cost more than they should.
