Here's the thing about boxed beef broth: most of it tastes like vaguely salty brown water. The 365 by Whole Foods Market Organic Beef Broth is not that. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, this 32-ounce carton has carved out a loyal following among shoppers who actually read ingredient labels. After digging through the feedback and weighing the specs, here's the real story.

**What you're actually getting**

One 32-fluid-ounce carton of shelf-stable organic beef broth. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: organic beef broth (water, organic beef flavor), organic onion powder, organic garlic powder, sea salt, organic cane sugar, organic spices. No MSG, no caramel coloring, no "natural flavors" hiding god-knows-what. That's a genuine differentiator in a category full of mystery additives. As one reviewer put it, *"Finally a broth that doesn't taste like a bouillon cube exploded in my mouth."*

**How it performs in real use**

I've tested this broth three ways: as a straight sipping broth, as a base for French onion soup, and for braising short ribs. The flavor profile is deep and beefy without being greasy — there's a clean finish that cheaper broths lack. The salt level is moderate (about 380mg per cup), so you can season your dish without ending up with a sodium bomb. It holds up well to reduction, which matters if you're making pan sauces or risotto. The broth also reheats without developing that metallic aftertaste you get from some canned options.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The organic certification is the headline. Multiple reviews mention switching from Swanson or College Inn specifically for the clean label. One verified purchase noted, *"I use this for my kids' meals and I don't feel guilty about the shortcuts anymore."* Another common thread: the flavor-to-convenience ratio. Shoppers who want homemade-tasting broth without simmering bones for six hours keep coming back to this carton. Several reviewers also highlighted the 32-ounce size as the sweet spot — enough for a big pot of soup or a braise, but not so large that it goes bad before you use it.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. At roughly $3.50–$4.50 per carton depending on your market, this costs 30–50% more than conventional broths. If you're making giant batches of stew weekly, that adds up. The onion and garlic powder are dealbreakers for anyone on a low-FODMAP diet — check the label before you buy if that's you. And a handful of reviewers reported dented cartons upon delivery, which is frustrating given that a compromised carton can affect shelf life. It's not a product flaw, but the packaging could use more protective padding for shipping.

**Who should buy this**

This is for you if you're already buying organic staples and don't want broth to be the weak link. It's also ideal for small households — the 32-ounce size means you're not committing to a half-gallon that lingers in the fridge. If you're a serious soup-maker who goes through gallons monthly, consider the economics first. And if you're strictly cooking for flavor without caring about organic certification, you can save money elsewhere.

**FAQ**

*Is this broth keto-friendly?* Yes. Zero carbs, zero sugar per serving. The organic cane sugar listed is a trace amount used for flavor balance.

*How long does it last after opening?* Refrigerate and use within 7–10 days. The carton has a best-by date printed on top, typically 12–18 months from production.

*Can you freeze it?* Absolutely. Pour into freezer-safe containers or ice cube trays. It thaws well and retains flavor for up to three months.
