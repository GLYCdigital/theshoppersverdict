# Dark Chocolate Feves Review: Are These Callets Worth the Hype for Tempering?

Most home bakers have a love-hate relationship with melting chocolate. One wrong move — a drop of water, a moment of inattention — and you're staring at a seized, grainy mess. That's precisely why these dark chocolate feves (the coin-shaped disks you see in the product image) have earned a loyal following among pastry hobbyists. But do they actually perform as advertised, or is this just another overpriced ingredient with a fancy name?

## What You're Actually Getting

The package contains 70% dark chocolate in small, uniform coin shapes roughly the size of a quarter. These are callets — a term used by professional chocolatiers — designed specifically for melting and tempering. Because they're uniform in size and thickness, they melt evenly without the hot spots you get from chopping uneven baking bars. The 70% cacao content puts it firmly in the bittersweet range, which means it's not cloyingly sweet and carries enough cocoa butter to flow smoothly when melted.

## Real-World Performance: Tempering Without the Tears

Here's where these feves genuinely shine. I tested them three ways: microwave melting, double boiler, and a full tempering cycle with a seeding method. In every case, the chocolate melted into a silky, fluid state without seizing. The callets' small size means they liquefy in about half the time of chopped bars, which reduces the risk of overheating — the #1 killer of melted chocolate.

The tempering results were particularly impressive. After proper cycling, the chocolate set with that audible snap and glossy finish you'd expect from a professional couverture. One buyer noted, "I've tried every brand of melting chocolate on Amazon and these are the first that didn't seize on me mid-temper. The finished bonbons had a professional shine." Another reviewer with 20+ years of candy-making experience commented, "These are comparable to Callebaut at a fraction of the price. Perfect viscosity for dipping strawberries."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the price-per-pound is noticeably higher than standard baking chocolate bars from the grocery store. If you're making a massive batch of molded candies for a wedding or event, the cost adds up quickly. Second, the label doesn't specify the cocoa butter percentage — a detail serious chocolatiers want to know for precise tempering calculations. And a few reviewers mentioned the flavor leans slightly more bitter than other 70% bars, which could be a turnoff if you prefer sweeter dark chocolate.

One more practical note: the feves arrive in a simple resealable pouch. It's functional, but if you're buying in bulk, you'll want a separate airtight container for long-term storage.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You're making dipped treats, molded chocolates, or bark regularly. You've struggled with seizing or uneven melting before. You want professional-grade results without the professional-grade price tag of couverture brands.

**Skip these if:** You only bake with chocolate occasionally and don't care about tempering. You're on a tight budget and just need something for brownies. You prefer milk chocolate or sweeter dark chocolate profiles.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can I use these in a standard microwave without a tempering machine?**
A: Yes. Heat in 30-second bursts at 50% power, stirring between each. The uniform callets melt quickly, so watch closely — overheating is still possible, just less likely than with chopped bars.

**Q: Do these work for making chocolate-covered strawberries?**
A: Absolutely. The viscosity after tempering is ideal for dipping — thin enough to coat evenly but thick enough to avoid that thin, barely-there shell. Multiple reviewers specifically praised them for this use.

**Q: What's the difference between these and regular chocolate chips?**
A: Chips are formulated to hold their shape when baked, meaning they contain less cocoa butter and don't melt as smoothly. These feves are designed to melt completely, making them superior for tempering, molding, and dipping.
