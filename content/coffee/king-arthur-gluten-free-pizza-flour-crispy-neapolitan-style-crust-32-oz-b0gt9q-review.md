# King Arthur Gluten Free Pizza Flour: Crispy Neapolitan-Style Review

Let's be honest: most gluten-free pizza flour is a compromise. You get a cracker-thin base that shatters, or a dense, chewy puck that tastes more like bread than pizza. King Arthur's Gluten Free Pizza Flour aims for the middle—and according to 229 Amazon reviewers who've rated it 4.6 out of 5 stars, it mostly hits the mark.

This isn't a complete mix. You're getting a 32-ounce bag of flour blend (rice flour, tapioca starch, and a few binders) that you'll need to pair with yeast, water, and olive oil. The included recipe on the bag is solid, but the QR code linking to King Arthur's video tutorial is the real MVP—it walks you through the exact hydration and kneading technique needed for gluten-free dough.

## What's in the Bag and How It Performs

The flour itself is finely milled, which matters. Cheaper gluten-free blends feel gritty; this one has a texture close to all-purpose flour. That fineness translates directly to the crust—when baked at 500°F on a preheated pizza steel, the exterior develops those characteristic leopard-spot char marks and a shattering crispness. The interior stays tender with a slight chew, which is remarkably close to a proper Neapolitan crust.

One reviewer noted, *"I've tried every GF pizza flour on the market. This is the only one that made my husband (who eats normal pizza) ask for seconds."* Another praised its forgiveness: *"I forgot it on the counter for an extra hour of proofing and it still turned out great."*

The 32-ounce bag yields roughly four to five 12-inch pizzas, depending on how thick you roll it. That's significantly better value than single-use mixes that cost the same and make one pizza.

## Where It Falls Short

This flour demands proper equipment. If you're baking on a standard sheet pan at 425°F, you'll get a serviceable flatbread, but you won't unlock the crispy, blistered texture that makes this flour special. A pizza steel or stone preheated for at least 45 minutes is non-negotiable.

The dough is also sticky—substantially sticker than wheat dough. Hand-stretching it is an exercise in frustration unless you keep your hands lightly oiled. A stand mixer with a dough hook helps, but it's not a quick stir-and-bake situation. Plan for about 90 minutes from mixing to oven.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The GF pizza perfectionist** who already owns a pizza steel and wants restaurant-quality crust at home
- **The newly gluten-free** who's tired of cardboard-texture frozen pizzas and wants a reliable, forgiving base recipe
- **The value-conscious baker** who wants a multi-use flour, not a single-use kit

Skip it if you're looking for a five-minute, no-fuss pizza night, or if your oven tops out below 450°F.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this flour require a special recipe, or can I use my regular pizza dough recipe?**
A: Use King Arthur's recipe on the bag or website. Gluten-free dough needs more hydration and a different proofing approach than wheat dough—substituting it 1:1 into a standard recipe will yield dry, crumbly results.

**Q: Can I make this dough ahead of time?**
A: Yes. The dough keeps in the fridge for up to 48 hours. Let it come to room temperature for about 30 minutes before stretching.

**Q: Is this flour certified gluten-free?**
A: Yes, King Arthur's facility is certified gluten-free by the Gluten Intolerance Group, and the flour is tested to below 20 ppm.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're willing to invest in a pizza steel and follow the technique. **Skip it if** you want convenience over quality.

At 4.6 stars, this is one of the highest-rated gluten-free pizza flours on Amazon—and for good reason. It delivers a genuinely crispy, Neapolitan-style crust that doesn't taste like a compromise. The learning curve and equipment requirements are real, but the payoff is the closest thing to pizzeria pizza you'll make at home without gluten.

**Rating: 4.6/5 — Recommended.**
