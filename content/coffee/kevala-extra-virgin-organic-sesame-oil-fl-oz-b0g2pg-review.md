## What you're actually getting

This is a 16.9 fl oz bottle (500ml) of cold-pressed, unrefined sesame oil from organic seeds. The "extra virgin" label isn't marketing fluff here — it means the oil comes from the first pressing of the seeds, without heat or chemical solvents. The color is a deep golden-amber, and the aroma hits you the moment you crack the seal: warm, toasty, almost popcorn-like. It's noticeably fresher than the toasted sesame oils that have been sitting on shelves for months.

One thing that stands out immediately: the dark glass bottle. It's not just aesthetic — light is the enemy of polyunsaturated oils, and this packaging protects the oil's integrity. You can see the difference in color and smell versus clear plastic bottles that have been light-struck on store shelves.

## How it actually performs

I've used this oil three ways over the past few weeks: as a finishing drizzle on roasted vegetables, in a ginger-sesame vinaigrette, and stirred into fried rice at the end of cooking.

For cold applications, it's exceptional. The flavor is layered — nutty, slightly sweet, with a clean finish that doesn't coat your mouth with greasiness. A teaspoon in a salad dressing transforms it from "fine" to "what did you put in this?" 

For cooking, you need to respect its limitations. Because it's unrefined, the smoke point sits around 350°F. That means it's perfect for low-to-medium heat stir-fries and finishing, but don't try to sear a steak in it. One reviewer put it well: "I use it for finishing and marinades, not for frying. It's too good to burn."

The oil is also surprisingly versatile beyond Asian cooking — try a few drops over hummus, scrambled eggs, or even vanilla ice cream with a pinch of flaky salt. The nuttiness plays well with unexpected partners.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as toasted sesame oil?**
No. Toasted sesame oil is made from roasted seeds and has a darker, more intense flavor. This is cold-pressed from raw seeds, giving it a lighter, fresher profile. Both have their place, but they're not interchangeable in recipes.

**How should I store it after opening?**
Keep it in a cool, dark place away from the stove. The dark bottle helps, but refrigeration extends freshness even further — just note it'll become cloudy and thick when cold, which is normal. Let it come to room temperature before using.

**Does it actually taste different from cheaper sesame oils?**
Yes, noticeably. The cheaper stuff often has a burnt or flat character from high-heat processing. This has a clean, sweet nuttiness. Side-by-side, the difference is obvious.
