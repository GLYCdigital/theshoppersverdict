**The snack that actually fits your macros — if you can stop at one**

Most vegan protein bars fall into two camps: chalky disappointment or sugar bombs disguised as health food. These mini peanut butter bars from All Natural Essentials sit in a rare third category — they're genuinely low sugar, genuinely high protein, and they taste like an actual treat. The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews isn't inflated; it reflects a product that does what it claims.

**What you're actually getting**

The package contains roughly 25–30 bite-sized squares (depending on pack size you order), each clocking in at about 50 calories. The nutrition panel is where these shine: 10g of plant protein per serving (about 4 pieces), 2g net carbs, and zero added sugar. The protein comes from a pea and rice protein blend, which is smart — those two sources complement each other to create a complete amino acid profile, something single-source vegan proteins often lack.

**How they actually perform**

The texture is the surprise here. Most vegan bars with this macro profile end up dry and crumbly, but these have a dense, fudgy consistency that holds together well. They're not gooey like a fresh-baked brownie — more like a firm, chewy nougat. The peanut butter flavor is present but not overwhelming; you get roasted nut notes with a subtle saltiness that balances the sweetness.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I've tried every keto peanut butter snack on Amazon. These are the only ones that don't taste like I'm eating a vitamin."* Another noted, *"My kids steal them from my desk. That's the highest compliment I can give a 'health' bar."*

**Where they stumble**

The sweetener situation deserves mention. Erythritol and monk fruit are used to keep sugar at zero, but erythritol has a well-documented cooling effect on the palate. Some reviewers find this off-putting, with one describing it as *"a weird minty aftertaste that lingers."* It's not deal-breaking, but it's noticeable if you're sensitive to sugar alcohols.

The mini size cuts both ways. Yes, it helps with portion control — in theory. But several reviews admit to eating half the bag in one sitting. At roughly 200 calories for 4 pieces, that's not catastrophic, but it does undermine the "healthy snack" positioning if you lack restraint.

**Who should buy these**

- **Keto and low-carb dieters** — 2g net carbs fits almost any macro budget
- **Vegans who need portable protein** — these pack easily for travel or desk drawers
- **People who hate chalky protein bars** — the texture is genuinely better than most competitors
- **Not for you if:** you're sensitive to sugar alcohols, or you need a full meal replacement (these are snacks, not meals)

**Three questions shoppers actually ask**

**Q: Do these need refrigeration?**
No, they're shelf-stable. The texture stays consistent at room temperature, though some buyers prefer them cold for a firmer bite.

**Q: How do they compare to Quest or RXBAR?**
Quest bars have more protein (20g vs 10g per serving) but also more sugar alcohols. RXBARs are cleaner ingredient-wise but significantly higher in sugar. These sit in the middle — less protein per serving but better macros for strict low-sugar diets.

**Q: Can you taste the pea protein?**
Surprisingly, no. The peanut butter flavor masks it well. The pea protein aftertaste that plagues many vegan bars is largely absent here.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** you need a genuinely low-sugar, plant-based protein snack that doesn't taste like cardboard, and you're willing to pay a slight premium for the convenience.

**Skip it if:** you're on a tight budget, sensitive to erythritol, or you know you'll destroy the whole bag in one Netflix session.

These aren't revolutionary, but they're reliable. For a vegan, low-sugar protein snack that actually satisfies a sweet craving without wrecking your macros, these earn their 4.2 out of 5. Just practice portion discipline — or hide them from yourself.
