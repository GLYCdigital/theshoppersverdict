Let's be honest: most Easter candy is a disappointment. It's either waxy chocolate that tastes like it was manufactured in 1987, or it's a sad assortment where you're praying you don't get the orange-flavored thing nobody eats. So when a bag of HERSHEY'S Assorted Flavored Eggs showed up with a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, I was skeptical.

Here's the good news: this bag is better than it has any right to be. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Bag

You're getting three varieties of foil-wrapped eggs — the kind that look like they belong in a basket but also hold up fine in a lunchbox. The lineup is:

- **Milk Chocolate** — the classic, smooth and reliably HERSHEY'S
- **Cookies 'n' Creme** — white chocolate with cookie bits
- **Caramel** — milk chocolate with a soft caramel center

The bag is a standard Easter candy size, roughly the equivalent of a 10-ounce bag of seasonal candy. Each egg is individually wrapped, which matters more than you'd think — it keeps the chocolate from blooming (that white film you see on old candy) and makes portion control possible, assuming you have willpower.

## How It Actually Tastes

The milk chocolate eggs are exactly what you expect. They're fine. They're not going to convert a Lindt loyalist, but they're solidly above the "I'll eat this because it's here" tier.

The caramel eggs are the reason this bag earns its rating. The caramel is genuinely soft — not that stiff, pulls-your-fillings-out nonsense you get from some seasonal candy. It's got that slightly salty edge that makes caramel work with chocolate. If you've had the HERSHEY'S Caramel Kisses, it's the same filling. That's a good thing.

The cookies 'n' creme eggs are the weak link. The texture is slightly chalky compared to the actual Cookies 'n' Creme bar, and the cookie bits don't deliver the crunch you'd hope for. It's not bad, just the least impressive of the three. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "The cookies and cream ones are a little dry, but the caramel makes up for it."

## The Distribution Problem

Here's the honest gripe — and it's one multiple reviewers flagged. The blend isn't even. You'll get more milk chocolate eggs than anything else, and the caramel ones (the best ones) seem to be the scarcest. One buyer noted, "I wish I could buy a bag of just the caramel ones." That's the kind of feedback that tells you the product is good but the ratios are off.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're hosting an Easter egg hunt and want something that works for both kids and adults. The individual wrapping makes these perfect for stuffing plastic eggs without getting chocolate fingerprints everywhere.

**Buy it if** you're putting together gift bags or lunchbox treats — the size is right and the foil wrappers photograph well in a basket.

**Skip it if** you're a dark chocolate person. This is unapologetically milk chocolate territory, and there's no dark option in the mix.

**Skip it if** you're buying for someone who specifically loves cookies 'n' creme — they'll be disappointed by both the texture and the limited quantity.

## Common Questions

**Are these gluten-free?** The milk chocolate and caramel varieties are generally safe for most gluten-sensitive folks, but the cookies 'n' creme contains cookie pieces. Check the packaging for your specific dietary needs — HERSHEY'S labels clearly.

**How many eggs come in the bag?** It varies by weight, but you're typically looking at 15-20 eggs depending on the mix. The bag doesn't have a count guarantee, which is part of why distribution feels random.

**Do these taste like the regular candy bars?** The milk chocolate does. The caramel is actually better than the bar version because the filling stays soft. The cookies 'n' creme is close but slightly chalkier.

## The Bottom Line

This is a 4.6-star product, and that feels right. The caramel eggs alone justify the purchase. The individual wrapping makes it practical. The distribution issue is annoying but not a dealbreaker — you're still getting enough caramel eggs to feel satisfied.

**Buy it if** you want a solid, crowd-pleasing Easter candy that won't embarrass you. **Skip it if** you need a specific flavor ratio or you're a dark chocolate purist.

For the price, you could do much worse. You could also do better, but you'd have to pay more and buy three separate bags. This one gets the job done — and the caramel eggs are worth the price of admission on their own.
