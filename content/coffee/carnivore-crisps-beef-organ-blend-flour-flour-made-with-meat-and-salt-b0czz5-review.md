**This is not flour. That's the first thing you need to understand about Carnivore Crisps Beef Organ Blend Flour.** It's dehydrated, ground beef organs — liver, heart, and kidney — mixed with salt. It looks like flour in the bag, but it behaves nothing like it in the kitchen. And that's exactly why 160 Amazon buyers are split down the middle, landing this at a lukewarm 3.2 stars.

**What you actually get:** A resealable bag of fine, reddish-brown powder that smells intensely mineral and liver-forward. The ingredient list is refreshingly short — beef organs and salt. No maltodextrin, no tapioca starch, no "natural flavors" hiding anything. For carnivore purists, that's a genuine win. The texture is closer to fine cornmeal than wheat flour, with a slight grittiness that doesn't fully dissolve during cooking.

**The real-world performance is where things get divisive.** In testing, this product works — but only if you understand its limitations. It absorbs liquid aggressively, so you'll need roughly 30% more eggs or butter than standard flour recipes call for. It also has zero gluten, which means no structure, no rise, and no elasticity. Your baked goods will be dense, crumbly, and distinctly organ-flavored.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I wanted to love this for my carnivore diet but the liver taste is overwhelming. Even mixed with cheese and eggs, all I taste is organs."* That's the most common complaint — the flavor. It's not subtle. If you're someone who tolerates liver in cooking, you'll manage. If you're not, this will ruin whatever you make.

**Where it genuinely shines:** For savory applications where organ flavor complements other strong ingredients. Think meatloaf binders, sausage extenders, or crispy coatings for fried chicken. One reviewer noted: *"Used it to bread chicken thighs with pork rinds and this flour — best fried chicken I've had since going carnivore."* That's the sweet spot. It's a flavor enhancer and nutrient booster, not a flour substitute.

**The honest downsides:** First, price-per-recipe is brutal. You'll need 1-2 cups for most baking, and at roughly $1.50 per ounce, a single loaf of "bread" costs $8-12 in flour alone. Second, the organ flavor is non-negotiable — it doesn't bake out or mellow. Third, the gritty texture never fully disappears in baked goods, leaving a sandy mouthfeel in cakes or muffins.

**Who should buy this:** Strict carnivore dieters who want to boost organ meat intake without eating liver straight. Keto bakers who make savory, heavily seasoned dishes where organ flavor won't clash. Anyone doing elimination diets who needs a zero-carb thickener for soups or gravies.

**Skip it if:** You're expecting a 1:1 wheat flour replacement. You dislike liver's taste. You bake sweet goods — cookies, cakes, or breads — because the flavor won't work there. Or you're budget-conscious, because this is a premium-priced niche product.

**FAQ:**

*Can I use this 1:1 in regular baking recipes?* No. Expect to adjust liquids and add binders like eggs or xanthan gum. Start at 25% of the flour amount in a recipe and work up.

*Does the organ taste go away when cooked?* Not really. High heat slightly mellows it, but the flavor is inherent. Pair it with strong spices or cheese.

*Is it actually keto-friendly?* Yes — zero net carbs per serving, with the caveat that organ meats contain naturally occurring sugars, though negligible amounts.

**The Verdict: 3.3/5**

Buy it if you're a carnivore purist who wants organ nutrition in a versatile format and can handle the flavor. Skip it if you want a neutral flour substitute or bake sweet items.

**Bottom line:** This is a nutrient-dense specialty ingredient, not a flour replacement. Judge it on those terms, and you won't be disappointed. Judge it as flour, and you'll join the 40% of reviewers who rated it 2 stars or below.
