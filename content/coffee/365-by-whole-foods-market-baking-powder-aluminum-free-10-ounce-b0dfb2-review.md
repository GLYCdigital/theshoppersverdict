Let’s be honest: baking powder isn’t the most exciting purchase you’ll make this week. But if you’ve ever bitten into a muffin that tasted faintly of tin foil, you know why this matters. The 365 by Whole Foods Market aluminum-free baking powder exists to solve one specific problem — that metallic bitterness that comes from sodium aluminum sulfate, a common leavening agent in cheaper powders. And for most home bakers, it does the job admirably.

**What you actually get**

This is a straightforward 10-ounce canister of double-acting baking powder. The label is clean, the product is certified aluminum-free, and the can features a plastic lid that seals reasonably well. The powder itself is fine and consistent — no clumping out of the box, which is more than I can say for some budget brands that arrive pre-caked. You’re getting a standard leavening agent with one meaningful difference: it uses sodium acid pyrophosphate and monocalcium phosphate instead of aluminum-based acids.

**How it performs in real baking**

The double-acting mechanism matters. It releases a small amount of gas when mixed with liquid, then a larger burst in the oven. In practice, this means your pancakes get a decent initial rise on the griddle, and your banana bread doesn’t collapse mid-bake. I tested it in a standard buttermilk biscuit recipe and a lemon loaf, and both came out with even crumb structure and no bitter finish. The absence of aluminum isn’t just a marketing talking point — it genuinely changes the flavor profile of delicate bakes like angel food cake or shortbread.

One thing worth noting: because this is double-acting, you can’t just swap it one-for-one into recipes designed for single-acting powder without adjusting your timing. Most modern recipes assume double-acting, so this is rarely an issue, but old cookbook formulas might behave differently.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 240 ratings average 4.8 stars, and the pattern in feedback is consistent. **Where it falls short**

The price is the main sticking point. Per ounce, this runs notably higher than classic brands like Clabber Girl or Rumford’s aluminum-free option. If you bake once a week, the difference is negligible. If you’re running a small bakery or feeding a large family, you’ll feel it. The 10-ounce size is also the ceiling — there’s no bulk option available, so heavy users will be reordering frequently.

The lid, while functional, isn’t airtight. If you store it near a humid stove or leave it open, it will clump within a few weeks. Transfer to an airtight jar if you bake infrequently.

**Who should buy this**

- Home bakers who notice the metallic aftertaste in cheaper powders and want a clean-flavored alternative
- Anyone baking for health-conscious households where aluminum additives are a concern
- Gluten-free bakers — the reliable double-action helps compensate for the lack of gluten structure
- Skip it if you bake professionally in volume or need a cost-per-batch below what this offers

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this the same as Rumford’s aluminum-free powder?**
Functionally similar — both use phosphate-based acids. Rumford’s is slightly cheaper per ounce in some regions. The 365 version is a solid alternative if you’re already shopping Whole Foods or Amazon.

**Q: How long does it stay active after opening?**
Stored properly in a cool, dry place with the lid sealed, expect 6-12 months of full potency. Test by mixing a teaspoon with hot water — if it bubbles vigorously, it’s still good.

**Q: Can I use this in recipes that call for regular baking powder?**
Yes, it’s a direct 1:1 substitute. If the recipe calls for aluminum-based powder, this works exactly the same — just without the metallic aftertaste.
