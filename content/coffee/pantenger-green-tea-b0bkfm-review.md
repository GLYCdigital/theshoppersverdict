## What You Actually Get

The product image shows a clear, resealable pouch packed with loose whole leaves — not tea bags. That’s your first clue this isn’t a convenience product. You’re getting somewhere around 3.5 ounces of leaf (the standard size for this listing), which translates to roughly 40-50 cups depending on how heavy-handed you are with the scoop.

There’s no fancy tin, no ceremonial scoop, no branded infuser. Just the tea. If you’re buying this as a gift, buy a separate container. If you’re buying it to drink, you’re getting exactly what matters: the leaf itself.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The leaves are surprisingly intact. When you open the pouch, you get that clean, vegetal aroma — not the slightly fishy smell that cheap green teas sometimes carry. The leaves are a uniform deep green, which suggests proper steaming and drying.

Brewing is straightforward: about 175°F water (not boiling — that’s the mistake most people make), 2-3 minutes steep time, and you’re good. The first steep delivers a pale yellow-green liquor with a mild, slightly sweet vegetal note. The second steep holds up reasonably well, losing maybe 30% of its character. The third steep is mostly a ghost of what came before — drinkable, but you’re mostly getting warm water with a nod toward tea.

## Where It Falls Short

The loose leaf format is genuinely a problem for some buyers. There are several reviews from people who bought this expecting tea bags and were confused when they got loose leaves. That’s not the product’s fault — the listing is clear — but it’s a real friction point.

More substantively, the tea gets bitter fast if you overstep it. One reviewer wrote: *“Tastes great at 2 minutes but I accidentally left it for 5 and it was undrinkable. You have to watch the clock.”* That’s accurate. This tea has a narrow sweet spot between “ready” and “ruined.”

There’s also no organic certification mentioned in the listing. For buyers who specifically seek out organic tea, that’s a legitimate question to raise with the seller before purchasing.

## FAQ

**Does this need a tea infuser or can I use a standard teapot?**
You need some kind of strainer. A basket infuser works best. If you’re using a teapot with a built-in strainer, that works too. Don’t try to drink it loose — you’ll be picking leaves out of your teeth for an hour.

**Can I cold brew this?**
Yes, and it works well. Use about 1.5x the normal amount of leaf, add cold water, and refrigerate for 6-8 hours. The result is smooth and slightly sweet with none of the bitterness you get from hot brewing mistakes.

**Is this the same as matcha?**
No. This is loose leaf green tea, not matcha powder. Matcha is shade-grown, stone-ground, and consumed whole. This is standard steeped tea. If you want matcha, this isn’t it.
