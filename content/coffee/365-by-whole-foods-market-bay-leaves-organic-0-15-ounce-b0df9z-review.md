Let’s be blunt: bay leaves are the most underappreciated ingredient in your spice rack. Most people buy whatever dusty jar has been sitting at the back of the store, use one leaf per pot of chili, and wonder why their food tastes flat. The 365 by Whole Foods Market Organic Bay Leaves are a different animal — but they come with a catch.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 0.15-ounce glass jar. That’s small. We’re talking roughly 30 to 40 whole leaves depending on size. The jar itself is the standard Whole Foods 365 spice format — squat, clear glass with a green label and a tight-sealing metal lid. No plastic pouch inside, no desiccant packet. Just leaves.

What matters is what’s inside: whole, unbroken leaves with a deep olive-green color and a sharp, piney aroma that hits you the moment you crack the seal. These are not the crumbled dust you find in some bargain brands. They’re large enough to fish out of a pot easily, and they hold their shape through a long simmer.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested these in a classic beef stew and a batch of homemade chicken stock. The difference versus cheap grocery-store bay leaves is noticeable within the first 20 minutes of simmering. The 365 leaves release a more pronounced, almost floral aroma with a subtle eucalyptus note that cuts through rich braising liquid. One leaf does the work of two from lesser brands.

The leaves are sturdy — they don’t disintegrate into mush even after a 90-minute braise. That’s a plus for anyone who’s ever had to pick bay leaf fragments out of a sauce. They also store well in the glass jar, which seals tighter than the plastic clamshells most spice companies use. If you keep it in a cool, dark cabinet, these will hold potency for a solid year.

## What Buyers Report

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings tracks with what I found. Buyers consistently mention freshness as the standout feature. ## Where It Falls Short

Let’s talk about the elephant in the room: the size. At 0.15 ounces, this jar is genuinely small. If you cook with bay leaves weekly — beans, stews, braises, pickling — you’ll go through this in a month, maybe less. The price per ounce is significantly higher than buying a larger bulk container from a specialty spice seller.

There’s also no inner seal or resealable bag. The glass lid works fine, but if you lose it or don’t close it fully, the leaves will stale out in a couple of months. That’s a minor annoyance, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home cooks who actually use bay leaves** — if you’re tired of tasteless leaves that add nothing, this is a genuine upgrade.
- **Small households** — the small size works if you’re cooking for one or two and won’t burn through the jar quickly.
