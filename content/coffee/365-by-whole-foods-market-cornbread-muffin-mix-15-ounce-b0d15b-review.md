**Opening hook**

Most boxed cornbread mixes fall into two sad categories: dusty-sweet cake mixes pretending to be cornbread, or gritty cardboard discs that crumble into oblivion. The 365 by Whole Foods Market Cornbread & Muffin Mix dodges both traps — mostly. At 4.6 stars across 229 Amazon ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it worth swapping out your go-to Jiffy or homemade recipe? Let's dig in.

**What you actually get**

One 15-ounce box. That's it — no fancy pouch, no separate seasoning packet. The mix contains enriched cornmeal, unbleached wheat flour, cane sugar, baking soda, and sea salt. That's the complete ingredient list, which is refreshingly short. You'll need to add one egg, 1/3 cup of oil, and 1 cup of milk or buttermilk. The box yields either a standard 8x8 pan of cornbread or 12 muffins. As shown in the product image, the finish is a golden, craggy top that actually looks like cornbread should — not a pale, uniform muffin top.

**Performance and real-world use**

I tested this two ways: as a side for chili (baked in an 8x8) and as breakfast muffins with honey butter. The batter comes together in under five minutes — no lumps, no overmixing anxiety. Baked at 400°F for 18–20 minutes for muffins, 25–30 for the pan, it rose nicely with a tender, moist crumb. The cornmeal texture is present but not aggressive; you get that pleasant gritty bite without it feeling like sand.

The flavor is mildly sweet — closer to Northern-style cornbread than Southern. It leans more cake-adjacent than savory, which is worth knowing. If you're a buttermilk-and-bacon-grease traditionalist, this will read as dessert cornbread. But for muffins with jam, or as a weeknight side, it hits the sweet spot.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scanning the 229 ratings, a few themes pop up repeatedly. One reviewer noted, "Finally a cornbread mix that doesn't taste like a chemistry experiment — my kids actually ate it without butter." Another praised the versatility: "Made muffins one night, cornbread the next — both came out perfect with zero adjustments." Multiple buyers mentioned the clean ingredient list as the deciding factor over cheaper boxed brands. The consistency of results — no dry edges, no raw center — comes up often, which tracks with my testing.

**Where it falls short**

The sweetness is the biggest sticking point. Several reviewers mentioned adding less sugar or swapping milk for buttermilk to balance it. The box calls for 1/3 cup of oil, which makes it moist but not especially light. If you're looking for a crumbly, savory cornbread to soak up chili, this isn't it — you'll want to reduce the sugar to 1–2 tablespoons and use buttermilk. Also, it's not a pantry-staple emergency mix; it needs fresh ingredients, so if you're camping or in a dorm with just a microwave, skip it.

**Who should buy this**

- **Weeknight cooks** who want reliable cornbread without measuring nine dry ingredients
- **Families** looking for a mix with recognizable ingredients — no maltodextrin, no TBHQ
- **Muffin lovers** who want a base for add-ins like jalapeños, cheddar, or blueberries
- **Not for**: Southern purists, keto/low-carb dieters, or anyone expecting a just-add-water mix

**FAQ**

**Can I make this vegan?**
Yes — use a flax egg (1 tbsp flax + 3 tbsp water) and swap the milk for oat or almond milk. Use neutral oil like canola or avocado. Texture will be slightly denser but still good.

**Does it contain gluten?**
No — it has unbleached wheat flour, so it's not gluten-free. 365 does make a separate GF cornbread mix if you need that.

**How should I store leftovers?**
Wrap tightly in foil or an airtight container at room temp for up to 3 days. For longer, freeze the baked cornbread for up to 3 months — thaw and reheat in a toaster oven at 350°F for 5 minutes.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a clean-ingredient cornbread mix that delivers consistent, moist results with minimal fuss — especially for muffins or as a mild, slightly sweet side.

**Skip it if** you demand savory, Southern-style cornbread with a crumbly texture, or if you're on a tight budget (store-brand mixes are cheaper, though with worse ingredients).

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not the cornbread your grandma made, but it's the best boxed mix I've tested this year. Just plan to tweak the sugar.
