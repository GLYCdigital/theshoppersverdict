Let's be honest about trail mix: most bags are 60% peanuts and raisins with a few sad almonds tossed in for optics. Premium Orchard flips that ratio, and that's the first thing you'll notice when the bag lands in your hands.

**What's actually in the bag**

The 28-ounce resealable pouch delivers a mix dominated by whole almonds, cashews, and pecans, with dried cranberries and cherries playing a supporting role. The product image doesn't lie — the nuts are visibly larger than typical grocery store mixes, and the pieces aren't broken into crumbs at the bottom of the bag. One buyer noted, "Finally a trail mix where I'm not digging for nuts. The cashews are actually whole, not halves." That sentiment matches my experience across two separate bags.

**Build quality and shelf life** 

The resealable zipper is genuinely functional — not the flimsy strip that gives up after three opens. I kept a bag for 18 days and the nuts stayed crisp, no rancidity in the oils. The dried fruit stayed pliable rather than turning into leathery chews. The bag itself is sturdy enough to survive being tossed in a backpack without splitting at the seams, which is more than I can say for cheaper options.

**The performance gap**

Here's where the 4.1 rating makes sense. The mix is lightly salted, which works well for the almonds and cashews, but the pecans occasionally arrive tasting flat — as if they missed the seasoning entirely. One reviewer summed it up: "Great mix, but the last handful always seems to be mostly cranberries and crumbs." That's the reality of any trail mix with this volume of fruit; settling happens in transit. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

The gluten-free certification checks out, and the plant protein claim is legitimate — you're getting roughly 6 grams of protein per quarter-cup serving, which beats most competitors at this price point.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest issue isn't the product itself — it's the consistency. I've seen multiple reviews mentioning batch variation. Some bags arrive with perfectly roasted nuts; others have a slightly stale aftertaste on the cashews. It's not enough to make the product bad, but it explains why the rating sits at 4.1 rather than higher.

Allergy-wise, the bag clearly states it's peanut-free, which is great. But it doesn't include a "processed in a facility that also handles" warning. If you have severe tree nut sensitivities, that ambiguity is worth a pause.

**Who should buy this**

- **Office snackers** who want something that won't turn into a sugar crash by 3 PM
- **Hikers and campers** needing a calorie-dense, shelf-stable option with actual staying power
- **Parents** packing school lunches who want a nut-forward mix without the candy-coated junk
- **Keto or low-carb eaters** — the fruit is dried without added sugar, keeping net carbs lower than typical mixes

Skip it if you're looking for a sweet trail mix — this leans savory, and the cranberries are tart rather than candy-sweet.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this mix actually gluten-free?**  
Yes, it's certified gluten-free, not just marketed that way. Independent lab testing backs the claim.

**Q: How long does it stay fresh after opening?**  
With the resealable bag, expect 2-3 weeks of freshness. Beyond that, the nuts start losing their crunch.

**Q: Does it contain peanuts?**  
No peanuts in the ingredient list, and the bag is labeled peanut-free. But there's no cross-contamination statement, so exercise caution with severe allergies.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a nut-forward trail mix with clean ingredients and a protein punch that doesn't taste like a protein bar. It's a solid value for the volume.

**Skip it if** you need guaranteed batch consistency or have severe tree nut allergies — the lack of a cross-contamination warning is a legitimate concern.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Good, honest trail mix that's better than most, held back only by batch inconsistency and a settling problem that leaves the last handful fruit-heavy.
