Let's be direct: most spice bundles on Amazon are a trap. You get one giant bag of dusty cassia bark, some broken star anise that's lost its oil, and cloves that smell more like cardboard than clove. The Fruidles 3-pack isn't that. Here's what you're actually getting.

**What's in the bundle**

Three separate resealable pouches — not one mixed bag. You get Ceylon cinnamon sticks, whole star anise, and whole cloves. The cinnamon is the standout. If you've only ever used cassia from the grocery store, the difference is immediate when you open the pouch: it's lighter in color, sweeter in smell, and rolls into thin layers rather than the thick, hard bark you're used to. The star anise pods are intact — eight-pointed stars, not crumbled fragments. The cloves are small and oil-rich, the kind that leaves a tingle on your tongue if you bite into one.

**How it performs in coffee**

This is coffee gear, so let's talk about what matters. I dropped a cinnamon stick and two star anise pods into a French press with coarse-ground beans, let it steep four minutes, and poured. The cinnamon came through as a soft, sweet warmth — not the harsh, woody bite you get from cassia. The anise added a subtle licorice note in the background, not a cough-drop explosion. One stick and one pod is enough for a full 8-cup pot. For cold brew, I steeped a stick and three cloves overnight — the clove stayed restrained, which surprised me, because whole cloves usually dominate everything they touch.

**Where it falls short**

No batch date on the packaging. That's the honest flaw. If you order from a high-turnover seller, you're fine. If it's been sitting in a warehouse for six months, the cloves will still be fine but the cinnamon will lose some of its volatile oils. The clove quantity is also modest — roughly two ounces — which is fine for occasional mulled wine or baking, but if you're making chai weekly, you'll burn through it in a month. And the cinnamon sticks are shorter than some competitors — averaging 2-3 inches, which is fine for most uses but awkward if you want a tall garnishing stick in a drink.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the cinnamon really Ceylon, or is it labeled Ceylon but actually cassia?**
A: It's genuine Ceylon — the thin, flaky layers and sweet aroma are unmistakable. Cassia is hard, thick, and spicy-hot; this is soft and delicate. You can easily tell them apart side by side.

**Q: How much should I use in coffee?**
A: Start with one stick per 8 cups. If you want a stronger cinnamon presence, crack the stick in half before adding — the exposed inner surface releases more oil. For cold brew, one stick overnight gives a gentle sweetness without bitterness.

**Q: Do the resealable pouches actually keep the spices fresh?**
A: Yes, for reasonable use. They're thick gauge plastic with a proper zipper seal, not the flimsy tear-open bags you usually get. If you're storing longer than three months, transfer to glass jars — the pouches are good, but not airtight forever.
