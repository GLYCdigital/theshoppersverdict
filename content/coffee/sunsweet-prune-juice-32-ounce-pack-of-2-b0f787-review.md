## What You Actually Get

The pack ships with two 32-ounce plastic bottles. That's 64 ounces total, which breaks down to eight 8-ounce servings per bottle. The ingredient list is refreshingly boring: 100% prune juice from concentrate, water, and nothing else. No added sugar, no preservatives, no artificial anything.

As shown in the product image, the bottles are standard screw-cap plastic — nothing fancy, but they pour cleanly and reseal tight. The juice itself is a deep, dark amber with a thick, syrupy mouthfeel that tells you this isn't watered-down juice.

## How It Performs (The Part That Matters)

Prune juice has one job, and Sunsweet nails it. The active ingredient is sorbitol, a naturally occurring sugar alcohol that draws water into the colon. One 8-ounce glass on an empty stomach typically produces results within 30 minutes to 2 hours. That's the physics of it, and it works consistently.

The taste is where you need to check your expectations. This is not "prune-flavored apple juice." It's intensely tart and earthy, with a concentrated plum flavor that coats your mouth. Some reviewers describe it as "an acquired taste," which is diplomatic. Others say it's "surprisingly good over ice." I'd put it this way: if you've only had prune juice from a diner breakfast menu, this is twice as concentrated and three times as assertive.

Temperature matters. Ice-cold, it's far more palatable than room temperature. Mixing it 50/50 with water or seltzer cuts the intensity without killing the effect. And here's a pro tip from the review threads: warm it up slightly (not hot, just warm) and it goes down smoother for some people.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the sugar content: one 8-ounce serving has 36 grams of naturally occurring sugar. That's not added sugar, but it's still a significant glycemic load if you're diabetic or watching carbs. If you're using this daily, budget for it.

Second, the taste genuinely turns some people off. If you're already squeamish about prune juice, this concentrated version won't convert you. Start with a 4-ounce serving mixed with apple juice if you're new to it.

Third, once opened, a bottle lasts about 3 weeks in the fridge. If you're a light user, that's a real constraint — you might not finish both bottles before they start to lose freshness.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should I drink for constipation relief?**
Start with 4 ounces (half a glass) on an empty stomach. If no results within 4 hours, try the full 8 ounces. Don't exceed 16 ounces in a day — you'll get cramps.

**Q: Does this need to be refrigerated before opening?**
No. It's shelf-stable until opened. After opening, refrigerate and use within 3 weeks.

**Q: Is this safe for daily use?**
For most people, yes. But if you're taking prescription laxatives, have kidney issues, or are diabetic, check with your doctor first — the potassium and sugar content are significant.
