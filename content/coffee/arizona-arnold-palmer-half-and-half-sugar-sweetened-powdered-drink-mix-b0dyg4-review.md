I'll be straight with you: I was skeptical that a powdered version of AriZona's Arnold Palmer could hold a candle to the iconic bottled drink. The 4.2-star rating across 210 reviews suggested I wasn't alone in wondering. After testing it across multiple batches — cold, over ice, and in a few experiments — here's what I found.

**What's actually in the canister**

You get a single plastic tub of powder. The label says it makes 8 quarts, which is roughly 16 standard 16-ounce glasses. That's the equivalent of about 10-12 bottles of the ready-to-drink version. There's no scoop included, which is mildly annoying — you'll want a tablespoon handy for consistent measuring. The powder itself is fine and light-colored, almost like a pale iced tea mix.

**The taste test: Does it nail the Half and Half?**

Yes and no. The tea-lemonade balance is genuinely impressive. It captures that distinctive AriZona flavor profile — the brisk black tea cut with bright, tart lemonade — better than I expected from a powder. One reviewer nailed it: "Tastes just like the bottle, honestly could not tell the difference in a blind taste test."

But here's the catch: the sweetness. The label's recommended ratio produces a drink that's noticeably sweeter than the bottled version. Multiple reviewers flagged this, with one writing, "It's very sweet — I use about 30% less powder than directed." I tested it both ways, and they're right. At the full strength, it's cloying. At about two-thirds strength, it's nearly indistinguishable from the original.

**Real-world performance**

The powder dissolves well in cold water — that's not nothing. Many drink mixes require warm water or aggressive shaking; this one mixes cleanly with a spoon in about 30 seconds. That said, it settles out after sitting for 15-20 minutes. You'll see a fine layer of sediment at the bottom of your glass. It's not harmful, but if you're the type to make a pitcher and sip all day, budget for a stir before each pour.

The caffeine content is minimal — essentially trace. This isn't a substitute for your morning coffee or tea ritual. It's a leisure drink, best for lunch, afternoon, or with a meal.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, value: "A fraction of the price of buying bottles, and my kids go through it fast." Second, convenience: the canister is compact, resealable, and doesn't need refrigeration. Third, taste fidelity: most reviewers agree it's close enough to the original that the difference isn't worth paying extra for bottles.

**Where it falls short**

The sweetness is the biggest issue, and it's not minor. If you follow the label directions, you'll likely find it too sugary. That's a design flaw, not a preference issue — the bottled version isn't this sweet. Also, the lack of a scoop is a genuine oversight. And if you're expecting a caffeine lift, you'll be disappointed. This is flavored refreshment, not an energy source.

**Who should buy this**

- **Cost-conscious families** who go through Arnold Palmer by the gallon and want to cut their drink budget substantially
- **Iced tea and lemonade fans** who like the flavor but want portion control — you can make a single glass instead of opening a bottle
- **Campers, boaters, or anyone without fridge space** — the powder is shelf-stable and compact

**Skip it if** you're sensitive to sweetness (you'll be adjusting every batch) or you're looking for a caffeinated beverage.

**FAQ**

**Q: How much powder do I use per glass?**
A: The label suggests 2 tablespoons per 8 ounces, but try 1.5 tablespoons first. Most reviewers prefer it slightly under-strength to control the sweetness.

**Q: Does it taste like the bottled Arnold Palmer?**
A: At adjusted strength, yes — very close. At full label strength, it's noticeably sweeter than the bottle.

**Q: Can I make it hot like tea?**
A: Technically yes, but it's designed for cold preparation. Hot, the lemonade flavor becomes overwhelming. Stick with cold or over ice.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want the Arnold Palmer flavor at a fraction of the bottled cost and don't mind adjusting the ratio to your sweetness preference.

**Skip it if** you're picky about exact taste replication or need caffeine.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Great value and near-authentic flavor, docked points for the over-sweetened label ratio and missing scoop.
