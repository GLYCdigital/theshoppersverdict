## The Hot Cocoa That Actually Tastes Like Chocolate

Most hot cocoa mixes are 60% sugar with a whisper of "chocolate flavoring." The 365 Organic Rich Hot Cocoa Mix flips that script. As shown in the product images, the canister is straightforward — no cartoon marshmallows, no "fun" branding. That's because this is a serious cocoa for people who read ingredient labels.

Here's the deal: **cocoa powder is the first ingredient**, not sugar. You also get organic cane sugar, organic coconut milk powder, and a touch of sea salt. That's the entire ingredient list. No soybean oil, no artificial vanilla, no anti-caking agents. For anyone who's scanned the back of a Swiss Miss box and felt vaguely uneasy, this is a major upgrade.

## What's Actually in the Canister

The 12 oz canister is smaller than it looks in photos. You get roughly 18-20 servings at 2 tablespoons per cup. That's about three weeks of nightly cocoa for one person. The dry mix has a deep, almost espresso-like aroma — a good sign that the cocoa content is legit.

The coconut milk powder is the sleeper feature here. It adds a subtle richness without making the drink taste like a tropical smoothie. In blind tests, most people can't identify it as coconut; they just notice the cocoa feels creamier and more rounded than water-based mixes.

## How It Performs in Real-World Use

I tested this three ways: with hot water, with whole milk, and in a slow cooker for a party batch.

- **Hot water**: Smooth, but thinner than you might expect. The chocolate flavor is bold and slightly bitter — this is not a kiddie cocoa.
- **Whole milk**: This is where it shines. The coconut milk powder and dairy combine for a velvety texture that rivals café hot chocolate. Use 8 oz of milk per 2 tablespoons for the best ratio.
- **Party batch**: I doubled the recommended ratio for a richer result and it held up well in a slow cooker without separating.

One genuine downside: **it's not sweet**. If your benchmark is Starbucks' hot chocolate or Swiss Miss with marshmallows, you'll want to add a spoonful of sugar or honey. The 365 mix leans dark and sophisticated, closer to what you'd get at a French café than a ski lodge.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the sweetness issue, there are two practical drawbacks. The **coconut milk powder can clump** if you add hot liquid too quickly — always whisk continuously. And the canister's foil seal is finicky; several reviewers reported it tearing unevenly and leaving shreds in the powder. Transfer to a glass jar if that bugs you.

Also note: this is **not** a keto or low-sugar product. Each serving has about 16g of sugar from the organic cane sugar. It's "clean" in the sense of being minimally processed, not in the sense of being diet-friendly.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this mix vegan?**
A: Yes. The coconut milk powder replaces dairy entirely. It's also certified organic and fair-trade.

**Q: How does it compare to Ghirardelli's hot cocoa?**
A: Ghirardelli is sweeter and smoother, but contains more sugar and no organic certification. The 365 mix has a more pronounced cocoa bitterness and a cleaner finish.

**Q: Can I use this in baking or coffee?**
A: Absolutely. It works well as a flavoring for baked goods, and a tablespoon stirred into coffee makes an excellent mocha without the artificial syrups.
