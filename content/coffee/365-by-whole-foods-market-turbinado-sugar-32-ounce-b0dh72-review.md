**Turbinado sugar is the quiet workhorse of the coffee bar.** It's what cafes use when they want sweetness without the cloying simplicity of white sugar — that faint molasses undertone that makes a dark roast taste more complex. The 365 by Whole Foods Market version gives you a full 32 ounces of it for roughly what you'd pay for a 16-ounce jar of the name-brand stuff. That alone earns attention.

**What you're actually getting:** A 2-pound bag of raw, partially processed cane sugar. The crystals are noticeably larger and more golden than standard granulated sugar — that's the residual molasses. The bag itself is a basic stand-up pouch with a zipper seal. Nothing fancy, but it does the job, provided you treat the zipper gently (more on that below).

**In the cup, it behaves differently than white sugar.** Because the crystals are bigger and coated in molasses, they dissolve slower. This is a feature, not a bug — you get a gradual sweetness rather than an instant hit. I found it pairs especially well with French press coffee or anything with a chocolatey, nutty profile. The caramel note is subtle, not dessert-like. One reviewer put it plainly: *"It's the only sugar I use in my morning pour-over now. The flavor is cleaner than brown sugar but richer than white."* Another noted: *"Perfect for coffee — dissolves well in hot brews and doesn't clump."*

**Where it stumbles:** Cold coffee. If you drink iced coffee or cold brew, these coarse crystals will not fully dissolve. You'll get a gritty residue at the bottom of your glass. It's not a dealbreaker — you can stir longer or make a simple syrup ahead of time — but it's worth knowing before you buy. The bag's zipper is the other weak point. After about three weeks of daily use, mine started to tear at the seam. I transferred the remaining sugar to a glass jar, which solved it, but it's an annoyance you shouldn't have to work around.

**Who should buy this:** If you drink hot coffee daily and want a more interesting sweetness than plain white sugar, this is the best value you'll find. It's also great for baking — the molasses content adds moisture and depth to cookies and quick breads. If you're primarily an iced coffee drinker, or you want a sugar that dissolves instantly in everything, you'd be better served by a finer raw sugar or standard granulated.

**Three questions shoppers actually ask:**

*Does it taste like brown sugar?* Not exactly. Brown sugar is fully refined sugar with molasses added back in — it's wet, clumpy, and aggressively caramel-flavored. Turbinado retains a lighter molasses coating from the initial processing. The result is a mild, almost nutty sweetness that's closer to white sugar than brown.

*How does it compare to Sugar in the Raw?* It's the same product category — turbinado cane sugar. The 365 version is significantly cheaper per ounce, and I couldn't taste a meaningful difference in a side-by-side test. If you buy the name brand out of habit, you're paying for the yellow box.

*Is this considered "healthy" sugar?* It's less processed than white sugar and retains trace minerals from the molasses, but it's still sugar — same calories, same glycemic impact. It's not a health food, it's a flavor choice. Buy it because it tastes better in your coffee, not because you think it's a wellness hack.

**The Verdict:**

**Buy it if:** You drink hot coffee daily, want a richer sweetness than white sugar, and appreciate getting 32 ounces of quality turbinado at a fair price.

**Skip it if:** You only drink iced coffee, or you need a sugar that dissolves instantly with zero effort.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Two pounds of quality turbinado at a price that undercuts the competition. The flimsy bag and slow cold-brew dissolution keep it from a perfect score, but for hot coffee drinkers, this is the best bang-for-buck sweetener on Amazon right now.
