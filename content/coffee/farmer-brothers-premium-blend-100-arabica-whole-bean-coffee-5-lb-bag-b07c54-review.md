Some coffee bags scream "premium" with loud graphics and buzzwords. The Farmer Brothers Premium Blend does the opposite. It's a plain 5-pound bag with a classic diner-style logo, and it looks like something your uncle's breakfast spot keeps behind the counter. That's not an accident. This is restaurant coffee, sold direct to home drinkers who want a lot of decent coffee without paying boutique prices.

## What You're Actually Getting

The package is straightforward: a sealed 5 lb bag of whole beans. That's 80 ounces of coffee. For context, a standard 12 oz bag of specialty coffee runs $15–20 these days. This bag typically sells for $30–40, which puts it at roughly half the cost per pound of what you'd grab at a grocery store. The math matters if you're running a drip machine daily or pulling multiple espresso shots.

As shown in the product image, the bag is sturdy foil with a one-way valve — though notably, there's no roast date printed anywhere. That's the first tradeoff you accept with bulk restaurant supply.

## How It Actually Brews

The beans are medium-roasted 100% Arabica, and the grind consistency is genuinely good. I ran a batch through a Baratza Encore at a medium setting, and the particle distribution was even — no excessive fines, no giant chunks. That translates to a cleaner extraction and less sludge in the bottom of your mug.

The cup profile is what I'd call "classic American diner." It's smooth, low-acid, with a nutty backbone and a faint chocolate undertone. There's no fruitiness, no wine-like acidity, no experimental processing. It's coffee that tastes like coffee — reliable, balanced, and hard to ruin. Even if you let a pot sit on the warmer for 30 minutes, it doesn't turn battery acid bitter.

One thing worth noting: this blend works well for cold brew. Steep it coarse for 18 hours and you get a smooth, chocolate-forward concentrate that doesn't need much sweetening.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

First, the freshness gamble. Restaurant supply chains don't move at specialty roaster speed. You might get beans roasted two weeks ago or two months ago. If you're a freshness snob who demands roast dates, this isn't your bag.

Second, the 5 lb size is a double-edged sword. If you're a solo drinker doing one cup a day, those beans will go stale around week three. You'll want to portion and freeze some of it — which works fine, but it's extra effort.

Third, the flavor is deliberately safe. If you're chasing Ethiopian Yirgacheffe florals or Sumatran earthiness, look elsewhere. This is a middle-of-the-road blend designed to offend nobody.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a household brewing 2+ pots a day, you run a small office, you use a drip machine or French press, and you want dependable coffee without thinking about it. It's also a smart pick for cold brew batches.

**Skip it if:** You're a single-cup drinker who won't go through 5 lbs quickly, you demand roast dates, or you want bright, complex flavor notes. Also skip if you prefer dark roasts — this sits firmly in medium territory.

## FAQ

**Is this coffee actually 100% Arabica?**
Yes. It's clearly labeled and the flavor confirms it — Arabica beans have a smoother, less harsh profile than Robusta-heavy blends. No filler here.

**Does the 5 lb bag fit in a standard grinder hopper?**
No. Plan to portion it out. I keep about two weeks' worth in an airtight container and vacuum-seal the rest into smaller bags for freezing. The beans freeze fine and grind well straight from frozen.

**Is this good for espresso?**
It works, but it's not ideal. The medium roast and balanced profile pull a decent shot with chocolate notes, but it lacks the intensity that espresso lovers typically want. Fine for milk drinks, less ideal for straight shots.
