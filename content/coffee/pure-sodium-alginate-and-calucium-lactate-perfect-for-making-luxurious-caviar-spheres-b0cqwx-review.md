## Performance and Build Quality

The powders themselves perform as expected. The sodium alginate dissolves cleanly when you use a blender or immersion blender — skip hand-whisking or you'll battle frustrating lumps. One buyer noted, "I've tried three different brands and this one consistently gives me the clearest spheres without that bitter aftertaste." That tracks with the 4.4 average.

The calcium lactate is where patience matters. It's finely milled but stubborn — expect to whisk for several minutes in cold water. A warm water bath speeds this up dramatically (around 40°C/104°F works best). Several reviewers mentioned this exact workaround, so it's a known quirk, not a dealbreaker.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a measuring spoon is genuinely annoying. Spherification is chemistry — you need precision. Guessing ratios produces mushy blobs or rock-hard spheres. If you don't own a 0.1g-accurate scale, budget for one. That's an extra cost the listing doesn't mention upfront.

Also, the calcium lactate powder can clump if you store it carelessly. The resealable pouch helps, but keep it in a cool, dry cabinet. Humidity kills this product's shelf life.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this for reverse spherification of acidic liquids like citrus juice?**
A: Yes — that's exactly what the calcium lactate formulation is designed for. Basic spherification with high-acid liquids will fail, but the reverse method works reliably. The recipe card covers this.

**Q: How long do the spheres hold their shape?**
A: In a serving liquid, 20–30 minutes before they start softening. In plain water, longer. If you need them stable for hours, rinse them after setting and keep them in the same liquid you used to make them.

**Q: Is this safe for people with seafood allergies?**
A: Sodium alginate comes from brown seaweed, not shellfish. Most people with shellfish allergies tolerate it fine, but if you have a severe seaweed sensitivity, consult your doctor first.
