**Fresh Finest Bulk 5LB Ground Cinnamon Powder – The Bulk Spice That Actually Tastes Like Cinnamon**

Most bulk spices are a gamble. You buy a five-pound bag of something, get it home, open it up, and it smells like sawdust with good intentions. That is not what's happening with Fresh Finest's 5LB ground cinnamon. I've been through enough cinnamon in the last year — between overnight oats, coffee dusting, and a baking habit that borders on compulsive — to know the difference between "cinnamon" and "cinnamon-like powder." This is the former.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

Here's where this cinnamon separates itself from the grocery store stuff: potency. I made a batch of cinnamon rolls that called for two tablespoons. Using this powder, it was almost too much — the flavor was deep, slightly sweet, with that warm, woody complexity that real Ceylon-adjacent cinnamon brings. The grind is fine and consistent, no gritty bits, no stems. It dissolves cleanly into batters and doesn't leave that weird chalky residue on top of coffee that cheap cinnamon does. I've also used it in a dry rub for pork shoulder, and the spice held its own against smoked paprika and brown sugar — that's the test of a quality cinnamon.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest tradeoffs, because they exist.

First, the dust factor. This is a fine powder, and when you pour it directly from the bag, it puffs up. Open it slowly, and don't try to scoop while the bag is fully open — you'll end up with cinnamon on your counter, your shirt, and inexplicably, your face.

Second, there's no measuring scoop. For a 5-pound bag, including a simple scoop would cost them pennies. You'll need to buy one or use your own.

Third — and this matters if you're not a heavy user — this is a *lot* of cinnamon. The shelf life is solid (roughly 2-3 years stored properly), but if you use cinnamon once a month, you're better off with a smaller quantity. This is for people who bake weekly, run a food business, or make their own spice blends.

**FAQ**

**Is this Ceylon cinnamon or cassia?**
It's a cassia blend, which is the standard for baking in the U.S. It's stronger and more pungent than Ceylon. If you need pure Ceylon for health reasons or specific recipes, this isn't it. If you want bold flavor, this is.

**How should I store a 5-pound bag?**
Keep it in the resealable bag, squeeze out excess air, and store in a cool, dark cabinet away from the stove or dishwasher heat. Do not refrigerate — condensation will cause clumping.

**Is this safe for someone with celiac disease?**
The label indicates a gluten-free facility handling, and the ingredient list is pure cinnamon. However, if you have severe sensitivities, contact the manufacturer to confirm cross-contamination protocols before purchasing.
