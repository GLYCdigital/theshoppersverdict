Let's be upfront: veggie straws are not health food. They're a snack that makes you *feel* like you're eating vegetables while delivering mostly potato and corn flour. But within that category, the 365 by Whole Foods Market Original Veggie Straws punch well above their weight. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this store-brand bag has quietly become the benchmark for what a mass-market veggie straw should be.

## What's actually in the bag

You get one 6-ounce bag of straws made from potato flour, corn flour, and a blend of spinach, tomato, and beetroot powders (that's where the orange-and-green coloring comes from — no artificial dyes). Each straw is about 2.5 inches long, hollow in the center, and salted lightly. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: flours, vegetable powders, sunflower or safflower oil, salt, and a bit of sugar. Nothing you need a chemistry degree to pronounce.

## Real-world performance

The first thing you'll notice is the texture. These straws are *crisp* — not hard, not stale, not chewy. The crunch has a clean snap that doesn't turn into shards in your mouth like some cheaper brands. They hold up well in a lunchbox for several hours without going limp, and they don't leave that greasy film on your fingertips that you get from other popular straw brands. That's because Whole Foods uses a lighter hand with the oil.

Flavor-wise, think "salted potato chip with a whisper of earthiness." The vegetable powders add a subtle savory depth rather than a pronounced "I'm eating a salad" taste. If you're expecting the straws to taste strongly of spinach or tomato, you'll be disappointed — they taste mostly like a mild, slightly sweet potato snack. That's fine. It's what makes them versatile enough to pair with hummus, dip, or just eat straight from the bag.

## What buyers keep saying

The praise in the reviews clusters around two themes. First, freshness: *"These are always crunchy and never stale, even when I order them in bulk,"* one reviewer noted. Second, the ingredient transparency — shoppers specifically call out that there's no artificial coloring, which matters for parents. Several reviewers mention switching from the big-name competitor brands after trying these, citing the cleaner taste and less greasy finish.

## Where it falls short

Two honest complaints appear repeatedly. The salt level is modest. If you're accustomed to aggressively seasoned snacks, these will taste almost bland. A shake of sea salt or a dip in something salty fixes it, but it's a real adjustment.

Second, the bag arrives looking about two-thirds full. Veggie straws are fragile, and they settle significantly during shipping. You're still getting the full 6 ounces — the bag is just larger than it needs to be. It's a packaging choice that protects the product but feels slightly deceptive on first unboxing.

## Who should buy these

- **Snackers who find standard veggie straws too oily** and want something crisper and cleaner-tasting
- **Anyone watching sodium** but still craving something crunchy and salty
- **Hummus and dip enthusiasts** — the sturdy straw shape makes an excellent scooping vehicle

## FAQ

**Q: Are these actually healthy?**
They're healthier than potato chips — less fat, fewer calories per serving, and some actual vegetable content. But the first two ingredients are potato and corn flour. Treat them as a snack, not a serving of vegetables.

**Q: How do they compare to Sensible Portions or other brands?**
They're less oily, slightly less salty, and have a cleaner ingredient list. The crunch is comparable. If you prefer more salt, stick with the competitors.

**Q: Do they stay fresh after opening?**
Yes, if you seal the bag properly. They'll hold crunch for 5-7 days. Beyond that, they start to soften. The 6-ounce size is well-suited to finishing within that window.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely crisp, less-greasy veggie straw with a short ingredient list and honest flavor. **Skip it if** you need heavy salt or expect these to taste strongly of vegetables — they're a mild, pleasant snack, not a health supplement.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A store-brand snack that outperforms its pricier competitors on texture and ingredient quality. The salt level is the only real tradeoff, and that's easily adjusted. Worth a spot in your pantry.
