Let's cut through the cooking oil aisle confusion. You've seen the shelf stacked with olive, canola, and "vegetable" blends. Most of them break down when you crank the heat. Kevala's Avocado Oil solves that specific problem — and a few others you didn't know you had.

As shown in the product image, this is a straightforward, no-nonsense bottle. Single ingredient: avocado oil. That's it. No expeller-pressed blends, no "light" versions hiding refined palm oil, no added preservatives. For anyone who's flipped a bottle over and squinted at a paragraph of chemical names, this label is refreshingly boring — and that's a compliment.

**What you actually get**

One bottle of cold-pressed, non-GMO avocado oil. The packaging is a standard plastic squeeze bottle with a flip-top cap. It's functional, and honestly, the squeeze design beats a pour spout when you're dressing a salad or drizzling a pan one-handed. The oil itself is a clean, light golden color — clear, not cloudy, which suggests proper filtration without heavy processing.

**Real-world performance**

This is where avocado oil earns its keep. The smoke point sits around 480–500°F. That means you can get a stainless steel pan screaming hot, add this oil, and sear a steak without your kitchen filling with acrid smoke. Olive oil starts breaking down around 375°F. Butter burns at 350°F. If you regularly cook at high heat — stir-frying, searing, roasting vegetables at 425°F — this oil doesn't degrade into bitter, burnt flavors.

The flavor profile is the second quiet win. You don't taste it. That sounds like a criticism, but it's the point. Avocado oil has a neutral, buttery-clean taste with zero vegetal punch. A buyer on Amazon put it simply: *"Finally an oil that doesn't make everything taste like salad dressing."* That's the experience. It lets the seasoning and the food itself be the star.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about tradeoffs. The plastic bottle is the most common complaint. Oil stored long-term can slowly interact with plastic; glass is better if you're buying in bulk or storing for months. If that's you, transfer it to a glass container when it arrives.

Second, price. This costs more per ounce than your standard giant jug of canola oil. That's the reality of a non-GMO, single-origin product. If you're frying a whole turkey, this isn't your oil. For everyday high-heat cooking, the cost difference is a few cents per meal — worth it for the heat performance and clean taste.

**How should I store it?** In a cool, dark cabinet away from the stove's heat. It's stable at room temperature, no refrigeration needed. Use within a few months of opening.

**Is it really non-GMO?** The label states non-GMO, and it's a single-ingredient product. There's no soybean, corn, or canola component to worry about — unlike blended oils that hide GMO ingredients.
