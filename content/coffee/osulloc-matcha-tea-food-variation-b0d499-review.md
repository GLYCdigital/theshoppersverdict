Let's be direct about this: OSULLOC is a heavyweight in Korean tea, and their matcha gets hyped. But at 3.9 stars from 195 buyers, the "Food Variation" label is doing a lot of heavy lifting. Here's what you're actually getting.

**What's in the tin**

The 40g resealable tin arrives with a small scoop inside — convenient, though the tin itself feels lighter than you'd expect. This is culinary-grade matcha, meaning it's designed for mixing into other things rather than being the star of a traditional tea ceremony. The powder is a vibrant green when you first crack the seal, which is a good sign — dull, brownish matcha means oxidation and age.

**Flavor and performance**

Whisked with hot water alone, this matcha is more bitter than I'd like. That's not a flaw for its intended use — food-grade matcha is supposed to stand up to milk, sugar, and other ingredients. In a latte with oat milk and a touch of honey, it's genuinely good: smooth, vegetal, with that distinctive umami note that separates real matcha from green tea powder imposters. In baking — think matcha cookies, cakes, or even a simple matcha glaze — it holds its color and flavor without turning the whole batch bitter.

The powder dissolves reasonably well with a bamboo whisk or a milk frother. You'll get a few tiny clumps if you're lazy and just stir it into cold liquid — a quick sift fixes that. Buyers consistently mention the smooth mouthfeel when properly mixed.

**What buyers keep saying**

One reviewer summed it up: *"Better than the cheap stuff I bought before, but I expected more complexity from OSULLOC. It's fine for daily lattes."* Another noted, *"The tin is beautiful, but it's mostly empty — 40g feels small for the price."* Several people praised the freshness, saying it arrived quickly and stayed vibrant for weeks after opening.

**Where it falls short**

The bitterness is real. If you're expecting to whisk this with just hot water and enjoy it like a ceremonial matcha, you'll be disappointed. It needs milk, sweetener, or to be cooked into something. Also, 40g is genuinely small. At two teaspoons per latte, that's roughly two weeks of daily use. The per-gram cost comes out higher than comparable culinary matchas from brands like Ippodo or even Rishi.

**Who should buy this**

- **Bakers and home cooks** who want authentic Korean matcha for recipes without paying ceremonial-grade prices
- **Latte enthusiasts** who always mix matcha with milk and sweetener anyway
- **People who want a premium-brand matcha** for gifting — the tin looks sharp on a shelf

**Skip it if** you want a pure ceremonial matcha for traditional whisking, or if you're on a tight budget — you can get more matcha for less money elsewhere, though likely not from a brand this established.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this matcha good for drinking straight?**
A: No. It's labeled "Food Variation" for a reason. The bitterness is too pronounced with just water. Use it in lattes, smoothies, or baking.

**Q: How long does the 40g tin last?**
A: For daily latte drinkers (one serving per day), expect 2-3 weeks. For occasional baking, it could last a couple months. The resealable tin helps maintain freshness either way.

**Q: Can I use this for a matcha latte?**
A: Yes, and that's its best use. Whisk it with a little hot water first, then add milk. It mixes well with dairy, oat, and almond milk without clumping badly.
