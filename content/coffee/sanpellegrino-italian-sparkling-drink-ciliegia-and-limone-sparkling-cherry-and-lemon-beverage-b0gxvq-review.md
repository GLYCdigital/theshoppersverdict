**Category note:** You'll notice this isn't a coffee maker — it's the beverage that pairs perfectly with one. This Sanpellegrino Ciliegia and Limone review covers the sparkling cherry-lemon drink that's become a favorite for coffee drinkers who want something cold and crisp between espresso shots.

## What You're Actually Getting

Sanpellegrino's Ciliegia (cherry) and Limone (lemon) comes in slim 11.15-ounce cans that look elegant on any counter. The product image shows the classic deep blue can with the iconic gold crest — it's the same premium aesthetic the brand has used since 1899. Each can delivers carbonated mineral water blended with real fruit juice (cherry and lemon), not artificial flavoring. The ingredient list is short: sparkling mineral water, fruit juices from concentrate, natural flavors, and sugar. No high-fructose corn syrup, no artificial colors.

## Real-World Performance

I'll be direct: this is not a soda. It's a lightly sweetened sparkling fruit beverage that sits somewhere between Italian soda and club soda with a splash of juice. The cherry comes through first — earthy, slightly almond-like, not candy-sweet. The lemon hits after, providing that citrus cut that keeps it from feeling heavy. Carbonation is aggressive on first pour — genuinely crisp — then settles into a gentler fizz.

One thing that matters: serve it properly chilled. At room temperature, the flavors flatten and the sweetness reads as more syrupy. Ice-cold from the fridge (or over ice), the cherry-lemon balance shines. The 11.15-ounce can is a sensible single serving — not so much that it goes flat before you finish, but enough to feel satisfying.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the carbonation is fleeting — leave a can half-drunk for 20 minutes and you're left with lukewarm flat juice water. Second, the sweetness is genuinely subtle. If your baseline is Mountain Dew or even Sprite, this will taste almost tart. That's a feature for some, a dealbreaker for others. Third, the convenience format costs more per ounce than buying the 750ml bottles. If you're stocking a fridge for daily drinking, the economics don't favor individual cans.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Coffee drinkers** who want a palate cleanser between cups — the citrus cuts through coffee's bitterness beautifully.
- **Cocktail enthusiasts** — this makes an excellent spritz base or a lighter alternative to tonic water.
- **Soda drinkers trying to step down** from heavy syrups to something more refined.

**Skip it if:** you want a sweet, syrupy cherry soda, or you're on a strict budget per can.

## FAQ

**Is this a healthy drink option?** It has about 120 calories per can with 28g of sugar — less than a typical soda (which often hits 39-40g), but it's not sugar-free. It's a "better choice," not a "health drink."

**Does it actually taste like cherry?** Yes, but it's a tart, dark cherry flavor — closer to fresh cherries or kirsch, not maraschino syrup. The lemon is more subtle than the cherry.

**Can you mix it with alcohol?** Absolutely. It works well with gin (think a French 75 variation) or as a lighter spritz with Aperol or Campari. The carbonation holds up well in a tall glass with ice.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** you appreciate European-style sparkling fruit drinks, want something more sophisticated than soda, or need a crisp non-coffee beverage to alternate with your espresso.

**Skip it if:** you want bold sweetness, need aggressive carbonation that lasts, or are watching per-can costs closely.
