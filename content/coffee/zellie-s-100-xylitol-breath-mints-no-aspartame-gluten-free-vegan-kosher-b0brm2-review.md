## What You Actually Get

The tin is the standout here: it's durable, doesn't crack like plastic alternatives, and the sliding mechanism stays shut in pockets and bags. ## Real-World Performance

Here's where this earns its 4.5-star rating. The xylitol in Zellie's mints dissolves relatively quickly — about 60 to 90 seconds — but the fresh-breath effect lingers longer than you'd expect from such a small mint. After a double espresso, one mint killed the coffee breath within a minute. After garlic-heavy pasta, I needed two, but they did the job.

Xylitol's real advantage over sugar-based mints is dental: it's clinically shown to reduce cavity-causing bacteria. So these aren't just masking bad breath — they're actively working against the bacteria that cause it. That's a meaningful difference if you're someone who cares about oral health beyond just freshening up.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. These mints dissolve quickly — they're not gum, and they won't give you 20 minutes of continuous chewing. If you need long-lasting breath protection through a work meeting, you'll be reaching for the tin more than once. The tin is also compact, which is great for portability but means you'll reorder more frequently than with a bulk plastic container.

And while xylitol is safe for humans, it's extremely toxic to dogs. If you have a canine companion who sniffs around your bags, keep these secured. That's not a product flaw, but it's a real consideration for pet owners.

## FAQ

**Is xylitol safe for daily use?**
Yes, for humans. The FDA recognizes xylitol as safe, and it's widely used in dental products. Just keep it away from dogs — even small amounts can be fatal to them.

**How do these compare to sugar-free gums or other xylitol mints?**

**Are these actually vegan and gluten-free?**
