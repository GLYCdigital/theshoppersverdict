# Barilla Protein+ Elbows: The High-Protein Pasta That Doesn't Taste Like Cardboard

Let me be upfront: most protein pastas I've tested belong in the emergency pantry, not your weeknight dinner rotation. They crumble, taste like chalk, or turn into glue the second they hit boiling water. Barilla's Protein+ Elbows are different — and with a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, I'm not the only one who thinks so.

## What You're Actually Getting

Each 14.5-ounce box contains a standard elbow macaroni shape, but the ingredient list tells a different story. Barilla blends traditional semolina with lentil flour, chickpea flour, and pea protein to hit 10 grams of protein per 2-ounce dry serving — roughly double what regular elbows deliver. That's the equivalent of about 1.5 eggs worth of protein added to your pasta dish.

The box itself is unremarkable: standard cardboard packaging, clear nutrition panel, and Barilla's recognizable blue-and-white branding. Nothing fancy, but the product inside has some genuinely clever engineering.

## Performance in the Kitchen

I cooked these three ways: classic stovetop boil, baked into a casserole, and dropped into a soup. Here's what matters:

**Boiling (the test that matters most):** The box says 8 minutes. I pulled mine at 7:30 and they were perfect — tender but with a noticeable bite. The texture is where these differ from regular elbows. They're firmer, almost al dente by default, and the surface feels slightly less smooth. It's not a bad thing, but don't expect the soft, pillowy texture of traditional semolina pasta.

**In a baked dish:** This is where they genuinely shine. They held their structure through a 30-minute bake with sauce and cheese — no mush, no disintegration. Regular elbows often turn into paste in this scenario.

**In soup:** Also solid. They stayed intact without puffing up into weird, bloated shapes. That's a common failure point for legume-based pastas.

One practical note: these are more forgiving than regular pasta if you slightly undercook them, but they punish overcooking hard. At 10 minutes, they become rubbery and unpleasant. Set a timer.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.8-star rating holds up in the review text. A recurring theme is how well this works for families with picky eaters:

> "My kids can't tell the difference. I'm not saying that to be nice — I've tested it twice. They just think it's macaroni."

Another buyer focused on the protein math:

> "I'm not a gym person, but I need more protein in my diet. This gets me there without eating more meat. The taste is 95% there compared to regular pasta."

The most common criticism, unsurprisingly, is the price. Multiple reviewers noted it costs roughly 30-40% more than standard elbows per serving. A few also flagged the texture as "different" — not worse, just not what they expected.

## Honest Drawbacks

**The texture ceiling.** If you're expecting these to be indistinguishable from Barilla's regular elbows, you'll be disappointed. They're firmer, and there's a slight legume note in the finish — not unpleasant, but detectable if you're paying attention.

**The price per pound stings.** The 14.5-ounce box is smaller than the standard 16-ounce pasta box, and it costs more. You're paying a premium for the protein fortification. If that's not your priority, regular elbows are the better value.

**Overcooking is fatal.** This isn't a "set it and forget it" pasta. It needs attention during the last few minutes of cooking. Walk away and you'll be chewing on rubber bands.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're trying to increase protein intake without overhauling your diet. You cook pasta for picky kids who reject "healthy" alternatives. You like pasta salads or baked dishes where pasta needs to hold its shape. You're an athlete or fitness-focused eater who wants a convenient protein source.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight grocery budget — the per-serving cost is meaningfully higher. You're extremely sensitive to texture differences in pasta. You need gluten-free (this contains semolina, so it's not for you).

## FAQ

**Q: Is this pasta gluten-free?**
No. The protein comes from lentils, chickpeas, and peas, but the base is still semolina wheat. Celiac sufferers or those with wheat sensitivities should avoid it.

**Q: How does the protein content compare to regular pasta?**
Regular elbow pasta has about 7-8g of protein per 2oz dry serving. Barilla Protein+ delivers 10g — roughly 25-30% more. It's not a dramatic jump, but it adds up across a week of meals.

**Q: Does it taste like lentils or beans?**
There's a subtle legume note, but it's very mild. Most reviewers, including those with picky kids, say it's barely noticeable — especially with any kind of sauce. The texture difference is more pronounced than the flavor difference.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

Barilla Protein+ Elbows are the rare protein-fortified pasta that actually deserves a spot in your pantry rotation. It handles real-world cooking scenarios — boiling, baking, soup — with genuine competence, delivers a meaningful protein bump, and doesn't punish you with weird off-flavors. The texture is different enough that purists will notice, and the price premium is real. But for anyone who wants more protein without a lifestyle overhaul, this is a genuinely smart swap.

**Buy it if** you want extra protein without changing what you cook. **Skip it if** you're a semolina purist or shopping on a strict budget.
