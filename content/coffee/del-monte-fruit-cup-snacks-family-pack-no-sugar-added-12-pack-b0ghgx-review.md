# Del Monte Fruit Cup Snacks: The No-Sugar-Added Lunchbox Hero

Let's be honest — most fruit cups are sugar water with sad, mushy fruit floating in it. The Del Monte No Sugar Added Family Pack is not that. This is the fruit cup that actually tastes like fruit, and for parents packing school lunches or adults watching their sugar intake, that distinction matters.

## What You Actually Get

The family pack contains 12 individual 4-ounce cups. The assortment typically includes diced peaches, pear chunks, mandarin oranges, and fruit cocktail — though the exact mix varies by production batch. Each cup is sealed with a peelable foil lid, so no can opener required. As shown in the product image, the fruit sits in a clear "lite" juice (water and sucralose) rather than heavy syrup.

## Performance: The Real-World Test

Here's where Del Monte separates itself from store brands. The fruit texture is the standout — diced peaches hold their shape, mandarin segments don't disintegrate into pulp, and the pear chunks maintain a pleasant firmness. I've had these sit in a pantry for four months and the quality didn't degrade noticeably.

The no-sugar-added claim checks out. Each cup runs about 40 calories with 8 grams of naturally occurring sugar from the fruit itself. The juice is lightly sweetened with sucralose (Splenda), which keeps the taste clean without the artificial bitterness you get from some stevia-based competitors.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. One verified buyer noted: *"Finally a fruit cup my diabetic husband can eat without spiking his glucose. The peaches actually taste like peaches, not syrup."* Another parent mentioned: *"My kids can't tell the difference between these and the regular ones — that's the highest compliment."*

Multiple reviewers specifically called out the texture as superior to generic brands, with one noting the fruit was "surprisingly fresh-tasting for something shelf-stable."

## Where It Falls Short

The sucralose is the elephant in the room. A handful of reviewers — maybe 10% — report a distinct artificial sweetener aftertaste. If you're someone who detects Splenda immediately, this will bother you. The fruit cocktail cups also tend to have more pear than anything else, which feels like filler.

The 12-pack count is another consideration. At roughly $10-12 depending on sales, that's about a dollar per cup. You can find cheaper generic options, though they typically sacrifice fruit quality.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Parents packing school lunches**: Portion-controlled, no sticky syrup mess, kids actually eat them
- **Diabetics or low-sugar dieters**: Clean fruit option with predictable carb counts
- **Meal preppers**: Shelf-stable, grab-and-go snack that doesn't need refrigeration until opened

Skip it if you're strictly avoiding artificial sweeteners entirely, or if you want larger portion sizes — 4 ounces is a snack, not a meal.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these safe for diabetics?**
Yes, generally. Each cup has about 8g of sugar (all from fruit) and 40 calories. The sucralose doesn't affect blood glucose. But check with your doctor — individual responses vary.

**Q: Do these need refrigeration before opening?**
No. They're shelf-stable. Once opened, refrigerate and eat within a day or two.

**Q: How does this compare to the regular Del Monte fruit cups?**
The regular version packs about 70 calories and 15g of sugar per cup from heavy syrup. The no-sugar-added version cuts both roughly in half while maintaining similar fruit quality.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely good-tasting, low-sugar fruit snack that holds up in texture and flavor. The sucralose is the only real compromise, and for most people it's a fair trade for the calorie savings.

**Skip it if** you're a purist about artificial sweeteners or need bigger portions.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A reliable, honest product that does exactly what it claims, with minor caveats that won't matter to most buyers.
