## True Citrus 10 Packets Review: The Honest Take on Crystallized Citrus

Let's cut through the noise: True Citrus packets aren't trying to be a sugary sports drink or a neon-colored powder. These are simply dehydrated citrus crystals—lemon, lime, or orange depending on the variety you grab—that dissolve into water. The question isn't whether they're fancy. It's whether they actually deliver on the promise of "real fruit taste" without the squeeze.

### What You Actually Get

The 10-count box contains individually sealed packets, each holding roughly 2 grams of crystallized citrus. As shown in the product image, the packets are slim, flat pouches—think of a single-serve electrolyte stick but smaller. There's no scooping, no measuring, no sticky juice bottles to deal with. Tear, pour, stir.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: just crystallized citrus (lemon, lime, or orange depending on the variant) and sometimes a touch of natural flavor. That's it. No artificial colors, no high-fructose corn syrup, no chemical sweeteners. For shoppers scanning labels like I do, that's a significant point in its favor.

### Real-World Performance

I tested these in three scenarios: room-temperature tap water, ice-cold bottled water, and carbonated water. The crystals dissolve within 15–20 seconds with a quick stir. No clumping, no chalky residue at the bottom of the glass. That's better than most powdered drink mixes I've tried, which tend to leave a film or require vigorous shaking.

Flavor-wise, it's tart. Genuinely tart. This isn't a sweetened lemonade—it's closer to what you'd get if you squeezed a fresh wedge into your glass. If that's what you want, it delivers. If you're expecting a sweeter profile, you'll need to add your own sweetener. The lime version has a slightly more aggressive bite; the orange is milder and almost floral.

### What Buyers Consistently Praise

The packaging is genuinely practical. The thin packets slip into a wallet, a jacket pocket, or a lunchbox without adding bulk. Travelers particularly like that these aren't liquids, so they breeze through TSA. One reviewer noted, "I keep a couple in my work bag for when the office water tastes like pool water." Another mentioned using them to make impromptu salad dressing by mixing the crystals with olive oil and vinegar—a clever workaround if you're camping or in a hotel room.

The versatility is real. Beyond water, these work in iced tea, sparkling water, or even yogurt for a citrus kick. The single-ingredient nature means no weird aftertaste, which several long-time users specifically highlighted.

### Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. Ten packets is a modest supply. If you're using one per day, that's less than two weeks. The price per packet, when you do the math, isn't a bargain compared to a bag of fresh lemons—you're paying for convenience and portability, not cost efficiency.

The tartness, while authentic, will miss the mark for anyone accustomed to sweetened drink mixes. There's no sweetness at all unless you add it. And the packets aren't resealable, so you can't use half a serving and save the rest. Each packet is a single-use commitment.

One more thing: if you're expecting a strong, punch-you-in-the-face citrus flavor, you'll be underwhelmed. This is subtle and natural-tasting, which some will love and others will find too weak.

### Who Should Buy This

- **Travelers and commuters** who want to improve hotel or office water without carrying bottles of juice
- **Health-conscious drinkers** who avoid artificial sweeteners and want a clean, single-ingredient flavor boost
- **Campers and hikers** who need lightweight, compact flavoring that won't leak or spoil
- **Cocktail enthusiasts** who want a quick citrus kick for drinks without juicing fresh fruit

Skip it if you want a sweetened lemonade experience, need bulk value for daily use, or prefer the flavor intensity of fresh-squeezed citrus (which still beats any dehydrated version).

### FAQ

**Q: Does this taste like real lemon juice?**
A: It's close but not identical. The dehydration process concentrates the citrus oils, so the aroma is strong, but the acidity is slightly muted compared to fresh-squeezed. It's far more authentic than artificial lemon flavoring.

**Q: Can I use these in hot drinks like tea?**
A: Yes, they dissolve fine in hot water. The flavor is actually more pronounced when warm. Just note that the citrus oils can become slightly more bitter if the water is boiling—use warm, not scalding.

**Q: Are these keto-friendly or sugar-free?**
A: The plain citrus versions contain no added sugar and are naturally carb-free, so they fit keto and low-sugar diets. Some flavored varieties may have added sweeteners, so check the specific packet label.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a clean, portable, single-ingredient way to make plain water more interesting without artificial anything. It's a smart, functional product that does exactly what it claims.

**Skip it if** you demand sweetened flavor profiles, need larger quantities for daily use, or can't get past the tartness. For those shoppers, a bottle of fresh lemon juice or a bulk sweetened mix will serve you better.

**Rating: 4.0/5** — True Citrus earns its stars through ingredient integrity, portability, and consistent performance. It loses one star for value and the lack of resealability, but for the right user, these are minor tradeoffs for a genuinely useful product.
