Let's cut to the chase: you're not here because you're curious about dairy farming. You're here because you put heavy cream in your coffee and you want to know if the Whole Foods house brand actually holds up. Short answer: yes, with a caveat or two.

**What you're actually getting**

One pint of certified organic heavy cream. That's 16 fluid ounces, 800 calories, and exactly one ingredient: organic cream. No carrageenan, no mono-and-diglycerides, no polysorbate 80 — just cream from pasture-raised cows. The carton is the standard gable-top design you'd expect, with a tamper-evident seal under the cap that's easy to peel without destroying your fingernails.

**The real-world performance**

I tested this three ways: in black coffee, steamed for a latte, and whipped for a topping. In black coffee, it does what heavy cream should — rich body, slight sweetness, and it cuts bitterness without muting the bean's character. It doesn't separate or float in oily pools like some lighter creams do.

Steaming is where this cream shows its personality. Because there are no stabilizers, it foams beautifully and holds its structure — but it's less forgiving than gums-laden creams. If you overheat it past 150°F, it can break and turn slightly grainy. Watch your thermometer and you'll get microfoam that rivals what you'd get from a dedicated barista milk.

Whipped cream is where the single-ingredient purity really shines. It fluffs up in about three minutes with a hand mixer, tastes clean and dairy-forward, and doesn't leave that chalky film that stabilizer-heavy creams do. The tradeoff: it deflates faster in warm conditions, so serve it promptly.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews is backed by real consistency. One reviewer put it plainly: "I've been using this cream in my coffee for three years. It's always fresh, always the same. Never a bad carton." Another noted the practical angle: "The pint is perfect — I can use it up before it goes bad, which I couldn't do with a quart."

**Where it falls short**

Honest downsides matter. First, the mouthfeel is thinner than ultra-pasteurized competitors. If you're used to the almost buttery viscosity of Organic Valley or Horizon, this will feel slightly lighter in your cup. Second, it's sensitive to temperature — leave it on the counter while you brew and you'll notice it doesn't steam as consistently. Finally, availability is the real issue: you can't get this on Amazon with Prime shipping in most regions, so you're likely buying it in-store at Whole Foods.

**Who should buy this**

- **The daily coffee drinker** who uses heavy cream every morning and wants a pint that stays fresh
- **The ingredient purist** who reads labels and avoids gums and stabilizers
- **The home barista** who steams milk and wants clean, predictable dairy flavor

Skip it if you need a cream that tolerates neglect, or if you're outside Whole Foods' delivery range and can't find it locally.

**FAQ**

**Is this actually organic, or is it just branding?**
It's USDA-certified organic. Whole Foods' 365 brand is their house label, and they're strict about supplier compliance. You can verify the certification on the carton.

**How long does it last after opening?**
Most reviewers report 7–14 days past the printed date. Keep it in the coldest part of your fridge, not the door.

**Does it work for keto coffee?**
Yes — it's zero-carb, zero-sugar, and adds rich fat to your brew. Just watch portion size; a pint is 800 calories total.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want clean, single-ingredient heavy cream for coffee or cooking and you can get it fresh from Whole Foods. **Skip it if** you prioritize the thickest possible mouthfeel or need Amazon delivery.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not the richest cream on the shelf, but it's honest, consistent, and exactly what it claims to be. That's worth something.
