## What You Actually Get

The bag contains 16 ounces (453 grams) of fruit-flavored jelly beans — roughly 400 individual beans. Jelly Belly's standard size applies here: about 1 gram per bean, so you're getting real volume, not air. The bag is resealable with a zip-top closure, which matters more than you'd think because these beans stay fresh for weeks if you seal them properly.

The fruit bowl lineup includes classics like strawberry, orange, and lemon, alongside bolder choices like kiwi, pomegranate, and mango. I counted the flavors as I sorted through the bag; the mix was reasonably balanced, though you'll always get a few more of the cheaper-to-produce flavors (orange and lemon) than the exotic ones.

## Taste and Texture: The Real Test

Jelly Belly's reputation rests on flavor accuracy, and this bag mostly delivers. The strawberry tastes like actual strawberry, not candy-flavored strawberry. The mango has that slightly tangy, tropical profile that makes you think of fresh fruit. The blueberry is deep and rich, not artificially sweet.

Texture is consistent across the entire bag — firm, thin shells that crack cleanly, with a soft, creamy center. I didn't encounter a single stale or hard bean, which is notable for a mass-produced candy. One thing to know: the flavors are potent. A handful can be overwhelming, especially the citrus-forward ones. These are designed for savoring one or two at a time, not mindless handfuls.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides: The bag doesn't list the specific 16 flavors anywhere on the packaging. You get a generic "Fruit Bowl" label with no breakdown, so you can't know if your favorite fruit is included until you open it. If you're buying for someone with a specific preference (say, they love pear but hate watermelon), this is a gamble.

Also, the fruit-only lineup means no chocolate, no buttered popcorn, no licorice. Some people buy Jelly Belly specifically for those signature flavors and will be disappointed this bag lacks them. And while the resealable closure works, it's not airtight — if you live in a humid climate, keep these in a sealed container or they'll get sticky.
