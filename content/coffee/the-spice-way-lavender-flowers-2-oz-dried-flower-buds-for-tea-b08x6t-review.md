Let’s be honest: most dried lavender you find on Amazon smells like a candle aisle exploded. It's dusty, weak, and tastes like potpourri when you steep it. The Spice Way's 2 oz pouch of culinary-grade buds is not that. It's the rare dried flower that holds onto its essential oils well enough to actually flavor food—not just perfume your kitchen.

## What's actually in the pouch

You get 2 ounces (56 grams) of whole lavender buds in a clear, resealable stand-up pouch. As shown in the product image, the buds are a deep violet-blue with minimal green stem material visible from the outside. The pouch itself is nothing fancy—standard food-grade plastic with a zipper seal. It's fine for the first few weeks, but if you're not using this daily, plan to decant into a mason jar. The seal is functional, not airtight.

## Real-world performance: Tea, baking, and beyond

I tested this three ways: hot tea, shortbread cookies, and a sleep sachet. The tea is where The Spice Way shines. A teaspoon steeped for five minutes gives a floral, slightly sweet cup with a honeyed finish—no bitterness, no soapy aftertaste. That's the mark of properly dried culinary lavender. The buds are harvested before full bloom, which keeps the essential oil content high and the flavor clean.

In baking, the buds held up well. I ground a tablespoon with sugar for shortbread and got a subtle lavender presence that didn't overpower the butter. The consistency was consistent throughout the dough—no surprise clumps of dust or oversized stems. That said, one reviewer noted, "I found a few stems in my batch, but nothing that ruined the product." That matches my experience. It's not perfectly sifted, but it's far cleaner than most competitors at this price point.

For non-culinary use, a small sachet in my pillowcase lasted about three weeks before the scent faded. That's solid for a 2 oz bag that also needs to cover your cooking.
