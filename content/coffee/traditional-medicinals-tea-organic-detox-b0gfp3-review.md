Let's be straight about something before we start: a cup of tea is not a detox. Your liver and kidneys do that job whether you buy this box or not. What Traditional Medicinals *does* sell here is a well-made, organic herbal blend that supports hydration and digestion while tasting genuinely good. That's worth reviewing honestly.

**What's actually in the box**

You get 16 tea bags, each individually wrapped in foil. That's a small thing, but it matters — herbal teas lose volatile oils quickly, and the individual wrapping keeps the ginger and dandelion root potent months after opening. The box lists dandelion root, burdock root, ginger, licorice root, and fennel as the main players. All certified organic, all caffeine-free, and the tea bag itself is unbleached and plastic-free.

**How it performs in the cup**

Steep it for 10–15 minutes as directed, and you get a deep amber liquor that smells like gingerbread and tastes like a spiced root beer that grew up and got serious. The licorice root adds natural sweetness — there's zero added sugar — while the ginger gives it a warm, slightly peppery finish. It's genuinely pleasant, and that's rare for "wellness" teas that usually taste like mowed grass.

I've been cycling through a box for two weeks, drinking it in the evening after dinner. It's become my wind-down ritual. The fennel and ginger settle the stomach, and the dandelion is a mild diuretic — you'll notice an extra bathroom trip or two, which is the point. But if you're expecting dramatic "flushing" effects, you'll be disappointed. This is a gentle botanical beverage, not a colon cleanse.

**What 229 Amazon buyers are saying**

The 4.6-star average holds up when you read the actual reviews. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've bought this multiple times. It tastes great and helps with bloating after heavy meals."* Another long-term user said: *"I drink this every night instead of dessert — the licorice sweetness kills my sugar cravings."*

The few 3-star reviews cluster around two complaints: the licorice flavor is too assertive for some, and a couple of buyers expected more dramatic physical effects. One reviewer put it plainly: *"It's a nice tea, but don't expect to feel 'detoxed.' It's just good tea."* That's a fair take.

**Where it falls short**

The licorice root is the star of the show, and that's a problem if you're not a fan. It's sweeter and more forward than the ginger or dandelion, so if you're buying this for the dandelion, you'll be fighting the licorice the whole cup.

The price is the other issue. At roughly $0.50–0.60 per bag, this is three to four times the cost of a standard black tea bag. You're paying for the organic certification and the individual wrapping, which is legitimate — but it's a premium, not a bargain.

**Who should buy this**

- **The evening wind-down drinker** who wants a caffeine-free, naturally sweet herbal tea that actually tastes good
- **The bloat-prone eater** looking for a gentle digestive aid after heavy meals
- **The sugar-craver** who needs a zero-calorie dessert replacement that doesn't feel like a deprivation

**Skip it if** you hate licorice, expect a dramatic physical cleanse, or just want cheap tea — this is a specialty product, not an everyday staple.

**FAQ**

*Is this tea actually a detox?*
No. It's a mild diuretic and digestive aid. The herbs support your body's natural processes, but they don't "flush toxins" in any measurable way. Buy it for the taste and gentle effects, not the marketing.

*Does it contain caffeine?*
None. Every ingredient is naturally caffeine-free, making it safe to drink in the evening.

*How many cups do you get per box?*
16 individually wrapped bags. At one cup per day, that's a little over two weeks of tea.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a high-quality, organic herbal tea that tastes like a treat, helps with bloating, and gives you a caffeine-free evening ritual. **Skip it if** you're allergic to licorice or expecting a miracle cleanse in a teabag.
