**The snack that doesn't pretend to be candy — and that's exactly why it works**  

Most "healthy" snacks fail the first bite test. Either they're dense as cardboard or they're secretly just sugar with a fiber halo. Monkey Brittle does something different: it's genuinely crispy, genuinely plant-based, and it doesn't insult your palate with artificial sweeteners.  

I've been testing this for three weeks. Here's the real story.  

## What's actually in the bag  

You get a 5-ounce resealable bag (about 8 servings per package). The base is seeds and nuts — sunflower seeds, pumpkin seeds, and almonds — held together by a date-and-maple syrup binder. No refined sugar, no corn syrup, no weird stabilizers. The ingredient list is short enough to read in one breath.  

As shown in the product image, the texture is shattered-glass brittle — thin, craggy pieces that snap cleanly. It's not a granola cluster situation; it's closer to a peanut brittle texture, minus the sticky teeth-pulling.  

## How it actually performs  

The crunch is the headline. I opened my first bag on a Tuesday and finished it 12 days later. The last handful was nearly as crisp as the first — the resealable zipper does its job. That's rare for anything brittle-based, which usually turns soft within days.  

Flavor-wise, expect toasted nuttiness with a gentle maple sweetness that arrives at the end, not the front. It's not dessert. It's a snack that satisfies the crunch craving without triggering a sugar spike.  

I crumbled it over Greek yogurt and also ate it straight. Both work. It held up in oatmeal without turning to mush, which surprised me.

## Where it falls short  

Honesty time. Two issues kept this from a perfect score.  

First, **breakage**. Because it's brittle, shipping takes a toll. Expect a decent amount of crumbs and smaller pieces at the bottom of the bag. It doesn't affect taste, but if you were planning to gift it, know the presentation won't be pristine.  

Second, **the sweetness level**. If you're coming from honey-roasted peanuts or chocolate-covered pretzels, this will taste almost savory. It's sweetened with dates and a touch of maple, not sugar. I appreciate that, but it's a real adjustment for people expecting candy-like flavor.  

Also note: it contains tree nuts (almonds). If you're in a nut-free household, this isn't for you.  

## Who should buy this  

- **Parents** looking for a lunchbox snack that won't get thrown away or traded — the crunch appeals to kids, and the ingredients don't give you pause.  
- **Low-sugar dieters** (keto, diabetic, or just cutting back) who miss the texture of brittle but don't want the sugar bomb.  
- **Meal-preppers** who want a shelf-stable topping for yogurt, oatmeal, or smoothie bowls that adds protein and crunch without added sugar.  

**Skip it if** you want a sweet candy-like snack, need uniform pieces, or have a tree nut allergy.
