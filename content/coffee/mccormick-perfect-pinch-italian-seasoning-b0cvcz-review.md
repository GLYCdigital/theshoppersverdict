**The Short Version:** McCormick Perfect Pinch Italian Seasoning is the rare dried herb blend that doesn't taste like dusty afterthought. If you cook Italian food more than twice a week, this bottle earns its cabinet space. But it's not a pure-herb product, and the price-per-ounce math gets ugly if you're a heavy user.

---

**What You're Actually Getting**

The bottle is a 2.48-ounce glass shaker with McCormick's signature "Perfect Pinch" cap — the one with the flip-top that dispenses a surprisingly even sprinkle. The blend itself: oregano, basil, marjoram, thyme, rosemary, plus salt and sugar. That last part matters. It's not a pure spice blend; it's a seasoning. The salt and sugar are doing heavy lifting to make everything taste "Italian" instantly.

As shown in the product image, the herbs are coarsely ground — not powdered, not whole-leaf. That texture matters. It clings to pizza and pasta without turning into a green paste when it hits hot oil.

---

**How It Performs in Real Cooking**

I used this on three things in one week: a quick marinara, roasted chicken thighs, and garlic bread. The marinara got a solid, rounded flavor with zero additional salt needed. The chicken picked up a crusty, herb-forward finish that held up to a 400°F roast. The garlic bread — this is where the blend shines. The salt content means it seasons the butter itself, not just the top of the bread.

One thing that surprised me: the cap. It's not a joke. You can flip it open, shake over a pan, and get even coverage without dumping a clump in one spot. That's rare in dried herbs. Most jars either clog or pour like sand.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides.

First, the salt. If you're on a low-sodium diet or you're seasoning something that already has salt (like cured meats or salty cheeses), this blend will push you over the edge. It's not a pure-herb seasoning; it's a seasoning salt with herbs. Read the label before you buy.

Second, the bottle size. At 2.48 ounces, a frequent cook will run through this in three to four weeks. The price per ounce isn't terrible, but it's not a value play. If you're feeding a family or meal-prepping, budget for a restock.

Third, the flavor profile skews sweet. The sugar content is noticeable in dishes where you don't want any sweetness — like a savory roast or a simple grilled vegetable toss. It works great for pasta, but it's not the neutral Italian herb blend some shoppers assume they're getting.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Busy home cooks** who want one bottle to handle pasta, pizza, and weeknight chicken without measuring spoons
- **College students or new cooks** who don't want to invest in six separate herb jars
- **Anyone who's tried cheap store-brand Italian seasoning** and found it tasteless — this is a genuine step up

**Skip it if** you're strictly controlling sodium, or if you want a pure herb blend you can control yourself. In that case, buy individual dried oregano and basil and skip the salt.

---

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this the same as McCormick's old Italian seasoning?**
A: No. The Perfect Pinch line is a different formula — coarser grind, more aggressive salt/sugar content, and a better shake top. It's an upgrade.

**Q: Can I use this in a spice grinder or for dry rubs?**
A: Yes, but remember it's already salted. If you're making a dry rub, cut the salt in your recipe by half.

**Q: How long does it stay fresh?**
A: McCormick puts a "best by" date on the cap. Most buyers report it stays potent for six months to a year. After that, the herbs fade.
