**The chili crisp market is crowded.** Lao Gan Ma owns the nostalgia corner, Fly By Jing owns the premium shelf, and a dozen TikTok-famous brands fight for your pantry space. So when Redbloom shows up claiming "gut-healthy" and "no seed oils," it's fair to be skeptical. But after two weeks of putting this stuff on everything from fried eggs to roasted broccoli, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you're actually getting**

The 8-ounce glass jar arrives with a satisfying pop when you crack the seal. Inside, you'll find a deep red oil packed with visible chili flakes, crispy garlic, shallot pieces, and what looks like sesame seeds. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: organic avocado oil, organic chili peppers, organic garlic, organic shallots, organic apple cider vinegar, sea salt, and organic sesame seeds. That's it. No preservatives, no sugar, no MSG, no soybean or canola oil hiding in there.

**Performance and real-world use**

The texture is what surprised me most. Unlike many chili crisps that are 80% oil with a few floaters, Redbloom packs a generous amount of crunchy bits into every spoonful. The heat builds slowly—starting gentle, then settling into a warm, pleasant burn around a 6/10. It's not a face-melter, but it's got more kick than the "mild" stuff you'll find at most Asian grocers.

Where this shines is as a finishing oil. Drizzle it over congee, eggs, dumplings, or plain rice and it transforms the dish. The apple cider vinegar adds a subtle tang that cuts through fatty foods beautifully—something I haven't found in other chili crisps. It also works as a marinade base for grilled meats, though I'd recommend mixing it with a bit of soy sauce and honey to round it out.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Digging through the 204 ratings, the recurring theme is relief at finding a chili crisp that doesn't wreck your stomach. One reviewer wrote, *"I have IBS and most chili oils destroy me. This one doesn't—I can actually enjoy spicy food again without paying for it later."* Another said, *"Finally a chili crisp that doesn't list soybean oil as the first ingredient. **Where it falls short**

The crunch doesn't last. That crispy garlic and shallot—the best part of any chili crisp—starts softening within two to three weeks of opening. Refrigerating slows it down but doesn't stop it. You'll want to use this fast, which is a problem because...

The price stings. At roughly $12–14 per 8-ounce jar, you're paying a significant premium over the $4–5 you'd drop on a classic brand. For a condiment you might use daily, that adds up. The 8-ounce size is also smaller than the 12-ounce jars most competitors offer, so heavy users will reorder frequently.

**Who should buy this**

You're the target buyer if you're avoiding seed oils for health reasons, dealing with a sensitive gut but still want heat, or you're willing to pay more for cleaner ingredients and a more complex flavor profile. Skip it if you're on a tight budget, go through chili crisp like it's water, or you prefer the familiar fermented, funkier flavor of traditional Sichuan-style crisps.

**FAQ**

**Is this actually spicy?**
It's medium heat—noticeably warmer than ketchup but nowhere near habanero territory. If you're someone who adds hot sauce to everything, you'll find this comfortable. If you want face-sweating heat, look elsewhere.

**How should I store it after opening?**
The brand recommends refrigeration, which helps preserve the crunch and prevent the oil from going rancid. But cold oil thickens, so let it sit at room temperature for 10–15 minutes before using.

**Can I cook with this or is it just a finishing oil?**
Both work, but it's best as a finishing drizzle. Cooking at high heat will destroy the delicate flavor compounds in the avocado oil and mute the chili's brightness. Use it raw or add it at the last minute.

**The verdict**

Redbloom delivers exactly what it promises: a clean, gut-friendly chili crisp that doesn't compromise on taste. The ingredients are genuinely better than 90% of what's on the shelf, and the flavor is complex enough to justify the premium price for most people. The softening crunch and the cost are real drawbacks, but if you're prioritizing ingredient quality over getting the most bang for your buck, this earns its place in your pantry.

**Buy it if** you're done with seed oils and want a chili crisp that won't upset your digestion.
**Skip it if** you're on a budget or you go through a jar every two weeks—you'll burn through cash fast.
