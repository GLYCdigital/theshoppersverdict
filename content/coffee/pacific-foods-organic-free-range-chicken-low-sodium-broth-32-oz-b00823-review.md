**Review Body:**

You don't buy boxed broth because you're lazy. You buy it because you're smart—you know that making stock from scratch takes six hours, a stockpot the size of a small child, and a freezer full of chicken carcasses you don't have. Pacific Foods' Organic Free Range Chicken Low Sodium Broth is the shortcut that doesn't taste like a shortcut. Here's the real story after digging through 235 Amazon ratings and the nutrition panel.

**What's actually in the carton**

Each 32-ounce tetra pak contains chicken broth made from organic free-range chicken (it's the first ingredient, not water—that's rare), plus organic carrot, onion, and celery juice concentrates, yeast extract, and salt. That's the entire ingredient list. No MSG, no caramel color, no "natural flavors" hiding something questionable. One carton yields about four 1-cup servings.

**The low-sodium math that matters**

Here's the number that should make you sit up: **140mg of sodium per cup.** Compare that to regular chicken broth at 860–900mg per cup, and you're looking at a 94% reduction. But here's the part that surprises people—it doesn't taste like watered-down broth. The yeast extract provides enough savory depth that you won't reach for the salt shaker. As one Amazon reviewer put it: *"Finally a low sodium broth that doesn't taste like hot water with a vague chicken memory."* That sentiment echoes through the reviews.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The carton is shelf-stable until opened, which is convenient for pantry stocking. Once opened, you've got 7–10 days in the fridge—a tight window, so plan accordingly. The packaging is a standard paperboard carton with a plastic pour spout that works fine, though it can drip if you pour too aggressively. No complaints about leakage in transit, which is worth noting since Amazon reviews for liquid products are often littered with "arrived damaged" stories. That's conspicuously absent here.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- **Taste authenticity**: Multiple reviewers note it tastes like homemade stock, not commercial broth. One wrote: *"I use this for pho and ramen bases and my family thinks I simmered it for hours."*
- **The clean ingredient list**: Parents feeding kids and people with dietary restrictions appreciate knowing exactly what's in it.
- **Cooking versatility**: It holds up well as a braising liquid, rice-cooking base, and soup starter without overwhelming other flavors.

**Where it falls short**

The 32-ounce size is genuinely annoying. Standard broth cans are 48 ounces, so if you're making a big pot of soup, you'll need two cartons—which means you're buying two of something that costs more per ounce than the big cans. The yeast extract flavor profile is slightly different from traditional simmered stock; it's a touch more "umami-forward" and less "roasted chicken." Most people won't notice, but purists will. And the short post-open window means if you only use a cup for a recipe, the rest might go to waste.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home cooks on low-sodium diets** who refuse to sacrifice flavor—this is the best option in its class
- **Parents** who want controlled sodium for kids' soups and sauces
- **Meal preppers** who use broth regularly and can finish a carton within a week
- **Anyone who's tried other low-sodium broths and been disappointed** by the watery results

**Skip it if** you're making broth-heavy dishes in bulk (get the 48-oz low-sodium version instead), or if you're on a tight budget—organic costs more, and there's no way around it.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can you use this interchangeably with regular chicken broth in recipes?**
A: Yes, but adjust salt. Since it has 94% less sodium, you'll need to season dishes more aggressively. For soups, taste before adding salt—the yeast extract provides more flavor than you'd expect.

**Q: Is this actually organic and free-range verified?**
A: Yes. USDA Organic certification covers both the chicken and the vegetables. Free-range is verified through certification standards, not just a label claim.

**Q: How does it compare to making stock from scratch?**
A: It's 90% there. The flavor is clean and chicken-forward, but you lose the gelatin body and collagen richness of homemade stock. For soups and braises, it's excellent. For a simple sipping broth, it's actually better than most homemade versions because the sodium is controlled.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want genuinely good-tasting low-sodium broth with clean ingredients and don't mind the smaller carton size. **Skip it if** you need bulk quantities or want a traditional roasted-chicken flavor profile.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — One of the best low-sodium broths on the market, docked slightly for the awkward 32-ounce size and the 7-day post-open window.
