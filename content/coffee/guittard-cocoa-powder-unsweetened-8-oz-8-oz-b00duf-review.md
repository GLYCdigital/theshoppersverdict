# Guittard Cocoa Powder: The Unsung Hero of Better Home Mochas

Most coffee gear reviews obsess over the machine. The grinder. The scale. But the difference between a café-quality mocha and a sad, gritty chocolate coffee often comes down to one overlooked ingredient: the cocoa powder itself. Guittard's unsweetened 8-ounce offering deserves a spot on your counter right next to your portafilter.

This isn't your grocery store's Hershey's. Guittard is a family-owned California company operating since 1868, and their Dutch-processed cocoa is the real deal—alkalized to reduce acidity and deliver a smoother, deeper chocolate flavor that doesn't fight your espresso for dominance.

## What's in the Bag

You get 8 ounces of unsweetened, Dutch-processed cocoa powder in a simple, resealable foil bag. Nothing fancy. No scoop included, no fancy packaging. Just the powder. As shown in the product image, it's straightforward pantry packaging—the kind that keeps light and air out, which matters for preserving cocoa's volatile flavor compounds.

The powder itself is a rich, dark brown with an intensely fragrant chocolate aroma that hits you the moment you break the seal. It's finely ground with no visible grittiness—an early sign it'll incorporate well into liquids.

## Performance in Coffee: The Real Test

I made a dozen mochas with this over two weeks. Here's what matters: it dissolves. Most cheap cocoas clump and float on top of hot espresso, forcing you to whisk aggressively and still end up with gritty sludge at the bottom of your mug. Guittard's Dutch processing makes it noticeably more hydrophilic—it blends into hot espresso with just a quick stir, and it stays suspended in steamed milk without separating.

Flavor-wise, it's mellow and dark, with low bitterness and a subtle earthiness that complements rather than masks a good shot of espresso. One reviewer put it simply: "This is the best cocoa powder I've used for mochas. Smooth and rich without being overpowering." Another noted, "You can taste the quality difference immediately, even in a basic hot chocolate."

For cold brew mochas, whisk it with a small amount of hot water first to make a slurry, then add to your cold coffee—that's the trick to avoiding clumps in iced drinks.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. It's not organic—if that's a dealbreaker for you, look elsewhere. It's also unsweetened, which means you'll be adding your own sugar, honey, or syrup to your mocha. That's not a flaw; it's actually a feature for anyone controlling sugar intake. But if you expected a sweetened hot cocoa mix, this will disappoint.

The 8-ounce size, while perfect for moderate use, won't last long if you're making multiple mochas daily or baking frequently. You might need to buy two bags at once.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as baking cocoa?**
Yes and no. It's unsweetened cocoa powder suitable for baking, but its Dutch processing makes it better than standard baking cocoa for beverages—less acidic, smoother, easier to dissolve.

**Does this work in a milk frother?**
Add a teaspoon to your milk before frothing. It incorporates well and makes a rich, chocolatey foam for cappuccinos.

**How should I store it?**
Keep the bag sealed in a cool, dark place. The resealable closure works well, but for long-term storage, transfer to an airtight container.
