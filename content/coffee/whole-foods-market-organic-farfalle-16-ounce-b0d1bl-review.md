**Opening Hook**

Most organic pastas fall into one of two camps: they either disintegrate into a starchy mess or taste like cardboard pretending to be wheat. Whole Foods Market's Organic Farfalle sits in a rare third category — it actually behaves like pasta should. The question isn't whether it's organic (it is, certified by Oregon Tilth). The real question is whether it's worth hunting down when your grocery shelf already has six other bow-tie options. After testing this 16-ounce bag, I have a clear answer.

---

**What You Actually Get**

This is a straightforward package: one 16-ounce bag of dried farfalle, also known as bow-tie pasta. The pieces are uniform — roughly 1.5 inches across — with the classic pinched center and crimped edges. The color is a warm, pale gold, which tells you it's made from real semolina rather than bleached flour. There's no fancy box, no window, no resealable zipper. Just a clear bag with the Whole Foods 365-style branding. If you're expecting premium packaging, you'll be underwhelmed — but you're paying for the grain, not the bag.

---

**Performance and Build Quality**

Here's where this pasta earns its keep. I cooked it in salted boiling water and checked at 10, 11, and 12 minutes. At 11 minutes, the farfalle hit that sweet spot: firm through the center, with the ruffled edges catching just enough water to stay tender without going soft. The pinched middle — the thickest part of any bow-tie — cooked through evenly, which is something cheaper pastas mess up constantly. That's the durum wheat semolina doing its job; it holds structure under heat.

The texture after draining is noticeably resilient. Leftover pasta (should you have any) reheats without turning into a gluey brick — a real advantage over generic store brands. The surface is slightly rough, which means cream sauces cling rather than slide off. In a side-by-side against a conventional farfalle, the Whole Foods version had better sauce adhesion and a cleaner wheat flavor — nutty, not bland.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the 16-ounce size is slightly smaller than the standard 1-pound (16 oz) boxes from Barilla or De Cecco — same weight, but the bag feels lighter because the pasta is less dense. If you're feeding a family of four, you'll need the whole bag for a main course.

Second, there's no resealable closure. Once you open it, you're committed to transferring it to a container or using a clip. Minor, but annoying.

Third, the price. Organic certification costs money, and Whole Foods passes that along. You'll likely pay 30–50% more than conventional farfalle. If organic sourcing isn't a priority for you, this is a hard sell.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The home cook who makes pasta salads** — the texture holds up for hours without turning mushy.
- **The organic shopper** — Oregon Tilth certification means no synthetic pesticides, and it's non-GMO verified.
- **The sauce lover** — the ruffled edges and rough surface grip creamy and oil-based sauces better than smooth pasta.

**Skip it if** you're on a strict budget, need resealable packaging, or prefer a softer, more neutral pasta that disappears under heavy sauce.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does this pasta take to cook?**
A: 11–12 minutes for al dente. Start checking at 10 minutes — the pinched centers take slightly longer than the edges.

**Q: Is this pasta suitable for gluten-free diets?**
A: No. It's made from 100% durum wheat semolina and contains gluten.

**Q: Can I use this for baked pasta dishes like pasta al forno?**
A: Yes, but undercook it by 2 minutes (9 minutes) since it'll continue cooking in the oven. The structure holds up well.
