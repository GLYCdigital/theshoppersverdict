# Maesri Variety Curry Paste 8-Pack: Is This the Shortcut to Authentic Thai at Home?

You've tried the jarred curry pastes from the supermarket aisle—the ones that taste more like salt and oil than lemongrass and galangal. This 8-pack from Maesri is not that. It's the brand many Thai restaurants quietly use, and it shows up in a 4-ounce can that packs more punch than you'd expect from something so small.

## What You're Actually Getting

This variety pack gives you six cans total: two each of green, red, and masaman curry paste. Each can holds 4 ounces—enough for roughly one solid batch of curry for 4-6 people when mixed with a can of coconut milk. The packaging is straightforward: pull-tab lids, no fancy labels, just the essentials.

The ingredient lists are refreshingly clean. Green curry brings fresh green chilis, lemongrass, galangal, and kaffir lime peel. Red uses dried red chilis with a deeper, earthier profile. Masaman leans into roasted spices—cinnamon, nutmeg, and star anise—giving it that distinctive mild, aromatic character.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where Maesri separates itself from cheaper options. The paste is thick and fragrant right out of the can—you can smell the lemongrass before you even heat the pan. Sauté it in a little coconut cream for a minute, add the rest of the can of coconut milk, and you've got a curry base that tastes like it simmered for hours.

One thing I noticed immediately: the color. This paste is vivid—the green is genuinely bright, not muddy. That's a good sign that the chilies weren't heat-treated into oblivion.

The masaman is the standout for anyone who finds most Thai curries too spicy. It's sweet, nutty, and warming without any real heat. The red sits in the middle—approachable but present. The green delivers a legitimate kick that builds as you eat.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The Amazon reviews consistently highlight the same things. One reviewer put it simply: "This is the only curry paste I use now. Tastes like my favorite Thai restaurant." Another noted the portion control: "Perfect size for two people. No waste, no half-used jars in my fridge."

The variety format also earns repeated praise—people like not committing to 12 cans of one flavor. And Maesri's track record matters: this brand has been making curry paste since 1977, and it shows in the consistency.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. All three pastes contain shrimp paste. That's authentic, but it means vegans and anyone with shellfish allergies need to look elsewhere. The green curry is also spicier than many Western palates expect—if you're sensitive to heat, start with half a can.

The cans are small, which is great for freshness but means you'll pay a slight premium per ounce compared to larger jars. And if you prefer controlling your own heat level, these pastes come pre-spiced—you can't dial the chili down.

## Quick Answers to Common Questions

**How much coconut milk do I need per can?** One standard 13.5-ounce can of coconut milk per 4-ounce can of paste. Use a few tablespoons to sauté the paste first, then add the rest.

**Do these need refrigeration before opening?** No—they're shelf-stable. Once opened, refrigerate and use within a week. Most people finish a can in one meal.

**Can I use these for something besides curry?** Absolutely. A teaspoon stirred into coconut soup, marinades, or even scrambled eggs adds serious depth.

**Buy it if:** You want authentic Thai curry flavor that rivals your local restaurant, and you appreciate the convenience of perfectly portioned, pantry-stable cans.

**Skip it if:** You're vegan, allergic to shellfish, or prefer building curry from scratch with whole spices.
