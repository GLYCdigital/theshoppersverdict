Let's be blunt: the snack cracker aisle is a graveyard of overpriced, under-filled boxes. So when a store brand like 365 Everyday Value steps up with a Mini Sandwich Crackers pack that's pulling a 4.5-star average from 225 Amazon ratings, that's worth a closer look. Here's the real story after digging through the specs and what buyers actually say.

**What you're getting**

The box contains six individually wrapped 1.5-ounce pouches — roughly six crackers per serving. That's the standard lunchbox format, nothing revolutionary. But here's the detail that matters: these aren't actually "mini" crackers. As one reviewer noted, "They're regular-sized sandwich crackers, just in smaller pouches." If you're expecting those tiny bite-sized rounds, adjust your expectations. What you get is a classic round cracker with a cheddar-style filling, similar in size to a standard Ritz-style sandwich cracker.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The cracker itself holds up well. Multiple reviewers describe them as "crispy" and "fresh" with a buttery flavor that doesn't taste like artificial cheese powder. The filling is smooth and evenly distributed — no dry clumps or empty centers. One buyer put it simply: "Tastes just like the name brand, honestly better."

For a snack that's going to sit in a backpack or lunch bag, durability matters. Most customers report good structural integrity, though a few noted cracked crackers upon arrival. That's common with any sandwich cracker shipped in cold weather — the cheese hardens and the crackers become brittle. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you're stocking up before winter.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the recurring theme. Over and over, reviewers mention these cost less than name-brand alternatives while delivering comparable taste. One parent wrote: "My kids can't tell the difference between these and the expensive ones." Another appreciated the portion control: "Perfect size for a snack without wrecking my calorie budget."

The lower sodium content also gets callouts — around 190mg per serving versus 220-250mg in some competitors. If you're watching salt intake but still want a satisfying savory snack, that's a real advantage.

**Where it falls short**

The single-flavor limitation is the biggest drawback. You're getting cheddar or nothing. No pizza, no peanut butter, no variety. For families with different preferences, that means buying multiple boxes or supplementing with other brands.

The "mini" mislabel also bugs some buyers. If you're specifically looking for smaller crackers for toddlers or portion-controlled bites, these aren't it — they're standard size, just smaller packages.

And while the price is competitive, it's not dramatically cheaper than store-brand options at other major retailers. The savings are real but modest — think 10-15%, not half off.

A: No. The crackers are standard sandwich-cracker size. "Mini" refers to the snack pack format, not the cracker dimensions. If you need genuinely smaller crackers for young kids, look elsewhere.
