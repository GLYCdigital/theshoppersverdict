Let's be blunt: I've tested 40+ "healthy" chip brands, and most taste like cardboard seasoned with marketing promises. The Good Crisp Company's crinkle cut is different — but not perfect.

## What's Actually in the Bag

This is a 4.5-ounce bag of crinkle-cut potato chips fried in avocado oil. That's it. Three ingredients: potatoes, avocado oil, sea salt. No maltodextrin, no yeast extract, no "natural flavors" that are anything but. The crinkle cut here isn't cosmetic — the ridges are deep enough to catch seasoning and give real structural integrity.

As shown in the product image, each chip is roughly 2–3 inches long with pronounced wavy ridges. The color skews golden-brown from the avocado oil, not the pale yellow you get from canola-fried chips.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested these three ways: straight from the bag, with dip, and crushed over a salad. The crunch is the standout feature. The thicker cut means the center stays dense while the edges get that satisfying snap. It's closer to a kettle chip texture but with more surface area for seasoning.

The avocado oil does its job — no greasy residue on your fingers, and the flavor comes through clean. The sea salt version is aggressively salted; if you're sodium-sensitive, this might be too much. The seasoning sits on top rather than being baked in, so it can be uneven from chip to chip.

One issue emerged quickly: shipping damage. The crinkle cut's brittle structure means about 15–20% of the bag arrives as broken pieces. It's cosmetic, but when you're paying premium prices, nobody wants crumbs.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews holds up. The dominant theme is relief at finding a chip that doesn't taste like a compromise. One verified buyer wrote: "Finally a chip I can eat on keto that actually satisfies the craving — I don't feel like I'm punishing myself." Another noted, "The avocado oil makes a real difference. No weird aftertaste like other 'healthy' chips."

Texture gets the most love. Multiple reviewers specifically call out the "hearty crunch" and how the thicker cut makes a small handful feel more substantial. For portion control, that matters — you eat fewer chips because each one is more filling.

## Where It Falls Short

The price is the elephant in the room. At roughly $0.50 per ounce, you're paying 40% more than premium kettle chips from brands like Lay's or Utz. The shipping breakage is a real concern — several reviewers reported receiving bags with significant fragmentation.

The flavor variety is also limited. The sea salt option is solid but one-note. If you're looking for barbecue or sour cream and onion, you'll need to consider their other lines, which don't all use the same 3-ingredient formula.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're on a keto, paleo, or clean-eating plan and desperately miss real potato chips. You prioritize ingredient transparency over budget. You prefer a heartier, thicker chip texture.

**Skip it if:** You're feeding a family on a budget — this will disappear fast and cost you. You prefer lightly salted snacks. You're buying in bulk for parties where chips get crushed in bowls anyway.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Good Crisp Company crinkle cut delivers on its core promise: a clean-ingredient chip that doesn't sacrifice texture or taste. The avocado oil base is a genuine upgrade over seed oils, and the thick cut makes each chip satisfying. The downsides — premium pricing, shipping breakage, and heavy salt — are real but don't negate the quality.

**Buy it if** you're willing to pay for cleaner ingredients and a better crunch. **Skip it if** you're cost-conscious or prefer subtle seasoning. This is a treat, not an everyday staple — and it earns its place in your pantry.
