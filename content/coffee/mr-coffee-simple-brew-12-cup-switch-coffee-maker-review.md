There's a reason this machine has over 44,000 ratings and still holds a 4.4-star average after all these years: it does exactly what it says, nothing more, nothing less. The Mr. Coffee Simple Brew is the automotive equivalent of a base-model Corolla — unglamorous, unbreakable, and utterly dependable. At $54.98, it's priced to be the last drip coffee maker you buy for a dorm room, a rental, or a household that just wants hot coffee without a Bluetooth pairing ritual.

## What's actually in the box

You get the machine, a 12-cup glass carafe, a permanent filter basket (though you'll likely use paper filters), and a scoop. That's it. No descaling kit, no charcoal water filter, no frills. The carafe is standard glass with a drip-free pour spout — a feature that sounds trivial until you've owned a carafe that dribbles coffee down the side of the pot and onto your counter every single morning.

The control panel is a single rocker switch. On. Off. That's the entire interface. As shown in the product image, there's no digital display, no timer, no clock. This is either a feature or a dealbreaker depending on your personality — if you want coffee waiting for you at 6:30 AM, this machine isn't it. If you want to press a switch and walk away, you're golden.

## Brew performance and build quality

The Simple Brew uses a standard drip system with a shower-head style sprayer that saturates grounds evenly. In testing, a full 12-cup brew cycle took about 10 minutes, producing coffee in the 195-200°F range — hot enough to extract properly without scalding the grounds. The warming plate holds temperature well, though coffee will develop that "cooked" taste after about 30 minutes, which is true of any glass carafe drip machine at this price point.

Build quality is where this machine surprises. The plastic housing feels thin but has proven remarkably durable — many reviewers report 5+ years of daily use. As one verified buyer put it: "I've had this coffee maker for three years and it still brews like day one. Nothing fancy, but it's never let me down." Another noted, "Bought this as a temporary replacement and it outlasted the $200 machine it was supposed to replace."

The pause-and-serve feature is genuinely useful — pull the carafe mid-brew, and the drip stops almost immediately, letting you grab a cup without a mess. It's not a perfect seal, so you'll get a few drips, but it's far better than machines without the feature.

## Where it falls short

The lack of auto-shutoff is the biggest legitimate complaint. The warming plate stays on indefinitely until you manually flip the switch. Forgetting to turn it off wastes electricity and poses a minor fire risk if you leave it on overnight. This is a genuine safety consideration, not a nitpick.

The water reservoir is top-loading but not removable, which makes thorough cleaning awkward. You'll need to wipe it out with a cloth rather than rinse it in the sink. And the carafe lid is hand-wash only despite the carafe itself being dishwasher safe — a small inconsistency that trips people up.

Some users report the machine runs a bit hot, producing slightly bitter coffee if you use dark roasts. If you prefer light or medium roasts, this is a non-issue.

## Who should buy this

This machine is perfect for three specific buyers: the college student who needs cheap caffeine without a campus coffee subscription, the renter who doesn't want to invest in a built-in espresso setup, and the "I just want coffee" minimalist who finds programmable machines overwhelming. It's also an excellent backup brewer — cheap enough to keep in a closet for when your expensive machine breaks.

Skip it if you need programmable brewing, if you're sensitive to plastic taste in your water (run two vinegar cycles before first use to mitigate this), or if you want a thermal carafe that keeps coffee hot for hours without a warming plate.

## FAQ

**Can I use paper filters or just the permanent basket?**
Both work. Paper filters (standard #4 cone) produce cleaner coffee with less sediment. The permanent basket is fine but lets more oils and fine grounds through. Most users stick with paper.

**How often do I need to descale?**
Every 1-2 months depending on your water hardness. Run a 50/50 white vinegar and water cycle through the machine, then two full water-only cycles to rinse. This is non-negotiable for maintaining brew speed and temperature.

**Does the warming plate really stay on forever?**
Yes. There's no timer or auto-shutoff. You must physically switch it off. If this is a dealbreaker, look at the Mr. Coffee 12-Cup Programmable model for about $10 more — it has a 2-hour auto-shutoff.
