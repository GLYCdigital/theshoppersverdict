Let's cut to the chase: most "healthy" mayonnaise is a marketing lie wrapped in a squeeze bottle. Canola oil, soybean oil, added sugar — the stuff is everywhere. So when Chosen Foods claims their 100% avocado oil mayo is different, it's worth checking the fine print. I did. Here's what you're actually getting.

**What's actually in the jar**

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: avocado oil, filtered water, pasteurized egg yolk, organic vinegar, sea salt, and a touch of honey. That's it. No soybean oil, no canola oil, no modified food starch, no hidden sugars beyond that tiny honey hit (which comes in at under half a gram per serving). One tablespoon sets you back 100 calories and 11 grams of fat — but it's monounsaturated fat, the heart-healthy kind that's actually good for you.

You get a 24-ounce jar in the classic version, and the squeeze bottle is a nice convenience upgrade if you're tired of digging mayo out of a jar with a knife.

**How it actually performs**

Here's where Chosen Foods surprised me. Most avocado oil mayos I've tested have one of two problems: they're either too thin and runny, or they have a weird aftertaste from poorly refined oil. This one nails the texture — it's genuinely thick and creamy, closer to Hellmann's real mayo than to the watery stuff you get from some natural brands.

The flavor is neutral with a slight richness that doesn't overpower. I used it in a classic tuna salad and a batch of deviled eggs, and honestly, I couldn't tell it wasn't conventional mayo. That neutral profile is the key selling point — you're getting the health upgrade without the taste penalty that usually comes with it.

One thing worth noting: it emulsifies beautifully. If you're making a homemade aioli or a creamy dressing, this base is stable and won't break as easily as cheaper alternatives.

**What buyers consistently say**

With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the pattern is remarkably consistent. Buyers who switched from conventional mayo for health reasons are overwhelmingly satisfied. One reviewer put it simply: "Finally a mayo I don't feel guilty eating. Taste is indistinguishable from the name brands."

Another verified purchase highlighted its staying power: "I was skeptical at first but this has become our household staple. It doesn't separate in the fridge like some other natural mayos I've tried."

The criticism, predictably, focuses on price and a few quality-control quirks. "Great product but way too expensive for the squeeze bottle — it clogs constantly and you waste half of it," noted one frustrated buyer.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the price. Chosen Foods runs about double the cost per ounce of conventional mayo, and even pricier than some competitor avocado oil brands. If you're making gallon-sized batches of potato salad for a cookout, that adds up fast.

Second, the squeeze bottle has a real design flaw. The nozzle is narrow, and refrigerated mayo is thick, so it clogs. Multiple buyers reported this. If you can, buy the jar version and decant it into your own squeeze bottle — or just use a knife.

Third, that honey. If you're strict about Whole30 or paleo protocols, the honey technically disqualifies this product. It's a tiny amount, but it's there. Check your program's rules before committing.

**Who should buy this**

The keto crowd and anyone watching their omega-6 intake is the obvious target — avocado oil is almost entirely monounsaturated, which is a solid swap for most seed oils. If you're someone who eats mayo multiple times a week and wants a cleaner option without changing your recipes, this is the purchase. Families with egg allergies should note it does contain egg, so it's not an egg-free alternative.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does it taste like avocado?* No — that's the point. The oil is refined enough that it's flavor-neutral. You get creaminess without any green or grassy notes.

*Is it actually keto-friendly?* Yes, and it's a great option for keto. One gram of carbs per serving, zero sugar added beyond the trace honey.

*How does it compare to Primal Kitchen's avocado mayo?* Both are solid, but Chosen Foods has a slightly thicker texture and a more neutral flavor profile. Primal Kitchen uses lime juice which adds a tangy kick — Chosen Foods is cleaner tasting.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely cleaner mayo that doesn't sacrifice taste or texture, and you're willing to pay a premium for the better fat profile.

**Skip it if** you're on a strict budget or you don't care about seed oils — conventional mayo is cheaper and the taste difference is negligible in baked goods.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely good product with one design flaw (the squeeze bottle) and a price tag that keeps it from being an everyday staple for everyone. Worth the upgrade if you're health-conscious and mayo is a regular part of your rotation.
