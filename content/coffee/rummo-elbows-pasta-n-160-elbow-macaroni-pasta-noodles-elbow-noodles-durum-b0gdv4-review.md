Let's be honest: elbow macaroni is the workhorse of the pasta world. It's what you throw into a pot when you need mac and cheese for a kid's birthday party or a quick tuna casserole on a Tuesday. Nobody reviews elbows the way they review a rare imported tagliatelle. And yet, here we are — because Rummo's N.160 elbows are different, and the 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews backs that up.

**What you actually get**

The bag arrives at 17.6 ounces (500 grams), which is slightly less than the standard 1-pound American pasta box. That's the first thing you'll notice. The second is the texture. As shown in the product image, these elbows have a matte, almost chalky appearance — that's the bronze-die cut at work. Instead of being pressed through Teflon dies that leave pasta glassy and slick, Rummo uses bronze dies that scrape the surface, creating tiny ridges and micro-cracks. That's not a defect; it's the whole point.

Rummo also slow-dries this pasta at low temperatures for up to 50 hours, versus the high-heat blast drying most commercial pasta gets in a few hours. That longer process retains more of the durum wheat's natural flavor and creates a denser, more resilient structure.

**Cooking performance**

Drop these in well-salted boiling water and you'll notice two things immediately. First, the water turns slightly cloudy — that's the surface starch from the bronze cut, which is exactly what you want for sauce adherence. Second, these take longer than standard elbows. The bag says 11–13 minutes for al dente. I tested at 10 minutes and found them still slightly chalky in the center; at 12 they were perfect — firm to the tooth but fully cooked through.

The real test is what happens after cooking. These elbows hold their shape remarkably well. I made a batch, drained them, and left half in the colander for 15 minutes while I finished the sauce — no clumping, no sticking together. The next day, reheated leftovers in the microwave still had structure. Standard supermarket elbows become a gluey paste under those conditions.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The texture theme dominates the reviews. One buyer wrote, "These are the first elbows I've had that actually hold onto the cheese instead of letting it pool at the bottom of the bowl." Another noted, "I used these for a baked mac and cheese and they came out creamy but the pasta itself still had bite — that never happens with the cheap stuff." Multiple reviewers mention that the slightly larger size of these elbows (they're a bit longer and wider than Barilla's) makes them better for chunky sauces and baked dishes.

**Where it falls short**

The price is the elephant in the room. You're paying roughly triple what you'd pay for a store-brand box. For a weeknight mac and cheese where you're drowning the pasta in cheddar and milk, you genuinely won't taste the difference. The longer cooking time is also a real inconvenience — this isn't a 9-minute dinner pasta. And if you grew up on soft, tender elbows, the firmer bite might feel undercooked to you. That's not a flaw in the product; it's just a different standard.

**Who should buy this**

Buy these if you're making a baked pasta dish, a serious mac and cheese with a bechamel sauce, or a pasta salad where the noodles need to stand up to dressing and other ingredients. Buy them if you're hosting and want a pasta that won't turn to mush in a chafing dish. Skip them if you're just boiling elbows for a quick weeknight side dish — you won't notice the difference once butter and salt are involved.

**FAQ**

*Do these taste different from regular elbows?*
Slightly. The slower drying preserves more wheat flavor, so you get a subtle nuttiness that mass-market pasta lacks. But don't expect a dramatic flavor shift — the texture is the real upgrade.

*Are these gluten-free?*
No. They're made from 100% durum wheat semolina and contain gluten.

*Can I use these for cold pasta salad?*
Absolutely. In fact, that's one of their best uses — the firm bite and sauce-gripping surface hold up beautifully to vinaigrettes and mayonnaise-based dressings without going soggy.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you care about pasta texture and are making a dish where the noodle matters — baked pastas, premium mac and cheese, pasta salads. **Skip it if** you just need a cheap, fast elbow for everyday meals.
