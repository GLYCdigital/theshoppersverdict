**The Mochi Ice Cream That Actually Tastes Like Mango**

Let's be direct: most mango mochi ice cream you find in the grocery freezer aisle tastes like artificial flavoring wrapped in chewy rice dough that's either too hard or too gummy. Bubbies Alphonso Mango is the exception, and it's the reason this 7.5oz box has earned a 4.6-star rating across 229 Amazon reviews.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The box contains six individually wrapped pieces, each weighing 1.25 ounces. That's roughly two servings by FDA standards, though most people I've talked to finish the box in one or two sittings. Each piece is a thin layer of mochi — made from glutinous rice flour, sugar, and corn syrup — wrapped around a core of mango ice cream.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short. The first ingredient is Alphonso mango puree, not "mango flavoring" or "natural and artificial flavors." That's the entire ballgame here. Alphonso mangoes, often called the king of mangoes, have a distinct honeyed, almost saffron-like sweetness that's completely different from the generic mango flavor used in most frozen desserts.

**How It Performs**

The defining test for any mochi ice cream is whether you can bite through the shell without it shattering or sticking to your teeth. Bubbies nails this. Straight from the freezer, the mochi layer is soft, slightly chewy, and thin enough that you taste the ice cream immediately. The ice cream itself is dense and creamy — not icy, not whipped into oblivion.

One thing to know: this is a "eat it fast" product. Once you take a piece out of the freezer, you have about five minutes before the mochi starts to soften and become sticky. Don't let it sit out while you finish your work email. Take it directly from freezer to mouth.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The reviews consistently highlight two things. First, the authentic flavor. As one shopper put it, "Tastes like real mango, not candy." Another noted, "The texture is perfect — soft and chewy, not like the freezer-burned stuff at the grocery store." Second, the portion control works. Several reviewers mentioned that the individual wrapping prevents the "eat the whole pint" problem that comes with traditional ice cream.

**Where It Falls Short**

The price is the sticking point. At roughly $7-9 per box depending on where you buy it, you're looking at over a dollar per piece. That's expensive for what is essentially a small dessert bite. Some reviewers also noted that the mango flavor, while authentic, is subtle — if you're used to intensely flavored mango sorbet, this may taste mild to you.

The other limitation is logistics. This requires freezer space and careful handling. It's not something you can toss in a lunch bag and eat later. And if your freezer runs warm or you let the box thaw during shipping, you'll end up with a sticky, misshapen mess.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Mango purists** who want real Alphonso flavor, not artificial mango extract
- **Portion-control shoppers** who struggle with eating an entire pint of ice cream in one sitting
- **Anyone who's tried cheap mochi ice cream** and wondered what the hype is about — this is the benchmark

**Skip it if** you're on a tight dessert budget, you prefer intensely sweet frozen treats, or you don't have reliable freezer space.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many pieces are in the box?**
Six individually wrapped mochi pieces, each 1.25 ounces, totaling 7.5 ounces.

**Q: Does it need to stay frozen?**
Yes. Keep it at or below 0°F. Once thawed, the mochi becomes sticky and the texture is ruined.

**Q: Is this gluten-free?**
The mochi shell is made from glutinous rice flour, which is gluten-free. The ice cream contains no gluten ingredients. However, it's not certified gluten-free, so if you have celiac disease, check the packaging for allergen statements.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you appreciate authentic mango flavor and want a premium frozen dessert that's worth every calorie. **Skip it if** the price-per-bite doesn't justify itself for you — because at $1.25 per piece, this is a treat, not a staple.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Bubbies delivers exactly what it promises: real Alphonso mango, properly textured mochi, and a dessert that respects its ingredients. The price is high, but you're paying for quality that most competitors don't match.
