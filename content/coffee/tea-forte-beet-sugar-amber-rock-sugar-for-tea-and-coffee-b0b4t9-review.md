Let me be blunt: you don't need beet sugar. Your coffee will taste fine with the bag of granulated stuff in your pantry. But if you've ever watched a sugar cube slowly dissolve in a cup of tea, turning the amber liquid a shade darker as it melts, you understand what Tea Forte is selling here — and it's not just sweetness.

This is a 6.7-ounce jar of amber rock sugar crystals, each one roughly the size of a pea, made from beet sugar. The product image shows them piled in a clear glass jar with a small wooden scoop included. It looks like something you'd find in a boutique café, not a grocery aisle.

**How it actually performs**

Drop one or two crystals into a hot cup of coffee or tea, and here's what happens: they dissolve slowly, over the course of a minute or two, releasing sweetness gradually rather than all at once. That slow release means you can pull the crystal out mid-dissolution if you've oversweetened — something you simply cannot do with granulated sugar or liquid sweetener.

The flavor is where this gets interesting. Beet sugar has a faint, almost caramel-like undertone that plays remarkably well with dark roast coffee and black tea. It's not a strong molasses hit like raw cane sugar; it's subtle, rounding out bitter notes rather than covering them. One reviewer put it well: "It sweetens without changing the flavor profile of my pour-over. Just a clean, gentle sweetness."

Because the crystals are large, they dissolve faster in hot liquids than you'd expect from their size — about 60-90 seconds in a steaming mug, stirring occasionally. In cold brew or iced tea, plan ahead: they'll take several minutes and leave a syrupy layer at the bottom if you don't stir thoroughly.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many crystals should I use per cup?**
A: Start with one for light sweetness, two for a standard coffee or tea. Three approaches dessert-level sweetness. The crystals are surprisingly consistent in size.

**Q: Does beet sugar taste different from cane sugar?**
A: Barely, but the caramel undertone is detectable in plain black coffee or tea. Most people describe it as "rounder" or "smoother" than white sugar. It's subtle — not the strong molasses flavor of brown sugar.

**Q: Will this work in a coffee maker's sugar dispenser?**
A: No. The crystals are too large for standard dispenser mechanisms. Use it for stirring into finished drinks.
