**Bottom line up front:** If you're tired of watery almond milk ruining your pour-over, this is the nut milk that actually behaves like dairy in coffee. Elmhurst's unsweetened hazelnut milk is a two-ingredient product (hazelnuts, water) that delivers a surprisingly rich, barista-friendly texture. But it's not a flavored creamer — the hazelnut note is subtle, and that separation issue is real. Here's the full breakdown.

## What you actually get

The 32-fluid-ounce carton is shelf-stable, which is a genuine convenience. You can keep a backup in the pantry and rotate it into the fridge as needed. As shown in the product image, it's a standard paper carton — nothing fancy, but the packaging does the job. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: hazelnuts and water. No sunflower lecithin, no gellan gum, no "natural flavors" hiding anything. For that alone, it earns points with the clean-label crowd.

## Performance in coffee

Here's where this product either wins you over or loses you. The texture is genuinely different from other nut milks. Elmhurst uses a cold-press process that keeps more of the nut solids in suspension, which gives it a viscosity closer to 2% dairy milk. It steams well — you'll get microfoam, not the giant bubbles you see with thinner alternatives.

In a hot Americano, it holds up without curdling, which is more than I can say for most refrigerated almond milks. The flavor is roasted, slightly sweet from the nuts themselves, and nutty — but don't expect the punch of a Torani hazelnut syrup. This is a background note, not a leading flavor.

**The catch:** it separates. In cold brew, it's fine. In hot coffee, you'll see white flecks unless you shake the carton vigorously before each pour. Several reviewers mentioned this. One wrote, "Great taste but it separates in coffee no matter how much I shake it." That's consistent with what I found. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

## What buyers consistently praise

The clean ingredient list is the most common compliment. Reviewers repeatedly mention that it's one of the few nut milks without added sugar or stabilizers. The creamy texture also gets consistent praise — people who switched from oat milk or almond milk note that this feels more substantial. One reviewer said, "Finally a nut milk that doesn't taste like water. This is the only one I use in my espresso now."

## Where it falls short

- **Separation in hot coffee:** The most common complaint by far. It doesn't break or curdle, but it visibly separates. Shake hard, every time.
- **Subtle flavor:** If you want a hazelnut-forward coffee, this won't deliver. The nuttiness is gentle — some might call it weak. It's better suited to those who want a hint of flavor, not a dessert drink.
- **Price per ounce:** It's more expensive than shelf-stable almond milk. You're paying for the higher nut content, and it shows in the mouthfeel — but budget shoppers will notice.

## Who should buy this

- **Pour-over and drip drinkers** who want a clean, unsweetened nut milk that doesn't water down their coffee
- **Latte enthusiasts** who want to froth something dairy-free without buying a barista blend with added oils
- **Keto or low-carb folks** — no sugar, minimal carbs, just fat and protein from the nuts

**Skip it if** you want a sweet, flavored creamer, or if you mainly drink iced coffee (the separation is less noticeable but still present).

## FAQ

**Q: Does this taste like hazelnut coffee creamer?**
No. It's unsweetened and the hazelnut flavor is subtle — more like roasted nuts than a candy-like syrup. If you're used to flavored creamers, this will taste almost plain.

**Q: Will it froth for lattes?**
Yes, with caveats. It steams and froths better than most almond milks — you'll get decent microfoam — but it's not as stable as oat milk. Use a steam wand, not a French press, and expect the foam to dissipate faster.

**Q: How long does it last after opening?**
Refrigerate after opening and use within 7–10 days. The shelf-stable packaging means unopened cartons last months in the pantry.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a two-ingredient nut milk that actually has body in coffee and you don't mind shaking the carton before every use.

**Skip it if** you're looking for a sweet, hazelnut-flavored creamer experience — this is a milk, not a syrup.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a genuinely good product that's held back by separation issues and a flavor that's more subtle than many buyers expect. For the clean-label, unsweetened crowd, it's a solid pick.
