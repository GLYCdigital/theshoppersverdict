**The 60g Grinder That Makes You Question Why You Paid $150 for a Hand Grinder**

Here's the thing about manual coffee grinders: most of them cap out around 20-30 grams. That's fine for a single pour over, but useless if you're camping with three other people who all want coffee at sunrise. The CEVING 60g changes that math. It's the first mainstream hand grinder that actually holds a full 60 grams of beans — enough for a liter of French press or six cups of pour over — without making you stop, dump, and grind again. At $29.99, it's also half the price of most "budget" burr grinders that hold half the beans. I've had one on my counter for three weeks and another in my camping kit. Here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the grinder itself (black, 8.2 inches tall, weighs about 1.4 pounds), a stainless steel catch cup that doubles as the storage lid, and a cleaning brush. No manuals worth reading, no plastic scoop, no nonsense. The body is a matte ABS plastic with a brushed steel top knob — it looks more expensive than it is. The burr is a true stainless steel conical, not the ceramic junk you find on $20 grinders. The 40-click external adjustment dial sits at the base, and you turn it to choose your grind size. The catch cup twists off with a quarter turn and holds exactly 60 grams of ground coffee.

**Performance: Where It Wins and Where It Works**

Grind consistency is the headline. I ran a side-by-side test against a $120 Timemore C2. At medium-fine (setting 15 on the CEVING), the particle distribution was visibly similar — maybe 10% more fines in the CEVING, which is acceptable for pour over and actually helps French press body. At coarse settings (28-35), it avoids the boulder-and-dust problem that plagues cheap grinders. This is a burr grinder that behaves like one.

The 40 external settings are genuinely useful. The dial clicks with a satisfying detent, and I can reliably return to my espresso setting (setting 8) after brewing pour over (setting 18) without fiddling. That's rare at this price. The external adjustment means you don't have to disassemble anything mid-brew to change grind size.

But the 60g capacity has a tradeoff. Grinding 60 grams of a medium roast takes me about 3 minutes of steady cranking. Light roasts are closer to 4.5 minutes. The crank arm is comfortable, but there's no gear ratio advantage — you're doing the work. For a single cup, you're looking at 45 seconds of grinding, which is fine. For a full batch, set a timer.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The 4.6-star average across 590 ratings matches my experience. The praise clusters around three things: capacity, value, and grind quality. One reviewer wrote, "I've owned grinders at 4x the price and this produces comparable grounds. The 60g capacity made my camp mornings actually pleasant." Several buyers mention buying a second one as a gift, which tracks with the $30 price point.

**Where It Falls Short**

Be honest about three things before you buy. First, the external dial has no hard stop at either end. It spins freely, and if you throw it in a bag, it can shift settings. I now check my setting before every grind — it takes two seconds but it's a habit. Second, the plastic body is fine for counter use and gentle travel, but it won't survive a fall onto rocks. If you're a "throw everything in a duffel" camper, get a padded sleeve or accept the risk. Third, the catch cup is steel on the outside but has a plastic inner ring that can trap oils over time. A weekly brush-out keeps it clean, but it's not as easy to fully rinse as a glass catch cup.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy it if you're a pour over or French press drinker who wants one grinder for home and travel without spending $100+. Buy it if you camp or tailgate with more than one coffee drinker — the 60g capacity is a genuine game-changer. Buy it as a gift for someone who's "getting into" specialty coffee; it's the best $30 gateway grinder I've tested. Skip it if you're an espresso purist who needs ultra-fine grind precision at the margins — the 40 clicks are enough for most home machines, but a $200+ grinder still wins on micro-adjustment. Skip it if you have arthritis or wrist issues; the cranking effort is real.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask**

*How fine can it grind for espresso?*
Setting 1-4 produces a fine, powdery grind suitable for pressurized basket espresso machines like the De'Longhi Stilosa or Breville Bambino. For non-pressurized baskets, you'll need to dial in between 3-6, and you might find the steps slightly coarse. It's capable, but a dedicated espresso grinder it is not.

*Is the 60g capacity actually 60g of beans, or do you have to tamp them down?*
It's a true 60g. The chamber is wide enough that 60g of medium roast beans sits at the rim without packing. Light roasts (which are denser) fit with room to spare. I've measured it.

*Does it come with a warranty?*
CEVING includes a 12-month replacement warranty. A few reviewers reported a broken burr after a drop and received a replacement unit within a week — worth knowing since the price is low enough that some brands would just shrug.

**The Verdict**
