**Performance in the kitchen**

This butter behaves like the real thing because it is the real thing. The only difference is that the lactose has been converted into glucose and galactose through an enzymatic process. That means the fat content, the water content, and the protein structure are all identical to standard butter. I tested it three ways:

- **Coffee:** It emulsifies in hot coffee beautifully. A tablespoon blended in gives you that creamy, velvety mouthfeel without any separation. No greasy slick on top.
- **Baking:** I made shortbread cookies. They spread exactly as they should, browned evenly, and had that crisp-tender crumb. No spreading issues, no greasiness. It behaves like a standard stick of butter in every way.
- **Pan-searing:** It browns and foams properly. The milk solids toast the same way, giving you that nutty brown butter flavor.

**FAQ**

**Does it actually taste like butter?**
Yes. In blind taste tests, most people cannot distinguish it from conventional butter. It has the same creamy, salty, dairy-forward flavor.

**Can I use it for baking at high temperatures?**
Absolutely. It has the same smoke point as regular butter (around 350°F for pan frying, higher for baking), because the fat content is identical.

**Is it truly lactose-free, or just low-lactose?**
It's enzymatically treated to break down lactose into simple sugars, making it digestible for most people with lactose intolerance. If you have a severe dairy allergy (to casein or whey), this is still a dairy product and is not safe for you.
