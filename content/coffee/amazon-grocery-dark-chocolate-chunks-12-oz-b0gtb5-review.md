**Skip the baking aisle. This 12-ounce bag of dark chocolate chunks from Amazon Grocery might be the most underrated value in your pantry right now.**

I've tested dozens of chocolate products for baking and snacking, and what surprised me most about these chunks is how close they come to premium brands at roughly half the price. The 60% cacao content hits that sweet spot — dark enough to taste sophisticated, but not so bitter that you'll scare off casual chocolate fans.

**What you actually get**

The 12-ounce bag arrives as a standard stand-up pouch filled with roughly three cups of chocolate chunks. As shown in the product image, these aren't uniform chips — they're irregular, roughly chopped pieces that range from pea-sized to about the size of a quarter. That irregularity is actually a feature for baking: chunks melt at slightly different rates, creating pockets of chocolate throughout your cookies or muffins rather than uniform distribution.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: unsweetened chocolate, sugar, cocoa butter, soy lecithin, and vanilla. No weird stabilizers or artificial flavors.

**Real-world performance**

I put these through three tests: melting, baking, and straight-up snacking.

For melting, the chunks softened smoothly in a double boiler with no seizing or graininess. The chocolate has a decent snap at room temperature and a clean melt on the tongue — not waxy like some budget options.

In cookies, they performed better than standard chips. The chunk format means you get pools of chocolate in some bites and lighter distribution in others, which mimics the texture of bakery-style cookies. One Amazon reviewer noted: *"These melt beautifully and don't lose their flavor in the oven. My chocolate chip cookies came out tasting like they came from a professional bakery."*

For snacking, they're satisfying but dangerous — it's very easy to eat a quarter of the bag without noticing.

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about the tradeoffs before buying.

The lack of a resealable zipper is genuinely annoying. Once opened, you'll need a bag clip or a transfer to an airtight container. For a product meant to last through multiple baking sessions, this feels like a cost-cutting miss.

Chunk size inconsistency is a real issue for precision bakers. If you need uniform pieces for even melting in something like chocolate bark or molded candies, you'll need to chop them yourself. This isn't a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home bakers** who make cookies, brownies, or muffins regularly — the chunk format elevates baked goods
- **Dark chocolate preferers** who find milk chocolate too sweet and want something between 55-70% cacao
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want premium flavor without paying premium prices

**Skip it if** you need uniform chocolate chips for precise recipes, or if you're strictly a milk chocolate person — these will taste bitter to you.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these gluten-free?**
A: Yes, the ingredient list contains no gluten sources. However, they're processed in a facility that handles wheat, so those with severe celiac should exercise caution.

**Q: Can I use these for chocolate melts or fondue?**
A: Yes, but add a teaspoon of coconut oil or shortening per cup to keep the melted chocolate fluid. The 60% cacao content makes it slightly thicker than candy melts when melted alone.

**Q: How long do they stay fresh after opening?**
A: Stored properly in an airtight container at room temperature, expect 6-8 months of peak quality. They'll last longer, but flavor degrades gradually.
