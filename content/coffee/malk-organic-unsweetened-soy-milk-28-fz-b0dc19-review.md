**Malk Organic Unsweetened Soy Milk: The Clean-Label Contender for Coffee Drinkers**

Most plant milks in the coffee aisle are chemistry projects. Gums, emulsifiers, and “natural flavors” hide the fact that the star ingredient is often water and oil. Malk’s Organic Unsweetened Soy Milk is the opposite: a 28-ounce carton with exactly two ingredients — organic soybeans and water. That purity is why it holds a 4.5-star average across 225 Amazon ratings. But is a two-ingredient milk actually good in coffee, or just good on a label? I tested it for a week in drip, espresso, and cold brew.

---

**What’s in the box (and what’s not)**

You get one 28-fluid-ounce carton. No scoops, no frother, no extras — this is a straight milk replacement. The packaging is a standard gable-top carton, not the shelf-stable Tetra Pak you might expect. That means it lives in the fridge from day one, which is fine for most households. The nutrition panel is the real story: 80 calories per cup, 7 grams of protein, zero added sugar, and no calcium or vitamin D fortification. If you drink soy milk for protein, this delivers. If you drink it as a calcium substitute, you’ll need to get that elsewhere.

---

**Performance in coffee: the real test**

Here’s where Malk either wins you over or loses you. I steamed it for a flat white using a Breville Bambino Plus. The froth was airy and stable, with microfoam that held its shape for a solid three minutes — genuinely impressive for a milk with no stabilizers. The texture is thin, closer to skim dairy than oat milk. That’s not a flaw, but it’s a shock if you’re used to the syrupy body of Oatly Barista.

Flavor is the bigger variable. Unfermented soy has a distinct, slightly grassy bean note. In dark-roast espresso, it disappears and the coffee’s chocolate notes shine. In a light-roast pour-over, that beany character fights the coffee’s acidity and can taste almost savory. One Amazon reviewer wrote, *“Finally a soy milk that doesn’t taste like cardboard — but it’s not sweet, so don’t expect a dessert latte.”* Another noted, *“It separates in iced coffee if you don’t shake it hard. Shake well, every time.”* Both observations match my experience.

---

**What buyers consistently praise**

The ingredient list is the headline. Reviewers repeatedly mention that their kids drink it, that it doesn’t upset sensitive stomachs (no added gums or carrageenan), and that it tastes like “actual soybeans, not vanilla-flavored water.” The protein content also gets love — 7 grams per cup is higher than most almond or oat milks. For cold brew drinkers, it blends cleanly with no oily slick on top, which is a common complaint with nut milks.

---

**Where it falls short**

Three honest tradeoffs. First, the thin texture. If you want a latte that feels like a milkshake, this isn’t it. Second, the beany flavor is polarizing — some love it, some find it off-putting in anything but strong coffee. Third, no fortification. You’re paying a premium for a clean label, but you’re also giving up the calcium and vitamin D that most soy milks add. For a 28-ounce carton priced around $4–5, that’s a real consideration for daily drinkers.

---

**Who should buy this**

- **Clean-label purists** who read every ingredient and refuse gums or oils.
- **Protein-focused coffee drinkers** who want a plant milk with actual nutritional substance.
- **Dark-roast espresso fans** who want a neutral, non-sweet milk that doesn’t overpower.

Skip it if you need a thick, creamy barista blend, if you prefer lightly sweetened milks, or if you rely on fortified plant milks for calcium.

---

**FAQ**

**Does Malk soy milk froth for latte art?**  
Yes, if you steam it right. It produces fine microfoam, but the bubbles collapse faster than oat milk. Pour your art within 30 seconds of steaming.

**Does it taste like plain soy milk or is it sweet?**  
It’s unsweetened and tastes like roasted soybeans. There’s a natural, mild sweetness but zero added sugar. Expect a clean, slightly nutty flavor.

**How long does it last after opening?**  
About 7–10 days refrigerated. The carton says use within 7 days, but most buyers report it stays fresh closer to 10. Shake vigorously before each use — separation is normal.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you value a two-ingredient plant milk and drink mostly dark-roast coffee. **Skip it if** you need a thick, sweet, or fortified milk for daily lattes.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — exceptional ingredient integrity, solid frothed performance, but the thin texture and beany flavor won’t convert everyone. For the clean-label crowd, this is the soy milk to beat.
