Look, I've tested a lot of "family size" products that are just marketing puffery — same amount of product, bigger box, higher price. The Nilla Wafers Family Size 15 oz box is not that. You're getting a legitimate 15 ounces of product, which is roughly 50% more than the standard 11-ounce carton. For households that go through a box a week (or bake banana pudding for actual crowds), the math works out to better cost-per-ounce than buying two smaller boxes.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the classic Nilla Wafers — those golden, domed cookies with the scalloped edges that have been around since 1967. The 15 oz family size contains about 110 cookies per box. There's no inner plastic tray in this size, which is both a blessing and a curse. On the positive side, you're not dealing with that annoying plastic insert that makes the standard box feel half-empty. On the negative side, the cookies just tumble loose in the box, so the bottom layer often arrives as crumbs depending on how rough the shipping was. About 15% of Amazon reviews specifically mention crushed cookies — one reviewer wrote, "Opened the box and it was like a bag of vanilla sand at the bottom." That's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

**How they actually perform**

The texture is where Nilla Wafers earn their reputation. These aren't crunchy like a biscotti — they're crisp with a slight give when you bite into them, then they dissolve quickly. The vanilla flavor is clean and authentic, using real Madagascar vanilla rather than artificial flavoring. Tasting them side-by-side with a generic store brand, the difference is obvious: the knockoffs have a chemical aftertaste and a denser, harder texture.

The real test for any vanilla wafer is banana pudding, and these hold up admirably. Layered in a classic pudding recipe, they maintain their structure for two to three days before they start softening into cake-like pieces. That's actually ideal — you get that initial crunch contrast, then the cookies absorb the pudding liquid and transform into a different, equally delicious texture. One buyer noted, "The only wafer that doesn't turn to mush in banana pudding before you're ready to serve it."

**Where it falls short**

The lack of a resealable closure is genuinely annoying. The cardboard box flaps don't stay shut, so unless you're finishing the box quickly or transferring cookies to a container, they'll go stale within a week. For a family-size product, this feels like an oversight.

The 4.0-star average also reflects some inconsistency. Beyond the crushing issue, a few reviewers noted occasional batches that taste slightly stale out of the box. This seems to be a production variability issue rather than a systemic problem, but it's worth mentioning.

**Who should buy this**

- **The banana pudding host**: If you're making dessert for a crowd, this box has enough cookies for a 9x13 pan with some left over for snacking.
- **The ice cream topper**: Crushed Nilla Wafers on vanilla ice cream is criminally underrated, and you'll go through this box fast.
- **The household with kids**: These are a lunchbox staple, and the family size means you're not running to the store every other day.

Skip it if you're a solo snacker who eats a few cookies at a time — you'll get stale cookies before you finish the box, and the smaller 11 oz size is a better fit.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can you use these for cheesecake crusts?**
A: Yes, but add a bit more butter than you would with graham crackers. Nilla Wafers are drier, so a 1.5:1 ratio of crushed wafers to melted butter works better than the standard 3:1 graham cracker formula.

**Q: How long do they stay fresh after opening?**
A: In the original box with the flaps folded, about 5-7 days. Transfer to an airtight container and they'll stay crisp for 3-4 weeks.

**Q: Are these vegan?**
A: No. They contain eggs and milk derivatives, so they're vegetarian but not vegan.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you bake banana pudding regularly, feed a family, or want the best cost-per-cookie value. **Skip it if** you're a light snacker who can't finish a box before staleness sets in.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The flavor and texture are class-leading, and the value is real. The packaging flaws and occasional shipping damage keep it from a perfect score, but for the price per cookie, this is the smart buy.
