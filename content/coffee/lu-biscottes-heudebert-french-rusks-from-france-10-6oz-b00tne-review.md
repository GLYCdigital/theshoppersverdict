# Lu Biscottes Heudebert Review: French Rusks That Outperform Your Toaster

Let's be honest — you don't *need* a box of twice-baked French bread slices shipped across the Atlantic. But after testing Lu's Biscottes Heudebert against my standard morning toast routine for two weeks, I understand why this product has earned 4.5 stars from 225 Amazon shoppers. These aren't just fancy crackers. They're a legitimate breakfast upgrade.

## What's Actually in the Box

The 10.6-ounce package contains roughly 24 rectangular slices of golden-brown rusk. Each piece is about 4 inches long, lightly toasted on both sides, with a subtle honey-gold color that tells you they've been properly oven-baked. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: wheat flour, sugar, butter (3.5%), yeast, salt, and a touch of malt extract. No preservatives, no palm oil, no artificial anything.

As shown in the product image, these aren't fluffy bread slices. They're dense, crisp, and surprisingly sturdy — think of them as the lovechild of a croissant and a crouton.

## How They Actually Perform

Here's where Biscottes Heudebert surprised me. The texture is genuinely different from anything you'll get from a standard toaster. Because they're twice-baked, the interior is honeycombed with tiny air pockets that stay crunchy for weeks — not hours. I left an open package on my counter for ten days and the last slice was just as crisp as the first.

The flavor is where these shine. There's a gentle butteriness that reminds me of a good croissant, plus a faint sweetness that doesn't veer into dessert territory. They're complex enough to eat plain but neutral enough to build on.

I tested them three ways:
- **With butter and jam** – the classic move. The rusk holds up better than toast, no sogginess.
- **With avocado and egg** – the crisp texture stands up to wet toppings without turning mushy.
- **Plain with coffee** – this is where they truly belong. The slight sweetness cuts through dark roast bitterness perfectly.

## What Shoppers Are Saying

The Amazon reviews consistently highlight two things. First, authenticity: "Tastes exactly like the ones I had in France — brings back memories of breakfast at my grandmother's house," one reviewer notes. Another echoes that sentiment: "Finally, real French biscottes. The American substitutes are too sweet and crumbly."

The second theme is durability. Multiple buyers mention keeping these for emergency breakfasts or travel snacks because they don't go stale. One reviewer specifically praised them as "perfect for camping — no crumbs, no mess, just eat and go."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's address the elephant in the room: the price. At roughly $5–7 for what's essentially fancy toast, you're paying a premium for the French import label. If you're on a tight grocery budget, standard bread will serve you fine.

The bigger issue is fragility. The box contains loosely packed slices, and multiple reviewers report receiving broken pieces. One customer noted, "Half the box arrived as crumbs — still tasty, but disappointing for the price." If you're buying these for a gift or special occasion, factor in the shipping lottery.

Finally, these contain wheat gluten and are made in a facility that processes nuts. If you have celiac disease or serious gluten sensitivity, these aren't for you — no matter how good the reviews are.

## Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can I eat these if I'm watching my weight?**
A: Each rusk is about 30 calories with minimal sugar. They're lighter than most crackers and significantly less caloric than croissants. Just watch your toppings.

**Q: How long do they stay fresh after opening?**
A: In my testing, they stayed perfectly crisp for two weeks in a sealed container. The lack of preservatives means they'll eventually soften, but they last far longer than regular bread.

**Q: Are these sweet or savory?**
A: Both. The sugar content is modest (about 6g per 100g), giving them a subtle sweetness that pairs with jam or cheese equally well.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a shelf-stable, genuinely French breakfast staple that outperforms toast in every way. The texture and flavor justify the price for anyone who eats breakfast at home regularly.

**Skip it if:** You're gluten-free, on a strict budget, or need something that survives rough shipping without breaking.
