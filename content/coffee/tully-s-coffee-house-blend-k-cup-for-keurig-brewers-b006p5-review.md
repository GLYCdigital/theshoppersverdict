You've had one too many Keurig cups that taste like hot brown water. You want something that actually delivers café-level flavor without leaving the house. Tully's Coffee House Blend is one of the few K-Cups that consistently threads that needle — and with a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, I'm not the only one saying it.

Tully's is a Pacific Northwest roaster with a cult following, and this House Blend is their flagship. It's a medium roast designed to be approachable — not acidic, not burnt, just balanced. The pods are standard 0.38 oz K-Cups, compatible with all Keurig 1.0 and 2.0 brewers, plus most single-serve machines that accept the standard pod shape.

## What you actually get

The box contains 24 or 48 foil-sealed pods depending on which listing you grab. Each pod is nitrogen-flushed, which keeps the grounds fresh for months on your shelf. No fancy packaging gimmicks — just functional, sturdy pods that I've never seen arrive crushed or leaking, which is more than I can say for some budget brands.

## How it performs in real use

I brewed this at both the 8-ounce and 10-ounce settings on a Keurig Supreme. At 8 ounces, you get the full character: notes of cocoa and a hint of toasted nut, with a smooth, almost creamy mouthfeel. At 10 ounces, it thins out slightly but holds its flavor far better than most medium roasts — no watery disappointment.

The aroma is the standout. As soon as the brew starts, your kitchen smells like an actual coffee shop. That's not marketing fluff; several reviewers noted the same. One buyer put it this way: *"First time I've had a K-Cup that smells as good as it tastes. My whole office asked what I was brewing."* Another long-time customer said, *"I've been buying these for three years. They're the most consistent pod I've found — never a bad cup."*

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the limitations. If you're a dark roast devotee who wants heavy, smoky, almost bitter intensity, this isn't it. The House Blend is medium through and through. It won't punch you in the face with boldness — it's more like a friendly handshake.

Also, the price. At roughly $0.60–$0.75 per pod depending on the listing, you're paying a premium over generic options. That's on par with Starbucks or Peet's pods, but if your goal is absolute budget savings, you'll find cheaper. The tradeoff is quality — and most buyers seem to accept it willingly.

One more minor gripe: the box doesn't have a resealable opening. Once you tear it open, you're committed to storing loose pods. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Who should buy this

- **The home office worker** who wants a consistent, pleasant cup without fiddling with grinders and pour-overs.
- **The gift-giver** shopping for a coffee lover who owns a Keurig — this is a safe, well-received choice.
- **The medium roast loyalist** who finds Starbucks Pike Place too bitter and Dunkin too thin.

Skip it if you need dark roast intensity or you're strictly chasing the lowest cost per cup.

## FAQ

**Is Tully's House Blend strong enough for iced coffee?**
Yes, but brew it at 6 ounces to keep concentration. At the standard 8–10 ounce setting, the ice will dilute it noticeably.

**Does it work in a Keurig Mini or other compact brewers?**
Yes. Standard K-Cups fit all Keurig models, including the Mini, K-Express, and K-Slim. No adapter needed.

**How does it compare to Tully's other roasts?**
It's smoother and less acidic than their French Roast. If you want more depth, try their Hawaiian or Italian — but House Blend is the most balanced entry point.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliably smooth, aromatic medium roast that turns your Keurig into something worth waking up for. **Skip it if** you demand dark roast intensity or need the absolute cheapest pod on the shelf.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — the best medium roast K-Cup I've tested, held back only by a premium price and a flavor profile that won't satisfy everyone.
