Let me be blunt: most protein pancake mixes taste like cardboard dust dressed up with fake sugar. The Flourish Protein Pancake & Waffle Mix isn't that. It's also not a magic breakfast that tastes exactly like IHOP — but it's genuinely close, and for the macros, that's worth something.

What you're actually buying is a 1.5-pound bag of mix that delivers 20 grams of whey protein isolate per serving, plus flax seed for fiber and omega-3s. As shown in the product image, the ingredient list is refreshingly short — no hydrogenated oils, no artificial colors, no "natural flavors" hiding a chemistry set. The base is oat flour and pea protein alongside the whey, which gives it a more complex texture than single-source protein blends.

**The Real-World Test**

I made these three ways: classic pan pancakes, a Belgian waffle, and a mug version in the microwave. The batter is thinner than traditional pancake mix — expect that. It spreads quickly in the pan, so use a smaller ladle than you normally would. Here's the key move: let the batter rest for 3–4 minutes after mixing. That lets the flax and oat flour hydrate, which eliminates the grittiness that plagues most protein mixes.

Texture-wise, you get a fluffy interior with a slightly crisp edge — nothing like the rubbery hockey pucks that gave protein pancakes their bad reputation. The waffle version is the standout; it crisps up beautifully and holds syrup without turning to mush.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews tracks with what I found. The consistent praise centers on the protein quality and the fact that it doesn't taste like a protein product. One reviewer put it well: "My kids couldn't tell these weren't regular pancakes. That's the highest compliment I can give." **Where It Falls Short**

The stevia aftertaste is real. It's subtle — not the throat-burning artificial sweetness you get from some competitors — but if you're sensitive to sugar alcohols or stevia, you'll notice it. A splash of vanilla extract or cinnamon masks it completely, but you shouldn't have to doctor a mix to enjoy it.

The price is the bigger issue. Per serving, this runs about 40–50% higher than standard pancake mix. That's the protein tax, and it's not outrageous — but it's not a budget breakfast either. Also, the 1.5-pound bag yields about 8 servings. If you're feeding a family of four, that's two breakfasts per bag. Plan accordingly.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Gym-goers who want breakfast to count toward their protein intake** — 20g per serving is meaningful, not token.
- **Parents trying to sneak nutrition into picky kids** — the mild flavor and fluffy texture genuinely pass the kid test.
- **Meal preppers** — the dry mix keeps for months and makes for a quick weekday breakfast.

Skip it if you need a massive bag, you're on a tight grocery budget, or you're strictly avoiding stevia-based sweeteners.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this mix work for overnight oats or baking?**
A: Not really. It's formulated for pancakes and waffles. Using it as a flour substitute in other recipes gives unpredictable results. Stick to the intended use.

**Q: How much water or milk should I add?**
A: The package suggests 1/3 cup liquid per 1/2 cup mix. I found that slightly thin. Use 1/4 cup plus a tablespoon for thicker pancakes, and let the batter rest as I mentioned.

**Q: Is the protein actually absorbable, or is it just for marketing?**
A: It's whey protein isolate — that's the most bioavailable form. You're getting real protein, not collagen or pea protein masquerading as something better.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely good-tasting high-protein breakfast without DIY protein powder experiments. The texture and flavor are above average for the category, and the clean ingredient list justifies the premium.

**Skip it if:** You're fine with regular pancakes and get your protein elsewhere, or if stevia bothers you. You can make the same macros from eggs and oatmeal for a fraction of the cost — you're paying for convenience and taste here.
