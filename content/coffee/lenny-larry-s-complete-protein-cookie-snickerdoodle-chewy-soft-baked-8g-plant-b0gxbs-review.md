You know the drill: a "protein cookie" that tastes like compressed cardboard with a sad dusting of cinnamon. Lenny & Larry's isn't that. The Snickerdoodle Complete Protein Cookie is one of the better-known names in the protein snack game, and after 220 ratings holding a solid 4.4 stars, it's earned its shelf space. But is it actually good, or just "good for a protein cookie"? Let's dig in.

**What you're actually getting**

Each box contains a single 4.25-ounce cookie — that's roughly the size of a bakery-style cookie, not those sad little 2-ounce diet biscuits. The macros are the headline: 16 grams of complete plant protein from a pea and brown rice blend, 4 grams of fiber, and only 5 grams of sugar. That's a genuinely solid protein-to-sugar ratio for something that lists sugar as the third ingredient and leads with enriched flour.

As shown in the product image, the cookie is thick, golden-brown, and generously coated in a cinnamon-sugar crust. It's designed to be soft-baked, and in most batches, it delivers — a dense, chewy bite that's closer to a real cookie than most competitors manage.

**Real-world performance**

I've eaten a lot of protein cookies. Most are either hockey pucks or crumbly messes that disintegrate before they hit your mouth. Lenny & Larry's gets the texture mostly right: the cookie has a satisfying chew, holds together well, and doesn't require a glass of milk to choke down. The cinnamon flavor is present but restrained — you'll taste it, but it's not a punch-you-in-the-face snickerdoodle. If you're expecting the aggressive cinnamon-sugar hit of a fresh-baked Cinnabon, temper expectations.

One buyer put it plainly: *"These are my go-to when I need a sweet fix but don't want to blow my macros. The texture is way better than Quest cookies."* Another noted the tradeoff: *"Taste is good, not great. But for 16g of protein, I'll take it."*

That's the honest consensus. This isn't a gourmet dessert; it's a functional snack that happens to taste decent. The 300+ calories per cookie are worth flagging — if you're snacking on these daily, that's real caloric weight. The 5g of sugar is the win; the calorie count is the cost.

**Where it stumbles**

Inconsistent batches are the most common complaint in the reviews. Some buyers report receiving cookies that are dry, hard, or noticeably stale — a quality-control issue that's frustrating when you're paying a premium. The Snickerdoodle flavor itself is also on the subtle side; if you're a cinnamon maximalist, you might find it underwhelming.

Also worth noting: these are *big* cookies. At 4.25 ounces each, they're a substantial snack, not a light nibble. If you're used to 100-calorie snack packs, this will feel like a meal.

**Who should buy this**

- **Gym-goers who want a dessert-like protein hit** without blending another shake
- **Office workers** who need a shelf-stable, non-melting snack for the desk drawer
- **Vegans and plant-based eaters** — this is genuinely complete plant protein, not a soy-heavy compromise
- **Anyone trying to cut sugar** — 5g per cookie is genuinely low for something this indulgent-tasting

**Skip it if** you need a low-calorie snack, want an intense cinnamon flavor, or are sensitive to texture inconsistencies.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these actually good for you?**
A: They're a processed food, so "healthy" is relative. But 16g protein, 4g fiber, 5g sugar, and no cholesterol (plant-based) makes them a solid choice compared to a standard cookie. Just respect the 300+ calories.

**Q: Do they taste like real snickerdoodles?**
A: Close, but not exact. The cinnamon is there, the sweetness is right, but it's a denser, less delicate crumb than a bakery cookie. Think "hearty snickerdoodle" rather than "light and fluffy."

**Q: How do these compare to Quest or other protein cookies?**
A: Lenny & Larry's are softer and less artificial-tasting than Quest's fiber-forward cookies. They're also bigger and more filling. The tradeoff is more calories per cookie.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely tasty, high-protein snack that doesn't taste like a protein bar pretending to be dessert. **Skip it if** you're watching calories strictly or expect bakery-level cinnamon intensity.

This is a 4.2-star product through and through — solid, dependable, and better than most of its category. It won't change your life, but it'll make your 3 PM snack break a lot more satisfying.
