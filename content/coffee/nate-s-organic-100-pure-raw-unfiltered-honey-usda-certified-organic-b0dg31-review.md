**What You Get In The Bottle**

As shown in the product image, this is a 12-ounce squeeze bottle — not a glass jar. That's a deliberate choice. The squeeze design means you can drizzle it straight into your coffee or tea without chasing a honey dipper around the kitchen. The honey itself is USDA Certified Organic, which matters because honey is one of the most adulterated products in the grocery store. Many "raw" labels are marketing fluff; Nate's has third-party certification behind it.

The texture is where this honey immediately differentiates itself. It's thick, cloudy, and contains visible pollen particles. That cloudiness is the unfiltered marker — this honey hasn't been heated to 160°F and blasted through fine mesh like most commercial brands. You're getting it closer to how the bees made it.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I've been using this in coffee, tea, oatmeal, and straight off the spoon for several weeks. The flavor profile is floral and mild with a clean finish — it sweetens coffee without overpowering the roast. That's a strength for daily use, though if you're hunting for a dark, molasses-like buckwheat honey, this isn't it.

The crystallization issue is real, and it's the single biggest point of confusion among buyers. Because this honey is raw, it will crystallize — often within two to four weeks. That's not spoilage; that's the natural glucose structure solidifying. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"I thought it went bad when it turned solid, but after reading up on raw honey, I realized that's actually a good sign. Warm water bath fixed it in 10 minutes."* That's the tradeoff of buying raw: you trade shelf-stable convenience for actual authenticity.

The squeeze bottle works well initially, but if the honey crystallizes before you finish the bottle, the cap can clog. Plan to use it regularly or be prepared to run warm water over the nozzle.

**FAQ**

*Is crystallized honey safe to eat?*  
Yes, completely. Crystallization is a natural process in raw honey and doesn't affect safety or nutrition. To reliquefy, place the bottle in a warm water bath (under 110°F) for 10-15 minutes. Don't microwave it — that destroys the beneficial enzymes.

*Is this actually different from regular store honey?*  
Yes, significantly. Most commercial honey is heated and ultra-filtered for clarity and shelf life, which strips out pollen and enzymes. Nate's skips those steps, which is why it's cloudy and crystallizes faster — that's the proof of its raw status.

*Does this count toward my daily sugar intake?*  
It does. Honey is still sugar — about 17 grams per tablespoon. The organic and raw attributes don't change the caloric reality. Use it as a sweetener, not a health supplement.
