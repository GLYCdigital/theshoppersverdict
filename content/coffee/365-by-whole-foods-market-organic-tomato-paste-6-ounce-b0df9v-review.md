## What's Actually in the Can

This is a 6-ounce can of double-concentrated tomato paste — roughly 12 tablespoons of usable product. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: organic tomatoes. That's it. No citric acid, no salt, no sugar, no "natural flavors" hiding anything. As shown in the product image, the can itself is straightforward — red label, clear branding, nothing flashy. It's certified organic by Oregon Tilth and the can lining is BPA-free, which matters if you're cooking acidic tomatoes regularly.

The 6-ounce size is the standard recipe-friendly portion. Most recipes calling for tomato paste want 2-6 tablespoons, so this gives you two to three uses per can. It's not the huge #10 can restaurants buy, but it's also not a wasteful 3-ounce tube that disappears in one batch.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this paste earns its keep. Because it's double-concentrated, you need less. When I tested it side-by-side against Cento and Contadina, one tablespoon of the 365 paste delivered the same depth as two tablespoons of the others. That means the per-use cost is actually competitive, even if the can price seems higher upfront.

The texture is dense and smooth, not gritty or fibrous. It cooks down into sauces without leaving tiny tomato solids floating around. The flavor profile leans sweet-tart with real tomato character — not cooked-to-death or metallic like some canned pastes. Since there's no added salt, you're in control of seasoning, which is ideal for low-sodium diets or recipes where you're adding umami boosters like anchovies or soy sauce.

One practical note: this paste caramelizes beautifully. When you're making a soffritto or blooming the paste in oil before adding liquid, you'll get that deep, almost meaty browning that builds flavor foundations. Thinner pastes just steam and never reach that stage.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the limitations. The 6-ounce can has no resealable lid. Once opened, you're committed to either using it within a week or freezing portions. I'd love to see this in a squeeze tube format for convenience — that's the one area where Amore or Mutti tubes beat it.

The double concentration also means you'll need to adjust some recipes. If you're following a standard American recipe written for single-concentrate paste, reduce the amount by roughly half or add a splash of water to compensate. It's a minor tweak, but worth knowing before you dump in the full two tablespoons a recipe calls for.
