## What You Actually Get

A 4-pound (1.81 kg) resealable bag of yellow moong dal — split mung beans that have been hulled and split in half. The bag shows Jiva's transparent sourcing story on the back, including the farm region and harvest date. It's USDA Organic certified, which means third-party verified, not just marketing.

## Real-World Performance

These beans cook in 12-15 minutes on a stovetop simmer without pre-soaking. That's fast for any legume. The texture lands creamy but not mushy — individual splits hold their shape enough for salads, yet break down fully for classic dal preparations.

The flavor is clean and slightly sweet, which is what you want from moong dal. No earthy bitterness or metallic aftertaste that plagues lower-grade imports. As shown in the product image, the splits are uniformly yellow — no discolored or aged beans mixed in.

Protein content sits at roughly 24g per 100g dry weight, making this a legit budget protein source. A 4-pound bag yields about 36 servings (half-cup cooked each), which works out to pennies per meal.

## Where It Falls Short

The 4-pound bag is genuinely awkward to store. It's not a standard pantry shelf size. Transfer to a glass jar or container immediately — the resealable zipper works but isn't airtight enough for long-term storage in humid climates.

Batch consistency wobbles slightly. Two of the bags I've tested had a higher percentage of smaller split pieces, which cook about 3-4 minutes faster and can turn mushy if you're not watching. Not a dealbreaker, but if you're making a precise recipe, check your cook time.

The price per pound is also higher than bulk Indian grocery store options — you're paying a premium for organic certification and the pre-cleaning convenience.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these beans need soaking before cooking?**
A: No. Rinse, then simmer directly for 12-15 minutes. Soaking makes them softer but isn't required.

**Q: Are these the same as regular yellow lentils?**
A: Moong dal is split mung beans, not lentils. Different legume entirely. They cook faster than most lentils and have a milder flavor.

**Q: How should I store the beans after opening?**
A: Transfer to an airtight container in a cool, dark place. Use within 6-8 months for best freshness. The resealable bag works short-term but isn't bug-proof for long storage.
