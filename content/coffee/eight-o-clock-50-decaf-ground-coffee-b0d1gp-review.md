Let's be honest about "half-caf" coffee: most of it tastes like someone watered down a regular cup and called it a day. Eight O'Clock's 50% Decaf doesn't do that. It's a genuinely thoughtful blend — half regular beans, half decaffeinated beans, roasted together to a consistent medium profile. For anyone who wants a second (or third) cup without climbing the walls, this is worth a serious look.

**What's actually in the bag**

You're getting a 12-ounce bag of pre-ground coffee. The grind is uniform — not too fine, not too coarse — which means it plays nice with standard drip machines, pour-overs, and French presses. The aroma when you crack the bag open is classic Eight O'Clock: nutty, slightly sweet, with that familiar roasted depth. No chemical aftertaste, no "decaf weirdness" that plagues cheaper half-caf products.

**How it performs in real use**

I brewed this three ways: standard drip, a pour-over cone, and a French press. In the drip machine, it delivered a clean, balanced cup with moderate body — think diner coffee but with more character. The pour-over brought out a bit more brightness, and the French press gave it a heavier mouthfeel without turning muddy. Bitterness is minimal, even if you let it sit on the burner too long. That's a real plus for a half-caf blend.

Caffeine-wise, it's exactly what the label promises: roughly half the kick of a regular cup. If you're sensitive to caffeine but miss the ritual of a full mug, this hits the spot. If you're looking for a decaf that tastes indistinguishable from regular, this isn't quite there — the decaf beans do soften the edge slightly — but it's remarkably close.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 225 ratings at 4.5 stars, a few themes keep coming up. One reviewer put it simply: "Finally a half-caf that doesn't taste like hot water." Another noted, "I can drink two cups after dinner and still sleep fine — but I still get that coffee satisfaction." Several mention it's become their daily driver because it bridges the gap between "I want coffee" and "I don't want to be wired at 8 PM."

**Where it falls short**

The biggest practical gripe: there's no resealable zipper on the bag. Once you open it, you're committed to either finishing it quickly or transferring it to an airtight container. Coffee goes stale fast once exposed to air, and this bag doesn't help you slow that clock. Also, if you own an espresso machine, skip this — the grind is too coarse for pressurized baskets, and you'll get a thin, under-extracted shot.

**Who should buy this**

- **The two-cups-a-day drinker** who wants a second mug post-lunch without the jitters.
- **The pregnant or caffeine-sensitive coffee lover** who refuses to drink flavorless decaf.
- **The evening coffee ritualist** who wants a proper cup with dessert, not a caffeine spike before bed.

**Skip it if** you need full-strength caffeine in the morning — this won't wake you up the way a dark roast will — or if you're exclusively an espresso drinker.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this actually 50% caffeine, or just a marketing label?**  
A: It's a real blend — half regular beans, half decaffeinated beans, roasted together. You get roughly half the caffeine of a standard cup.

**Q: Can I use this in a Keurig-style machine with a refillable pod?**  
A: Yes, the grind size works well in refillable K-cups. Just don't overpack it — use about two tablespoons per cup.

**Q: How does it compare to Eight O'Clock's regular ground coffee?**  
A: It's slightly softer on the palate and less punchy, but the roast character is consistent with their regular medium roast. You won't feel like you're drinking a different brand.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely drinkable half-caf that doesn't sacrifice flavor for lower caffeine. **Skip it if** you need full-strength coffee or only brew espresso.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses a few points for the non-resealable bag and espresso incompatibility, but for drip and pour-over drinkers, this is a solid daily choice at a fair price.
