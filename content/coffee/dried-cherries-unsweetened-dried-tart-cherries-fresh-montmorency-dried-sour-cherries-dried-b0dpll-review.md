## Texture and Taste: The Real Story

Here's where these cherries divide opinions. They're **not** the dry, brittle craisins you're used to. These are softer, almost plump — closer to a dried date in moisture content. Bite into one and you get a tart punch upfront that lingers into a mild sweetness. The tartness is genuine Montmorency — think cherry pie filling before the sugar, not a sweet Bing cherry.

The moisture level is the standout feature. I've bought "unsweetened dried cherries" before that were basically cherry-flavored shoe leather. These hold their chew. That said, the softness means they clump together in the bag — nothing a quick shake doesn't fix, but worth knowing.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. **Occasional pit fragments** show up. It's not every cherry, and it's not every bag, but it happens. If you're feeding these to kids under five, crush or chop them first. That's a real safety consideration, not a nitpick.

Second, the value math gets tricky. The smaller sizes are convenient but pricey per ounce. If you're using these for baking or daily snacking, the larger bag is the only sensible option — the per-pound cost drops significantly. Also, if you prefer a crunchy, dehydrated texture, these will disappoint. They're moist, not crisp.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these actually unsweetened?**
A: Yes. Ingredient list is just Montmorency tart cherries. No added sugar, no sweeteners, no oil. The sweetness you taste is natural fruit sugar.

**Q: How should I store them?**
A: Keep the bag sealed in a cool, dry place. They'll stay pliable for months. In summer heat, refrigerate — they can sweat and get sticky otherwise.

**Q: Can I use these in place of fresh cherries in recipes?**
A: Yes for baked goods and compotes, but rehydrate first. Soak in warm water or juice for 10-15 minutes to restore moisture before mixing into batters or sauces.
