Let me be straight with you: I've tested dozens of "healthy" snack alternatives that crumble into dust the moment they hit your mouth. The Enlightened Bada Bean Bada Boom doesn't do that. These crunchy broad beans (also called fava beans) genuinely deliver on the crunch promise — a satisfying, audible snap that makes you forget you're eating something with actual protein and fiber.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The product image shows a bright bag with the beans spilling out, and that's accurate. You're looking at roasted broad beans coated with various seasonings. The base ingredient is simple — broad beans, oil (sunflower or canola depending on batch), and seasoning blends. Nothing mysterious.

Each 1.6-ounce bag contains roughly 5-6 grams of plant protein and 5 grams of fiber. For context, that's comparable to a hard-boiled egg in protein but with triple the fiber. The calorie count sits around 130-150 per bag depending on flavor, which makes portion control straightforward since each bag is a single serving.

**Performance and Build Quality — The Crunch Test**

Here's where Bada Bean wins. The beans are roasted, not fried, which gives them a density that holds up. I've left an open bag on my desk for two days — still crunchy. That's rare in this category.

The texture is closer to a roasted chickpea than a chip. Dense, substantial, and slow to chew. This actually works in your favor: you eat slower, feel fuller, and don't blow through the bag in 90 seconds like you would with potato chips.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

Scrolling through the 204 Amazon ratings at 4.1 stars, a pattern emerges. Buyers repeatedly mention the crunch and the macros. One reviewer put it simply: "Finally a snack that doesn't taste like cardboard but still has protein." Another noted, "I eat these when I'm craving chips and they actually kill the craving."

The protein-per-calorie ratio earns consistent praise, especially from people tracking macros. At roughly 130 calories and 6 grams of protein, it outperforms most crackers and chips in the same calorie range.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The sweet flavors — particularly Honey BBQ and Cinnamon — have a visible sugar coating that creates a sticky residue on your fingers. It's not unpleasant, but it's not the clean-eating experience you might expect from a "plant-based protein" snack.

The Sour Cream & Onion flavor drew complaints about an artificial aftertaste. One verified buyer wrote, "Tastes like the powder they put in instant ramen — not great." I'd avoid that flavor if you're sensitive to processed seasonings.

The bag size is also worth flagging. At 1.6 ounces, these are snack-sized portions. If you're buying for a family or planning to pack lunches, you'll need multiple bags. This pushes the per-ounce cost higher than comparable snacks.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Macro trackers** looking for a crunchy snack that contributes protein and fiber instead of empty calories
- **Gluten-free eaters** who miss the texture of pretzels or crackers — these deliver that satisfying bite
- **Office snackers** who need something shelf-stable that won't go stale in a desk drawer
- **Keto-adjacent dieters** — low net carbs (around 3-4g after fiber) make these workable

**Skip These If**

You're expecting a chip substitute. These are denser, harder, and take more chewing. If you want something light and airy, look elsewhere. Also, if you're allergic to legumes, obviously avoid these entirely.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Are these actually plant-based?**
Yes. The ingredients list shows broad beans, oil, and seasonings — no dairy, no animal products. The Sour Cream & Onion flavor uses plant-based flavoring, confirmed by the manufacturer's labeling.

**How do these compare to roasted chickpeas?**
Bada Beans are denser and crunchier. Roasted chickpeas tend to be lighter and more powdery inside. Broad beans give a more substantial bite that lasts longer.

**Will one bag satisfy a craving?**
For most people, yes — but barely. The texture forces slower eating, which helps with satiety. If you're a volume eater, you'll want two bags.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely crunchy, protein-forward snack that doesn't taste like a health food compromise. The Sea Salt and Honey BBQ flavors are the standouts.

**Skip it if:** You're looking for a light, chip-like texture or you're sensitive to artificial flavorings in savory varieties.

These won't replace your favorite chips, but they'll earn a permanent spot in your snack rotation. At 4.2 out of 5, they're a solid choice for anyone trying to eat better without sacrificing the satisfying crunch of a real snack.
