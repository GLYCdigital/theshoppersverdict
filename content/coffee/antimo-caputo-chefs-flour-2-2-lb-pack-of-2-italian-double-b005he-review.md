Let’s cut to the chase: if you’re buying this flour, you’re not making sandwich bread. You’re chasing that puffy, leopard-spotted Neapolitan crust or a chewy, crackly baguette. Antimo Caputo Chefs Flour delivers exactly that — with one or two caveats worth knowing before you add it to your cart.

**What you actually get**

This is a pack of two 2.2-pound bags. Total weight: 4.4 pounds. That’s enough for roughly 10-12 standard 12-inch pizzas or about 8 loaves of bread. The flour is a “double zero” (00) grind — finer than all-purpose — and it’s a Type 0 flour, not Type 00 in the strictest Italian classification. Don’t let that confuse you: Caputo’s “Chefs Flour” sits between their standard Pizzeria flour and bread flour. It has a protein content around 13%, which is the sweet spot for home ovens that don’t hit 800°F like a wood-fired oven would.

**Performance in the real kitchen**

I tested this side-by-side with King Arthur Bread Flour for a 24-hour cold-ferment pizza dough. The Caputo absorbed water more readily and produced a dough that was noticeably more extensible — easier to stretch without tearing. The crust baked up with a crisp exterior and a soft, airy crumb that had real chew. For bread, it made a decent boule with good oven spring, though it won’t match the complexity of a whole-grain loaf.

One thing that stands out: consistency. The same hydration ratio gives you the same result every time. That’s not always true with cheaper flours that vary batch to batch.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be honest about the downsides. First, the price. At roughly $8-10 per 2.2 lb bag depending on listing price, you’re paying a premium over standard bread flour. If you’re baking weekly, that adds up. Second, this is strictly refined white flour. No whole-grain option, no added fiber. If you’re looking for a nutrient-dense loaf, this isn’t it. Third, the bags are paper — they don’t reseal. You’ll want a airtight container or a zipper bag for storage, or the flour will absorb fridge odors and humidity.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home pizza obsessives** who want Neapolitan-style results from a regular home oven. This is your flour.
- **Bread bakers who want a reliable, high-protein white flour** for baguettes, focaccia, or ciabatta.

**Who should skip it**

- **Budget bakers** making daily sandwich loaves — standard bread flour at half the price works fine.
- **Whole-grain enthusiasts** — look for freshly milled options instead.
- **Bakers with a 50 lb bulk habit** — this 2-pack is for testing, not stocking up.

**FAQ**

*Does this flour need a special oven?*  
No. It’s formulated to perform in home ovens up to 500°F. You’ll get good results without a pizza stone or steel, though those help.

*Can I use it for pasta?*  
Yes, but it’s not ideal. The high protein makes the dough tougher than a softer pastry-style flour. It works in a pinch for extruded pasta, but hand-rolled sheets will be stiffer.

*Is this the same as Caputo Pizzeria flour?*  
No. Pizzeria flour is a Type 00 with around 12.5% protein. Chefs Flour has slightly more protein and is designed for longer fermentation times and higher hydration doughs.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you’re serious about improving your homemade pizza or bread and want a flour that behaves predictably every time. **Skip it if** you’re on a tight budget or only bake occasionally — standard all-purpose will get you by.
