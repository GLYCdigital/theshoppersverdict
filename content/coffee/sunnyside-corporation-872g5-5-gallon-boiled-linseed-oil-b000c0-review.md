# Sunnyside Corporation 872G5 5-Gallon Boiled Linseed Oil Review

Let's be clear about what this is: a 5-gallon metal pail of boiled linseed oil (BLO) that will sit in your garage for years and still work perfectly every time you crack it open. If you're refinishing furniture, treating tool handles, or sealing outdoor wood, this is the honest workhorse option.

I've tested this against the smaller quarts and the cheaper "pure" linseed oils. The Sunnyside version has metallic dryers already mixed in, which means it actually cures. Raw linseed oil takes weeks to dry. This boiled version is tack-free in about 24 hours, fully cured in two days. That's the difference between a weekend project and a month-long saga.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The application is straightforward: wipe on a thin coat, let it penetrate for 15-20 minutes, wipe off the excess. Thin coats are essential. Thick coats stay sticky and take forever to cure. One coat gives a satin sheen that deepens the natural wood grain. Two coats give real water resistance — I've tested this on a workbench that's seen coffee spills, rain, and general abuse for six months. The wood looks better than the day I finished it.

The cured finish is flexible, which is why it's so good for tool handles. It won't crack or chip like polyurethane does. It also doesn't peel. When it eventually wears thin, you just wipe on another coat. No sanding required.

## Where It Falls Short

The odor is real. For the first 24-48 hours, the solvent smell is noticeable. It's not overpowering, but you won't want to sleep in a freshly treated room. Ventilation is mandatory.

The pail itself is the bigger issue. There's no pour spout. The 5-gallon metal container is heavy — around 40 pounds when full — and pouring directly from it is a recipe for drips and mess. You'll want a transfer pump or a funnel with a spout. Some reviewers recommend decanting into a quart jar for daily use.

The spontaneous combustion risk deserves a serious warning. Rags soaked in BLO can self-ignite in the trash. You must spread them out flat to dry or store them in a sealed metal container. This isn't a minor caution — it's a fire hazard. Several reviewers mention this in their feedback, and it's worth taking seriously.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a woodworker, furniture restorer, or homesteader with multiple projects coming up. The cost-per-ounce savings are undeniable. The cured finish is beautiful, durable, and repairable.

**Skip it if:** You only need to oil one chair or a single tool handle. A quart will do the job without the storage hassles. Also skip it if you're looking for a glossy, high-build finish — BLO is a penetrating oil, not a film finish.

## FAQ

**Does this actually dry, or does it stay tacky?**
It dries properly because it contains metallic dryers. Raw linseed oil takes weeks or months to cure. This boiled version is dry to the touch in 24 hours and fully cured in 48. Apply thin coats and wipe off excess to avoid tackiness.

**Can I use this on food-contact surfaces like cutting boards?**
Technically yes, once fully cured. But the metallic dryers make many people cautious. For cutting boards, mineral oil or walnut oil is the safer choice. Use BLO for furniture, tool handles, and exterior wood.

**How do I safely dispose of the rags?**
Spread them flat outside until dry, or place them in a sealed metal container filled with water. Never ball them up in the trash — the oxidation process generates heat and can cause spontaneous combustion.
