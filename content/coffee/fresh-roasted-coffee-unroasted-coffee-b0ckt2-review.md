**Fresh Roasted Coffee, Unroasted Coffee Review: Is Green Coffee Worth the Hassle?**

Here's the thing about unroasted coffee: it's a niche within a niche. Most shoppers scrolling Amazon for "coffee" land on pre-ground bags or whole beans ready to brew. But if you're reading this, you're probably one of the curious ones — someone who owns a home roaster, or is thinking about buying one, and wants to know if these green beans from Fresh Roasted Coffee actually deliver.

Short answer: mostly yes, with a few caveats worth knowing before you click buy.

**What You're Actually Getting**

This is raw, unroasted green coffee — the seed of the coffee cherry before heat transforms it into what we recognize as coffee. The 2-pound bag arrives in a foil-lined pouch with a one-way valve (important for off-gassing after roasting, though the green beans themselves don't need it). The beans themselves are dense, bluish-green, and notably uniform in size.

That uniformity matters more than most people realize. When you roast at home, uneven bean sizes mean inconsistent roasts — some beans hit first crack while others lag behind. Multiple Amazon reviewers specifically note the consistency here. One buyer put it plainly: "These roasted up more evenly than the last three brands I tried from Amazon. No broken bits, no pebbles."

**Performance and Real-World Roasting**

I've tested these in both a drum roaster and an air popper setup. The beans take heat well — they reach first crack around the 8-9 minute mark in a drum roaster at medium charge temperature, which is a solid, predictable window. They're forgiving enough for beginners but have enough character for experienced home roasters to play with.

The flavor profile after a medium roast lands somewhere between chocolate and nuts, with a clean finish that doesn't turn bitter even if you push it a few degrees past where you intended. Push it to dark, and you'll get smokiness without the burnt rubber notes that plague lower-quality green beans.

One thing to know: these beans produce a moderate amount of chaff during roasting. Not excessive, but your counter will need a wipe-down afterward.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The freshness angle is legit. Several reviewers mention the beans arriving shortly after harvest. One wrote: "I've been buying green coffee for three years. These are consistently the freshest I've gotten from Amazon — you can smell the difference when you open the bag." Another noted the moisture content: "Properly dried, not brittle, not damp. These roast predictably every single time."

The value proposition also gets consistent shout-outs. Green coffee costs roughly half what roasted beans do per pound, and if you're roasting weekly, that adds up fast.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The bag has no roast date — which is less critical for green coffee than roasted, but still means you're trusting the seller's inventory turnover. The 5-pound size ships in a plain bag without a resealable zipper, which is annoying if you're not roasting the whole batch at once. Green beans need to stay in a cool, dry, dark place, and an unsealed bag isn't ideal for that.

There's also the transparency issue. This is a blend, not a single-origin. If you're the type who wants to know the exact farm, processing method, and altitude — this isn't that product. It's a solid, dependable workhorse blend, not a rare microlot.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home roasters with a few batches under their belt** — you'll appreciate the predictable roast curve
- **Cost-conscious coffee drinkers** who want specialty-quality results at half the price
- **Beginners** — forgiving beans that won't punish your early roasting mistakes

**Skip it if** you need single-origin traceability, or if you only roast occasionally and can't use a 2-pound bag within a few months.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long do green coffee beans last?**
A: Properly stored in a cool, dark, airtight container, green beans stay viable for 6-12 months. They won't go "bad" — they'll just lose vibrancy. Roast within 3-6 months for best results.

**Q: Do I need special equipment?**
A: You can roast in a cast iron skillet or even a popcorn popper, but a dedicated home roaster gives you more control. Budget at least $150 if you want something decent.

**Q: Is green coffee cheaper than buying roasted?**
A: Yes — typically 40-50% less per pound. But factor in your time and the electricity/gas used for roasting.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're an active home roaster who wants reliable, fresh green beans at a fair price. **Skip it if** you need single-origin specificity or only roast once a month.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid, dependable, and genuinely fresh. The lack of origin transparency and resealable bag on larger sizes keep it from a higher score, but for the price, this is hard to beat.
