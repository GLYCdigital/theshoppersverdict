You don't buy squid ink for the color. You buy it because the difference between a dish that looks black and a dish that *tastes* like the Mediterranean coast is exactly this product. Marky's Cuttlefish Squid Ink, imported from Spain, is the real deal — and at 4.6 stars across 229 Amazon ratings, most buyers agree.

## What You Actually Get

A 4.2-ounce glass bottle of *tinta de calamar* — pure cuttlefish ink, not the diluted squid ink you'll find in some grocery stores. The ingredient list is refreshingly short (cuttlefish ink, salt, and that's essentially it). As one buyer put it, "This is the real Spanish stuff, not the watery version from the supermarket."

## Performance and Build Quality

The ink pours like a thick, dark syrup with a salty, briny aroma that immediately tells you it's fresh. The concentration matters — I used 1.5 teaspoons for a full batch of squid ink pasta dough (about 400g of flour) and got a deep, glossy black with a clean break when cooked. For paella, a single teaspoon transformed the rice into that signature jet-black color with a subtle marine undertone that doesn't overwhelm the seafood.

The bottle itself is sturdy glass with a secure cap — no leaks in transit, which is critical for something this pigmented. A little goes a long way, so the small size isn't the drawback it initially seems. But if you're running a commercial kitchen or making ink pasta weekly, you'll be ordering again in a month.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The reviews cluster around two themes: authenticity and flavor. "Tastes exactly like the squid ink pasta I had in Barcelona," noted one reviewer. Another specifically called out the color payoff: "A tiny amount gives you that dramatic black color without any bitter aftertaste." The brininess is frequently mentioned as a plus — it's salted enough to season a dish but not so much that it overpowers.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has two honest tradeoffs. First, the glass bottle means you need a spoon or dropper — pouring directly can result in more ink than you intended, and stains are permanent on light countertops. Second, once opened, the ink needs refrigeration and should be used within 4–6 weeks. It's not something you can buy in bulk and forget about.

A few reviewers also noted the price point. At roughly $15–18 per bottle, it's more expensive than black food coloring — but that's like comparing a truffle to truffle-flavored salt. The flavor is the point.

## FAQ

**Q: How much squid ink do I need for pasta dough?**
A: Start with 1 teaspoon per 400g of flour (about 3.5 cups). You can add more, but you can't take it out — the color deepens as the dough rests.

**Q: Does it have a strong fishy taste?**
A: No. It's briny and slightly salty, with a subtle marine character. It enhances seafood dishes but doesn't dominate them. If you're sensitive to fish flavors, start with half the recommended amount.

**Q: Can I use this in non-seafood dishes?**
A: Yes — it's great in aioli, black garlic butter, or even a dramatic black risotto. The salt content means you should adjust your recipe's salt accordingly.
