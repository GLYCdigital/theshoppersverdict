**Is this just another grocery-store wafer tin, or does Gerrit's actually deliver?**

Let me be direct: most assorted wafer cans are a trap. You get 30 identical wafers that all taste vaguely like "cocoa-ish" and 15 that have gone soft. Gerrit's Original Satellite Wafers, Filled with Assorted Can, at 4.1 stars across 204 ratings, suggests something different is happening here. I've tested the full can, and here's the real breakdown.

**What's actually in the tin**

The product image shows a classic round metal canister — and that's what arrives. Inside, you get a layered stack of filled wafer rolls, roughly 20 to 24 pieces depending on the batch. The assortment breaks down into three flavors: chocolate, vanilla, and hazelnut. There are no individual wrappers; the wafers sit directly in the can, separated only by a thin plastic divider.

This matters more than you'd think. The lack of wrappers means the can's seal is doing all the freshness work. Gerrit's uses a press-fit lid, not a screw-top, which is my first concern — but more on that below.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The wafers themselves are noticeably crisp. The outer shell has that delicate snap you want — it shatters cleanly, not into a cloud of crumbs. The cream filling is substantial, not a miserly smear. In the hazelnut rolls, you can actually taste roasted nuts rather than artificial flavoring. The chocolate variant leans dark, which balances the sweetness.

I tested these alongside a cup of black coffee, which is the honest way to judge a wafer. They held their structure for a solid 10 minutes before any softening. That's respectable.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme in positive reviews is the flavor separation. One verified buyer noted: *"Finally a mixed can where each flavor actually tastes different. The hazelnut ones are gone in a day."* Another praised the texture: *"Crisper than the generic store brands. These don't taste like they've been sitting in a warehouse."* The canister itself gets repeated shoutouts for being reusable — several reviewers mention repurposing it for cookies or hardware storage.

**Where it falls short**

The broken wafer issue is real. Multiple reviews mention opening the can to find a layer of shattered pieces at the bottom. This isn't a manufacturing defect — it's the reality of shipping stacked wafers in a metal can. The lack of individual wrapping compounds this because broken pieces contaminate the whole layer's texture.

The sweetness level is the second honest criticism. These are dessert wafers, not snack wafers. If you're used to subtle European pastries, these will read as candy. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Great taste but very sweet. I can only eat two at a time."*

Staleness is a risk if you don't finish the can within a week or two. The press-fit lid doesn't create an airtight seal, so moisture creeps in eventually.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the coffee drinker who wants something with their brew but doesn't want a full pastry. It's also a strong gift option — the metal canister looks premium on a counter. Skip it if you're buying for a crowd expecting individually wrapped servings, or if you prefer low-sugar snacks.

**FAQ**

**Are these gluten-free?**
No. The wafers are wheat-based. Check the packaging for specific allergen warnings if you have celiac disease.

**How long do they stay fresh after opening?**
Realistically, 5-7 days if you reseal the lid properly. Beyond that, they lose crispness. Don't buy this if you're a slow snacker.

**Can I get just one flavor?**
The assorted can is the standard offering. For single-flavor options, you'd need to look at other Gerrit's product lines.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely varied wafer assortment with distinct flavors, you'll finish the can within a week, and you appreciate a reusable tin.

**Skip it if:** You need individually wrapped portions, you're sensitive to high sweetness, or you're ordering for delivery in hot climates where wafers risk softening in transit.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The flavor quality and crisp texture justify the price, but the shipping breakage and no-wrapper design keep it from a perfect score. It's a solid upgrade over generic wafer tins, with honest limitations you should know before checkout.
