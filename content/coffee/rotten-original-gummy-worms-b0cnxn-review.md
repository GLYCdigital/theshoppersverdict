## The Sour Candy That Respects the Name

Most gummy worms labeled "sour" are a lie. You get a light dusting of citric acid that fades before you finish chewing. Rotten Original Gummy Worms aren't playing that game. These deliver a face-puckering hit that builds as you chew, then settles into a sweet-tart finish that actually justifies the "rotten" branding. As shown in the product image, the worms come coated in a visible layer of sour crystals — and that's not marketing. The coating is aggressive, and it's the reason this bag earned a 4.0 from 200+ Amazon shoppers.

## What's Actually in the Bag

You're getting a standard 5-ounce bag of mixed-color worms — red, orange, yellow, green, and purple. There's no resealable zipper, which is a real oversight for a product this sticky. The worms themselves are thicker than standard supermarket gummy worms, closer to a sour patch kid texture than a Trolli worm. Each piece has decent heft and a dense chew that doesn't disintegrate into mush. The sour coating is applied unevenly in the best way — some pieces hit harder than others, which keeps the bag interesting.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these over three sittings — fresh from the bag, after 24 hours, and after a week in a partially-open bag. Fresh is the sweet spot. The sour bite is immediate and sharp, followed by a mellow fruity sweetness that's more generic than distinct. Don't expect nuanced flavor profiles here; think "red tastes like cherry-ish" rather than "this is a handcrafted raspberry extract." That's not a knock — this is a candy bag, not a tasting menu.

The 24-hour mark is where these shine. The coating melds slightly into the gummy, creating a more balanced sour-sweet ratio. After a week open, the worms lose their snap and turn softer, almost stale. Seal the bag properly or finish it quickly. Multiple reviewers noted this, with one saying, "Great flavor for the first two days, then the texture drops off fast."

## Where It Falls Short

The sour crystals are the star, but they're also the problem. For anyone with mouth sensitivity, this is genuinely uncomfortable. The coating also leaves a chalky residue on your fingers that transfers to whatever you touch — your keyboard, your phone, your couch. Plan for napkins. The flavor underneath the sour is decent but not distinctive; if you're looking for complex fruit notes, this isn't your bag. And the packaging oversight is frustrating — a simple zipper would've kept these fresh longer and addressed the most common complaint.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a sour candy purist who's been burned by weak "sour" claims. You want a candy that commits to the bit. You're stocking a party bowl or movie night and want something that stands out from standard gummy bears.

**Skip it if:** You prefer mildly tart candy or have a sensitive mouth. You're buying for young kids — the sour level genuinely surprises adults. You want a resealable bag or plan to store these long-term.

## FAQ

**Are these actually sour?**
Yes, aggressively so. The citric acid coating is heavy and builds as you chew. This isn't a sugar-coated gummy with a hint of tartness.

**How do they compare to other sour gummy worms?**
They're firmer and denser than Trolli worms, with a more intense sour coating. The flavor underneath is less fruity and more generic, but the texture and sour kick are superior.

**Do they contain common allergens?**
The ingredients list includes corn syrup, sugar, gelatin, and citric acid. No peanuts or tree nuts in the standard formulation, but always check the label for your specific needs.
