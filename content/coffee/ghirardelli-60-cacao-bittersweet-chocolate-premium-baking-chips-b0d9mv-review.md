If you've ever bitten into a "chocolate chip" cookie and gotten a waxy, overly sweet disc that tastes more like sugar than cocoa, you know the frustration. Ghirardelli's 60% Cacao Bittersweet Chips are the corrective — but they're not for everyone. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the bag**

You're getting a standard 12-ounce bag of chips (check the current listing for the exact size, as it varies by seller). The chips themselves are uniform, roughly the size of a small pea, with a deep mahogany color. No waxy sheen, no chalky residue. As shown in the product image, the bag is straightforward — no fancy packaging, just the recognizable Ghirardelli gold and blue. One note: there's no zipper seal, so plan to transfer them to an airtight container after opening.

**Performance and flavor**

These chips are built for bakers who want the chocolate to be the star. At 60% cacao, they sit firmly in bittersweet territory — noticeably darker and less sweet than standard semi-sweet chips (which typically run 40-50% cacao). In a blind taste test, you'd pick these out immediately: there's a fruity, almost wine-like undertone, with a clean finish that doesn't coat your mouth with sugar.

In baking, they hold their shape admirably. Unlike cheaper chips that flatten into puddles, these maintain distinct pockets of chocolate — ideal if you like visible chips in your cookies. They melt smoothly when you need them to, making them equally solid for ganache or melted-drizzled applications. The chips are slightly larger than Nestlé Toll House, so expect a more pronounced chocolate bite.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across early reviews, the recurring theme is the flavor depth. One shopper noted, "These taste like actual chocolate, not sugar with cocoa powder." Another mentioned they "made the best brownies I've ever baked — the bitterness balances the sweetness perfectly." Bakers specifically called out the melt quality: "Smooth, no grainy texture, even when I microwaved them in 20-second bursts."

**Where it falls short**

The price is the biggest hurdle. At roughly 30-40% more per ounce than standard semi-sweet chips, you're paying a premium for the higher cacao content. If you're baking for a crowd or making a double batch of cookies, the cost adds up fast.

The flavor profile is also a genuine limitation. If your family prefers milk chocolate or even standard semi-sweet, these will read as bitter. I'd recommend testing a single batch before committing to a bulk purchase. And again, the lack of a resealable bag is a minor annoyance — chocolate absorbs odors easily, so keep them sealed tight.

**Who should buy this**

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I substitute these 1:1 in a recipe calling for semi-sweet chips?**
A: Yes, but expect a less sweet result. You may want to add 1-2 tablespoons of extra sugar to the dough if you're not used to bittersweet chocolate.

**Q: Do these chips melt well for dipping or drizzling?**
A: Yes. Melt in 20-30 second bursts in the microwave, stirring between each, or use a double boiler. They melt smoothly without seizing if you avoid water contact.

**Q: How should I store them after opening?**
A: Airtight container in a cool, dark place (not the fridge — they'll absorb odors). They'll keep for 6-12 months this way.
