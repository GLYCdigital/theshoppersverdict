# Brisk Lemon Iced Tea, 12 Fl Oz Cans (Pack of 12)

## The TL;DR

Brisk Lemon Iced Tea is a shelf-stable, canned sweet tea that delivers exactly what it promises: a consistent, thirst-quenching lemon tea with a crisp bite. It’s not fancy, it’s not artisanal, and it’s not trying to be — but for a grab-and-go cold drink that tastes like the fountain version you remember, it’s hard to beat for the price and convenience.

## What You Actually Get

A 12-pack of 12-fluid-ounce aluminum cans. Total volume: 144 ounces (1.125 gallons) of ready-to-drink iced tea. The cans are recyclable, stackable, and — critically — **no refrigeration needed until opened**. This matters if you’re stocking a cooler, a dorm fridge, or an office mini-fridge that’s already stuffed.

Each can is the standard soda-can size, so it fits in any car cup holder and most backpack side pockets. The packaging is consistent with what you’d see on a grocery shelf: "bright green label, bold Brisk branding, and a clear Lemon designation. Nothing premium, but nothing cheap either."

## Performance and Real-World Use

The first sip hits with a **sweetness-forward profile** — this is not a dry, unsweetened tea. The lemon flavor is present but not overpowering; it reads more like a lemonade-tea hybrid than a straight tea with a lemon squeeze. There’s a slight carbonation-free fizz at the top of the can that fades quickly, which is typical for canned teas.

The mouthfeel is **smooth and slightly syrupy** — expected given the high-fructose corn syrup base. It’s not watery, but it’s also not thick like a nectar. The aftertaste is clean, with a mild tannin bite that reminds you it’s actually tea.

For real-world use: "it’s **best served ice-cold**. At room temperature, the sweetness becomes cloying and the lemon flavor flattens. If you’re planning to drink these away from a fridge, bring a cooler. Also worth noting: the cans are **not resealable**, so once you crack one, you’re finishing it. At 12 ounces, that’s rarely a problem, but if you’re a slow sipper, factor that in."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.7-star rating with 235 reviews isn’t an accident. Recurring themes in customer feedback:

- "**Taste consistency** — multiple reviewers note it tastes just like the fountain version at convenience stores. One 5-star review says: *Tastes exactly like what you get at the gas station, but without the watered-down ice.*"
- "**Value for a 12-pack** — buyers repeatedly mention the per-can cost beats single-serve bottles at convenience stores."
- "**Convenience for on-the-go** — parents, commuters, and office workers cite the portability and no-prep nature as the main reason for repeat purchases."

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs:

- "**Sugar content** — each can packs 34 grams of sugar. That’s not a diet drink. If you’re watching sugar intake, this is a once-in-a-while treat, not a daily staple."
- "**Artificial flavor notes** — the lemon is clearly a flavoring, not real lemon juice. It’s pleasant, but you can tell. If you’re expecting fresh-squeezed lemon, you’ll be disappointed."
- "**No resealable lid** — minor, but worth mentioning."
- "**Sweetness level is fixed** — if you like your tea less sweet, there’s no dialing it down. You’re getting the standard Brisk formula."

## Who Should Buy This

- "**Cold-drink loyalists** who want a consistent, sweet tea fix without brewing or steeping."
- "**Busy parents** packing school lunches or sports coolers — the cans are durable and survive being jostled."
- "**Office workers** who want a break from soda and coffee but don’t want to fuss with tea bags and hot water."
- "**Campers and road-trippers** — shelf-stable means you can stock up without refrigeration."

**Skip it if** you’re looking for a low-sugar option, a premium tea experience, or a resealable format. Also skip if you prefer lightly sweetened or unsweetened tea — this won’t scratch that itch.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this carbonated?**
A: No. It’s flat like brewed iced tea. There may be a faint initial fizz from the canning process, but it dissipates quickly.

**Q: Does it need to stay refrigerated?**
A: Before opening, no. It’s shelf-stable. After opening, you must refrigerate — but realistically you’ll finish the can in one sitting.

**Q: How does it compare to bottled Brisk?**
A: It’s essentially the same formula, but the can version often tastes slightly fresher (no plastic aftertaste) and stays colder longer when pre-chilled.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, sweet, lemon-forward iced tea in a portable, no-fuss format. It’s a solid value for the convenience and taste consistency.

**Skip it if** sugar content is a dealbreaker, or if you prefer a more authentic, less sweet tea profile.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the sugar load and the non-resealable cans, but for what it is, it does the job exceptionally well.
