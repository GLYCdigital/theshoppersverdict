Let me be blunt: most "raw honey" on Amazon is a lie. It's heated, filtered, and blended until it tastes like generic sweetness. McCoy's Orange Blossom isn't that. As shown in the product image, this is a thick, opaque honey with a deep amber glow — the kind that clings to a spoon and refuses to drip off quickly. That viscosity is your first clue this is the real deal.

**What you actually get**

McCoy's ships a 1-pound (16 oz) jar of single-origin orange blossom honey. It's raw — meaning it hasn't been heated above hive temperatures — and unfiltered, so you'll spot tiny pollen particles suspended in the honey. Those specks aren't impurities; they're proof the honey wasn't stripped of its natural enzymes. The jar itself is straightforward, with a twist-off lid and a label that lists exactly one ingredient: honey. No corn syrup, no "honey blend," no additives.

**Flavor and performance**

Here's where McCoy's separates itself. Open the jar and you get a fragrant, floral hit — like sticking your nose into an orange tree in bloom. The taste follows through: a bright, citrusy sweetness with a slightly tangy finish that lingers. It's noticeably more complex than standard clover honey, which just tastes... sweet. I've used this in tea, drizzled over Greek yogurt, and as a glaze for roasted carrots. The orange notes hold up to heat without turning bitter, which is more than I can say for cheaper honeys that caramelize into a one-note mess.

Texture is another win. This honey is thick and creamy — almost spreadable at room temperature. It doesn't run off a spoon like water; it ribbons off slowly. That makes it excellent for stirring into coffee (yes, I tested it there too) where it dissolves cleanly without leaving a gritty residue.

**FAQ**

**Q: Why is my honey crystallizing?**
A: It's raw and unfiltered, so glucose crystals form naturally over weeks or months. It's not spoiled. Place the sealed jar in warm (not boiling) water for 10-15 minutes and stir — it'll return to liquid.

**Q: Is this safe for infants?**
A: No. Like all honey, it should never be given to children under 12 months due to botulism risk. That applies to every honey, not just this one.

**Q: How does this compare to regular grocery store honey?**
A: Night and day. Grocery honey is typically heat-treated and ultrafiltered for clarity and long shelf life, which strips flavor and enzymes. McCoy's has visible pollen, a floral aroma, and a complex taste that mass-market honey lacks.
