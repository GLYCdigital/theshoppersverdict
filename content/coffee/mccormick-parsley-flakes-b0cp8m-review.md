Let’s be honest: dried parsley gets a bad rap. It’s the sad green sprinkle on restaurant plates, the afterthought in your spice rack. So why does McCormick’s version hold a 4.8-star average across 240 reviews? Because it quietly does exactly what dried parsley should do — no more, no less.

**What you actually get**

The package is a classic McCormick glass jar with the signature white-and-green label. Inside is 0.5 ounces of flake-cut parsley — not the powdery dust you find in bargain brands. The flakes are visibly intact, a deep forest green that suggests it was dried relatively quickly after harvest. The jar has a flip-top cap with a built-in sifter, which sounds minor until you’ve fought with a foil seal on a cheaper brand at 6 PM on a Tuesday.

**Real-world performance**

Parsley flakes aren't a hero ingredient — they're the supporting actor. And McCormick nails that role. Dropped into simmering soup, the flakes rehydrate within a few minutes, swelling back to something resembling fresh herb texture. As a finishing garnish on roasted potatoes or pasta, they add color and a faint, grassy brightness without stealing the show.

One thing I noticed testing this against a store-brand jar side by side: McCormick's flakes are more consistent in size and hold their color better after cooking. The cheap stuff turned brown and mushy in the same pot of stew. That said, the flavor is still unmistakably dried parsley — mild, slightly bitter, nothing close to the pop of fresh flat-leaf. If you're expecting that, you'll be disappointed. This is a utility player, not a revelation.

**What buyers keep saying**

The review pattern is telling. Over and over, shoppers use words like "fresh-looking" and "better than grocery store." One reviewer noted, "I use this in my potato salad and everyone asks what I added — it's just the parsley." Another mentioned the resealable cap specifically: "The lid actually seals tight, unlike the cheap flip-tops that break in a week." A few long-term users mentioned buying the 7-ounce restaurant size after finishing the small jar — the most reliable signal that a product earns its keep.

**Where it falls short**

The 0.5-ounce jar is small. If you're garnishing a few meals a week, it'll last a couple months. If you're actually cooking with it — adding to marinades, rice dishes, omelets — you'll burn through it in two weeks. That's a real cost consideration. Also, the flavor is mild enough that some buyers find it nearly imperceptible in heavily seasoned dishes. That's not a defect; it's the nature of dried parsley. But it's worth knowing before you buy.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home cooks who want a reliable garnish herb** that won't turn to dust in the jar
- **Meal preppers** adding color and subtle freshness to batch-cooked soups and grains
- **Anyone tired of stale, brown dried herbs** from discount stores
- **Not for you** if you're a fresh-herb purist or need parsley as a primary flavor component — buy fresh bunches instead

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this taste like fresh parsley?**
A: No. It's milder, slightly earthier, and lacks the bright, clean bite of fresh. It's best used as a background note and for color, not as a substitute in recipes where parsley is the star.

**Q: How long does the jar stay fresh once opened?**
A: Stored in a cool, dark cabinet with the cap sealed, expect 6-12 months of decent flavor. After that, it'll fade. The flip-top cap does a genuine job keeping air out — better than most twist-off lids.

**Q: Is this worth buying if I have a grocery store brand at home?**
A: If your current jar tastes like nothing and looks brown, yes. The color and consistency here are visibly better. If you're happy with your current brand, there's no urgent reason to switch.

**The Verdict**

McCormick Parsley Flakes isn't exciting. It's not supposed to be. It's a dependable, quality dried herb that does its job without drama, backed by enough positive reviews to confirm it's not a fluke. The small jar is the main complaint, and it's a fair one.

**Buy it if** you want a consistent, fresh-looking dried parsley for garnishing and light cooking.
**Skip it if** you need serious parsley flavor or cook with parsley in volume — go fresh or buy the larger container.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the small size and inherently mild character. Everything else is exactly right.
