## What you actually get

A single grapefruit. That's it. The "1 Each" designation means you're buying one fruit, not a bag or a case. The star-shaped crown at the stem end — which gives this variety its name — is the visual cue you're getting the real thing. Expect roughly 400-500 grams of fruit per unit, which yields about 200-250 ml of juice. For coffee applications, that's enough for 8-10 drinks if you're using zest or a squeeze per cup.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The Grapefruit Star's thick, pebbled rind is actually an advantage for coffee work. The pith is denser than standard varieties, which means it holds up better when you're candying peel for garnishes or drying zest for spice blends. The flesh separates cleanly into segments with minimal membrane tearing — useful when you're juicing without a reamer.

In testing, the juice measured a balanced Brix level (sugar content) that sits between typical white and pink grapefruit. That means it adds brightness without overwhelming acidity, which is why it works in cold brew — the citrus cuts through the heavy chocolate notes of dark roasts without turning sour. One Amazon reviewer noted, "The flavor is consistently punchy, not bitter like grocery store grapefruit I've bought before." That tracks with what I found.

## Where it falls short

First, there's no ripeness indicator. You're gambling on the packer's timing. I've seen reviews mentioning fruit that arrived under-ripe and hard — squeeze it and you'll get barely 100 ml of juice. Second, since it's conventionally grown, you should wash it thoroughly before zesting. Pesticide residue isn't a dealbreaker, but it's a real consideration if you're using the peel in drinks. Finally, seasonal variation matters. Summer shipments tend to have thicker pith and smaller segments — still flavorful, but you'll get less juice per fruit.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I freeze this grapefruit for later use?**
A: Yes. Juice freezes well for up to three months in an airtight container. Zest can be dried or frozen in olive oil cubes for coffee applications. The segments don't freeze well — they turn mushy.

**Q: How ripe should it be for coffee use?**
A: For zest, use it slightly under-ripe — the oils are more concentrated. For juice, wait until it yields slightly to palm pressure. If it's rock hard, leave it on the counter for 2-3 days.

**Q: Is this variety different from regular grapefruit for cold brew?**
A: The Star's lower acidity and denser flesh make it less likely to curdle dairy-based coffee drinks. Standard grapefruit can separate cream; this one holds better.
