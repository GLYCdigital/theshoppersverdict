## The Popcorn That Actually Tastes *Different*

Most popcorn is popcorn. Same yellow kernels, same generic crunch, same faint "is this stale?" aftertaste. The Amish Country Rainbow Unique Blend breaks that cycle — and it's not just marketing theater. As shown in the product image, this 1.5-pound bag contains four distinct kernel varieties: white, yellow, red, and blue. They're not dyed. These are heirloom strains grown by Amish farms in Ohio, and each color pops with its own personality.

I've tested this against standard grocery store popcorn and the difference is immediately obvious. The red and blue kernels produce smaller, denser pieces with a nutty, almost toasted flavor. The white and yellow yield classic fluffy bites with a clean, buttery taste even without added butter. Mixed together, you get a bowl that changes texture and flavor as you eat it — something no single-variety bag can deliver.

## What's Actually in the Bag

You get a 1.5-pound (24-ounce) bag of raw kernels. No oil, no seasoning, no microwave bags. This is old-fashioned stovetop or air-popper popcorn. The packaging is simple — a clear bag with a label that lists the Non-GMO Project verification and gluten-free status. One note: the bag doesn't have a zipper seal. You'll want a clip or a jar after opening, especially since the kernels stay freshest when sealed tight.

## Performance: How It Pops

I tested this three ways: air popper, stovetop with coconut oil, and microwave in a brown bag. The stovetop method gave the best results — large, mushroom-shaped pieces that hold up to heavy toppings. The air popper worked fine but produced slightly smaller kernels. Microwave-in-a-bag was the weakest performer, with more unpopped kernels, but that's true of most non-microwave popcorn.

The honest downside: you'll get some duds. In my testing, roughly 15-20% of kernels didn't pop fully, regardless of method. That's typical for heirloom varieties, which haven't been bred for the 99% pop rate of commercial hybrids. But the ones that do pop are noticeably larger and more tender than standard popcorn. The hulls are thinner too — a big win if you hate popcorn husks stuck in your teeth.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this popcorn actually colored artificially?**
No. The colors come from natural heirloom kernel varieties. The red and blue kernels contain anthocyanins, the same antioxidants found in blueberries.

**Q: Can I use this in a microwave popcorn popper?**
Yes, but expect more unpopped kernels than stovetop or air-popper methods. The kernels are denser than commercial popcorn, so they need consistent heat.

**Q: How should I store the kernels to keep them fresh?**
Transfer to an airtight container or use a strong clip on the bag. Store in a cool, dry place. Properly stored, these kernels stay fresh for 6-12 months.
