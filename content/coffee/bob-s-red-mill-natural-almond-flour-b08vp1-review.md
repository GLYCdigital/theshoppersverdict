## What You Actually Get

The 16-ounce (one pound) bag arrives as a finely milled powder made from blanched almonds — meaning the brown skins are removed before grinding. That matters more than you'd think. Almond flour with skins left on produces dense, speckled baked goods that look more like whole-wheat bread. Bob's is consistently pale, fine, and uniform, which is exactly what you want for macarons, pie crusts, or keto muffins that don't scream "health food."

As shown in the product image, the bag features a resealable zipper top. That's not just convenience — it's functional. Almond flour goes rancid faster than wheat flour due to its natural oils, and that seal genuinely extends freshness.

## Performance in Real Baking

I've tested this in three applications: fathead pizza dough, almond flour pancakes, and a simple shortbread cookie. The texture is the headline. At roughly the consistency of fine cornmeal, it incorporates into batters without grittiness. Pancakes came out with crispy edges and tender centers — no sandy mouthfeel, which is the most common failure with cheaper almond meals.

One thing worth noting: this is *not* a 1:1 substitute for wheat flour. It has no gluten, so structure comes from eggs and binders like xanthan gum. If you're new to gluten-free baking, follow recipes written specifically for almond flour rather than improvising. Bob's itself publishes free recipes on their site, which is a nice bonus.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the tradeoffs. First, price. At roughly $8–10 per pound depending on your seller, this costs 3–4x what all-purpose flour runs. If you're baking almond flour goods weekly, that adds up fast.

Second, storage is non-negotiable. Leave this bag in your pantry for a few months and you'll notice a slightly bitter, stale taste. Refrigerate or freeze after opening — it's a minor habit that preserves the quality you paid for. Some reviewers complain about receiving bags that were already past their prime, which points to Amazon's warehousing conditions rather than Bob's manufacturing. Buy from a seller with good turnover.

Third, the bag is only 16 ounces. For serious bakers, that's maybe two recipes. You'll be reordering frequently.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Is this the same as almond meal?**
No. Almond meal includes the skins and is coarser. This is blanched and finely ground — use it where texture matters.

**Can I freeze this to extend shelf life?**
Yes. Transfer to an airtight container and freeze for up to six months. Thaw at room temperature before baking.

**Does it taste strongly of almonds?**
Mildly. The flavor is subtle and nutty, which complements most baked goods without overwhelming them.
