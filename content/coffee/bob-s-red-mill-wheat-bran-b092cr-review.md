## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**Price:** You're paying a premium. Generic wheat bran runs about 30-40% cheaper per ounce. If you're baking daily or making large batches of bread, the cost difference adds up.

**Clumping:** The fine grind that makes it great for baking is a liability with liquids. Dump it straight into hot oatmeal and you'll get lumps. Plan for a whisk or a blender.

**Not a meal replacement:** Some shoppers misunderstand what bran is. This isn't a protein powder or a meal shake base. It's a fiber supplement and baking ingredient. If you're expecting to blend it with water and call it lunch, you'll be disappointed.

## FAQ

**Can I use this as a flour substitute?**
No. Wheat bran is the outer layer of the wheat kernel — it has no gluten and won't bind like flour. Substitute up to ¼ of the flour in a recipe, but expect denser results. Don't go 1:1.

**How should I store it?**
The resealable bag works, but I transfer mine to an airtight glass jar. Stored in a cool, dark pantry, it stays fresh for 3-4 months. Refrigeration extends that to 6 months.

**Is this actually different from store-brand bran?**
Yes, in grind and freshness. Store brands are often milled in larger batches and sit on shelves longer. Bob's finer grind and fresher milling make a noticeable difference in baking texture and flavor.
