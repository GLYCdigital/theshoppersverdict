**The Short Version:** If you're looking for a dried apricot that tastes like an actual apricot — not a sugar-coated fruit leather imposter — NUTS U.S. delivers. These are whole, pitted California Blenheim apricots, sun-dried the old-fashioned way. They're soft, chewy, and intensely fruity. But at 4.2 stars from 210 ratings, there are a few consistency issues worth knowing before you click buy.

---

**What You Actually Get**

A 2.5-pound bag of whole dried apricots. That's roughly 80-100 pieces depending on size. As shown in the product image, these are full fruits — not halves, not diced pieces. Each apricot is pitted, which is a genuine convenience if you've ever bitten into a "pitless" dried apricot and found a crunchy surprise.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: apricots. That's it. No sulfur dioxide, no added sugar, no preservatives. The color is a deep amber-brown rather than the bright orange you see with sulfured apricots — that's normal and actually a good sign.

---

**Real-World Performance**

The texture is where these shine. Properly dried Blenheim apricots should be soft and yielding, almost jammy inside. When NUTS U.S. gets it right, these are exactly that — tender, chewy, with a honey-like sweetness that doesn't need any help from added sugar.

One buyer put it well: *"Finally, apricots that taste like apricots, not candy. Soft, not rock hard like some brands."* Another noted: *"The flavor is incredible — you can tell they're fresh California fruit, not imported and shipped halfway around the world."*

The 2.5-pound size is practical. It lasts a while for snacking, and if you're into baking — these chop beautifully into scones, granola, or tagines — you won't run out mid-recipe.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

Here's the honest part. Multiple reviewers mention texture inconsistency between batches. *"First bag was perfect — soft and moist. Second bag was noticeably drier, more leathery,"* one customer reported. That's the nature of sun-dried fruit — moisture content varies by harvest and drying conditions. It's not a defect, but it is a gamble.

Also, while the bag is labeled resealable, some buyers report the zip closure is flimsy or missing entirely. *"The bag didn't have a working zipper on mine,"* one reviewer noted. Plan to transfer to a glass jar or use a clip.

And the color can be off-putting if you're used to supermarket apricots. The natural brown hue is correct, but shoppers expecting bright orange may think the fruit is old. It's not — that's what unsulfured apricots look like.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Are these sweetened?**
No. The sweetness comes entirely from the fruit itself. Blenheim apricots are naturally high in sugar when dried, so they taste sweet — but there's no added cane sugar, corn syrup, or artificial sweetener.

**How should I store them?**
Keep them in an airtight container in a cool, dark place. They'll last 6-12 months. If they get too dry, a slice of bread in the container for 24 hours will rehydrate them slightly.

**Are these good for baking?**
Excellent. Because they're whole and soft, they chop easily. They hold their shape better than cheaper diced apricots and don't turn to mush in muffins or quick breads.
