## Performance: The Descaling Workhorse

Here's where this earns its keep. For coffee makers — drip machines, espresso machines, pour-over kettles — citric acid is the active ingredient in virtually every commercial descaler. Essencea's crystals dissolve completely in warm water with no residue. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the container isn't resealable in any meaningful way. The lid snaps on, but it's not airtight, and citric acid absorbs moisture from the air over time, which can cause clumping. Transfer it to a glass jar with a proper lid — this is the single most common complaint in the reviews.

Second, pure citric acid is stronger than the diluted commercial formulas. If you eyeball the ratio instead of measuring, you risk damaging rubber gaskets and O-rings in your machine. The people who complain about "ruined seals" almost always admit to guessing at the concentration. Follow the 1-2 tablespoons per quart guideline and you'll be fine.

Third, there's no fragrance. Commercial descalers often include a lemon scent to mask the process. This has no smell — which is actually a sign of purity, but some people expect the lemon experience.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this in my Keurig or Nespresso?**  
Yes. Use 2 tablespoons per 16 ounces of water, run a full cycle, then run two plain water cycles to rinse thoroughly. This is the same active ingredient as the official Keurig descaler.

**Q: How long does the 5LB container last?**  
For a standard 12-cup drip machine, one batch uses about 1-2 tablespoons. At monthly descaling, expect 3-4 years from one container. If you also clean your dishwasher and laundry, it'll go faster but still lasts over a year.

**Q: Is food-grade citric acid safe for consumption?**  
Yes, it's a common food additive (E330) used in canning and candy making. The non-GMO claim refers to the fermentation process — it's derived from corn or beets, not petroleum.
