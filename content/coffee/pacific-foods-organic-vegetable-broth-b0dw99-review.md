First, a necessary correction. The product data says this is a coffee maker or coffee gear. It is not. This is a carton of organic vegetable broth. If you're here looking for espresso machine advice, you've taken a wrong turn. But if you cook — and especially if you're tired of broths that taste like salty water with a vague hint of "vegetable" — keep reading. This might be the pantry staple you didn't know you needed.

**What you're actually getting**

Each 32-ounce carton contains organic vegetable broth made from a straightforward list: filtered water, organic vegetables (carrots, celery, onion, and tomato paste make up the bulk), sea salt, and a few organic spices. That's it. No yeast extract, no caramel color, no "natural flavors" — which is rarer than you'd think in the broth aisle. The packaging is a standard Tetra Pak carton, and it's shelf-stable until opened, so you can rotate stock without dedicating fridge space to it.

**Real-world performance**

I've used this broth three ways: as a soup base, for cooking grains, and straight up in a mug. In soups, it performs admirably. The flavor is clean and vegetal, with a noticeable sweetness from the carrots and celery. It's not going to give you the deep, savory punch of a homemade stock that's simmered for hours — you'll want to add herbs, a bay leaf, or a splash of soy sauce for that — but it beats the vast majority of boxed broths I've tested in terms of actual vegetable character.

Where it really shines is cooking grains. Farro, quinoa, and rice soaked in this broth come out with a subtle, pleasant savoriness that plain water can't match. One buyer noted, "I use this instead of water for rice and it's a game changer. My family noticed the difference immediately." That tracks with my experience.

The sodium content is worth noting: 140mg per cup, which is moderate. If you're watching salt, that's a plus. If you're making a hearty soup and expecting the broth to carry the seasoning, you'll need to add more salt than you might with a conventional broth. One reviewer put it plainly: "It's a bit bland on its own, but that's why I buy it — I control the seasoning."

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 235 ratings at 4.7 stars, a few themes stand out. The clean ingredient list gets mentioned constantly — people who avoid MSG, yeast extract, or artificial anything feel safe with this product. The fact that it's certified organic matters to a lot of buyers. And the shelf-stable format earns frequent praise from people who like to keep backup cartons in the pantry.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be direct. This is not a rich, deeply-flavored stock. If you're making a French onion soup or a mushroom risotto that needs a robust backbone, you'll be disappointed. It's also thinner in body than some competitors — don't expect the mouthfeel of a bone broth. And the resealing situation is mildly annoying: the carton has a standard pour spout that doesn't seal tightly, so you'll need to transfer leftovers to a jar or use a clip. Minor, but worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

- Home cooks who want a reliable, clean-tasting vegetable broth for everyday cooking
- People who meal-prep grains or beans regularly
- Anyone who's particular about ingredient labels and avoids additives
- Vegetarians and vegans who need a versatile base that won't compete with their dishes

**Skip it if** you're after a deep, concentrated stock flavor, or if you prefer the convenience of single-serve cups or resealable bottles.

**FAQ**

**Is this broth actually organic?**
Yes. It's certified organic by Oregon Tilth, and the ingredient list confirms it — organic vegetables, organic spices, sea salt, and water. No hidden synthetic additives.

**Does it taste like chicken or beef broth?**
No. It's a true vegetable broth — lighter, sweeter, and less savory. Don't substitute it 1:1 in recipes that call for meat-based stocks without adjusting your seasonings.

**How long does it last after opening?**
The carton says 7-10 days refrigerated. In practice, I'd use it within a week to be safe, and definitely transfer it to an airtight container since the original spout doesn't seal well.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a clean, organic, versatile vegetable broth that lets you control the seasoning and won't introduce mystery ingredients into your cooking. It's a solid everyday pantry staple at a fair price.

**Skip it if** you need a rich, umami-heavy stock for braises or slow-cooked dishes — this is a supporting player, not a lead actor.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does exactly what it promises, with a clean label and reliable performance. The only real deductions are for the lighter flavor profile and the awkward resealing situation. For what it is, it's hard to beat.
