# Gourmanity Honey Drops Made With Lavender Honey: The Coffee Lover’s Sweetener You Didn’t Know You Needed

Most honey in a jar is a sticky, drippy hassle. You get it on your hands, on the counter, and somehow on the ceiling. Gourmanity’s Honey Drops solve that problem entirely—these are crystallized lavender honey pieces that you drop straight into your hot coffee or tea. No spoon, no mess, no sticky residue.

As someone who's tested dozens of alternative sweeteners for coffee, I was skeptical. Crystallized honey usually means hard, flavorless sugar rocks. These are different. The 7 oz jar contains small, golden nuggets that look almost like coarse sea salt crystals. One drop in a steaming mug of black coffee dissolves in about 30 seconds with a gentle stir, releasing a soft, floral sweetness that’s noticeably more complex than plain sugar or standard honey.

## What You Actually Get

The jar is straightforward: 7 ounces of honey drops, which translates to roughly 140-160 individual drops depending on size. Each drop is about the size of a small pea—enough to sweeten a standard 8-10 oz cup. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: lavender honey and natural lavender oil. No corn syrup, no artificial flavors, no preservatives.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these three ways: in hot black coffee, in iced tea, and straight off the spoon (yes, I did that).

**Hot coffee:** Drop one crystal in, stir for 20-30 seconds. The honey melts into a smooth sweetness that doesn't clump or sink to the bottom like granulated sugar sometimes does. The lavender flavor is present but not overpowering—think a whisper of floral, not a potpourri bomb. It complements the bitterness of dark roast beautifully.

**Iced tea:** This is where you need patience. In cold liquid, the crystals take 3-5 minutes to fully dissolve. You'll want to stir aggressively or let it sit. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**Direct consumption:** The crystals are pleasantly chewy, like honey-flavored rock candy. A bit surprising, but not unpleasant.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest talk: the lavender flavor is gentle, not bold. If you’re expecting a strong floral punch like you’d get from a lavender syrup, you’ll be disappointed. It’s more of an aromatic background note.

Also, the drops are not ideal for cold beverages unless you’re patient. And at the price point (around $15-18 depending on current listings), it’s pricier per ounce than standard honey. You’re paying for the format and the flavor.

## FAQ

**Q: How many drops should I use per cup?**  
Start with one. If you prefer a sweeter drink, add a second. Two drops give a noticeably stronger honey presence.

**Q: Can I use these in baking?**  
Yes, but they need to be dissolved in warm liquid first. They won’t incorporate into dry ingredients well.

**Q: Do they expire?**  
Honey doesn’t really spoil, but the lavender aroma will fade after about 12-18 months. Store in a cool, dry place.
