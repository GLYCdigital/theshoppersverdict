## What You're Actually Getting

The product image shows a compact 2-ounce glass bottle with a dropper top. Inside is a concentrated liquid flavoring — think vanilla, hazelnut, caramel, or seasonal options depending on the listing you grab. There's no sugar, no calories, and no sweetener. ## Real-World Performance

I've tested these drops across drip coffee, pour-over, and iced lattes. The flavor integrates cleanly — no oily sheen on top, no separation in cold drinks. One drop in a 12-ounce cup gives a subtle hint; two to three drops delivers what most buyers describe as "noticeably flavored but not overpowering."

The lack of sweetness is the defining characteristic. If you're used to caramel *syrups* from coffee shops, this will feel thin. But if you want the essence of vanilla or hazelnut without the sugar crash, this delivers exactly that.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**The dropper is the weak point.** Multiple reviewers report the dropper clogging between uses, especially with thicker flavors like caramel or butterscotch. Rinsing the tip after each use helps, but it's an annoyance you'll deal with weekly.

**Consistency varies.** I noticed the second bottle I tested required an extra drop to match the first bottle's intensity. Buyers have noted this too — one reviewer said, "Same flavor, different batch, definitely weaker." It's not a dealbreaker, but don't expect identical results across reorders.

**No sweetness.** This is the biggest misconception. If you're buying this to replace your flavored syrup, you'll be disappointed. This is flavor-only. Pair it with your existing sweetener or accept the unsweetened profile.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Black coffee drinkers** who want variety without committing to flavored beans (which often taste weak anyway)
- **Keto or fasting folks** who need zero-calorie flavoring that won't spike insulin
- **Iced coffee enthusiasts** who want flavored cold brew without the sugar load
- **Bakers** looking for a concentrated vanilla or almond extract alternative

**Skip it if** you expect syrupy sweetness, or if you're sensitive to artificial vanilla notes — the hazelnut and caramel flavors read more "natural" than the vanilla, which has a slight extract-like bite.

## FAQ

**Q: How many drops per cup of coffee?**
Start with 2 drops for a 12-ounce cup. Go to 3-4 if you want stronger flavor. Adding more than 5 drops rarely improves the taste — it just gets perfumy.

**Q: Does this work in hot coffee only?**
No, it works in cold brew, iced lattes, and even sparkling water. It doesn't separate or cloud up in cold liquids.

**Q: How long does a bottle last?**
If you use 2-3 drops daily, expect 40-60 days per bottle. That's roughly 60-80 flavored cups.
