The AeroPress has been sitting on coffee-geek shelves for nearly two decades, and it still outsells machines that cost ten times more. There's a reason for that. At $31.96, it's arguably the cheapest way to make genuinely excellent coffee — if you're willing to learn its quirks.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the main brewing chamber (a clear polypropylene cylinder), a plunger with a rubber seal, a filter cap that screws on, a scoop, a stirrer, and 350 paper micro-filters. That's it. No carafe, no heating element, no electricity required. The whole thing measures roughly 4.5 inches wide and 12 inches tall fully assembled. It weighs about 8 ounces empty — light enough that hikers and travelers have made it a cult staple.

**How it actually performs**

The brewing method is simple: put a paper filter in the cap, add coffee, pour in hot water, stir, press. The air pressure forces water through the grounds in 20-30 seconds, extracting a concentrated cup that you can drink straight (AeroPress style) or dilute with hot water for something closer to drip coffee.

The resulting cup is noticeably cleaner than French press — no silt at the bottom. It's richer and more full-bodied than most pour-over methods. You can control strength by adjusting coffee dose and water temperature. The standard recipe (one scoop, 175-185°F water, 60-second steep) produces a smooth, low-acidity cup that's forgiving of mediocre beans.

**What 25,866 buyers consistently say**

The Amazon reviews at 4.6 stars tell a consistent story. One top reviewer sums it up: *"I've owned a $2,000 espresso machine and a Chemex. This makes better coffee in less time with zero mess."* Durability comes up constantly — people report owning the same unit for 8-10 years. The speed is the other recurring theme: *"I went from 15 minutes of drip machine hassle to under 3 minutes from bean to cup."*

**Where it falls short**

The single-cup limitation is real. If you're brewing for two or more, you're pressing twice — which takes about 4 minutes total, but it's more fuss. The included paper filters need replacing ($8-10 for 350), and while reusable metal filters exist (about $12), they let more fine particles through, changing the texture. The plastic construction, while durable, can develop a slight coffee odor over time unless you occasionally soak it in a baking soda solution.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the solo coffee drinker who wants cafe-quality results without a countertop appliance. It's ideal for campers, RV dwellers, office workers, and anyone who values speed and ease over ritual. It's also perfect for beginners — it's nearly impossible to make a truly bad cup with this thing.

Skip it if you regularly brew for a household of multiple drinkers, if you prefer the oily, full-body richness of French press, or if you want a set-and-forget machine with a thermal carafe.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the AeroPress actually better than a French press?**
Different, not universally better. The AeroPress gives a cleaner cup with less sediment and more clarity. A French press delivers more body and mouthfeel from the oils. If you like bold, textured coffee, keep your French press. If you want smooth, bright coffee without grit, the AeroPress wins.

**Q: How many cups does it make at once?**
One standard mug (about 8-10 ounces) at full strength. You can make a more concentrated "shot" and dilute it with hot water to stretch it to 12-14 ounces, but the chamber physically maxes out around 10 ounces of liquid.

**Q: Do I need the AeroPress Go or other accessories?**
No. The Original works fine as-is. The Go version ($39) adds a travel mug and smaller footprint. A metal filter is worth the $12 upgrade if you dislike buying paper filters, but it's not essential.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy it**

**Buy it if** you want great coffee fast, hate cleaning complicated machines, or travel and camp. **Skip it if** you need to brew for multiple people at once or refuse to learn a new brewing method.

At $31.96, it's a low-risk purchase with a near-zero learning curve and a cult following that's earned over millions of cups. For the price of four café lattes, you get a brewer that will likely outlive your next three phones.
