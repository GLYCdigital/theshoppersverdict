If you've been burned by lavender syrups that taste like soap or dried buds that turn into chewy bits in your coffee, this extract is the fix. Kate Naturals Lavender Extract is one of those niche pantry items that quietly earns its place — and with 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly working for a lot of people.

Here's the honest breakdown before you add it to your cart.

**What you actually get**

The 2-fluid-ounce bottle arrives sealed with a tamper-evident cap. The liquid is clear and thin — it's an alcohol-based extract, not an oil. That matters. Oil-based flavorings separate and float on top of hot coffee; this blends right in. The label lists lavender extract and alcohol, with no artificial colors or sweeteners. One bottle holds roughly 120 teaspoons of extract, which sounds small until you realize you only need a few drops per serving.

**How it performs in real use**

I tested this three ways: in a hot oat-milk latte, in cold lemonade, and in shortbread cookies.

The latte was the standout. Two drops in the milk before steaming gave a floral aroma without turning the drink bitter. The extract held up to heat — no chemical aftertaste, no fading. In cold lemonade, it behaved like a cocktail bitter: a few drops added complexity without requiring sugar. The shortbread needed more — about 1/2 teaspoon per batch — but the flavor came through clean and even after baking.

The concentration is genuinely strong. One enthusiastic pour ruined a batch of whipped cream with an overpowering perfume note. Start small.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring theme in reviews is how far the bottle goes. One buyer wrote, *"I've made 30+ lattes and the bottle still looks full — this is absurdly concentrated."* Another mentioned using it in a lavender-vanilla pound cake that *"tasted like a fancy bakery, not like lotion."* Multiple reviewers specifically call out that it doesn't have the "soapy" flavor cheap lavender products have.

**Where it falls short**

The scent is noticeably stronger than the taste. When you first open the bottle, the floral hit is intense — if you're sensitive to lavender aromas, this might bother you before you even use it. The flavor is more subtle, so you may need to experiment with dosage.

The bigger annoyance is the bottle opening. There's no dropper or restrictor cap — it's a straight open pour. Given how concentrated the extract is, this is a real design flaw. You'll want to buy a small dropper bottle or use a toothpick to transfer drops. It's a minor fix, but it's worth knowing before you accidentally pour a quarter-teaspoon into your morning coffee.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home latte artists** who want a floral note without buying another syrup that goes bad in three weeks
- **Bakers** making lavender shortbread, scones, or macarons who need a consistent, alcohol-based extract
- **Cocktail enthusiasts** experimenting with lavender old fashioneds or lemonade spritzers

Skip it if you're looking for a ready-to-use lavender syrup for iced coffee — this is a concentrate, not a sweetener, and it won't add sugar or body to your drink.

**FAQ**

**Q: How much should I use in coffee?**
A: Start with 1-2 drops in a 12-ounce latte. If you want a stronger flavor, add one drop at a time. More than 4 drops tends to taste perfumey.

**Q: Is this safe for baking at high temperatures?**
A: Yes — the alcohol evaporates during baking, leaving the lavender flavor behind. Use about 1/2 to 1 teaspoon per batch of cookies or cake.

**Q: Does it contain sugar or calories?**
A: No. It's alcohol-based extract with no sweeteners, so it won't affect your macros or blood sugar.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, long-lasting lavender flavor that works in hot drinks, cold drinks, and baked goods without the cloying sweetness of syrups.

**Skip it if** you prefer pre-sweetened flavoring or you're not willing to work with a concentrated extract that requires careful measuring.

Rated 4.6/5 — it earns the high score through concentration and clean flavor, losing points only on the inconvenient bottle design and the scent-vs-taste mismatch. Four stars feels right for most buyers; five if you're already comfortable working with extracts.
