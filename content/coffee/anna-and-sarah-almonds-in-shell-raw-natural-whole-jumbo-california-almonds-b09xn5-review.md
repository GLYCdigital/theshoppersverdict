## What you actually get

The Anna and Sarah product ships as a single clear bag containing whole, unprocessed California almonds. The shells are a light tan color, clean, and mostly free of the powdery dust you see on cheaper in-shell nuts. As shown in the product image, these are marketed as "jumbo," and that's not marketing fluff — the kernels inside are noticeably larger than standard supermarket in-shell almonds.

One bag yields roughly 3.5 to 4 cups of shelled almonds. That's a solid amount for a 2-pound purchase, though you're paying for shell weight too — typically 35–40% of the total weight is inedible shell. That's just the nature of buying in-shell.

## Performance and real-world use

Cracking these is genuinely pleasant. The shells are thin enough that a standard nutcracker handles them with modest pressure, and they break into large, clean pieces rather than shattering into a mess of fragments. I timed myself: about 45 seconds to crack and extract a dozen almonds. If you're using these for baking, factor in that prep time. If you're snacking, it's part of the ritual.

The kernels themselves are where this product earns its rating. They're plump, uniformly sized, and have that sweet, buttery raw almond flavor that tells you they're fresh. No bitter aftertaste, no stale cardboard notes. One buyer noted, "These are the freshest in-shell almonds I've found on Amazon. The flavor is noticeably sweeter than the ones I get from the bulk bin at my local grocery store." That tracks with my experience.

One review put it plainly: "About 8% of my bag arrived with cracked shells. They were still edible but I had to use them first before they went stale." You'll want to inspect your bag on arrival and prioritize any damaged shells.

**Second, the weight variance.** While most bags hit close to the advertised 2 pounds, a handful of reviewers reported getting noticeably less. The lack of a resealable closure is also annoying. Once opened, you need to transfer the almonds to an airtight container or they'll absorb ambient odors and lose freshness within a few weeks.

## FAQ

**How do I know if the almonds are fresh on arrival?**
Shake each nut near your ear. Fresh almonds produce a dull thud; stale ones rattle because the kernel has shrunk away from the shell. Also check for any musty or sour odor when you crack the first few.

**Can I roast these in the shell?**
Yes. Spread them on a baking sheet at 350°F for 10–12 minutes, shaking halfway through. The shells darken slightly and the kernels toast beautifully. Let them cool completely before storing — they'll crisp up as they cool.

**How should I store them long-term?**
Transfer to an airtight glass or plastic container. For storage beyond 3 months, freeze them in a zip-top freezer bag. Raw almonds have high oil content and will go rancid at room temperature eventually — usually around 4–6 months after cracking.
