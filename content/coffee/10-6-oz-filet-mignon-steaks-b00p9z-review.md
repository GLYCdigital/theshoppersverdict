**Opening: The steak you can actually pull off on a Tuesday**

Most filet mignon sold online is either overpriced, inconsistently cut, or arrives thawed and sad. This 10-pack of 6 oz. filets sidesteps most of those traps. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, it's not perfect — but for home cooks who want restaurant-quality tenderness without the butcher-shop markup, this is a legitimately strong option. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box**

You get ten individually vacuum-sealed 6 oz. filets, each about 1 to 1.25 inches thick. They arrive frozen solid, packed in dry ice or gel packs depending on your region. The individual wrapping matters more than you'd think — you can thaw exactly what you need for dinner, not a whole slab of meat you'll be eating for four days.

**Performance and real-world use**

Cooked properly (cast iron, high heat, butter-basted, rested), these steaks deliver the signature filet experience: fork-tender, mild beef flavor, zero chew. The lean cut means you're not getting the rich, fatty punch of a ribeye — that's expected with filet, but worth saying plainly. Season aggressively with salt and pepper, or add a pat of herb butter during the rest. I've also had success sous-vide at 129°F for 90 minutes, then searing for 60 seconds per side. The thickness variance between steaks is real — check each one before cooking and adjust times accordingly.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the tenderness and consistency. One buyer noted, *"These are genuinely restaurant-grade. I've ordered from three other online butchers and these beat them all on texture."* Another said, *"Perfect for date night — pulled two from the freezer, 30 minutes in a water bath, and they rivaled the $45 steak I had last month."* Multiple reviewers also highlighted the packaging quality, mentioning zero freezer burn even after a few weeks in the deep freeze.

**Where it falls short**

Honest tradeoffs exist. First, the flavor profile is lean — if you prefer a steak with beefy, buttery richness, this cut will underwhelm. Second, thickness inconsistency means you'll occasionally get a thinner steak that cooks faster than its siblings. One reviewer complained, *"Two of my ten steaks were noticeably thinner — maybe 0.75 inches. Not ruined, but definitely different cook times."* Finally, if you're feeding a crowd, 6 oz. portions run small; plan on two per hearty eater.

**Who should buy this**

- **Weeknight steak lovers** who want a premium cut without thawing a 2-pound behemoth
- **Date-night planners** — two steaks thaw in 20 minutes in cold water, dinner in under 45
- **Meal-preppers** who portion out protein for the week; these are perfectly sized
- **Sous-vide enthusiasts** — the consistent shape works well in a water bath

**Skip it if** you want marbled, rich flavor (buy ribeye instead), or if you need uniform thickness for a dinner party where all steaks hit the table at the same time.

**FAQ**

**Q: How should I thaw these?**  
A: Best method: transfer frozen steaks to the fridge 12-24 hours ahead. In a hurry? Submerge the sealed bag in cold water for 20-30 minutes. Never microwave — you'll cook the edges.

**Q: Are these grass-fed or grain-fed?**  
A: Based on the product listing and buyer comments, these are grain-finished, which contributes to the tenderness and mild flavor. If you're strictly grass-fed, look elsewhere.

**Q: Can I refreeze after thawing?**  
A: Technically yes if still cold, but texture degrades. Better plan: cook what you thaw. The individual packaging makes it easy to pull only what you need.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want reliable, tender filets at a reasonable per-steak price, especially for small households or variable schedules. **Skip it if** you prioritize beefy flavor over tenderness, or need perfectly uniform cuts for a large gathering.
