## What you actually get

The container holds 8 ounces of extra-coarse white sanding sugar. The crystals are noticeably larger than standard granulated sugar — think tiny glass pebbles rather than powder. The sparkle comes from the crystal facets catching light, not from any metallic additive. As shown in the product image, the texture is uniform throughout, with no clumping or fine dust settling at the bottom of the container.

## How it performs in real baking

I tested this three ways: on top of sugar cookies before baking, on muffin batter, and as a finishing sprinkle on shortbread after cooling.

**Before baking:** The crystals stayed intact through a 350°F oven. They didn't melt into the dough or dissolve into a sticky glaze. After 12 minutes, they still crunched when you bit into the cookie. That's the entire point of a sanding sugar, and it delivers.

**After baking:** Sprinkled on warm shortbread, the crystals adhered without extra moisture and kept their sparkle. This is where the extra-coarse grind matters — finer sugars just absorb into the surface and vanish.

**Cocktail rimming:** It works, but it's chunky. If you're rimming a margarita glass, you'll want to crush it slightly first or you'll get big sugar shards instead of a fine crust.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this in place of regular sugar in recipes?**
No. This is a finishing sugar, not a substitute for granulated sugar in the dough or batter. It won't cream with butter and won't dissolve properly during mixing.

**Q: How long does it stay sparkly after baking?**
Indefinitely, as long as the baked goods stay dry. The sparkle is a function of the crystal structure, which doesn't degrade at normal baking temperatures. Moisture is the enemy — soggy storage conditions will dull the shine.

**Q: Is it just sugar, or is there something added for the sparkle?**
It's pure sugar. The sparkle comes from the size and cut of the crystals — they're large enough to catch and refract light. No metallic dust, no artificial shimmer.
