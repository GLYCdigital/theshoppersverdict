Let's be honest about what's happening here. You're looking at a 2.2-pound can of cherries that costs about the same as a mid-tier bottle of bourbon. And for most people, that seems absurd. But if you've ever ordered a proper Manhattan at a decent bar and wondered why the cherry tasted like actual fruit instead of red-dyed corn syrup — this is the answer. The Luxardo Gourmet Maraschino Cherries are the gold standard in the cocktail world, and they've earned that reputation through sheer, uncompromising quality.

**What's actually in the can**

Pop the lid and you'll find roughly 80–90 deep burgundy cherries floating in a thick, dark syrup that smells faintly of almond and kirsch. These are Marasca cherries — a specific sour variety grown in Italy — preserved with sugar and their own pits, which impart that signature bitter-almond note. The texture is the first thing you'll notice: firm, dense, almost meaty. These aren't the squishy, neon-bright orbs from your childhood sundae memories. They hold their shape, they don't collapse under pressure, and they deliver a concentrated, candied-cherry flavor with a slight boozy warmth. As one Amazon reviewer put it, *"I can never go back to the bright red ones. These taste like what a cherry is supposed to be — deep, rich, and complex."*

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The can itself is utilitarian — a plain metal cylinder with a pull-tab lid. No glass jar here, which actually works in the product's favor. The cherries stay protected from light, and the syrup doesn't leak. The 2.2-pound size is the same one bars and restaurants use, which means you're getting serious volume. For home use, that's a double-edged sword. The label says refrigerate after opening and consume within 30 days. If you're making cocktails nightly, you'll blow through it. If you're an occasional drinker, you'll either waste a third of the can or start putting Luxardo cherries on everything — which, honestly, isn't the worst outcome. One buyer noted, *"I put these on ice cream, in yogurt, and straight out of the can. They're dangerous to have around."*

**Where it falls short**

Let's be direct: the price is the barrier. At roughly $30–35 for this can, you're paying a premium that's hard to justify if you just want a garnish for a Friday night Old Fashioned. The smaller 400g jar is a better entry point for casual users — you'll pay less and actually finish it before the 30-day window closes. Also, the sweetness level is intense. These are preserved in a heavy syrup, not a light one. If you prefer less sugar, you might find them cloying. And the almond flavor from the pits is distinct — some people genuinely don't like it. It's a polarizing taste, though most find it addictive.

**Who should buy this**

- **Cocktail enthusiasts** who make Manhattans, Old Fashioneds, or Whiskey Sours regularly — these elevate the drink noticeably
- **Home entertainers** who host parties and want bar-quality garnishes without paying bar prices per cherry
- **Baking and dessert hobbyists** who want a premium ingredient for Black Forest cake, chocolate tortes, or sundae bars

**Frequently asked questions**

*Do these need to stay refrigerated before opening?*
No. The unopened can is shelf-stable for up to two years. Once opened, they need to go in the fridge, and you should use them within about a month.

*What's the difference between these and regular maraschino cherries?*
Everything. Standard maraschinos are typically brine-cured and dyed red with artificial coloring. Luxardo preserves the fruit in its own syrup with no artificial dyes — the dark color is natural. The flavor is dramatically deeper, with that characteristic bitter-almond finish.

*Can I use the syrup for anything?*
Absolutely. The syrup is excellent in cocktails — try a splash in a bourbon sour or a ginger ale-based drink. Some people also drizzle it over vanilla ice cream or use it in glaze for pork or duck.

**The verdict**

The Luxardo Gourmet Maraschino Cherries are the real deal. They're expensive, they're intensely sweet, and the 30-day post-opening window is genuinely inconvenient for light users. But for anyone who takes their cocktails seriously — or simply wants to taste what a cherry garnish is supposed to be — there's nothing else on the market that comes close. The texture, the flavor complexity, and the sheer quality are unmatched.

**Buy it if** you're a cocktail hobbyist who wants bar-quality results at home, or if you're hosting events and want to impress guests with a small but memorable upgrade.

**Skip it if** you rarely make cocktails, you're on a tight grocery budget, or you prefer a lighter, less candied cherry flavor. In that case, grab the smaller jar and see if it's worth it before committing to the big can.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — one point deducted for the price and the unrealistic 30-day consumption window for home users. But the cherries themselves? Flawless.
