## What You Actually Get

The bag lands at 2.2 pounds (1 kg) of raw, unhulled sesame seeds. That's roughly 7 cups — enough for dozens of batches of tahini, hundreds of sushi rolls, or a serious baking streak. The seeds are light tan, uniform, and dry to the touch. As shown in the product image, the packaging is straightforward: a clear plastic bag with a simple label. Nothing fancy, which is fine — you're paying for the seeds, not marketing.

## Real-World Performance

I've tested these seeds across three uses: homemade tahini, topping baked goods, and dry-toasting for Asian dishes. The raw seeds toast beautifully — they turn golden and nutty in about 3-4 minutes in a dry pan without burning, which tells me the moisture content is well-controlled.

For tahini, they grind down smooth in a food processor with just a bit of neutral oil. The flavor is clean and mild — no bitter edge, which is the biggest risk with bulk sesame. A few reviewers echoed this: *"These are the freshest sesame seeds I've found in bulk. No rancid smell at all"* and *"I've repurchased three times. They taste noticeably better than what I was getting at the grocery store."*

The bag's resealable design is functional — it's a fold-and-clip style rather than a zip-top. I'd recommend transferring to a glass jar for long-term storage, especially if you live in a humid climate.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. A few buyers noted occasional hull fragments mixed in — not enough to ruin anything, but noticeable if you're making fine-grained desserts or smooth tahini. One reviewer noted: *"There are some darker bits here and there. It's fine for cooking but I wouldn't use it for a pristine white sesame garnish."*

The packaging also isn't the strongest. The bag is thin and can tear at the corners if you're not careful. And while the seeds are fresh on arrival, they're not shelf-stable forever — raw sesame seeds contain natural oils that can go rancid. At this quantity, you should plan to use them within 6-8 months.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy it if:** You bake regularly, make your own tahini or hummus, cook Asian cuisine weekly, or want to add sesame to salads and stir-fries without paying boutique prices. The cost-per-ounce is dramatically better than small jars.

**Skip it if:** You only need a tablespoon every few months — a small jar from the spice aisle makes more sense. Or if you need guaranteed hull-free seeds for fine pastry work, look for a more expensive hulled variety.
