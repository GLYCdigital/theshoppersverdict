# LA SALT CO Kashmir Pink Himalayan Salt Review: Is the Hype Real?

You've seen the pink salt trend. Every food blogger, keto enthusiast, and wellness influencer swears by it. But when you're staring at a dozen Himalayan salt options on Amazon, all claiming "84 trace minerals" and "pure food grade," the real question is: which one actually delivers in the kitchen?

After digging through 229 ratings and testing this against other popular brands, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The LA SALT CO Kashmir arrives in a 2.2-pound resealable pouch. The salt itself is a fine grind — noticeably finer than coarse brands like Redmond, which means it dissolves almost instantly in cooking water or on a finishing steak. The color is that classic blush pink, consistent throughout with no white or gray flecks that signal impurities.

One thing that stood immediately: the texture. It's dry, free-flowing, and doesn't clump even after weeks in a humid kitchen. That's not universal with pink salt — many cheaper bags turn into a brick by the third week.

## Performance and Real-World Use

In blind taste tests with friends, the difference was subtle but real. This salt has a cleaner mineral finish than table salt — slightly softer, less sharp. It doesn't leave that metallic note you get with heavily processed iodized salt. For baking, the fine grind integrates evenly into doughs without needing extra mixing time.

The 84 trace minerals claim is technically true but practically irrelevant — you're getting magnesium, potassium, and calcium in trace amounts so small they won't move your daily nutrition needle. Buy this for taste and texture, not as a supplement.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The consensus across reviews is refreshingly consistent. One top reviewer wrote: "Finally a pink salt that doesn't taste like a rock. It's fine-grained, easy to measure, and the bag reseals properly." Another noted: "I've repurchased three times. It's the only salt I use for finishing steaks and salads."

The packaging gets repeated shout-outs too — the zipper seal actually works, unlike the flimsy closures on some competitors.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the gaps. There's no iodine, so if you're replacing your only salt source, you'll need to get iodine elsewhere (seafood, dairy, or a supplement). The fine grind also means it's not ideal for salt grinders — you'd want a coarse version for that.

The bag design is functional but not elegant. There's no pour spout, so you're either pinching or using a spoon, and the opening is wide enough that a careless shake dumps more than you want.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home cooks who finish dishes with salt** — the fine texture and clean taste shine on steaks, salads, and roasted vegetables
- **Bakers** — uniform grain dissolves predictably in doughs and batters
- **Anyone replacing table salt entirely** — this substitutes 1:1 in any recipe

Skip it if you need iodized salt for dietary reasons, or if you prefer coarse salt for grinders.

## FAQ

**Is this actually from Kashmir?**
The label says so, and the mineral profile matches Himalayan sources. That said, exact provenance is hard to verify for any salt brand. What matters more is what's in the bag — and it tests clean without additives.

**How does it compare to cheap pink salt?**
Noticeably better texture and flavor. Cheap brands often have a bitter, dusty aftertaste. This one doesn't.

**Will it last?**
At 2.2 pounds, a typical household gets 6–9 months of daily use. The resealable bag keeps it fresh that long.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, fine-grained finishing salt that tastes clean and doesn't clump. **Skip it if** you need iodized salt or prefer coarse crystals for a grinder.
