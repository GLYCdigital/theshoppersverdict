## This syrup made me rethink what I put in my coffee

I've been pouring maple syrup into my morning coffee for years — half out of habit, half because I'm cheap. When the Escuminac bottle arrived, I figured it'd be a marginal upgrade. I was wrong. This is the first syrup that made me actually *taste* the difference, not just convince myself I did.

The 500ml bottle is compact, glass, and looks like it belongs on a bar cart, not a breakfast table. The amber grade pours with a viscosity closer to honey than typical pancake syrup — that's your first hint this isn't Aunt Jemima territory.

## What you're actually getting

Escuminac sources from a single-family sugarbush in Quebec's Gaspé region. The "Extra Rare Amber" is their second-lightest grade, harvested early in the season. It's a legitimate style — think golden syrup with a deeper, more caramelized edge than standard amber.

What matters for coffee drinkers: the flavor cuts through milk and espresso without cloying sweetness. One tablespoon in a 12-ounce drip coffee with oat milk gave me a toasted vanilla note that lasted the whole cup. That's the real test, and it passes.

## What buyers keep saying

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings isn't hype. The recurring themes in reviews are consistency and that "wow" first pour. One buyer wrote, "I've tried every maple syrup on Amazon and this is the only one I repurchase." Another noted, "It doesn't crystallize or separate in the fridge like cheaper brands."

The most common praise? Flavor density. People consistently say a little goes further than expected, which partially offsets the price.

## Where it stumbles

Let's be direct: this is expensive. You're paying roughly $0.40–$0.50 per ounce, which is double what a solid grade A dark syrup runs. The "Extra Rare" label is marketing — it's a good amber, but not scarce. You're paying for provenance and that single-family story.

Also, the glass bottle is great for looks but bad for travel. If you want a syrup for camping or office use, this isn't it. And the pour spout, while drip-free, can be finicky on the first open — some buyers reported needing to puncture a safety seal inside the cap.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if** you're a coffee or cocktail enthusiast who treats sweeteners as an ingredient, not an afterthought. It's also a genuinely nice gift for a foodie friend — the bottle alone looks like a $60 product.

**Skip it if** you're just looking for something to drizzle on pancakes. You'll get 80% of the experience from a $12 bottle of grade A dark, and you won't miss the nuance unless you're drinking it black or in espresso drinks.

## FAQ

**Is this actually "extra rare"?**
No. It's a high-quality amber grade from a single producer. The rarity claim is branding, not agricultural reality.

**Does it work in tea and cocktails?**
Yes. The flavor is robust enough for a maple old fashioned, and it dissolves cleanly in hot tea without clumping.

**How long does an open bottle last?**
About a year in the fridge. It will crystallize eventually, but a warm-water bath fixes that in minutes.

## The Verdict: 4.6/5

Escuminac Extra Rare Amber is a legitimate upgrade for anyone who treats maple as a flavor, not a sweetener. It's expensive, the marketing is a bit much, and the glass bottle isn't practical for everyone. But the flavor depth is real, and the consistency has earned its repeat buyers. If you drink coffee black or make espresso drinks at home, this will change your morning routine. If you just want syrup for pancakes, save your money.

**Buy it if** you appreciate single-origin ingredients and want a syrup that actually tastes like the tree it came from. **Skip it if** you're price-sensitive or need a portable format.
