Let's be honest: at $19.94, the Amazon Basics 5 Cup Drip Coffee Maker isn't trying to win barista awards. It's trying to get hot coffee in your mug with minimal fuss and maximum value. After digging through 1,261 ratings and the specs, here's what actually matters.

**What you get**

The box contains the brewer base, a 25-ounce glass carafe, a permanent mesh filter basket, and a scoop. No paper filters included — you'll need #4 cone filters or you can use the reusable mesh basket that ships with it. The unit measures about 9.5 inches wide by 10 inches deep, which is genuinely compact. It fits under my upper cabinets with room to spare, something my previous 12-cup brewer couldn't manage.

**Brew performance**

The 5-cup capacity is the headline number, but let's translate that: 25 ounces. That's three standard 8-ounce mugs, or two generous travel mugs. If you're pouring into a 16-ounce commuter cup, you'll get one full cup and a partial second. That's the tradeoff for the small footprint.

Brew temperature lands in the acceptable range — hot enough for immediate drinking, but it won't scorch your tongue like some commercial machines. The warming plate keeps things drinkable for about 30-40 minutes before the coffee starts tasting cooked. One consistent complaint across reviews is the lack of auto-shutoff. One buyer put it plainly: "I've left it on twice and come home to a burnt smell. You have to remember to flip the switch."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The pause-and-serve feature gets frequent mentions. As one reviewer wrote: "I can pour a cup mid-brew and it doesn't drip everywhere. That's all I need." The drip-free spout on the carafe also earns repeated shout-outs — no more coffee running down the side of the pot onto the warming plate.

The mesh filter is another crowd-pleaser. It's fine enough to keep grounds out of your cup without the ongoing cost of paper filters. Just note that it's a #4 cone size, and the basket doesn't have markings to confirm — you'll want to double-check the manual before buying replacements.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the missing auto-shutoff, the brew basket sits at an angle that can cause water to pool on one side if you don't distribute grounds evenly. It's a minor annoyance, but one that can lead to under-extracted coffee if you're not paying attention. Also, the "keep warm" plate is either on or off — there's no temperature adjustment, and it runs hot enough that leaving coffee for an hour produces a bitter, burnt result.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the single drinker or light household that wants a no-frills machine for under $25. It's also a solid first coffee maker for a dorm room, small apartment, or vacation cabin where you don't want to risk a pricey machine. If you're brewing for a family of four or you need programmable start times, look elsewhere — this is manual operation only.

**FAQ**

*Is the carafe dishwasher safe?* The carafe is glass and top-rack dishwasher safe, but the base and basket should be hand-washed. The warming plate scratches easily.

*Does it use standard #4 filters?* Yes, both the permanent mesh basket and paper #4 cone filters work. The basket is unmarked, so verify before buying.

*How long does a full brew cycle take?* Roughly 6-7 minutes for 25 ounces, which is average for this class.

**The Verdict**

Buy it if you want an affordable, compact brewer that handles single-person coffee duty without drama. Skip it if you need a timer, larger capacity, or hotter brew temperatures. For the price, the Amazon Basics 5 Cup delivers exactly what it promises — no more, no less.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Great value for the price, held back by the missing auto-shutoff and limited capacity.
