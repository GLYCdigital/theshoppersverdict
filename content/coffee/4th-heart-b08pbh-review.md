Let me cut through the noise here: 4th & Heart isn't a coffee maker, but if you're serious about your cup, this ghee deserves a spot on your counter. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings on Amazon, it's clearly earning its keep in kitchens. The question is whether it actually improves your coffee or just complicates your morning.

## What you're actually getting

The jar contains 8 ounces of grass-fed ghee — essentially clarified butter with the milk solids removed. That's it. One ingredient. No palm oil, no soy lecithin, no "natural flavors" hiding anything. The texture is smooth and spreadable, closer to soft butter than the waxy ghee you find at some specialty stores.

As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward glass jar with clean labeling. Nothing fancy, but that's kind of the point.

## How it performs in coffee

This is where the rubber meets the road. Drop a spoonful into hot coffee and it melts quickly, but here's the critical part: it doesn't just float on top like oil. Because ghee has a high smoke point and stable fat structure, it emulsifies reasonably well with agitation. Stir it properly and you get a velvety mouthfeel without the greasy residue that cheaper butter leaves behind.

The flavor is subtle — think nutty, slightly sweet, with zero of the sour tang you'd get from regular butter. It doesn't taste like popcorn or movie theater butter. It just makes your coffee taste... richer.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.6 rating isn't accidental. Common threads across reviews:

- **Keto and paleo users love it.** One reviewer noted, "Finally a fat that doesn't break my fast but makes my coffee feel like a treat." That's the key use case here.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**Price.** At roughly $0.75–$1.00 per ounce depending on the listing, this is significantly pricier than buying a pound of grass-fed butter and clarifying it yourself. You're paying for convenience and consistent quality.

**The aroma.** Out of the jar, there's a faint grassy, almost barnyard-like scent. It dissipates in hot coffee, but if you're the type who sniffs everything before using it, prepare yourself.

**Not a creamer substitute.** If you're expecting half-and-half sweetness or oat milk creaminess, this isn't that. It's a fat, not a creamer. Some buyers seem to miss this distinction and are disappointed when their coffee doesn't turn white and sweet.

## FAQ

**Q: Does 4th & Heart ghee need refrigeration?**
A: No. It's shelf-stable for up to 12 months unopened. Once opened, you can keep it at room temperature or refrigerate — it just hardens when cold. Most people keep it in the pantry.

**Q: How much should I use in coffee?**
A: Start with one teaspoon. Blend it with an immersion blender or whisk vigorously for that bulletproof-style texture. More than a tablespoon and you'll feel like you're drinking melted butter.

**Q: Is this just overpriced butter?**
A: Sort of, but it's clarified butter with consistent quality and no milk solids. If you clarify your own butter weekly, skip this. If you want grab-and-go convenience, it's worth the premium.
