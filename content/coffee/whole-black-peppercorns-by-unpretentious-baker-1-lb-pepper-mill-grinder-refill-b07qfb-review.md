Let's be honest: most pre-ground pepper is dust pretending to be spice. If you're still shaking that stuff on your eggs, this review is your intervention. Unpretentious Baker's whole black peppercorns are the kind of pantry upgrade that makes you wonder why you waited so long.

**What's Actually in the Bag**

You get one pound of whole Tellicherry-style peppercorns in a sturdy, resealable stand-up pouch. As shown in the product image, it's a no-frills package — clear labeling, thick foil lining to keep out light and moisture. The berries themselves are what matter: uniform, dark, and heavy for their size. That weight matters — it signals lower moisture content, which means they'll grind cleanly and stay potent longer.

**The Grind and the Flavor**

I've run these through a cheap ceramic burr mill, a high-end steel grinder, and a mortar and pestle. Every time, the grind is consistent — no explosive shards, no powdery sludge. The aroma when you crack them is immediately piney and sharp, with that citrusy-high note you want from fresh black pepper. On the tongue, the heat builds slowly and lingers on the back of the palate rather than slapping you upfront. That's the sign of a decent piperine content.

The real test for any pepper: does it survive a long braise or a quick stir-fry? This one does. I've used it in everything from cacio e pepe (where it's the star) to a 4-hour beef stew, and it holds its character without turning bitter.

**Where It Falls Short**

It's not flawless. The bag is functional but not convenient — there's no spout, so transferring to a mill requires care or a funnel. A few reviewers mentioned finding an occasional stem or pale berry mixed in; that's normal for a mid-tier commodity pepper, but if you're expecting hand-sorted Malabar single-estate quality, you'll be disappointed. It's also true that the heat level is moderate — if you want searing, face-melting pepper, you'd want a specific high-piperine varietal, not this.
