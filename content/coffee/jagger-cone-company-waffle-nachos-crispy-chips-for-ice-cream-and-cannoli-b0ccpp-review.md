# Jagger Cone Company Waffle Nachos Review: Clever Concept, Fragile Execution

Waffle cones are great. Waffle *nachos* — triangles of crispy, cinnamon-sugar waffle you can dunk in gelato or load with cannoli cream — is a genuinely smart idea. The Jagger Cone Company version sounds perfect on paper. But with a 2.9-star average across 150 ratings, the reality is more complicated. I tested these chips with vanilla bean ice cream, cannoli filling, and straight out of the bag. Here's what you need to know before buying.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains a plastic clamshell of waffle cone pieces cut into rough triangles — think tortilla chip size but waffle-textured. They're dusted with cinnamon sugar and have that classic waffle cone aroma: buttery, slightly toasted, with a hint of vanilla. The pieces are thin, maybe 2-3mm, and surprisingly delicate. There's no dip cup, no serving tray, no frills — just the chips.

## Real-World Performance

Let's address the elephant in the room: breakage. As shown in the product image, these chips are supposed to be intact triangles. In practice, my container had about 25% crushed pieces at the bottom, and that's consistent with what buyers report. One reviewer noted, "Opened the box and heard the sound of broken waffle before I even opened the clamshell." Another said, "Half of them were shattered into crumbs."

That said, when you get intact chips, they're genuinely good. The texture is crisp and airy — not dense or tough like some waffle cones. They held up impressively well against ice cream, maintaining crunch for several minutes before softening. The cinnamon-sugar level is restrained, letting the waffle flavor come through. For cannoli, they're a joy — scoop up filling and get that satisfying snap.

## What Buyers Praise

The concept itself gets consistent love. "My kids loved these with strawberry gelato," one reviewer wrote. "Perfect for dessert charcuterie boards." Another buyer appreciated the versatility: "Great for dipping in chocolate or just eating plain." When the chips survive shipping, people genuinely enjoy them.

## Where It Falls Short

The breakage issue is the headline problem, but there are others. The packaging isn't resealable, so unless you finish the container in one sitting — unlikely, given the portion size — the remaining chips go stale fast. Within two days, they lose that crisp snap and turn chewy. At the current price point, that's a real value problem.

Portion size also disappoints. You get roughly 30-40 chips per container depending on breakage. That's enough for two people as a novelty dessert, but not for a party or family gathering. Several reviewers called out the quantity as underwhelming relative to cost.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy if:** You're making a dessert charcuterie board and want something unique that'll impress guests. Or if you love cannoli and want an easier vehicle than traditional shells. They're also great for kids' ice cream parties where regular cones get too messy.

**Skip if:** You need a reliable, repeat-purchase snack. The breakage lottery and non-resealable packaging make these frustrating for everyday use. Also skip if you're expecting cone-like durability — these are delicate, not sturdy.

## FAQ

**Are these like waffle cone pieces?**
Yes, but thinner and crispier than typical waffle cones. Think of them as waffle cone chips rather than pieces of a full cone. They're designed for dipping, not for holding scoops of ice cream.

**How should I store leftovers?**
Transfer to an airtight container immediately after opening. They'll stay crisp for 2-3 days max. In the original packaging, they go stale within 24 hours.

**Can I use these for ice cream sundaes?**
They work well crumbled over sundaes, but as whole chips, they're better suited for dipping or serving alongside ice cream rather than directly in it.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.3/5**

The Jagger Cone Company Waffle Nachos are a great idea executed with fragile packaging and premium pricing. When you get intact chips, they're excellent — crisp, flavorful, and genuinely fun. The breakage rate and quick staleness hold this back from being a reliable buy.

**Buy it if** you're planning a dessert board and can accept some crushed pieces as a garnish. **Skip it if** you need a sturdy, dependable waffle snack — you'll be frustrated by the crumbs and the price.

*Price check: Given the 2.9-star average and breakage complaints, wait for a sale or consider making your own waffle cone chips at home. The concept is worth trying once, but this execution needs work.*
