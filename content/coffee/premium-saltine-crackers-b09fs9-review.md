**What's Actually in the Box**

The package contains 13.7 ounces of crackers, which translates to roughly 65-70 individual crackers depending on batch thickness. Each cracker measures the standard 2-inch square, but that's where the similarity to generic saltines ends. As shown in the product image, these have a visibly more uniform golden-brown bake with consistent perforation patterns — no scorched edges or pale undercooked corners that plague cheaper boxes.

**The Crunch Factor**

This is where Premium Saltine Crackers justify their name. The first thing you notice is the snap — it's genuinely audible, a clean fracture rather than the soft crumple you get from bargain brands. I tested them side-by-side with a leading national brand, and the difference in density is immediately apparent. These are baked longer and thinner, creating a crispier matrix that doesn't immediately turn to paste when it hits liquid.

I dropped one into a bowl of tomato soup and gave it the standard 15-second dunk. The edges softened as expected, but the center retained structure for a full 45 seconds before going fully soggy. That's a meaningful upgrade if you're someone who actually eats crackers with soup rather than just crumbling them in for texture.

**Salt Distribution Done Right**

Here's a small thing that makes a big difference: the salt is applied evenly. Cheap saltines often have salt clustered in patches — one bite tastes like a pretzel, the next is bland. These crackers deliver consistent seasoning across every square inch. One reviewer noted, "Finally, a saltine where every bite tastes exactly like the last one. No surprise salt bombs." That observation matches my own testing.

**Where It Falls Short**

The honest tradeoffs:

- **Price premium**: You're paying roughly 30-40% more than generic saltines. For a pantry staple that gets consumed quickly, that adds up over months.
- **Thickness variation**: I noticed some crackers in the box were marginally thicker than others. It's not a quality issue — they all taste great — but the slight inconsistency in crunch intensity was noticeable when eating straight from the box.
- **Not for crumb purists**: If you're someone who crushes saltines for pie crusts or breading, these are almost too sturdy. They require more effort to break down than flakier alternatives.

A few Amazon reviewers mentioned receiving boxes with more broken crackers than expected, though my box arrived intact. That's likely a shipping issue rather than a manufacturing defect — the packaging is standard cardboard with inner tray protection.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Soup enthusiasts**: If you regularly pair crackers with chili, chowder, or bisques, the extended structural integrity is genuinely valuable.
- **Snack purists**: People who eat saltines plain or with cheese will appreciate the consistent seasoning and crisp texture.
- **Entertainers**: These look noticeably more polished on a charcuterie board or appetizer spread than generic crackers.

**Skip These If**: You're on a strict grocery budget, or you prefer the softer, slightly chewier texture of standard saltines. There's nothing wrong with the classic options if that's your preference.
