**The decaf coffee that doesn't taste like a compromise.**

Most decaf coffees are a sad exercise in subtraction — you lose the caffeine *and* whatever made the coffee worth drinking in the first place. Nescafé Clásico Decaf doesn't play that game. It keeps the bold, roasted character of the original Clásico line while stripping out the stimulant. For anyone who loves the taste of strong coffee but can't handle the caffeine after 2 PM, this jar is a legitimate answer.

---

**What's in the jar**

You're getting a 7-ounce (200g) glass jar of freeze-dried instant coffee granules. The grind is fine enough to dissolve in seconds — hot water, cold water, milk, it doesn't matter. The jar itself is the classic Nescafé Clásico design: red lid, gold label, unmistakable on any shelf. As shown in the product image, it's a substantial container, not a sample-size trial.

One jar yields roughly 90-100 cups depending on how strong you brew. That puts the per-cup cost well under 20 cents — cheaper than almost any pod, K-Cup, or café pour-over.

---

**Real-world performance**

The standout feature here is the flavor profile. Nescafé Clásico has always been one of the bolder instant coffees — dark, slightly smoky, with a cereal-like undertone that reminds you it's a true coffee, not a flavored sugar drink. The decaf version retains most of that character. It's not identical to the caffeinated original — the decaffeination process does mute the top notes slightly — but it's remarkably close.

It also handles cold brewing well. Stir a heaping teaspoon into cold water and you get a smooth iced coffee with no chalky residue or sour bite. That's a real advantage over ground coffee, which needs steeping and filtering.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The decaffeination process leaves a faintly flat, slightly hollow finish compared to the caffeinated Clásico. It's subtle — most people won't notice unless they do a side-by-side taste test — but purists will catch it.

The bigger practical gripe: the plastic seal under the lid is a genuine nuisance. Multiple reviewers report needing pliers or a knife to pry it off. It's a minor annoyance, but when you're craving coffee and wrestling with packaging, it matters.

Also, if you prefer light, fruity, single-origin coffees, this isn't for you. Clásico is unapologetically bold and dark. That's the point.

**FAQ**

**Does this dissolve in cold water?**
Yes. Stir a teaspoon into cold water and it dissolves in about 20 seconds with no clumping. It makes a genuinely good iced coffee.

**How does it compare to the regular Nescafé Clásico?**
About 90% similar. The body and roast character are there, but the decaf has a slightly softer finish. Most people won't notice the difference unless they taste them side by side.

**Is this actually 100% caffeine-free?**
It's 97% caffeine-free by FDA standards. A tiny trace remains, but it's negligible — safe for nighttime drinking and pregnancy.
