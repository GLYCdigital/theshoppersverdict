## What You Actually Get

The product image shows a classic Kraft Real Mayo jar — white label, red accents, no frills. You’re getting a 30-ounce container of real mayonnaise (not a "dressing" or "spread" — the FDA has rules, and Kraft meets them). The ingredient list is straightforward: soybean oil, water, egg yolks, vinegar, salt, and a few stabilizers like calcium disodium EDTA. No sugar, no artificial flavors. That’s it.

## Performance and Real-World Use

This is where Kraft Real Mayo earns its 4.7-star rating. The texture is genuinely creamy — not gloopy, not runny. It spreads evenly on bread without tearing it, and it holds its structure in cold dishes. I made a batch of chicken salad with it and left it in the fridge overnight; the mayo didn’t weep or separate, which is a common issue with cheaper brands.

The flavor profile is what I’d call "classic American mayo" — tangy from the vinegar, savory from the egg yolks, with a slight sweetness that doesn’t overpower. It’s not as sharp as Duke’s or as neutral as Hellmann’s; it sits comfortably in the middle. For most home cooks, that’s the sweet spot.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and Kraft Real Mayo has two honest drawbacks. First, it’s made with **soybean oil**. If you’re on a paleo or seed-oil-free diet, this is a hard pass — look for avocado-oil or olive-oil mayo instead. Second, the jar isn’t huge. At 30 ounces, a family that goes through mayo quickly will be reordering every couple of weeks. The cap can also be stiff on the first twist — a minor annoyance, but worth loosening before you squeeze.
