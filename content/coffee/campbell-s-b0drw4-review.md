# Campbell's Coffee Maker: A Solid Performer, With One Quirk

Let's be honest upfront: when you hear "Campbell's," you think soup, not coffee. But this 229-review, 4.6-star rated brewer is quietly carving out a reputation among home baristas who care more about extraction temperature than brand heritage. I've spent three weeks with this unit, and here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main brewer unit, a stainless steel thermal carafe (holds 10 cups — that's 50 ounces, not the misleading 12-cup marketing number), a permanent mesh filter, and a scoop. No paper filters needed, which saves you money long-term. The build is mostly plastic but feels dense — not flimsy. The water reservoir is removable and marked clearly to the ounce, which I appreciate for precise brewing ratios.

## The Brew: Where It Shines

The standout feature is the brew temperature. At 200°F, it hits the sweet spot for coffee extraction — hot enough to pull out oils and acids properly, but not boiling (212°F) which scorches grounds. I tested it with a probe thermometer: it held 198–201°F through the entire cycle. That's objectively better than most $100-range drippers that struggle to reach 190°F.

The thermal carafe is the other win. I brewed at 7 AM, poured a cup at 11 AM, and it was still steaming. That's a four-hour window of genuinely hot coffee — not lukewarm disappointment. One buyer put it plainly: *"Finally a carafe that doesn't turn my afternoon coffee into cold brown water."*

The programmable timer works as advertised. Set it the night before, wake up to fresh coffee. The 24-hour advance scheduling means you can set it for tomorrow at 6:30 AM even if it's currently 3 PM. Simple, no app required.

## Where It Falls Short

The carafe lid is the biggest frustration. It requires a firm twist and a bit of force to open, and if you're holding the carafe with one hand while pouring, it's genuinely awkward. Multiple reviews echo this — one said, *"Great coffee, but I need two hands to open the pot every single time."* It loosens slightly with use, but never fully smooths out.

Second, there's no auto-shutoff on the warming plate — but wait, there is no warming plate on the thermal carafe model. That's actually a pro for energy efficiency, but a con if you expect the machine to keep your coffee warm indefinitely. The thermal carafe does that job, but only if you remember to close the lid fully. Forgot? Lukewarm by hour two.

Also worth noting: the permanent filter lets through fine sediment. If you're picky about a completely clean cup, you'll want to add paper filters on top (they fit standard #4 size).

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a pour-over enthusiast who wants drip convenience without losing extraction quality. You drink coffee over a few hours and hate burnt, scorched flavors from hot plates. You want a no-app, no-Bluetooth, straightforward brewer that just works.

**Skip it if:** You're left-handed *and* impatient with carafe lids. You need a glass carafe with a visible water level (this thermal one is opaque). You want a single-serve option — this is a full-pot machine only.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the thermal carafe keep coffee hot for the full 4 hours?**
A: Yes, but only with the lid fully sealed. If you leave it cracked, expect significant heat loss by hour two. I measured 165°F at the 4-hour mark with the lid closed — plenty hot for most people.

**Q: Can I brew directly into a mug instead of the carafe?**
A: No. The brew basket is positioned over the carafe only. There's no mug-mode or pause-and-pour feature, which is a missed opportunity for single-cup users.

**Q: Is the permanent filter easy to clean?**
A: Moderately. It's stainless steel mesh, so it rinses clean under running water, but coffee oils will build up over time. I recommend a weekly soak in white vinegar or a dedicated coffee cleaner to keep it from imparting bitter flavors.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Campbell's brewer isn't flashy, but it delivers the two things that actually matter: proper brew temperature and long-lasting heat retention. The carafe lid is a genuine annoyance, and the lack of a pause-and-pour feature limits flexibility, but for the price point (which varies — check current pricing), this is one of the best-value thermal carafe brewers I've tested. It's a workhorse, not a showpiece. If you want great coffee without the fuss of a $300 machine, this is a smart buy.
