Let me be upfront: I test a lot of snack products, and most "guacamole and chips" combo packs are a disappointment — the guac tastes like green-tinted paste and the chips crumble on the first dip. The Sabra Snackers 2.8 oz kit breaks that pattern. Here's the full breakdown after side-by-side testing against three other grab-and-go guac options.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two components: a 2 oz cup of Sabra's classic guacamole and a 0.8 oz bag of rolled tortilla chips. That's roughly 12-14 chips and about 3 generous scoops of dip. As shown in the product image, the guac cup has a peel-back foil lid, and the chips come in a separate mini bag — not loose in the box, which I appreciate. Total weight is 2.8 oz, and the calorie count lands around 150-170 depending on how aggressively you scoop.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The guacamole is the star here. It's Sabra's standard recipe — Hass avocados, lime juice, onion, garlic, and jalapeño — and it tastes genuinely fresh. The texture has small avocado chunks rather than being whipped into a uniform paste. That matters because it means the dip actually clings to the rolled chips instead of sliding off. When I tested it after 30 minutes out of the fridge, it still held its green color without oxidizing into that unappetizing brown layer.

The rolled tortilla chips deserve their own mention. They're thicker than standard restaurant-style chips and have a satisfying crunch that lasts through multiple dips. They're also shaped like small cylinders, which makes them naturally good for scooping — you don't get the "chip breaks in the guac" problem that plagues flat chips.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 225 Amazon ratings at 4.5 stars tell a consistent story. One verified buyer noted: "The guac actually tastes like real avocado, not the artificial stuff in other snack packs." ## Where It Falls Short

The honest tradeoffs: this is a snack, not a meal replacement. At 2.8 oz, you're getting roughly 150 calories — fine for a mid-afternoon craving, not enough for lunch. The chips are packaged in a thin foil bag inside the box, so if you toss this in a backpack or gym bag, you risk crushing them before you eat. I'd recommend opening the box carefully and eating the chips first if you're transporting it. Also, the guac has onion and garlic mixed in — that's a flavor win for most people, but if you're looking for a clean, avocado-only dip, this isn't it.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for three specific scenarios: office workers who keep snacks in their desk drawer, parents packing school lunches (the 2.8 oz size fits standard lunchbox compartments), and anyone who wants a portion-controlled guac fix without committing to a full 15 oz tub that turns brown after two days. It's also a decent travel snack for road trips or flights, though pack it in your carry-on, not your checked bag.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the guacamole need to be refrigerated before opening?**
A: Yes. It arrives shelf-stable but should go in the fridge once you receive it. Once opened, eat it within the same day — it's a single-serve portion, so that's not hard.

**Q: Are the chips gluten-free?**
A: The rolled tortilla chips are made from corn, and Sabra labels this product gluten-free. If you have celiac, check the packaging for the specific certification on your batch.

**Q: How does this compare to buying a big tub of guac and chips separately?**
A: Price per ounce is higher with the Snackers, but you're paying for freshness and convenience. A full tub goes brown within 48 hours of opening, and you'll likely waste half of it if you're only snacking. This eliminates that waste.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely fresh-tasting guac snack with sturdy chips, no prep, and zero waste. It's the best grab-and-go option in this category I've tested.

**Skip it if** you need a meal-sized portion, want avocado-only guac without onion and garlic, or are on a tight per-ounce budget.
