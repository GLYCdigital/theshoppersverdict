Let me be direct about the elephant in the room: this is a green tea spread, not a coffee product. But if you're reading this, you're probably like the 215 shoppers who rated it 4.3 stars—curious whether a Korean pantry staple can upgrade your morning routine. Short answer: it can, especially if you're tired of the same boring toast and want something that actually tastes like *tea*, not sugar with green food coloring.

**What's actually in the jar**

The 7.05 oz (200g) glass jar arrives sealed with a foil lid. Inside is a smooth, pale-green spread with the consistency of a thick custard—think Nutella, but less oily and more dense. The ingredient list is short: milk, sugar, green tea powder from Jeju Island (where OSULLOC grows its own leaves), and a few stabilizers. No artificial colors, no palm oil. That's a genuine plus for anyone who reads labels.

**How it performs in real life**

The flavor is the headline. This isn't a timid matcha whisper—it's a full-bodied, slightly grassy green tea taste with a pleasant bitterness that cuts through the sweetness. Spread it on warm toast and the heat releases the aroma nicely. But where it really shines is in liquid applications. Drop a spoonful into hot milk and it melts into a creamy green tea latte in about 30 seconds, no whisking required. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"Tastes like a high-end café matcha latte, but at home."* Another noted: *"I put it on everything—bagels, rice cakes, even vanilla ice cream. It's dangerously good."*

The texture holds up well at room temperature. It doesn't separate or get runny, and a little goes a long way—a thin layer covers a slice of bread thoroughly.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. At 200g, this jar is smaller than it looks in photos. If you're making daily lattes, expect to replace it weekly or bi-weekly. The sugar content is also significant—this is a sweet spread, not a health food, despite the green tea halo. One frustrated buyer wrote: *"Tastes great, but I wish the jar was bigger for the price."* Another noted: *"It's sweeter than I expected; I was hoping for more bitter tea flavor."*

The price per ounce runs higher than typical spreads, so if you're strictly budget-minded, this is a treat, not a staple.

**Who should buy this**

- **Matcha lovers** who want a convenient, no-fuss way to get authentic green tea flavor without buying ceremonial-grade powder and a bamboo whisk.
- **Toast/bread enthusiasts** looking to rotate beyond butter and jam—this pairs exceptionally well with plain croissants, brioche, or even savory crackers for a sweet-savory contrast.
- **Home baristas** who enjoy flavoring lattes but are tired of syrups with artificial ingredients.

**Skip it if** you want an unsweetened tea product, need a larger bulk jar, or prefer spreads with a thicker, nut-butter-like consistency.

**Three questions shoppers actually ask**

**Q: Is this the same as matcha spread?**
A: Close, but not identical. It uses green tea powder from Jeju, which has a slightly earthier, less vibrant profile than ceremonial matcha. It's still unmistakably green tea, just with a more mellow, rounded taste.

**Q: Does it need refrigeration?**
A: Not mandatory, but I'd recommend it after opening. The jar says store in a cool place, and refrigeration keeps the texture firm and extends freshness. It spreads fine straight from the fridge—just give it a minute.

**Q: Can I use it for baking?**
A: Yes, but adjust your recipe's sugar since the spread is already sweet. It works well as a filling for rolled cakes, a swirl in cheesecake batter, or folded into buttercream frosting.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an authentic, versatile green tea spread that elevates toast and makes instant café-quality lattes at home. The flavor is genuinely good, the texture is reliable, and it earns its 4.2-star score.

**Skip it if** you're watching sugar intake, need a larger quantity, or expect a bitter, unsweetened matcha experience.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A quality product with a real flavor punch, held back only by its modest size and price point. Worth trying at least once if green tea is your thing.
