## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main brewing unit, a glass carafe with a stainless steel handle, a permanent mesh filter (no paper filters needed — that's a recurring cost eliminated), and a measuring scoop. The base unit measures roughly 11 inches deep and 8 inches wide, which puts it firmly in the compact category. One detail that stands out from the product images: the water reservoir is marked with clear measurement lines, not just vague "2-6 cup" indicators. That's a small thing that matters when you're half-awake at 6 AM.

## Performance and Build Quality

The brewing performance is where this unit earns its keep. Multiple buyers note the coffee comes out at a consistent temperature — one reviewer mentioned, "My last three machines all brewed lukewarm by the second pot. This one stays hot from the first cup to the last." That's thermal consistency, which is harder to achieve than most manufacturers admit.

The stainless steel exterior feels solid, not like the thin-gauge metal you find on budget machines. The warming plate maintains serving temperature without scorching the coffee — a balance that cheaper units frequently miss. The permanent filter does a decent job; expect a small amount of sediment if you're using a fine grind, but nothing that ruins the cup.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and the Kopiko Candy has two honest weaknesses. First, the carafe lid doesn't seal completely when you tilt it past about 45 degrees — expect a dribble if you're pouring aggressively into a travel mug. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

Second, there's no programmable timer. If you want coffee waiting for you when you wake up, you'll need to physically start the brew cycle. At this price point, that's a reasonable trade-off, but it does rule this out for anyone whose ideal morning involves rolling out of bed to a fresh pot.

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right machine for:

- **First-time coffee makers** who want something dependable without overthinking it
- **Two-person households** brewing 2–4 cups daily — the 5-cup capacity is honest, not inflated
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who want to skip paper filters and ongoing costs

Skip it if you need programmability, brew more than 5 cups at once, or pour aggressively into oversized mugs.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Does the permanent filter let grounds through?**
A: With a medium grind, no. With a very fine espresso-style grind, you'll see light sediment at the bottom of your cup. Use a medium-coarse grind for best results.

**Q: How long does a full pot stay hot on the warming plate?**
A: Buyers report 30–40 minutes before noticeable temperature drop. After an hour, it's still warm but not piping hot.

**Q: Can you brew single cups or does it require a full pot?**
A: You can brew as little as one cup, but the heating element distributes heat most evenly with at least two cups in the basket.
