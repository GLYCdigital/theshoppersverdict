**The Nesquik you remember, minus the sugar crash — but is the trade-off worth it?**

Nesquik has been the gold standard for chocolate milk since most of us were kids. The No Sugar Added version is the same familiar red-and-white canister, but with a formula that swaps sugar for sucralose. The question isn't whether it tastes like the original — it doesn't, quite. The real question is whether the trade-off works for your kitchen.

**What you actually get**

The 10.8-ounce canister arrives sealed with a foil lid. Inside is a fine, dark cocoa powder that looks denser than the classic mix. A single serving is 2 tablespoons, which yields about 12 servings per container. Each serving runs 20 calories, 1g net carbs, and 1g protein. The ingredients list is short: cocoa processed with alkali, sucralose, salt, and natural flavors. No maltodextrin, no sugar alcohols, no weird fillers.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this three ways: cold milk, hot milk, and blended into a protein shake. Cold milk is where it shines. Two tablespoons stir into 8 ounces of cold milk in about 15 seconds with a spoon — no shaker bottle needed. There's no clumping, no chalky residue, and the powder dissolves fully without leaving that gritty film on the glass.

Hot milk is a different story. The cocoa flavor flattens out noticeably at higher temperatures. It's not undrinkable, but it tastes thinner and less rich than the cold version. If you're planning to use this for hot chocolate, temper your expectations.

Blended into a protein shake, it adds a pleasant cocoa note without overpowering the vanilla or peanut butter. This is where I found the most value — it's a legit way to turn a boring shake into something closer to a chocolate malt.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings is well-earned. The most common praise centers on the flavor-to-calorie ratio. One reviewer put it plainly: *"This is my go-to for chocolate milk that doesn't wreck my macros. Tastes close enough to the real thing that I don't miss the sugar."* Another noted, *"I've tried every sugar-free chocolate powder on Amazon. This one doesn't have that weird protein-powder aftertaste."*

The sucralose formulation matters here. Unlike many sugar-free mixes that use stevia or monk fruit (which carry a distinct bitter or licorice note), Nesquik's sucralose base tastes closer to real sugar. If you've been burned by other "no sugar added" chocolate powders, this is the one that breaks the pattern.

**Where it falls short**

The sucralose sweetness has a subtle cooling finish that some people notice, especially in the first sip. It's not offensive, but it's there. If you're hyper-sensitive to artificial sweeteners, you'll taste it. 

The canister size is also a minor frustration. The classic Nesquik comes in a 21.8-ounce tub; this one tops out at 10.8 ounces. For a family that goes through chocolate milk regularly, that's a restock every week and a half. At the current price per ounce, it's more expensive than the sugary original — you're paying a premium for the sugar-free formulation.

**Who should buy this**

This is a targeted product, not a universal one. Buy it if you're managing blood sugar, tracking macros, or following a low-carb diet and you miss chocolate milk. It's also a smart buy for parents who want to limit their kids' sugar intake without giving up the breakfast ritual entirely. 

Skip it if you're expecting a perfect replica of classic Nesquik — it's not. Skip it if you primarily want hot cocoa, because this formula isn't built for that.

**FAQ**

*Does it work in hot milk?*
It mixes fine, but the flavor thins out. Cold milk is the intended use case.

*Is it actually keto-friendly?*
Yes. 1g net carbs per serving, no added sugar, and no maltodextrin. It fits standard keto macros.

*How does it compare to the original Nesquik?*
It's about 85% of the flavor, with a slightly different sweetener profile. Most buyers find it close enough.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want chocolate milk without the sugar spike and you're willing to pay a slight premium. **Skip it if** you're a purist who wants the exact original taste or you're looking for a hot cocoa mix.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely good sugar-free chocolate powder that earns its rating with clean ingredients, smooth mixing, and a flavor that's close enough to nostalgic. Not perfect, but the closest thing to a guilt-free chocolate milk on Amazon right now.
