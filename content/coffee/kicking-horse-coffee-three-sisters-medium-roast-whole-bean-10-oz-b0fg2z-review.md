**Kicking Horse Coffee, Three Sisters, Medium Roast, Whole Bean — Review**

Here’s the thing about Canadian coffee: it doesn’t mess around. Kicking Horse Coffee, based in the Rocky Mountains of British Columbia, has built a cult following for its bold, no-nonsense roasts. The Three Sisters blend—a medium roast named after a trio of mountain peaks—is their most balanced offering. But is it worth your counter space and $15+? Let's dig in.

**What you actually get**

The bag is 10 ounces, not the standard 12. That’s the first thing you need to know. For the price point, you're paying a slight premium per ounce compared to grocery-store staples like Starbucks or Peet's. What you're getting in return: 100% organic Arabica beans, fair trade certified, kosher, and roasted in small batches. The whole beans are uniform in size and color — no chaff, no broken pieces, no oily sheen that signals over-roasting.

As shown in the product image, the packaging is a sturdy resealable bag with a one-way valve for degassing. It keeps beans fresh for a solid three weeks after opening, which matters if you're grinding daily.

**Performance and flavor profile**

I brewed this three ways: pour-over, French press, and espresso. The standout characteristic is its low acidity. The tasting notes — chocolate, molasses, and a hint of nuttiness — come through clearly, especially in a French press where the oils aren't trapped by paper filters. It's a heavy-bodied cup that sits on the palate. If you're someone who finds light roasts too sour or acidic, this is your lane.

The "medium" label is generous. This roast behaves like a medium-dark. The beans are a deep chestnut brown with visible surface oils. If you prefer true medium roasts like a Breakfast Blend, this might surprise you. But if you like a cup that has presence without being burnt, Three Sisters hits that sweet spot.

One buyer summed it up: *"Smooth and rich without being bitter. I've tried every Kicking Horse roast and this is the one I keep coming back to."* Another noted: *"Great flavor, but the bag is smaller than I expected. I'm going through it in about a week."*

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The 10 oz bag is the biggest complaint across the 220+ ratings. At roughly $0.50 per ounce, you're paying more than you would for comparable organic whole beans. If you drink a pot a day, this bag lasts about 8-9 days. That's a recurring cost that adds up.

The roast inconsistency is a minor issue. A few buyers reported that their batch tasted noticeably darker than the previous one. When you're paying premium prices, consistency matters. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth noting that small-batch roasting means slight variation between bags.

Also — and this is a real consideration — if you're a light-roast enthusiast who enjoys fruity, floral notes, skip this. Three Sisters is unapologetically earthy and chocolate-forward. It doesn't have the brightness that defines single-origin light roasts.

**Who should buy this**

Three Sisters is ideal for the home brewer who likes a robust, comforting cup — someone who drinks coffee black or with a splash of milk, not someone who needs a complex tasting journey. It's also a solid choice for drip coffee makers, where the low acidity prevents that bitter, over-extracted edge.

It's a reliable daily driver. If you're looking for a special-occasion coffee with exotic flavor notes, look elsewhere. If you want a consistent, ethical, great-tasting cup you can set your watch by, this is it.

**FAQ**

*Is Kicking Horse Coffee actually fair trade?*
Yes. The brand is certified fair trade by Fairtrade International, and they source from cooperatives in Central and South America. The certification covers all their blends.

*How should I grind these beans?*
For pour-over, use a medium-coarse grind. For French press, go coarse. For espresso, fine. The beans are dense, so if you're using a blade grinder, pulse in short bursts to avoid overheating them.

*Does this coffee work in a cold brew setup?*
It shines cold brewed. The low acidity and chocolate notes translate beautifully into a smooth concentrate that doesn't need sweetener.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a bold, low-acid, ethically sourced coffee that's reliable for daily brewing. You appreciate a roast that has flavor depth without requiring a ritual to enjoy.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget (the 10 oz size is a premium cost), or you prefer light roasts with bright, fruity acidity. Also skip if you need a coffee that lasts more than a week — you'll be reordering often.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good coffee that loses points on price-per-ounce and slight roast variability. If Kicking Horse bumped this to a 12 oz bag at the current price, it would be a five-star slam dunk. As it stands, it's a delicious but slightly pricey daily driver.
