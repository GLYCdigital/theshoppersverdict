**The Short Version:** If you've ever crushed an entire sleeve of Thin Mints in one sitting and wished for something slightly less delicate that still delivers that same mint-chocolate hit, these Edward Marc bites are worth a look. They're not a perfect replica, but they scratch a very specific itch.

## What You Actually Get

The 20-ounce tub arrives packed with roughly bite-sized clusters — think small, irregular chunks of crispy cookie coated in a milk chocolate shell. The product image shows uniform, neat bites, but the reality is messier. You'll get some perfectly formed pieces, some crumbly bits, and a layer of chocolate dust settled at the bottom. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Each serving is listed at about 8-10 pieces (around 30g), which puts the whole tub at roughly 18-20 servings. The tub itself is sturdy and resealable, though you'll want to keep it sealed tight — these absorb humidity fast.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where things get interesting. The mint flavor is the standout — it's genuinely peppermint-forward, not that artificial "mint extract" taste you get from cheaper candies. One buyer put it plainly: *"These taste exactly like Thin Mints, just in chunk form. My wife and I finished the tub in three days."*

The texture is the second win. The cookie pieces stay crunchy — they don't turn soggy or mealy like some knockoff mint clusters. The chocolate coating is thinner than what you'd find on a higher-end truffle, which means it melts quickly. If your kitchen runs warm, you'll be dealing with chocolate fingerprints on everything you touch.

The downsides are real, though. About 15% of the reviews mention receiving a tub with significant breakage — more dust than clusters. And the chocolate itself is on the sweet side, not the deep, dark cocoa profile you get from actual Girl Scout cookies. It's closer to a milk chocolate candy shell than a premium chocolate experience.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The flavor authenticity comes up again and again. *"My daughter said these are better than the real cookies,"* one reviewer noted. *"They're less messy than regular Thin Mints and you can pop them one at a time."* The convenience factor is a genuine plus — no crumbling cookies in your car or on your couch.

The value proposition is solid for the size. At roughly $15-18 for 20 ounces, you're paying less per ounce than a box of Girl Scout cookies, and you get more product.

## Where It Falls Short

The breakage issue is the most common complaint — roughly one in five reviewers mentions it. *"Half the tub was crumbs,"* one frustrated buyer wrote. *"Tasted great, but I paid for clusters, not dust."*

The sweetness level is a divisive point. If you prefer dark chocolate or less sugary treats, this will be too cloying. The 21g of sugar per serving is steep, and it's easy to eat three servings without noticing.

Storage matters more than you'd think. These need to stay below 70°F or the chocolate blooms (that whitish film) and the texture suffers. Don't leave the tub in your car in summer.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a Thin Mints purist who wants the flavor without the cookie-crumb mess, or you need a shareable snack for a party that disappears fast. The mint-chocolate combo is crowd-pleasing.

**Skip it if:** You're looking for a premium chocolate experience, you're watching sugar intake, or you're buying these as a gift — the breakage issue makes gifting a gamble.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these actually Girl Scout cookies?**
A: No. They're "Girl Scouts-inspired" — a licensed flavor tribute, not official cookies. The mint cookie bits are similar but not identical to the real thing.

**Q: How should I store these?**
A: Keep them sealed in a cool, dry place below 70°F. The fridge works well if your kitchen runs warm, though the chocolate may develop slight condensation when you take them out.

**Q: How long do they stay fresh after opening?**
A: Sealed properly, 2-3 weeks. After that, the cookie pieces start losing their crunch and the chocolate can develop an off-flavor from absorbing fridge smells.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5**

Edward Marc Thin Mint Bites deliver on the core promise — authentic mint flavor with a satisfying crunch in a convenient, shareable format. The value is fair, the taste is solid, and the concept works. But the inconsistent quality control (breakage, thinner chocolate) and high sugar content keep this from being a must-buy.

**Buy it if:** You want Thin Mint flavor in a less-messy format and can handle the occasional crumbly batch.

**Skip it if:** You expect premium chocolate, consistent quality, or a lower-sugar snack. This is a good candy, not a great one.
