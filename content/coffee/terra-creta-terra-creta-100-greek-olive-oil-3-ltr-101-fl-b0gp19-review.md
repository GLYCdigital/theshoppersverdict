The 3-liter format is the headline. Most premium olive oils come in 500ml or 750ml bottles that cost $15–$25 for a fraction of this volume. Terra Creta's 3L tin undercuts that pricing model significantly, which is why it's earned a 4.6 rating from 229 Amazon buyers. **What's actually in the tin**

You get 3 liters (101 fluid ounces) of extra virgin olive oil from Crete, Greece. The Terra Creta 100 line is their everyday workhorse — a blend of Koroneiki olives, the dominant cultivar on the island. It's cold-extracted, unfiltered, and carries a PDO (Protected Designation of Origin) certification from the Chania region. The tin itself is a standard 3L metal container with a resealable cap, though you'll likely want to transfer this into a glass cruet for daily pouring — the tin's opening is wide and prone to glugging.

**How it performs in real kitchens**

This oil has a distinct personality. The nose is grassy with a noticeable green tomato aroma. On the palate, expect a medium-intensity fruitiness that finishes with a proper peppery bite at the back of the throat — a hallmark of fresh, high-polyphenol oil. It holds up well in sautéing up to medium heat (around 350°F), but it's best used raw: drizzled over salads, finished over grilled vegetables, or dipped with crusty bread. I wouldn't deep-fry with this; save that for a cheaper, more neutral oil.

Buyers consistently praise the freshness. One reviewer noted, "This tastes like it was pressed last week, not shipped across the ocean." Another highlighted the value: "I use olive oil daily for my family of five, and this lasts about two months. Way better than buying tiny bottles every week." The tin also does its job protecting the oil from light — a genuine advantage over clear glass bottles that accelerate oxidation.

**Where it stumbles**

The 3L size is a commitment. Olive oil has a shelf life of roughly 18–24 months from harvest, but once opened, you should use it within 3–4 months for optimal flavor. A single person or light cook will struggle to finish this before it starts to fade. The tin also lacks a practical pour spout — you'll either wrestle with the wide opening or invest in a separate dispenser. And while the flavor is excellent, it's not a delicate, single-estate boutique oil; if you want something more nuanced for finishing fine dishes, this is a step down from specialty bottles.

*Is this actually extra virgin?* Yes — verified by third-party lab testing and carrying EU-certified PDO status. The peppery finish is a reliable indicator of authentic early-harvest oil.

*How should I store it after opening?* Keep the tin sealed in a cool, dark cupboard. Don't refrigerate — cold temperatures cause clouding and condensation. If you decant into glass, use a dark bottle.

*Can I cook with this at high heat?* It's fine for medium heat sautéing and roasting up to 400°F. For deep frying or searing, use a higher smoke-point oil like avocado.
