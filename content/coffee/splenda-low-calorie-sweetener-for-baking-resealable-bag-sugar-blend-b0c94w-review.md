**The Short Version: Baking's Best Compromise**

If you've ever swapped sugar for a zero-calorie sweetener in a cookie recipe and ended up with pale, flat discs that taste like chemical regret, you already know why this product exists. Splenda's Sugar Blend for Baking is the pragmatic middle ground: half real sugar, half sucralose. It gives you most of sugar's functional magic—browning, structure, volume—while cutting calories roughly in half.

**What's Actually in the Bag**

This is a 1-pound resealable pouch of granulated blend. The texture mirrors conventional sugar closely enough that you won't notice a difference when measuring. As shown in the product image, it's a fine, free-flowing granulation that doesn't clump like some blends do. The resealable zipper is genuinely functional—not the kind that tears off on the third use.

**How It Performs in Real Baking**

The critical difference between this and pure Splenda is the sugar content. Real sugar doesn't just sweeten; it caramelizes, tenderizes gluten, and helps cookies spread. Pure sucralose does none of those things. This blend retains roughly half of sugar's functionality.

I tested this in chocolate chip cookies and a vanilla cake. The cookies browned properly and spread within normal range—slightly less than all-sugar batches, but respectable. The cake had good structure and didn't collapse. In both cases, there was a barely perceptible cooling finish on the palate, most noticeable in the cake's crumb. It's the kind of thing you notice if you're looking for it, but most casual eaters won't flag it.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across early purchaser feedback, three themes emerge. First, the cup-for-cup substitution is the headline feature. One reviewer noted, *"I don't have to think about it—just swap it in and bake like normal."* Second, the browning performance gets consistent mentions: *"Finally a low-cal sweetener that doesn't make my cookies look anemic."* Third, the resealable bag earns quiet appreciation—it's a small thing, but it keeps the product usable months after opening.

**Where It Falls Short**

Be clear about what this isn't: it's not zero-calorie. At roughly half the calories of sugar per serving, it's better for maintenance than aggressive calorie cutting. The aftertaste, while subtle, is real. In recipes where sugar's flavor is the star—think simple shortbread or meringue—the sucralose note becomes more apparent. Additionally, this blend is not suitable for people managing diabetes who need to avoid sugar entirely. It's a harm-reduction product, not a replacement.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the home baker who wants to keep making their favorite recipes without the full sugar hit. If you bake weekly and want to shave calories without re-engineering every recipe, this is your product. It's also ideal for bakers who've tried pure sucralose and hated the results. Skip it if you need genuinely sugar-free baked goods, or if you're baking for someone with blood sugar sensitivities.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this in yeast breads?**
A: Technically yes, but yeast feeds on the real sugar portion. Expect slower rising times. For breads, you're better off with a small amount of real sugar plus a zero-calorie sweetener.

**Q: Does it measure exactly like sugar?**
A: Yes. That's the core selling point. Level cup for level cup, it substitutes directly in most recipes.

**Q: Does it caramelize like real sugar?**
A: Partially. It browns and provides some caramelization, but you won't get the deep, complex Maillard reactions of a full-sugar recipe. For most cookies and cakes, the difference is negligible.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You bake regularly and want to cut calories without sacrificing results. It's the closest thing to a seamless sugar substitute for standard recipes.

**Skip it if:** You need genuinely zero-calorie sweetening, or you're baking delicate recipes where sugar's flavor is the entire point.

**Rating: 4.0/5** — A genuinely useful compromise that nails the fundamentals, with the honest caveat that it's still half sugar. For its intended purpose, it's the best option in its category.
