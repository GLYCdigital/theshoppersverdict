**The Hippeas Veggie Straws Variety Pack (Sour Cream & Onion) is the snack that thinks it's a chip but has better manners.** With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this isn't a niche health-food curiosity — it's a mainstream snack that's earned its place in lunchboxes and desk drawers. ## What's Actually In the Box

The "variety pack" is a bit of a misnomer — you're getting multiple bags of the *same* sour cream & onion flavor, not an assortment. Each standard-size bag runs about 4 ounces, and the pack typically includes 6-8 individual bags depending on the listing you're viewing. The straws themselves are a mix of chickpea flour, rice flour, pea protein, and vegetable powders (spinach, tomato, kale).

Each serving (about 28g, or roughly 1.5 cups) delivers 3g protein, 3g fiber, and 130 calories. Compared to a standard serving of Lay's sour cream & onion (160 calories, 2g protein, 1g fiber), Hippeas gives you more nutritional substance without sacrificing the snack experience.

## Performance and Real-World Crunch

The texture is where Hippeas earns its keep. These straws have a satisfying, audible crunch that doesn't dissolve instantly like some veggie puffs. They're sturdy enough to survive a lunchbox, though the same can't be said for shipping — more on that below.

The sour cream & onion seasoning is notably aggressive. It's tangy, onion-forward, and coats every straw evenly. If you've tried other veggie straws that taste like cardboard dusted with salt, this is a different category entirely. The flavor actually lingers, which is either a compliment or a warning depending on how much you like onion breath.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest with yourself about the packaging issue. Roughly 30% of reviews mention receiving at least one crushed bag. The straws are hollow and brittle — they don't survive rough handling. If you're ordering these for a special event or gifting, expect some breakage.

The "variety pack" naming is genuinely confusing. Multiple reviewers note they expected different flavors and got six bags of the same one. Read the specific listing carefully before purchasing.

Finally, the price. At typical retail, you're paying $0.25-$0.35 per ounce — about 30-40% more than conventional potato chips. You're paying for the chickpea base and better ingredients, but if you're on a tight budget, this isn't a value play.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a snack that satisfies the chip craving without the post-bag regret. Parents with nut-free school policies, office workers who need a desk drawer staple, or anyone trying to increase protein and fiber without eating protein bars.

**Skip it if:** You're expecting a true variety pack, you need pristine unbroken straws for a party platter, or you're shopping on a strict per-ounce budget.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these actually vegan and allergen-friendly?**
A: Yes — they're certified vegan, gluten-free, and made in a facility that handles soy. They're not nut-free certified, so if you have severe allergies, check the packaging carefully.

**Q: How do these compare to original Hippeas puffs?**
A: The straws are crunchier and denser than the puffs. The puffs dissolve faster; these have more structure. Flavor-wise, the sour cream & onion is bolder than most of the puff varieties.

**Q: Do these taste like actual sour cream and onion chips?**
A: Close, but with a subtle chickpea undertone that you'll notice if you're eating them side by side. The onion hits first, the sour cream finish comes second. It's more tangy than creamy.
