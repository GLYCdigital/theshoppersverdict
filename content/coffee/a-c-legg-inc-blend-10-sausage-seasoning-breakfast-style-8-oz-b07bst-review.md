## The Breakfast Sausage Seasoning That Actually Tastes Like Breakfast

Most store-bought sausage seasonings fall into two camps: aggressively salty or weirdly sweet. A.C. Legg's Blend 10 sits in a third category — the one that tastes like the sausage you remember from a proper diner breakfast. This is a commercial-grade blend that's found its way into home kitchens, and after working with it, I understand why it has a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings.

**What you're actually getting**

The 8-ounce bag is unassuming — a simple foil pouch with a clear label. Inside is a finely ground spice mixture that includes black pepper, sage, red pepper flakes, and brown sugar. The math matters here: one ounce of seasoning per five pounds of ground meat. That means this single bag seasons approximately 40 pounds of sausage. For context, that's enough for a serious batch of patties, a freezer-stocking session, or a family that goes through breakfast meat weekly.

**How it performs in real use**

I mixed a batch with 80/20 ground pork — the fat content matters, and I'll get to that in a moment. The seasoning incorporates quickly with minimal mixing, and there's no clumping or uneven distribution like you get with some cheaper blends. The texture is fine enough that it doesn't leave gritty spice chunks in the finished patties.

Flavor-wise, this is where Blend 10 earns its keep. The sage is prominent but not medicinal. There's a clean black pepper kick that builds after a few bites, and the brown sugar provides just enough sweetness to round out the edges without making it taste like breakfast candy. The red pepper adds warmth rather than heat — this is not a spicy sausage seasoning, which is exactly right for a breakfast profile.

One note: this seasoning performs best with fattier meat. Using lean ground turkey or 93/7 beef will result in drier, less flavorful sausage. Stick with pork at 80/20 or 85/15 for the intended result.

**What buyers consistently say**

The praise in the ratings is remarkably consistent. One buyer noted, "This is the only sausage seasoning I use now — tastes like what you'd get from a butcher shop." Another mentioned, "I've made 20 pounds of sausage for holiday breakfasts and everyone asks what my secret is." The yield per bag is a recurring theme in positive reviews — people are genuinely surprised at how far this goes.

**Where it falls short**

The sage is the dividing line. If you're used to the sweeter, maple-forward breakfast sausages from major grocery chains, Blend 10 will taste noticeably more savory and herbal. That's a feature for some, a flaw for others. There's also no smaller size available — you're committing to 8 ounces, which is a lot of seasoning if you only make sausage occasionally. The seasoning does have a shelf life; store it in an airtight container and it'll hold for a year, but don't expect it to last indefinitely.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home sausage makers** who want a reliable, consistent breakfast blend without mixing five different spices themselves
- **Meal preppers** who batch-cook and freeze sausage patties for weekday breakfasts
- **Hunters** processing their own venison or pork who want a proven commercial recipe
- **Anyone** tired of bland, overly salty store-bought sausage seasoning

**Three questions shoppers ask**

*Can I use this with ground turkey or chicken?* Technically yes, but add a couple tablespoons of olive oil or butter per pound to compensate for the lower fat content. The flavor will still come through, but texture will be drier.

*How much do I use per pound of meat?* The ratio is 1 oz per 5 lbs — that's about 1.6 tablespoons per pound. Weigh it the first time; eyeballing it leads to inconsistent results.

*Does this work for stuffed sausage links?* Yes. The grind is fine enough that it won't clog a stuffer, and the seasoning holds up well to the longer cooking time of links versus patties.

**The Verdict**

A.C. Legg Blend 10 is a professional-grade breakfast sausage seasoning that delivers authentic flavor without the fillers and anti-caking agents found in cheaper grocery store blends. The sage-forward profile won't suit everyone, and the bulk sizing is a real commitment, but for the price per pound of finished sausage, this is exceptional value.

**Buy it if** you make sausage regularly and want a dependable, balanced breakfast blend that tastes like it came from a proper butcher.

**Skip it if** you prefer sweeter, milder breakfast sausage or only make a pound or two a year — you'll never use the bag up.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a small deduction for the lack of a trial size, not for the seasoning itself.
