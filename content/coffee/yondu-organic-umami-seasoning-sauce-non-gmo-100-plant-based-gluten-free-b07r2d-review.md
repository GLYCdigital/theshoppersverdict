## What You Actually Get

The 250ml glass bottle contains a dark, viscous liquid made from fermented soybeans, sea salt, and water — that’s essentially it. No preservatives, no added sugar, no yeast extract shortcuts. The fermentation process (similar to traditional Korean *doenjang* production) breaks down proteins into free amino acids, which is where the savory depth comes from. As shown in the product image, the minimalist label keeps things honest about what’s inside.

## Performance in the Kitchen

This is where YONDU earns its keep. I tested it three ways: as a soup base, a stir-fry glaze, and a salad dressing component.

**Soup:** One tablespoon dissolved into 2 cups of hot water produced a broth with surprising complexity — rich, slightly nutty, with that deep savory note that usually requires simmering bones or mushrooms for hours. It won't replace a proper stock, but it gets you 80% there in 30 seconds.

**Stir-fry:** Mixed with a splash of rice vinegar and a touch of maple syrup, it created a glaze that clung to vegetables and tofu without turning gummy. The umami came through clearly without the one-note saltiness of standard soy sauce.

**Dressing:** Whisked with olive oil and lemon, it added a savory backbone that made a simple green salad feel intentional.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be direct about the downsides.

**Salt management is real.** One teaspoon contains roughly 15% of your daily sodium. If you're accustomed to seasoning with regular salt, you need to actively reduce it elsewhere. This isn't a dealbreaker, but it's a learning curve.

**The bottle is small.** At 250ml, frequent cooks will go through it in 3-4 weeks. At roughly $15-18 per bottle, that's a recurring cost, not a pantry staple you buy once.

**The flavor profile isn't universal.** That fermented tang — think a milder, cleaner version of fermented black beans — doesn't work in every dish. Desserts, delicate fish, or light cream sauces? Skip it. This is a savory-forward tool, not a universal condiment.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Plant-based cooks** missing that "meaty" depth in dishes — this is your shortcut
- **Busy home cooks** who want restaurant-level broth without simmering bones for hours
- **Gluten-free households** needing a soy-sauce alternative that actually has flavor complexity, not just saltiness

**Skip it if:** you prefer brighter, acidic flavors in your cooking, or you're watching sodium strictly and don't want to manage another salty ingredient.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I substitute this for soy sauce 1:1?**
A: No. It's more concentrated and saltier. Start with half the amount of soy sauce called for, taste, then adjust. You'll likely need less than you think.

**Q: Does the fermentation taste come through strongly?**
A: Noticeably, yes, but it's milder than miso or doenjang. Think "clean savory" rather than funky. It blends well in cooked dishes; raw applications show it more.

**Q: Is this actually versatile enough for a full meal rotation?**
A: Yes, but within limits. It shines in soups, stews, marinades, grain bowls, and roasted vegetables. It won't work in baking or delicate cream-based sauces.
