## What you actually get

The bag contains 16 ounces of finely granulated coconut sugar — roughly the same volume as a standard pound of table sugar. The granules are finer than typical brown sugar but coarser than white sugar, with a light tan color and a faintly sweet, almost nutty aroma. It's certified organic by USDA, non-GMO verified, and kosher. Nothing else in the bag — no anti-caking agents, no fillers, no flow agents. Just dehydrated coconut palm sap.

## Real-world performance in coffee

Here's where it gets interesting. In hot black coffee, MADHAVA dissolves reasonably well — about 15–20 seconds of stirring gets you to a mostly dissolved state, with a few stubborn grains lingering if you're impatient. The flavor shift is noticeable but pleasant: you get a gentle caramel undertone that plays nicely with medium and dark roasts, muting bitterness without masking the bean's character.

For iced coffee? Budget extra stirring time. The granules are denser than white sugar, so they sink and clump in cold liquid. I found that dissolving it in a splash of hot water first makes a huge difference — that's the workaround most reviewers landed on too.

One buyer noted: "I was skeptical about the taste, but it's actually delicious in coffee — like a subtle brown sugar without being cloying." Another said: "Takes a bit longer to dissolve than regular sugar, but worth it for the lower glycemic response."

## Where it falls short

Let's be direct: this is not a diet product. At 15 calories per teaspoon, it's nearly identical to table sugar. The benefit is metabolic, not caloric — the lower glycemic index means a slower blood sugar rise, which matters for diabetics or anyone managing insulin sensitivity. If you're counting calories, this won't move the needle.

The flavor is also polarizing. Some tasters describe it as "earthy" or "slightly smoky," and in light roasts or delicate teas, that can overwhelm. If you drink your coffee black and prefer a clean, neutral sweetness, stick with white sugar. This one has an opinion.

## FAQ

**Does coconut sugar actually taste like coconut?**
No. The name refers to the source (coconut palm sap), not the flavor. You'll taste caramel and molasses notes, not coconut.

**Is it keto-friendly?**
No. It contains carbs and sugars — roughly 4 grams per teaspoon. This is for low-glycemic eating, not ketogenic diets.

**How does it compare to maple sugar or date sugar?**
Maple sugar is sweeter and dissolves faster but is pricier. Date sugar has similar caramel notes but doesn't dissolve well at all in liquids. Coconut sugar sits in the middle — better solubility than date sugar, less sweet than maple.
