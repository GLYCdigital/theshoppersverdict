Here's the thing about chai mixes: most of them taste like cinnamon sugar with a vague tea afterthought. Oregon Chai's powdered mix isn't that. As shown in the product image, this is a straightforward 14-ounce box of powder — but what's inside punches well above its weight class.

I've tested this against loose-leaf chai, concentrate cartons, and three other powdered brands. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains a fine, sand-colored powder that's a blend of black tea, ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, clove, and black pepper — plus evaporated cane juice and honey for sweetness. One box yields roughly 8-10 servings depending on how strong you like it. The instructions call for 2-3 tablespoons per 8 ounces of hot milk or water.

This isn't a single-serve packet situation. You're mixing your own cup, which means you control the strength. That's a feature, not a bug.

## Real-World Performance

**The taste is the story here.** The spice blend is genuinely balanced — the cardamom and ginger come through first, followed by a warming cinnamon finish. The black tea base is present but not bitter. It drinks like a proper chai, not a dessert in a mug.

**Dissolving is effortless.** Stir it into hot milk and it's fully incorporated in about 20 seconds. No clumps, no chalky film on top. I also tested it cold — blended with ice and milk, it makes a solid iced chai without needing a shaker or frother.

**Value math:** At roughly 10 servings per box, you're looking at well under a dollar per cup. A comparable chai latte at a coffee shop runs $4-6. Even factoring in milk, you're saving 70% or more.

## Where It Falls Short

**The sugar content is real.** At 18 grams per serving, this isn't a health food. If you're watching carbs or sugar intake, this mix will blow your budget before lunch. There's no sugar-free version of the powdered format.

**Storage is finicky.** The powder absorbs humidity and turns into clumps within a week if you leave it in the original box. Transfer it to an airtight jar immediately — that's the single most common complaint in the reviews.

**Milk matters more than you'd think.** Made with water, this falls flat — thin and overly sweet. With whole milk or oat milk, it transforms. If you're dairy-free, use a barista-grade oat milk for best results.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with water instead of milk?**
Yes, but it won't taste as good. The spices and sweetness are calibrated for milk's creaminess. Water yields a thinner, sweeter drink.

**Q: How long does an open box stay fresh?**
In an airtight container, 2-3 months. In the original box, expect clumping within a week in humid climates. Use a dry spoon every time — moisture is the enemy.

**Q: Is this the same as the Oregon Chai concentrate?**
No. The concentrate is liquid and has a slightly different spice profile. The powder is sweeter and more convenient. Both are good — this one wins on shelf stability and price per serving.
