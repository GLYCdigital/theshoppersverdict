**Skip the grocery-store jam aisle. This Dalmatia fig spread is a different category of fruit preserve — and if you've only ever had mass-market fig "spreads" that are mostly high-fructose corn syrup, you're in for a surprise.**

I've tested this against three other fig jams side by side, and the difference is immediately obvious. The Dalmatia spread is dark, almost mahogany, with visible fig seeds and a texture that's more like a thick compote than a jelly. One glance at the ingredient list confirms why: figs, citric acid. That's it. No grape concentrate to stretch it, no pectin to firm it up, no added sugar to mask the fruit.

**What you actually get:** An 8.5 oz glass jar with a metal lid, packed with whole Adriatic figs that have been slow-cooked down. The jar is modest — this isn't a Costco-sized tub — but the density means a little goes a long way. A thin layer covers a slice of toast thoroughly.

**Performance and real-world use:** On toast, it's excellent — the fig flavor is deep and slightly caramelized, with a natural sweetness that doesn't cloy. But where it really shines is on a cheese board. I paired it with aged manchego and a sharp cheddar; the fig's earthy sweetness cut through the salt beautifully. It also works as a glaze for roasted pork or chicken — brush it on in the last 10 minutes of cooking and you get a glossy, flavorful finish. In yogurt, it stirs in cleanly, though the seeds add a pleasant crunch that some might find unexpected.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The ingredient purity is the standout. One reviewer put it well: *"I bought this for the fact that it's pure fig — no added sugar, no corn syrup. The taste is authentic and rich."* Others mention the texture as a selling point: *"It's not overly sweet like American jams. You can actually taste the fruit."* The award mention on the label isn't marketing fluff — Dalmatia has won multiple international food awards, and the quality shows in the depth of flavor.

**Where it falls short:** The price. At roughly $8–10 per jar, this is three to four times the cost of a standard grocery jam. If you're looking for a daily PB&J spread, that's hard to justify. Also, the fig seeds are unmistakable. If you're used to smooth, seedless spreads, the texture will feel gritty at first. And because it's preservative-free, once opened, you've got about three weeks to finish it before it starts to ferment. The 8.5 oz jar is smaller than it looks.

**Who should buy this:** If you're building a charcuterie board for a dinner party, this is the spread that will earn you compliments. If you're a home cook who likes experimenting with glazes and reductions, the concentrated flavor is worth the price. If you're someone who appreciates single-origin, minimally processed foods, this fits that ethos perfectly.

**Skip it if:** You want a cheap, everyday jam for kids' lunches, or if you dislike any texture in your preserves.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Does this need refrigeration before opening?**  
A: No, it's shelf-stable until opened. After opening, refrigerate and use within 3-4 weeks.

**Q: Is it really just figs?**  
A: Yes. The ingredient list is literally "figs, citric acid." No added sugar, no pectin, no preservatives.

**Q: How does it compare to fig preserves from a supermarket?**  
A: It's noticeably less sweet and more intensely fig-flavored. Most commercial fig jams use grape concentrate or added sugar to bulk them up; this doesn't.
