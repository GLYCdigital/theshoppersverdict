## Is This Gift Basket Worth Your Money?

Chocolate-covered pretzels are the safest gift in the candy world — universally liked, easy to share, and hard to mess up. Asher's Chocolates has been making candy in Pennsylvania since 1892, and their Chocolate Covered Pretzels Gift Basket is one of the most popular options on Amazon. But at a 3.9-star average across 195 ratings, the story isn't all sweetness. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting a 14-ounce assortment of pretzel rods and twists covered in milk, dark, and white chocolate. The basket packaging is functional — a simple handled basket with cellophane wrap and a ribbon — not the elaborate wicker showpiece some product photos suggest. If you're gifting, it's presentable as-is. If you're buying for yourself, you'll be eating out of the basket for a week.

The chocolate is the real selling point. Asher's uses actual chocolate (not the waxy compound coating you find on supermarket pretzels). It has that proper snap when you bite through, and the chocolate melts at body temperature the way it should. This is a meaningful quality difference.

## Taste and Texture: The Real Story

The pretzel-to-chocolate ratio is well-executed. The pretzels retain their crunch — they're not stale or soft — and the salt provides that essential sweet-salty contrast. The milk chocolate version is the crowd-pleaser; the dark chocolate has enough bitterness to feel sophisticated without being harsh.

One thing buyers consistently mention: the white chocolate pretzels are the sleeper hit. They're creamier than expected and disappear first.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The praise centers on freshness and taste. One verified buyer noted, "These are the best chocolate pretzels I've had. The chocolate is thick and creamy, not that cheap waxy stuff." Another appreciated the variety: "Nice assortment — something for everyone in the family."

But there's a recurring complaint that matters: breakage. Multiple reviewers reported arriving with a significant number of broken pretzels. One put it bluntly: "Half the basket was crumbs." The packaging doesn't provide enough cushioning, and pretzel rods are fragile. It's not a dealbreaker for taste, but it matters for gifting.

## Where It Falls Short

**Breakage is the biggest issue.** Expect some shattered pieces. If you're gifting and the presentation matters, this is a real risk.

**Price.** At roughly $20-25 per 14 ounces, you're paying a premium over comparable chocolate pretzels from grocery brands. You're paying for the Asher's name and the gift-ready packaging. Whether that's worth it depends on your use case.

**The basket isn't fancy.** If you're picturing an elaborate gift presentation, lower your expectations. It's a simple basket, not a showpiece.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're sending a gift to a chocolate lover who appreciates quality over quantity. The Asher's brand carries weight with candy enthusiasts, and the taste genuinely is a step above grocery-store options.

**Skip it if:** You're buying for yourself and care about value, or if you're gifting someone who'll notice broken pretzels. In those cases, shop local or choose a sturdier candy option.

Asher's Chocolate Covered Pretzels Gift Basket delivers where it counts — the chocolate is genuinely good, the pretzels stay crunchy, and the assortment satisfies. But the breakage issue and the premium price keep it from being a no-brainer.
