# Michaels Of Brooklyn Home Style Sauce, 32 Oz: A Jar That Actually Tastes Homemade

Let's be honest — most jarred pasta sauces taste like they were engineered in a lab, not simmered on a stove. Michaels Of Brooklyn is the exception. This 32-ounce jar has earned a 4.7-star rating across 235 Amazon reviews, and after looking at what buyers actually say, that score feels earned — with a few caveats worth knowing before you add it to your cart.

## What You're Actually Getting

The 32-ounce jar is substantial — roughly four to five servings depending on how generously you sauce your pasta. The ingredients are refreshingly simple: tomatoes, onions, garlic, olive oil, and a proprietary blend of Italian seasonings. No added sugar, no corn syrup, no water to stretch it out. As one reviewer put it, "This tastes like my grandmother's gravy, not like something from a can."

## The Flavor Profile That Wins People Over

The first thing you notice is the depth. This isn't a bright, acidic marinara — it's a slow-simmered sauce with a sweetness that comes from caramelized onions rather than sugar. The tomato base is rich without being heavy, and the seasoning is balanced enough that you won't need to add anything. Multiple reviewers mention using it as a base for lasagna and baked ziti, and several say it's the closest they've found to authentic Brooklyn Italian deli sauce.

The texture is where you'll either love it or need to adjust. It's thick — almost stew-like — which makes it cling beautifully to pasta. But if you're used to a thinner, more traditional marinara, you'll want to add a splash of pasta water or a little olive oil to loosen it up.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The word "authentic" shows up repeatedly in the reviews. One Amazon customer wrote: "I've tried every jarred sauce at my local supermarket and this is the only one that doesn't taste like it came from a factory." Another noted: "The 32-oz jar is perfect for a big family dinner — I used it for Sunday sauce with meatballs and had leftovers."

Value also gets consistent shout-outs. At roughly $8-10 per jar, it's pricier than the $3 store brands, but cheaper than most specialty sauces — and reviewers agree the quality justifies the premium.

## Where It Falls Short

The glass jar is the biggest practical complaint. Several reviewers received their order with cracked or shattered jars due to shipping. Amazon's return policy covers this, but it's a hassle you should be aware of.

The chunky onion pieces are the second issue. They're visible and substantial — great if you want texture, problematic if you're feeding kids or anyone who dislikes onion bits in their sauce. A quick blitz with an immersion blender solves this, but you shouldn't have to modify a sauce that's supposed to be ready-to-use.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're making a weekend lasagna or baked ziti and want a sauce that tastes like you simmered it for hours. You're tired of sugary, thin jarred sauces. You appreciate short ingredient lists and honest flavors.

**Skip it if:** You need a quick weeknight marinara that pours straight from the jar. You're on a tight budget — this is a premium product. You or your family have texture sensitivities to chunky vegetables.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this sauce spicy?**
A: No, it's not spicy at all — it's savory and slightly sweet from the onions. If you want heat, you'll need to add your own red pepper flakes.

**Q: How does this compare to Rao's or Carbone?**
A: It's comparable in quality but has a different character. Rao's is brighter and more tomato-forward; Michaels Of Brooklyn is deeper and more savory. It's also typically a dollar or two cheaper.

**Q: Can I freeze this?**
A: Yes. It freezes well for up to three months. Portion it into freezer bags before freezing for easy defrosting.

Skip it if you need a pour-and-go sauce for busy weeknights.**
