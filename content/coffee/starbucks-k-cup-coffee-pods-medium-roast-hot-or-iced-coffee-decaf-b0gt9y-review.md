**The iced coffee trick that actually works**

Most K-Cups fail at iced coffee — brew hot, dump over ice, and you get watery brown dishwater. Starbucks solved that with these medium roast pods by making them dual-purpose: brew at 6oz for a concentrated shot that holds up over ice, or 8oz for a standard hot cup. It's a small tweak that changes everything.

If you've been burned by weak iced coffee from a Keurig, these pods are worth a serious look.

**What's in the box**

You get a standard 12-count box of K-Cup pods. Each pod holds 10.3 grams of ground Starbucks medium roast coffee — the same Pike Place-style blend you'd get in-store. No filters needed, no grinding, no measuring. Drop it in, press brew, done.

The pods work in any standard Keurig 1.0 or 2.0 machine. Nothing proprietary here.

**How it performs hot**

Brewed at 8oz, this is classic Starbucks medium roast. It's smooth with a slight cocoa note and a clean finish — no bitter bite, no acidic burn. It drinks well black, but it also holds up nicely with milk or cream without getting lost.

The body is fuller than most store-brand medium roasts. It won't knock you out like a dark roast, but it's got enough depth to stand up to breakfast or an afternoon slump.

**How it performs iced — the real test**

Here's where these pods earn their keep. Brew at 6oz into a cup packed with ice. The concentrate dilutes perfectly as the ice melts, giving you a proper iced coffee that tastes like it came from a cafe, not a machine.

One warning: 6oz of concentrate is *not* a lot. If you use a 16oz or 20oz tumbler, you'll need two pods or you'll end up with mostly ice and milk. Plan accordingly.

Across 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, the pattern is clear. One reviewer put it simply: *"Finally, a K-Cup that doesn't taste watery over ice. This is my new go-to."*

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

The iced coffee method requires a 6oz brew setting. Some older Keurig models don't offer that — they only do 8oz minimum. If your machine can't brew small, these pods are just standard medium roast K-Cups, and the iced trick won't work.

Price is the other issue. You're paying a premium for the Starbucks name. Generic medium roast pods cost less per cup. If you're purely cost-driven, these aren't the budget choice.

And if you like strong coffee, the medium roast won't satisfy. These are smooth and balanced, not bold. Dark roast fans should look elsewhere.

**Who should buy these**

- **Iced coffee devotees** with a Keurig that has a 6oz brew option — this is the best K-Cup iced coffee solution I've tested
- **Starbucks regulars** who want the same flavor at home for less than a drive-thru run
- **Office workers** who need a reliable, no-fuss cup that tastes decent black or dressed up

**Skip these if** you prefer dark roasts, your Keurig can't brew small sizes, or you're shopping on a strict budget.

**FAQ**

**Do these work in all Keurig models?**
Yes, any standard Keurig 1.0 or 2.0 machine. But for iced coffee, you need a model that supports a 6oz brew size. Check your machine's settings before buying.

**Can I use these for cold brew?**
No. These are designed for hot brewing, then pouring over ice. You can't make cold brew with a Keurig pod.

**How many cups per pod?**
One pod = one cup. For iced coffee in a large tumbler, plan on two pods.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want cafe-quality iced coffee at home and your Keurig has a small brew setting. These pods deliver consistent Starbucks flavor with none of the watery disappointment that plagues other K-Cups.

**Skip it if** you're a dark roast loyalist, your machine can't brew 6oz, or you're counting every penny on coffee.
