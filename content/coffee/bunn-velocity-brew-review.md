# BUNN GRB Velocity Brew Review: 3-Minute Coffee in 2026

At **$124.95**, the Velocity Brew sits in the middle of the drip coffee market — more than a Mr. Coffee, less than a Technivorm Moccamaster. Let's find out if it's worth it.

## Who Should Buy This

**Speed-first coffee drinkers** who want their morning cup in under 4 minutes. The Velocity Brew's claim to fame is genuinely impressive: cold water in, hot coffee out, in about 3 minutes. That's roughly half the time of a standard drip brewer.

**Long-term owners** who'd rather buy once and forget about it. BUNN has a reputation for durability that borders on legendary. One reviewer, a 30+ year BUNN customer, summed it up: *"I just purchased my fourth BUNN coffee maker in thirty years. I hope to get another ten years of service."* Four machines in three decades — that's 7.5 years per machine on average.

**Simple-machine loyalists** who don't want Wi-Fi, touchscreens, or app connectivity. The Velocity Brew has exactly one switch: on and off. No clock to set, no timer to program, no beeps, no error codes. It just works.

**Households that drink full pots** of coffee daily. This is a 10-cup machine, and it's happiest when making a full pot. If you drink multiple cups throughout the morning, this is your machine.

## Who Should Skip This

**Single-serve drinkers** who want one cup at a time. The Velocity Brew's internal tank holds enough hot water for 10 cups. If you just want a single 8-ounce serving, you're heating a lot of water you don't need.

**Morning planners** who want coffee waiting when they wake up. There's no programmable timer. You have to turn it on and wait 3 minutes. That's the trade-off for the speed.

**Apartment dwellers** with limited counter space. The Velocity Brew is tall (14.5 inches) and needs the water heated internally, which means it's always warm to the touch. It also needs to sit under a cabinet with adequate clearance.

## The 3-Minute Promise: Does It Deliver?

The Velocity Brew uses BUNN's proprietary internal tank system. Cold water goes in the top, passes through an internal hot water tank that's kept at brewing temperature (around 200°F), and is sprayed over the grounds almost immediately. There's no waiting for the water to heat up — it's already hot.

The result is real: **coffee in 3 to 4 minutes** from the moment you flip the switch. Reviewers consistently confirm this. One 5-star reviewer called it simply: *"Best coffee maker. I love having coffee ready in three minutes. Hot water for tea immediately."*

Is 3 minutes worth the trade-off of always-on power consumption? BUNN says the internal heater uses about as much energy as a 40-watt light bulb when idle. For context, that's roughly $20-30 a year in electricity — less than a modest coffee bean subscription.

## Build Quality: The BUNN Difference

The single most common theme across 17,000+ reviews is longevity. BUNN coffee makers — particularly the Velocity Brew — are built differently from standard consumer drip machines.

- **Metal internal components** instead of plastic — the spray head unscrews for cleaning and the internal tubing can be descaled
- **No electronic board to fail** — the only electrical component is the heating element and the on/off switch
- **Spray-head design** distributes water evenly over the coffee bed, which users say produces noticeably better extraction than standard drip heads

The trade-off? The Velocity Brew feels substantial. It's heavy. The all-plastic exterior hides serious metal guts. If you're used to lightweight $30 coffee makers, this will feel like overkill. That's the point.

## The Hot Water Feature: Better Tea, Faster

One underrated aspect: because the Velocity Brew keeps water at brewing temperature at all times, you get **near-boiling water on demand**. Multiple reviewers mention using it for tea, instant soup, hot chocolate, or even just hot water for cleaning.

The tank holds enough hot water for 10 cups. After brewing coffee, you have hot water available for the rest of the day — pour in cold water and it heats and dispenses through the brew basket (or just dispense through the brew basket with a filter in place for clean hot water).

## The Downsides You Need to Know

**No programmable timer** is the most common complaint. If you want coffee waiting at 7 AM, this isn't the machine for you. You'll flip the switch at 6:57 AM and have coffee at 7:00 AM. Some people find this inconvenient. Others see it as a feature — minimal complexity, fewer things to break.

**No auto shutoff** means you have to remember to turn it off. If you brew and leave for work, the heating element stays on. BUNN recommends unplugging when not in use if energy consumption is a concern. A simple outlet timer solves this.

**The water reservoir has no window.** You lift the top lid and look inside to check the water level. This is a minor inconvenience that users of glass-carafe brewers will notice.

**10 cups minimum.** The heating tank is sized for a full pot. If you brew half a pot, you're still heating the same amount of water — the excess just stays in the tank as hot water. It works fine, but it's less efficient.

## Temperature and Taste

BUNN machines are known for brewing at the **optimal coffee extraction temperature** of 195-205°F — hotter than many consumer machines. This means better extraction from the grounds, which translates to fuller flavor and more complexity in the cup.

The Velocity Brew also uses a **spray-head design** that distributes water evenly over the grounds rather than dumping it in one spot. This prevents channeling (where water finds paths through the coffee bed, leaving some grounds under-extracted) and produces a more balanced brew.

Coffee stays hot in the carafe. BUNN uses a thicker glass carafe than most competitors, and the brew temperature is high enough that the coffee in the carafe stays at drinking temperature without a burner plate. Many reviewers note this as a significant upgrade from their previous machines.
