If you're scrolling through coffee makers on Amazon, you've probably seen the Mt. Comfort Coffee maker hovering around that 4.3-star mark. Here's the thing — with 215 ratings, that's not a huge sample size, but it's enough to spot patterns. And the patterns here are surprisingly good for a machine in this price tier.

Let me start with what you actually get. The box includes the 12-cup coffee maker, a glass carafe, a permanent mesh filter (nice touch — saves you money on paper filters), and a measuring scoop. No frills, no milk frother, no built-in grinder. It's a straightforward drip machine that does one thing well.

**The Brew Performance**

This is where Mt. Comfort earns its keep. The machine hits a consistent brew temperature around 200°F, which is the sweet spot for proper extraction. I tested it side-by-side with a Cuisinart that costs nearly twice as much, and honestly, the Mt. Comfort produced a cleaner cup. No bitter bite at the end, no sour under-extraction. The showerhead distributes water evenly across the grounds — that's a detail cheap machines screw up constantly.

The brew cycle takes about 6 minutes for a full pot, which is average. What impressed me is the pause-and-serve feature. You can pull the carafe mid-brew to grab a cup, and it stops dripping immediately. It doesn't make a mess on the warming plate like some budget units do.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The most common praise in the reviews centers on reliability. One verified buyer wrote, *"I've had this for 8 months and it still brews like day one. My last three coffee makers died within a year."* Another noted, *"The coffee actually tastes better than my $150 machine. I don't know what they did differently, but it works."*

The programmable timer gets consistent shoutouts too. It holds your settings even if the power blips, which sounds minor until you wake up at 6 AM to a cold machine that lost its programming overnight.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let me be straight about the downsides. The 40-ounce water reservoir is the biggest one. That's 10 standard cups, not 12 — the marketing overstates capacity. If you're brewing for a household of three or more, you'll refill it mid-pot, which is annoying.

The glass carafe is another tradeoff. Coffee stays hot for roughly 30 minutes on the warming plate before it starts tasting scorched. There's no thermal carafe option, so if you sip slowly or work from home, you'll be microwaving refills.

The filter basket is also slightly undersized. If you use coarse-ground coffee and heap your scoops, you'll get overflow into the reservoir. Use a level scoop and you're fine, but it's a limitation worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is ideal for the person who wants reliable, good-tasting coffee without paying for features they'll never use. It's perfect for couples, solo drinkers, or small offices. If you're a coffee snob with a burr grinder and a scale, you'll appreciate the consistent extraction. If you're a family of five brewing two pots daily, the small reservoir will frustrate you.

**FAQ**

**Is the permanent filter any good, or should I buy paper filters?**
The mesh filter works well — it lets more oils through, which means a fuller-bodied cup. That said, you'll get a little sediment at the bottom of your last cup. If that bothers you, use #4 paper filters alongside it.

**How loud is it during brewing?**
It's noticeably quieter than most budget machines I've tested. The drip mechanism doesn't gurgle or sputter, and the warming plate doesn't crackle. Normal conversation volume easily covers it.

**Does the timer actually work reliably?**
Yes, and this is a strong point. The internal memory holds your program through power outages and unplugging. Multiple buyers confirmed it works as advertised.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a simple, dependable drip coffee maker with proper brew temperature and a clean cup. It's a great value for its performance.

**Skip it if** you need more than 10 cups per brew, want a thermal carafe, or prefer a machine with built-in grinding and milk steaming.

The Mt. Comfort Coffee maker earns a solid 4.2 out of 5. It's not flashy, but it does the essentials right — consistent temperature, even extraction, and a clean pour. At this price point, that's a winning formula.
