If counter space is a premium and you just want a reliable cup of coffee without a 12-step brewing ritual, the Keurig K Mini is worth a hard look. At $49.99, it's not the cheapest single-serve machine out there, but it's also not trying to be. It's trying to be the smallest, simplest, most no-nonsense K-Cup brewer you can buy — and for the most part, it succeeds.

**What you actually get**

Open the box and you'll find the K Mini itself (in this case, the "Evergreen" colorway, which is a muted sage green that looks better in person than in photos), a drip tray, and a starter pack of documentation. No water filter, no descaling solution, no sample pods. Keurig keeps it lean here. The machine measures under 5 inches wide — 4.5 inches to be exact — and weighs just over 6 pounds. It's genuinely small. As shown in the product image, it's designed to tuck into tight corners, on crowded countertops, or in a dorm room where every inch counts.

**Performance and real-world use**

The K Mini brews two sizes: 6 ounces and 12 ounces. That's it. You press one button, it heats water in roughly 90 seconds, and you get your coffee. The 12-ounce setting runs a bit fast for a full cup — the water passes through the pod quicker than you'd get with a stronger brew — but that's standard for Keurig's Mini line. The 6-ounce setting produces a noticeably more concentrated cup, which is what I'd recommend for anyone who drinks their coffee black.

The removable drip tray is the standout feature here. It accommodates travel mugs up to 7 inches tall, and when you remove it completely, you can fit a standard 8-inch thermos underneath. That's a real advantage over the older K-Mini models that forced you to shim the machine to fit a taller mug. One buyer noted, "I was skeptical about the height claim, but my 6.5-inch Yeti fits with room to spare. This is the first Keurig that hasn't made me feel like I'm doing gymnastics to brew into my travel mug."

The water reservoir is integrated into the machine — there's no removable tank. You fill it from the top with a measuring cup or a pitcher. This keeps the footprint small, but it's a tradeoff: you're filling the reservoir every time you brew. If you're making multiple cups in a row, you'll be refilling between each one. That's fine for a single person, less so for a household with multiple coffee drinkers.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The size is the overwhelming theme in customer feedback. "I live in a 400-square-foot apartment and this is the only coffee maker that doesn't dominate my kitchen," one reviewer wrote. Another praised the simplicity: "No settings to mess with, no cleaning cycles, no fuss. It just works." The quick heat-up time — about 90 seconds from cold start — also gets consistent love. And the Evergreen color has been a pleasant surprise for several buyers who expected a cheap-looking plastic finish but got a matte, modern appliance.

**Where it falls short**

The cord is short. At roughly 24 inches, you'll need an outlet close by. If your kitchen layout doesn't cooperate, you're buying an extension cord or repositioning the machine. The lack of any strength or temperature control is also worth noting. You can't make a stronger cup by changing a setting — you either brew smaller or choose a bolder pod. And because the reservoir is fixed, cleaning is slightly awkward. You can't toss the tank in the sink; you're wiping down a cavity.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the single coffee drinker, the dorm dweller, the RV owner, or anyone with a cramped counter. It's also a solid choice for an office desk or a secondary machine in a bedroom. If you need to brew for more than one person regularly, or you want customizable brew strength, look elsewhere — the Keurig K-Express or a full-size brewer will serve you better.

**FAQ**

*Does it work with any K-Cup pod?*  
Yes, it accepts all standard K-Cup pods, including generic and store brands. It also works with the reusable My K-Cup filter if you want to use your own ground coffee.

*How often do I need to descale?*  
Keurig recommends every 3-6 months depending on water hardness. The machine will not remind you, so set a calendar alert. A descaling solution costs about $8 and takes 20 minutes.

*Can I brew something other than coffee?*  
Yes, any K-Cup works — tea, hot cocoa, apple cider. Just note that the machine doesn't have a "hot water only" button, so for things like instant oatmeal you'd need to brew a blank pod or use a reusable filter without coffee.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want the smallest possible K-Cup brewer that still delivers a solid 6-12 oz cup, and you don't mind manually filling the reservoir each time.

**Skip it if:** You need to brew multiple cups back-to-back, want adjustable strength or temperature, or your outlet situation requires a longer cord.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The K Mini does exactly what it promises with minimal footprint and zero complexity. The short cord and fixed reservoir are honest annoyances, but for the right buyer, this is the best space-saving coffee maker at this price point.
