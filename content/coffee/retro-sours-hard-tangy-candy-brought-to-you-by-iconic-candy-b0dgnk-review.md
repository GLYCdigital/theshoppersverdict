Let me be upfront: I've tested dozens of "retro" candies that trade on nostalgia but deliver nothing but sugar dust and disappointment. Retro Sours from Iconic Candy isn't that. This is a hard candy that actually commits to its name.

## What You're Actually Getting

The product image shows glossy, jewel-toned hard candies in five colors — and that's accurate. Each piece is roughly the size of a standard jawbreaker, with a visible sour powder coating on the exterior. The bag holds a generous portion, though there's no weight listed on the packaging that arrived. Expect enough for a serious candy bowl or a week of desk grazing.

The flavors skew classic: cherry, lemon, orange, green apple, and blue raspberry. Nothing experimental, but that's the point. This is a comfort-food candy, not a chef-driven tasting menu.

## How It Performs — The Real Test

Here's where Retro Sours separates itself from the pack. The sour kick hits immediately on contact with the tongue — a sharp, citric-acid tang that makes your cheeks tighten. It's not the fake "sour" you get from candies that just dust the outside with sugar. This lingers for a solid 30-45 seconds before mellowing into the fruit flavor underneath.

The hard candy structure is dense and slow-dissolving. A single piece held me for roughly 15 minutes of steady sucking, which is solid value compared to softer candies that vanish in five. The texture is smooth — no chalky residue or gritty mouthfeel that plagues cheaper hard candies.

One thing the product photos don't show: the sour coating is aggressive. After three or four pieces in quick succession, I felt the telltale tingle on my tongue and the roof of my mouth. If you have any mouth sores or sensitivity, pace yourself.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.4-star average across 220 ratings reflects genuine enthusiasm. A recurring theme in positive reviews: the flavors taste authentic rather than artificially generic. One verified buyer wrote, "These actually taste like the candy I remember from the 90s, not like a cheap knockoff." Another noted, "The sour is real. But the criticism is worth noting too. Several reviewers flagged inconsistency between batches — one bag might have a heavier sour coating than the next. A few mentioned the candies can crack if you bite down too hard, which is standard for hard candy but worth knowing if you're a biter, not a sucker.

## Where It Falls Short

The packaging is the weakest link. The bag has no zip closure, so once opened, you're committed to finishing it or finding your own storage. Given the candy's tendency to absorb moisture and get sticky, that's a genuine oversight.

Flavor intensity varies piece to piece. Some candies in my test batch had a pronounced sour punch; others tasted closer to standard hard candy. It's not a dealbreaker, but it means you can't predict exactly what you're getting with each piece.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a sour-candy purist who's been disappointed by weak, sugar-forward imitations. You want a candy that respects the genre and delivers an honest sour experience. It's also a strong choice for stocking stuffers, party favors, or a nostalgic gift for anyone who grew up in the 80s or 90s.

**Skip it if:** You have a sensitive mouth, you prefer chewy or gummy textures, or you need a resealable bag. If your idea of sour is Warheads-level face-melting intensity, this lands on the moderate end of the spectrum — noticeably sour, but not punishing.

## FAQ

**How does it compare to other sour hard candies?**
