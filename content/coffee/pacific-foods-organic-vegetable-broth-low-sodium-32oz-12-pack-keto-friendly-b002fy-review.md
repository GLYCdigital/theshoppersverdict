## What's Actually in the Box

Twelve 32-ounce aseptic cartons. That's 384 fluid ounces of broth — roughly 48 cups. At typical grocery prices, you're saving 15-20% versus buying single cartons. The shelf-stable packaging means you can stack these in a pantry corner for months, which matters when you buy in bulk.

## Performance in Real Cooking

I tested this broth three ways: as a soup base, for cooking rice, and sipped straight from a mug (the real test of any broth).

As a soup base, it delivers clean, savory depth without competing with your other ingredients. The low sodium — 140mg per cup versus the 570mg in regular versions — means you actually control the salt. That's the point, and Pacific Foods nails it.

For rice and grains, it's a straight upgrade over water. The vegetables come through subtly, and the lack of salt bomb means your dish won't taste like a salt lick by the time you've seasoned it properly.

Sipped solo, it's... fine. It's thinner than homemade stock. You'll notice it lacks the body that comes from simmering bones or veggie scraps for hours. But it's not watery in a bad way — it's just honest about being a convenience product.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs.

First, the mouthfeel. If you're used to rich, gelatinous homemade stock, this will feel thin. It's a 10-calorie-per-cup broth — you're not getting body without fat. Add a teaspoon of olive oil or a pat of butter when cooking if you want that silkiness.

Second, storage. Those 32oz cartons are awkward. They don't stack well in most fridge doors, and once opened, they need to be used within 7-10 days. A 12-pack is a commitment — make sure you have pantry space before ordering the case.

Third, flavor consistency. A few reviewers noted some cartons taste more onion-forward than others. This is organic produce — crop variation happens. It's not a dealbreaker, but don't expect identical taste every single carton.

## Who Should Buy This
