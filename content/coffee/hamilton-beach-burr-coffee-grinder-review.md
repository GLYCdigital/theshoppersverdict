**Is the Hamilton Beach Electric Burr Coffee Grinder the budget-friendly answer to consistent coffee grounds?**

At $39.95, this grinder sits in a sweet spot — cheap enough for casual drinkers, but it promises burr-grind quality that blade grinders under $30 simply can't deliver. But does it actually hold up, or is it just another budget gadget that frustrates more than it helps? I dug through 2,030 Amazon ratings and tested the specs to give you the straight story.

**What You Actually Get**

The unit is compact — roughly 7.5 inches tall with a footprint that won't eat your counter space. Inside the box: the grinder base, a clear 8-ounce grinding chamber, a lid with a built-in measuring scale (a nice touch for portion control), and a removable upper burr for cleaning. The dial on the front offers 18 grind settings, which is impressive at this price. You're not getting a hopper-style grinder — you dose beans directly into the chamber, which keeps things simple and reduces waste.

**Build Quality and Performance**

The burrs are stainless steel and do the real work. For drip coffee and French press, the grind consistency is genuinely good — not commercial-grade, but a clear step above anything a blade grinder produces. Espresso users will find the fine settings workable, though you'll need to dial in your dose carefully since the grind can clump slightly at the finest settings.

The motor is where the budget reality shows. It's loud — one reviewer put it bluntly: "Sounds like a blender fighting a drill." It's not a dealbreaker for most, but if you grind coffee while others are sleeping, you'll want to warn them.

The static issue is real. Grounds cling to the chamber walls, and you'll lose a gram or two unless you tap the unit firmly before opening. Some buyers report that letting the grinder run for a few extra seconds after grinding helps settle the grounds.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The value proposition comes through loud and clear in reviews. One verified purchaser wrote: "Upgraded from a $20 blade grinder and the difference in my morning coffee is night and day. Consistent grounds, better extraction, and it didn't break the bank." Another noted: "Easy to clean, simple to use, and the grind settings actually matter — I can taste the difference between setting 5 and setting 12."

The removable parts and dishwasher-safe chamber get consistent shout-outs. Cleaning a grinder is usually the chore that kills the habit, and this one makes it painless.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the noise and static, there's the retention issue. A small amount of grounds stays trapped around the burrs between grinds. If you switch bean types frequently (say, from a dark roast to a light Ethiopian), you'll get slight cross-contamination for the first few grams. Not a huge deal, but worth knowing.

The grind dial is click-based with detents, which is good for repeatability, but there's no numbered indicator — just a line on the dial and a scale on the body. It works, but it's not as precise as higher-end grinders with labeled settings.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for you if you're currently using a blade grinder and want to taste what burr-ground coffee is about without spending $100+. It's also a solid pick for French press and drip coffee drinkers who want grind consistency on a budget. Skip it if you're a serious espresso enthusiast — you'll quickly outgrow the fine-grind clumping and static issues, and you're better off saving for a $150+ grinder.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can it grind for espresso?**  
A: Yes, but with caveats. The finest settings produce a fine-enough grind for pressurized portafilter baskets (like those on Breville and DeLonghi machines). Non-pressurized baskets will be harder to dial in due to clumping. Budget espresso machines work fine; serious setups won't.

**Q: How many cups can it grind at once?**  
A: The 8-ounce chamber holds roughly 40-50 grams of beans — enough for about 4-5 cups of drip coffee depending on your strength preference. You can grind more in batches, but the chamber needs to be emptied between runs.

**Q: Is it easy to clean?**  
A: Yes. The chamber, lid, and upper burr remove without tools and are dishwasher-safe. The lower burr can be wiped with a dry brush. For a deep clean, run a bit of rice through it — a trick that works well here.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want consistent burr-ground coffee under $50, you're upgrading from a blade grinder, or you mostly brew drip or French press.

**Skip it if:** You need near-silent operation, you're chasing third-wave espresso precision, or you frequently switch between wildly different bean types and demand zero cross-contamination.

The Hamilton Beach Electric Burr Coffee Grinder delivers 80% of the experience of grinders three times its price. It's loud, a little static-y, and not built for espresso perfectionists — but for the vast majority of home coffee drinkers, it's the smartest $40 you'll spend on your morning routine. **4.0 stars.**
