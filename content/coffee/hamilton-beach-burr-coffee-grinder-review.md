If you've been shopping for a burr coffee grinder on a budget, the Hamilton Beach Electric Burr Coffee Grinder (model 80385) has likely crossed your radar. At roughly $40, it's one of the cheapest ways to get true burr grinding instead of the inconsistent chopping you get from blade grinders. But is it worth buying, or does the low price tag hide compromises that will frustrate you down the line? Based on over 2,000 reviews, here's what you need to know.

## The Good: Solid Value for Everyday Brewing

The most common praise for this grinder is straightforward: it works well for the price. Users consistently report that the 18 grind settings deliver noticeably more consistent grounds than blade grinders. If you're coming from a $15 blade grinder that leaves you with a mix of powder and bean chunks, this is a genuine upgrade.

For medium roast drip coffee and French press, users find the grind consistency entirely acceptable. The 16-ounce hopper means you can grind enough for a full pot without refilling, and the push-button operation is simple enough that anyone in the household can use it. Several long-term owners mention they've had theirs for over a year with no issues, which at this price point is impressive.

The stainless steel finish is another plus — it looks better than most budget grinders and won't show every fingerprint.

## The Not-So-Good: Noise, Mess, and Coarse Limitations

The most consistent complaint across reviews is the mess. Even users who like the grinder acknowledge that coffee dust accumulates on the counter during use. The plastic cup that catches the grounds has a pour spout that's too small for clean transfer — several reviewers note they need to use a spoon or funnel to avoid scattering grounds everywhere.

Noise is the second major theme. While all coffee grinders are noisy, multiple reviewers describe this one as louder than expected, with one calling it "not coffee grinding noisy, but motor bearing noisy." It's not a dealbreaker for most, but if you're grinding early in the morning while others sleep, you'll want to close the kitchen door.

A smaller but recurring complaint: the coarsest setting isn't coarse enough for some French press enthusiasts. If you prefer a very coarse grind for immersion brewing, you may find the upper end of the range limited.

## Who Should Buy It

This grinder is ideal for anyone moving up from a blade grinder on a tight budget. If you're brewing drip coffee, Aeropress, or French press (and aren't ultra-particular about your coarse grind), it'll serve you well. It's also a good secondary grinder for an office or vacation home where you don't want to risk a more expensive unit.

## Who Should Skip It

If you're serious about espresso, skip this. The grind range doesn't go fine enough for consistent espresso pulls, and the step adjustments between settings are too wide for dialing in. Serious coffee enthusiasts who want stepless adjustment and ultra-consistent particle size should look at grinders in the $100-plus range.

## Bottom Line

The Hamilton Beach 80385 is a genuine burr grinder at a blade-grinder price. It has real compromises — mess, noise, and limited coarse range — but for drip coffee drinkers who want noticeably better flavor without spending big, it's a solid entry point. Just keep a towel nearby.
