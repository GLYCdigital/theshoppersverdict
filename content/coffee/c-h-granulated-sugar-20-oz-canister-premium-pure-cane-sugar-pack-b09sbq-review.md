Sugar isn't the sexiest purchase in your coffee routine, but it's the one you notice when it's wrong. Lumpy, beet-tasting, or slow-to-dissolve sugar ruins a perfectly good pour-over. C&H's 20 oz canister of pure cane granulated sugar avoids all those failures — and at 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's clearly earning its place in cabinets.

**What you actually get**

The 20 oz plastic canister arrives with a flip-top lid that snaps shut with a satisfying click. As shown in the product image, the canister is compact — about the size of a large jar of pasta sauce — so it fits on a crowded counter or inside a cabinet without dominating shelf space. The pour spout is narrow enough to control the stream, which matters when you're sweetening a single mug and don't want a sugar avalanche.

**Performance in real use**

The defining characteristic here is the crystal size. C&H granulated sugar is finer than bulk bin sugar, which means it dissolves aggressively. Stir it into hot coffee and it's gone in three swirls. In iced coffee or cold brew, it dissolves completely without that grainy layer sitting at the bottom of your glass — a real problem with coarser sugars. One reviewer put it plainly: "Finally a sugar that doesn't leave sand in my cold brew." That tracks with what I found.

The canister's resealable lid matters more than you'd think. Unlike bagged sugar that spills, tears, and invites ants, this container keeps moisture out well enough that the sugar stays free-flowing for weeks. In a humid environment, you might notice slight hardening after a month, but the flip-top design minimizes that compared to a twist-off cap.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 225 ratings, three themes dominate. First, the pure cane sourcing — several reviewers specifically noted they can taste the difference from beet sugar, describing it as "cleaner" and "less metallic." Second, the resealable canister gets repeated shout-outs for convenience and pest prevention. Third, the fine grind. As one customer wrote, "Dissolves instantly in iced tea — no stirring required." That's the kind of specific, repeatable win that justifies the price over generic bagged sugar.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The 20 oz size is genuinely small. A typical coffee drinker using two teaspoons per cup will burn through this in about three weeks. If you're feeding a family or running a home office with multiple sweet-tea drinkers, you'll be reordering frequently — and the per-ounce cost is higher than buying a 5 lb bag. The plastic canister also isn't airtight. Over several months, it can absorb pantry odors, and in very humid climates the sugar can eventually clump despite the lid. Neither is a dealbreaker, but they're real limitations.

**Who should buy this**

This canister is ideal for three types of shoppers: the cold brew enthusiast who needs fast-dissolving sugar, the single coffee drinker who wants a tidy, resealable container that won't attract pests, and anyone who can taste the difference between cane and beet sugar. If you're a heavy user brewing for multiple people daily, skip it and buy the larger bag — you'll save money and avoid the reorder cycle.

**FAQ**

*Is this the same as regular white sugar?* Yes, it's standard granulated white sugar, but from pure cane rather than sugar beets. Many people find cane sugar has a cleaner, less bitter aftertaste.

*Can I use it for baking?* Absolutely. It measures cup-for-cup like any granulated sugar and works in cookies, cakes, and sauces. The fine crystals actually cream butter more easily.

*Does the canister keep sugar fresh long-term?* It's good for 2-3 months if you keep the lid snapped shut. Beyond that, consider transferring to an airtight glass jar for maximum freshness.

**Skip it if:** You're a high-volume user who needs bulk pricing, or you want an airtight container for long-term storage.
