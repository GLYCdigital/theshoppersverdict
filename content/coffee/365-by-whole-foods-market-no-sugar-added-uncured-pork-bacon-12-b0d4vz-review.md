Here's the thing about bacon: most of it is cured with sugar. That's not inherently bad — it's how you get that classic sweet-savory caramelization. But if you're on a keto diet, managing blood sugar, or just trying to cut added sweeteners, the bacon aisle becomes a minefield of hidden dextrose, maple syrup, and brown sugar.

365 by Whole Foods Market No Sugar Added Uncured Pork Bacon sidesteps that entirely. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: pork, water, salt, celery powder, and smoke. No sugar. No dextrose. No honey. No "natural flavors" hiding anything. That's it.

## What You Get

This is a 12-ounce package of uncured bacon, meaning it's preserved with celery powder (a natural nitrate source) rather than synthetic sodium nitrite. The slices are thinner than premium thick-cut options — think standard grocery store thickness, not artisanal butcher-shop slabs. Each package runs roughly 18-20 slices depending on how the belly was portioned.

## Real-World Performance

In the skillet, this bacon behaves well. The thinner slices crisp up in about 6-7 minutes over medium heat — faster than thick-cut, which means you need to watch it more closely. The fat renders cleanly without excessive splatter, and the strips hold their shape without curling into useless little spirals.

In the oven (my preferred method), lay it on a rack over a sheet pan at 400°F for 15-18 minutes, and you get consistently flat, crunchy strips. The lack of sugar means it browns rather than caramelizes — you won't get those dark, sticky edges that sugar-cured bacon develops, but you also won't get burned spots where sugar crystallized and scorched.

Flavor-wise, this is a cleaner, porkier bacon. The smoke comes through as a gentle background note rather than a dominant hickory punch. Salt level is well-balanced — noticeable but not overpowering, and it doesn't leave that lingering metallic aftertaste some uncured bacons have.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The thinner slices tear more easily when you're peeling them apart straight from the fridge. Letting the package sit at room temperature for 5 minutes before opening helps, but it's still a minor annoyance.

The milder smoke flavor won't satisfy anyone who wants that aggressive, campfire-intensity bacon experience. If you've been eating Wright or Farmland thick-cut, this will taste noticeably more subdued.

And availability is genuinely frustrating. This is a Whole Foods brand product on Amazon, and stock comes and goes. ## FAQ

**Is this bacon actually sugar-free?**
Yes. The ingredient list contains no sugar, no dextrose, no maple syrup, and no artificial sweeteners. The label's "No Sugar Added" claim checks out.

**Is uncured bacon healthier than cured bacon?**
It's a tradeoff. Uncured bacon uses celery powder, which contains naturally occurring nitrates, versus synthetic sodium nitrite. Both are effective preservatives. The main advantage here is the absence of added sugar and synthetic additives, not a fundamentally different safety profile.

**How should I cook this bacon?**
Oven method works best: 400°F for 15-18 minutes on a rack. In a skillet, medium heat, 6-7 minutes per batch, and don't walk away — the lack of sugar means it goes from done to burnt faster than you'd expect.
