## Is This Diet Cocoa Actually Drinkable?

Most protein hot chocolates taste like punishment. The BariWise version, at 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, appears to be the exception. Designed originally for bariatric patients, this 100-calorie packet delivers 15 grams of protein in a format that actually resembles a treat. The question is whether it holds up as a daily drink or just a post-op necessity.

## What's in the Packet

Each box ships with 7 single-serve envelopes. As shown in the product image, the mix is a fine powder that looks like standard cocoa. One packet mixed with 8 ounces of water yields a 100-calorie drink with 15g protein, 3g fiber, and 1.5g fat. The ingredient list is clean: whey protein isolate, cocoa processed with alkali, and sucralose for sweetness.

## Real-World Performance

Mixability is where most protein powders fail, and BariWise gets it right. The powder dissolves in cold water with just a spoon — no shaker bottle required. Hot water works too, though you'll want to stir rather than microwave the packet. The texture lands somewhere between Swiss Miss and a thin hot chocolate, which makes sense given the low-fat formulation.

Taste-wise, the chocolate flavor is mild and slightly malty. The sucralose sweetness comes through immediately but fades to a clean finish. Compared to the bitter, chalky offerings from competitors like Syntrax or Quest, this is noticeably smoother. One reviewer noted, *"I've tried every protein hot chocolate on the market and this is the only one I can finish without forcing myself."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The protein-to-calorie ratio dominates the feedback. *"Perfect for my post-op diet — I get my chocolate fix without wrecking my macros,"* wrote one verified buyer. Multiple reviewers mention using it as a coffee creamer replacement, adding a packet to their morning brew for a mocha-style drink. The portion control also gets consistent mentions — the single-serve packets prevent the "just one more scoop" problem.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. This is not gourmet hot chocolate. The mouthfeel is thin — there's no creaminess or body to speak of. If you're expecting a rich, indulgent mug, you'll be disappointed. The chocolate flavor is also more "cocoa powder" than "chocolate bar," with a slightly processed taste that some might find artificial.

Cost is another factor. At roughly $1.50 per packet, you're paying a premium for the protein and portion control. If you just want hot cocoa, a $4 tub of Swiss Miss serves you better. The individual packets also generate more packaging waste than bulk options.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're tracking protein intake and want a low-calorie chocolate fix — specifically if you're post-bariatric surgery, on a high-protein diet, or cutting weight. **Skip it if** you simply want a cozy, creamy hot chocolate; the texture won't satisfy that craving.

## FAQ

**Can I use milk instead of water?**
Yes, but it changes the macros. Using 8oz of skim milk adds 80 calories and 8g protein, bringing the total to 180 calories and 23g protein. Whole milk will add more fat and calories.

**Does it contain sugar?**
No. It's sweetened with sucralose (Splenda) and contains 1g of naturally occurring sugar from the cocoa. Keto-friendly at 3g net carbs per serving.

**Will it work in a protein shake or smoothie?**
Yes. The powder blends well with ice and fruit. Some buyers report using it as a chocolate base for homemade protein ice cream.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — BariWise Hot Chocolate delivers exactly what it promises: a decent-tasting, protein-packed hot drink that won't derail your diet. The mixability and clean taste put it ahead of most competitors, though the thin texture and per-serving cost keep it from being a daily staple for everyone.

**Buy it if** you need a high-protein chocolate fix that actually tastes good. **Skip it if** you're looking for a rich, indulgent treat — this is functional food, not dessert.
