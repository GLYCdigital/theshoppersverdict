Let's get one thing straight: not all medium-grain rice is created equal, and the SEKKA Extra Fancy proves it with every batch. This is the rice you see in high-end sushi counters and upscale Japanese restaurants, and after 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clear I'm not the only one who noticed.

## What You're Actually Getting

The 15-pound bag arrives as whole, unpolished grains with a pearly sheen that signals freshness. The "Extra Fancy" grade isn't marketing fluff — it's a USDA classification that means at least 92% of the kernels are whole and unbroken. Compare that to standard rice, which tolerates more broken grains and chalky spots, and you'll taste the difference immediately.

As shown in the product image, these grains are uniformly plump with a slight translucency. That's the signature of properly milled Japanese-style rice, and it translates directly to how the rice cooks.

## The Performance Test

Here's where SEKKA separates itself from the grocery store bag. Cook it properly — rinse until the water runs clear, use a 1:1.1 rice-to-water ratio, let it rest 10 minutes after cooking — and you get rice that:

- **Holds its structure**: Each grain stays distinct but clings to its neighbors. Perfect for chopsticks, terrible for people who like fluffy, separate rice.
- **Has that signature chew**: There's a pleasant resistance when you bite down, followed by a clean release. This is the "umami" texture Japanese rice lovers chase.
- **Stays fresh for days**: Day-old SEKKA rice reheats remarkably well. I've made fried rice on day three that tasted better than most takeout.

The aroma is subtle — a clean, slightly sweet fragrance without the floral punch of jasmine or the nuttiness of basmati. It's the flavor of restraint, which is exactly what you want when pairing with delicate fish or simple seasonings.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The price is the biggest hurdle — you're paying a 30-40% premium over Calrose or Nishiki. Is it worth it? If you eat rice daily, yes. If rice is a once-a-week side dish, probably not.

The other issue is unforgiving nature. This rice demands proper technique. Under-rinse it and you'll get gummy, sticky clumps. Overcook it by two minutes and it turns to paste. There's no "set it and forget it" with SEKKA — it rewards attention and punishes neglect.

Finally, the packaging is basic. A simple zip-top bag with no measuring cup, no moisture-control features. It's not a dealbreaker, but at this price point, a sturdier bag with a better seal would be appreciated.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home sushi makers**: If you're rolling your own rolls or making poke bowls, this is the rice you want. It holds up to vinegar seasoning without turning to mush.

**Q: Is this rice gluten-free?**

**Q: How long does it stay fresh once opened?**
A: Stored in a cool, dry place in the resealable bag, it'll hold quality for 6-8 months. For longer storage, transfer to an airtight container in the refrigerator — it'll keep for up to a year.

**Q: Can I use this in a rice cooker?**
