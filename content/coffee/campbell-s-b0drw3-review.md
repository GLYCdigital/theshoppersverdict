**Campbell's Coffee Maker Review: Solid Brews, Zero Gimmicks**

You know a coffee maker is doing its job when you stop thinking about it. That's the Campbell's 12-cup programmable brewer in a nutshell — it's not flashy, it's not smart, and it won't win design awards. But after 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in real kitchens. Here's the honest breakdown before you hit "add to cart."

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the main unit, a 48-ounce glass carafe, a permanent mesh filter (so you can skip paper ones), and a measuring scoop. No fancy milk frother, no Bluetooth app, no "smart" anything. Assembly takes under a minute — the carafe clicks into place, the reservoir lifts off for filling, and you're done. The build is mostly plastic, but it's thick, matte-finish plastic that doesn't flex or creak. Weight feels reassuring at roughly 7 pounds.

**Performance: Where It Matters**

The headline feature is the brewing temperature. Campbell's maintains a steady 200°F throughout the cycle, which is right in the sweet spot for proper extraction — hot enough to pull out oils and sweetness, not so hot that it scorches the grounds. In my side-by-side test against a $150 drip machine, the Campbell's cup had noticeably more depth and less of that acrid edge you get from cheaper brewers that run too hot.

Brew time for a full carafe clocks in around 6 minutes 40 seconds. The showerhead distributes water evenly across the grounds — no dry pockets, no channeling. The permanent filter catches fines well; you won't see sludge at the bottom of your mug. If you prefer paper filters, standard #4 cones fit fine.

The 24-hour programmable timer works as advertised. Set it the night before, wake up to fresh coffee. The auto-shutoff kicks in at 2 hours, which is a nice safety touch. One thing I genuinely appreciated: the "brew pause" feature actually works cleanly. Pull the carafe mid-cycle to pour a cup, and the drip stops almost immediately — no mess on the warming plate.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The top recurring theme in reviews is reliability. One buyer put it plainly: *"Had mine for 14 months, used every single day, and it brews exactly the same as day one. My last three coffee makers died within a year."* Another praised the simplicity: *"Finally, a coffee maker that doesn't need an engineering degree. My 70-year-old mom figured it out in two minutes."* The temperature consistency gets repeated love too — *"First coffee maker where my dark roast doesn't taste bitter. I actually taste the chocolate notes now."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the flaws. First, there's no grinder. If you buy whole beans, you need a separate grinder — that's an extra $30-50 and counter space. Second, the carafe lid doesn't seal tightly. Coffee left on the warming plate loses heat faster than sealed-carafe competitors, so if you brew a full pot and sip over an hour, the last cup will be lukewarm. Third, the water reservoir only has measurement marks starting at the 4-cup line. If you're brewing a single mug, you're guessing.

Also worth noting: the warming plate runs hot. It keeps coffee at a good drinking temp for about 45 minutes, but after an hour, the bottom of the carafe's contents start developing that scorched taste. Brew what you'll drink.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the daily drip drinker who wants consistent, good-tasting coffee without paying for Wi-Fi connectivity or latte art skills. It's ideal for a 2-4 person household that goes through a pot a day. It's also a smart pick for older users who want something uncomplicated — the physical dials and buttons are large, tactile, and clearly labeled.

Skip it if you're a single-cup drinker (a pour-over or single-serve machine fits better), if you need a grinder integrated, or if you routinely brew and then don't drink for over an hour — the heat retention will disappoint you.

**FAQ**

**Is the permanent filter as good as paper?** Practically, yes. It lets through slightly more natural oils, which some coffee drinkers prefer for body. If you're sensitive to sediment, use paper #4 filters — they fit perfectly.

**Does the timer work if the power goes out?** No. Like most non-smart brewers, it resets to default after a power outage. You'll need to reprogram the time. Annoying, but rare.

**Can you brew half a carafe without issues?** Yes, but the showerhead distributes less evenly below 3 cups. For best results brewing small amounts, use a bit less coffee than the ratio suggests — the extraction runs slightly hotter on short cycles.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a no-nonsense, consistent drip brewer that makes genuinely good coffee, and you don't care about smart features. It's an honest workhorse at a fair price.

**Skip it if:** You need a grinder built-in, you're a solo drinker who brews single cups, or you expect piping-hot coffee after 60 minutes on the plate.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It doesn't try to be more than it is, and that's exactly why it works.
