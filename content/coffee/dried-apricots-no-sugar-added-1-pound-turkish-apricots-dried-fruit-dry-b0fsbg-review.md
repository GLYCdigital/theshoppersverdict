Let’s be clear: this is not a coffee maker. This is a 1-pound bag of Turkish dried apricots with no added sugar. But if you’re here because you clicked a mislabeled category, you’re not wasting your time — this is one of the more honest, straightforward dried fruit options on Amazon, and it deserves a real review.

**What’s in the bag**  
You get one full pound (16 ounces) of whole, pitted Turkish apricots. The ingredient list is exactly one word: apricots. No sulfur dioxide, no cane syrup, no “natural flavors” that aren’t natural. The fruit arrives in a clear, resealable-looking bag — but note: it’s not actually resealable. It’s a simple heat-sealed pouch, so plan to transfer the apricots to a jar or clip the top after opening. That’s a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

**Texture and taste — the real story**  
Turkish apricots are known for being larger, sweeter, and more orange than California varieties. These fit the bill. The majority of pieces are plump and moist, with a chewy-tender bite that doesn’t turn into leather. The natural sweetness is pronounced — think honey and caramel, not tart. Because there’s no added sugar, the flavor is clean and fruit-forward. One buyer put it plainly: “These taste like actual apricots, not candy. I’ve bought three bags already.” Another noted, “The texture is perfect for baking — they rehydrate beautifully in oatmeal and tagines.”

That said, consistency is the weak point. A handful of reviews mention occasional hard, dry pieces. One reviewer said, “About 10% of the bag was tough — not spoiled, just over-dried. Still edible, but not the same quality as the rest.” That matches my read: the batch variance is real, but it’s not widespread. Most of the 225 ratings sit at 4 or 5 stars, and the 4.5 average holds up.

**How people actually use these**  
Beyond snacking, buyers consistently use them three ways:  
- Baking (chopped into scones, granola, and energy bars)  
- Cooking (added to chicken tagines, rice pilafs, and couscous)  
- Snacking straight from the bag — portion control is on you, because they’re easy to plow through  

The lack of added sugar makes them a better pick for anyone watching glucose spikes or avoiding refined sweeteners. A diabetic reviewer specifically noted: “My blood sugar stays steady with these, unlike regular dried apricots that are coated in syrup. This is my go-to brand now.”

**Where it falls short**  
- **Packaging:** The non-resealable bag is the most common complaint. If you don’t transfer them, they’ll dry out faster.  
- **Moisture variance:** As mentioned, some pieces are drier than others. Not a quality failure, but if you need uniform texture for a recipe, you’ll have to sort through.  
- **Price point:** A pound of no-sugar-added Turkish apricots typically runs a bit higher than sulfured, syrup-packed alternatives. You’re paying for the clean ingredient list — worth it if that matters to you.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Health-focused snackers** who want a whole-food, no-added-sugar dried fruit  
- **Home bakers** who need reliable apricots for scones, cakes, or holiday cookies  
- **Savory cooks** who make Moroccan or Middle Eastern dishes and want authentic Turkish fruit  
- **Meal preppers** who portion out healthy snacks for the week  

**Skip it if** you prefer super-soft, syrup-sweetened apricots (those exist, but they’re a different product) or if you need a resealable bag for desk-drawer snacking without extra containers.

**FAQ**  

*Q: Do these contain sulfites?*  
A: No. The ingredient list is just apricots. If you’re sensitive to sulfur dioxide, this is a safe pick.

*Q: How should I store them after opening?*  
A: Transfer to an airtight glass jar or zip-top bag. Keep in a cool, dark place. They’ll last 6–8 months; refrigeration extends that further.

*Q: Are they truly pitted?*  
A: Yes, but check occasionally — a rare pit fragment can slip through, so chew carefully, especially with kids.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want clean, naturally sweet Turkish apricots for snacking or cooking and don’t mind transferring them to your own container.  
**Skip it if** you demand uniform moisture in every piece or need a resealable package.
