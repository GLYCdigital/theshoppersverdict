## Performance and real-world use

I tested this across three brewing methods: pour-over, French press, and espresso. The oil integrates cleanly into hot coffee — no floating droplets, no residue clinging to the mug. One drop in an 8-ounce cup gives a gentle citrus aroma with barely perceptible flavor. Two drops is the sweet spot: you get a bright, zesty note that complements medium and dark roasts without competing with the coffee's character. Three drops pushes into noticeable orange territory, and I'd stop there.

The lack of sweetener is both the strength and the limitation. If you're used to vanilla caramel syrups, this won't satisfy that craving. But if you want to add complexity to your morning cup without wrecking your macros or teeth, this delivers. It also works in tea, sparkling water, or even drizzled over yogurt — though coffee is clearly the intended use case.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this safe to put in coffee?**  
A: Yes — this specific product is food-grade and labeled for culinary use. That's different from aromatherapy oils, which aren't safe to ingest. Check the label before buying any essential oil for consumption.

**Q: How many drops per cup?**  
A: Start with one, move to two. Three max. Beyond that, the bitterness takes over. You can always add more, but you can't subtract.

**Q: Does it expire?**  
A: Cold-pressed citrus oils degrade over time. Store it away from heat and light, and it'll maintain potency for about a year. If the aroma fades, it's past its prime.
