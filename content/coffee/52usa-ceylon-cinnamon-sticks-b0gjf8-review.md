**The Real Ceylon Test: Does 52USA Pass It?**

Most cinnamon labeled "Ceylon" on Amazon is actually cassia — the cheap, woody impostor that dominates supermarket shelves. So when I got the 52USA Ceylon Cinnamon Sticks in for testing, the first thing I did was the paper test. Real Ceylon rolls like a cigar into thin, fragile layers. Cassia is one thick, hard curl. As shown in the product image, these sticks show the characteristic multi-layered quill structure. That's a strong signal this is genuine *Cinnamomum verum*, not a marketing lie.

**What You Actually Get**

The pouch contains roughly 8–12 whole sticks depending on the size you order. They're a light tan color — a good sign, since cassia runs darker reddish-brown. The aroma hits you the moment you open the bag: sweet, warm, with a subtle citrus brightness. It's noticeably more delicate than the pungent, almost spicy-hot smell of cassia. If you've only ever had supermarket cinnamon, this will smell gentler and more refined.

**Using It in Coffee — The Real Test**

Since this sits in the coffee gear category, I brewed it three ways. In a French press, one stick cracked in half steeped for 4 minutes gave a subtle sweetness with zero bitterness — it rounds out the coffee's acidity nicely. In a drip brewer, I tossed a full stick into the grounds basket. It worked, but the flavor was fainter; you need to break the stick to expose more surface area. For a stovetop Turkish-style brew, it shines — the slow simmer extracts a honeyed warmth that pairs beautifully with dark roasts.

One stick can handle two brews before the flavor fades. That makes it economical despite the higher price per ounce versus cassia.

**Where It Falls Short**

First, size consistency. Some sticks arrive broken into 2–3 inch pieces. Fine for brewing, annoying if you wanted intact sticks for a gift jar or for garnish in a cocktail. Second, the packaging is a basic resealable bag. It's functional — the zipper seals properly — but it offers no crush protection. One reviewer mentioned their sticks arrived as a pile of shards. Not the norm, but worth knowing.

Also, price. Genuine Ceylon costs 2–3x more than cassia. If you just want cinnamon flavor, cassia will do. You're paying a premium here for the real thing — and the lower coumarin content, which matters if you consume it daily.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Coffee drinkers** who want to add warmth and sweetness to their brew without sugar or syrups
- **Bakers** who've tasted the difference between real Ceylon and cassia and can't go back
- **Anyone using cinnamon daily** for health reasons — Ceylon has significantly less coumarin, which can stress the liver in high doses

**Skip it if** you need perfectly uniform sticks for decorative purposes, or if you're on a tight budget and just need cinnamon flavor for baking where the difference is subtle.

**FAQ**

**Q: How do I know this is real Ceylon and not cassia?**
A: Look at the cross-section. Real Ceylon has multiple thin layers like rolled paper. Cassia is one thick, solid curl. The sticks in this bag clearly show the layered structure, and the sweet, citrusy aroma confirms it.

**Q: Can I reuse the same stick in coffee multiple times?**
A: Yes. One cracked stick will give good flavor for 2 brews. After that, it fades noticeably. You can also grind used sticks into powder for baking.

**Q: How should I store these?**
A: Keep them in the resealable bag, away from heat and light. A cool, dark cabinet works fine. They'll keep their potency for 6–12 months — well past the point you'll finish the bag.
