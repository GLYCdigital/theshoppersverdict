Let's be honest: most "healthy" tortilla chips taste like cardboard dipped in regret. The Siete Taco Seasoning Maiz chips are not that. These strike a genuinely rare balance — they taste like a treat while keeping the ingredient list clean enough that you won't feel guilty finishing the bag.

## What You're Actually Getting

The 7.5-ounce bag contains lime-tinted corn chips dusted with a proprietary taco seasoning blend. The base is stone-ground white corn, but here's the twist: Siete swaps traditional frying oil for avocado oil and adds cassava flour to the mix. That combination gives the chips a slightly denser, more substantial crunch than standard restaurant-style tortilla chips.

As the product image shows, the seasoning is visible on every chip — flecks of chili, garlic, and onion powder coat the surface rather than settling at the bottom of the bag. That's not always the case with flavored chips, where you get three seasoned chips and the rest taste plain.

## How They Perform in Real-World Use

I tested these three ways: straight out of the bag, with fresh salsa, and with guacamole. The texture holds up admirably across all three. The chips are thick enough to scoop without shattering, yet they still crack cleanly when you bite — no stale, chewy toughness.

The taco seasoning itself is the star. It's savory with a mild chili warmth and a hint of lime that cuts through the richness of dips. I'd call the heat level a 3 out of 10 — noticeable but accessible. The flavor profile reminds me of the seasoning packet from a taco kit, but more refined and less salty. That said, salt fiends might find these slightly under-seasoned; the sodium content is notably lower than mainstream flavored chips.

## Where It Falls Short

The 7.5-ounce bag is smaller than it looks. Standard tortilla chip bags run 10-13 ounces, and this one disappears fast. At the premium price (typically $5-6 per bag), you're paying for quality, not quantity. If you're feeding a crowd or meal-prepping for a week, buy two bags.

The spice level is also worth flagging. While the taco seasoning is flavorful, it won't satisfy anyone looking for a spicy kick. The heat is more aromatic than aggressive — think mild enchilada sauce rather than hot salsa.

## FAQ

**Are these chips actually grain-free?**
Yes. The base is white corn (a grain, technically), but the blend includes cassava flour, and they're certified grain-free by Siete's standards. If you're strictly paleo, these fit the bill. If you're celiac, they're made in a gluten-free facility.

**How do they compare to regular Doritos?**
Different beast entirely. Doritos are corn chips with a dusting of processed cheese powder; these taste like actual taco seasoning — cumin, chili, garlic, onion. The texture is crunchier and less oily than Doritos.

**Do they stay fresh after opening?**
The bag has a resealable zipper, which helps. In testing, they stayed crisp for about four days after opening. Beyond that, they soften slightly — standard for any tortilla chip.
