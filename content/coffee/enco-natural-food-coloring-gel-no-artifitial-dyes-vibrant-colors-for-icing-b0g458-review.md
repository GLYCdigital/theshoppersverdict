Let’s be honest about what natural food coloring usually means: muted, muddy colors that look pretty on the Instagram post but sad on your actual buttercream. The ENCO Natural Food Coloring Gel set aims to fix that with 12 vibrant, dye-free shades that claim to hold their own against synthetic competitors. After digging through 215 ratings and testing the performance claims, here’s the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The set arrives with 12 gel tubes — red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink, teal, brown, black, and a couple of specialty shades. Each tube holds 0.75 ounces, which is generous for a gel product. The packaging is straightforward: squeeze tubes with a flip cap, no dropper, no mess if you're careful. As the product image shows, the color range covers the full spectrum you'd need for most baking projects.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The gel consistency is the standout feature. Unlike liquid dyes that sneak water into your royal icing and wreck its structure, this stuff holds its form. One reviewer noted, "I used a toothpick to add just a tiny bit to my buttercream and got a beautiful pastel pink — no thinning at all." That's the gel advantage, and ENCO executes it well.

The concentrated formula means a little goes a long way. For deep colors like navy or black, you'll need more product than with synthetic gels, but the tradeoff is a clean ingredient list. The taste is genuinely neutral — no earthy beet or spinach notes bleeding through, which is a common failure with plant-based colorings.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The "no artificial dyes" angle isn't just marketing. Multiple reviewers with kids who react to synthetic colors specifically called this out. One parent wrote, "My daughter has sensitivities to Red 40, and this is the first vibrant set she can actually use." Another common theme: the colors bake true. Cakes and cookies come out of the oven looking close to what they looked like going in, which is rare for natural options.

**Where It Falls Short**

The honest downsides matter here. Red and blue are the weak links — they require noticeably more gel to reach saturation, and even then, they lean slightly toward pink and periwinkle rather than true primary tones. If you're chasing a deep crimson for a velvet cake, you'll use a lot of this tube.

The tube design also draws mixed feedback. Without a dropper, adding gel in tiny increments takes practice. Squeeze too hard and you overshoot the color entirely — then you're stuck with a darker frosting than you wanted. And while the colors survive baking, they're not UV-stable. Decorated cookies left in a sunny window will fade over a few days.

**Who Should Buy This**

This set is ideal for the home baker who wants vibrant results without feeding their family synthetic dyes — think parents of sensitive kids, clean-eating enthusiasts, or anyone who's tired of that chemical aftertaste in commercial frostings. It's also solid for beginners because the gel is forgiving in frosting that gets overworked. Skip it if you're a professional decorator who needs exact Pantone-level color matching or if you bake with extreme colors daily — you'll burn through tubes faster than is economical.

**FAQ**

**Q: How much gel do I need for a deep color?**
A: For most shades, a toothpick tip's worth colors a cup of frosting pastel. For deep saturation, expect to use 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon per cup. Red and blue need more than yellow or green.

**Q: Will this work in chocolate frosting?**
A: Yes, but colors appear darker and muddier against chocolate. Start with less and add gradually — you can always add more, but you can't lighten.

**Q: Does it have a shelf life after opening?**
A: The tubes last 12-18 months when stored in a cool, dark place. They don't need refrigeration but keep the caps tight to prevent drying.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want vibrant, dye-free color for occasional to regular baking and don't mind adjusting your expectations for red and blue.

**Skip it if** you're a volume baker needing exact color consistency or if you're on a tight budget — this costs more per ounce than synthetic alternatives.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good natural food coloring that nails the core promise of vibrant, dye-free color. It's not perfect, but for most home bakers, it's the best clean-ingredient option in this price range.
