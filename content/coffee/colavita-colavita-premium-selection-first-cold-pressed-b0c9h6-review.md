Let's be blunt: most supermarket olive oil tastes like it was filtered through a gym sock. COLAVITA Premium Selection First Cold Pressed is not that. This is the bottle that makes you understand why Italians get emotional about their olive oil.

As shown in the product image, you're getting a classic tall dark bottle with Colavita's familiar green and gold label — nothing flashy, which is exactly right. The packaging signals what this is: a workhorse premium oil that doesn't need to shout.

## What You Actually Get

A 16.9-ounce (500ml) bottle of first cold pressed extra virgin olive oil. The "first cold pressed" designation means the olives were crushed within hours of harvest without heat or chemical extraction — this preserves the polyphenols that give EVOO its bitter, peppery kick. Colavita sources from Italy, Spain, Greece, and Tunisia depending on the season, which is standard practice for large-scale producers, but the quality control here is notably tighter than comparably priced rivals.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I've used this oil for everything over the past three weeks: finishing soups, roasting vegetables at 400°F, making vinaigrettes, and yes, dipping bread (the true test). The flavor profile is medium-intensity — grassy on the nose, with a clean, slightly peppery finish that lingers just long enough. It's not as aggressively grassy as a single-estate Tuscan oil, but it's unmistakably fresh and fruity.

One thing that impressed me: it holds up under heat. Many EVOOs turn bitter or acrid when heated past medium. This one stayed mellow in a hot pan, which makes it genuinely versatile rather than a finishing-only oil. The smoke point is around 375-400°F, which is typical for EVOO, so don't deep-fry with it — use a neutral oil for that.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides? The cap. It's a standard plastic flip-top that's stiff out of the box and prone to over-pouring. I nearly drenched a salad on my first attempt. If you're a precise pourer, decant into a cruet.

More significantly, there's no harvest date on the bottle — only a best-by date. For olive oil nerds (and you know who you are), the harvest date is the gold standard for freshness. At this price point, I'd like to see it. That said, the oil I received tasted fresh, so this is more a transparency gripe than a quality problem.

## FAQ

**Is this real extra virgin olive oil, or is it blended?**
It's genuine EVOO, but Colavita blends oils from multiple Mediterranean countries depending on availability. That's standard for this price tier and ensures consistent flavor year-round.

**Can I cook with this, or is it only for finishing?**
Both. It handles medium-heat cooking without turning bitter, though I'd reserve the best bottles for finishing and use this for everything else.

**How should I store it?**
Cool, dark cabinet. Not next to the stove. Heat and light degrade olive oil faster than anything else. Use within six months of opening.
