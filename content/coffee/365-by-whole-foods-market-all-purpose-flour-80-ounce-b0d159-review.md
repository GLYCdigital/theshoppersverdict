Let's be direct: most all-purpose flour is a commodity. You grab whatever's on the shelf, and it works. The 365 by Whole Foods Market All Purpose Flour, 80 Ounce, is different — not because it's flashy, but because it's *consistent*. And in baking, consistency is everything.

This is unbleached and unbromated flour, which matters if you care about what's actually in your food. Bleached flour uses chemical agents like chlorine dioxide to speed up aging; this skips all that. The protein content sits around 11.7%, which is the sweet spot for AP flour — high enough to give you structure in yeast breads, low enough to keep cakes and cookies tender.

**What you're actually getting**

An 80-ounce (5-pound) bag of flour. That's it — no scoop, no container, no frills. The bag itself is standard paper, which brings me to my first honest gripe: there's no zipper. If you don't transfer this to an airtight container within a week, you're inviting pantry moths and stale flour. Most reviewers mention this, and it's a fair point. My advice: buy a cheap 6-quart Cambro or use a large mason jar setup.

**Real-world performance**

I put this through the paces — sourdough loaves, pizza dough, chocolate chip cookies, and a batch of pie crust. The hydration behavior is predictable. It absorbs water at a normal rate, develops gluten reliably, and doesn't turn gummy when overworked. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"Switched from King Arthur to save money and honestly can't tell the difference in my bread. Same rise, same crumb."* That's the sentiment I keep seeing — people who expected a downgrade from premium brands found this performs at the same level.

The grind is consistent, too. No gritty particles, no uneven sifting. This matters for pastry work where texture is everything.

**Where it falls short**

The cons are straightforward. The paper bag is the biggest issue — a few buyers reported it arriving with small tears or holes, though the flour itself survived. It's also not organic, which some Whole Foods shoppers expect by default. If organic is non-negotiable for you, look elsewhere. And while the 80-ounce size is a value play, it's not *huge* savings — you're looking at roughly 15-20% less per pound than buying two standard 5-pound bags. Worth it, but not a game-changer.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home bakers** who go through flour weekly and want consistent protein content
- **Budget-conscious cooks** who want unbleached flour without the King Arthur price tag
- **Bread makers** who need reliable gluten development for sourdough or yeasted loaves

Skip it if you bake once a month, need organic certification, or don't want to deal with transferring flour to a storage container.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this flour good for sourdough?**
A: Yes. The 11.7% protein gives you enough gluten strength for good oven spring. Hydrate at 70-75% and you'll get a nice, open crumb.

**Q: How long does it stay fresh in the bag?**
A: About 1-2 months if you keep it sealed with a clip in a cool, dry place. For longer storage, transfer to an airtight container or freeze it — flour freezes well for up to 6 months.

**Q: Can I use this for pasta making?**
A: It'll work, but you'll get better results with 00 flour or semolina. This is a generalist, not a specialist.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you bake regularly and want dependable, unbleached flour at a fair price. **Skip it if** you need organic, hate transferring flour to containers, or only bake a few times a year.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not flashy, but it's honest, reliable flour that performs like brands costing 30% more. For 80 ounces of consistent quality, that's a solid deal.
