# Liquid Death Convicted Melon Review: The Devil's in the Details

Liquid Death has built a cult following on one simple premise: make water feel dangerous. The tallboy cans scream metal album art, the brand leans hard into satire, and people either love it or roll their eyes. But strip away the skulls and the "murder your thirst" tagline — what's actually in the can?

I tested Convicted Melon, the flavored sparkling water with agave, across several days and multiple cans. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You're Actually Getting

Each 19.2-ounce tallboy contains sparkling water, natural melon flavor, and 5 grams of agave sugar per can. That's the full ingredient list — nothing artificial, no stevia aftertaste, no preservatives. The can is infinitely recyclable aluminum, which is genuinely better for the environment than plastic bottles.

The packaging is where Liquid Death earns its reputation. As shown in the product image, the Convicted Melon can features bold green and black graphics with a menacing melon character. It looks sharp in a fridge, and yes, it's a conversation starter. But you're not drinking the can.

## Performance and Taste

Here's where Convicted Melon surprised me. The carbonation is **aggressive** — sharper than LaCroix or Polar. It hits your tongue with real bite, which makes it feel more like a soda replacement than a wimpy sparkling water. That intensity holds up too. I left a can open for 25 minutes and it still had noticeable fizz, which is rare in this category.

The melon flavor is the star. It tastes like ripe honeydew, not the artificial "candy melon" you get from cheaper brands. There's a subtle sweetness from the agave that rounds out the acidity — it's not cloying, but it's definitely sweeter than plain sparkling water. If you're used to zero-sugar options, you'll notice the difference immediately.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Amazon reviewers at 4.3 stars (215 ratings) largely echo my experience. One verified buyer wrote: *"Finally a sparkling water that doesn't taste like TV static. The melon is actually melon, not perfume."* Another noted: *"My kids think it's soda. I let them think that. It's basically juice with bubbles."*

The most common praise centers on the carbonation strength and the genuine fruit flavor. Multiple reviewers mention using it as a mixer for cocktails — the agave sweetness plays well with tequila and vodka.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**Price is the biggest hurdle.** At roughly $2.50 per can (depending on the seller), you're paying a significant premium. A 12-pack of LaCroix costs about the same as 8 of these. You're paying for the brand, the can design, and the agave. Whether that's worth it depends entirely on your budget.

**The branding gimmick can get old.** The "murder your thirst" schtick is clever for about three cans. After that, you realize you're drinking water with a bit of sugar and melon flavor — and the can is doing a lot of heavy lifting.

**Inconsistent carbonation.** A few reviewers reported flat cans within the same case. My experience was generally positive, but it's worth noting that quality control isn't perfect. If you're buying a case, there's a chance one or two cans will disappoint.

## Who Should Buy This

**Get it if:**
- You're a sparkling water enthusiast who finds LaCroix too weak
- You want a soda alternative that still has some sweetness
- You're hosting a party and want drinks that look interesting in the cooler
- You mix cocktails and want a flavored sparkling water with actual flavor density

**Skip it if:**
- You're on a tight grocery budget
- You prefer zero-calorie drinks (the 5g of sugar per can adds up)
- You're sensitive to aggressive carbonation
- You don't care about packaging aesthetics

## FAQ

**Q: Is this actually healthier than soda?**
A: Yes, significantly. It has 5g of sugar per can versus 39g in a can of Coke. But it's not sugar-free — if you're watching sugar intake, this isn't a zero-calorie option.

**Q: Does the agave make it taste like a sweet tea or juice?**
A: No. The agave adds mild sweetness that balances the melon tartness, but it's still clearly a sparkling water. It's closer to a lightly sweetened seltzer than a juice drink.

**Q: Is this carbonated enough to use as a mixer?**
A: Absolutely. The aggressive bubbles hold up well against ice and liquor. This is actually one of its best uses — it's a better tonic alternative than most sparkling waters.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a bold, genuinely flavorful sparkling water that feels like a treat rather than a compromise. The agave sweetness, aggressive carbonation, and legit melon flavor justify the premium price — if you're not counting pennies.

**Skip it if** you're happy with $0.50 seltzer or you need zero-sugar options. The branding markup is real, and you might find yourself annoyed that water costs as much as craft soda.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's expensive, and the gimmick can grate. But the product inside the can is genuinely good. Liquid Death isn't just a marketing stunt; it's a legitimately well-made sparkling water that happens to wear a leather jacket.

If you're curious, buy a single can first before committing to a case. Your taste buds — and your wallet — will tell you the rest.
