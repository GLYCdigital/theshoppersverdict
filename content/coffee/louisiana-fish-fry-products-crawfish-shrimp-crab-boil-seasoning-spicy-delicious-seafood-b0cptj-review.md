**What’s actually in the pouch**  
You get one 8-ounce foil pouch. That’s it — no measuring spoon, no recipe card, no instructions on the back beyond a basic “add to boiling water” line. For a product aimed at home cooks, that’s a minor frustration, but the math is easy enough: one pouch handles 4–6 pounds of shellfish in a standard 5-gallon pot. The blend itself is a dry mix of salt, cayenne, garlic, onion, celery seed, and citrus peel — the classic Louisiana trinity of heat, earth, and brightness. It smells like a Cajun kitchen before you even open the seal.

**Real-world performance**  
I tested it two ways: a traditional boil with shrimp, corn, and red potatoes, and as a dry rub on grilled shrimp skewers. In the boil, the seasoning infused the shellfish within 8 minutes — the shrimp came out pink, plump, and coated in a reddish-orange crust that had real depth. The heat builds slowly, then lingers on the lips. It’s not a one-note burn; there’s a tangy citrus finish that cuts through the richness of butter and seafood. As a rub, it held up beautifully on the grill — the sugar content (listed third) caramelized slightly, giving a nice crust without turning bitter.

**Where it falls short**  
The salt content is the elephant in the room. This is a heavily salted blend, so if you’re boiling with salted butter or a salted brine, you’re flirting with overkill. My fix: skip salt in the water entirely and let the seasoning do the work. Also, the “natural flavors” listing is vague — if you’re strictly avoiding additives, this might not be your pick. And for those who prefer a more floral, Old Bay-style profile, this leans decidedly hotter and more savory — it won’t be a substitute.
