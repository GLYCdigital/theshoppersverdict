# The Serious Baker's Yeast: Caputo Lievito Dry Yeast Review

Let's get one thing straight: this isn't the yeast you grab for a Sunday morning cinnamon roll emergency. Antimo Caputo's Lievito Active Dry Yeast is the choice of people who plan dough days in advance — the ones who talk about hydration percentages and autolyse times. And for that specific baker, this 1.1-pound pack is a quiet workhorse.

## What You're Actually Getting

The package contains 500 grams (1.1 lbs) of active dry yeast in a vacuum-sealed foil bag. Inside the box, you'll find just that — no scoop, no clip, no storage container. The yeast itself is a fine, pale beige powder that's noticeably more finely milled than grocery-store brands like Fleischmann's or Red Star. That finer grind matters: it dissolves into your dough water without needing to be "proofed" or bloomed first, which saves a step and reduces the chance of killing the yeast with water that's too hot.

## Performance in Real Dough

Here's where Caputo earns its reputation. This yeast is bred for long, cold fermentation — the kind of slow proof that develops the complex, slightly tangy flavor you want in Neapolitan-style pizza or artisan bread. At 0.1% to 0.5% hydration (that's a fraction of a gram for a standard dough), it reliably carries a dough through 24 to 48 hours in the refrigerator without over-proofing or collapsing. The activity curve is predictable, which is what professionals value.

One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"I use this for 48-hour cold ferment pizza dough. It's consistent every single time — my crust has never been better."* Another noted, *"I was skeptical about switching from instant yeast, but this is actually easier to work with. It doesn't need to be dissolved separately."*

That consistency is the real story. Caputo's yeast is manufactured in Italy and packaged under modified atmosphere, so the activity level is uniform from batch to batch — something you can't always count on with commodity yeast brands.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest practical gripe is the packaging. There's no resealable zipper on this bag. Once you open that vacuum seal, you need to fold the top over and clip it — and if you're using yeast only weekly, you'll want to transfer it to an airtight jar immediately. The foil bag isn't designed for repeated access.

The second issue is scale. This is a lot of yeast for a home baker. Active dry yeast loses roughly 50% of its potency over 12 months, even when stored properly. Unless you're making pizza dough weekly or baking bread daily, you'll likely toss a portion of this pack. That's a waste of money, even at this competitive price per gram.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're making Neapolitan-style pizza at home with 24-hour proofing, maintaining a sourdough starter that needs occasional backup, or baking bread multiple times per week. The value per gram is excellent — it's roughly half the cost of buying individual 3-strip packets from the grocery store.

**Skip it if** you bake occasionally or follow same-day recipes. Standard instant yeast from the supermarket will serve you fine, and you won't be throwing away stale product.

## Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Is this the same as instant yeast?**
No. It's active dry yeast — it has a slightly larger granule size than instant, though Caputo's is finer than most. In practice, you can use it interchangeably in most recipes without blooming, just reduce the amount slightly (about 20% less than you'd use of instant).

**Q: How should I store it after opening?**
Transfer the unused portion to an airtight container and keep it in the freezer. It'll maintain potency for up to a year. Don't leave it in the opened foil bag at room temperature — it'll lose strength in weeks.

**Q: Can I use this for sourdough starter maintenance?**
Yes, with a caveat. It's fine for reviving a sluggish starter or as a backup, but it won't give you the wild yeast complexity you get from natural fermentation alone.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐½**

The Antimo Caputo Lievito Active Dry Yeast is a precision tool for a specific job — long-fermentation doughs that reward patience. It performs flawlessly, dissolves easily, and delivers consistent results batch after batch. The packaging is the sole real weakness, and the pack size is overkill for casual bakers.

**Buy it if** you're serious about pizza or bread and want reproducible, professional-grade fermentation. **Skip it if** you're a weekend-only baker who'd be better served by a small jar of instant yeast.

For the dedicated dough maker, this is the best value in yeast on the market — just bring your own jar.
