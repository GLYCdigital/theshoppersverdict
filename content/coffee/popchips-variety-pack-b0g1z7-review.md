**Is this the snack variety pack that actually earns a permanent spot in your pantry? I tested all four flavors over three weeks to find out.**

Here's the honest truth: most variety packs are a scam. You get one good flavor, two mediocre ones, and a bag of something nobody touches. The Popchips variety pack avoids that trap — mostly. With 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly doing something right. But let's dig into whether it's worth your money.

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting 20 individual 0.8-ounce bags: five each of Sea Salt, Barbecue, Sour Cream & Onion, and Cheddar. That's roughly 160 calories per bag, which makes portion control automatic — no standing over a family-size bag wondering where the last third went. The packaging is straightforward Mylar-style bags that reseal poorly if you don't finish them, but at this size, you will.

**Performance and real-world use**

Popchips aren't fried or baked — they're pressure-popped, which gives them a distinctly different texture. They're lighter and airier than kettle chips but with more crunch than you'd expect. The Sea Salt flavor is the baseline — clean, salty, and dangerously easy to plow through. It's the flavor I kept reaching for during week two.

The Cheddar has that artificial-cheese powder coating that'll either transport you back to childhood or make you check the ingredient list twice. It's the most polarizing of the four. The Sour Cream & Onion is solid but suffers from seasoning inconsistency — one bag had visible clumps of powder, another was practically naked.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The portion control and crunch factor come up repeatedly in reviews. One buyer noted, "These are my go-to for work lunches — I can grab one bag and not feel like I need to eat the whole thing." Another praised the texture specifically: "Finally a chip that's actually crunchy but doesn't leave my hands covered in grease."

**Where it falls short**

The BBQ flavor is the weak link. It leans heavily sweet — closer to honey barbecue than the smoky, tangy profile most BBQ chip fans expect. If you're buying this pack specifically for the BBQ, you'll be disappointed.

The price-per-ounce math also doesn't favor this pack. You're paying a convenience premium for the single-serve format. If you're just looking to snack at home, buying a full-size bag of your favorite flavor and portioning it yourself will save you roughly 30–40%.

**Who should buy this**

The Popchips variety pack is ideal for lunchbox packing, office snacks, road trips, or anyone trying to keep portions in check without giving up crunchy snacks entirely. It's also a smart choice if you're hosting and want to offer variety without committing to multiple full-size bags. Skip it if you're a strict budget shopper or if you're specifically craving traditional fried-chip texture — these won't scratch that itch.

**FAQ**

**Are Popchips actually healthier than regular chips?**
They're lower in fat than fried chips — about 40% less per serving — and they're gluten-free. But they're still processed snack food with sodium. They're a reasonable swap, not a health food.

**How long do these stay fresh after opening the box?**
The individual bags keep them fresh for months if stored properly. Once you open a bag, finish it — at 0.8 ounces, that's not hard.

**Can I buy just one flavor instead of the variety?**
Yes, Popchips sells single-flavor packs on Amazon, but the variety pack is usually the better value per bag if you want multiple flavors.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want portable, portion-controlled crunchy snacks with genuine flavor variety, or you're stocking a lunchbox or office drawer.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight snack budget, hate sweet-leaning BBQ flavors, or expect the dense crunch of traditional fried chips.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The portion control and crunch factor earn top marks, but the BBQ flavor and inconsistent seasoning in the Sour Cream & Onion bags keep it from perfection. It's a solid, repeatable purchase for snackers who value convenience over cost-per-ounce.
