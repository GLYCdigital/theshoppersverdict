## What's Actually in the Can

You get a standard 6-ounce can of small, pitted California-grown black olives packed in water, salt, and ferrous gluconate (that's what keeps them black — it's safe, just so you know). The "small" designation means roughly 150-180 olives per can, which matters more than you'd think. These aren't the giant Texas-sized olives that hog a salad bowl; they're the workhorse size that spreads evenly across a supreme pizza or tuna salad.

## How They Perform

The first thing you'll notice cracking open the can: these olives have structure. They're not the soft, almost mealy texture you get from some bargain brands. Early California uses a firming process that keeps the flesh snappy — you get a clean bite, not a squish. The brine is mild, with a gentle saltiness that doesn't scream "preservative." One buyer noted, "Finally, black olives that don't taste like they've been sitting in a warehouse for years. These taste fresh and have a nice bite to them."

The small size is actually the smart play here. On a taco salad, you get olive in every other forkful without one bite being all olive. For pizza, they distribute evenly across the surface rather than clustering. If you're making olive tapenade or chopping for pasta salads, the smaller starting size means less knife work.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. Small olives mean more sorting and prep if your recipe calls for larger pieces. You'll get the occasional bruised or slightly soft olive mixed in — probably one or two per can. It's not a quality failure, just the nature of the product, but worth knowing before you buy.

The bigger annoyance: there's no resealable lid. You're working with a standard pull-tab can, so if you don't use the whole thing at once, you need your own container. Also, if you specifically want olives for martinis or charcuterie boards where size matters visually, these aren't it. Those big, dramatic olives you see at bars? Different product.

## FAQ

**Are these actually ripe olives?**
Yes — "ripe" here means they've been fully cured and darkened. They're California-style black olives (the type you grew up seeing on pizza), not Kalamata or green olives. That's exactly what they should be.

**Do these need refrigeration after opening?**
Yes. Transfer to a covered container and refrigerate. They'll keep about 2 weeks in brine. Any longer and the texture degrades.

**Are these good for cooking?**
Excellent. The firm texture holds up well in lasagna, casseroles, and baked dishes where softer olives would disintegrate. One reviewer specifically praised them for "holding shape in a baked chicken casserole."
