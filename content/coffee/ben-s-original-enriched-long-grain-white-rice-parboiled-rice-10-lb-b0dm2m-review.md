Let’s be clear about what this is: BEN'S ORIGINAL Enriched Long Grain White Rice is parboiled rice, not your standard white rice. That distinction matters more than most shoppers realize.

Parboiling means the rice is steam-pressured while still in its hull, which drives B-vitamins from the bran into the endosperm before milling. The result? A grain that’s nutritionally closer to brown rice but cooks with white rice’s speed and neutrality. What lands in your pot is a firmer, more separate grain that doesn’t clump into a sticky mass.

**What’s in the bag**

You’re getting a 10-pound poly bag of long-grain parboiled rice — roughly 40 servings. It’s enriched with iron, niacin, thiamine, and folic acid, which is standard but worth noting if you’re feeding a family. The bag itself is sturdy with a zip-close top, though you’ll likely transfer to a canister anyway since the resealable track isn’t the most durable under repeated use.

**How it actually performs**

I tested this using the standard 2:1 water-to-rice ratio with a pinch of salt. Bring to a boil, cover, reduce to low, and let it simmer for 25 minutes. The grains came out distinct and al dente — think pilaf texture rather than sushi rice. It absorbed water evenly with no scorching on the pot bottom, and the aroma was clean and neutral.

The real win here is forgiveness. Standard white rice turns to paste if you forget it on the burner. This parboiled version held on warm for a full 15 minutes and still fluffed with a fork without clumping. That’s a genuine advantage for busy cooks.

One buyer noted: *“I switched from regular white rice and the texture took getting used to, but now I prefer it — it holds up in fried rice and leftovers reheat without turning to glue.”* Another mentioned: *“This is the only rice my husband will eat with beans. It doesn’t get mushy.”*

**Where it falls short**

The texture is the dividing line. Parboiled rice is firmer and chewier — almost pasta-like in bite. If your frame of reference is soft, fluffy long-grain like jasmine or standard white, this will feel dense. Several reviews on Amazon echo this: *“It’s not what I expected. Too hard in the middle,”* wrote one verified purchaser. That’s not a defect; it’s the nature of parboiled rice. But it’s the #1 reason some buyers return it.

The 25-minute cook time is also a real tradeoff. When you’re rushing dinner, those extra 10 minutes matter. And while the 10-pound size is economical, it’s a commitment — if you don’t like the texture, you’re stuck with a huge bag.

**Who should buy this**

This is for anyone who makes rice ahead for meal prep, cooks for a household that eats rice multiple times weekly, or needs a rice that survives reheating without degrading. It’s also a smart pick if you want some of brown rice’s nutritional edge without the 45-minute cook time or earthy flavor.

Skip it if you’re a texture purist who wants classic fluffy white rice, or if you only cook rice occasionally and prefer the convenience of 90-second microwave pouches.

**FAQs**

*Does this rice need rinsing before cooking?*  
No. Unlike standard white rice, parboiled rice doesn’t have excess surface starch. Rinsing is optional and won’t hurt, but it’s unnecessary.

*Can I use a rice cooker?*  
Yes. Use the same 2:1 ratio and select the white rice setting. You may need to add 5-10 minutes to the cycle since parboiled grains absorb water more slowly.

*Is parboiled rice healthier than regular white rice?*  
Marginally. The steaming process forces more B-vitamins into the grain, and it has a slightly lower glycemic index than standard white rice. It’s not brown rice, but it’s a reasonable middle ground.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** you prioritize rice that holds its structure through leftovers, stir-fries, and busy weeknights, and you don’t mind a firmer bite.

**Skip it if:** you want soft, fluffy white rice and a shorter cook time.
