Let’s cut through the noise: most sugar-free sweeteners fail the coffee test. Powdered stevia clumps, sucralose leaves a metallic ghost on your tongue, and monk fruit can cost more than your beans. The NuNaturals Stevia Syrup takes a different approach — it’s a liquid, plant-based sweetener designed to dissolve cleanly into both hot pour-overs and iced cold brews. With 210 ratings averaging 4.2 stars, it’s clearly working for many, but it’s not a sugar twin. Here’s the full story.

**What You Actually Get**

The bottle arrives as a 16-ounce (473 mL) squeeze-top container with a pump mechanism. As shown in the product image, it’s a straightforward, no-nonsense design — clear labeling, no fancy branding. The syrup itself is a thick, amber-colored liquid made from stevia leaf extract, water, and natural flavors. No erythritol, no sugar alcohols, no artificial fillers. For keto, paleo, or diabetic shoppers, that ingredient list alone sets it apart from most competitors.

One pump delivers roughly 1/2 teaspoon of sweetness equivalent to about 2 teaspoons of sugar. The pump is convenient, but here’s a practical tip: store the bottle upright, and give it a firm press. The syrup is viscous, and a lazy half-press will give you a weak, watery squirt that under-sweetens your cup.

**Real-World Performance in Coffee**

I tested this in three scenarios: black drip coffee, iced lattes, and cold brew. In hot coffee, the syrup dissolved within seconds — no stirring marathon, no gritty sediment. That’s the biggest win here. In iced drinks, it mixed cleanly without sinking to the bottom, which is a common failure point for granulated sweeteners.

The taste is where opinions split. The syrup has a clean, upfront sweetness with a very slight cooling or herbal note in the finish — characteristic of high-purity stevia extracts. It’s not the same mouthfeel as sugar; it doesn’t add body or caramel notes. But for a zero-calorie option, it’s notably cleaner than the bitter, chemical aftertaste you get from aspartame-based syrups. One reviewer noted, “I’ve tried every liquid stevia on Amazon, and this one has the least aftertaste by a mile. It’s the only one I use in my morning pour-over.” Another countered, “It’s sweet, but it’s not sugar. If you’re looking for a 1:1 replacement, you’ll be disappointed.”

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the ratings, three themes dominate. First, versatility — people use it in coffee, tea, oatmeal, and even cocktails. Second, the clean ingredient profile — no maltodextrin or dextrose, which are common hidden carbs in powdered sweeteners. Third, value — a single bottle lasts 2–3 months with daily use, making it cheaper per serving than single-serve sweetener packets.

**Where It Falls Short**

The texture is the main friction point. The syrup is thick, and if you leave the pump’s tip uncleaned, it can crystallize slightly and clog. Some users also report that the pump dispenses too much or too little inconsistently depending on how the bottle is tilted. And let’s be honest — the flavor won’t fool anyone. It’s stevia-forward. If you hate the taste of stevia in any form, this won’t convert you.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Keto and low-carb dieters** who want a liquid sweetener that won’t spike blood sugar and doesn’t contain sugar alcohols (which cause GI distress for some).
- **Iced coffee drinkers** who are tired of powder clumping in cold drinks.
- **Households with multiple sweetener users** — the pump makes portion control easy for everyone.

Skip it if you’re strictly looking for a sugar replacement in baking (the syrup’s liquid form throws off dry ingredient ratios) or if you already know you dislike stevia’s distinct flavor profile.

**FAQ**

*Q: Can I use this in baking instead of sugar?*  
A: Yes, but adjust liquids down by about 1/4 cup per 1 cup of sugar replaced. The syrup adds moisture, so reduce other wet ingredients to avoid a gummy texture. For simple syrups or sauces, it works perfectly as-is.

*Q: Does this need refrigeration after opening?*  
A: No, but keep it away from direct heat and sunlight. Stored at room temperature, it has a long shelf life. If you live in a hot climate, refrigerating it can make the syrup too thick to pump easily.

*Q: How does it compare to liquid sucralose or Splenda?*  
A: It’s less bitter and has a more rounded sweetness. Sucralose tends to have a lingering metallic finish, while this stevia syrup has a cleaner, faster onset of sweetness. The tradeoff is the subtle herbal note unique to stevia.

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 4.2/5**

For everyone else, this is a solid, pump-friendly upgrade over gritty powders and chemical-laden liquid sweeteners. It earns its 4.2-star rating honestly — a functional, no-fuss product with one clear compromise on flavor authenticity.
