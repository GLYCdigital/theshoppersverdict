Let me be blunt: most matcha sold on Amazon is garbage. It's dull brownish-green, tastes like grass clippings, and turns bitter the second you look at it wrong. So when a 4.4-star organic ceremonial grade shows up, I'm skeptical but hopeful. After testing Crafti's offering for two weeks across different preparations, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The pouch contains 30 grams of matcha powder — the standard ceremonial size that lasts about 15-20 servings if you're using the traditional 2-gram scoop. The packaging is a matte, resealable pouch with a nitrogen-flush seal. As shown in the product image, it's opaque, which matters more than most people realize: light exposure degrades matcha's chlorophyll and amino acids rapidly. Crafti got this right.

The powder itself is a striking, vivid emerald green — the kind of color that tells you the tea plants were shade-grown for 20+ days before harvest. It's stone-ground to an ultra-fine consistency. When you open the pouch, you get that sweet, vegetal aroma that signals fresh ceremonial grade, not the hay-like smell of culinary grade.

**Performance and real-world use**

I tested this three ways: traditional usucha (thin tea) with just hot water and a whisk, a latte with oat milk, and cold-brewed overnight.

For usucha, this is genuinely smooth. At 175°F water, there's no astringency — just a clean, buttery finish with a natural sweetness. The umami comes through without any of the fishy or seaweed notes cheaper matchas develop. It holds up well even if you're a minute slow with the whisk, which is a real advantage for beginners.

As a latte, it integrates beautifully. No grit, no settling, no bitter aftertaste cutting through the milk. Cold-brewing overnight produces a surprisingly sweet, refreshing drink that doesn't need sweetener.

The one workflow complaint: this powder is so fine it clumps if you don't sift it first. A fine-mesh strainer or a proper bamboo whisk is non-negotiable. Skip either and you'll be fishing out lumps.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 220 ratings at 4.4 stars tell a consistent story. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've tried every matcha on Amazon under $40 and this is the first one that doesn't make me wince. The color is unreal — like pure green velvet."* Another noted: *"My barista friend tasted it blind and thought it was a $60 brand."*

The most common praise centers on lack of bitterness and the vibrant color. Several reviewers mention repurchasing, which is usually the strongest signal of genuine satisfaction.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest gap: no harvest date on the package. Matcha is perishable — it peaks within about six months of harvest. Without a harvest date, you're gambling on whether you're getting spring 2026 or something that's been sitting in a warehouse. Crafti doesn't disclose this on the listing, which is frustrating for a premium product.

The price is also a consideration. At roughly $0.70-$0.90 per gram depending on current pricing, it's positioned above budget-friendly options like Jade Leaf but below ultra-premium imports like Ippodo. You're paying for the convenience of Amazon delivery and organic certification, which has value, but it's not a bargain.

**Who should buy this**

- **Matcha beginners** who want a forgiving, non-bitter introduction without buying a $50 tin blind
- **Latte drinkers** who want a clean, grassy-free base that actually tastes like tea
- **Anyone tired of bitter, dull matcha** from grocery stores and willing to pay a small premium for quality

**Skip it if** you're a matcha purist who demands harvest-date transparency, or you're on a tight budget and would rather get a larger quantity of decent culinary grade for cooking.

**FAQ**

**Is this actually ceremonial grade?**
Visually and in taste, yes. The color, texture, and complete lack of bitterness match what you'd expect from proper ceremonial grade. That said, without harvest-date disclosure, you can't verify it's peak-season tea.

**How much caffeine does it have?**
About 30-40mg per 2-gram serving — roughly a third of a cup of coffee. The L-theanine content provides a calm, focused energy rather than a jolt.

**Does it need a bamboo whisk?**
Technically no, but practically yes. A regular whisk or frother works, but you'll get better suspension and fewer clumps with a proper chasen. Sifting first is non-negotiable either way.
