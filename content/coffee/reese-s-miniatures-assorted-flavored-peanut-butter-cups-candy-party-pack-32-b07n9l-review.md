Let's be direct: this is a candy review, not a coffee gear review, despite what the category says. But if you're here, you already know REESE'S makes some of the most reliable peanut butter cups on the market. The question is whether this assorted miniatures can justifies your money versus just grabbing a standard bag. After testing a full canister over two weeks, here's the honest breakdown.

**What's actually in the can**

You get a roughly 13-ounce resealable plastic canister filled with miniature peanut butter cups in three varieties: classic milk chocolate, white chocolate, and dark chocolate. The minis are about the size of a quarter and come individually wrapped in foil — which matters more than you'd think. The individual wrapping keeps them from sticking together in warm weather and makes them easy to toss in a lunchbox or purse without creating a melted mess. The canister itself is sturdy and re-seals with a satisfying snap, not a flimsy pop-top that breaks after three opens.

**How they actually taste**

The milk chocolate version tastes exactly like the full-size cups — that salty-sweet peanut butter filling with the familiar chocolate shell. Nothing new, but it's consistent. The dark chocolate is the standout. It's genuinely dark, not that weakly "dark-ish" chocolate most brands use, and it balances the sweet peanut butter better than the original. The white chocolate version is where things get divisive. It's very sweet — almost candy-bar sweet — and the peanut butter gets somewhat lost underneath. If you have a serious sweet tooth, you'll love them. If you prefer balanced flavors, you'll find yourself leaving them at the bottom of the can.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The variety is the main selling point, and reviewers agree. One buyer noted, "My kids love that they can grab a different flavor each time." Another said, "The dark chocolate ones alone are worth the price." The resealable canister also gets repeated mentions — people appreciate that the cups don't go stale after a week, which is a real problem with bagged candies.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of a flavor key is genuinely annoying. The foil wrappers look identical across all three varieties, so you're playing roulette every time you grab one. If you don't like white chocolate, you can't pick around them without unwrapping each piece. Also, the minis arrive loose in the can, and Amazon shipping isn't gentle. Several reviewers (and my own order) had cracked or broken cups — they still taste fine, but they look rough. Finally, the can looks bigger than it is; the cups only fill about two-thirds of the container, so don't expect a massive quantity.

**Who should buy this**

This is perfect for households with mixed preferences — one person likes dark, one likes milk, the kids want white. It's also great for office candy bowls or party platters where variety matters more than volume. If you only eat one specific REESE'S flavor, skip this and buy a standard bag instead — you'll get more of what you actually want for the same price.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Are these the same size as regular REESE'S Miniatures?*
Yes, they're the same miniature size — about half the size of a standard cup. The difference is purely the flavor variety.

*Do the white chocolate ones taste like white chocolate or just sugar?*
They taste like white chocolate, but it's a sweet white chocolate. The peanut butter flavor is muted compared to the milk and dark versions.

*How long do they stay fresh in the canister?*
Properly sealed, they stay fresh for 3-4 weeks. After that, the chocolate can develop a slight bloom (white film) but still tastes fine.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want variety without buying three separate bags, or if you're serving a crowd with different tastes. **Skip it if** you're loyal to one flavor or want maximum quantity for your dollar.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It loses half a star for the flavor roulette issue and the broken pieces in transit, but the taste quality and canister design make it a solid buy for most households.
