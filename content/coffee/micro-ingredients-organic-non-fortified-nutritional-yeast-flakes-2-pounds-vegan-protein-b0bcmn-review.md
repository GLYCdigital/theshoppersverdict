Let's be clear about something upfront: this is not your standard nutritional yeast. Micro Ingredients sells an organic, **non-fortified** version, which puts it in a smaller category than the fortified stuff most shoppers grab. That distinction matters more than you might think — and it's the reason this review exists.

## What you actually get

The product image shows a straightforward 2-pound stand-up pouch. Inside are fine, pale-gold flakes — noticeably smaller and thinner than Bragg's larger flakes. The bag is resealable, which is good because two pounds is a lot of nooch. There's no scoop included, no spoon, just the yeast. One 2-pound bag equals roughly 96 tablespoons, so at typical usage (1–2 tbsp per meal), it'll last a couple of months even in a heavy household.

## How it performs in real use

I tested this three ways: sprinkled on popcorn, stirred into pasta sauce, and blended into a cashew-based "cheese" sauce.

**Texture:** The fine grind is actually an advantage here. It dissolves faster than thicker flakes, which means fewer gritty bits in sauces. It also clings better to popcorn and roasted vegetables — less falls to the bottom of the bowl. One reviewer put it simply: *"It's like powder-fine, so it mixes into everything without that chalky texture you get from some brands."*

**Taste:** Here's the honest tradeoff. Because it's non-fortified, you're missing the synthetic vitamins that give fortified yeasts their sharp, almost tangy, "cheesy" punch. This one is more savory and umami-forward, with a milder, earthier finish. If you're using it as a cheese replacement, you'll likely need to add salt and perhaps a touch of garlic powder to hit that flavor profile. But if you want a subtle, clean nutritional boost, it's actually more versatile — it won't overpower other ingredients.

**Performance in cooking:** It thickened my sauce nicely without clumping, and in the cashew cheese it blended completely smooth. No complaints there.

## Where it falls short

Be honest with yourself about the non-fortified aspect. If you're vegan, you need B12. This product has **zero B12** — it's not added, so you must get it elsewhere. That's not a flaw if you know what you're buying, but it's a dealbreaker for some.

The packaging is functional but not premium. The zipper seal holds, but if you live somewhere humid, flakes will clump over time. Transfer to a glass jar if that's you.

Also, the earthy flavor profile won't fool anyone into thinking it's parmesan. It's its own thing.

## FAQ

**Is non-fortified nutritional yeast healthier than fortified?**
Not inherently. Fortified versions add synthetic B vitamins (including B12). Non-fortified has only what the yeast naturally produces — which is B-complex except B12. "Healthier" depends on your diet. If you eat meat or take a B12 supplement, non-fortified is fine. If you're fully plant-based, you need B12 from somewhere.

**How does this compare to Bragg's?**
Bragg's is fortified, has larger flakes, and a stronger cheesy flavor. Micro Ingredients is finer, milder, and cleaner. If you want the classic "cheese" substitute, Bragg's wins. If you want a clean, subtle yeast for cooking, this is better.

**Can I use this for baking?**
Not for leavening. It's deactivated yeast — no rising power. Use it for flavor and nutrition only.
