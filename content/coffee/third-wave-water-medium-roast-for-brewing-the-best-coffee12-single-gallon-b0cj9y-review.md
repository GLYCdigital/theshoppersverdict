---

You've dialed in your grind size, your ratio, and your brew time. But your coffee still tastes flat — or worse, harshly bitter. The culprit isn't your beans or your technique. It's your water. Tap water varies wildly in mineral content, and that inconsistency directly impacts extraction. Third Wave Water's Medium Roast packets aim to fix that, one gallon at a time.

**What you actually get**

The box contains 12 single-use foil sachets. Each one treats one gallon of distilled or reverse-osmosis water. You're not getting water — you're getting the mineral blend that makes your water brew coffee the way specialty roasters intend. The medium roast formulation targets roughly 150 parts per million total hardness, a spec that aligns with what the Specialty Coffee Association recommends for optimal extraction. The minerals include magnesium and calcium, which are the ions that actually pull flavor compounds out of coffee grounds.

**How it performs in the real world**

I tested this against straight tap water and plain distilled water using the same beans, grinder, and pour-over setup. The differences were real but not dramatic. With the Third Wave Water-treated batch, the acidity in a Kenyan AA medium roast softened considerably — the bright, almost citrusy notes became rounder and more integrated. Bitterness that showed up in the tap-water brew (my local water runs hard) disappeared entirely. The body felt slightly heavier, more syrupy.

The biggest practical win? Consistency. If you brew every day, you know the frustration of a coffee that tastes great on Tuesday and mediocre on Thursday with zero changes to your process. These packets eliminate that variable entirely. Every gallon performs identically.

**What buyers tend to praise**

Long-term users frequently describe the switch in terms of "finally tasting what the roaster intended." One reviewer noted, "I thought my grinder was the problem. Turns out my water was. This made my cheap beans taste like they cost twice as much." Another common thread: it's dead simple. No measuring minerals, no pH strips, just tear, dump, stir.

**Where it stumbles**

The economics are the sticking point. Each packet costs roughly $1.25 to $1.50, and you still have to buy distilled water on top of that — typically another $1.50 to $2 per gallon. You're looking at roughly $3 per gallon of brew water. If you're using $15-per-pound beans, that's a meaningful add-on cost.

Second, the improvement is most noticeable with bright, acidic coffees. If you're brewing a dark roast that's already low-acid and smooth, you may barely detect a difference. This is a tool for someone chasing clarity and nuance, not for someone who just wants a decent morning cup.

Third, the "one gallon" math is annoying if you brew smaller batches. A 12-ounce bag of coffee typically uses about 4 to 5 gallons of water over its life, so you'll blow through three packets per bag. That's roughly $4.50 of water treatment per bag of coffee — a real cost to weigh.

**Who should buy this**

Buy it if you're a pour-over or drip brewer using single-origin medium roasts and you've plateaued on flavor quality. Buy it if you live in an area with hard water or chlorinated municipal supply. Buy it if you're entering home barista competitions or just want to replicate café-level consistency.

Skip it if you're brewing dark roasts exclusively, if you're on a tight budget, or if you're perfectly happy with your current cup. Also skip it if you don't have a reliable source of distilled water — using it with tap water defeats the entire purpose.

**FAQ**

**Can I use these packets with tap water?**
No. The mineral formulation assumes a blank slate. Tap water already contains minerals and impurities, so adding the packet will overshoot the target hardness. Use distilled or reverse-osmosis water only.

**How long does treated water stay fresh?**
Store it in a sealed container in a cool, dark place and use it within two weeks. The minerals won't spoil, but the water can pick up off-flavors from the air or container over time.

**Will this work for espresso?**
Yes, but consider the Espresso Profile version instead. Espresso extraction is more concentrated, so the mineral balance differs slightly. The Medium Roast formula works in a pinch but isn't optimized for it.

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 4.0/5** — It does exactly what it claims, with precision and zero fuss. The only real knock is the cost-per-gallon and the fact that its benefits are most apparent with lighter, more acidic coffees.

**Buy it if** you're serious about brew consistency and want to eliminate water as a variable. **Skip it if** you're happy with your current coffee or don't want to deal with sourcing distilled water.

For the home brewer who's already invested in a decent grinder and scale, this is the next logical upgrade — and it's far cheaper than a new brewer.
