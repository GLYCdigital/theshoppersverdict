You wouldn't think a box of pasta could generate 235 ratings and still hold a 4.7-star average. But here's the thing about 365 by Whole Foods Market Organic Linguine — it's not just another box of dried noodles. It's the pasta that makes you notice the difference between "fine" and "actually good."

**What you're getting**

The 16-ounce box contains a single pound of linguine made from organic durum wheat semolina. That's it. No eggs, no additives, no preservatives. The ingredient list is refreshingly short — one ingredient, actually. The pasta is bronze-die cut, which gives each strand a slightly rough, porous surface rather than the slick, shiny finish you get from cheaper Teflon-die extruded pasta.

As shown in the product image, the box is straightforward Whole Foods branding — clean, minimal, with clear organic certification labeling. Nothing flashy, but the package does its job.

**How it performs**

Drop it in salted boiling water and set a timer for 9 minutes. That's the sweet spot. The linguine comes out al dente with genuine resistance — the kind of firm bite that holds up against hearty ragùs or creamy sauces without collapsing. The rough texture matters more than you'd think. A simple olive oil and garlic toss clings to these strands noticeably better than it does to smoother commercial pastas.

One thing I appreciate: the cook time is forgiving. At 8 minutes, it's slightly firm. At 10, it's still perfectly edible — not a gummy mess. That's a real advantage for weeknight cooking when you're juggling a skillet and a screaming kid.

The pasta water gets nicely starchy too, which helps emulsify sauces. If you're the type who reserves a cup before draining, you'll get restaurant-quality body in your final dish.

**What buyers consistently say**

Scrolling through the reviews, a few themes keep surfacing. One shopper noted, *"This is my go-to pasta now. It holds up better than the name brands and I love that it's organic."* Another praised the texture: *"Tastes fresher than boxed pasta has any right to. The bite is perfect."* Multiple reviewers mentioned using it for everything from classic spaghetti carbonara to cold pasta salads without issues.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. There's no resealable closure on the box. Once opened, you're either cooking the whole pound or transferring the remainder to a zip-top bag or container. Minor, but worth knowing.

The price per ounce runs higher than non-organic store brands — typically 20-40% more depending on your market. If organic certification doesn't matter to you, you can find cheaper linguine that'll get the job done, though likely with a slicker texture and less sauce adherence.

**Who should buy this**

This pasta is for you if: you're cooking Italian dishes regularly and want a reliable, organic base that performs consistently. It's also ideal for anyone who's noticed that cheaper pasta turns to mush when reheated — this one holds its structure better in leftovers.

Skip it if you're on a tight budget and pasta is purely a vehicle for sauce, or if you prefer fresh pasta from the refrigerated section anyway.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Is this pasta gluten-free?* No. It's made from organic durum wheat semolina and contains gluten.

*How many servings per box?* The 16-ounce box yields roughly 8 servings of 2 ounces dry each, or about 4 generous dinner portions. That's standard for a pound of pasta.

*Does it work in a pasta maker or for baked dishes?* It's excellent in baked pastas like baked ziti — it holds up to double cooking. Not ideal for a pasta machine; that's for fresh dough.
