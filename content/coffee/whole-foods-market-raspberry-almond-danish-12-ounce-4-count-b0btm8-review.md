If you’ve ever grabbed a danish from a gas station and regretted it immediately, this Whole Foods Market Raspberry Almond Danish is the corrective experience. It’s not artisanal bakery-level, but it’s a solid step above most grocery store pastry cases — and for a 12-ounce box with four pastries, it’s a convenient weekend treat that doesn’t require a trip to a dedicated patisserie.

**What’s actually in the box**  
You get four individually wrapped danishes (3 ounces each). The wrapping is practical — you can pull one out and leave the rest sealed, which matters if you’re not eating all four in one sitting (though let’s be honest, the math on that is tempting). The pastry itself is a classic morning bun shape: swirls of laminated dough with raspberry filling ribboned through the center and a visible almond streusel on top.

**Performance: taste and texture**  
The first bite delivers what the package promises: the outer layers are genuinely flaky, not the dense, bread-like texture you get from cheaper commercial danishes. The raspberry filling is the standout — it’s tart, with real seed texture, and it doesn’t taste like artificially flavored jam. The almond component is present but shy; if you’re expecting a strong marzipan hit, this isn’t it. It’s more of a subtle nutty backdrop that keeps the sweetness in check.

One thing reviewers consistently note: the pastry holds up. One buyer said, “I kept the second one for the next morning and it was still crispy, not soggy like most store-bought.” That’s a real advantage — most mass-produced danishes turn into sad, damp discs by day two. The downside? This isn’t a “heat it up” pastry. If you microwave it (don’t), you’ll get a rubbery mess. It’s best eaten at room temperature or briefly toasted in a toaster oven — but even then, don’t expect the flakiness to survive a reheat.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
- **Freshness perception**: Multiple reviews mention it tastes “like it came from a bakery” — specifically, the butter flavor is noticeable, not masked by sugar.  
- **Portion control**: The 3-ounce size is satisfying without being a sugar bomb. One reviewer called it “the right size for coffee, not a full breakfast.”  
- **Ingredient transparency**: Whole Foods’ label lists recognizable ingredients (no palm oil shortenings or artificial preservatives), which matters to shoppers scanning for additives.

**Where it falls short**  
- **Value**: At roughly $6–7 for four pastries, you’re paying a premium. A local bakery will often sell a larger, fresher danish for the same price. This is convenience pricing, not bargain pricing.  
- **Shipping fragility**: The most common complaint in Amazon reviews — danish arrived with layers separated or crumbs in the box. One reviewer bluntly put it: “Two of the four were broken into pieces. Taste was good, but presentation was rough.”  
- **Freezing is a trap**: If you try to freeze these for later, the filling crystallizes and the pastry absorbs moisture. Eat them within 3–4 days of opening or share them.

**Who should buy this**  
- **The coffee snob who wants a quick pairing**: If you’re brewing a pour-over or espresso and want something better than a granola bar but don’t want to bake, this hits that niche.  
- **The office treat provider**: Individually wrapped, shelf-stable for a few days, and no one’s going to complain about a raspberry almond danish at a 10 a.m. meeting.  
- **The Whole Foods loyalist**: If you’re already shopping there, this is an easy grab-and-go addition to your cart.  
- **Skip it if**: You’re on a tight budget, you need pastries for a crowd (you’ll need 3+ boxes), or you expect a croissant-level laminated texture — this is closer to a danish, not a viennoiserie masterpiece.

**FAQ**  
*Q: Can I reheat these?*  
A: Only in a toaster oven at 300°F for 3–4 minutes. Microwaving ruins the texture. Even then, it’s better at room temp.

*Q: Are these gluten-free?*  
A: No. Standard wheat flour pastry. Not suitable for gluten-sensitive diets.

*Q: How long do they stay fresh after opening?*  
A: Sealed, they last about a week. Once opened, 2–3 days before the pastry softens noticeably.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a dependable, better-than-average supermarket danish with real raspberry flavor and a short ingredient list — and you don’t mind paying for the convenience.  
**Skip it if** you’re price-sensitive, need quantity, or expect bakery-fresh flakiness that survives shipping.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, honest pastry that earns its stars for taste and texture, loses a few for value and fragility in transit. For the occasional coffee pairing, it’s a fine call.
