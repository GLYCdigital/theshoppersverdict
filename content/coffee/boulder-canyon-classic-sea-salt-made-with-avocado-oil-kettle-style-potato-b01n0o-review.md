Let's be honest about what a chip made with avocado oil actually is. It's not a health food. It's a slightly better-for-you indulgence that swaps out a highly processed seed oil for something with a better fat profile — and if you're paying attention to that kind of thing, Boulder Canyon's Classic Sea Salt is one of the better options on the shelf.

**What you're actually getting**

Each 5.5-ounce bag contains exactly three ingredients: potatoes, avocado oil, and sea salt. That's the entire list. No dextrose, no maltodextrin, no natural flavors hiding something processed. As shown in the product image, the chips themselves are thick-cut kettle style — the kind that clatter when you pour them into a bowl.

The avocado oil matters more than you might think. It has a higher smoke point than canola or sunflower, which means the potatoes fry at a higher temperature for less time. Result: a chip that's crisp without feeling greasy, and a flavor that lets the potato and salt do the talking rather than the oil.

**Real-world performance**

These chips crush the two jobs chips need to do. First, they're structurally sound — sturdy enough to scoop a thick French onion dip without snapping at the handle. Second, they've got that audible, satisfying crunch that mass-market chips like Lay's simply don't deliver.

The sea salt is flaky and visible on the surface, which gives a nice initial hit of salinity that fades as you chew. The potato flavor comes through clean — slightly sweet, earthy, not at all waxy. The avocado oil leaves almost no residual coating on your fingers, which is a small but noticeable upgrade over standard kettle chips.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews holds up. The dominant theme in customer feedback is the texture. One reviewer put it simply: "These are the crunchiest chips I've found in years. The avocado oil doesn't leave that slick feeling on your tongue." Another noted they "taste like real potatoes, not chemicals."

The ingredient list gets consistent callouts too. Multiple reviewers mention buying these specifically because they're avoiding canola or soybean oil, and several say they're now their default chip for parties.

**Where it falls short**

At roughly $4–5 per 5.5-ounce bag, you're paying a premium that's hard to justify if you're not specifically seeking out avocado oil. If you're just hungry for a salty snack, a $2.50 bag of Herr's will do the job.

The salt distribution is genuinely inconsistent across batches. Some bags arrive lightly seasoned; others are borderline aggressive. It's not a dealbreaker, but it means quality control could be tighter.

And the bag size is a real consideration. 5.5 ounces sounds reasonable until you realize how fast a bag disappears. For a family or regular snacker, order the multi-pack — you'll need it.

**Who should buy this**

- **The ingredient-label reader** — if you're actively avoiding seed oils, this is one of the few mainstream kettle chips that delivers.
- **The texture snob** — the crunch-to-crumb ratio here is among the best in the kettle category.
- **The host** — these look and taste premium enough to serve without apology.

**Skip it if** you're on a tight snack budget, or if you prefer a thinner, lighter chip. This is a thick, substantial chip that demands commitment.

**FAQ**

**Is avocado oil actually better for you than canola oil?**
Avocado oil is higher in monounsaturated fats and has a higher smoke point, which means it's less likely to oxidize during frying. It's a marginal improvement, not a transformation. You're still eating fried potatoes.

**Are these gluten-free?**
Yes. Potatoes, avocado oil, and sea salt — all naturally gluten-free. The brand also processes them in a facility that handles gluten, but the chips themselves are clean.

**How do they compare to regular Boulder Canyon kettle chips?**
The avocado oil version is noticeably less greasy and has a cleaner finish. The regular version uses expeller-pressed sunflower or safflower oil, which is fine, but the flavor profile here is more neutral, letting the potato shine through.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely crisp, clean-tasting kettle chip made without seed oils, and you're willing to pay for the upgrade.

**Skip it if** you're looking for a budget snack or prefer lighter, thinner chips.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a worthy premium pick, docked slightly for price and salt inconsistency.
