Let’s be blunt: most grocery store dark chocolate is either waxy, overly sweet, or pretentiously overpriced. The 365 by Whole Foods Market 70% Dark Chocolate Bar sits in a strange middle ground — it’s a store-brand bar that performs well above its price point. After testing it against four other dark chocolate bars in the same range, here’s what I found.

**What you actually get**

The bar comes in a 2.8-ounce (79g) format, which is slightly smaller than the standard 3.5-ounce bar from brands like Green & Black’s or Lindt. It’s divided into 18 neat squares — convenient for portioning if you have more self-control than I do. The packaging is minimal, matte-finish paper with clear labeling. No gimmicks.

Flip it over, and the ingredient list is refreshingly short: organic cocoa mass, organic cane sugar, organic cocoa butter, and vanilla extract. That’s it. No soy lecithin, no artificial flavors, no palm oil. For shoppers scanning labels, this alone justifies consideration.

**Build quality and flavor performance**

The snap is clean and sharp — a good indicator of proper tempering. The melt is smooth, not waxy. Flavor-wise, this is a competent, workmanlike 70% bar. You get immediate notes of roasted cocoa and a mild sweetness that fades into a gentle, lingering bitterness. It won’t blow you away with berry undertones or tobacco complexity like a fine single-origin bar, but it also won’t punish you with the acrid harshness that plagues cheaper 85%+ bars.

One thing I tested: pairing it with coffee. The moderate bitterness and sugar level (about 8g per serving) make it an excellent coffee companion — it complements a medium roast without overwhelming it. If you’re using it for baking, the consistent melt and stable structure hold up well in ganache and brownies.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings reflects genuine satisfaction. One reviewer called it “the best store-brand dark chocolate I’ve tried — smooth and not overly sweet.” Another noted, “I use it for baking and snacking. Great value for organic chocolate.” The most common praise? Value. Multiple reviewers compared it favorably to bars costing twice as much.

**Where it falls short**

This isn’t a connoisseur’s bar. If you’re used to craft chocolate from makers like TCHO or Dick Taylor, you’ll notice the absence of nuanced flavor notes. The cacao is blended from multiple origins, so flavor varies somewhat between batches — a consistent enough issue that some reviewers mentioned it in passing. And at 2.8 ounces, it’s smaller than you might expect for the price. If you eat chocolate daily, you’ll go through this in two sittings.

**Who should buy this**

- **Everyday snackers** who want clean, organic chocolate without the premium markup
- **Bakers** who need a reliable, consistent dark chocolate that melts predictably
- **Coffee drinkers** who want a solid chocolate to pair with their morning brew
- **Skeptical store-brand shoppers** who think house labels are inferior — this will change your mind

**Frequently asked questions**

**Is this chocolate vegan?** Yes. All ingredients are plant-based, and there’s no dairy or milk powder in the ingredient list.

**Does it contain soy or gluten?** No soy lecithin, no gluten-containing ingredients. It’s processed in a facility that handles other allergens, so cross-contamination is possible if you have severe sensitivity.

**How does it compare to Lindt 70%?** The 365 bar is less sweet, has a cleaner ingredient profile, and costs less per ounce. Lindt has a slightly smoother mouthfeel due to added cocoa butter, but the 365 bar wins on purity.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want honest, organic dark chocolate that performs well in every situation without breaking the bank. It’s the sensible choice for daily consumption.

**Skip it if:** You’re chasing complex flavor profiles or need a single-origin bean story. For that, you’ll need to spend significantly more.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a reliable, well-made bar that undercuts premium competitors on price without sacrificing quality. For the price-to-performance ratio, this is hard to beat.
