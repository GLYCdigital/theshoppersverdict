## The brewing ritual matters

Here's where most people screw up. This is not a scoop-and-stir instant mix. You need to brew it properly:

1. Boil 2 cups of water
2. Add 3-4 tablespoons of the mix
3. Steep for 3-5 minutes — never longer than 7
4. Strain, then pour over a tall glass of ice

The result, when done right, is a deeply amber liquid with that unmistakable malty, slightly smoky, vanilla-adjacent flavor. It's sweet but not cloying. The creamer gives it body without tasting artificial. Add condensed milk on top if you want it richer, but honestly, it's already sweet enough straight.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the cons. This mix contains FD&C Yellow #6 and palm oil. If you're someone who reads ingredient labels and cares about artificial additives, this isn't the product for you. There are cleaner Thai tea options, but they're harder to find and don't taste quite as authentic.

The bitterness issue is real. If you forget about your steeping tea and come back 10 minutes later, it's ruined — astringent, slightly metallic, and no amount of sugar will fix it. Set a timer.

Also, this is pre-sweetened. If you prefer controlling your own sugar level, this will be too sweet for you. There are unsweetened versions of ChaTraMue, but this is not one of them.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this instant or does it need brewing?**
A: It needs brewing. It's a loose-leaf blend, not a powder you can dissolve in cold water. Boil water, steep, strain, serve over ice.

**Q: Can I use this for hot tea?**
A: Technically yes, but it's designed for iced tea. Hot, it tastes overly sweet and the creamer flavor becomes more pronounced. Stick to iced.

**Q: How long does an opened bag stay fresh?**
A: If you keep the bag sealed and stored in a cool, dark place, it'll stay good for 6-8 months. The resealable strip helps, but don't trust it indefinitely — transfer to an airtight container if you're a slow user.
