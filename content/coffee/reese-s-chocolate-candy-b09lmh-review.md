This listing ships the classic 1.5-ounce cups, individually wrapped in the familiar orange foil. **Flavor and texture breakdown**

The peanut butter filling is what sets Reese's apart from knockoffs. It's dense, slightly salty, and has that signature crumbly-smooth texture that melts slowly rather than dissolving instantly. The chocolate shell snaps cleanly when you bite through it — that's the tempered cocoa butter doing its job. One buyer noted: "These taste exactly like they did 20 years ago. The peanut butter is salty enough to balance the sweetness, and the chocolate isn't waxy like cheaper brands."

At room temperature, the filling stays firm but pliable. Cold from the fridge, the chocolate gets extra snappy and the peanut butter becomes almost fudge-like — worth trying if you've never done it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme in reviews is freshness and authenticity. Multiple reviewers mention receiving product with good shelf life and no staleness. One wrote: "These arrived fresh — not chalky or old-tasting like some Amazon candy orders." Another highlighted the value: "Cheaper than the grocery store per ounce, and the cups were intact, not crushed."

**Where it falls short**

Here's the honest part. During warmer months, several reviewers reported melted or misshapen cups. One said: "The chocolate had white streaks on it — clearly it melted and re-hardened in transit." Amazon's warehouse isn't climate-controlled for chocolate, so this is a real risk if you're ordering in July in a hot region.

Also worth flagging: the nutrition facts. Two cups run 210 calories, 13g fat, and 21g sugar. This is a treat, not a staple. If you're watching sugar intake, portion control matters — the individual wrappers help, but the two-cup serving size is easy to blow past.
