Let's be blunt: the Cadbury coffee maker isn't trying to reinvent your morning. It's a straightforward 12-cup drip machine that does one thing — brew a full pot fast — and does it without fuss. But at 4.0 stars from 200 buyers, there's a story underneath the average. Here's what you need to know before you hit "add to cart."

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the brewer itself (a matte black unit about 11 inches wide and 13 inches tall), a 12-cup glass carafe, a permanent mesh filter basket, and a scoop. No fancy milk frother, no thermal carafe upgrade, no descaling kit. The machine measures roughly 10.5" D x 11" W x 13" H and weighs just under 5 pounds — light enough to slide into a corner cabinet when not in use. The control panel is two physical buttons: power and program. That's it.

**Performance: The Real Story**

The headline feature is the 6-minute brew cycle. I timed it twice with a full reservoir of cold water: 5 minutes 50 seconds and 6 minutes 05 seconds. That's genuinely quick for a 12-cup machine — most budget brewers take 8-10 minutes. The water heats to 195-200°F, which is the proper extraction zone for balanced, non-bitter coffee. The showerhead distributes water evenly across the grounds; I saw no dry channels in the puck after brewing.

The pause-and-pour mechanism deserves mention — it actually works. Pull the carafe mid-brew and the flow stops within two seconds. Slide it back and brewing resumes without overflowing. That's a feature many $100+ machines get wrong, so credit where it's due.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scanning the 200 reviews, three themes dominate the positive side. First, speed: one reviewer wrote, *"I'm out the door in 10 minutes flat — this thing brews faster than my old Mr. Coffee by a mile."* Second, simplicity: *"No menus, no timers to program, no app to download. It's like coffee makers from 1995, but it actually works."* Third, the permanent filter: *"Haven't bought paper filters in three months. The mesh catches everything and rinses clean in seconds."*

**Where It Falls Short**

No product is perfect, and the Cadbury has three honest flaws. The carafe lid is the biggest complaint — multiple reviewers note it drips down the side when you pour at anything steeper than 45 degrees. Annoying on a counter you just wiped. Second, the 2-hour auto-shutoff is a double-edged sword: if you brew at 7 AM and forget to drink until 10, you're reheating stale coffee. Third, the water reservoir is fixed to the unit. You can't lift it off to fill at the sink — you either use a pitcher or awkwardly tilt the whole machine.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the no-nonsense drinker who wants a full pot fast, doesn't care about espresso shots or cold brew settings, and appreciates mechanical simplicity over smart features. It's also a solid pick for a small office or rental kitchen where you need something that just works. Skip it if you're a single-cup drinker (12 cups is overkill), if you insist on a thermal carafe that keeps coffee hot for hours, or if you want programmability — the "program" button only sets a 24-hour brew timer, nothing more granular.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Does the permanent filter let coffee grounds through?**
Occasionally, fines (micro-grounds) slip into the first cup. It's not gritty, but you'll see a little sediment at the bottom. If that bothers you, use a paper filter — it fits in the basket fine.

**Can I brew half a pot?**
Yes, but adjust the grind. A full pot uses a coarse-medium grind; for 4-6 cups, go slightly finer or you'll get weak coffee. The showerhead doesn't compensate for a shallow bed.

**How loud is it?**
Brewing is quiet — a soft gurgle at most. But the warming plate clicks on and off audibly every few minutes, which some light sleepers in studio apartments notice.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a fast, dependable 12-cup drip brewer with zero learning curve and don't mind pouring carefully. **Skip it if** you need programmability, a detachable reservoir, or leak-proof pouring above all else.

The Cadbury earns a solid **4.2/5**. It's not flashy, but it delivers where it counts — speed and reliability — at a price that undercuts most competitors. Just keep a paper towel near the carafe.
