## The Unsexy Truth About Sugar Packets

Sugar packets aren't exciting. They're not a shiny espresso machine or a fancy grinder. But if you drink coffee daily — at home, in the office, or on the go — Domino's 3.54g packets are the quiet workhorse your morning routine depends on. The question is whether this 100-count box actually earns its place in your pantry.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains 100 individual paper packets, each holding 3.54 grams of pure cane sugar. That's roughly one teaspoon per packet — the standard serving for a standard cup of coffee. The packets are sealed on all sides, which matters more than you'd think: no moisture getting in, no sugar clumping into a solid brick after three weeks of humid weather.

Domino uses cane sugar rather than beet sugar. For most people, the difference is imperceptible. For those with a sensitive palate, cane sugar has a marginally cleaner sweetness with less of the funky aftertaste that some beet sugars carry. It dissolves quickly in hot coffee — no stirring marathon required.

## How It Performs in Real Life

The packets are the kind of product you don't think about until they're gone. They're reliable. Each packet tears cleanly along the perforated edge — no awkward ripping that sends sugar across the counter. The pour is controlled; you won't dump half the packet into your mug if you're not paying attention.

The portion size is the real selling point. At 3.54g, you get a consistent, repeatable sweetness level every time. No "a little more" or "a little less" depending on how steady your hand is. That consistency is why these packets show up in hotels, diners, and office break rooms across the country.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, 100 packets isn't as many as it sounds. If you drink two cups a day with one packet each, that's 50 days per box. If you take two packets per cup — you're looking at less than a month. The per-packet cost is reasonable, but you'll be reordering regularly.

Second, the portion size cuts both ways. At 14 calories per packet, it's easy to lose track of how much sugar you're adding across the day. Two packets per coffee, three coffees a day — that's 84 calories and roughly 21 grams of sugar before lunch. If you're watching your intake, this isn't the product to help you moderate. The packets make it effortless to add sugar without thinking.

Third, there's no resealable storage option. The box itself is simple cardboard. Once opened, you'll want to store the remaining packets in a container if you live somewhere humid.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want consistent portioning, need portable sugar for travel or work, or appreciate the convenience of grab-and-go packets that never clump.

**Skip it if:** You're trying to cut back on sugar, you have a bulk dispenser at home you prefer, or you go through sugar fast enough that buying a 5-pound bag makes more sense economically.

## The Verdict

**4.2 out of 5 stars.** Domino's sugar packets deliver exactly what they promise: consistent, fresh, portable sugar with zero fuss. The 100-count box is a practical staple for coffee drinkers, office workers, and travelers. It's not exciting, but it's dependable — and sometimes that's exactly what you need at 6 AM.

The downsides are real but minor. The quantity runs out faster than you'd expect, and the portion control that makes it convenient can also make it easy to over-sweeten without noticing. But for the price and the reliability, this is a solid buy. Just don't expect it to change your life — it's sugar in a paper wrapper, and it does the job.
