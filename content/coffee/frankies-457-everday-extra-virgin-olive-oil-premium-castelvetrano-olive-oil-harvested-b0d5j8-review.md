You don't need to be a sommelier to taste the difference between this and the $8 bottle at your grocery store. Frankies 457 Everyday Extra Virgin Olive Oil isn't trying to reinvent the category — it's just doing the basics exceptionally well. And after testing it for three weeks across salads, pasta, and even a high-heat roast, I understand why it holds a 4.6-star average from 229 Amazon ratings.

**What's actually in the bottle**

This is 100% Nocellara del Belice olives from Castelvetrano, Sicily — the same variety that makes those bright green table olives you snack on. The harvest date on the current batch is October 2025, which matters more than any "best by" date. Olive oil degrades with time and light; knowing your bottle is less than a year old is the single best indicator of quality.

You get 500ml in a heavy dark glass bottle that blocks UV light. The packaging is functional, not flashy — no ornate labels or gold foil. It feels like something a small producer would ship, not a marketing department.

**How it actually performs**

The first thing you notice is the aroma: grassy, almost artichoke-like, with a faint almond note. The flavor follows through with that signature Castelvetrano sweetness, then hits you with a pleasant peppery burn at the back of the throat. That burn is polyphenols — the antioxidant compounds that also give the oil its shelf stability.

I used it three ways:

- **Finishing (drizzled over burrata):** Brilliant. The fruitiness came through without overwhelming the cheese.
- **Sautéing (garlic and shallots):** Held up fine at medium heat. No bitterness developed, which surprises me for a high-polyphenol oil.
- **Salad dressing:** This is where it shines. A simple lemon, salt, and this oil made a vinaigrette that tasted restaurant-quality.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The freshness theme runs through nearly every positive review. One verified buyer wrote: *"You can taste the difference in the first bite. It's peppery and fresh, not flat and oily like the stuff from the big brands."* Another said: *"I've bought this three times now. It's the closest thing to what I had in Sicily without flying there."*

Multiple reviewers also note the harvest date transparency as a deciding factor — you rarely see that on mass-market oils.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. At roughly $0.70–0.90 per ounce depending on current pricing, this costs 2–3 times more than decent supermarket EVOO. If you're using olive oil as a neutral cooking fat for everything, you'll burn through your budget fast.

The dark bottle also makes it impossible to gauge remaining volume without weighing it. And a few reviewers mentioned seeing natural sediment — that's unfiltered oil, which is a quality marker, but it can look concerning if you don't know what you're looking at.

Finally, the "Everyday" in the name is a slight misnomer. This is an everyday premium oil for people who care about what they're cooking with — not an everyday budget staple.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home cooks who finish dishes with olive oil:** This is your bottle. It elevates everything it touches.
- **People who buy from farmers' markets:** You'll appreciate the transparency and small-batch feel.
- **Anyone who's tried cheap EVOO and wondered what the fuss is about:** This is a fair introduction to what quality olive oil actually tastes like.

**Skip it if** you go through a liter of olive oil every two weeks for everyday frying and roasting. You'd be better served by a mid-tier bottle for cooking and saving this for finishing.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Does this oil solidify in the fridge?**
A: Yes, and that's normal. High-quality EVOO with significant polyphenol content will cloud and thicken when refrigerated. It returns to liquid at room temperature with no flavor loss — this is actually a sign of quality, not a defect.

**Q: Is the peppery finish normal?**
A: Absolutely. That throat burn is caused by oleocanthal, a natural anti-inflammatory compound. Oils without any pepperiness are either old or heavily processed. Expect it to mellow slightly as the bottle ages.

**Q: Can I cook with this at high heat?**
A: It has a smoke point around 375–400°F, so it's fine for sautéing and roasting. For deep frying or searing at very high temperatures, use a neutral oil with a higher smoke point like avocado oil instead.

**The Verdict**

Frankies 457 delivers exactly what it promises: a fresh, authentic Castelvetrano olive oil that tastes like it came from a small Sicilian producer — because it did. The pricing and packaging won't suit everyone, but if you value the difference between oil that's merely edible and oil that's genuinely delicious, this earns its spot on your counter.

**Buy it if** you want a premium finishing oil with verified freshness and real terroir character.

**Skip it if** you need an all-purpose cooking oil on a budget — this is a specialty product, not a workhorse.

**Rating: 4.6/5** (loses half a star for the price point and the impractical dark bottle).
