Some coffees are coffee. And then there's Major Dickason's Blend — the dark roast that's been a Peet's cornerstone since 1969, and for good reason. This is the blend that converted countless "I only drink Starbucks" people into Peet's loyalists. With 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly resonating. But is it the right dark roast for *you*? Let's dig in.

**What You Actually Get**

This is whole bean coffee — 18 ounces of it in Peet's standard retail bag. The beans are a deep, oily dark roast, which tells you immediately this isn't a timid morning cup. The aroma out of the bag is intense: dark chocolate, toasted nuts, and a hint of earthiness. You're getting a blend of Central and South American beans, plus a bit of Indonesian for that signature Peet's complexity.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I ground these beans at a medium-coarse setting for French press and a finer grind for espresso. In both cases, the flavor profile held up remarkably well. The cup is full-bodied with a syrupy mouthfeel, delivering what Peet's calls "a rich, complex, full-bodied cup" — and honestly, that's not marketing fluff. There's a satisfying bittersweet chocolate note up front, followed by a clean finish that doesn't linger with harsh bitterness.

For drip coffee makers, this blend shines brightest. It cuts through milk and sweeteners without disappearing, making it a solid choice for lattes or Americanos. Espresso shots come out thick with a nice crema, though the dark roast intensity might be a bit much for espresso purists who prefer lighter single origins.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the reviews, a few themes pop up repeatedly. One reviewer notes, "This is the only coffee I buy now. Rich, smooth, and never bitter." Another says, "Finally, a dark roast that doesn't taste burnt. The flavor is deep without being harsh." That sentiment — smooth and complex rather than charred — comes up again and again. Multiple buyers mention it's become their "daily driver" after trying a dozen other brands.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the bag lacks a one-way degassing valve — a standard feature on most specialty coffee bags. That means once you open it, the beans are exposed to oxygen and will stale faster than you'd like. If you're a slow brewer, transfer the beans to an airtight container after opening.

Second, this is a *bold* coffee. If you prefer light roasts with bright, fruity acidity, you will not like this. It's unapologetically dark, and that's the point. Third, the price fluctuates on Amazon, and it's occasionally cheaper at grocery stores — worth checking before you commit.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Dark roast loyalists** who want a complex, non-burnt cup
- **Home espresso users** who need a forgiving bean that produces good crema
- **French press and drip drinkers** who want a full-bodied morning brew
- **Gift-givers** who know someone who takes coffee seriously

**Skip it if** you're a light roast fan, you hate oily beans, or you prefer single-origin coffees with identifiable terroir. This is a blend, and it's designed to be consistent, not exotic.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this coffee actually dark roasted or just medium-dark?**  
A: It's a full dark roast — the beans are visibly oily and shiny. But unlike many dark roasts, it avoids the burnt, ashy taste that plagues cheaper options.

**Q: How should I grind these beans?**  
A: Depends on your brew method. Coarse for French press, medium for drip, fine for espresso. The beans are forgiving, so you don't need a $500 grinder to get a good cup.

**Q: Does it go stale quickly?**  
A: The lack of a one-way valve is a real issue. I'd recommend transferring to an airtight container within a week of opening and using within three weeks for optimal freshness.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a bold, complex dark roast that delivers consistent quality across brew methods. It's a crowd-pleaser for anyone who likes their coffee with character.

**Skip it if** you're a light roast devotee or you're sensitive to caffeine intensity — this blend packs a punch.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses points only for the packaging flaw and its unapologetic boldness. Otherwise, this is a benchmark dark roast that earns its cult following.
