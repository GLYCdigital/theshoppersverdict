**The short version:** If you're looking for a bovine colostrum supplement that actually delivers on its immune and gut-health promises without a boutique supplement price tag, Micro Ingredients is the real deal. But it comes with a caveat: you have to be willing to work around the taste and texture.

## What You're Actually Getting

An 8-ounce bag of freeze-dried bovine colostrum powder sourced from grass-fed, pasture-raised cows. That's it — no fillers, no artificial sweeteners, no soy lecithin. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward white-to-cream powder with a nutrition label that lists just one ingredient.

The freeze-drying process matters here. Most budget colostrum brands use spray-drying, which uses high heat and degrades the delicate immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM) and growth factors that are the entire point of taking colostrum. Freeze-drying preserves these bioactive compounds. Micro Ingredients doesn't publish a standardized IgG percentage on the label, which is a transparency miss — but the fact that they offer third-party testing results on request is a reasonable counterbalance.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where things get honest. This powder does not dissolve in water. It clumps, floats, and generally behaves like powdered milk that never fully hydrates. If you try to chug it in a glass of water, you'll be chewing your supplement. That's not a defect — it's the nature of colostrum — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Where it shines is in a smoothie, or mixed into coffee or oatmeal. One buyer put it plainly: *"I mix it in my morning coffee with a splash of oat milk and honestly can't taste it at all. Tried it in water once. Never again."* Another said: *"The taste is like a very mild, slightly sweet milk powder. Not bad, but definitely not something I'd drink alone."*

The recommended serving is 1 scoop (about 3 grams) daily, though many users report taking 2 scoops for the first month as a "loading phase." At one scoop a day, this bag lasts roughly 75 days. At two scoops, about 38 days.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides, because they exist:

**The scoop inconsistency issue.** Some buyers report receiving a scoop in the bag; others don't. That's annoying for a supplement where dosing matters. A kitchen scale solves it (3 grams = roughly 1 teaspoon), but it shouldn't be a coin flip.

**The taste and texture.** No sugar-coating this — it's not pleasant on its own. The flavor is mildly sweet and milky, but the texture in liquid is gritty and chalky. If you're someone who needs supplements to taste neutral in water, this isn't it.

**No IgG standardization.** The label doesn't tell you the immunoglobulin concentration. For a product where the active compounds are the entire selling point, this is a transparency gap. It's likely fine — freeze-dried colostrum typically retains 20-30% IgG — but "likely" isn't "guaranteed."

## FAQ

**Q: Can I take this while pregnant or breastfeeding?**  
A: There's limited research on colostrum supplementation during pregnancy. Consult your OB-GYN first. The immune-modulating effects, while generally positive, aren't fully studied in pregnant populations.

**Q: How long until I notice effects?**  
A: Most reviewers report gut-related changes within 2-4 weeks of daily use. Immune benefits typically take longer — think 6-8 weeks of consistent use. This isn't an overnight supplement.

**Q: Does it need to be refrigerated?**  
A: No, but store it in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. The freeze-dried format is shelf-stable. Just make sure the bag is sealed tightly — moisture is the enemy here.
