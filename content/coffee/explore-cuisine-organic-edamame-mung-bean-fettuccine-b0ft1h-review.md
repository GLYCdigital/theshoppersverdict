Let's be straight about this: most "healthy" pasta alternatives taste like cardboard glued together with regret. The EXPLORE CUISINE Organic Edamame & Mung Bean Fettuccine is not that. It's also not fettuccine — not really. Here's what 220 Amazon reviewers at 4.4 stars are actually saying.

**What's in the bag**

You get a 8-ounce bag of green fettuccine-style noodles made from exactly two ingredients: organic edamame and organic mung beans. That's it. No added starches, no xanthan gum, no "natural flavors" hiding anything. As shown in the product image, the noodles have a vibrant green color that darkens slightly when cooked — a visual reminder this is a whole-food product, not something extruded in a lab.

**Cooking reality check**

The package suggests 5-7 minutes of boiling. Ignore that. Most reviewers report the sweet spot is 8-9 minutes for al dente texture. The noodles release a noticeable beany aroma while cooking — one reviewer described it as "smelling like a stir-fry for the first five minutes." That smell dissipates once drained, but if you're sensitive to legume scents, open a window.

Here's where it gets interesting: this pasta holds its shape remarkably well. Unlike chickpea or black bean pastas that disintegrate into mush if you glance away from the pot, this one tolerates a minute or two of over-cooking. Multiple reviewers noted they could reheat leftovers without the noodles turning to paste.

**The texture debate**

This is the make-or-break factor. The texture is dense, chewy, and substantial — closer to firm soba noodles than Italian fettuccine. One reviewer wrote, "It's not pretending to be wheat pasta. It's its own thing — and once I stopped comparing, I genuinely enjoyed it." Another said, "My kids noticed the difference immediately and weren't fans. My husband and I loved it."

That's the honest split. If you're expecting a white-flour fettuccine experience, you'll be disappointed. If you want a high-protein noodle that actually tastes good, this delivers.

**The nutrition math**

Per 2-ounce serving: 24g protein, 13g fiber, 190 calories. Compare that to traditional fettuccine at roughly 7g protein and 2g fiber per serving. This is arguably the best protein-to-calorie ratio of any pasta alternative on Amazon. For anyone tracking macros — bodybuilders, diabetics, keto-adjacent eaters — this is genuinely useful food.

**Where it falls short**

The price per ounce stings. You're paying a significant premium over wheat pasta, and the 8-ounce bag goes fast — one reviewer noted it feeds "two hungry adults or three light eaters." The beany flavor, while mild, does come through in the final dish. It pairs well with bold sauces (pesto, spicy marinara, sesame-ginger) but disappears under delicate cream sauces. And the green color can look unappetizing to picky eaters — this isn't a pasta you can sneak past a child who expects beige.

**Who should buy this**

- **Macro-trackers** who want 24g of plant protein without a protein powder aftertaste
- **Gluten-free eaters** who are tired of rice-based pasta that falls apart
- **Meal-preppers** who need leftovers that survive a microwave reheat without turning to glue
- **Adventurous cooks** who treat pasta as a canvas, not a comfort-food replica

**Skip it if** you're serving kids who expect classic fettuccine, or if you want a neutral-tasting noodle that takes a backseat to your sauce.

**FAQ**

*Does it taste like beans?* Mildly. The edamame flavor is present but subtle — most reviewers say it's closer to a nutty, earthy note than a pronounced beany taste. Pair with assertive sauces.

*How does it reheat?* Better than any legume pasta I've tested. Microwave with a splash of water for 60 seconds and the noodles stay intact. Some reviewers even praised cold leftovers in pasta salad.

*Is it actually organic?* Yes. USDA-certified organic, non-GMO, and the facility handles only legumes — no cross-contamination risk with wheat or gluten.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Buy it if you want a legitimately nutritious noodle that doesn't taste like punishment. Skip it if you're a wheat pasta purist.**

This isn't a dupe for traditional fettuccine. It's a different product category — a high-protein, high-fiber noodle that happens to look like pasta. For that purpose, it's one of the best options on the market. The 4.4-star rating holds up, with the caveat that texture expectations matter. If you're ready for a chewy, earthy, nutrient-dense noodle, this earns its place in your pantry.
