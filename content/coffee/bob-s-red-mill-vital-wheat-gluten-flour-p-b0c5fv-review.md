## What You Actually Get

The package is straightforward: a 22-ounce (623g) re-sealable pouch of pale tan powder. That's it — no scoop, no measuring cup. But what matters is what's inside. Per the label, each 2-tablespoon serving packs 12 grams of protein and about 6 grams of gluten — the key structural protein that gives bread its rise and chew. The bag is resealable, which matters because this stuff absorbs humidity fast.

## Performance: Where It Shines

If you bake bread regularly, you already know the frustration: your dough rises, but the crumb is dense, or the loaf collapses after baking. Add one to two tablespoons of this per cup of flour, and the difference is immediate. The gluten strengthens the dough network, trapping more CO2 from the yeast. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"I add this to my whole wheat bread recipe and it went from hockey puck to fluffy sandwich loaf. Night and day difference."*

It also works for:

- **Pasta dough** — makes it more elastic and less likely to tear when rolling thin
- **Pizza crust** — gives that chewy, Neapolitan-style bite
- **Vegan seitan** — the primary ingredient; pure gluten with no additives

A 22-ounce bag lasts through roughly 15–20 standard loaf recipes, which makes it economical despite the upfront cost.

## Where It Falls Short (Honestly)

First, this is not for anyone with celiac disease or gluten intolerance. The label warns of processing on shared equipment — that's a hard stop for sensitive individuals. Even if you're just gluten-averse, skip it.

Second, the powder is fine and lightweight. Scooping it creates a fine dust that can irritate your nose and throat. A simple dust mask or just scooping slowly helps, but it's a minor annoyance.

Third — and this is the one I see people mess up — you cannot just dump in extra for "more structure." Too much creates a rubbery, dense crumb that's tough to chew. Follow the ratio: 1–2 tablespoons per cup of flour, max. One reviewer learned this the hard way: *"I added 4 tablespoons to a baguette recipe and ended up with a tire. Use the recommended amount."*

## Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can I use this in a bread machine?**
Yes. Add it directly to the flour mixture before running the cycle. It works with standard bread machine recipes — just reduce the total flour by about 2 tablespoons per cup to keep the liquid ratio balanced.

**Q: Does it expire or go bad?**
The bag has a best-by date, but stored in a cool, dry place, it stays viable for 6–8 months past that date. The protein content degrades slowly, so it'll still work, just with slightly less rise.

**Q: Is it the same as regular flour?**
No. It's concentrated gluten — about 75–80% protein by weight versus 10–13% in all-purpose flour. Don't substitute it 1:1 for flour in any recipe.
