Let me be blunt: most “keto” nut butters taste like someone blended sawdust with stevia and called it dessert. The ELAVI Keto Cashew Butter isn’t that. It’s actually good, which puts it ahead of 90% of the low-sugar spread category.

**What you’re actually getting**

The jar is 12 ounces of spread made from roasted cashews, coconut oil, and a touch of monk fruit. No palm oil, no soy lecithin, no added sugar. The ingredient list is short enough to read in one breath — that’s a good sign.

The macros are the headline: 1g net carbs and 2g sugar per 2-tablespoon serving. For context, standard cashew butter runs 4-5g net carbs and 3-4g sugar per serving. ELAVI cuts that by roughly 75% without resorting to sugar alcohols, which is why it doesn’t trigger that cooling, weird aftertaste you get from erythritol-heavy products.

**How it actually performs**

Texture is where this spread shines. It’s smooth and drip-able at room temperature, similar to a well-made almond butter but with more silkiness. It spreads cleanly on celery sticks, rice cakes, or straight off a spoon. The cashew base gives it a natural sweetness that means you don’t miss the sugar — you get a buttery, slightly toasted flavor that reads as “dessert” without being cloying.

One issue: oil separation. Multiple 4-star reviewers noted a layer of oil on top when the jar arrives. One buyer wrote, “I had to stir for a solid three minutes before it came together. Once mixed, it was great, but be prepared for the mess.” Stir slowly and use a butter knife, not a spoon — the liquid splashes otherwise.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The kid-friendly angle is the most common theme in positive reviews. Parents repeatedly say their children eat this without complaint, which is rare for low-sugar products. One reviewer noted, “My 6-year-old asked for this over Nutella after trying it on toast. I nearly fell over.” Another said it’s become their go-to for school snacks since it doesn’t spike blood sugar.

The other consistent praise is the clean ingredient profile. Several reviewers with dietary restrictions — keto, paleo, or just anti-additive — appreciated that this doesn’t taste like a compromise.

**Where it falls short**

The price is the biggest objection. At roughly $0.50-$0.60 per ounce, this costs about 40% more than conventional cashew butter at a typical grocery store. You’re paying for the low-carb formulation, and that’s a legitimate premium.

The jar size is also smaller than it appears in product photos. The 12-ounce size is standard for specialty spreads, but if you’re used to 16-ounce jars, budget accordingly. And the oil separation issue is inconsistent — some jars arrive perfectly mixed, others don’t.

**Who should buy this**

- Parents doing low-carb or keto diets who still want their kids to enjoy spreads without the sugar load
- Anyone tracking net carbs who misses the taste of real nut butter — this satisfies that craving
- People avoiding sugar alcohols who want a sweet spread without erythritol or maltitol

**Skip it if** you’re on a tight budget, or if you prefer a crunchy texture — this is smooth only, and separation fussiness will annoy you.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this actually kid-approved or just “kid-friendly” marketing?**  
A: The reviews suggest real kid approval. Multiple parents report their children choosing this over conventional sweet spreads. The cashew flavor is naturally sweet without being sugary, and the smooth texture is easy for little hands.

**Q: How does this compare to regular cashew butter nutritionally?**  
A: Regular cashew butter has about 4-5g net carbs per serving. ELAVI has 1g net carbs. That’s the difference that matters for keto. The taste difference is minimal — ELAVI is slightly less sweet but compensates with a richer roasted flavor.

**Q: Does it need refrigeration after opening?**  
A: The label says refrigerate after opening to maintain freshness. It stays spreadable at fridge temperature, though it firms up slightly. Give it five minutes at room temperature before using if you prefer the looser texture.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely good-tasting low-carb spread that your kids will actually eat. The clean ingredients and 1g net carbs justify the premium for keto households.  
**Skip it if** price sensitivity is your priority or you can’t handle stirring separated oil — cheaper, higher-carb options exist.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid product, real downsides are minor. The oil separation issue and premium pricing keep it from a perfect score, but for the keto dessert-spread niche, this is among the best available.
