## What's Actually in the Box

This is a serious quantity play. You're getting 30+ individually foil-wrapped tea bags spanning the Stash lineup: classic black teas like English Breakfast and Earl Grey, green teas, mint blends, chai, and a solid chunk of caffeine-free herbals including chamomile and their popular Lemon Ginger. The foil wrappers aren't decorative — they're double-sealed, which matters more than most people realize. Loose tea in a paper bag goes stale in weeks. These stay aromatic for months, which is why several reviewers specifically mentioned the freshness holding up even at the bottom of the box.

The Non-GMO Project Verified label is real, not marketing fluff. Stash has carried this certification for years, and their sourcing is consistent — no artificial flavors, no questionable additives.

## How It Performs in Real Life

I brewed through a solid chunk of this box over two weeks, both hot and iced. The black teas are the strongest performers. Their English Breakfast is properly brisk — good with milk, holds up to a 3-minute steep without turning bitter. The Earl Grey uses real bergamot oil, not flavoring, and you can taste the difference. The green teas are smoother than most bagged options, though they're not going to convert a loose-leaf snob.

The herbal side is where the variety shines. The Lemon Ginger is a standout — actually spicy, not just lemony-sweet. Chamomile is calming without tasting like hay. If you're rotating one cup per day, you'll hit something different nearly every morning for a month.

## Where It Falls Short

Two honest gaps. First, the individual wrappers don't list caffeine content. Black and green teas are caffeinated; herbals aren't — but you'll have to check the included flavor guide or memorize the lineup to know which is which. If you're sensitive to caffeine, that's a minor annoyance at 6 AM.

Second, intensity varies. Stash leans toward refined, approachable flavors. If you want a bold, heavy-bodied black tea or an aggressively floral jasmine, some of these will taste muted. That's a feature for most drinkers, but a limitation for the "strong tea or nothing" crowd.
