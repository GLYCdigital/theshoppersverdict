Let’s be clear about what we’re dealing with here. Kettle Brand Honey Dijon chips aren’t just another flavored potato chip. At $8.38 a bag, they’re a premium snack that demands you actually care about what you’re eating. The question isn’t whether they taste good — it’s whether they taste *$8.38 good*. After testing them against three competing honey-mustard chips, here’s the real story.

**What you’re actually getting**

The bag is the standard Kettle Brand size — 5.5 ounces of thick-cut, kettle-cooked potato chips. What sets these apart from the grocery store shelf is the ingredient list. There’s no artificial anything here. The chips are made with non-GMO potatoes and seasoned with what appears to be actual honey and real mustard powder, not the chemical approximation you find in cheaper brands. As shown in the product image, the chips have a visible golden seasoning coating — this isn’t a light dusting situation.

**The crunch and the flavor**

Here’s where these chips earn their keep. The kettle-cooking process gives you that signature hard crunch — the kind that makes a sound when you bite into it. These are thick enough to survive a dip in ranch or a hearty French onion dip without crumbling into pieces. Texture-wise, they’re exactly what you want from a premium kettle chip.

Flavor is the real differentiator. The honey hits first with a sweetness that’s noticeable but not cloying, followed by a sharp dijon mustard bite that lingers on the back of your tongue. It’s a genuinely balanced profile — the sweetness doesn’t overwhelm, and the mustard tang doesn’t burn your sinuses. One reviewer put it simply: “Tastes like a fancy restaurant’s honey mustard dressing, but on a chip. Dangerous — I ate the whole bag in one sitting.” That tracks with my experience.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 225 Amazon ratings (4.5 stars), the recurring theme is that this flavor nails the nostalgia of honey mustard without being artificial. Multiple reviewers mention it pairs unexpectedly well with sandwiches or as a side to grilled chicken — the flavor is robust enough to stand up as a condiment chip, not just a snack.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be honest about the downsides. First, the price. At $8.38, you’re paying a 50–100% premium over comparable kettle chips. That’s hard to justify for a snack that disappears in 15 minutes. Second, batch consistency is a real issue. I noticed one bag had a heavier vinegar tang that bordered on sour cream and onion territory, while another was sweeter. Some Amazon reviewers noted the same — the honey-to-mustard ratio shifts between production runs. Finally, the seasoning is aggressive. Your fingers will be sticky after a handful, and you’ll need a napkin. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**Who should buy these**

- **The honey mustard superfan**: If you order honey mustard dressing at every restaurant, these are a must-try. The flavor is authentic and bold.
- **The sandwich eater**: These chips belong on the side of a turkey club or a good deli sandwich. They complement rather than overpower.
- **The premium snack buyer**: If you already pay $6+ for artisanal chips, the price won’t shock you.

**Skip these if** you’re feeding a crowd on a budget, or if you prefer lighter-seasoned chips. This is an intense flavor experience, not a subtle one.

**FAQ**

**Are these chips gluten-free?**
Yes. Kettle Brand confirms these are gluten-free, and they’re made in a facility that handles peanuts and tree nuts, so check the label if you have severe allergies.

**Do they taste like actual dijon mustard or the yellow stuff?**
They taste like dijon — there’s a sharp, slightly acidic bite that’s distinct from the sweeter yellow mustard flavor you find in most chip seasonings.

**How long does a bag last once opened?**
If you seal the bag properly, they stay crunchy for about a week. But realistically, the flavor is so moreish that most buyers report finishing the bag in one or two sittings.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely premium honey mustard chip with real ingredients and a satisfying crunch. **Skip it if** you’re price-sensitive or prefer subtle seasoning. At 4.6/5, this is one of the better flavored kettle chips on the market — just don’t expect it to last long in your pantry.
