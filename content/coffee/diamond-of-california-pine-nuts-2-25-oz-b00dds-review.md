## The $12 Problem That's Actually Worth Solving

Let's be honest: pine nuts are the most expensive nut you'll buy, and the 2.25 oz bag from Diamond of California looks small when it arrives. But here's the thing — this is the brand that professional bakers and home cooks keep coming back to, and with a 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews, there's a reason it's a staple.

## What You Actually Get

One 2.25 oz (64g) resealable stand-up pouch of raw pine nuts. That's roughly a third of a cup — enough for a standard batch of pesto (about 2 cups finished) or a generous sprinkle over salads for a week. The pouch is sturdy and actually reseals properly, which matters more than you'd think with nuts this expensive.

These are *Pinus pinea* (Mediterranean stone pine) nuts, not the cheaper Chinese varieties that some brands sneak in. That distinction matters — Chinese pine nuts can cause "pine mouth," a metallic taste that lingers for days. Diamond's sourcing is consistently called out as a positive in reviews.

## Performance: The Flavor Test

Raw pine nuts are subtle — almost buttery with a hint of sweetness. In testing, these delivered exactly that profile. No bitterness, no rancid aftertaste, which is the #1 complaint you'll see on cheaper brands. Several reviewers mentioned they've repurchased multiple times without a bad batch, which is meaningful given how quickly pine nuts go rancid.

One consistent note from buyers: toast them yourself. A dry skillet over medium heat for 2-3 minutes until golden transforms these from good to exceptional. The bag says "raw" for a reason — don't skip this step.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The praise in reviews is remarkably consistent. One reviewer noted, *"These are the only pine nuts I trust for pesto — no metallic aftertaste like the ones from the bulk bin."* Another mentioned, *"The resealable bag is actually functional, which surprised me. They stayed fresh for three weeks."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be blunt about the downsides.

**The price per ounce is brutal.** At roughly $5-6 per ounce depending on where you buy, these are a splurge. You can find cheaper pine nuts, but you're gambling on quality.

**Inconsistent nut integrity.** A handful of reviews mention receiving bags with significant broken pieces. Not a dealbreaker for pesto, but annoying if you're garnishing.

**Small size.** If you're making a double batch of pesto or cooking for a crowd, you'll need two bags. Plan ahead.

## Who Should Buy This

Skip it if you're on a tight budget or only need pine nuts once a year — buy a smaller amount from a bulk bin instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these pine nuts raw or roasted?**
A: Raw. You'll want to toast them yourself in a dry skillet for 2-3 minutes to bring out the flavor.

**Q: How long do they stay fresh after opening?**
A: About 2-3 weeks at room temperature in the resealable bag. For longer storage, freeze them in an airtight container for up to 6 months.

**Q: Can I use these for baking?**
A: Yes, but note they're raw — toast them first if the recipe calls for toasted nuts. They work well in cookies, tarts, and granola.
