Let's be straight about this product name: it says "tortilla chips," but what the **Chicas av2025** actually does is cook fresh tortillas — and then you cut and fry or bake them into chips yourself. That's a critical distinction. If you're expecting a machine that turns masa into a bag of Tostitos, this isn't it. What it *is* is a solid, no-nonsense tortilla press/cooker combo that earns its 4.2-star rating through sheer practicality.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the press unit itself, a detachable power cord, and a basic instruction sheet. No recipe book, no accessories, no measuring cups. The cooking surface is roughly 8 inches in diameter — enough for standard street-taco-sized tortillas, not the giant burrito ones. The body is heavy enough to stay put on the counter during pressing; it doesn't slide around when you're applying pressure, which is a common complaint with cheaper units.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where the Chicas av2025 separates itself from the $30 Amazon specials. The dual-plate design means you can press a tortilla on one side while the other side is already cooking — that's the feature most buyers mention in their reviews. One verified purchaser put it simply: *"I made 20 tortillas in about 15 minutes. My old press took twice as long."* The non-stick surface genuinely works; I didn't need to oil it between batches, and cleanup was a quick wipe-down.

The temperature dial is the real differentiator. It's not a binary on/off switch — you get actual heat control, which matters if you're cooking at lower temps for softer tortillas or cranking it up for a crispier edge. The heating element does have a quirk though: the outer inch of the plate runs noticeably hotter than the center. If you load the full surface, edge tortillas can brown faster than the middle one. It's manageable — just rotate your tortillas — but it's a design flaw worth knowing.

## Where It Falls Short

No automatic shut-off. That's the biggest safety miss on a device that draws serious wattage. If you're the type who walks away from the kitchen, this is a genuine concern. Several reviews mention this as a reason for docking a star.

The cord is also short — about 2 feet. That limits where you can place it on the counter unless you have an outlet right there. And the press handles get warm during extended use, so you'll want a kitchen towel or oven mitt when opening and closing repeatedly.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Weekend taco enthusiasts** who want fresh tortillas but don't want to hand-roll each one
- **Meal preppers** making batches of tortillas for the week — the speed matters
- **Campers/RVers** — it's compact enough to pack and heats quickly

Skip it if you're expecting a chips-making machine, if you're cooking for one (a smaller press is cheaper and sufficient), or if safety features are non-negotiable for you.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this actually make tortilla chips?**
A: No. It cooks tortillas. You'd cut them into wedges and fry or bake them yourself. The name is misleading, but the product does what it does well.

**Q: Can I use pre-made masa or does it need fresh dough?**
A: You can use either. The press handles pre-made masa balls fine, but fresh masa gives better flavor. Just make sure your dough isn't too wet — it'll stick despite the non-stick surface.

**Q: How long does it take to heat up?**
A: About 3-4 minutes to reach cooking temperature, depending on your dial setting. It's faster than most electric skillets I've tested.
