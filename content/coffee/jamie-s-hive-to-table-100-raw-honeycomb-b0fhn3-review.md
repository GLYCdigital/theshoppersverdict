If you've ever watched a beekeeper pull a dripping frame from a hive and thought, "I want that in my coffee," this is the closest you'll get without a bee suit. Jamie's Hive To Table delivers exactly what the label promises: chunks of honeycomb suspended in raw honey, straight from the apiary. It's not a gimmick — it's a genuinely different way to sweeten your morning brew.

## What's Actually in the Jar

Open the lid and you're hit with a deep, earthy floral aroma that processed supermarket honey simply doesn't have. The jar contains a solid block of honeycomb — real beeswax cells filled with honey — surrounded by liquid raw honey that's naturally runny at room temperature. As shown in the product image, the comb pieces are substantial, not tiny shards. Expect a mix of light and dark comb sections, which indicates genuine batch variety.

Each jar weighs in at 14 ounces net. The comb itself is soft enough to cut with a butter knife when the jar is warm, but firms up noticeably in cooler conditions. You get the honey, the wax, the pollen, and the propolis — nothing filtered, nothing pasteurized.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The obvious use case is coffee, and honestly, this is where it shines. Drop a spoonful of comb into a hot mug of black coffee and stir. The honey dissolves slowly while the wax softens. You get a gentle, floral sweetness that builds rather than hits all at once. The wax floats on top, and you can either fish it out or chew it — chewing releases more honey and leaves a pleasant, faintly sweet aftertaste.

For toast, oatmeal, or cheese boards, it's equally solid. The comb adds texture and a mild crunch that liquid honey lacks. One buyer noted, *"I put a chunk on sourdough with butter and it was transcendent — the wax melts slightly and the honey runs everywhere."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The price per ounce is steep compared to a standard jar of raw honey — you're paying a premium for the comb and the novelty. If you just want honey for everyday sweetening, this is overkill.

Texture is the bigger issue. The wax is chewy and some buyers find it unpleasant. One review summed it up: *"The honey is fantastic but the wax gets stuck in my teeth and I'm not a fan of chewing it."* If you're not prepared to chew wax, you'll be fishing comb out of your coffee cup every morning.

Batch consistency is also a minor complaint. Some jars arrive with honey that's partially crystallized, making the comb harder to cut cleanly. It's not a defect — raw honey crystallizes naturally — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I eat the wax?**
Yes, the entire comb is edible. The wax has no flavor and simply provides texture. Chew it like gum, then swallow or spit it out — your choice.

**Q: How do I store it after opening?**
Keep it at room temperature, tightly sealed. Don't refrigerate — cold accelerates crystallization and makes the comb brittle. It doesn't spoil, but it will eventually crystallize over months.

**Q: Is this actually different from honey with honeycomb pieces mixed in?**
Yes. This is solid comb suspended in liquid honey from the same harvest. Cheaper products use comb bits mixed into regular honey, which loses the structural integrity and the slow-release sweetness.
