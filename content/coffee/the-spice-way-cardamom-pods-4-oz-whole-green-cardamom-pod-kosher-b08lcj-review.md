If you're grinding cardamom straight into your coffee beans or simmering it in chai, you already know the difference between fresh whole pods and that dusty pre-ground stuff. The Spice Way's 4 oz bag of whole green cardamom sits firmly on the fresh side — and for the price, it's hard to beat.

**What you're actually getting**

The bag contains 4 ounces of whole green pods — roughly 300 to 350 individual pods depending on size. As shown in the product image, they arrive in a kraft paper bag with a clear window so you can see the pods before opening. The pods are plump and firm, not brittle or crushed. That matters because the essential oils that give cardamom its sweet, eucalyptus-tinged punch live in the seeds inside those pods. Once crushed, those oils start evaporating. Whole pods keep them sealed.

I'll be direct about the packaging: there's no zipper. The bag is heat-sealed on arrival, but once you open it, you need your own clip or a transfer to a glass jar. Cardamom is one of those spices that goes flat within a few months of opening if left exposed. Plan accordingly.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these three ways: cracked into a French press with medium-roast coffee, simmered whole in milk for chai, and ground fresh for a spice rub.

For coffee, two cracked pods per 12 oz cup delivered that bright, almost citrusy lift without turning the brew bitter. The pods cracked easily under a spoon — no hammer needed. In chai, three pods simmered for ten minutes released enough aromatic depth to make the whole kitchen smell like a café. The seeds inside were dark brown and glossy, which is the sign of a properly dried pod. Faded seeds mean old stock; these were fresh.

**Where it falls short**

Honesty requires me to flag two things. First, the bag isn't resealable — already mentioned, but it's the most common complaint in the reviews. Second, expect a few pale pods in each batch. They're not bad — just less visually uniform. If you're gifting this or plating it for a photo, you'll want to pick those out. Neither issue affects flavor.

Also worth noting: if you need ground cardamom, this isn't your product. These are whole pods. You'll need a mortar and pestle or a spice grinder. That's not a flaw — it's the entire point — but it's worth saying plainly.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home baristas** who add cardamom to their coffee grounds or brew Arabic-style coffee (gahwa). The volume and freshness justify the price.
- **Chai makers** who go through a bag monthly. The pods hold up well in simmering milk.
- **Bakers** who want whole pods for infusing into custards, rice puddings, or syrups.

**Skip it if** you only need a few pods for a single recipe, or if you want pre-ground spice. This is a bulk purchase for regular use.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many pods are in a 4 oz bag?**
A: Roughly 300-350 whole pods. For coffee, using 2-3 per cup, that's about 100 cups of cardamom-infused brew.

**Q: Do these pods work in a coffee grinder?**
A: Yes. Crack them slightly first, then grind with your coffee beans. The pods grind fine and won't damage a burr grinder. Blade grinders work too.

**Q: How should I store them after opening?**
A: Transfer to an airtight glass jar in a cool, dark cabinet. Avoid the fridge — moisture is the enemy. Properly stored, they'll stay potent for 6-8 months.
