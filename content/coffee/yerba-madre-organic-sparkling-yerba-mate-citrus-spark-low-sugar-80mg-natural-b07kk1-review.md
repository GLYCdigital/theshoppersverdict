## The Coffee Alternative That Actually Feels Different

Let's be direct: if you're looking at Yerba Madre Citrus Spark, you're probably tired of coffee's acid stomach, afternoon crash, or the 300mg buzz-and-burn of energy drinks. This 12oz can delivers 80mg of caffeine from organic yerba mate — roughly the same as a strong cup of coffee, but with a completely different delivery curve. The mate caffeine binds to other plant compounds, which means a slower release. No spike, no sudden drop. Several reviewers describe it as "smooth focus" rather than "wired."

## What You Get in the Pack

The most common listing is a 12-pack of 12oz slim cans. Each can carries 80mg natural caffeine, 25 calories, and 4g of sugar — sweetened with organic stevia. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: carbonated water, organic yerba mate, organic lemon juice, organic lime juice, organic stevia, natural flavors. That's it. No artificial colors, no preservatives, no "natural flavors" hiding mystery compounds. It's fair trade certified and USDA organic, which matters if you're particular about sourcing.

## Real-World Performance

I'll be honest about the first sip: the carbonation hits hard. This is a strongly carbonated drink, and multiple Amazon reviews note the same thing. One reviewer wrote, "The fizz is intense at first — almost like a soda that was shaken. Give it a few minutes and it calms down." If you're expecting a gentle sparkling water, adjust expectations. It's closer to a craft soda in carbonation level.

The citrus flavor is genuine — lemon and lime come through clearly, not artificial. The stevia aftertaste is present but mild, especially if you drink it cold. I found it works best over ice, which also helps tame the carbonation. The flavor profile is tart, slightly bitter from the mate, and dry on the finish. It's not sweet like a soft drink, and it's not as bitter as plain brewed mate.

## Where It Falls Short

The 3.7-star average reflects real issues. The most common complaint is the carbonation level — it's genuinely aggressive for a mate drink and can trigger hiccups or indigestion in sensitive people. If you're prone to bloating from carbonated drinks, this might not be your daily driver.

The price is the second friction point. At roughly $2.50-3.00 per can, it's expensive as a daily coffee replacement. A 12-pack runs $30-36 depending on seller. Subscribe & save helps, but it's not cheap.

A smaller subset of reviewers mention the stevia aftertaste as a dealbreaker. If you're sensitive to stevia's bitter-metallic note, this will bother you more than the caffeine curve helps.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a coffee alternative with real caffeine but fewer side effects, you prioritize organic and fair trade sourcing, or you're trying to reduce sugar but don't want artificial sweeteners. It's also solid for afternoon pick-me-ups when a full coffee would wreck your evening sleep — 80mg is moderate enough to metabolize by bedtime.

**Skip it if:** You hate carbonated beverages, you're sensitive to stevia, or you need caffeine in large doses. This won't replace a pre-workout or a triple espresso. It's also not ideal if you're on a tight budget — coffee is considerably cheaper per serving.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Is this actually healthy, or is it just marketing?**
The evidence is solid here. Organic yerba mate has antioxidant and polyphenol content, and at 80mg caffeine per can, you're within moderate intake guidelines. The 4g sugar and 25 calories are genuinely low. The main health consideration is the carbonation and stevia — neither is harmful, but both can bother sensitive stomachs.

**How does the caffeine compare to coffee?**
A standard 8oz coffee has 80-100mg. This can has 80mg, but the delivery is slower because mate contains theobromine and other compounds that modulate caffeine absorption. Most reviewers report feeling more even energy for longer. If you need a rapid wake-up, coffee wins. If you need sustained focus, mate is better.

**Does it taste like traditional yerba mate?**
Partially. You'll notice the mate's characteristic vegetal, slightly bitter backbone, but the lemon and lime dominate. It's significantly more approachable than drinking loose-leaf mate, but it's not a citrus soda. If you've never had mate, think of it as a tart, lightly sweet sparkling tea with more complexity.
