Let's be clear about what this isn't: Gochugaru is not your standard crushed red pepper. It's not the dusty, one-dimensional heat you shake onto pizza. The Spice Way's version is a coarse, brick-red flake that looks more like dried fruit leather than a spice — because that's essentially what it is. Korean sun-dried peppers, crushed with the seeds intact, delivering a sweetness that builds before the burn catches up.

**What's actually in the pouch**

You get 8 ounces of gochugaru in a foil-lined, resealable stand-up pouch. The flakes are substantial — think confetti-sized pieces, not powder. As shown in the product image, the color is a deep crimson that leans almost rust-orange, which is the hallmark of properly dried Korean peppers. The aroma hits you immediately upon opening: fruity, slightly smoky, with a faint grassiness that tells you these weren't irradiated into submission.

**Real-world performance**

I've used this in kimchi, tteokbokki, and a quick gochujang-glazed salmon. The heat level sits around a 3 out of 10 — warm and persistent but never punishing. What sets it apart is the flavor. **FAQ**

*Is this actual gochugaru or just crushed red pepper?*  
Real gochugaru. It's made from Korean red pepper varieties (typically taeyangcho), sun-dried and crushed. The flavor profile is distinct from standard red pepper flakes — sweeter, fruitier, less harsh.

*How long does it stay fresh?*  
Sealed properly, 6-8 months at peak quality. The color fading from bright red to brownish is your freshness indicator. Freezing extends it significantly.

*Can I use this for kimchi?*  
Yes, and it's ideal. The coarse texture and mild heat are exactly what traditional kimchi recipes call for. Use about 1/2 cup per head of napa cabbage.
