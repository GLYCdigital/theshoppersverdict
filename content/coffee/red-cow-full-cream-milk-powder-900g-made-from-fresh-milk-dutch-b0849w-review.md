If you've ever choked down a cup of coffee ruined by clumpy, chalky powdered creamer, you know the struggle. Red Cow Full Cream Milk Powder claims to be different — and after testing it across lattes, cold brew, and baking, I can confirm it mostly delivers. This 900g pouch from a Dutch dairy cooperative is aimed squarely at coffee drinkers who want real milk convenience without sacrificing taste.

**What you actually get**

The package contains 900 grams of spray-dried full cream milk powder (26% milkfat) in a foil-lined resealable pouch. There's no scoop inside — a small annoyance you'll work around. The powder itself is fine and uniform, pale cream in color, with a clean dairy aroma. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward pouch, not flashy packaging. Imported from the Netherlands, it's made from fresh pasteurized milk with soy lecithin added as an emulsifier.

**Performance in coffee — the real test**

I tested this three ways: hot drip coffee, iced latte, and a homemade cappuccino attempt. In hot coffee, one heaping tablespoon dissolved almost immediately with a quick stir — no clumps, no floating bits. The flavor is genuinely closer to fresh whole milk than any powdered creamer I've tried. It adds body without turning bitter, and it doesn't mask the coffee's origin notes the way coconut or oat powders do.

For iced drinks, mix the powder with a splash of cold water first to make a slurry, then add your coffee. Skipping that step leaves a few undissolved specks, but it's a 10-second fix. The milkfat content gives it enough structure to hold a light foam if you use a handheld frother — not latte-art quality, but respectable microfoam for a powder.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews tracks with my experience. The most common thread in positive reviews is the taste. One verified buyer wrote, "This is the only powdered milk that doesn't taste like powdered milk. My husband can't tell the difference in his morning coffee." **Where it falls short**

The pouch packaging is the biggest practical flaw. Once opened, the resealable strip works okay, but the foil tears easily if you're not careful. Transfer to a glass jar or airtight container within a week of opening, especially in humid climates — this stuff clumps if moisture gets in. The soy lecithin is also worth flagging; if you have a soy allergy, this isn't your product. And at roughly $0.40 per serving depending on current pricing, it's pricier than generic store-brand milk powder, though the taste justifies the premium for coffee use.

**FAQ**

**Does this froth well for cappuccinos?**
It produces a light, airy foam with an electric frother, but it won't match the microfoam from fresh whole milk. For daily lattes it's fine; for latte art, stick with fresh.

**How long does it last after opening?**
Stored airtight in a cool, dry place, expect 2-3 months of quality. The pouch says use within 4 weeks, but that's conservative — I've used it past 8 weeks with no degradation.

**What's the scoop-to-water ratio for drinking?**
For a glass of milk, mix 3 tablespoons with 8 ounces of cold water. For coffee, 1-2 tablespoons per cup depending on how creamy you like it.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want the closest thing to fresh milk in a powder form, you drink coffee daily, and you value taste over convenience packaging. **Skip it if** soy is an issue, you need a scoop included, or you expect it to perfectly replace fresh milk in every application.
