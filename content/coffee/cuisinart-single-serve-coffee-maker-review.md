# Cuisinart Single Serve Coffee Maker Review: 72oz Pod Machine Tested

The single-serve coffee market is dominated by one name: Keurig. But Cuisinart — a brand with decades of kitchen cred — has been chipping away at that dominance with machines that promise **better build quality, larger reservoirs, and more features** at competitive prices.

The **Cuisinart Single Serve Coffee Maker** (model SS-10) is their flagship entry. With a **72-ounce reservoir** — nearly double most Keurig machines — and over **19,000 Amazon reviews** at **4.2 stars**, it's the most credible Keurig alternative on the market.

At **$121.99**, it's priced similarly to a mid-range Keurig. The question: does Cuisinart's bet on build quality over brand recognition pay off?

## Who Should Buy This

**High-volume pod drinkers** who go through multiple cups a day. The 72-ounce reservoir holds enough water for 8-12 cups before refilling — significantly more than the 40-60 ounce tanks on most Keurig machines. You'll refill once, maybe twice a week instead of daily.

**Build-quality conscious buyers** who notice the difference between a flimsy-feeling machine and one with heft. Multiple reviewers mention that the Cuisinart "has a much more solid feel" compared to Keurig machines. It's heavier, the lid mechanism feels tighter, and the drip tray is sturdier.

**K-Cup and ground coffee switchers** who want both options without buying a separate brewer. The included reusable filter cup lets you use your own grounds. It's not as elegant as the K-Cup system, but it's there when you need it.

**Detail-oriented coffee drinkers** who want control over brew temperature and cup size. This machine offers multiple temperature settings and six brew sizes from 6 to 12 ounces.

## Who Should Skip This

**Light sleepers** with open-concept homes. This machine is loud. Reviewers consistently describe the noise level as a downside. One 5-star reviewer said plainly: *"Yes it is loud-ish... but what isn't plastic."* Expect a noticeable pump-and-heat cycle sound.

**Minimalists** who want a simple "press and go" experience. The Cuisinart is feature-rich, which means more buttons, more menu options, and more things that can go wrong or require attention.

**Keurig ecosystem loyalists** who value the sheer variety of K-Cup brands. Cuisinart's machine works with all standard K-Cups, so compatibility isn't an issue — but the overall user experience is different.

## Build Quality: Where Cuisinart Wins

The single most common thread in positive reviews is the **physical quality** of the machine. Where Keurig machines can feel hollow and plasticky, the Cuisinart SS-10 has genuine heft. The water tank is thick-walled, the brew head seals with a satisfying click, and the drip tray doesn't wobble.

One reviewer, a former Keurig user, put it succinctly: *"The quality is superb compared to the Keurig brand makers. It has a much more solid feel and better quality insides so to speak."*

Made in Vietnam (noted on the packaging), the build quality reflects Cuisinart's kitchen-appliance heritage. This is a brand that makes stand mixers and food processors — they know how to build things that last.

## The 72-Ounce Reservoir: Real Convenience

This is the headline feature. The **72-ounce water tank** means you can brew 6-ounce cups all day without a refill. For a household of two daily coffee drinkers, that's about 5-6 days between refills.

The reservoir is removable and dishwasher-safe, which makes cleaning easy. It also has a nice integrated handle for carrying to the sink.

One caveat: the tank sits on the back of the machine, which adds to the overall depth. Make sure you have counter depth to spare — this machine needs about 10 inches front to back.

## Brew Performance: Consistent and Hot

The Cuisinart SS-10 brews at **195-205°F** — the optimal range for coffee extraction. The water is visibly hot during brewing (you can see steam rising), and the resulting cup is served at drinking temperature immediately.

Brew size options:
- **6 oz** — strong, concentrated brew, ideal for small mugs
- **8 oz** — standard cup size
- **10 oz** — generous single serving
- **12 oz** — large travel mug size

The temperature setting can be adjusted. This matters for different roast levels — lighter roasts benefit from higher brewing temperatures, while darker roasts can tolerate (and sometimes prefer) slightly lower temps.

The included **reusable filter cup** works with ground coffee and produces a decent cup — not quite as good as a dedicated drip brewer, but respectable for a pod machine. The key is using a medium-fine grind and not overfilling.

## The Noise: The Real Downside

Let's talk about the elephant in the room. The Cuisinart SS-10 is **audible**. During the heating and brewing cycle, it produces a humming-pumping sound that's louder than most Keurig machines. One reviewer described it as "a little noise when making a cup of coffee, but it is consistent."

Is it a dealbreaker? For most people, no. Coffee makers make noise. But if you're in a quiet environment — early morning before others wake up, or in a shared office — it's noticeable enough to mention.

## Long-Term Reliability

With 19,000+ reviews, we have a solid picture of long-term reliability. The majority of Cuisinart SS-10 owners are satisfied years into ownership. When issues occur, they tend to be:

- **Pump failure** after heavy use (2-3+ years) — the most common repair
- **Descaling needs** — harder water areas require more frequent descaling cycles
- **LCD display issues** — some units develop dim or unreadable displays over time

The machine has an integrated rinse cycle that helps with maintenance. Cuisinart recommends descaling every 3-6 months depending on water hardness.
