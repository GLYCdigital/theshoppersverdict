If you've ever fought a stuck Moen cartridge for an hour with needle-nose pliers, only to lose and call a plumber, this little tool is about to become your best friend. The Moen UT3322 is a niche product that does exactly one thing — and does it well enough to earn a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings.

## What You Actually Get

The UT3322 is a cartridge puller, plain and simple. It's a T-handle tool roughly 6 inches long with a threaded tip that screws directly into the cartridge stem. There's no storage case, no extra bits, no instructions booklet worth mentioning — just the tool itself with a small hang hole for pegboard storage.

What matters is the business end. The threads are precision-cut to match Moen's cartridge stems exactly. That's the entire game here — if the threads don't catch, the tool is useless. Based on the ratings, Moen got this right.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this tool earns its keep. The threaded tip screws into the cartridge, then you pull straight out using the T-handle. The mechanical advantage is significant — what used to require twisting, rocking, and swearing now comes out with steady, even pressure.

One reviewer put it plainly: *"I fought a stuck cartridge for two hours with every tool I owned. This thing popped it out in under a minute."* That's not an isolated experience. The common thread across positive reviews is that the UT3322 turns a two-hour frustration into a five-minute job.

The tool works on Moen Chateau single-handle faucets and positive shut-off cartridges. If you've got a Moen faucet from the 1980s or 1990s — which are still everywhere — this is likely the exact tool you need.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the limitations. First, this is a one-trick pony. It only fits Moen Chateau cartridges. If your faucet is a different brand or even a different Moen model, you're out of luck. Check your faucet model before buying.

Second, the handle is plastic. It's sturdy enough for the job — nobody's reporting breakage — but at this price point, some shoppers expect all-metal construction. It's a minor gripe, but worth noting if you plan to use this professionally. The plastic handle also means you can't apply brute-force torque; you're relying on smooth, steady pulling rather than yanking.

Finally, it won't help if the cartridge stem is already broken off flush with the valve body. That scenario requires a different extraction tool entirely.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my Moen 1225 cartridge?**
A: Yes, the 1225 cartridge is the standard Chateau positive shut-off cartridge, and this tool is designed for exactly that. It also works with 1222 and 1224 cartridges.

**Q: Can I use this with a pull-out sprayer faucet?**
A: No. The UT3322 is specifically for Chateau series and single-handle positive shut-off cartridges. Pull-out models use different cartridges that require other extraction methods.

**Q: Do I need to turn off the water before using this?**
A: Absolutely. Always shut off the water supply at the shut-off valves under the sink before removing any cartridge. The tool removes the cartridge, but you'll still have water pressure in the lines.
