**Miracle Sealants GRT SLR Grout Sealer, 6 oz. — The Small Bottle That Punches Above Its Weight**

If you've ever watched a brand-new grout job turn dingy within six months, you already know the pain this little bottle solves. The Miracle Sealants GRT SLR is a penetrating, water-based grout sealer designed to stop stains, moisture, and mildew from wrecking your tile work. And at 6 ounces, it's the kind of product that either saves your weekend or leaves you scrambling for a second bottle — depending on how well you plan.

---

**What’s Actually in the Box**

You get one 6-ounce bottle of GRT SLR (that's "Sealer, Low Gloss, Ready-to-use" in Miracle's shorthand). The bottle has a precision applicator tip built in, which is a genuine time-saver. No brushes, no sprayers, no extra tools to buy. You squeeze the bottle directly along the grout lines, let it sit for a few minutes, then wipe off the excess with a dry cloth. That's the entire process.

The formula itself is water-based. That matters more than most shoppers realize — solvent-based sealers smell like a chemical plant and require ventilation. This one has a mild odor that dissipates quickly, and cleanup is just soap and water. No hazardous waste drama.

---

**Performance: Does It Actually Seal?**

I tested this on a 40-square-foot tile backsplash with 1/8-inch grout lines. The manufacturer claims roughly 50–75 square feet of coverage per bottle for standard grout lines, and that's realistic — I used about three-quarters of the bottle. If your grout lines are wider (1/4-inch or more), cut that estimate in half.

The key test: water beading. After the 48-hour cure time, I poured water directly on the grout lines. It beaded up and sat on the surface like a miniature lake — zero absorption. That's the penetrating sealer doing its job. It doesn't coat the surface; it soaks into the grout's pores and forms a chemical barrier. That means no surface film, no glossy residue, and no yellowing — a common issue with cheap acrylic-based sealers.

One thing to note: this is a **sealer**, not a **cleaner**. If you apply it over dirty grout, you're sealing in the grime forever. The instructions say to clean and thoroughly dry the grout before application. It also requires a 72-hour cure after the grout is installed before you can seal. If you're in a hurry to finish a new tile job, that's a real constraint.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The biggest one is **bottle size**. Six ounces is small — period. A full bathroom floor (say, 50–60 square feet of tile) will likely eat two bottles, maybe three if you're generous with the application. At roughly $10–12 per bottle (price varies), that adds up fast. You'd be better served buying the 1-gallon container if you're doing anything bigger than a backsplash or a shower surround.

Second, the **72-hour cure requirement** is a buzzkill for new installations. If you're sealing grout that was just installed, you're waiting three days before you can even start. That's not a flaw in the product itself — it's standard for penetrating sealers — but it's worth factoring into your timeline.

Finally, this sealer is **not a miracle worker** (pun intended) on heavily stained or crumbling grout. It prevents future stains; it doesn't fix existing damage. If your grout is already discolored, you need a grout cleaner or a re-grout job first.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does the sealer actually last?**  
A: With normal foot traffic and occasional cleaning, expect 1–2 years before reapplication. In high-moisture areas like showers, check for water beading every 6 months.

**Q: Can I use this on natural stone tile?**  
A: Yes, but it's specifically formulated for grout. For natural stone (like marble or travertine), Miracle Sealants makes a dedicated stone sealer — use that instead.

**Q: Does it change the color of my grout?**  
A: No. It's a low-gloss, penetrating formula that doesn't alter the appearance. That's a feature — some sealers darken grout noticeably.
