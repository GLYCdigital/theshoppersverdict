**Bottom line up front:** The hykolity LED Wall Pack Light delivers commercial-grade illumination at a price that undercuts most electrical supply houses. If you need to flood a driveway, barn, or loading area with light and want zero maintenance for years, this is a serious contender. But it's not the right fixture for every situation — here's the honest breakdown.

## What's actually in the box

The fixture arrives fully assembled. You get the wall pack itself — a rectangular die-cast aluminum housing measuring roughly 12.5 x 8 x 5 inches — plus a pre-wired photocell sensor, mounting hardware, and a wire nut kit. There's no bulb to install because the LEDs are integrated into the unit. That's the double-edged sword: no replacement cost, but when the LEDs eventually die (rated at 50,000 hours, roughly 17 years at 8 hours/night), you replace the whole fixture.

Wiring is straightforward — three wires (black, white, green) into a standard junction box. The included gasket seals against the wall, so you don't need extra caulk in most installations.

## Performance: this thing is unapologetically bright

At 120W and 15,000 lumens, this isn't a subtle accent light. As one buyer put it, "It lights up my entire backyard like a stadium. My neighbors two houses down commented on it." The 5000K daylight color temperature is crisp and clean — great for security cameras that need to capture detail, but not cozy.

The beam spread is wide and even, covering roughly a 30-foot radius from a 15-foot mounting height. I've seen these used on commercial properties and residential garages alike, and the coverage pattern doesn't leave dark corners like some cheaper wall packs that throw a hot spot in the center and fade to nothing at the edges.

The photocell has been reliable in most reports. It kicks on at true dusk and shuts off at dawn without flickering or cycling. One long-term reviewer noted: "Installed in January, it's now August and I haven't touched it once. Works exactly as advertised."

## Build quality that justifies the price

The die-cast aluminum housing feels substantial — not the flimsy stamped steel you see on budget fixtures. The tempered glass lens is sealed with a rubber gasket, and the fixture carries an IP65 waterproof rating. Multiple buyers report installations in coastal Florida and Pacific Northwest rain zones with zero moisture intrusion after a year-plus. That's the kind of real-world durability that separates a $50 fixture from a $150 one.

Heat management is handled by the aluminum fins on the back. Even after running all night in summer heat, the housing stays warm but not scalding — a good sign for LED longevity.

## Where it falls short

No fixture is perfect, and here's the honest tradeoff list:

**The photocell is fixed.** You can't adjust the sensitivity. In snowy regions, reflected light off fresh snow can keep the fixture off until it gets truly dark. Same issue if you have a bright security light nearby. It's a minor annoyance, but worth knowing.

**No motion sensor option.** This is always-on from dusk to dawn. For a residential backyard, that's 12 hours of full-blast light every night. If you want motion-activated lighting, look at a different fixture — this isn't it.

**The 5000K color temperature is harsh.** It's excellent for security and work areas, but if you're lighting a porch where you want ambiance, this will feel clinical.

**Mounting considerations.** The fixture is designed for wall mounting with the light projecting outward. If you need to mount it under an eave or soffit, the photocell placement may cause it to read ambient light incorrectly. Check your mounting location before you buy.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You need reliable, bright, always-on exterior lighting for a driveway, parking area, barn, workshop exterior, or commercial property. You want a fixture you can install once and forget for a decade. You value the security benefit of a well-lit perimeter.

**Skip it if:** You want motion-activated lighting, need adjustable photocell sensitivity, or you're lighting a cozy residential porch where 15,000 lumens would be overkill. Also skip if you're in an area with strict dark-sky ordinances — this throws a lot of light.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this indoors?**
Technically yes, but it's overkill and not designed for indoor aesthetics. Stick to exterior use.

**Q: What's the actual power draw?**
It draws 120W at 120V. That's roughly 0.1 kWh per hour of operation — about $4-5 per month if it runs 12 hours nightly, depending on your local rates.

**Q: Does it come with a warranty?**
Yes, hykolity backs it with a 5-year limited warranty. That's solid for the price point.

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The hykolity LED Wall Pack Light is a workhorse. It's bright, well-built, and priced to move. Just confirm your mounting location and light sensitivity needs before you commit.
