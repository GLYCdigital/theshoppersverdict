If you've ever tried polishing a ring or pendant with a cheap single-speed grinder, you know the problem: too fast and you burn the metal or fling the piece across the room. Too slow isn't an option because most budget grinders don't have a slow. The OPAVIOC Jewelry Polisher solves that with genuine variable speed control, and that alone makes it worth a serious look.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the polished aluminum bench grinder body, two buffing wheels (one sewn cotton, one loose muslin), two shaft adapters for different wheel arbor sizes, a wrench, and the LED work light assembly. One thing to note: there's no grinding stone included, despite the "grinder" in the name. This is a dedicated polishing/buffing machine, not a general-purpose grinder. If you need to shape metal, you'll want a separate tool.

The machine measures roughly 12 inches wide and weighs about 8 pounds — compact enough for a small bench without dominating your workspace.

## Real-World Performance

The star feature is the speed dial. At the lowest setting (around 2800 RPM), you can safely polish thin gold plating or work on acrylic without generating enough heat to cause damage. Crank it to max (6800 RPM) and you can knock tarnish off silver or bring a mirror finish to stainless steel in seconds. I found the sweet spot for most jewelry work sits around 3500–4500 RPM, and the motor holds its speed well even under moderate pressure.

The motor runs surprisingly quiet for this class — noticeably smoother than the vibrating, rattling $40 grinders that dominate the budget end of Amazon. It won't replace a $500 Foredom flex shaft for professional jewelers, but for a home workshop or serious hobbyist, the performance-to-price ratio is strong.

One thoughtful detail: the built-in LED light actually points at the work area rather than just glowing vaguely downward. At 4.6/5 across 229 ratings, this is a consistent point of praise from users.

## Where It Falls Short

The included buffing wheels are the weak link. They're thin, and the sewn wheel sheds threads during the first few uses. Budget for aftermarket wheels immediately — a set of quality muslin and felt wheels runs about $15 and transforms the machine's effectiveness.

The dust ports are another mild annoyance. They're sized for the included flex hose but don't match standard shop vac fittings, so if you want dust collection, you'll need an adapter. For a tool that creates fine metal and compound dust, this is a missed opportunity.

Finally, the motor is brush-type, so it's not meant for continuous commercial use. If you're running it for hours daily, expect brush wear — but for hobbyist and light professional use, this is a non-issue.
