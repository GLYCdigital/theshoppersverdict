If you're tired of every ceiling fan looking like it came from the same cookie-cutter catalog, the Progress Lighting Springer Collection DC Motor Windmill Fan is here to break the mold. This isn't just airflow — it's furniture for your ceiling. And with a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it's not just a pretty face either.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box, you're looking at a substantial package. The fan ships with all 12 blades (each with that weathered, reclaimed-wood aesthetic), the DC motor housing, mounting hardware, and a handheld remote control. Note: the light kit is NOT included — more on that later.

The windmill design is the star here. As shown in the product image, those 12 blades radiate outward like a classic farm windmill, finished in a distressed wood tone that pairs well with farmhouse, industrial, and even transitional interiors. The motor housing has a matte black finish that keeps the whole thing from feeling kitschy.

## Performance and Build Quality

The DC motor is where this fan separates from the $150 big-box alternatives. DC motors run cooler, quieter, and more efficiently than AC motors, and this one delivers. On low speed, it's effectively silent — you'll hear the room, not the fan. Even at the top of its 6 speeds, the whir is more "gentle whoosh" than "helicopter takeoff."

Airflow is genuinely impressive for the blade count. The 12 blades move serious volume without the harsh, choppy breeze you get from 3-blade modern fans. The reverse function for winter operation is standard but works flawlessly.

Build quality is solid. The mounting bracket is heavy-gauge steel, and the blades have a protective coating that resists warping and fading. One buyer noted, *"Installed in our screened porch — after 8 months of humidity and direct sun, the blades still look brand new."* That's the kind of durability you want at this price point.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 229 ratings, a few themes emerge. The aesthetic is the #1 reason people buy this fan — and it doesn't disappoint in person. One reviewer wrote, *"Every guest who walks in asks about the fan. It's the focal point of the room."* Another praised the remote: *"The six speeds actually make a difference. Low is perfect for sleeping, high actually cools the whole room."*

Energy efficiency also gets consistent shout-outs. DC motors draw significantly less power than AC motors at equivalent airflow — you'll notice it on your electric bill if you run it daily.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the assembly. Twelve blades means twelve times the screws, twelve times the alignment checks. Plan for a solid 2–3 hour install if you're doing it solo. Multiple reviewers mentioned this. One said, *"It took me an entire evening. Bring a friend and a magnetic screwdriver."*

The light kit being sold separately is a genuine annoyance. At this price point, a basic integrated light shouldn't be an upsell. Factor in an additional $40–60 if you need illumination.

Finally, at the highest speed, some units develop a slight wobble. The included balancing kit addresses it, but it's an extra step you shouldn't have to deal with. One reviewer noted, *"A small weight clip fixed it in five minutes. Minor issue, but annoying at this price."*

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a conversation-piece ceiling fan that performs as well as it looks. It's ideal for farmhouse-style living rooms, covered porches, large bedrooms, or any space where the fan is a design element, not an afterthought.

**Skip it if:** You need a quick, cheap install with an integrated light. For a builder-grade bedroom or rental property, there are simpler options. Also skip if your ceiling is under 8 feet — the windmill profile hangs lower than standard fans.
