The $7.99 paint brush set that has 71,000 Amazon ratings is either a hidden gem or a classic case of "you get what you pay for." After digging through the feedback and the specs, here's the real story.

**What's actually in the box**

You get five brushes: three angle brushes (1-inch, 1.5-inch, 2-inch) and two flat brushes (1-inch, 2-inch). The synthetic bristles are flagged for both latex and oil-based paints, plus stains — which checks out. The ferrules are rust-resistant (good for exterior work), and the handles are unfinished hardwood. Nothing fancy, but at $1.60 per brush, this is a "buy them, use them hard, replace them without guilt" proposition.

**How they actually perform**

The 2-inch angle brush is the workhorse here. The bristle tips are cut clean enough that you can cut in along ceilings and door frames without dragging tape across every edge. Multiple reviewers note the spring-back — the bristles return to shape after being pushed into corners, which is the difference between a $2 brush and a $15 brush. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"I've bought $10 brushes that don't hold a straight line this well."*

The flat brushes handle cabinet work and trim without leaving brush marks, provided you don't overload them. They're not going to rival a high-end Purdy or Wooster, but for a homeowner doing a bedroom repaint or refinishing kitchen cabinets, the finish quality is more than acceptable.

**Where they fall short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The handles are plain wood with no ergonomic contouring — after a few hours of cutting in, your hand will feel it. The bristles do shed a bit on the first pass, so wash them with warm soapy water and let them dry before your first real project. And critically, there's no wide brush here. The largest is 2 inches, which means this kit handles trim, edges, cabinets, and touch-ups — but you'll need a separate roller or a 4-inch brush for broad wall sections.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Can these brushes handle oil-based paint?**
Yes. The synthetic bristles are compatible with both latex and oil-based paints. Just clean them with mineral spirits or paint thinner instead of water when using oil-based products.

**Q: How do they compare to a $15+ brush from a paint store?**
The difference is real but small. Premium brushes hold slightly more paint, have better ergonomic handles, and may shed less initially. For a homeowner doing one to two rooms, the difference won't show up in the final finish.

**Q: Are these good for exterior painting?**
Yes, with a caveat. The synthetic bristles and rust-resistant ferrules handle exterior latex fine. But since they're not premium brushes, you'll likely replace them after a season of heavy exterior use — which at this price, is perfectly fine.
