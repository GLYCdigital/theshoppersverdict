If you've stared at a harsh overhead light while trying to apply makeup or shave, you already know the problem. The RUIYEY three-light vanity bar attacks that issue directly with milky glass shades that scatter light instead of blasting it. At 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. Here's what 30 minutes of install time and a week of daily use taught me.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the main fixture bar, three milky glass shades, a mounting bracket, wire nuts, and mounting screws. The bar measures 19.7 inches wide with a 5.9-inch backplate — sized appropriately for a standard 24- to 36-inch vanity mirror. The shades are the star here: frosted glass that wraps the bulb completely, not just a glass disc in front. That matters because it means light goes sideways and downward, not just straight out at your face.

## Build Quality and Installation Reality

The housing is stamped steel with a powder-coated matte black finish. It's not aircraft-grade aluminum, but for the price point, it feels substantial. The finish has a slight texture that hides water spots — a thoughtful touch for a bathroom environment.

Installation took me 40 minutes, and that includes running to the hardware store for a longer ground screw. The universal bracket slots into standard 4-inch junction boxes. The instructions are minimal but adequate: three wires (hot, neutral, ground), three screws, done. One genuine tip: install the shades *before* wiring if you have tight clearance. The glass shades thread on with a knurled ring, and over-tightening will crack them. Hand-tighten until snug, then stop.

## How It Performs Where It Counts

The RUIYEY uses G9 bi-pin bulbs (not included), and this is where the fixture earns its keep. With three 4-watt LED G9s, the output is roughly equivalent to a 150-watt incandescent — plenty for a single vanity. The milky glass does what it promises: light diffuses evenly across the mirror with zero glare. No shadows under your chin, no squinting at your reflection.

The fixture is also damp-rated, meaning it survives the steam from a hot shower. After a week of daily use in a bathroom with poor ventilation, there's zero corrosion or fogging inside the glass.

## Where It Falls Short

Three honest complaints emerged from my testing and the reviews:

**Bulbs aren't included.** This is the biggest hidden cost. Three quality G9 LED bulbs run $15–25. Factor that into your budget.

**The glass is fragile during install.** One reviewer reported cracking a shade by over-tightening. The replacement process involves contacting the seller, which adds friction.

**No dimmer compatibility guarantee.** While G9 LEDs are generally dimmable, the fixture itself doesn't specify dimmer compatibility. If you want dimming, verify your chosen bulbs support it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners** replacing an outdated 1990s brass bar — the install is straightforward with basic tools
- **Rental property owners** who need a durable, neutral fixture that won't look dated in five years
- **Small bathroom owners** with a single vanity — this provides even, shadow-free lighting without needing a separate sconce setup

**Skip it if** you have a double vanity wider than 60 inches (you'll need two units), or if you insist on smart-home integration — there's no built-in dimming or motion sensing here.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this fixture upside down (shades pointing up)?**
A: Yes, the mounting bracket allows 360-degree rotation, so you can point the shades up, down, or sideways. Just note that pointing up collects more dust in the glass.

**Q: What size bulbs does it take?**
A: G9 bi-pin base, max 5 watts per socket. Stick with 4-watt LEDs for the best balance of brightness and heat management.

**Q: Is this truly damp-rated for shower areas?**
A: Yes, it's rated for damp locations, meaning it handles humidity and steam. It's not rated for direct water contact, so don't mount it inside the shower itself.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want hotel-bathroom lighting without the hotel-bathroom price. The milky glass diffusion is genuinely effective, and the matte black finish hides the wear that plagues cheaper fixtures.

**Skip it if** you're not prepared to buy bulbs separately, or if you need a fixture with integrated dimming.
