Your water heater just died. You call a plumber, and they quote you $450 for a part that costs a fraction of that online. Sound familiar? The Honeywell Water Heater Gas Valve with PIEZO ignition is the exact replacement part that DIY homeowners buy to skip that markup. But is it worth the hassle of swapping it yourself? Here's what 210 Amazon reviewers found.

## What's in the Box

You get the gas valve itself — that's it. No gasket, no mounting screws, no thermocouple. The valve features a standard 3/4-inch NPT thread that screws directly into most water heater tanks, and the temperature dial is clearly marked with OFF, PILOT, and ON positions plus a temperature range from 55°F to 155°F. The PIEZO igniter is built into the valve body, which is the main selling point here — no more fumbling with a long lighter to relight your pilot.

## Performance and Build Quality

This is a genuine Honeywell part, not some aftermarket knockoff, and it shows in the construction. The valve body is solid die-cast metal with a smooth-turning knob. Installation takes about 30-45 minutes if you're comfortable with basic plumbing — shut off the gas, disconnect the old valve, thread in the new one, reconnect the thermocouple and pilot tube.

The PIEZO ignition works reliably when it works. Press the red button, you hear the click, and the pilot lights. That's the experience most buyers report. One reviewer noted, "**Replaced my old Honeywell valve in about 20 minutes. The piezo igniter is a huge upgrade from using a lighter every time.**"

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The universal fit is the standout feature. This valve replaces a wide range of Honeywell models, and buyers repeatedly mention it as a direct drop-in replacement for their specific water heater brands — Rheem, AO Smith, and Bradford White units all use Honeywell valves. The price point versus a plumber's labor charge is another recurring theme. DIYers report saving $200-400 by doing this themselves.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest truth: quality control is inconsistent. A notable number of buyers received units with faulty thermocouples right out of the box. The thermocouple is the safety device that senses the pilot flame — if it's defective, the gas valve won't stay open and your pilot won't stay lit. One frustrated reviewer said, "**Installed it, pilot lights but won't stay lit. Had to go buy a separate thermocouple for $15 at Home Depot to make it work.**"

That's a real headache when you've already drained your water heater and have no hot water. Also, the lack of an included gasket means you'll need to buy one or reuse your old one — always a gamble with a rubber seal that's been compressed for years.

## Who Should Buy This

This valve is perfect for the moderately handy homeowner who has done basic plumbing before and understands gas safety. If you can use a pipe wrench and know how to check for gas leaks with soapy water, you can handle this swap. You should skip it if you're not comfortable working with gas lines, or if your water heater is more than 10 years old — at that point, replacing the entire unit is usually the smarter investment.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this fit my water heater?**
A: Check your current valve's model number. This Honeywell valve replaces most models with a standard 3/4-inch NPT connection. If your existing valve is a Honeywell, there's a 90% chance this is the right match.

**Q: Can I relight the pilot without a lighter?**
A: Yes — that's the whole point of the PIEZO ignition. Press the red button and it generates a spark. No external ignition source needed.

**Q: How long does installation take?**
A: Plan for 30-60 minutes including draining the tank partially and checking for leaks. You'll need a pipe wrench and pipe thread sealant rated for gas.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a competent DIYer with a dead Honeywell valve and want to save a few hundred dollars in plumber fees. The PIEZO ignition is genuinely convenient, and the universal fit solves a real problem.

**Skip it if** you're not comfortable with gas work, your water heater is past its prime, or you can't afford to wait for a replacement if the thermocouple turns out to be defective.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A good part at a fair price, held back only by inconsistent quality control on the thermocouple. For the price of a plumber's visit, you could buy this valve and a backup thermocouple and still come out way ahead.
