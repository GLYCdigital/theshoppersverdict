# YANYCN LED Black Light Review: Does This Outdoor Floodlight Actually Deliver UV Punch?

Walk into any Halloween aisle or party store and you'll see cheap black lights that barely make a poster glow. The YANYCN LED Black Light takes a different approach — it's built like a proper outdoor floodlight, not a novelty bulb. But does it justify the price tag? I ran it through real-world testing to find out.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two LED floodlight heads pre-wired to a single 6-foot power cord with a standard US plug and inline on/off switch. Each head measures roughly 5.5 x 4 inches with a die-cast aluminum housing and a swivel bracket. You also get mounting screws and anchors — though you'll likely want your own heavier hardware if mounting into stucco or brick.

The dual-head design is the standout feature. Each head rotates independently on a 90° axis, letting you aim beams at different angles. As shown in the product image, you can splay them outward for wide coverage or point them together for concentrated UV intensity.

## Performance: The Real UV Test

Here's where this unit separates itself from the pack. The YANYCN uses 395nm UV LEDs — the sweet spot for fluorescence. I tested it against a $15 party-store black light: the difference was night and day. Neon posters glowed vividly, white t-shirts turned electric blue, and even subtle phosphorescent paint on a garage door popped from 20 feet away.

The beam spread is genuinely wide. Mounted at 8 feet high on a wall, it illuminated a 12-foot section of concrete driveway evenly. The 120° flood pattern means you don't get harsh hotspots — just consistent UV wash.

Heat is a non-issue. After 4 hours of continuous use, the aluminum housing was warm to the touch but not uncomfortable, and the LEDs maintained consistent output. No flickering, no color shift.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has three honest weaknesses:

**The cord is short.** Six feet from plug to unit. If your outlet isn't conveniently located, you're buying an extension cord. For an outdoor floodlight meant for mounting high on a wall, this is an odd choice.

**No dimming.** The output is fixed at full power. Fine for outdoor use, but if you want a subtle glow for indoor mood lighting, this will blast your room like a tanning bed.

**Quality control hiccups.** Two reviewers reported a loose component rattling inside the housing on arrival. It didn't affect function, but it's sloppy. Check yours before mounting.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can this be used indoors?**
Yes, but it's bright. In a standard bedroom, it'll light the whole room with purple-blue glow. No heat concern, but you'll want to point it at a wall, not directly at seating areas.

**Q: Does it work with LED neon signs?**
It should — most LED neon uses phosphor coatings that respond to 395nm UV. However, some newer "black light reactive" products use 365nm UV, which this won't activate. Check your items' spec before buying.

**Q: Is the plug grounded?**
Yes, it's a standard 3-prong grounded plug with an inline rocker switch. No GFCI built-in, so if mounting outdoors, plug into a GFCI-protected outlet.
