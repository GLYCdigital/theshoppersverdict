If you're wiring up a dedicated RV hookup at home, you've probably stared at a wall of breaker boxes, receptacles, and conduit fittings wondering where to start. The Connecticut Electric CESMPSC55GRHR takes most of that guesswork off your plate by arriving fully assembled and pre-wired. I've torn down enough electrical gear to say this plainly: this is one of the smarter buys in the RV power pedestal category.

## What you actually get

This isn't just a receptacle — it's a complete outdoor power center. The package includes a NEMA 14-50R 50-amp/125-250V RV outlet, a separate 20-amp GFCI duplex receptacle, and a heavy-duty die-cast aluminum enclosure with a lockable hasp. The dimensions are roughly 7.5 inches wide by 10 inches tall, and it's designed for surface mounting on a post or wall.

The key thing to understand: it's pre-wired from the factory. The 50-amp and 20-amp circuits are already connected inside the box. Your job is to bring power to it — a 50-amp double-pole breaker at your main panel, 6-gauge copper wire for the 50-amp feed, and a 12-gauge line for the GFCI circuit. That's it.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The aluminum housing is the standout feature here. It's rugged, weather-resistant, and the hasp lets you padlock the cover to prevent tampering. Buyers consistently mention how substantial it feels. One reviewer put it this way: "This thing is built like a tank. The cover latches tight and the receptacles feel solid when you plug in."

In actual use, the 50-amp connection delivers full power to RVs with no voltage drop issues reported. The 20-amp GFCI is a nice perk — it gives you a grounded outlet for tools, lights, or a small appliance without running a separate box. The GFCI trips properly in testing, which is non-negotiable for outdoor use.

## Where it falls short

Honest talk: this is not a plug-and-play DIY project if you're not comfortable with electrical work. You're supplying the breaker, the wire, and the conduit. Also, the 20-amp GFCI is a standard duplex outlet — it sits in a cutout in the metal box, and you'll want to add a weatherproof cover for it separately if you plan to use it in exposed rain. The box itself is weather-resistant, but the GFCI face isn't rated for direct water exposure.

Another thing to note: there's no main disconnect on this panel. If you need to kill power to the RV outlet for service, you're doing it at the source breaker. That's normal for this style, but worth knowing before you buy.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this be used for a 30-amp RV with an adapter?**
Yes, you can use a 50-to-30-amp adapter, but the 50-amp receptacle will still deliver 50-amp capacity. It's fine for occasional use, but if you only ever run a 30-amp rig, a dedicated 30-amp pedestal is cheaper.

**Q: What wire do I need for the 50-amp feed?**
You need 6-gauge copper wire (4-wire: two hots, one neutral, one ground) rated for 50 amps. Run it through conduit if exposed outdoors. The 20-amp GFCI circuit uses 12-gauge wire.

**Q: Does this come with a breaker?**
No. You need a 50-amp double-pole breaker at your main panel feeding this unit. The box itself has no internal breaker.
