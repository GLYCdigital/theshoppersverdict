## What You're Actually Getting

The F13T5 is a 21-inch T5 linear fluorescent tube, rated at 13 watts. It produces 4100K cool white light — that's the neutral-to-slightly-cool range that works well in kitchens and workshops where you need to see what you're doing without harsh blue tones. The actual tube measures 20.5 inches from pin to pin, which is the standard F13T5/21 dimension. If your under-cabinet fixture takes a 21-inch T5, this drops straight in.

It's worth noting the packaging situation. KOR ships these in a simple cardboard sleeve, which is fine when the box isn't crushed. But multiple buyers report arriving with cracked ends or shattered tubes. That's a logistics issue, not a product issue — but it's real, and it's the most common complaint in the reviews.

## Performance and Build Quality

The light output is solid for a 13-watt tube. At roughly 1,100 lumens, it's not blinding, but it's plenty for task lighting over a counter or workbench. The 4100K temperature sits in a sweet spot — brighter than warm white, less clinical than daylight. Buyers consistently describe it as "bright and clear" without the sickly green tint that plagues cheap fluorescents.

Startup behavior matters with T5 tubes. Cheap ones flicker and buzz for minutes before stabilizing. The KOR unit fires up quickly and holds steady. One buyer noted, "Works perfectly in my under-cabinet fixture — no flicker, no hum, just clean light." That matches the general sentiment across the reviews.

The rated lifespan is 9,000 hours. That's roughly three years of typical under-cabinet use (about three hours a day). It's not extraordinary — most T5s claim similar — but the failure rate in the reviews is low. Most negative feedback centers on shipping damage, not premature burnout.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the packaging. If you're ordering these, expect the possibility of a cracked tube. Amazon's return policy covers it, but it's an annoyance. Second, this is a fixed 4100K — if your kitchen has warm wood tones and you prefer 2700K or 3000K lighting, these will clash. Third, they're not dimmable. If you have a dimmer switch on your under-cabinet setup, these won't respond.

One more thing: these are fluorescent, not LED. They contain a small amount of mercury, so disposal requires proper recycling. The energy efficiency is decent but not LED-level. If you're upgrading an existing T5 fixture, these make sense. If you're building new, consider LED retrofits instead.

## FAQ

**Will these fit my GE under-cabinet fixture?**
If your fixture takes a 21-inch T5 (F13T5/21), yes. Measure the tube length pin-to-pin — it should be 20.5 inches. This is the standard dimension for this bulb type.

**Can I use these in a T8 fixture?**
No. T5 and T8 have different diameters and different pin spacing. T5 pins are smaller and closer together. Don't force it — you'll damage the fixture.

**How do these compare to LED replacements?**
LEDs use less power (around 9-10 watts for equivalent output), last longer (25,000+ hours), and contain no mercury. But they cost more upfront. If your fixture is in good shape and you want a cheap fix, these fluorescents are fine. If you're planning to keep the fixture for years, LED is the better long-term investment.
