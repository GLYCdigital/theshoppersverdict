## What You Actually Get

The box contains the cylinder itself — a steel tank standing about 30 inches tall and weighing around 60 pounds when full — with a CGA 580 valve installed and capped. That valve is the industry standard for 100% argon, so it threads directly onto most TIG welders and MIG regulators without any adapter hunting. The tank is stamped with its hydrostatic test date, which matters because a tank past its test date can't be refilled until recertified. This one arrives clean, unpainted (bare steel), and ready to connect.

## Real-World Performance

The 40 cu/ft capacity is ideal for light-to-medium work: TIG welding on thin steel, stainless, or aluminum, plus purging and back-purging lines. At a typical 15-20 CFH flow rate for TIG, you'll get roughly 2 to 2.5 hours of actual arc time before the gauge starts dropping. That's enough for a weekend project or a solid week of evening repairs. For MIG with argon (like welding aluminum), you'll burn through it faster — expect around 1.5 hours.

The tank holds pressure well. Multiple buyers report checking the gauge weeks after delivery and seeing it still pegged at 2,000+ psi. One reviewer noted, "I was skeptical it would be truly full, but my regulator read 2,200 PSI right out of the crate. Two months later, still holding." That consistency matters because a leaky valve or a poorly sealed tank would bleed money.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the weight. At ~60 pounds, this is not a grab-and-go tank. It's fine in a garage or workshop, but if you're mobile — say, doing on-site repairs — you'll want a 20 cu/ft bottle instead. The lack of a handle or valve cap is also a real gripe; you're carrying it by the neck or the base, and a dropped tank is a hazard. Spend $15 on a bolt-on handle.

The bigger issue is refill logistics. Many local welding supply shops won't refill a customer-owned cylinder — they'll only exchange it for a rental tank, which means you lose your owned bottle. Before buying, call your local Airgas, Praxair, or independent supplier and confirm they'll refill (not exchange) customer cylinders. If they won't, this purchase becomes a one-time-use proposition unless you find a shop that will.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this tank DOT-certified and safe for refilling?**
A: Yes, it's a DOT-3AA steel cylinder with a current hydrostatic test date stamped on the neck. When you take it for refill, the shop will verify that date. Keep the tank dry and stored upright to maintain certification.

**Q: Does the CGA 580 valve fit my TIG welder's regulator?**
A: Almost certainly. CGA 580 is the standard for argon and argon/CO2 mixes up to 75% argon. If your regulator is designed for argon, it will thread right on. If you have a CO2 regulator (CGA 320), you'll need an adapter.

**Q: Can I exchange this tank at a local store?**
A: Not usually. Exchanges swap your owned tank for a rental — you lose your bottle. You need to find a shop that does "refill only" service. Call ahead; many independent shops will, but big chains often won't.
