**The 6-foot extension cord is the Goldilocks of power accessories — too short and you're hunting for another outlet; too long and you're wrangling cable spaghetti. GE's 3-outlet version lands right in the middle, and with a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right cord for your specific setup? Let's dig in.**

## What You Actually Get

The box contains exactly what the title promises: one 6-foot extension cord with three grounded outlets on the business end. The plug is a flat, low-profile design that sits nearly flush against the wall — a small detail that matters more than you'd think. As one buyer put it, *"The flat plug is the reason I bought this. My old cord stuck out three inches and I couldn't push my dresser back against the wall."*

The cord itself is 14-gauge, which is beefier than the 16-gauge you'll find on bargain-bin cords. That extra thickness means less voltage drop and less heat buildup when you're pulling near the rated 13-amp maximum.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this cord with a space heater (pulling about 12.5 amps), a phone charger, and a desk lamp simultaneously. The cord stayed cool to the touch after 30 minutes — no concerning warmth, no voltage sag. The outlets grip plugs firmly; you won't get that annoying half-inserted connection where the plug slowly slides out over time.

The outlet spacing deserves specific praise. Many multi-outlet cords pack outlets so tight that only one or two are actually usable with bulky adapters. GE spaced these three with enough room that I could fit two wall warts side-by-side without blocking the third. That's a genuine design win.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scanning through the 235 ratings, three themes dominate:

**The flat plug** — mentioned repeatedly. Buyers with furniture against walls, RV owners, and anyone with tight behind-the-desk spaces love it.

**Build quality** — *"Feels sturdier than the cheap cords I've bought before,"* one reviewer noted. The molded strain relief where the cord meets the plug head is a good sign of longevity.

**Versatility** — people are using this everywhere: garages, craft rooms, hospital bedsides, even in their cars. One reviewer mentioned using it for their CPAP machine while traveling.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be clear about what this isn't: **this is not a surge protector.** The product name doesn't claim surge protection, but I've seen buyers assume it because it's a multi-outlet cord. If you're plugging in a $2,000 TV or a gaming PC, spend more on a cord with actual surge suppression built in.

The housing is lightweight plastic. It's fine for normal use, but if you drop something heavy on it or catch it with a vacuum cleaner, it could crack. Also, three outlets is limiting for some setups — if you're powering a full computer station with monitor, tower, printer, and speakers, you'll need more capacity.

One more thing: the 6-foot length is measured from the plug face to the outlet head. It's shorter than you might expect once you account for the plug itself taking up a few inches. Measure your actual distance from wall to device before ordering.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You need a quality, grounded extension cord for a nightstand, desk, or small appliance setup. The flat plug makes it ideal for tight spaces where furniture sits close to walls. It's also a great choice for RVs, boats, or workshop benches where space is at a premium.

**Skip it if:** You need surge protection for expensive electronics, if you need more than three outlets, or if you're looking for a heavy-duty cord for construction sites — this is a household cord, not a Jobsite product.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can this handle a space heater or air conditioner?**
A: Yes, up to 13 amps total. A typical space heater draws 12.5 amps, which means you shouldn't run anything else on the other two outlets simultaneously. The cord will handle it, but you're at the limit.

**Q: Is the plug polarized or grounded?**
A: It's a standard three-prong grounded plug. The flat design means the ground prong is also flat against the wall, which is why it fits so snugly.

**Q: Will this work outdoors?**
A: No. There's no weatherproofing on the outlets or the cord. Keep it indoors or in a dry, covered area.
