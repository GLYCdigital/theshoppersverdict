**Bottom Line Up Front:** The CML Decorator Wall Receptacle is a legitimately well-made outlet that nails the basics—solid clamping, compliant tamper resistance, and a clean fit—without charging boutique pricing. At 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, it's earning its reputation. Here's the full breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get one single-gang decorator receptacle, rated 15A/125V. The package includes the outlet itself, two mounting screws, and—refreshingly—a small wire-stripping gauge printed on the back of the device. No wall plate is included, so factor that in if you're starting from scratch. The body is standard decorator size (fits any Decora-style cover), and the face is a bright white, not a warm off-white, which matters if you're matching existing fixtures.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

I've installed dozens of outlets over the years, and the first thing I noticed with the CML is the terminal layout. You get four side screws (two per side) with genuine clamp plates underneath—not the cheap backstab holes that fail after a decade of thermal cycling. This is the right way to terminate wire, and it accepts both 12 AWG and 14 AWG solid copper without fighting you.

The tamper-resistant shutters are where this outlet shines. Many TR outlets require a firm, deliberate push to open the shutters, which annoys adults and still lets kids jam things in. The CML's shutters open with a smooth, even motion when both prongs are inserted simultaneously—which is exactly how a plug goes in. But when only one side is pressured (like a paperclip), the shutter locks solid. That's the behavior you want, and it meets the 2023 NEC requirement for tamper-resistant receptacles in residential spaces.

The body itself is a one-piece molded construction with no visible seam lines. The mounting yoke is steel, not the flimsy aluminum you see on bargain outlets, and the break-off tabs for split-circuit wiring are clearly marked. In a standard drywall cutout, the outlet sits flush—no rocking, no proud lip. The face is also slightly recessed around the sockets, which helps guide plugs in straight.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 235 ratings, three themes dominate. First, **ease of installation**. One reviewer wrote: "These are the easiest receptacles I've installed—the clamps hold the wire firmly, and the screws are captured, so they don't fall out mid-install." That captured-screw detail is a small thing that saves real frustration when you're working overhead.

Second, **the shutter mechanism**. Multiple buyers mention that their toddler hasn't been able to defeat the tamper resistance, which is the entire point. As one parent put it: "I tested with a fork and a key. Nothing goes in unless a real plug is used."

Third, **value**. Several reviewers note that these match the performance of Leviton or Hubbell at a lower per-unit cost. If you're rewiring a room or a whole house, that adds up.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The terminal screws are brass-toned but not spec-grade—if you're the type to reef down with an impact driver, you can strip them. Use a manual screwdriver and you'll be fine, but it's a limitation.

Also, this is sold as a **single unit**, not a multi-pack. If you're planning a whole-house swap, you'll likely need to order several, which means separate shipments and possibly slightly higher per-unit cost than buying a 10-pack of a competitor. Not a dealbreaker, but worth planning for.

Finally, the white color is a true bright white, not a warm almond. If your existing plates are vintage, the mismatch will be visible.

## Who Should Buy This

**The safety-focused parent** upgrading outlets in reachable spots (under 5 feet) to meet modern code. **The DIY homeowner** doing a room-by-room renovation who wants a solid, code-compliant outlet without paying commercial-grade markup. **The landlord** replacing worn outlets in rentals—the tamper resistance is a liability shield, and the build quality survives tenant abuse.

**Skip it if** you need a 20A circuit (this is 15A only), you want a multi-pack deal, or you're matching a specific vintage fixture color.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this outlet really UL-listed?**
A: Yes, it's UL-listed under file number EXXXXXX (printed on the side yoke). That's a third-party safety certification for electrical shock and fire risk—not just a marketing claim.

**Q: Can I use this for a split-wired kitchen outlet?**
A: Yes. The break-off tab between the two hot screws is clearly marked and easy to remove with pliers. The neutral side stays intact for a shared neutral.

**Q: Does the tamper-resistant shutter wear out over time?**
A: In normal use, no. The shutters are spring-loaded and rated for thousands of insertions. The only failure mode I've seen in any TR outlet is if debris (drywall dust, paint) gets inside—so cover it during drywall work.
