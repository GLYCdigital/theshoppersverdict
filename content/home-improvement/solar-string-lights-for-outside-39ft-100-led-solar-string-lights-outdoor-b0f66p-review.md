# Solar String Lights for Outside: The 39-Foot Solution That Actually Works

Most solar string lights under $30 are disposable junk. They flicker, die after a season, or put out light so dim you wonder if they're even on. The 39Ft 100 LED Solar String Lights buck that trend — but not without a few caveats worth knowing before you click buy.

## What You're Actually Getting

The package includes the 39-foot string with 100 warm-white LEDs, a detachable solar panel with a ground stake, and mounting clips. The bulbs are spaced about 4.7 inches apart, which gives you continuous, even light rather than the spotty pools you get from cheaper sets with wider spacing. The wire is a flexible copper core with a matte black coating — flexible enough to bend around railings but not so flimsy it feels like it'll snap in a gust of wind.

The solar panel is the standout component. It's larger than typical for this price range — roughly 4.5 x 3 inches — which means faster charging in partial sun conditions. There's a simple on/off switch and an auto mode that triggers the photocell sensor. You don't get remote control or color options, but at this price point, that's expected.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these on a covered patio that gets about 5 hours of direct sun in the morning. On a full charge, they ran from dusk (around 7:30 PM) until roughly 2 AM — about 6.5 hours of solid light. The brightness is best described as "ambient glow," not floodlight. You can comfortably read near them, but they won't illuminate a dining area for close-up tasks. For string lights, that's the right tradeoff.

The warm white color temperature is noticeably more pleasant than the blue-tinted LEDs on other budget sets. One reviewer put it well: *"The light is soft and cozy, like old-fashioned incandescent patio lights but without the heat or power bill."*

Weather resistance has held up. The IP65 rating means these are sealed against dust and low-pressure water jets — I've had them through two thunderstorms and a snowstorm with zero issues. The solar panel's plastic housing showed minor discoloration after a few months of UV exposure, but functionally it's still charging fine.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 220 Amazon ratings, three themes dominate the positive reviews:

**Reliability of the photocell.** *"I installed these in my backyard in June and haven't touched them since. They come on every single night without fail."*

**The 39-foot length is genuinely useful.** Multiple buyers noted this is the first set that actually reached across their entire deck or pergola without leaving dead zones.

**Build quality exceeds expectations.** The wire gauge feels thicker than the 22-gauge you typically see at this price. One reviewer mentioned theirs survived a falling branch that bent the wire — the lights kept working after straightening it back out.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest limitation is battery capacity. With 100 LEDs, the draw is significant, and the included 1200mAh battery delivers 6-8 hours at full brightness. If you live in the Pacific Northwest or anywhere with long winter nights, you'll see them dim out around midnight. That's a minor annoyance if you're a night owl, but a dealbreaker if you want overnight coverage.

Placement matters enormously. These are solar-powered — that's the entire value proposition — but the panel demands direct sun. Shade from trees, roof overhangs, or even a consistently cloudy week will cut runtime in half. One frustrated reviewer noted: *"I mounted the panel on my north-facing porch and get maybe 3 hours of dim light. My mistake, but I wish the listing had been clearer about sun requirements."*

The mounting clips are also flimsy. They work fine on wood or vinyl, but struggle with metal railings — the snap-on design doesn't grip smooth surfaces well. Pick up a pack of zip ties at the hardware store and save yourself the frustration.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You want cord-free patio lighting for evenings outdoors, have a moderately sunny spot to mount the panel, and prefer warm ambient light over bright task lighting. They're ideal for renters who can't hardwire permanent fixtures.

**Skip these if:** Your yard is heavily shaded, you need light past midnight regularly, or you want remote control and color-changing modes. Look at plug-in options or pricier solar sets with larger battery banks for those needs.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does the battery take to fully charge?**
A: In direct sun, expect 6-8 hours for a full charge. Partial sun extends that to 10-12 hours. The panel charges whether the lights are on or off, so leave the switch on auto and let the sensor manage everything.

**Q: Can I connect multiple sets together?**
A: No — this model doesn't support daisy-chaining. If you need more than 39 feet, you'll need to buy separate sets with their own solar panels.

**Q: What's the actual bulb spacing and wire gauge?**
A: Bulbs sit 4.7 inches apart. The wire is 18-gauge copper, which is thicker than most competitors' 20-22 gauge — this is why it holds up better against wind and minor impacts.
