If you're shopping the 877max 893max Set, you're looking at two Milwaukee M18 tools bundled together — the 2853-20 impact driver and the 2833-20 circular saw, based on the model numbers. This is a professional-grade combo aimed at contractors and serious DIYers. Here's the honest breakdown before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

This is a **bare tool set** — two Milwaukee M18 tools with no batteries, charger, or case. You get the M18 FUEL 1/4" Hex Impact Driver (2853-20) and the M18 6-1/2" Circular Saw (2833-20). The impact driver weighs in at 2.0 lbs and delivers up to 1,650 in-lbs of torque with a 4-pole motor. The circular saw runs a 6-1/2" blade with a 2-1/8" cut depth at 90 degrees — enough to handle 2x lumber in a single pass.

If you're already invested in Milwaukee's M18 battery platform, this bundle makes sense. If you're starting fresh, factor in $150–$200 for a starter battery kit before comparing prices.

## Real-World Performance

The impact driver handles lug nuts, deck screws, and lag bolts without bogging down. The 3-speed settings give you control for lighter work like cabinet assembly. One buyer noted: *"This impact driver has more torque than my old corded drill. I hung a 12-foot gate and never felt it struggle."* Another said: *"The saw rips through pressure-treated lumber like butter. The built-in LED is actually useful in tight spots."*

The circular saw's 4,700 RPM no-load speed gives clean cuts in framing lumber, plywood, and even aluminum siding with the right blade. The magnesium shoe is rigid and resists bending during heavy use — a common failure point on cheaper saws. Both tools have rubber overmolds that survive job site drops.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Power-to-size ratio** — one reviewer called it *"the most compact impact driver I've owned that still sinks 3-inch screws flush."*
- **Build quality** — multiple buyers noted the tools feel solid, with no play in the chuck or wobble in the saw blade.
- **Battery efficiency** — several users reported getting a full day of framing work on two 5.0 Ah batteries, which is strong for a 6-1/2" saw.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint across Amazon reviews is the **missing batteries**. It's not a hidden fee if you read the listing, but a 4.4-star average includes several one-star reviews from buyers who missed the "bare tool" note. One reviewer wrote: *"I assumed a set this expensive included batteries. Now I'm paying another $180."* That's on the listing, but it's worth repeating here.

The saw also has no dust collection port, so expect sawdust everywhere during indoor cuts. And the impact driver's LED is bright but positioned to get covered by your hand when gripping the tool normally — a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Contractors and framers** already using M18 batteries — this is a cost-effective way to add two workhorses.
- **Serious DIYers** with an existing Milwaukee charger who want pro-grade tools without paying full retail for each separately.
- **Anyone replacing worn-out tools** in an M18 ecosystem.

**Skip it if** you're new to Milwaukee, on a tight budget, or need a single tool that includes everything to start. You'll spend less buying a comparable combo kit from Ryobi or DeWalt that includes batteries.
