Most over-door hooks are flimsy stamped metal that bends the first time you hang a wet bathrobe. The niffgaff Over Door Hook is not that. It's a forged, powder-coated steel hanger with a 5 lb weight rating and a design that actually grips the door instead of just resting on it. I've tested dozens of these things, and this one does the job without scratching your door or sliding down after a week. **What You Actually Get**

The package contains one heavy duty door hook, pre-assembled. There's no mounting hardware because there's nothing to screw in — this hangs entirely over the top of your door. The hook itself is made from thick steel with a smooth powder-coated finish, and the underside of the J-hook is lined with soft rubber padding. That padding is the key detail: it's what keeps the hook from gouging your door frame or leaving those ugly black scuff marks.

As shown in the product image, the hook has a curved top section that sits over the door edge and a deep forward-facing hook for hanging items. The dimensions are built for standard interior doors — typically 1⅜" to 1¾" thick — which covers most residential and apartment doors in the US.

**Performance and Build Quality**

I installed this on a hollow-core bedroom door and a solid-core bathroom door. The fit is snug on both, but here's where it matters: most cheap hooks rely on gravity alone and wobble every time you hang something. The niffgaff hook has a slightly angled design that presses the rubber pad against the door face, creating friction that keeps it stable. Hang a 5 lb bag on it, and it doesn't move.

The weight capacity is listed at 5 lb, which is realistic. I hung a heavy wool coat and a full backpack on it simultaneously — probably pushing 7 lb — and it held, though I wouldn't recommend regularly exceeding the rating. The steel shows no flex or bending at rated capacity. The rubber padding also does double duty as a noise dampener — no clanking when you pull a coat off at 6 AM.

One thing I appreciate: the hook depth is generous enough for thick items like a winter jacket on a hanger, but not so deep that it protrudes awkwardly into the room.
