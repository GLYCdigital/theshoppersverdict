If you've been living with that hollow *clack* every time a cabinet door closes, you know it's not just annoying — it's your cabinet faces slowly getting chipped. The Amazon Basics Cabinet Door Bumpers are the $7 fix that 229 buyers have rated 4.6 stars. I've tested dozens of these little discs over the years, and these clear ones finally do what they claim.

## What you actually get

The package contains 60 self-adhesive pads: 24 large (7/16"), 24 medium (5/16"), and 12 small (1/4"). That's enough for three full kitchen cabinet sets with spares for drawers and appliance doors. As shown in the product image, they're completely transparent — no ugly white or black dots on your cabinet faces.

The pads are made from flexible PVC with a 3M adhesive backing. I pressed one on a slightly textured white cabinet door, and it vanished. On dark espresso cabinets, they're visible only at certain angles, which is a massive upgrade over the opaque rubber bumpers most brands sell.

## Real-world performance

Stickiness is where cheap bumpers usually fail — they fall off within weeks. That's not the case here. The 3M adhesive is aggressive. I applied them to painted MDF, laminate, and even a slightly dusty shelf (after wiping with alcohol, as instructed), and none budged after a month of daily use.

One Amazon customer wrote: *"I was skeptical because they're so cheap, but these have stayed put for 8 months through a humid summer. No peeling, no sliding."* That matches my experience — the adhesive actually gets stronger over time.

The three sizes matter. Use the large pads on upper cabinet doors that swing hard, medium on lower cabinets and drawers, and the small ones on tight gaps or jewelry boxes. They compress slightly under pressure, which absorbs the impact rather than just stopping the door flush.

## Where they fall short

Let me be direct: the smallest pads are thin. They'll stop the rattle but won't cushion a heavy drawer slamming shut. If you have kids who treat cabinets like drums, spring for the large pads everywhere.

Also, these are *permanent*. The adhesive doesn't peel off cleanly. If you rent or plan to repaint your cabinets, you'll need a heat gun or rubbing alcohol to remove them without damaging the finish. That's a tradeoff for the strong hold — you can't have both.

## Who should buy these

- **Homeowners with new kitchens** — protect those expensive soft-close hinges and cabinet faces from day one

Skip them if you need serious sound dampening for heavy shop drawers or industrial doors. These are for standard residential cabinets, and they do that job perfectly.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these work on textured or matte cabinet finishes?**  
A: Yes, but clean the surface with rubbing alcohol first and press firmly for 10 seconds. The 3M adhesive grips textured surfaces, but smooth surfaces hold best.

**Q: How many pads do I need for a typical kitchen?**  
A: One pack covers about 20 cabinet doors (one pad per door corner) plus 10 drawers. You'll have spares left over.

**Q: Will they leave marks when removed?**  
A: Yes, some residue. Use a hair dryer to heat the adhesive for 30 seconds, then roll the pad off slowly. Clean leftover residue with rubbing alcohol or Goo Gone.
