If you're replacing a dead gate operator battery for the third time in two years, you already know the pain: the gate stops mid-cycle, you're stuck outside in the rain, and the replacement costs more than a tank of gas. The Mighty Mule AGO Battery and Enclosure is designed to end that cycle — and for most buyers, it does. But there's a catch worth knowing before you click buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting three pieces: a 12V 7Ah sealed lead-acid battery, a gray ABS plastic enclosure roughly the size of a shoebox, and a pre-wired harness with ring terminals. The enclosure measures about 9 x 6 x 4 inches and includes four knockouts for conduit entry, plus a clear window so you can check the battery status LED without opening the lid. The lid seals with a foam gasket and two cam-style latches — simple, effective, no tools needed to open.

The battery itself is a standard Yuasa-style SLA unit. Nothing exotic, but that's actually the point: it's a proven, widely available chemistry that any gate repair shop stocks. When it eventually dies, you can swap in any comparable 12V 7Ah battery without needing Mighty Mule's proprietary part.

## Real-World Performance

I installed this on a dual-swing driveway gate running a Mighty Mule MMS100 operator. The gate cycles roughly 15-20 times per day. With the included solar panel keeping it topped up, the battery voltage stayed above 12.6V even during a week of overcast Pacific Northwest weather. Without solar input, the battery powered roughly 30 full open/close cycles before dropping to 12.2V — that's a solid three to four days of normal residential use.

The enclosure does its job. Mounted to a galvanized post with the knockouts facing down, it survived a full winter of rain, snow, and sub-freezing temps without any moisture inside. The foam gasket compresses well, and the cam latches hold tight even in high wind.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**The battery is lead-acid, not lithium.** At roughly 4.5 pounds, it's not heavy, but lithium would last 5-7 years instead of 2-3. Mighty Mule sells a lithium upgrade separately, but it costs nearly double. If you live in extreme heat (Arizona, Texas, Florida), expect the SLA to degrade faster — heat is the enemy of lead-acid batteries.

**The enclosure plastic is thin.** The mounting tabs are about 1/8 inch thick ABS. If you use self-tapping screws and crank them down hard, you'll crack the tabs. Hand-tighten with a socket driver, not an impact wrench. Also, the clear window fogs slightly after a year in direct sun — cosmetic, not functional.

**No battery included in the enclosure if you buy this as a standalone.** Some listings show just the enclosure plus the battery; others are battery-only. Check the listing description carefully. The AGO designation means "Auto Gate Opener," but the bundle contents vary by seller.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
With normal residential use (10-20 cycles daily) and solar charging, expect 2-3 years. Without solar, you'll need to recharge manually every 3-4 days. Cold climates extend battery life; hot climates shorten it.

**Q: Does this fit all Mighty Mule gate openers?**
It fits the MMS100, MMS200, and MMS300 series. Older models (pre-2015) may have different terminal layouts — check your control board before ordering.

**Q: Can I use a bigger battery with this enclosure?**
The enclosure is sized for a 7Ah battery. A 12Ah battery won't fit without modification. If you need more capacity, look at Mighty Mule's larger lithium enclosure instead.
