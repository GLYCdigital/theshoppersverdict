## What You're Actually Getting

The 16-ounce bottle looks small on your shelf, but don't let that fool you. The concentrate dilutes at 1 tablespoon per gallon of water, meaning that single bottle stretches to **32 gallons of feed solution**. For a typical indoor collection of 10-15 houseplants fed weekly, that's a solid 2-3 months of coverage. The measuring cap has clear line markings, though they're small enough that you'll want reading glasses if you're over 40.

The formula itself is what sets it apart: OMRI-listed organic, with nutrients derived from seaweed extract and other natural sources. The NPK ratio sits at 2-3-1 — lower numbers than synthetic fertilizers, but that's the point. It feeds soil biology, not just roots.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this on three groups: a leggy pothos, a sad-looking peace lily, and a container tomato plant. The pothos showed new growth within 10 days. The peace lily perked up noticeably after two weekly feedings. The tomato plant — this is where organic fertilizer typically struggles — produced steady growth without the explosive leafy flush you'd get from synthetic Miracle-Gro. That's the tradeoff: slower, steadier, healthier.

Where it genuinely shines is **safety margin**. Synthetic fertilizers can burn roots if you mis-measure. This stuff is forgiving. One reviewer noted, *"I accidentally double-dosed my ferns and nothing happened — they actually looked better."* I wouldn't recommend testing that theory, but it speaks to the formula's gentleness.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides, because every product has them:

1. **The cap is a design flaw.** The pour spout is wide, and the concentrate is thin. One slip and you've dumped triple the needed amount. Use a separate measuring spoon if you're prone to rushing.

2. **The smell.** Organic fertilizer smells like... organic matter. For about 2-3 hours after application, you'll notice a mild earthy, compost-like odor. It fades, but if you're sensitive to smells, ventilate the room.

3. **Slow visible results.** If you're used to synthetic fertilizers that green up plants in 48 hours, this will feel sluggish. It's a marathon, not a sprint.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should I fertilize with this?**
A: Every 2-4 weeks during active growing season (spring through early fall). In winter, cut back to monthly or skip entirely for dormant plants.

**Q: Can I use it on seedlings?**
A: Yes, but dilute to half-strength (½ tablespoon per gallon) for the first two weeks. The gentle formula won't shock young roots.

**Q: Does it work for hydroponics?**
A: Technically yes, but it's not ideal. The organic compounds can gum up hydroponic systems. Stick to soil or soilless mixes.
