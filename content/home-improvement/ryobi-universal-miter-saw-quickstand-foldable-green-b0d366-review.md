**If you've ever balanced a miter saw on a pair of sawhorses and a sheet of plywood, you already know why the RYOBI Universal Miter Saw QuickStand exists.** This thing solves a problem every DIYer hits eventually: how do you get a heavy sliding compound miter saw off the ground, stable, with proper workpiece support, without dedicating permanent floor space to it? The green foldable stand has 235 Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars — which says a lot for a tool accessory that has to work with *other* brands' tools.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the assembled stand frame, two sliding material support arms, universal mounting brackets, and the hardware kit. Set up takes about 60 seconds once you've done it once — pull the legs apart, lock the crossbars, and you're ready. The mounting brackets slide along the top rails and clamp down, so you're not locked into RYOBI-specific hole patterns. As shown in the product images, the green steel frame is beefy — this isn't a flimsy folding table pretending to be a work support.

The numbers that matter: the stand holds up to 300 pounds of saw and material, and the side extensions slide out to give you nearly 8 feet of total workpiece support. That's genuinely useful for crown molding or long baseboards. The whole thing folds down to about 6 inches thick for wall storage.

## Performance and Build Quality

The steel frame feels solid under load — no wobble when you're pushing a 12-inch sliding compound saw through hard maple. The rubber feet grip concrete and plywood subflooring without walking. The material supports lock securely at whatever extension you need, and they're marked so you can set both sides to the same length for repeat cuts.

What buyers consistently mention in their reviews is how well it handles saws that aren't RYOBI. One reviewer noted, *"I mounted a DeWalt 12-inch on this and it fit perfectly — the brackets have enough adjustment that I didn't have to drill anything."* Another praised the portability: *"Folds up thin enough to slide behind my workbench. Takes up less space than a folding chair."* The universal bracket design is the standout feature — it genuinely works across brands.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The stand weighs roughly 47 pounds. That's fine for rolling it from the garage to the driveway, but if you're hauling this to a second-story job site daily, your back will complain. It's a workshop stand that happens to fold, not a lightweight job-site companion.

The included hardware is generic. Some saws need longer bolts or extra washers to mount securely — check your saw's base thickness before you start. A few reviewers mentioned raiding their bolt bin to get a proper fit. Also, the legs don't have independent leveling feet. On uneven ground, you'll be shimming or rotating the whole stand to find flat spots. For a $150+ accessory, that's a legitimate oversight.

## Who Should Buy This

Get the RYOBI QuickStand if you own a miter saw (any brand) and you're tired of working on the ground or on unstable sawhorse setups. It's ideal for the serious DIYer or semi-pro who does crown molding, deck work, or finish carpentry where long-board support matters. Skip it if you need a featherweight stand for daily jobsite hopping, or if you only make occasional 2x4 cuts — in that case, a cheaper folding workbench will do.

## FAQ

**Will this fit my DeWalt/Makita/Milwaukee miter saw?**
Almost certainly. The universal brackets adjust both width and depth to accommodate most major brands. The 300-pound capacity covers everything from 10-inch sliders to 15-inch stationary saws. If your saw has a non-standard base, the included hardware kit can be supplemented with bolts from any hardware store.

**How much floor space does it take when folded?**
It compresses to roughly 6 inches thick, about 40 inches wide, and 26 inches tall. It leans flat against a wall or slides under a workbench. It won't fit in a standard car trunk, but it's fine in an SUV, truck, or trailer.

**Does it work for cutting long boards solo?**
Yes — that's the main selling point. The sliding extensions give you nearly 8 feet of total support, so a 10-foot board doesn't tip or sag while you're cutting. The support arms lock at your chosen length, so you can set them once and keep cutting.
