# WORKPRO Cable Cutter: A Compact Workhorse for the Everyday DIYer

Most cable cutters in this price range are cheap stamped metal that bend on the first job. The WORKPRO 7-1/2 inch heavy duty wire rope cutter is not that tool. After handling one and putting it through a series of cuts on various materials, I can tell you this is a serious piece of kit that punches well above its weight class — with a couple of honest limitations you should know about before you buy.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the cutter itself and nothing else. No sheath, no spare parts, no gimmicks. That's fine — the tool is the story here. It measures 7.5 inches from tip to handle, weighs roughly 10 ounces, and features drop-forged carbon steel jaws with induction-hardened cutting edges. The handles are wrapped in a textured non-slip grip that runs nearly the full length, which gives you solid purchase regardless of hand position.

As shown in the product image, the design is straightforward: compound leverage mechanism, spring-loaded jaws, and a safety lock that keeps the tool closed when not in use. The cutting edges are precision-ground and meet cleanly along the full length — a detail that matters more than most people realize.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this cutter on three materials: 1/8-inch galvanized cable, 3/16-inch wire rope, and some stubborn 12-gauge solid copper wire. The results were telling.

On the galvanized cable, the WORKPRO sheared through cleanly with minimal effort — no crushed strands, no frayed ends. The compound leverage design means you're not fighting the tool; it does the work. The 3/16-inch wire rope took a bit more pressure but still cut through in a single squeeze, leaving a surprisingly clean edge. Copper wire was almost effortless, which makes it a solid option for electricians working on larger gauge jobs.

What impressed me most was the cutting geometry. Many cutters in this class crush the cable as they cut, leaving a mangled mess. The WORKPRO's sharp edges and proper alignment prevent that. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "Cuts through wire rope like butter. No frayed ends, no mess." That matches my experience.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

With 220 ratings and a 4.4-star average, the feedback pattern is clear. ## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. The 7.5-inch size is both a feature and a limitation. Anything thicker than about 3/16-inch steel cable is going to be a struggle. I attempted a cut on some 1/4-inch wire rope with a steel core, and it took multiple squeezes and left a slightly deformed cut. If you routinely work with heavy cable, you need a larger bolt cutter, not this tool.

The compound leverage also means the jaws open relatively wide, which is fine for most jobs but makes precision work on small, tight spaces awkward. And while the cutting edges survived my testing, some users report that the tool struggles on hardened steel or spring wire. One reviewer noted: "Great for soft metals and cable, but don't expect it to handle hardened wire."

## Who Should Buy This

This cutter is ideal for the DIYer who does occasional electrical work, hangs fencing, or needs a reliable tool for cutting various cables and wires around the home or shop. It's also a solid choice for electricians and telecom installers who need a compact, portable cutter that fits in a tool pouch.

Skip it if you're a professional rigger or work with heavy-duty steel cable daily — you'll want a larger, more specialized tool.

## FAQ

**Will this cut through braided steel cable?**
Yes, up to about 3/16-inch diameter. Thinner braided cable cuts cleanly without fraying. Anything thicker will require multiple passes or a bigger tool.

**Can it cut through nails or screws?**
No. This is designed for cable and wire, not hardened fasteners. Attempting to cut nails will damage the cutting edges.

**Does it come with a warranty?**
WORKPRO offers a standard manufacturer warranty, and the tool is backed by Amazon's return policy. Most buyers report no issues requiring support.
