Let’s be blunt: most kitchen sinks are just holes with plumbing attached. The Delta Rivet Workstation tries to be more — and for the most part, it pulls it off. At 4.2 stars across 210 ratings, this 16-gauge drop-in sink has earned a reputation, but it’s not without compromises. Here’s what you need to know before you cut into your countertop.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the 33-inch single-bowl sink (16-gauge 304 stainless steel), a sliding cutting board, a stainless steel mesh rack, a bottom rinse grid, and the drain assembly. The workstation tracks are built into the sink walls — these are the defining feature. Accessories glide along them so you can prep, drain, and rinse without taking up counter space.

That’s a solid kit. Delta didn’t skimp on the essentials, and the 16-gauge steel is the real deal. It’s noticeably thicker than the 18- or 20-gauge sinks most builders install. When you knock a cast iron pan against the wall, you won’t hear that cheap tinny ring.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The workstation concept genuinely works. As one buyer put it, “The cutting board slides right over the sink and suddenly I have extra counter space for prepping ingredients. It’s a game-changer for small kitchens.” That’s the core value proposition, and it holds up daily.

The 9-inch depth handles stockpots and roasting pans without splashing. The rear drain placement means water doesn’t pool at the front where you work. The brushed finish hides water spots reasonably well, though it will show hard water marks in areas with mineral-heavy water.

Sound damping is decent, not spectacular. Delta added pads to the exterior, and they take the edge off, but the moment you run a garbage disposal, you’ll know it. It’s quieter than a bare steel sink, not as quiet as a fireclay model.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The thick steel is the most repeated compliment. Multiple reviewers note the sink feels solid and premium compared to cheaper alternatives. The workstation tracks get consistent love — the ability to slide a cutting board or rack across the sink is the reason most people choose this over a standard drop-in.

Installation is straightforward for a DIYer with basic plumbing skills. The drop-in design means you don’t need special clips or undermount hardware. One reviewer noted, “Installed in an afternoon. The template was accurate, and the sink seated flush with no gaps.”

## Where It Falls Short

The drop-in rim is the biggest tradeoff. Unlike an undermount sink, the rolled edge sits above your countertop. That lip collects water, crumbs, and grime. You’ll be wiping the seam regularly. If you’re renovating with new counters, spend the extra for an undermount model. If you’re replacing an existing drop-in sink, this is the practical choice.

The included accessories are functional but not premium. The cutting board is thinner than a dedicated butcher block, and the mesh rack can scratch if you slide heavy cast iron across it. They’re useful, but you’ll likely upgrade them over time.

A few reviewers reported minor scratches out of the box. Stainless steel is forgiving, and they buff out with a fine abrasive pad, but check your unit on arrival.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a standard drop-in cutout and want to upgrade to a workstation sink without redoing your countertop. You cook often, need extra prep space, and value thick steel that won’t dent. You’re comfortable with basic DIY installation.

**Skip it if:** You’re doing a full kitchen remodel with new countertops — go undermount for a cleaner look. You hate cleaning seams and crevices. You need whisper-quiet operation. You want premium accessories included.

## FAQ

**Will this fit my existing sink cutout?**
Measure your current cutout. The 33-inch model requires a 33-inch cutout. Delta lists exact dimensions in the product specs. If your existing sink is 33 inches, it’s a direct swap. When in doubt, measure twice.

**Can I use a standard garbage disposal?**
Yes. The drain is standard 3.5-inch size, compatible with most disposals. Just ensure the disposal flange seats properly in the deep basin.

**Does the cutting board actually hold up?**
It works for everyday prep — chopping vegetables, slicing fruit, portioning meat. It’s not a heavy-duty butcher block. Treat it as a convenience accessory, not a professional tool.

## The Verdict

**4.2/5 — Buy it if you want a solid, thick-steel workstation sink for a drop-in install. Skip it if you’re remodeling with new counters and want a cleaner undermount look.**

The Delta Rivet is a genuine upgrade over builder-grade sinks. The thick steel, functional tracks, and complete accessory package justify the price for most homeowners. Just go in knowing the drop-in rim is a maintenance point, and the accessories are decent but not exceptional. For the right buyer, this is a kitchen workhorse that will serve well for years.
