**What you actually get**

The adapter is a single-piece unit: a standard E26 screw-in base on one end, a 360°-rotating head on the other, and three grounded NEMA 5-15R outlets arranged around the sides. The housing is molded thermoplastic — not metal — which keeps it light and prevents scratching your fixture's threads. As shown in the product image, the rotation mechanism sits at the neck, letting you point the outlets in any direction before you lock it down with the collar ring.

**Performance and build quality**

The 360° rotation is the headline feature, and it works smoothly. Unlike cheaper adapters that use a friction ball joint that sags under weight, this one uses a positive detent ring. You rotate to where you need it, tighten the collar, and it stays. One buyer noted: *"I have a ceiling fan with a single bulb socket in an awkward corner. This let me aim the outlets toward the wall so my camera charger doesn't dangle in the walkway."* That's the use case this thing nails.

The grounded outlets are genuine three-prong — not the fake "2-prong with a metal tab" you see on bargain adapters. The contacts are snug; plugs don't wobble. I'd trust this with a lamp, a phone charger, and a low-wattage LED work light simultaneously without concern.

**Where it falls short**

The 660W max rating is the real constraint. That's about 5.5 amps at 120V. Fine for LED strips, a soldering iron, or a small desk fan. It is **not** fine for a space heater (1,500W), a hair dryer, or even a shop vacuum. If you push it, the thermal protection inside the base will trip — which is a safety feature, but it's also a hard stop that ruins your workflow.

Second, the screw-in base is deep. In shallow porcelain fixtures — like some flush-mount ceiling lights — the adapter may not seat fully, leaving a visible gap. A few reviewers reported the base loosening after weeks of vibration (ceiling fans are the culprit). The fix is simple: snug it with pliers wrapped in tape, but it's an extra step you shouldn't need.

Also, this is not UL-listed. It claims CE and RoHS compliance, but if you're a stickler for third-party safety certification on anything that carries household current, that's a legitimate hesitation.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this with a dimmer switch?**
A: The adapter itself has no dimming function. If your fixture is on a dimmer, it only works with dimmable bulbs — and the outlets pass through dimmed voltage, which can damage non-dimmable electronics. Don't plug a TV or computer into a dimmer-controlled socket.

**Q: Will it fit inside a standard glass globe shade?**
A: Usually yes. The adapter is about 2.2 inches across and 3 inches tall. Most standard globes have 4-inch openings. But measure your shade first — if it's a tight decorative globe, the three plugs sticking out sideways may not fit. Plan for plugs with right-angle heads to save space.

**Q: Is it safe to leave plugged in 24/7?**
A: For low-draw devices (LED bulbs, chargers), yes. The housing stays cool to the touch in normal use. For anything near the 660W limit, don't leave it unattended. That's not this adapter's fault — that's physics with any socket-mounted splitter.
