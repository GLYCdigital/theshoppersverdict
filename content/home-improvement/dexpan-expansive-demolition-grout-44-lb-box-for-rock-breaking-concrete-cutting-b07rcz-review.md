Let's be blunt: if you're expecting a jackhammer substitute that cracks concrete in five minutes, Dexpan will frustrate you. But if you need to split rock or concrete where explosives are illegal, vibration is a hazard, or noise complaints are a real threat, this is the most elegant solution you'll find — provided you respect the physics.

## What You Actually Get

As shown in the product image, the 44 lb. box contains four sealed 11 lb. bags of gray powder resembling portland cement. That's it. No additives, no measuring scoop, no drill guide. You supply the water, a clean mixing bucket, and a hammer drill with a 1.5" to 2" bit. Each bag yields roughly 0.87 cubic feet of slurry when mixed at the recommended 1.2 quarts of water per 11 lbs. of powder — enough for about 3–4 holes at 18" depth. Don't expect this single box to demolish a driveway slab; it's sized for focused rock splitting or concrete sectioning.

## How It Actually Performs

The science is straightforward: Dexpan is a calcium oxide-based expansive agent that hydrates and swells to generate approximately 18,000 psi of lateral force. You drill a line of holes spaced 10–12 inches apart, fill them with the mixed slurry, and wait. Within 10–24 hours, the material expands and fractures the rock along your drill line. That's the entire trick — and it works remarkably well when you follow the geometry.

Real-world buyers consistently confirm this. One contractor noted: *"I used this to split a 4-foot granite boulder in my backyard. Drilled 6 holes, poured the mix, and woke up to a clean split. No noise, no dust storm, no angry neighbors."* Another reviewer highlighted its utility in tight spaces: *"Demolished a concrete footer inside a finished basement. Jackhammer would have cracked the foundation walls. Dexpan did it silently."* The common thread: patience pays off.

Temperature matters. Dexpan works in conditions from 32°F to 104°F, but the expansion timing shifts — colder temperatures slow the reaction, hotter weather accelerates it. If you're working in freezing conditions, you'll need to insulate the holes or use the cold-weather formula. That's not a flaw; it's a material property you must plan around.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the speed issue: this is not demolition for impatient people. You'll spend an hour drilling, 20 minutes mixing and pouring, then wait up to a full day. If you need the rock gone today, this product won't help. Second, the drilling requirement is non-negotiable. You need a quality hammer drill — a standard rotary drill won't cut it in granite or reinforced concrete. Third, hole spacing is critical. Too tight, and you waste product; too wide, and you get an irregular fracture. Several reviewers noted that the learning curve on spacing is real, and misaligned holes produce chunks rather than clean splits.

One more practical gripe: the 44 lb. box is heavy and awkward to handle, and the four bags inside aren't resealable once opened. If you only need one bag, you're storing the rest with exposure to humidity, which can degrade the powder's reactivity. Buy only what you need for the immediate job.

## FAQ

**Q: How much area will a 44 lb. box cover?**
A: Roughly 12–16 holes drilled at 1.5" diameter and 18" deep, spaced 10–12" apart. For a 6" thick concrete slab, that translates to about 4×4 feet of controlled breaking. For larger jobs, order multiple boxes — and note you'll need to mix each 11 lb. bag separately.

**Q: Can I use Dexpan in wet or rainy conditions?**
A: Yes, but protect the drilled holes from direct rain before pouring. The slurry needs consistent moisture to hydrate properly; heavy rain can wash it out or dilute it. Cover the area with a tarp or plastic sheeting after pouring.

**Q: Is it safe to use indoors?**
A: Yes, and this is a major advantage. There's no explosive force, no flying debris, and the expansion is purely lateral pressure. That said, wear safety glasses and gloves while mixing — the powder is alkaline and can irritate skin and eyes.
