Let's be blunt: most magnetic screen doors are cheap, flimsy things that last one season before the magnets lose their snap and the mesh sags. The LONGEAS version tries to fix that with an "upgraded" mesh that's supposedly stronger than fiberglass. Does it deliver, or is this just more marketing fluff? After digging through 220 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the screen door (sized to fit doors up to 39 inches wide), a roll of double-sided adhesive tape, push pins, and a set of small magnets for weighing down the bottom corners. The mesh itself is a polyester blend that feels noticeably thicker than the cheap fiberglass you find on $15 competitors. It's not mesh you'd use for a window screen — it's more like a heavy-duty netting with a tight weave that still lets air through.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The headline feature — the upgraded mesh — isn't hype. Multiple buyers mention pets specifically. One reviewer wrote: "My dog runs through this thing constantly and hasn't torn a single hole. The old fiberglass one lasted two weeks." That tracks with the material: polyester mesh has more give than fiberglass, so it stretches under pressure rather than ripping.

The magnetic closure is also smarter than most. Instead of just a center split with magnets, the entire perimeter has a magnetic strip that seals the screen flush against the door frame. This is a big deal for keeping bugs out — the common complaint with cheaper screens is gaps at the corners where insects sneak in. The full-frame closure eliminates that weak point.

## Where It Falls Short

The magnets at the bottom of the split are the weak link. They work fine on flat thresholds, but if your door has a raised threshold (anything over half an inch), the magnets drag across it and don't fully seal. You'll get a small gap at the bottom. One reviewer noted: "It works great on my sliding door, but the bottom doesn't fully close on my front door with a taller threshold." That's a real design limitation, not a defect.

Installation also requires patience. The adhesive strips hold well on smooth, clean frames, but if your door frame is textured or has chipping paint, the adhesive won't grip. You'll need to fall back on the included push pins — which work but leave small holes. Budget 20 minutes for installation if you're handy, 40 if you're not.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the **pet owner** first and foremost. If your dog or cat treats the screen door like a doggy door, the reinforced mesh is worth the extra cost over cheap alternatives. It's also for anyone with kids who will inevitably yank on the screen — the polyester weave handles rough treatment far better than fiberglass.

Skip it if you have an unusually tall door (over 83 inches — this is sized for standard doors) or a threshold that's noticeably raised. You'll end up with a screen that doesn't fully seal at the bottom, and you'll be annoyed every time you see it.

## Three Common Questions

**Will it fit a double door?** No. This is designed for single doors up to 39 inches wide. For double doors, you'd need two units with creative installation, which isn't practical.

**Can I trim it if my door is narrower?** Yes, but carefully. The magnetic strips run along the edges, so you can cut about 1–2 inches from each side without losing the seal. Beyond that, you risk cutting into the magnetic perimeter.

**Does it block UV or provide privacy?** Not really. The mesh is dark and see-through from inside, but from outside in daylight, you can see silhouettes. It's for airflow and bug control, not privacy.

## Final Verdict

**Buy it if** you have pets, kids, or a standard door frame and want a screen that survives more than one summer. **Skip it if** you have a raised threshold or textured door frames that will defeat the adhesive mounting.

At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, this is one of the better magnetic screen doors on the market — and the mesh upgrade is a legitimate improvement, not a gimmick. It's not perfect, but for the price, it's a solid investment in not swatting flies all season.
