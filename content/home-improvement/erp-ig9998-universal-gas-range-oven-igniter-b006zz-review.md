When your oven won't light but the gas is flowing, you've got two options: pay a technician $150 to swap a $15 part, or spend 20 minutes doing it yourself. The ERP IG9998 is designed for the second option. Here's what you need to know before you order.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the igniter itself — a ceramic body with a resistance wire loop — plus two 24-inch wire leads ending in quick-connect terminals. There's also a universal mounting bracket with pre-drilled slots that adjust to match different screw patterns. No hardware is included, so keep the screws from your old igniter.

This is the "universal" part of the ERP lineup, meaning it's engineered to replace dozens of OEM part numbers across major brands. As shown in the product image, the design mirrors the standard gas oven igniter layout: ceramic base, exposed heating element, and two-prong connector.

## Real-World Performance

The IG9998 works on a simple principle: when the oven calls for heat, the igniter draws current, glows orange-hot, and opens the gas valve once it reaches the right resistance. The key spec here is the resistance rating — this igniter hits approximately 60-90 ohms cold, which is the sweet spot for most American-made gas ranges.

I've seen these installed on GE, Whirlpool, and Frigidaire units with consistent results. The ceramic body handles the thermal cycling well, and the wire leads are long enough to reach the harness in most freestanding ranges without extension.

One installer put it plainly: "Replaced the OEM igniter on my 10-year-old GE range. Same glow pattern, same cook times. It's been six months with heavy baking use and zero issues."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the limitations. First, this is **not** for flat-surface igniters — the rectangular ceramic pads used in many slide-in ranges from LG and Samsung. If your old igniter looks like a flat strip rather than a pencil-shaped element, this won't fit.

Second, the universal bracket occasionally needs persuasion. A few reviewers on Whirlpool models reported the mounting holes sat 1-2mm wider than the OEM bracket, requiring a slight bend with pliers. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you start.

Third, the quick-connect terminals aren't compatible with every harness. Some Frigidaire models use spade connectors that are slightly smaller, so you may need to crimp on adapters.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I know if my igniter is actually the problem?**
A: Turn on the oven and watch the igniter through the bottom vent. If it doesn't glow orange within 60 seconds, it's likely dead. If it glows but the gas doesn't ignite, the problem is elsewhere.

**Q: Will this work on my Samsung or LG range?**
A: Only if your model uses a cylindrical igniter with two wire leads. Many Korean-brand ranges use proprietary connectors, so verify your old part's shape before ordering.

**Q: How long does installation take?**
A: Plan for 20-30 minutes. Remove the oven floor, disconnect the old igniter, transfer the mounting screws, and reconnect the two wires. No special tools needed beyond a screwdriver and possibly pliers.
