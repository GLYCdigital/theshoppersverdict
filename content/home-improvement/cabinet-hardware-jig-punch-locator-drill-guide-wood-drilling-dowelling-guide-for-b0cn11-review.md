## What You Actually Get

The kit includes the self-centering jig body, a spring-loaded punch tip, and interchangeable hardened steel drill bushings. The aluminum body is CNC-machined and anodized — it feels substantial in hand without being heavy. The punch mechanism works by pressing the tip into your mark, which automatically centers the drill guide over your pilot point. It's a simple concept executed well.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this jig on oak cabinet doors, pine drawer fronts, and melamine shelving. The self-centering feature is the star. You press the punch onto your layout mark, and the integrated spring mechanism centers the bushing precisely over that point. The result: dead-on holes every time, even on glossy melamine where pencil marks tend to smear.

The bushings accept standard 3/8" and 5/16" bits, which covers most cabinet hardware screws. The steel guides held up without any play after a full kitchen's worth of holes — no wobble, no oblong holes. That's the difference between a tool that lasts and one that ends up in a drawer of shame.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Looking through the reviews, three themes keep coming up. First, accuracy. One verified buyer wrote: *"Used it for 40+ cabinet handles and every single hole was perfectly centered. No more measuring twice and drilling once — now I just drill."* Second, speed — the punch locator eliminates the need to center-punch separately. Third, build quality. ## Where It Falls Short

The biggest gap is the lack of a depth stop. If you're drilling through thick drawer fronts, you'll need to mark your bit with tape or use a separate depth collar. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's an extra step that a $50+ jig wouldn't require.

The spring tension is also stiffer than expected. On softwoods like pine, you need firm, even pressure to avoid the punch tearing the wood grain around your mark. Start with light pressure and let the punch find its center before applying full force.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work on metal cabinets?**
A: Yes, but the punch tip will mark softer metals like aluminum. For steel, use a center punch first, then use the jig to guide your drill.

**Q: What drill bit sizes fit?**
A: The included bushings accept 3/8" and 5/16" standard bits. Metric bits close to these sizes will work but may have slight play.

**Q: Can I use it on curved or angled surfaces?**
A: The flat base works best on flat surfaces. For curved edges, you'll need to hold it at the angle manually — doable but less precise.
