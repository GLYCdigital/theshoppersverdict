## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the deck-mount faucet body, the waterfall spout, a hand shower with a 59-inch stainless steel hose, the diverter handle, and all mounting hardware. As shown in the product image, the finish is a polished chrome that reviewers consistently describe as "mirror-like" and evenly applied. You're getting a two-handle setup — separate hot and cold controls — with a center diverter that switches flow between the waterfall spout and the handheld sprayer.

## Performance and Build Quality

This is where the FSCEPIXI earns its keep. The waterfall spout is wide — roughly 7 inches across — and at standard home water pressure (around 50-60 PSI), it produces a genuinely even, glassy sheet of water that fills the tub quietly. That's the key differentiator. Cheap waterfall faucets often spit or channel water into uneven rivulets; this one doesn't. Multiple reviewers specifically note the "smooth, wide flow" and how it "doesn't splash" — a common complaint with lesser waterfall designs.

The body is solid brass with a ceramic cartridge, which matters for longevity. One reviewer put it bluntly: "This thing is heavy. You can tell it's not hollow pot metal." The lever handles operate with smooth, progressive resistance — no gritty grinding or over-travel. The hand shower is a functional bonus, not an afterthought: it has a decent spray pattern for rinsing the tub or washing hair, and the hose doesn't kink.

## Where It Falls Short

Nothing's perfect, and this faucet has two recurring complaints worth knowing before you buy.

**The diverter is stiff.** Switching between the waterfall and the hand shower requires a firm pull on the diverter knob. It's not broken — it's just a positive mechanical action. If you have arthritis or limited grip strength, this could be a daily annoyance. One reviewer mentioned their elderly mother "needs two hands" to switch modes.

**Installation isn't plug-and-play.** The deck mount requires a 1-1/8 inch mounting hole and substantial clearance underneath for the supply lines and mounting nuts. If you're replacing an existing faucet with a smaller hole size, you'll need to enlarge the opening — which means a hole saw and careful work on your tub deck. This isn't a "take out the old and drop in the new" job unless your current setup already matches.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my existing tub faucet holes?**
A: Check the hole diameter first. It needs 1-1/8 inches minimum. If your current faucet uses a smaller hole (common with older fixtures), you'll need to enlarge it. Measure before you order.

**Q: Does the hand shower have enough reach?**
A: Yes — the 59-inch hose covers a standard 60-inch alcove tub completely. You can reach both corners without pulling the hose taut.

**Q: Is the waterfall flow strong enough to fill a deep tub quickly?**
A: It's comparable to a standard 2.2 GPM tub filler — not a high-volume rain shower head. It fills a large soaker tub in about 10-12 minutes. If you want faster fill, look for a 3+ GPM model, but be aware of your local flow restrictions.
