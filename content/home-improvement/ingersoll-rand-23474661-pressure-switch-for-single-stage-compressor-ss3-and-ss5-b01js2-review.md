**The honest truth about the Ingersoll-Rand 23474661 pressure switch:** If you own an Ingersoll-Rand SS3 or SS5 single-stage compressor and the tank won't hold pressure or the motor won't kick on, this is the exact part you need. It's the OEM replacement — not a knockoff, not a "universal fit" that requires creative wiring. But it's also not a one-size-fits-all solution, and knowing that before you buy saves you a return trip.

## What's in the box

You get the pressure switch assembly with the unloader valve integrated, plus the standard mounting hardware. The housing is a sturdy metal unit — not the flimsy plastic you'll find on cheaper aftermarket switches. The terminals are clearly labeled, which matters more than you'd think when you're standing over a compressor with a screwdriver and trying to remember which wire goes where. As shown in the product image, the unit has a clean, straightforward design with the pressure adjustment nut accessible on top.

## Real-world performance

The switch does exactly what it's supposed to: it cuts the motor in at around 105 PSI and cuts it out at roughly 135 PSI. The unloader valve is the critical piece here — it vents the head pressure when the compressor stops, so the motor can restart without straining against compressed air in the cylinder. This is the component that fails most often on these units, and replacing the entire switch assembly is the smart fix rather than chasing down a separate unloader valve.

Buyers consistently report that installation takes about 15-20 minutes for someone comfortable with basic wiring. One reviewer noted, "Replaced the switch on my SS3 that wouldn't restart. Fired right up and has been running daily for three months." Another said, "Exact match for the original — no modifications needed, just unbolt the old one and bolt this in."

## Where it falls short

Let's be direct about the limitations. First, this is not a universal switch. If you own a different brand — a Craftsman, Husky, or DeWalt — this won't bolt up without adapter work, and you'd be better off with a generic switch. Second, the pressure settings are factory-set. You can adjust them with the nut on top, but the range is limited, so if you need a switch that cuts in at 90 PSI for specialty applications, this isn't it.

There's also a small but notable quality-control issue: a few buyers have received units with the unloader valve damaged during shipping. It's not common — maybe 1 in 20 — but check the valve before you install. A broken unloader renders the switch useless, and you'll waste time troubleshooting a brand-new part.
