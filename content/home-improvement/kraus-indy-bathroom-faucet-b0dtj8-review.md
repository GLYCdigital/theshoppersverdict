Let's be blunt: most bathroom faucets under $100 are zinc alloy with a chrome veneer that starts flaking in year two. The Kraus Indy isn't that. It's a solid brass unit with real weight, and it shows up ready to work.

**What's actually in the box**

Kraus keeps it simple. You get the faucet body, two lever handles, supply lines with 3/8-inch compression fittings, mounting hardware, and a template. No drain assembly — that's the first gotcha. You'll need to buy a separate pop-up drain if your sink doesn't have one. Plan for $20–30 extra.

The finish options matter here. The Indy comes in brushed nickel, chrome, matte black, and oil-rubbed bronze. As shown in the product image, the matte black version looks sharp against white vessel sinks. The brushed nickel is the safest bet for matching existing hardware, but it's more prone to showing water spots than the darker finishes.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The first thing you notice is the weight. This faucet has substance — the brass body feels solid when you tighten the mounting nut. The ceramic cartridge is the star of the show. Handles turn with a precise, damped action that cheap faucets can't fake. No wobble, no grinding, just smooth operation.

The 8.6-inch spout reach is genuinely useful. It clears most vessel bowls and puts the stream where you need it, not against the back of the sink. The 5.6-inch height gives you enough room for hand-washing without splashing your shirt. Aerated flow is steady at 1.2 GPM — meets water-restriction codes and still feels substantial.

Installation is straightforward for anyone who's done a faucet before. The supply lines are pre-attached and flexible, which saves you from wiggling under the sink with a wrench. A homeowner with basic tools can handle this in 45 minutes. If you're replacing an older faucet, check your supply line connections — the 3/8-inch compression fittings are standard, but older setups sometimes need an adapter.

**What buyers keep saying**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews holds up when you read the actual feedback. One consistent theme: quality exceeds price expectations. A verified buyer wrote, "Feels like a $200 faucet. Solid, heavy, and the finish is flawless." Another noted, "Install took 30 minutes and the handles are buttery smooth."

The most common complaint? The missing drain. Several reviewers mention the frustration of unboxing and realizing they need to order a separate piece before installation can finish.

**Where the Indy falls short**

The missing drain is the headline issue. It's a cost-saving move that complicates what should be a one-stop purchase. Also, the finish durability varies by color. The oil-rubbed bronze is more delicate than the others — a few buyers report it wearing at contact points within a year.

The lever handles are cross-shaped rather than the modern joystick style. Some people love the classic look; others find them less ergonomic for one-finger operation. If you want a single-handle faucet, this isn't it — the Indy is two-handle only.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY homeowners** who want a solid brass faucet without paying solid brass prices
- **Rental property owners** who need durability without luxury cost
- **Anyone renovating a half-bath** who wants modern styling that won't look dated in five years

Skip it if you need a single-handle faucet, want a complete kit with drain included, or prefer ultra-minimalist design.

**FAQ**

*Is the Kraus Indy compatible with vessel sinks?*
Yes, the 8.6-inch spout reach and 5.6-inch height clear most vessel bowls. Measure your sink depth first — anything over 4 inches deep might put the stream too close to the back edge.

*Does it come with a drain?*
No. You'll need to purchase a pop-up drain separately. Kraus sells matching drains, but any standard 1-1/4-inch drain will work.

*What's the warranty?*
Kraus offers a limited lifetime warranty on the faucet body and finish, with a 5-year warranty on parts. That's solid coverage for this price point.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a brass-bodied faucet with smooth ceramic valves and a finish that'll hold up past the return window. The missing drain is annoying but manageable — budget for it and you're still well under what comparable brass faucets cost elsewhere.

**Skip it if** you want a complete kit with everything in one box, or if you prefer single-handle operation.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid value, honest construction, one frustrating omission.
