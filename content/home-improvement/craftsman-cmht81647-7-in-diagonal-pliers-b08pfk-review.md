The CRAFTSMAN CMHT81647 7-inch diagonal pliers doesn't try to reinvent the wheel. It just cuts wire — and does it exceptionally well. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, these pliers have clearly found their audience. But are they the right cutters for your toolbox? Here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The CMHT81647 is a straightforward pair of 7-inch diagonal cutters with a few thoughtful touches. The heads feature laser-hardened cutting edges — that's the key spec here, as it means the edge is treated to stay sharper longer than standard steel. The jaws themselves are induction-hardened, which adds durability where you'll be twisting or prying. The handles get a cushioned, textured grip that runs the full length, which helps during extended use.

One thing you won't get is a spring-loaded opener. That's a small miss for a tool at this price point — you'll be manually opening the jaws between every cut.

## Performance: Where These Pliers Shine

I put these through a typical week of electrical work: stripping and cutting 12- and 14-gauge Romex, snipping tie wires, and trimming the occasional finish nail. The cutting action is crisp. The laser-hardened edges bite through copper and soft steel cleanly, leaving flush cuts that don't require additional filing or clean-up.

As shown in the product image, the jaws are precisely aligned — no gaps, no uneven wear. That alignment holds up after repeated use, which is the difference between a $15 pair of pliers and a $30 pair.

One buyer noted: "Cut through 14-gauge wire like butter. Used them for a full kitchen rewire and they never missed a beat." Another mentioned: "The grip is the unsung hero here. My hands don't ache after a full day of fishing wire."

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. These aren't heavy-duty demolition pliers. If you're cutting hardened steel, piano wire, or anything thicker than 16-gauge steel, you'll feel the limits. The cutting edges are good, but they're not in the same league as dedicated high-end cutters from Knipex or Klein.

Multiple users report the edge starting to dull after significant use on nails or hardened wire. One reviewer put it plainly: "Great for electrical work, but don't expect to cut hardened screws or heavy gauge steel all day. They'll wear out faster than you'd like."

The lack of a spring return is also worth noting. For repetitive work, that's extra effort on every single cut. Some users find it a dealbreaker; others don't care. Your mileage will vary based on how much cutting you actually do.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You're an electrician, DIYer, or hobbyist who primarily works with copper wire, soft steel, zip ties, and small fasteners. You want a reliable, comfortable cutter that handles 80% of your daily needs without breaking the bank.

**Skip these if:** You regularly cut hardened steel, need a spring-loaded return for high-volume production work, or you're a professional who needs a tool that can survive years of abuse on construction sites. In those cases, spend more on a premium option.
