# Talllor Waterfall Sink Review: A Statement Piece That Demands Attention

Most bathroom faucets are forgettable. You turn them on, wash your hands, move on. The Talllor Waterfall Sink is not that faucet. From the moment water hits the wide, flattened spout, it transforms into a single, glassy sheet that pours into the basin like a miniature dam release. It's the kind of detail guests notice and comment on. But a dramatic look only matters if the hardware behind it holds up — and that's where this review gets interesting.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the faucet body, the dual-function handle assembly, a flexible supply hose, mounting hardware, and a silicone gasket. No drain assembly is included — that's a separate purchase. The unit itself is substantial, weighing in around 7 pounds, which tells you immediately this isn't a hollow, chrome-plated plastic fixture. The brushed finish on the brass body has a satin feel that's pleasant to touch and notably resistant to the fingerprint smudging that plagues polished surfaces.

## Performance and Build Quality

The waterfall effect is the headline feature, and it delivers. As shown in the product image, the spout has a wide, flattened opening — roughly 4 inches across — that creates a laminar flow. Turn the handle to about 50 percent and you get a smooth, air-free sheet of water that's genuinely mesmerizing. At full pressure, the flow widens further and the cascade becomes more aggressive, though it never splatters excessively.

The dual-function handle is a thoughtful touch. Most waterfall faucets use a single-lever design that makes temperature control guesswork. Here, one handle rotates for temperature while a second, smaller lever adjusts flow rate. It takes a moment to get used to, but the independent control is genuinely useful — you can set a comfortable temperature and then adjust volume without scalding yourself.

One owner put it plainly: *"I was skeptical about another Amazon faucet, but the brass body is heavy and solid. The waterfall effect is exactly as shown — no cheap tricks with the aerator."* That sentiment echoes through the reviews, with multiple buyers noting the weight and material quality exceeded expectations at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

The installation requirement is the biggest hurdle. The Talllor needs a 1.38-inch deck hole, which is larger than the standard 1.25-inch opening most countertops have. If you're replacing an existing faucet, you'll likely need a carbide hole saw to widen the opening. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's a surprise if you're not prepared.

The second issue concerns shallow sinks. The waterfall exits the spout at a relatively horizontal angle, so if your basin is under 6 inches deep, water can hit the back wall and splash forward. One reviewer noted: *"Works beautifully with my deep vessel sink, but I can see how it would splash on a standard shallow builder-grade basin."* Measure your sink depth before committing.

## Who Should Buy This

This faucet suits two specific buyers. First, the homeowner renovating a bathroom with a vessel sink or deep rectangular basin — someone who wants a focal point that elevates the whole room. Second, anyone with hard water, since the brushed finish hides mineral deposits far better than chrome or nickel.

Skip it if you're working with a standard shallow drop-in sink, or if you're not comfortable widening the mounting hole yourself. This is not a weekend swap for a novice DIYer without the right tools.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the waterfall effect require high water pressure?**
A: No, but it looks best above 40 PSI. Below that, the sheet narrows and can look like a thick stream rather than a full waterfall.

**Q: Will this fit a 1-inch or 1.25-inch existing hole?**
A: No. The Talllor requires a 1.38-inch opening. You'll need to widen the hole, which means drilling through porcelain or stone — doable, but plan for it.

**Q: Is the finish prone to water spotting?**
A: The brushed texture hides spots significantly better than polished finishes. Occasional wiping keeps it looking new, but it's forgiving between cleanings.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuine conversation-piece faucet with solid brass construction and don't mind a slightly more involved installation. **Skip it if** you have a shallow sink or want a true drop-in replacement with zero modification.

The Talllor Waterfall Sink earns a **4.2 out of 5** — a striking, well-built fixture with real presence, held back only by installation quirks and splash considerations on certain sink types. For the right bathroom, it's a showstopper.
