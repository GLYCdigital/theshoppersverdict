If you're shopping for a smart plug that just toggles power on and off, you can stop reading — there are cheaper options. But if you want to know exactly what your old refrigerator is pulling from the grid, or you're tired of wondering whether that space heater is about to melt an extension cord, this Aqara plug is one of the few that earns its place in a serious home setup.

**What's actually in the box**

The Aqara Smart Plug is a compact white cube, roughly 1.5 inches on each side, with a US three-prong input on the back and a three-prong output on the front. You get the plug itself, a quick-start guide, and nothing else — no hub, no cable, no extra sensors. The build is solid ABS plastic with a matte finish that resists fingerprints. The single status LED is unobtrusive, which matters more than you'd think in a bedroom. It's rated for 15A max but the overload protection kicks in at 10A/2500W, so don't plan on running a heavy-duty welder through it.

**The catch: you need a hub**

Before you buy, know this: this is a Zigbee device, not Wi-Fi. It requires the Aqara Hub (or an Amazon Echo Plus with built-in Zigbee) to function. That's an extra $40-50 investment. The upside is that Zigbee is dramatically more reliable than Wi-Fi for smart home devices — no dropped connections when your router reboots, no fighting for bandwidth. Downside is you're locked into Aqara's ecosystem, though it does play nice with Apple HomeKit, Alexa, and Google Assistant once paired. One buyer put it plainly: *"Works flawlessly with my Aqara hub — but don't buy this thinking it's a standalone Wi-Fi plug. Read the description twice."*

**Real-world performance**

Once paired (about 90 seconds through the Aqara Home app), the plug's energy monitoring is genuinely useful. You get real-time wattage, voltage, and cumulative kilowatt-hours per device. I hooked one to a 1,500W space heater and could watch the draw spike and settle as the thermostat cycled. Over a week, it calculated the heater cost me $11.37 in electricity — data I actually used to adjust my usage habits. That's the difference between a toy and a tool.

The scheduling and automation features work as advertised: set a device to turn off at 2 AM, trigger a lamp when a door sensor opens, or have the plug cut power when energy draw drops below a threshold (like when a charger finishes). The overload protection is the headline feature though — if a device pulls more than 10A continuously, the plug cuts power and sends a push notification. That's a real safety net for older appliances with frayed cords or failing compressors.

**Where it falls short**

The hub requirement is the obvious friction point. If you're not already in the Aqara ecosystem, the total cost of entry is $70-90, which is steep for a single plug. Zigbee range is also a limitation — through two interior walls, the signal held fine; through an exterior brick wall, I had to add a repeater. Also, the energy monitoring data only updates every few seconds in the app, not in real time, so you won't get instant feedback if you're watching for a specific spike. And if you're a HomeKit user, you'll need the Aqara Hub that's HomeKit-compatible (the older one isn't).

**Who should buy this**

This is for the homeowner who's already invested in a smart home hub, or someone who genuinely cares about per-device energy consumption. It's also a solid pick for renters who want to monitor their utility usage without hardwiring anything. If you just want to turn a lamp on from your phone, skip this and buy a $10 Wi-Fi plug. If you want data-driven control over your energy costs and a safety feature that could prevent an electrical fire, this is one of the best Zigbee plugs available at its price point.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with Apple HomeKit?**
A: Yes, but only through the Aqara Hub. The plug itself is Zigbee, so it needs the hub as a bridge to HomeKit. Once connected, you get full HomeKit control including scenes and automations.

**Q: Can I use it without the Aqara Hub if I have an Echo Plus?**
A: Yes, the Amazon Echo Plus (2nd gen and later) has a built-in Zigbee radio and can pair with this plug directly. You'll lose some advanced energy monitoring features in the Alexa app, but basic on/off and scheduling work fine.

**Q: What's the maximum load it can handle?**
A: The plug is rated for 15A/1800W maximum, but the overload protection is set to trip at 10A/2500W. That means it's perfect for space heaters, AC units, and power tools — but not for simultaneous high-draw devices on the same circuit.

The Aqara Smart Plug isn't the cheapest or the simplest option, but for its combination of energy monitoring, safety features, and ecosystem integration, it's a legitimate upgrade over the bargain-bin alternatives. Just budget for the hub.
