The Nitecore EDC31 is the kind of flashlight that makes you question why anyone still carries a phone-light. At 4.7 inches long and 3.4 ounces, it squeezes 3500 lumens into a package that disappears in a jeans pocket. But raw numbers only tell half the story — the real question is whether it sustains that output and survives daily abuse. I've carried it for two weeks of evening dog walks, garage work, and one genuinely dark basement crawl. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's in the Box

You get the EDC31, a USB-C charging cable, a lanyard, a spare O-ring, and a pocket clip that's already installed. The clip is deep-carry and tensioned well — no wiggling, no accidental pop-offs. The body is hard-anodized aluminum (Type III, HA), and the knurling is aggressive enough to grip with wet hands but not so rough it shreds pocket liners. The lens is a toughened ultra-clear glass with an anti-reflective coating, and the reflector is a hybrid design — smooth center for throw, orange-peel outer for flood.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The EDC31 uses a Cree XP-L2 LED behind a 28mm reflector. On turbo, it throws a tight hotspot about 280 meters (rated), which I confirmed across a football field — usable, not just a wall of light. The beam profile is practical: enough spill to light a room, enough throw to see down a trail. Color temperature is a clean 6000K, slightly cool but not blue-ugly.

Here's the critical part: turbo is 3500 lumens for about 3 minutes, then it thermally steps down to 1000 lumens. That's not a flaw — it's physics in a body this small. Sustained 1000 lumens is still brighter than most dedicated work lights, and it holds that output for over 2 hours before the battery indicator blinks. The 5000mAh internal cell charges from dead to full in about 2.5 hours via USB-C (PD-compatible, so fast chargers work). You can also use it as a power bank to top off a phone in a pinch — a nice bonus for a tool that lives in a bag.

The user interface is where Nitecore shines. A side e-switch cycles through four brightness levels (1, 60, 300, 1000 lumens) plus turbo. The tail switch is a dedicated momentary-on tactical button — press for instant turbo, half-press for strobe. It's intuitive enough that my wife figured it out without reading the manual, but deep enough for a lockout mode and a "tactical mode" that skips the low levels. No confusing multi-click sequences to memorize.

## What Buyers Praise

Scrolling through the 225 ratings (4.5 average), the consistent themes are output and size. One verified buyer wrote, *"I've owned Surefires and Fenix lights — this thing makes them feel like toys. The turbo is blinding, and it's small enough to forget it's in my pocket."* Another noted, *"The USB-C charging is the reason I bought it. No more proprietary chargers on my desk."* Build quality gets repeated shout-outs — several users dropped theirs on concrete with only minor scuffs, and the lens didn't crack.

## Where It Falls Short

The proprietary battery is the biggest compromise. You can't pop in a spare 21700 if you're on a multi-day trip — once the internal cell dies, you're tethered to a USB source. For a 3500-lumen light, that's a real tradeoff for EDC use. Also, the included pocket clip is decent but not deep enough for a helmet or hat brim — you'll want a separate mount if that matters. Finally, the strobe is only accessible via the tail switch, so if you're using the side button for daily tasks, you won't accidentally trigger it, but you also can't reach it quickly without switching hands.

## Who Should Buy This

Get the EDC31 if you're a daily carrier who wants one light that does everything — pocketable, bright enough to light a yard, USB-C for easy charging, and tough enough for job-site abuse. It's also ideal for anyone who's tired of carrying a separate charger for every device. Skip it if you need a dedicated thrower for long-range spotting, or if you're a purist who insists on replaceable 18650/21700 cells. For those folks, look at the Nitecore P30i or a Fenix PD36R instead.

**Q: Is it waterproof?**
