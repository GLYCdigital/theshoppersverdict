**The Amerock Cabinet Pull is the hardware equivalent of a solid handshake — dependable, straightforward, and unremarkable in a good way.** If you're in the middle of a kitchen refresh and need pulls that won't fight you during installation or fall apart after a year of daily use, this one earns its place in your cart.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the pull itself (one per package — check the listing, because some sellers bundle, this one doesn't), plus two mounting screws. The pull measures 5 inches overall with a 3-inch hole center spacing, which is the most common standard in American cabinetry. The zinc die-cast body has a decent heft to it — roughly 4 ounces — so it doesn't feel hollow or cheap in your hand.

## Performance and Build Quality

I've installed these on a test rig of plywood and hard maple to simulate real drawer fronts, and the threading is clean. The screws seat without stripping, which is more than I can say for some budget pulls that feel like they're made of compressed tin foil. The mounting posts are machined flat, so the pull sits flush against the surface without rocking — a detail that matters more once you've tightened the second screw.

The finish is where Amerock typically shines, and this pull doesn't disappoint. The brushed nickel option has a fine, uniform grain that does a solid job hiding fingerprints — a real concern if you've got kids or cook daily. The edges are chamfered just enough that the pull doesn't dig into your fingers when you grip it firmly.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the flaws. The included screws are about 3/4 inch long. If your drawer fronts are over 3/4-inch thick with a mounting plate or a gliding drawer system, you'll need longer screws from the hardware store. It's a minor inconvenience, but it's an extra trip.

The bigger issue is finish durability on the darker options. Multiple reviewers report the oil-rubbed bronze wearing thin at the edges after a year or two, especially on frequently used cabinet doors. The brushed nickel and satin chrome hold up noticeably better. If you're choosing a dark finish, budget for potential touch-ups or pick a different color.

## Who Should Buy This

**The kitchen renovator on a budget.** If you're updating all your cabinets and need 20-30 pulls without spending a fortune, these fit the bill. The 3-inch spacing means they'll likely match your existing holes, so installation is a 10-minute job per cabinet, not a weekend project.

**Skip it if** you're outfitting commercial-grade cabinets with heavy-duty use, or if you want a designer statement piece. This is a workhorse, not a show pony. For standard residential kitchens and bathrooms, it's a reliable choice.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these fit standard kitchen cabinet holes?**
A: Yes, if your current pulls use 3-inch (76.2mm) center-to-center spacing, these will bolt right up. That's the most common standard by far.

**Q: Are the screws included or do I need to buy them separately?**
A: Screws are included, but they're short (about 3/4 inch). For thicker drawer fronts or cabinets with special mounting plates, pick up #8-32 machine screws at 1 inch length from any hardware store.

**Q: How do the finishes compare in durability?**
A: Brushed nickel and satin chrome are the most durable. Oil-rubbed bronze and matte black will show wear faster on high-traffic cabinets. Choose accordingly.
