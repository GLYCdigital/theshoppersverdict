You've seen the videos: a person walks up to their door, it clicks open, no keys, no phone, just their face. The Lockly Visage Zeno Series promises that experience. But at this price point, you're paying for more than a gadget — you're paying for security theater or actual security? I tested it for three weeks on a main entry door. Here's the unvarnished truth.

**What's in the Box**

The box is heavier than you'd expect. Inside you get the lock body, a solid metal interior assembly, the exterior keypad/face module, two physical keys (hidden behind a sliding cover), mounting screws, a rechargeable battery pack, and a USB-C charging cable. No deadbolt is included — you'll reuse your existing one or buy a compatible model. Installation took me 40 minutes with a screwdriver and a drill for the strike plate reinforcement. The instructions are clear enough, but the included paper template is easy to misalign — measure twice, drill once.

**Performance: The Face Unlock Reality**

The 3D biometric camera is the headline feature, and it genuinely works. Walk up normally and the lock recognizes you in under a second — no phone, no code, no fumbling. It uses infrared illumination, so pitch-black porches aren't a problem. I tested it with sunglasses, a backwards hat, and a face mask; it cleared all three in under two seconds. That's impressive.

Where it stumbles: the 50-face storage limit. That sounds like a lot until you add family, close friends, and regular visitors. You'll find yourself curating your face list. And the lock doesn't tell you when you're near the cap — you just start getting "face not recognized" errors.

**The Fingerprint Reader and Keypad**

The fingerprint sensor on the front edge is fast — roughly 0.4 seconds from touch to unlock. It handles dry, slightly sweaty fingers without issue. The keypad is a fallback, not the star, but it's well-built with tactile buttons and a backlight that adjusts to ambient light. In direct sun, the numbers stay visible, which is more than I can say for many competitor locks.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews isn't inflated. The recurring praise centers on speed and security. One buyer noted: *"I've had three other smart locks. This is the first one where I don't wait — it's just open before I reach for the handle."* Another highlighted the spoof resistance: *"My last lock's camera got fooled by a printed photo. This one doesn't flinch at screens or photos. That's why I upgraded."* The build quality also gets consistent shoutouts — the full metal housing feels like it belongs on a $300+ lock, not a plastic toy.

**Where It Falls Short**

The battery life claim is the biggest stretch. Lockly advertises 6 months of use. In reality, with face unlock as your primary method (which it will be — it's the whole point), you're charging every 4-6 weeks. The included battery pack is proprietary, so you can't swap in AAs in a pinch. You'll want a second battery (sold separately) charged and ready.

The physical footprint is also worth noting. As shown in the product image, the lock body extends 1.4 inches from the door. On a standard interior door or a narrow-frame entry, it looks bulky. It also requires a 2-1/8" bore hole and a 1" latch bore — verify your door's dimensions before ordering, or you'll be making a router trip.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Tech-forward homeowners** who want keyless entry without pulling out a phone every time
- **Families with kids** who lose keys and codes — face unlock removes the friction entirely
- **People with mobility issues** who struggle with fumbling for keys

**Skip it if:**

- **You rent** — the installation is semi-permanent and your landlord may object
- **You have a wide social circle** — 50 faces max is a real constraint
- **You hate charging devices** — this is a monthly battery routine, not yearly

**FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask**

**Q: Can someone unlock it with a photo of my face?**
No. The 3D camera maps depth and requires a live face. Photos, videos, and even a silicone mask won't trigger it. I tested a high-res photo on a phone screen — no luck.

**Q: Does it work with Alexa or Google Assistant?**
No direct voice assistant integration. It works with the Lockly app for remote access, activity logs, and temporary codes, but don't expect "Alexa, unlock the door." For security reasons, that's arguably a feature, not a bug.

**Q: What happens when the battery dies?**
The physical key override works, but it's hidden behind a sliding cover on the bottom. You'll need a paperclip or fingernail to access it. The lock also gives you audible and app warnings at 20% and 10% battery.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want the fastest, most frictionless keyless entry available right now and you're willing to manage a monthly charging habit. The face recognition is genuinely impressive, the anti-spoofing tech is class-leading, and the build quality justifies the premium.

**Skip it if** you need voice assistant integration, have a busy household with more than 50 regular faces, or want a low-maintenance battery experience.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect, but it's the closest thing to "touchless entry that just works" I've tested. The battery life and face limit are real tradeoffs, but the speed and security make up for them.
