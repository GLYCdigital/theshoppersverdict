**The $15 Fix That Keeps Toddlers Out of the Linen Closet**

If you've got a curious toddler and bi-fold closet doors, you already know the problem: those flimsy hook-and-eye latches either don't line up, get bent, or are exactly the kind of thing a determined 2-year-old figures out in about 30 seconds. The Safety Innovations Bi-fold Door Lock (2-pack) is the solution that keeps showing up in parenting forums and Amazon carts — and after looking at what it actually does, it's easy to see why it holds a 4.7-star rating from 235 buyers.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes two complete locks, each with two mounting plates and a spring-loaded latch mechanism. The design is refreshingly simple: one piece mounts to the top edge of your bi-fold door, the other to the door frame. When the door closes, the latch snaps into place automatically. To open it, you squeeze the release tabs. No keys, no magnets, no batteries — just mechanical friction that a small child can't quite figure out.

The mechanism is made from ABS plastic, which is where I have my one hesitation. It's sturdy enough for daily use, but if you're the type who slams closet doors, this might not survive years of abuse. That said, the spring mechanism is the standout feature — it engages smoothly and releases cleanly, which is more than I can say for most childproofing hardware in this price range.

**Real-World Performance**

I've seen these installed on everything from standard 2-panel closet doors to the wider 4-panel models, and the fit is consistently solid. The mounting plates are designed with slotted screw holes that give you about a quarter-inch of adjustment — this matters more than you'd think, because bi-fold doors are rarely perfectly aligned. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I installed these on three different closet doors in about 15 minutes total. The alignment slots saved me — my doors are all slightly crooked and these still latch perfectly."

Another buyer with a particularly determined child confirmed the effectiveness: "My 2-year-old figured out every other lock we tried. He pulled, pushed, and shook this one for a full week before giving up. It's been 4 months and he still can't open it."

The latch doesn't interfere with the folding action of the doors, and it's quiet — no rattling when the door is closed.

**Where It Falls Short**

The plastic construction is the main compromise. At roughly $7.50 per lock, you're paying for the mechanism and the convenience, not premium materials. If you have a door that gets heavy use, consider that the screws included are basic Phillips-head options that can strip in hollow-core doors. Pre-drilling tiny pilot holes solves this, but it's an extra step the instructions don't mention.

Also worth noting: the release tabs require a deliberate squeeze-and-pull motion. Adults with arthritis or limited hand strength might find it slightly awkward at first, though most users report it becomes second-nature within a day.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is a no-brainer if you have:
- **Toddlers or young children** who've discovered the joy of unrolling toilet paper or "organizing" your shoes
- **Bi-fold closet doors** in bedrooms, hallways, or laundry rooms — anywhere you need childproofing
- **Rental properties** where you can't drill large holes or install permanent hardware (the screw holes are small and easily filled)

**Skip it if** you need a lock for a solid wood door, want something that works with smart home integration, or you're looking for a high-security solution — this is childproofing, not a deadbolt.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Will this work on doors that aren't perfectly square?**  
Yes — the slotted mounting plates provide enough adjustment to handle minor misalignment. Just loosen the screws, position the latch, and tighten.

**Q: Can an adult open this easily while carrying laundry?**  
Generally yes. The squeeze release is designed for one-handed operation, though it takes a bit of practice to do smoothly with a full load.

**Q: Does it leave visible holes if I remove it?**  
Four small screw holes per lock. They're easy to fill with spackle and touch-up paint if you're renting or moving.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a simple, effective, non-destructive way to keep little hands out of closets — and you value a product that installs in minutes without special tools or skills.

**Skip it if** you're hoping for a heavy-duty, metal, permanent lock or you need something that works on doors other than bi-folds.

For the price of a couple of fancy coffees, you get two reliable child safety locks that actually do the job. That's a solid trade. The 4.6/5 rating is earned — just take the extra minute to pre-drill pilot holes and these will serve you well for years.
