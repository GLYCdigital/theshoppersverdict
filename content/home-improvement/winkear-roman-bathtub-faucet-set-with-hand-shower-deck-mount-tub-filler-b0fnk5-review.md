Let’s be blunt: most deck-mount tub fillers under $200 are cheap zinc alloy with paint that flakes off in a year. The WINKEAR Roman Bathtub Faucet Set isn’t that. It’s a solid brass unit that looks like it costs twice what it does — but it has a couple of quirks you need to know before you drill holes in your tub deck.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the deck-mount faucet body, a separate hand shower with a 60-inch stainless steel hose, the diverter assembly, and mounting hardware. The spout has a classic Roman curve with a clean, brushed nickel finish (also available in matte black and chrome). As shown in the product image, the hand shower sits on a cradle mount beside the main spout — a two-handle configuration with separate hot and cold valves.

The body is solid brass, not the hollow-feeling pot metal you see on budget competitors. It’s heavy — you’ll notice the heft when you lift it out of the box. That weight translates to stability once mounted.

**Real-World Performance**

Water flow is strong. The spout delivers a wide, even stream that fills a standard tub quickly. The diverter is a push-button on the spout face — push it in and water redirects to the hand shower, pull it out and it returns to the tub. It’s simple and mechanical, so there’s less to fail than with a rotary valve.

The hand shower has multiple spray settings and a rubber nozzle face that’s easy to wipe clean of hard water buildup. Pressure is decent even with the diverter engaged, though you’ll notice a slight drop — that’s physics, not a defect.

Installation is straightforward if you’re handy. The deck mount requires a 1.5-inch hole for the main body and a separate hole for the hand shower cradle. Standard 8-inch centers line up with most pre-drilled tub decks. One buyer noted, *“Took me about two hours to install including cutting the holes. The instructions are basic but the design is intuitive.”*

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The finish holds up. Multiple reviewers who’ve had the faucet installed for over a year report no chipping, tarnishing, or water spotting. One long-term owner said, *“I was skeptical about the price, but after 14 months it still looks brand new. The brass body is the real deal.”*

The dual-function diverter gets consistent thumbs-up. It’s a feature you don’t see on many faucets in this price range, and it works reliably. Another reviewer highlighted, *“The push-button diverter is genius. No more twisting small knobs with wet hands.”*

**Where It Falls Short**

The hand shower hose is 60 inches. For a standard tub, that’s fine. But if you have a deeper soaking tub or want to reach the far end for rinsing, you’ll be stretching. Several buyers mentioned this — one said, *“Great faucet, but I wish the hose was longer. It barely reaches the back of my deep tub.”*

The finish options are limited to three colors, and the brushed nickel has a slightly cool tone that might not match warmer fixtures. Also, the handles are on the smaller side — fine for most, but if you have large hands or arthritis, they can feel fiddly.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **DIY homeowners** replacing an aging tub filler — the deck-mount design is forgiving if your existing holes are on standard centers.
- **Bathroom renovators** building a freestanding tub setup who want a premium look without paying premium prices.
- **Anyone upgrading from a wall-mount faucet** — the Roman style adds a spa-like feel to any bathroom.

Skip it if you need a longer hand shower hose, prefer a single-handle design, or have a tub deck thicker than 1.5 inches — you’ll need to verify clearance before purchasing.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this faucet include the drain and overflow assembly?**
A: No. It’s just the faucet, hand shower, and mounting hardware. You’ll need to buy a separate tub drain kit.

**Q: Will this fit a 6-foot freestanding tub?**
A: The spout reaches about 6 inches over the tub edge. If your tub deck is wider than that, the water stream may land inside the tub wall rather than in the center. Measure your deck depth first.

**Q: Can I install this myself or do I need a plumber?**
A: If you’re comfortable with basic plumbing (connecting supply lines and applying thread sealant), it’s a DIY job. If you’re cutting new holes in a stone or tile deck, consider a professional for that part.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a solid brass, good-looking tub filler that won’t break the bank and you’re okay with a shorter hand shower hose.

**Skip it if** you need extended reach, prefer a single-handle minimalist look, or demand a huge selection of finish colors.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — This is a genuinely well-built faucet that delivers on its promise. The brass construction and reliable diverter justify the price. The short hose and limited finish options keep it from a perfect score, but for most buyers, this is a smart, durable purchase that will look great for years.
