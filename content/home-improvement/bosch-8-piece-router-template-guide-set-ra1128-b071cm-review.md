Let me be blunt: if you're routing dovetails, hinge mortises, or pattern-cutting with a template, the difference between a $20 no-name guide set and the BOSCH RA1128 isn't just polish — it's precision that shows up in your joinery. This 8-piece set has earned its 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews, and after looking at what it actually offers, I get why.

**What's in the box**  
You get six aluminum guide bushings with hardened steel inserts (sizes: 5/16", 3/8", 1/2", 5/8", 3/4", 7/8"), plus two brass lock nuts and a small wrench. As shown in the product image, each guide has a stepped shoulder that seats cleanly into your router's sub-base — no wobble, no shims needed. The 5/16" guide is the standout here; most budget sets skip it, but it's essential for tight-radius template work like inlay or small hinge plates.

**Performance and build quality**  
The steel inserts are the real story. Cheaper guides are often all-zinc, which deforms after a few aggressive passes against a hardboard template. The RA1128's hardened steel collar rides against the template edge with minimal friction and zero galling — even after hours of use, the guides stay round and true. I've seen reviewers note this specifically: *"Used for 20+ hinge mortises, still measures perfectly round."* That durability matters when you're paying for precision.

Fit is universal — these mate with standard 3/4" sub-bases, which covers most Bosch, DeWalt, and Porter-Cable routers. The brass lock nuts thread cleanly and hold firm under vibration; no creeping mid-cut. Depth is consistent across all six sizes, so switching guides doesn't require re-leveling your base.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The overwhelming feedback centers on accuracy. One reviewer wrote: *"My template cuts are now dead-on — zero tear-out, zero drift."* Another highlighted the value: *"Half the price of the Freud set, same results."* The included wrench is a small touch, but it fits the lock nuts perfectly and stores in a drawer without taking up space.

**Where it falls short**  
The lock nuts are the weak point. They're smooth brass — no knurling — so you'll need that wrench every time you swap guides. If you're switching sizes mid-project frequently, that's a minor annoyance. Also, there's no storage case. The guides arrive in a thin plastic bag, which is fine for a toolbox drawer but not for a job-site trailer. At this price point, a small foam insert would've been nice.

**Who should buy this**  
This set is for the serious hobbyist or professional who does template routing weekly. If you're cutting dovetails by hand or using a router jig for drawer fronts, the 5/16" guide alone justifies the cost. It's also a solid upgrade for someone stepping up from a cheap kit — the difference in cut quality is immediately noticeable. Skip it if you only route freehand or use a fixed-base router without a removable sub-base; you won't leverage the precision.

**FAQ**  
*Q: Will this fit my Bosch 1617EVS?*  
Yes — the RA1128 is designed for Bosch routers, and the 1617's sub-base accepts these guides directly. It also fits many other brands with standard 3/4" openings.

*Q: Can I use these with a plunge router?*  
Yes, but only if your plunge base has a removable sub-base plate. If it's fixed, you'll need an adapter plate — check your router's manual first.

*Q: Are the guides truly interchangeable with other brands?*  
Yes, they follow the industry-standard 3/4" pattern, so they'll work with templates designed for Porter-Cable or Freud guides.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you need precise, repeatable template cuts and want a set that won't wear out after a season of heavy use. **Skip it if** you're on a bare-bones budget or only route occasionally — the lack of a case and wrench-only lock nuts are minor nags, but for the price-to-performance ratio, this is the best 8-piece set under $60.
