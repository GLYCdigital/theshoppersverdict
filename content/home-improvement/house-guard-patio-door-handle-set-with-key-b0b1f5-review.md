**What you actually get**

The box contains the exterior handle with key cylinder, the interior handle with thumb turn, a strike plate, mounting screws, and two keys. The handles are die-cast zinc with a powder-coated finish — this isn't the hollow aluminum you'll find on bargain sets. The exterior key cylinder is the standout feature: it lets you lock the door from the outside without needing to reach around and fiddle with a thumb latch, which is a genuine security upgrade over standard sliding door hardware.

The package dimensions run roughly 10 x 4 x 3 inches, and the handles fit the standard 1-inch-thick sliding glass door panel that's common in most US homes. Installation requires a Phillips screwdriver and about 20 minutes if you've got basic DIY skills. The screws pass through pre-drilled holes in the door frame, so you're not drilling into glass or aluminum — that's a relief for anyone nervous about cracking a door panel.

**Real-world performance**

The key cylinder operates smoothly, and the thumb turn on the interior side is ergonomically placed. Locking and unlocking has a satisfying, positive action — no mushy half-latches. The handles themselves have a solid heft that communicates quality the moment you pick them up. Multiple buyers note that it "feels much more secure than the factory handle" and that the keyed entry "gives peace of mind when the kids are home alone." The powder coat resists scratching from keys and rings, which is more than I can say for cheaper painted hardware.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The security upgrade is the dominant theme across positive reviews. One verified purchaser wrote, "Replaced the cheap plastic handle that came with my sliding door. **Where it falls short**

The most common complaint is the included screws. Several buyers report they're soft and strip out during installation, especially when driving into hardwood frames. My advice: spend two bucks on better quality #8 or #10 stainless screws before you start. The strike plate alignment also requires patience — if your door frame isn't perfectly square, you'll need to adjust the plate position or file the hole slightly to get a smooth latch engagement. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's not a 100% drop-in fit for every door.

**FAQ**

A: It fits standard 1-inch-thick door panels with the typical 3-3/8-inch bolt hole spacing. Measure your existing handle's screw spacing before ordering — if it matches, you're good.

**Q: Can I rekey it to match my house keys?**

**Q: How secure is the lock, really?**
