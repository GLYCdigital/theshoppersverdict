## Where It Falls Short

**No thermostat included.** This is the big one. Ceramic heat emitters run at full wattage constantly. In a small enclosure (under 15 gallons), a 100W or 150W will cook your reptile without a dimmer or thermostat. Budget an extra $25–40 for a thermostat — it's non-negotiable for responsible reptile keeping.

**Fragile ceramic.** The element is brittle. One reviewer noted their bulb cracked when they overtightened it into the socket. Hand-tighten only, and handle by the threaded base, not the ceramic body.

**The 2-pack is only economical if you need two.** If you have a single enclosure, you're buying a backup you may not use for years. The upfront cost still beats buying two singles separately, but it's worth considering whether you actually need the second unit.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with a dimming thermostat?**
A: Yes. The ceramic element responds well to dimming. Most reviewers report stable temps with both dimming and pulse-proportional thermostats. Just don't use an on/off thermostat that cycles aggressively — the thermal mass of ceramic handles it, but dimming is gentler on the element.

**Q: What's the difference between 60W, 100W, and 150W?**
A: General rule: 60W for enclosures under 15 gallons, 100W for 20–40 gallons, 150W for 50+ gallons or tall enclosures. Ambient room temperature matters — a cold basement needs more wattage than a warm living room.

**Q: Does this emit any light?**
A: None. That's the entire point of a ceramic heat emitter. It produces infrared heat only, which is why it's safe for 24/7 use with nocturnal species.
