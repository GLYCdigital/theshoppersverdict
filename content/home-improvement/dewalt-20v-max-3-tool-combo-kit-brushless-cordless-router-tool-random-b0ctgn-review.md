If you've been eyeing a cordless router but hesitated because battery tools "just don't have the guts," this DEWALT 20V MAX 3-Tool Combo Kit is the argument you've been waiting for. The star here isn't the drill or impact driver — it's the brushless compact router that makes this kit genuinely different from the stack of generic drill/driver bundles clogging every hardware aisle.

**What's actually in the box**

You get three bare tools: the DCF787 brushless drill/driver, the DCF809 brushless impact driver, and the DCW600B brushless compact router. Two 20V MAX 5Ah batteries and one charger round out the kit. No bag, no case — just tools and power. The router includes a fixed base with a clear sub-base, a 1/4-inch collet, a chip shield, and an adjustment wrench. A 1/4-inch and 1/2-inch chuck come pre-installed on the drill and driver, respectively.

**Real-world performance**

The router is the headline act, and it earns the attention. The variable speed dial spins from 8,000 to 24,000 RPM — enough range to handle softwoods without tear-out and hardwoods without bogging down. I ran a 1/4-inch round-over bit through red oak at medium speed, and the brushless motor didn't complain once. The integrated LED ring around the collet is genuinely useful; it lights the cut line directly, not the ceiling.

The drill and driver are the same proven DCF787/DCF809 platforms DEWALT has shipped for years. The drill delivers 340 unit watts out (UWO) — enough for 1-inch spade bits through studs — and the impact driver's 1,400 in-lbs of torque sinks 3-inch deck screws without pre-drilling. Neither is a flagship, but both are workhorses.

Battery life is the practical tradeoff. With a 5Ah pack, I got roughly 45 minutes of intermittent routing on a single charge — fine for trim work, marginal for a full cabinet build. The drill and driver sip power by comparison; you'll swap packs for the router long before the others need it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.8-star average across 240 ratings reflects a pattern of genuine enthusiasm. One verified buyer wrote, "The router surprised me — I used it to flush trim a laminate countertop and it never stalled." Another noted, "This is the kit I reach for daily. The router fits where my corded one can't." The compact footprint of the router — just 5.4 inches tall — comes up repeatedly as the reason buyers chose this kit over cheaper alternatives.

**Where it falls short**

The router's fixed base is the biggest limitation. You can't do plunge cuts — no hinge mortises, no inlay work — without buying the optional plunge base separately, which adds $50–60 to the cost. If you only need edge profiling and flush trimming, you're fine. If you need plunge capability, budget for the accessory.

The two-battery configuration is tight for routing-heavy days. A third pack or a rapid charger would have made this a no-compromise kit. And the lack of any storage solution is annoying at this price point — a soft bag would cost DEWALT pennies and save buyers from hunting for a box.

**Who should buy this**

This kit is ideal for the serious DIYer or trim carpenter who's already invested in the 20V MAX platform. If you're starting from scratch, it's a smart entry point — the router alone justifies the price, and the drill/driver cover the other 80% of everyday tasks. It's also a strong choice for cabinet installers and finish carpenters who need a router that travels well between job sites.

Skip it if you need a plunge router for joinery, if you're a production shop that routes for hours daily (go corded), or if you already own a decent corded router and just need a drill/driver — you'd be paying for a tool you won't use.

**FAQ**

*Can the router handle hardwood?* Yes, but take lighter passes. You'll get cleaner cuts in walnut or maple with two shallow passes at 16,000–18,000 RPM than one deep pass at full speed.

*Are these tools compatible with other DEWALT batteries?* Yes, any 20V MAX battery works, including the newer PowerStack packs. They'll also run on flexvolt batteries, though the extra weight isn't worth it.

*Does the router accept standard bits?* Yes, the 1/4-inch collet fits every standard router bit shank. No proprietary nonsense.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely capable cordless router without buying a separate kit, and you need a solid drill/driver pair to boot. **Skip it if** plunge routing is non-negotiable or you're on a tight budget — you'll spend more on the plunge base and extra battery than the kit's initial savings justify.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the missing plunge base and stingy two-battery config. Everything else punches well above its weight.
