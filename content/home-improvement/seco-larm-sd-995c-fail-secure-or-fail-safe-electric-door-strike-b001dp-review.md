If you're securing a door that absolutely must not fail open during a power outage — think a server room, gun safe, or medication closet — the Seco-Larm SD-995C deserves a hard look. This isn't a smart lock with apps and fingerprint readers; it's a workhorse access control component designed to be hidden inside a door frame and triggered by a keypad, card reader, or push button.

**What's Actually in the Box**

Seco-Larm doesn't pad the package with junk. You get:
- The strike body with a brushed stainless steel faceplate
- Both flush mount and surface mount brackets
- A 12V/24V AC/DC solenoid rated for continuous duty
- Two plug-in voltage jumpers (one for fail-secure, one for fail-safe)

That's it. No power supply, no controller — you're expected to already have a 12V or 24V trigger source. The dual-voltage capability is a genuine plus; it means you can tie it into an existing alarm panel or standalone power supply without buying a converter.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The housing is cast metal, not stamped sheet steel. The faceplate is solid stainless. This matters because a flimsy strike will flex under repeated slamming, causing misalignment and eventual failure to latch. At roughly 1.5 pounds, the SD-995C has the heft of a commercial-grade unit.

The fail-secure/fail-safe switch is a physical jumper you move between two pins — takes about 30 seconds with a screwdriver. Fail-secure (default) means the strike stays locked when power is cut; fail-safe means it unlocks. This flexibility is rare at this price point, and it's the reason many buyers choose it over cheaper fixed-mode strikes.

One thing buyers consistently note is how quietly it operates. A few reviewers describe it as a "soft thunk" rather than a loud clack. That's a plus if you're installing it near bedrooms or offices.

**Where It Falls Short**

First, the fixed faceplate dimensions. At 4-7/8" x 1-1/8", it won't fit slimmer aluminum storefront frames without cutting. Measure your existing cutout *before* buying.

Second, there's no integrated door position switch. If you need to know whether the door is actually closed (for an alarm system or automated locking), you'll need the SD-995C-12V variant or add a separate magnetic contact. That's an extra cost and an extra hole to drill.

Third, installation isn't plug-and-play. You'll need to know which wires go where on your power supply, and you'll need a metal file or grinder to enlarge some standard residential cutouts. This is not a beginner-friendly project.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work with a standard 12V doorbell transformer?**
A: No. Doorbell transformers supply ~10VA, which isn't enough to drive the solenoid reliably. You need a regulated 12V or 24V DC power supply rated at least 500mA, or an AC supply from an access control panel.

**Q: Can I use this with a smart home system like Alexa or HomeKit?**
A: Not directly. You'd need a smart relay or dry-contact controller (like a Z-Wave or Wi-Fi relay) to trigger the strike. The strike itself is purely a mechanical latch — no smarts built in.

**Q: How do I switch between fail-secure and fail-safe?**
A: Remove the faceplate, locate the two-pin jumper, and move it to the opposite position. It's clearly labeled inside the housing. Takes under a minute.
