**What's Actually in the Box**

You get one sealed filter unit. That's it. No pre-filter wrap, no gloves, no instructions (you won't need them — it's a one-way drop-in). The filter itself is a cylindrical HEPA cartridge with a perforated plastic outer shell. If you're replacing an AROE filter, it's the same form factor: 8.2 inches tall, 6.5 inches in diameter at the base, and roughly 1.5 pounds. The rubber gasket around the bottom lip is noticeably stiffer than the OEM part, which is a good sign — that's what prevents unfiltered air from sneaking past the seal.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The filtration stack is where this filter earns its rating. It's a three-layer system: a coarse fiber outer layer, an H13 True HEPA pleated core, and a thin activated carbon layer. The H13 rating means it captures 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns — that's the standard for true HEPA, not the "HEPA-type" marketing nonsense you see from cheaper brands. In real-world use over three weeks, my AROE MK01's air quality sensor dropped from 45 µg/m³ (moderate) to 8 µg/m³ (good) within about 25 minutes in a 300-square-foot bedroom. That's on par with the OEM filter performance.

The carbon layer is the weak point. It's roughly 2mm thick, which handles light cooking odors and bathroom smells but won't touch heavy smoke or strong chemical fumes. If you need serious VOCs or odor control, you'll want a dedicated carbon filter — this one is primarily for particle removal.
