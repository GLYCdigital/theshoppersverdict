You're replacing a dead GFCI or upgrading a bathroom outlet, and the pile of options on Amazon all look identical. Here's what separates the GFI Electrical Self-Test from the pack — and where it stumbles.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the receptacle itself, a set of wire nuts, and a basic instruction sheet. No wall plate. That's standard for this category, but worth knowing before checkout. The unit is a standard duplex 15A/125V configuration with a slim can that's noticeably shallower than many competitors — roughly 0.7 inches less depth than a Leviton, which matters more than you'd think when you're wrestling a multi-gang box.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature here is the self-test function. Unlike older GFCIs that only cycle when you press the test button, this one runs an internal diagnostic every few seconds. The green LED confirms status at a glance — solid green means protected, no light means the outlet has tripped or failed. That's genuinely useful for rentals or hard-to-reach outlets where you'd otherwise never know protection was compromised.

Installation is straightforward if you've wired a receptacle before. The terminals accept #14 and #12 AWG solid copper, and the back-wire clamps hold firmly. The tamper-resistant shutters are smooth — no fighting to insert plugs, which is a common complaint with cheaper TR outlets.

Buyers consistently mention the slim profile as the selling point. One reviewer noted, *"Fits flush in an old work box where my previous GFCI stuck out a quarter inch."* Another praised the LED visibility: *"Can see the green light from across the room — instant peace of mind."*

## Where It Falls Short

The most consistent complaint involves nuisance tripping. Several buyers report the self-test occasionally false-trips on circuits with shared neutrals or older knob-and-tube wiring. One customer wrote, *"Trips randomly once a week with no load on it. Replaced with a different brand and problem disappeared."* If your house has pre-1970s wiring, budget for an electrician's diagnosis or consider a different unit.

The other issue is QC inconsistency. A handful of units arrive DOA or with the LED flickering. At this price point, that's not unusual, but it's worth testing each outlet before installing it in the wall. The included wire nuts are also poor quality — thin and prone to stripping. Use your own.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a homeowner or landlord doing a straightforward swap on modern wiring, want visible status indication, and appreciate the slimmer profile for tight boxes. **Skip it if** you're working with older wiring, need a wall plate included, or require a unit with a longer track record — Leviton and Eaton have decades of reputation behind them.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this GFCI work without a ground wire?**
A: Yes, it will function and provide protection without a ground present. It's code-compliant for replacing ungrounded outlets, but you must label it "No Equipment Ground" per NEC requirements.

**Q: How often does the self-test run?**
A: The unit performs an internal test approximately every 3 seconds. It does not require manual testing, though you should still press the test button monthly to verify trip functionality.

**Q: Can I install this in a 2-gang box with existing outlets?**
A: Yes, and this is where the slim design helps. It fits comfortably alongside other devices, but verify box depth is at least 2.5 inches before starting.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, feature-forward GFCI with real utility for modern homes. The self-test LED is a genuine safety upgrade, and the slim profile solves a real installation headache. But the nuisance trip reports on older wiring and the no-plate-included gotcha keep it from top marks.
