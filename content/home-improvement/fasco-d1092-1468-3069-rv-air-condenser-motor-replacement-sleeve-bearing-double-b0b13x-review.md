Your RV's AC unit starts rattling like a coffee can full of bolts at 2 AM in a Walmart parking lot. The blower wheel wobbles, the motor hums but won't spin, and the repair shop wants $400 plus labor. The Fasco D1092 is the part that fixes this — and at a fraction of that cost.

This is the direct replacement for the Dometic Duo-Therm condenser fan motor found in Penguin and Brisk Air rooftop units. If you've been searching "1468-3069," this is the cross-reference you've been chasing. Fasco is the OEM manufacturer for many of these motors, so you're not buying a knockoff — you're buying the original part under a different label.

**What's actually in the box:** The motor itself, pre-wired with a 4-pin connector on one end and bare leads on the other. That's it. No mounting hardware, no capacitor, no instructions beyond a basic diagram. The shaft is 3/8-inch diameter, double-ended, with one end longer for the blower wheel and the other for the fan blade. It's a 115-volt, 1/4-horsepower unit rated at 1075 RPM. Check your existing motor's spec plate before ordering — if you have a 230-volt unit or different shaft length, this isn't your part.

**Real-world performance:** The sleeve bearing design is the defining feature here. It's quieter than the original ball-bearing motor on most units — several buyers noted their AC runs noticeably smoother. One reviewer wrote, *"Replaced the original motor that seized after 9 years. This one spins up instantly and the airflow is actually stronger than before."* Another mentioned, *"Direct fit for my 2019 Keystone Cougar. Took 45 minutes with basic hand tools."*

The motor delivers consistent airflow at the rated 1075 RPM. It's not a high-performance upgrade — it's a faithful replacement that restores factory cooling. In 95-degree heat, buyers report their ACs keeping interiors at 72-75 degrees, which matches original performance specs.

**Where it falls short:** The sleeve bearing is the tradeoff. Ball bearings last longer under continuous vibration, and sleeve bearings are more susceptible to wear in roof-mounted applications where the motor sits horizontally. Expect 4-6 seasons with regular use — not the 10+ years some original equipment motors lasted. Also, the wiring situation. You're splicing into your roof unit's existing harness. If wiring isn't your strength, budget extra time or call a mobile RV tech. One reviewer noted, *"The connector didn't match my older Dometic unit — had to cut and splice. Not a big deal if you know what you're doing."*

**Who should buy this:**
- DIY RV owners with a seized or noisy condenser motor who want to save $300+ in labor costs
- Owners of Dometic Penguin or Brisk Air units built between 2005-2020 — these are the most common fitment years
- Anyone comfortable with basic electrical splicing and a 1/2-inch wrench

**Skip it if:** Your motor is still under warranty, you have a 230-volt system, or you're not comfortable working with 115-volt AC wiring on your roof. Also, if your unit is older than 2005, verify the shaft dimensions against your existing motor first.

**Three questions shoppers ask:**

*Is this a direct fit for my Dometic unit?* For Duo-Therm Penguin and Brisk Air models, yes — it's the exact OEM cross-reference. Older units may have different shaft lengths, so measure yours before ordering.

*Does it come with a capacitor?* No. The run capacitor is part of your rooftop unit's control board, not the motor. If your AC wasn't cooling before the motor seized, the capacitor is likely fine.

*How hard is the install?* Moderate DIY. You'll remove the shroud, unbolt the motor, transfer the blower wheel and fan blade, and splice three wires. Plan for 45-90 minutes on your first attempt.

**Buy it if** you're handy, your motor is confirmed dead, and you want to save $300. **Skip it if** you want a permanent 10-year solution — the sleeve bearing means you'll likely replace it again in 5-6 seasons.
