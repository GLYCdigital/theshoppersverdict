If you're a working electrician, you don't have time for a meter that lies to you. The Klein Tools CL800 isn't the flashiest clamp meter on the shelf, but it's the one that shows up on job sites for a reason. After digging through the specs, user feedback, and real-world performance data, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The CL800 arrives as a complete kit: the meter itself, a pair of CAT IV-rated test leads, a 9V battery (installed), and a carry pouch. The housing is a rugged two-piece molded design with a protective rubber boot that actually stays on — no slipping off mid-job like some cheaper meters. The 1.3-inch jaw opening handles most residential and commercial conductors up to 750 MCM, which covers anything you're likely to encounter in panel work.

**Performance that matters**

The headline feature here is True RMS. What does that mean practically? If you're measuring current on a circuit with VFDs, LED drivers, or any switching power supply, a standard averaging meter can under-read by 10-15 percent. The CL800 gives you accurate readings on those non-linear loads, and that's the difference between chasing a phantom "overload" and actually finding the problem.

The auto-ranging is responsive — you get a stable reading within about two seconds on most measurements. The 400-amp clamp covers typical residential service work, and the 40A low-current range with 0.01-amp resolution is where this meter earns its keep. You can actually see a 0.25-amp parasitic draw instead of it showing as 0.0 on a cheaper meter.

Users consistently praise the accuracy. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've compared this side-by-side with my Fluke 376 and it's within 0.2% on every measurement I've thrown at it. At half the price, that's hard to argue with."* Another noted: *"The NCV feature caught a back-fed breaker that I would have missed — the beeper and light saved me from a nasty surprise."*

**Where it falls short**

The backlight is the most common complaint — it shuts off after about 10 seconds, and you have to press the button again. When you're working in a dark panel with both hands occupied, that's an annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

The lack of a LoZ (low-impedance) mode is a more genuine limitation. On shared neutral circuits or when checking for phantom voltage, the CL800 can show a reading on a conductor that's actually dead but capacitively coupled. You'll need to verify with a Wiggy or a meter with LoZ features. For a meter at this price point, it's a notable omission.

The lead storage slots are also tight. If you upgrade to silicone or thicker-jacketed leads, you'll struggle to get them coiled back in the molded channels.

**Who should buy this**

This is the sweet spot for residential and light commercial electricians, HVAC techs, and serious DIY homeowners who work on their own panels. If you're a plant electrician working on 480V gear daily, you might want to step up to a meter with LoZ and a brighter display. If you're an apprentice or someone who needs a reliable meter without taking out a second mortgage, this is your tool.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Is this meter CAT III rated?**
A: Yes, it's CAT III 600V and CAT IV 300V rated. That's appropriate for most panel work and branch circuit troubleshooting. It's not rated for high-energy distribution systems, so don't use it on utility-side connections.

**Q: Can it measure DC amps?**
A: No. This model is AC current only. If you need DC amp readings for automotive or solar work, look at the CL800's sibling models or the Klein CL120.

**Q: Does it come with a carrying case?**
A: A soft pouch is included, but it's not a hard case. The rubber boot protects against drops, and the pouch is fine for tossing in a tool bag.

**The verdict**

The Klein Tools CL800 delivers 90 percent of what a $400 meter offers at roughly half the price. It's accurate, durable, and has the features that actually matter for everyday electrical work. The missing LoZ mode and short backlight timer are real tradeoffs, but they're not dealbreakers for most users.

**Buy it if** you need a reliable True RMS clamp meter for commercial or residential work and want Fluke-adjacent performance without the Fluke price tag.

**Skip it if** you work on industrial three-phase systems daily, need DC amp measurement, or regularly troubleshoot ghost voltage issues — those jobs call for a meter with LoZ and expanded ranges.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A professional-grade tool that respects your budget.
