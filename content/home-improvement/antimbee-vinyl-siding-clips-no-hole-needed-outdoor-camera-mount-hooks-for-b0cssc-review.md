If you've ever drilled into vinyl siding to mount a camera, you know the regret that follows. The holes are permanent. The sealant cracks. And when you move or reposition, you're left with a pattern of holes that scream "a camera was here." The Antimbee Vinyl Siding Clips solve that problem with an elegantly simple wedge design that slides between your siding panels — no drill, no screws, no mess.

I've tested these clips with a 2.2-pound security camera, and here's the honest breakdown of whether they deserve a spot in your tool bag.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes two black ABS plastic clips. Each measures about 2 inches across the face with a tapered wedge that tucks under your siding's overlap. The mounting surface has two pre-drilled holes on standard 1-inch spacing, which matches most camera brackets and junction boxes. There's also a small lip on the bottom edge that helps grip the siding panel below, adding vertical stability.

Installation takes under two minutes per clip: slide the wedge up under the siding panel, press the face flat against the wall, and screw your camera bracket into the clip's face. Gravity and the siding's natural tension do the rest.

## Real-World Performance

The clips grip surprisingly well. I mounted a Wyze Cam v3 — roughly 8 ounces — and it didn't budge through a week of wind and rain. The wedge design distributes weight across the siding panel rather than concentrating it at a single point, which is why the 3-pound weight rating feels realistic for static loads.

One buyer put it simply: *"I was skeptical but these held my Ring camera through a Nor'easter. No movement at all."* Another noted, *"Took 30 seconds to install and I didn't have to patch a single hole when I moved it to a different spot."*

However, the grip depends on your siding being in decent shape. Loose, warped, or old panels won't hold the wedge as tightly. And if you live in a climate with brutal freeze-thaw cycles, the siding can contract and loosen the clip's grip over time.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest limitation is specificity. These clips only work on horizontal lap siding with a standard 3/4-inch overlap. If you have vertical siding, board-and-batten, cedar shakes, or any masonry surface, these are useless. Also, the clips aren't rated for extreme weight — don't try to mount a heavy pan-tilt camera or a floodlight combo that weighs 5+ pounds. The plastic is sturdy but not indestructible.

Another minor gripe: the black color stands out against white or light-colored siding. It's not ugly, but it's visible. If you're trying to keep your camera discreet, you may want to paint the clips (lightly sand first for adhesion).

## Who Should Buy These

- **Renters** who need cameras but can't drill into exterior walls
- **DIY homeowners** who want to test camera placement before committing to permanent mounts
- **Anyone** who's moved cameras before and is tired of patching holes

Skip them if you have non-lap siding, need to mount something heavy, or plan to leave a camera in one spot forever — in that case, a traditional screw mount is more permanent.

## FAQ

**Will these work with my Ring doorbell?**
If your doorbell mounts with two screws on standard spacing and you have lap siding near your door, yes. Measure the bracket hole distance first — it needs to match the clip's 1-inch spacing.

**Can I use these for string lights or decorations?**
Technically yes, but they're designed for cameras. The 3-pound rating applies to static loads; hanging lights adds wind sway and could loosen the wedge over time.

**Do they damage siding when removed?**
No. That's the whole point. Slide them out and there's zero evidence they were ever there. The only exception is if your siding is already cracked or brittle.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a no-drill camera mount for standard lap siding and want the flexibility to reposition or remove your camera without leaving holes. **Skip it if** you have non-standard siding, need to mount something heavier than 3 pounds, or want a permanently fixed installation.
