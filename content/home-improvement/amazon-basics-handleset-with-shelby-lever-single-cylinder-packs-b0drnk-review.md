**The $40 Handleset That Feels Like It Costs More — Until You Install It**

Let me be direct: most sub-$50 handlesets feel like they were stamped out of recycled soda cans. The Amazon Basics Handleset with Shelby Lever isn't that. The main body is zinc die-cast with a weight that surprises you when you pull it out of the box. The lever has a smooth, confident return action — no wobble, no springy cheapness. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, buyers mostly agree. But there's a catch hiding in the installation, and you need to know about it before you commit.

**What's actually in the box**

You get two complete assemblies: the keyed entry handleset (exterior lever + interior thumb turn) and a matching dummy lever for a second door. Both share the same Shelby lever design — a clean, transitional curve that works on craftsman or contemporary homes. Hardware includes the latch, strike plate, and all mounting screws. Notably, the latch is adjustable between 2-3/8" and 2-3/4" backsets, which fits the vast majority of pre-drilled exterior doors. The finish is listed as polished brass, though it's a plated coating over zinc, not solid brass.

**Performance and real-world use**

Locking mechanism is a standard single-cylinder design — key outside, thumb turn inside. The cylinder uses a 5-pin tumbler with a decent anti-pick shield. For a budget lock, the keyway feels crisp; keys insert without catching or grinding. The interior thumb turn is positive and doesn't require fiddly alignment to engage the deadbolt.

But here's where the rubber meets the road: installation. The included instructions are adequate but not exceptional. The latch assembly is straightforward, but the strike plate screws are soft metal. Multiple reviews mention stripping them with a standard screwdriver. One buyer wrote, "The screws that came with it are garbage — I had to run to the hardware store for better ones." That's a fair criticism. The fix is simple: swap in #8 stainless steel screws and the thing holds solid.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- **Value for money.** "I've installed $100 handlesets that feel cheaper than this," one verified purchaser noted.
- **Clean aesthetic.** The Shelby lever profile is slim and modern without being trendy.
- **Smooth operation.** The latch retracts effortlessly, and the lever returns to horizontal without sagging.

**Where it falls short**

- **Finish longevity.** The polished brass plating is the weak point. With daily use, expect wear at the thumb turn and lever grip within a year. If you live in coastal or humid environments, this will accelerate.
- **Door thickness limitation.** Specs say 1-3/8" to 1-3/4" doors. If your exterior door is a custom 2" thick, this won't fit without significant modification.
- **No smart features.** This is a purely mechanical lock. No keypad, no Bluetooth, no auto-lock. If you want smart functionality, look elsewhere.

**Who should buy this**

- **First-time homeowners** furnishing multiple doors on a budget — the two-piece set covers front and side entries affordably.
- **Landlords** who need reliable, replaceable hardware that tenants won't easily break.
- **DIYers** comfortable with a screwdriver and an afternoon — this is not a "call a locksmith" product.

**Skip it if**

- You need a **smart lock** with app access or keyless entry.
- Your door is **thicker than 1-3/4"** or has a non-standard bore.
- You want **true brass or oil-rubbed bronze** that will patina gracefully over decades.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this fit a standard pre-drilled door?**
A: Yes, in most cases. It uses a 2-1/8" bore hole and adjustable latch backsets for 2-3/8" or 2-3/4". If your door is pre-drilled for a standard handleset, this drops in.

**Q: Can I rekey it to match my existing house key?**
A: The cylinder is a standard 5-pin design, so a locksmith can rekey it. But the included keys work fine, and you get two keys in the box.

**Q: Is the dummy lever functional?**
A: No — it's a non-turning decorative lever. It's meant for a second door where you don't need locking (like a storm door or closet). It does not have a latch mechanism.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, good-looking mechanical handleset without paying premium prices, and you're willing to swap the strike plate screws during installation.

**Skip it if** you need smart features, have a non-standard door thickness, or demand a finish that will look pristine for a decade.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's a genuine value, but the soft screws and plating durability keep it from being a flawless recommendation. For the price, you could do far worse, and honestly, you'd have to spend twice as much to do meaningfully better.
