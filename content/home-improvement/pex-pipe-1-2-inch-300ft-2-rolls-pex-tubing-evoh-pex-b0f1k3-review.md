## What You're Actually Getting

Open the box and you get two individually coiled rolls — one red, one blue — each 150 feet. That's a deliberate choice worth appreciating. A single 300-foot spool is a wrestling match in a crawlspace. Two 150-foot rolls let you pull one while the other stays coiled and protected. The EVOH layer is the headline feature: it's an oxygen-diffusion barrier that makes this pipe suitable for radiant floor heating systems, not just domestic water. If you're running a closed-loop hydronic system, this is the correct pipe. If you're only doing potable water lines, you're paying a small premium for a capability you won't use.

## Build Quality and Real-World Handling

The pipe is marked PEX 5006, ASTM F876/F877 compliant — that's the standard you want for buried or concealed applications. Wall thickness feels consistent throughout, and the coils hold their shape without crushing flat at the tie points. The red and blue coloring is solid, not surface-printed, so it won't rub off on your hands or inside conduit.

Here's the tradeoff you need to know: PEX-B has more coil memory than PEX-A. In warmer conditions (above 60°F), it lays out reasonably flat after a few minutes. In a cold garage or winter install, it will fight you on straight runs. One verified buyer noted, *"Had to use a heat gun on straight runs in my unheated basement — once warmed, it behaved fine."* That's the reality. Budget extra time for cold-weather installs.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this pipe approved for underground burial?**
A: Yes, PEX 5006 with ASTM F876/F877 compliance is rated for underground use, but you must use continuous runs without joints below grade. The EVOH barrier doesn't change burial ratings.

**Q: Can I use this with expansion fittings?**
A: No. This is PEX-B, not PEX-A. It requires crimp or cinch fittings. Attempting expansion fittings will damage the pipe.

**Q: Does the red/blue color indicate different materials?**
A: No. Same pipe, same specifications. Color is purely for hot/cold line identification.
