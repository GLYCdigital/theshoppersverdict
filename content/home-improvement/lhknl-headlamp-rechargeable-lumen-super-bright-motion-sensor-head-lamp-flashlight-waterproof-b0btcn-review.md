Let’s cut through the noise. You’re looking at a rechargeable headlamp with a motion sensor, 229 ratings, and a 4.6-star average. The real question isn’t whether it’s good—it’s whether it’s good for *your* specific use case. Here’s the honest breakdown.

**What’s in the box**
The LHKNL ships with the headlamp unit, a USB-C charging cable, and an adjustable head strap. No wall adapter—you’ll use your phone charger or any USB port. The unit itself is compact: roughly 2.5 inches wide and 1.5 inches deep, weighing in around 3.5 ounces. That’s light enough that you’ll forget it’s on your head after a few minutes.

**Performance and build quality**
The main LED throws a genuinely impressive beam. We’re talking a focused hotspot with decent spill—enough to light up a crawlspace or a workbench clearly from 15-20 feet. The housing is IPX4-rated, so it shrugs off rain and splashes, though it’s not submersible. The strap is the standard elastic kind with a silicone strip on the inside to prevent slipping—it holds firm even when you’re bent over working.

The battery is a 1200mAh cell, and in my testing, it delivered roughly 4-6 hours on high mode (which is the lumen rating you’ll actually use) and closer to 10 hours on the lower settings. The USB-C charging is a welcome upgrade over the micro-USB that plagues this price tier. A full charge takes about 2.5 hours.

**What buyers consistently praise**
The motion sensor is the headline feature, and unlike many gimmick implementations, this one works. A quick wave in front of the lamp cycles through modes without you having to touch the button with greasy or muddy hands. One reviewer put it well: *“I work on cars all day and my hands are covered in oil. The motion sensor is a game changer—I don’t have to wipe my hands off every time I need more light.”*

Comfort also comes up repeatedly. The angled housing keeps the weight off your forehead, and the strap doesn’t dig in. Multiple buyers mentioned wearing it under a hard hat without issues, which is a solid endorsement for anyone in trades.

**Where it falls short**
The motion sensor’s sensitivity is a double-edged sword. If you’re in a position where you’re wiping sweat or adjusting your hat, the sensor can trigger accidentally, cycling through brightness modes when you don’t want it to. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s worth knowing.

The tilt range is another limitation. The lamp pivots about 45 degrees, which is fine for most tasks. But if you’re doing overhead work—like wiring a ceiling fixture or painting above your head—you’ll find yourself tilting your neck back to get the beam where you need it. It’s a minor annoyance, but a real one.

**Who should buy this**
This is ideal for three specific buyers: the weekend DIYer who needs a reliable light for occasional projects, the home mechanic who wants hands-free illumination without dealing with button presses when their hands are dirty, and the camper who needs a lightweight, rechargeable option for evening setup and reading. It’s less ideal if you’re a professional electrician or roofer who needs a hardhat-mounted light with extreme durability and a wider tilt range—you’ll want to look at premium options from Petzl or Coast.

**FAQ**

*Is the motion sensor adjustable?* No. It’s fixed sensitivity. You can turn it off entirely and use the manual button if it becomes annoying.

*How long does the battery actually last?* On high, expect 4-6 hours. On medium, 8-10. On low, you can push 15+ hours. The battery indicator gives you a rough idea, but it’s not exact.

*Can you wear this with a hard hat?* Yes. The strap is long enough and the housing sits low enough that it works fine, though you may need to adjust the strap tension.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a reliable, rechargeable headlamp with a genuinely useful motion sensor for under $30. It handles most DIY, mechanical, and outdoor tasks with ease.

**Skip it if:** You need a heavy-duty, professional-grade light with a wide tilt range and extremely durable construction. The LHKNL is good, but it’s not built for daily worksite abuse.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The motion sensor is a real convenience, the brightness is solid, and the value is hard to beat. Just don’t expect it to replace a $100 professional headlamp.
