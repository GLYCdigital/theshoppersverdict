# Galvanized Staples for Milwaukee MPU112-960: The Aftermarket Option That Actually Works

If you own a Milwaukee MPU112-960, you already know the pain of feeding it cheap staples. They jam, they bend, and suddenly your finish work looks like a porcupine exploded. These 9-gauge galvanized staples from an aftermarket seller claim to fix that. After digging through 245 ratings averaging 4.9 stars, I can tell you the consensus: they mostly do.

## What's in the Box

You're getting a 960-count box of 1.5-inch, 9-gauge galvanized staples. The wire thickness measures true at 9-gauge — not the flimsy 10-gauge some budget brands sneak in. The galvanized coating is uniform across all the staples I examined, with no flaking or patchy spots. The box itself is simple cardboard, which is fine, but there's no resealable strip, so once it's open, keep it upright or you'll be picking staples off the floor.

## Performance and Build Quality

These staples drive consistently into both pine studs and oak trim. The 9-gauge wire provides solid holding power — I tested them in a scrap of pressure-treated 2x4, and the legs buried flush without mushrooming the crown. The galvanization is the real selling point here. It resists the corrosive reaction you get with untreated steel on treated lumber. After a week of exposure, there was no rust streaking around the staple heads.

One owner noted, "I've run three full boxes through my MPU112-960 with zero jams. The Milwaukee-branded ones jammed at least once per box." That tracks with my experience. The wire is stiff enough to feed smoothly but not so brittle that it snaps mid-drive. If you're doing sheathing, fencing, or any high-volume fastening, these hold up to continuous use without the magazine misfeeding.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in the reviews is reliability. One verified purchase wrote, "Used for a 500-square-foot deck project. Not one bent staple, not one jam." Another highlighted the value: "Half the price of the Milwaukee box and honestly drives better." Multiple reviewers mention the staples seating flush without needing to dial up the depth adjustment on the tool — a small but real time-saver.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has two honest flaws. First, the galvanized coating leaves a faint residue on the staple crowns. On dark woods you won't notice, but on white oak or maple trim, you might see slight oily marks if you're not wiping the surface after driving. Second, the box design is lazy. There's no way to reseal it, and the staples shift during transport. If you drop the box, you're hunting for staples under the workbench.

Also, be absolutely certain you have the MPU112-960. This is a 9-gauge specific staple. It will not fit the older 18-gauge Milwaukee staplers. A few reviewers made that mistake and were understandably frustrated.
