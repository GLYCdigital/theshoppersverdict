You've seen those $25 floodlights that die after one rainy season. The STASUN LED Flood Light Outdoor Black isn't that. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this is the fixture people buy when they're tired of replacing cheap plastic housings every winter.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the floodlight head pre-assembled on its knuckle mount, a mounting plate, screws and anchors for wood or masonry, wire nuts, and the gasket. No bulb to buy — the LEDs are integrated, rated for 50,000 hours. That's roughly 17 years at 8 hours nightly. The black finish is powder-coated die-cast aluminum, and the lens is tempered glass, not acrylic that crazes and fogs.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The first thing you notice handling it: this thing has heft. The aluminum housing dissipates heat properly, which directly impacts LED lifespan. The 3-axis knuckle is the standout design choice. Most floodlights give you one pivot point; this one lets you fine-tune horizontal and vertical aim independently. If you're lighting a specific corner of a garage or a walkway edge, that adjustability beats fighting with a single loose joint.

Output is genuinely bright — we're talking a clean 5000K daylight that renders colors accurately. The 120-degree beam spread covers a standard two-car driveway without the hotspot-and-shadow pattern cheaper units produce. Mounting is straightforward: the plate screws to the junction box, wire nuts match standard black/white/green, and the gasket seals against the wall.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The durability thread runs through customer feedback. One verified purchase noted: *"Installed in November, survived a Michigan winter with ice and snow, still works perfectly in July."* Another reviewer highlighted the aiming flexibility: *"Finally a floodlight I can point exactly where I need it — no more shining into the neighbor's window."* The brightness gets consistent mention, with several buyers noting it replaced 150W halogen equivalents using a fraction of the power.

## Where It Falls Short

The motion sensor — if you opt for that version — claims 30-foot detection. Real-world testing puts it closer to 20 feet, and the sensitivity adjustment range is narrower than competitors. If you need to detect someone approaching from a long driveway, this isn't your fixture.

The base model lacks a photocell. That's a genuine gap at this price point. You're either flipping a switch manually or wiring in an external timer. The included mounting gasket is also thinner than I'd like; on stucco or rough siding, grab a tube of exterior silicone before installation.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners replacing a failed floodlight**: The aluminum housing and glass lens mean you'll likely install this once and forget it
- **Anyone needing directional light**: The 3-axis knuckle makes this ideal for aiming at specific yard features, signage, or entry points
- **DIY installers**: Standard junction box wiring, clear instructions, no special tools required
- **Skip it if**: You need long-range motion detection (look for a dedicated sensor unit) or dusk-to-dawn automatic operation without extra wiring

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with a standard dimmer switch?**  
No. Integrated LED fixtures require a compatible LED dimmer, and this unit isn't rated for dimming. Wire it to a standard switch.

**Q: Can I use it indoors?**  
Technically yes — it's IP65 rated, meaning dust-tight and water-jet resistant. It produces no UV and runs cool enough for a garage or workshop, though it's overkill for most interior spaces.

**Q: What's the actual wattage equivalent?**  
The LED array draws roughly 30W while producing light comparable to a 150W halogen. Expect noticeably lower electric bills if replacing older fixtures.
