**The headline number is 1500 lumens from just 12 watts.** That's roughly 125 lumens per watt, which is genuinely efficient — you're getting more light than a 100W incandescent while using about 88% less energy. In real-world use, this thing is *bright*. I'm talking "need to squint for a second when you flip it on in a dark room" bright. If you're replacing a dim, tired CFL or an old halogen work light, the difference is immediately obvious.

Build quality is solid for the price point. The plastic base feels durable, and the bulb doesn't rattle or feel cheap in hand. The 220-degree beam angle is a real advantage here — it throws light wide rather than in a narrow cone. That makes it a strong choice for ceiling fixtures in hallways, laundry rooms, or anywhere you want even coverage.

**Here's what surprised me:** the bulb runs cooler than most LEDs I've tested in this class. The base gets warm after extended use — noticeably warm, not scorching — but the glass stays comfortable to touch. That's a meaningful safety plus in enclosed fixtures.

## Where It Falls Short

**The biggest limitation is that it's non-dimmable.** If you have dimmer switches, skip these entirely — they'll flicker and buzz, and you'll be annoyed. Full stop.

**The 5000K daylight color temperature is a double-edged sword.** It's excellent for task lighting, garages, and workspaces where you want crisp, alert light. It's terrible for living rooms, bedrooms, or anywhere you want a warm, relaxing ambiance. The light has a cool, almost clinical blue-white cast that can feel harsh in the evening.

**Heat buildup in enclosed fixtures is worth noting.** While the bulb itself runs cooler than expected, it's still an LED pushing 1500 lumens — enclosed dome fixtures will trap heat and shorten lifespan. Great Eagle rates it for 15,000 hours, but that assumes reasonable ventilation.
