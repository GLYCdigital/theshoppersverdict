# hooga Circadian Rhythm Full Spectrum LED Light Bulb – 2700K Review

Most "circadian" bulbs are marketing gimmicks that shift between vaguely warm and vaguely cool white. The hooga Circadian Rhythm bulb actually does something different — it drops to a genuine 1400K deep amber that mimics a fire-lit evening. That alone makes it worth a hard look if you're serious about sleep hygiene.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains a single A19-size bulb (E26 base) rated at 800 lumens at 2700K — roughly equivalent to a 60W incandescent. It's a full spectrum LED with a CRI of 95+, which is genuinely high for a dimmable smart-free bulb. No app, no hub, no remote — just a bulb that works with your existing wall dimmer. That's the entire pitch: physical dimming that shifts color temperature naturally as you lower the brightness.

## How It Performs in Real Use

I tested this bulb on a standard Lutron Diva dimmer for two weeks. At full brightness, it's a clean, warm white — indistinguishable from a quality 2700K LED. The magic starts below 50% brightness. The bulb transitions from 2700K to 2100K, then to 1400K deep amber as you dim toward minimum. At 10% and below, the light is genuinely amber — like a candle or a salt lamp, but with enough output to read by without straining.

Dimming is smooth down to 1% with zero flicker visible to the naked eye. I confirmed this with a 240fps slow-motion phone capture — no PWM banding whatsoever. This is a significant advantage for photosensitive individuals; several verified Amazon buyers specifically mention this. One reviewer wrote, *"I'm extremely sensitive to LED flicker and this is the first dimmable LED I've found that doesn't trigger my migraines."*

Color rendering is excellent for a warm bulb — 95+ CRI means reds and skin tones look natural, not muddy. The 800-lumen max output is sufficient for a bedside lamp, living room floor lamp, or reading light. It's not bright enough to be a primary overhead in a large room, but that's not what this bulb is for.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in verified reviews is sleep improvement. Multiple reviewers report falling asleep faster and staying asleep longer after replacing their standard LED bulbs in bedside lamps. The 1400K minimum is the standout feature — most competing circadian bulbs bottom out at 1800K or 2000K, which is still noticeably "electric." At 1400K, the light mimics a real flame.

Build quality is also consistently praised. The bulb has a frosted glass finish that diffuses light evenly — no harsh hotspots. It's slightly heavier than typical LED bulbs, which suggests a beefier heat sink. One reviewer noted, *"I've had cheap circadian bulbs die within months. This one feels solid and runs much cooler than my previous bulb."*

## Where It Falls Short

The bulb runs warm at full brightness. After 4 continuous hours, the base measured 118°F on my IR thermometer — not dangerous, but noticeably hot if you're using it in a tight lampshade. It's rated for enclosed fixtures, so this is acceptable, but plan for airflow.

The warm-only CCT range is a limitation, not a flaw. You cannot switch this bulb to cool white during the day. If you need one bulb for both daytime task lighting and evening wind-down, this isn't it — you'll need a separate brighter bulb for morning use.

Finally, the 800-lumen max is modest. It's perfect for lamps and sconces, but inadequate for a kitchen or home office where you need 1500+ lumens. Pair it with a brighter bulb for functional lighting.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're building a sleep-optimized bedroom or living room lighting setup. You use wall dimmers and want a bulb that naturally shifts warmer as you dim. You're sensitive to LED flicker and have been burned by cheap dimmable LEDs. You want a "candle mode" without buying a separate amber bulb.

**Skip it if:** You need one bulb to do everything (daytime bright + night dim). You don't use dimmers — this bulb's CCT shift only happens when dimmed. You're expecting a full smart home integration with app control or scheduling.
