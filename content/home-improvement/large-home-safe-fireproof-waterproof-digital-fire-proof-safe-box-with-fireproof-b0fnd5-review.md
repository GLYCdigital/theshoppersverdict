Let's be honest: most fireproof safes under $300 are glorified metal boxes with a lock. This large home safe from the fireproof waterproof line is not that. With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's earned a reputation — but is it the right safe for *your* valuables? Here's what I found after digging through the specs and customer experiences.

## What You Actually Get

The box arrives with the safe itself, two emergency override keys, a digital keypad with programmable 1-8 digit PIN, mounting bolts, and an interior shelf. The exterior dimensions run roughly 19" wide by 16" tall by 18" deep — large enough for a 15-inch laptop, a stack of passport folders, and a small jewelry box side by side. The interior is carpeted, which matters more than you'd think; documents slide out without snagging.

## Real Fire and Water Protection

This unit is rated for 60 minutes at 1850°F — that's a genuine one-hour fire rating, not the 30-minute "better than nothing" you see on budget safes. The water seal is rated for 24 hours of submersion, which covers the overwhelming majority of burst-pipe and flood scenarios. One buyer noted: *"After a kitchen fire, my birth certificates and deed came out cool to the touch. No warping, no water damage."* That's the real-world validation you want.

## Build Quality and Daily Use

The steel body is what you'd expect at this price — solid, not military-grade. The digital keypad responds quickly and accepts a PIN up to 8 digits. You get 5 attempts before a 5-minute lockout, which is a meaningful theft deterrent. The door hinges feel substantial, and the locking bolts throw positively when you close it. The pre-drilled floor mounting holes are a genuine safety feature — a thief who can't lift a bolted-down 85-pound safe is a thief who leaves empty-handed.

## Where It Falls Short

The backup key situation needs a blunt warning: if you lose the override keys *and* the battery dies, you're calling a locksmith. The battery compartment sits inside the safe, so you can't swap batteries from outside — plan ahead and change them yearly. Also, the 1850°F rating is for paper. If you're storing hard drives or USB backups, you need a UL 72-rated media safe. At roughly 85 pounds, expect to recruit help getting it upstairs — this isn't a one-hand carry.

## Who Should Buy This

- **New homeowners** with a handful of irreplaceable documents (birth certificates, deeds, social security cards) who need certified protection without paying commercial-safe prices
- **Gun owners** storing a handgun or two plus ammunition separately — the interior depth accommodates a compact case
- **Renters** who can't bolt into walls but want a heavy, portable deterrent that's awkward to carry out

Skip it if you need to protect computer backup drives or if you're in a wildfire zone where you might need 2+ hours of fire resistance — step up to a higher-tier certified model.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I mount this to a wall or only the floor?**
A: Floor is the intended install. The mounting holes are on the bottom. Wall-mounting an 85-pound safe requires heavy-duty anchors and isn't recommended.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
A: Most users report 12-18 months with normal use. The low-battery warning beeps about 30 days before failure — don't ignore it.

**Q: Does it come with a mounting template?**
A: Yes, a paper template is included. Drill four holes, insert the expansion anchors, bolt it down. Takes about 15 minutes with a hammer drill.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want certified fire and water protection for paper documents at a price that won't make you wince, and you're willing to bolt it down and maintain the battery.

**Skip it if** you're protecting digital media, need two-hour fire rating, or want a safe you can move easily between properties.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — This is a strong mid-tier safe that does exactly what it promises. The backup key vulnerability and paper-only fire rating keep it from a perfect score, but for the price, it's a legitimate upgrade over anything with a flimsy combination dial.
