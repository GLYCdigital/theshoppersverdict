Let’s cut through the noise: most interior door knobs under $30 are flimsy, hollow stamped metal that feels like a toy. The Gobrico 613 square rosette set is not that. It’s a genuinely heavy piece of hardware — the kind you notice the moment you pick it up. But is it worth swapping out every knob in your house for? Here’s what 215 buyers and my own teardown of the specs say.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes two complete passage sets (knob, square rosette, latch, strike plate, and screws). Each knob is solid zinc alloy, not the brass-plated steel you usually find at this price. The rosette measures roughly 3-1/8 inches square, which is slightly larger than standard round plates — a deliberate design choice that covers up old bore holes if you’re replacing mismatched hardware. The latch is adjustable for 2-3/8 or 2-3/4 inch backsets, so it fits virtually any standard pre-drilled US door.

## Build and Feel: The Real Story

The standout feature here is weight. At about 1.2 pounds per knob, these have a substantial, premium feel that rivals hardware costing three times as much. The turning action is smooth right out of the box — no gritty resistance or wobble on the spindle. The square rosette design isn't just aesthetic; it gives you more surface area to grip when tightening the set screws, which means less stripping of threads over time.

One thing to note: these are passage knobs only. There’s no locking mechanism, no keyhole, no privacy function. If you need a bedroom or bathroom lock, you’ll need to buy the privacy version separately or look at a different line entirely.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The praise in the reviews is consistent and specific. One buyer wrote, *“Replaced all 12 knobs in my house. The weight alone told me these weren't cheap. My contractor even asked where I got them.”* Another mentioned, *“The square plate covers the old holes from my previous round knobs perfectly — no patchwork needed.”* Across the 215 ratings, the recurring themes are: substantial feel, easy installation (about 10 minutes per door if your bore holes align), and a clean, modern look that works with both farmhouse and contemporary trim.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides. First, the brushed zinc finish is a fingerprint magnet. In a hallway you touch daily, expect to see smudges within a week. It’s not a dealbreaker — a quick wipe fixes it — but if you have kids, you’ll be wiping often.

Second, the included screws are soft. Several reviewers reported the Phillips heads stripping if you overtighten with a power driver. Use a manual screwdriver or set your clutch low. It’s a minor annoyance, not a failure.

Third, there’s no visual distinction between the two knobs in a set — they’re identical, which is fine for passage doors but means you can’t tell at a glance which side is which if you’re installing in a double-door setup.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You’re renovating a whole house or flipping a property and want a consistent, modern look without paying $40+ per knob. They’re ideal for hallways, closets, and connecting doors where you don’t need locks.

**Skip them if:** You need privacy locks for bedrooms or bathrooms, or if you have a historic home where a square plate will look out of place against traditional trim. Also skip if you’re sensitive to visible fingerprints — go with an oil-rubbed bronze or matte black finish instead.

## FAQ: Three Real Questions

**Q: Will these fit a pre-drilled 2-1/8 inch bore hole?**
A: Yes, the latch and knob cylinder are standard 2-1/8 inch diameter. The square rosette is larger than the hole, so it covers it completely. If your existing hole is slightly oversized from a previous knob, the square plate hides the gap better than round ones.

**Q: Can I use these on a metal door?**
A: Technically yes, but the included screws are wood screws. You’ll need to buy machine screws and possibly drill pilot holes for the rosette. Not ideal — stick to wood or composite doors.

**Q: How do they hold up after a year of use?**
A: Based on buyer feedback, the latch mechanism stays tight and the finish doesn’t chip or peel. A few users noted the turning action loosens slightly after 6-8 months but remains functional. Re-tightening the set screws fixes it.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Gobrico 613 square rosette knobs deliver exactly what they promise: heavy-duty construction, a clean modern look, and easy installation at a price that undercuts big-box store equivalents. They’re not perfect — the fingerprint finish and soft screws are real annoyances — but for the money, these are one of the better value picks for a whole-house refresh. Buy them if you want a solid, substantial feel without paying designer prices. Skip them if you need locking functionality or prefer a finish that hides daily wear.
