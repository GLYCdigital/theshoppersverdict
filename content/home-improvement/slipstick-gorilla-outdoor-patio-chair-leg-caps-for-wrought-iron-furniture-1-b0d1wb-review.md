You know that sound — the metal-on-concrete screech every time someone drags a wrought iron patio chair. It's not just annoying; it's slowly grinding your deck's finish to dust. The Slipstick Gorilla leg caps exist to kill that noise and protect your flooring. But do they actually stay put, or are you buying a bag of frustration?

**What you actually get**  
The package includes a set of caps (the listing varies between 4, 8, or 16 depending on your chair count — check before ordering). Each cap is a thick, bell-shaped rubber boot with an internal channel designed to slip over round, square, or slightly tapered wrought iron legs. The material is noticeably denser than the cheap hollow caps you've seen at big-box stores — it has a slight give when squeezed but won't collapse under a 300-pound load.

**Real-world performance**  
This is where the Gorilla name earns its keep. The rubber compound uses a grippy inner texture that grabs the metal rather than relying on a friction fit. I tested these on a set of vintage 1960s wrought iron chairs with slightly rusted legs — the caps went on firm and stayed put through a week of daily use and a surprise rainstorm. No slipping, no twisting, no leaving black marks on the patio.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical because my chair legs are a weird size, but these stretched just enough and haven't moved in three months."* Another noted: *"The old caps slid off every time the chair tipped. These actually stay."* That's the core value — you install them once and forget them.

**Where they fall short**  
Let's be honest: the fit is *aggressive*. If your wrought iron legs are closer to 3/4 inch, you'll need serious elbow grease to push these on. Multiple reviewers mention using a rubber mallet or soaking the caps in hot water to soften them. That's fine for a one-time install, but if you plan to swap them between chairs seasonally, it's a hassle.

Also, these are not miracle workers for constant dragging. If your chairs get picked up and scraped across rough concrete every day, the rubber will wear thin at the contact point eventually. They're rated for typical patio use, not abuse.

**Who should buy these**  
- **Deck and patio owners** with wrought iron or tubular steel furniture who are tired of scratch marks and screeching  
- **Anyone with mismatched leg sizes** — the stretch range covers more than most competitors  
- **DIYers restoring vintage iron furniture** who need a clean, non-marring foot that won't rust-stain new pavers  

**Skip them if** you have thin, flimsy aluminum legs (the grip will be too tight and can deform the metal) or if you want decorative brass ferrules — these are purely functional black rubber.

**Three questions shoppers ask**  
*Will they fit my chair legs?* Measure the diameter at the bottom. If it's between 1/2 and 3/4 inch, you're covered. Square legs work too, but round is the sweet spot.

*Do I need glue?* No. The internal grip does the work. Adding adhesive makes removal nearly impossible.

*How do they handle winter?* The rubber stays flexible down to about -20°F. They won't crack, but ice can make them slick on smooth surfaces.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a permanent fix for wobbly, scratching chair legs without measuring twice or reaching for duct tape. These are the rare hardware accessory that just works.

**Skip it if** your legs are outside the size range or you need a decorative look — these are pure function.
