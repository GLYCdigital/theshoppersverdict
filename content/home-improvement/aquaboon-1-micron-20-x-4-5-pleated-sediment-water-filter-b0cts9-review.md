# Aquaboon 1 Micron 20" x 4.5" Pleated Sediment Water Filter Review

Most sediment filters are cheap, disposable, and forgettable. The Aquaboon pleated cartridge is none of those — for better and worse. At 229 ratings and a 4.6 average, it's clearly doing something right. But the real question is whether the higher upfront cost and washable design actually save you money over time, or just complicate a simple process.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains a single 20-inch by 4.5-inch pleated cartridge. Nothing else — no O-rings, no housing wrench, no installation guide beyond a basic spec sheet. The filter uses standard 1-inch NPT connections, so it drops into virtually any whole-house filter housing built for 20x4.5 cartridges. That includes popular systems from iSpring, Aquasana, Pentek, and the generic housings sold at big-box hardware stores.

The construction is noticeably sturdier than the blue or white spun filters you're used to. The outer pleats are rigid enough to hold their shape under water pressure, and the core is a solid polypropylene cylinder that won't collapse or deform. As shown in the product image, the pleating is dense and uniform — that's the key to its performance.

## Real-World Performance

The 1-micron rating is the headline feature, and it delivers. This filter catches particles a standard 5-micron spun filter lets through — fine sand, rust flakes, and that grey silt that stains fixtures. If you're on well water, this is a meaningful upgrade in water clarity.

The pleated design gives you roughly four times the surface area of a comparable spun filter. That translates directly to longer service life. One reviewer noted, "I was changing my old 5-micron spun filter every 3 weeks. This one went 2 months before I even noticed a drop in pressure." That's the experience most buyers report — the filter keeps flowing long after a spun cartridge would have strangled your water pressure.

The washable claim is legitimate but needs qualification. Hosing off the exterior removes the bulk of the sediment, and many users report getting 2-3 uses out of a single cartridge. But fine particles lodge deep in the pleats, and after the second wash, flow rates noticeably decline. Treat "washable" as "extends life," not "lasts forever."

## Where It Falls Short

The 1-micron rating is a double-edged sword. If your water has heavy sediment loads — think iron bacteria, clay, or lots of fine sand — this filter will clog faster than you'd like. It's not uncommon to see 2-3 month change intervals on problem wells. A 5-micron or 30-micron filter would last longer but let more through. Know your water before you commit.

The price per cartridge is also higher than basic spun filters. You're paying for the longevity and the washability, but the breakeven point only works if you actually utilize the wash-and-reuse cycle. If you're the type who just swaps filters without cleaning, you'd save money with cheaper spun cartridges.

## Who Should Buy This

This filter is ideal for homeowners on well water with moderate sediment — sand, rust, and general particulates. It's also a smart pick for anyone using a whole-house system on municipal water with aging pipes that shed scale. If you have extreme sediment loads or iron bacteria, step up to a backwashing system instead.

## FAQ

**How often should I replace this filter?**
With typical well water, expect 3-6 months. On heavy sediment, 2-3 months. Wash it monthly to extend life, but replace when pressure drops noticeably after cleaning.

**Does it fit my existing housing?**
It fits any standard 20" x 4.5" housing with 1" NPT threads. Check your current cartridge size — if it's 20x4.5, this drops right in.

**Can I use this for drinking water?**
Yes, it's safe for potable water. But it only removes sediment — you still need carbon or a reverse osmosis system for chemical and biological contaminants.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're tired of replacing spun filters monthly, you have moderate sediment in your water, and you're willing to spend a few minutes hosing the cartridge down between changes.

**Skip it if:** Your water is heavily contaminated with fine silt or iron — this will clog too fast to be practical. Also skip if you want a set-and-forget solution with zero maintenance.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The price per cartridge is higher, but the longevity and washability make it a genuine value for most homeowners. It's a smart upgrade from standard spun filters, with the only real downside being the maintenance commitment it asks of you.
