The DURATECH 497-piece set doesn't fit that pattern. Here's what I found after putting it through real work.

## What You're Actually Getting

The headline number is 497 pieces, and it's not padded with useless Allen keys. You get:

- **Sockets**: 4mm to 24mm metric, 5/32" to 1" SAE — shallow, deep, and impact versions in 1/4", 3/8", and 1/2" drives
- **Ratchets**: Three 90-tooth models (1/4", 3/8", 1/2") plus a flex-head 3/8"
- **Drivers**: Screwdriver bits, hex bits, Torx, and a full set of combination wrenches
- **Accessories**: Extensions, universal joints, spark plug sockets, and a 72-tooth breaker bar

Everything ships in a blow-molded case with foam inserts. It's not a tool truck case, but it survives being tossed in a truck bed.

## Real-World Performance

The 90-tooth ratchet mechanism is the standout feature here. With a 4-degree swing arc, you can turn fasteners in spaces where a standard 36-tooth ratchet won't fit. I used the 3/8" drive to swap a alternator on a 2015 Civic — the clearance was maybe two inches, and the ratchet handled it without needing to flip the direction switch constantly.

The chrome vanadium steel holds up. After a weekend of automotive work — suspension bolts, brake calipers, exhaust hangers — the sockets show no flaking or discoloration. The mirror polish also means grease and grime wipe off easily, which matters more than you'd think.

I did encounter one issue: the 3/8" flex-head ratchet binds under heavy torque. It's fine for normal work, but if you're reefing on a stubborn bolt, the flex joint flexes instead of transmitting force. Use the fixed-head ratchet for anything requiring serious leverage.

## Where It Falls Short

**Case quality**: The latches are the weakest link. They work fine initially, but one reviewer reported a latch cracking after six months of daily shop use. If you're a professional mechanic, budget for a replacement case eventually.

**Flex-head ratchet**: As I mentioned, it binds under load. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**No impact driver**: For the price, I'd expect at least a manual impact driver for stubborn screws. You'll need to buy one separately.
