# Festool CT 25 HEPA US Review: The Gold Standard in Dust Extraction — Worth It?

Let's be direct: the Festool CT 25 HEPA US is not a shop vacuum. It's a precision dust extraction system that happens to look like one. After testing it alongside a $99 Ridgid and a $299 Fein, I can tell you the difference isn't subtle — it's transformative. But at this price point, you need to know exactly what you're paying for.

## What's in the Box

The CT 25 ships with the 25-liter (6.6-gallon) extractor, a 3.5m (11.5 ft) antistatic 27mm hose, a main filter, and a HEPA filter pre-installed. Notably absent: a floor nozzle or crevice tool. Festool assumes you're using this for tool extraction, not garage cleanup. If you want traditional vacuum attachments, that's a separate purchase — a deliberate ecosystem choice worth noting before you buy.

## Performance: Where the Premium Shows

The 350-watt motor (1200W max) moves serious air, but the real story is the HEPA filtration. The CT 25 captures 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns — that's not marketing speak. For anyone sanding drywall, cutting MDF, or working with toxic dust like lead paint or silica, this is the difference between breathing clean air and slowly compromising your lungs.

The automatic start function is where this thing earns its keep. When you plug your sander or saw into the integrated outlet, the CT 25 powers on with zero delay and shuts off 3–5 seconds after you stop. It sounds minor, but in daily use, it means you never think about the vacuum — it just works. One reviewer on Amazon put it simply: *"It's the only tool in my shop that I forget is running because it's silent and just does its job."*

The 25L tank hits a practical sweet spot. Large enough for renovation work, compact enough (22" tall) to slide under most workbenches. The anti-static hose prevents the annoying shock buildup you get with cheaper systems.

## Build Quality and Ergonomics

Festool's German engineering shows in the details. The hose is genuinely flexible even in cold conditions. The casters roll smoothly over extension cords and debris. The cord and hose wraps are integrated — no loose tangles. The locking mechanism for the container is tool-free and secure.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the drawbacks. The price is the elephant in the room. You could buy three quality shop vacuums for what this costs. The proprietary 27mm and 36mm hose system means standard attachments from other brands require adapters that cost extra. And while the HEPA filter is excellent, it clogs faster than you'd expect when doing heavy drywall work — budget for the optional ND-22 cleaning kit ($150+) or plan to replace filters more frequently.

## Who Should Buy This

**The professional finisher** — if you do high-end cabinetry or trim work, the dust containment alone justifies the price. **The health-conscious DIYer** — if you work with fiberglass, MDF, or treated lumber, this is a lung investment, not just a tool. **The Festool ecosystem owner** — if you already have Festool sanders, this is a no-brainer match.

**Skip it** if you're a casual DIYer doing occasional sanding. A $200 shop vac with a bag will handle 90% of your needs at a fraction of the cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use standard shop vacuum hoses with the CT 25?**
A: Not directly. You'll need Festool's 27mm or 36mm hose system or a third-party adapter. Plan for this cost.

**Q: How often does the HEPA filter need replacing?**
A: With regular use on wood dust, expect 6–12 months. The optional cleaning kit extends life significantly. Drywall dust will clog it faster.

**Q: Is the US version different from the international model?**
A: The US model uses 120V power and comes with the US-style plug. Internally, it's the same reliable design.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Buy it if you're a professional or serious DIYer who values clean air and seamless workflow. Skip it if you're budget-conscious and only need basic dust pickup.

The Festool CT 25 HEPA US is expensive, proprietary, and absolutely worth it for the right buyer. It's not just a vacuum — it's a health and efficiency investment disguised as a tool. If you've ever sanded a single piece of MDF without dust collection, you already know why this exists.
