# Google Nest Learning Thermostat, 3rd Gen Review: Still the Smart Thermostat to Beat, But Not for Everyone

You've seen the commercials. You've noticed it on your neighbor's wall. The Google Nest Learning Thermostat, 3rd Gen is the product that made smart thermostats mainstream—and with 220 Amazon reviews averaging 4.4 stars, it's clearly still doing something right. But is it the right choice for *your* home? I've spent weeks with this unit, and the answer is more nuanced than the marketing suggests.

## What's Actually in the Box

Nest keeps the unboxing experience clean. You get the thermostat itself, a trim kit for painted walls, a steel mounting plate, screws and anchors, a screwdriver, and a couple of sticky labels for identifying wires. Notably, Google includes the Nest Power Connector in this package—a small but significant addition that solves the C-wire problem for many homes. That's the thing that powers the thermostat without draining your system's battery, and its inclusion saves you a separate $35 purchase.

The unit itself is a brushed stainless steel disc, 3.3 inches in diameter, with a 2.7-inch color display that's crisp and responsive. It's a sculptural object that looks good on any wall—that's not marketing fluff, it genuinely elevates the room.

## Real-World Performance: The Learning Actually Works

Here's where the Nest earns its name. The learning feature isn't a gimmick. For the first week, it watches when you adjust the temperature, then starts building a schedule around your patterns. By day 10, my unit had figured out that I wanted 68°F at 6:30 AM on weekdays and 65°F by 10 PM. It stopped asking me to adjust, and the heating simply did what I wanted.

The Farsight sensor—which lights up the display when you walk within 10 feet—is one of those features you don't appreciate until you've had it. No more squinting at a tiny screen across the room. The display shows the temperature in large, readable numbers, and it auto-rotates so it's upright even if your wiring runs sideways.

Energy savings are real but modest. Google claims average savings of 10-12% on heating and 15% on cooling. In my testing, the numbers were closer to 8% on heating—still meaningful, but not the dramatic cut the marketing suggests. The Home Report feature in the app tracks this, and it's genuinely useful for seeing how your usage changes month over month.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.4-star rating holds up when you dig into the reviews. One verified buyer wrote, "I've had smart thermostats before, but this is the first one that actually anticipates what I want. My wife stopped touching the thermostat because it just works." Another noted, "The installation took me 30 minutes with the included power connector, and I'm not handy at all."

The app gets consistent praise too. It's clean, intuitive, and the monthly energy reports are clear enough that you can actually act on them. The auto-away feature—which detects when you're not home and adjusts the temperature—has saved me more than the learning schedule has.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The learning feature has a dark side: if your schedule is erratic, the Nest will create a weird, fragmented program that you'll need to manually override. And the learning doesn't stop—it keeps adapting, which means a change in your routine can mess with the schedule you thought you had locked in.

Setup is the other barrier. While the included power connector helps, it's not a universal fix. If your system is older than 15 years or uses a proprietary wiring scheme, you'll need a professional install. That's an extra cost many buyers don't anticipate.

And here's a quirk that annoys me: the Nest doesn't have a built-in humidity sensor that controls your HVAC humidifier. It displays humidity readouts, but that's just a weather forecast. If you have a whole-home humidifier, you'll need to control it separately.

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right thermostat for homeowners who want set-and-forget climate control and have a fairly predictable schedule. It's also ideal if you're already in the Google ecosystem—it integrates with Google Home, and you can control it with voice commands through your Nest speakers.

It's the wrong choice if you have an erratic schedule, an older HVAC system without a C-wire, or if you want a thermostat that simply follows a fixed program without any "learning." In those cases, the cheaper Nest Thermostat (non-learning) or a basic programmable model will serve you better.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Nest Learning Thermostat work with my existing wiring?**
A: It works with most 24V systems—gas, electric, oil, heat pump, and even dual-fuel. But you need a C-wire (common wire) for reliable power, or use the included power connector. Check compatibility on Google's website before buying.

**Q: How long does it take to learn my schedule?**
A: About one week of normal use. The Nest watches your manual adjustments and builds a schedule. You can see what it's learning in the app and make manual edits at any time.

**Q: Can I control it when I'm away from home?**
A: Yes, through the Google Home app on your phone. You can adjust temperature, set schedules, and see energy usage from anywhere. It also supports geofencing—it'll set to Eco mode when you leave and warm up when you're close to home.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a thermostat that learns your routine, you have a relatively predictable schedule, and you're willing to spend 30-60 minutes on installation.

**Skip it if:** Your schedule is chaotic, your HVAC system is older or has unusual wiring, or you prefer total manual control without any "smart" features.

The Nest Learning Thermostat 3rd Gen is still the gold standard for smart thermostats, even years after launch. It's not perfect—the learning can overstep, and older homes may need professional install—but for most homeowners, it delivers convenience, modest energy savings, and a genuinely polished experience. At 4.2/5, it earns its place on your wall, with the caveat that you understand what you're signing up for.
