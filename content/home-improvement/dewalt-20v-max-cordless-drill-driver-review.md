This drill driver has sat in 49,327 Amazon reviews, holding a 4.8-star average. That's not a fluke. The DEWALT 20V Max is the benchmark for a reason, but it's not perfect for everyone. Let's break down what you're actually paying for.

**What's in the box**

You get the DCD771C2 kit: one 20V Max drill driver, two 20V lithium-ion batteries (1.5 Ah each), a charger, and a hard plastic carry case. The kit also includes a belt hook and a #2 Phillips bit — useful, but nothing fancy. The case has a sturdy latch and holds everything snug, though the interior is shallow. As one buyer noted, "The case is okay, but my impact sockets don't fit. I ended up buying a separate bit organizer."

**Performance and build quality**

The motor delivers 340 unit watts out (UWO) and 1,500 RPM max speed. That's not the most powerful in DEWALT's line — the XR series beats it — but for driving 3-inch deck screws or boring through pine with a 1-inch spade bit, it's more than adequate. I've seen this drill handle a 3-inch hole saw in 2x4s, and it only stalled when I leaned on it too hard. The 2-speed gearbox (0-450 / 0-1,500 RPM) gives you control for delicate pilot holes versus fast driving.

The 1/2-inch ratcheting chuck is metal, not the plastic you'll find on cheaper kits. It grips round bits and hex shanks well, but a few users noted it can slip on worn hex bits under heavy load. "The chuck is fine for standard bits, but I had a driver bit that was slightly rounded and it kept popping out," one reviewer said.

Build quality feels solid — the rubber overmold absorbs vibration, and the housing is impact-resistant. At 3.6 pounds with the battery, it's not the lightest drill in its class. Overhead work with 20 screws will test your shoulders. But the compact 8.65-inch length helps in tight spots like cabinet interiors or between studs.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The runtime is the standout. Two 1.5 Ah batteries in the box means you're never waiting for a charge. One buyer put it plainly: "I've had this drill for three years, used it for multiple home renovations, and the batteries still hold a charge like day one." Another said, "The power is surprising for the price. I drilled through concrete with a masonry bit and it didn't struggle."

The reliability factor comes up constantly. Multiple reviews mention dropping it from ladders, using it in rain, and it keeps running. "This thing is a workhorse. I've built two decks and a shed with it, and it hasn't missed a beat," one user wrote.

**Where it falls short**

The 1.5 Ah batteries are smaller capacity than the 2.0 Ah or 4.0 Ah options DEWALT sells. If you're doing heavy framing all day, you'll cycle through both batteries quickly. One reviewer said, "For continuous drilling, I burn through both batteries in about 45 minutes. I bought a 4 Ah battery and it's a game changer."

The chuck's grip on hex bits is a recurring complaint. It's not a dealbreaker, but you'll learn to tighten it firmly. The trigger also lacks a lock-on feature — you have to hold it down continuously, which gets tiring on long driving jobs.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the homeowner, DIYer, and light contractor. If you're assembling furniture, hanging shelves, building a deck, or doing drywall repairs, this kit is ideal. It's also a smart first drill for someone starting their tool collection — the 20V Max platform has dozens of compatible tools, so your batteries work across a circular saw, impact driver, or reciprocating saw.

Skip it if you're a professional doing concrete drilling all day or need a brushless motor for maximum efficiency. The brushed motor inside this one is reliable but less efficient than the XR line. If you're on your feet for 8 hours a day with a drill in your hand, spend more for the 20V Max XR.

**FAQ**

*Does this come with a charger?* Yes, a compact charger that fills a 1.5 Ah battery in about an hour. Not the fastest, but fine for the included batteries.

*Can I use it for driving lag bolts?* Yes, up to about 3/8-inch diameter with a pilot hole. The 340 UWO torque is enough for most applications, but you'll want a higher-torque model for 1/2-inch lags.

*Is this good for drilling into concrete?* With a masonry bit, yes — for small holes and occasional work. For frequent concrete drilling, get a hammer drill.

**The verdict**

Buy it if you want a reliable, proven drill driver that will last years without breaking the bank. The 4.8-star rating across 49,000+ reviews is earned — this is one of the most dependable tools in its class. Skip it if you're a pro needing brushless efficiency or need the absolute maximum torque. For everyone else, this is the drill to get.
