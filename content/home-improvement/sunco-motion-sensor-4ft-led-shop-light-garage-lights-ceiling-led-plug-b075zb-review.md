**The Review**

Let me be direct: garage lighting is where most LED shop lights go to disappoint. Either they're too dim, the motion sensor triggers on every passing car, or the "linkable" feature requires a degree in electrical engineering. The Sunco 4ft Motion Sensor LED Shop Light sidesteps most of that. Here's what you actually need to know before buying.

**What's in the box**

You get one 4-foot LED fixture rated at 4400 lumens, 5000K daylight color temperature. That's a proper cool white — think surgical suite, not cozy reading nook. The housing is a slim aluminum profile with a frosted polycarbonate lens that diffuses light evenly. There's a 5.5-foot power cord with a standard plug, plus a link cable to daisy-chain up to four fixtures. Mounting hardware is included: two brackets with screws and drywall anchors. Notably absent is a junction box adapter — you'll need the separate Sunco accessory if you want to hardwire it instead of using the plug.

**Performance and build**

The PIR motion sensor is the headline feature, and it works. It detects movement up to 15 feet away with a 100-degree field of view. In a two-car garage, I mounted it centered on the ceiling and it catches you the moment you walk through the side door. The light stays on for a preset 3 minutes of no motion before shutting off. That's not adjustable, so be aware: if you're doing fine work under the fixture and staying still, it will turn off on you. It's a minor annoyance, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

The 5000K output renders colors accurately — important if you're matching paint or doing wiring where you need to distinguish wire jacket colors. At 4400 lumens, one fixture comfortably lights a single parking bay. Two fixtures, linked together, will make a standard two-car garage feel like a showroom. The link cable is generous at about 6 feet, giving you placement flexibility.

Build quality is solid for the price point. The aluminum housing acts as a heat sink, and after three hours of continuous operation, the housing was warm but not hot — that's a good sign for LED longevity. The frosted lens eliminates the harsh diode hotspots you see on cheaper fixtures.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The convenience factor is the dominant theme in Amazon feedback. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"Walk in, lights on. Walk out, lights off. I haven't touched a switch in months."* Multiple reviewers mention the motion sensor's range as a standout — it picks up movement before you're fully through the door, which is exactly what you want when your hands are full of groceries or tools.

The brightness-to-price ratio also gets consistent mention. Owners of single-car garages and workshops report that one unit replaces a 2-bulb fluorescent fixture with noticeably better output. The linkable feature earns praise from people who've daisy-chained three or four units across a long workshop — they're running all of them off a single outlet and a single motion trigger.

**Where it falls short**

The non-adjustable sensor timeout is the most common complaint. If you're working under the light and staying relatively still — soldering, sorting screws, detailing a car — it will shut off mid-task. You learn to wave an arm to reset it, but that's a workaround, not a feature.

The other issue is the lack of an included junction box adapter for hardwiring. The product is sold as a plug-in fixture, so this isn't a defect — but if you want a clean ceiling-mount without a visible cord running to an outlet, budget an extra few dollars for the adapter. Some buyers also note that mounting brackets are simple L-brackets; they hold fine, but they're not the most elegant solution for a finished garage.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the homeowner who wants walk-in-walk-out lighting without thinking about switches. It's ideal for garages, basements, laundry rooms, and utility spaces where you're carrying things and your hands aren't free. It's also a strong option for workshops where you want broad, even light and don't want to fiddle with a pull chain in the dark. If you have a finished garage where aesthetics matter, the visible motion sensor dome might bother you — consider the non-sensor version instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this outdoors in a covered patio or carport?**
A: No. It's rated for indoor use only (damp locations at best). If you need outdoor motion-sensing light, look for a fixture with an IP65 rating or higher.

**Q: How many can I link together and how do they behave?**
A: Up to four fixtures link with the included cable. The motion sensor on the first fixture controls all linked units — they turn on and off together. You cannot link four and have each sensor work independently.

**Q: Will the motion sensor work through a window or glass door?**
A: No. PIR sensors detect heat signatures, and glass blocks the infrared signal. It needs a clear line of sight to the space you want to monitor.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want zero-fuss, hands-free lighting in your garage or workshop and don't want to wire a switch. The 4400-lumen output is genuinely useful, the sensor range is generous, and the linkable design covers large spaces with one trigger point.

**Skip it if:** You need an adjustable auto-off timer, you're hardwiring into a junction box and don't want to buy an extra adapter, or you're lighting a space where you'll work stationary for long stretches — the fixed 3-minute timeout will get old quickly.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does exactly what it claims, with a handful of minor annoyances that don't outweigh the convenience. For the price, it's one of the better motion-sensing shop lights you can buy on Amazon right now.
