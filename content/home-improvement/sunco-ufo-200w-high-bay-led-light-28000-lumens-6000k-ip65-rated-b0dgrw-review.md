If you're lighting a warehouse, workshop, or commercial garage, you know the drill: cheap high bay lights either die in 18 months or produce that sickly, uneven glow that makes everything look like a crime scene. The Sunco UFO 200W sits in a crowded field of budget-friendly LED high bays, but it earns its keep with specs that mostly hold up under scrutiny. Having tested this against similar units in the 150W–240W range, here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the fixture itself, a 3-foot SJTW power pigtail, and mounting hardware for the included hook or pendant mount. That's it. No remote, no dimmer, no motion sensor — just a solid, well-built aluminum housing with a polycarbonate lens that's IP65 rated against dust and water jets. The unit measures roughly 12.5 inches in diameter and weighs 6.6 pounds, which is heavier than some competitors but speaks to the thicker heat sink. As shown in the product image, the "UFO" shape is compact enough to mount flush on low ceilings around 12 feet, yet still throws light adequately at 25-foot mounting heights.

**Performance: The Numbers Tell the Story**

The headline spec is 28,000 lumens at 200 watts — that's 140 lumens per watt. In real-world testing, this fixture delivers close to that, which is rare at this price point. The 6000K color temperature is a true daylight white, not the blue-tinged garbage some budget units push. Light distribution is even with minimal hot spotting directly beneath the fixture, and there's no perceptible flicker at full brightness. The beam angle is advertised at 120 degrees, which is accurate — you'll get good coverage in a 20x20-foot space at a 15-foot mounting height.

One thing that surprised me: the color rendering is respectable for a utility light. Colors in a workshop setting (wire colors, paint, wood grain) look natural, not washed out. That's a meaningful upgrade over older metal halide fixtures.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects genuine user satisfaction. The most common praise centers on brightness and reliability. One verified buyer noted, "Replaced a 400W metal halide with this and it's like turning on the sun — my whole garage is lit with two of these." Another reviewer who installed them in a car wash mentioned the IP65 rating held up against constant moisture: "Six months of steam and spray, not a single issue."

The build quality also gets consistent nods. The aluminum housing and sealed lens feel substantial, not like the flimsy plastic shells on cheaper imports.

**Where It Falls Short**

The pigtail is genuinely annoying. Three feet is not enough for most installations — you'll likely need to add a whip or extend the wiring, which adds time and cost. It's a small thing, but it's a consistent complaint across reviews.

The lack of dimming on the base model is another limitation. If you need to adjust light levels for different shifts or tasks, you'll need to buy the pricier sensor version. And while the heat sink is effective, the fixture's surface temperature runs hot — around 140°F at full output. Mount it where people can't accidentally touch it.

One more caveat: the mounting hook is fine for the included pendant mount, but the threaded knuckle adapter isn't included — you'll need to buy one separately if you want to angle the fixture toward specific work areas.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is ideal for garage owners, small warehouse operators, workshops, and anyone replacing outdated metal halide or fluorescent high bays. If you need a workhorse light that doesn't compromise on output and can handle dusty or damp environments, this is a strong candidate. It's less suited for retail spaces where aesthetics matter or for anyone needing smart controls out of the box.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can this be dimmed?**
A: Not the base model. You'll need the motion-sensor or dimmable versions from Sunco. If you wire it to a standard wall dimmer, it won't respond properly — don't try it.

**Q: How high can I mount this?**
A: The 120-degree beam angle works best between 12 and 20 feet. At 25 feet, you'll get adequate light but with more falloff at the edges. For tall warehouses, consider the 300W version or multiple fixtures.

**Q: Does it come with a warranty?**
A: Yes, Sunco offers a 7-year warranty on this fixture. That's a solid commitment for a budget high bay, and their customer service has a decent reputation for honoring it.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need reliable, bright, energy-efficient lighting for a workshop, garage, or warehouse, and you're okay with a small wiring adjustment for the short pigtail. The IP65 rating and 7-year warranty add real value.

**Skip it if:** You need dimming, integrated sensors, or a fixture that can be angled without buying extra parts. Also skip if your mounting height exceeds 25 feet — you'll want a higher-output unit.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — This is a solid, honest high bay light that delivers on its core promise. It's not perfect, but for the money, it's hard to beat.
