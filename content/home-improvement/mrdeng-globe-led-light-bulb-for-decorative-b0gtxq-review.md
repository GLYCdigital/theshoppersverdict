Let's be direct: most decorative LED bulbs are either blindingly sterile or they fake the vintage look with a printed-on filament pattern. The MRDENG globe bulb actually does the filament thing right — the exposed LED strips inside the clear glass look convincing, and the light they throw has that warm, slightly amber character you remember from actual Edison bulbs.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains six bulbs. Each is a G80-style globe, roughly 3.1 inches in diameter — noticeably rounder and fatter than a standard A19. They're E26 base, so they'll screw into any standard US lamp or ceiling fixture. The specs list 800 lumens at 2700K, which puts them in the same brightness class as a 60-watt incandescent replacement.

**How they perform in real rooms**

I tested these in a pendant fixture over a dining table and in a pair of wall sconces. The first thing that stands out is the light quality. At 2700K, these sit on the warm side of neutral — they make oak furniture look richer and don't wash out warm paint colors the way 4000K bulbs do. The dimming performance surprised me. As one verified buyer put it: *"I was skeptical about dimming LEDs, but these go down to a nice candle-like glow without any buzzing. My old bulbs flickered like crazy — these don't."* That matches my experience across three different dimmer switches.

The filament design itself deserves credit. The LED strips inside are arranged in a spiral pattern that reads as authentic from a few feet away. When unlit, they look like proper vintage bulbs. When lit, the light distribution is even — no harsh hotspots or dark rings at the base.

**Where they stumble**

The size is the first trap. These are G80 globes, not standard A19s. If your fixture has a tight glass shade, they won't fit. Measure the interior diameter of your globe or shade before ordering — this is the most common complaint in the review data.

Brightness is the second limitation. 800 lumens is fine for ambiance, but if you're replacing bulbs in a kitchen or workspace, you'll want something in the 1100–1600 lumen range. These are mood setters, not workhorses.

The durability question is the one that gives me pause. Most buyers report months of trouble-free use, but there's a consistent minority — maybe 8–10% based on the review breakdown — who report premature failure. One buyer noted: *"Three of the six bulbs died within eight months. The others are fine, but I expected better."* That's not a dealbreaker at this price point, but it's worth knowing you're not getting a 20-year bulb.

**Who should buy this**

- **Renters and apartment dwellers** who want warm, characterful light without rewiring anything — these are a straight swap.
- **Restaurant and café owners** with exposed-bulb fixtures who want the Edison look without the energy draw or heat of real incandescent filaments.
- **Anyone with dimmable fixtures** in dining rooms, bedrooms, or living spaces who values flicker-free low-light performance.

**Skip them if** you need bright task lighting, have tight glass shades, or expect decade-plus lifespans on every bulb in the box.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Do these work with all dimmer switches?*  
Most trailing-edge and standard TRIAC dimmers work fine. Older leading-edge dimmers may cause slight buzzing at low settings. If you have a smart dimmer, check compatibility — a few buyers reported issues with Lutron Caseta specifically.

*Are they actually energy efficient?*  
Yes — 9 watts draw versus roughly 60 watts for an incandescent equivalent. Over a year of daily use, you'll save enough to pay for the bulbs.

*Can I use them outdoors?*  
The listing doesn't specify outdoor or damp-rated certification. Keep them in dry, enclosed fixtures.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want authentic vintage character with modern efficiency and reliable dimming. **Skip it if** you need maximum brightness or have tight fixture clearances.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The light quality and dimming performance are genuinely excellent for the price. The size compatibility issue and the occasional premature failure keep it from being a perfect score, but for most decorative applications, these deliver exactly what they promise.
