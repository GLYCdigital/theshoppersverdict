## What You Actually Get

The set arrives with one right-angle ratcheting driver and 45 bits. The bit assortment is genuinely useful: Phillips #0–#3, slotted 3–6mm, Torx T10–T40, hex 2–6mm, and a handful of security bits (Torx with holes) for tamper-resistant fasteners. Everything packs into a compact storage sleeve that fits in a drawer or tool bag without hogging space.

The driver itself is the star. The 72-tooth ratchet mechanism is the headline feature — that translates to a 5-degree working arc, which means you can turn a fastener in incredibly tight quarters. The alloy body feels solid in hand, and the chrome vanadium steel bits resist cam-out on worn screws.

## How It Performs in Real Use

I put this through the jobs that typically frustrate people: tightening a loose hinge screw in a corner cabinet, removing a recessed panel screw behind a washing machine, and driving screws into a tight spot under a workbench. The ratchet action is smooth and positive — no skipping, no slop. The direction switch is a simple sliding collar that clicks into place and stays there.

The 5-degree arc is the real differentiator. A standard ratcheting screwdriver needs maybe 15–20 degrees of swing; this thing works in a fraction of that. That's the difference between finishing a job and cursing while trying to wedge a tool sideways.

One thing worth noting: the handle length. At roughly 4 inches, it's compact — which is great for access but less ideal for torque. For heavy-duty tightening, you'll wish it were longer. For the tight-space work it's designed for, it's a fair tradeoff.

## Where It Falls Short

The friction-fit bit sleeve is the weakest link. Bits stay put during normal use, but if you toss the set loose into a tool bag, you'll find bits scattered at the bottom. A locking case or a magnetic holder would fix this — it's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

The handle length, as mentioned, limits torque. If you're driving long screws into hardwood, this isn't your primary driver — it's a specialty tool for tight spots. Use it for that, and you'll be happy.
