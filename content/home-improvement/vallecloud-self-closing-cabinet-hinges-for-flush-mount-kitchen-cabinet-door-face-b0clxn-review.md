## What's in the Box

You get a set of hinges (available in 2, 5, or 10-packs), mounting screws for both the cabinet frame and the door, and a small 4mm hex wrench for adjustments. The hinges themselves are the standard euro-style concealed type — you'll need a 35mm bore hole in the door, which is the industry standard for cabinet hinges. The flush mount design means they sit flush against the cabinet frame when closed, giving a clean, minimal look that works well with modern kitchens.

## Performance and Build

The stainless steel construction is genuinely solid. These aren't the flimsy zinc-alloy hinges you'll find at the big-box store for a few bucks less. In a kitchen environment — where humidity, heat, and constant use take their toll — the corrosion resistance matters. The hydraulic damping mechanism is smooth on the closing stroke, and the self-closing action kicks in reliably in the last 15-20 degrees of travel.

The 3-way adjustability is where these shine. You get vertical, horizontal, and depth adjustment, which means you can fine-tune door alignment after installation. This is critical for flush-mount doors where even a millimeter of misalignment is visible. One buyer noted, "I was able to get all six of my cabinet doors perfectly aligned in about 20 minutes. The hex wrench that comes with it is actually usable, not a cheap throwaway."

## Where They Fall Short

Here's the honest part. Despite the "self closing" name, these are **not** soft-close hinges. The hydraulic damper slows the closing, but it doesn't catch and gently pull the door shut like a true soft-close mechanism. If you're replacing old hinges with the specific goal of eliminating door slams, these will help, but they won't give you that premium "expensive cabinet" feel.

The other issue is the 35mm bore requirement. If you're retrofitting existing cabinets that have standard American-style hinges with visible knuckles, you'll need to drill a 35mm hole and likely use a template. That's a doable DIY project, but it's more involved than a simple screwdriver swap. Also, the included screws are sized for standard cabinet frames — if you have particleboard or thinner material, grab longer screws from your local hardware store.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY renovators** replacing damaged euro-style hinges on existing cabinets with 35mm holes
- **Kitchen remodels** where you want concealed hinges for a clean, modern look

**Skip these if** you're looking for true soft-close functionality, or if you're working with non-standard cabinet construction that doesn't accommodate euro-style hinges.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these compatible with IKEA cabinets?**
A: No. IKEA uses their own proprietary hinge system with different mounting plates. These are standard euro-style hinges with screw-in cups.

**Q: What's the maximum door weight these can handle?**
A: Each hinge handles roughly 15-20 pounds. For a standard kitchen cabinet door (3-5 lbs), you need just one hinge per door. For heavier or wider doors, use two.

**Q: Do I need to buy a 35mm Forstner bit separately?**
A: Yes, if your doors don't already have the bore holes. The bit costs about $10-15 and is a one-time purchase if you don't own one.
