### The 10-pack that makes recessed lighting a weekend job

Most 10-packs of recessed lights are either cheap junk with mismatched color temps or overpriced commercial units that need special housings. The ECOELER LED Ceiling Disk Downlight sits in a sweet spot: it's a slim, IC-rated wafer light with a color-selectable switch built into each unit. For a 1,200-square-foot basement or a kitchen reno on a budget, this is the pack to start with.

### What's actually in the box

You get 10 complete fixtures — each with a pre-wired junction box, a 6-inch connector cable, and the disk itself. No separate drivers, no clunky transformers. The junction box has knockouts for standard NM-B cable (14/2 or 12/2), and the connectors are push-in style — no wire nuts needed. That's a real time-saver if you're wiring a whole room.

One thing I noticed from the product images: the disk is only 0.3 inches thick, which is why it slides into 2x4 ceiling bays without a problem. The IC rating means you can lay insulation directly over the housing — critical for attic installations where code requires airtight fixtures.

### Performance: brightness and color that actually work

Each light pushes 1100 lumens at 12 watts. That's roughly equivalent to a 75-watt incandescent, and it's noticeable — a 10-pack in a 15x15-foot room gives you even, shadow-free coverage. The 120° beam angle is wide enough for general lighting; you won't need to stagger them as tightly as narrower-beam cans.

The color temperature switch on each unit cycles through 2700K (warm), 3500K, 4000K (neutral), 4500K, and 5000K (daylight). I set mine to 4000K in the kitchen and 2700K in the hallway — same fixture, different mood. It beats buying two separate packs.

Buyer feedback backs this up. One reviewer wrote, *"Installed 10 in my garage in two hours. The 5000K setting is crisp and clean, no flicker even on a cheap dimmer — wait, these aren't dimmable, so that's fine."* Another noted, *"Color switch is a lifesaver. I thought I wanted daylight, but 3500K is perfect for the living room."*

### Where the ECOELER pack falls short

The big one: **these are not dimmable.** If you're planning a media room or bedroom with dimmer switches, stop here — you'd need the ECOELER dimmable version (slightly pricier). Also, the junction box is chunky. It's fine for new construction or remodeling with open joists, but if you're trying to retrofit into existing 4-inch or 6-inch can housings, you'll need an adapter ring that doesn't come in the box.

One more caveat: the push-in wire connectors are solid, but they don't accept aluminum wiring. If your home has aluminum branch circuits, you'll need copper pigtails and a licensed electrician.

### Who should buy this — and who should skip

**Buy it if:** You're a DIYer wiring a basement, garage, or rental property. The color-selectable feature and slim profile mean you can stock one SKU for multiple rooms. The price per unit (when divided across 10) beats most single-unit wafer lights at big-box stores.

**Skip it if:** You need dimmable lighting, or you're replacing fixtures inside existing recessed cans without wanting to modify the housing. This is a "rip out the old can" kind of light — not a retrofit insert.
