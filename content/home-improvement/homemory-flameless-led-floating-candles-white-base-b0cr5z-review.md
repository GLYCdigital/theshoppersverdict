## What You Actually Get

The package includes six flameless floating candles, each measuring roughly 2 inches in diameter and 1.5 inches tall. The white base is solid plastic, not cheap hollow stuff — it floats level and doesn't tip when nudged. Each candle runs on 2 AA batteries (not included, which is the one annoyance). The LED is recessed behind a translucent "3D wick" that mimics the look of a burning cotton wick. From three feet away, you'll struggle to tell these from real flames.

## Build Quality and Performance

I tested these in a black ceramic bowl, a bathtub, and a glass vase. The flicker is the standout feature — it's random, organic, and slow. No strobe effect, no fake "blinking" pattern. It genuinely reads as candlelight.

Battery life is the headline number: 48 hours continuous. I ran a pair for a full weekend wedding setup (Friday setup through Sunday teardown) on the same batteries. They were still flickering when I packed them up. That's not marketing math — that's real-world endurance.

The white base does its job. Against dark water, the candle body visually disappears, leaving just the flame floating. That's the entire trick of floating candles, and Homemory nailed it. The wax-textured exterior is smooth but not glossy — it picks up light like real paraffin.

## Where It Falls Short

No remote. For a product in this price tier, that's the biggest miss. If you're setting up 18 of these in a pool for an event, you'll be flipping 36 AA battery compartments by hand. A remote-controlled variant exists from Homemory, but this specific listing doesn't include one. Check the listing carefully if that matters to you.

Second issue: "Floating" doesn't mean "waterproof." The bottom battery compartment is sealed with a rubber gasket, but it's not rated for submersion. If a candle tips over and water gets in, the LED is done. Dry them completely after each use or they'll corrode.

Third: The white base will show water spots and mineral deposits after a few uses in hard water. Wipe them down after each event.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these be used outdoors in a pool?**
A: Yes, for still water. Wind will blow them around since they're light (about 4 oz each). A breeze creates drifting, not sinking. But heavy rain or splashing risks water ingress through the battery compartment.

**Q: How do you turn them on?**
A: There's a small switch on the bottom. Twist the base off, flip the switch, reassemble. No timer function, no remote, no auto-off. Budget 30 seconds per candle for setup.

**Q: Do they come with batteries?**
A: No. Each candle needs 2 AA batteries, so 12 total for a 6-pack. Factor that into your cost — quality alkaline batteries will run you another $10–12.
