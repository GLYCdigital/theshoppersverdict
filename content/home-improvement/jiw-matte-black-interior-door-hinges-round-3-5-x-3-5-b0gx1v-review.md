If you're swapping out dated brass or polished chrome hinges for a modern matte black look, the JIW 3.5" round hinges deserve a hard look. This 5-pack runs about $20–25 depending on the day, which lands squarely in the "reasonable for what you get" zone. But the real question isn't price—it's whether these hold up and whether the round style fits your doors without a fight.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get five complete hinges: each with two plates, a steel pin, and the mounting screws needed to install one hinge. The set covers two standard interior doors (two hinges per door) plus a spare, which is smart thinking. The screws are the standard Phillips flat-head wood screws you'd expect, nothing fancy. The hinges themselves are solid steel with a ball-bearing race in the knuckle—that's the feature that matters for long-term smooth operation, especially on heavier doors that tend to sag over time.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The matte black finish is the star here. It's applied evenly with no drips, no thin spots, and it's genuinely resistant to fingerprints—a huge win if you're installing these on high-traffic doors where kids and pets are constantly pushing through. The round design is a clean, modern look that pairs well with contemporary or transitional interior styles.

Installation is straightforward *if* you're working with new doors or pre-hung frames. The ball-bearing action is noticeably smooth right out of the box, and it stays that way. One buyer noted, *"These felt more expensive than they were. The action is buttery smooth and the finish hasn't chipped after six months of daily use."* That tracks with what I'd expect from a hinge in this price tier.

The 3.5" x 3.5" size is the standard for most interior doors, so you're not gambling on odd dimensions. Each hinge is rated to handle doors up to about 80 pounds, which covers virtually every hollow-core and most solid-core interior doors you'll encounter.

**Where These Fall Short**

The round shape is the catch. If you're replacing existing hinges, your door edge and jamb already have rectangular mortises cut into them. A round hinge will leave gaps at the corners—you'll either need to fill and repaint, or carefully chisel out the mortise to match the round profile. That's real work, and it's not a five-minute swap.

The included screws are functional but unremarkable. For a solid-core door, I'd swap in better screws—the ones provided can strip if you're driving them into hardwood with an impact driver. It's a $2 fix, but worth mentioning so you're not frustrated mid-install.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **DIYers renovating a whole house**: The 5-pack pricing and consistent finish make this an efficient way to update multiple doors at once.
- **Anyone matching modern black hardware**: If your door handles, hinges, and light switches are all matte black, these complete the look perfectly.
- **Landlords or flippers**: Durable, good-looking, and priced to move—these are a solid value play.

**Skip them if** you're doing a quick hinge swap on existing doors and don't want to deal with mortise modifications. For that job, look for rectangular hinges in the same finish.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these fit standard interior doors?**
A: Yes. 3.5" x 3.5" is the standard size for most interior doors. Just verify your door thickness—these work with 1-3/8" and 1-3/4" doors.

**Q: Do they come with screws?**
A: Yes, each hinge comes with mounting screws. They're decent for hollow-core doors; consider upgrading for solid-core or heavy doors.

**Q: How many hinges per door do I need?**
A: Two per standard interior door is typical. Three is better for solid-core or extra-wide doors. The 5-pack covers two doors with a spare.

**The Verdict**

**Skip it if** you want a drop-in replacement for existing rectangular hinges without any carpentry work—the round design will require some fitting.
