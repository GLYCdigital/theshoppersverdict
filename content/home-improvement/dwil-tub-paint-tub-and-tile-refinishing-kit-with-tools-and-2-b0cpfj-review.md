Refinishing a bathtub used to mean either paying $500+ for a professional or crossing your fingers with a cheap rattle can. The DWIL Tub Paint and Tile Refinishing Kit sits in the middle, and at 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep for a lot of DIYers. But is it the right call for your bathroom? Let's get into it.

## What's Actually in the Box

As shown in the product image, this isn't just a can of paint. The DWIL kit bundles the essentials: the refinishing paint itself, a bonding primer, rollers, foam brushes, sanding pads, and mixing sticks. That's a genuine advantage — most competing kits make you hunt down applicators separately, and the included sanding pads are the right grit for scuffing up glossy tile.

## Performance and Build Quality

The paint formula is a two-part epoxy that self-levels as it cures. Applied correctly, you get a smooth, glossy surface that looks like a factory finish. It's genuinely impressive how close this gets to professional reglazing results.

Here's the catch — and it's a big one — **surface prep is everything**. The kit's instructions are clear: clean, sand, clean again, apply primer, then paint. Skip any step and you'll be chipping paint off your tub within months. Multiple buyers report exactly this scenario. One reviewer wrote: "I didn't sand enough and it peeled within 6 weeks. Second attempt with proper prep has held up over a year."

The paint itself is thick and covers well. One quart handles a standard tub with some left over for touch-ups. The self-leveling action means brush marks usually disappear, though you need to work quickly since it starts setting in about 20 minutes.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The value proposition is the headline. Buyers repeatedly mention saving $400–$800 versus professional reglazing. The finish quality gets strong marks too:

- "Looks like a brand new tub. My guests thought I had it replaced."
- "The included tools made this actually doable for a first-timer."
- "Covered years of stains and discoloration in two coats."

## Where It Falls Short

The odor is no joke. This is a solvent-based epoxy, and the fumes linger for days. You need windows open, fans running, and ideally a place to sleep that isn't adjacent to the bathroom. Several reviewers note the smell persisted for 48+ hours.

Drying time is another consideration. You're looking at 24 hours before light use, and a full 72 hours before the tub should see water. That's a long time to be showering at the gym.

The kit also doesn't include gloves or a respirator. Buy a proper respirator with organic vapor cartridges — this isn't optional.

## Who Should Buy This

This kit is ideal for the **budget-conscious homeowner** with a structurally sound but cosmetically dated tub. If your tub has chips or cracks, stop — you need repair materials, not paint. It's also great for **landlords prepping rentals** or anyone selling a home who wants to boost bathroom appeal without a full remodel.

**Skip it** if you're not willing to commit to the prep work or if you need the tub functional within 48 hours.

## FAQ

**How long does DWIL tub paint last?**
With proper prep and care (no abrasive cleaners), expect 2–4 years before it needs recoating. That's shorter than professional reglazing but at a fraction of the cost.

**Can I use this on shower tile walls?**
Yes, but it's a bigger surface area, so plan for multiple coats and more cure time. The adhesion on ceramic tile is solid if you scuff it well.

**Does it yellow over time?**
The white formula has held up well in most reviews, though prolonged sun exposure can cause slight yellowing. Fine for most bathrooms.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're patient, detail-oriented, and want to save hundreds on a tub refresh. **Skip it if** you can't handle the fumes, the cure time, or the prep work — a bad application will waste your money and leave a mess.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid DIY solution with real results, held back only by demanding prep and a strong odor. Worth it if you do it right.
