**$195 for three cardboard rectangles. Let's talk about whether that's insane or actually smart.**

Here's the thing about 4-inch media filters: they're a different animal than the 1-inch fiberglass disposables most homeowners grab at the hardware store. The Filtrete 16x25x4 MPR 1550 sits in a permanent filter cabinet — typically a Honeywell F100 or similar media housing — where the filter *is* the system. You're not swapping this quarterly. You're installing it, forgetting it for 6–15 months, then repeating. That changes the math entirely.

---

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get three (3) individual Filtrete MPR 1550 filters, each measuring 16x25x4 inches. The 4-inch depth is critical — these are designed to fit media cabinets with a 4-inch wide slot, *not* standard residential 1-inch filter grilles. If your furnace has a standard 1-inch return slot, stop reading. This doesn't fit.

Each filter uses Filtrete's electrostatic pleated media — 100% synthetic, no fiberglass. The MPR 1550 rating sits on the upper-mid tier of Filtrete's lineup: it captures roughly 90% of airborne particles in the 0.3–1.0 micron range, which covers bacteria, smoke, and pet dander. It's not their top-tier MPR 1900, but it's a solid "allergy season" performer.

---

**Performance: Where the 4-Inch Pays Off**

The physics advantage here is real. A 4-inch deep pleat gives you dramatically more surface area than a 1-inch filter — roughly 2.5x the media. That means:

- **Lower pressure drop** for the same filtration level. Your blower works less, which translates to marginally lower energy bills.
- **Longer service life.** Filtrete claims up to 12 months, and that's realistic in a normal household. With two dogs and a wood stove, I'd plan on 6–9 months.
- **Better particle capture under load.** The deep pleats don't clog as quickly, so the filter maintains airflow as it loads up — unlike 1-inch filters that strangle your system in month two.

One buyer noted: *"I installed this in my Honeywell F100 cabinet in March and checked it in November. It looked dirty but not packed solid. My previous 1-inch filters were toast by 8 weeks."* That tracks with the physics.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

**Price.** At $195 for three, you're paying $65 per filter. Comparable MERV 13 media filters (which is roughly what MPR 1550 equates to) run $35–45 each from brands like AirBear or Nordic Pure. You're paying a premium for the Filtrete name and the brand's electrostatic media, which does hold a charge longer than generic pleats. Whether that's worth 40–50% more is your call.

**Fitment confusion.** The listing says "Fits Honeywell" — that's technically true, but it fits Honeywell *media cabinets* like the F100/F200 series. It does not fit a standard 1-inch filter slot. Multiple reviews mention ordering this and discovering it didn't fit their system. **Measure your filter cabinet depth before ordering.** If your current filter is 1 inch thick, this won't work — period.

**Airflow restriction.** MPR 1550 is not a high-airflow filter. It's rated for particle capture, and that comes at a cost. If your furnace has a single-speed PSC blower (common in homes built before 2010), the added static pressure can reduce airflow by 10–15%, potentially causing heat exchanger overheating in extreme cases. Modern ECM blowers handle it fine. Older systems — not so much.

**FAQ**

**Q: How often do I actually need to replace this filter?**
A: Filtrete says 12 months. In a typical 2-person household with no pets, 9–12 months is realistic. With pets or heavy dust, plan on 6 months. Check monthly — if the media looks uniformly gray and dusty, replace it.

**Q: Does MPR 1550 really filter smoke and viruses?**
A: It captures particles in the 0.3–1.0 micron range at ~90% efficiency. That includes smoke particles and many bacteria-sized particles. It's not a HEPA replacement, but it's meaningful — especially compared to a MERV 4 fiberglass filter that captures almost nothing below 10 microns.

**Q: Can I wash and reuse this filter?**
A: No. It's a disposable pleated media filter. Washing destroys the electrostatic charge and collapses the pleat structure. Attempting to clean it will void the performance and can damage your HVAC system. This is not a washable filter.
