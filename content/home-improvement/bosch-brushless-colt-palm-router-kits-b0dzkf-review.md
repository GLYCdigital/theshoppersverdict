If you’ve used the original BOSCH Colt, you know the drill: a capable little router that demanded patience with its brushed motor. This new Brushless Colt™ Palm Router Kit fixes the biggest complaint while keeping the palm-sized ergonomics that made the original a favorite. But at 4.0 stars across 200 ratings, it’s not a perfect tool. Here’s what you need to know before you buy.

**What’s in the Box**

This kit is the reason to choose it over the bare tool. You get the router motor, a fixed base, a plunge base, an edge guide, a centering cone, and a 1/4-inch collet. The plunge base has a micro-adjust depth rod that clicks in 1/128-inch increments — genuinely useful for hinge mortises or inlay work. The fixed base has a clear sub-base that’s 45mm, which matches most template guides. Both bases share the same motor housing, so swapping takes about 15 seconds.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The brushless motor is the headline. It spins at 25,000–30,000 RPM, and the difference from the old brushed version is immediate. At lower speeds (think 25k for large bits), the motor holds torque without bogging down. On a test run with a 3/8-inch straight bit in oak, the cut was clean with no burning at a moderate feed rate. The variable speed dial is on the motor body, which means you adjust it before mounting — a minor annoyance when you’re mid-project and want to change speeds.

The LED ring is a genuine upgrade. Three lights circle the bit, so you actually see the cut line and the bit entry point. In dark cabinet interiors, this is a game-changer. The motor housing is compact — 5.2 inches tall — so it fits in tight spaces where a full-size router won’t.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The torque and control get consistent shoutouts. As one buyer put it: *“I was skeptical about the brushless upgrade, but this thing powers through hardwood like my old Colt never could.”* Another noted: *“The plunge base is smooth and the depth adjustment is spot-on. I used it for dovetail hinges and didn’t need to rework a single mortise.”* The kit value also earns praise — buying the bases separately would cost about 60% more.

**Where It Falls Short**

The collet wrench is the weak link. It’s thin stamped steel, and several reviewers — including me — found it prone to stripping when you really crank down on a bit. Buy a proper 3/4-inch collet wrench immediately. Also, the dust port on the fixed base is small — about 1 inch — and clogs quickly with MDF dust. You’ll want a vacuum adapter with a larger hose or you’ll be stopping to clear it constantly. The plunge base has no dust port at all, so expect dust everywhere on plunge cuts.

One more thing: the on/off switch is a rocker on the motor body. It’s not a paddle trigger, so you can’t switch it off mid-cut with a finger. That’s a safety consideration if you’re used to trigger-style routers.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Cabinet makers and trim carpenters** who need precise edge work and hinge mortises in tight spaces.
- **DIYers upgrading from a trim router** who want the brushless longevity and the dual-base versatility without buying two tools.
- **Anyone with a dust collection setup** — it works fine with a shop vac, just plan for the port size.

Skip it if you only need a trim router for occasional roundovers and already own a fixed base version — the upgrade cost isn’t justified. Also skip it if you’re left-handed; the switch placement favors right-hand use.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this kit work with 1/2-inch bits?**  
No. It’s a 1/4-inch collet only. For 1/2-inch shank bits, you need a full-size router.

**Q: Can I use it in a router table?**  
Technically yes, but the 5.2-inch height and palm design make it awkward. The fixed base has mounting holes, but it’s better suited for hand-held work.

**Q: How does it compare to the DeWalt DW611?**  
The Bosch has a better plunge base and LED lighting. The DeWalt has a softer-start feature and a slightly better depth adjustment mechanism. If plunge work is your priority, get the Bosch. For general edge routing, either works.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a palm router that handles hardwood without bogging down, and you value the plunge base for mortise work.

**Skip it if:** You’re on a tight budget and only need a basic trim router — the brushed version is cheaper and fine for light duty.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The brushless motor and dual-base kit make this a smart upgrade for serious woodworkers, but the flimsy wrench and clunky dust port keep it from being perfect. Buy a better wrench, plan your dust collection, and this will be your most-used tool for years.
