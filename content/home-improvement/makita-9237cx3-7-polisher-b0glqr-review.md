If you're serious about paint correction, you already know the difference between a polisher and a toy. The Makita 9237CX3 is the former — a legitimate rotary machine that's been the default choice for professional detailers for over a decade. The question isn't whether it works; it's whether it's the right tool for *your* garage.

## What's in the Box

The 9237CX3 ships with the polisher body, a 7-inch backing pad, a 7-inch wool bonnet, the side handle, and a wrench. No case, no spare brushes, no instructional DVD — just the essentials. The included wool pad is decent for heavy cutting but you'll want a foam pad kit (and a 5-inch backing plate) for finishing work. Budget for that if you're starting from scratch.

## Performance and Build Quality

This is a 10-amp, 600–3,500 RPM variable-speed rotary. The numbers don't tell the whole story. The soft-start feature is the standout: the motor ramps up smoothly instead of snapping to full torque, which means you won't sling compound across the panel or gouge clear coat on your first pass. The speed dial is stepless and precise — you can dial in exactly 1,200 RPM for compounding, drop to 800 for glazes.

The motor holds its speed under load. On a 7-inch wool pad with heavy cutting compound, most rotaries bog down and stall on curved panels. This one grunts through it. The spiral bevel gears run quiet and cool, and the labyrinth seal genuinely keeps dust out — one reviewer noted, *"I've used this daily in a body shop for 3 years and it still runs like new."* That's consistent with what I've seen in pro shops.

## What Buyers Praise

Owners consistently highlight three things: durability, smoothness, and power. One 4.6-star review said, *"This is the only polisher I trust on single-stage paint. The variable speed is buttery and the trigger lock is actually usable."* Another noted, *"I've burned through two cheaper rotaries in a year. This one has done 40+ cars and hasn't missed a beat."* The consensus: it's a buy-once, cry-once tool.

## Where It Falls Short

No tool is perfect. At 5.7 pounds, the 9237CX3 is heavier than it looks, and the weight sits forward. After 30 minutes of buffing a hood, your forearms will remind you to take a break. The stock side handle is also a weak point — it's a thin, hard plastic tube that gets slippery when compound splatters. Most serious users buy a rubberized aftermarket handle.

The cord exit is another gripe. There's no rubber boot where the cable meets the housing, so if you wrap the cord tightly around the tool for storage, you risk fraying it over time. It's a simple fix (heat-shrink tubing works), but it's an oversight on an otherwise pro-grade unit.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Professional detailers and body shops**: This is your workhorse. It'll pay for itself in a month.
- **Serious hobbyists** with a garage and a car worth protecting: If you're ready to move beyond DA polishers for heavy correction, this is the step-up tool.
- **Anyone with single-stage or classic paint**: The rotary action handles oxidation removal far better than any dual-action unit.

Skip it if you're a weekend warrior doing a once-a-year wax — a dual-action polisher is safer and easier to learn. The 9237CX3 has a learning curve; rotary tools can burn through clear coat if you linger in one spot.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this safe for beginners?**
A: No. Rotary polishers require practice. Start on a scrap panel or a beater car. The soft-start helps, but technique is everything.

**Q: Can I use a 5-inch backing plate on this?**
A: Yes. Many users swap to a 5-inch plate for tighter control on bumpers and pillars. The spindle is 5/8-11 UNC, so it accepts standard aftermarket plates.

**Q: Does it come with a case?**
A: No. It ships in a cardboard box with the tool, pad, wool bonnet, side handle, and wrench. You'll want a tool bag or case for storage.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a professional-grade rotary that will outlast your car, and you're willing to master the technique. **Skip it if** you need a beginner-friendly polisher or want a lightweight tool for frequent overhead work.

The Makita 9237CX3 earns its 4.6-star rating. It's not the cheapest rotary, and it's not the lightest. But it is the most reliable, most consistent 7-inch polisher under $200, and it's the one I'd recommend to anyone who wants to stop buying replacements.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A pro-tier tool with minor ergonomic flaws that don't outweigh its durability and performance.
