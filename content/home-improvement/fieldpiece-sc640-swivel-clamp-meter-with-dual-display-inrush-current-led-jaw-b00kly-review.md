Most clamp meters make you choose between a compact body and a jaw that actually fits around fat conductors. The Fieldpiece SC640 tries to solve that with a head that swivels a full 180 degrees. It's not a gimmick — it changes how you work in tight breaker panels, but it comes with a few tradeoffs worth knowing before you spend your money.

**What's in the box**

You get the SC640, a pair of TL4 test leads with silicone insulation, a K-type thermocouple bead probe for temperature readings, and a soft carrying pouch. The meter runs on two AA batteries — not included, which is mildly annoying at this price point. The pouch is basic but functional. The leads are decent quality; silicone stays flexible in cold weather, which matters if you work outside in winter months.

**Build quality and daily driver feel**

The housing is tough ABS with a rubber overmold that gives a solid grip. It's IP40 rated — fine for dry indoor work, but don't dunk it or leave it out in rain. The jaw opens to 1.18 inches, which handles most residential and light commercial conductors. The swivel mechanism is the star feature. It clicks into detents every 45 degrees, so you can orient the display toward your face while the jaw wraps around a wire at an awkward angle. After a week of using it, you'll wonder why every clamp meter doesn't do this.

The dual display is genuinely useful. You can watch voltage and current simultaneously — essential when you're checking a motor's draw against its nameplate rating while the system is running. Inrush mode captures the initial surge on startup, which is critical for diagnosing locked compressors or failing capacitors. The 100ms response catches spikes that cheaper meters miss.

**What buyers consistently praise**

With 240 ratings at 4.8 stars, the feedback is overwhelmingly positive. One reviewer noted, "The swivel head is a lifesaver in tight panels — I don't have to contort my arm to read the screen anymore." Another mentioned the dual display: "Being able to see volts and amps at the same time saves me an extra step on every call." Multiple HVAC techs specifically call out the inrush function as accurate and reliable for diagnosing compressor start issues.

**Where it falls short**

No magnet. For a meter in this class, that's a real miss. You'll be setting it down on ladders, ductwork, or whatever surface is available. You can buy a magnetic hanger separately, but it's an extra purchase and an extra thing to lose.

The backlight button sits flush with the case and requires a firm press. With work gloves on, you'll often hit the wrong button or need two tries. It's a minor frustration, but it happens every time you work in a dim mechanical room.

The temperature probe is a nice inclusion, but it's a slow response type. If you're doing serious HVAC delta-T measurements, get a dedicated thermocouple. The included one is fine for spot checks, not precision work.

**Who should buy this**

- **HVAC and refrigeration techs** — the inrush mode and dual display are directly useful for motor diagnostics
- **Electricians doing residential service work** — the swivel jaw and CAT IV 600V rating cover most panel work
- **Facility maintenance pros** — the combination of functions replaces two separate meters in your bag

**Skip it if** you're a hobbyist doing occasional automotive or home electrical work — a basic meter at half the price covers your needs. Also skip it if you need a backlit display with glove-friendly buttons; this one requires a deliberate press.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the swivel jaw durable over time?**
A: The detent mechanism is robust. Multiple long-term owners report no loosening after a year of heavy use. The jaw is spring-loaded and snaps back to closed position reliably.

**Q: Can it measure DC current?**
A: Yes, it handles DC amps up to 600A, plus AC amps. It also reads capacitance, frequency, resistance, and temperature — it's a full multimeter plus clamp.

**Q: Is it true RMS?**
A: Yes, and that matters when you're reading non-linear loads like VFDs or electronic ballasts. The readings stay accurate on distorted waveforms.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you work on HVAC systems or do electrical service calls where tight panels and motor startups are daily reality. The swivel jaw and dual display are worth the premium over cheaper meters.

**Skip it if** you only need occasional voltage checks or you're on a tight budget — the extra features won't earn their keep.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely useful tool with a couple of minor annoyances that don't outweigh its strengths.
