# Narex Richter Bevel Edge Chisel Set of 5

If you're a woodworker who's been limping along with a mixed bag of garage-sale chisels or the entry-level Narex standard line, the Richter set is the upgrade you've been circling. This Czech-made set sits in a sweet spot: it delivers 80% of the performance of boutique brands (Lie-Nielsen, Veritas) at roughly half the price. But "80%" still means there's work to do before these are truly furniture-grade tools.

## What's in the Box

The set covers the essential dovetail and joinery sizes: 1/4", 3/8", 1/2", 3/4", and 1". Each chisel has a chromium-vanadium steel blade (slightly harder than the standard Narex's 61-62 HRC), a brass ferrule, and an octagonal beech handle that's comfortable for both pushing and mallet work. The blades are 4.5 inches long — enough reach for most joinery, though not a bench chisel length.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the Richter separates from the pack. The backs arrive *flat*. Not "mostly flat" like most chisels under $50 each — genuinely flat. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: "I've flattened Lie-Nielsens that needed more work than these out of the box." That's a massive time-saver because back flattening is the single most miserable task in chisel setup.

After a quick lap on 1000-grit and a polish on 8000-grit, the edge takes a scary-sharp polish. The Cr-V steel is noticeably harder than the standard Narex line — edge retention feels roughly 30-40% longer between honing sessions, which matters when you're cutting dovetails across a full board. The 25° factory bevel is usable, but I'd recommend re-grinding to 25° primary with a 30° micro-bevel for better edge stability.

The octagonal handles deserve their own mention. They provide positive indexing in your hand — you always know the orientation of the bevel without looking. That's a small thing that becomes huge when you're paring half-blind dovetails.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reviewers across 229 ratings (4.6 average) keep landing on the same themes. One long-time user said: "I own Veritas PM-V11 chisels too. These get 90% of the work done for 50% of the price." Another noted the steel quality: "Holds an edge far better than my old Marples — I'm reaching for these every time now."

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The blades arrive with a working edge but not a honed one — expect 10-15 minutes per chisel on stones before first use. The handles are functional but plain; no polished finish or exotic wood here. If you're buying these as a gift for a woodworker, they'll appreciate the tool, not the aesthetics. And the 1/4" chisel is slightly thicker behind the edge than ideal for ultra-tight dovetail pins — manageable, but not perfect.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Furniture makers and serious hobbyists** who want premium performance without Lie-Nielsen prices
- **Dovetail and joinery specialists** who need flat backs and edge retention for repetitive cuts
- **Anyone upgrading from the standard Narex line** — the steel and finish improvements justify the price jump

Skip it if you're a beginner who doesn't yet know how to flatten a chisel back, or if you need show-grade handles. For pure function-per-dollar, this set is hard to beat.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these need to be flattened out of the box?**
A: The backs are remarkably flat — most users report minimal lapping needed. But you should still polish the backs and hone the bevels before first use. Budget about 15 minutes per chisel.

**Q: Can these take mallet strikes?**
A: Yes. The steel is hardened to 61-62 HRC, which is tough enough for mallet work without chipping. The brass ferrule prevents handle splitting. Just use a wood or leather mallet, not metal.

**Q: How do these compare to the regular Narex chisels?**
A: The Richter line uses better steel (Cr-V vs. standard), has flatter backs, and better handle ergonomics. The edge holds noticeably longer. It's worth the premium if you do serious joinery.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want near-premium chisel performance without paying $60+ per chisel. **Skip it if** you need show-piece aesthetics or want tools that are razor-ready with zero setup.
