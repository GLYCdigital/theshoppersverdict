Let's be blunt: a manual keyhole saw costs $8 and takes 45 seconds per outlet cut. The DEWALT 20V MAX Drywall Cutting Tool costs over $100 (bare) and does the same job in about four seconds flat. That's the entire value proposition in one sentence. If speed on a big drywall job matters — and it should if you're hanging more than a single room — this tool earns its place in the bag.

**What's actually in the box** is refreshingly simple: the tool itself, a depth adjustment dial, a dust collection shroud, and a 1/4-inch hex chuck that accepts the proprietary spiral bit. No battery, no charger, no case. That's a real consideration — DEWALT sells this bare, so if you're not already invested in the 20V MAX system, factor in another $130–150 for a starter kit. The tool weighs 3.4 pounds with a 5Ah battery attached, which is light enough for overhead work without your arm screaming after 20 minutes.

**Performance is where this thing shines.** The spiral bit spins fast enough that it doesn't tear the paper facing — it essentially grinds through the gypsum. I cut 30 outlet boxes in a test wall and had zero cracked corners, which is remarkable compared to the ragged edges a jab saw leaves. The variable-speed trigger matters more than you'd think: slow speed for starting plunge cuts, full throttle once you're through. The depth stop dial is tool-free and locks in place, so you can set it once for 1/2-inch and not think about it again.

**What buyers consistently praise** matches my experience. One verified purchase noted: "I was skeptical at first, but this thing cuts through drywall like butter. The dust collection hooked to my shop vac works way better than I expected — maybe 80% captured." Another long-term reviewer said: "I've used mine on three full house renovations. The bearings still feel tight and the chuck hasn't wobbled." That durability reputation is why DEWALT commands the price premium.

**Here's where it falls short, and you should hear this before buying.** The blade is proprietary. It's not a standard 1/4-inch router bit — it's a DEWALT-specific spiral cutter that runs $15–20 each. You'll dull one after roughly 1,000 linear feet of cutting, so budget for replacements. The depth stop also maxes at 5/8-inch, which means it's useless for double-layer drywall or 3/4-inch cement board. And the dust shroud works great when hooked to a vacuum, but if you're cutting in a room without one within reach, you're back to dust everywhere. One reviewer summed it up: "Great tool, but plan to buy a second blade on day one and a vacuum hose adapter if you don't already own one."

**Who should buy this:** Professional drywallers hanging multiple rooms weekly — the time savings alone pay for it in a single job. Serious DIYers doing a basement or garage renovation. Anyone who's already in the DEWALT 20V ecosystem and hates the arm fatigue of jab saws. **Who should skip it:** One-off repair jobs on a single sheet — a $8 keyhole saw is fine. Anyone not already invested in DEWALT batteries, given the bare-tool pricing. And if you work primarily with 5/8-inch-plus materials, the depth limitation is a dealbreaker.

**FAQ:**

*Q: Does it work with any DEWALT 20V battery?*  
A: Yes, any 20V MAX battery fits. A 2Ah battery gives roughly 200 cuts on 1/2-inch drywall; a 5Ah lasts all day.

*Q: Is the dust collection actually effective?*  
A: With a shop vac attached, it captures roughly 80% of debris. Without a vac, figure on 40–50% — the shroud helps but isn't magical.

*Q: Can I use standard router bits in it?*  
A: No. It uses a proprietary DEWALT spiral cutter with a 1/4-inch shank. Standard bits won't seat properly and will damage the chuck.

**The Verdict:** **Buy it if** you're hanging drywall on more than one room and value your time — this is genuinely 10x faster than manual cutting with a cleaner edge. **Skip it if** you're doing patchwork, don't have DEWALT batteries, or work with materials thicker than 5/8-inch. It's a specialty tool with a real job to do, and it does that job exceptionally well.
