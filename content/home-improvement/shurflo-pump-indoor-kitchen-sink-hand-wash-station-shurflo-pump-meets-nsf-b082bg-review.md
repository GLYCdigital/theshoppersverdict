## What's Actually in the Box

You get the complete assembly: a 5-gallon fresh water tank, a grey water catch basin, the sink bowl itself, a foot-operated pump, and all necessary hoses and fittings. The frame is powder-coated steel that feels sturdier than the price suggests. As shown in the product image, the tank sits above the sink, so gravity assists the pump — that matters when you're refilling at a job site without a dedicated water hookup.

The footprint is roughly 20 inches deep by 16 inches wide, which slides into tight trailer corners or behind a tailgate. It's not lightweight (about 25 pounds empty), but it's manageable for one person to move between stations.

## Real-World Performance

The foot pump is the star here. Press down and you get a steady stream — release and it stops immediately. No handles to touch, no cross-contamination between washing hands and turning knobs. That's a big deal for health inspectors who watch how you operate, not just what you've installed.

Flow rate is adequate: roughly 0.5 gallons per minute, which gives you a solid rinse without wasting your freshwater supply. That 5-gallon tank translates to 50-60 hand washes before a refill — enough for a full service day at a moderate-volume setup.

The NSF certification on the tank and components is the differentiator. Several buyers noted their inspectors signed off immediately once they saw the certification tag. One reviewer wrote, "Health department walked through, saw this unit, and didn't even ask questions. Worth every penny for that alone."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the pump. It's a diaphragm pump, which means it needs priming when the tank drops below a certain level or sits unused. You'll hear the pump running but see no water — then suddenly it catches and sprays. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's annoying mid-service.

Temperature is the bigger limitation. No heating element means you're washing with whatever temperature the water was when you filled the tank. In cooler months, that's cold water. Plan accordingly or budget for a separate inline heater.

Assembly is doable with basic tools, but the instructions are minimalist. Expect to spend 30-45 minutes figuring out hose routing. A couple of reviewers mentioned leaking connections at the tank outlet — check those fittings before first use.

One more thing: the grey water tank is open-topped, not sealed. That's fine for most applications, but if your local code requires a sealed waste tank, this won't pass.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You run a food truck, concession trailer, or farmers market booth that needs a compliant, portable hand-washing station. It's also solid for camping setups where you want a real sink experience, or for a workshop where you need quick hand hygiene without plumbing.

**Skip it if:** You need hot water, a sealed waste containment system, or a permanent installation. This is a portable solution, not a replacement for a plumbed sink.
