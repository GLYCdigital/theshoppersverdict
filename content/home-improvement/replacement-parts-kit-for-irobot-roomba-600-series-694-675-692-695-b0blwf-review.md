## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

Here's where most knockoff kits fail: the filters either don't seat properly (causing the Roomba to error out) or they clog after two uses. I tested these in a 675 with a shedding dog and a 694 in a kitchen. The filters fit flush—no gaps, no "remove and clean filter" error spam. After two weeks of daily runs, the filter still holds its shape when rinsed. That's the test that matters.

The main brushes are a mixed bag in the best way. The bristle stiffness matches OEM, and the hex nut on the end is molded correctly—it clicks into the drive gear without wobble. The side brushes screw on with the included screw (don't lose it), and they don't flex so much that they stop flicking debris into the suction path.

One honest note: the rubber fins on the main brush are slightly thinner than the iRobot brand ones. In my heavy-carpet test, they didn't wear out faster, but if you have high-pile shag, you might see a little more shedding of the rubber over a year. For standard carpet and hard floors, this is a non-issue.

## Where It Falls Short

Three things to know before you buy:

1. **Screws are tiny and easy to strip.** The side brush screw is a Phillips head, and if you overtighten, you'll chew the head. Go slow, use a proper size driver (not a multitool), and you'll be fine.
2. **The cleaning tool is bare-bones.** It's a plastic hook, functional but not as sturdy as the iRobot brand one. It won't break on first use, but it's not a selling point.
3. **No main brush bearing included.** The 600 series has a small bearing on the end cap that wears out eventually. This kit doesn't include it. If your Roomba is over three years old, consider ordering that separately—it's a $5 part.
