If you've ever watched a security camera feed of someone propping open a fire door and wandering off, you know the problem. The Detex EAX500 doesn't solve that problem with cameras or cloud subscriptions. It solves it with a 95-decibel siren and a simple principle: when that door opens, the whole building knows.

This is a purpose-built exit alarm for one specific job — securing doors that must remain accessible for emergency egress but shouldn't be used as a casual exit. Think back doors of retail stores, receiving entrances, or any door where inventory tends to walk out.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the alarm unit, a surface-mount backplate, mounting screws, and a 9V battery. The unit measures roughly 10.5 x 3 x 2 inches — compact enough to mount on the door frame or directly on the door itself without looking obtrusive. The front face has a membrane keypad for arming and disarming, plus a red LED status light that's visible from across a room.

Installation takes about ten minutes. The backplate screws to the surface, the alarm snaps on, and the battery connects. There's no wiring, no transformer, no licensed electrician. One person with a drill and a screwdriver handles the whole job.

**How it performs in real use**

The siren is the headline feature, and it delivers. At 95dB, it's comparable to a smoke detector on steroids — loud enough to hear through a closed commercial door and across a moderate-sized warehouse. Multiple Amazon reviewers confirm this. One writes, "Tested it on the back door of our shop — heard it clearly from the front counter with two doors between us."

The alarm triggers when the door opens while armed. It also has a tamper switch that fires the siren if someone tries to pry the unit off the wall. That's a thoughtful touch for a device that's often mounted in low-traffic areas where someone could work on it undisturbed.

Battery life is the other strong suit. Detex claims up to 24 months on a standard 9V, and customer reports generally support that. Several reviewers mention replacing batteries after 18-24 months of daily use. There's a low-battery indicator LED that flashes to warn you before the unit goes dead, which is critical — a silent exit alarm is just a plastic box on the wall.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The reliability gets steady shout-outs. One reviewer sums it up: "Installed three of these on emergency exits three years ago. Zero false alarms, zero failures. The batteries are the only maintenance." Another notes the deterrent effect: "Employees stopped using the back door for smoke breaks within a week. The alarm works."

The keypad arming is also well-received. It takes about three seconds to enter the code, and the unit gives a short chirp to confirm arming status. That's fast enough that authorized staff won't be tempted to disable it permanently.

**Where it falls short**

The 5-minute alarm duration is the biggest pain point. Once triggered, the siren blares for a full five minutes unless you have the key. If someone opens the door accidentally — a delivery driver, a confused visitor — you're listening to that siren for a long time. The key override is the only way to silence it early, and the included barrel key is small enough to lose in a desk drawer.

The plastic housing is another consideration. It's impact-resistant and fine for normal use, but this isn't a heavy-duty unit for high-abuse environments like schools or correctional facilities. If you need something that can take a beating, you're looking at Detex's metal-bodied models at twice the price.

**Who should buy this**

This alarm shines for small to mid-sized businesses. Retail shops with back doors, auto repair garages, storage units, offices with employee-only entrances — these are the sweet spot. It's also a solid option for landlords securing basement or utility room doors in multi-unit buildings.

It's overkill for a home interior door, but for an exterior door on a workshop or detached garage, it works well. The deterrent value alone often solves the problem — most people won't touch a door with a visible alarm unit.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Can this be used on a door that must stay unlocked during business hours?**
Yes. The alarm arms and disarms with the keypad. Leave it disarmed during open hours, arm it when you close. The tamper switch still works when disarmed, so someone ripping it off the wall triggers the siren regardless.

**Does it work with a door closer or panic bar?**
It works with any standard door. Mount it on the door itself or the frame — both configurations are supported with the included hardware. It does not interface with panic bars or electronic access control systems.

**What happens when the battery dies?**
The low-battery LED flashes for several weeks before failure. When the battery is completely dead, the alarm simply won't arm — it won't fail in a way that locks a door or traps someone inside.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a loud, reliable, no-wiring exit alarm for a commercial or semi-commercial door and you don't want to pay an electrician for a hardwired system. It installs in minutes, runs for two years on one battery, and the 95dB siren does exactly what it promises.

**Skip it if** you need a metal-bodied alarm for a high-abuse environment, or if you can't tolerate the 5-minute siren duration on accidental triggers. Also skip it if you're looking for a smart alarm with phone notifications — this is deliberately simple, and that's the point.

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews is earned. It's a single-purpose tool that does its job without fuss. For the price, there's nothing else on the market that matches its combination of simplicity, loudness, and battery life.
