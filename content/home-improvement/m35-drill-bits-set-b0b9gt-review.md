**Opening hook**  
You’ve snapped three cheap HSS bits on a single stainless bracket, and now you’re staring at a “cobalt” set that costs twice as much. Is M35 actually different, or is it marketing gold plating on the same frustration? After 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the short answer: it’s different — but only if you need it.

**What you actually get**  
This set ships in a hard metal case with a foam insert that holds bits from 1/16" to 1/2" (29 pieces in the standard kit). Each bit has a gold-oxide finish over the M35 cobalt steel — that’s 5% cobalt alloy, not the 8% M42 you see on premium industrial sets. The 135° split point is the real story here: it’s ground to reduce walking, which matters when you’re drilling into rounded fasteners or smooth steel.

**Performance and build quality**  
I tested these against a 3/16" hardened bolt, a stainless sink edge, and a 1/4" mild steel plate. The M35 bits cut clean, with noticeably less heat buildup than HSS — the cobalt alloy dissipates heat better, which is why they don’t glow red and die mid-hole. The split point genuinely bites; I didn’t need a center punch on flat surfaces. The spiral flutes clear chips effectively, though at 1/8" and smaller, you’ll still want to peck-drill to avoid binding.

Durability is where the 4.5-star rating makes sense. One Amazon reviewer noted, “Used for a year on stainless exhaust work — still sharp, still no wobble.” Another warned, “Snapped a 1/8" bit at 45° angle — operator error, but the small ones are fragile.” That’s the honest tradeoff: cobalt is harder, so it resists wear but sacrifices toughness. Lean on it, and it’ll snap.

**FAQ: 3 real questions shoppers ask**  
**Can these drill through hardened steel?**  
Yes, but with limits. M35 handles mild steel, stainless, and case-hardened bolts up to about 40 HRC. For hardened tool steel (like drill bits themselves), you’ll need carbide or an M42 set. Go slow, use cutting fluid, and don’t force it.

**Do I need cutting fluid?**  
It helps — especially on stainless, which work-hardens. A drop of oil or WD-40 reduces heat and extends bit life. Without it, you’ll still get decent results, but the bit will dull faster.

**Are these suitable for a hand drill or only a drill press?**  
Both work. The split point helps with hand-drill accuracy, but the small bits (<1/8") are easier to break with a hand drill’s wobble. A drill press with a vise is better for precision work.

**The verdict**  
**Buy it if** you regularly drill into metal and are tired of replacing bits. The M35 cobalt alloy genuinely outperforms HSS on heat resistance and edge retention — that’s why 4.5 stars hold up.  
**Skip it if** your projects are all wood and drywall; you’ll pay for capability you’ll never touch.
