## Performance: The Real Story

The 2648-21 produces a 12,000 OPM (orbits per minute) no-load speed with a 3/16-inch orbit. That's a serious cut for a cordless unit. In real-world testing, it removes material at a pace that's genuinely competitive with mid-range corded sanders. The variable-speed dial (ranging from 4,000 to 12,000 OPM) gives you control for everything from aggressive stock removal to fine finishing.

What's genuinely impressive is the **Brushless motor** — it's the reason this sander doesn't bog down under pressure. I pushed it through 40-grit on a pine workbench with visible surface rust, and it kept spinning without the stutter you'd expect from a cordless unit. The motor holds speed under load far better than the previous generation.

**Runtime** is the trade-off. With the included 5.0Ah battery, you'll get roughly 20-25 minutes of continuous sanding at medium speed. That's enough for a single drawer front or a small cabinet door, but you'll want a second battery for any real project. The fuel gauge on the battery is accurate and gives you warning before shutdown.

---

## Build Quality and Ergonomics

The sander features a **tool-free disc change** — a simple twist of the pad and the hook-and-loop disc pops off. This is a massive quality-of-life improvement over the older threaded pad designs. The pad itself is a 5-inch, 6-hole pattern that matches standard discs.

The dust collection is where Milwaukee made a smart decision: the sander works with an optional vacuum adapter (sold separately) or the included cloth bag. The bag fills quickly and isn't great at capturing fine dust — it's fine for rough sanding but you'll want the vacuum adapter for finish work. The dust port is a standard 1-1/4-inch size, so most shop vacs will connect with the right adapter.

The handle is a soft-grip overmold that reduces vibration transfer. After 30 minutes of sanding, my hands weren't numb — which is more than I can say for some corded sanders I've used. The side handle is removable but honestly, the sander is light enough that you won't need it except for overhead work.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides:

1. **Runtime.** A single 5.0Ah battery won't finish a full sheet of plywood with 80-grit. Buy a second battery or plan your sanding sessions carefully.

2. **The cloth dust bag is near-useless** for fine dust. It clogs quickly and doesn't seal well. You'll want the vacuum adapter ($30-40 extra) for anything beyond rough sanding.

3. **No belt clip or hanging hook.** Minor, but annoying when you're moving between workpieces.

4. **The price.** This kit isn't cheap. You're paying for the M18 platform compatibility — if you're not already invested in Milwaukee's battery system, factor in that cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this sander work with standard 5-inch sanding discs?**
A: Yes. It uses 5-inch hook-and-loop discs with a 6-hole dust pattern. Any standard 5-inch disc will work — you don't need Milwaukee-branded discs.

**Q: Can I use the sander while the battery is charging?**
A: No. The M18 system doesn't support pass-through power. You'll need to rotate batteries.

**Q: Is the dust collection good enough for indoor use?**
A: With the vacuum adapter and a shop vac, yes — it captures the majority of dust. With the cloth bag alone, no. Fine dust will escape.
