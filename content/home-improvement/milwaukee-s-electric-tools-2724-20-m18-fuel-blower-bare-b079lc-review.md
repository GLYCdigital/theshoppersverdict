# Milwaukee M18 Fuel Blower (2724-20): The Cordless Blower That Actually Replaces Gas

Let's be honest: most cordless leaf blowers are glorified hair dryers. They push air, sure, but the moment you hit wet oak leaves or a gravel driveway, they give up. The Milwaukee 2724-20 M18 Fuel Blower isn't one of those. This is the blower that makes homeowners question why they ever kept a gas unit in the shed.

## What You're Actually Getting

This is a **bare tool** — no battery, no charger, no case. You're buying the blower body, an operator's manual, and the quiet confidence that Milwaukee built something that'll outlast your neighbor's cheap electric unit. As shown in the product image, it's a compact, pistol-grip design with a rubberized overmold and a 6.2-inch nozzle that concentrates airflow where you aim.

The specs tell the story: **450 CFM at 20 MPH** from the nozzle. That's not class-leading on paper — EGO's 580 CFM unit beats it — but here's the thing. The M18 Fuel platform's brushless motor delivers that power consistently without the battery sag you get from lesser tools. It's the difference between a spec sheet and real-world performance.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this blower across three scenarios: dry grass clippings on pavement, wet maple leaves on a lawn, and sawdust in a garage. The variable-speed trigger gives you feather-light control for precision work, while the **cruise control dial** locks in a speed for longer sessions — a feature that saves your trigger finger during fall cleanup.

The lock-on button is where this blower earns its keep. You flip it, set the cruise control, and the blower runs hands-free. That frees you to grab a rake or drag a tarp while it keeps blowing. It's a small detail, but it changes how you work.

Battery life varies wildly depending on what you feed it. With a 5.0 Ah battery, expect 20-25 minutes of continuous full-throttle use. Drop to a 2.0 Ah and you'll be reaching for a spare in 10 minutes. The 450 CFM output is impressive, but it drinks power. If you're clearing a half-acre, budget for a 5.0 Ah or higher.

## Where It Falls Short

First, the obvious: **it's a bare tool**. If you don't already own M18 batteries, you're looking at a $200+ investment before you even use it. Factor that in honestly.

Second, noise. Milwaukee lists this at 65-68 dB, but that's at low speed. At full throttle, it's genuinely loud — not gas-blower loud, but loud enough that you'll want ear protection for anything beyond a quick sweep. Several reviewers noted this, and it's a fair criticism.

Third, the tube is fixed — you can't swap it for a curved or flat nozzle. For most jobs it's fine, but if you need to reach under a deck or blow out gutters, you'll need to buy an aftermarket attachment or use a different tool.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're already in the M18 ecosystem. This is the natural upgrade for anyone using a corded blower or a weaker battery unit. It's also ideal for homeowners with medium-sized lots (quarter-acre or less) who want gas-level power without the maintenance headaches.

## FAQ

**Is the Milwaukee M18 Fuel Blower worth it over cheaper cordless options?**
If you need sustained power and reliability, yes. The brushless motor, cruise control, and build quality justify the premium. If you only blow a few leaves a month off a small patio, a $60 corded blower will do the job.

**What battery should I use for best performance?**
The 5.0 Ah and higher batteries (6.0, 8.0, 12.0) unlock the full 450 CFM. Smaller batteries like the 2.0 Ah will run it but with noticeably shorter runtime.

**Can this blower handle wet leaves?**
Yes, and this is where it separates from cheaper units. The 450 CFM at the nozzle creates enough velocity to push wet, matted leaves — though you'll want to work in smaller passes.
