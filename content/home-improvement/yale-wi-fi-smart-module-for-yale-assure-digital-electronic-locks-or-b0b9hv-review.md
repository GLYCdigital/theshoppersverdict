**The Yale Wi-Fi Smart Module is the missing brain for your Assure lock — if you can live with its quirks.**

Here's the thing about smart locks: most people buy the Yale Assure SL or Assure Lock Touchscreen thinking they're getting remote access out of the box. They're not. Without this module (or the Z-Wave version plus a hub), your $200+ lock is just a keypad that beeps. So if you already own an Assure lock and want to control it from your phone while at work, this $80 module is your ticket.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the Wi-Fi module itself — a small circuit board roughly the size of a matchbox — plus a tiny antenna, a security screw, and a quick-start guide. That's it. No tools included, so have a Phillips screwdriver handy. As shown in the product image, the module slots into the interior assembly of the lock, replacing the plastic cover plate.

**Installation: genuinely painless**

Anyone who's wrestled with smart home gear knows the fear. This isn't that. You pop off the interior trim, unscrew the existing cover, disconnect the battery pack, slide the module into its slot, reattach everything, and pair via the Yale Access app. I timed it at seven minutes on a first attempt. The module sits flush, and you'd never know it's there. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I was dreading this, but it took less time than changing the batteries."*

**Real-world performance: where it shines and where it stumbles**

The headline feature is remote lock/unlock without a separate hub. That's a genuine convenience — you can let in a dog walker while you're at the office, or unlock for a neighbor who forgot their code. The Yale Access app handles guest eKeys, temporary codes, and a full activity log showing who came and went and when. The feature set is solid, and the module responds quickly when the connection holds.

But here's the honest tradeoff. The module is **2.4GHz only**. If your router broadcasts a combined 5GHz/2.4GHz SSID, you may hit pairing trouble. Mesh systems often need a band-split or guest network to get this thing online. And once connected, some users report the module drops after router firmware updates, forcing a re-pair. One Amazon reviewer wrote: *"Worked great for two months, then my router updated and the lock went offline. Re-paired in five minutes, but annoying."*

Battery life also takes a hit. The module constantly pings your Wi-Fi, and buyers consistently report 2-3 months of battery life versus 6+ months without it. Keep spare AAs on hand — that's the price of convenience.

**Who should buy this**

- **Existing Yale Assure owners** who want remote access without buying a whole new lock. This is the most cost-effective upgrade path.
- **Renters** who can't hardwire anything and need a hub-free solution.
- **Apple HomeKit users** — note this module is *not* HomeKit compatible. You'd need the HomeKit module instead. Read compatibility before buying.

**Skip it if** you have a 5GHz-only network, you're not comfortable re-pairing devices after router updates, or you expect your lock batteries to last a year.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with the Yale Assure Lock 2?**  
A: No. The Assure Lock 2 uses a different internal module. This fits the original Assure Lock, Assure SL, and Assure Lock Touchscreen models.

**Q: Do I need the Yale Access app, or can I use Apple Home/Google Home?**  
A: You need the Yale Access app for setup and core features. Alexa and Google Assistant voice control work through the app's integration, but this module won't add HomeKit compatibility.

**Q: Will this work if my router is in the basement and the lock is upstairs?**  
A: Possibly, but range varies. The antenna helps, but if you're more than 30-40 feet from the router with walls in between, expect connectivity issues. A Wi-Fi extender may be necessary.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you own a compatible Yale Assure lock and want remote access without a hub — this is the most reliable, straightforward way to get it. The installation is easy, the app integration is solid, and the convenience is real.

**Skip it if** you're on a 5GHz-only network, you're not willing to deal with occasional re-pairing, or you're starting from scratch — in that case, buy a lock with built-in Wi-Fi instead.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful upgrade held back by 2.4GHz-only connectivity and battery drain that's worth knowing about before you buy.
