**The Ryobi 18V ONE+ Drill Driver bundle is the boring, practical choice — and that's exactly why it's one of the best-rated drill kits on Amazon at 4.8 stars from 240 buyers.**

Let me be direct: this isn't a flashy brushless flagship. It's a workhorse. And for 90% of homeowners, DIYers, and weekend warriors, that's what you actually need.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the Ryobi 18V ONE+ Drill Driver (model P252 or similar based on the bundle), two 2.0Ah lithium-ion batteries, a charger, a double-ended bit driver, a set of drill bits, and a carrying bag. The kit shown in the product image also includes a basic screwdriver bit assortment — enough to get you through a furniture assembly or curtain rod install without a second trip to the hardware store.

The drill itself is compact — about 9 inches from chuck to tail with the battery attached — and weighs in around 3.5 pounds. That's manageable for overhead work, though you'll feel it after 20 minutes of continuous driving.

**Performance You Can Actually Feel**

Here's where this drill separates itself from cheap no-name kits: 515 in-lbs of torque. That's roughly 38% more than Ryobi's older generation drills. What does that mean in practice? You'll drive 3-inch deck screws into pressure-treated lumber without pre-drilling pilot holes. You'll bore 1-inch paddle bits through pine studs without bogging down. You'll run a 2-1/2-inch hole saw through 3/4-inch plywood — slowly, but it'll get there.

The two-speed gearbox gives you 0-400 RPM for driving screws and 0-1,500 RPM for drilling. The low speed has enough clutch settings (24 total) to avoid stripping screws in softwoods or drywall. Set it to 3-4 for drywall, 8-10 for softwood, and 15+ for hardwood — you'll learn the sweet spots within an hour of use.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Reading through the 240 verified reviews, three themes keep coming up: battery life, value, and the ONE+ ecosystem.

One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've had Ryobi tools for 6 years and this is the first one I bought as a gift for my son. He's built two sheds and a chicken coop with it. Zero issues."*

Another noted: *"The two batteries are the real selling point. I never run out mid-project because one's always charging."*

That's the smart play here. The 18V ONE+ platform includes over 300 tools — circular saws, impact drivers, leaf blowers, even a pressure washer. Buy this bundle and you're locked into a battery system that'll serve you for a decade. Ryobi's been running the same battery format since 1996, and they've committed to keeping it compatible going forward.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the weaknesses.

First, the keyed chuck. Most drills in this price range — and even cheaper ones — use keyless chucks that tighten by hand. This one requires the included chuck key. It's secured with a small metal clip so you won't lose it immediately, but if you're used to one-handed bit changes, this will feel like a step backward. It's a cost-cutting measure, and it's the most common complaint in the reviews.

Second, the 2.0Ah batteries. They're fine for light work, but if you're drilling 50+ holes in concrete or driving deck screws all afternoon, you'll burn through both packs and wait for a recharge. The charger takes about 45 minutes per battery. For heavy users, spring for the 4.0Ah or 6.0Ah versions separately.

Third, no belt clip and no magnetic bit holder. Minor, but annoying when you're on a ladder and need to set the drill down safely.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy this if: You're a homeowner tackling furniture assembly, curtain rods, basic shelving, fence repairs, or light deck work. You want one battery platform that'll cover future tool purchases. You value reliability over raw power.

Skip this if: You're a professional contractor doing 8-hour days with a drill. You need a brushless motor for maximum runtime. You want a keyless chuck and don't want to carry a chuck key. In those cases, look at Ryobi's brushless line or step up to Milwaukee or DeWalt.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this drill handle masonry or concrete?**
A: With a proper carbide-tipped masonry bit, yes — for occasional anchor holes up to 1/4 inch. For serious concrete work, get a rotary hammer instead. The 1,500 RPM top speed helps, but it's not a hammer drill.

**Q: Will these batteries work with my old Ryobi tools?**
A: Yes. All Ryobi 18V ONE+ batteries and tools are cross-compatible. That's the entire point of the platform. Your old circular saw will run on these, and these batteries will work in newer Ryobi tools.

**Q: Is this a good first drill for a beginner?**
A: Yes, genuinely. The clutch settings prevent screw stripping, the two-speed gearbox is intuitive, and the included bits cover most starter projects. You won't outgrow it for years of normal household use.
