# AceMining Upgraded Door Jammer & Sliding Patio Door Stopper Review: Does This Adjustable Security Bar Actually Work?

You've seen the hotel room videos. The water glass on the handle. The chair wedged under the knob. The AceMining Upgraded Door Jammer is the grown-up version of those DIY hacks — a deliberately over-engineered piece of metal that physically prevents a door from opening, even if someone has a key.

The premise is simple: a telescoping steel bar that wedges between your door and the floor. The execution, as I found after testing this on multiple door types, is where things get interesting.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the folded steel jammer, two rubber pads (one attached to each end), and an instruction card. That's it. No screws, no mounting hardware, no permanent installation. The unit expands from 28 inches to 48 inches, which covers everything from a standard 32-inch entry door to a 72-inch sliding patio door — though for wider sliders you'll want to angle the bar rather than run it perfectly vertical.

The construction is solid. This is 14-gauge steel, not the flimsy aluminum you find on bargain door stops. The telescoping sections lock with a threaded collar that takes about three full turns to tighten. Once locked, there's zero play in the bar.

## Performance: The Honest Test

I tested this three ways: on a solid-core front door with tile flooring, on a hollow-core interior door with hardwood, and on a sliding glass patio door with a track.

On the tile front door, the jammer was effectively immovable. I threw my shoulder into it — the door didn't budge, and the bar didn't slide. The rubber pads have a high-friction texture that grips both the door and the floor simultaneously. This is the security theater that actually works.

The sliding patio door was the surprise win. Most jammer-style products struggle here because the bar needs to wedge against the track, which creates an uneven surface. The AceMining's adjustable foot rotated and found a solid purchase on the track's edge. With the bar angled at about 15 degrees off vertical, the slider was completely locked.

Here's the catch: carpeted floors. The rubber pads can't bite into plush carpet — the bar just sinks and slides. If your primary door is on carpet, this product won't work for you. That's a hard limitation, not a nitpick.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the carpet issue, there are two real drawbacks. First, at 2.4 pounds, this isn't a featherweight. If you're a minimalist traveler, the weight and folded bulk may not justify the peace of mind. Second, the adjustment collar requires some elbow grease to tighten fully. If you have arthritis or limited hand strength, you'll struggle to get a rock-solid lock.

There's also a learning curve for sliding doors. You need to figure out the right angle and floor position so the bar doesn't interfere with the door's closing mechanism. It took me three tries to find the sweet spot on my slider.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a frequent traveler who stays in hotels or rentals, a renter who can't modify your door frame, or a homeowner with a sliding patio door that needs a second layer of defense.

**Skip it if:** Your doors are on carpet, you're looking for a permanent security solution (buy a deadbolt instead), or you need something that mounts to the wall.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this damage my door or floor?**  
The rubber pads are soft enough that they won't scratch painted doors or hardwood. On tile, you might see slight rubber marks that wipe off with a damp cloth.

**Q: Can I use this on a double-door entryway?**  
Yes, but you'll need to wedge it against the active door only. It won't secure both doors simultaneously.

**Q: How much force can it withstand?**  
The steel bar itself won't bend under human force. The weak point is the floor grip — on a solid surface, it's effectively immovable. On slick surfaces like polished concrete, it could slide under sustained force.
