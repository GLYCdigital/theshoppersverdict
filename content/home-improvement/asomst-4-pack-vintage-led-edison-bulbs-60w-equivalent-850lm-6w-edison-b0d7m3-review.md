# ASOMST 4-Pack Vintage LED Edison Bulbs: Retro Looks, Modern Performance

Each bulb is rated at 6 watts, produces 850 lumens, and runs at 2200K color temperature. That's a deep amber glow, closer to a real carbon-filament bulb than the sterile 2700K most "warm" LEDs fake.

The packaging is standard retail — a cardboard box with foam inserts. Nothing fancy, but the bulbs arrived intact in virtually every review I read. One buyer noted, "Came well-packaged, no breakage, and all four fired up instantly."

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

Here's where this set separates from the $12 four-pack junk on Amazon. The glass envelope is thick and genuinely clear — not the frosted plastic that scratches if you look at it wrong. The filament is a single vertical loop, not the fake zigzag pattern that looks cheap up close. At 850 lumens, these are noticeably brighter than most 60W-equivalent Edison bulbs, which typically land around 600-700 lumens.

Dimming is the big test for any LED claiming vintage cred. ASOMST's driver holds up. Multiple reviewers confirm smooth dimming down to low levels without the strobing or buzzing that plagues cheap units. One verified buyer wrote: "I was skeptical because my previous 'dimmable' bulbs flickered like a horror movie. These go down to about 5% with zero complaints." That matches the spec sheet — the driver is designed for leading-edge TRIAC dimmers, which covers most US household dimmer switches.

Heat is also handled properly. The base uses a ceramic heat sink, not the flimsy aluminum that transfers heat into your fixture. In enclosed fixtures, this matters — plastic-based bulbs can fail fast. These run warm but not hot, which should translate to the rated 15,000-hour lifespan.

## Where It Falls Short

The ST58 shape is bigger than a standard A19. If you have shallow wall sconces or tight pendant shades, these will poke out awkwardly. Measure your fixture depth before ordering — I saw at least one reviewer grumble about exactly this.

The amber tint isn't for everyone. At 2200K, this is candlelight territory. If you're hoping for a "warm white" that still looks white-ish, these will feel orange to you. That's the tradeoff for authentic vintage character.

Finally, dimmer compatibility isn't universal. Leading-edge TRIAC dimmers work great. If you have an older trailing-edge dimmer or a smart dimmer, check compatibility before buying. One reviewer noted their Lutron smart dimmer worked fine, but another had issues with a cheap off-brand dimmer.
