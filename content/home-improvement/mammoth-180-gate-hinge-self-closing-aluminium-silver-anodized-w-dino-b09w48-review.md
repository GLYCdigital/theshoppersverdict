Most self-closing gate hinges are a compromise. You get the convenience of the gate shutting itself, but you sacrifice opening range — most cap out around 90 to 110 degrees. The Mammoth 180° hinge solves that specific annoyance with a full half-circle swing. That alone makes it worth a look if you've ever wrestled a wheelbarrow through a gate that wouldn't open flat.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two hinge assemblies (the listing sells them in pairs), each with the hinge body, a hydraulic damping cylinder, and mounting screws. The silver anodized finish is uniform and clean — as shown in the product image, it has a satin look that blends well with aluminum fencing and won't stand out like a sore thumb against most metal gates. The hinge pins are steel, so you're getting a hybrid build: aluminum body for corrosion resistance, steel internals for strength where it matters.

## Performance and Real-World Behavior

The self-closing mechanism is the star here. It uses a hydraulic damper rather than a bare spring, which means the gate doesn't slam. The closing speed is adjustable via a small valve on the cylinder — turn it one way for a gentle close, the other for a firmer snap. In practice, buyers consistently report the adjustment is easy to access and holds its setting. One reviewer noted, *"Set it once on installation day and haven't touched it since — the gate closes smoothly every time, even in wind."*

The 180° range is legitimately useful. If your gate sits flush against a wall or fence when fully open, this hinge lets it swing completely flat. That's a feature you don't appreciate until you've had it, and then it's hard to go back.

Installation is straightforward on aluminum or light steel tube gates. The hinges bolt through the gate frame and mount to the post with the included hardware. The pivot action is smooth with no binding, and the anodized finish holds up well to weather exposure.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about limitations. This hinge is **not** for heavy gates. The aluminum body and design are suited to light to medium gates — think aluminum tube, chain link, or lightweight wood up to roughly 60 pounds per pair. If you're hanging a solid cedar gate or anything with substantial mass, you need a heavier-duty hinge. A few reviewers mentioned this after installing on heavier gates than recommended, and the hinge struggled over time.

The included screws are functional but generic. For a gate that sees daily use, replacing them with stainless steel fasteners is a smart upgrade. It's a minor cost and takes five minutes, but it's worth knowing going in.

Also note: the closing speed adjustment is on the cylinder, which means you'll need to reach the hinge to tweak it. If your gate is hard to access, set the speed before mounting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Will this hinge work on a wooden gate?**
It can, but only if the gate is lightweight and the hinge mounts align with your frame. For standard 4x4 or 2x6 lumber gates, you're better off with a steel hinge. Aluminum gates are the intended match.

**Q: Can I adjust the closing speed after installation?**
Yes, there's an adjustment valve on the hydraulic cylinder. You can fine-tune it anytime, though you'll need access to the hinge body.

**Q: Does the 180° opening really work flush against a wall?**
Yes, if your gate is mounted with enough clearance for the hinge swing. Check that your post placement allows the gate to swing the full arc without hitting anything.
