If you've ever wrestled a rusty exhaust pipe that just won't slip over the muffler inlet, you know the drill: heat it, swear at it, beat it, repeat. The Astro Pneumatic Tool 78835 Exhaust Pipe Stretcher Kit attacks that problem from a smarter angle — it cold-stretches the pipe itself so parts mate properly instead of you fighting a tapered fit. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this kit has clearly earned its place in serious home garages and pro shops.

## What's Actually in the Box

The kit arrives with ten forged steel dies ranging from 1.5 inches up to 2.75 inches, all nestled in a blow-molded case that keeps things organized. Each die has a tapered pilot that drives into the pipe end and a shoulder that flares it outward. The two largest dies (2.5" and 2.75") handle the bigger truck and SUV pipes, while the smaller sizes cover standard passenger cars. You also get the main drive shaft and a flat anvil plate — that's everything you need except the hammer.

## Performance: Where This Tool Shines

The core advantage here is cold working. You mount the die in a vise, slip the pipe over the pilot, and drive the flaring shoulder home with a hammer. The tapered design gradually expands the pipe mouth in a controlled way — no heat, no oxidation, no burned gloves. For exhaust work, that's a massive time-saver.

The dies are heat-treated, and owners consistently note they hold up to serious abuse. One buyer put it simply: "I've used this on dozens of exhaust jobs over two years and the dies still look new." The kit handles both mild steel and aluminized pipe without complaint, and the 1.75" die is the sweet spot for most aftermarket muffler connections.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: this is a hammer tool. You need a solid bench vise and a 3-pound hammer minimum to get the job done efficiently. If you're working on the ground under a car, you'll struggle to get the leverage you need — plan on pulling the pipe or dropping the exhaust section.

Thin-wall pipe is also a concern. Swing too hard on the smaller dies and you can split or distort the pipe edge. Several reviewers noted you need to work in stages, rotating the pipe and striking evenly. It's not a design flaw, but it does require technique that a beginner might not expect.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with a hydraulic press instead of a hammer?**
A: Yes, and it works beautifully. The dies are designed to handle press force, and many owners report cleaner results with a 12-ton press than with a hammer.

**Q: Will this work on stainless steel exhaust pipe?**
A: Yes, but go slower. Stainless work-hardens faster than mild steel, so use lighter strikes and more passes to avoid cracking.

**Q: Does the kit include any lubricant or cutting compound?**
A: No, but a little anti-seize or grease on the die pilot makes the job noticeably smoother — especially on the larger dies.
