Let's be honest about felt pads: most are junk. You peel, stick, and within three weeks they're either glued to your floor or curled up like a dead leaf. The X-Protector Brown Screw-on Felt Pads take a different approach — they literally screw into your furniture. That's either brilliant or terrifying, depending on how you feel about putting holes in your dining table legs. Here's the real story after digging through 229 ratings.

## What you actually get

The package contains felt pads with a metal thread insert molded into the center. You get a selection of sizes (the variation matters — check your leg diameter before ordering), plus the screws themselves. The felt is dense, not that fluffy craft-store stuff that flattens in a week. It's brown, which sounds boring until you realize it disappears against walnut, cherry, or any dark-stained furniture.

## How it performs in real use

Installation is where this divides the room. You need to drill a pilot hole — the product image shows this clearly, but some buyers still miss it. One reviewer noted: *"Took 10 minutes per leg with a cordless drill. Once on, they're not going anywhere."* That's the tradeoff. Adhesive pads take 30 seconds. These take 10 minutes. But you'll never re-stick them.

The payoff is stability. Because the pad is mechanically fastened, it doesn't shift when you slide chairs across tile. The felt thickness actually absorbs impact — you'll notice the difference on hardwood if you've been living with bare wood legs or worn-out stickies.

## What buyers consistently praise

The "set it and forget it" factor dominates the positive reviews. *"Six months in, still tight, still quiet"* is a recurring sentiment. Multiple owners of heavy pieces — china cabinets, solid oak tables, recliners — report zero movement, which adhesive pads simply can't promise at that weight. The brown color also gets specific shout-outs for matching furniture instead of looking like a white band-aid.

## Where it falls short

Let's be direct about the cons. First, this is wood-only. If your furniture has metal or plastic legs, skip this entirely — you'll be drilling holes you can't un-drill. Second, the screw head sits slightly recessed but above the felt surface. If you drag furniture aggressively for years, the felt can compress and expose the metal head to your floor. Third, the pilot hole requirement is a genuine barrier if you don't own a drill. This isn't a renter-friendly solution unless you're okay with small holes in your furniture.

## Who should buy this

- **Heavy furniture owners** — anything over 50 pounds that you move occasionally
- **Hardwood floor people** — the grip prevents scratches from sideways sliding
- **DIY-competent buyers** — if you own a drill, this is a 20-minute project
- **Skip it if** you rent, have metal legs, or want instant installation

## FAQ

**Will these work on chair legs that are already slightly tapered?**
Yes, but measure the bottom diameter carefully. The pad needs full contact with the leg end. If your leg tapers to less than the pad size, you'll need to trim — doable but not ideal.

**Do I really need to pre-drill?**
Yes. The screw is self-tapping, but hardwood will split without a pilot hole. Use a bit slightly smaller than the screw shaft. This isn't optional.

**Can I remove these later?**
The pad unscrews, leaving a small hole in the wood. If you're switching pads, you can reuse the same hole. If you're removing them permanently, wood filler handles it.

## The Verdict
