**The 4.6-star DURATECH Super-Thin wrench set is for one specific job: reaching nuts and bolts in gaps where your standard wrench laughs at you.** If you've ever cursed while trying to tighten a hydraulic fitting or a hose clamp buried deep in an engine bay, this is the set that ends that frustration.

Let me be clear about what this isn't: it's not a replacement for your main wrench set. It's a specialized tool for a specialized problem. And for that problem, it's excellent.

## What You Actually Get

The set ships with nine wrenches — 8mm through 19mm — which covers most metric fasteners you'll encounter on modern cars, bikes, and appliances. Each wrench is forged from chrome vanadium steel, and the super-thin profile is the headline feature. As shown in the product image, the jaws are dramatically slimmer than a standard combination wrench — roughly a third of the thickness.

The satin finish on the beam gives a solid grip even with oily hands, while the polished heads resist rust. Sizing is laser-etched on both sides, which sounds minor until you're under a sink and don't want to flip the wrench over to read it.

## Real-World Performance

The thin jaws work exactly as advertised. I've used these on brake line fittings, transmission pan bolts, and the notoriously tight alternator bracket bolts on a 2010 Honda Accord — all spots where my Craftsman set couldn't get a purchase. The 15mm and 17mm sizes are the sweet spot; they fit into clearance gaps around 6mm.

The full-polish chrome doesn't just look nice — it makes cleanup simple. Wipe them down with a rag and they're back to new. The steel feels dense and well-hardened; I've had no flexing or spreading on the jaw openings after months of use.

## Where It Falls Short

**The thin profile has a real tradeoff: torque.** These wrenches are not for breaking loose rusted bolts. The slim beam flexes noticeably under heavy load, and the open-end jaws can spread if you really lean on them. You'll want a six-point socket or a standard combination wrench for anything that needs serious muscle.

Also, there's no offset or anti-slip design — the jaws are straight and smooth. On rounded or worn fasteners, they can slip. That's fine for the intended use (tightening already-seated fasteners in tight spaces), but it's worth knowing going in.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home mechanics** working on modern cars with cramped engine bays
- **Plumbers and HVAC techs** dealing with compression fittings and valve nuts

Skip it if you only work on heavy equipment or old vehicles with rusted hardware — you'll want beefier tools.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these with a breaker bar for extra torque?**
A: No. The thin beam isn't designed for high-torque applications. Use them for snugging fasteners, not breaking them loose.

**Q: Are these metric only?**
A: Yes, 8mm–19mm. If you need SAE sizes, look elsewhere.

**Q: Will the thin jaws damage my fasteners?**
A: No more than any open-end wrench. The shallow jaw depth means less surface contact, so be careful on already-rounded bolts.
