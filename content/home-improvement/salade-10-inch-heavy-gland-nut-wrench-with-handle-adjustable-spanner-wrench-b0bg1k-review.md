## Where It Falls Short

No tool is perfect, and this one has two honest flaws. First, the adjustment mechanism requires deliberate tightening before each use. If you forget, the jaw can slip under heavy torque — a minor annoyance that becomes a real problem when you're upside down under a sink. Second, the jaw has no protective coating. It's bare steel, which means it can score softer fittings if you're not careful. Use it on brass with a light touch, and you'll be fine.

The 10-inch length is also a double-edged sword. It's great for leverage, but it's too long for tight spaces like under a pedestal sink. For those jobs, you'll want a stubby option in your bag.
