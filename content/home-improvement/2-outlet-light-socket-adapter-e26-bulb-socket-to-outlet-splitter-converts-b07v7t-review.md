**Review Body:**

You've got a ceiling light socket doing absolutely nothing, and you're one trip to the hardware store away from paying an electrician $150 to add an outlet. Stop. This $12 adapter turns that useless E26 bulb socket into two live outlets in about ninety seconds — no wire nuts, no voltage tester, no permits.

**What you actually get**

The package contains one adapter: a brass-threaded E26 base on one end, two standard three-prong grounded outlets on the other. That's it. No instructions beyond a sticker warning you to not exceed 15 amps, which is fine because anyone who's changed a lightbulb can install this. Twist it in clockwise, tighten until snug, and you're done.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The brass threads seat firmly — no wobble, no arcing. The housing is a heat-resistant phenolic plastic rated for 125V/15A, which means it'll handle a 1,500-watt space heater on one outlet and a phone charger on the other without complaint. The outlets grip plugs tightly; you won't get that annoying sagging plug that falls out at 2 a.m.

Buyers consistently report using these in garages, attics, and closets. One reviewer noted: *"I put this in my workshop ceiling and hung two LED shop lights. Works perfectly, no sparking, and it's been up for six months."* Another used it to add an outlet above a kitchen cabinet for a Wi-Fi extender — a spot where no electrician would bother running wire.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest gotcha: you cannot run a bulb and a plug simultaneously. The adapter replaces the bulb entirely. If you need both, you're buying a different product. Also, the socket it's installed in must be controlled by a switch that you'll need to leave in the ON position. If your family flips switches out of habit, expect a "why isn't the light working" conversation.

There's also no cover plate. The exposed metal threads and wiring junction are visible if you install it flush against a ceiling. It's not dangerous, but it's not pretty either. In a closet or garage, nobody cares. In a living room, you will.

**Who should buy this**

- **Garage and workshop owners**: Hang it from a ceiling socket and power two LED shop lights or a battery charger.
- **Apartment renters**: Add outlets without drilling holes or modifying the unit. Unscrew it when you move out.
- **DIY home improvers**: Need a temporary outlet for a paint sprayer or sander in a room with no wall outlets.
- **Christmas light fanatics**: Two outlets for timer-controlled exterior lights, straight from a porch ceiling socket.

Skip it if you need a permanent, code-compliant outlet installed by a licensed electrician, or if you want to keep the lightbulb functional alongside the outlets.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Is this safe to use with a space heater?**
Yes, up to 1,500 watts total across both outlets. The adapter is rated for 15 amps at 125V. Don't chain extension cords or plug in another high-draw appliance on the same circuit.

**Q: Will it work in a lamp socket?**
It will thread onto any standard E26 socket, including floor and desk lamps. Just remember the lamp switch controls the outlets, and the bulb is gone.

**Q: Does it come with a cover?**
No. It's bare metal and plastic. If you want a finished look, you'll need to source a separate cover or use it in an inconspicuous location.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have an unused ceiling socket and need quick, temporary outlets for shop lights, chargers, or seasonal decorations. It's a three-minute install, handles real loads, and costs less than a pizza.

**Skip it if** you need a permanent, inspected outlet, want to keep a bulb in the socket, or require a flush, finished appearance.
