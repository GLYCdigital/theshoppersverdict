**Bottom line up front:** The Stabila 36520 24-inch electronic magnetic level is the digital level you buy when you're done replacing cheap tools. It's accurate, sealed against the elements, and built like the German engineering tank you'd expect. But at this price, it's not for everyone.

---

**What's in the box**

You get the level itself — a rigid 24-inch aluminum extrusion with a sealed digital module — plus a protective cap for the LCD and the required 9V battery. No case, no belt clip, no extras. Stabila keeps it simple. The unit weighs about 2.2 pounds and the frame is noticeably stiffer than cheaper levels, which matters when you're spanning uneven surfaces.

---

**Real-world performance**

The headline feature here is the IP65 rating. In plain English: this level survives dust, rain, and the occasional drop into a wet trench without the electronics glitching. I've used it on deck framing after a morning shower and the display stayed crisp. The sealed housing isn't just marketing — the module is fully encapsulated, so there's no seam for moisture to work into.

Accuracy is the other big story. The digital readout gives you degrees to 0.1° resolution, which is critical for setting drainage slopes or aligning machinery bases. The backlit LCD is legible in direct sun and in dim crawlspaces, a feature you don't appreciate until you're flat on your back under a sink. The bubble vials — Stabila's famously clear acrylic — remain usable even if the battery dies, which is a thoughtful redundancy.

The rare-earth magnets are genuinely strong. On steel studs or a metal door frame, the level locks in place and frees both hands for marking. The magnetic force is strong enough that you'll need a deliberate pull to remove it — that's a compliment.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The price is the first barrier — this costs 3–4 times what a decent torpedo level runs. If you only hang a shelf twice a year, that's hard to justify.

Then there's the battery door. It's secured with a screw, so swapping in a fresh 9V on site requires a screwdriver. It's a minor annoyance, but when you're on a ladder and the battery dies, it's the kind of friction you notice.

Also absent: an angle-lock or hold function. Once you remove the level from the surface, the reading disappears. For transferring an angle to a miter saw, you'll need to memorize the number or write it down. Some competitors offer this at a similar price point, and its absence here feels like a deliberate line-item cut.

**Who should buy this**

- **Pros and serious DIYers** who need repeatable, precise measurements for tile, concrete, or machinery work.
- **Anyone who frequently works outdoors or in dusty environments** — the sealed housing is a genuine durability upgrade.
- **Framers and metal stud installers** who need hands-free operation with strong magnetic grip.

**Skip it if** you're a weekend warrior doing basic hanging and leveling, or if a $30 stick level with a decent vial already covers your needs.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the digital display work if the battery dies?**
A: Yes — the bubble vials remain functional independent of the electronic module. You lose the 0.1° readout, but you can still do standard level/plumb work.

**Q: Is this level truly waterproof or just water-resistant?**
A: The IP65 rating means it's sealed against dust and low-pressure water jets. It can handle rain and splashes, but it's not designed for submersion. Don't drop it in a pond and expect it to survive.

**Q: How accurate is it compared to a standard spirit level?**
A: The digital readout resolves to 0.1° (about 1/16 inch per foot), which is roughly 10 times more precise than reading a bubble by eye. For slope work, that's the difference between "looks right" and "actually right."
