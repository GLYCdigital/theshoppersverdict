Let's be direct: most rechargeable table lamps are either cheap plastic junk that dies in an hour or overpriced designer pieces that belong in a catalog, not a working restaurant. The Yogaret Rechargeable Table Lamp sits somewhere more interesting—and for $30–40 (depending on current pricing), it earns its place on your tables.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the lamp itself, a 2A USB charging cable, and a basic manual. That's it. No wall adapter, no spare parts, no carrying case. The lamp measures roughly 4.5 inches wide at the base and stands about 11 inches tall—compact enough for standard bistro tables without blocking sightlines across the dining room.

The body is aluminum with a matte powder-coat finish, available in black or white. The base has a weighted rubber ring underneath that keeps it from sliding on tablecloths. The diffuser is frosted acrylic, not glass—which matters if you're in a high-turnover environment where things get knocked over.

**Performance and real-world use**

This is where the Yogaret earns its keep. The 2700K warm white LED is genuinely pleasant—it renders food colors accurately without casting that sterile blue-white glow you get from cheap LED candles. Three brightness levels cycle via a touch sensor on the base: low (roughly 20 lumens), medium (40), and high (60). For context, that's enough to read a menu comfortably at medium, and high works for dishes that need more presentation light.

Battery life is the headline spec. Yogaret claims 6 hours at medium brightness, 10 at low, and 3 at high. Independent testing on Amazon backs this up with one reviewer noting, "Used it for a full Saturday dinner service (5 hours) on medium, still had charge left."

The USB charging port is on the underside of the base, which keeps the lamp looking clean but means you'll need to pick it up to charge. A full charge takes about 3 hours. There's no battery indicator light—you only know it's dying when the light starts flickering. That's a minor annoyance, but in a busy service you'll learn the rhythm.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 225 ratings, the recurring themes are build quality and aesthetics. One verified purchase wrote: "These look way more expensive than they are. We bought 12 for our patio and guests keep asking where we got them." Another noted: "The light is soft but bright enough for outdoor dining at dusk. Perfect for our tapas bar."

Multiple reviewers specifically mention the weight and stability—the base doesn't tip over when someone bumps the table, which is the #1 complaint on cheaper alternatives.

**Where it falls short**

The touch controls are the weak point. They work reliably when your hands are dry, but in a restaurant setting—where servers and guests have just handled food or drinks—the sensor can be finicky. You may need to press twice to cycle brightness. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

The plastic base is another compromise. It's sturdy, but at this price point you might expect all-metal construction. The aluminum shade and stem are solid, so the lamp doesn't feel cheap overall—just the base gives slightly under pressure.

Finally, there's no auto-off timer. If you're using these for a dinner service that runs late, you'll need to manually turn each one off. For a 12-table setup, that's an extra two minutes of closing time.

**Who should buy this**

Restaurant and cafe owners looking for cordless ambient lighting that looks intentional. Also: event planners, Airbnb hosts, and anyone setting up a permanent patio dining area. If you need something rugged enough for daily commercial use with a 4.5-star track record across 225 reviews, this is a solid choice.

Skip it if you need a lamp for reading tasks (60 lumens max is too dim) or if you expect premium all-metal construction at a budget price.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can you leave it plugged in and use it as a corded lamp?**
Yes, but it's not ideal. The USB port is on the bottom, so the cable creates a slight angle. It works fine on a shelf or sideboard, but on a dinner table the cable will be visible.

**Q: How do you replace the battery?**
You don't. It's a sealed internal lithium-ion pack. The expected lifespan is 2–3 years of daily use before capacity noticeably drops. At this price, replacement is more practical than repair.

**Q: Is it waterproof?**
No. IPX4 rating means it handles splashes and light rain, but it's not rated for submersion or heavy downpours. Keep it under a patio umbrella.

**Skip it if:** You need a reading light, want all-metal construction, or can't tolerate occasional touch-sensor quirks.
