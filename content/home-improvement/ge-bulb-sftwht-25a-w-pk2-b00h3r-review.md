Let's cut the chase: you're here because a bulb in your oven, microwave, or range hood just died, and you need a replacement that actually fits and works. The GE Bulb SFTWHT 25A/W PK2 is a 25-watt, soft white incandescent appliance bulb with a candelabra (E12) base. It's a simple, old-school product that does exactly one job — and does it well.

**What you get**

Two bulbs in a blister pack, each rated at 25 watts, 120 volts, and 2700K soft white color temperature. The E12 candelabra base is the narrow screw-in type you'll find in most appliance fixtures. As shown in the product image, these are compact — roughly 3 inches long — so they slide into recessed sockets without protruding into your food or blocking airflow.

**Performance and real-world use**

This is an incandescent bulb, which means instant full brightness — no warm-up delay like some CFLs. The soft white output is genuinely pleasant, not harsh or bluish. In an oven, you'll see your food clearly while it cooks; in a range hood, it lights the cooktop without glare.

The tradeoff is lifespan. GE rates these at about 1,000 hours. In an oven that runs an hour a day, that's roughly three years — acceptable. But if your fixture vibrates (think microwave door or range hood), expect less. One buyer noted, "Burned out in about 8 months in my range hood — but honestly, that's what I expect from an incandescent." Fair point.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.3-star average across 215 ratings reflects a product that does what it says. Repeated themes in feedback:

- "Exact fit for my GE microwave — the original bulb was this same model."

That last point matters. Appliance bulbs die at 6 PM on a holiday. Having the spare on hand is worth the few extra dollars.

**Where it falls short**

Honestly, the biggest downside is the technology itself. Incandescent bulbs are inefficient — 90% of the energy becomes heat, not light. In an oven, that's not a huge deal, but in a table lamp or vanity fixture, you're wasting electricity. Also, no shatter-resistant coating means if you drop one on tile, you're sweeping up glass. And if your fixture runs on 12V (common in RVs), these won't work — you need a bulb explicitly rated for that voltage.

**Who should buy this**

- **HVAC and appliance techs** who need a reliable, known-fit replacement for service calls.
- **Homeowners with older appliances** — GE, Whirlpool, Frigidaire — that spec a 25W candelabra bulb. This is the match.
- **Anyone who prefers warm, incandescent light** in a small fixture and doesn't want to gamble on LED compatibility issues with dimmer switches or enclosed sockets.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work in a standard lamp?**
A: No. The E12 candelabra base is smaller than the standard E26 medium base. It's designed for appliances, chandeliers, and small fixtures — not table lamps.

**Q: Can I use this in my oven?**
A: Yes, if your oven's fixture is rated for a 25W bulb and uses an E12 base. Check your owner's manual to confirm wattage — exceeding 25W can damage the fixture.

**Q: Is this LED or incandescent?**
A: Incandescent. If you want LED, look for a 25W-equivalent with an E12 base — but verify it's rated for high temperatures if going in an oven.

**The Verdict**

**Skip it if** you're looking for long-term energy savings or need a shatterproof option. For those cases, a shatter-resistant LED with an E12 base is worth the extra cost — just confirm it's oven-rated.
