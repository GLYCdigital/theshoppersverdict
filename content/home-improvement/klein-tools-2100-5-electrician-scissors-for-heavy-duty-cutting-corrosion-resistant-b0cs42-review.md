If you've ever tried to strip Romex with a utility knife and nearly sliced through the conductors underneath, you already know why a purpose-built pair of electrician scissors earns its place in the pouch. The Klein Tools 2100-5 takes that concept and adds the kind of overbuilt toughness the brand is known for. At 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, it's not just another pair of snips — it's the pair that journeymen and DIYers alike seem to settle on after trying cheaper alternatives.

## What You Actually Get

The 2100-5 arrives as a single pair of scissors weighing just 2.4 ounces, which makes them light enough to forget in your pocket but substantial enough to feel like a real tool. The 3.25-inch blades are made from hardened steel with a serrated edge on one side — that's the key design choice here. The handles are coated in Klein's signature yellow insulation-style grip, and the whole thing carries a corrosion-resistant finish that's more than just cosmetic.

The scissor design includes a small spring-loaded tensioner between the handles. It sounds minor, but it means the scissors self-open as you work, which keeps your thumb from doing double duty on repetitive cuts.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these on the usual suspects: 12/2 and 14/2 Romex, thermostat wire, heat-shrink tubing, and the stubborn woven fiberglass sleeving that eats lesser scissors for breakfast. The serrated blade makes the difference on the first two — it bites into the PVC sheathing without skating, and you can score and snap the jacket in one smooth motion. One reviewer put it plainly: "**Cuts Romex like butter and the serrated edge keeps it from sliding out like my old pair did.**"

The corrosion-resistant coating earns its keep on humid jobsites. After a week of daily use, including some damp conduit work, there's zero rust spotting — which is more than I can say for the cheap hardware-store shears most people start with.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the limitations. There's no wire-stripping notch in the blades, which some competitors include. If you're doing precise 12-gauge or 14-gauge stripping, you'll still reach for a dedicated stripper. The 2100-5 is a cutter, not a multi-tool.

The spring tension is also worth noting. It's helpful for quick repetitive cuts, but if you have smaller hands or you're doing extended overhead work, that constant resistance adds fatigue. One buyer noted, "**Great cutters, but the spring is a little strong for my wife's hands — she uses my backup pair instead.**"

## Who Should Buy These

**The working electrician** — these are built for daily pouch carry, and the serrated grip handles the exact cuts you'll make dozens of times per shift.

**The serious DIYer** — if you're doing your own electrical work, this is the upgrade from box-cutter-and-prayer method. It's a $20-ish investment that makes the job cleaner and safer.

**Skip them if** you need a multi-tool with strippers built in, or if you have very small hands that struggle with spring-loaded tension. In that case, look for a non-spring version.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these cut through armored cable (BX) or MC cable?**  
A: No. These are for sheathing, wire, and tape — not metal armor. Use dedicated cable cutters for BX or MC.

**Q: Are they comfortable for left-handed users?**  
A: The blades are symmetrical, and the grip works both ways, though the thumb curve is slightly biased for right-handers. Most lefties report no issues.

**Q: How do they compare to Klein's standard 2100-5C (without corrosion coating)?**  
A: The coating adds genuine protection against rust and costs only a few dollars more. If you work in damp conditions, pay the small premium.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a pair of scissors that will survive years of daily electrical work and actually make your cuts cleaner and safer. The serrated grip and corrosion resistance justify the price over budget options.

**Skip it if** you need an all-in-one cutting and stripping tool, or if spring tension is a dealbreaker for your hand size.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting a half-star for the missing stripper notch and the spring tension, but this is the pair I'd grab first from a full toolbox.
