Some bike racks look like they’ll tip over if you sneeze near them. This one doesn’t. The HEALTH LINE PRODUCT 4-bike stand is a welded steel unit that holds four bicycles in a vertical position, taking up roughly the floor space of a single adult bike. It’s the kind of thing you buy once, bolt down, and forget about — mostly.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains the welded steel frame, four wheel trays, four rubber-coated hook arms, and the hardware bag (bolts, washers, and a hex key that’s too small for the job — keep reading). The frame measures about 70 inches tall and 24 inches deep; the footprint is compact enough for a narrow garage wall or a mudroom corner. The hooks pivot slightly, which helps when you’re sliding a bike in at an angle. The wheel trays are stamped steel with a rubber insert that grips the tire without chewing it up.

**Real-world performance**  
I tested this with a mix of bikes: a 29er mountain bike (32 lbs), a commuter with a rear rack, and two kids’ bikes. The frame held all four with zero lateral wobble. The vertical storage position means the front wheel lifts off the ground, so you’re not fighting handlebar tangles. The rubber hooks held rims securely — no paint scratches after a week of daily in-and-out. One owner noted, “I have two e-bikes and two road bikes on this thing. It doesn’t budge.” Another said, “The hooks are spaced wide enough that drop bars don’t hit the frame.”

The wall-mount option is the smarter choice if you have kids or pets. The included anchors let you secure the top rail to a stud, which eliminates any chance of tipping. Freestanding, it’s stable on flat concrete but slightly less so on carpet — the feet are bare metal, no rubber pads.

**Where it falls short**  
Assembly is the sore spot. The bolts are pre-threaded into the frame at the factory, but they’re torqued tight, and the included hex key strips under pressure. You’ll want a socket wrench with a 10mm socket. The instructions are a single sheet with small diagrams — doable, but not beginner-friendly. One reviewer complained, “Took 40 minutes and two trips to the toolbox because the key bent.”

Also, the wheel trays are sized for standard tires. If you ride fat bikes (3.5-inch tires or wider), the trays won’t seat properly. You can rig a strap, but that’s a workaround, not a feature.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Multi-bike households** with 2–4 bikes in a garage or shed.  
- **Apartment dwellers** with a wall to anchor to — vertical storage frees floor space.  
- **Commuters** who want bikes off the ground and out of the way but don’t want to drill into studs for a ceiling hoist.  

Skip it if you have a single bike (overkill), a fat bike, or if you’re unwilling to spend 30–40 minutes with a wrench on assembly night.

**FAQ**  
*Can this hold e-bikes?*  
Yes, up to about 50 lbs per hook based on owner reports, but anchor it to a wall. Freestanding, a heavy e-bike can tip the whole rack if you lean on it.

*Does it scratch rims?*  
The hooks have a rubber sleeve. As long as you slide the bike in straight, no scratches. If you slam it in at an angle, the metal edge can catch.

*Will it fit a bike with fenders?*  
Mostly. Fenders don’t interfere with the hook or tray, but the front fender may contact the frame above the hook on some models. Measure your bike’s wheelbase — anything over 48 inches may angle outward slightly.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you need a solid, no-nonsense 4-bike stand and don’t mind a bit of assembly effort. **Skip it if** you have fat tires or want a tool-free setup. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, this is one of the better values in vertical bike storage — just keep a real wrench nearby.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses points only for the weak hex key and fixed-width trays. The steel frame and stability are genuinely excellent for the price.
