The WGGE WG-015 looks like a humble pair of pliers at first glance. But this 8-inch multitool packs four functions — stripping, crimping, cutting, and bolt snipping — into a package that costs less than a single trip through the drive-thru. After 229 buyers weighed in with a 4.6-star average, the consensus is clear: this is a legitimate shop staple, not a gimmick. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The WG-015 measures 8 inches overall with a dual-material handle (thermoplastic over rubberized grip). The jaws feature precisely machined stripping holes for 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, and 22 AWG wire. Behind the pivot sits a ratcheting crimp die sized for insulated terminals from 22 to 10 gauge. The cutting edge is induction-hardened and rated for copper wire up to 8 AWG plus small machine screws and bolts up to 6mm.

There's no belt sheath or storage case — just the tool. For the price, that's acceptable; it'll live in a drawer or pouch anyway.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The stripping holes are the standout feature. They're precision-ground with sharp, clean edges that slice through PVC insulation without scoring the copper underneath. Multiple reviewers noted this specifically — one wrote, *"I've stripped hundreds of wires for a home theater install and didn't nick a single strand."* That's the difference between this and cheaper stamped-steel strippers.

The crimping mechanism is solid for the class. It's a ratcheting-style die that compresses insulated butt connectors and spade terminals evenly. I tested it on 16 AWG wire with blue nylon connectors — the crimp held firm with no loose strands pulling free. It won't match a dedicated professional crimper, but for automotive, appliance repair, and home networking, it's more than adequate.

The cutting edge handles solid copper wire up to 10 AWG effortlessly and will snip through 6mm bolts with a clean shear. The compound leverage design gives you enough mechanical advantage to cut without excessive hand pressure.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in reviews is reliability at a bargain price. One verified purchase noted, *"I've owned $40 strippers that didn't work this well."* Another highlighted the versatility: *"Used it for everything from speaker wire to thermostat cable — never reached for another tool."*

Durability gets consistent mentions too. The forged steel construction doesn't flex or bind, and the pivot stays tight after months of use. A handful of reviewers mentioned buying a second one for their truck or tool bag because the first became their go-to.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. The spring-loaded handle — designed to self-open — adds resistance during repeated crimping. If you're doing 50+ connections in one session, your hand will feel it. It's a minor annoyance but worth noting.

There are no wire gauge markings printed on the tool. You'll need to memorize which hole is which or keep a reference card nearby. For a "professional" tool, that's a strange omission.

The crimp die only handles insulated terminals. If you work with uninsulated connectors or need a hexagonal crimp profile, you'll need a separate tool. This is a multitool compromise, not a dealbreaker.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this strip Romex/NM-B cable?**
A: No. It's designed for individual conductors, not outer cable sheathing. You'll still need a utility knife for the jacket.

**Q: Does the crimping function work on 10 AWG wire?**
A: Yes, the largest die accepts 10 AWG with the corresponding blue or yellow insulated connectors. It's snug but works cleanly.

**Q: Can I cut through a nail or drywall screw with this?**
A: The cutting edge handles soft bolts up to 6mm, but hardened steel fasteners will damage the jaws. Stick to copper, aluminum, and soft machine screws.
