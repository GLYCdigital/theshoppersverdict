Replacing a tankless water heater’s gas line isn’t the glamorous part of a home upgrade — but get it wrong and you’ve got a code violation, a gas leak, or a unit that starves for fuel and throws error codes all winter. The EZ-Fluid Full Port Gas Flexible Line is built specifically to solve that last problem, and with 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it’s clearly doing something right. Here’s the breakdown after digging through the specs and what real buyers are saying.

**What You’re Actually Getting**

The box contains a 3/4-inch full port flexible gas connector with yellow epoxy coating, pre-attached brass fittings on both ends, and thread sealant tape. The full port design is the headline feature here — unlike standard 3/4-inch gas connectors that neck down to 1/2-inch at the fittings, this one maintains a true 3/4-inch bore throughout. For a tankless water heater demanding 150,000+ BTU, that difference matters. Restricted flow means lower gas pressure at the burner, which means longer heating times and premature component wear.

The yellow epoxy isn’t just cosmetic. It’s a corrosion barrier that also makes the line instantly identifiable as gas — which inspectors appreciate. The braided stainless exterior adds a layer of puncture resistance beyond the corrugated inner core.

**Real-World Performance**

Installation is straightforward if you’re comfortable with gas fittings: thread the brass ends, use the included sealant, and tighten. The full port bore delivers the CFM your unit needs. One buyer noted: *“My Rinnai was throwing a code 11 (ignition failure) with the old 1/2-inch line. Swapped to this and it fired right up. The difference in gas delivery is real.”* Another praised the build: *“Fittings are solid brass, not the cheap zinc alloy you see on some connectors. Feels like a professional-grade part.”*

The line itself is rated for standard residential natural gas and propane applications, and the epoxy coating held up well in humid environments according to multiple reviewers.

**Where It Falls Short**

The stiffness is the most common complaint. Because it’s a full port design, the corrugation is larger and the braided exterior doesn’t flex like a standard appliance connector. *“Plan your routing before you tighten anything,”* warned one reviewer. *“This thing does not want to bend around corners. I had to buy an extra elbow fitting to make it work.”* That’s fair feedback — measure twice, and consider whether your water heater’s position allows a mostly-straight run.

A smaller number of users mentioned the included gaskets can squeeze out if you reef on the fittings with a wrench. Hand-tighten then a quarter turn with a wrench is the correct approach. Over-tightening brass fittings is a classic DIY mistake, and this connector won’t forgive it.

**Who Should Buy This**

**Skip it if** you’re connecting a standard tank water heater (which doesn’t need full port flow) or if your install location requires tight 90-degree bends — you’ll fight the stiffness.
