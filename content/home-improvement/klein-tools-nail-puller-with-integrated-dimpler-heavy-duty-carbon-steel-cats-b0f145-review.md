You've got a nail head flush with the drywall, and your standard claw hammer keeps gouging the surface. That's the exact problem Klein Tools set out to solve with this nail puller — and it mostly nails it (pun intended).  

Here's what's in the box: one tool, no frills. The forged carbon steel head measures about 6 inches, with a curved, beveled claw on one end and a built-in dimpler on the other. The handle is overmolded with a textured rubber grip that actually stays put when your palms get sweaty. Total weight lands around 12 ounces — heavy enough to feel solid, light enough to toss in a tool belt.  

**Real-world performance**  
The integrated dimpler is the headline feature. Instead of grabbing a separate center punch to pre-dent the drywall before pulling the nail, you flip the tool over, press the rounded tip against the wall, and give it a firm tap. It creates a clean, shallow divot that hides the eventual patching — no cracked compound, no uneven edges. One reviewer put it this way: "Saved me from having to buy a punch set I'd use once a year. This thing does both jobs in one pass."  

The claw itself is where Klein's build quality shows. It's hardened and beveled at a sharper angle than typical pry bars, so it slides under nail heads without chewing up the wood. I tested it on both finishing nails and stubborn ring-shank nails in pine — the grip held firm, and the nail came out straight, not bent. The handle's shock absorption matters here too; after pulling a dozen nails from a stud, my palm wasn't throbbing like it does with a cheap bar.  

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Across 235 Amazon ratings (4.7 stars), the recurring themes are:  
- "The dimpler works exactly as advertised — no more digging around for a punch."  
- "Handle is comfortable for long jobs; doesn't slip even with gloves on."  
- "Head is machined precisely — it doesn't wobble or flex under pressure."  

**Where it falls short**  
The dimpler depth is fixed. If you're working with 5/8-inch drywall or pushing into a dense hardwood like oak, the divot might be shallower than you want — you can't adjust the pressure or depth. It's a minor gripe for most, but a real limitation for pros doing fine finish work.  

Also, this isn't a demolition tool. The claw is designed for nails, not prying trim or popping baseboards. If you need a wrecking bar, buy one. Expecting that from this tool will leave you frustrated and possibly bent.  

**Who should buy this**  
- **DIYers patching drywall after removing baseboards or crown molding** — this eliminates a separate punch from your toolkit.  
- **Carpenters and finish workers** who pull nails daily and want one tool that does the job cleanly.  
- **Anyone tired of gouging walls with a hammer claw.**  

Skip it if you only pull nails once a year and already own a center punch — that combo works fine, and you won't miss the $20.  

**FAQ**  
**Q: Can this pull nails that are flush with the surface?**  
A: Yes, the claw is beveled to slide under flush heads. You may need a firm tap to seat it, but it grips without slipping.  

**Q: Does the dimpler work on metal studs?**  
A: No — it's designed for drywall and wood. For metal studs, you'll need a different tool.  

**Q: Is the handle replaceable?**  
A: No, it's molded over the steel shaft. But Klein's warranty covers manufacturing defects, and the grip is durable enough for years of use.  

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you're a DIYer or pro who patches drywall after nail removal and wants a cleaner result with less effort.  
**Skip it if** you need a multi-purpose pry bar or rarely touch drywall repairs.  

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The integrated dimpler is a genuinely smart design, and Klein's build quality justifies the price. It's not perfect (fixed depth, no demolition duty), but for its intended job, this is the best nail puller I've used.
