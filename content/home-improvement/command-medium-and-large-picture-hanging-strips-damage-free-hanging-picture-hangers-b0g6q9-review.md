# Command Medium and Large Picture Hanging Strips, Damage Free Review: The Rental-Saver That Actually Works

Let's be direct: hanging pictures without leaving craters in your walls is the holy grail for renters, dorm dwellers, and anyone who's tired of patching holes. The Command Medium and Large Picture Hanging Strips are the most popular answer to that problem, and after looking at the 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, the consensus is clear — these work, with a few caveats worth knowing before you buy.

## What You Actually Get in the Box

This combo pack includes 6 medium strips (12 lb capacity per set of two) and 6 large strips (16 lb capacity). You'll also get 12 adhesive strips total, which means you can hang roughly 6 pictures if you use two per frame. The package includes clear instructions and a helpful "ready to hang" indicator tab that turns from white to clear when the adhesive has bonded.

As shown in the product image, the strips are white and low-profile — once mounted, they sit flush behind your frame, so you won't see bulky hardware peeking out. That's a meaningful upgrade over traditional wire-and-hook systems that leave visible metal.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The key to success here is preparation. The strips use a foam-based adhesive that bonds mechanically with the surface — you press the frame against the wall for 30 seconds, then let it cure for at least an hour before hanging anything heavy. The "ready to hang" tab is genuinely useful; it takes the guesswork out of whether you've waited long enough.

One buyer noted, *"I've used these in three different apartments and never had a single frame fall. Just follow the timing and wipe the wall first."* That's the pattern across positive reviews — people who succeed clean the mounting surface with rubbing alcohol, wait the full hour, and respect the weight limits. People who fail typically skip those steps.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The standout feature is the removal system. When you're ready to take a frame down, you pull the release tab straight down — not out — and the strip stretches lengthwise, losing its grip and separating cleanly from the wall. Reviewers repeatedly confirm this works as advertised. One 5-star review put it simply: *"Zero damage, zero residue. It peeled off like it was never there."*

Another consistent win is the holding power when used correctly. Frames mounted with the large strips on smooth drywall stay put — one reviewer mentioned hanging a 14-pound mirror with a single set of large strips and it held through a full year without shifting.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and these strips have real limitations. First, they are single-use. Once you remove a strip, the adhesive is compromised — you'll need to buy more. That's a hidden cost if you're the type who rearranges decor frequently.

Second, surface compatibility is narrower than the packaging suggests. Textured walls are a hard no — the foam strips won't make full contact with popcorn or orange-peel finishes. If you live in an older building with anything but smooth paint, you'll be disappointed. One 3-star reviewer warned: *"On my lightly textured walls, they lasted three days before the frame slid off."*

Finally, the 30-second press-and-hold is physically awkward for larger frames. Getting both strips to seat properly while holding a 20-pound mirror requires either a second person or a lot of patience.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Renters** who want to personalize without losing their security deposit
- **Apartment dwellers** with smooth painted drywall who change wall art seasonally
- **Anyone** hanging lightweight-to-moderate frames (under 16 lbs) who hates nail holes
- **Not for you** if you have textured walls, wallpaper, or need to hang anything over 16 lbs — get a stud-finder and a proper anchor instead

## FAQ

**Q: How long do I need to wait before hanging my picture?**
A: Press the frame firmly for 30 seconds, then wait at least 1 hour. For heavy items near the 16 lb limit, waiting 24 hours is safer — the bond strengthens over time.

**Q: Will these work on painted drywall without damaging the paint?**
A: Yes, if the paint is in decent condition. The adhesive bonds to the paint, not the drywall. When removed correctly, the paint stays intact. Failing or peeling paint is the one exception — if your paint is already chipping, the strip will take it with it.

**Q: Can I reuse the strips after removing them?**
A: No. The stretch-release mechanism permanently deforms the foam. Once removed, they're trash. Consider buying the refill packs if you plan to move frames around.
