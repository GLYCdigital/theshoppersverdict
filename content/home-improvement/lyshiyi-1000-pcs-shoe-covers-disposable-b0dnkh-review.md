Let’s cut to it: if you’re a real estate agent, home stager, or property inspector, you go through shoe covers like a hummingbird goes through nectar. Buying them in 50-count boxes is a fool’s errand. The LYSHIYI 1000 Pcs Shoe Covers Disposable pack solves the quantity problem in one go. But quantity isn’t the only question — the real test is whether these things survive a single showing without ripping.

## What you actually get

Open the box (a plain, unbranded cardboard unit) and you’ll find 1,000 shoe covers, typically packed in 10 smaller bundles of 100. That’s not a typo — a thousand. Each cover is a lightweight, non-woven polypropylene film with a sewn-on blue elastic band around the opening. They’re not individual wrapped, so if you plan to hand them out at the door, grab a handful and go. The dimensions run roughly 15 inches tall and about 12 inches wide at the base — enough to fit up to a men’s size 12 shoe without stretching the seams.

## Real-world performance

The critical moment is the elastic. LYSHIYI uses a thick, braided band — not the flimsy threadbare kind that snaps on the second wear. It grips mid-calf snugly enough to stay put while you walk, kneel, or climb a step ladder, but it won’t leave red marks on your legs after an hour of showings. That’s a genuine improvement over cheaper packs.

The material itself is where you need to temper expectations. It’s thin — think heavyweight grocery bag, not Tyvek. On clean hardwood, tile, or carpet, it holds up fine for a full 30–60 minutes of walking. But step on a stray screw, a piece of gravel tracking in from outside, or a sharp corner of a floor register, and you’ll feel the give immediately. A few reviewers reported splits after stepping on debris; one noted, “They’re fine for clean floors, but don’t wear them into a garage or unfinished basement.”

Traction is the bigger concern. The bottom is smooth with no grip pattern. On dry concrete or carpet, you’re fine. On wet tile, a polished concrete floor, or a bathroom right after the cleaners finished, you’re skating. If your work involves damp conditions, wear these with care — or double-check the floor before you step.

## Where it falls short

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the thin material means these are strictly for indoor, clean-floor use. If your job involves walking through active construction zones, wet basements, or exterior properties, these will tear — invest in a heavier-duty reusable cover instead.

Second, the lack of traction is a real safety issue on smooth, wet surfaces. It’s not a dealbreaker for most, but it deserves a mention. Third, the packaging is basic — no dispenser, no resealable bag. If you’re not careful, the loose covers will spill out of the box and you’ll be fishing them off the floor of your trunk.

## Who should buy these

You should buy this pack if you’re a real estate agent doing 5–10 showings a week, a home stager, an appraiser, or a photographer who needs a reliable, cheap cover for indoor shots. You should skip it if you’re a contractor, a painter, or anyone walking through unfinished or dirty environments — you need something with a thicker sole and better grip.

## FAQ

**Do these fit over work boots?**
Yes, up to about a size 12. The elastic stretches to accommodate bulkier footwear, though the fit gets snug around the ankle. For size 13+ or wide boots, look for an oversized variant.

**Are these waterproof?**
They’re water-resistant, not waterproof. Light spills and damp grass are fine. Standing water or heavy rain will soak through within minutes.

**Can these be reused?**
Technically, yes, for a second light use. But they’re designed as single-use. After one day of walking, the elastic loosens and the sole shows wear. Don’t count on more than two uses.
