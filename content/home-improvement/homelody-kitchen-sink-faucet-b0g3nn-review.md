## The Smart Faucet That Actually Gets It

Most "smart" kitchen faucets are gimmicks — a touch sensor that misfires, a sprayer that droops, or a finish that shows every fingerprint. The HOMELODY Kitchen Sink Faucet (B0G3NN) sidesteps most of those traps. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, it's not perfect, but it delivers the two things that matter most: reliable touch control and a sturdy build that won't wobble.

## What's in the Box

You get the faucet assembly, a pull-down sprayer head, braided stainless steel supply lines (standard 3/8" compression fittings), a mounting deck plate, and the touch-control module. The installation hardware is complete — no surprise trips to the hardware store. The unit weighs in around 8 pounds, which tells you it's not cheap plastic dressed up to look premium.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The body is solid brass, not zinc alloy — that's a meaningful distinction at this price point. Zinc faucets corrode and develop hairline cracks after a couple of years; brass holds up. The brushed nickel finish is applied evenly and does resist smudges, which matters in a busy kitchen.

The pull-down sprayer is where budget faucets usually fall apart. Here, the magnetic dock holds the head firmly in place. It won't sag after you let go — a complaint you see constantly in reviews of cheaper competitors. The sprayer offers two modes (stream and spray) toggled by a button on the head. The hose extends to about 20 inches, which covers most sink basins without pulling the entire faucet sideways.

Touch control is the headline feature. You tap the spout or handle to start or stop water. It works with wet hands, soapy hands, even hands covered in raw chicken juice — that's the whole point. One buyer put it simply: "The touch feature is the reason I bought this, and it hasn't failed me in 4 months of daily use." The sensor has a built-in delay of about a second, which is the tradeoff for not accidentally triggering it every time you brush past.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in positive reviews is reliability. People aren't writing about how beautiful it looks — they're writing that it just works. One verified purchaser noted: "Installation took me 30 minutes, and I'm not handy. The connections are solid and there are no leaks." Another emphasized durability: "The finish hasn't chipped or spotted, and I have hard water." That's the kind of feedback that matters.

## Where It Falls Short

The touch delay is real. If you're doing a quick rinse while cooking, that extra second before water flows gets old. It's a deliberate safety feature, but it's still a friction point.

The non-removable deck plate is a genuine limitation. If you're replacing an existing single-hole faucet and want a clean, minimalist look, you're stuck with the plate. It looks fine, but it's not an option — it's mandatory.

Some reviewers also noted that the sprayer head's stream pressure is slightly lower than a traditional non-touch faucet. It's not weak, but if you're used to a high-pressure jet for blasting food residue, you may need to switch to spray mode more often.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're upgrading from a basic faucet and want touch convenience without paying $300+ for a big-name brand. It's ideal for home cooks who constantly have messy hands, families with kids who leave sticky fingerprints everywhere, and anyone replacing a leaky faucet on a budget.

**Skip it if** you need a specific single-hole mount, want the absolute highest water pressure available, or you're the type who gets annoyed by even a half-second delay in water flow. Also skip if you're looking for a commercial-style faucet with a spring arm — this is a standard gooseneck design.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the touch sensor work if the power goes out?**
A: No. The faucet requires the included battery pack (4 AA batteries) or an AC adapter. Without power, the manual handle still works, but the touch function is dead.

**Q: Will this fit a 3-hole sink?**
A: Yes — the included deck plate covers all three holes. Just note it's non-removable, so you can't later expose a single-hole setup.

**Q: How hard is installation?**
A: Moderate. If you've replaced a faucet before, it's a 30–45 minute job. The braided lines are pre-attached, and the mounting hardware is straightforward. You'll need a basin wrench for tight spaces.

## The Verdict

The HOMELODY Kitchen Sink Faucet earns a **4.2/5**. It's not flawless — the touch delay and fixed deck plate are real annoyances. But for the price, you're getting brass construction, a fingerprint-resistant finish, and touch control that actually works. If those features matter to you, this is one of the better values on Amazon right now.

**Buy it if** you want smart-faucet convenience without the premium markup. **Skip it if** you can't tolerate sensor lag or need a true single-hole mount.
