If you’ve ever hung string lights on a patio or tried to plug in a leaf blower on a dark porch, you know the struggle: one outdoor outlet, zero lighting, and a tangle of extension cords. The mirrea Classic Outdoor Wall Sconce with Built-in GFCI Outlet tackles both problems with one wall-mounted unit. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it’s clearly finding its audience—but it’s not a universal fit. Here’s who should buy it and who should keep scrolling.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the assembled sconce (backplate, arm, glass shade), a pre-wired GFCI receptacle, mounting hardware, and a gasket. The unit is built from die-cast aluminum with a powder-coated finish—the same material grade you’d expect from a $100+ dedicated outdoor light. The GFCI outlet sits on the side of the fixture, angled slightly downward, with a spring-loaded cover that seals shut when not in use.

The sconce takes one standard medium-base bulb (E26). It’s rated for damp locations, meaning it handles rain and snow but shouldn’t be submerged or blasted directly by a sprinkler head. As shown in the product image, the glass shade is open at the bottom—classic lantern styling that throws light downward and outward.

**Real-World Performance**

Wiring is straightforward if you’re comfortable with basic electrical work. The fixture connects to standard 120V house current, and the GFCI provides ground-fault protection for anything you plug into it. That’s a significant advantage over a standard outlet: if a cord gets wet or a tool shorts out, the GFCI trips before someone gets shocked.

The outlet placement is the smart design choice here. It’s positioned on the side of the fixture, not underneath, so water doesn’t pool on the face of the receptacle. The cover clicks shut firmly, and the gasket compresses against the housing to keep moisture out. Multiple buyers note they’ve run holiday lights, electric grills, and pressure washers from this unit through full rainstorms without tripping the breaker.

One thing to know: the light and the outlet share the same junction box. If you trip the GFCI, the light goes out too. That’s standard wiring for this type of fixture, but it’s worth remembering when you’re troubleshooting.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dual-function design is the headline. One buyer wrote, *“Replaced a plain porch light and added outlet access without calling an electrician to run new wire. The outlet is perfectly positioned for holiday lights.”* Another noted, *“The casting feels heavy and solid—not like the thin stamped metal you see on cheap fixtures.”*

Finish durability gets repeated mentions. Several reviewers installed this in coastal areas and report no corrosion after a year of salt air exposure. That’s a strong signal for buyers in humid or marine climates.

**Where It Falls Short**

The bulb issue is real. The glass shade is fully exposed, so a standard incandescent or a cheap LED with visible diodes looks bad. You’ll want a decorative filament bulb (ST19 or similar) to match the classic aesthetic—that’s an extra $8–$12 purchase.

The junction box depth requirement trips up some DIYers. The backplate needs a box recessed to roughly 1.5 inches. If your existing box sits proud of the wall, the fixture won’t sit flush, and you’ll need a box extension ring. That’s a common retrofit headache, not a mirrea-specific flaw, but it’s worth checking before you buy.

Finally, the outlet is a single receptacle, not a duplex. You get one plug. If you need two, you’ll need an adapter or a different product.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Homeowners with a porch or patio lacking outlet access**—this solves the problem without running new wiring.
- **Anyone in wet or coastal climates** who needs a fixture that survives humidity and salt air.
- **DIYers comfortable with basic electrical connections** (or hiring an electrician for a quick install).

Skip it if you need a duplex outlet, if your existing junction box is non-standard, or if you want a fixture that hides the bulb entirely.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this fixture truly weatherproof?**
A: It’s rated for damp locations, meaning it’s built to handle rain, snow, and humidity. The outlet cover seals tightly, and the gasket keeps moisture out. It’s not rated for direct water spray (like a pressure washer aimed at it), so keep that in mind.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: If you’ve wired a light fixture before, yes. The GFCI is pre-wired, so you’re connecting three wires (hot, neutral, ground). If that sounds unfamiliar, hire a licensed electrician—it’s a 30-minute job for a pro.

**Q: Does the outlet work when the light is off?**
A: Yes. The light and outlet are on the same circuit, but the switch controls the light only. The outlet stays powered as long as the breaker is on. If you want the outlet switched too, that requires a separate wiring configuration.

**Skip it if** you need a duplex outlet, want a fully enclosed bulb, or aren’t prepared to deal with junction box depth issues during installation.
