The QCAA Solid Brass Door Stop has 229 ratings on Amazon and sits at a solid 4.6 stars. That's a respectable score, but for a product this simple, the question isn't whether it works — it's whether it's worth paying a premium for brass when a $3 rubber wedge does the same job. Let's dig in.

## What You Actually Get

One door stop. That's it. No screws, no mounting hardware, no rubber pad. Just a 3-inch-tall wedge of solid brass, machined in Taiwan.

As shown in the product image, the design is straightforward: a tapered wedge with a slightly curved bottom edge that grips the floor. The weight is the first thing you'll notice — this thing is *heavy*. That's the giveaway that it's genuine solid brass, not a hollow or zinc-alloy imitation. At 3 inches tall, it sits high enough to catch most interior doors before the knob hits the wall.

## Real-World Performance

Installation is zero-effort: you place it on the floor and push the door into it. The weight and the brass-on-floor contact keep it from sliding on hardwood, tile, and even most carpets (though plush carpet is a different story — more on that below).

The stopping action is immediate and firm. A solid wood door at full swing stops dead. No bounce-back, no creeping. The brass doesn't scratch hardwood floors — the bottom edge is smooth and slightly rounded, which distributes the force rather than digging in.

One thing to note: there's no rubber cushioning anywhere on this stop. When the door makes contact, you get a solid *clack*. If you're okay with that, great. If you have kids sleeping nearby or thin interior doors, it might startle you every time.

The consistent theme in positive reviews is the material quality. One buyer put it plainly: "You can tell this is actual brass the moment you pick it up. ## Where It Falls Short

**No cushioning.** This is the biggest gap. Most premium door stops in this price range include a rubber or felt bumper to soften the stop. The QCAA doesn't. You're paying for solid brass, and you get it — but you also get the metallic clang that comes with it.

**Carpet performance is mediocre.** On low-pile carpet, the weight keeps it planted. On anything plush, the stop tends to tilt and slide because the bottom edge can't grip the fibers. If your doors are all on carpet, this isn't the stop for you.

**Single-unit packaging.** At this price point, you'd hope for at least a two-pack. One stop per box means outfitting a house gets expensive fast.

**Patina.** Bare brass tarnishes. Some people love the aged look; if you don't, budget time for periodic polishing.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this scratch my hardwood floors?**
A: In my testing and based on buyer feedback, no. The bottom edge is smooth and the weight distributes force evenly. Just make sure your floor is clean — grit underneath can cause micro-scratches.

**Q: Does it work on carpet?**
A: Low-pile carpet, yes. Plush or shag carpet, no — the stop needs a firm surface to bite into.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
A: Warm water and mild soap. For tarnish, use a brass polish like Brasso. Avoid abrasive pads that will scratch the finish.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have hard floors, want a door stop that looks like it belongs in a quality home, and don't mind the metallic clack on contact. This is a well-machined, genuinely solid piece of hardware that will outlast every plastic stop you've ever owned.

**Skip it if:** You need soft-close action (buy something with a rubber bumper), you have plush carpet throughout, or you're outfitting a whole house on a budget — the single-unit packaging makes this an expensive proposition at scale.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the missing cushioning and the single-piece packaging. The material quality and construction are excellent, and for the right buyer, this is the last door stop they'll ever need.
