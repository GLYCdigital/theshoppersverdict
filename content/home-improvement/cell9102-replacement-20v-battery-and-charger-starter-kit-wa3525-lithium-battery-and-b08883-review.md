## What's Actually in the Box

You get one 20V lithium-ion battery (2.0Ah capacity), one rapid charger, and a basic wall plug. No tool, no case, no frills. The battery uses standard 18650 cells — nothing exotic — which is good news for longevity and replacement cost. The charger outputs 20V at 2A, which math suggests should fill the battery in about 60 minutes. In my testing, that's accurate: 58 minutes from flat to full LED green light.

The physical build is solid. The battery shell has a matte texture that grips well in gloved hands, and the slide-on rails are machined cleanly — no wobble when seated in a drill. The charger's base has rubber feet and a small ventilation grille; it runs warm but never hot.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this battery through a week of typical DIY work: driving 3-inch deck screws, drilling through ¾-inch plywood, and trimming fascia with a circular saw. The 2.0Ah capacity is the sweet spot for light-to-medium tasks. I got **45 minutes of continuous drilling** before noticeable slowdown — that's respectable for a third-party cell. On a 5.5Ah OEM battery, you'd get more, but you'd also pay 3x the price.

The charger deserves specific praise. Unlike some cheap replacements that trickle-charge for hours, this one uses a proper constant-current/constant-voltage algorithm. It shut off cleanly at full charge, and the LED switches from red to green without the flicker you see on bargain units. One buyer noted: *"Charged my dead battery to full in about an hour — faster than my original charger from the tool kit."* That matches my experience.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. Three issues recur in negative reviews:

- **No fuel gauge** — You can't tell remaining charge without mounting it to a tool. For a drill, that's fine. For a saw or leaf blower, it's an annoyance.
- **Charger footprint** — The charger is *big*, roughly the size of a paperback book. It won't fit in a compact toolbag's side pocket. One reviewer called it *"clunky but functional."*
- **Compatibility limits** — This is the big one. It fits 20V Max tools from major brands (Ryobi, DeWalt, Craftsman, etc.), but **not** older 18V-only tools, and not some newer brushless models that require proprietary communication protocols. Check the seller's compatibility list *before* buying — returns are easy, but wasted time isn't.
