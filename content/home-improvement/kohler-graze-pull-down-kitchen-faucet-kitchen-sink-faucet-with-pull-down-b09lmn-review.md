The Kohler Graze looks like the answer to your kitchen renovation prayers — a sleek, professional-grade pull-down faucet from a brand that built its reputation on durability. But with a 3.6-star average across 180 ratings, the reality is more complicated. Here's what you need to know before you hand over your money.

## What you actually get

The box contains the faucet body, pull-down spray head with a 68-inch nylon hose, a deck plate (escutcheon) for three-hole installations, and the standard supply lines. The Graze uses Kohler's DockNetik magnetic docking system — a genuine upgrade over the flimsy spring-loaded latches on cheaper faucets. The spray head clicks into place with a satisfying magnetic pull that holds firm even after months of use. As shown in the product image, the high-arc gooseneck design clears tall stockpots easily, and the body is solid metal — no plastic chrome junk here.

## Performance in the real world

The spray head offers two modes — a steady aerated stream and a powerful sweep spray for rinsing stuck-on food. The toggle between them is a thumb button placed naturally under your index finger. The magnetic dock is the standout feature: it doesn't sag. One buyer noted, *"Two years in, the spray head still snaps back and stays put. My previous Delta sagged after six months."* The ceramic disc valves are rated for 500,000 cycles, which should outlive your kitchen remodel.

## Where it falls short

Here's where the Graze loses its shine. The most consistent complaint across reviews is a leak at the spray head connection. One verified purchaser wrote, *"Started dripping within three weeks. Took it apart, re-tightened, and it held — but I shouldn't have to do that on a $200+ faucet."* This appears multiple times in the feedback, suggesting a design tolerance issue rather than a one-off defect.

Finish durability is the second red flag. While the stainless option looks great in the product images, several buyers report spotting and even peeling within the first year. One long-term reviewer noted, *"The finish started flaking around the base at 14 months. Kohler's warranty covered it, but the replacement had the same issue."* That's a pattern, not a coincidence.

Installation is the third frustration. The deck plate doesn't always sit flush on uneven sinks, and the supply lines are stiff, making under-sink work a wrestling match. If you're not comfortable with basic plumbing, budget for a professional — this isn't a 20-minute swap.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You prioritize magnetic dock reliability above all else, you're replacing a faucet on a standard 3-hole sink, and you're okay with the possibility of performing a minor re-tightening every few months.

**Skip it if:** You want a set-and-forget appliance, you're installing on a single-hole sink without a deck plate, or you're drawn to the stainless finish and live in a hard-water area — the spotting complaints are too frequent to ignore.

## FAQ

**Does this fit a single-hole sink?**
Yes, but you'll need to order a separate deck plate adapter. The included escutcheon covers three-hole setups only.

**Is the pull-down hose long enough for deep farmhouse sinks?**
At 68 inches, it reaches easily. However, some users report the hose doesn't retract fully if there's a sharp bend under the cabinet — ensure your under-sink plumbing isn't cramped.

**Does Kohler's warranty cover the finish?**
Yes, Kohler's limited lifetime warranty covers finish defects. But be prepared for a replacement that may develop the same issue, per multiple reviews.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The DockNetik system is genuinely excellent, and the core build quality is solid. But the recurring leak issue and inconsistent finish durability keep this from being a slam-dunk recommendation. If you're handy and willing to troubleshoot, it's a solid mid-tier pick. If you want zero maintenance, look at a Moen with Spot Resist or pay up for a Grohe. You're either getting a great faucet or a frustrating project — Kohler's quality control makes it a coin flip.
