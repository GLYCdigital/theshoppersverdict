## Performance and Real-World Use

The headline spec is the 10/4 gauge stranded copper wire. That's four conductors (two hots, one neutral, one ground) at 10 AWG each. For a 30-amp, 120/240-volt generator feed, this is the correct gauge — 12-gauge cords will run hot and drop voltage over long runs. At 100 feet, buyers report no noticeable voltage sag even when running a 30-amp load through a transfer switch.

One buyer noted: *"Ran my whole house off a 9,500-watt generator during a 3-day outage. The cord never got warm, let alone hot. My electrician checked the voltage at the panel and it matched the generator output within a volt."*

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my generator that has a 30-amp twist-lock outlet?**  
A: If your generator has a NEMA L14-30 receptacle (four pins, twist-lock), yes. That's the standard 30-amp 120/240V outlet on most portable generators over 5,000 watts. Check your generator's outlet — if it's a 3-prong RV-style, you'll need an adapter.

**Q: Can I bury this cord underground for a permanent installation?**  
A: No. This is rated for portable use only. Direct burial requires UF-rated cable and proper conduit. If you want a permanent generator hookup, have an electrician run the correct wire.

**Q: Does a 100-foot cord reduce my generator's power output?**  
A: With 10-gauge wire at 30 amps, the voltage drop over 100 feet is roughly 3–4% — acceptable for most appliances. Running a full 30-amp load at maximum distance will cause some drop, but for typical household use, you won't notice it.
