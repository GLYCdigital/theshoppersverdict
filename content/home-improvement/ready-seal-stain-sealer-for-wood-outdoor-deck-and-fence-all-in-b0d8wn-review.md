**Review Body:**

If you’ve ever spent a weekend sanding a deck down to bare wood just to apply a stain that peels by September, you already know the pain this product claims to solve. Ready Seal positions itself as the "apply over anything" stain and sealer for exterior wood. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, the consensus seems to back that claim. But let’s dig into whether it actually delivers—or if there’s fine print you should know before you commit a weekend and a few hundred dollars.

**What You’re Actually Getting**

Ready Seal is a ready-to-use, oil-based, semi-transparent stain and sealer in one. No primer, no thinning, no mixing. You can apply it with a brush, roller, or sprayer. The product image shows the classic 5-gallon pail, which is the sweet spot for most homeowners: it covers roughly 500–600 square feet per coat, depending on wood porosity. For a standard 12x16 deck (about 384 sq ft of surface area including railings), one pail does the job with a little left over for touch-ups.

**The Real-World Performance**

The headline feature is the "no strip, no sand" application. This isn't marketing fluff—the oil-based formula is designed to penetrate weathered wood and even adhere to previously stained surfaces (as long as the old finish isn't peeling or flaking). One buyer noted, “I rolled it over a 15-year-old pressure-treated deck that hadn't been touched in years. It soaked in beautifully and looks like new cedar.” That’s the use case this product was built for.

The slow-dry oil formula is also a genuine advantage. It eliminates lap marks—those ugly darker stripes you get when a stain dries too fast and you overlap your strokes. You can work in direct sunlight without panic, which is rare in this category. Most reviewers who applied it with a sprayer followed up with a back-brush to even out absorption, and reported zero streaks.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The most repeated compliment across the 235 ratings is the durability. Multiple reviewers report two-plus years of protection through harsh winters and heavy UV exposure without peeling or flaking. One long-term review stated, “Applied in 2023, still looks like I stained it last month. No peeling, just a slight lightening in color.” That aligns with the product’s claim of being a true penetrating sealer rather than a film-forming coating.

**Where It Falls Short**

Here’s the honest tradeoff. Because it’s semi-transparent, it will *not* hide defects. Deep scratches, heavy gray weathering, or any old paint or solid stain will show through. One reviewer warned, “If your deck is badly weathered with cracks, this will highlight them, not hide them.” You also face a narrow weather window—you need 24–48 hours of dry conditions after application. A surprise rainstorm will leave blotchy spots that are difficult to fix without recoating the entire section.

Color accuracy is another point to watch. The can label renders colors darker than the final result, particularly on redwood and cedar. If you’re choosing between two shades, go with the darker one if you want the label look.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The "lazy perfectionist" homeowner** who wants a durable, good-looking finish without renting a floor sander and spending three days sanding.
- **The fence rebuilder** covering a large surface area where brush-only application would take forever—this sprays well and self-levels.
- **The previous-stain veteran** who has a deck that’s faded but not peeling, and wants to refresh it without a full strip.

**Skip it if** your wood is severely weathered, has old paint, or you live somewhere with unpredictable summer rain patterns that make a 48-hour dry window a gamble.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I apply this over a previous coat of solid stain?**
A: No. Ready Seal is semi-transparent and needs to penetrate the wood grain. Solid stains form a film that blocks penetration—you’ll need to strip that first.

**Q: How long does it actually last?**
A: Most buyers report 2–3 years on decks with foot traffic, longer on fences. Expect to reapply in year 3 if you want consistent protection.

**Q: Does it really have no lap marks?**
A: In our research, yes—as long as you maintain a wet edge and don't let sections dry before you back-roll. The slow-dry formula is the real deal, but it's not magic. Don't leave a half-finished section for an hour while you take a lunch break.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, low-effort refresh for a weathered deck or fence without the nightmare of stripping and sanding.

**Skip it if** your wood is structurally damaged, painted, or you need a solid, opaque color.

Rating: **4.6/5** — A genuinely user-friendly product for the right scenario, held back only by its limitations on badly damaged wood and the strict weather window. For the majority of homeowners with a tired-but-sound deck, this is the closest thing to a one-coat weekend solution you'll find.
