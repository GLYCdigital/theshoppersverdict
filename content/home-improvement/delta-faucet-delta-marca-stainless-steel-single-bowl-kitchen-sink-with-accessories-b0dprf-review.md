The Delta Marca single bowl sink sits in that awkward middle ground: not the cheapest 18-gauge box-store special, not a premium workstation sink either. After digging through 200 Amazon ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story — this is a solid value pick if you're remodeling on a budget, but it's not the last sink you'll ever buy.

**What's Actually in the Box**

Delta ships this as a kit, which matters more than most listings let on. You get the sink itself, a stainless steel bottom grid (which protects the finish from scratches), a basket strainer, and a cutting board that fits the rails. In the product image, the accessory package looks like an afterthought — it's not. The cutting board is thick enough to actually use, and the bottom grid fits flush rather than rattling around.

The sink measures roughly 33 x 22 inches with a single bowl at 7 inches deep. The 16-gauge steel is the sweet spot — 18-gauge is thinner and dents, 14-gauge is overkill for residential use. The brushed satin finish hides water spots better than mirror-polished alternatives.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The first thing you notice lifting it out of the box is the weight. This thing is dense. The sound-dampening pads cover maybe 70% of the underside, which translates to noticeably less clatter than bare steel. Drop a cast iron skillet and you'll hear a thud, not a ring. That's the difference between a $150 sink and a $400 sink, and Delta gets it right here.

Installation is straightforward for a drop-in or undermount setup — the rim is flat and true, which matters more than people think. No warped edges, no gaps against the countertop. The drain offset toward the rear is a thoughtful touch that leaves usable floor space in the cabinet below.

**What Buyers Actually Say**

The 4.0-star average tracks with what owners report. One verified buyer noted, *"The accessories alone are worth the price — I priced out a comparable Kraus and the grid and basket would've cost me an extra $80."* Another praised the finish: *"Six months in, no scratches, no water spots, and I'm not gentle with it."*

**Where It Falls Short**

The 7-inch depth is the biggest compromise. If you regularly wash large stockpots or bake sheets, you'll fight the splash. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Great sink, but my high-arc faucet turns this into a splash zone for anything taller than a cereal bowl."* Pair it with a low-profile faucet and it's manageable; pair it with a gooseneck and you'll be wiping down the counter constantly.

Also, no faucet is included. That's not a flaw for everyone, but if you're budgeting, add $120–250 for a decent unit. The included cutting board is thinner than dedicated boards — fine for veggies, not for heavy chopping.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **First-time remodelers** who want a quality sink without the premium markup
- **Homeowners replacing a builder-grade sink** — the 16-gauge steel and sound dampening are a genuine upgrade
- **Anyone who wants accessories bundled** — the grid and basket are ones you'd actually keep

Skip it if you're a serious home cook who needs deep basins for big pots, or if you want a workstation sink with integrated racks and cutting boards.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this sink fit a standard 33-inch cabinet base?**
Yes — the 33-inch width is designed for a 36-inch base cabinet. Measure your existing cutout before ordering; the specs are accurate but tight on some quartz countertops.

**Q: Is the stainless steel magnetic?**
Lightly. It's 304-grade stainless, so fridge magnets won't stick, but magnetic knife strips might. Not a functional issue, just don't expect zero magnetism.

**Q: Can I use a garbage disposal with this?**
Yes, the drain is standard 3.5-inch and the steel is thick enough to handle the vibration. The sound pads help here too.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a well-built, accessory-complete sink at a mid-range price and don't need extreme depth. **Skip it if** you regularly wash oversized cookware or want a workstation setup.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The build quality and included accessories justify the price. The shallow depth is a real limitation, but for most kitchens, this is a smart, honest purchase that'll hold up for years.
