You get 12 pieces total: a driver handle with a knurled grip, and 11 swaging heads ranging from 1/4-inch up to 7/8-inch. That covers the standard residential HVAC line set sizes — R-410A mini-splits typically need 1/4, 3/8, and 5/8, but having the larger sizes means you're not buying a second kit when you land a commercial job. The heads are machined from hardened steel with a smooth taper, and the bit holder slots onto the handle so you can keep the three or four sizes you use most right on the tool. No fumbling through a pouch mid-install.

**How it performs when it matters**

The swaging action is straightforward: insert the cone into the tube, drive it with the handle, and the copper expands evenly. What separates this set from bargain-bin alternatives is the surface finish. Multiple buyers noted the cones don't bind or leave scoring marks on the inside of the tubing. The handle has enough heft to transfer force without bending, and the knurled section gives you a solid grip. **FAQ**

**Q: Will this work on hard-drawn copper, or only soft?**
A: Soft copper only, unless you anneal hard-drawn tubing first. Heat the end with a torch until it glows faintly, let it cool, then swage. Same rule applies to any manual swage tool.

**Q: Can I use these heads to flare tubing, or just swage?**
A: Swage only. These expand the tube's outer diameter to accept a fitting. For 45-degree flare connections (like on some AC service valves), you'll need a separate flaring tool.

**Q: How much force does it take to swage 7/8-inch copper?**
A: Noticeably more than the smaller sizes. You'll need both hands and a stable work surface. If you're doing multiple 7/8-inch joints, consider renting a hydraulic swager for the day.
