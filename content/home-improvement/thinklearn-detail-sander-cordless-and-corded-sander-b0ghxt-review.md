## Performance and Build Quality

The dual-power system is genuinely useful, not just a marketing gimmick. Run it cordless for quick touch-ups on a ladder or in a closet without outlet access. When the battery drains mid-project (and it will—cordless sanding burns through charge fast), swap to the cord and keep going. No downtime, no frustration.

Reviewers consistently note the vibration control. One buyer put it simply: *"My hand didn't fall asleep after 20 minutes of use. That never happens with my old sander."* That's meaningful—detail sanding is precision work, and hand fatigue ruins accuracy.

The dust collection deserves specific praise. A perforated pad with a built-in vacuum attachment keeps most debris off your workpiece and out of your lungs. It's not shop-vac level, but it's leagues ahead of the "dust everywhere" experience you get with budget sanders.

The variable speed control (typically 8,000–13,000 OPM) gives you range for everything from stripping paint to fine finishing. Felt backing protects the work surface from gouging when you press too hard—and you will press too hard at least once.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs:

**Battery life is modest.** Expect 20–30 minutes of continuous cordless use. That's fine for small jobs but frustrating for larger projects. The cord saves you, but it's worth knowing going in.

**The dust bag fills quickly.** With the collection system working well, you'll be emptying it often—especially when sanding wood with heavy material removal.

**It's not a heavy-duty tool.** This is for fine work, trim, corners, and detail projects. If you're stripping an entire deck, buy a full-size orbital sander instead. You'll burn through this one trying.

**Charging time.** The included battery takes a couple of hours to fully charge. If you drain it mid-project, the cord is your friend—but plan ahead if you're working cordless-only.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this for wet sanding?**
No. This is a dry-sanding tool. The electrical components and dust collection system aren't sealed for moisture. Keep it dry.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
Real-world cordless run time lands around 20–30 minutes depending on pressure and speed setting. Keep the cord handy for extended work.

**Q: What materials can it handle?**
Wood, drywall, plastic, and light metal work. The variable speed helps match the tool to the material—lower speeds for plastics to prevent melting, higher for wood and metal.
