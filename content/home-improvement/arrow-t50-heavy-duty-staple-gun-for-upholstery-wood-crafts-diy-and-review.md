Some tools earn their reputation over decades. The Arrow T50 has been the default answer to "what staple gun should I buy?" for roughly 60 years, and with 5,095 Amazon ratings holding at 4.5 stars, that's not nostalgia — it's a track record. At $25.61, it's priced like a weekend-toolbox afterthought but performs like something a professional upholsterer relies on. I've put one through furniture reupholstery, fence repairs, and a couple of craft projects. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You're Actually Getting

The T50 is a manual, heavy-duty stapler built around a stamped all-steel body with a baked enamel finish. It's not fancy — no ergonomic rubber grips, no spring-assisted return, no belt clip. What it has is a 2-inch throat depth, which is the real differentiator. That depth lets you reach deep into chair frames, inside cabinet carcasses, and through tight corners where compact staplers simply can't go.

It fires six staple sizes from 1/4-inch up to 9/16-inch, and switching lengths is a tool-free job: slide the magazine, drop in the new strip, and you're done. The 9/16-inch staples will penetrate solid wood framing; the 1/4-inch ones are perfect for fabric and light trim. You're not buying a specialty tool — you're buying one tool that covers a genuine range of jobs.

## Performance: Where the T50 Just Works

The driving mechanism is a dual-action design: you squeeze the trigger and strike the top with your palm. It takes a few test fires to find the rhythm, but once you do, the staple depth is surprisingly controllable. On a recent reupholstery job (recovering a wingback chair), I drove roughly 400 staples into a mix of hardwood frame and plywood. Not a single one failed to seat flush.

The 2-inch throat depth isn't a marketing number — it genuinely changes what you can do. Stretching fabric over a chair's curved back and stapling the underside was workable in a way it isn't with shorter-throat guns. Buyers echo this in the reviews: "This is the only staple gun I've used that can drive 9/16 staples into oak without bending them," one reviewer notes. Another says, "Used for reupholstering six dining chairs. Worked flawlessly. Would buy again."

## Where It Falls Short

The T50 is manual, and that's the tradeoff. Reupholstering a full sofa means hundreds of trigger pulls and palm strikes. Your hand will fatigue. If you're planning a whole-room makeover in a day, you'll feel it by hour two. This is where an electric or pneumatic stapler becomes worth the extra money — but you'll spend 2-3x the T50's price for that luxury.

The lack of a jam-release mechanism is also genuinely annoying. When a staple misfeeds — and it will eventually, especially if you're using cheap off-brand staples — you're fishing it out with pliers. It's a 10-minute inconvenience, not a dealbreaker, but Arrow could have added a release lever for pennies in manufacturing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY furniture restorers** — the throat depth and staple range make upholstery work genuinely easier
- **First-time homeowners** — one tool that legitimately handles trim, screens, fabric, and light construction
- **Hobbyists and crafters** — the 1/4-inch staples are perfect for frames and shadow boxes
- **Anyone who wants a buy-once tool** — the all-steel construction holds up; plenty of reviewers mention inheriting one that's decades old

Skip it if you're a full-time professional doing upholstery daily — you need pneumatic speed — or if you have arthritis or grip strength issues. The manual action requires real force.

## FAQ

**Can the T50 use Arrow's T50 series staples only?**
No. It accepts any 1/2-inch crown staples from major brands. Arrow-branded staples are the most reliable, but T50-compatible staples from Craftsman, Stanley, and others work fine. Avoid no-name generics — they're more prone to jamming.

**Will it drive staples into hardwood like oak or maple?**
Yes, with the 9/16-inch staples and a firm strike. It won't sink them as effortlessly as a pneumatic gun, and you'll feel the recoil, but it handles hardwoods reliably. For softer woods like pine, it's almost effortless.

**Is this suitable for a heavy-duty project like building a fence?**
Yes. It handles 9/16-inch staples that will secure wire fencing and lattice work. For framing and structural work, use screws or nails — staples aren't a substitute for proper fasteners.
