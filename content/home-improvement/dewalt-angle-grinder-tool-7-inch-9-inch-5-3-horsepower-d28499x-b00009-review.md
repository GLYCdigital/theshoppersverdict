If you're shopping for a serious metal-fabrication grinder, the DEWALT D28499X is the tool you'll see on professional job sites. This isn't the 4.5-inch palm grinder you grab for weekend projects — it's a 12.5-pound brute designed for cutting thick steel, notching structural beams, and prepping heavy welds. The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews reflects a tool that pros respect, even if a few quirks keep it from perfection.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the grinder body, a 9-inch Type 27 guard, a 7-inch Type 27 guard, a side handle, a 5/8-11 spindle wrench, and the manual. The tool accepts both 7-inch and 9-inch wheels — the included guards swap in under a minute using the tool-free detent lever. The 5.3-horsepower motor spins at a fixed 6,000 RPM, which is the sweet spot for 9-inch wheels rated at 6,600 max. The paddle switch sits under your index finger with a lock-on button for extended cuts.

**Performance in the real world**

The motor is the story here. DEWALT claims 5.3 horsepower, and in practice that means this grinder doesn't flinch when you lean into a 1/4-inch plate cut. The 15-amp motor maintains speed under load far better than mid-tier grinders, which means faster cuts and less heat buildup on the workpiece. The spindle lock makes wheel changes a one-wrench operation.

That said, this is a two-hand tool, period. The 12.5-pound weight is manageable on horizontal work but brutal overhead. Multiple reviewers note exactly this — one fabricator wrote, "Great power, but you'll want a stand or a second person for overhead work. It's a workout." The vibration at full RPM is present but controlled, and the rubberized grip does cut some of it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The power-to-cost ratio comes up repeatedly. One Amazon reviewer with 5 stars put it plainly: "Bought this to replace a cheap 9-inch that died after three months. Night and day. Cuts through 1/2-inch flat bar like butter." Another praised the guard adjustability: "Being able to twist the guard without tools mid-cut is huge when you're working in tight spaces."

The dual-size capability gets consistent love too — buyers appreciate not owning two separate grinders for different wheel sizes.

**Where it falls short**

The fixed 6,000 RPM is the biggest limitation. At that speed, a 7-inch wheel spins slower than you'd want for thin sheet metal — you'll get more burr and heat than with a dedicated 7-inch tool running 8,500 RPM. The lack of a brake means the wheel free-spins for 8-10 seconds after shutdown, which is an annoyance when you're moving between cuts.

The 15-amp draw is also a real consideration. If your garage has 15-amp circuits with other loads, you'll trip breakers. This grinder wants a dedicated 20-amp circuit or at least a 12-gauge extension cord no longer than 50 feet.

**Who should buy this**

:heavy_check_mark: **Buy it if** you're a fabricator, welder, or serious metalworker who regularly cuts material thicker than 1/4 inch. The power and durability justify the weight.

:x: **Skip it if** you're a DIYer doing occasional angle-grinder work — a 4.5-inch unit at half the price and weight will serve you better. Also skip if your workspace has limited electrical capacity.

**FAQ**

*Q: Will this work with a standard household outlet?*
Yes, but only if the circuit is dedicated or lightly loaded. The 15-amp draw will trip shared circuits. Use a 12-gauge cord, never a lightweight homeowner extension cord.

*Q: Can I use this for cutting concrete or masonry?*
You can mount a diamond wheel, but the 6,000 RPM speed is slightly low for optimal masonry cutting. It's primarily a metal tool.

*Q: Does the paddle switch lock in the on position?*
Yes, there's a lock-on button for continuous operation, though you should never use it for freehand work.

**The Verdict**

The DEWALT D28499X earns its 4.2/5 rating through raw, dependable power. It's not the most refined grinder — no speed control, no brake, heavy as a boat anchor — but it's a workhorse that pros buy when they need to cut thick steel all day. If your projects match its capabilities, the weight and electrical demands are acceptable tradeoffs. For lighter work, buy something smaller.

**Buy it if** you're cutting steel professionally or tackling serious fabrication. **Skip it if** you need versatility or have circuit limitations.
