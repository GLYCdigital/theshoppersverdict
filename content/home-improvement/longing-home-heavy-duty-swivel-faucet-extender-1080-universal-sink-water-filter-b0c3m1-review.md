The **Longing Home Heavy Duty Swivel Faucet Extender** solves a real problem: water that doesn't reach where you need it. Whether you're washing a stockpot in a deep farmhouse sink or trying to rinse vegetables in a cramped apartment double-basin, this $20-ish accessory promises 1080 degrees of rotation and a heavy-duty build. But does it deliver, or is it another kitchen gadget that ends up in a drawer? I've tested it against two cheaper plastic extenders and one premium German brand. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the extender itself, a rubber gasket, and a small roll of PTFE tape. That's it. No wrenches, no adapters for oddball threads — which is fine, because the 15/16-inch female thread fits standard North American faucets. As shown in the product image, the unit is compact: roughly 4 inches long when collapsed, with a matte black zinc alloy body and a flexible stainless steel hose that extends about 8 inches. The swivel ball joint at the base is the star — it's machined metal, not the thin chrome plastic you see on budget models.

## Installation and Real-World Performance

Installation takes five minutes. Unscrew your aerator, wrap the threads with the included tape, screw on the extender hand-tight, and you're done. No tools required. The gasket seals well, and I had zero leaks at the faucet connection — the drip issue I'll mention below came from the swivel joint itself.

The 1080° rotation is technically real: you can spin the head three full rotations before it stops. But here's the thing — you'll never need more than about 270° in a real kitchen. The extra spins just mean the flexible hose twists around itself, which actually restricts water flow. At full extension, the stream drops from a solid jet to a lazy trickle. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: *"Works great for filling pots on the counter, but don't expect to rinse dishes with any force — it's a gentle stream, not a spray."* That matches my experience.

Where it shines: directed rinsing, filling tall vases, and reaching across a divided sink. The metal ball joint holds position firmly — no sagging, even with the hose fully extended. That's a genuine upgrade over plastic models that droop after a month.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 195 ratings, the standout compliments are about build quality and reach. *"This thing is solid. I've returned two plastic extenders that cracked at the threads. This one feels like it'll outlast my faucet,"* wrote one verified buyer. Another noted: *"Perfect for my deep sink — I can finally wash the back corners without splashing water all over the counter."* The universal fit gets consistent love too; nearly every reviewer mentions it threaded on without adapters.

## Where It Falls Short

The swivel joint is the weak point. Even with the PTFE tape applied correctly, I got a slow drip from the ball joint after about three weeks of daily use. Tightening helped but didn't eliminate it. If you have hard water, expect this to worsen over time. Also, the flexible hose has a tendency to kink if you bend it past about 60 degrees — and once kinked, it stays kinked. That's a design flaw, not a usage error.

One more thing: the matte black finish scratches easily. If you scrub the joint with a scouring pad, you'll see bare metal within a week. Use a soft cloth.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a deep or double sink and need directed, hands-free water flow. You've tried plastic extenders that cracked or sagged. You want something that attaches in minutes without calling a plumber.

**Skip it if:** You need high-pressure rinsing (this isn't a sprayer replacement), you have hard water that will worsen joint drips, or you're looking for something truly maintenance-free. Also skip if you have a pull-down faucet — you already have this functionality built in.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my pull-down faucet?**
A: No. Pull-down faucets have a hose inside the spout, not a threaded end. This extender only fits standard threaded aerators (15/16-inch). Check your faucet tip before ordering.

**Q: Does the 1080° rotation actually work?**
A: Yes, mechanically. But you'll realistically use about a quarter-turn in either direction. The extra rotation just twists the hose and reduces flow.

**Q: How do I stop the drip from the swivel joint?**
A: Apply PTFE tape to both the faucet threads *and* the extender's male threads, then tighten firmly. If it still drips after two weeks, you likely got a defective unit — Amazon's return policy covers it.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5**

The Longing Home Swivel Faucet Extender is a solid, well-built tool for a specific job. It's heavier and more durable than the $12 plastic options, and it genuinely holds position under use. But the pressure drop at full extension and the eventual joint drip keep it from being a slam-dunk recommendation. If your sink reach problem is real, this will solve it — just keep a wrench and a roll of PTFE tape handy for maintenance.

**Buy it if** you need durable, directed water flow into hard-to-reach sink corners. **Skip it if** you demand strong spray pressure or want zero maintenance. For the price, it's a fair value — just don't expect it to replace a proper pull-down sprayer.
