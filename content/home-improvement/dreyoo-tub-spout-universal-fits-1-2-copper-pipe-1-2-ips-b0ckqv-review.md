If you've ever stared at a tub spout that won't budge — or worse, bought a "universal" one that fits nothing — you know the pain. The Dreyoo Tub Spout claims to solve that with a dual-fit design for both 1/2" copper pipe and 1/2" IPS threads. After digging through 204 ratings and the actual product specs, here's what you need to know before you drain your tub.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the spout body, a threaded IPS adapter ring, a stainless set screw with hex wrench, and a rubber O-ring. As shown in the product image, the spout has a clean, modern profile — no ornate curves, just a straight cylinder with a subtle bevel at the opening. The diverter lever sits on top, angled for thumb access while you're in the tub. Finish options include brushed nickel (most popular), chrome, and oil-rubbed bronze.

The dual-fit mechanism is the headline feature. For 1/2" copper stub-out, you slide the spout over the pipe and tighten the set screw — no soldering, no plumber's putty needed. For threaded IPS pipes, you screw the included adapter onto the pipe first, then slide the spout over that. One product, two installation types. That's genuinely useful for DIYers who aren't 100% sure what's behind their tile.

## Real-World Performance

The spout body is solid brass with a PVD finish, not the thin zinc alloy you'll find at big-box stores for the same price. Buyers consistently report the finish holds up — one reviewer noted, *"Six months in, no spots, no peeling, looks as good as day one."* The water flow is unrestricted, which is good for filling a tub quickly, but it also means no pressure regulation. If your home has high water pressure, expect some splashing.

The diverter is where this spout earns its keep. Multiple reviewers mention the positive click when engaging the shower mode. One put it plainly: *"The lever actually stays where you put it. My old one would slowly drop back down mid-shower."* That's a real quality-of-life upgrade.

## Where It Falls Short

The set screw is the weak point. It's a 2mm hex that's easy to strip if you're heavy-handed. Use the included wrench, snug it gently, and you'll be fine — but if you're the type who torques everything, bring patience.

The IPS adapter adds about 1/4 inch of length. On most tubs that's invisible, but if your pipe stub is already flush with the tile, the spout may sit slightly proud of the wall. Measure your stub-out before buying.

A few reviewers noted the spout doesn't include a rubber gasket for the wall plate — you'll want plumber's putty or silicone caulk to seal the gap and prevent water creeping behind the tile.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a DIYer replacing a tub spout and you're not sure whether you have copper or threaded pipe. The dual-fit removes the guesswork, and the price is right. Also buy it if you're tired of diverter levers that sag — this one clicks.

**Skip it if:** You need a specific decorative style (this is minimalist only), or you have a 3/4" pipe stub (not compatible). If you have rock-hard water, the brushed finish will hide spots better than chrome, but no finish is immune.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my 1/2" copper pipe with a threaded adapter already soldered on?**
A: No. If you have a threaded male adapter on copper, you need the IPS version — which this is. Remove any old adapter first, or use the included IPS ring if your pipe is threaded.

**Q: Do I need plumber's tape for the threaded connection?**
A: Yes, for the IPS adapter-to-pipe connection, wrap 3-4 turns of Teflon tape. The spout-to-adapter connection is a slip fit held by the set screw — no tape there.

**Q: How do I remove this later without damaging the finish?**
A: Loosen the set screw fully, then twist the spout counterclockwise while pulling. If it's stuck, tap gently with a rubber mallet. Don't use pliers on the body — you'll mar the finish.
