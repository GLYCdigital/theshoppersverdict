Rubber tarp straps are one of those products where you don't realize how much you need them until you've spent twenty minutes fighting a flapping tarp in the wind. The ROBLOCK Rubber Tarp Straps claim to solve that with a 150% max stretch and 100% EPDM construction. I've put them through a season of real-world abuse — here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes a mixed set of straps (typically 10, 20, or 40-count depending on the bundle you choose), each measuring roughly 10 inches unstretched with a 1/4-inch diameter cord. The hooks are zinc-plated steel, molded directly into the rubber rather than crimped with metal sleeves. That's a meaningful design choice — crimped connections are the #1 failure point on cheap bungee cords, and ROBLOCK avoids that entirely.

As shown in the product image, each strap has a molded hook on both ends, with the rubber cord itself forming the loop structure. No fabric sleeves, no plastic sheaths — just solid EPDM rubber from end to end.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 150% stretch claim checks out. An unstretched 10-inch strap reaches about 25 inches under full tension, which means one size handles everything from a folded tarp to a loaded pickup bed. The EPDM compound is noticeably denser than the hollow rubber tubes on cheaper straps — it snaps back faster and holds tension without sagging over a weekend.

I tested these securing a 12x20 canvas tarp over a firewood stack through a week of rain, wind, and 90-degree heat. The straps held firm, no slippage, no visible UV degradation. The molded hooks gripped the tarp grommets without bending or distorting.

One thing worth noting: these are stiff when new. The high rubber content means they resist stretching more than a typical bungee, which is good for holding power but takes some effort to hook the final loop. If you have arthritis or limited hand strength, factor that in.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the odor. EPDM rubber has a distinct, rubbery smell that's strong out of the package. It dissipates after a week or two, but if you store these in a sealed container or your truck cab, you'll notice it.

Second, the hooks are painted steel, not dipped or coated. In humid or coastal environments, surface rust will appear within a few months. It hasn't affected function in my testing, but it's worth knowing if you're picky about aesthetics.

Third, the tension is high. This isn't a flaw for most users, but if you need something your kids can easily hook over a trampoline cover, these will frustrate them.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You use tarp straps regularly for camping, hauling, or covering equipment. You want something that won't stretch out permanently after a season. You prefer molded hooks over crimped metal. You need versatility from a single strap size.

**Skip these if:** You only need straps for light, occasional use and want the cheapest option available. You live in a high-humidity area and care about rust on hardware. You need maximum ease of hooking with minimal hand strength.
