Some tools solve a problem you didn't know you had. Then there are tools like the Milwaukee M12 Fuel 1/2" Right Angle Impact Wrench — a solution for a problem every mechanic knows intimately: the fastener that sits at an impossible angle, buried behind a bracket, with exactly zero clearance for a standard gun.

The right-angle impact wrench has been around for decades, but they've always been pneumatic, heavy, and tethered to a compressor. Milwaukee's M12 Fuel version changes that calculus, and with 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly earning its place in tool bags. Let's break down whether it earns a spot in yours.

**What's Actually in the Kit**

This is the kit version, so you're getting more than just the bare tool. The box includes the 2557-20 right angle impact wrench, one M12 RedLithium CP 2.0 battery, the standard charger, and a soft-sided carrying case. You're also getting the belt clip and the friction ring for the 1/2" anvil — not the pin-detent style, which matters for quick socket changes but means you need to be careful with heavy impact sockets that could walk off.

**The Real-World Numbers**

Milwaukee rates this at 150 ft-lbs of fastening torque and 200 ft-lbs of nut-busting torque. Those numbers put it squarely in "interior work" territory — think caliper brackets, suspension components, engine accessories, and interior trim. It's not breaking loose crankshaft bolts or seized lug nuts.

What makes it genuinely useful is the head design. The business end measures just 3.4 inches from the anvil to the back of the head, and the overall height is compact enough to slip between a frame rail and a crossmember. The four-mode Drive Control lets you dial in 650, 1,750, or 2,750 RPM, plus a bolt-removal mode that spins fast for removal but limits torque on reinstallation — a thoughtful touch for anyone who's ever snapped a bolt by trusting the clutch.

**What Buyers Actually Say**

The praise in the reviews is consistent and specific. One buyer noted, *"Used it to remove the transmission from a 2018 F-150 — the bolts on top of the bell housing were impossible with anything else. Fits where my 3/8" air ratchet couldn't go."* Another highlighted the battery efficiency: *"I've done two full brake jobs on a 4.0 battery and still had juice left. The brushless motor sips power compared to my old corded right angle."*

The gripes are equally telling. Multiple reviewers mention the weight — at 2.9 pounds with the 4.0 battery, it's not a one-handed tool for extended sessions. Several also noted that it bogs down on anything beyond its rated torque. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"This is not a replacement for a 1/2" high-torque impact. It's a complement. Know what you're buying."*

**Where It Falls Short**

The weight is the most honest criticism. The M12 Fuel compact impact wrench is a featherweight by comparison, and for overhead or awkward work, the right-angle design means you're often supporting the tool with your wrist at an odd angle. The 2.0 battery that comes in the kit is also undersized for continuous use — you'll want at least one 4.0 in your kit for anything beyond light duty.

The friction ring is another minor annoyance. It's faster to swap sockets, but if you're using deep impact sockets in dirty environments, they can occasionally work loose. A pin detent would have been more secure, though Milwaukee clearly traded that for convenience.

**Who Should Buy This**

This tool is for the serious DIYer or professional who's already invested in the M12 platform. If you're doing brake jobs, suspension work, or engine repairs on modern vehicles with cramped engine bays, this will save you hours of swearing at inaccessible bolts.

**Skip it if** you only work on older vehicles with generous clearance, or if your primary need is breaking loose rusted fasteners — you need a high-torque 1/2" impact for that, not a right-angle head.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Will this remove lug nuts?**
A: Not reliably. The 200 ft-lbs nut-busting torque is borderline for lug nuts torqued to 100-120 ft-lbs, but if they're rusted or over-torqued, you'll be reaching for a breaker bar. This is not a tire-changing tool.

**Q: Does it fit the M12 Fuel battery platform?**
A: Yes, it uses standard M12 batteries. The included 2.0 works, but I recommend a 4.0 or 6.0 for extended use — the higher capacity also balances the tool better.

**Q: How does it compare to the M12 Fuel stubby impact wrench?**
A: The stubby delivers more torque (250 ft-lbs) in a shorter package, but it has a straight head. The right-angle version trades torque for access. They're complementary tools, not competitors.
