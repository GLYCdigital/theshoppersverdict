# The VIQUA VH200: A Serious UV System for Homes That Actually Need One

If you're reading this, you probably already know why you need a UV water purifier. Maybe you've got coliform bacteria showing up in your well water tests. Maybe your neighbor down the road got sick from e.coli. Or maybe you're just tired of the bleachy taste of chlorine and want a chemical-free way to make your water safe.

The VIQUA VH200 is the system that keeps showing up in "best of" lists, and it's earned that spot. But it's not a magic box — it has specific requirements, real limitations, and a learning curve. Here's what you need to know before you drop the money.

## What You Actually Get

The VH200 ships as a complete disinfection assembly. Inside the box you'll find the stainless steel reaction chamber (316 grade, not the cheaper 304 you'll see on knockoffs), the 35-watt UV lamp, a quartz sleeve to protect the lamp, and the ballast/controller box that powers everything. The chamber measures roughly 36 inches long with 3/4-inch NPT inlet and outlet ports, so it's built to be plumbed inline with standard household piping.

The 9 GPM flow rate is the headline spec. That translates to roughly 540 gallons per hour — enough for a 3-4 bathroom home running multiple fixtures simultaneously, though you'll want to watch pressure drop if you're running the washing machine and two showers at once.

## Real-World Performance

UV disinfection is elegantly simple: water passes through the chamber, the 35-watt lamp emits 254-nanometer UV-C light, and that light scrambles the DNA of bacteria, viruses, and protozoa so they can't reproduce. The VH200's stainless steel chamber is polished to maximize UV reflection, which is why it achieves the NSF/ANSI 55 Class A certification — the highest standard for residential UV systems.

That certification matters. Class A means the system reduces bacteria and viruses by 99.99% (4-log reduction). Cheaper systems often only meet Class B, which is really just for "additional" treatment and won't protect you from contaminated well water.

Here's the catch: **UV only works on clear water**. The VH200 specs require a 5-micron sediment filter *before* the unit. If your well water has iron, manganese, or hardness, those need treatment too — UV doesn't remove dissolved minerals. Buyers who skip pre-filtration end up with cloudy water "shadowing" the UV light and rendering the system useless.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides, because there are a few.

**First, the clearance issue.** The manual specifies 4 inches of space above the chamber for lamp removal. If you're installing this in a tight crawlspace or under a slab, you may need to rework your plumbing to give yourself room. Plan this before you buy.

**Second, the continuous operation.** The VH200 doesn't include a flow sensor. That means the lamp runs 24/7, whether water is flowing or not. The lamp is rated for 9,000 hours (about a year), so you'll replace it annually regardless. If you want the system to only run when water flows, you'll need to buy VIQUA's separate flow switch — an added expense that should arguably be included at this price point.

**Third, the total cost of ownership.** The unit itself is just the beginning. You need the pre-filter (minimum $50-100), and annual lamp replacements run about $50-70 each. The quartz sleeve should also be cleaned every 6 months — not hard, but it's maintenance some buyers don't anticipate.

## Who Should Buy This

The VH200 is ideal for:

- **Well water homeowners** who've had bacteria detected in tests — this is the primary use case
- **Cottage/cabin owners** who want chemical-free treatment for seasonal properties
- **Anyone with a history of boil-water advisories** in their area who wants permanent peace of mind

Skip it if you're on municipal water with no bacterial concerns, if your water has heavy sediment or iron you haven't treated yet, or if you need a system that runs on battery backup (this requires standard 120V power).

## FAQ

**Q: Does this remove iron, sulfur, or hard water minerals?**
A: No. UV only kills microorganisms. For iron, sulfur, or hardness, you need separate treatment — typically an oxidation filter or water softener installed *before* the UV unit.

**Q: How do I know when the lamp needs replacing?**
A: The VH200 has an audible alarm and a red indicator light on the controller that activates when the lamp reaches end of life (around 9,000 hours). You'll also see a visible drop in water quality if you have test strips. Annual replacement is the safe bet.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: If you're comfortable with basic plumbing (cutting pipe, installing threaded fittings) and basic electrical (wiring the ballast to a power source), yes. The included manual is clear, and there are plenty of installation videos. If you're not confident, budget $200-400 for a plumber — this is one system you don't want to half-ass.

## The Verdict
