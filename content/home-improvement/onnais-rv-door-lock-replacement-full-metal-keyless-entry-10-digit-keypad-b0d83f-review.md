## What you actually get

The box contains the exterior keypad assembly, the interior lock mechanism, a deadbolt, mounting screws, and a rubber gasket for weather sealing. One key is included. The unit is designed to replace most standard RV door latches — the key dimension to check is the backset (the distance from the door edge to the center of the latch hole), which is 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" on most RVs. The ONNAIS accommodates both.

**Battery setup:** Two AAA batteries (not included) power the keypad. The interior side has a small LED indicator that flashes when battery is low. You program the codes using a sequence on the exterior keypad — up to two 10-digit user codes can be stored.

## Real-world performance

The keypad responds quickly. Touch the numbers, wait for the green light, turn the handle. It's smooth, and the deadbolt engages with a satisfying solid *thunk* that tells you it's seated. The handle action is spring-loaded, so it returns to position without sagging.

I've seen multiple reports of this lock surviving a full winter season without the mechanism freezing up — that's the benefit of the metal construction. Plastic locks get brittle below freezing, and the internal latch mechanism can bind. The ONNAIS doesn't have that problem.

One buyer noted: *"Installed on my 2021 Grand Design Imagine in about 20 minutes. The factory holes lined up perfectly, no drilling needed."* That tracks with what I've seen — the fitment is the strongest selling point here.

## Where it falls short

**The key override situation.** Some versions of this lock include a keyed cylinder on the exterior; others don't. If you get the version without the keyhole, a dead battery means you're locked out. The low-battery warning helps, but if you're boondocking for weeks at a time, carry spare AAAs or make sure your version includes the keyed backup.

**Dim keypad lighting.** In direct sunlight, the backlit numbers are hard to read. You'll memorize your code quickly, but if you're letting a friend or family member in, they'll struggle in bright conditions.

**The screws.** The included mounting screws are soft — several buyers stripped the Phillips heads during installation. Replace them with stainless steel #8 x 1" screws from the hardware store before you start. It'll save you a headache.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my RV door?**
Measure the backset and door thickness first. Standard RV latches use a 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" backset, and this lock accommodates both. Door thickness should be between 1-3/8" and 2". If your door matches those specs, it's a direct swap.

**Q: How do I change the code?**
The exterior keypad has a programming sequence. Enter your current code, press the program button (hidden behind a small cover), then enter your new code twice. The full manual is included, and it's straightforward — five minutes of fiddling and you'll have it memorized.

**Q: What happens when the batteries die?**
The interior LED gives you a low-battery warning several weeks in advance. If the batteries completely die, the version with the keyed cylinder can be opened with the physical key. The non-keyed version requires you to replace the batteries — which means you need to be inside or have the interior lock disengaged. Don't let that happen; change batteries annually.
