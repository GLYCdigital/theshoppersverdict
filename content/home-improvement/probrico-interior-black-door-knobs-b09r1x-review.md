Black hardware is having a moment, and Probrico's interior knob is the kind of product that makes you wonder why you ever paid $25 per knob at the big-box store. This is genuinely good hardware at a price that undercuts the name brands by half. But it's not perfect, and here's what you need to know before you buy a six-pack.

**What's actually in the box**

Each knob comes with the two knob halves, a rosette (that's the base plate), a latch assembly, a strike plate, and all the mounting screws. The latch is adjustable for both 2-3/8" and 2-3/4" backsets — just twist the mechanism to the right length, no tools needed. The knobs themselves are zinc alloy with a matte black powder-coat finish. At 2-3/8" diameter, they're slightly larger than standard residential knobs, which gives them a more substantial feel in hand.

**Real-world performance**

The first thing you notice is the weight. These aren't hollow, cheap-feeling knobs. The zinc alloy construction gives them a heft that suggests durability, and the matte finish has a nice, consistent texture that doesn't glare under hallway lighting. Installation took me about 10 minutes per door with a Phillips head screwdriver — no special tools required.

The turning action is smooth with just enough resistance to feel deliberate, and the latch retracts cleanly. The finish has held up well over several months of daily use; it resists fingerprints far better than the glossy black finishes I've tested. One buyer noted, "We replaced all the contractor-grade silver knobs in our house with these. The matte black looks way more expensive than what we paid." That matches my experience.

**Where it falls short**

The most common complaint I've seen echoed in the reviews and in my own testing: the strike plate screws are soft. They strip if you use a power driver on them without care. Use a manual screwdriver and you'll be fine, but know this going in.

Also, this specific listing is passage-only — no lock or privacy function. If you need bedroom or bathroom knobs with locking capability, make sure you're ordering the "privacy" variant. Several reviewers made this mistake, and the return process is annoying.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY homeowners** replacing all interior knobs at once. The price per knob makes a whole-house swap affordable.
- **Landlords** who want a modern look without the per-unit cost of Schlage or Kwikset.
- **Anyone matching matte black fixtures** — these pair well with black faucets, cabinet pulls, and light fixtures.

Skip it if you need keyed entry functionality, or if you're the type who prefers to torque down screws with an impact driver.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these fit pre-drilled standard doors?**
A: Yes, they fit standard 2-1/8" bore holes. The adjustable latch handles both common backsets, so no door modification is needed.

**Q: Will the black finish chip or scratch over time?**
A: The powder-coat finish is durable, but like any painted hardware, it can scratch if hit with something sharp. Normal daily use hasn't produced any wear in my testing.

**Q: Are these quiet?**
A: The latch is reasonably quiet, though not whisper-silent. There's a slight metallic click on close that's typical for this price point.

**The Verdict**

The Probrico interior knobs deliver 90% of the performance and look of knobs costing twice as much. The finish is excellent, the build quality is solid, and installation is straightforward. The soft screws and lack of locking function in this listing are real drawbacks, but neither is a dealbreaker if you know what you're buying.

**Buy it if** you want affordable, good-looking matte black hardware for a whole-house refresh. **Skip it if** you need locking functionality or you're a perfectionist about screw quality.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A strong value pick that punches above its price point.
