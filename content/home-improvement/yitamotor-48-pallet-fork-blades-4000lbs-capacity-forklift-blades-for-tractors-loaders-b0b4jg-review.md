## YITAMOTOR 48" Pallet Fork Blades Review: Cheap Forklift Attachments That Actually Work?

If you own a compact tractor or skid steer but don't want to drop $2,000 on a dedicated pallet fork frame, you've probably stared at these slide-on buckets. The YITAMOTOR 48" blades promise 4,000 lbs of capacity for a fraction of the cost. After digging through 225 ratings and user reports, here's the honest picture.

### What You Actually Get

The box contains two forged steel tines, each 48 inches long, with a formed bracket that slides over your existing loader bucket edge. No bolts, no holes to drill — you drop the bracket over the lip, and the weight of the load locks it down. There's also a pair of retaining pins that fit through pre-drilled holes to stop side-to-side drift. The blades themselves are 3.5 inches wide and 2 inches thick at the heel — that's beefy enough to make you trust the 4,000 lb rating, but only if your bucket edge is solid.

### Performance: Where It Excels and Where It Gets Dicey

The good news: for lifting pallets of bagged mulch, feed, or stacked lumber, these work exactly as advertised. The 48-inch length is the sweet spot — a standard 40x48 pallet sits fully supported with maybe an inch of overhang. The sharpened leading edge (it's tapered, not razor) slides under skids that have been sitting on dirt without much persuasion.

The catch is load distribution. That 4,000 lb capacity is *total* — split between both forks, and only if the load is centered. Multiple buyers report that off-center loads cause the bracket to twist, especially on thinner bucket edges. One reviewer noted: *"I moved 1,800 lbs of concrete pavers and the fork tilted slightly — had to re-center the pallet three times."* That's not a defect; it's physics. The slide-on design concentrates all force on the bucket lip, so a 1/4-inch steel edge will flex where a 3/8-inch edge won't.

### What Buyers Praise (and Quote)

The #1 recurring compliment is value. *"Cost me less than renting a forklift for a weekend,"* wrote one five-star reviewer. Others highlight the no-modification install: *"Took 10 minutes on my Kubota L3901 — didn't even need a second person."* The bolt-on load stops (two small L-brackets per fork) get specific thanks for keeping bags from sliding backward on uphill approaches.

### Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself

First, the finish is cosmetic. The black paint chips easily, and after a few months outdoors, you'll see surface rust on the tines. Not a structural issue, but if you're the type who oils your tools after every use, plan to repaint annually.

Second, the slide-on design has a real limitation: it raises your effective load height by the bucket depth. You'll lose about 4-5 inches of lift clearance compared to a dedicated fork frame. If your barn door is exactly 8 feet and your tractor maxes out just under that, measure twice.

Third — and this is the big one — the retaining pins are small. They prevent sideways drift but won't stop the whole assembly from sliding forward if you brake hard with a heavy load. Several buyers solved this with a single ratchet strap around the bucket. Do that from day one.

### Who Should Buy This

- **Small farm owners** moving occasional pallets of seed, feed, or hay — you'll use these 20 times a year, not 200.  
- **DIY builders** who need to unload a pallet of lumber or siding from a delivery truck.  
- **Budget-focused operators** who already own a loader bucket and can't justify a dedicated fork frame.  

Skip it if you run a commercial operation moving pallets daily, need precise hydraulic control (you can't tilt or level with these — they're static), or your bucket has a rounded or non-standard lip.

### FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Will these fit my John Deere 1025R or Kubota BX23?**  
Probably, if your bucket edge is straight and at least 4 inches deep. Measure the lip width first — the bracket opening is 4.5 inches. If your bucket is thinner, use a piece of angle iron as a shim.

**Can I use these with a skid steer?**  
Only if your skid steer has a bolt-on bucket edge. Most do not — they use a quick-attach plate. These are designed for compact tractors, not skid steers.

**How do I prevent the forks from sliding sideways?**  
Use the supplied pins, then add a ratchet strap across the top of the bucket as a backup. That combo eliminates drift even on rough terrain.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need an occasional pallet lifter for under $300 and your tractor has a standard bucket. For the price, the YITAMOTOR blades deliver surprising rigidity and real utility.

**Skip it if** you're lifting near max capacity daily, need tilt control, or expect automotive-grade paint. This is a farm tool, not a showpiece.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — the design is inherently limited, but it does exactly what it promises within those limits. For the occasional user, that's a great trade.
