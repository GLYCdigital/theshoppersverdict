## What's Actually in the Box

The Forensics VOC Detector ships with the handheld unit, a USB charging cable, and — notably — a NIST-traceable calibration certificate. That certificate matters. ## Real-World Performance

The unit uses a metal oxide semiconductor (MOS) sensor, which is the same technology found in most consumer VOC detectors. It reads total VOCs in parts per million (ppm) and displays a color-coded scale from green (safe) through yellow (caution) to red (danger). In practical testing, users report it catches elevated readings near fresh paint, adhesives, and even after heavy cleaning with chemical sprays.

One consistent thread across buyer feedback: "It detected my gas leak before the utility company did," reported one reviewer, though others note the readings fluctuate with humidity — a known limitation of MOS sensors. The USB data logging is a genuine differentiator. Plug it in, let it run for a few hours, and you get a CSV file showing exactly when VOC levels spiked. That's actionable data for troubleshooting a problem room.

## Where It Falls Short

The response lag is real. Moving from a clean room into a freshly painted one, expect 90 seconds or more before readings stabilize. That makes it poor for quick walk-through testing. The lack of an adjustable alarm is another miss — if you want it to alert you at a specific ppm threshold, you're out of luck. You'll be glancing at the screen.

Battery life sits around 8–10 hours, which sounds fine until you need overnight monitoring. The USB port handles that, but the cable is short (about 3 feet), so placement options are limited.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a homeowner investigating indoor air quality concerns, a landlord checking units between tenants, or a hobbyist woodworker using finishing products. The data logging alone justifies the price if you need to document air quality over time.

**Skip it if:** You need instant readings for quick assessments, or you want a device that alerts you audibly when thresholds are exceeded. Also skip if you're a professional requiring lab-grade precision — this is consumer-grade, not industrial.

## FAQ

**Q: How accurate is the NIST calibration really?**
A: It confirms the sensor was calibrated against NIST-traceable standards at the factory. For consumer use, readings are reliable within roughly ±10–15% — plenty for identifying problem areas, not precise enough for regulatory compliance.

**Q: Can this detect mold specifically?**
A: No. It measures total VOCs, and mold produces VOCs, but so do hundreds of other compounds. A high reading tells you something's off; it won't tell you it's specifically mold.

**Q: How often does it need recalibration?**
A: The manufacturer suggests annually. The device includes a calibration mode, but you'll need to send it in or purchase calibration gas — check current pricing before committing.
