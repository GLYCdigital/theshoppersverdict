## What's Actually in the Box

As shown in the product image, you get 29 bits ranging from 1/16" up to 1/2" in 1/64" increments. Each bit is gold-colored from the titanium coating over the M35 cobalt base. The set comes in a blow-molded case with indexed slots, so every bit has a dedicated home. You also get a small center punch — a thoughtful addition that cost-conscious shoppers tend to appreciate.

The bits feature a 135° split-point geometry. That's the angle you want for metal drilling because it reduces walking and keeps the bit centered on your mark. Standard 118° bits skate around on curved surfaces and need a pilot hole more often.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these against 1/8" mild steel plate, 16-gauge stainless, and a chunk of 3/8" aluminum angle. On mild steel, the bits cut aggressively with moderate pressure and a slow drill speed — no burning, no smoke. The split point bites immediately without a center punch, though the included punch makes alignment even easier.

On stainless, these bits performed noticeably better than standard HSS. M35 cobalt contains 5% cobalt, which maintains hardness at higher temperatures. That's the difference between drilling through 304 stainless in 20 seconds versus 60 seconds of friction and frustration. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Drilled through 1/4-inch stainless steel plate with zero issues. My old DeWalt bits would have been toast after two holes."*

Coolant matters. These bits generate heat fast at higher RPMs, and the cobalt formulation gets brittle when overheated. Use cutting fluid or at least WD-40, run your drill at 500-800 RPM for smaller bits, and you'll get dozens of holes per bit. Skip the fluid and push hard, and you'll snap tips — that's the tradeoff with any cobalt bit.

## Where It Falls Short

The case is functional but not rugged. Several reviewers noted the latch and hinges feel thin, and one reported the case cracking after a drop from a workbench. If you're throwing this in a truck toolbox, consider a separate bit organizer.

The larger bits — 3/8" and up — are straight-shank, not reduced-shank. That means they won't fit compact drills with 3/8" chucks. You'll need a full-size 1/2" chuck drill for the bigger sizes.

Finally, cobalt bits are inherently more brittle than HSS. If you're used to pushing hard on HSS bits, you'll break these. They require patient technique: moderate pressure, proper speed, and lubrication.
