## The Doorbell That Doesn't Steal Your Outlet

Most plug-in doorbells have one fatal flaw: they occupy your only accessible outlet. The TECKNET Wireless Doorbell solves that with a pass-through design — the receiver plugs into the wall and still leaves the front socket usable. It's a small detail that makes a real difference in hallways and kitchens where outlets are scarce. At 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, this isn't a niche product; it's a proven performer.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the plug-in receiver, one wireless transmitter button, mounting hardware (double-sided tape plus screws), and a user manual. The receiver is compact — roughly the size of a phone charger — and the button is IP44 water-resistant, which means it can handle rain and splashes, though not full submersion. The claimed range is 1,000 feet in open air, which is typical marketing math; expect a solid 200-300 feet through walls in a real home.

## Performance and Build Quality

The standout feature is the chime selection. TECKNET packed 58 distinct sounds into this unit — from classic ding-dong to seasonal holiday tunes. That's not a gimmick; it's practical. My neighbor uses the "Westminster" setting for the front door and a distinct "door knock" sound for the back, so you always know which entrance someone's at. Volume has four levels plus a boost mode, and on max, it's genuinely loud — audible from a basement workshop or upstairs bedroom.

Setup takes under five minutes. Plug in the receiver, pair the button (press and hold, then tap the receiver — it's instant), and you're done. No wiring, no hub, no app. The button uses a standard CR2032 battery that should last 1-2 years depending on traffic.

One thing I noticed in customer feedback: the build quality of the receiver feels solid, but the button has a plasticky, slightly hollow feel. It's not fragile by any means, but it doesn't have the premium heft of higher-end options like Ring.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Reviewers consistently highlight three things. First, the range — one verified buyer wrote, *"I have a long driveway and was skeptical, but it rings from the gate every single time."* Second, the volume control: *"Finally a doorbell I can hear from the back porch. The boost setting is a game changer."* Third, the pass-through outlet design gets frequent shout-outs in small apartments where every socket counts.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest limitation is the receiver's dependency on being plugged in. If you want the chime mounted on a wall away from outlets, you'll need an extension cord, which defeats the clean look. Also, a few reviewers mention that after a power flicker, the unit resets to the default chime — not a dealbreaker, but annoying if you've customized it. The button's size is another minor gripe; it's noticeably chunkier than slim competitors, which matters if you're mounting it on a narrow door frame.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters** who can't hardwire a doorbell and need a damage-free solution
- **Homeowners with large properties** who need a loud chime in multiple rooms
- **Anyone who's tired of losing an outlet** to a doorbell receiver

Skip it if you need a video doorbell (this is audio only) or if you want a battery-powered receiver you can place anywhere.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the transmitter work with multiple receivers?**
A: Yes. You can pair additional TECKNET receivers (sold separately) to the same button, so you can hear the doorbell on multiple floors.

**Q: How weatherproof is the outdoor button?**
A: It's IP44-rated — fine for rain and snow, but avoid direct, prolonged water exposure. Mount it under an eave or porch overhang for best durability.

**Q: Is the chime loud enough for a large house?**
A: With the boost feature on max volume, yes. Several reviewers with 2,500+ square foot homes confirmed they can hear it from the opposite end of the house.
