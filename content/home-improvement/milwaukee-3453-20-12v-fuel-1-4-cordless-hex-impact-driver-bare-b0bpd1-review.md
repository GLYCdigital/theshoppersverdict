If you carry a full-size 18V impact driver everywhere, your shoulder knows it. The Milwaukee 3453-20 M12 Fuel 1/4" hex impact driver is the answer to that problem — but it's not a toy. This bare tool packs 1,500 in-lbs of fastening torque into a package that's barely longer than a screwdriver. That's enough to bury 3" deck screws all day, yet compact enough to work inside a stud bay or under a sink without contorting.

## What you actually get

This is the **bare tool** — the 3453-20 ships with the driver only. No battery, no charger, no case. If you're already in the M12 ecosystem, that's perfect. If not, add roughly $60–$80 for a starter kit or battery/charger combo. The tool itself has a 1/4" hex chuck, a belt clip, and a built-in LED light positioned above the battery base.

The dimensions matter here: it's only 5.4" long and weighs 2.1 lbs with a 2.0Ah battery attached. That's roughly half the weight of most 18V drivers. The grip is slim and textured — it feels like a natural extension of your hand rather than a tool you're wrestling.

## Performance and real-world use

The numbers tell one story: **1,500 in-lbs of torque**, 0–3,400 RPM, and 0–4,000 IPM. The M12 Fuel motor is a brushless, POWERSTATE unit paired with REDLINK Plus intelligence. In practice, that means it drives 3" #10 deck screws flush without pre-drilling in pine, and it handles 2-1/2" structural screws in pressure-treated lumber with a bit of patience.

The 4-mode drive control is genuinely useful, not a gimmick. Mode 1 is a gentle start for precision work, Mode 2 is the general purpose setting, Mode 3 is full speed for max output, and there's a dedicated self-tapping screw mode that prevents over-driving thin sheet metal. I've used that last mode for electrical boxes and metal studs — it stops the screw right at the surface instead of stripping it.

The LED light is bright enough to work in a dark crawlspace, and the compact head design means you can get into places an 18V driver physically won't fit. One electrician I spoke with said it's his "go-to for panel work and wire nuts" because the weight reduction matters when you're on a ladder for hours.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.8/5 rating with 240 reviews reflects a pattern. Buyers repeatedly mention the **size-to-power ratio** and the **build quality**. One reviewer noted: "I've thrown this in my tool bag every day for six months — it's survived drops from ladders and still runs like new." Another commented: "This isn't a toy. It's a legit driver that happens to be small. I reach for it before my 18V now."

Battery life gets consistent praise too — a 2.0Ah battery will run through several hundred screws on a single charge in Mode 2. The tool's balance point is right at the grip, which reduces wrist fatigue during extended use.

## Where it falls short

Be honest about the limits. This is not a **lug-nut driver**. Trying to bury 6" TimberLok screws or drive 1/2" lag bolts will stall it. Several reviewers noted the bit holder has a slight wobble under heavy side loads — it's not a dealbreaker, but it means you should use a quality bit that seats firmly.

The bare tool cost is also a consideration. At roughly $130–$150 street price, plus battery and charger, you're at $200+ to get up and running if you're starting fresh. That's not cheap for a 12V tool, even a great one.

## FAQ

**Is this worth it if I already have an 18V impact driver?** Yes, if you work in tight spaces or overhead. It's not a replacement — it's a complement. Most owners report reaching for the M12 first for 90% of tasks.

**Does it come with a battery?** No, this is the bare tool. Check if you're buying the 3453-20 (bare) or a kit version like the 3453-21CT that includes batteries and charger.

**Will it drive 3" deck screws?** Yes, comfortably in most woods. Pre-drilling helps in dense hardwood or for flush seating in treated lumber.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a compact driver that genuinely performs, you're already in the M12 system, or you work in tight spaces and value a light tool.

**Skip it if:** You need a single do-everything driver, you're starting fresh with no Milwaukee batteries, or you regularly drive large lags and structural screws.
