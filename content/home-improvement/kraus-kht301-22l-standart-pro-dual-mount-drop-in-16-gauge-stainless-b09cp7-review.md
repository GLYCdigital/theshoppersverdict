# Kraus KHT301-22L Standart PRO Dual Mount Review: Is This 16-Gauge Workhorse Worth Your Kitchen Reno?

You've stared at the cabinet opening. Measured twice. And now you're sifting through hundreds of stainless steel sinks, all claiming to be "commercial grade" and "premium." Here's the shortcut: the Kraus KHT301-22L Standart PRO is one of the few sinks under $300 that actually delivers on the 16-gauge promise without cutting corners elsewhere.

I've analyzed the specs, dug through the 225 verified ratings, and pulled the real customer feedback. Here's what you need to know before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the sink itself, a cutout template, mounting clips for drop-in installation, a bottom rinse grid, a strainer, and a basket drain. The 22L designation means it's the large version — 33 inches wide, roughly 20 inches front-to-back, and 9.5 inches deep. That depth is the sweet spot: deep enough to hide a full stack of dinner plates, shallow enough that you're not bending awkwardly to reach the bottom.

The steel is T304 grade at a genuine 16-gauge thickness. If you're not familiar with gauge ratings, lower is thicker — 16-gauge is roughly 1.5mm, while the budget-friendly 18-gauge sinks are about 1.2mm. That extra 0.3mm translates to less denting, less oil-canning (those wavy distortions you see on thin stainless), and a sturdier overall feel.

## Real-World Performance

The brushed satin finish does what it claims. Water beads up and runs off rather than leaving spotty residue, and I've seen multiple reviewers confirm it resists fingerprints far better than the mirror-polished alternatives.

The SoundGuard system — a combination of rubber pads and sprayed-on undercoating — actually works. One reviewer noted, *"I was skeptical about the noise dampening, but running the garbage disposal doesn't sound like a construction site anymore."* That's consistent with what I've found: the undercoating covers roughly 80% of the exterior, which is better than many sinks at this price point.

The dual mount design is the standout feature. Most sinks force you to choose between drop-in or undermount at purchase time. Kraus gives you both options in one box. If you're remodeling and haven't finalized your countertop fabricator's template, this flexibility is a genuine lifesaver. You can start with a drop-in install and convert to undermount later if you change your mind.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The most repeated compliments in the reviews center on the thickness and the finish. One buyer wrote, *"This is my third stainless sink in 15 years. The difference in material quality is immediately obvious — it's solid, not flimsy."* Another praised the included grid: *"The bottom grid actually protects the sink from scratches. My previous sink looked terrible after two years; this one still looks new."*

The 9.5-inch depth gets frequent shoutouts too — tall users appreciate not having to hunch over, and short users can still reach the bottom comfortably.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The mounting clips for drop-in installation are functional but not foolproof. Several reviewers mentioned needing to re-tighten them after a few weeks of use as the sink settles. It's a 10-minute fix, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing going in.

The 33-inch width is a commitment. If your cabinet base is less than 36 inches, you'll be working with tight tolerances. Measure the actual interior width of your cabinet, not the nominal size — a 33-inch sink needs roughly 32.5 inches of clear opening, and you'll want at least 1.5 inches of clearance on each side for the clips.

The drain assembly included is functional but basic. If you're particular about premium hardware, you'll likely swap it out for a nicer strainer. That's an extra $20-30 you should budget for.

## Who Should Buy This

**The DIY renovator** — the dual mount design gives you flexibility if your countertop plans shift mid-project. The included template and clips are straightforward enough for a competent weekend installer.

**The family cook** — the deep basin handles big stockpots and sheet pans, and the noise dampening means you can wash up without waking the kids.

**Skip it if** you're working with a small sink base (under 33 inches), if you want a sink with a built-in drainboard, or if you're dead set on a farmhouse apron-front style.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the 16-gauge steel dent easily?**
A: No. The 16-gauge thickness is noticeably more rigid than the 18-gauge sinks. You'd need a deliberate hard impact to dent it, not just dropping a plate or pan.

**Q: Can I use this as an undermount sink if I bought it as drop-in?**
A: Yes — that's the whole point of the dual mount design. The sink has a rolled rim that works for both installation methods. Just be aware that undermount installation requires the countertop cutout to be precise, and you'll need to use a different mounting method (typically clips or adhesive) than what's in the box.

**Q: Does the brushed finish show scratches?**
A: Minor scratches from normal use are less visible than on polished stainless, but they do appear over time. The included bottom grid helps protect the basin floor. Most reviewers report the sink looks good for years with basic care.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely thick 16-gauge sink with installation flexibility, solid noise dampening, and a finish that won't drive you crazy with water spots. It's a legitimate upgrade over the flimsier 18-gauge options at comparable prices.

**Skip it if** you have a tight cabinet space, or if you're looking for a sink with additional features like a built-in workstation ledge or extra-small second basin.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Kraus KHT301-22L delivers on its core promise: a heavy-duty, well-finished stainless sink that performs like a premium product without the premium price tag. The minor gripes about mounting clips and the basic drain assembly are easily solved and shouldn't deter you from what is genuinely one of the best value sinks in this category.
