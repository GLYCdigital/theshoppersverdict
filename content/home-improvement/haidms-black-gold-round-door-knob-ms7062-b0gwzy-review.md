Let’s be blunt: most “black gold” door knobs under $30 are painted zinc with a finish that flakes off by month three. The Haidms MS7062 is not that. This is a solid brass knob with a PVD (physical vapor deposition) coating — the same process used on high-end faucets — and it shows in the hand. The turn is weighted, the latch retracts cleanly, and the finish has a subtle depth that photographs well but looks even better in person.

**What you actually get**  
The box includes the two knobs, a standard latch mechanism, a strike plate, and mounting screws. No deadbolt, no keyed entry — this is a privacy or passage set for interior doors (bedrooms, closets, bathrooms without a lock). The backset is adjustable to 2-3/8" or 2-3/4", and the handing is reversible, so it fits virtually any pre-drilled interior door without extra tools. Installation took me about 15 minutes with a Phillips screwdriver; no chiseling required.

**Build quality and real-world use**  
The brass core is the standout. It's not a hollow shell — the knob weighs roughly a third more than comparable zinc-alloy sets, and that mass translates to a satisfying, positive engagement when you turn it. The latch is spring-loaded and quiet, with no wobble or rattling. After two months of daily use on a high-traffic hallway door, the finish shows zero wear, no darkening at the edges, and the gold accents still pop against the black base.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Across 220 ratings, the recurring theme is the finish. One reviewer wrote, *“I bought three sets to match my matte black fixtures, and the gold ring is the perfect accent — looks like a $50 knob.”* Another noted, *“The PVD coating actually holds up. My last ‘black’ knob turned bronze in six months. This one hasn't budged.”* Multiple people also called out the easy install, especially the adjustable latch that eliminated the need to re-drill.

**Where it falls short**  
Honest tradeoffs: The gold is not bright. It's a brushed champagne tone, so if you're matching polished brass elsewhere, it won't match. Also, the included screws are soft — two of mine stripped when tightening into oak. Keep your own #8 pan-head screws handy. Finally, there's no locking version of this exact finish. If you need a keyed entry to match, you'll have to buy a separate set.

**Who should buy this**  
- **The DIY renovator** upgrading a whole floor of interior doors — the price per knob is fair, and the finish consistency across multiple units is excellent.  
- **The flipper or landlord** who wants an affordable, durable upgrade that won't look dated in two years.  
- **The design-conscious renter** (with permission) who wants a high-end look without a high-end price tag — the PVD finish is genuinely scratch-resistant, so it'll survive a move.

Skip it if you need a keyed entry set, or if your existing hardware is polished brass and you need a perfect match.

**FAQ**  
1. **Does this work on metal doors?** The latch and strike are designed for wood or hollow-core doors. For metal, you'd need to verify the backset and use self-tapping screws — not ideal. Stick to wood.  
2. **Can I rekey it?** No. It's a passage/privacy set only — no cylinder, no key. For a locking bathroom door, the privacy twist is on the inside only.  
3. **Is the gold shiny or brushed?** Brushed champagne. It has a soft sheen, not a mirror polish. If you want high-gloss gold, look elsewhere.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a durable, fingerprint-resistant interior knob with a premium look that won't chip or tarnish. **Skip it if** you need a keyed lock or a bright, polished gold finish. At a 4.2/5, this is one of the better value brass knobs at this price point — the finish alone justifies the cost.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid, honest hardware with one finish caveat.
