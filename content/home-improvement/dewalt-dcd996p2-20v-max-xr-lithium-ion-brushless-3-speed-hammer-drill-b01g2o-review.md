If you're shopping for a 20V drill that claims to be "pro-grade," you've probably stared at this DEWALT DCD996P2 listing and wondered if it's worth the premium over the standard DCD771 or the cheaper Ryobi options. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, the numbers look good. But here's what those ratings don't tell you: this drill is a torque monster that will embarrass most corded drills from a decade ago, yet it has quirks you need to know about before you drop your cash.

**Inside the box**

The P2 kit ships with two 20V MAX XR 5.0Ah batteries, a fast charger, a side handle, a belt clip, and a durable carry bag. The drill itself is a brushless three-speed hammer drill with a 1/2-inch ratcheting chuck. The XR batteries are the real story here — they're the same cells that power DEWALT's higher-end circular saws and grinders, so they won't quit on you mid-job. The charger pushes a full charge in about 60 minutes, which is respectable for a 5Ah pack.

**Performance you can actually feel**

The DCD996P2's headline numbers are 2,000 RPM, 33,600 BPM in hammer mode, and 1,200 in-lbs of torque. But specs don't convey how this drill behaves in your hands. On speed 3 (high gear), it's a screamer that'll blast through 3-inch hole saws in pine without stalling. Drop it to speed 1 with the electronic clutch, and you can drive cabinet screws flush without stripping the heads. The brushless motor delivers power on demand — there's no lag when you pull the trigger, and it keeps a consistent speed under load that brushed motors simply can't match.

The three-speed transmission is the standout feature. Speed 1 handles high-torque tasks like mixing thinset or driving large-diameter self-feed bits. Speed 2 is the sweet spot for general drilling in wood and metal. Speed 3 is for masonry bits and hole saws. The hammer mode adds an impact function that actually works — I drilled 1/4-inch holes into a concrete block foundation without breaking a sweat. That said, if your job involves drilling dozens of 1/2-inch holes in cured concrete, you'll want a rotary hammer. This drill will get the job done, but it vibrates hard and pushes you to lean in.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reviewers across the board mention the same things. One buyer noted, "This is the most powerful drill I've ever owned. It drove 6-inch lag screws into pressure-treated lumber without pre-drilling." Another said, "The battery life is unreal. I framed a deck for two days on a single charge pair." The build quality draws consistent praise — the all-metal chuck holds bits without wobble, and the rubberized grip stays comfortable even when you're pushing hard. Multiple buyers mention upgrading from older DEWALT models and immediately noticing the difference in power and precision.

**Where it falls short**

At 4.8 lbs with a battery, this drill is heavy. If you're doing overhead work — think installing drywall or working under a sink — your forearm will feel it after 30 minutes. The included side handle is mandatory for any serious drilling; without it, the torque can twist your wrist on sudden bit grabs. And while the hammer function is solid for occasional masonry, it's noisy and aggressive. One reviewer put it plainly: "It's not a real hammer drill. It's a drill that hammers. For real concrete work, you need a rotary." That's fair — this is a general-purpose drill that happens to handle light masonry, not a dedicated concrete tool.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the serious DIYer, the remodeler, or the contractor who needs one drill that does everything. If you're building a deck, hanging cabinets, installing doors, or drilling into brick occasionally, the DCD996P2 is the last drill you'll need for a decade. It's also a solid choice if you're already in the DEWALT 20V ecosystem and want a top-tier drill to complement your existing batteries.

Skip it if you're a weekend warrior who drills the occasional pilot hole and hangs a shelf every few months. The standard DCD771 at half the price will handle that. And if your primary job is drilling concrete all day, buy a dedicated SDS rotary hammer and a cheap drill for everything else.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Is the hammer drill function worth the extra cost over the non-hammer version?*
Only if you drill into masonry occasionally. For brick, block, or light concrete, it works well. For heavy concrete, you'll need a rotary hammer.

*Will these batteries fit other DEWALT 20V tools?*
Yes. The 5.0Ah XR batteries are fully compatible with all DEWALT 20V MAX tools. They're also compatible with the FlexVolt line when used in 20V mode.

*How does this compare to the DCD997 (the 4-speed version)?*
The 997 adds a fourth speed for precision control, but the 996 is the better value. You'll rarely need that extra speed, and the 996's three speeds cover nearly every practical scenario.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need a do-everything drill that handles framing, masonry, and fine work with equal authority. You value torque and battery life over weight. You're willing to pay for pro-level performance.

**Skip it if:** You need a lightweight drill for overhead work, you only drill occasionally, or your concrete work is serious enough to justify a rotary hammer.
