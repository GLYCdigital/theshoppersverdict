If you've ever fumbled a 10mm nut into an engine bay, you already understand why magnetic nut setters exist. The Wiha 70486 takes that concept and adds a visual organization system that actually works in practice. But at roughly double the price of generic alternatives, does the color-coding justify the premium? After digging through 200 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains six nut setters: 5.5mm, 6mm, 7mm, 8mm, 10mm, and 13mm. Each has a 1/4-inch hex shank that fits standard impact drivers and drill chucks. The magnetic tip holds fasteners firmly — Wiha rates the retention strength high enough for overhead work, and user feedback confirms this holds up.

The color-coding isn't just painted on. Each setter has a colored band around the shank, and the included storage case has matching color slots. As one reviewer put it, "I can grab the red one for 10mm without reading anything. It's saved me time on every job since I bought these."

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

These are machined from chrome-vanadium steel with precision-ground broaching. That matters more than you'd think. Cheap nut setters often have sloppy tolerances that round off fastener heads on stubborn bolts. The Wiha broaching engages the full hex surface, distributing torque evenly. Multiple buyers noted they've used these daily for months with zero wear on the drive ends.

The magnetic retention deserves specific praise. Unlike some magnetic setters where the magnet is so strong it's hard to release the fastener, Wiha struck a balance. Nuts stay put during placement but release cleanly once seated. One reviewer noted, "I drove 200 screws with these in a ceiling install and dropped exactly zero fasteners."

## Where It Falls Short

The size gaps are the biggest frustration. There's no 9mm, 11mm, or 12mm — and 12mm is common on automotive and appliance work. You'll likely need a second set to cover the full range, which eats into the value proposition.

The 2.5-inch length also limits reach. In recessed holes or deep-set fasteners, you'll need an extension. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Finally, the price. Wiha charges a premium for the brand name and German engineering. If you're a weekend DIYer who needs one size for one job, a $10 generic set gets the job done. These make sense for professionals or serious hobbyists who use them daily.
