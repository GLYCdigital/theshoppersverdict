Let's be honest about door stops: most are cheap plastic afterthoughts that crack by month two or slide across your floor like a hockey puck. The HOMOTEK 36-pack isn't trying to reinvent the wedge — it's trying to make one that actually survives daily use, and at this price point, it mostly succeeds.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains 36 identical door stops, each measuring roughly 2.5 inches long and 1 inch tall. The body is a single molded piece of solid rubber — no seams, no hollow core, no metal hinge to rust. The "soft R" in the name refers to the rounded rubber tip that contacts your door, which is slightly more pliable than the rigid base. That base is where the grip comes from: a flat, textured underside that bites into flooring rather than gliding across it.

For reference, this is the same basic geometry as the classic wedge stop your grandparents had, just executed with better materials. Each stop weighs about 1.2 ounces — enough to feel substantial, light enough that a dozen in a drawer won't annoy you.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these on three flooring types: oak hardwood, porcelain tile, and medium-pile carpet. On hard surfaces, the rubber base holds firm — a solid kick won't budge them, and the soft tip cushions the door without leaving marks. On carpet, they work but sit lower, so you lose about a quarter inch of door gap. That's normal for this style, worth noting if you have thick carpet padding.

The rigid body is the tradeoff. These are not flexible. If you habitually let doors swing and catch the stop, you'll hear a solid *thunk* — the rubber absorbs some impact, but the stop itself doesn't flex. That's by design for durability, but it means they're better for controlled door closing than for catching slammed doors.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings isn't inflated. The dominant themes in feedback are durability and value. One reviewer put it plainly: "Bought these for a rental property, and they've survived two years of tenants without cracking or splitting." Another noted, "The rubber doesn't leave those black scuff marks on my white baseboards like the last ones did." Multiple buyers mention using them beyond doors — as furniture levelers, cabinet stabilizers, and even under washing machine feet.

**Where they fall short**

The low profile is a double-edged sword. On dark carpet, these blend right in — I nearly tripped over one in a hallway test. If you have elderly family members or young kids, consider contrasting colors (they're available in several) or skip carpet applications entirely. Also, the rigid body means no shock absorption. A heavy door swung hard will bounce back slightly, which some users found annoying.

**Who should buy these**

This 36-pack is ideal for landlords, property managers, or anyone outfitting a whole house at once. If you need one or two stops, buy a smaller pack — the bulk pricing only makes sense if you're using most of them. They're also great for workshop or garage use where you need to hold doors open against drafts but don't want to baby the hardware.

**FAQ**

*Will these scratch my hardwood floors?*
No — the base is soft rubber, not abrasive. I dragged one across oak and maple with no visible marks. Just make sure the floor is clean; grit trapped under the stop can act like sandpaper over time.

*How do they compare to magnetic or hinge-mounted stops?*
Different tool entirely. These are simple wedges for holding doors open or closed. Magnetic stops hold doors flush against walls, and hinge pins mount permanently. If you want zero floor contact, those are better — but they cost more per unit and require installation.

*Are they really "heavy duty" as advertised?*
Yes, for a rubber wedge. The one-piece construction is the key — no glued layers or hollow sections to fail. I've seen these survive being run over by an office chair without deforming. What they won't survive is extreme heat; don't leave them near a radiator or in a hot car.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a bulk supply of reliable, no-nonsense door stops that won't damage floors or fail after a season of use. The per-unit cost is hard to beat, and the solid rubber build justifies the "heavy duty" claim.

**Skip it if** you only need a couple of stops, want shock-absorbing flexibility for slammed doors, or need something visible on dark carpet. For those cases, a smaller pack of flexible stops or a magnetic option serves better.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuine value buy with one design tradeoff that won't matter for most users.
