## What you actually get

The box contains the stainless steel drain body, the matte black square grate, a hair catcher basket, a hex key for the set screws, and an installation manual that's actually readable. The drain body measures roughly 4.7 x 4.7 inches at the flange, with the grate sitting at 4 inches square. The outlet is a standard 2-inch ABS connection, which means it glues directly to your existing PVC or ABS drain pipe without a reducer.

One thing I noticed immediately from the product photos: the matte black finish isn't a painted coating — it looks like a physical vapor deposition (PVD) finish, which is why it holds up better than the $15 drains that flake after six months.

## Build quality and real-world installation

The stainless steel body feels substantial in hand — not flimsy sheet metal. The flange is thick enough that it won't flex when you're pressing tile mortar around it. The grate sits recessed about 1/8 inch below the tile surface, which is the correct height for proper drainage without creating a tripping hazard.

Installation is straightforward if you're comfortable with basic plumbing: glue the body to your drain pipe, set it in mortar, tile up to the flange, then screw the grate down with the included hex screws. The set screws are a nice touch — they keep the grate firmly in place instead of relying on a friction fit that loosens over time.

**The one caveat:** this drain has no weep holes. If you're using a bonded waterproofing membrane like Schluter Kerdi or a liquid membrane that needs drainage at the drain flange, you'll need to either add weep holes yourself or pick a different drain. This matters more for curbless showers and wet rooms than for standard pan installations.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

First, removing the grate for cleaning is more difficult than it should be. The set screws hold tight — maybe too tight. Several reviewers mention needing to pry the grate up with a screwdriver, which risks scratching the finish if you're not careful. A small suction cup tool would've been a thoughtful inclusion.

Second, the matte coating is durable but not bulletproof. One reviewer reported a chipped corner after dropping a glass shampoo bottle on it. It's a shower floor — things get dropped. Just know the finish can mar if you're careless.

Third, as mentioned, no weep holes. This isn't a flaw for every installation, but it does limit compatibility with certain waterproofing systems. If you're not sure whether you need weep holes, ask your tile supplier before buying.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this drain fit a standard 2-inch PVC pipe?**
Yes. The outlet is 2-inch ABS, which glues directly to standard 2-inch PVC or ABS drain pipe. No adapter needed.

**Q: Can I use this with a Schluter Kerdi shower system?**
Technically yes, but the lack of weep holes means water trapped in the mortar bed can't escape. Schluter makes specific drains with weep holes for this purpose. If you're using Kerdi, buy the Kerdi drain instead.

**Q: How do I clean the hair catcher?**
The basket lifts out from underneath the grate. You'll need to unscrew the two hex bolts and pop the grate off. It's not tool-free, but cleaning takes about two minutes once you get the hang of it.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a durable, good-looking square drain for a standard tile shower and don't mind the slightly fiddly grate removal. The finish quality at this price point is genuinely impressive, and the build is solid enough for decades of use.

**Skip it if** you need weep holes for a membrane system, or if you want tool-free access to the hair catcher. For those cases, look at linear drains or models specifically designed for bonded waterproofing.
