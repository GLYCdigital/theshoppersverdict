# Bathroom Sink Faucet 4 Inch 2 Handle Centerset Lead-Free: The No-Nonsense Pick for Standard 3-Hole Sinks  

If your bathroom sink has three holes spaced exactly 4 inches apart, you’ve just found the faucet that makes sense. This centerset model skips the gimmicks and delivers what matters: lead-free materials, solid handles, and a fit that doesn’t require a deck plate or a plumber’s degree. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it’s clearly earning its place in real bathrooms — but let’s talk about whether it’s right for yours.

## What You Actually Get  

In the box: the faucet body (already assembled), two handles, a pop-up drain assembly with a lift rod, and mounting hardware. That’s it. No supply lines — a common omission at this price point, so budget an extra $8–12 for two 3/8-inch compression hoses if you don’t have spares. The chrome finish is bright and even, and the lead-free brass construction is stamped right on the body, which matters if you’re replacing an older fixture that might have questionable metallurgy.

## Real-World Performance  

Installation is the star here. The 4-inch centerset design means the spout and handles sit on a single base, so you’re not aligning three separate pieces. Hand-tighten the mounting nuts, connect your supply lines, and you’re done — most buyers report under 30 minutes. The quarter-turn ceramic cartridges are the right call: they’re smooth, precise, and won’t wear out like rubber washers. I’ve tested faucets at this price that feel wobbly after a week; this one stays rock-solid.

Water flow is consistent at 1.2 GPM — enough for hand-washing without splashing your counter. The spout height (about 5.5 inches) clears most standard vessels, and the reach is adequate for a typical 16-inch-wide sink. One buyer noted, *“Replaced a 20-year-old faucet and this thing feels like an upgrade — no drips, handles turn with a satisfying click.”* Another praised the finish: *“Three months in, still looks like day one. No spotting, no tarnish.”*

## Where It Falls Short  

Two honest gripes. First, the plastic aerator. It works fine — you’ll get a steady, aerated stream — but if you’re used to brass-threaded aerators, this feels like a cost-cutting move. It’s replaceable with a standard size, so not a dealbreaker. Second, the pop-up drain’s lift rod is slightly thin. It functions, but if you’re heavy-handed, you might bend it over time. Neither issue affects daily use, but they’re worth knowing.

Also, this faucet is *only* for 4-inch centers. If your sink has 6-inch or 8-inch spacing, skip it — you’ll need a wider model or a deck plate.

## Who Should Buy This  

- **DIY homeowners** replacing a worn-out centerset faucet — the install is genuinely beginner-friendly.  
- **Landlords or rental property owners** who want a durable, lead-free fixture that won’t generate complaint calls.  
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who refuse to sacrifice material quality for price — this is brass, not zinc alloy.  

Skip it if you need a single-handle design, a taller spout for filling pitchers, or if your sink uses non-standard spacing.

## FAQ  

**Q: Will this fit my existing 3-hole sink?**  
A: Measure the distance from the center of the left hole to the center of the right hole. If it’s exactly 4 inches, yes — this drops in with no adapter. If it’s 6 or 8 inches, it won’t fit.

**Q: Does it come with water supply lines?**  
A: No. You’ll need two 3/8-inch compression supply lines. They’re standard and available at any hardware store for under $10 total.

**Q: Is the finish really lead-free?**  
A: The body is lead-free brass, certified to NSF/ANSI 61 standards. The chrome plating is applied over that, so you’re getting safe water contact, not just a coating.
