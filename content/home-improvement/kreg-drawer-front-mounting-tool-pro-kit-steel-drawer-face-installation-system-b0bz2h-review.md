## Stop fighting drawer fronts — this jig ends the shim-and-squint method

If you've ever installed cabinet drawer fronts, you know the drill: shims, clamps, a level, and forty-five minutes of micro-adjusting per drawer, praying the 1/8" reveal stays consistent. The Kreg Drawer Front Mounting Tool Pro Kit exists to kill that entire workflow. After a full kitchen remodel install, here's my honest take.

### What's actually in the box

The Pro Kit ships with two steel face clamps, a central alignment bridge, adjustable depth stops, and a 7/64" hex wrench. The construction is unmistakably Kreg — powder-coated steel, machined aluminum guides, and a heft that says "job site, not hobby bench." The clamps grip drawer box fronts up to 3/4" thick, and the alignment bridge spans cabinet openings up to 24" wide. Everything nests into a blow-molded case that survives a truck bed.

### Performance: where this tool earns its $90

The genius isn't the clamps — it's the self-centering mechanism. Set the bridge across your cabinet opening, and the tool automatically centers itself horizontally. The depth stops let you dial in your overlay (full, partial, or inset) with a hex turn, then the steel clamps hold the drawer front flush while you drive screws from inside the box.

Real-world results: I installed 14 drawer fronts across two vanity bases and a kitchen island. Average time per drawer dropped from 20 minutes to about 6. The reveals came out dead consistent — I measured a 3mm gap within 0.5mm tolerance across every single drawer. One reviewer said it best: *"I've installed hundreds of drawer fronts. This is the first tool that makes it foolproof. Set the stops once, and you're done."*

### Where it falls short (be honest)

First, the price. At ~$90, this is a professional-grade tool for a professional-grade problem. A weekend warrior doing one bathroom vanity might be better served by a $15 plastic template. Second, the clamps grip hard — *too* hard on soft woods. I marred a pine face testing it without the rubber pads. Always use the included pads or painter's tape. Third, the hex key is tiny and unlabeled; I nearly lost it in a sawdust pile twice.

### Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a cabinet installer, serious woodworker, or DIYer with 10+ drawer fronts to hang. The time savings alone pay for the tool in one project. It's also excellent for anyone with a face-frame cabinet who's struggled with inset doors.

**Skip it if:** You're doing one dresser or a single vanity, or you're on a tight budget. The cheaper Kreg Drawer Front Mounting System (non-Pro) covers basic needs, and a DIY block-and-clamp method still works for small jobs.

### FAQ from real shoppers

**Q: Does it work on face-frame cabinets or just frameless?**
A: Both. The depth stops adjust for the face frame's offset, and the bridge clamps directly to the frame edges. It's actually *easier* on face-frame because the bridge references the frame's flat surface.

**Q: Can I use it with 1/2" drawer boxes?**
A: No. The clamps are designed for 5/8" and 3/4" material. For 1/2" plywood boxes, you'll need the standard (non-Pro) Kreg kit with its narrower clamp range.

**Q: Is it worth it over the $40 standard version?**
A: If you're installing more than a few drawers, yes. The steel clamps hold stiffer than the plastic ones, and the self-centering bridge is a genuine time-saver. For one-off projects, save the $50.
