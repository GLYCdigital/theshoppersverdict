# SUNHZMCKP Magnetic Screwdriver Set 66-Piece: The Budget Set That Punches Above Its Weight

You can spend $80 on a German-brand screwdriver set that lives in a foam-lined case, or you can spend a fraction of that on the SUNHZMCKP 66-piece kit and get 90% of the performance. For most home DIYers and weekend warriors, this set is the smarter buy—but it's not without a couple of compromises worth knowing about before you click "add to cart."

## What's in the Box

This is a complete kit, not a teaser. You get one ergonomic driver handle with a magnetic quick-release chuck, a 1/4-inch hex bit holder, and 64 bits ranging from #0 to #3 Phillips, slotted sizes 3mm to 6mm, Torx T10 through T40, hex sizes 1.5mm to 6mm, plus security bits (Torx with holes, spanner, and tri-wing) for electronics repair. A 6-inch extension bar would've been nice, but it's not in the case—more on that below.

The bits are organized in a clear, double-sided plastic case with labeled drive types. It's not a premium blow-molded box, but it keeps everything visible and in place. The case measures roughly 9 x 5 x 2 inches, so it slides into a tool drawer without hogging space.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

Here's where this set separates itself from the $15 Amazon specials. The bits are forged from S2 alloy steel, not the cheaper chrome-vanadium (CR-V) that bends under heavy torque. S2 is what professional mechanics expect from brands like Wera and Wiha—it's harder, resists cam-out (when the bit slips out of the screw head), and holds its edge longer. In practice, that means you can bear down on a stubborn #2 Phillips without rounding the tip.

The magnetic strength is genuinely useful. The bit holder grips 1/4-inch bits firmly, and the driver tip holds screws well enough that you can start them one-handed in awkward angles. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"The magnet is strong enough to hold a screw vertically, which I wasn't expecting at this price."* Another praised the ergonomics: *"The handle is comfortable for prolonged use—better grip than my old Craftsman."*

I tested this set driving deck screws and assembling a metal shelving unit. The bits seated cleanly, and the handle's rubberized grip provided solid traction even with oily hands. The knurled metal collar on the chuck makes quick bit swaps genuinely one-handed—a small detail that speeds up work.

## Where It Falls Short

The case is the weakest link. The hinge pins are thin plastic, and the latch requires a firm press to close securely. If you're rough with your tools, this case won't survive years of abuse. Plan on transferring bits to a dedicated organizer eventually—or handle the case gently.

The absence of an extension bit holder is a real miss. Reaching a screw set 3 inches deep in a furniture frame or appliance panel is frustrating without one. You'll need to buy a 6-inch magnetic bit extender separately (they're cheap, but it's an extra purchase).

Also, the bit labels on the case are printed, not molded. After heavy use, they may smear. Minor, but worth noting.

## FAQ

**Q: Are the bits actually magnetic, or just the holder?**
Both. The bits themselves have some magnetic retention, and the driver tip adds extra pull. Small screws stick to the bit tip directly.

**Q: Will this work with a power drill?**
No. This is a manual driver set only. The bits are 1/4-inch hex, so you *can* use them in a drill chuck, but the handle isn't designed for power use.

**Q: How does S2 steel compare to CR-V?**
S2 is noticeably harder and more brittle, which is ideal for screwdriver bits—they resist twisting and wear longer. CR-V is more flexible and better for general wrenches. For bits, S2 wins.
