# Smart Lock Review: The Fingerprint-First Deadbolt That Gets the Basics Right

Most smart locks try to do too much. They pack in cameras, doorbells, and so many app features you spend more time troubleshooting than actually locking your door. This Smart Lock (model B0FQCPMTMY) takes a different approach: nail the core deadbolt experience, make it genuinely fast to use, and skip the gimmicks. With a 4.3-star average across 215 ratings, it's clearly resonating with buyers. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the full deadbolt assembly — exterior keypad unit, interior turnpiece, latch, strike plate, and mounting hardware. The fingerprint sensor sits flush on the exterior keypad, and there's a traditional key cylinder hidden beneath a sliding cover. That physical key override is a big deal. Many competitors at this price point omit it entirely, leaving you dependent on a 9V battery jump or app backup. The package also includes two physical keys and a quick-start guide that's actually readable — no dense manual required.

## Installation: Easier Than You'd Expect

If you've replaced a deadbolt before, you can install this. The lock fits standard 2-3/8" and 2-3/4" backsets, and the included template marks the drill points clearly. One reviewer noted, "Took me 20 minutes with a screwdriver, and I'm not handy at all." The only tool you'll need is a Phillips screwdriver. The interior plate mounts with two screws, and the wiring harness connects with a simple plug — no soldering or wire nuts.

## Real-World Performance: Where It Shines and Stumbles

The fingerprint reader is the standout feature. It's capacitive, not optical, which means it reads your fingerprint's ridges rather than a photo of it. In practice, that translates to reliable unlocks even with dirty hands or after a day at the beach. One verified buyer wrote: "My fingers are often cracked from manual labor. This reads them 9 times out of 10, which is better than any other lock I've tried." Recognition takes about 0.8 seconds — noticeably faster than the 2-3 second lag on older models.

Battery life is rated at 8-12 months with normal use (about 10 unlocks per day). Four AA batteries power the unit, and a low-battery warning appears in the app well before failure. When the batteries do die, the key override saves you — provided you have the key on you. That's the one vulnerability: if you rely solely on the fingerprint and keypad, you'll need to keep a backup key somewhere accessible.

The app is functional but basic. You can create up to 50 user codes, assign temporary access for guests or service workers, and view lock/unlock history. It pairs via Bluetooth for setup, then connects over 2.4GHz Wi-Fi for remote access. Here's the catch: **no 5GHz support**. If your home relies on a mesh system broadcasting both bands under one SSID, you'll need to split the networks or position a compatible access point nearby. Several reviewers flagged this as their main frustration, with one noting: "Great lock, but I had to dig out an old router to make it work."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The pattern across positive reviews is clear: speed, reliability, and the physical key backup. Owners repeatedly mention how guests adapt quickly without instruction — the fingerprint sensor is intuitive enough that kids and elderly family members use it without a tutorial. The auto-lock feature, while not customizable, does engage reliably after 30 seconds, which gives peace of mind for anyone who's ever driven away wondering if they locked the door.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the Wi-Fi band limitation, the auto-lock timing is the biggest annoyance. It's fixed at 30 seconds — no way to adjust it to 10 or 60 seconds. If you step out to grab something from your car and close the door, it locks behind you. That's a minor inconvenience, but it's an unnecessary one. The lock also lacks HomeKit support, so iPhone users can't control it via Siri or include it in Home automations. Alexa and Google Home work fine, but Apple ecosystem integration is absent.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters** who want smart access without hardwiring — this installs over the existing deadbolt hole and removes cleanly
- **Families with kids** who lose keys — fingerprint access eliminates the "I'm locked out" problem
- **Anyone with a 2.4GHz network** who wants a reliable, no-subscription smart lock

## Skip It If

- Your home is on a 5GHz-only network or you can't split bands
- You're deeply invested in Apple HomeKit and need Siri integration
- You want granular auto-lock timing or geofencing-based triggers

## FAQ

**Q: Can I still use my physical key if the fingerprint sensor or batteries fail?**
A: Yes. The key cylinder is mechanical and works independently of the electronics. You'll always have a manual way in, provided you carry the key.

**Q: Does the lock work with existing deadbolt holes?**
A: It fits standard 2-3/8" and 2-3/4" backsets with a 1" or 1-1/2" latch bore. Most U.S. doors already have these dimensions. If you're unsure, measure the distance from the door edge to the center of the hole.

**Q: How many fingerprints and codes can I store?**
A: Up to 50 fingerprints and 50 user codes. That's plenty for a household, and you can assign temporary codes that expire automatically.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a fast, dependable smart lock with a physical key backup and don't mind the 2.4GHz requirement. **Skip it if** you're on a 5GHz-only network or need HomeKit integration. At 4.2/5, this is a solid mid-tier choice that prioritizes the fundamentals over flashy features — and for most households, that's exactly what you want in a door lock.
