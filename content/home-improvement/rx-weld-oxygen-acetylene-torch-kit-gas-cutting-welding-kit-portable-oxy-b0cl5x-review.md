Let me be straight with you: buying a cheap oxy-acetylene kit on Amazon feels like a gamble. Gas torches can kill you if assembled wrong, and the market is flooded with no-name kits that look identical in photos but fall apart in practice. The RX WELD kit sits in that crowded middle ground. ## What you actually get

The kit ships with the essentials: a forged brass torch handle, a cutting attachment with a 90-degree head, two welding tips (sizes 1 and 3), one cutting tip (size 2), oxygen and acetylene regulators, and a 20-foot twin hose set. The regulators are color-coded — green for oxygen, red for acetylene — with 0-4000 PSI and 0-400 PSI gauges respectively. That's a complete setup, nothing hidden. You'll still need tanks, a cart, and safety glasses, but you'll be cutting metal within an hour of unboxing.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The torch body is machined brass with a polished chrome finish. It's heavier than a $40 hardware store special — noticeably more substantial in hand. The needle valves turn smoothly, no gritty feel or sticking. Seats are cleanly cut. This matters because a poorly seated valve on an acetylene line is how fires start.

Cutting performance is where this kit earns its keep. Owners consistently report clean, fast cuts on 1/4-inch to 1/2-inch mild steel. One reviewer said, "Cut through 3/8 plate like butter once I dialed in the pressures." The cutting tip produces a sharp, concentrated preheat flame that doesn't wander. For welding, the #1 and #3 tips handle thin gauge sheet metal up to about 1/8-inch comfortably. You're not going to weld heavy structural steel with this, and you shouldn't try.

## What buyers rave about

The value proposition is the consistent theme across positive reviews. "This replaced a Victor setup I sold for twice what I paid for this kit. Honestly can't tell the difference in daily use," wrote one verified buyer. Another mentioned, "The regulators hold steady pressure without drifting — my old Harris units creeped all over the place." Multiple users noted the ergonomic grip is comfortable for extended cutting sessions, which is a thoughtful touch at this price point.

## Where it falls short

The hoses are the weakest link. They're PVC-lined and get stiff in cold conditions — one Minnesota user reported wrestling with them in a garage during winter. The 3/8-inch fittings sometimes need extra persuasion to seat properly; keep PTFE thread tape handy. More concerning: **there are no flashback arrestors included.** For a kit marketed to hobbyists, that's a glaring safety omission. You should absolutely add a set before first use — they're cheap insurance against a flashback traveling back into your regulators or tanks.

Some users also reported the cutting tip needs frequent cleaning to maintain a crisp flame pattern. That's normal maintenance for any oxy-fuel setup, not a defect, but worth knowing.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a weekend fabricator, auto restorer, or metal artist who needs a reliable torch for cutting, brazing, and light welding. The price-to-performance ratio is exceptional, and the brass components will outlast the cheap aluminum kits at similar price points.

**Skip it if:** You're a professional welder running daily production. The lack of flashback arrestors and the stiff hoses will annoy you, and you'll want a name-brand setup with swivel fittings and lifetime warranties.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this kit work with standard US tanks?**
Yes. The regulators use CGA-540 (oxygen) and CGA-510 (acetylene) fittings, which are the standard connections for all US welding supply tanks.

**Q: Can I weld aluminum with this?**
No. Oxy-acetylene welding aluminum requires specialized flux and technique, and the included tips aren't designed for it. Stick to steel and stainless.

**Q: What pressure settings should I start with?**
Set oxygen to 25 PSI and acetylene to 10 PSI for cutting 1/4-inch steel, then adjust based on your preheat flame. Never exceed 15 PSI on acetylene — it becomes unstable above that.
