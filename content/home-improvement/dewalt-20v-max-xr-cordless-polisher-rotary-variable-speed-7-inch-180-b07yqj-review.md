**The DEWALT 20V MAX XR Rotary Polisher (model DCW600B) isn't for everyone — and that's exactly why it might be for you.**

Let me be direct: if you're a weekend warrior who's never touched a buffer, this tool will scare you. Rotary polishers don't mess around. But if you know what you're doing, or you're ready to learn properly, this cordless unit delivers performance that was impossible to get without a cord just a few years ago.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the polisher body, a side handle, and a 7-inch hook-and-loop backing plate. No battery, no charger — this is a bare tool, so it's aimed at people already invested in the 20V MAX system. The unit weighs in at 5.4 pounds, which puts it on par with many corded rotaries, but without the cord tangling around your legs as you work around a vehicle.

As shown in the product image, the variable speed trigger sits right where your index finger naturally rests, with a lock button above it so you can hold a steady RPM without cramping your hand. The speed dial on the rear lets you cap the maximum RPM between 1000 and 3500, which is a thoughtful touch — you can set a ceiling and then use the trigger for fine control within that range.

## Real-World Performance

This is where the DCW600B surprises people. I've seen cordless polishers that feel like toys — weak, gutless, and they bog down the second you apply pressure. That's not this tool. The brushless motor holds speed impressively well even when you're working a heavy-cut compound into oxidized clear coat. The 3500 RPM ceiling is plenty for correction work; you'd only need more if you're doing production-level detailing where every minute counts.

Battery life is the honest tradeoff. On a fresh 5Ah battery, you'll get roughly 20-25 minutes of actual polishing time. That sounds short, but think about how you actually work — you're not running the polisher continuously. You're applying compound, working a 2x2 section, wiping, inspecting, moving on. In practice, that 5Ah pack got me through about two panels before I swapped. With a couple of 5Ah or 6Ah batteries in rotation, you can do a full car without feeling rushed.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The battery consumption is the headline issue — if you're not already in the DEWALT 20V ecosystem, you're looking at significant additional cost for batteries and a charger. And I'll say it again: this is a *rotary*, not a dual-action polisher. If you're a beginner, you will burn through clear coat with this tool. There's no way around that learning curve.

The dust and compound splatter is also something to prepare for. The tool has no dust extraction port, so you're working with a mess. That's true of most rotaries, but worth knowing before you buy.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Professional detailers** who want cordless freedom for mobile work or tight engine bays
- **Experienced DIYers** already in the DEWALT ecosystem who understand rotary technique
- **Anyone upgrading from a dual-action** who wants true correction capability

Skip it if you're a first-time polisher — start with a DA, learn on that, then consider this when you outgrow it.

## FAQ

**How long does the battery actually last?**
Realistic continuous runtime is 20-25 minutes per 5Ah battery. Section work stretches that to 30-40 minutes of actual detailing.

**Will this work on my car's clear coat?**
Yes, but only if you know rotary technique. Use low speed (1000-1500 RPM) for compounding, and always test on a small area first.

**Does it come with a battery and charger?**
No. This is a bare tool (DCW600B). You need existing DEWALT 20V MAX batteries, or you'll need to buy them separately.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're experienced with rotary polishers, already own DEWALT 20V batteries, and want cordless convenience without sacrificing correction power.

**Skip it if:** You're new to polishing or don't have compatible batteries — the total investment gets steep quickly.
