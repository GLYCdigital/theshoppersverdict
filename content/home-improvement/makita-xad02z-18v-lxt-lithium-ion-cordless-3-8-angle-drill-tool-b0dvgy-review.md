The Makita XAD02Z answers a question most drill owners don't ask until they're wedged between two studs with a standard drill that won't angle: *what happens when a compact driver just isn't compact enough?* This 3/8" angle drill is the tool you reach for when clearance matters more than raw power — and for 229 Amazon reviewers averaging 4.6 stars, it's delivering exactly that.

**What you actually get**

This is a bare tool. No battery, no charger, no case. You're buying the drill head and the LXT compatibility that lets it snap onto any 18V Makita battery you already own. The angle head measures just 3-7/16" from the top of the chuck to the bottom of the housing — that's the entire selling point. The body itself is a compact 1-3/16" wide, and at 3.4 pounds with a 5.0Ah battery attached, it won't fatigue you during overhead or confined work.

The chuck is a genuine 3/8" keyless metal unit, not the plastic one you find on bargain angle drills. That matters because angle drills get torqued sideways constantly.

**Performance and build quality**

The XAD02Z runs a two-speed gearbox: 0-600 RPM for driving screws and 0-1,700 RPM for drilling. The 66 in.lbs. of torque sounds modest on paper, but remember — this is an angle drill. It's designed for precision in tight spaces, not breaking loose lug nuts. In real-world use, reviewers consistently note it handles #8 and #10 screws in framing and plywood without bogging down.

The variable-speed trigger is genuinely progressive. Feather it for pilot holes, squeeze for speed. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "The speed control is so much smoother than my old corded angle drill. I can start a screw without stripping the head every single time."

The LED light sits at the base of the chuck and casts light directly onto the work area. The Afterglow feature keeps it lit for 10 seconds after you release the trigger — a small touch that matters when you're working in a dark crawlspace and need to reposition.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- **The compact head.** Overwhelmingly, reviewers mention this as the reason they bought it and the reason they keep it. "Fits between 2x4s like it was made for it" and "Finally, I can drill out the back of a cabinet without pulling the whole unit" appear repeatedly.
- **LXT battery compatibility.** If you own any Makita 18V tools, this shares your existing batteries and chargers. No new system to buy into.
- **Build quality.** The metal gear housing and rubberized overmold get consistent compliments for durability.

**Where it falls short**

The rubber overmold is a dust magnet. If you're doing drywall work or cutting into old lumber, the grip collects every bit of debris in the texture. It wipes clean, but it's a minor annoyance.

The bare-tool format is the bigger consideration. At roughly $130-150 street price, you're paying for the head alone. If you're not already invested in Makita's LXT platform, you're looking at another $80-130 for a battery and charger. That effectively doubles your cost.

Some users also note the stubby body puts your hand closer to the workpiece than they'd like. With thick work gloves, that can feel cramped. It's the price you pay for the compact design.

**Who should buy this**

- **Electricians and low-voltage installers** drilling through studs and joists in tight cavities
- **Cabinet installers** needing to drill drawer fronts and panel interiors at awkward angles
- **Plumbers and HVAC techs** working in crawlspaces and mechanical chases
- **DIYers already in the LXT ecosystem** who occasionally hit a tight spot and want a proper tool instead of contorting a standard drill

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this handle a 1-inch spade bit through studs?**
It'll do it, but slowly at the 600 RPM low range. The 66 in.lbs. of torque means you'll want sharp bits and steady pressure. For occasional use it works; for daily production framing, you'd want a full-size drill.

**Q: Does it work with 6.0Ah and 5.0Ah LXT batteries?**
Yes. The XAD02Z accepts all 18V LXT batteries. The larger 5.0Ah and 6.0Ah packs add noticeable weight but extend runtime significantly for continuous drilling.

**Q: Can this drive screws in addition to drilling?**
Yes — that's the 0-600 RPM low gear's primary job. It handles cabinet screws and deck screws up to about 2-1/2" in softwood without issue.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You work in tight spaces regularly and already own Makita LXT batteries. This is the right tool for precision angle drilling that a standard drill physically cannot reach.

**Skip it if:** You're starting from zero with Makita, or your work is mostly open-space drilling where a standard drill already suffices. The bare-tool format and specialized design make this a supplement, not a primary drill.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a specialty tool that does exactly what it promises, held back only by its bare-tool format and dust-collecting grip.
