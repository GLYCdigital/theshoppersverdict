# WORKPRO 13" Laminate Floor Cutter: The Clean-Cut Solution That Actually Delivers

## The Short Version

If you're laying laminate, luxury vinyl plank (LVP), or multi-layer flooring, you've got two choices: rent a massive, expensive saw or spend hours with a circular saw creating dust clouds in your living room. The WORKPRO 13" Laminate Floor Cutter is the third option — a guillotine-style cutter that makes clean, square cuts without power tools, dust, or noise. It's not perfect, but for most DIY flooring projects, it's a genuinely smart purchase that pays for itself in one room.

## What You Get

The box contains the cutter itself, a spare blade, and an Allen wrench for blade replacement. The unit weighs roughly 15 pounds and sits on a wide steel base with rubber feet. The cutting bed extends to handle planks up to 13 inches wide — that covers virtually all standard laminate and LVP planks on the market. There's a measurement scale printed on the base (both inches and centimeters), plus a flip-down stop for making repeated identical cuts, which is a lifesaver when you're cutting 30 identical planks for a row.

## How It Performs

The cutting mechanism is a sharpened steel blade mounted to a lever arm. You push down, the blade scores and snaps the plank cleanly. For laminate and vinyl planks up to 10mm thick, it produces a factory-quality cut edge with no chips or splinters. The blade is replaceable, and WORKPRO includes a spare right out of the box — decent value.

Users consistently report that it handles standard glue-down vinyl, click-lock laminate, and even some engineered wood products without issue. One Amazon reviewer summed it up: *"Cuts through laminate like butter. I laid 600 square feet without a single bad cut."* Another noted: *"Much easier than my circular saw. No dust, no noise, and the cuts are cleaner."*

The measurement stop is genuinely useful. Set it once, and every cut is identical — which matters more than you think when you're on your knees at hour six of a flooring job.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest limitations: This is not a tool for solid hardwood or anything over 10mm thick. Attempting to cut dense material will result in a crushed edge and a frustrated you. It also requires real downward force — one reviewer mentioned *"you need some upper body strength to press down on wider planks."* If you weigh under 130 pounds or have grip strength issues, consider a power saw instead.

The integrated measurement scale is convenient but not precision-grade. For cuts that need to be within 1/16 inch, use a tape measure and pencil mark, then align the cut line with the blade guide. The scale is a starting point, not gospel.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners laying laminate or LVP in a single room or two** — the cost of this tool beats renting a saw, and you'll never deal with dust again.
- **Contractors doing mid-sized jobs** — the speed and repeatability of the stop feature save real time.
- **Anyone who hates circular saw noise and dust** — this thing is silent and clean.

**Skip it if:** you're installing solid hardwood, working with planks over 10mm thick, or you need to make angled cuts (this tool does straight 90-degree cuts only).

## FAQ

**Q: Can this cut vinyl plank flooring?**
A: Yes. Standard rigid-core and WPC vinyl planks up to 10mm thick cut cleanly. It's one of the best tools for click-lock vinyl because there's no chipping of the edges.

**Q: How do I replace the blade?**
A: Use the included Allen wrench to remove the two bolts holding the blade in place, swap in the spare, and re-tighten. Takes about two minutes. Replacement blades are available on Amazon.

**Q: Does it work for cutting lengthwise (ripping) planks?**
A: No. This tool is designed for cross-cuts (width cuts). For ripping planks lengthwise, you'll still need a table saw or track saw.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're installing laminate, LVP, or vinyl flooring and want clean, dust-free cuts without renting power tools. It's accurate enough for DIY, fast enough for pros, and the included spare blade adds real value.

**Skip it if:** You're working with solid hardwood, planks thicker than 10mm, or you need angled cuts. Those jobs require different tools.
