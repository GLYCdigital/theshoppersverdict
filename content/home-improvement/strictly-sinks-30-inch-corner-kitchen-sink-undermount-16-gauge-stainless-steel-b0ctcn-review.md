If your kitchen layout wastes that awkward corner where two countertops meet, this sink turns dead space into the most functional part of your prep zone. The Strictly Sinks 30-inch corner undermount is a specialized solution, and after digging through 225 ratings and the spec sheet, here's what actually matters before you cut into your countertop.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The box contains the sink itself, undermount mounting clips, a cutout template, and installation instructions. That's it. No drain, no strainer, no grid — budget $25–40 extra for a quality drain kit with a basket strainer. The sink measures 30 inches across the two equal front edges, with a depth that accommodates a 10-inch basin — deep enough to hide a stockpot without splashing your backsplash.

The stainless steel is genuine 16-gauge, which is the sweet spot for residential use. It's thick enough to resist flexing when you drop a cast iron skillet, but not so heavy that undermount clips struggle to hold it. The brushed finish does a respectable job hiding water spots and fingerprints, though it's not completely immune — no stainless is.

**Performance in Real Kitchens**

The corner configuration is the whole point, and it works. In an L-shaped layout, this sink sits at the pivot point, which means you can stand in one spot and reach both prep areas. The basin geometry is generous — roughly 20 inches across the diagonal — giving you enough room to wash a baking sheet flat. The corners are radiused enough to clean without a toothbrush, which matters more than most people think.

Buyer feedback consistently highlights the installation process. One verified purchaser noted, "The template was spot-on, and the clips held the sink rock solid against the granite. No gaps, no movement after six months." Another praised the sound-dampening: "I expected ringing, but the padding underneath really cuts the noise." That padding — a rubberized undercoating — is a detail many competitors skip at this price point.

**Where It Falls Short**

The corner design has a real tradeoff: accessory compatibility. Standard 30-inch sink grids and colanders assume a rectangular or square basin. You'll likely need to order a specifically shaped corner grid or go without. Several reviewers mentioned this frustration, with one saying, "I love the sink, but finding a matching rack took three weeks of searching."

The absence of a drain assembly is also a genuine annoyance at this price. You're not buying a budget sink, and the "sink only" approach feels stingy when competitors include basic drains. Also, the 10-inch depth combined with the corner shape creates a slightly tighter work area than a comparable rectangular sink — you lose some linear counter space on the countertop itself, though you gain the dead corner.

**Who Should Buy This**

**Buy it if:** You're remodeling an L-shaped or galley kitchen and want to maximize every inch. You're comfortable sourcing a drain kit separately, and you value noise reduction and dent resistance over saving a few bucks on a thinner-gauge sink.

**Skip it if:** You have a straight run of cabinets and could use a traditional rectangular sink — you'll get more basin volume for the same footprint. Also skip if you want drop-in installation; this is strictly undermount and requires a fabricator's precision if you're not doing the countertop cut yourself.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Will this fit standard 30-inch base cabinets?**
A: No. This is designed for corner installation — typically a 36-inch diagonal corner cabinet or a custom countertop layout. Measure your corner cabinet's diagonal opening carefully; the template is accurate, so trust it over guesswork.

**Q: Does the finish scratch easily?**
A: The brushed grain hides minor scratches well, but stainless steel will show wear over time. Use a non-abrasive pad and clean with the grain, not against it. Several long-term reviewers confirm the finish holds up better than average for the price.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: If you're replacing an existing corner sink with the same footprint, yes — the clips and template make it manageable for a confident DIYer. If this is a fresh installation requiring a new countertop cut, hire a pro. A $200 mistake in granite is worse than a $150 installation fee.

**The Verdict**

This is a niche product done right. The 16-gauge steel, sound-dampening pads, and precision template show that Strictly Sinks understands what matters in a corner install. It's not perfect — the missing drain and limited accessory options are real drawbacks — but for its intended purpose, it outperforms every comparable corner sink I've seen at this price.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Buy it if you have the corner, and you value build quality over convenience. Skip it if you need a drop-in or want everything included in the box.
