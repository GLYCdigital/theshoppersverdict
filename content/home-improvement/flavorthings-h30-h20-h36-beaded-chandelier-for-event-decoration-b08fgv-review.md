If you've priced rental chandeliers for a wedding or corporate gala recently, you know the drill: $150–$300 per unit, delivery fees, and a pickup deadline that stresses your entire timeline. The FlavorThings H30/H20/H36 beaded chandelier undercuts that model entirely — but the tradeoffs aren't obvious from the product photos. Here's what 220+ buyers discovered after hanging these, and what you should know before checkout.

## What Actually Shows Up

The box contains three separate chandelier units — one at 30 inches, one at 20 inches, and one at 36 inches in drop length. Each features multiple strands of beads cascading from a central hub. The beads are a mix of round and faceted shapes, which is why they catch light so effectively. As shown in the product image, the overall silhouette reads as a classic tiered chandelier from a distance, but up close you'll notice the construction is simpler than it appears.

There's no wiring, no bulbs, and no electrical component whatsoever. These are purely decorative hanging pieces. You're getting the bead strands, a top ring for hanging, and nothing else — no hooks, no chains, no ceiling hardware. Buyers consistently mention this, and it's worth budgeting an extra $10–$15 for hanging supplies.

## Real-World Performance

The visual impact is genuinely strong. The faceted beads scatter light in a way that flat ornaments can't match. One reviewer noted, "The way these caught the string lights at our reception was stunning — guests kept asking where I rented them." That's the core value here: at typical event distances, these read as elegant, expensive decor.

The H30/H20/H36 length variety is a smart design choice. Mixing the three lengths across a ceiling creates depth that uniform chandeliers lack. For a 20-foot table arrangement or a dance floor canopy, the staggered drops look intentional and high-end.

Setup is where the experience gets real. Each unit arrives with beads individually strung on separate strands — you attach them to the top ring yourself. The 30-inch unit has roughly 40 strands; the 36-inch has closer to 50. Plan for 30–45 minutes per chandelier if you're methodical, less if you're not picky about strand spacing. This is the #1 complaint in reviews, and it's legitimate. The instructions are minimal — mostly diagrams — but the process is intuitive once you see the ring structure.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.4-star rating holds up for specific reasons. Reviewers repeatedly mention:

- **Photography value**: "Our photographer said these were the best decor investment we made. The sparkle in the reception photos is unreal."
- **Weight**: At roughly 3–4 pounds per fully assembled unit, hanging is genuinely simple with standard adhesive hooks or small ceiling screws.
- **Durability**: Multiple reviewers report using theirs for 5+ events without bead loss or strand breakage. The knots holding beads are tight and consistent.

## Where It Falls Short

The painted-bead issue is real. Lighter colors — especially champagne and pearl — can show minor scuffs from packing friction. Most buyers note it's barely visible once hanging, but if you're inspecting up close, you'll see it. Darker colors (black, navy) don't have this problem.

The lack of mounting hardware is the more practical annoyance. You'll need to buy hooks, fishing line, or zip ties separately. For a 10-chandelier setup, that's an extra $20–$30 and a trip to a hardware store you weren't planning.

**Q: How many do I need for a typical 150-guest reception?**
