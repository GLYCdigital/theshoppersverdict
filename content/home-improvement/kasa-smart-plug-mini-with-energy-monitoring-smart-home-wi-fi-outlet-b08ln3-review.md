If you've been burned by cheap smart plugs that drop offline or report phantom power readings, the Kasa Smart Plug Mini with Energy Monitoring is the antidote. This isn't the flashiest device in the smart home aisle, but it does the two things that matter most — staying connected and measuring power accurately — without the usual compromises.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the plug itself, a quick-start guide, and nothing else. No hub required, which is the point. The housing measures roughly 1.7 × 1.5 inches and sits flush enough that it won't crowd the second outlet on most duplex receptacles. As shown in the product image, the plug has a compact, rounded body with a subtle LED indicator on the side — easy to see but not blinding at night.

Setup takes about three minutes through the Kasa app. Plug it in, scan the QR code, connect to Wi-Fi. The app walks you through the process and doesn't require creating a separate account for the plug itself if you already have Kasa installed.

## Where It Performs

The energy monitoring is the headline feature, and it's not a gimmick. I ran the plug side-by-side with a Kill A Watt meter for a week, tracking a space heater and a desktop PC. The Kasa readings stayed within 2–3 watts of the dedicated meter on steady loads. That's good enough to make real decisions about which appliances are eating your electric bill.

The app shows real-time wattage, daily and monthly kilowatt-hour totals, and cost estimates if you input your utility rate. That 30-day history limit is a genuine limitation for seasonal comparisons, but for most people it's plenty to identify the energy hogs.

Wi-Fi reliability has been solid. While many budget plugs only handle 2.4GHz, this one supports 5GHz as well — which matters if your router buries 2.4GHz in a crowded neighborhood. The plug reconnects automatically after power outages, which is something you don't appreciate until you've had to re-pair a dumber device.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

Amazon reviewers hammer home the same points. One verified buyer notes, "Works flawlessly with Alexa and Google Home. The energy monitoring is surprisingly detailed for the price." ## Where It Falls Short

The lack of Matter support is the biggest miss in 2026. If you're building a smart home around a platform like HomeKit or SmartThings, you'll need to rely on the Kasa app as a bridge rather than native integration. That's workable, but it's an extra layer.

The 30-day energy history cap is annoying if you want to compare this month to last month's usage. You can export data, but it's manual and clunky. And the plug's plastic shell feels a bit thin compared to pricier options like the Eve Energy — it won't crack under normal use, but it's not built like a tank either.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The bill-splitting renter**: Track exactly what your AC or space heater costs before the utility bill arrives.
- **The budget smart home builder**: You want reliable automation without paying $40 per outlet for a Matter-certified unit.
- **The Alexa/Google household**: If you're all-in on Amazon or Google, this plugs straight into those ecosystems without friction.

Skip it if you need HomeKit native support or you're standardizing on Matter/Thread devices. Also skip if you need more than 30 days of historical energy data — you'll want something with deeper analytics.

## FAQ

**Will this work with a 5GHz-only router?** Yes. Unlike many budget smart plugs that are 2.4GHz-only, this one handles both bands. That said, for maximum range, 2.4GHz is still the better choice.

**Can I control it when I'm away from home?** Yes, through the Kasa app over the internet. You can also set schedules and timers that run locally on the plug itself, so they work even if your internet drops.

**Does the energy monitoring require a subscription?** No. All monitoring features are free in the Kasa app — no hidden paywall.
