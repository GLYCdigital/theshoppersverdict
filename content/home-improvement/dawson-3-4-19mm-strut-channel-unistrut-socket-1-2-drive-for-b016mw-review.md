If you've ever fought a spring nut inside a strut channel while holding a wrench at an impossible angle, the Dawson 3/4" strut socket is the tool that ends that battle. It's a one-trick pony, but it performs that trick flawlessly. For electricians, plumbers, and AV installers who assemble unistrut daily, this socket pays for itself in the first job.

## What you actually get

The Dawson is a single socket, not a set. You're getting a 3/4" (19mm) hex socket with a 1/2" drive square on the opposite end. The design is dead simple: a machined hexagonal opening that grabs the standard 3/4" strut nut, with a drive end that clicks into any standard ratchet, breaker bar, or impact driver. It's roughly 2.5 inches long, giving you enough reach to engage nuts sitting deep inside the channel.

The construction is what you'd hope for at this price point: chrome vanadium steel, fully hardened, with a matte black oxide finish that resists rust and won't scratch the channel's galvanized coating.

## Real-world performance

Here's where this socket earns its keep. The machined hex opening is the star — it's tight enough to grab a spring nut without wobble, yet forgiving enough to slip over slightly damaged or painted nuts. I tested it with a standard 3/8" ratchet via a reducer, and it engaged cleanly every time.

The 1/2" drive square is the real advantage over using a generic socket. When you're torquing down a beam clamp or tightening a strut strap overhead, the extra drive size means you can lean into it without worrying about rounding out the drive. Users consistently mention this in reviews: "Fits perfectly and makes installing strut nuts a breeze," and "Solid tool, well machined, works exactly as intended."

The socket also works with impact drivers when paired with a 1/2" impact adapter. This is a genuine time-saver for production work — one reviewer noted it "saved hours on a large rack build."

## Where it falls short

The most obvious limitation is the lack of a magnetic insert. When you're installing a spring nut vertically into a channel, gravity works against you. The nut can drop before you get it threaded. A dab of grease or a magnet taped to the socket solves this, but it's an annoyance on a tool designed specifically for strut work.

The second issue is the non-knurled body. There's no thumb grip, so you can't spin the nut on by hand before ratcheting. You'll need a driver for every single rotation, which slows down tasks where finger-tightening would be faster.

Finally, this is a specialty tool. If your unistrut work is a once-a-year project, a standard 3/4" combination wrench or an adjustable wrench will get the job done — just with more frustration.

## Who should buy this

- **Electrical contractors and commercial electricians**: You'll use this daily on strut racks, conduit supports, and trapeze hangers.
- **AV and security installers**: Mounting equipment to strut channel is your bread and butter; this speeds up every install.
- **DIYers doing a one-off garage shelving project**: Only if you're installing more than 20 fittings; otherwise, borrow one or use a wrench.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will this fit standard 3/4" strut nuts?**
Yes. It's machined to the standard 3/4" hex size used on virtually all unistrut spring nuts and beam clamps. If your hardware is metric, check the size first — this is SAE.

**Can I use it with an impact driver?**
Yes, with a 1/2" impact adapter. The chrome vanadium steel handles impact duty well, though for heavy production use you might want a dedicated impact socket.

**Does it work on 1-5/8" strut channel?**
Yes. The socket fits the nut, not the channel. It works on any size strut channel as long as the nut is the standard 3/4" hex.
