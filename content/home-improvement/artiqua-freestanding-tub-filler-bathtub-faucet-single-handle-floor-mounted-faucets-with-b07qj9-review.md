If you're shopping for a freestanding tub filler, you already know the drill: most options look great in renders but feel hollow and cheap in person. The Artiqua Freestanding Tub Filler breaks that pattern. With 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, this floor-mounted faucet has earned its place in bathrooms across the country. Here's the real story after digging through the specs, the feedback, and the fine print.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the floor-mounted faucet body, a single-handle control, the swivel spout assembly, mounting hardware, and the rough-in bracket. The finish options include brushed nickel and matte black, both of which use a PVD coating that holds up better than standard electroplating. As shown in the product image, the design is clean and minimal — no unnecessary flourishes, just a graceful curve from floor to spout.

One detail that matters: the spout reaches 12 inches from center, which clears most freestanding tubs without awkward positioning. The overall height lands around 40 inches, making it tall enough for comfortable filling without splashing.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

Here's where the Artiqua earns its keep. The body is solid brass, not the zinc alloy you'll find on cheaper competitors. That means it won't develop that telltale green corrosion at the threads, and it has real heft — you can feel the difference when you grip the handle. The single-handle cartridge operates smoothly with about a quarter-turn from off to full flow, and the swivel spout rotates a full 360 degrees with just enough resistance to stay where you put it.

Buyers consistently note the finish quality. One reviewer wrote, "The brushed nickel looks far more expensive than the price point suggests. After six months of daily use, it still looks brand new — no spots, no tarnish." Another praised the water flow: "It fills my tub faster than my previous wall-mounted setup. The pressure loss is minimal even with the taller riser."

The floor flange sits flush and covers the rough-in completely, which helps if your floor isn't perfectly level. The included mounting bracket is sturdy, and the set screws use hex heads rather than the stripped-prone Phillips heads you see on budget models.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, installation is not for beginners. Because this is floor-mounted, you need access below the subfloor to connect the water supply lines. If you're retrofitting an existing bathroom with a concrete slab foundation, this could be a dealbreaker — you'll need a plumber to run lines through the slab. Several reviewers mentioned hiring a professional and budgeting an extra $200–400 for labor.

Second, there's no diverter for a handheld sprayer. If you want to rinse down the tub or wash the dog, you'll need a separate handheld unit. The Artiqua is purely a filler — nothing more.

Third, the spout doesn't have a shut-off valve on the spout itself. You control flow entirely from the handle, which is standard for this style but worth noting if you expected a separate spout valve.

## Who Should Buy This

This faucet is ideal for three specific buyers:

- **The remodeler with subfloor access** — if you're building new or have a crawl space or unfinished basement below, installation is straightforward and the payoff is a high-end look.
- **The freestanding tub owner** — this pairs beautifully with alcove or freestanding tubs where wall-mounted options aren't feasible.
- **The finish snob** — the PVD coating genuinely holds up. If you've been burned by chipping finishes before, this is a safer bet.

Skip it if you're on a slab foundation without planned plumbing access, or if you need a handheld sprayer integrated into the unit.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this faucet fit all freestanding tubs?**
A: The 12-inch spout reach and 40-inch height accommodate most standard tubs. Measure your tub's rim depth and height before ordering to ensure proper clearance.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: Only if you're comfortable with plumbing rough-in work. The floor-mount requires connecting supply lines below the floor. If you're not confident, budget for a professional.

**Q: What's the flow rate?**
A: It delivers a solid 2.2 GPM at standard household pressure — plenty fast for filling a tub without waiting forever.
