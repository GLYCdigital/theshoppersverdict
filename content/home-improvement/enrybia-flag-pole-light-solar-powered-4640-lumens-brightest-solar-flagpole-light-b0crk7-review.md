**The Short Version**

If you've ever watched your American flag go dark at sunset, you know the disappointment. The enrybia Flag Pole Light attacks that problem with a 4640-lumen LED array that genuinely lights the entire flag — not just a patch of grass beneath it. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's not perfect, but it's currently one of the brightest solar flagpole lights you can buy without stepping up to hardwired systems.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the main light head, a separate solar panel, a 10-foot connecting cable, and a universal mounting bracket kit. The bracket is the star here — it accommodates poles from 0.75 to 2.5 inches in diameter, which covers most residential and commercial sectional poles. The solar panel detaches completely, so you're not forced to mount the light and panel together. That's a thoughtful design choice for anyone with a shaded yard.

**Performance in the Real World**

The 4640-lumen claim isn't marketing fluff. Multiple reviewers confirm the light output is genuinely bright, with one noting it "lights the flag beautifully and even illuminates a good portion of the yard." The beam angle is wide enough to cover a 3x5 foot flag mounted up to 20 feet high. The light sensor triggers at dusk automatically, and the motion sensor mode (if you want to save battery) adjusts sensitivity via a small switch on the unit.

Battery performance is where expectations need calibrating. On a full summer day of direct sun, you'll get 8-10 hours of light. In winter, or with partial shade on the panel, expect 4-6 hours. Several reviewers flagged this — one wrote, "Great light, but I had to relocate the solar panel to the roof to get enough charge in November." The 10-foot cable makes that possible, which is a genuine advantage over integrated-panel designs.

**What Buyers Praise**

The mounting flexibility gets consistent shout-outs. One reviewer with a telescoping flagpole noted the clamp "fits perfectly and doesn't wobble in wind." Another praised the build quality: "The aluminum housing feels solid, not like the cheap plastic lights I've tried before." The dusk-to-dawn sensor also gets credit for reliability — no false triggers from streetlights or passing cars.

**Where It Falls Short**

The set-screw mounting system is functional but unforgiving. Over-tightening will leave permanent marks on softer aluminum poles. Use thread-locker and snug them down gently — you don't need gorilla torque. The lack of a remote or timer means you can't adjust the schedule without physically accessing the unit. If you want lights off by 10 PM, this isn't the product for you. And while the 4640-lumen output is real, it draws hard on the battery; don't expect that brightness to last all night in spring and fall.

**Who Should Buy This**

**Buy it if:** You have a residential or light-commercial flagpole with some sun exposure, want a true dusk-to-dawn solution without running electrical lines, and value brightness over battery endurance. It's also a solid pick for remote locations where hardwiring isn't practical.

**Skip it if:** Your flagpole sits in deep shade most of the day, you need programmable timers, or you expect summer-level brightness in December. For those cases, look at a low-voltage hardwired spot light instead.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Will this fit my 20-foot sectional flagpole?**
Yes — the clamp adjusts from 0.75 to 2.5 inches, which covers standard sectional pole diameters. Just verify your pole's top section dimension before ordering.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
The internal battery provides 6-10 hours of light depending on season and sun exposure. Full 4640-lumen brightness lasts about 6 hours on a full charge; it dims gradually after that.

**Q: Can I mount the solar panel away from the flagpole?**
Yes, the panel detaches and connects via a 10-foot cable. Many reviewers mount the panel on a roof or fence to maximize sun exposure while keeping the light on the pole.
