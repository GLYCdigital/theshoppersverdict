Let me be blunt: most LED candelabra bulbs are either too bright, too cold, or too ugly to leave exposed in a chandelier. The Hizashi CA10/CA11 G2 tries to fix all three, and for the most part, it gets there.

**What you actually get**

The package contains six bulbs, each measuring 1.5 inches in diameter and 3.5 inches tall — that's the CA10/CA11 form factor that fits most standard chandeliers, sconces, and pendant fixtures with E12 bases. The glass is clear, not frosted, which means you see the full filament strip when the bulb is off. That matters more than you'd think in a bare-bulb fixture.

These are 4.5-watt LEDs rated at 230 lumens, 2700K color temperature, and a 15,000-hour lifespan. They're dimmable, which is the headline feature for this G2 revision. The box lists a 3-year warranty, though you'll likely go through a few fixtures before you ever need it.

**How it actually performs**

I ran these through a mix of fixtures — a six-arm chandelier, a pair of wall sconces, and a ceiling fan light kit. The first thing you notice is the light quality. At full brightness, it's a genuinely warm 2700K with no sickly green cast, which is rare at this price point. Color rendering is decent for the category; skin tones look natural, and wood tones don't turn orange.

Dimming is where the G2 earns its name. On a standard Lutron dimmer, these bulbs dropped to about 10% output before cutting out, with no flicker in the mid-range and no audible hum. That's a meaningful improvement over earlier Hizashi models and most budget candelabra LEDs I've tested. One buyer noted: *"I've replaced these in my dining room chandelier and the dimming is finally smooth — my old bulbs would strobe at 50%."*

The build quality is solid. The base is brass-plated, not plastic, and the glass feels substantial. The filament array is mounted straight and centered, which matters when the bulb is visible from every angle.

**Where it falls short**

The 230-lumen output is the tradeoff. If you're replacing 40-watt incandescent candelabra bulbs, this is roughly equivalent. But many older fixtures were designed around 60-watt equivalents, and these will feel dim by comparison. In a large dining room chandelier with six or more bulbs, you'll get ambiance, not task lighting. One reviewer put it plainly: *"They're beautiful but not bright. I needed reading light in my breakfast nook and these just don't cut it."*

The heat is also worth noting. The glass surface runs noticeably warm after extended use — not dangerous, but surprising for an LED. The base stays cool, so it won't stress the socket, but don't assume these run cool just because they're efficient.

**Who should buy these**

If you have a chandelier, sconces, or any exposed-bulb fixture where the bulb itself is part of the design, these are a strong choice. The vintage look is convincing, and the dimming performance is genuinely good.

Skip them if you need functional brightness in a vanity or utility fixture. You'll want something in the 400-lumen range instead.

**FAQ**

**Will these work with a smart dimmer or just manual ones?**
They work with most standard dimmers, including smart dimmers from Lutron and Leviton. Phase-cut dimmers are fine; just avoid PWM-style dimmers that aren't designed for LED loads.

**Can I use these in enclosed fixtures?**
Yes. The base stays cool enough for enclosed sconces, though the glass will run warm. Just ensure there's some airflow in the fixture.

**Do they buzz or flicker when dimmed low?**
No. The G2 revision specifically addressed this. At 10% output, there's a slight drop in color consistency, but no flicker or hum.

**The verdict**

The Hizashi CA10/CA11 G2 is one of the better budget candelabra LED options available. It nails the aesthetics, delivers acceptable brightness for ambient lighting, and improves on the dimming issues that plagued earlier versions.

**Buy it if:** You want exposed-bulb fixtures to look genuinely vintage without sacrificing LED efficiency, and your dimmer actually matters to you.

**Skip it if:** You need serious lumens from a small bulb, or you're outfitting a fixture with fewer than four bulbs where 230 lumens each won't be enough.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-executed niche product with one notable limitation (brightness) and one minor quirk (heat). Given the 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, buyers generally agree.
