## What You Actually Get

The Hyperlite comes in 65W and 100W configurations, sold as single units or 4-packs. The 100W version pushes roughly 13,000-15,000 lumens — enough to light up a two-car gas station bay or a warehouse aisle without breaking a sweat. The 65W sits around 8,500-9,500 lumens, which suits smaller covered areas like carports, loading docks, or walkways.

Each fixture includes the mounting hardware for surface mounting and pendant hanging. You're looking at a slim-profile aluminum housing with a polycarbonate lens — standard for this class. The IP65 rating means it's sealed against dust ingress and low-pressure water jets, so rain and humidity won't kill it.

## Real-World Performance

The efficiency numbers here are genuinely good. At roughly 130-150 lumens per watt, this beats most older fluorescent canopy fixtures by a landslide. If you're replacing a 250W metal halide, you'll cut your energy draw by 60% while getting comparable or better light output.

Heat management is the usual concern with LED fixtures in enclosed spaces. The Hyperlite uses a finned aluminum heatsink design — nothing revolutionary, but it's the right approach. The driver is integrated, which simplifies installation but means if the driver fails, you're replacing the whole fixture rather than just the power supply.

One thing that stands out from the product images: the optical lens design uses a frosted diffuser rather than individual reflectors. That gives you a wider, more uniform beam spread — good for general area lighting, but not ideal if you need concentrated downward light for precise tasks.
