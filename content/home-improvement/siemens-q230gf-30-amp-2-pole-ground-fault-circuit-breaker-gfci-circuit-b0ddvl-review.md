**Bottom line up front:** If you're wiring a hot tub, pool equipment, or any outdoor 240V circuit, this is the breaker you want — and the one your inspector wants to see. The Q230GF is Siemens' workhorse 30-amp, 2-pole GFCI, and after digging through 229 verified ratings, the 4.6-star average holds up. ## What's in the box

You get the breaker itself, period. No pigtails to fiddle with — the neutral lug is built into the breaker body, which is actually a plus for retrofit jobs since you don't need to run a separate wire to the panel's neutral bar. The load terminals accept up to #4 AWG copper or #2 aluminum, so it handles most 30-amp circuits without adapters. The wiring diagram is printed on the side label, which saves a trip to Google if you've done this before but need a refresher on the line/load orientation.

## Performance and build quality

The Class A GFCI protection trips at 5 milliamps of ground fault — that's the threshold that catches the small leaks that can stop your heart, not just the big shorts that blow a standard breaker. In real-world terms: if you've got a slight crack in a pool pump housing or a damp junction box outdoors, this thing catches it before it becomes a problem.

The 2-pole interruption is the feature that separates this from cheaper alternatives. Both 120V legs disconnect simultaneously. I've seen panels where someone used a single-pole GFCI with a handle tie to save money — that's dangerous, because one leg can stay live while the other trips. This breaker doesn't allow that scenario.

Build quality is typical Siemens: solid plastic housing, crisp mechanical action on the toggle, and terminals that grip wire firmly without needing gorilla torque. The self-test button runs an internal diagnostic every time you power up, and it's nice to have that confirmation rather than trusting a breaker that's been sitting for years.

## What buyers consistently praise

The phrase "passed inspection first try" shows up repeatedly in the verified reviews. ## Where it falls short

The self-test can nuisance-trip on startup with inductive loads. Several reviewers reported their pool pumps or air compressors tripping the breaker intermittently on cold starts, even though the wiring was correct. It's not universal — my guess is it's sensitivity to the inrush current combined with slight ground leakage from the motor itself. If you're wiring a motor-heavy circuit, budget for the possibility of an occasional false trip.

The physical size is also a consideration. This breaker is 2 inches wide (1 inch per pole), and if your panel is a tight squeeze — especially older models with narrow wiring gutters — you might struggle to route the load wires. Measure your panel's interior depth before ordering. It fits standard Siemens panels, but "standard" doesn't mean "spacious."

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with a 3-wire or 4-wire hot tub hookup?**
A: Both, but the wiring differs. For a 4-wire setup (2 hots, neutral, ground), the neutral connects to the breaker's load neutral lug. For a 3-wire setup (no neutral), you cap the neutral at the spa and the breaker still provides GFCI protection on the hots. Check your spa's manual — most modern spas want the 4-wire hookup.

**Q: Can I use this in a sub-panel?**
A: Yes, but the sub-panel must have a bonded neutral-to-ground connection at the main panel only. If the sub-panel has its own ground rod, you'll need to keep the neutral and ground isolated. This is a common inspection fail point, so verify your bonding before installing.

**Q: The test button doesn't trip the breaker when I press it. Is it broken?**
A: Possibly, but first check that the breaker is actually powered and in the ON position. The self-test function runs automatically at power-up — if you don't hear a faint click within 30 seconds of energizing, the breaker may be defective. Siemens has a solid warranty, but you'll want to test the breaker with a known-good circuit before assuming it's the wiring.
