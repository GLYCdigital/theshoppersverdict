If you’ve got outdoor floodlights that stay on until you remember to flip them off, or a porch light that’s been burning since 2019, the Honeywell Home Econoswitch RPLS740B1008 is the kind of product that quietly pays for itself. It’s a 7-day programmable switch timer that doesn’t try to be a smart home gadget—no Wi-Fi, no app, no voice control. Just solid, set-and-forget scheduling for lights, fans, or pumps.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains the timer switch, a wire nut pack, and a quick-start card. The unit itself is a standard single-pole switch (fits a regular wall box, Decora-style faceplate included). You’re getting 8 on/off events per day, independently programmable for each day of the week—that’s the “7-day” part. It handles up to 1800W resistive (like incandescent lights) or 1/2 HP for motors, which covers most ceiling fans and small pumps.

**Build and installation reality**  
This is a neutral-wire-required device. If your switch box has only two wires (hot and load), stop right here—this isn’t for you. For the rest, installation is straightforward: black to line, red to load, white to neutral, green to ground. The toggle switch on the side lets you bypass programming entirely for manual on/off, which is more useful than you’d think. The buttons are tactile and the menu structure is logical—you’ll spend 10 minutes setting it up, not an afternoon.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The standout feature, based on 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, is the astronomical clock. One reviewer put it simply: *“Set it once for sunset and it adjusts itself all year—I never touch it.”* That’s the real draw. Another common thread: reliability. *“Had it for 18 months, not a single glitch,”* noted a buyer using it for a pool pump. The 7-day flexibility also gets love from people with different weekday/weekend routines—you can have lights on at 6 AM Monday through Friday but not Saturday.

**Where it falls short**  
The backlit LCD is small and the light is faint. It’s fine at the switch, but don’t expect to read it from across the garage. The price is the bigger sticking point—$48 is steep compared to a $15 mechanical timer. You’re paying for the astro feature and the programmability, so if you only need a basic “off at midnight” schedule, this is overkill. Also, the manual override is momentary—tap to toggle—but some users wanted a full push-and-hold for a timed “on for 2 hours” mode. It has a vacation mode (random on/off for security), but it’s buried in the menu.

**Who should buy this**  
- **The outdoor lighting person** who wants sunrise/sunset tracking without touching settings seasonally.  
- **The pool pump or well pump owner** who needs a reliable, 7-day schedule that a cheap timer can’t hold.  
- **The “set it and forget it” buyer** who hates smart-home apps and wants a physical switch that just works.  

Skip it if your wiring has no neutral, or if you only need a simple countdown timer.

**FAQ**  

*Does it work with LED bulbs?*  
Yes, but check the minimum load—it needs about 15W to function properly. A single 5W LED won’t trigger it reliably. Most dimmable or standard LEDs in groups are fine.

*Can I use it for a 240V appliance?*  
No. This is strictly 120V, single-pole. For 240V loads, you’d need a different timer or a contactor.

*Does the schedule reset on power outage?*  
No—it has a backup battery (CR2032) that keeps the clock and program running. You won’t be reprogramming after a storm.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a dependable, no-frills timer with astro-smarts for outdoor lights or pumps, and you have a neutral wire. **Skip it if** you’re on a tight budget, have old wiring, or just need a basic off-timer.  

For $48, this isn’t the cheapest path, but it’s the most reliable one for the feature set. That 4.6-star rating holds up—it’s the rare timer that does exactly what it says, year after year.
