If you need to light up a loading dock, backyard, or commercial entryway, you want something that just works — no timers, no apps, no fiddling. It’s not fancy, but it delivers serious light output at a price that undercuts most commercial-grade competitors.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the fixture itself, a pre-wired photocell sensor, and mounting hardware. There’s no bulb to install — the LEDs are integrated, rated for 50,000 hours. The housing is die-cast aluminum with a tempered glass lens, and the whole thing is rated IP65 for water and dust resistance. It’s a solid, heavy unit — you can feel the build quality when you unbox it.

Wiring is straightforward: three wires (black, white, green) into a standard junction box. If you’ve swapped a light fixture before, you can handle this in under 20 minutes.

At 7800 lumens with a 5000K color temperature, this thing is *bright* — daylight-bright. It’s not a subtle accent light; it floods a wide area with crisp, white illumination. The beam spread is roughly 120 degrees, which covers a typical two-car driveway or a commercial storefront entrance without dark corners.

The photocell is the real story here. It reads ambient light accurately — turning on at dusk and off at dawn without the flickering or false triggers that plague cheaper sensors. One reviewer noted, *“I’ve had cheap wall packs that cycled on and off all night. ## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has a few tradeoffs worth knowing:

- **No motion sensor option** — This light runs all night, every night, at full output. If you wanted motion-activated security lighting, this isn’t it. You’re paying for constant illumination, not reactive lighting.
- **Cosmetic imperfections** — A handful of buyers report minor scratches or scuffs on the housing straight from the box. It doesn’t affect performance, but if you’re particular about aesthetics, inspect it on arrival.
- **Color temperature is fixed** — 5000K is cool white. If you prefer the warmer 3000K look for residential settings, you’ll need to look elsewhere.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the photocell work through glass or covered fixtures?**
A: No — the sensor needs direct exposure to ambient light. Mount it where it sees open sky, not under an eave or behind a screen.

**Q: Can I bypass the photocell and run it on a manual switch?**
A: Yes. If you want manual control, cap the photocell wire and wire the fixture directly to the switch. The instructions cover this.

**Q: Is it bright enough for a commercial parking lot?**
A: For a small lot (up to about 30 feet of coverage), yes. For larger areas, you’ll want multiple units or higher-wattage commercial fixtures.
