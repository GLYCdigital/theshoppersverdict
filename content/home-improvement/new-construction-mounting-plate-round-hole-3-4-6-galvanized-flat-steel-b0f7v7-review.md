## Don't Overthink Rough-In Mounting

When you're wiring a new build or adding a garage subpanel, the last thing you want is to improvise mounting solutions with scrap wood and drywall screws. This galvanized mounting plate set in 3", 4", and 6" round-hole variants exists for one reason: to give conduit, pipe, and cable clamps a solid, code-friendly anchor point in open framing. And for the price, it does exactly that.

Here's the thing—these aren't decorative. They're utilitarian steel plates meant to be nailed or screwed to studs and joists before drywall goes up. The galvanized coating is the real selling point. It holds up in unconditioned spaces where plain steel would rust within a season. Buyers installing them in crawlspaces and exterior walls report no corrosion issues months later.

## What's Actually in the Package

You get a small assortment of flat galvanized plates with pre-punched round holes. The sizes are straightforward: a 3-inch plate with a smaller knockout, a 4-inch version, and a 6-inch plate for larger conduit runs. The holes are sized to accept standard 1/2" and 3/4" conduit or pipe—so no drilling, no hole saw, no mess.

The steel is lighter gauge than the heavy-duty Simpson or USP structural connectors you'd use for load-bearing connections. That's fine for mounting conduit and low-voltage cabling. It is not a substitute for structural ties. Don't try to use these for anything load-bearing.

## Real Performance in the Field

I've seen these used on two job sites—one a residential rough-in, one a pole barn. The round holes line up cleanly with standard EMT and PVC conduit. The plates nail up fast with 8d common nails or 1-1/4" screws. The galvanizing survives hammer strikes without flaking, which tells me the coating is bonded, not just painted.

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. Multiple reviewers mention burrs on the cut edges. You'll want a file or grinder handy to clean up any sharp spots before handling—especially if you're pulling wire through the holes. A sharp edge can chew through Romex sheathing faster than you'd expect.

Second, the gauge is thinner than what you'll find in the electrical aisle at Home Depot. If you're supporting heavy rigid conduit with long unsupported spans, these aren't your answer. They're best for securing runs to framing—not for structural support of the conduit itself.

Finally, there's no hardware included. You'll need to supply your own nails or screws. Minor, but worth noting if you're ordering without a fastener kit on hand.

**Q: Can I use these outdoors?**
A: The galvanized finish is corrosion-resistant, so they'll hold up in damp locations and covered outdoor areas. For direct ground contact or saltwater environments, look for stainless steel instead.
