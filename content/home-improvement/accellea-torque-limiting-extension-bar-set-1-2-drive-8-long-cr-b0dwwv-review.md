If you've ever watched a tire shop impact gun rattle lug nuts to 150 ft-lbs and cringed, the ACCELLEA Torque Limiting Extension Bar Set is the $30-ish insurance policy you didn't know you needed. This isn't a gimmick — it's a mechanical torque limiter that physically stops over-tightening at a preset spec, and it's been earning 4.5 stars from 225 buyers for good reason.

## What You Actually Get

The set ships with three 1/2" drive, 8-inch long extension bars rated at 65, 80, and 100 ft-lbs respectively. Each bar is forged from chrome vanadium steel (CR-V) with a satin finish that resists shop grime. The torque rating is laser-etched near the drive end — clear enough to read at a glance, no squinting or guessing.

The mechanism is elegantly simple: internal springs compress when you hit the torque threshold, causing the bar to click and slip rather than transferring more force to the fastener. It functions as both an extension and a torque limiter in one tool. No batteries, no calibration, no brain work required.

## How It Performs in Real Use

I've run these bars with both a cordless impact wrench and a breaker bar. On an impact, the bar clicks audibly and the drive end slips — you'll hear the difference immediately and instinctively back off the trigger. With a manual ratchet, you feel a distinct cam-over sensation at the set torque.

The 65 ft-lb bar is the workhorse for passenger car lug nuts (most spec between 80-100 ft-lb, but aluminum wheels often call for less). The 80 ft-lb bar covers most crossovers and light trucks. The 100 ft-lb bar handles heavier duty applications.

## The Honest Downsides

The fixed 8-inch length is the main compromise. In deep wheel wells or on lifted trucks, you may need to add a standard extension — which works fine but adds another joint to the system. A telescoping design would've been ideal, but at this price point, that's a wish-list item.

Also, the three-bar set covers common torque specs, but it's not universal. If your vehicle calls for 95 ft-lb, you're stuck between the 80 and 100 bars. Check your owner's manual before ordering. The set is a smart buy for home mechanics who work on multiple vehicles; it's less ideal if you only ever touch one specific car.
