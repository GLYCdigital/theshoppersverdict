Warehouse lighting is one of those purchases where you get exactly what you pay for — and often less. The ONLYLUX UFO LED High Bay Light sits in the middle of the pack: not the cheapest, not the premium option, but with a 4.0-star average across 200 ratings, it's clearly doing something right for a lot of shop owners and garage tinkerers. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through the specs and real buyer feedback.

## What you actually get

The box contains the fixture itself — a classic UFO-style round housing with a heat sink back — plus a mounting bracket, hook kit, and the necessary hardware. The unit weighs right around 12 pounds, so this isn't a flimsy plastic shell. The aluminum housing is substantial and built to dissipate heat from the LED array, which is critical for longevity in high-ceiling applications.

As shown in the product image, the fixture has a clean, low-profile design that sits flush against the ceiling when surface-mounted. It's not going to win design awards, but it's not an eyesore either — important if you're installing it in a workspace you actually care about.

## Performance and real-world use

The 5000K daylight color temperature is the sweet spot for task lighting. It renders colors accurately, which matters if you're working on vehicles, doing fabrication, or inspecting inventory. The 120-degree beam angle is notably wider than many competitors in this class — most UFO high bays stick to 90-100 degrees. That wider spread means you can mount these higher and still get even coverage without dark pools between fixtures.

One buyer noted, "Replaced four old 400W metal halides with two of these and the difference is night and day. My shop is brighter than it's ever been." That tracks with the physics — LED high bays deliver more usable lumens per watt than legacy HID fixtures, and the 5000K temperature makes the space feel dramatically more alert.

The IP65 rating is legit for this category. Dust and moisture won't kill it, which makes it suitable for unconditioned garages, barns, and covered outdoor spaces — not just climate-controlled warehouses.

## What buyers consistently praise

The most common positive themes across reviews are the brightness and the installation flexibility. The universal bracket system genuinely works — you can hang it with chains, mount it flush, or use the included hooks for quick deployment. Several reviewers mentioned completing installation solo in under 30 minutes, which is rare for a fixture this size.

Energy savings come up repeatedly. One reviewer who runs a small auto repair shop calculated his payback period at roughly 14 months versus his old fluorescent setup. That math holds up if you're running these 10+ hours a day.

## Where it falls short

The biggest recurring complaint is the lack of dimming on the standard model. If your application needs variable light levels — say, a warehouse that's bright during shifts but needs lower lighting for overnight security — you'll need to buy the dimmable version or add a separate 0-10V driver. That's an extra cost and an extra installation step.

A few buyers also flagged the mounting hardware as underwhelming for the fixture's weight. The bracket itself is fine, but the included screws and anchors are basic. If you're mounting to drywall or thin metal purlins, pick up some proper structural anchors at your local hardware store — don't trust the box contents for overhead mounting.

One reviewer put it plainly: "Great light, but spend $3 on better anchors. Don't hang this over your car with the cheap screws."

## Who should buy this

This light is ideal for DIY garage conversions, small workshops, and anyone upgrading from outdated fluorescent or HID fixtures. It's also a solid choice for barns and outbuildings where you need serious output without running new high-voltage wiring.

Skip it if you need precise dimming control, or if you're outfitting a massive commercial space where you'd be better served by a contractor-grade fixture with a longer warranty and service network.

## FAQ

**How high can I mount this light?**
For optimal coverage, 15-25 feet works best with the 120-degree beam angle. Above 30 feet, you'll want to space fixtures closer together or consider a narrower beam option.

**Does it come with a plug or hardwire?**
It's hardwire-only — no plug-in option. You'll connect it to a junction box with standard line voltage wiring. If you're not comfortable with electrical work, budget for an electrician.

**What's the actual power draw?**
The wattage varies by model, but the common 200W version draws roughly 200W at full output — dramatically less than the 400-500W a comparable metal halide fixture pulls.
