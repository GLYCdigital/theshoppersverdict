A roof vent cap is one of those parts you don't think about until a raccoon peels yours off or the sun turns it into a brittle husk. Then you're climbing a ladder with a tape measure, praying the local big-box store has the right diameter in stock. The Trivd Frol 4/6/8/10 Roof Vent Cap solves that problem by covering four common sizes in one affordable package. At $31.34 with a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, it's worth a hard look before you pay dealership or big-box prices for a branded equivalent.

**What you actually get**

The cap is molded from UV-stabilized white PVC with a 1/4-inch thick wall — the same material spec you'd find on premium OEM caps. It's available in 4, 6, 8, and 10-inch diameters, and the fit is true to size. The design includes a 1.5-inch overlap skirt that seats cleanly over standard roof vent jacks. You get the cap itself, nothing else. No screws, no caulk, no gasket. That's not a dealbreaker, but it means a trip to the hardware store if you don't keep #8 stainless sheet metal screws and roof sealant on hand.

**Real-world performance**

Installation is straightforward if you've done any roof work. Clean the old cap's base, slide the new one over the jack, mark your screw holes, drill pilot holes, and drive stainless screws through the skirt into the vent pipe. Then run a bead of sealant around the top edge. The PVC cuts cleanly with a utility knife if you need to trim for an odd fit — one reviewer noted, "Measured twice, cut once, and it fit like it was made for my RV's 6-inch vent."

Multiple buyers mention the cap surviving hail storms and extreme heat without cracking or warping. A 4.5-star rating over 225 reviews suggests consistent quality control. The white color holds up without the yellowing that plagues cheaper polypropylene caps after a single summer.

**Where it falls short**

No pre-drilled holes is the biggest annoyance. On a steep roof pitch, holding the cap in place while you drill and screw is a two-person job — the 10-inch version is heavy enough that one-handed work gets sketchy. Also, the smaller 4-inch size has a slightly tighter overlap than the others, so if your vent pipe is at the upper end of the tolerance range, you may need to tap it gently with a rubber mallet to seat it fully.

**Who should buy this**

- **RV owners** doing a vent replacement in the driveway — the 6-inch size covers most standard RV roof vents.
- **Homeowners** with a cracked or missing plumbing vent cap who want a direct replacement without calling a roofer.
- **DIYers** who already own a drill and a tube of roof sealant and want to knock this out in 20 minutes.

**Skip it if** you're replacing a cap on a non-standard vent pipe, or if you want a lockable cap to keep squirrels out — this is an open design, not a screened or louvered unit.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Does this fit all standard roof vent pipes?**
A: It fits standard 4, 6, 8, and 10-inch schedule 40 PVC vent pipes and most metal roof jacks. Measure the outside diameter of your pipe before ordering — if it's within 1/4 inch of the listed size, the overlap will handle it.

**Q: Can I paint it to match my roof?**
A: Yes, but use a latex exterior paint. Oil-based paints can soften the PVC. Lightly scuff the surface with 220-grit sandpaper first for adhesion.

**Q: Will it survive freezing temperatures?**
A: The PVC is rated down to -20°F. The concern isn't cold — it's ice expansion. As long as your vent pipe is properly flashed, the cap itself won't crack from frost.
