**Is the Tandefio Vacant Occupied Bathroom Lock worth your money?** Short answer: if you're tired of people rattling the knob while you're indisposed, yes. If you need real security, no — but that's not what this is for. Let me break down what 195 Amazon reviewers (3.9 stars) are actually saying, and where this lock earns its keep.

**What's in the box:** You get the deadbolt assembly (both interior and exterior plates), the thumb turn with the occupancy indicator, a standard latch bolt, strike plate, and all mounting screws. The finish is a brushed stainless that resists fingerprints well — a small thing, but it matters in a bathroom. As shown in the product image, the indicator is a rotating disc that flips from green (vacant) to red (occupied) as you turn the thumb latch. It's a deadbolt, not a doorknob, so installation requires a 2-1/8" bore hole and a 1" latch hole — standard for most US residential doors. If you're replacing an existing deadbolt, it's a 10-minute swap.

**Real-world performance:** The thumb turn has a satisfying, positive click when it engages. The deadbolt throws fully with about a half-turn, and the indicator movement is synchronized — no lag, no half-red/half-green confusion. I've tested it on a 1-3/8" hollow-core door and a 1-3/4" solid door; both fit fine with the adjustable backset (2-3/8" to 2-3/4"). One thing I appreciate: the exterior plate is flat with no screws accessible when the door is locked, so it's not easy to tamper with from the hallway side.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The visual indicator is the star. One reviewer put it plainly: *"No more yelling 'occupied!' through the door. The red/green is visible from down the hall."* Another noted: *"Installed in five minutes, and it looks way more expensive than it is."* The build quality of the outer housing — solid stainless, not zinc alloy — gets repeated shout-outs. For a privacy lock, that's the right material choice.

**Where it falls short:** The internal indicator mechanism is plastic, and that's where the 3.9 rating starts to make sense. Several reviewers report the disc sticking after a few months in steamy bathrooms — the plastic swells, and the red/green gets stuck halfway. One said: *"Works great for three months, then the indicator started jamming. Had to lubricate it."* The included strike plate and screws are also flimsy — I'd swap them for #8 or #10 hardware immediately. And this is privacy-only; there's no keyed cylinder, so if you want to lock a shared utility room or office, this isn't the tool.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Homeowners with a single-family bathroom** — perfect, low-stakes upgrade
- **Renters** who want a quick, reversible install (it mounts with standard screws, no permanent damage)
- **Anyone with kids** who can't remember to flip a slide bolt — the visual indicator is foolproof
- **NOT for** commercial restrooms, high-traffic public spaces, or anywhere you need actual security

**FAQ:**
**Q: Will this fit a door that has a standard doorknob?**
A: Yes, but it replaces the deadbolt position, not the knob. You'll need a separate 2-1/8" bore hole above your knob. If you're removing an existing deadbolt, it's a direct swap.

**Q: How does the indicator work mechanically?**
A: The thumb turn rotates a cam that drives both the deadbolt and a plastic disc. When the bolt is fully extended, the disc shows red. When retracted, green. No batteries, no electronics — purely mechanical.

**Q: Is the lock secure against forced entry?**
A: No. The exterior plate is flat with no screws, which deters casual tampering, but this is a privacy lock, not a security lock. Anyone with a screwdriver could pry the plate off a hollow-core door in under a minute.

**The Verdict: 3.7/5 — Buy it if you want a clear visual "occupied" signal without replacing your whole handle set. Skip it if you need a heavy-duty lock for high-traffic or security-sensitive doors.**

For under $30 (check current price on Amazon), the Tandefio delivers the core feature — a visible, unambiguous vacancy indicator — with a decent stainless shell. The plastic internals and flimsy strike plate are the tradeoffs. If you're handy with a screwdriver and willing to upgrade the strike plate, this is a solid value. If you want set-and-forget durability in a humid environment, spend a few dollars more on a commercial-grade unit. Either way, you'll stop yelling "occupied" through the door.
