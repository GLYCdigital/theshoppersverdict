# BHTOP Roofing Nail Gun Review: Budget Coil Nailer With a Catch

If you're shingling a roof, the nail gun is the tool that makes or breaks your day. The BHTOP Roofing Nail Gun comes in at a tempting price point, and its spec sheet looks solid on paper: coil-fed design, full metal housing, 100-nail capacity. But with a 3.6-star average across 180 ratings, buyers are clearly split. I dug through the feedback to give you the straight story before you hand over your money.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the nailer itself, a 360-degree adjustable exhaust deflector, a depth adjustment wheel, and a small hex wrench for maintenance. That's it — no case, no belt hook, no oil. It weighs about 7.5 pounds, which is respectable for a coil roofing nailer. Many competitors in this class push 8.5-plus pounds, so that half-pound savings adds up over 200 shingles.

The tool accepts both wire-collated and plastic-collated coil nails from 3/4-inch to 1-3/4-inch, covering standard shingle and underlayment work. It operates at 70–120 PSI, though the manual and most users agree it runs best around 90–100 PSI.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Struggles

When it works, this nailer drives nails flat and consistent. The depth adjustment is genuinely tool-free — a thumb wheel on the side lets you dial in flush or slightly countersunk nails without hunting for an Allen key. The sequential trigger gives you decent control for starter rows and tricky valleys.

The problem is reliability. Multiple verified buyers describe the same pattern: it runs great for 50–100 nails, then jams. One reviewer wrote, "Worked amazing for the first 20 minutes, then started misfiring and jamming every 10 nails. I ended up hand-nailing the last half of my roof." Another said, "When it feeds, it's perfect. But clearing jams on this thing takes 5 minutes each time."

The jam issue appears tied to nail quality and angle. The gun handles 15-degree wire coils better than 30-degree plastic collation, based on buyer reports. If you feed it cheap deformed nails, expect frequent stoppages.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The positives are consistent too. The all-metal housing takes drops from ladders without cracking — several owners mention dropping it 10-plus feet with only cosmetic scuffs. The 360-degree exhaust redirect is genuinely useful; you can point it away from your face and keep debris off the shingles you just laid.

For light to moderate home use — a shed, a chicken coop, a single-story re-roof — buyers report good results. One satisfied customer noted, "For my 12-square garage roof, it never jammed once with quality nails. Can't beat the price."

## Where It Falls Short

The jamming issue is the headline, but there's more. This nailer is air-hungry. It needs a compressor delivering at least 4.2 CFM at 90 PSI. A 6-gallon pancake compressor won't keep up — you'll be waiting for the tank to refill constantly. Budget for a decent 8+ gallon unit if you don't already own one.

Spare parts are also scarce. Replacement o-rings, drivers, and feed springs are hard to find online, and BHTOP's customer service gets mixed marks for response time. If this dies after a year, you're likely buying a new gun rather than rebuilding.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a DIY homeowner tackling one or two small roofing projects, you already own a capable compressor, and you're willing to buy quality coil nails. The price is right for occasional use, and the metal housing will take the abuse.

**Skip it if** you're a professional roofer or planning a full house re-roof. The jamming frequency will cost you more in lost time than you saved on the purchase price. Spend the extra money on a Hitachi or DeWalt coil nailer — you'll earn it back in fewer stoppages.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with a small pancake compressor?**
A: No. You need at least 4.2 CFM at 90 PSI. A 6-gallon pancake compressor won't sustain firing speed — you'll fire a few nails, wait for the tank to refill, repeat. Get an 8-gallon or larger dual-stage unit.

**Q: What nails does it take?**
A: Coil nails from 3/4" to 1-3/4", in both 15-degree wire collation and 30-degree plastic collation. Buy quality nails — the gun jams far less with premium brands.

**Q: Does it come with a case?**
A: No. It ships in a plain box with the tool and a hex wrench. Budget for a soft case if you need to protect it between uses.

## The Verdict

The BHTOP Roofing Nail Gun is a 3.7/5 tool: genuinely capable when fed quality nails and used on modest projects, but frustratingly prone to jams on bigger jobs. It's a fair value for the DIYer doing one roof — not a professional tool, not a lifetime purchase.

**Buy it if:** You're a homeowner with one small-to-medium roofing project, a real compressor, and patience for the occasional jam.
**Skip it if:** You're a pro or tackling a full house — the downtime isn't worth the savings.
