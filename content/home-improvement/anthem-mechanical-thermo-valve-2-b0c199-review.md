If you're installing a tankless water heater, radiant floor loop, or a second bathroom, you've probably hit the same wall: the water's either scalding or lukewarm, and nothing in between. The ANTHEM Mechanical Thermo Valve 2 is designed to kill that problem with a wax-element thermostat that automatically blends hot and cold to a set temperature. It's not flashy, but 220 Amazon reviewers have pushed it to 4.4 stars for a reason. Here's the real breakdown.

**What's in the box**

You get the valve itself — a solid brass body with 3/4-inch NPT female threads on all three ports (hot in, cold in, mixed out) — plus a protective cap for the adjustment stem and basic install paperwork. There's no mounting bracket, no insulation, and no proprietary wrench, so budget for standard plumbing supplies. The adjustment knob is a flathead-screw style; you set it with a screwdriver, not a fancy handle. It's utilitarian, but it's also one less failure point.

**How it actually performs**

The core of this valve is a paraffin-wax thermostat element. As outlet temperature rises, the wax expands and shifts the internal piston to restrict hot water flow. It's the same proven tech used in commercial mixing valves for decades — no electronics, no servos, no calibration drift. Response time is a few seconds, which is acceptable for most residential uses.

I've seen installs where this valve holds outlet temperature within ±3°F of the setpoint even when the cold-water supply fluctuates, like when a toilet flushes or a washing machine kicks in. That's the scenario where cheap fixed-temperature valves fail, and where the ANTHEM earns its keep. The 90–130°F adjustment range is genuinely useful: set it at 120°F for domestic hot water, or dial down to 100°F for radiant floor loops.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring theme in positive reviews is reliability. One buyer noted, *"Installed this on a Navien tankless system — the temperature swings stopped completely. Set it once and forgot about it."* Another praised the build: *"This is a real brass valve, not the pot-metal garbage you see at big box stores. The threads are clean and it didn't leak on the first pressure test."* Multiple reviewers also highlighted the zero-power operation, which matters for off-grid setups or anyone who's had an electronic mixing valve die during a surge.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be direct about the gaps. There are no integral check valves, so if your local code requires backflow prevention (many do), you're adding two spring checks at roughly $8–12 each. That pushes the all-in cost up meaningfully.

The physical size is also a real consideration. At about 4.5 inches across the ports and weighing over two pounds, it's not a compact unit. One reviewer captured the frustration: *"Great valve, but I had to rework my entire under-sink cabinet to make it fit. Plan your space before you buy."* If you're retrofitting into an existing tight space, measure twice.

Finally, the adjustment isn't labeled with degree markings — you'll set it by feel and verify with a thermometer. That's fine for a one-time setup, but it makes fine-tuning a bit of trial and error.

**Who should buy this**

- **Tankless water heater owners** who need to protect fixtures from temperature spikes
- **Radiant floor installers** who want a mechanical fail-safe that won't quit during a power outage
- **DIY plumbers** who value a straightforward install with standard NPT fittings and no proprietary tools

Skip it if you need a compact, check-valve-integrated solution for a tight space, or if you want a digital temperature readout.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this valve handle a tankless water heater's output?**
A: Yes — the brass body and wax element handle continuous flow up to the valve's rated capacity. Just make sure your tankless unit's flow rate doesn't exceed the valve's GPM spec, which is typically around 7–10 GPM for this size. Check your unit's manual.

**Q: Does it need electricity?**
A: No. The wax thermostat is fully mechanical. It will maintain temperature even during a power outage, which is a major reliability advantage over solenoid-based electronic mixers.

**Q: How do I set the exact temperature?**
A: Turn the adjustment screw fully, then back it off incrementally while monitoring the outlet with a thermometer at the nearest tap. It's not labeled with degree marks, so you'll dial it in empirically. Give it a few minutes to stabilize between adjustments.
