**What’s in the box**  
You get the fixture itself, a swivel mounting bracket, and the necessary screws. No bulbs to fuss with — the LEDs are integrated. The housing is die-cast aluminum, which is a step up from the flimsy plastic you see on budget lights. The tempered glass lens is a nice touch; it’s not going to yellow or crack after a summer of UV exposure. One thing to note: the wiring comes out the back with a short pigtail, so you’ll need to wire it into a junction box or use a waterproof connector. It’s not a plug-and-play unit.

**Performance and real-world use**  
The 5000K daylight color is the star here. It’s crisp and clean, not the harsh blue-white you get from cheap LEDs. I’ve used it to light a 30-foot section of driveway, and it covers it evenly with no dark spots. The beam angle is wide enough for a basketball court or a loading dock, but you can tighten the spread by adjusting the 180-degree bracket. One buyer noted: *“I put this up over my garage, and it lights up the entire backyard like a stadium. My security camera footage is crystal clear now.”* Another said: *“Install took 15 minutes. The bracket is solid, and it hasn’t budged in a windstorm.”*

Heat is a common killer of cheap floodlights, but the aluminum fins on the back do their job. After two hours of continuous use, the housing was warm — not scorching. That’s a good sign for longevity.

**Where it falls short**  
No sensor is the biggest miss. For a light this powerful, you’d expect at least a photocell option. You’ll need to pair it with a timer or smart switch if you don’t want it blazing all night. The exposed wiring on the back is also worth mentioning — if you’re mounting it in a spot that gets direct rain, you’ll want to seal the connection properly. One reviewer hit the nail on the head: *“Great light, but I wish it had a dusk-to-dawn sensor. I have to remember to turn it off every morning.”*
