Let's be direct: the Goodyear Electric Trailer Dolly claims a 3,600-pound towing capacity, but that number tells you almost nothing about whether this unit will work for your specific trailer. The spec that matters — the one most shoppers overlook — is the 600-pound tongue weight limit. That's where this dolly either earns its keep or becomes an expensive mistake.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the assembled dolly unit, one removable 12V battery, a charger, and the joystick handle assembly. There's no remote, no phone app, no accessories to lose. The steel frame is powder-coated and the 10-inch pneumatic tires come pre-mounted. As shown in the product image, the battery sits in a cradle on the frame with a simple LED charge indicator — nothing fancy, but functional.

## Real-World Performance

The joystick control is the standout feature here. Push forward, the dolly moves forward. Twist the handle, it pivots. It's intuitive enough that most users report being comfortable backing a trailer into a garage spot within 10 minutes. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Moved my 19-foot bass boat around my side yard like it was a shopping cart. The learning curve is almost zero."*

Battery life is respectable but not class-leading. Expect roughly 45-60 minutes of continuous use on flat ground, less if you're pushing up even a slight incline. The removable battery is a genuine plus — buy a second one and you effectively have unlimited runtime.

The pneumatic tires are a bigger deal than they look. On cracked asphalt and gravel driveways, this dolly tracks noticeably smoother than competitors with solid rubber wheels. It's also less likely to bounce your trailer tongue around when crossing uneven transitions.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Three themes dominate positive reviews: ease of maneuvering, build quality, and the battery system. Multiple owners mention using it for boats, utility trailers, and even small campers. One review summed it up: *"I have a bad back and this replaced my wife and I pushing a 2,000LB trailer by hand. Worth every penny."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's talk about the elephant in the room. The 3,600-pound max trailer weight is misleading for many real setups. A 3,000-pound boat with a full tank of gas, gear, and a trolling motor battery can easily push tongue weight past 600 pounds. If that's your situation, the dolly's front wheel will lose traction and the motor will strain. Several reviewers hit exactly this wall.

The weight of the unit itself is also a factor. At roughly 100 pounds, lifting it into a truck bed or hanging it on a garage wall is a two-person job for most people. It's a tool that lives on the ground, not something you casually store out of the way.

And there's no remote. For a unit at this price point, that's a notable omission. You're always walking beside the trailer, which is fine for short moves but tedious if you're repositioning a trailer 50 feet down a driveway.

## Who Should Buy This

This dolly makes sense if you have a trailer with a tongue weight under 500 pounds — think small fishing boats, jet skis, utility trailers, or lightweight campers. It's ideal for homeowners with narrow driveways or tight garage layouts where a truck can't easily maneuver. It's also a strong option for anyone with physical limitations who wants to avoid the strain of manual trailer moving.

Skip it if you have a heavy boat or camper with serious tongue weight, if you need remote operation, or if you don't have a flat-ish surface to work on. This is not an off-road tool.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it handle a 3,600-pound trailer?**
A: Only if that trailer has a tongue weight under 600 pounds. Check your trailer's spec sheet — most loaded boats and campers will exceed this. Weigh your tongue first.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
A: Realistically 45-60 minutes of constant use on flat ground. Less on inclines or with heavy loads. The removable battery makes swapping in a spare easy.

**Q: Does it work on gravel or grass?**
A: The pneumatic tires help, but it's really designed for pavement and hard-packed surfaces. Soft grass or loose gravel will bog it down, especially with a heavy load.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have a trailer under 2,500 pounds with a confirmed tongue weight under 500 pounds, and you want precision maneuvering in tight spaces without breaking your back.

**Skip it if:** Your trailer is near the rated limit, you need remote operation, or you're working on rough terrain.
