Most landscape lights under $30 are plastic with a cheap brass-colored coating that peels within a season. The MALORY Brass Outdoor Spot Lights aren't that. At $29.69 for a fixture that's actually milled from solid brass, this is one of those rare budget finds that punches well above its price point. But it's got quirks you should know about before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

Each box contains one spot light fixture with a fully assembled brass head, a mounting stake, and roughly 3 feet of 12/2 low-voltage wire pre-connected to the fixture. You'll need a low-voltage transformer (sold separately) and your own landscape wire to connect multiple lights in a run.

As shown in the product image, the fixture is compact — the head is about 4 inches tall and 2.5 inches wide. The brass has a nice satin finish, not mirror-polished, which actually looks more natural in a garden setting. The stake is solid steel with a pointed tip that pushes into soil without needing a mallet in most cases.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 3000K warm white output is the sweet spot for landscape lighting. It's warm enough to feel inviting but not so yellow that it distorts colors. One buyer noted, "The light makes my hydrangeas look like they're lit by actual sunlight — the color rendering is that good." I'd agree; the CRI is noticeably better than the 2700K fixtures in the same price range.

The 12V AC/DC compatibility is a genuine feature, not marketing fluff. Most transformers output AC, but some newer solar-compatible and battery systems run DC. This fixture handles both without flicker or reduced output. If you're expanding an existing system, you don't have to worry about matching the power type.

The brass housing is the real deal. It's cast, not stamped, and feels substantial in hand. One reviewer who's had them installed for two winters reported, "Zero corrosion, zero fading — they look as good as the day I installed them." The sealed lens is rated IP65, which means it's fully protected against rain and hose spray.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 235 ratings, the themes are consistent. Durability tops the list — people are genuinely surprised a fixture at this price holds up in brutal weather. ## Where It Falls Short

The lead wire is short. At 3 feet, you'll almost certainly need to splice or extend it unless your transformer is right next to the installation point. This isn't a dealbreaker — landscape wire and connectors are cheap — but it's an extra step and an extra cost to factor in.

The non-replaceable LED is the bigger concern. When the diode eventually dies (LEDs typically last 25,000+ hours, so realistically 8–12 years), you're replacing the entire fixture, not just a bulb. At this price point, it's an acceptable tradeoff, but it's worth knowing going in.

The directional aim is adjusted via a knuckle joint that holds tight but requires a bit of effort to move. It's not a one-finger adjustment like some premium fixtures, so plan to spend a few seconds dialing in each light's angle.

## Who Should Buy These

If you're installing landscape lighting for the first time and want fixtures that won't look dated or fall apart in three years, these are your lights. They're also ideal if you're mixing brass fixtures into an existing system — the color and finish match well with higher-end brass hardware.

Skip them if you need replaceable bulbs or if your transformer is more than 50 feet from your lighting zone — the short lead wire will add to your total cost. Also consider something else if you want adjustable beam spread; these are fixed at a 40-degree spot pattern.

## FAQ

**Can these be buried directly in the ground?**
The stake goes into the soil, but the fixture head and wire connections should stay above ground. Use waterproof wire connectors (the silicone-filled type) for the splices.

**Do I need a special transformer?**
Any standard 12V landscape transformer works. Just verify your total wattage stays below the transformer's capacity. Each fixture uses about 4 watts, so you can run up to 12 of these on a 50W transformer.

**Will these work with my existing low-voltage system?**
Almost certainly. The 12V AC/DC compatibility means they'll work with any standard landscape transformer, regardless of brand or age.
