If you've ever wrestled a 4x8 sheet of plywood through a table saw alone, you know the pain — the sag, the bind, the dangerous kickback when the sheet catches. The Bora Portamate PM-2700 takes direct aim at that problem with an adjustable conveyor roller that's become a staple in 220 Amazon reviews averaging 4.4 stars. Here's the real story after digging through buyer feedback and spec sheets.

**What's in the box**

The PM-2700 ships fully assembled — a welded steel frame, two rubber-coated rollers, and a T-handle height adjustment knob. No tools, no confusing parts. You get a support stand that adjusts from 25.5 to 44 inches tall, which covers everything from a low workbench to a full-height table saw. The top roller section tilts through multiple angles, letting you create a slight downhill feed for gravity-assisted cutting or a flat deck for assembly work.

**Performance and build quality**

The steel construction is genuinely sturdy. Multiple reviewers mention supporting heavy loads — one user notes, "I've had 8-foot long 2x12s on this with no flex at all." The rubber-coated rollers spin smoothly and won't scratch finished surfaces, which matters if you're feeding cabinet-grade plywood or pre-finished shelving.

The adjustable height range is the standout feature. Most roller stands in this price bracket offer maybe 10 inches of travel; the PM-2700 gives you nearly 19 inches. That's the difference between owning one stand that works everywhere versus three cheap stands that each do one job. The folding mechanism is equally practical — collapse it flat and it hangs on a wall hook or slides behind a cabinet.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The versatility gets the most love. As one reviewer put it: "Works perfectly for my table saw and my miter saw station. Adjusts in seconds and locks solid." Another noted the roller quality: "Smooth rollers, heavy base, doesn't tip. Better than stands twice the price."

The quick-fold feature also earns repeated mentions. Woodworkers with small shops appreciate gear that disappears between uses.

**Where it falls short**

The rubber roller surface is a double-edged sword. While it protects workpieces, it creates friction. Light materials — think 1x2 trim or thin aluminum — can drag instead of glide. One reviewer captured it: "Great for sheet goods, but narrow boards don't roll as freely as I'd like." If you primarily feed narrow stock, look for a stand with ball-bearing rollers instead.

The height lock is a twist-knob design, not a quick-release lever. Adjusting mid-project means loosening, repositioning, and re-tightening — a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker. And the base, while stable on flat concrete, can rock slightly on uneven garage floors. A few reviewers reinforced the feet with plywood pads for outdoor use.

**Who should buy this**

- **Cabinet makers and furniture builders** feeding sheet goods through table saws or track saws — this is your primary support tool.
- **Job-site carpenters** who need one stand that adapts to varying work heights throughout the day.
- **Small-shop owners** with limited storage who need a stand that folds flat and disappears.

Skip it if you only ever work with narrow boards, or if you need a dedicated outfeed table with a massive footprint for production work.

**FAQ**

*Q: What's the actual weight capacity?*  
The manufacturer doesn't list a formal rating, but real-world use suggests 150–200 pounds is comfortable. Multiple reviewers report heavy hardwood slabs with no sag.

*Q: Does it fit a DeWalt or Ridgid table saw?*  
The 44-inch max height clears most job-site saws. Measure your saw deck height first — if it's above 44 inches, this won't work.

*Q: Is the roller height truly adjustable, or just the frame?*  
Both, actually. The frame adjusts for overall height, and the roller assembly itself tilts independently for fine-tuning the angle.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you regularly handle sheet goods or long lumber and want one stand that adapts to multiple tools. The build quality justifies the price, and the height range genuinely sets it apart.

**Skip it if** your work is all narrow stock, or you need a fixed outfeed table for a permanent station.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-built, versatile roller stand with a few tradeoffs around roller friction and adjustment speed. For most woodworkers, it's a solid upgrade over cheap single-height stands.
