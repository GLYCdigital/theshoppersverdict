## What You Actually Get

The package includes the main latch body, a strike plate, and all necessary mounting screws. The headline here is the material: SUS304 stainless steel. That's the same grade used in commercial kitchens and marine hardware — it resists rust and oxidation far better than the cheaper 201-grade steel or zinc alloys you'll find on most competitors at this price point.

The latch itself is a sliding bolt mechanism with a spring-loaded pin. You lift the handle, slide the bolt, and it locks into place. The dimensions are compact — roughly 4 inches long — making it suitable for gates up to about 1.5 inches thick.

## Real-World Performance

Mounting takes about ten minutes with a drill and a screwdriver. The screws included are decent, though I'd recommend swapping them for stainless ones if you're mounting in a coastal environment. The bolt slides smoothly right out of the box, and the spring tension feels solid — not too stiff, not floppy.

The locking mechanism is where this latch shines. The spring-loaded pin drops into a detent on the strike plate, which means the latch stays put even when the gate rattles in the wind. No more waking up to find your dog has nosed his way into the yard at 3 AM.

One thing I appreciate: the design prevents the bolt from sliding open accidentally when you're carrying something through the gate. You have to deliberately lift the handle and pull — it won't open on its own.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest talk: this isn't a heavy-duty security lock. It'll keep pets and curious toddlers in, but a determined adult could defeat it with a screwdriver. If you need actual security, you need a padlock hasp, not this.

The finish is brushed stainless, which shows fingerprints and water spots more than a painted finish would. Not a dealbreaker, but if you're a neat freak, keep a microfiber cloth handy.

Some users report the screws stripping if you overtighten them. The remedy is simple: drill pilot holes first. Don't skip that step.

The spring mechanism, while smooth, does require a bit of finger strength. If you have arthritis or limited hand mobility, this might be frustrating to operate one-handed.
