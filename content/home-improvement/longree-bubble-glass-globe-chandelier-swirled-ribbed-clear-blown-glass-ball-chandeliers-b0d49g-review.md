If you’ve been scrolling past a dozen identical drum pendants and brushed-nickel flush mounts, this Longree bubble glass chandelier is the visual reset your dining room has been waiting for. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it’s not just a pretty face — but it does come with a few caveats worth knowing before you click buy.

## What Actually Shows Up

The box contains six hand-blown glass globes, the metal frame (in your choice of finish), mounting hardware, and an instruction booklet. The globes are the star here — each one has a swirled, ribbed texture that catches light differently depending on the angle. The clear glass has a subtle bubble pattern trapped inside, which gives it that organic, high-end artisan look without the artisan price tag.

The canopy and arms are sturdy — this isn’t the flimsy, gold-painted plastic you sometimes get at this price point. The frame has a decent heft to it, which tells you the metal won’t bend when you’re adjusting the arms during installation.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

This is a six-light fixture, so you’re getting serious illumination. The ribbed glass does double duty: it scatters light to reduce the harsh glare you’d get with plain clear globes, and it creates that beautiful, textured shadow pattern on the ceiling when lit. One reviewer noted, "The swirls make the light look like it's dancing on the ceiling — guests always comment on it."

Installation is manageable for someone comfortable with basic wiring. The canopy mounts to a standard junction box, and the arms are pre-wired, which saves you the headache of threading wires through each arm. The fixture is compatible with sloped ceilings up to 45 degrees — a genuinely useful feature if you’re hanging it over a stairwell or in a vaulted great room.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The visual impact is the overwhelming theme in the reviews. Multiple buyers describe it as looking "way more expensive than it is." The hand-blown aspect means no two globes are identical, which adds to the bespoke feel.

Buyers also consistently mention the light quality. "I was worried the clear glass would be too harsh, but the ribbing diffuses it beautifully — it's warm and inviting, not blinding," one verified purchase wrote. Another noted that the fixture works surprisingly well in a bedroom with a dimmer switch, calling it "moody without being dark."

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be direct about the downsides. **No bulbs are included.** You’ll need six E12 candelabra-base bulbs, and if you want dimming capability, factor in the cost of dimmable LEDs — that’s an extra $20–30 you should budget for.

The bigger issue is the packaging situation. The glass globes ship in separate cartons inside the main box, and several reviewers reported receiving a cracked globe. Here’s the kicker: Amazon’s return policy on this item typically requires returning the entire fixture, not just the broken shade. One frustrated buyer wrote, "Had to uninstall the whole thing to return one broken globe. If you’re handy, it’s fine, but it’s a hassle."

Also note: the glass is delicate. You’ll want to wear gloves during installation because fingerprints on the ribbed texture are visible until you clean them off, and the glass is harder to polish than a smooth finish.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy this if:** You’re furnishing a dining room, entryway, or open-concept living space and want a statement piece that doesn’t scream "builder-grade." It’s also a strong choice for anyone with a sloped ceiling who’s been told their options are limited.

**Skip this if:** You want a fixture with included bulbs, you’re on a tight timeline and can’t afford a potential return-and-replace cycle for damaged glass, or you prefer a minimalist look — this fixture is busy by design.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use LED bulbs with this chandelier?**
A: Yes, and you should. The fixture takes E12 candelabra-base bulbs. LED bulbs run cooler, which matters here since the glass globes are sealed at the top and can trap heat.

**Q: Will this work on a vaulted ceiling?**
A: Yes — the canopy supports up to a 45-degree slope. For steeper angles, you’ll need an angled ceiling adapter, which isn’t included.

**Q: How long does installation take?**
A: Plan for 1.5 to 2 hours if you’re replacing an existing fixture and have basic wiring experience. The pre-wired arms help, but the glass globes are fiddly to secure.
