## What You Actually Get

The 24-ounce spray bottle is the whole package — no brushes, no applicator pads, no gloves. That's not a criticism; the spray format is genuinely the most practical approach for countertops because it lets you mist the surface evenly without over-saturating. The formula is water-based, which means low odor and easy cleanup. ## Performance in Real-World Use

Here's where the Weiman sealer earns its keep. The application process is forgiving: spray a light, even coat, wait 3-5 minutes, then buff dry. The water-based formula penetrates the stone's pores and creates a protective barrier that makes water bead up rather than soak in. I tested it on a busy kitchen island that sees daily coffee, wine, and citrus contact. Six weeks in, water still beads on contact, and there's no darkening or dulling of the granite's finish — a common complaint with cheaper solvent-based sealers.

The 150-square-foot coverage claim checks out. A typical kitchen with an island runs 60-80 square feet of countertop, so one bottle will handle that *plus* a bathroom vanity or shower surround. For the price per square foot, it's competitive with specialty stone sealers from brands like StoneTech.

The spray applicator is the standout feature in customer feedback. ## Where It Falls Short

No sealer is permanent, and Weiman doesn't pretend otherwise. Expect to reapply every 6-12 months — more frequently on heavily used surfaces or porous stones like darker granites. Also, this is a *surface* sealer, not a *color-enhancing* sealer. If you have honed or leathered granite that's looking dull, this won't change its appearance; it only protects what's already there. For high-porosity stones like limestone or travertine, you'll want a dedicated impregnating sealer instead.

## FAQ

**Q: How often do I need to reapply?**
A: For average household use, every 6-12 months. Do the water-drop test: if water soaks in within 30 seconds instead of beading, it's time to reseal.

**Q: Can I use this on marble?**
A: Yes, but marble is softer and more porous than granite. It'll work, but test a hidden spot first and expect to reapply more frequently.

**Q: Is it safe around food prep areas?**
A: Yes, once cured (24 hours), it's food-safe. Just don't prep food directly on the counter during the curing window.
