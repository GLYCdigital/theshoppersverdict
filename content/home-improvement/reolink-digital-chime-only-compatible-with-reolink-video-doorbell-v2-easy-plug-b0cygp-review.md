**The Review Body**

Most video doorbells have a dirty little secret: they rely on your existing mechanical chime, which often sounds like a dying mosquito or doesn't work at all if you have a digital transformer. Reolink’s answer is this dedicated Digital Chime, a $30-ish accessory that cuts the cord—literally. But is it a necessary add-on or just another plastic brick? After digging into the specs and 220+ user ratings (4.4 stars), here is the honest, no-spin breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The box is minimal: the chime unit itself, a quick-start guide, and a wall plug. There’s no wiring involved. The unit is compact—roughly the size of a small Wi-Fi extender—and plugs directly into a standard US outlet. As shown in the product image, it’s a white, rounded rectangle that won't win design awards but won't offend your decor either. The setup process is refreshingly simple: pair it with your Reolink doorbell via the mobile app, pick a tone, and you're done. No transformer math, no drilling holes.

**Performance & Real-World Use**

This is where the Reolink chime shines. The headline spec is the 90dB volume. To put that in perspective, that's louder than a typical alarm clock and about the same as a lawnmower from a distance. Multiple buyers confirm it’s genuinely loud. One reviewer noted, *"I can hear it clearly in my basement workshop with the door shut—my old mechanical chime was useless down there."* That’s the primary win: audibility.

The 260-foot Wi-Fi range is generous, allowing you to place the chime in a central hallway or even a back room, not just near the front door. It offers 20 distinct chime tones and a volume slider, which is more flexibility than most OEM chimes provide. The connection to the doorbell is rock-solid in the reviews; users rarely report missed rings or lag.

**Where It Falls Short**

Here’s the catch that might make you pause. This is a closed-loop system. It is **only** compatible with Reolink Video Doorbells (the wired PoE/Wi-Fi models, not the battery-only Argus series). If you ever switch brands, this becomes e-waste. That’s a significant lock-in factor.

The second issue is power dependency. Because it’s plugged into the wall, a power outage disables it. If your doorbell is PoE-powered (via a switch with backup), you might still get a doorbell press, but you won't hear the chime. That’s an edge case, but worth knowing. Also, a few users mentioned the tones sound slightly "digital" or tinny—a fair trade-off for the volume, but not as rich as a mechanical bell.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The "I Can't Hear It" Homeowner:** If your existing chime is quiet, broken, or located far from your living space, this solves that instantly.
- **The Reolink Ecosystem Devotee:** If you already have Reolink cameras and a doorbell, this integrates seamlessly into the app you already use.
- **The Renter:** Since it requires zero hardwiring, you can take it with you when you move, unlike a transformer-connected chime.

**Skip It If:** You own a non-Reolink doorbell (it will not work, no hacks) or you already have a loud, functioning mechanical chime that you don't mind.

**FAQ: Real Questions, Straight Answers**

**Q: Can I use two Reolink Digital Chimes in the same house?**
A: Yes. The Reolink app allows you to add multiple chimes to a single doorbell. This is a common setup for large homes or two-story layouts where one chime isn't enough.

**Q: Does it require the Reolink Home Hub or a subscription?**
A: No. It connects directly to your doorbell over your local Wi-Fi network. No subscription is required for the chime function itself; you only need the Reolink app.

**Q: Can I connect it to my existing mechanical doorbell wires?**
A: No. This is a plug-in digital unit only. It does not have terminals for hardwiring. It completely replaces your mechanical chime's function, not connects to it.

**The Verdict**

The Reolink Digital Chime is a niche product that does exactly what it promises, and it does it loudly. It solves a real problem (poor audibility) with a simple, elegant solution. The 4.4-star rating is earned, primarily due to its ease of use and volume. However, the strict proprietary compatibility is a double-edged sword—it's fantastic if you're all-in on Reolink, a dealbreaker if you're not.

**Rating: 4.2/5**

- **Buy it if:** You own a Reolink Video Doorbell and struggle to hear it, or you want a plug-and-play chime without touching your wiring.
- **Skip it if:** You don't own a compatible Reolink doorbell, or you prefer a hardwired solution that works during power outages.
