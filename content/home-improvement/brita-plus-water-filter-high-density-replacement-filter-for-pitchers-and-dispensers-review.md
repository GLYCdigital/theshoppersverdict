The Brita Plus isn't your older sibling's pitcher filter. While standard Brita filters have been the default dorm-room and apartment-kitchen staple for decades, the Plus line steps up the game with a denser carbon block that targets twice the contaminants. At $27.64 for a four-pack, you're looking at roughly $6.91 per filter — which covers two months of use. That's about 12 cents a day for water that's been stripped of chlorine, copper, mercury, and cadmium, all verified by WQA and NSF/ANSI certification.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get four individually wrapped filters. Each one measures about 4 inches tall and 2.5 inches in diameter, with the standard Brita twist-and-lock top that clicks into place in seconds. The packaging is straightforward — no proprietary connectors, no special tools, no alignment tricks. If you've changed a Brita filter before, you'll have this done in under two minutes. The high-density construction is visibly different from the standard white filters; the carbon block feels denser and heavier in the hand.

## Performance: Where the "Plus" Earns Its Name

The filtration claim isn't marketing fluff. Standard Brita filters reduce around 10 contaminants. The Plus line targets 20, with the headline additions being copper, mercury, and cadmium — heavy metals that can leach from aging plumbing. That's meaningful if you live in an older building or rely on municipal water that's passed through miles of pipes.

Taste-wise, the difference is noticeable within the first glass. Chlorine off-flavors disappear immediately, and there's a clean, neutral profile that makes tap water taste bottled. Users on Amazon consistently confirm this. One verified buyer noted, "I was skeptical about the 'plus' marketing, but the water genuinely tastes crisper than with the old filters. My wife noticed before I even told her I'd switched."

The flow rate is adequate but not quick. You'll wait about 30 seconds for a full glass through a standard pitcher. That's the tradeoff for denser media — and it gets slower as the filter approaches its two-month lifespan. Budget for that.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 4,231 ratings at 4.8 stars, the pattern is clear: people value the ease of use and the tangible water quality improvement. The two-month lifespan is also a practical sweet spot. Three months risks bacterial buildup in the media; one month is wasteful. Brita's recommendation aligns with what water filtration experts would suggest anyway.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: this is a taste-and-odor filter, not a water purifier. It's NSF/ANSI 42 and 53 certified for the contaminants listed — chlorine, copper, mercury, cadmium — but it does **not** remove lead, cysts, or PFAS. If your water has known lead issues, you need a certified lead-removal filter or a reverse osmosis system. Brita's marketing doesn't hide this, but some shoppers assume "2x contaminants" means more than it does.

The slower flow rate is also worth flagging. If you're filling a large dispenser or a 64-ounce bottle, you'll wait. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real difference from standard filters.

## Who Should Buy This

This four-pack is ideal for households using Brita pitchers or dispensers who want better contaminant reduction without switching systems. It's especially suited for:

- **Renters** with older plumbing who want heavy metal reduction without a permanent installation
- **Bottled-water refugees** looking to cut both cost and plastic waste without sacrificing taste
- **Coffee and tea drinkers** who notice off-flavors from tap water in their brews — the chlorine removal alone is worth it

Skip it if you're on well water with known bacterial issues, or if you need lead-specific filtration.
