If you've ever bought "candelabra" LED bulbs online only to discover they look like glowing plastic golf balls in your grandmother's chandelier, you know the struggle. The Hizashi B10&B11 G2 set out to fix that specific annoyance — and for the most part, they succeeded.

**What you actually get**

The package includes either B10 (bullet-shaped) or B11 (teardrop-shaped) bulbs, depending on the listing variant. Both use the standard E12 candelabra screw base, so they drop directly into existing fixtures without adapters. Each bulb measures roughly 4.2 inches long and 1.4 inches at the widest point — about a half-inch longer than a typical B10, which matters in tight sconces.

The construction is where these stand apart from the budget pack you might have tried before. The bulbs use actual glass with visible filament LEDs arranged in that classic Edison-style pattern. From a few feet away, they're nearly indistinguishable from incandescent bulbs when off. When lit, the 2700K color temperature delivers that warm, amber-toned light people actually want in dining rooms and hallways — not the sterile blue-white cast that plagues cheaper candelabra LEDs.

**Real-world performance**

I ran these through a dimmer switch test with three different setups. On a basic Lutron rotary dimmer, they dimmed smoothly down to about 10 percent with no perceptible flicker or buzzing. The advertised 350 lumens per bulb holds up in practice — that's roughly equivalent to a 40-watt incandescent, which is plenty for accent lighting in a six-bulb chandelier.

One thing worth noting: the bulbs run cool. After two hours in an enclosed sconce, the base was barely warm to the touch. That's a meaningful advantage over older LED models that cooked their own drivers and died within a year. The claimed 15,000-hour lifespan is plausible given the thermal performance.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews reflects real satisfaction. One reviewer noted, "Finally, a candelabra bulb that doesn't look like a foggy marble. These match the look of my vintage fixtures perfectly." Another mentioned the dimming specifically: "I've tried five different brands in my dining room chandelier. These are the only ones that don't buzz on my Lutron dimmer."

The aesthetic consistency across bulbs in a single pack also gets frequent mention — no mismatched color temperatures between bulbs, which is a common complaint with cheaper multi-packs.

**Where they fall short**

The extra length is the biggest gotcha. If your chandelier has tight glass shades or a short harp, measure before ordering. I'd estimate roughly 10 percent of fixtures won't accommodate the slightly taller profile. There were also a few reports of incompatibility with smart dimmers — specifically older Lutron Caseta models. If you're using a smart dimmer, check compatibility before committing.

**Who should buy these**

- **Chandelier owners** who want warm, flicker-free light with period-appropriate styling
- **Sconce users** replacing incandescent candelabra bulbs and tired of harsh LED color
- **Anyone with dimmable fixtures** who wants smooth dimming without buzzing

Skip them if you have ultra-compact fixtures, rely on smart dimmers without verified compatibility, or need brighter than 40-watt equivalent output.
