# QiyuanLS Socket Ceiling Fan with Light Review: A Smart Fan That Screws In Like a Lightbulb

Most ceiling fans require a weekend, a voltage tester, and a prayer that your attic access isn't blocked. The QiyuanLS 19-inch smart ceiling fan sidesteps all of that by doing something almost suspiciously simple: it screws directly into your existing E26 light socket. No wiring. No junction box. No electrician. If you can change a lightbulb, you can install this fan in under ten minutes.

That's the headline. But is the convenience worth the tradeoffs? After digging through 215 ratings and the spec sheet, here's what you need to know before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the fan unit with an integrated 24W LED panel, a handheld RF remote, and mounting hardware that's almost unnecessary given the tool-free design. The 19-inch blade span is compact — this is not a statement piece. The finish is a matte white/black option depending on the variant, and the whole unit weighs just a few pounds, which means it won't stress a ceiling socket rated for standard light fixtures.

The socket-mount design is the hero feature. You twist out your existing bulb, screw in the fan's base, and secure the canopy. It's genuinely that simple. For renters, this is a game-changer — you can take it with you when you move and reverse the install in minutes.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The DC motor is where this fan punches above its weight class. At low speeds, it's near-silent — one reviewer noted, "I can't hear it running at all on speed 1, and the air movement is still noticeable from across the room." That's consistent with DC motor behavior: more torque per watt, less hum, lower energy draw than comparable AC fans.

Airflow tops out at around 300 CFM (cubic feet per minute), which is modest. A standard 52-inch fan moves 4,000-6,000 CFM. The QiyuanLS is designed for close-range cooling — a desk, a bed, a reading nook — not for circulating a 20x20 living room. Multiple reviewers explicitly stated this limitation, with one writing, "It's perfect for my office, but I wouldn't use it as the main fan in my bedroom."

The 24W LED panel is dimmable via the remote and app, with color temperature adjustability (typically 3000K-6000K). It's adequate for ambient lighting. If you need task-level illumination for detailed work, keep your existing lamp.

## Smart Features: Where It Shines and Stumbles

The smart integration is genuinely useful. The fan pairs with both the Tuya/Smart Life app and works with Alexa and Google Assistant. You can control speed, light dimming, and scheduling from your phone or voice. The RF remote works without Wi-Fi, which is a practical redundancy.

The catch: app setup requires a 2.4GHz network. If your router broadcasts a combined SSID, you'll need to split bands or temporarily disable 5GHz. It's a common smart-home annoyance, but worth flagging. One customer wrote, "Took me 20 minutes to figure out the band issue. Once I did, it connected instantly."

## Where It Falls Short

- **Coverage area:** 19 inches is small. This is a personal fan, not a room fan. Buyers expecting whole-room circulation will be disappointed.
- **Light output:** 24W is decent for ambiance but underwhelming for task lighting. If you need bright overhead light, look elsewhere.
- **Aesthetics:** The compact design looks fine, but it's utilitarian. It won't elevate a dining room's decor.
- **App friction:** The 2.4GHz requirement is a real hurdle for some users, and the app is generic Tuya — functional but not polished.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters** who want ceiling fan airflow without permanently modifying their space.
- **Anyone with a ceiling-mounted light socket** in a bedroom, office, or small den who wants smart control without an electrician.
- **Smart-home enthusiasts** who want voice control over fan speed and dimming without buying a separate smart fan controller.

**Skip it if** you need to cool a large room, want a designer fixture, or require bright task lighting from your ceiling mount.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this fan be installed on a sloped ceiling?**
A: No. The socket-mount design requires a flat, horizontal ceiling socket. Sloped ceilings will cause the fan to hang at an angle and may affect balance.

**Q: Does the remote require line-of-sight?**
A: No, it's RF (radio frequency), so it works through walls and from across the room. No pointing required.

**Q: Can I use this without Wi-Fi?**
A: Yes. The RF remote controls all functions — speed, light, dimming, and reverse. Smart features are an addition, not a requirement.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The QiyuanLS socket ceiling fan is a niche product executed well. It solves a real problem — getting ceiling fan airflow without an electrician — and adds genuinely useful smart features at a price that undercuts most hardwired smart fans. The tradeoffs are clear: it's small, the light is modest, and setup requires a 2.4GHz network. But if your use case matches its capabilities, this is a smart, practical buy.

**Buy it if** you want tool-free installation, smart control, and quiet airflow in a small-to-medium space. **Skip it if** you need whole-room circulation or bright overhead lighting.
