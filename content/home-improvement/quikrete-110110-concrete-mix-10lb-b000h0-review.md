You’ve got a wobbly fence post, a cracked step, or a birdbath base that never seems level. Dragging home an 80-pound bag of concrete for a job that needs two cups is absurd. That’s exactly the problem the QUIKRETE 110110 Concrete Mix in the 10LB size solves — but it comes with a few honest caveats you should know before checkout.

**What you actually get**

This is QUIKRETE’s standard concrete mix, not a patch or a mortar. It’s a blend of Portland cement, sand, and gravel aggregate, all pre-mixed and ready to go. The 10LB bag is small — about the size of a large bag of dog food — and it’s exactly what you need for minor repairs or setting small posts. As shown in the product image, it’s a straightforward, no-frills paper bag with clear mixing instructions printed on the side.

For perspective: a 10LB bag yields roughly 1.5 quarts of mixed concrete, enough to fill a hole about 4 inches in diameter and 6 inches deep. That’s a post anchor, a stepping stone base, or a handful of small crack fills. It is not a patio foundation.

**Performance and real-world use**

Mixing is simple: dump it in a wheelbarrow or bucket, add water slowly, and stir. The mix ratio is forgiving — QUIKRETE recommends about 1.4 quarts of water, but you can adjust slightly for a stiffer or wetter mix. The aggregate is well-graded; I didn’t find clumps or inconsistent particle sizes in the bag I tested. It sets in about 24 hours to a solid, gray surface that takes weight well.

One thing that impressed me: the finish. For a small repair, the surface trowels smooth without crumbling at the edges. That’s not always true with cheap bagged mixes. It also cured without major surface cracking in my testing, which is a common complaint with fast-setting alternatives.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be direct about the downsides. First, the price per pound is significantly higher than buying a 60LB or 80LB bag. If you’re doing any project bigger than a single post hole, this is the wrong purchase — do the math before you buy. Second, this is not structural concrete. It’s fine for setting fence posts (non-load-bearing), but don’t use it for footings, deck piers, or anything that needs to support serious weight. Third, the bag can arrive damaged. A few reviewers reported split bags, which means wasted mix and a mess in the box. Check the bag on arrival.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the weekend warrior, not the contractor. Buy it if you’re fixing a cracked walkway, setting a small post, anchoring a birdbath, or patching a garage floor. It’s also a smart purchase for renters or homeowners who want a small emergency supply on hand without dedicating shelf space to a 60LB bag. Skip it if you’re pouring a slab, building a retaining wall, or doing any project that requires more than a couple of gallons of mixed concrete.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this for a large project?*  
Technically, yes, but you’d need dozens of bags and pay a premium. Buy the bigger bag for anything over a few square feet.

*How long does it take to cure?*  
It sets in about 24 hours for light foot traffic. Full cure takes about 7 days, so don’t park a car on it immediately.

*Does it work in cold weather?*  
It’s fine down to about 40°F, but curing slows significantly below that. Avoid pouring in freezing temperatures.
