You know that moment when you're grinding a weld bead in a corner that's too tight for an angle grinder, and you're holding your breath because the cord is about to snag on something? The DEWALT DCG426B kills that problem. This is a cordless die grinder that actually behaves like a corded one — most of the time.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the die grinder body, a side handle, a spanner wrench, and a 1/4-inch collet. No battery, no charger, no case. That's the "tool only" deal — if you're new to DEWALT's 20V Max system, budget for a battery and charger before you can use it. If you already own 20V Max tools, you're set.

The tool itself is compact: 11.7 inches long, 2.9 pounds without the battery. The variable speed dial sits at the base of the tool — it's a thumb wheel you can adjust even with gloves on, which matters more than you'd think when you're mid-task.

## Performance That Surprises

The brushless motor is the story here. It spins between 8,000 and 28,000 RPM, and the variable speed control is genuinely useful — you can drop to low RPM for polishing or deburring soft metals, then crank it up for serious stock removal. The electronic brake stops the accessory almost instantly when you release the trigger, which is a safety feature you'll appreciate the first time a cutoff wheel grabs.

In real-world use, this thing eats through tasks a corded grinder would handle. I've used it to port-match an intake manifold, clean up aluminum castings, and cut through 1/8-inch steel with a cutoff wheel. The side handle threads into either side, so lefties aren't screwed. Vibration is manageable — not as smooth as a high-end corded unit, but better than most cordless die grinders I've tested.

Battery life is the honest tradeoff. With a 5Ah battery, you'll get roughly 20–25 minutes of continuous heavy use. For occasional jobs — a few minutes of grinding here and there — a single battery gets you through a day. For production work, you'll want two batteries and a fast charger. The brushless motor does stretch runtime noticeably compared to the older brushed version, so that's something.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.8-star average across 240 ratings isn't an accident. The recurring theme in reviews is the power-to-portability ratio. One buyer summed it up: *"I was skeptical a cordless die grinder could keep up with my pneumatic one. This thing is close — and I don't have to drag a compressor around anymore."* Another noted the variable speed as the deciding factor: *"The speed control is what sold me. I can polish one minute and grind the next without switching tools."*

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a work light is genuinely annoying. At 28,000 RPM, you're often working in dim conditions — under a car, inside a cabinet, behind a machine. A simple LED would've cost DEWALT pennies. Buy a headlamp.

The collet wrench is functional but thin — don't lose it, because aftermarket replacements are hit-or-miss. And while the tool is compact, the battery adds bulk at the base. It's not a dealbreaker, but it affects how the tool balances in tight spots.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a DIYer or professional who already owns DEWALT 20V Max tools, you do occasional grinding/deburring/polishing work, and you're tired of dragging a cord or a compressor around.

**Skip it if:** You need a dedicated production grinder that runs all day (buy a corded model), or you're starting from scratch with no DEWALT batteries — the total cost with batteries and charger approaches $250, and a corded unit at half the price might serve you better.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this use standard 1/4-inch and 1/8-inch accessories?**
A: Yes. It ships with a 1/4-inch collet, but DEWALT sells an optional 1/8-inch collet (part number DW4902) that costs about $10. You can also use 2-inch and 3-inch cutoff wheels with the appropriate arbor.

**Q: How does it compare to a pneumatic die grinder?**
A: Power-wise, it's close to a mid-range air grinder. Torque drops somewhat under heavy load, but the convenience of no compressor, no hose, and no moisture issues in the air line often outweighs the slight power loss. For occasional use, the cordless wins. For continuous production grinding, air is still king.

**Q: What battery should I use?**
A: The 5Ah DCB205 or the 6Ah DCB206 are the sweet spots — they balance runtime against the added weight at the base. The 2Ah batteries work but drain fast under heavy load. Avoid the flexvolt batteries here; they're heavier and add no performance benefit to this tool.

## The Verdict

**4.6/5 stars.** The DEWALT DCG426B is the best cordless die grinder I've tested at this price point. The brushless motor delivers genuine power, the variable speed is functional rather than gimmicky, and the build quality is typical DEWALT — tough and dependable. It's not perfect: the missing work light and tool-only packaging are real annoyances. But if you're already in the 20V Max ecosystem, this is a no-brainer addition to your arsenal. If you're not, weigh the total cost of entry before you commit.

**Buy it if** you want cordless convenience without sacrificing real grinding power. **Skip it if** you need all-day runtime or you're starting from zero with DEWALT batteries.
