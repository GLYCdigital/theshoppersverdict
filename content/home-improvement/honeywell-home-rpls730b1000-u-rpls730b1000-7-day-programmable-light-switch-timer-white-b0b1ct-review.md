If you’re still flipping the same light switch every single day like it’s 1995, the Honeywell Home RPLS730B1000 is the upgrade you didn’t know you needed. This isn’t a gimmicky smart switch that demands an app, a Wi-Fi password, and a PhD in network troubleshooting. It’s a straightforward, hardwired 7-day programmable timer that does exactly one thing — turns lights on and off on your schedule — and does it reliably. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, it’s clearly earning its keep in real homes, not just spec sheets.

## What’s Actually in the Box

You get the switch itself, a wire nut pack, and a basic instruction manual. That’s it. No wall plate is included, so if you’re replacing an existing switch, you’ll reuse the old cover plate — unless you want a fresh one, buy separately. The unit measures a standard single-gang size, so it fits most existing boxes without modification. It’s rated for 120VAC, 60Hz, and handles up to 960 watts of incandescent lighting or 1/2 HP for motors, which covers ceiling fans and most household fixtures.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The faceplate is a matte white plastic that resists fingerprints decently, and the buttons have a crisp, tactile click. The display is backlit — a small but welcome touch for hallway or garage installs where you’re fumbling in the dark. Wiring is straightforward: line, load, neutral, and ground. The neutral wire requirement is worth noting — if your switch box only has two wires (hot and load), this unit won’t work without running a new wire. That’s the single biggest installation hurdle buyers mention.

Once installed, programming is where the Honeywell earns its keep. You get four independent on/off events per day, and each day of the week can hold a different schedule. That means you can have the porch light on from dusk to 11 PM on weekdays, but keep it on until 1 AM on weekends. The astronomical clock feature is the standout — it calculates sunrise and sunset for your zip code and automatically shifts your “on at dusk” times as the seasons change. Set it once in the fall, and you don’t touch it again until daylight saving time. That’s a genuinely useful feature most competing timers lack.

One buyer summed it up well: *“I set it once for our front porch light and haven’t touched it in months. The sunset tracking is spot-on.”* Another noted: *“The manual override is intuitive — tap to turn on, tap again to turn off, and it doesn’t mess up the schedule.”* That’s a common pain point with cheaper timers, which often require you to skip or reset programs just to flip a light on temporarily.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest: the programming interface is not modern. You’re navigating through numbered menus and holding down buttons to set values — it works, but it feels like programming a VCR from 2003. Budget 10-15 minutes with the manual for your first setup. It’s not hard, but it’s not intuitive either.

The display is small, and at arm’s length, reading the tiny day-of-week indicator can be a squint. Also, the sunset offset caps at ±2 hours. If you live in the Pacific Northwest or Alaska, where winter days are drastically shorter, you may need to manually tweak schedules anyway, which somewhat defeats the astronomical feature.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for three specific buyers:

- **Porch light and exterior lighting owners** who want dusk-to-dawn automation without buying a photocell sensor. The astronomical clock handles it automatically.
- **Vacation homeowners** who need a consistent, foolproof schedule that doesn’t depend on an internet connection or app updates.
- **Anyone tired of smart-home dependency** — if your Wi-Fi drops, this switch still does its job. No cloud, no account, no subscription.

Skip it if you want app control from your phone or if your switch boxes lack a neutral wire. Also, if you need random vacation mode to simulate occupancy, this doesn’t offer that feature.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with LED bulbs?**
A: Yes, as long as the total load is under 960 watts. LEDs draw far less, so you can often run multiple fixtures on one switch. Just don’t exceed the rating.

**Q: Will the schedule reset after a power outage?**
A: The timer has a battery backup that preserves your program for a limited time. It won’t remember the current time indefinitely, but short outages won’t wipe your settings.

**Q: Can I use this for a bathroom exhaust fan?**
A: Yes, up to 1/2 HP. It’s a common use — set it to run for 15 minutes after you shower, then shut off automatically.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want dependable, set-and-forget lighting control without smart-home complexity. The astronomical clock alone justifies the price for exterior lights.

**Skip it if** you need phone app control, lack a neutral wire, or refuse to spend 10 minutes with a manual.
