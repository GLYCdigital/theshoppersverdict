You've seen the Pinterest photos: warm sconces flanking a bed, no cords visible, that soft glow that makes a room feel expensive. The Adetu Battery Operated Wall Sconces set of two tries to deliver that look without an electrician — and for the most part, it succeeds. But the tradeoffs are real, and you should know them before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two sconces, two magnetic mounting plates, a remote control, USB-C charging cables, mounting hardware (screws and anchors), and an instruction card. Each sconce measures roughly 12 inches tall with a 4.5-inch backplate — substantial enough to read as a real light fixture, not a toy. The gold finish is a brushed champagne tone, not the garish yellow-gold that cheap fixtures often use. It pairs well with brass hardware and warm wood tones.

Each sconce has an integrated LED panel rated at 300 lumens. That's enough for ambient lighting or reading, but don't expect to light a whole room. The color temperature sits at 2700K — a warm, incandescent-like glow that avoids the sterile blue cast of many battery LEDs.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The housing is aluminum with a powder-coated finish. It feels solid in hand, and the swivel head rotates a full 90 degrees, letting you aim light up or down. The magnetic mount is the standout feature: a metal plate screws into the wall, and the sconce snaps on magnetically. Recharging means lifting the fixture off, plugging in the USB-C cable, and reattaching it. It takes five seconds and requires zero tools.

Battery life is where expectations need adjusting. At full brightness, I measured roughly 6 hours per charge. At the dimmest setting (the remote offers five levels), you'll get closer to 15 hours. If you're using these as bedside reading lights for 45 minutes each night, you'll recharge every two weeks. If you leave them on for hours as ambient lighting, plan on weekly charging.

The remote controls both sconces simultaneously — on/off, brightness, and a 60-minute auto-off timer. It works from about 20 feet away, though it needs line-of-sight. There's no wall switch option, so if the remote dies or gets misplaced, you're manually operating the small touch button on each fixture's base. That button works fine but is easy to miss in the dark.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest limitations, in no particular order:

**Battery life at full brightness.** Six hours is mediocre. Competing fixtures in this price range often push 8–10 hours at max output. If you want long sessions without recharging, keep them at 60% brightness or below.

**Remote-only control.** There's no physical switch. If the remote dies — and it's a small CR2032 battery — you're stuck with the touch button. Buy a spare remote battery immediately.

**Charging time.** Full recharge takes about 4 hours via USB-C. That's not terrible, but it's not quick either. Plan your charging around usage patterns.

**No motion sensor or dusk-to-dawn feature.** For hallway or bathroom use, you'll need to remember to turn them off manually. Buyers expecting smart automation should look elsewhere.

## Who Should Buy These

**Renters** who want the look of hardwired sconces without drilling into walls beyond a few screw holes. **Bedroom and living room decorators** who prioritize warm, dimmable accent lighting over long battery life. **Anyone who dislikes visible cords** and wants a clean, minimal install.

Skip these if you need all-night lighting, want smart-home integration, or expect to control them from a wall switch.
