Let’s cut through the noise: most handheld shower heads are either weak trickles or overpriced plastic gadgets that break in six months. The Delta Faucet ProClean is different—it’s built like a tool, not a bathroom accessory. With a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, it’s clearly earning its keep in real homes. Here’s the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the main shower head, the handheld wand, a 60-inch stainless steel hose, a mounting bracket, and the required washers and thread tape. No tools needed—the whole thing threads onto a standard 1/2-inch shower arm by hand. The head itself is metal with a brushed nickel finish that resists fingerprints better than chrome. The handheld is surprisingly hefty, which tells you it’s not hollow plastic.

**Performance: where it shines**

The headline feature is the "ProClean" spray—a concentrated, high-pressure jet that’s genuinely useful. One buyer put it simply: *"I use it to rinse the dog after muddy walks and to blast soap scum off the shower walls. It actually has force, not just noise."* That tracks. The focused stream hits hard enough to clean grout lines without a separate pressure washer attachment. For pet owners, this alone justifies the price.

The magnetic docking is another win. The handheld snaps into place with a satisfying click and stays put—no more wand drooping down mid-shower. The 60-inch hose gives you full reach across a standard tub, and the pause valve on the wand lets you stop flow without losing your temperature setting.

**Where it falls short**

The 7 spray settings sound impressive, but in practice, half of them are variations of "gentle rain." The massage mode is the weakest setting—it feels like a lukewarm drizzle rather than an actual massage, which is disappointing for a product marketed around pressure. Also, the plastic collar where the hose meets the wand is the most common failure point. One reviewer noted: *"Worked great for 8 months, then the collar cracked and started leaking. Delta replaced it, but I shouldn't have to babysit a shower head."* If you habitually twist the wand while using it, be gentle.

**Who should buy this**

- **Pet owners**: The ProClean spray is a game-changer for muddy paws and kennel cleanup.
- **Renters or apartment dwellers**: Tool-free install means you can take it with you when you move, and it fits any standard shower arm.
- **Anyone with low water pressure**: The focused jet amplifies weak flow better than most wide-head designs.

Skip it if you primarily want a luxury rainfall experience—this is a utility-focused shower head, not a spa fixture. And if you have children who will yank the hose aggressively, budget for a replacement hose down the line.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my existing shower arm?**
A: Yes, if you have a standard 1/2-inch threaded arm. It’s the universal size in North America. If you have a custom or European fixture, you’ll need an adapter.

**Q: How do I clean the nozzles if they clog?**
A: The rubber nozzles are flexible—rub your thumb across them while the water runs to dislodge mineral buildup. No soaking in vinegar needed.

**Q: Does the handheld reach a full-size bathtub?**
A: The 60-inch hose covers most standard tubs, but if you have an extra-long soaking tub, measure first. You can buy a longer hose separately.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need real cleaning power from your shower head, want tool-free installation, or have pets that make messes. **Skip it if** you want a spa-like rainfall experience or need a unit that ignores rough handling.

At 4.2/5, this is a solid, functional upgrade for the money. It’s not perfect—the weak massage mode and plastic hose collar are real flaws—but for its intended purpose as a cleaning tool, it’s one of the best options under $100. Delta’s lifetime warranty covers the finish and function, so the risk is manageable.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A practical, high-pressure performer with minor durability concerns. Buy it for the ProClean spray, not the massage feature.
