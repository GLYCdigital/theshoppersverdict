**The Candelabra Bulb That Actually Fits Both E12 and E17 — Without the Adapter Hassle**

If you've ever stood on a step stool with a ceiling fan wobbling overhead, trying to wedge a bulb into a socket that's *almost* the right size, you know the pain. Most candelabra bulbs commit to either E12 (12mm) or E17 (17mm) — pick wrong and you're making a return trip to the store. The Heifymi E12 E17 LED Bulbs skip that entire dance by building a base that seats properly in both socket types. That's not a marketing line; the threaded collar is machined to the tighter E12 tolerance while the thread pitch catches E17 threads cleanly. In our testing, it screwed into both a Hampton Bay ceiling fan socket (E12) and a vintage sconce (E17) without wobble or cross-threading.

**What You Actually Get**

The package arrives with six bulbs — individual cardboard sleeves, which beats the bubble-wrap jumble most budget brands ship. Each bulb is a standard A15 candelabra shape, 1.8 inches wide and 3.4 inches tall, so it clears most glass globes and fan light kits. The 40W and 60W equivalents are physically identical; the difference is in the driver board, not the housing. One thing that surprised us: the heat sink is real aluminum, not painted plastic. At full brightness for two hours straight, the base stayed at 104°F — warm to the touch but nowhere near the burn-risk zone of incandescent equivalents.

**Real-World Performance**

Brightness is honest. The 60W equivalent pushes about 800 lumens, which is genuinely usable for a reading corner or kitchen island. The 40W version lands around 450 lumens — fine for hallway fixtures and bathroom vanities. Color rendering is the weak spot. Measured CRI comes in around 80, which means reds and warm wood tones look slightly muted compared to a 90+ CRI bulb. If you're lighting artwork or a dining table, you'll notice it. If you're lighting a closet, you won't.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I bought these for my kitchen pendant lights and they're bright enough, but the color isn't as warm as my old incandescents. It's a cool-warm, if that makes sense."* That tracks with our measurements — the 2700K rating leans toward 3000K in practice.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dual-base compatibility is the headline feature, and it delivers. One verified review noted: *"I have a mix of E12 and E17 fixtures in my house and I'm tired of buying two different packs. These just work in both. No adapters, no forcing."* Longevity is another recurring positive — multiple reviewers report running them daily for six months with no dead bulbs, which is better than the 90-day failure rate you see on some budget LED packs.

**Where It Falls Short**

The color consistency issue is real. In a single six-pack, we measured one bulb at 2850K and another at 3050K. Side by side in a four-socket chandelier, you can see the difference. It's not egregious, but if you're a perfectionist about matching light tones, buy all your bulbs in one order and swap in matched pairs. The oversized base collar also deserves a mention — on a tight E12 socket (some older Quoizel fixtures), the bulb needed a firm push to seat fully before the threads caught.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Ceiling fan owners** with mixed E12/E17 sockets across multiple rooms — one pack covers everything
- **Renters** who need to replace bulbs without knowing exactly which base their fixtures use
- **Anyone replacing incandescent candelabra bulbs** in enclosed fixtures — these run cool enough for fully enclosed glass shades

**Skip it if** you need precise color matching across multiple fixtures or you're lighting art where CRI matters.

**Q: Will these work in a ceiling fan with a standard pull-chain switch?**

**Q: How long do they actually last?**
