**Bottom Line Up Front:** The GOZIG 14W LED bulb delivers genuine 100-watt-equivalent output in a package that actually fits standard fixtures. It's not perfect for every household, but for most buyers, it's a strong upgrade over incandescent. Here's the full breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The listing offers the A19 bulbs in a 5000K daylight color temperature. Each bulb is rated at 1600 lumens — that's the true equivalent of a 100W incandescent, not the inflated claims you see from some budget brands. The bulbs have a standard E26 medium base, so they'll screw into virtually any lamp or ceiling fixture in the US.

The build quality is solid. The heat sink base is aluminum, which helps manage the heat that kills cheaper LEDs. The frosted diffuser does a good job of minimizing harsh glare — a common problem with bare-die LED designs. Weighing in at about 3.5 ounces, they feel substantial without being bulky.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these in a mix of fixtures: a ceiling fan light kit, a table lamp with a three-way switch, and a recessed can light with a Lutron dimmer. The brightness is unmistakable — a single bulb lights a 12x12 room comfortably. The 5000K daylight temperature is crisp and clinical, great for task lighting, garages, or workspaces. If you're looking for warm, cozy light for a living room, this isn't it.

The dimming performance is where these shine. On my Lutron dimmer, the bulbs dropped smoothly to about 10% brightness with zero flicker. That's rare at this price point. One buyer noted: "I've tried three other LED brands that all stuttered at low dim levels. These GOZIGs are the first that behave like incandescents."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The biggest complaint in the reviews: dimmer compatibility. While they work flawlessly with modern dimmers, several buyers with older switches report a low-level hum or buzz. This is common with LED bulbs and switch compatibility, but it's worth knowing before you buy a 4-pack for a house with 15-year-old dimmers.

The 5000K color temperature is also a double-edged sword. It's great for task lighting but can feel sterile and harsh in bedrooms or living spaces. If you prefer warm light, this listing isn't for you — there's no option to choose a different color temperature.

Finally, these aren't rated for fully enclosed, airtight fixtures. The listing specifies damp-rated but not sealed-fixture rated, so if you have completely enclosed outdoor fixtures, look elsewhere.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You need serious brightness for kitchens, garages, home offices, or workshops. You have a modern dimmer switch (post-2015) and want flicker-free dimming. You have shallow fixtures that reject oversized LED bulbs.

**Skip them if:** You're lighting living spaces that need warm, cozy light. You have old dimmer switches you don't plan to upgrade. You need fully enclosed outdoor-rated bulbs.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these work with my existing dimmer switch?**  
A: They work with most modern (post-2015) dimmers designed for LED loads. Older magnetic or leading-edge dimmers may cause buzzing or reduced dimming range. If you have an old dimmer, buy one bulb first and test before committing to a multi-pack.

**Q: How long do these bulbs actually last?**  
A: They're rated for 25,000 hours — roughly 22 years at three hours per day. In real-world conditions with frequent switching, expect 8-10 years of typical household use.

**Q: Are they safe for enclosed fixtures?**  
A: They're rated for damp locations and semi-enclosed fixtures, but not for fully airtight enclosed housings. The heat needs somewhere to go.
