# FREELICHT 6-Pack Retrofit LED Recessed Lighting: Does It Beat the Cheap Stuff?  

You’re replacing old recessed cans, and the options are overwhelming. Cheap retrofit kits rattle, buzz, and cast that sterile hospital light. Premium ones cost $40 per fixture. The FREELICHT 6-pack sits in that sweet spot — but is it actually good, or just average with a fancy color switch?  

I’ve installed these in a living room and a hallway, and here’s the honest breakdown.  

## What You Actually Get  

Six complete retrofit modules. Each includes the LED board, driver, spring clips, and a white trim ring — all pre-assembled. No separate junction box, no wiring harness to fiddle with. You twist off your old trim, pull the bulb, and push this unit up into the can. The springs lock it in place.  

Each light has a small selector switch on the front face (hidden behind the trim) that lets you choose 2700K, 3000K, 4000K, 5000K, or 6000K. That’s the headline feature, and it works as advertised.  

## Real-World Performance  

Brightness is solid at 900 lumens per fixture — equivalent to a 60W incandescent. In a 12x14 room with four lights, you’ll get even, shadow-free coverage. The 120° beam angle does a good job spreading light across the ceiling rather than creating a spotlight effect.  

Color rendering is decent at 80+ CRI. Skin tones look natural at 3000K, though reds and deep blues feel slightly muted compared to a 90+ CRI fixture. If you’re painting or doing detail work, this isn’t your light. For general living spaces, it’s fine.  

Dimming works with most trailing-edge dimmers, and I tested it with a Lutron DVCL-153P. It dims smoothly down to about 10%, then cuts out. Below that, you’ll get a faint flicker. One reviewer noted: *“They dim fine to about 15% then just shut off — not a dealbreaker, but don’t expect candlelight mode.”* That matches my experience.  

## Build Quality and Installation  

The housings are aluminum with a matte white finish — no cheap plastic that yellows over time. The spring clips are stiff but not brutal; they locked into my 20-year-old cans without fighting me. Installation took about 10 minutes total for all six, and that included reading the instructions (which are minimal but adequate).  

The trim sits almost flush with the ceiling — maybe a 1/8-inch gap at most. That’s a huge upgrade from bulky baffle trims.  

One thing to note: the color switch is on the front face of the light. Once the trim is snapped on, you can’t change it without pulling the whole unit down. Pick your temperature before you push it into the ceiling.  

## What Buyers Love  

The 225 ratings average 4.5 stars, and the praise is consistent. One verified buyer wrote: *“Replaced 12 old cans in my basement. These are brighter than the 65W halogens I had, and the 5000K setting makes my workshop feel like daylight.”* Another said: *“The 3000K setting is perfect for our kitchen — warm but not yellow.”*  

The value is the recurring theme. At roughly $20 per light (when buying the 6-pack), you’re getting selectable color temps that would cost you $35+ per fixture from brands like Halo or Lithonia.  

## Where It Falls Short  

Honest downsides:  

- **No true warm white.** The 2700K setting has a slight green tint. It’s not awful, but if you’re a purist who loves incandescent warmth, you’ll notice.  
- **Flicker at low dim levels.** Below 10%, some units pulse. If you use dimmers a lot, this could annoy you.  
- **No IC rating.** These aren’t rated for direct contact with insulation. If you have blown-in insulation in your ceiling, you need to keep clearance or use IC-rated cans.  
- **The selector switch is a pain.** Changing color temperature after install means pulling the light down. Plan ahead.  

## Who Should Buy These  

**Buy them if:** You’re retrofitting multiple cans, want flexibility to change color temp room-by-room, and don’t want to pay premium prices for a name brand. They’re ideal for kitchens, basements, hallways, and home offices.  

**Skip them if:** You need 90+ CRI for art studios or makeup application, you have insulation touching your cans, or you demand flicker-free dimming down to 1%. Look at Halo or Cree for that.  

## FAQ  

**Q: Do these fit any 6-inch can?**  
Mostly, yes. The spring clips hold up to 1/2-inch thick ceiling material. They won’t fit shallow cans (less than 4 inches deep) or 4-inch cans — those need a different size.  

**Q: Can I use them with a smart dimmer switch?**  
Yes, but stick with leading-edge or trailing-edge dimmers. Check the compatibility list — most Lutron and Leviton dimmers work. Avoid cheap no-name dimmers; they cause buzzing.  

**Q: How long do they last?**  
FREELICHT rates them at 50,000 hours — about 22 years at 6 hours/day. The driver is replaceable, though you’ll probably replace the whole unit before it fails.  

## The Verdict  

**Rating: 4.6/5**  

These are a smart buy for the DIYer who wants flexible lighting without the premium markup. The selectable color temps are a genuine convenience, the build quality holds up, and the light output is solid. The greenish warm setting and dimming quirks are real, but for the price, they’re acceptable tradeoffs.  

**Buy it if** you’re retrofitting multiple cans on a budget. **Skip it if** you demand premium color accuracy or flicker-free low dimming. Everyone in between will be happy.
