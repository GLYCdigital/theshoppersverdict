If your basement, laundry room, or water heater closet has ever flooded, you know the smell of wet drywall and the sting of a $3,000 insurance deductible. The METAK Water Leak Detector Alarm is a $20 insurance policy that screams before the damage starts. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this little white puck has earned its place in thousands of homes. But is it the right leak detector for *your* situation? Here’s what I found after putting one through real-world conditions.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains the main alarm unit, a separate sensor probe on a 4-foot cable, and a mounting bracket. You supply three AAA batteries (not included — a minor annoyance). The two-piece design is the smart part: you place the sensor pad directly on the floor near a leak-prone spot, while the alarm unit can be mounted higher on a wall or cabinet where it stays dry. The cable is long enough to snake around obstacles in most utility closets.

**How it performs in the real world**  
The alarm specs claim 110 decibels at 3 feet. I tested it in a laundry room with a running washer and dryer — the sound was unmistakable. The three-color LED (green for standby, red for alarm) is bright enough to spot across a dim basement. Trigger response is nearly instant; a wet paper towel placed on the sensor set it off in under two seconds. The sensor uses two metal pads, not the flimsy wire mesh some competitors use, so it won't corrode as fast.

The battery drain is the one spec I'd push back on. METAK advertises 2 years on a set of batteries, but with the LED flashing during alarms and normal standby draw, I'd plan for 12–18 months. Not a dealbreaker — it's a five-minute swap — but set a calendar reminder.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Across the 229 Amazon ratings, three themes pop up repeatedly. First, reliability: "Caught a slow leak from my water heater that I would have missed for weeks," one verified buyer writes. Second, ease of setup — no Wi-Fi, no app, no pairing. One reviewer sums it up: "Took 60 seconds out of the box. This just works." Third, the loud alarm: "I heard it from the second floor with the basement door closed."

**Where it falls short**  
The biggest limitation is obvious: no phone notifications. If you're on vacation and a pipe bursts, this alarm will scream into an empty house. The METAK is a sentry, not a messenger. If you need remote alerts, you're looking at a smart system like Flo by Moen or a Zigbee sensor — at 3–5x the price. Also, the sensor probe is bare metal, so if you place it directly under a condensation drip from an AC line, you can get false triggers from a single drop. Mounting it a quarter-inch off the floor with the included stand mitigates this.

**Who should buy this**  
This is perfect for renters and homeowners who want cheap, immediate protection without a smart-home ecosystem. Buy it if you have a sump pump, water heater, washing machine, or dishwasher within 4 feet of a floor drain. It's also a no-brainer for RV owners and cabin owners who want a basic canary in the coal mine. Skip it if you travel frequently for work or maintain a second property — you need remote alerts, and this won't give them.

**FAQ**  

**Q: Does it work if the power goes out?**  
Yes — it runs entirely on AAA batteries. No outlet required, which is actually a plus for flood-prone areas where outlets may be low.

**Q: Can I connect multiple units?**  
No, each unit operates independently. There's no hub or mesh networking. You'll need one per location, which is fine at this price point.

**Q: How do I test it?**  
Wet a paper towel and press it on the sensor pads. It should alarm within 2–3 seconds. Do this monthly, and wipe the pads dry with a cloth afterward.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want dead-simple, loud, dependable leak detection for under $25 and you're home most of the time. **Skip it if** you need remote alerts or have a history of false-trigger-sensitive environments (like a humid crawlspace). For the price, it's one of the best basic leak detectors on Amazon — just pair it with a monthly battery check.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — minus a half star for the overstated battery life and the lack of any smart connectivity.
