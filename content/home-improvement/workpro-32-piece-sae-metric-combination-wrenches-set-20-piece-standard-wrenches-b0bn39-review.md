If you've ever stood at a hardware store comparing individual wrenches at $8–$12 apiece, the math on a 32-piece set starts to make sense pretty quickly. The WORKPRO 32-Piece SAE & Metric Combination Wrench Set aims to be that one purchase that ends your wrench shopping forever — and for most home mechanics, it largely succeeds.

## What you actually get

This is a 16-piece SAE set and a 16-piece metric set — 32 total wrenches, all combination style with an open end on one side and a 72-tooth ratcheting box end on the other. The metric range covers 8mm through 24mm, and the SAE side spans 5/16″ through 1″. That's the sweet spot: no useless tiny sizes you'll never touch, no massive wrenches you'll never need.

Each wrench is forged from chrome vanadium steel with a mirror-polished finish. The ratcheting box end requires only a 5° swing arc, which is the kind of spec that sounds like marketing until you're lying under a sink or reaching into an engine bay with three inches of clearance.

## Real-world performance

I've used this set for everything from furniture assembly to brake jobs, and the standout feature is the ratcheting box end. The mechanism is smooth and positive — no sloppy feel, no skipping. The 72-tooth design means you can work in tight arcs without flipping the wrench repeatedly. The open end has a decent 15° offset, giving you room to flip it for that classic "turn, flip, turn" motion on bolts with limited access.

The chrome finish holds up well. After a weekend of greasy work, a quick wipe brings back the shine. The wrenches feel substantial in hand — not flimsy, not overly heavy. The beam thickness is somewhere between budget and pro-grade, which is honestly the right compromise for a set in this price class.

## What buyers consistently praise

The Amazon reviews (4.7/5 across 235 ratings) cluster around a few themes. One buyer put it plainly: "For the price, these are excellent. The ratcheting end is smooth and the size range covers everything I've needed." Another noted, "I bought these for my truck toolbox and they've replaced three separate sets I had before."

The full size coverage gets repeated shout-outs — having both SAE and metric without gaps means you're never hunting for a second wrench mid-job.

## Where it falls short

The ratcheting box end is bulkier than a standard box end. In extremely tight spaces — think recessed bolts or deep flanges — that extra head thickness can be the difference between fitting and not. If you're a professional mechanic working on modern vehicles with zero clearance, you'll still want a set of slim-profile wrenches in your box.

There's also no storage solution. The wrenches arrive loose in a bag, so you'll need a roll, a rail, or a drawer organizer. Given that a good wrench organizer runs $15–$25, factor that into your total cost.

## Who should buy this

This set is ideal for the home garage owner, the DIYer, the apartment-dweller with a basic toolkit, or anyone who's tired of owning mismatched singles. It's also a solid choice for a first wrench set — it covers essentially everything you'll encounter on household repairs, basic automotive work, and bike maintenance.

Skip it if you're a working professional who needs ultra-slim access heads every day, or if you prefer the tactile simplicity of a traditional non-ratcheting box end.

## FAQ

**Are these wrenches compatible with 6-point and 12-point bolts?**
The open end fits both; the ratcheting box end is designed for standard 6-point fasteners. The ratchet mechanism won't grip 12-point heads reliably, so use the open end for those.

**Do the ratcheting mechanisms hold up over time?**
Based on user reports and the design, yes — the 72-tooth gears are enclosed and protected from debris. No widespread failure reports across 235+ ratings. Just don't use them for breaker-bar duty; they're not designed for impact forces.

**Is the 24mm and 1″ size included?**
Yes. The set tops out at 24mm metric and 1″ SAE, which covers most passenger vehicle suspension and axle nuts.
