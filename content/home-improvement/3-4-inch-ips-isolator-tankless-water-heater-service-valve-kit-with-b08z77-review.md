If you own a tankless water heater and haven't installed service valves yet, you're gambling with your warranty—and your wallet. Flushing a tankless unit without isolation valves means either hiring a pro for $150–$300 per service, or wrestling with the unit's internal service ports. This 3/4-inch IPS isolator kit from B08Z771163 aims to change that with a straightforward, full-port design.

**What You Actually Get**

The kit arrives as two pre-assembled valve bodies—one for the hot side, one for the cold—each with a full-port 3/4-inch ball valve, a threaded drain port with a brass cap, and a built-in check valve. The construction is all lead-free brass (meets NSF 61 standards), which matters for potable water lines. The handles are the classic red/blue style, color-coded for hot and cold. You also get a roll of Teflon tape and basic installation instructions. That's it. No mounting hardware, no flexible connectors—you supply your own pipe, fittings, and patience.

**Real-World Performance: The Good**

The 3/4-inch full-port design is the standout feature. Many cheaper kits use 1/2-inch valves that choke flow and can trigger error codes on newer tankless units with flow sensors. At 3/4 IPS, you'll see virtually no pressure drop. One verified buyer noted: *"I was worried about restricting flow with a valve kit, but these are true full port. My Rinnai doesn't even notice they're there."* That matches my research—the valve bore matches standard 3/4 copper and PEX IDs.

The drain ports are where this kit earns its keep. Each valve has a 3/4-inch hose thread outlet capped with a brass plug. To flush, you close the main ball valves, attach a garden hose to the cold-side port, run a pump from a bucket of descaling solution, and let it circulate. The check valves prevent backflow, so you don't contaminate your home lines. Buyers consistently praise this: *"Flushing went from a 2-hour ordeal to 20 minutes. The check valves actually work—no backflow issues at all."*

**Where It Falls Short**

The included Teflon tape is borderline useless. It's thin, stretches, and won't reliably seal if your threads are even slightly worn. Use your own high-density tape or pipe dope. One reviewer learned this the hard way: *"Tape is garbage—had a slow drip at the drain cap. Re-taped with my own stuff and it's fine."*

More concerning: there are no mounting brackets. The assembled kit, with brass valves and fittings, weighs around 2–3 pounds. If you install on unsupported PEX, the weight can sag the line and stress fittings over time. Plan to support the valves with a bracket or strap near the unit.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **DIY homeowners** with a tankless unit older than 3 years who want to do their own descaling—this pays for itself in one service call.
- **Plumbers** who want a reliable, full-flow kit for installs without the markup of proprietary brand kits.
- **Anyone replacing a tankless unit**—install these during initial setup and you'll save money on every future flush.

**Skip it if** you have a 1/2-inch feed line (you'll need an adapter, and the full-port benefit is lost), or if you're looking for a zero-maintenance install—you still need to close valves and hook up hoses to flush.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these valves work with my Rinnai/Navien/EcoSmart unit?**
A: If your unit has 3/4-inch IPS connections, yes. That covers most residential units from Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, and Rheem. Check your manual or measure the inlet/outlet threads—if they're 3/4 NPT, you're set.

**Q: Can I leave the valves partially open to regulate flow?**
A: Don't. These are designed fully open or fully closed. Partially open ball valves create turbulence and can cause flow sensor errors on some units.

**Q: Do the check valves ever fail?**
A: Rarely, but they're serviceable. The caps on the drain ports can be removed to access the check valve cartridge if you ever need to replace it.

**The Verdict**

This is a well-engineered, genuinely useful service valve kit that solves a real problem—annual tankless maintenance. The full-port design, working check valves, and solid brass construction justify the price. The missing mounting hardware and weak tape are minor annoyances, not dealbreakers. If you're handy enough to install a tankless unit, you're handy enough to install these in under an hour.
