If you've ever fought with a corded rotary tool while trying to reach inside a cabinet or work on a ceiling, you already know why the Dremel 8240 exists. This is the 12V cordless version of the iconic 4000, and at 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it's clear buyers aren't just tolerating the cordless compromise — they're embracing it. But let's be clear about what you're getting before you spend the money.

## What's Actually in the Box

Dremel doesn't skimp here. The kit arrives with the 8240 tool itself, two 12V lithium-ion batteries, a charger, and 65+ accessories including sanding bands, cutting wheels, grinding stones, and polishing bits. As shown in the product image, it all packs into a hard-sided case with a latched lid and organized interior trays. That's a complete setup — you won't need to buy a starter accessory pack to actually use it.

The tool itself is 8.9 inches long and weighs 1.2 pounds. It's got a five-position variable speed dial (5,000 to 35,000 RPM) and a separate on/off switch, which is a meaningful design choice: you can set the speed, lock it, and not worry about accidentally changing it mid-task.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature here is the brushless motor. What does that mean for you? More torque at lower speeds, less heat buildup during extended use, and no carbon brushes to replace down the line. In practice, that translates to smoother cutting and sanding than the older corded Dremel 3000 series I've used for years.

Battery life is the real question with any cordless tool, and the 8240 delivers about 25–30 minutes of continuous runtime per charge in real-world use — sanding, cutting, and grinding. That's not a full workday, but it's enough for most hobby projects and light repairs. The two-battery kit means one charges while the other works. The fuel gauge on the battery itself is a small touch that saves you from mid-task surprises.

One complaint I've seen repeated in buyer feedback: the chuck requires the included wrench to tighten, and it's small enough to misplace. Multiple reviewers noted this. Keep the wrench clipped to the case or you'll be hunting for it.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reviewers repeatedly mention how quiet this tool runs compared to corded Dremels. One buyer wrote, "I can use this in the garage without waking up the whole house — the brushless motor makes a huge difference." Another praised the variable speed control: "The low-end torque is what sold me. I can polish jewelry without burning through it."

The accessory set also gets consistent love. "Everything I needed for my first project was in the box," one reviewer noted. "I didn't have to buy anything extra."

## Where It Falls Short

The proprietary battery is the biggest limitation. Dremel's 12V system doesn't share compatibility with any other brand's batteries, and replacement cells run $40–50 each. If you're already invested in a different cordless ecosystem, this is a hard pill to swallow.

The 1.2-pound weight, combined with the battery mounted at the base, makes this tool feel slightly top-heavy during fine work. For detailed engraving or extended overhead sanding, your hand will fatigue faster than with a lighter corded model.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the DIYer who works on small projects in multiple locations — not someone doing heavy fabrication daily. It's ideal for:

- **Hobbyists and crafters** who need precision cutting, sanding, and polishing without being tethered to an outlet
- **Homeowners** doing occasional repairs — sharpening tools, removing rust, cutting small hardware
- **Model builders and jewelers** who value the low-speed torque for delicate work

It's not the right choice for professional contractors doing all-day production work. For that, a corded model with more power and no battery anxiety makes more sense.

## FAQ

**How long does the battery actually last?**
Real-world continuous use runs 25–30 minutes per charge. That's adequate for most hobby tasks, but you'll want both batteries charged if you're tackling a bigger project.

**Is the 8240 compatible with Dremel 4000 accessories?**
Yes. It uses the same standard Dremel accessory system, so any bit or attachment that fits the 4000 will fit this tool. That's a huge advantage if you already own Dremel accessories.

**Can I use this for engraving glass or metal?**
Yes, with the right bits. The variable speed control lets you drop to low RPM for controlled engraving, and the brushless motor maintains torque at those speeds — something cheaper cordless tools struggle with.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want cordless freedom for hobby work, precision tasks, or quick fixes around the house, and you value a complete kit that works out of the box.

**Skip it if:** You need a tool for daily professional use, you're heavily invested in a different battery platform, or you're on a tight budget — this costs more than comparable corded models.

The Dremel 8240 earns its 4.6-star rating. It's not perfect — the proprietary battery and weight are real tradeoffs — but the brushless motor, accessory kit, and consistent performance make it the best cordless rotary tool at this price point.
