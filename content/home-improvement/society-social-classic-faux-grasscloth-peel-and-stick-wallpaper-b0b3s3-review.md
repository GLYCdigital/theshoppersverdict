**The Short Version: Pretty from a distance, fussy up close**

If you want the look of expensive grasscloth without the $100-per-roll price tag or the messy paste, this Society Social peel-and-stick paper delivers — but only if your walls are smooth, your patience is high, and you’re not expecting commercial-grade durability. At 3.7 stars across 185 ratings, buyers are split between “transformed my rental” and “peeled off by morning.” Here’s the unvarnished truth.

---

**What’s in the Roll**

Each roll is 20.5 inches wide by 18 feet long (about 30.7 square feet). That’s enough for roughly half an accent wall in a standard bedroom — order two rolls for a full 8-foot wall, and always buy 10% extra for pattern matching and mistakes. The paper itself is a vinyl-polyester blend with a printed texture that mimics the irregular slubs of natural jute. The backing is standard release liner, and it cuts cleanly with a sharp utility knife or scissors.

---

**Real-World Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Sticky**

**The texture is the star.** As one buyer put it, “I’ve had real grasscloth in my dining room for 8 years, and my guests couldn’t tell the difference from across the room.” The pattern doesn’t repeat visibly every few feet, which is a common cheap-wallpaper tell. It also hides minor drywall flaws — small dings and light orange-peel texture disappear under the weave.

**Adhesion: The catch.** On smooth, primed walls, it sticks fine. But multiple reviewers noted the same issue: “I put it in my bathroom and the edges curled within two weeks, especially near the shower.” Humidity is this paper’s enemy. It’s also not forgiving on textured walls — the paper won’t conform to deep knockdown or popcorn, and the adhesive grabs only the high points, leaving air pockets that eventually loosen.

**Installation is a two-person job.** Because the material is thin (roughly 0.008 inches), it stretches and tears if you pull too hard. Several buyers recommended using a squeegee instead of your hands and having someone hold the bottom edge while you align the top.

---

**Where It Falls Short — Honest Tradeoffs**

- **Thinness:** This isn’t a heavy-duty wallcovering. It’s closer to heavy gift wrap than commercial wallpaper. If you have pets that rub against walls or kids who bump them, expect scuffs and tears.
- **Pattern matching:** The “grasscloth” texture has subtle horizontal lines that must align at seams. If you’re off by even 1/8 inch, you’ll see a shadow line. Plan for waste.
- **Removal:** Despite being “peel and stick,” it leaves adhesive residue on walls older than 5 years with flat paint. You’ll need a warm water and dish soap solution to clean up.

---

**Who Should Buy This**

✅ **Renters** who want a high-end look without permanent damage — on smooth, primed walls.  
✅ **DIY beginners** who want a forgiving, repositionable material (you can peel and re-stick multiple times before it loses tack).  
✅ **Accent wall projects** in low-humidity spaces like living rooms, home offices, or bedrooms.  

❌ **Bathrooms or kitchens** — humidity will defeat the adhesive.  
❌ **Textured or unprimed walls** — you’ll fight it the whole way.  
❌ **Perfectionists** who can’t tolerate seam lines — this paper demands patience.

---

**FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Can I use this in a rental without damaging walls?**  
A: Yes, on smooth, clean, primed walls. It removes cleanly if you pull at a 45-degree angle slowly. But on older flat paint, expect some adhesive residue that requires soapy water to remove.

**Q: Does it look like real grasscloth?**  
A: From 3 feet away, yes. Up close, the texture is printed, not woven — so it’s smoother to the touch and lacks the natural fiber variation. It’s convincing, not identical.

**Q: How many rolls do I need for a 10x10 room?**  
A: For one 8-foot accent wall (roughly 80 sq ft), you need 3 rolls (30.7 sq ft each). For a full room, measure wall height × width, divide by 30.7, then add 10% for waste.

---

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have smooth walls, a low-humidity room, and want an affordable grasscloth look with repositionable installation. It’s a solid choice for renters and accent-wall projects.

**Skip it if:** You need something durable for high-traffic areas, have textured walls, or want a set-and-forget product. For those cases, spend more on a thicker woven paper or traditional paste wallpaper.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A genuinely good-looking product that’s let down by thin material and humidity sensitivity. Worth it for the right project, but know its limits before you commit.
