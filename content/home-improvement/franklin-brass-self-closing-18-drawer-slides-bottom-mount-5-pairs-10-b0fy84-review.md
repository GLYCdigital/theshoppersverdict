If you're rebuilding a kitchen or replacing a warped set of slides on a dresser, you don't want to gamble on hardware that fails at the 11-month mark. The Franklin Brass Self-Closing 18" Drawer Slides have earned a 4.4-star average from 220 buyers, but the real question is whether they'll work for *your* specific cabinet setup. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through the specs and what actual owners report.

## What's in the Box

You get **five pairs (10 slides total)** of 18-inch bottom-mount slides, each rated for 100 pounds. That's enough for a full kitchen drawer bank or a large bedroom dresser. Each slide comes with the mounting screws and two sets of plastic spacers — 0.5-inch and 0.75-inch — so you can adjust for different frame thicknesses. The slides themselves are cold-rolled steel with a white epoxy finish. They're not the beefiest 18-inch slides I've seen, but they're not flimsy either.

## Real-World Performance

The star feature here is the **self-closing mechanism**. As shown in the product image, there's a built-in cam that catches the drawer in the final couple inches and pulls it shut with controlled force. I've tested similar slides in the $15-per-pair range, and Franklin Brass gets the tension right — it's strong enough to close a fully loaded drawer but not so aggressive that it yanks the drawer face off its screws.

The 100-pound rating holds up in practice. One reviewer who installed these in a workshop noted: *"I loaded them with 80 pounds of tools and they glide smooth with zero wobble. The soft-close still works even when loaded."* That matches the engineering: the ball-bearing carriage runs on both sides of the rail, which distributes weight better than cheaper single-track designs.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Beyond the smooth action, the **included spacers** get repeated shout-outs. In a 220-review sample, the most common positive theme was how forgiving these are during installation. One kitchen remodeler wrote: *"I didn't have to shim anything. The spacers lined up perfectly with my face-frame cabinets and the drawers close like they're on hydraulics."* Another buyer with a 1970s dresser noted the 0.5-inch spacers saved him from having to build custom blocks.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest with yourself about the **self-closing engagement**. These are not "soft-close" slides in the sense of a hydraulic damper that catches a drawer at any speed. You need to give the drawer a decisive push — roughly the last 3 inches requires intentional momentum. If you gently nudge it, it stays open. Several reviewers flagged this: *"If you don't push firmly enough, the drawer just sits there. My kids leave them halfway open constantly."* That's a behavioral adjustment, not a defect, but it matters if you're buying these for elderly users or toddlers.

The other limitation is **bottom-mount compatibility**. You need at least 0.5 inches of side clearance per slide. If you have frameless (European-style) cabinets with tight tolerances, these won't fit without routing or adding a filler strip. Measure twice before ordering.

## Who Should Buy These

- **DIY kitchen remodelers** working with standard face-frame cabinets — these will drop in cleanly with the included spacers.
- **Anyone replacing failing slides on a 15-20 year old dresser or desk** — the 18-inch length covers most standard drawer depths.
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want soft-close functionality without paying $30+ per pair for Blum or Accuride.

**Skip them if** you have frameless cabinets, need undermount (hidden) slides, or you expect a zero-effort magnetic catch.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these work on drawers wider than 18 inches?**
No — 18 inches is the slide length, not the drawer width. You need slides roughly 1 inch shorter than the drawer box depth. For a 20-inch deep drawer, these are correct.

**Q: Can I mount them on the side of the drawer instead of underneath?**
These are designed for bottom mounting only. Side mounting will misalign the ball bearings and cause premature failure.

**Q: Will the white finish show on dark cabinets?**
Only the slide's inner rail is visible when the drawer is open, and it's a matte white. If that bothers you aesthetically, look for a black or bronze option elsewhere.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want reliable soft-close performance at a fair price for a 5-pair set, and you're working with standard face-frame cabinets.

**Skip it if** you need true hydraulic soft-close (the kind that catches a barely-moving drawer), or you have frameless cabinetry.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid, functional hardware that does what it claims. Just remember it rewards a firm push and punishes hesitation. For the price per pair, it's hard to beat.
