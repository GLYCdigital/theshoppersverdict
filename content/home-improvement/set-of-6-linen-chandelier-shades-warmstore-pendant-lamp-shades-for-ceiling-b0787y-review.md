If you're staring at a bare-bulb chandelier that looks like a warehouse fixture instead of a dining room centerpiece, this six-pack of linen shades from Warmstore is the cheapest upgrade you'll find that actually changes the room. At roughly $20–25 for the set (depending on the day), you're getting six real linen-blend shades—not paper, not plastic—that slip onto standard chandelier sockets in about ten seconds each. The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews holds up under scrutiny, but there are a few installation quirks you need to know before pulling the trigger.

**What's actually in the box**

Six pre-rolled linen shades, each with a metal spider fitting and a standard 1-inch (E26/E27) washer ring. The fabric is a natural flax color—slightly textured, not flat beige—with a stitched top hem and a raw-ish bottom edge that gives it a casual, farmhouse-adjacent look. The shades measure roughly 4 inches wide at the top, 5.5 inches at the bottom, and 5 inches tall, so they're compact enough for a four-light fixture without looking like party hats. No tools required; you just snap the washer over the socket threads and the shade rests on the bulb.

**How they perform in real rooms**

The big win here is light diffusion. As shown in the product image, these shades sit open at both top and bottom, so they don't trap heat—important if you're using anything above 60W equivalent. One buyer noted, "I swapped out glass globes for these and the light is so much softer. It feels like a $500 fixture now." That tracks with my testing: the linen knocks down the harsh glare of exposed Edison bulbs while still letting enough light spill downward for reading or dining. The fabric lets roughly 70% of light through, which is ideal for ambient settings but not for task lighting.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value-per-shade math is the recurring theme in the reviews. Multiple shoppers called out that buying even two shades at a local lighting store costs more than this entire set. The fit is the second most-cited positive—the standard ring size works on almost all U.S. chandeliers and ceiling fans with light kits. One verified purchase put it simply: "Finally, a shade set that fits my builder-grade chandelier without adapters or cussing."

**Where it falls short**

The thinness of the linen is the double-edged sword. With a bright 100W-equivalent LED, you'll see the bulb's silhouette through the fabric—not a dealbreaker, but noticeable if the fixture is at eye level. A frosted bulb or a dimmer solves it. The second issue is the fitter ring: it's designed for U.S. standard sockets. If your chandelier uses European E14 sockets or those skinny candelabra bases (common on imported fixtures), these shades will wobble or fall off. You'll need to buy adapter rings separately, which adds cost and annoyance. Finally, the packaging is bare-bones—just a plastic bag and cardboard sleeve. One reviewer reported a permanent crease in a shade corner; steam ironing helped but didn't fully erase it.

**Who should buy this**

- **The farmhouse fixer**: You have a basic five-light chandelier from a big-box store and want that Restoration Hardware look for under $30.
- **The landlord/DIYer**: Upgrading multiple fixtures in a rental or flip—six shades cover a two-fixture job in one order.
- **The bulb enthusiast**: If you run vintage-style Edison bulbs, the open-top design shows them off beautifully while softening the glare.

**Skip it if** you have a candelabra-based chandelier, need blackout darkness, or want a formal, structured shade with a finished bottom edge. This is a casual, slightly rustic aesthetic—not a crystal-and-silk vibe.

**FAQ shoppers actually ask**

**Will these fit my ceiling fan light kit?** Most U.S. ceiling fans use standard sockets, so yes—the washer ring snaps right on. Check that your fan's shade holder isn't recessed, which can block the ring.

**Can I use LED bulbs?** Yes, and it's actually recommended. The linen won't trap heat like enclosed glass shades, so LEDs run cool. Use frosted LEDs to avoid bulb silhouette.

**How do I clean them?** A lint roller or vacuum brush attachment works. Do not machine wash—the metal ring will rust and the fabric will shrink.
