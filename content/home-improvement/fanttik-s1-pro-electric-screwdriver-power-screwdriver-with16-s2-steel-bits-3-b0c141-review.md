**The Fanttik S1 Pro is the screwdriver you grab when you're assembling a desk at 11 PM and don't want to wake the neighbors with a drill.**

This 4.7-star-rated electric screwdriver (235 ratings) sits squarely in the "precision fastening" category — think IKEA furniture, laptop repairs, electronics, and light cabinet work. It's not a drill, and it doesn't pretend to be. At roughly the size of a thick marker, the S1 Pro weighs in around 300 grams and fits comfortably in a palm, with a textured grip that stays put even when your hands get sweaty mid-project.

**What's actually in the box:**

- The S1 Pro driver body
- 16 S2 steel bits: 8 Phillips (PH000–PH3), 3 slotted, 3 Torx (T6–T20), 2 hex (H3, H4)
- A 60cm USB-C charging cable
- A magnetic extension rod (useful for recessed screws)
- A soft storage pouch

The S2 steel bits are the real standout here. S2 is a shock-resistant alloy that holds edges far longer than the CR-V (chrome vanadium) bits most budget drivers ship with. In practical terms, you won't be swapping out stripped bits after three uses.

**Performance and real-world use:**

The S1 Pro offers three speed settings — 150, 200, and 220 RPM — plus a manual torque mode that caps at 5 Nm. For context, that's roughly the torque you'd get from a firm wrist twist on a manual screwdriver. It's deliberately low to prevent cam-out (when the bit slips and strips the screw head) on softer materials. That's a smart design choice for its target use.

Battery life is the headline feature. Fanttik claims 2,000 screws per charge, and while I'd take that with a grain of salt, real-world testing from buyers backs it up. One Amazon reviewer noted: *"I assembled a full queen bed frame, two nightstands, and a bookshelf without needing to recharge. The battery indicator never even dropped below half."* The USB-C charging is a welcome modern touch — no proprietary dock to lose.

**What buyers consistently praise:**

The torque control gets the most love. Multiple reviewers mention the "smart stop" feature — the driver automatically stops when it senses resistance, preventing over-tightening that strips screw heads. That's genuinely useful for anyone who's ever ruined a project by overtightening the last screw.

The compact size is the second most-cited win. As one reviewer put it: *"Fits in my tool drawer where my drill never could. I reach for this 10 times more often."* For electronics work, the fine control at low speed is a game-changer compared to a full-size drill.

**Where it falls short:**

The 5 Nm torque ceiling is the obvious limitation. If you're mounting a TV bracket into studs or working with lag bolts, skip this — you'll need an impact driver. This is strictly for pre-drilled holes and light fastening.

The magnetic bit holder is adequate but not great. Under heavy pressure, bits can pop loose — annoying when you're mid-drive and the bit lands behind a dresser. The lack of a work light on the base model is a miss; the pricier S1 Pro Plus adds one, and honestly, it should be standard at this price point.

**Who should buy this:**

- **Apartment dwellers** assembling flat-pack furniture without a drill
- **Electronics hobbyists** who need precise, low-torque fastening for PC builds, laptops, and small appliances
- **DIY novices** who want something safer and more controllable than a full-size drill
- **Not for** mechanics, framers, or anyone working with hardened fasteners

**FAQ:**

**Q: Can it handle drywall screws?**
A: Yes, for light drywall into pre-drilled holes. But for fresh drywall without pilot holes, the 5 Nm torque will struggle — you're better off with a drill.

**Q: How long does a full charge take?**
A: About 90 minutes via USB-C. There's no fast-charge feature, so plan ahead for bigger projects.

**Q: Does it come with a case?**
A: A soft pouch, not a hard case. The bits have a dedicated organizer tray, but the whole kit won't survive being tossed in a toolbox with heavier tools.
