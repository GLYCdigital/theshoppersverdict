## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the R8184G4009 primary control — a compact metal-bodied relay unit roughly 5 x 4 x 2 inches — plus the wiring sub-base and mounting hardware. This is the **intermittent ignition** version, meaning it sparks continuously while the burner runs rather than only during startup. That's the configuration most residential oil furnaces from the 1990s through the 2010s use.

The critical spec: this is a **direct replacement for the R8184G1009, R8184G1017, and R8184G1025** models. If you're swapping out one of those, the wiring connections match pin-for-pin. No adapter plates, no rewiring the transformer leads, no cuss words. It also crosses over to Beckett and Carlin burner controls on many furnace models — check your owner's manual or the cross-reference chart before ordering.

## How It Performs in the Real World

The R8184G4009's job is straightforward: prove flame, keep the burner running, and kill it fast if something goes wrong. The **cad cell flame detection system** monitors light from the burner flame. If the flame flickers out, the control shuts down the burner within 0.8 seconds — fast enough to prevent raw oil from pooling in the combustion chamber, which is the kind of failure that turns a service call into a fire department visit.

The 35-second safety lockout timing is the industry standard. If the burner fails to ignite within that window, the control locks out and requires a manual reset via the red button on the face. That's normal behavior, not a defect — and it's the same timing used across Honeywell's residential oil controls.

Buyers consistently report smooth installations. One reviewer noted, *"Replaced the old R8184G on my Beckett burner in about 20 minutes. Same wiring, same mounting holes. Fired right up and my furnace hasn't hiccuped since."* Another mentioned the unit's longevity: *"Installed this three years ago, zero issues. Solid Honeywell quality."*

## Where It Falls Short

There's a compatibility trap here. The R8184G4009 **does not** replace the newer R8184G1009, which has a different wiring scheme. If you order without checking your old control's model number, you'll be making a return trip to the parts store. Verify before you buy.

The 1/4-inch spade terminals are snug — genuinely snug. If you have arthritis or work in tight spaces, seating those push-on connectors can take some finger strength. A pair of needle-nose pliers makes the job easier, but it's worth knowing going in.

Also, there's no diagnostic LED on this unit. When it locks out, you're troubleshooting with a multimeter or calling a tech. For a homeowner who likes visible feedback, that's a small frustration — but it's also typical of this generation of controls, not a Honeywell-specific flaw.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my Carlin burner?**
A: Likely yes. The R8184G4009 is a universal replacement for many residential oil burners, including Beckett, Carlin, and Wayne models. Cross-reference your burner model and old control number before ordering.

**Q: How do I know if my control has failed?**
A: If your burner motor runs but the ignition doesn't spark, or if the control locks out repeatedly (red reset button pops), suspect the control. A multimeter test of the cad cell and relay will confirm.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: If you're comfortable working with 120V wiring and have basic electrical knowledge, yes. The wiring is color-coded and the sub-base matches old Honeywell models. If you're unsure, hire a licensed oil burner technician — oil furnaces aren't the place to guess.
