Let's cut through the noise: a kitchen sink is one of those purchases you touch every single day for a decade. Get it wrong and you're staring at scratches, dents, and rattling pans every morning. The Elkay Gauge double bowl top mount has been a consistent bestseller for a reason, but it's not without compromises. Here's the real story after digging through 220 verified ratings (4.4 stars) and the spec sheet.

## What you actually get

The box contains the sink itself (no faucet, no drain kit — budget for those separately), a mounting template, and the top-mount clips. The sink measures 33 inches wide, 22 inches front-to-back, and the bowls are split roughly 50/50 at 9 inches deep each. That depth is the standout spec — most comparably priced sinks top out at 7-8 inches.

The steel is 18-gauge, which is the sweet spot for residential use. It's not commercial-grade 16-gauge, but it's substantially thicker than the 20-gauge tin cans you'll find at big-box stores. The satin finish runs with the grain, which does double duty: it hides scratches from cast iron skillets and doesn't show every water spot like mirror-polished alternatives.

## Real-world performance

The sound-dampening system is where Elkay earns its keep. The sink comes with rubberized pads and a heavy undercoating applied to roughly 60% of the exterior. In practice, that means you don't get that hollow "DING" when you drop a spoon or run the disposal. Multiple reviewers noted this specifically — one wrote, "I was expecting to hear every dish, but my wife actually asked if the disposal was broken because it was so quiet." That's the kind of feedback that tells you the engineering works.

The 9-inch depth is genuinely useful. I've seen buyers complain that standard-depth sinks splash water onto the counter with every rinse. This one gives you real clearance for a 6-quart Dutch oven without water running over the rim. The larger bowl handles a full sheet pan diagonally, which is a quality-of-life win for anyone who bakes.

## Where it falls short

The top-mount installation is the weak point. The included template is thin cardstock that tears if you look at it wrong, and aligning the clips requires patience and a second pair of hands. One reviewer summed it up: "The sink is great. The install was not. Give yourself an extra hour and a half." If you're not confident with a jigsaw and level, budget for a professional installer.

The fixed center divider is another consideration. It eats about 3 inches of total width, so the smaller bowl measures just 14 inches across. That's fine for rinsing produce but tight for a 12-inch colander or a large cutting board. If you do a lot of prep work, a single-bowl design or a 50/50 split with a removable divider might serve you better.

Finally, there are no pre-drilled faucet holes. You'll either need to drill through the rim (doable but nerve-wracking) or purchase a deck-mount faucet that clamps to the countertop. This caught several buyers off guard, so factor it into your planning.

## Who should buy this

This sink is the right call if you're doing a full kitchen remodel on a budget and want a workhorse that won't dent or rattle. It's especially well-suited for households with garbage disposals, since the sound insulation is genuinely effective. It's also a solid pick for anyone upgrading from a shallow apartment sink who wants real depth without paying commercial prices.

Skip it if you're renovating a small galley kitchen where every inch of counter space matters — the 33-inch footprint is substantial. Also skip it if you're a prep-heavy cook who lives by your colander and cutting boards; the narrow second bowl will frustrate you.

## The verdict

**Buy it if** you want a deep, quiet, durable sink that looks like it costs twice as much, and you're willing to put in the installation effort or hire someone who knows what they're doing.

**Skip it if** you need a compact footprint, want a pre-drilled faucet setup, or do all your prep work in the sink itself.

The Elkay Gauge is a 4.2/5 — a genuinely well-built sink let down by installation friction and a divider that eats into the secondary bowl's utility. For the price, it's one of the best values in the double-bowl category, provided you know what you're signing up for.
