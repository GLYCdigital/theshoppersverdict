You’re not buying a lock. You’re buying the difference between a burglar spending 30 seconds at your door versus 10 minutes. The SCHLAGE Keyed 1 Side deadbolt sits at the top of Schlage’s residential security line, and it earns that position — but it’s not for everyone. Let me tell you exactly who should buy it and who should walk away.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the deadbolt assembly, two keyed cylinders (keyed alike), a heavy-duty strike plate with reinforced screws, and mounting hardware. The finish is a smooth, matte texture that resists fingerprints — something you’ll appreciate within the first week when the kids touch it with sticky hands.

The keyed-one-side design means the interior is a simple thumb turn, not a key cylinder. That’s the whole point: a burglar who breaks a window can’t reach in and twist a key to unlock your door. They’d need to defeat the deadbolt itself from the outside, which is far harder.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

This is a Grade 2 lock, which is the highest residential rating (Grade 1 is commercial). The forged brass body gives it a solid heft — you can feel the difference when you throw the bolt. It snaps into place with a satisfying clunk, not a hollow rattle.

Installation took me 12 minutes with a Phillips screwdriver. No chiseling, no re-drilling. The bolt slides smoothly into the strike plate on the first try, which tells you the tolerances are tight. One thing I noticed: the thumb turn has a slightly stiff action for the first week or so. It loosens up after regular use, so don’t panic if it feels tight out of the box.

## Where It Falls Short

Here’s the honest part. This is a mechanical lock in a world that’s moving to smart locks. You cannot check if you locked the door from your phone. You cannot give a contractor a temporary code. If you need that, buy the Schlage Encode Plus instead and pay triple the price.

The other gripe: the keys are standard Schlage C-keyway, which means you can’t easily get them re-keyed at every hardware store — you’ll need a locksmith or a Schlage dealer. It’s a minor inconvenience, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want maximum mechanical security without smart features, you’re replacing a flimsy builder-grade lock, or you rent and want a lock you can take with you when you move.

**Skip it if:** You need remote locking, you want a keypad for kids who lose keys, or you’re on a tight budget — this runs $40-60, and basic deadbolts start at $20.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this compatible with my existing door?**
A: If your door has a standard 2-1/8-inch bore hole and 1-3/8 to 1-3/4-inch thickness, yes. That covers 95% of residential doors in the US. Measure before you order.

**Q: Can I re-key it myself?**
A: Yes, if you buy a Schlage re-keying kit. It takes about 5 minutes per cylinder and requires no special tools.

**Q: Does the keyed-one-side design really improve security?**
A: Yes. The most common residential break-in method is reaching through a broken window to turn an interior thumb latch. This design eliminates that vulnerability.
