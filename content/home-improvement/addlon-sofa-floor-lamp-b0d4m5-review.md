The addlon Sofa Floor Lamp is the kind of product that makes you wonder why every arc lamp doesn't ship this way. As someone who's tested a dozen of these over-the-sofa lights, I can tell you the category is full of wobbly poles and harsh, single-temperature bulbs. This one gets the fundamentals right — and then adds a few genuinely useful touches.

**What's actually in the box**

You get five pole sections, a weighted round base, the arc arm, a fabric drum shade, a foot switch with a dimmer knob, and the hex wrench for assembly. The base is solid metal, not the hollow plastic you'll find on cheaper models. It weighs just over 11 pounds, which matters when you're stretching a 72-inch arc over your couch. As shown in the product image, the matte black finish (also available in white) looks clean and modern.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The metal pole sections thread together with a twist-lock mechanism. Assembly took me about 20 minutes on the floor, and the only mildly annoying part was lining up the threads on the middle sections. The shade is a nice linen-feel fabric that diffuses the light well — no harsh hotspots on your ceiling or walls.

The three color temperatures are a real selling point. The foot switch cycles through warm (3000K), neutral (4000K), and daylight (5000K). For context, 3000K is the cozy glow you want for TV watching, while 5000K works better for reading or tasks. The dimmer knob on the switch is smooth — you can go from a 1200-lumen max down to a barely-there 200 lumens. That's a genuinely useful range.

Buyers consistently highlight the stability. One reviewer wrote, "I have two dogs that crash into this thing daily and it hasn't budged." Another noted, "The weighted base makes it feel like a $200 lamp, not a $60 one." That tracks with my testing — even with the arc fully extended, a firm nudge won't send it toppling.

**Where it falls short**

The foot switch is my biggest gripe. It's a press-and-cycle design: off → 3000K → 4000K → 5000K → off. If you want warm light, you're pressing three times every time you turn it on. A simple rotary switch or a two-button design would've been better. Also, the cord is 6 feet long — short enough that you'll need an outlet fairly close to your sofa.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers** who want a statement light without mounting anything to walls
- **Living room setups** where you need ambient light over a couch, not focused task lighting
- **Anyone tired of harsh overhead lights** — the dimmer range is genuinely useful for evening wind-down

Skip it if you need a reading light with a focused beam, or if you hate assembling things in general. This isn't a five-minute setup.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the lamp work with smart home systems?**
A: No. It's a manual foot switch with a dimmer. No app, no voice control. If that's a dealbreaker, look elsewhere.

**Q: What bulb does it use?**
A: The LED is integrated — you can't swap it. The rated lifespan is around 25,000 hours, which is roughly 10 years of nightly use.

**Q: Will it fit over a wide sectional?**
A: The arc reaches about 72 inches horizontally, and the shade hangs about 45 inches from the floor. For a standard three-seat sofa, it works. For a deep L-shaped sectional, measure your overhang first — the shade may not clear the back cushions.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy it if you want a stable, versatile arc lamp with real dimming range. Skip it if you need app control or a spot-reading beam.**

For the price, this is one of the better arc lamps I've tested. The weighted base, the three color temps, and the smooth dimmer put it a clear step above the generic models that flood Amazon. The foot switch design is mildly annoying, but it's not enough to sink an otherwise excellent product. If you're furnishing a living room and want functional, adjustable ambient light, this is a solid pick.
