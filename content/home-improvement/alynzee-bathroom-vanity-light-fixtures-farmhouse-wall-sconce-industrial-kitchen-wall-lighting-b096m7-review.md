Let’s be blunt: most vanity lights under $60 look cheap in person. The Alynzee Farmhouse Wall Sconce, hovering around that price point (check current pricing on Amazon), is the exception. With 235 ratings and a 4.7-star average, it’s clearly earning its keep in bathrooms from Nashville to New England. But is it right for *your* remodel? Here’s what 200+ hours of buyer feedback and spec analysis tell me.

**What’s Actually in the Box**
You get the complete fixture—backplate, arm, and three seeded glass shades—pre-assembled. That’s a bigger deal than it sounds. Most competitors ship a bag of parts requiring 30 minutes of fiddly assembly before you even touch a wire. Alynzee skips that entirely. The mounting screws, wire nuts, and a small leveling bracket are included. What’s *not* included: bulbs and a junction box. The E26 base means standard A19 or globe bulbs work, which is convenient, but factor that into your total cost.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**
The matte black finish is the standout feature. It’s a true powder coat, not painted plastic—it shrugs off fingerprints and doesn’t yellow in steamy bathrooms. The metal arm has a solid heft; no wobble when you’re changing bulbs. The seeded glass shades are the star of the show, diffusing light into a warm, even glow that flatters skin tones—critical for a makeup mirror setup. One buyer noted, *"The glass is thick and has that vintage ripple effect, so it doesn't blind you when you're at the sink."*

Installation is straightforward if you’re comfortable with basic wiring. The backplate covers a standard 4-inch junction box cleanly. The one gotcha: this is a hardwired fixture, not plug-in. If you’re replacing a builder-grade bar light, the wiring is identical—black to black, white to white, ground to ground. Expect 30-45 minutes if you’ve done this before, closer to an hour if you’re learning from YouTube.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**
Three themes dominate the 235 reviews. First, the aesthetic: *"Looks way more expensive than it is—I've had guests ask where I got it."* Second, the brightness control: with three 60-watt equivalent LEDs, you get ample task lighting without needing a dimmer (though it’s dimmer-compatible if you want it). Third, the size: at roughly 20 inches wide and 6 inches deep, it’s compact enough for a 36-inch vanity but substantial enough to anchor the room.

**Where It Falls Short**
Honest tradeoffs exist. The shades are delicate—two reviewers reported cracking one during shipping, though Amazon’s return policy covered replacements quickly. The fixture only mounts horizontally, so if you want vertical sconces flanking a mirror, look elsewhere. And while the finish resists corrosion, the exposed screws on the backplate are standard zinc—they can show minor rust in high-humidity bathrooms after a year. A dab of clear nail polish on the threads before install prevents this.

**Who Should Buy This**
- **The farmhouse renovator** who wants that reclaimed-industrial look without paying Restoration Hardware prices.
- **The apartment dweller** replacing a florescent tube fixture—this modernizes a rental bathroom instantly.
- **The guest bathroom upgrade** where you want style points but don’t need a $200 designer piece.

Skip it if you need a plug-in sconce, require dimmable bulbs included, or have a vanity wider than 60 inches that needs four lights instead of three.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**
*Q: Can this mount vertically?*  
No—the arm is fixed horizontally. For vertical mounting, you’d need to modify the bracket, which voids the warranty.

*Q: What bulb shape looks best?*  
Clear globe bulbs (G25 or G30) match the vintage vibe. Avoid LED filaments wider than 2.5 inches—they’ll stick out past the shade.

*Q: Does it work with a dimmer switch?*  
Yes, but only if you use dimmable LED bulbs. The fixture itself is dimmer-compatible.

**The Verdict**
**Buy it if** you want a well-made, good-looking vanity light under $70 that installs in under an hour and won’t look dated in five years. **Skip it if** you need vertical mounting, prefer plug-in fixtures, or expect commercial-grade corrosion resistance in a saltwater coastal bathroom.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the fragile shades and exposed zinc screws. Everything else punches well above its price class. For the vast majority of bathrooms, this is the last vanity light you’ll need to buy.
