**The GHORA 22-in Socket Fan Light is the kind of product that makes you wonder why every rental apartment doesn't have one.** If you've got a standard ceiling light socket and a room that turns into a sauna by 3 PM, this screw-in fan light solves two problems at once — without calling an electrician or asking your landlord for permission.

Here's what you're actually getting: a 22-inch diameter fan with an integrated 800-lumen LED light that screws directly into any standard E26 ceiling socket. The box includes the fan light unit, a handheld remote (battery included), and mounting hardware. That's it. No wiring, no junction boxes, no breaker flipping. You twist it in like a light bulb and it's done.

**The real genius is the installation — or rather, the lack of it.** As one buyer put it, "I literally screwed it into my existing light socket and it worked. Took me longer to find the ladder than to install the fan." That's the core appeal. For renters, dorm dwellers, or anyone who's stared at a ceiling fan installation video and noped out, this removes every barrier.

**Performance-wise, the GHORA punches above its weight for a socket-mounted fan.** The six-speed motor moves a surprising amount of air — at full speed in an average 12x12 room, you'll feel it from across the space. The reversible direction is a genuinely useful feature: flip it for winter to push warm air down from the ceiling. The 800-lumen light with three color temperatures (warm, neutral, daylight) covers most needs, and the dimming function works smoothly from the remote.

**But let's be honest about the tradeoffs.** First, 800 lumens is not a lot of light. That's roughly equivalent to a single 60-watt incandescent bulb. In a bedroom or hallway, fine. In a living room where you want overhead illumination for reading or tasks, you'll be supplementing with lamps. Second, the remote is infrared, not radio frequency. You need line-of-sight to the unit — no controlling it from the couch with your back turned, and definitely not from another room.

**Buyer feedback over these 200+ ratings consistently lands on two themes.** The overwhelming majority praise the painless install and immediate functionality. One reviewer noted, "Perfect for my workshop — I get airflow and light without rewiring my old fixture." The criticisms cluster around two issues: a handful of units arrived with a slight imbalance causing wobble at higher speeds, and several buyers found the light dimmer than expected. The wobble is fixable (the included balancing kit helps), but it's worth knowing going in.

**Who should buy this?** Three specific types of people:

- **Renters** who want ceiling fan airflow without drilling holes or modifying fixtures
- **Anyone with a standard light socket** in a bedroom, office, or small den who needs air movement now
- **DIY-averse homeowners** who avoid electrical work entirely — this is genuinely plug-and-play

**Skip it if** your room exceeds 150 square feet, you need serious task lighting from overhead, or you want a fan that's whisper-quiet at top speed (it's audible, though not offensive).

**The Verdict:** At its price point, the GHORA 22-in Socket Fan Light delivers exactly what it promises — instant airflow and decent lighting from a socket you already have. The installation simplicity alone justifies the purchase for most people. It's not a replacement for a hardwired ceiling fan in a large primary living space, but for bedrooms, offices, garages, and rental units, it's a smart, inexpensive upgrade. **Buy it if** you want ceiling fan airflow without the installation headache. **Skip it if** you need bright overhead lighting or have a room larger than 150 square feet.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this require a ceiling fan-rated electrical box?**
A: No. That's the whole point. It screws into a standard E26 light socket rated for a light bulb. No fan box, no extra support, no hardwiring. Just make sure your socket is securely mounted — a loose fixture will wobble regardless of the fan.

**Q: How loud is it at full speed?**
A: It's noticeable but not obnoxious — think white noise rather than a jet engine. On lower speeds (1-3), it's genuinely quiet. If you're a light sleeper, run it on speed 2 or 3 overnight.

**Q: Can I use it with a smart home system or wall switch?**
A: The fan has its own RF receiver, so it works independently of your wall switch. You can turn it on/off at the wall, but all fan speed and light controls are via the included IR remote. No Alexa/Google Home integration — this is a simple, standalone device.
