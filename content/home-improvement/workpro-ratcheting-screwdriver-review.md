## The Quick-Load System

WORKPRO's bit loading system is one of the best in this price range. The bit cartridge slides out of the handle, and bits are held in individual spring-loaded slots. Each bit clicks into place with positive retention — they won't fall out when you open the cartridge, but they're easy to remove when needed.

The 12 included bits cover:
- **Phillips** #0, #1, #2, #3
- **Slotted** 3/16", 5/32", 1/8"
- **Torx** T15, T20, T25
- **Hex** 3/16", 5/32"

That's the most common sizes for furniture assembly, electronics, and general home maintenance. Missing from the set: square-drive (Robertson) bits, which are common in deck screws and Canadian construction, and smaller Torx sizes for precision work.

The mechanism for loading and unloading bits is described by one reviewer as *"practical and fast"* — referring to it as a *"meccanismo per agganciare/sganciare gli inserti pratico e veloce"* (practical and fast mechanism for hooking/releasing inserts).

## Ratcheting Action: Smooth and Positive

The ratcheting mechanism has three positions: **forward** (tighten), **reverse** (loosen), and **locked** (standard screwdriver). The selector switch is at the base of the handle and clicks positively between positions.

The ratcheting action itself is smooth with a satisfying mechanical sound. It engages within about 5 degrees of rotation, which is good for a tool at this price. Premium tools engage at 3-4 degrees; the WORKPRO's 5-degree engagement is barely noticeable in practice.

A reviewer praised the mechanics: *"Brilliant Screwdriver. Each piece sits in place and is easy to take out. The mechanics are smooth, and it has some good weight to it. As good as bigger name brands."*

## Bit Retention and Quality

Once a bit is locked into the magnetic chuck, it's **not coming out** until you want it to. The retention is strong — you can shake the tool forcefully without the bit dislodging. This is critical for safety and frustration-free work.

The bits themselves are made from **S2 steel** with a black oxide finish. S2 is a good mid-range tool steel — harder than the CR-V (chrome vanadium) found on cheaper bits, but not quite as hard as the S2 premium bits used by Wiha or Wera. For DIY and light trade use, they're more than adequate.

One note: magnetic strength at the tip is moderate. It'll hold a screw for alignment but don't expect it to pick up dropped fasteners from a distance.
