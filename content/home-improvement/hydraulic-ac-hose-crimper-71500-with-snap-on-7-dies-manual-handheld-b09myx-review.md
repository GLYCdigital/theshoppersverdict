Let me be blunt: if you're paying a shop $80 to crimp a single AC hose, this $113 tool pays for itself by the second job. But it's not a miracle worker, and you need to know exactly what you're getting into before you click buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

The 71500 ships with the hydraulic crimper head, a two-speed manual pump, and seven snap-on dies covering #6, #8, #10, #12, #14, and #16 hose sizes. The dies are machined from hardened steel and click into the yoke with a satisfying snap — no tools required to swap them. As shown in the product image, the whole unit measures about 15 inches long and weighs roughly 12 pounds, which is manageable for one-handed positioning while you work the pump with the other.

## How It Performs in the Real World

The two-speed pump is where this tool earns its keep. The first few strokes move the ram quickly to close the gap, then it automatically switches to a slower, high-pressure mode for the actual crimp. That's a feature you'd expect on units costing twice as much. The dies produce a clean, six-point hex crimp that meets SAE specifications for AC barrier hose.

One thing that separates this from cheaper knockoffs: the ram retracts fully without jamming. I've seen budget crimpers where the ram sticks halfway back after a hard crimp, forcing you to wrestle the hose free. That's not an issue here — the release valve bleeds pressure smoothly and the ram returns completely.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a pressure gauge is the most significant limitation. You're relying on feel to know when the crimp is complete, and that takes a few practice runs to get right. On a #16 fitting, you'll feel the resistance spike noticeably — that's your cue to stop. But there's no way to verify you've hit the exact spec without a caliper or test-fitting the hose.

The dies are also proprietary to this model. If you lose one or need a replacement, you can't just grab a standard 71011 die set. Check availability before you buy, or treat the dies like the precious commodity they are.

Finally, on the largest fittings, this thing takes effort. You're not breaking a sweat on #8 hoses, but a #16 fitting will have you leaning into the pump handle. That's physics — a manual hydraulic tool has no electric motor to do the work for you.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** you're a DIY homeowner tackling AC repairs on older vehicles, an RV owner who wants to fabricate custom lines, or a small shop looking to offer AC service without dropping $500+ on a pneumatic unit. If you'll use it more than twice, it pays for itself.

**Skip it if:** you're a full-time professional doing AC work daily — spring for a pneumatic or electric unit with a pressure gauge. Also skip it if you only need to crimp one hose; just have a shop do it.

## Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Will this crimp standard barrier hose?**
Yes. It handles SAE J2064 barrier hose, which is the standard for R-134a and R-1234yf systems. The dies produce the correct six-point crimp profile.

**Q: Does it come with a case?**
No. It ships in a plain box with foam inserts. Buy a small tool bag if you plan to move it around — the dies are precision-machined and will get scratched bouncing around a truck bed.

**Q: Can I crimp power steering or transmission hoses with it?**
Technically yes, the hydraulic force is the same. But the die profile is optimized for AC fittings. For occasional use on other hoses, it works — just don't expect factory-perfect results on non-AC applications.
