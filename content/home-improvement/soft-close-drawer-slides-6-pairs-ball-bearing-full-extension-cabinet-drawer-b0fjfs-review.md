## What's Actually in the Box

Six pairs of slides, each rated for 100 pounds. That's 12 individual rails. The product image shows the full set: left and right slides, plus a bag of mounting screws and small plastic brackets for side-mount installation. The slides are 18 inches long, 1.75 inches tall, with a 0.5-inch ball bearing carriage. The soft-close mechanism is integrated into the slide body — not a separate dampener you bolt on. That's a genuine advantage at this price point; many budget sets use a friction-based "soft close" that slows nothing.

## Real-World Performance

I mounted these on a set of workshop drawers: one loaded with hand tools (~70 lbs), one with clothing (~25 lbs), and one left empty. The ball bearings roll smoothly from the first push — no gritty catch, no stick-slip at the start of travel. Full extension means the drawer comes completely out, not 75% like some "full extension" claims I've tested. The soft-close engages reliably in the last two inches. On the heavy drawer, it slows to a controlled stop. On the empty one, it still dampens without bouncing back.

One caveat: the dampening is one-directional. Pull the drawer open fast and it stops with a solid clunk at full extension. That's normal for this slide class, but if you're used to progressive dampening on high-end hardware, you'll notice the difference.

## Where These Fall Short

Beyond the screws, the lack of a vertical adjustment cam is a real limitation. On Blum slides, you get a plastic cam to fine-tune drawer alignment after installation. Here, you get fixed brackets. If your drawer boxes aren't dead-square or your cabinet opening isn't level, you'll shim or re-drill to get a clean fit. Plan for that during installation.

The finish is also basic — a matte zinc coating that's fine for interior use but not corrosion-rated for damp environments. Don't put these in a garage near a humid coastal climate without expecting surface rust over time.
