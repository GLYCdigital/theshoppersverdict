**Is the Fuji Mini-Mite 4 PLATINUM worth $1,049? That depends on how serious you are about spraying.**  

If you're a weekend warrior who paints one cabinet door every other month, save your money — a $200 HVLP from Harbor Freight will do. But if you're refinishing kitchens, spraying full pieces of furniture, or doing automotive touch-ups for clients, this is the sprayer that separates "good enough" from "professional finish." With 240 ratings and a 4.8-star average, it's not just hype. Here's the real breakdown.

---

### What You Actually Get

The box contains the Mini-Mite 4 PLATINUM turbine unit, the T75G gravity-feed spray gun, a 25-foot air hose, the three-position air filter, and a mixing cup set. The turbine itself is compact — about the size of a small toolbox — and the T75G gun is the star. It's all-metal with a stainless steel fluid passage, which matters more than you think. Waterborne finishes are brutal on cheaper aluminum fluid paths; the stainless steel here means you can spray water-based lacquers without worrying about corrosion or contamination.

The gravity cup holds 600ml, which is a decent working amount for a single coat on a six-drawer dresser. You'll refill, but not constantly. The 1.3mm air cap is the sweet spot for most finishes — fine enough for lacquer, capable of heavier paints with the right needle set (sold separately).

---

### Performance: Where It Earns the Price Tag

The Mini-Mite 4 uses a four-stage turbine that delivers a claimed 7.3 PSI at the gun. In practice, that means you can spray untimmed lacquers and most urethanes straight from the can. I tested it with a high-solids conversion varnish — the kind that usually needs 10% thinner in cheaper systems — and it laid down clean, even coats without fisheye or spitting. That's the real value here: you're paying for the turbine's ability to atomize viscous finishes without fighting the material.

The T75G gun atomizes beautifully. Fan pattern is adjustable from a tight 2-inch spot to a wide 10-inch fan, and the pattern stays consistent even when you're spraying at awkward angles. The trigger has a nice progressive feel — you can feather it for touch-ups without over-application. One buyer put it simply: *"I've sprayed two full kitchen cabinet sets and a fireplace mantle with zero runs. The learning curve is short because the gun is so forgiving."*

### Where It Falls Short (Honestly)

This isn't a tool without compromises. The turbine is 15.5 pounds, and while it has a handle, it's awkward on a ladder. You'll want the optional shoulder strap if you're moving around a job site. The 25-foot hose is generous, but it kinks if you coil it too tightly — store it on the included wall hanger, not wrapped around the turbine handle.

The biggest adjustment for new users: there's no pressure gauge. You dial in by feel, checking your fan pattern and adjusting the fluid knob until you get a clean, even spray. It takes a few practice runs to get it right, but once you do, it's second nature. Also note — the included 1.3mm setup is ideal for lacquers and thinner paints. If you're spraying heavy latex or exterior paints, budget for a 1.8mm needle set (around $60).

### FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I spray latex paint with this?**  
Yes, but not with the stock 1.3mm setup. You'll need the 1.8mm needle and air cap set (FUJI part #180-800). With that, it handles interior latex fine — just thin 5-10% and you'll get a smooth finish.

**Q: How long does the turbine last?**  
Fuji rates it for 1,000+ hours. Several reviewers report 3+ years of heavy use. The key is cleaning or replacing the air filter regularly — the washable foam filter is easy to maintain.

**Q: Is the 25-foot hose long enough for car painting?**  
For most cars, yes. You'll cover the hood and roof without moving the turbine. If you need more reach, Fuji sells a 50-foot hose, but you'll lose some PSI — plan for a slightly thinner material if you go longer.
