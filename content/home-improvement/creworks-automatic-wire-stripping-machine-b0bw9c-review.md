If you're stripping more than a dozen wires a day by hand, your thumb already knows the pain. The CREWORKS Automatic Wire Stripping Machine promises to end that — pull the trigger, insert the wire, and get a clean strip in one motion. At 3.7 stars across 185 ratings, it's clearly not perfect. But for the price of a nice dinner, is it the right shortcut for your bench? Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the stripper itself, a hex wrench for blade adjustment, and a short manual that's mostly pictures. The unit is compact — roughly the size of a large paperback — with an ABS housing and a visible blade assembly up front. There's a dial for strip length (up to about 1 inch) and a tension knob for blade depth. It runs on standard AC power via an included cord, so no batteries to worry about.

## How It Performs on Real Jobs

The core mechanism is straightforward: you press the wire into the blade channel, the machine pulls it through, cuts the jacket, and a second set of rollers pulls the insulation off. On standard PVC-jacketed THHN wire — the stuff in most household and automotive projects — it works impressively well. Set the depth correctly and you'll get clean strips on 14 AWG and 12 AWG all day without nicking the copper. One buyer noted, *"I stripped 200 wires for a control panel in about 20 minutes. Hand strippers would've taken me two hours."*

The adjustable stop-length dial is genuinely useful. Set it to 1/4 inch for pigtails, switch to 3/4 inch for terminal connections — repeatable results without measuring each time. That's the machine's real value: consistency on repetitive jobs.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. The CREWORKS machine has a documented weakness with soft-jacket wire. Silicone-insulated wire — increasingly common in hobby and automotive work — tends to squish and deform in the feed rollers before the blade can cut cleanly. You'll get frayed edges or the insulation bunches up mid-strip. Not a dealbreaker if you mostly use PVC, but know it going in.

The 1-inch maximum strip length is also limiting. If you're stripping long lengths for battery cables or heavy-gauge power distribution, this isn't your tool — you'll need multiple passes, which is awkward and can damage the conductor. Multiple Amazon reviewers mention this exact frustration, with one writing, *"Great for short strips, useless for anything over an inch."*

Quality control is the biggest wildcard. A meaningful number of the 185 ratings cite out-of-box blade misalignment — the machine chews wire instead of cutting it until you loosen the hex screws and realign everything. It's a 10-minute fix if you're mechanically inclined, but it's annoying at this price point.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a hobbyist, electrician, or small-shop fabricator doing repetitive stripping of standard PVC wire in short lengths. If you're tired of hand-cramping on 50+ strips a day, this will pay for itself in time saved within a few projects.

**Skip it if:** You work primarily with silicone-jacket wire, need strip lengths over 1 inch, or you don't want to fiddle with blade alignment out of the box. A quality pair of manual strippers like the Klein Katapult or IRWIN Vise-Grip will serve you better.

## FAQ

**Q: What wire gauges does it handle?**
A: The manufacturer specs 10–24 AWG. In practice, it handles 12–18 AWG most reliably. Anything below 20 AWG can be finicky — the rollers sometimes crush thin conductors before the blade engages.

**Q: Does it work on stranded wire?**
A: Yes, but you must reduce the blade depth compared to solid wire. Stranded conductors are easier to nick. Test on a scrap piece first and adjust the tension knob in small increments.

**Q: Is it safe to operate one-handed?**
A: Mostly yes — the trigger requires a deliberate squeeze and the blade is recessed. But the machine has a habit of pulling the wire in quickly, so keep fingers clear of the feed channel. It's not a toy; unplug it when adjusting blades.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The CREWORKS Automatic Wire Stripping Machine is a solid value for its intended niche: fast, repeatable stripping of short PVC-jacketed wire. It's not a professional-grade tool, and the soft-wire limitations and QC issues are real. But if you're doing volume work with standard wire and don't want to drop $200+ on a pneumatic stripper, this earns its place on the bench. Just budget 10 minutes to check the blade alignment before your first real job.

**Buy it if** you're stripping standard 12–18 AWG wire in bulk. **Skip it if** you need long strips, work with silicone jackets, or demand perfection out of the box.
