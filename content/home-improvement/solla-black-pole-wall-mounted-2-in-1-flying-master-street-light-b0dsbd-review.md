Let's be blunt: most wall-mounted floodlights are a single beam aimed at one spot, leaving your driveway's far corner a pitch-black guessing game. The SOLLA Black Pole Wall Mounted 2-in-1 Flying Master Street Light sidesteps that with two independently pivoting heads on a single pole mount. It's a practical solution for anyone tired of tripping over garden hoses or squinting at house numbers after sunset.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the pre-assembled dual-head fixture, a wall-mounting bracket, a hardware kit with screws and anchors, and a wiring connector set. No bulbs to hunt down—the integrated LEDs are sealed in, rated for roughly 50,000 hours. The unit measures about 18 inches wide with the arms extended, and the black die-cast aluminum body gives it a commercial-streetlight look rather than a cheap plastic glow. As shown in product photos, the heads rotate nearly 180 degrees vertically and about 90 degrees horizontally, so you can angle one down the driveway and the other across the patio.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The housing is the standout here. It's not the featherweight aluminum you see on $25 floodlights—this has heft, and the black powder coat resists chipping. Buyers consistently mention surviving storms and freezing winters without water intrusion. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Installed in October, been through three snowfalls and a nor'easter—no moisture inside the lens, no rust on the bracket."*

Light output is genuinely useful. The dual heads deliver a combined beam that covers roughly 2,400 square feet, with a cool daylight color temperature (about 6000K) that makes motion detection obvious. The integrated photocell flips it on at dusk and off at dawn, and you can override it with the included remote to keep it lit manually. The motion sensor triggers within about 30 feet and holds the light on for 1 to 3 minutes depending on your setting.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The two most repeated positives in the 210 ratings are the wide coverage and the aiming flexibility. A garage owner wrote: *"Finally can see my whole side yard—the swivel heads let me point one at the gate and one at the trash cans. No more fumbling for keys in the dark."* Another noted the install was straightforward: *"Took 20 minutes with a voltage tester and wire nuts. The bracket lines up with a standard 4-inch junction box."*

**Where It Falls Short**

This is hardwired only. If you were hoping for a solar panel or battery backup, look elsewhere—this requires a 120V connection and basic electrical comfort. The 30-foot motion sensor range is fine for a typical porch or garage wall, but if you're covering a long driveway, you'll need a second unit. Several buyers also flagged the included screws as undersized for masonry; plan to use toggle bolts or concrete anchors if you're mounting on brick or stucco. Finally, the remote is small and easy to misplace—keep it in a drawer near the breaker box.

**Who Should Buy This**

If you have a garage, workshop, or side yard with multiple shadow zones, this dual-head design earns its keep. It's also a strong pick for rental properties or back entrances where you want reliable dusk-to-dawn lighting without fiddling with timers. Skip it if you're not comfortable with basic wiring or if you need solar flexibility.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Does it work with a standard light switch?**
Yes. Once wired to your house current, the photocell and motion sensor handle the rest. Flipping the wall switch off and on within 2 seconds forces constant-on mode.

**Can I replace the LED chips if they burn out?**
No—the LEDs are integrated into the sealed housing. At 50,000 hours, that's roughly 17 years of nightly use, but when they go, you replace the whole fixture.

**Is the light bright enough to read by?**
It's bright enough to work under, but it's a cool white flood—fine for tasks, not cozy. If you want warm ambient light, this isn't it.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need wide, adjustable coverage for a garage, driveway, or side yard and you're comfortable with a hardwired install. **Skip it if** you need solar power, extended motion range, or warm light color.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid construction, genuinely useful dual-head design, and a few fixable quirks with the mounting hardware and sensor range. For the money, it's one of the more thoughtful wall-mounted floodlights in this category.
