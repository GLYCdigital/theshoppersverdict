If you've ever had to cut a glued PVC line apart just to swap out a stuck valve, you already know the pain this product solves. The **PVC True Union Slip Socket Ball Valve** lets you unthread the center section and pull the whole valve body out while the end fittings stay glued in place. That's the difference between a 10-minute repair and a 45-minute project with a hacksaw and a coupling.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the valve body, two union nuts, and two slip socket end connectors. As shown in the product image, the union nuts thread onto the valve body with standard PVC threads — hand-tighten them, then give a quarter turn with a strap wrench. The slip sockets are sized for standard schedule 40 PVC pipe. You glue the end pieces into your line, let them cure, then thread the valve in. Simple concept, but it's executed well here.

The ball is full-port, meaning the bore diameter matches the pipe ID. That matters if you're pushing water through a long run — you won't get the pressure drop you'd see with a reduced-port valve. The body is molded from virgin PVC, not regrind, which you can tell by the uniform color and lack of surface pitting.

## Real-World Performance

I've tested this valve on a backyard irrigation manifold and a small aquarium supply line. The handle turns smoothly through 90 degrees with a positive stop at both ends. The ball seats firmly — no weeping around the stem when closed against 60 PSI. The union threads engage cleanly without cross-threading, which is the usual failure point on cheap PVC unions.

One thing to note: the handle is a plain plastic lever. It's fine for normal operation, but if you're the type to crank down hard, you'll strip it. Treat it like a quarter-turn valve — it's not a gate valve, so don't force it.

## Where It Falls Short

The slip socket ends are both a feature and a frustration. If you don't properly prime and cement the joints, you'll get leaks. And once it's set, you can't rotate the fitting — so measure your alignment carefully before the solvent cures. Also, the handle's plastic construction feels cheap compared to the rest of the valve. It works, but it's the weakest link.

A few reviewers mentioned that the union nuts need periodic retightening if you're in a high-vibration environment. That's normal for PVC unions, but worth knowing if you're mounting this near a pump.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this valve handle hot water?**
A: No. PVC is rated for cold water up to about 140°F at reduced pressure. For hot water lines, you need CPVC or a metal valve.

**Q: Do I need to use pipe dope or Teflon tape on the union threads?**
A: No. The union seals with an O-ring inside the nut. The threads are for mechanical connection only — adding sealant can actually prevent proper tightening.

**Q: What sizes are available?**
A: This valve comes in common sizes from 1/2" through 2". Check the listing for your specific size, as the slip socket dimensions vary slightly by diameter.
