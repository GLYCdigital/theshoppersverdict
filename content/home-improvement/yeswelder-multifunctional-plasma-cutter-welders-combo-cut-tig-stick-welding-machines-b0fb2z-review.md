**Is this the one-machine shop you've been looking for, or a compromise too far?** In a world where a decent plasma cutter alone runs $400+, and a half-decent TIG welder another $500, YESWELDER's CUT/ combo promises to replace both (plus stick welding) in a single box. The question isn't whether that's appealing — it's whether the machine actually delivers on all three fronts without making you regret the purchase.

## What's Actually in the Box

Unboxing reveals a surprisingly compact unit — roughly the size of a small carry-on suitcase, weighing in around 35-40 pounds. You're getting:

- The combo machine with a clear digital display for amps and pressure
- Plasma torch with pilot arc (this is the standout feature at this price point)
- TIG torch with a manual gas valve
- Stick welding electrode holder and ground clamp
- Air regulator/filter for the plasma side
- Basic consumables to get started (electrodes, tips, nozzles)

Notably absent: a TIG foot pedal, argon regulator, or any gas hoses. Budget for those separately.

## Real-World Performance

**Plasma cutting is where this machine shines.** The pilot arc is the real deal — it starts the arc without touching the workpiece, which means you can cut rusty, painted, or coated metal without fighting to establish a ground. On 110V, you'll cleanly slice through 1/8-inch to 3/16-inch steel with ease; bump it to 220V and 3/8-inch is realistically within reach. The drag cutting capability is a huge convenience for new users — you don't need to maintain a perfect standoff distance.

**TIG mode is functional but barebones.** DC TIG on steel and stainless is smooth and controllable for a machine in this class. The high-frequency start works reliably, and the arc is stable down to about 15 amps. But there's no pulse function, no AC capability for aluminum, and no built-in gas solenoid. As one buyer noted: *"I had to get a flowmeter with a valve and manually start the gas before welding. It's workable, but it's not like my buddy's Lincoln."*

**Stick welding is a bonus, not a feature.** It'll run 6013 and 7018 rods acceptably for repair work, but don't expect smooth, professional beads. The arc is a bit aggressive, and duty cycle drops noticeably at higher amps.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs:

**The TIG is crippled without a foot pedal.** You're stuck with fingertip amperage control on the torch, which is fine for tack welding but frustrating for longer beads. A foot pedal will set you back another $60-100.

**Proprietary consumables.** The plasma torch uses YESWELDER-specific tips and electrodes. They're not expensive, but you can't grab standard fittings at your local welding supply if you're in a bind.

**No AC TIG.** If you need to weld aluminum, this machine simply cannot do it. That's a hard limitation many buyers discover only after purchase.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a hobbyist, farmer, or small shop owner who needs cutting and welding capability without dedicating floor space and budget to three separate machines. The plasma cutter alone justifies the purchase for most buyers.

**Skip it if:** You're a professional fabricator who relies on TIG for critical work, or you need to weld aluminum. This is a capable multi-tool, not a production machine.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this run on a standard 120V household outlet?**
A: Yes, and it cuts 1/8-inch steel fine on 110V. For thicker material, you'll want 220V. It auto-detects the input voltage — no switching required.

**Q: Does it come with a TIG foot pedal?**
A: No. You'll need to purchase one separately (expect $50-100) for proper TIG amp control. The fingertip control on the torch works but is limiting.

**Q: Can I weld aluminum with this machine?**
A: No. It's DC-only TIG, which is for steel and stainless steel. Aluminum requires AC TIG, which this unit doesn't support.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The YESWELDER CUT/ combo is a genuine value proposition for the right buyer. The plasma cutter is legitimately good, the stick mode is workable, and the TIG is serviceable with the right accessories. It's not a professional-grade machine, and it won't replace a dedicated setup for serious fabrication. But for a home shop or farm operation, it's a smart, space-saving investment that does more than its price tag suggests.

**Buy it if:** You need plasma cutting capability and occasional welding on steel, and you're realistic about the TIG limitations.

**Skip it if:** You already have a dedicated welder and just need a plasma cutter — in that case, buy a standalone unit with standard consumables.
