## What You're Actually Getting

These are zinc alloy pulls with a brushed gold finish (PVD-coated, which matters — more on that below). The square bar profile is what sets them apart from the endless round or curved options on Amazon. As shown in the product image, they have a clean, geometric silhouette that reads more "modern farmhouse" than "builder-grade gold."

## Real-World Performance

**Finish durability is the make-or-break for gold hardware, and this is where JOY DECOR earns its rating.** The PVD coating is the same process used on higher-end faucets — it's deposited at a molecular level rather than painted on. Multiple buyers noted it shrugs off daily kitchen use:

> "Six months in and they still look brand new. No tarnishing, no chipping, and I wipe them down weekly with all-purpose cleaner."

That tracks with what I'd expect from PVD at this price. The brushed texture also does double duty — it hides fingerprints far better than polished gold, which is a real consideration if you have kids or cook heavily.

Installation is straightforward: two screws per pull, and the included screws are appropriately sized for standard 1/2-inch to 3/4-inch cabinet faces. The square profile gives you a solid grip surface — no slipping when your hands are greasy from cooking.

## Where They Fall Short

**No product is perfect, and the honest downsides here are manageable but real.** First, the included screws are standard Phillips heads. If you have kids who like to "help" or want a more finished look, you'll want to swap in hex-head screws — they're cheap, but it's an extra step.

Second, while the finish is consistent *within* a batch, a few reviewers noted slight variation between orders. If you're outfitting a whole kitchen, buy all your pulls at once. This is common across every hardware brand at this price point, so it's not a dealbreaker — just smart planning.

Third, there's no drilling template in the box. If you're replacing existing hardware, you're fine — the hole spacing is standard. But if you're installing on new cabinets, measure twice. It's not a flaw, just a heads-up.
