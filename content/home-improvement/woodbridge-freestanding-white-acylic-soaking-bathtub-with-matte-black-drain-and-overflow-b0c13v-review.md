If you're shopping for a freestanding soaking tub, you've probably seen the WOODBRIDGE name everywhere. With 225 ratings and a 4.5-star average, it's one of the more popular options in the sub-$1,000 category. But popularity alone doesn't tell you whether it's the right tub for your bathroom. Here's what you actually need to know.

## What You Get

The box contains the tub itself, a matte black drain and overflow assembly, and an adjustable leveling system. The tub measures 68 inches long, 30 inches wide, and 24 inches high — a substantial footprint that fits most standard master bathrooms but will feel cramped in a 5-foot alcove. The soaking depth is a genuine 20 inches, which is deeper than most builder-grade tubs and makes a meaningful difference when you're actually submerged to the shoulders.

The acrylic is three layers thick with fiberglass backing. That matters because thin acrylic flexes under weight and transmits cold from the floor. This one holds its shape and, per owner feedback, keeps water noticeably warmer for longer than cheaper fiberglass tubs.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The surface is high-gloss white with a subtle curve to the backrest that follows your lumbar spine. It's not a dramatic ergonomic contour like some Japanese soaking tubs, but it's enough to keep you from sliding down during a 30-minute soak. The interior is smooth with no visible seams or rough edges — a detail that separates decent acrylic molding from sloppy manufacturing.

One thing reviewers consistently mention is how quiet the tub is. One owner wrote, "I was worried about the hollow sound you get with cheap tubs, but this one feels solid. No echo when you fill it." That's the fiberglass reinforcement doing its job. Another praised the leveling feet: "The floor in my 100-year-old house isn't flat at all. The adjustable feet saved me — I got it perfectly level in about 20 minutes."

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest installation gotcha: there's no tile flange on the back edge. If you're placing this against a wall, you need to account for waterproofing before you set the tub. Several reviewers mentioned this after the fact, and it's the kind of oversight that turns into a mold problem three years down the road. Plan for it. Use a waterproof membrane or backer board that extends behind the tub rim.

The matte black finish on the drain and overflow looks sharp in photos, but it's high-maintenance. Hard water spots are immediately visible, and it shows fingerprints every time you touch it. If you have hard water, budget for a squeegee or switch to the chrome version.

At 85 pounds empty, it's not outrageously heavy, but it's awkward. The shape makes it hard to grip, and you'll need two people plus furniture sliders to get it up stairs without scratching the finish.

## Who Should Buy This

This tub is ideal for a master bath remodel where you want a luxury focal point without spending $2,000. It works especially well for homeowners who are comfortable with basic plumbing — the drain hookup is standard and the instructions are clear enough for a confident DIYer.

Skip it if you're renovating a small guest bath, if you need a tub that tucks flush against three walls, or if you prefer a gloss finish on your fixtures to hide water marks.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this tub fit through a standard 30-inch door?**
A: Yes, but just barely. The tub is 30 inches at its widest point, so you'll need to angle it through. Measure your doorways and hallways before ordering — turning corners is where people get stuck.

**Q: Does the tub come with a faucet?**
A: No. The drain and overflow are included, but you'll need to purchase a freestanding tub filler separately. Factor that into your budget — a decent floor-mounted faucet runs $150-400.

**Q: Is the acrylic surface prone to scratching?**
A: It's more scratch-resistant than standard fiberglass, but it's not indestructible. Use a soft cloth and non-abrasive cleaner. One reviewer mentioned minor scratches from cleaning with a scouring pad — avoid those.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a deep, comfortable soaking tub with solid construction and don't mind handling the wall waterproofing yourself. **Skip it if** you're on a tight schedule, want a plug-and-play installation, or prefer low-maintenance chrome fixtures.

At 4.6/5, this is one of the better value freestanding tubs on the market. It delivers the soaking experience people pay double for, with the caveat that installation requires some forethought. Just don't skip the waterproofing.
