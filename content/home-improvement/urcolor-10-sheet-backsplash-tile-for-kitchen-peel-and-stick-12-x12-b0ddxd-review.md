**Performance and Real-World Installation**

This is where URCOLOR earns its keep. The adhesive backing is aggressive, so once that sheet touches the wall, it’s staying. That’s a double-edged sword: it means no peeling corners over time, but it also means zero forgiveness during alignment. Measure twice, peel once. The PVC material cuts cleanly with a sharp blade, and you can trim it to fit around outlets and edges without specialized tools.

The finish looks convincingly like ceramic tile from a distance of three feet. Up close, it’s clearly vinyl—there’s a slight sheen and the grout lines are flat, not recessed. For a rental or a quick refresh, it’s excellent. For a forever-home flip, it’s a stopgap.

One thing to note: this is not splash-proof in the waterproof sense. It will handle steam and occasional splatters, but you can’t scrub it with abrasive cleaners, and standing water at the seams can eventually lift the adhesive. Wipe it dry after cooking.

**Where It Falls Short**

Two honest downsides, and they’re worth knowing before you buy.

First, the 12” x 12” format means more seams than larger-format peel-and-stick tiles. With subway-pattern sheets, you’ll see visible joint lines where sheets meet. You can minimize this by staggering the sheets, but you can’t eliminate it.

Second, the curl issue. A few sheets in every batch seem to arrive with edges that don’t lay flat. You can fix this with a hair dryer on low heat to soften the adhesive, but it’s an extra step you shouldn’t have to take.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I apply this over existing tile?**  
Yes, if the existing tile is smooth and clean. Rough or heavily textured tile will prevent proper adhesion and cause bubbles.

**Q: How do I remove it without damaging drywall?**  
Use a heat gun or hair dryer to soften the adhesive, then peel slowly from a corner. You may need to patch minor residue with adhesive remover.

**Q: Does it come with grout?**  
No. The grout lines are printed into the design. If you want real grout lines, you need actual tile.
