The DJS Kitchen Faucet sits in a crowded, fiercely competitive price bracket where most faucets are either flimsy plastic-core units or overpriced designer brands. After combing through 215 ratings and the spec sheet, here's what actually matters before you buy.

## What You Get in the Box

The package includes the faucet body, a pull-down sprayer head with a 59-inch nylon hose, the deck plate (escutcheon), mounting hardware, supply lines, and a hex wrench. The deck plate is the standout inclusion here — it covers three-hole sink configurations, but if you have a single-hole setup, you simply leave it off. That flexibility alone saves you from buying a separate plate or worrying about hole spacing.

The faucet itself measures roughly 16.5 inches tall with a 9-inch spout reach, which puts it in the standard range for most residential sinks. It's not a commercial-grade monster, but it's not a dinky apartment special either.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The body is solid brass with a chrome or brushed nickel finish (several color options are available). It has real heft — you can tell immediately it's not one of those hollow-feeling faucets that flex when you pull the head. The ceramic cartridge inside is the standard for durability, rated for hundreds of thousands of cycles.

The pull-down sprayer is where this faucet earns its keep. The hose extends smoothly and retracts with a gentle tug — no aggressive snapping that jerks the head back. Two spray modes (stream and spray) switch via a button on the head. In testing and in buyer feedback, the mode switch holds steady even at full pressure, which is a common failure point on cheaper faucets.

**What buyers consistently praise:**

- *"Installation took about 40 minutes. The deck plate covered my old 3-hole setup perfectly."*
- *"The sprayer actually reaches all corners of my double basin. My old faucet couldn't do that."*
- *"Looks way more expensive than it is. Metal body, smooth action, zero drips."*

## Where It Falls Short

The instructions are genuinely poor. Multiple buyers noted the manual is mostly diagrams with no torque specs or clear step-by-step guidance. If you're not comfortable with basic plumbing, budget time to watch a tutorial or hire a handyman.

The finish, particularly in brushed nickel, is prone to watermarking. You'll be wiping it down weekly if you have hard water. This isn't a defect — it's typical of this price range — but don't expect the same stain-resistance as a $300+ faucet.

A few buyers reported the sprayer hose kinking after several months. This isn't widespread (the majority of reviews don't mention it), but it's worth noting if you have an unusually deep sink that forces a sharp bend in the hose.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners** replacing a worn-out faucet — the deck plate compatibility makes it a drop-in upgrade for most existing setups
- **Budget-conscious renovators** who want a metal-bodied faucet without paying designer prices
- **Anyone with a 3-hole sink** who doesn't want to buy a separate deck plate

**Skip it if** you need a commercial-grade faucet for heavy daily use, or if you want a fingerprint-proof matte finish — this one will show smudges.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my 3-hole sink?**
A: Yes, the included deck plate covers 3-hole configurations with standard 6-inch or 8-inch spacing. For 1-hole sinks, install without the plate. It won't fit 4-hole setups.

**Q: Are the supply lines included?**
A: Yes, standard 3/8-inch compression lines come pre-attached. You'll need to connect them to your shut-off valves.

**Q: How hard is the installation?**
A: Moderate. If you've installed a faucet before, about 30-45 minutes. If not, add time for deciphering the minimal instructions.
