The $50 drill aisle is crowded with plastic-bodied toys that stall on the first screw. The AVID POWER 20V MAX stands apart — and a 4.3-star average across 215 ratings confirms it's not just another Amazon special. But is it the right drill for *your* toolbox? Let's dig past the marketing.

## What's Actually in the Box

As shown in the product image, you're getting more than a bare drill. The kit includes:

- **The drill itself** — 20V MAX brushed motor, 19+1 clutch, two-speed gearbox
- **One 1.5Ah battery** (this matters — we'll get to it)
- **Charger** plus a spare battery? No — check the listing carefully. Standard kits ship with one battery.
- **24-piece accessory kit** — drill bits (HSS and masonry), screwdriver bits, socket adapters, and a flexible shaft
- **Keyed chuck wrench** and belt clip

The accessory bundle alone justifies part of the price. Those bits aren't junk — they'll handle drywall, softwood, and light metal without snapping.

## Performance: Where It Surprises and Where It Falters

The 19+1 clutch is the headline feature, and it earns its place. Dial it to 3-4 for cabinet screws and you won't strip softwood. Crank it to 19 for 2-inch deck screws and the motor keeps turning without the clutch chatter you get on cheaper drills. The two-speed gearbox (0-400 and 0-1,500 RPM) gives you genuine low-speed torque for driving, not just a token "slow mode."

That said, this is a **brushed** motor in a 20V-class tool. It's not a DeWalt or Milwaukee — and it doesn't pretend to be. For drilling 1-inch holes through pine studs or assembling furniture, it's more than adequate. Framing a deck or drilling through concrete with a 3/4-inch bit? You'll feel the motor strain, and the 1.5Ah battery will drain fast.

The keyed chuck is the biggest practical annoyance. Every bit change requires the wrench — which lives in the included case but *never* stays there. If you're used to keyless chucks on modern drills, this feels dated. It does grip bits more securely than many budget keyless chucks, so there's a tradeoff.

## What Buyers Actually Say

## Where It Falls Short — Plainly

- **Battery capacity is the weak link.** The 1.5Ah pack is fine for light work but won't survive a full day of projects. Budget for a spare (around $25-30) if you buy this.
- **The chuck requires a key.** If you lose it, you're stuck. Glue it to the case or buy a spare upfront.
- **No brushless motor.** Efficiency and runtime suffer at high load. You'll notice the difference under sustained drilling.
- **The included case is flimsy.** It protects the tool in storage, but don't toss it in a truck bed.

## Who Should Buy This

**The weekend warrior.** If you're assembling furniture, hanging shelves, drilling into drywall and softwood, or doing light repairs — this is your drill. The accessory kit means you won't need to buy bits separately for months.

**The first-time homeowner.** The clutch protects your projects from your inexperience. The price won't sting if you use it twice.

**Skip it if** you're a contractor, framer, or anyone drilling through concrete or steel daily. You need a brushless 18V or 20V tool from a pro brand.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Does this drill come with two batteries?**

**Q: Can it drive screws into concrete or masonry?**

**Q: Is the keyed chuck really a problem?**
