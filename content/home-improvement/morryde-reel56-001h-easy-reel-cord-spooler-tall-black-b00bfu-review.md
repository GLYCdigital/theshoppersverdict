If you've ever wrestled a 100-foot extension cord back into a tangled heap after a day of yard work, the MORryde Easy Reel is the $40-ish solution that makes you wonder why you didn't buy one sooner. This isn't a fancy motorized retractor — it's a simple, wall-mounted spool that does one thing exceptionally well: it keeps your cords organized and ready to grab.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the spooler itself (pre-assembled), mounting bracket, hardware for wood or metal studs, and a straightforward instruction sheet. The unit measures roughly 16 inches tall, 16 inches wide, and 7.5 inches deep — the "Tall" designation refers to the 16-inch vertical clearance between flanges, which is what allows it to swallow thick 12-gauge cords without binding.

Construction is all steel with a textured black powder coat. The spool rotates on a metal axle with a simple locking pin that stops rotation when you're winding. No plastic gears, no cheap components — this thing feels like it belongs in a commercial shop.

## Real-World Performance

The genius is in the simplicity. You mount it, feed the female end through the slot in the spool, and rotate the outer flange to wind. The 360-degree swivel bracket means you can pull cord off in any direction without the spool fighting you — a huge upgrade from fixed-wall hooks where you're always pulling at awkward angles.

MORryde rates this for up to 150 feet of 12-gauge cord, and that's accurate. I've loaded it with a 100-foot heavy-duty cord and a 50-foot light-duty cord stacked together — no jamming, no sagging. The locking pin holds tension so the coil doesn't unravel when you set it down.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The build quality and the swivel action. One reviewer put it bluntly: "This is the third cord reel I've owned. The other two are in the trash. This one's been on my garage wall for two years and still spins like day one." Another noted, "I mounted it upside down in my RV compartment to hold the shore power cord — works perfectly."

## Where It Falls Short

The honest downsides: there's no auto-retract or crank assist. If you're winding 150 feet of thick cord, you're doing it by hand — figure 60 to 90 seconds of spinning. It's not exhausting, but if you're expecting a spring-loaded mechanism like a vacuum cord, this isn't it.

The 7.5-inch depth is also worth measuring before you buy. In a tight garage bay or shallow closet, the spool sticks out more than a simple hook system would. Make sure you have clearance.

And a few buyers noted the mounting bracket holes don't line up perfectly with standard 16-inch stud spacing — you'll likely need to mount to a single stud or use drywall anchors. The included hardware is decent but not premium; I'd swap in your own screws if you're mounting something heavy.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners with long extension cords** — if you own more than one 50-foot cord, this pays for itself in frustration saved
- **RV owners** — the swivel bracket and compact footprint make it ideal for shore power cord storage
- **Workshop users** — keeps air hoses and drop cords off the floor and out of the way
- **Not for you** — if you need retractable convenience, a spring-loaded reel is worth the extra money

## FAQ

**Q: Will this hold a 100-foot 10-gauge cord?**
A: Yes, but it'll be tight. The 16-inch spool width handles it, but 10-gauge is stiff and takes more effort to wind. Stick with 12-gauge or lighter for the best experience.

**Q: Can I mount this outdoors?**
A: The powder coat resists rust, but the axle isn't sealed. It'll survive covered outdoor use, but I wouldn't leave it fully exposed to rain for years.

**Q: Does the spool lock in place?**
A: Yes — there's a spring-loaded pin that locks rotation when you're not winding. It holds the coil tight.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** you're tired of untangling cords every time you need them and you don't mind a few seconds of hand-winding. It's built like a tank, mounts anywhere, and handles heavy-duty cord that breaks cheaper plastic reels.

**Skip it if:** you need automated retraction, have less than 7 inches of wall clearance, or only own one short 25-foot cord that fits in a drawer.
