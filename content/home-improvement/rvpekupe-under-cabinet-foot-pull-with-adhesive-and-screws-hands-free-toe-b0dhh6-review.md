# Is the RVPEKUPE Under Cabinet Foot Pull Worth It? A Hands-On Review

You've got your arms full of groceries, an infant on your hip, or a cast on your dominant hand. The last thing you want to do is juggle everything just to open a lower cabinet. That's the exact problem the RVPEKUPE Under Cabinet Foot Pull solves — and with 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly working for a lot of people. But is it right for *your* kitchen? Let's dig in.

## What's in the Box

The package is refreshingly straightforward. You get the foot pull itself — a solid aluminum bar with a powder-coated finish that comes in several color options — plus two mounting kits. There's a 3M VHB adhesive strip for no-drill installation and a full set of screws with wall anchors for a permanent mount. A small alignment template is included too, which I appreciated; it saved me from the classic "measure twice, drill once" anxiety.

The unit measures roughly 4 inches long and protrudes about half an inch from the cabinet face when installed. That's compact enough to be unobtrusive but substantial enough to find with your toe without looking down.

## Real-World Performance

I mounted mine on a lower cabinet in a busy kitchen for three weeks of testing. The adhesive installation took under two minutes: clean the toe kick with rubbing alcohol, peel the backing, press for 30 seconds. Done. It held through daily use, including some enthusiastic kicks from my two-year-old.

The motion is a downward pull rather than a push. You hook your toe over the top edge and pull down. The spring-loaded mechanism releases the cabinet latch with about 8-10 pounds of force required — enough to feel secure, not so much that it's difficult. I found it easiest to use the top of my foot rather than my toes, which took a day to figure out.

One thing that impressed me: the mechanism is completely mechanical. There's no motor, no battery, no sensor to calibrate. It's the same concept as a standard toe kick latch but with a more ergonomic foot interface. That simplicity means there's virtually nothing to break.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The consensus among the 225 reviewers is overwhelmingly positive. One verified buyer wrote: "I broke my wrist last month and this has been a lifesaver. I can open every lower cabinet in my kitchen with my foot now." Another mentioned: "I was skeptical about the adhesive but it's been 4 months and hasn't budged."

The most common praise centers on the dual mounting options — renters appreciate the no-drill route, homeowners like the security of screws. The low-profile design also gets consistent mentions, with buyers noting it doesn't catch on socks or get in the way of cleaning.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. The adhesive mount works great on smooth surfaces, but if your toe kick has texture, paint drips, or old glue residue, the VHB tape won't get full contact. You'll need to use the screws, which means drilling into your cabinet. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you buy.

Also, the downward-pull motion is distinct from traditional toe kicks. If you're replacing an existing latch, there's a learning curve. Some reviewers noted their first few days involved a lot of missed grabs. It's not a flaw, but it's a real adjustment.

Finally, the color options are limited. You're mostly choosing between black, white, and silver. If your cabinets have a specific finish, you might not find a perfect match.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a renter who can't drill into cabinets but wants hands-free access. You're recovering from an arm injury or have limited hand mobility. You frequently carry things into the kitchen and want to reduce juggling. You have kids who leave cabinet doors open and want a latch that's easy for adults but less accessible for toddlers.

**Skip it if:** You have textured or uneven cabinet surfaces that won't accept adhesive well. You dislike the idea of a foot-operated mechanism and prefer standard handles. You want a fully hidden solution — while low-profile, this is visible when you look at the toe kick.

## FAQ

**Q: Will the adhesive damage my cabinets when removed?**
A: The 3M VHB tape is strong, but it comes off cleanly with a bit of patience. Use a plastic scraper and rubbing alcohol to remove any residue. No permanent damage in my testing.

**Q: Does it fit all standard cabinet latches?**
A: The foot pull replaces the existing latch mechanism. It's designed for standard under-cabinet spring latches, which cover the vast majority of kitchen cabinets. If you have an unusual locking system, measure the spacing first.

**Q: How much force does it take to activate?**
A: Roughly 8-10 pounds of downward pressure. That's easy for an adult but generally too much for a toddler to manage accidentally.
