## What You Actually Get

The box contains the mixer itself — no drill, no gloves, no carry case. The unit measures roughly 17 inches from top to bottom, with a 1/4-inch hex shank that fits any standard 3/8-inch drill chuck. The shaft is stainless steel; the mixing fins are molded polypropylene. Total weight is around 1.5 pounds, so it won't strain your wrist during a 10-minute mix.

The design is straightforward: two stacked paddle sets at slightly offset angles, which creates a vortex that pulls paint downward from the top and pushes it up from the bottom. This is a significant upgrade over single-paddle mixers that only churn the surface layer.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested this with a 5-gallon bucket of latex primer, a 2-gallon bucket of oil-based enamel, and a 1-gallon can of drywall mud (thinned). The AYZOTEK handled all three without complaint, but the sweet spot is clearly liquid paint.

At 400-500 RPM on a corded drill, it created a smooth, consistent vortex in about 45 seconds. The offset fins break up sediment at the bottom of the bucket — something I've seen cheaper mixers fail at entirely. The hex shank seated firmly in my drill chuck with no wobble, even at higher speeds.

One buyer noted: *"I've used this on three different drills, and it hasn't slipped once. The hex shank is a huge improvement over the round ones that spin loose."* That matches my experience — the grip is solid.

## Where It Falls Short

This isn't a universal tool, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. The polypropylene fins flex noticeably when mixing anything thicker than paint. I tried it with unthinned joint compound, and the paddles twisted under load, forcing me to slow down significantly. If you're mixing thinset, drywall mud, or epoxy, get a heavy-duty spiral mixer instead.

Also, be careful with thin paints like stains or lacquers. Run the drill above 600 RPM and you'll whip air into the liquid, creating bubbles that take time to settle. Slow and steady is the rule here.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my drill?**  
A: Yes, if your drill has a 3/8-inch or 1/2-inch chuck. The 1/4-inch hex shank is universal across all major drill brands.

**Q: Can I use it to mix epoxy or resin?**  
A: Yes, for liquid epoxies with a working time over 30 minutes. But it's not for paste-type epoxies — the fins will flex.

**Q: How do I clean it?**  
A: Wipe with a rag and mineral spirits or paint thinner immediately after use. The stainless steel shaft won't rust, but dried paint on the fins is annoying to remove.
