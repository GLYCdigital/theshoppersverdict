Most gate latches are flimsy afterthoughts — thin zinc alloy pieces that corrode within a season and bend the first time a gust of wind slams the gate. The Alise SUS304 stainless steel slide lock is not that. This is a properly built piece of hardware that treats a gate latch like it actually has a job to do.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the main slide lock body, the striking plate, and mounting screws. No surprises, no filler. The selling point here is the material: genuine SUS304 stainless steel, not the "stainless-look" coated zinc you'll find on cheaper alternatives. The plate is visibly thickened compared to standard latches — you can feel the weight difference before you even mount it.

As shown in the product image, the design is straightforward: a surface-mount slide bolt that works on left or right-opening gates. The dimensions are compact enough for residential gates but substantial enough for heavier barn or yard doors.

## Real-World Performance

Installing this lock takes about ten minutes with a drill and a screwdriver. Surface-mount design means no mortising or routing — you just mark, drill pilot holes, and screw it down. It works equally well on wood posts, metal tubing, and vinyl fence gates.

The build quality is where this thing earns its keep. The slide mechanism moves with a satisfying, solid click when properly aligned. One reviewer noted, "I've replaced three cheap latches in two years. This one feels like it'll outlast the fence." That sentiment echoes across the 229 ratings — durability is the consistent theme.

The thickened steel matters in practice. Standard latches flex under load, which lets gates rattle in the wind or pop open during storms. This one holds firm. The SUS304 stainless steel means no rust streaks running down your gate after a rainy season — a common complaint with lesser hardware.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has a couple of quirks. The most common complaint involves alignment. The striking plate needs to be positioned precisely; if it's off by even a few millimeters, the bolt binds and won't slide smoothly. One reviewer warned, "Measure twice. If your gate has settled or sagged, the bolt won't line up." This isn't a design flaw — it's physics — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

The sliding mechanism can also feel stiff out of the box. A drop of silicone lubricant or white lithium grease resolves this quickly, but it's an extra step some buyers won't expect.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners with wooden yard gates** who are tired of replacing corroded, flimsy latches every year
- **Anyone in coastal or high-humidity areas** where rust is the #1 killer of exterior hardware
- **DIYers who want a straightforward installation** without complex mortising or drilling

Skip it if you need a keyed lock for security purposes — this is a privacy/restraint latch, not a high-security solution. Also skip it if you're working with a severely sagging gate that won't align with any latch.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work on metal tube gates?**
A: Yes. The surface-mount design works on flat metal surfaces. You'll need self-tapping screws for metal — the included screws are sized for wood.

**Q: Can it be installed vertically instead of horizontally?**
A: Technically yes, but the slide mechanism is designed for horizontal operation. Vertical mounting may cause the bolt to drift over time.

**Q: How much weight can it hold against a gate?**
A: The latch isn't load-rated like a hinge, but the thickened steel construction easily handles the force of a heavy wooden gate pushing against it.
