If you've bought cheap landscape lights before, you know the drill: plastic housing cracks by year two, the seal fails, water gets in, and you're digging up a dead fixture in the dark. The Gardencoin Solid Brass Spotlight is the antidote to that cycle — but it's not without a couple of gotchas that are worth knowing before you click buy.

**What's actually in the box**

You get one complete spotlight, pre-wired with an 18-inch lead. The housing is machined from solid brass — not brass-plated zinc, not painted aluminum. That matters because the threads on the tilt collar and the rotation ring are cut into the metal itself, so they don't strip the way plastic threads do after a season of adjustment. The included bulb is a 4W GU5.3 LED (about 350 lumens, 3000K warm white), and the fixture accepts any standard MR16/GU5.3 replacement, so you're not locked into a proprietary chip. There's no transformer in the box — this is a 12V AC/DC fixture, so you'll need a low-voltage landscape transformer (typically 60W or larger, depending on circuit size).

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The IP65 rating is the headline spec here, and in practice it holds up. The lens gasket is a thick, compressible silicone ring that seats firmly against the brass bezel, and the rear cable gland uses a compression fitting that grips the wire without crushing it. That's the kind of detail that separates a $40 brass fixture from a $15 plastic one. One reviewer noted, "Two brutal winters and a monsoon season in Florida, and these still look like the day I installed them." That matches the 4.7/5 average across 235 ratings — the long-term corrosion complaints are rare, and almost always trace to someone burying the connector improperly.

The beam is adjustable through 360° rotation and 90° tilt, and the locking collar is a real threaded ring, not a friction hinge. That means once you set the angle, it stays put — even in high winds or when a sprinkler head whacks the housing. The beam itself is a tight, focused spot (about a 25° spread) that's ideal for washing a facade, highlighting a specimen tree, or lighting a walkway sign. Keep in mind: 350 lumens is on the subtle side. For a 20-foot oak, you'll want to swap in a 6W or 7W MR16 bulb, which this fixture handles fine.

**Where it falls short**

Two honest tradeoffs. First, the raw brass finish will oxidize. Some buyers expect a polished, jewelry-like look forever; instead, you'll see a gradual deepening to a bronze-brown over the first year. That's not a defect — it's the nature of uncoated brass outdoors — but if you want it to stay shiny, you'll need to apply a clear lacquer yourself. Second, the 18-inch pigtail is short for most installations. You'll likely need to extend it or position a waterproof junction box within reach. Budget for a pair of silicone-filled wire nuts or a waterproof connector kit, because a standard twist-on nut will fail in the ground within a season.

**Who should buy this**

- **The set-and-forget homeowner:** If you're done with replacing $12 plastic fixtures every spring, the brass build pays for itself in three years.
- **Landscape lighting enthusiasts:** The MR16 compatibility means you can dial in exact color temperature and output — try a 2700K dimmable LED for a softer residential look.
- **Anyone with corrosive environments:** Coastal salt air or poolside chlorine will eat aluminum; brass shrugs it off.

Skip it if you need a floodlight-style wide beam, or if you're on a strict budget and only need lights for one season — a $15 plastic unit will technically work for that.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with a standard landscape transformer?**
Yes. It accepts 12V AC or DC from any low-voltage transformer. Just verify the total wattage on your circuit doesn't exceed transformer capacity.

**Q: Can I use a dimmer with it?**
Only if you use a dimmable MR16 LED bulb and a compatible 12V dimmable transformer. The included 4W bulb is not dimmable.

**Q: Will the brass scratch or dent easily?**
It's softer than steel, so yes, it can dent if whacked by a mower. But it won't crack, and minor dings can be buffed out or left as character.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a permanent, low-maintenance fixture that will outlast your current landscaping phase. The brass body, sealed internals, and standard bulb socket make this a genuinely smart long-term buy.

**Skip it if:** You need broad area lighting, or you're unwilling to add waterproof connectors and possibly a brighter bulb to the initial cost.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid, durable, and flexible. The short pigtail and uncoated brass are minor annoyances, not dealbreakers. This is the last spotlight you'll buy for that spot.
