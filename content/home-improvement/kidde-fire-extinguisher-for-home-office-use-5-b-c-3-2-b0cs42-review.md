Let's cut through the safety-equipment confusion: this Kidde 5-B:C extinguisher is not the all-purpose unit most people think they're buying. It's specifically rated for flammable liquids (Class B) and electrical fires (Class C) — think grease fires in the kitchen, a toaster shorting out, or a breaker panel sparking. It will not handle a burning bookshelf or trash can.

That said, for its intended job, this little 3.2-pound unit is outstanding — and at 4.9 stars across 245 ratings, buyers overwhelmingly agree.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the extinguisher itself and a small instruction manual. That's it. No wall bracket, no mounting hardware — a $10 accessory you'll want to order alongside it. The cylinder measures roughly 12 inches tall and 4 inches in diameter, small enough to tuck under a kitchen sink, in a desk drawer, or in a car trunk without eating up space.

The pressure gauge is front-and-center with a clear green "operating" zone. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "I don't know anything about fire safety, but the gauge is idiot-proof. Green means good."

## Performance and Build Quality

The trigger mechanism requires a firm squeeze — roughly what you'd expect from a quality unit. The discharge stream reaches about 8-10 feet, and the full discharge lasts 12-14 seconds. That's a meaningful window: enough to knock down a typical kitchen grease fire or control an electrical blaze while you evacuate.

The metal valve assembly feels solid, not cheap plastic. The pull pin is secured with a standard tamper seal that breaks cleanly when needed. Multiple reviewers noted the unit held pressure for years between checks — one buyer reported "stored in a hot garage for three years, gauge still in the green."

## Where It Falls Short

The Class A limitation is the biggest gap. If you want one extinguisher to handle everything — wood, paper, fabric, plus liquids and electrical — you need a 5-lb ABC unit like the Kidde Pro 210, which costs roughly twice as much and weighs nearly double. This 5-B:C unit is a specialty tool for kitchens, workshops, garages, and offices where the primary fire risk is grease or electrical.

The missing mounting bracket is an annoyance, not a dealbreaker. But it means the "ready when you need it" promise depends on you buying extra hardware or stashing it in a drawer. Also note: the 3.2-lb agent capacity means shorter discharge time than larger units — 12-14 seconds is adequate but not generous.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're equipping a kitchen, apartment, office, garage, or car with a lightweight, no-brainer extinguisher for grease and electrical fires. It's ideal for renters who need something compact and non-permanent, or as a backup unit to a larger ABC extinguisher.

**Skip it if:** You want a single do-everything extinguisher for a basement, woodshop, or garage with mixed fire risks, or if you need something that mounts permanently to a wall without extra purchases.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this extinguisher good for a kitchen grease fire?**
Yes — that's exactly what the 5-B rating covers. Keep it near the stove but not directly above it (heat can affect the pressure gauge). Aim at the base of the flames, not the top.

**Q: How do I check if it's still good?**
Monthly, glance at the gauge — needle in the green means ready. The tamper seal should be intact. Check the expiration date stamped on the cylinder; replace or recharge when it passes.

**Q: Can I use this on a trash can fire?**
No. That's a Class A fire (paper, wood, cloth). This unit won't extinguish it effectively, and you could waste your discharge trying. You need an ABC-rated extinguisher for mixed-use protection.
