Let me be direct: the Streamlight 75763 Stinger LED HPL is not a flashlight for people who want a pocket-sized EDC. It's a duty-grade tool built for people whose job depends on seeing clearly at night—police officers, security guards, mechanics, and anyone who's ever needed to illuminate something 100 yards away and needed to do it *right now*.

This is the modernized version of the classic Stinger that's been riding in patrol cars since the 1990s. The "HPL" in the name stands for High Performance Lighting, and it's not marketing fluff. The LED emitter pushes 37,000 candela—that's the peak beam intensity, which translates to a hotspot that stays tight and usable well past 300 meters. For comparison, most consumer flashlights in the $50 range are lucky to hit 10,000 candela.

## What's Actually in the Box

Streamlight keeps it utilitarian. You get:

- The Stinger LED HPL flashlight itself (7.3 inches, machined aircraft aluminum with an anodized finish)
- A 120V AC charging base for home or station use
- A 12V DC charging base for vehicle installation
- The proprietary 3-cell NiMH battery pack

No batteries included—because the battery *is* included, pre-installed in the light. The charging system is the real selling point here. You drop the light into either base and it charges hands-free. No cables, no fiddling with ports, no forgetting to plug it in. For someone who uses a light professionally, this is the difference between a tool that's always ready and one that's dead when you need it.

## Real-World Performance

The beam pattern is where the Stinger HPL earns its keep. It's a tight, focused hotspot with a softer spill—not a flood light. That's intentional. If you're searching a dark parking lot or checking under a vehicle, you want to see *far*, not just wide. The three modes (high, medium, low) give you 600, 275, and 130 lumens respectively, which covers everything from room clearing to close-up work.

Run times are solid but not class-leading: about 3.5 hours on high, 6.5 on medium, and 14 on low. The battery takes roughly 3 hours to fully recharge from the bases. That's acceptable for a light this size, but it's not the 20-hour endurance you'd get from a larger 18650-based light.

Build quality is genuinely impressive. The body is CNC-machined from aircraft-grade aluminum with a Type II anodized finish. The knurling is aggressive enough to grip with wet hands. It's IPX4 rated for water resistance—fine for rain, not for submersion. The tail-cap switch is a single-button design that's easy to operate with gloves, though there's no mode memory.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the proprietary NiMH battery pack. When it eventually wears out—typically after 2-3 years of daily use—you're looking at a $40+ replacement from Streamlight. There's no swapping in standard AA or 18650 cells. This is a closed system.

Second, the weight. At 11.5 ounces with battery, this isn't a light you clip to your pocket and forget. It's meant for a belt holster, a glove box, or a duty bag. If you want a pocket light, look elsewhere.

Third, the single switch with no mode memory. Every time you power on, you cycle through high → medium → low → off. If you need low mode constantly, you'll burn through the sequence every time. A dual-switch design with instant access to a specific mode would be better for tactical use.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a professional who needs a reliable, always-charged light with serious throw. Police officers, night-shift security, roadside assistance technicians, and anyone who works on vehicles at night will appreciate the DC charger base and the rugged build.

**Skip it if:** You want a lightweight EDC light for walks or general household use. The Stinger HPL is overkill for that—you'd be carrying unnecessary weight and paying for features you won't use.
