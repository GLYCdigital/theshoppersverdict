If your basement floods even once, you'll spend more on cleanup than this pump costs. The FOTING Full 304 Stainless Steel Sump Pump is built for homeowners who want a submersible workhorse that won't rust out in two seasons. At 4.1 stars across 204 ratings, it's not perfect — but for the price point, it punches well above its weight in materials and output.

**What's actually in the box**  
The pump arrives fully assembled with the tethered float switch pre-wired. You get the pump body, a 10-foot power cord, and the float switch arm. No check valve, no discharge pipe, no mounting hardware — those are on you. The unit measures 11.5 inches tall with a 7.5-inch diameter base, so it fits most standard sump pits, but the float adds height when fully extended. The 304 stainless steel isn't just a thin wrap — the entire housing, motor casing, and intake screen are solid stainless, which is the main selling point here.

**Performance and real-world use**  
The 1HP motor moves a claimed 4600 gallons per hour at a 10-foot lift, which is genuinely high capacity for a residential pump. In practice, buyers report it clears a 3-foot-deep pit in under a minute. The float switch activates with about a 9-inch water level change — wide enough to prevent rapid cycling, but it means the pump runs longer per cycle. That's fine for most basements, but if you have a shallow pit, the pump may not fully drain before the float drops.

The stainless steel matters more than you'd think. Painted cast iron pumps chip, and plastic housings crack in cold weather. This unit shrugs off humidity and mild acids in groundwater. One buyer noted: *"Had a cast iron pump rust through after 14 months. This one's been in a wet pit for 8 months and the housing looks brand new."* That's the durability story — and it's backed by the material choice.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Customers across Amazon highlight three things: the build quality, the flow rate, and the quiet operation. The motor is sealed and runs at a reasonable 65-70 dB — audible but not deafening. Multiple reviewers mention it handles high-volume inflow from heavy rains without bogging down. One long-term owner wrote: *"Replaced a 1/2 HP pump that couldn't keep up. This 1HP clears the pit twice as fast and the float switch hasn't stuck once in a year."*

**Where it falls short**  
The tethered float is the biggest limitation. It needs at least 14 inches of basin diameter to swing freely — if your sump pit is narrow (common in older homes), this pump physically won't fit. You'll need a vertical float switch model instead. Also, there's no check valve included. Without one, water drains back into the pit after each cycle, which makes the pump cycle more often. Factor in an extra $15-20 for a quality brass check valve.

The 22-pound weight also makes installation a two-person job if you're working in a cramped corner. And while the stainless is corrosion-resistant, the float switch housing is plastic — that's the first part to fail if you have aggressive water chemistry. It's replaceable, but it's a weak point.

**Who should buy this**  
This pump is for you if: you have a large sump pit (14+ inches), you deal with heavy groundwater or storm surge, and you want something that'll outlast a cheap plastic pump. It's also ideal for irrigation or dewatering applications where you need high volume on demand. Skip it if you have a narrow pit, a small crawlspace, or if you're on a tight budget — a 1/3 HP pump at half the price might be sufficient for light seepage.

**FAQ**  

*Can this pump handle solids?*  
No. It has a 0.4-inch intake screen meant for clear water or fine sediment. Don't pump mud, sand, or debris — you'll clog it.

*Is the float switch adjustable?*  
The tethered float has a fixed activation range of about 9 inches. You can adjust the tether length on the rod, but the switch point is set by the float's pivot position — not fully adjustable.

*Does it run continuously?*  
No, it's designed for automatic cycling via the float switch. Running it dry for more than 5 minutes will damage the seal.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you need a high-capacity, corrosion-proof pump for a standard-size sump pit and want to set it and forget it for years. **Skip it if** your pit is under 14 inches wide, or if you're looking for a budget pump for occasional light-duty use — this is a serious tool, not a toy.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The stainless construction and raw power are exceptional at this price. The float switch geometry and missing check valve cost it a half-star, but for most homeowners, this is a solid, long-term investment in basement protection.
