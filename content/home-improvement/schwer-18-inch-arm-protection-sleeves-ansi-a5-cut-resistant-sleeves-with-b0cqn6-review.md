## What You're Actually Getting

The package includes two sleeves — one for each arm — at 18 inches long. That's enough coverage to protect from your wrist nearly to your elbow, which matters more than most shoppers realize. Standard 12-14 inch sleeves leave a dangerous gap right where your forearm rests against sharp edges. Schwer's extended length closes that gap.

The material is a high-density knit using what appears to be a polyethylene-based fiber blend (the same family as Dyneema or Spectra). It's rated ANSI A5, meaning it withstands at least 2,200 grams of cutting force in standardized testing. For reference, most cheap sleeves on Amazon are A2-rated — a 1,000-gram threshold. That's a meaningful difference when you're working with glass, sheet metal, or sharp utility blades.

Both ends feature elastic bands to hold the sleeve in place. The knit itself is breathable — a critical detail for anyone who's worn non-breathable cut-resistant sleeves in summer heat.

## Real-World Performance

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings tells a consistent story. Buyers repeatedly mention the sleeves staying put during active work — a common failure point with cheaper alternatives that slide down mid-task. One reviewer noted, "I do HVAC installs and these stay up all day. The elastic doesn't roll or pinch." Another praised the A5 rating specifically: "I had A2 sleeves before and could feel the blade pressure through them. These stop it cold."

The breathability holds up in practice. Multiple reviewers mention wearing them in hot environments — roofing, kitchen work, warehouse shifts — without the swampy feeling that comes with denser cut-resistant materials. The knit weave allows air circulation while maintaining the protective rating.

Durability looks solid based on feedback. Several buyers report months of regular use with no fraying, loose threads, or loss of elasticity in the bands. The material resists snags better than the cheaper A2 sleeves, which tend to develop holes at stress points.

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be straight about the limitations. The sizing runs narrow. If you've got larger arms — think 16-inch biceps or bigger — the upper elastic band might feel tight. One reviewer mentioned, "I have bigger forearms from lifting and these are snug. Not uncomfortable, but I wouldn't want them any tighter." Consider sizing up if you're between sizes.

There's no thumb loop or hand coverage. That means these protect your arms only — you'll still need cut-resistant gloves for hand protection. Some competitors integrate thumb loops for added stability, which Schwer skips. It's not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you order.

The sleeves also lack any grip or padding features. If you're specifically looking for vibration dampening or grip assistance, these aren't that product. They're pure cut protection, nothing more.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I machine wash these sleeves?**
A: Yes, but use cold water and hang dry. Heat from dryers can degrade the protective fibers over time and reduce the cut resistance rating.

**Q: Do these fit women or people with smaller arms?**
A: The standard size works well for most women and men with average arm circumference. The narrow fit is more of an issue for larger users than smaller ones.

**Q: What's the difference between ANSI A2 and A5?**
A: A5 withstands more than double the cutting force of A2 (2,200+ grams vs. 1,000 grams). For serious cut hazards, A5 provides meaningfully better protection.
