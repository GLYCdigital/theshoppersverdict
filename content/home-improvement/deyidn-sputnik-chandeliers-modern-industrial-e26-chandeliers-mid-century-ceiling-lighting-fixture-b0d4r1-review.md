The Sputnik chandelier is having a serious moment, and the Deyidn version is one of the most popular ways to get that look without emptying your wallet. At $53.99 with a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it's positioned squarely in the "affordable statement piece" territory. But does it hold up as a permanent fixture, or is it just a cheap knockoff that'll wobble after six months? I've dug through the specs, the customer feedback, and the installation realities. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

Opening the box, you get the main chandelier body with six arms pre-wired, a ceiling canopy, mounting screws, wire nuts, and a set of instructions that are... adequate. The arms are steel with a matte black finish—not powder-coated plastic, which is a pleasant surprise at this price point. The fixture measures roughly 22 inches in diameter and hangs about 11 inches tall from the canopy, so it reads as a substantial piece, not a dainty accent.

The key detail here is the E26 socket. That's the standard American screw-in base. It means you're not locked into a proprietary bulb type. You can drop in $2 Edison bulbs, $8 LED globes, or even smart bulbs if you want app control. That flexibility is a big deal for a fixture in this category.

**Installation: The Reality Check**

If you're handy with a voltage tester, this is a 45-minute install. The wires are color-coded and pre-stripped, and the canopy is wide enough to cover a standard 4-inch junction box. Several reviewers mention it took them under an hour, with one noting, *"I'm not an electrician and I had it up in 40 minutes. The hardest part was deciding which bulbs to use."*

The catch? The mounting plate is a bit thin. If you're mounting to drywall without a junction box, use the included drywall anchors—don't skip that step. Also, the arms are fixed at the factory. You can't tilt them up or down to adjust the profile. For an 8-foot ceiling, that's fine. For a 7-foot ceiling with a tall person underneath, you might want to consider shorter bulbs.

**What Buyers Are Loving**

The overwhelming sentiment in the 235 ratings is that this looks far more expensive than it is. The mid-century silhouette is authentic—those angled arms with exposed bulbs create that classic atomic-age starburst effect. Reviewers consistently praise the finish: *"The matte black is actually matte, not shiny like some photos suggest. It looks premium in my dining room."*

Another recurring theme is the light quality. Since it uses exposed bulbs, the fixture acts as a statement light source. With clear Edison bulbs, you get warm, ambient glow. With frosted globes, you get more diffused, functional light. Buyers love that they can customize the look without changing the fixture.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be straight: the bulbs aren't included, and that's an additional $15–$25 you need to budget. Some buyers were caught off guard by this, expecting a ready-to-hang fixture. It's not.

The canopy hardware feels utilitarian. It works, but it's not going to win any design awards. If you're pairing this with a high-end brass or nickel fixture elsewhere in the room, the black steel and basic canopy might feel mismatched. This is a fixture that shines in industrial, farmhouse, or modern spaces—not in ornate traditional rooms.

One reviewer flagged a minor issue: *"The included wire nuts were a bit small for the gauge of wire in my older home. I had to run to the hardware store for larger ones."* It's a small hassle, but worth knowing if you're working with older wiring.

**Who Should Buy This**

**Buy it if:** You want a genuine Sputnik look without paying $200+ at a lighting showroom. You're comfortable with basic wiring and want a fixture that accepts standard bulbs. You have an 8-foot ceiling or higher and want a statement piece in a dining room, entryway, or bedroom.

**Skip it if:** You need a flush-mount (this hangs down roughly 11 inches). You want a dimmable fixture without buying dimmable bulbs separately. You're expecting a heavy, premium-feeling fixture—this is lightweight steel, not solid brass.

**Three Questions Shoppers Ask**

**Q: Can I use this on a sloped ceiling?**
A: The canopy is designed for a flat ceiling. For sloped ceilings, you'd need an adapter, which isn't included. It's not ideal for that application.

**Q: What bulb type looks best?**
A: Clear Edison bulbs (ST58 or ST64 shape) give the classic mid-century vibe. For brighter, more functional light, go with frosted LED globes. Stick with warm white (2700K) for the most flattering glow.

**Q: Is it hard to clean?**
A: The exposed arms and bulbs collect dust, but a dry microfiber duster handles it in about 30 seconds. Just make sure the power is off when you clean around the sockets.

**The Verdict**

The Deyidn Sputnik Chandelier delivers exactly what it promises: an affordable, authentic mid-century statement piece with standard sockets and a finish that punches above its weight. It's not a premium fixture, but it doesn't need to be. At $53.99, it's a low-risk way to transform a room's character. Budget for quality bulbs, use the anchors, and you'll have a fixture that looks like it cost three times as much.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Buy it if you want the look. Skip it if you need flush-mount or expect heavy-duty construction.
