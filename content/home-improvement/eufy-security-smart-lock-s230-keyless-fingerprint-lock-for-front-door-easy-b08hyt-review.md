If you've been burned by smart locks that require a separate hub, a subscription, or a PhD in networking to install, the eufy Security Smart Lock S230 is designed to be the antidote. It's a keyless fingerprint deadbolt that replaces your existing lock in about 20 minutes and connects directly to your Wi-Fi — no bridge, no monthly fee. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's clearly working for most people. But "most" isn't "all," and there are a few things you need to know before you buy.

## What's in the Box

You get the exterior keypad/fingerprint module, the interior handle assembly with a physical turn knob, a mounting plate, screws, and two physical keys. Wait — keys? Yes, despite the "keyless" branding, eufy includes two backup keys that work via a hidden keyhole on the bottom of the exterior unit. That's a smart safety net, though it's not immediately obvious.

The lock is available in two finishes: satin nickel and matte black. Both look premium and solid — the exterior module is made of zinc alloy, which gives it a reassuring heft compared to the plastic-bodied cheap locks on Amazon.

## Real-World Performance

The fingerprint sensor is the star here. It's a semiconductor capacitive reader, not the optical kind you see on cheaper units. That means it reads the ridges of your finger rather than taking a picture, so it works with dry skin, slightly wet fingers, and even minor cuts. In practice, it unlocks in under half a second. You place your finger, the deadbolt retracts, and you're in. It's genuinely faster than fumbling for a key.

You can store up to 50 fingerprints, which covers a large family or a small office. There's also a touch keypad for entering a PIN code, and you can generate temporary passcodes for guests, dog walkers, or contractors — all through the eufy Security app.

The app itself is straightforward. You get real-time lock/unlock notifications, a full activity log, and the ability to lock or unlock remotely. The auto-lock feature is one I've come to rely on: set it to 30 seconds, 1 minute, or 2 minutes after the door closes, and it'll re-lock itself. This alone is worth the upgrade if you have kids who never remember to lock the door.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reviewers across the board highlight two things: installation ease and the fingerprint response. One buyer wrote, "Took me 15 minutes to install, and the fingerprint scanner works every single time — even with my dry old-man fingers." Another noted, "The auto-lock is a game changer. I haven't worried about whether I locked the door in three months."

Battery life is also a common point of praise. The lock runs on four AA batteries (not included), and eufy claims up to 12 months of use. Real-world reports suggest 6–9 months with heavy use, which is still solid. A low-battery warning pops up in the app well before you're in trouble.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the touch keypad is not backlit and uses capacitive sensing. In cold weather, or if your fingers are wearing gloves, you'll miss presses. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real annoyance.

Second, the Wi-Fi setup is picky. The lock only connects to 2.4GHz networks, and if your router broadcasts both bands under the same SSID, the app may have trouble pairing. You'll likely need to temporarily disable your 5GHz band during setup. This is a common complaint in the reviews, and it's a genuine friction point.

Third, and most critically, there's no traditional key override on the exterior — the backup keys are hidden but usable. However, if you lose them and the battery dies completely, you're locked out until you can get a USB power bank to the charging port on the bottom. The lock does have a USB-C emergency power port, but you need to have a power bank handy and the physical access to reach the port. Plan accordingly.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters and homeowners who hate carrying keys** — this is a primary use case, and the lock performs well here.
- **Families with kids or elderly members** — fingerprint access means no lost keys, and temporary codes for caregivers are easy to set up.
- **People who want remote monitoring without a subscription** — eufy doesn't charge for app access or notifications, which is increasingly rare.

Skip it if you need a physical key as a primary method, or if you have a 5GHz-only router and aren't comfortable troubleshooting Wi-Fi settings.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the eufy S230 work with Amazon Alexa or Google Home?**
A: Yes, via the eufy Security app you can link it to Alexa and Google Assistant for voice-controlled locking (unlocking via voice is disabled for security, which is correct).

**Q: Can I use it on a door without a pre-existing deadbolt hole?**
A: No — this is a replacement deadbolt. You'll need an existing 2-1/8" bore hole with a 1" latch hole. If you're drilling fresh, budget extra time.

**Q: Is the fingerprint sensor secure against fake fingerprints?**
A: The capacitive sensor is much harder to fool than optical readers. It's not bank-vault level security, but for a residential front door, it's more than adequate.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want reliable keyless entry with no subscription fees and you're comfortable with a 20-minute install. **Skip it if** you need a physical key as a backup, or you're on a 5GHz-only network and don't want to fiddle with router settings.

The eufy S230 isn't perfect — the keypad visibility and Wi-Fi setup are genuine gripes — but the core experience of touching a fingerprint and walking in is exactly what a smart lock should be. At this price point, it's a strong value for most households.
