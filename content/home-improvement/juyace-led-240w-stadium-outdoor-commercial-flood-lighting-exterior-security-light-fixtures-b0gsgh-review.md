# Juyace LED 240W Stadium Flood Light: Commercial-Grade Brightness Without the Electrician

Most flood lights in this price range promise stadium-level output and deliver porch-light performance. The Juyace LED 240W is one of the rare exceptions. At $129.99, it's punching well above its weight class — but it's not for everyone. Here's the real breakdown after digging through specs and 225 buyer ratings.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the fixture itself (pre-wired with a 3-foot lead), the adjustable mounting bracket, and hardware for surface mounting. No bulb to install, no driver to wire separately — it's a single integrated unit. The housing is die-cast aluminum with a tempered glass lens, and the whole thing is rated IP66. That's not marketing fluff; it means it's sealed against powerful water jets and dust ingress. You can mount this thing on a barn wall and forget it for a decade.

## Performance: The Numbers Match the Hype

Here's where this light separates itself from cheaper competition. The Juyace outputs roughly 28,800 lumens at 5000K daylight white. To put that in perspective: a standard 60W equivalent LED bulb outputs about 800 lumens. This single fixture delivers the equivalent of 36 of those bulbs. The beam angle is a wide 180 degrees, so it floods an area rather than spotlighting a narrow strip.

I've seen buyer photos in the reviews showing a full two-car driveway lit edge-to-edge plus half the backyard. One reviewer wrote: *"Installed this on a pole barn at 20 feet — the entire 40x60 apron is lit like a Walmart parking lot."* Another noted: *"Compared it side-by-side with a 150W metal halide I replaced. This is not just brighter; the color rendering is cleaner — no orange haze."*

Color temperature sits at 5000K, which is crisp and clinical. If you prefer a warmer, residential tone, this isn't the light for you — it's built for security and visibility, not ambiance.

## Build Quality and Real-World Durability

The thermal management is the unsung hero here. The back of the housing is finned aluminum, which actively draws heat away from the LEDs. That matters because heat is the #1 killer of LED fixtures. Several buyers reported running these for 8+ hours nightly for months with zero lumen drop or flicker.

The mounting bracket is a U-shaped yoke that rotates 180 degrees, allowing you to aim the light precisely where you need it. One buyer mentioned: *"The swivel is stiff enough to hold position but easy enough to adjust on a ladder. No stripped bolts, no crossthreading."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, there's no built-in photocell or motion sensor. If you want dusk-to-dawn operation, you're buying a separate timer or smart switch. That adds cost and complexity.

Second, the size. This is not a subtle fixture. It measures roughly 12 x 9 inches and projects a few inches off the wall. On a large commercial building or pole barn, it looks right at home. On a suburban garage, it looks like a searchlight. Measure your mounting surface before you commit.

Third, there's a faint hum from the driver when the light is on. It's not audible from more than a few feet away, but in a dead-silent backyard at 2 AM, you'll notice it. Multiple reviewers flagged this, so it's not a unit defect — it's a design trait.

## Who Should Buy This

Buy it if you need to light a large area — a driveway, a parking pad, a workshop exterior, a farm building, or a commercial loading zone. It's also a strong choice for replacing outdated metal halide or high-pressure sodium fixtures that cost more to run and produce worse light.

Skip it if you need a subtle residential fixture, a motion-activated security light for a small porch, or a warm-color light for entertaining. This is a workhorse, not a decorative piece.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Does this light work on a standard 120V household circuit?**
Yes. It's pre-wired for 120V-277V, so it works on standard US residential voltage. No special transformer or driver needed. If you're comfortable wiring a ceiling fan or outlet, you can install this — though hiring an electrician for outdoor mounting is always safer.

**Q: How many of these do I need for a basketball court?**
For a half-court setup (roughly 50 x 47 feet), two units mounted at opposite corners will give you competitive playable light. For a full court, plan on four. The 180-degree beam spreads wide but not infinitely — overlapping coverage is better than trying to stretch one unit.

**Q: Will this survive a harsh winter with ice and snow?**
Yes, based on buyer reports. The IP66 rating covers water ingress, and the aluminum housing handles thermal cycling. One reviewer in Minnesota reported: *"Survived -20°F with ice buildup on the lens. No cracking, no moisture inside."* The only caution is clearing heavy snow off the lens if it accumulates — the light won't melt it on its own in extreme cold.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need commercial-grade illumination for a large outdoor area and don't want to pay commercial prices. The Juyace LED 240W delivers stadium-level brightness, solid weatherproofing, and a robust build for $129.99.

**Skip it if:** You need motion sensing, want a compact fixture, or require warm color temperature. This is a big, bright, cool-white workhorse — and it knows it.
