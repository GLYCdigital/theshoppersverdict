If you've priced out cabinet hardware lately, you know the sting: $8–$12 per pull at the big-box store, and suddenly a simple refresh costs more than the furniture itself. The KOOFIZO UZP30 10-pack undercuts that per-handle cost by a wide margin while delivering solid stainless steel construction. ## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains ten UZP30 pull handles, each measuring 3.5 inches in overall length with a 3-inch (76.2mm) hole spacing. That's the industry-standard center-to-center distance, meaning these will bolt onto virtually any pre-drilled cabinet door or drawer face sold in North America. You also get twenty Phillips-head mounting screws—two per handle—which is enough for the full set without a separate trip to the hardware store.

The handles themselves are solid stainless steel, not the hollow zinc-alloy castings that dominate the budget end of Amazon. The satin finish is consistent across all ten pieces, and the profile is a simple, ergonomic curve that clears your knuckles when gripping.

## Real-World Performance

The stainless construction matters more than the price tag suggests. In bathrooms, the constant humidity cycle and incidental water splashes will pit cheaper plated hardware within months. Stainless doesn't have a coating to fail—it's corrosion-resistant through and through. Buyers confirm this durability. One reviewer notes, "These replaced cheap plastic pulls that yellowed after a year. Six months in the bathroom and they still look brand new." Another mentions the finish consistency: "All ten matched perfectly. No weird color variation between pieces."

Installation is straightforward for anyone who's changed cabinet hardware before. The pre-threaded holes are clean, and the screws bite evenly. However, that brings me to the first real caveat: the included screws are soft Phillips heads. On dense hardwood doors, you'll want to pre-drill pilot holes and use a magnetic driver bit set to a low torque setting. One buyer shared the warning: "I stripped two screws on oak cabinets before switching to my own #6 screws. The handles are great—the screws are the weak point."

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the screw quality, the satin finish is worth a second thought. The product images show a soft, brushed look—if you're matching high-gloss chrome fixtures or mirror-polished appliances, these will clash. They're also not "soft-close" or dampened in any way; they're simple pulls, which is fine for cabinets but not a feature upgrade.

The 3-inch spacing is standard, but it's worth measuring your existing holes before ordering. If your current pulls use a 3.5-inch or 2.75-inch spacing, you'll need to drill new holes or look for a different model. This is a common gotcha that shows up in the Q&A section.
