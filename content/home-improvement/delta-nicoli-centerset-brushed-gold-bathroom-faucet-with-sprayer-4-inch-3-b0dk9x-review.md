The Delta Nicoli Centerset with Sprayer is the rare bathroom faucet that actually earns its premium price. At $155, it's not cheap — but after 240 ratings averaging 4.8 stars, buyers consistently say it looks and performs like a fixture costing twice as much. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the faucet body, pull-down sprayer head, supply lines (already connected — thank you, Delta), mounting hardware, and a quick-install guide. The centerset design means it's one piece with three pre-aligned holes: two for handles, one for the spout. You'll need a 4-inch centerset configuration on your sink, which covers most single-basin vanities.

Finish quality is where this faucet stands apart. The brushed gold uses PVD — the same process used on high-end cabinet hardware — so it doesn't just look metallic, it behaves metallic. It resists water spots, fingerprints, and the green corrosion that eats cheaper gold finishes within months.

## Real-World Performance

The headline feature is the pull-down sprayer. It's genuinely useful, not a gimmick. The spray head clicks into two modes — aerated stream for normal washing, and a focused spray for rinsing toothpaste out of the sink basin or cleaning around the drain. The hose extends about 20 inches, which covers the full sink radius without pulling taut.

Delta's Diamond Seal Technology is the quiet hero here. Instead of a rubber washer that degrades over time, the valve uses a diamond-coated ceramic disc. That means no drips, no mineral buildup on the seal, and a smoother handle action from day one. Buyers consistently mention how effortless the single-handle lever feels.

One buyer put it plainly: "The sprayer is the main reason I bought this, and it doesn't disappoint. The stream is strong without splashing, and the gold finish has held up perfectly through six months of daily use."

Another reviewer noted: "I was nervous about gold looking cheap. It doesn't. This is the first thing guests comment on."

## The Tradeoffs You Should Know

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**The sprayer hose adds resistance.** When you pivot the spout left or right, you can feel the hose dragging inside the faucet body. It's not annoying enough to return the product, but it's noticeable compared to a fixed-spout faucet.

**The finish is a commitment.** Brushed gold is trending, but it's a statement. If your bathroom has chrome or stainless fixtures, this faucet will clash. Plan to swap your towel bars, light fixtures, and mirror frame to match — or skip this unless you're doing a full remodel.

**Centerset only.** Some sinks have 8-inch widespread holes. This faucet won't fit those without an adapter plate, which looks awkward. Measure your sink before ordering.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're upgrading a powder room or primary bath and want a high-end look without a high-end price. You appreciate the sprayer for cleaning. You're willing to commit to the gold finish across your bathroom hardware.

**Skip it if:** You have an 8-inch widespread sink, you're mixing finishes in the same room, or you prefer a minimalist faucet without the sprayer bulk.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the brushed gold finish tarnish or fade?**
No — the PVD finish is baked into the metal at the molecular level. Buyers report no fading after a year of daily use. It's the same process used on premium door hardware.

**Q: How hard is installation?**
Moderate DIY. The supply lines are pre-attached, which saves the most frustrating step. You'll need a basin wrench for the mounting nuts. Most buyers report 45–60 minutes including cleanup. If you're not comfortable under a sink, budget for a plumber.

**Q: Will the sprayer fit in a shallow sink?**
The spray head is compact enough for standard 5-inch-deep vanity sinks. If you have a vessel sink or extremely shallow basin, you may find the sprayer angles awkwardly. Check your sink depth before ordering.
