If you run a ProTeam backpack vacuum daily, you already know the pain of swapping bags mid-job. Cheap paper bags tear at the collar, leak dust into the motor, and clog before they're half full. The ProTeam 107313 Intercept bags are engineered to fix those specific failures — and at 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, they're clearly doing something right.

## What You're Actually Getting

This package contains the ProTeam Intercept Micro Filter Bags with an open collar design, sized for 10-quart capacity units. That's the standard size for ProTeam's Super CoachVac, ProGuard, and QuadVac backpack models — plus several canister units. The open collar means the bag slides directly onto the vacuum's intake tube without needing a separate adapter ring or cardboard collar assembly. It's a friction-fit system that locks securely when you push it home.

The filtration is where these bags separate from the pack. The Intercept material uses a three-layer microfiber construction that traps particles down to 1 micron. For context, that's the size range where fine drywall dust, concrete fines, and soot live — the stuff that blows straight through standard paper bags and into your lungs or motor. The bags also have reinforced seams running the full length, which addresses the classic failure point where paper bags split along the sides.

## Performance in Real Conditions

Contractors and restoration pros are the primary buyers here, and the feedback reflects that use case. One reviewer noted, "I do fire restoration and these bags handle the fine ash without any dust escaping when I open the vacuum." That's the 1-micron filtration doing its job — the bag contains the fines, not your filter.

The 10-quart capacity is genuinely useful on backpack units. You'll get through a full day of light-to-moderate debris without a bag change. The micro-filter material also holds up to wet-ish debris better than paper, though you shouldn't treat these as wet/dry bags — they're not.

The dominant theme in reviews is fit and reliability. Multiple buyers mention that these bags seat correctly on the first try, with no wrestling or tape required. ## Where It Falls Short

The honest tradeoffs start with price. These bags cost noticeably more per unit than generic replacements that claim compatibility. You're paying for the ProTeam brand and the Intercept filtration — and if you're a weekend DIYer who empties a bag every few months, the premium may not justify itself. Buyers doing light household cleanup can likely get away with cheaper paper bags.

The second issue: no fill indicator. Many competing bags have a colored stripe or window that changes when the bag is full. The Intercept bags don't — you'll notice reduced suction when it's time to swap. If you're running a crew, that means training everyone to watch for the suction drop or you'll burn out motors from overfilled bags.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You run a ProTeam backpack or canister vacuum professionally — especially if your work involves drywall, concrete, plaster, or restoration. The 1-micron filtration and collar durability will save you money on motor repairs and filter replacements in the long run.
