**GE Disposall 3/4 HP Review: Solid Performance, Zero Frills**

Most garbage disposals under $100 are either gutless wonders that choke on celery or noisy beasts that rattle your plumbing. The GE Disposall Continuous Feed 3/4 HP sits right in the middle—and for a lot of homeowners, that's exactly the sweet spot. With a 4.2-star average from 210 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in real kitchens. But is it the right disposal for yours? Let's dig in.

**What's in the Box**

You get the disposal unit itself, a mounting assembly (sink flange, rubber gasket, and mounting ring), a discharge tube with gasket, a hex wrench for manual jam clearing, and a wire connector. The EZ mount system is genuinely tool-friendly—no plumber's putty or specialty wrenches required. The unit measures 12.5 inches tall with a 5.5-inch diameter body, which is compact enough to fit most standard sinks. Note: there's no power cord included, so budget for one if you're not hardwiring.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The 3/4 HP motor is the workhorse here. It chews through chicken bones, apple cores, and coffee grounds without complaint. The galvanized steel turntable and stainless steel shredder ring resist corrosion and stay sharp through heavy use. As one buyer put it: *"I've had this for 8 months and it's handled everything from pork ribs to corn husks without a single jam."*

That said, the 26-ounce grinding chamber is on the smaller side. You'll want to feed scraps gradually rather than dumping a full plate at once. The lack of auto-reverse means if something does jam—and it will, eventually—you'll be fishing for the hex wrench to manually spin the motor. It's a 30-second fix, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

**Where It Falls Short**

Noise is the biggest compromise. This is a loud disposal—not deafening, but you'll hear it clearly in adjacent rooms. There's minimal sound insulation, so if you're a light sleeper or have an open-concept kitchen, this might annoy you. The mounting gasket also sits flush with the sink, which is fine, but the lack of a dishwasher drain connection on some models means you'll need to verify compatibility if you're hooking up a dishwasher.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **First-time homeowners** who need a reliable, affordable disposal that installs easily
- **Renters or landlords** who want something durable without spending premium money
- **Small households** (1-3 people) that don't generate massive food waste volumes

Skip it if you have a large family that cooks multiple meals daily, or if noise sensitivity is a dealbreaker—look at a model with sound-reducing technology like the InSinkErator Evolution series.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this fit a standard 3.5-inch sink drain opening?**
Yes, the EZ mount system is designed for standard sink flanges. If you have a deeper or custom sink, measure the opening diameter first—it won't fit a 4-inch flange without an adapter.

**Q: Can I connect a dishwasher to this disposal?**
Yes, but check the model specifics. Some versions have a dishwasher inlet port; others don't. Verify before purchasing if you're replacing an existing disposal that has dishwasher drainage.

**Q: How hard is it to install if I've never done plumbing?**
If you can use a screwdriver and a wrench, you're qualified. The EZ mount locks into place without special tools. Expect 30-45 minutes including the electrical connection—just turn off the breaker first.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want dependable 3/4 HP performance at a budget-friendly price, and don't mind a little noise.

**Skip it if:** You need a whisper-quiet disposal, generate heavy food waste daily, or want jam-proof auto-reverse technology.

This GE Disposall is a straightforward, honest workhorse. It won't win any awards for refinement, but it'll grind through years of dinner scraps without drama. For the price, that's a fair trade.
