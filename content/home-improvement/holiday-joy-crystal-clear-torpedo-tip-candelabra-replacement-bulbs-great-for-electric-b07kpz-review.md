## The Problem With Candelabra Bulbs

If you own a chandelier, sconce, or any fixture with E12 candelabra sockets, you know the struggle. Traditional incandescent torpedo bulbs burn out constantly. LED replacements often look wrong—too big, too blue, or too obviously "LED" with visible diodes. The Holiday Joy Crystal Clear Torpedo Tip bulbs aim to solve that, and for the most part, they deliver.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes six bulbs, each with a standard E12 candelabra base. The glass is genuinely crystal-clear—no frosted coating, no visible LED array inside. From arm's length, these look like classic incandescent torpedo bulbs. The shape is the traditional narrow torpedo that tapers to a point, which matters more than you'd think. Many LED candelabra bulbs are too fat at the base and won't fit through the narrow openings on enclosed fixtures.

Each bulb draws 4.5 watts and puts out roughly 40-watt equivalent light at a warm 2700K color temperature. That's a useful spec to know because some competitors run cooler at 3000K, which makes your dining room look like a dentist's office.

## Performance in Real Use

I tested these in a six-socket chandelier with a dimmer switch first—that was a mistake. These bulbs are not dimmable. If your fixture is on a dimmer, skip these entirely; you'll get flicker, buzzing, and eventual failure. But in standard on/off fixtures, they perform admirably. The light is warm without being yellow, and the color matches adjacent incandescent bulbs closely enough that I couldn't spot a difference.

The build quality is solid. The glass feels substantial, not thin and cheap like some budget LED bulbs. The base screws in smoothly and seats firmly without that loose, wobbly fit you sometimes get with off-brand candelabra bulbs.

## Where They Fall Short

The non-dimmable limitation is the big one. If you have a dimmer switch on your fixture, look elsewhere. That's a significant chunk of chandelier owners, and it's frustrating the product page doesn't make this clearer.

The size is another consideration. These run about 4 inches long, which is slightly longer than some OEM incandescent torpedo bulbs. In a tight enclosed globe or a shallow sconce, they may not fit. Measure your fixture's internal depth before ordering.

Heat dissipation is also worth noting. The base gets warm to the touch after extended use—not dangerous, but noticeably warmer than the cool-running LED chips suggest. In a fully enclosed fixture with no airflow, that could shorten lifespan.
