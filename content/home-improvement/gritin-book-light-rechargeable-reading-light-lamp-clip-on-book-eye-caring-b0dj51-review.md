## What You Actually Get

The box contains the light itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a one-page quick-start guide. That's it — no wall adapter, which is standard at this price point. The light head is a slim LED panel measuring roughly 3.5 inches long, mounted on a flexible gooseneck arm that extends about 12 inches. The clip base is spring-loaded and opens to around 2.3 inches, which means it handles hardcovers, paperback spines, tablet cases, and even a standard desk edge without complaint.

The build is mostly matte plastic, but it doesn't feel cheap. The gooseneck holds its position well — a common failure point on cheap clip lights — and the clip spring has solid tension. At 3.2 ounces, you'll forget it's attached after a few minutes of reading.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The headline feature here is the three color temperature modes: warm white (around 3000K), natural (4000K), and cool white (6000K). The "eye-caring" claim isn't just marketing — the warm setting genuinely reduces glare, and there's no visible PWM flicker even at low brightness, which matters if you're sensitive to LED strobing.

What makes this light stand out from the $12 generic options is the stepless dimming. A single touch cycles through brightness levels, but holding the button ramps the intensity smoothly from a faint 5% glow to a full 100% that can illuminate an entire page spread. The 40-hour runtime at minimum brightness is accurate in real-world testing — one reviewer noted, *"I read two hours every night for three weeks before needing to charge it."* At 100% brightness, expect closer to 4-5 hours, which is still respectable.

The USB-C charging is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade over the micro-USB ports found on older models. Full charge takes about 2 hours.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 240 ratings, three themes dominate. First, the battery life — multiple reviewers mention going weeks between charges. Second, the color temperature options, particularly for nighttime reading. As one verified buyer put it: *"The warm setting doesn't wake up my spouse, and the cool setting is bright enough for detail work like sketching."* Third, the clip strength — several purchasers use it on music stands and kitchen cabinets, not just books.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The single LED battery indicator only shows green (charged), red (charging), or blinking red (low). There's no percentage readout, so you'll occasionally hit a dead battery mid-chapter. The clip also lacks rubber padding — on a softcover book, leaving it clamped overnight can leave faint pressure marks on the cover. It's not damage, but it's noticeable on matte finishes.

The gooseneck, while sturdy, is also a bit stiff when you first get it. It loosens up after a few days, but expect some initial resistance when positioning.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Night readers** who want a warm, low-glare option that won't disturb a sleeping partner
- **Travelers** who need a light that survives long trips without a charger

Skip it if you primarily read on a tablet or phone with a built-in backlight — you're paying for features you won't use.
