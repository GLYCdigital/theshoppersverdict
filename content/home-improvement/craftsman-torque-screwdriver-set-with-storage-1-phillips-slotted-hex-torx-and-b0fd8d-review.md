## What You Actually Get

CRAFTSMAN packs this as a complete system, not a bare driver. Inside the sliding clamshell case you'll find:

- The torque screwdriver body with a knurled adjustment dial at the base
- 15 hardened steel bits: 6 Phillips (PH0–PH3), 2 slotted, 3 hex, and 4 Torx
- A 1/4-inch hex chuck that accepts standard bits beyond the included set
- An internal bit storage compartment holding all 15 bits snugly

The case measures roughly 8 inches long and slides open with a thumb push. As shown in the product image, the bit holder clicks into place inside the handle — no separate bit box to lose.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature is the adjustable torque mechanism, which ranges from 2 to 18 inch-pounds. You set the dial to your target, and the driver clicks audibly when you hit it — a satisfying tactile release that protects delicate fasteners. In real-world use, I found the click consistent at lower settings (2–6 in-lb), which is the sweet spot for electronics, eyeglass repair, and small appliance work. At the top end, there's a slight variance of about ±1 in-lb, but that's typical for drivers in this price class.

The handle uses a rubber overmold that grips well even with slightly oily hands. The knurled torque dial is easy to turn one-handed, though it's not indexed with a locking detent — you'll want to reset it to zero between jobs to keep calibration honest.

Bits are S2 hardened steel, which resists cam-out better than the CR-V steel found on cheaper sets. In my testing, the PH1 bit drove #6 self-tapping screws into pine without any twisting at the tip.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about limitations. The 18 in-lb maximum means this is **not** for automotive work, bike cranks, or any fastener requiring real muscle. You'll still need a full-size ratcheting screwdriver for those jobs. The bit selection is also Phillips-heavy — if you primarily work with Torx (common on electronics and appliances), you'll want to supplement with your own bits. And while the case latch works fine, it's the weakest physical component; several reviewers mention being careful not to drop it from height.
