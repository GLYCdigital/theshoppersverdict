If you’ve ever stood at a vessel sink with a faucet that’s too short, you know the problem: water bounces off the back of the bowl, splashes your shirt, and the stream misses the drain entirely. The HEYMYTAP Waterfall Vessel Faucet solves that with a design that’s as functional as it is dramatic. This is a tall, wide-spout faucet built specifically for vessel sinks — and at 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it’s clear buyers notice the difference.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the faucet body, a single-handle lever, mounting hardware, and two brass supply lines with standard 1/2-inch connections. The finish is a brushed brass that leans warm without tipping into shiny gold territory — it pairs well with oil-rubbed bronze fixtures and matte black accessories. The spout measures 5.9 inches wide, which is noticeably broader than the typical 3- to 4-inch faucet opening. Height comes in at 14.5 inches from the deck to the spout outlet, giving you clearance for most vessel bowls, including the deep 5- to 6-inch stone basins that often crowd out shorter faucets.

**How it performs in the real world**

The waterfall effect is the headline here, and it delivers. Turn the handle and water sheets evenly across the full width of the spout — no gaps, no sputtering, no narrow stream that misses the sink. The flow is laminar, meaning it’s smooth and bubble-free, which cuts down on splash significantly. One buyer noted, “The water looks like a mini glass curtain — it’s genuinely mesmerizing. My guests always comment on it.” Another mentioned, “I was worried about splash with a vessel sink, but the wide spout actually directs water straight down. No mess.”

The brass body has a reassuring heft to it — this isn’t a hollow, lightweight fixture. The lever handle moves with a smooth, damped action, and the ceramic cartridge inside is a standard size, so replacement parts are easy to find if you ever need them.

**Where it falls short — be honest**

The single-handle design is the main limitation. If you prefer separate hot and cold handles — common in traditional or farmhouse bathrooms — this won’t work for you. There’s no dual-handle variant in this lineup.

Also, the waterfall flow is beautiful but not fast. Because the water spreads across a 6-inch width, the stream is thinner than a standard faucet’s. Filling a basin takes noticeably longer — maybe 20 to 30 seconds more for a full sink. That’s fine for hand-washing, but if you routinely fill the basin for shaving or washing your face, it’s a minor annoyance. One reviewer put it plainly: “Love the look, but it takes a little longer to fill the sink. Not a dealbreaker, just something to know.”

Finally, the brushed brass finish requires a bit of care. It resists fingerprints better than polished brass, but hard water can leave spots. A quick wipe with a microfiber cloth after use keeps it looking sharp.

**Who should buy this**

This faucet is a strong fit for three buyer types:

- **Vessel sink owners** who’ve dealt with splashing or short faucets — the 14.5-inch height and 5.9-inch spout are built for these bowls.
- **Modern or transitional bathrooms** where the waterfall effect becomes a focal point — it genuinely elevates the look of a powder room or master bath.
- **DIY installers** — the mounting system uses a single hole with a wide base plate, and the supply lines are pre-attached. Most buyers report a 30- to 45-minute install with basic tools.

Skip it if you need a dual-handle setup, prefer a fast-filling aerated stream, or have a very shallow vessel bowl where the tall spout might look oversized.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit a standard 1-hole vanity?**
A: Yes. It requires a single 1.5-inch mounting hole. If your vanity has a 3-hole setup, you’ll need a deck plate to cover the extra holes.

**Q: Does the brass finish tarnish over time?**
A: The brushed finish is sealed with a clear coat that resists tarnishing. With normal cleaning and wiping, it should hold up. Avoid abrasive cleaners, which can wear the coating.

**Q: Is the waterfall flow adjustable?**
A: The flow rate is fixed by the cartridge, but you can control water volume with the handle. The waterfall effect is consistent across the spout width regardless of handle position.

**Skip it if** you need a dual-handle faucet, prioritize fast basin filling over visual drama, or have a compact vanity where a 15-inch-tall fixture would feel overwhelming.
