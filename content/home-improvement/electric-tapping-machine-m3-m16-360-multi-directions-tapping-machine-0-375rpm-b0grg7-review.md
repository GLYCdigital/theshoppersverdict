The Electric Tapping Machine M3-M16 with its 360° multi-directional arm is the tool that turns a nerve-wracking manual operation into a controlled, repeatable process. ## What You Actually Get

The package includes the tapping head unit, the articulated 360° arm assembly, a mounting clamp, and a set of tap collets covering the M3-M16 range. The motor drives a spindle with a 0-375 RPM variable speed control—that's the sweet spot for tapping, not drilling. The reverse function is automatic: you set your depth, the machine backs the tap out when it hits the stop. No more reversing by feel, no more snapped taps from hesitation.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature—that 360° multi-directional arm—isn't marketing fluff. As shown in the product image, the articulated arm locks at virtually any angle, which means you can tap the side of a vertical frame, the underside of a bracket, or an awkward corner without flipping the entire workpiece. For anyone who's contorted around a milling table with a hand tapper, this is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade.

The variable speed control is the other star. At lower RPMs, you get the feel and control of hand tapping but with mechanical consistency. The depth stop is precise, and the auto-reverse triggers reliably. One reviewer noted, "The threads come out clean every time. No more cussing when a tap breaks on the third hole." That sentiment echoes across the ratings—tap breakage is the #1 reason people buy this, and it delivers.

## Where It Falls Short

The 360° arm is only as good as its anchor. If you clamp this to a flimsy workbench, the leverage amplifies every wobble. You'll want a solid steel bench or the optional floor stand. That's not a flaw in the machine, but it's an added cost and consideration.

The collet system is functional but not fast. Switching from an M6 to an M10 tap means loosening, swapping, and re-tightening—a few minutes of work. If you're constantly changing tap sizes, that time adds up. Also, at $389, this is squarely aimed at working professionals and serious hobbyists. If you tap a handful of holes a year, you're better off with a manual tap wrench.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Fabricators and machinists** who tap dozens of holes daily across multiple materials
- **Maintenance shops** that need to tap bolts in place on equipment without disassembly
- **Advanced DIYers** building projects with thick aluminum or steel where broken taps are costly

Skip it if you're a weekend warrior tapping occasional M5 holes in soft metal. A quality hand tapper and patience will serve you fine.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this tap stainless steel?**
A: Yes, but use cutting fluid and run at lower RPMs. It handles M3-M16 in stainless, though M14-M16 will test the motor's torque. The auto-reverse helps prevent the tap from seizing.

**Q: Does it work horizontally or upside down?**
A: The 360° arm locks at any angle, including horizontal and inverted positions. Gravity doesn't affect the spindle's operation, though you'll want to ensure the arm is fully tightened before starting.

**Q: What's the actual difference between this and a drill with a tapping attachment?**
A: A drill spins at thousands of RPM and has no depth control. This machine runs at 0-375 RPM with a depth stop and auto-reverse. Those three features are the difference between clean threads and broken taps.
