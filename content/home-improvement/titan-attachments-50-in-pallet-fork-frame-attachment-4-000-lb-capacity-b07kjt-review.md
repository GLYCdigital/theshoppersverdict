If you own a compact tractor or skid steer and you're still moving pallets with a bucket and a prayer, this Titan 50-inch fork frame is the upgrade that pays for itself in a weekend. At 4,000 pounds of rated capacity, it's not the heaviest fork frame on the market — but for the price, it's the one most homeowners and small-acreage operators should buy first.

**What's actually in the crate**

You get the welded frame assembly with two 42-inch forks, the Class 1/2 quick-attach plate, and the hardware to bolt the forks in place. The frame itself is 50 inches wide, which suits sub-compact and compact tractors — think Kubota BX and L series, John Deere 1 and 2 series, or any skid steer with a universal quick attach. The forks slide on the frame and lock with pins; you can adjust their spacing to handle anything from a narrow pallet to a bulky implement crate.

The steel is genuine. The frame uses heavy-wall square tubing, and the fork tines are 3.5 inches wide with a forged tip profile. That taper matters more than most shoppers realize — flat-stock forks with squared-off tips make pallet entry a fight. These slide in cleanly, even on pallets that have taken a beating.

**Real-world performance**

The 4,000-pound rating assumes the load is centered and you're lifting with the loader's capacity in mind. If your tractor's loader maxes out at 1,500 pounds, that's your real limit — the frame isn't the weak link. Buyers running these on full-size skid steers report moving logs, seed bags, and equipment skids without flex in the frame. One reviewer who runs a landscaping crew noted, "I've had this on my skid steer for two seasons. We've moved 2,000-pound granite slabs and it hasn't blinked."

The quick-attach plate is the standout feature. It's a true universal mount, so you'll swap between bucket and forks in under a minute without tools. If you've ever wrestled with pin-on attachments, you'll understand why this is worth the money alone.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme across 229 ratings at 4.6 stars is value. Shoppers repeatedly say the same thing: it's half the price of the dealer-brand equivalent and does the same job. One verified buyer put it bluntly: "My Kubota dealer wanted $1,600 for the same setup. This was $600 and I can't tell the difference." **Where it falls short**

The paint is cosmetic. Expect surface rust within months if you store it outside. Hit the frame with a wire brush and a coat of rust-inhibitive primer before first use — it's a 30-minute job that saves you from ugly scaling later.

There are no chain hooks or tie-down points welded on. If you want to lift engine blocks or hang slings, you'll need to weld your own D-rings onto the frame. Not a dealbreaker for most, but worth knowing before you plan a lift that needs rigging.

Finally, the 42-inch forks are the only length offered in this package. If you regularly move 48-inch pallets with overhang, you may want longer tines — but for standard 40x48 pallets, these are the right depth.

**Who should buy this**

- **Hobby farmers and homesteaders** moving feed, hay, and equipment pallets on a compact tractor — this is your sweet spot.
- **Landscapers and small contractors** running a skid steer who need a second fork frame they can leave on a dedicated machine.

**Skip it if** you need a 5,000+ pound capacity for heavy commercial lifting, or if you need fork lengths beyond 42 inches.

Yes. The Class 1/2 universal quick-attach plate fits the standard JD quick-attach system on 1 and 2 series tractors. No adapter needed.

**Q: Can I leave the forks on while using the bucket?**  
The frame is quick-attach, so you swap between forks and bucket in under a minute. Leaving it on permanently limits your loader's maneuverability, but there's no mechanical reason you can't.

**Q: How much does the frame weigh?**  
Roughly 300 pounds. It's manageable with two people for initial setup, but you'll want the loader or a jack to position it for mounting the first time.
