You're about to lay tile, tap together dovetail joints, or persuade a stubborn metal stamping into place. You grab a hammer — and immediately regret it when the head bounces off the surface and the impact rattles up your wrist. That's the problem the Goldblatt 16 oz Low Recoil Rubber Mallet solves. It's a dead-blow hammer, and it does exactly what the name promises.

## What's in the box

You get the mallet itself — a 13-inch fiberglass handle with a cushioned, textured grip — plus two sets of rubber faces. The standard face is mounted; a firmer spare is included in the package. The head is solid steel underneath, filled with steel shot that redistributes on impact. That's the dead-blow mechanism: instead of bouncing back, the shot absorbs the kinetic energy and transfers it into the work surface.

## Real-world performance

I've used this malllet for three things: seating ceramic tile, persuading a tight-fitting drawer slide, and tapping a chisel through thin-set mortar. The dead-blow action is noticeable immediately. There's no rebound — the head lands and stays. That makes it far more precise than a standard rubber mallet, because you're not fighting the bounce on every strike.

The fiberglass handle is a genuine upgrade over the wooden handles on cheaper mallets. It's stiff, so you get good energy transfer, but the grip absorbs the mild vibration that does make it through. At 16 oz, it's light enough for controlled, repetitive work. You can swing it all afternoon on a tile job without your forearm screaming.

## Where it falls short

Honest talk: 16 oz is a light mallet. If you're breaking up a concrete slab, removing thick porcelain tile, or driving anything that needs real mass behind the strike, this isn't your tool. It's designed for controlled, moderate impact — not demolition.

The rubber faces are also consumables. If you're striking abrasive surfaces daily, they'll wear down. The spare helps, but replacements aren't as widely stocked at big-box hardware stores as you'd like. Check Amazon before you need them, because you will eventually.
