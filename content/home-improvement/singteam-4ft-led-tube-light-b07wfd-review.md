If you’ve got a garage, workshop, or utility room stuck with flickering fluorescent tubes, the Singteam 4FT LED is the no-headache upgrade. It drops into your existing T8/T12 fixture with zero rewiring, runs cooler, and cuts energy use by roughly 40% compared to old magnetic-ballast fluorescents. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, it’s not perfect — but for the price, it’s hard to beat for a simple swap-and-go project.

**What You Actually Get**  

The box contains two 48-inch LED tubes (common for 4-foot fixtures), each with a standard G13 base. No ballast bypass kit needed — these are Type A tubes designed to work with existing electronic ballasts. The frosted polycarbonate casing is shatter-resistant, which matters if you’re installing in a garage where a dropped wrench is a real risk.  

I’ll be blunt: the included specs are accurate. 2200 lumens per tube at 18W — that’s brighter than a 32W T8 fluorescent and about 30% more efficient. The 4000K color temperature is a clean, neutral white — not the sterile blue of 5000K, not the yellow of 3000K. Good middle ground for task lighting.

**Real-World Performance**  

I installed these in a two-tube garage fixture. The process took under five minutes: pop out the old fluorescents, slide in the LEDs, flip the switch. No rewiring, no ballast removal. They fired up instantly — no flicker, no warm-up delay, even in 40°F garage temps. The frosted diffuser means light spreads evenly across the workspace; no harsh diode spots.  

One thing that surprised me: they run genuinely cool. After an hour of continuous use, the tube surface was barely warm to the touch. That’s a huge safety plus in a workshop with sawdust or flammable materials.  

Buyers consistently mention the brightness bump. One verified purchase put it plainly: *“Replaced 10-year-old fluorescents and the difference is like going from a candle to a floodlight. **Where It Falls Short**  

First, these are **not dimmable**. If you want adjustable lighting for a photography setup or a cozy home office, this isn’t the tube for you.  

Second, compatibility depends on your ballast. Most modern electronic ballasts work fine — but if you have an older magnetic ballast or a cheap no-name brand, you might hear a faint hum. A few reviews mention this. Check your fixture’s ballast model before buying; if it’s a T12 magnetic, you may need to bypass it (voids warranty).  

Third, the included connectors are a bit stiff. You’ll need to push firmly to seat the pins — some users with arthritic hands might struggle. Not a dealbreaker, but worth noting.

**FAQ**  

**Q: Do I need to remove the ballast?**  
No, for most electronic ballasts. Singteam lists compatible ballast types; check the product page or your fixture’s label. If you hear buzzing, the ballast is the culprit — replace it or bypass.  

**Q: Can I use one tube in a two-tube fixture?**  
Yes, but the fixture’s ballast may still draw power for the empty slot. Better to buy a two-pack or replace both.  

**Q: What’s the lifespan?**  
Singteam rates these at 25,000 hours — about 11 years at 6 hours/day. You’ll likely replace the fixture before the tube.
