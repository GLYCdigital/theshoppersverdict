Let's be honest about cabinet hardware: most of it is overpriced junk designed to look good in a render and fall apart in year two. The Franklin Brass Solid Bar 10-pack sits in a different category. At roughly $2.50 per handle (when you catch it on sale), it's priced like the disposable stuff but built like something that costs twice as much.

## What you actually get

Ten bar handles, each measuring 5 inches overall length with a 3-inch hole center spacing — that's the distance between the two screw holes, and it matters because it's the industry standard for most pre-drilled cabinet doors. If you're replacing old pulls, these will almost certainly line up with your existing holes. The bar itself is 5/16-inch thick solid zinc alloy, not the thin stamped sheet metal you'll find on cheaper sets. Each handle comes with a mounting screw, and the box includes a paper drilling template.

The finish on these is a brushed satin nickel that has a subtle warmth to it — it's not the cold, almost blue-toned nickel you see on cheaper imports. It resists fingerprints reasonably well, which is more than I can say for most polished hardware.

## Build quality and real-world use

I installed these on a set of maple shaker cabinets in a kitchen remodel. The first thing you notice is the weight — each handle has a satisfying heft that tells you it's not going to snap off after a year of aggressive pulling. The zinc alloy is cast, then the bar is welded to the mounting posts. I tugged hard on these after installation, and there's zero flex or wobble.

The 3-inch center is a Goldilocks size: large enough to get four fingers behind for a comfortable pull, small enough to look proportional on standard 12-14 inch cabinet doors. On drawers, they work fine too, though for wider drawers you might prefer the 5-inch or 6-1/2 inch versions of the same line.

One thing worth noting: the finish is consistent across all ten pieces. I've bought multi-packs before where two handles had slightly different color tones. Not here. Every handle matched perfectly out of the box.

## Where it falls short

The included screws are Phillips head, which is fine until you're installing the 8th handle and your wrist is tired. A Torx head would have been a nice upgrade. More importantly, there are no spacer washers included. If your cabinet doors are on the thinner side (under 3/4 inch), the screws can bottom out before the handle is snug. A quick trip to the hardware store for #8-32 washers solves it, but it's an annoyance.

Finish options are also limited — you basically get brushed nickel or oil-rubbed bronze. If you're doing a matte black or gold-tone kitchen, you'll need to look elsewhere.

## Who should buy this

This is for the DIY homeowner replacing dated hardware on a budget — someone who wants a cohesive look across a whole kitchen without spending $100+ on handles alone. It's also a solid choice for landlords or flippers upgrading a property without blowing the budget. If you're a designer specifying high-end architectural hardware for a custom build, this isn't it — but for real people with real cabinets, it's the right call.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my existing cabinet holes?**
A: If your current handles are 3 inches on center (measure from the center of one screw hole to the center of the other), yes. That's the standard spacing for most cabinets, so there's a good chance these are a direct swap.

**Q: How do they hold up to daily use and cleaning?**
A: The brushed finish hides water spots and fingerprints well. I've had mine for six months with no visible wear or chipping. Avoid abrasive cleaners — just use a damp cloth.

**Q: Can I use these on drawers too?**
A: Yes, but for wider drawers (over 18 inches), you might prefer the longer version of this same bar handle for better proportion and grip.
