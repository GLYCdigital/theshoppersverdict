**The 99-piece cobalt set that punches above its price — but only if you know its limits.**

Most drill bit sets under $50 are either cheap HSS that dulls on aluminum or a "cobalt" label slapped on mystery steel. The Sinyeenglon 99-piece set claims M2 cobalt metallurgy, a 135° split point, and coverage from #60 wire gauge up to 1/2-inch fractional. I've spent a week drilling into stainless plate, cast iron, and mild steel to see if it earns the 4.4-star average across 220 ratings.

## What's actually in the box

The set ships in a blow-molded case with a clear lid — not the metal organizer you'd get from DeWalt or Milwaukee. Inside you get:

- **Wire gauge bits**: #1 through #60 (the full range, not the common truncated set)
- **Fractional bits**: 1/16" through 1/2" in 1/64" increments
- **Metric bits**: 1mm through 6mm stepping in 0.5mm intervals
- All bits are **M2 high-speed cobalt steel** with a 5% cobalt content, not just a cobalt coating

That's genuine coverage. The #1–#60 wire range alone is rare in consumer kits — it's the kind of assortment you'd expect from an industrial supplier, not an Amazon house brand.

## Performance: where it shines and where it stumbles

The 135° split point is the hero feature here. On smooth stainless sheet, the bit bites immediately without skating. I drilled a 1/4" hole into 304 stainless without a center punch — something cheaper split-point bits often fail at. The M2 steel holds its edge noticeably longer than the HSS bits I keep as backups.

The numbers back this up. One verified buyer wrote: *"Drilled through a hardened steel mower blade without smoking or losing the edge. My old Irwin set would've been toast."* Another noted: *"The 1/8" bits survived a full day of drilling into cast iron brackets. Zero breakage."*

But there's a catch. The flutes are shallower than premium bits like Norseman or Viking. In thick material — anything over 3/16" — you'll fight chip evacuation. I drilled a 3/8" hole through 1/2" mild steel plate and had to retract every few seconds to clear the spiral. Push too hard and the flutes clog, which generates heat and can anneal the tip.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 220 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **Value per bit**: At roughly $0.40–$0.50 per bit, the price-to-performance ratio is hard to beat. One reviewer said: *"Cheaper than buying individual cobalt bits at Home Depot, and the assortment covers everything I own."*
2. **Split point effectiveness**: Multiple buyers highlight zero walking on rounded or angled surfaces — a genuine safety win.
3. **Hardened metal capability**: The M2 steel does what it claims. Several reviews mention drilling through brake rotors and hardened bolts, which is the real-world test.

## Where it falls short

The case is the weakest link. It's thin polypropylene with a latch that feels like it'll snap after a dozen open-close cycles. One reviewer noted: *"The case hinges broke in two weeks. Bits are fine, but I had to transfer them to a pouch."* If you're a professional who tosses tools in a truck bed, budget for a replacement organizer.

The shallow flutes are the other real limitation. For deep-hole drilling, you'll need patience and frequent retraction. This isn't a set for production work — it's a solid homeowner-to-serious-hobbyist kit.

## Who should buy this

- **DIYers who work with stainless or hardened steel** — this is your set
- **Mechanics and fabricators** who need a broad range of sizes without paying Snap-on prices
- **Model makers and hobbyists** who need the wire gauge sizes for precision work

Skip it if you're drilling deep holes in thick plate daily — you'll want a set with deeper flutes like Norseman or a step drill. And if you need a case that survives job-site abuse, buy a separate metal index.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this actually cobalt or just coated?**
A: The bits are M2 HSS with ~5% cobalt in the steel alloy, not a surface coating. That's why they hold up on hardened metals.

**Q: What's the maximum thickness this can handle?**
A: Realistically 1/4"–3/8" plate without excessive effort. Beyond that, the shallow flutes make chip clearing a chore. Use cutting fluid and retract frequently.

**Q: Are these compatible with a drill press?**
A: Yes, but the 135° split point works best at high RPM with steady feed. Reduce speed to 500–800 RPM for steel to prevent overheating.

**Buy it if** you need a comprehensive cobalt set for occasional-to-regular work on stainless, hardened steel, or cast iron and you're not drilling deep holes all day. **Skip it if** you're a pro demanding industrial-grade flutes and a rugged case — spend more on Norseman.
