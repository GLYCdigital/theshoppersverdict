If you're running Ethernet, coax, or audio cabling and you're tired of those flimsy, off-brand wallplates that crack when you sneeze near them, the Leviton QuickPort Single Gang Wallplate is the quiet upgrade your low-voltage work deserves. It's not flashy, but it's the kind of component that separates a clean installation from a hack job.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the wallplate itself — nothing else. No screws (it uses standard 6-32 wallplate screws, which are typically already on your existing outlets or included with your electrical box), no keystone jacks, no mounting hardware. The plate accepts one QuickPort-style keystone module, which snaps in from the front without tools. As shown in the product image, the face is clean and minimal, with a subtle Leviton logo at the bottom. It's available in multiple colors — white, ivory, and light almond being the common ones — so you can match your existing trim.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

This is where Leviton earns its reputation. The plate is molded from a thick, flame-retardant thermoplastic that doesn't flex or bow when you tighten the screws. The surface has a smooth, satin finish that resists yellowing better than cheaper PVC plates. The keystone opening is precisely machined — your QuickPort jacks seat with a reassuring click, and they stay put. No wobble, no half-seated connectors that eventually push through the front when you plug in a cable.

I installed two of these for a home office network run, pairing them with Leviton's Cat6 QuickPort jacks. The fit into a standard single-gang old-work box was snug but not forced. The screw holes lined up perfectly — no wrestling or re-drilling. The plate sat flush against the drywall with no gaps, even on a slightly uneven surface.

One thing I noticed: the snap-in retention tabs are stiff. On the first jack, I thought I was going to break something. You're not — it just needs a firm, even push. Once seated, the jack is locked in solidly. A small flathead screwdriver can pop it back out if you need to swap connections, which is a nice touch for future changes.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. First, this is a single-gang, single-port plate. If you need two Ethernet drops at one location, you'll need the multiport version — this one only fits one keystone. Second, the stiff snap-in mechanism I mentioned earlier. It's a feature, not a bug, but if you have arthritis or limited hand strength, seating a jack might take a bit of effort. Finally, it costs a couple dollars more than generic no-name plates. You're paying for the precision and durability, and for most installers, it's worth it.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit a standard electrical box?**
Yes. It fits both new-work and old-work single-gang boxes. Standard 6-32 screws are what you'll use, and the screw spacing is the industry-standard 3.25 inches.

**Q: What jacks are compatible?**
It's designed for Leviton QuickPort keystone modules — both the standard line and the QuickPort series. That covers Cat5e, Cat6, Cat6a, coax, HDMI, and audio jacks. Third-party "universal" keystones may fit, but stick with Leviton for a guaranteed flush fit.

**Q: Is it paintable?**
Yes, but lightly. A thin coat of latex paint adheres fine. Avoid heavy coats or oil-based paint, which can fill the snap-in channel and make keystone insertion difficult.
