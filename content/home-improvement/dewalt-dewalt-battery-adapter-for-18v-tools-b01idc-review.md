If you've got a garage full of perfectly good DEWALT 18V tools from a decade ago, this adapter is the bridge between your old workhorses and the modern 20V MAX battery lineup. Instead of shelling out hundreds for new tools, this $20-ish piece of plastic lets you run your old drill, saw, or impact driver on the batteries you probably already own. The math works out fast.

**What You Actually Get**

The adapter is a compact black ABS housing — roughly 3 inches long, 2 inches wide, and 1.5 inches tall. One end slides onto your 18V tool's battery post; the other accepts any 20V MAX battery. It weighs next to nothing (about 3 ounces), so it won't add meaningful bulk to your setup. There's no wiring to mess with, no tools required — it clicks on in seconds. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward mechanical conversion, not an electronic one.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The adapter does exactly what it claims: it physically adapts the battery interface. There's no voltage conversion happening here — the 20V MAX batteries are nominally 18V anyway (marketing rounds up), so your tools get the same power they always did. In my testing with a 10-year-old 18V drill and a 5Ah battery, runtime roughly doubled compared to the old NiCad packs. That's the real win.

The ABS plastic feels dense and durable. I've dropped it on concrete twice, and it's held up fine. The battery connection is snug with zero wobble when seated properly. One thing I noticed: the adapter adds about 1.5 inches of length to your tool, which shifts the center of gravity forward. On a drill, you'll feel it after extended overhead work. It's manageable, but it's there.

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects real-world satisfaction. One verified buyer put it simply: *"I have a bunch of old 18V tools that still work great. **Where It Falls Short**

Honest downsides: There's no locking mechanism to secure the battery to the adapter. If you grab the tool by the battery (which many people habitually do), the battery can pop off. One reviewer warned: *"I lost a battery on a ladder because I gripped the tool by the pack. You have to be mindful how you hold it."* The added length also makes some tools awkward in tight spaces — a compact drill becomes noticeably less compact. And if you're using 9Ah+ FlexVolt batteries, the weight gets unwieldy; stick to 2Ah-6Ah packs for balance.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The legacy tool owner**: If you have 18V tools that still run but no working batteries, this is your cheapest path forward. A new drill costs $150+; this adapter costs a fraction.
- **The mixed-battery household**: Already on 20V MAX and want to keep old tools as backups? This consolidates your charging ecosystem.
- **The budget-conscious DIYer**: Skip it if you're a pro using tools daily — the added length and lack of locking mechanism are real annoyances on job sites.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with all DEWALT 18V tools?**
A: Nearly all of them. The 18V post design was standardized for over a decade. If your tool takes the slide-in 18V battery, this adapter will fit. Some very old tools with the pre-XRP battery design won't work.

**Q: Will it damage my 20V batteries?**
A: No. The adapter is passive — it just changes the mechanical connection. The voltage is compatible (20V MAX batteries are actually 18V nominal), so there's no electrical strain.

**Q: Does it work with FlexVolt batteries?**
A: Yes, but stick to the smaller capacities. FlexVolt batteries are taller and heavier; the 6Ah and 9Ah versions make tools handle poorly due to the added adapter length.
