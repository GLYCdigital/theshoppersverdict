# Kroias Standing Bathtub Faucet Review: A Tall Statement Piece That Delivers

A freestanding tub filler is the chandelier of a master bathroom. It’s dramatic, functional, and impossible to ignore. The Kroias Standing Bathtub Faucet makes a strong case for being that centerpiece—but before you hand over your credit card, you need to know what you’re really installing.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the floor-mounted tub filler, a single-handle lever control, and the mounting hardware. As shown in the product image, this is a tall unit—roughly 40 inches from floor to spout—designed to arc over the edge of a freestanding soaking tub. The body is brass with a brushed nickel finish, and it feels substantial in hand, not hollow or cheap.

There’s no handheld sprayer, no diverter valve, no extra trim kit. This is a bare-bones, high-pressure tub filler. If you want a shower attachment, keep shopping. If you want a clean, minimalist floor-mounted filler, this is the entire package.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Installation is the biggest hurdle, and it’s not for the faint of heart. This faucet requires a floor rough-in with a dedicated supply line. That means you’re cutting into your subfloor or concrete slab. If you’re renovating, this is a non-issue. If you’re swapping out an existing wall-mounted faucet, this is a project, not a swap.

Once it’s in, the performance is where this faucet earns its 4.6-star rating. The single-handle lever is smooth and precise—you can dial in a slow trickle or crank it to full flow without guessing. The tall neck clears the tub rim by a comfortable margin, which means no more hunching over to fill a deep soaker tub. The flow rate is strong, and the aerated stream keeps splashing to a minimum.

The brushed nickel finish is the quiet standout. It doesn’t show fingerprints the way chrome does, and water beads and runs off rather than leaving hard-water spots. After a few weeks of daily use, it still looks like it did on day one.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The reviews at 4.6 out of 5 stars (229 ratings) consistently hit the same notes. One buyer noted, “The height is perfect—I don’t have to bend down to fill my tub anymore.” Another called out the build quality: “Solid brass, heavy base plate. This isn’t a cheap import that wobbles.”

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the single-handle lever is functional but basic. There’s no integrated diverter, no temperature memory, and no pressure-balancing valve built in. You’ll need to add a mixing valve in the wall if you want anti-scald protection, which is an extra cost and an extra plumbing step.

Second, the base plate is wide—about 8 inches across. On a small bathroom floor, that footprint can feel bulky. It’s also a dust trap; the crevice under the base needs occasional cleaning.

Third, this faucet is tall. That’s the point, but if your tub sits close to a window ledge or a shelf, the arc of the spout might interfere with clearance. Measure twice, buy once.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You’re doing a full bathroom remodel with a freestanding tub, you want a statement piece that doesn’t require a wall for mounting, and you’re prepared for a floor rough-in.

**Skip it if:** You’re looking for a quick swap of an existing faucet, you want a handheld sprayer for rinsing the tub, or you’re on a tight budget that doesn’t include professional plumbing help.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this faucet work with any freestanding tub?**
A: It works with any tub you can position within reach of the spout. The 40-inch height clears standard tub rims, but deep soaking tubs with tall sides may require you to position the faucet slightly farther back.

**Q: Is the finish durable?**
A: Yes. The brushed nickel is a PVD-style finish that resists corrosion and tarnishing. It’s not prone to chipping, but avoid abrasive cleaners—stick to a soft cloth and mild soap.

**Q: Do I need a special valve for this faucet?**
A: You need a standard floor-mount rough-in kit (often sold separately). For hot water safety, install a pressure-balancing or thermostatic mixing valve in the wall before the floor valve. This is not included.
