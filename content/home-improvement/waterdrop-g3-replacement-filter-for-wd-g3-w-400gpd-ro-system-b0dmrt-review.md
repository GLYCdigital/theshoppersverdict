## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains a single integrated filter cartridge — the G3's clever design combines sediment, carbon block, and RO membrane into one twist-in unit. That's the tradeoff Waterdrop made: you sacrifice the granular replacement of traditional 3-stage systems for a 10-second swap that doesn't require a plumber's license.

As shown in the product image, the filter has a chunky plastic housing with a threaded collar at the base. Installation is literally twist-off, twist-on. No leak-check anxiety, no o-rings to misalign.

## Real-World Performance

The G3's 400GPD rating means it processes about 0.28 gallons per minute — enough for a family of four with a 2:1 pure-to-drain ratio. That's not the most efficient ratio (some competitors hit 1.5:1), but it keeps the filter compact.

The critical spec here is what happens as the filter ages. Waterdrop rates this at 24 months or 2,200 gallons, whichever comes first. In practical terms, you'll notice TDS creep around month 18-20 if you have hard water. One verified buyer noted:

> "Replaced the original after 22 months. TDS was creeping up to 35 ppm from the tap's 280. New filter dropped it back to 5 ppm immediately."

That matches what I'd expect. The RO membrane holds up well, but the carbon block in the integrated cartridge is the first to exhaust. If you have chloramine in your water supply (common in municipal systems), plan on replacing at the 18-month mark rather than pushing to 24.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**Cost:** At $60-70 per cartridge, you're looking at $120-140 annually. Generic alternatives exist, but they're a gamble. I've seen reports of knockoff filters leaking at the collar joint or clogging within six months. The $10-15 savings isn't worth a flooded cabinet.

**Compatibility:** This filter ONLY fits the G3 and G3P800 models. If you own a G2 or G3 600GPD, this won't work. Double-check your model number before ordering.

**Flow rate drop:** Expect noticeable slowdown as the filter approaches end-of-life. If you're filling a 32-ounce bottle and it takes 90 seconds instead of 45, that's your signal.
