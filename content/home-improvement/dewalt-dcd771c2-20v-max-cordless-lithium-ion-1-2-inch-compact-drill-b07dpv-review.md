The Dewalt DCD771C2 is the drill that shows up on nearly every "best cordless drill for homeowners" list, and with a 4.8-star average across 240+ Amazon ratings, it's earned that spot. But is it the right drill for *you*? Let's cut through the hype and look at what this compact 20V MAX actually delivers.

**What's in the Box**

You get the DCD771C2 drill/driver, two 20V MAX lithium-ion batteries (the compact 1.3Ah size), a charger, a belt hook, and a rigid carry bag. The inclusion of two batteries is the real story here — many competitors at this price point only ship one. Having a spare means you can keep working while one charges, which is a genuine productivity boost for weekend projects.

The drill itself is compact. At just 6.3 inches from nose to tail, it's one of the shortest in Dewalt's lineup. That matters when you're driving screws into tight cabinet corners or working between joists. As one Amazon reviewer put it, "This thing fits where my old drill couldn't even get close."

**Performance and Build Quality**

The DCD771C2 features a two-speed gearbox: 0-450 RPM for high-torque screw driving and 0-1,500 RPM for drilling. The high-speed setting chews through wood and drywall with ease, and the 300 unit watts out (UWO) of power — roughly equivalent to 30-35 ft-lbs of torque — is plenty for driving 3-inch deck screws and boring holes up to 1/2-inch in wood.

The 1/2-inch ratcheting chuck grips bits securely, and the keyless design means bit changes take seconds. The rubber overmold on the handle provides a comfortable, non-slip grip even with sweaty hands on a hot jobsite.

A note on the motor: this is a brushed motor, not brushless. That's the biggest technical compromise. Brushless motors are more efficient and last longer, but they cost significantly more. For a homeowner tackling weekend projects, the brushed motor here is perfectly adequate. For a contractor running the drill all day every day, you'd want to step up to a brushless model.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the reviews, a few themes jump out. First: reliability. Multiple buyers mention owning the drill for years without issues. One reviewer noted, "I've had this drill for three years of serious DIY work — built a deck, a shed, and countless furniture projects. Still runs like day one."

Second: the value proposition. Buyers repeatedly call out the two-battery kit as the deciding factor. "Bought this over a more expensive brushless model because the extra battery made it a better deal," one wrote.

Third: the balance and weight. At 3.6 pounds with the battery attached, it's light enough for overhead work without fatiguing your arm.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the drawbacks. The 1.3Ah batteries are the compact size, which means they run out faster than the larger 2.0Ah or 5.0Ah packs. If you're doing a full day of heavy drilling, you'll be swapping batteries frequently. The charger is also a standard 30-minute unit, not the faster 60-minute or 12-minute rapid chargers.

There's no battery fuel gauge on the tool itself. You won't know the charge level until the drill starts slowing down or the battery dies. It's a minor annoyance, but a real one.

And as mentioned, the brushed motor won't match the runtime or power of a brushless model like the Dewalt DCD791 or DCD805. If you're a pro or a serious hobbyist pushing the tool daily, the premium is worth it.

**Who Should Buy This**

This drill is ideal for:

- **First-time homeowners** who need one reliable tool for furniture assembly, curtain rods, and basic repairs
- **DIYers** tackling weekend projects like building shelves, repairing fences, or installing drywall
- **Gift-givers** who want a solid, brand-name tool kit without spending $200+

**Skip it if** you're a contractor or heavy user who needs maximum runtime, brushless efficiency, or a hammer drill function for masonry. Also skip it if you need a drill with a battery charge indicator.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this drill powerful enough for driving lag bolts or mixing drywall compound?**
A: For occasional lag bolts up to 3/8-inch, yes. For continuous heavy-duty work like mixing mud or drilling through steel, no — you'll want a higher-torque model.

**Q: Do the batteries fit other Dewalt 20V tools?**
A: Yes. The 20V MAX batteries work across the entire Dewalt 20V MAX platform, including circular saws, impact drivers, and reciprocating saws. If you buy into the Dewalt ecosystem, these batteries are versatile.

**Q: How long do the batteries take to charge?**
A: The included charger brings a fully drained 1.3Ah battery to full in about 30 minutes. A practical tip: keep one battery on the charger while using the other, and you'll rarely hit a dead end.
