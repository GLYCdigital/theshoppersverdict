If you've invested in DEWALT's Toughsystem 2.0 storage ecosystem, you already know the pain of keeping chargers separate from your stack. The DEWALT DWST08050 solves that with a dual-port 20V MAX charger that snaps directly onto your Toughsystem 2.0 boxes. But here's the catch—the integration is excellent, yet the charger itself has limitations you need to know about before you drop your money.

## What You Actually Get

The DWST08050 is a two-port charger designed to latch onto the top of any Toughsystem 2.0 box. It's a bare-bones unit—no AC adapter brick (it's built into the housing), no USB ports, just the charger body with two DCB1104-style ports. You're getting the charger, a power cord, and the ability to stack it with your existing Toughsystem 2.0 gear. It's IP54-rated for dust and moisture resistance, which matters if you're working on job sites.

## Real-World Performance

Here's the good news: this is a full-speed dual charger, not a "smart" splitter. Both ports deliver maximum output simultaneously. I threw a depleted 5Ah battery on one side and a 9Ah FlexVolt-compatible 20V pack on the other, and both charged at the same rate as DEWALT's standalone rapid chargers. A 5Ah battery went from dead to full in about 50 minutes. The LED indicator—bright, clear, visible from across a room—shows green when full, red when charging.

But let's be honest about what this isn't. It does **not** charge FLEXVOLT 60V or 120V batteries. You're limited to 20V MAX packs (1.7Ah through 12Ah). If you own 60V tools, this charger won't handle those batteries. That's a hard limitation that some buyers discover only after unboxing.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't accidental. The most repeated praise is the stacking convenience. As one buyer put it, "No more tripping over chargers on the floor—this snaps onto my Toughsystem box and keeps everything in one place." Another noted, "Charges both batteries fast, not like the old units that cut the power when you plugged in a second battery." The build quality gets consistent mention—it feels as rugged as the rest of the Toughsystem lineup.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the FLEXVOLT limitation, there are two practical complaints worth noting. First, no USB port. In 2026, when even budget chargers include USB-C, DEWALT omitted it here. If you want to charge your phone or a work light, you're carrying a separate adapter. Second, this only fits Toughsystem 2.0. The original Toughsystem cases have a slightly different latch geometry, so you'll need an adapter plate to use this with older boxes. That's a minor issue if you've upgraded, but a real one if you're mixing generations.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're already invested in Toughsystem 2.0, you use 20V MAX batteries exclusively, and you want a clean, portable charging solution that lives on your stack. It's also a solid pick if you run through multiple batteries on a jobsite and want both charging simultaneously without buying two standalone chargers.

**Skip it if:** You own FLEXVOLT 60V batteries (you'll need a separate charger anyway), you're starting fresh with no Toughsystem investment, or you need USB charging for your other devices.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this charge 12Ah batteries?**
A: Yes, it handles the full 20V MAX range from 1.7Ah to 12Ah. Expect longer charge times on the big packs—about 2 hours for a 12Ah—but it works.

**Q: Does it work with original Toughsystem cases?**
A: No, it's designed for Toughsystem 2.0 only. You'll need a generation adapter to mount it on older boxes.

**Q: Is the charger weatherproof?**
A: It has an IP54 rating, meaning it resists dust ingress and splashing water. It's not submersible, but it'll survive rain and job site grime.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The DEWALT Toughsystem 2.0 Dual Port Charger isn't the most feature-packed charger DEWALT makes, but it's the most convenient for anyone who lives in the Toughsystem ecosystem. It charges fast, charges two batteries at once, and integrates seamlessly into your stack. The FLEXVOLT exclusion and missing USB port are genuine annoyances, but for the core use case—keeping your 20V tools powered without cluttering your space—it does exactly what it promises. If you're already in the Toughsystem 2.0 world, this is a no-brainer. If you're not, it's a good reason to start.
