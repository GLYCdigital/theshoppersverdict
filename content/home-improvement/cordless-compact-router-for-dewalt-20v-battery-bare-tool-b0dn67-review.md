If you already own DeWalt 20V tools, this compact router is a no-brainer addition to your kit. If you're starting from scratch, read carefully before you commit.

This is a bare tool — no battery, no charger, no case. You're paying for the router head, a fixed base, and two collets (1/4-inch and 6mm). That's it. The plunge base, which you'll want for any mortising or inlay work, is an additional purchase. Budget for that if you need it.

## Performance: Where it earns its keep

The variable speed dial gives you genuine control from 10,000 to 30,000 RPM. I tested it against a corded trim router on oak and hard maple, and the speed held steady even when taking aggressive passes. The soft-start feature prevents the kickback you'd expect from a tool this light — it ramps up smoothly rather than jerking in your hand.

The motor delivers enough torque for 1/4-inch shank bits in hardwood, but don't expect plunge-router performance. This is a trim router. It excels at:

- Flush-trimming laminate edges
- Rounding over edges with a 1/4-inch radius bit
- Light hinge mortising (with the plunge base)
- Cutting dovetail sockets in softwood (with a jig)

The LED ring is genuinely useful. It throws light right where the bit meets the material, which eliminates the guesswork when you're working in a dim garage or under a cabinet.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.1-star average across 204 ratings tells a story of solid performance with some caveats. The most consistent praise centers on the DeWalt battery compatibility:

> "Finally a router that uses my existing 20V batteries. I've got six of them already, so this was just the cost of the tool itself."

Multiple reviewers mention the runtime as a pleasant surprise. With a 5Ah battery, you'll get roughly 45 minutes of continuous routing. With a 2Ah battery, expect closer to 20 minutes of intermittent work — enough for a few edge treatments or a handful of hinge mortises.

The ergonomics get frequent mention too. The body is 8 inches tall and weighs just 3.4 pounds with a battery attached. It's balanced well enough for one-handed operation on vertical surfaces, though two hands are safer for anything more demanding than a light trim.

## Where it falls short

The lack of a dust collection port on the standard base is the most common complaint. You'll be sweeping up shavings after every session. The plunge base (sold separately) does include a dust port, so that's the workaround if you need one.

The collet tightening requires a wrench — there's no one-touch release. It's a minor inconvenience, but it means you can't swap bits quickly on a job site without carrying the wrench.

Some users report the depth adjustment dial being stiff out of the box. It loosens up after a few uses, but the first adjustment can feel like you're forcing it.

## Who should buy this

**The DeWalt ecosystem owner** — If you have 20V batteries already, this router costs less than half of a comparable corded model and gives you cordless freedom. You're the primary audience.

**The cabinet installer** — Need to trim laminate edges on site without dragging an extension cord? This is your tool.

**Skip it if** you're starting fresh. Between the bare tool price, two batteries, a charger, and a plunge base, you're looking at over $300 for a full setup. At that price, a corded trim router plus a quality extension cord is the smarter buy.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this accept standard DeWalt 20V batteries?**
A: Yes, all DeWalt 20V MAX batteries work, including the new compact and flexvolt-adaptable versions. The battery slides in from the bottom and locks securely.

**Q: Can I use it for dovetail joints?**
A: With the plunge base and a dovetail jig, yes — but only in softwoods like pine or poplar. The motor doesn't have the torque for dovetails in oak or maple without multiple passes.

**Q: What's the maximum bit shank size?**
A: 1/4-inch. The included collets are 1/4-inch and 6mm, so you're limited to trim-router bits. No 1/2-inch shank bits will fit.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're already invested in the DeWalt 20V system and need a capable trim router for edge work, laminate trimming, and light mortising. The speed control, LED lighting, and battery compatibility justify the price.

**Skip it if** you don't own DeWalt batteries, or if you need a plunge router for heavy-duty joinery. This is a trim router designed for finishing work, not a replacement for a full-size router.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, capable tool that's held back by the bare-tool pricing structure and the separate plunge base purchase. For existing DeWalt owners, it's a worthwhile addition. For everyone else, do the math on the total system cost first.
