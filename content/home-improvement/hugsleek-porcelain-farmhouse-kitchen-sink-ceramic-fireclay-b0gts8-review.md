# Hugsleek Porcelain Farmhouse Kitchen Sink Review

Most farmhouse sinks under $500 are a compromise. They're either enameled steel that dings if you look at it wrong, or composite granite that stains from tomato sauce. The Hugsleek Porcelain Farmhouse Sink takes a different route — it's genuine fireclay, the same material used in commercial kitchens, fired at over 2,000°F to fuse the glaze into the ceramic body. That's not marketing talk; that's why this sink has held up for buyers who actually cook.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the sink itself — no faucet, no drain assembly, no mounting hardware. You're paying for the ceramic piece and nothing else. The unit measures 33 inches wide, 18 inches front-to-back, with a 10-inch basin depth. The fireclay body is roughly 0.75 inches thick at the rim, which gives it that substantial, solid feel you expect from a farmhouse sink.

One thing to note before ordering: there are no pre-drilled faucet holes. The sink is designed for a wall-mount or deck-mount faucet. If you're replacing a standard 4-hole sink setup, factor in the cost of a new faucet and potentially a plumber to reconfigure the supply lines.

## Real-World Performance

The depth is the standout feature. At 10 inches, you can submerge a full stockpot or a 9x13 baking sheet flat without water sloshing over the rim. The sloped basin floor is subtle but effective — water channels toward the drain without pooling in corners, which is a common complaint with cheaper ceramic sinks.

The fireclay glaze is genuinely tough. One reviewer noted, "I've dropped ceramic plates and a glass measuring cup in this sink, and the sink came out unscathed every time." That matches the material's reputation. The surface resists scratching from steel wool and doesn't absorb stains from coffee or beet juice. A quick wipe with a non-abrasive cleaner brings it back to bright white.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the weight. At roughly 85 pounds, this sink requires cabinet reinforcement. The manufacturer recommends adding support brackets or a plywood base under the sink area, and you'll want a second person for lifting during install. This isn't a one-person Saturday project unless you're experienced with under-mount installation.

The impact risk is real. Fireclay handles scratches and heat beautifully, but it's brittle. One buyer reported, "I dropped a cast-iron skillet from about six inches and it put a chip in the rim." That's a rare failure, but it's worth knowing before you commit.

Also note: the 60/40 split means the smaller basin is about 14 inches wide. It's fine for rinsing produce or washing a few dishes, but you won't fit a full sheet pan in it.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're remodeling a farmhouse-style kitchen, you cook with heavy pots and need a deep basin, or you want a sink that looks premium without the $1,000+ price tag of designer fireclay brands.

**Skip it if** you're on a tight timeline (the weight and install complexity add labor time), you regularly drop heavy cast iron, or you need a faucet-hole configuration that matches your existing setup without extra work.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the fireclay stain from coffee or red wine?**
No. The glaze is non-porous, so liquids bead up and don't penetrate. Let it sit overnight and it wipes clean.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
If you have under-mount sink experience and a helper, yes. If you're a first-timer, budget for a professional. The weight and the need for precise sealing make DIY risky.

**Q: Does it come with a drain or mounting clips?**
No. You'll need to purchase a drain assembly and clips separately, plus a faucet. Budget an extra $100–$150 for these items.
