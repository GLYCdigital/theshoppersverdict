**The Short Version: A Commercial-Style Workhorse That Demands a Little Effort**

If you've ever watched a restaurant kitchen line and thought, "I want that faucet in my home," the Kraus Artec Pro is exactly what you're picturing. It's the spring-coil, high-arc, professional-grade look that's become the unofficial uniform of serious home cooks. But before you add it to your cart, know this: it's not a five-minute swap. It's a real installation project, and a few quirks mean it's not for everyone.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the faucet body, a two-function spray head, the spring coil assembly, a metal deck plate, mounting hardware, and supply lines. The finish options include stainless steel and matte black — both are spot-welded, not painted, so they hold up better than budget alternatives. As shown in the product image, the spray head has a clean toggle switch on the front, which is your control for switching between a solid stream and the high-pressure rinse mode.

The faucet stands roughly 24 inches tall at the arc, with a 15-inch reach from the base to the spout tip. That's a meaningful number if you have a deep farmhouse sink or routinely fill 16-quart stockpots. The spring coil is genuine stainless steel, not a plastic sleeve over a wire, which is the first thing to check on any faucet in this category.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The stream mode delivers a clean, aerated flow that's quiet and splash-free — good for everyday filling and washing. Flick the toggle and the rinse mode kicks out a pressurized, needle-like spray that blasts stuck-on food off plates and loosens caked residue from sheet pans. This is the feature that makes the Artec Pro worth considering over a standard pull-down faucet.

The build quality is the standout. The body is all metal, the handle is a solid lever with smooth, predictable action, and the spring coil genuinely holds its position without sagging. One buyer noted, "The spray power is incredible — it's like having a pressure washer at the sink." Another said, "The metal feels thick, not cheap like the last faucet I bought." That sentiment tracks with the 4.2-star average across 210 ratings.

**Where It Falls Short**

The installation is the most common complaint. The mounting system uses a threaded shank with a plastic nut that's tight to access in tight under-sink spaces. Several reviewers specifically mentioned the deck plate being "impossible to seat flush" on uneven countertops. If you're not comfortable with plumbing work, budget for a professional install or a lot of patience.

The second issue is the spray head dock. There's no magnetic or positive locking mechanism — the head rests in a cradle and relies on hose tension to stay put. Over time, that tension can relax, and the head can droop. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a noticeable polish miss at this price point.

**Who Should Buy This**

This faucet is for the home cook who actually uses their kitchen — the person who cans tomatoes, brines turkeys, and scrubs cast iron. It's also a strong fit for a garage or workshop sink where you need real pressure to clean equipment. If you have a shallow sink or a standard 8-inch-deep basin, the high arc is wasted on you. And if you're looking for a quick weekend swap with no surprises, this isn't it.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit a standard 3-hole sink?**
A: Yes, the deck plate covers 1-hole and 3-hole configurations. But if your countertop is thicker than 1.5 inches, the mounting hardware gets tight. Measure before you buy.

**Q: How loud is the rinse mode?**
A: The high-pressure spray is audible — it's a "whoosh" that's noticeably louder than a standard pull-down. It's not annoying, but it's not library-quiet either.

**Q: Does the spray head leak at the toggle switch?**
A: Some early reviews mentioned minor drips at the switch. Kraus has since revised the gasket, and recent feedback suggests this is largely resolved. If you get a unit with a drip, the replacement gasket is cheap and easy to swap.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Buy It If You Cook Like You Mean It**

**Buy it if:** You want real commercial spray power, you have a deep sink, and you're prepared for a legitimate installation project.

**Skip it if:** You want a tool-free install, you have a shallow sink, or you need a positive-locking spray head dock.

The Kraus Artec Pro delivers where it counts — spray performance and build quality — and stumbles on installation ergonomics and a minor dock design flaw. For the serious home cook, those tradeoffs are worth it. For everyone else, there are easier, cheaper options.
