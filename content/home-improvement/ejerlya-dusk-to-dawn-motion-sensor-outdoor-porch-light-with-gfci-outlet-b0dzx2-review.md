**The Ejerlya Dusk to Dawn Motion Sensor Outdoor Porch Light** solves a problem you didn't know you had: that dead exterior outlet you've been meaning to install for string lights, a security camera, or a holiday inflatable. This fixture pairs a dual-head motion light with a built-in GFCI receptacle, which sounds gimmicky until you realize how practical it is. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it's clearly working for most buyers. Here's the full breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the assembled fixture, a mounting plate, wire nuts, screws, and a rubber gasket. The unit measures roughly 11 inches wide by 8 inches tall — compact enough for standard porch ceilings but substantial enough to look intentional. Each of the two heads houses a 20W LED (1600 lumens total, 5000K daylight). The GFCI outlet sits between the heads, angled slightly downward to shed rain. You also get a photocell on the front face and a PIR sensor under each lamp.

Installation is straightforward if you're comfortable with basic wiring: black to black, white to white, ground to ground. The GFCI needs its LINE and LOAD terminals respected — if you're feeding additional outlets downstream, read the included diagram carefully. Most buyers report a 30-45 minute install for a straightforward replacement.

## Real-World Performance

The dusk-to-dawn photocell is the standout feature. It triggers reliably at twilight and shuts off at dawn without the flickering that plagues cheap sensors. The motion detection is genuinely useful: each head sweeps 180 degrees, and they operate independently. One reviewer noted, "The left head catches people coming up the walk while the right covers the driveway — no more walking into darkness."

The override function (flip the switch off/on quickly) locks the lights on for the night. That's a thoughtful touch for when you're hosting or moving cars.

Light output is solid for a residential fixture. The 1600-lumen total illuminates a typical 20-foot porch and walkway clearly. Color temperature is a cool daylight — good for security, less cozy for ambiance. If you prefer warm light, this isn't the fixture for you.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **GFCI integration:** "No more ugly separate outlet box on my brick. This looks clean and works perfectly for my Christmas lights."
- **Motion sensitivity:** Multiple buyers mention the sensors catch movement from side angles that their old fixture missed entirely.
- **Build quality:** The aluminum housing feels substantial, and the tempered glass lens resists hazing. One long-term reviewer reported zero issues after 8 months of coastal exposure.

## Where It Falls Short

The motion sensor range is roughly 30 feet — fine for most porches, but if you have a long driveway, you'll need to mount it high and angle the heads down. One buyer complained, "I expected it to catch my car pulling in from the street, but it only triggers when I'm about 15 feet away." That's an expectation issue more than a defect, but worth knowing.

The mounting plate is the weakest link. It's functional but thin-gauge steel that can flex if you over-torque the screws. Use the included gasket and hand-tighten only. Also, the 5000K color temperature is a dealbreaker for anyone wanting warm porch lighting — this is strictly security-grade light.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners with no exterior outlet** near the front door — this solves two problems in one fixture.
- **Security-conscious buyers** who want wide-angle motion coverage without a separate sensor system.
- **DIYers replacing an old porch light** who want an upgrade without calling an electrician for a separate GFCI run.

Skip it if you need warm lighting for ambiance, or if your driveway exceeds 30 feet from the mounting point.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the GFCI outlet work independently from the light?**
A: Yes. The outlet has power whenever the circuit is live, regardless of whether the lights are on. It also has its own test/reset buttons.

**Q: Can I use this with a dimmer switch?**
A: No. This requires a standard on/off switch. A dimmer will cause erratic photocell behavior and may damage the LEDs.

**Q: What's the lifespan of the LED modules?**
A: The manufacturer rates them at 50,000 hours. They're integrated, so when they fail, you replace the whole head assembly — not practical to service.
