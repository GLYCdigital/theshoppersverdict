If you're installing Schluter's Ditra-Heat floor warming system, the RS1 thermostat is the natural brain for the operation. But here's the catch: it's not a standalone purchase. You'll need the separate power module, and that changes the value equation. With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, owners generally love it—but let's dig into what that rating actually means before you commit.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains the RS1 touchscreen thermostat, the floor sensor probe with 10 feet of lead wire, and mounting hardware. That's it. The critical piece—the Ditra-Heat-E-WiFi power module that handles the 120V or 240V connection to your heating cables—ships separately. As shown in the product image, the thermostat itself is a sleek, flush-mount unit with a 3.5-inch touchscreen. It's designed to sit in a standard single-gang electrical box, so retrofits are manageable if you have access to the wall cavity.

**Performance and real-world behavior**

The RS1's standout feature is the integrated floor sensor. Unlike thermostats that only read air temperature, this one measures the actual tile surface temperature. That matters for tile—you want the floor at 85°F, not the room at 72°F. Owners consistently report accurate, stable floor temperatures once calibrated. One reviewer noted, "The floor sensor makes a huge difference—my bathroom tile hits exactly 84°F every morning, and the room doesn't overheat."

The Wi-Fi app works reliably for scheduling. You can set different temperatures for weekdays versus weekends, and the geofencing feature—which drops the temperature when you leave and raises it as you return—is a genuine energy saver. Voice control through Alexa or Google Assistant works, though it's basic: "Set floor to 85" or "Turn off floor heat." No granular room-by-room control, but for a single-zone system, that's fine.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The integration with Ditra-Heat systems is seamless. Since Schluter designs the thermostat to work with their own cables and uncoupling membranes, there's no compatibility guesswork. One long-term owner wrote, "I've had this running for two winters now. Set it once, forgot about it. The floor is warm every morning, and my energy bill went up less than I expected." The touchscreen is also bright and readable, with a clean white-on-black display that looks premium on the wall.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest complaint is the cost of entry. The RS1 alone runs around $200, and the required power module pushes the total past $300. Compare that to a basic programmable thermostat at $50—though that comparison isn't entirely fair since the RS1 does far more. Still, budget-conscious DIYers should price the complete system before committing.

The app also lacks energy tracking. You won't get monthly consumption graphs or cost projections. For a product marketed as "smart," that's a notable gap. And the menu structure—nested screens for scheduling, sensor calibration, and Wi-Fi setup—feels like it was designed by engineers, not UX designers. It works, but it's not intuitive on day one.

**Who should buy this**

This thermostat makes sense for three specific buyers: homeowners installing a new Ditra-Heat system who want integrated control without juggling multiple apps; tech-savvy renovators who'll use geofencing and voice control daily; and anyone who's already invested in the Schluter ecosystem and wants a single-brand solution. Skip it if you're looking for a budget option, or if you only need basic manual control—a simple programmable thermostat will do the job for a fraction of the price.

**FAQ**

*Does the RS1 work with non-Schluter heating cables?*  
No. It's designed exclusively for Schluter's Ditra-Heat cables. If you're using another brand's floor heating, this isn't compatible.

*Can I install this myself, or do I need an electrician?*  
The thermostat is DIY-friendly, but the power module connects to your home's electrical system. Unless you're comfortable with line-voltage wiring, hire a licensed electrician for that part.

*Does it require the WiFi power module, or can I use it as a manual thermostat?*  
You need the power module regardless—it's the relay that switches the heating cables on and off. The RS1 is just the control interface.

**The verdict**

The Ditra-Heat-E-RS1 is a well-executed thermostat for a specific system. It's accurate, reliable, and the app integration works as advertised. The downsides—the mandatory power module cost and lack of energy reporting—keep it from being a slam dunk. But if you're building a Ditra-Heat floor and want smart control, this is the best option in Schluter's lineup.

**Buy it if:** You're installing Ditra-Heat and want precise floor temperature control with app scheduling and geofencing.  
**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget, or you need detailed energy consumption data to justify the investment.
