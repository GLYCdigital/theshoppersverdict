If you're shopping for a touch-free faucet that won't die in a year, the Sloan SF-2150 is the real deal — but it's not a simple plug-and-play upgrade. This is commercial plumbing hardware, and it behaves like it.

Sloan is a name you see in every airport, stadium, and office building restroom in America. The SF-2150 brings that institutional reliability into a package you can actually buy as a consumer. At a 4.1-star average across 204 ratings, it's clearly working for most buyers — but the complaints that do exist are worth understanding before you commit.

## What's in the box

You get the faucet body (brass, chrome or brushed nickel finish), a metal handle, the sensor module, mounting hardware, and a hose. That's it. **No batteries. No AC adapter.** The SF-2150 runs on 4 C-cell batteries (which last 3-5 years in normal use, per Sloan's spec) or you can wire it to a 6V transformer if you prefer hardwired power. Budget for that extra cost upfront.

## Real-world performance

The sensor is the star here. It has an adjustable range from 4 to 9 inches, and the 0.5-second response time is genuinely fast — no more waving your hands around like you're conducting an orchestra. Water temperature mixes through a separate handle, so you set your heat once and the sensor just handles on/off.

The flow rate is 1.5 GPM, which is standard for commercial fixtures. It feels substantial in use — this isn't a flimsy residential unit. The brass body and metal components give it real heft, and it's built to take abuse.

One recurring theme in positive reviews: **"Works perfectly after 6 months of daily use in a rental property — no drips, no sensor issues."** That tracks with what I'd expect from Sloan's engineering.

## Where it struggles

The sensor is both the feature and the flaw. Several buyers report false activations when the basin is shallow or has a reflective surface. One reviewer noted: *"The sensor picks up the sink itself and runs randomly. Had to angle the sensor head down more aggressively to stop it."* That's fixable but annoying.

Installation is the bigger friction point. The standard deck hole requirement is 1-3/8", which is narrower than many residential sinks. If you're retrofitting over an existing faucet hole, you'll likely need Sloan's optional mounting plate. This is not a 20-minute swap for most homeowners.

Battery access is also a minor hassle — you have to pull the entire faucet body from above the counter to replace the C-cells. No under-sink access hatch. Plan for that.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're outfitting a rental property, Airbnb, office, or any space where a faucet needs to survive constant use. The reliability payoff is real. Also good for anyone with mobility issues who needs touch-free operation.

**Skip it if:** You're a DIY homeowner looking for a quick weekend swap on an existing sink. The mounting constraints and separate power purchase make this more hassle than it's worth for a simple residential kitchen or bath upgrade.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this faucet work without electricity during a blackout?**
A: Yes, if you're running on batteries. The optional AC adapter requires power, but the battery configuration keeps the faucet operational during outages.

**Q: What's the actual battery life?**
A: Sloan rates it at 3-5 years with normal commercial use. Several reviewers confirm 2+ years of daily use without replacement. The sensor's "low power" mode extends battery life significantly.

**Q: Will this fit my standard kitchen sink?**
A: Probably not without the mounting plate. The SF-2150 expects a 1-3/8" hole. Most residential sinks have a larger 1-1/2" to 1-3/4" opening. Measure before you buy.
