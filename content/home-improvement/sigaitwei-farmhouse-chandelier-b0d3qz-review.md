The farmhouse aesthetic has a dirty secret: half the "rustic" chandeliers on Amazon are cheap metal shells with plastic faux-wood accents that look great in photos and awful in person. The Sigaitwei Farmhouse Chandelier at $80.89 isn't that. It's a legitimately solid, all-metal fixture that earns its 4.7-star rating — but it's not without tradeoffs. Here's what 235 buyers and I want you to know before you click buy.

**What's in the box**

You get the assembled chandelier frame, six candle sleeves, a mounting canopy, downrods in three lengths (8", 12", and 16"), and all mounting hardware. The packaging is genuinely protective — multiple buyers noted it arrived without dents or scratches, which matters for a black matte finish. The frame measures 31.5 inches wide and 9.8 inches tall, with the hanging height adjustable from 18 to 48 inches. That's enough range to clear a standard 8-foot dining table or drop it lower over a kitchen island.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

This is where the Sigaitwei separates itself from the sub-$60 competition. The frame is welded — not screwed together at stress points. The metal is a legit 1.5mm gauge, not the flimsy stamped tin you'll find on cheaper fixtures. One buyer put it plainly: *"I've installed three chandeliers in the last two years. This one felt the most substantial by a wide margin."*

The six E12 candelabra sockets are ceramic, not the cheap plastic bases that crack when you tighten bulbs. Each socket has a 40-watt max rating, so you can run 60-watt-equivalent LEDs without worry. The light spread is genuinely 360 degrees — no dark spots on one side, which is a common complaint with cheaper multi-arm fixtures.

Installation takes about 45 minutes if you've wired a fixture before. The canopy mounts flush to the ceiling with a standard 4-inch base, and the included mounting plate accommodates both round and octagonal junction boxes. If you're a first-timer, the instructions are clear enough, but you'll want a helper — the frame weighs about 14 pounds, and holding it while connecting wires is a two-person job.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The aesthetic is the headline. The matte black finish with the curved candle arms hits the farmhouse look without veering into "theme restaurant" territory. As one reviewer noted: *"I was worried it would look too rustic. It actually looks clean and modern — the black finish tones down the farmhouse vibe."*

The height adjustability gets repeated shout-outs. Multiple buyers mentioned using it over dining tables with 9-foot ceilings and over stairwells with 12-foot ceilings using the included downrods. The 48-inch max drop covers most residential scenarios without needing to buy extension rods.

**Where it falls short**

Two honest gripes. First, bulbs are not included. That's not unusual, but at $80, it's a bit stingy. Six quality LED candelabra bulbs will run you $15–20 extra, putting your real total near $100.

Second, the black matte finish is a dust magnet. It's not a fingerprint disaster like glossy black, but in a kitchen or dining area with cooking grease in the air, you'll be wiping it down monthly. One buyer put it bluntly: *"Looks amazing — until you realize it needs dusting every week to stay looking amazing."*

Also worth noting: the candle sleeves are friction-fit, not screwed in. They stay put, but if you're installing on a sloped ceiling (which this fixture technically supports), they can shift slightly during installation. Not a dealbreaker, just a minor annoyance.

**Who should buy this**

- **Farmhouse-style dining rooms** — this is the sweet spot. The 31.5-inch width fills a table without overwhelming it.
- **Kitchen islands** — the 360-degree light spread means no dark corners for food prep.
- **Stairwells and entryways** — the adjustable drop and substantial presence make it work in vertical spaces.
- **Rental property owners** — at this price, you can outfit multiple units without breaking the bank, and the build quality will survive tenant turnover.

**Skip it if** you want a fixture with included bulbs, or if you're looking for a brass/antique gold finish — this is matte black only.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this be installed on a sloped or vaulted ceiling?**
A: Yes, but you'll need a sloped ceiling adapter (about $10) for anything over a 15-degree angle. The mounting plate is flat, so a standard adapter is required for vaulted installs.

**Q: What bulbs does it take?**
A: E12 candelabra base, max 40 watts per socket. LED bulbs are recommended — six 60-watt-equivalent LEDs will give you plenty of light without heat buildup.

**Q: How do I adjust the hanging height?**
A: The downrods are interchangeable. Remove the canopy, unscrew the rod coupler, and swap in the length you need. Takes about 5 minutes per rod.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely well-built farmhouse chandelier that won't look dated in three years and won't sag, rattle, or corrode. At $80.89, it's a solid value for the build quality.

**Skip it if** you're on a strict $80 budget (remember the bulbs) or you hate dusting fixtures — the matte black finish demands regular attention.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deduct half a star for the missing bulbs and the dust magnet finish. Everything else is legitimately excellent for the price.
