**The Fingerprint Keypad Deadbolt with Handle Set** sits in an awkward middle ground: it's not a full smart lock with Wi-Fi or app control, but it's also a step up from a basic keypad deadbolt. After digging through 210 ratings and the specs, here's the real picture for shoppers weighing convenience against compromises.

## What You're Actually Getting

The package includes the deadbolt assembly, the integrated handle set (interior and exterior), a mechanical key override, and mounting hardware. The exterior unit combines the fingerprint sensor, a backlit numeric keypad, and the handle in one piece — no separate lever to buy. The interior side is a simple turn-knob with a thumb turn for manual locking.

Build quality is the first thing that stands out. The exterior is solid zinc alloy with a matte black finish that genuinely resists smudging — a small detail, but one that keeps the lock looking clean after weeks of use. The fingerprint sensor sits flush in the handle, which makes the whole unit look cohesive rather than bolted-together.

## Real-World Performance

The fingerprint reader is the star here. With dry, clean fingers, it unlocks in about 0.8 seconds — faster than most phone sensors. It stores up to 50 fingerprints, which covers a large family plus frequent guests. The keypad also accepts PIN codes, giving you a backup when fingerprints fail.

But here's the catch that shows up repeatedly in buyer feedback: **wet or sweaty fingers are a problem.** One reviewer noted, "Works great 90% of the time, but after I washed dishes or stepped in from rain, it took three or four tries." That's consistent with capacitive sensor limitations — not a defect, but a real-world constraint. The keypad becomes your fallback, and it works fine, but the backlight doesn't activate until you touch the keys. In pitch darkness, you're fumbling.

Installation is straightforward if you're moderately handy. The lock fits standard 2-1/8" bore holes and 1-3/8" to 2" door thicknesses. Expect 30–45 minutes with a screwdriver and a drill if you're replacing an existing deadbolt. The included template is clear, and the wiring is a single plug connector — no complicated harness.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The convenience factor is the most-cited positive. "No more digging for keys when my hands are full of groceries" appears in various forms across multiple reviews. The integrated handle design also earns consistent praise — buyers like not having to buy a separate lever, and the handle itself has a solid, non-wobbly feel.

Battery life is another strong point. The unit runs on four AA batteries (not included), and multiple reviewers report 6–8 months of use before needing a replacement. There's a low-battery warning beep that gives you about two weeks of lead time, which is adequate.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the wet-finger issue, the lack of smart features is the biggest limitation. There's no app, no Wi-Fi, no remote access, and no integration with Alexa or Google Home. If you want to check whether you locked the door from work, this isn't your lock. It's purely physical access control.

The mechanical key override is also a weak point — the key cylinder feels a bit cheap and the key is small and easy to lose. It works, but it's not something you'd want to rely on daily.

One more consideration: at this price point (which sits below most comparable smart locks), you're trading away user management features. There's no way to delete individual fingerprints without resetting the entire unit and re-enrolling everyone. If you have a tenant move out or a guest you no longer trust, you're reprogramming from scratch.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want key-free entry for your family without paying for smart features you won't use. You have dry hands most of the time and live in a climate where rain isn't a daily occurrence. You're comfortable with basic DIY installation.

**Skip it if:** You need app control or remote monitoring. You live in a humid or rainy climate where wet fingers are the norm. You manage a rental property and need individual user deletion. Or you want a lock that's easy to use in complete darkness.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use just the keypad without fingerprints?**
Yes. You can set up to 50 PIN codes and use them exclusively. The fingerprint sensor is optional, not mandatory.

**Q: Does this fit a standard door?**
It fits doors 1-3/8" to 2" thick with a standard 2-1/8" bore hole. Most US residential doors qualify, but measure first.

**Q: What happens when the batteries die?**
The lock beeps for about two weeks before dying. After that, use the mechanical key to unlock. The key cylinder works independently of the electronics, so you're never locked out.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

This is a solid, functional fingerprint deadbolt that delivers on its core promise — convenient, key-free entry — at a price that undercuts most competitors. The build quality is good, the fingerprint sensor is fast when conditions are right, and the integrated handle saves you money. But the wet-finger sensitivity, lack of smart features, and cumbersome user management keep it from being a top-tier choice.

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, no-frills biometric lock for a single-family home with dry-handed occupants. **Skip it if** you need app connectivity, live in a rainy climate, or manage a property with changing users. For the right buyer, it's a genuinely good value. For others, the tradeoffs will be dealbreakers.
