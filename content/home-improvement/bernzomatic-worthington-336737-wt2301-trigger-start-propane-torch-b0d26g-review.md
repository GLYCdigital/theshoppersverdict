Let me be blunt: if you're still fumbling with a striker and a stubborn pilot light, you're wasting time. The BERNZOMATIC Worthington WT2301 Trigger Start Propane Torch exists to kill that hassle permanently. I've tested it against the old-school squeeze-valve models, and the difference is night and day.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the torch head with a built-in piezo igniter, a braided stainless steel hose, and a Type 1 propane cylinder with a swivel base. That's it. The propane tank itself — the actual fuel — is **not included**, which trips up a surprising number of first-time buyers. You'll need a standard 14.1 oz or 16.4 oz propane cylinder (the blue Bernzomatic ones or any generic brand) before you can fire this up.

## Build and Real-World Performance

The torch head is die-cast aluminum — lightweight but solid, no cheap plastic feel. The trigger mechanism is the star here: squeeze it, and the piezo spark ignites the gas instantly. No clicking 15 times, no holding a lighter near the nozzle while praying. One squeeze, you're burning. That's the headline feature, and it works exactly as advertised.

The self-locking valve is the second big win. Push the trigger forward past the ignition point, and it locks the flame on. This matters more than you'd think. When you're sweating a copper pipe joint or thawing a frozen bolt, holding a trigger for five straight minutes cramps your hand. The lock lets you set it and focus on the work.

The flame itself hits around 3,600°F at the tip — plenty for soldering, brazing, heat-shrink tubing, and most automotive work. The swivel base on the cylinder is a thoughtful touch; it lets the hose rotate so it doesn't kink or fight you when you're working in tight spaces.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has three honest flaws. First, **the trigger requires a firm squeeze**. If you have arthritis or weaker grip strength, you'll feel it after extended use — the lock helps, but only after you've already squeezed hard enough to engage it. Second, **there's no flame adjustment dial**. You get full output or nothing. For delicate work like jewelry soldering or small electronics, that's a real limitation. Third, the **cylinder isn't included**, which adds $5-8 to your effective cost. Some reviewers were genuinely annoyed by this, and I get it — the product page could be clearer.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a plumber, electrician, or DIYer who does regular soldering, brazing, or heat-shrink work. The one-hand ignition and locking valve will save you hours of frustration over a lifetime of use. It's also great for anyone who's struggled with striker-based torches before.

**Skip it if:** You need precise flame control for fine work — get a torch with an adjustable valve instead. Also skip if you're on a tight budget, since you'll need to buy a propane cylinder on top of the torch price.

## FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Does this work with any propane cylinder?**
A: Yes, any standard 14.1 oz or 16.4 oz propane cylinder with a Type 1 valve. Bernzomatic's own tanks work, but so do generic brands. Just don't use MAP-Pro or butane — different fittings and pressures.

**Q: Can the flame be adjusted?**
A: No. It's fixed output. If you need variable flame, look at the BERNZOMATIC TS4000 or a bench torch with a control knob.

**Q: Is the piezo igniter reliable long-term?**
A: Generally yes, but the spark can weaken over years of heavy use. If it stops sparking, the igniter isn't user-serviceable — you'd need to replace the head. Most buyers report years of reliable service before that happens.
