If you've ever balanced a 2x4 on two sawhorses and prayed it didn't slide, you already understand why the Black & Decker WM125 Workmate has been a garage staple for decades. This isn't a fancy tool—it's a 350-pound-capacity portable workbench with a party trick: built-in clamping jaws that turn any project into a two-handed job. At 4.4 stars from 220+ buyers, it's clearly earning its keep. But it's not without quirks. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box: the main bench top with the iconic twin-screw clamping system, four interlocking steel legs, a lower shelf/tray for tools, and the hardware bag. Assembly runs about 20 minutes—mostly because the leg brackets use bolts that demand patience (more on that below). The unit weighs roughly 19 pounds and folds down to a flat footprint you can slide behind a truck seat or prop in a closet. No tools required for the folding mechanism itself; it collapses via a simple trigger release.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The clamping system is the whole point. Those twin jaws open up to about 3.5 inches and close with a half-turn of the handles. I've locked everything from picture frames to a 2x4 into them, and the grip is rock-solid—no slipping, no marring on softer wood if you use the included rubber jaw pads. One buyer put it simply: *"I use it for everything from sanding to sawing. The clamps hold better than my standalone vises."*

Stability is the second win. At 30.5 inches tall with a wide stance, it doesn't tip even when I'm putting serious force into a hand plane. The 350-pound rating isn't marketing fluff—I've had a DeWalt miter saw on it for weeks without flex. The lower tray is a genuinely useful catch-all for screws and drill bits, keeping your work surface clear.

## Where It Falls Short

The hardboard top is the biggest compromise. It's smooth and flat, but it's not meant for heavy drilling or chisel work—you'll chew it up fast. If you're planning to hammer or pound on it, either lay down a scrap board or buy the pricier WM425 model with a solid wood top.

Assembly is the second gripe. The bolts that attach the legs to the frame are small and thread into soft metal. I read one review that warned, *"The screws strip if you look at them wrong—use a hand wrench and go slow."* I followed that advice and it was fine, but if you're the type to zip things with a drill, you'll be making a trip to the hardware store.

Finally, there's the height. At 30.5 inches, it's comfortable for most people under 5'10". Taller users will find themselves hunching. This is a portable bench, not a permanent workstation—keep expectations in check.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers or hobbyists with limited space**: It folds flat and lives in a closet.
- **DIYers who work on the go**: Throw it in the trunk for weekend projects at a friend's place.
- **Anyone who hates wrestling with C-clamps**: The integrated jaws are a genuine time-saver.

**Skip it if** you're a professional carpenter who needs a heavy-duty stationary bench, or if you're over 6 feet tall and value a back-friendly work height.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it hold a table saw or miter saw safely?**
A: Yes, up to the 350-pound limit. The top is 27.5 inches wide, so a compact job-site saw fits fine. Just don't expect the vibration dampening of a dedicated saw stand.

**Q: Is the clamping mechanism durable?**
A: Generally yes, but the threaded rods benefit from occasional silicone spray. A few buyers reported the handles getting stiff after months of heavy use—lubrication fixes it.

**Q: Does it come fully assembled?**
A: No, you'll assemble the legs and attach the top. It's straightforward, but budget 20–30 minutes and use hand tools only.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a versatile, space-saving workbench that clamps, holds, and folds away without complaint. It's the best budget portable bench under $100.

**Skip it if** you need a solid-wood top for heavy chiseling or you're tall enough to find 30.5 inches painful.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The clamps and portability make it a must-have for casual DIYers, but the soft top and fiddly assembly keep it from perfection. Worth every penny for the right buyer.
