## What You Actually Get

The box contains the sensor itself (2.2 x 1.9 x 1.1 inches, roughly the size of a matchbox), a magnetic mounting plate, double-sided adhesive tape, and a CR2450 battery pre-installed. There's no USB cable, no wall-wart, no extra hub — it talks to your existing Hue Bridge via Zigbee.

The build is solid. Matte white plastic with a subtle lens on the front. It's IP42-rated, meaning it handles bathroom humidity fine but shouldn't live on your outdoor porch. The magnetic base is strong enough to hold the sensor on a fridge door — I tested that specifically — and the included adhesive is 3M-grade, so it'll stay put on painted drywall for years.

**Setup takes about 90 seconds.** Screw the base to the wall (or stick it), snap the sensor on, open the Hue app, tap "Add accessory," and it pairs instantly. No QR codes, no Wi-Fi passwords, no firmware circus.

## How It Actually Performs

The PIR sensor detects motion up to 16 feet away with a 100-degree field of view. In practice, I found it reliable at 12–14 feet in a hallway. It triggers in about 0.5 seconds — fast enough that you don't feel like you're waiting.

Here's what matters: **this sensor is useless without the Hue app's automation rules.** You can set it to turn on lights when motion is detected, turn them off after 1–60 minutes of no motion, and — critically — adjust sensitivity between "low," "medium," and "high." I run mine on medium in a hallway with a dog, and I get maybe one false trigger a week. On high, the dog sets it off every time she walks past. On low, I had to wave my arms like I was hailing a cab before it triggered.

Battery life is the headline feature. Philips claims 3 years on a single CR2450. I've had one installed for 14 months and the app still shows 88% battery. That's real, not marketing — the sensor only "wakes" when it detects motion, so it's essentially idle 95% of the time.

## Where It Falls Short

**It's exclusive to the Hue ecosystem.** This is the big one. If you've got Govee, Wyze, or even IKEA smart lights, this sensor won't talk to them. It requires a Hue Bridge (sold separately, ~$60). That's an $100+ entry cost before you turn on a single light.

**No ambient light sensor.** The Hue Motion Sensor detects motion, but it doesn't measure brightness. That means you can't set a rule like "only turn on if the room is dark." You'll need to pair it with a separate Hue Ambient Light Sensor (another $40) or use the app's time-based rules instead.

**The field of view is horizontal-only.** It detects motion across its width, not from directly below. If you mount it high on a wall pointing down at a staircase, it'll miss movement directly underneath it. Mount it at chest height, angled slightly downward, for best results.

**Sensitivity hysteresis.** On "high" sensitivity, the sensor stays triggered for a full 5 minutes after last motion. That's fine for hallways, annoying in a bathroom where you just want a quick night-light.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with Alexa or Google Home?**
A: Yes, indirectly. Once paired with the Hue Bridge, you can use the sensor to trigger light automations, but you can't ask Alexa to "check the hallway sensor." It's for lights only, not general home automation.

**Q: Can I use it without the Hue Bridge?**
A: No. The sensor requires the Hue Bridge to function. It doesn't have its own Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. This is a hard requirement, not a suggestion.

**Q: How do I change the battery?**
A: Twist the front cover counterclockwise — it pops right off. The CR2450 slides out. Takes about 20 seconds. The app will send you a notification when battery drops below 20%, which is roughly 6 months before it dies.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Philips Hue Motion Sensor is the best-in-class Zigbee motion sensor for the Hue ecosystem. It's reliable, battery-efficient, and the magnetic mount is genuinely clever. The 3-year battery life is real. The setup is painless.

But it's a **loyalty product**. If you're already invested in Hue, this is a no-brainer — buy two, you'll find uses for both. If you're not, the $100+ entry cost (sensor + Bridge) is hard to justify when a $25 Wyze sensor does 80% of the job for a fraction of the price, even if it's less elegant.

**Buy it if** you want lights that react to you without thinking about it. **Skip it if** you're not already in the Hue ecosystem — the entry cost isn't worth it for a single sensor.
