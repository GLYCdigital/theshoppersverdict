# WRISIN Black Shower Faucet Set: The Budget Matte Black Option That Actually Holds Up

Walk through any big-box home center and you'll see matte black shower systems going for $250–$400. The WRISIN set lands at a fraction of that, and the question isn't whether it looks good — it's whether the internals survive year three. After combing through 215 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the honest picture.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the full package: a two-handle wall-mount faucet body, tub spout with diverter, fixed shower head, handheld sprayer, 59-inch stainless steel hose, and mounting hardware. The valve body is solid brass with a ceramic disc cartridge — that's the same internals you'd find in fixtures twice the price. The finish is a true matte black, applied via a physical vapor deposition (PVD) process rather than cheap paint, which means it won't flake off when you're scrubbing hard water deposits.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The standout feature here is the water flow. The fixed shower head delivers a strong, consistent spray at standard home water pressure — several buyers noted it actually outperformed their previous Delta or Moen heads. The handheld unit has multiple spray modes and the hose feels substantial, not like the flimsy plastic-wrapped hoses on cheaper sets.

The two-handle setup gives you separate hot and cold controls, which means precise temperature adjustment once you dial it in. The ceramic cartridge turns smoothly and doesn't feel gritty or loose — a common failure point on budget fixtures.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

One buyer summed it up: *"I've installed three different brands of black shower systems from Amazon — this is the first one where the finish actually stayed flawless after a year of daily use."* That's the recurring theme. Multiple reviewers mention the matte finish resisting water spots that plague glossy black fixtures. Another common thread: the install is manageable for a competent DIYer. As one reviewer put it, *"Took me about two hours including removing the old fixture. The threads on the valve body are clean and didn't cross-thread."*

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest tradeoff. This set does NOT include a pressure-balancing valve. If someone flushes a toilet while you're showering, you'll feel a sudden temperature spike. That's a real safety concern for households with kids or elderly residents. Building codes in many jurisdictions now require pressure-balancing valves in new construction — check your local code before committing.

The tub spout diverter is also a pull-up style that requires a firm tug. It works fine, but it's not the smoothest action — one reviewer noted it took several weeks to loosen up. And the instructions are bare-bones. The included diagram assumes you know your rough-in depth and valve orientation. If you're not comfortable with basic plumbing, budget for a plumber or watch a few install videos first.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners replacing an aging chrome fixture** — the PVD finish will look sharp for years, and the price leaves room in the budget for a plumber if you get stuck.
- **Landlords or rental property owners** — solid brass internals and a finish that won't scuff mean fewer service calls.
- **Anyone with hard water** — the matte finish hides mineral deposits far better than gloss black or chrome.

**Skip it if you need a pressure-balancing valve** for code compliance or safety, or if you want a single-handle (one-lever) control rather than dual handles.

**Q: How does the matte black finish hold up to cleaning products?**

**Q: Is the handheld shower head detachable?**

**Skip it if** code compliance or temperature safety is a concern, or you prefer the convenience of a single-handle control.
