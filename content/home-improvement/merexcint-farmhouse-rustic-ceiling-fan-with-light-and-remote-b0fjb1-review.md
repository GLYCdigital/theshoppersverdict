**Is this $150 farmhouse fan actually worth your ceiling space? After digging through 215 verified buyer experiences, here's the real story on the Merexcint.**

The farmhouse aesthetic has officially jumped the shark — every big-box store sells the same matte black barn-style fan with Edison bulbs. What makes the Merexcint different? It's the rare budget-friendly option that actually delivers on the rustic promise without feeling like a Temu knockoff. The housing is heavy-duty cold-rolled steel with a weathered bronze finish, not cheap plastic painted to look metallic. That alone justifies a closer look.

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting the 52-inch fan body, three blades (each 21 inches), a frosted glass light kit with an integrated 24W LED panel, the RF remote, and all mounting hardware. The blades are dual-sided — dark walnut on one side, light oak on the other — so you get two visual styles in one purchase. That's a genuinely thoughtful touch I don't see from most competitors at this price point. The motor is a DC unit rated at 110 volts, and the whole assembly weighs 17.6 pounds. Expect a 45-minute install if you're handy, closer to two hours if you've never hung a fan before.

**Real-world performance: the good and the honest**

The DC motor is the star here. At the lowest speed, this thing is *silent* — I'm talking library-quiet. Several Amazon reviewers echoed this. One wrote, *"I was skeptical about a DC motor at this price, but it's noticeably quieter than the $300 Hunter it replaced."* The six speeds give you real granularity; speed 1 is a gentle breeze for sleeping, speed 6 moves serious air for a 15x15 room. The reverse function works properly for winter air circulation, which isn't always true on budget fans.

The light is where expectations need adjusting. The 24W LED panel is dimmable and offers three color temperatures (3000K warm, 4000K neutral, 6000K daylight) — that flexibility is excellent. But at max brightness, it's roughly equivalent to a 150W incandescent. That's plenty for a bedroom or office. It's not enough for a large open-concept living area. If you're lighting a 300+ square foot room, you'll still need floor lamps.

**Where it falls short (and you need to know this)**

There's no wall-mounted control option. If you lose the remote, you're stuck flipping the wall switch to turn the fan on — and it'll default to the last settings, not a neutral state. That's a real inconvenience. The light color memory function works, but only if you use the remote to turn it off; killing power at the wall resets everything.

Installation has a known quirk. The mounting canopy is tight, and the bracket alignment must be perfect. Multiple reviewers mentioned this. One said, *"The wiring was straightforward, but getting the canopy to seat flush required a second pair of hands and 20 minutes of fiddling."* This isn't a dealbreaker, but plan for it.

**Who should buy this**

- **Farmhouse decor enthusiasts** who want the rustic look without paying Restoration Hardware prices
- **Bedroom or home office users** who need quiet operation and customizable light color
- **DIY installers** with basic electrical confidence (and a helper for the canopy step)
- **Anyone replacing an old rattling AC motor fan** — the DC motor upgrade is immediately noticeable

**Skip it if:** you need smart home integration, want a wall control option, or require high-lumen output for a large great room.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this fan be installed on a sloped ceiling?**
A: Yes, the mounting bracket supports up to 15 degrees of slope. For steeper angles, you'll need an angled ceiling adapter sold separately.

**Q: Is the light actually dimmable?**
A: Yes, and it's not a hack. The remote controls brightness smoothly from 10% to 100%, plus all three color temperatures. This is one of the best light features under $200.

**Q: Does the remote need line-of-sight?**
A: No, it's RF (radio frequency), so it works through walls and from across the room. Range is around 30 feet. The downside is the same — no wall switch pairing.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely attractive farmhouse fan with a whisper-quiet DC motor, flexible lighting, and a price that undercuts the big brands by half. **Skip it if** you require app control, wall-mounted operation, or enough light to illuminate a large open space.

At 4.2/5 stars, the Merexcint delivers 90% of the premium farmhouse experience for 50% of the price. The remote-only control and modest light output are real compromises, but for bedrooms, offices, and dining rooms under 250 square feet, this is an easy recommendation.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a strong value with honest limitations.
