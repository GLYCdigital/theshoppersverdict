If you've ever tried to router a door lip with a straight bit and a straightedge clamped to the door, you know the drill: two passes, constant measuring, and the sinking feeling when the bit wanders off-line. The Freud 99-001 eliminates all of that. This is a specialized bit with one job, and it does that job exceptionally well. But "specialized" means it's not for everyone — here's the full breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The 99-001 is a door lip router bit with a 1/2-inch shank, a ball-bearing pilot guide, and a carbide cutting edge that produces a 1/4-inch deep lip. The bit's design is simple: the bearing rides along the door's face while the cutter removes material from the edge, creating a uniform lip for inset doors or flush overlays. It's packaged in Freud's standard clear plastic case, and the bit itself shows the quality machining you'd expect from a brand that's been making cutting tools for decades.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this bit on solid oak, poplar, and MDF doors. The first thing you notice is the smoothness — no chatter, no burning, even on a full-depth pass. The TiCo coating (titanium-cobalt carbide) keeps the edge cool and sharp, which matters when you're routing a dozen doors in an afternoon. The ball-bearing pilot tracks the door face precisely, so as long as you hold the router base flat, you get a consistent lip every time.

One buyer summed it up: *"I was skeptical about the pilot bearing method, but it's dead-on accurate. Routed 30 cabinet doors in an afternoon with zero tear-out."* Another noted: *"The finish is so clean I didn't even sand the lip before staining."* That tracks with my experience — the cutting geometry leaves a smooth, ready-to-finish surface.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the 235 ratings, three themes dominate. First, accuracy: the bearing-guided design means no setup time and repeatable results. Second, durability: multiple reviewers report using it for years on professional jobs without noticeable dulling. Third, value: at this price point, it's cheaper than a dedicated door-lip jig system and takes up far less space in your tool bag.

**Where It Falls Short**

The 99-001 has two real limitations. First, it's a fixed-size bit — you get a 1/4-inch lip, period. If you need a 3/8-inch or 1/2-inch lip for thicker doors, you'll need a different bit. Second, the pilot bearing is pressed in and not replaceable. When it eventually wears out — and it will, after heavy use — you're buying a whole new bit, not just a $8 bearing. One reviewer noted: *"Great bit, but I wish the bearing was serviceable. I had to toss it after about 200 doors when the bearing started to wobble."*

Also, the 1/2-inch shank means you need a router with a 1/2-inch collet. Most full-size routers have one, but if you're working with a compact trim router, this bit won't fit.

**Who Should Buy This**

This bit is for cabinetmakers, finish carpenters, and serious DIYers who install inset doors regularly. If you're building a built-in bookcase with 10 doors, or refacing kitchen cabinets, this bit will pay for itself in saved time on the first project. It's also a smart buy for anyone who's struggled with freehand routing and wants a foolproof method.

Skip it if you're a casual DIYer doing one-off projects, or if you only work with 1/4-inch collet routers. For that use case, a cheaper straight bit and a clamped guide will get the job done — just with more setup time.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this bit cut a lip on a pre-hung door?**
A: Yes, if you can access the edge with the router base flat. It's designed for doors that are already mounted or clamped on a workbench.

**Q: Does it work on hollow-core doors?**
A: It will cut the face veneer and the underlying particleboard, but the lip will only be as solid as the core material. For solid wood or plywood doors, it's ideal.

**Q: What RPM should I use?**
A: Freud recommends 16,000-24,000 RPM. Start at the lower end for hardwoods and increase speed for softwoods or MDF.

**The Verdict**

The Freud 99-001 is a professional-grade bit that delivers exactly what it promises: clean, accurate door lips in one pass, with no setup. The fixed size and non-replaceable bearing are minor annoyances, but they don't diminish the bit's core value.

**Buy it if:** You do cabinet or door work regularly and want consistent, fast results.
**Skip it if:** You need variable lip depths, or you only own a 1/4-inch collet router.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a specialized tool done right, with minor longevity concerns.
