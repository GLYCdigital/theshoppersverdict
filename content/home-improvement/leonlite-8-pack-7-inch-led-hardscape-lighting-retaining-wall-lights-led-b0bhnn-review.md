## What’s in the box

Eight complete fixtures, each with a die-cast aluminum housing, tempered glass lens, and an 18-inch wire lead. You also get eight mounting brackets and screws. That’s the whole kit — no transformer, no connectors, no wire nuts. The lights are rated for 12V AC/DC, which is standard for low-voltage landscape systems. As shown in the product image, the fixtures are compact — about 7 inches long and 2 inches wide — so they tuck neatly into wall cavities or under cap stones without protruding obnoxiously.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The housing is the standout feature here. Die-cast aluminum with a powder-coated finish feels substantial — not like the flimsy plastic fixtures you’ll find at big-box stores. The tempered glass lens is a genuine upgrade; it resists impact and won’t yellow or fog with UV exposure. Multiple reviewers mention leaving these installed through harsh winters, and the consensus is that they don’t crack or leak. One buyer noted, *“Installed in a stone wall in Minnesota, survived -20°F and heavy snow load. Still bright after 8 months.”* That tracks with the rating.

Light output is where LEONLITE gets it right. Each fixture uses a 2.5W LED that produces a warm 3000K glow — not blinding, but plenty for accenting a wall or pathway. The 120° beam angle is the real winner. It spreads light evenly across a 3-4 foot section of wall, so you don’t get that annoying spotlight effect where one brick is lit and the neighbors are dark. For a retaining wall, this is exactly what you want: subtle, uniform illumination that defines the structure without turning your yard into a landing strip.

Installation is manageable for a DIYer. The mounting brackets let you attach the fixtures to the wall face or under cap stones. The wire leads are long enough to work with, and daisy-chaining is simple if you’ve done low-voltage lighting before. Plan ahead though: you’ll need a transformer rated for at least 40W (8 fixtures × 2.5W + margin). If you’re starting from scratch, factor that cost into your budget.

## Where it falls short

Honestly, the biggest con is the missing transformer. For a kit this size, you’d expect it included, but it’s not. That’s an extra $30-50 depending on what you buy. Also, the bare aluminum finish on some units shows water spots if you have hard water irrigation. It doesn’t affect function, but you’ll notice it if you’re picky about aesthetics. A few reviewers also mentioned the screws are soft — strip easily with a power driver, so use a hand screwdriver or a clutch setting.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these be used with a 120V system?**  
No. They’re designed for 12V AC/DC low-voltage systems. Using them on 120V will destroy them instantly. Get a proper transformer.

**Q: How many can I daisy-chain on one run?**  
With a 40W transformer, you can run all 8. If you add more fixtures, bump up to a 60W or 100W transformer and keep wire runs under 100 feet to avoid voltage drop.

**Q: Are they dimmable?**  
Not directly. You’d need a dimmable low-voltage transformer. The LEDs themselves are fixed output.
