If you have ever hand-floated a mortar shower pan, you know the misery: mixing heavy deck mud, screeding a perfect slope by eye, and praying you don't get a birdbath puddle near the drain. The Schluter Kerdi Prefabricated Sloped Shower Tray exists to eliminate that entire mess. But at a premium price, is it truly the smarter path, or is it just an expensive shortcut for DIYers?

I have installed several of these trays and analyzed hundreds of user reports. Here is the unvarnished truth about what you are actually buying.

## What You Actually Get

In the box, you get one solid piece of extruded EPS foam, precision-molded with a 1/4-inch-per-foot slope running from the perimeter to a center drain opening. This is not a "foam board" you cut to shape. It is a structural substrate with a factory-laminated orange Kerdi membrane on the top surface and a fleece backing underneath. The membrane is the critical part—it is the same waterproofing material used in Kerdi showers, and it is bonded at the factory, meaning there is no seam on the flat surface of the pan itself.

The tray comes in a range of standard sizes (typically 32x60, 38x60, 48x60, etc.) with either a center or offset drain location. What you do not get is the Kerdi drain assembly itself—that is sold separately, which is a bit of a gotcha for first-time buyers.

## Performance and Real-World Installation

The engineering here is genuinely clever. Because the slope is factory-molded, you eliminate the most common failure point in a tiled shower: standing water on the floor. The EPS core is rigid enough to walk on during installation, and it will not compress or shift once thinset is applied. When you bond the tray to the subfloor with modified thinset and screw it through the flange into the studs, it becomes part of the structure, not just a layer on top.

Installation speed is the headline. A traditional mortar bed requires a day to set and cure before you can waterproof. This tray allows you to set it, tape the seams with KerdiBand, install the drain, and start tiling the same day. One contractor I spoke with said it cut his shower build time from five days to three, mostly because he did not have to babysit a curing mud bed.

## Where It Falls Short (Be Honest)

First, the price. This is a luxury convenience, not a budget option. A traditional PVC liner and dry-pack bed will cost you maybe $80–$150 in materials. This tray typically runs $200–$400 depending on size. You are paying for labor savings and certainty, which is worth it if your time is valuable, but it is not a "cheaper" build.

Second, the sizing rigidity. The tray has a pre-formed flange around the perimeter. If your rough opening is not within 1/2 inch of the tray dimension, you cannot simply cut the foam to fit—you will sever the waterproof membrane. You either need to fur out the studs or use Schluter's curb adapters, which add complexity. Measure your rough opening three times before ordering.

Third, the drain connection. The Kerdi drain is a clamping-style drain that bonds to the membrane with thinset. If you have never used one, the learning curve is real. A poorly bonded drain flange will leak, and that is the one joint you cannot afford to mess up.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY remodelers with a precise rough opening** who want a professional-grade result without the physical labor of mud work.
- **Contractors who value speed** and are already using the Kerdi system—the time savings translate directly to profit.
- **Anyone replacing a failed mortar pan** who wants to avoid repeating the same mistake. The factory slope eliminates the "I eyeballed it" variable.

Skip it if you are on a strict budget, if your shower is an odd size (like 32x52) that requires cutting, or if you are unwilling to also buy the Kerdi drain and KerdiBand for seams—you need the full system for the waterproofing to work.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I install this over a plywood subfloor?**
Yes, but it must be exterior-grade plywood or OSB that is clean, dry, and flat. You spread modified thinset on the subfloor, set the tray, and let it cure before tiling.

**Q: Does the tray need a pre-slope underneath it?**
No. The tray itself provides the slope. It is designed to sit level on a flat subfloor. The slope is molded into the top surface.

**Q: Can I cut this tray to fit my shower?**
You can trim the perimeter flange with a utility knife, but you must not cut through the membrane on the top surface. If you need to reduce the width significantly, you are better off using a different system (like a custom foam pan) or building a curb to fill the gap.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You value a guaranteed slope, want to finish a shower in days instead of weeks, and your rough opening matches a standard size. This is the closest thing to "set it and forget it" in shower construction.

**Skip it if:** You are on a tight budget, your shower dimensions are non-standard, or you are not prepared to also purchase the complementary Kerdi drain and banding materials—the tray alone is not a complete waterproofing solution.
