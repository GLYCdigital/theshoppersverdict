**The Delta Boyd isn't just another kitchen faucet — it's the one that makes you realize how often you touch a faucet with raw chicken hands.**

At $246, this isn't an impulse buy. But after 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clear Delta has done something right. Let me break down whether the Boyd actually earns its price tag or if you'd be better off with a cheaper pull-down model.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the Boyd faucet assembly, a separate counter-mounted soap dispenser, the Touch2O battery module, and a 6-AA battery pack that mounts under your sink. Full installation takes about 45 minutes if you're handy with a basin wrench. The faucet measures 15.5 inches from base to spout tip, with a 9-inch clearance to the sink bottom — enough for most standard-depth sinks.

What you don't get: a sprayer. This is a fixed gooseneck design with a high-arc spout but zero pull-down function. That's the single biggest decision point for most buyers.

## Real-World Performance

The Touch2O feature is the headline act, and it genuinely works. A light tap on the spout starts and stops the flow — perfect when you've got dough on your hands or you're trying to rinse a cutting board without gripping the handle. The sensor is capacitive, not infrared, so it doesn't accidentally trigger when you're just reaching past it.

Water flow is a solid 1.8 GPM with an aerator that produces a clean, non-splashing stream. The spout swivels a full 360 degrees, which helps compensate for the lack of a pull-down function — you can pivot it out of the way or angle it toward either basin of a double sink.

The soap dispenser is a nice touch, holding about 10 ounces of liquid soap. It mounts through a separate hole in the counter, and the pump mechanism is serviceable. It won't win awards for feel — the plastic pump has a bit of play — but it keeps your counter looking cleaner than a standalone bottle.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The praise in the Amazon reviews is consistent: "Installation was straightforward, the finish matches my stainless sink perfectly, and the tap feature is surprisingly responsive." Multiple buyers mention that the brushed nickel finish hasn't tarnished or water-spotted after months of daily use. One reviewer noted, "I've had mine for 8 months and it still looks brand new — the PVD finish is no joke."

But the critiques are equally predictable. The most common complaint is battery life. One reviewer wrote: "Love the faucet, hate that I'm changing batteries every 4-5 months. It's not hard, but it's a reminder this isn't a 'set it and forget it' product." A few buyers also reported the soap dispenser pump failing within the first year — Delta's warranty covers it, but it's still an inconvenience.

## Where It Falls Short

The fixed spout is the real limitation here. If you routinely fill 8-quart stock pots or need to rinse the far corners of a deep farmhouse sink, you'll find yourself wrestling with the geometry. The gooseneck arc is generous, but it's not a replacement for a pull-down wand.

The battery dependency is the other tradeoff. Delta sells an optional AC adapter, but it's not included — and at this price point, it feels like it should be. Also, the touch sensor can occasionally misfire if you bump the spout while wiping down the counter.

## Who Should Buy This

The Boyd is ideal for homeowners with standard-depth sinks who want the touch convenience without the complexity of a smart faucet. It's particularly good for:

- Households with kids who leave sticky fingerprints on everything
- Anyone who cooks frequently and hates gripping a handle with messy hands
- Buyers who want a clean, classic gooseneck silhouette with a matching soap dispenser

Skip it if you need a pull-down sprayer, have a deep farmhouse sink, or don't want to deal with battery changes.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Touch2O feature work if the batteries die?**
A: Yes — the faucet still operates manually with the handle. You just lose the tap-on/tap-off function until you replace batteries.

**Q: Will the soap dispenser fit any countertop?**
A: It requires a standard 1-1/8-inch hole, which is the same size as most existing soap dispenser holes. If you're replacing an old dispenser, it should drop right in.

**Q: Is this faucet compatible with a garbage disposal air switch?**
A: Yes, but you'll need a separate hole for the air switch. The Boyd uses one hole for the faucet and one for the soap dispenser, so plan your countertop layout accordingly.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want touch convenience, love the classic gooseneck look, and don't mind swapping batteries twice a year. The build quality and finish are legitimately premium at this price.

**Skip it if** you need a pull-down sprayer or want a zero-maintenance faucet. The battery dependency and fixed spout are real compromises.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Boyd nails the fundamentals with excellent build quality and genuinely useful touch tech. It just doesn't do everything a modern kitchen might demand.
