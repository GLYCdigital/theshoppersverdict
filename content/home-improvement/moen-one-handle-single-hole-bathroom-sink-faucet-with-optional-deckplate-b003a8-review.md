**A bathroom faucet is a 10-year purchase.** You'll touch it twice a day, every day, and the difference between a good one and a frustrating one is measured in small annoyances — drips, wobbles, awkward handles. The Moen One-Handle Single Hole is the kind of faucet that disappears into your routine because it just works. But it's not without quirks. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the faucet body, a single lever handle, the optional 6-inch deck plate (that's the "Option" in the title), supply lines, and mounting hardware. The chrome finish is what you see in the product image — polished, reflective, and standard for the price point. What matters more is underneath: Moen's Duralock quick-connect system. Instead of wrestling with threaded nuts and a basin wrench in a cramped cabinet, you push the supply lines in until they click. It's genuinely tool-friendly for a change.

The faucet measures about 6.5 inches tall with a 4.5-inch spout reach. That's compact enough for a half-bath or powder room but substantial enough to actually wash your hands under without scraping your knuckles.

## Performance and Build

The single-lever handle controls both temperature and flow with one hand — standard, but the motion is smooth and precise. No jerky starts, no guessing where "just right" is. The aerated stream is the standout feature here. At full pressure, it runs quiet and doesn't splash back at you. That's a small thing until you've used a faucet that sprays water across the counter every morning.

The body is solid brass, not the zinc alloy you'll find on cheaper competitors. That translates to a faucet that doesn't feel hollow or flimsy when you use it. The ceramic disc cartridge inside is the same one Moen uses in their higher-end lines, and it's rated for 500,000 cycles. You will likely replace this faucet because you want a new look, not because it broke.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

With 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, the pattern is clear. Over and over, reviewers mention the installation. One buyer put it simply: "Took me 20 minutes to install and I'm not handy at all." Another noted, "The quick-connect lines are genius — no more fighting under the sink." The deck plate flexibility also gets consistent love from people who replaced older 3-hole setups without wanting to redo their countertop.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The chrome finish is a fingerprint magnet. In a busy family bathroom, you'll be wiping it down weekly to keep it looking sharp. It's not a defect — it's chrome being chrome — but if you hate maintenance, consider a brushed nickel or matte black finish instead.

The lever handle position is the bigger issue. On shallow countertops where the sink sits close to the backsplash, the handle can end up tight against the wall, making it awkward to grip and turn. Check your counter depth before buying. If you have less than 2 inches between the sink center and the wall, this could be a daily annoyance.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners replacing a worn-out faucet** — the quick-connect system genuinely saves you an hour of frustration
- **Anyone with a 3-hole sink who doesn't want to replace the countertop** — the deck plate covers those extra holes cleanly
- **People who value reliability over flash** — this is a workhorse, not a showpiece

Skip it if you want a tall, modern vessel-style faucet or if you need a matte finish to match a contemporary bathroom.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the optional deck plate work on any 3-hole sink?**
A: It covers holes spaced 6 inches apart center-to-center, which is the standard for most 3-hole bathroom sinks. Measure your sink first — if your holes are 4 inches apart, this won't fit.

**Q: Are the supply lines included long enough?**
A: The included lines are about 18 inches. That's fine for most under-sink setups, but if your shutoff valves sit far from the faucet, buy longer lines. They're standard 3/8-inch compression fittings.

**Q: Will this work with a vessel sink?**
A: No. This is designed for standard drop-in or undermount sinks with the faucet mounted to the countertop. The spout is too short for vessel bowls.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, easy-to-install faucet that will outlast your countertop. The deck plate flexibility alone makes it worth considering, and Moen's warranty backs the cartridge for life.

**Skip it if** you're after a design statement or have a tight backsplash situation that would make the lever awkward.
