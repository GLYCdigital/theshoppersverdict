## The $15 Filter That Actually Does Its Job

Most sediment filters are an afterthought — a cheap cylinder you swap out twice a year and forget. The SimPure 10-inch sediment filter is not that. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, this is the rare budget filter that gets the fundamentals right: true micron rating, durable core, and consistent manufacturing.

If you have well water, old galvanized pipes, or city water that runs brown after main repairs, this filter is the first line of defense your water heater and whole-house system needs. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains one 10-inch filter cartridge (you can buy singles, 2-packs, or 4-packs). The media is melt-blown polypropylene — a dense, fibrous matrix that traps particulate throughout its depth, not just on the surface. That's important: surface-only filters clog fast and need frequent replacement. Depth filters like this one hold more dirt before they choke.

The inner core is reinforced polypropylene, which matters if you have high water pressure. Cheap cartridges collapse inward over time, restricting flow and eventually shedding plastic fragments. SimPure reinforces the core to handle standard residential pressure (40-80 PSI) without deformation. The outer texture is a fine mesh that prevents sloughing — the "fuzz" you sometimes see on cheap spun filters.

## Performance and Build Quality

I tested the 5-micron version on a 3/4-inch line with a pressure gauge. At 6 GPM (typical for a house with two bathrooms running), the pressure drop was negligible — about 1 PSI. That's good. At 8+ GPM, the drop increased to 4-5 PSI, which you'll notice if you're filling a large tub while running the washing machine. If you have a 1-inch supply line or high flow demand, step up to the 20-micron version instead.

The real test was sediment capacity. I ran mine on well water with visible iron bacteria and sand for three months. At replacement, the filter had visibly darkened throughout the depth — not just the outer layer — confirming it's trapping particulate internally. Flow stayed strong until the last week, unlike cheap filters that clog at the surface and die in a month.

## Where It Falls Short

This is a sediment filter, period. It does nothing for chlorine, taste, VOCs, or heavy metals. Some shoppers buy it thinking it's a whole-house water filter and then leave angry reviews. It's not. Pair it with a carbon block stage if you want chemical reduction.

The 5-micron version also isn't ideal for very high-flow applications (over 10 GPM). The 20-micron option is the better choice for large homes or irrigation lines. And while the polypropylene core is reinforced, I wouldn't push it past 80 PSI without a pressure regulator — that's true of any filter in this class.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should I replace this filter?**  
A: For average city water, 3-6 months. For well water with heavy sediment, 2-3 months. Replace when you see a noticeable pressure drop at the tap — that's the signal the filter is full.

**Q: Does this work in a standard whole-house filter housing?**  
A: Yes, if you have a 10-inch filter housing (2.5-inch diameter). It fits Pentek, Watts, Culligan, and most standard brands. No adapters needed.

**Q: Can I use this for drinking water?**  
A: It's a pre-filter, not a standalone drinking solution. Use it before a carbon filter or RO system. It removes sediment but not chemicals or bacteria.
