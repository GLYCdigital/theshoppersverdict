# Milwaukee M12 Fuel Impact Driver Review: Small Body, Serious Muscle

Most compact impact drivers make you choose between fitting in tight spaces and actually driving screws. The Milwaukee M12 Fuel 1/4 HEX Impact Driver refuses that tradeoff. At just 4.6 inches from nose to tail, this is the tool you grab when you're working inside cabinets, between studs, or under a sink—and it still delivers the kind of torque that makes you forget you're not holding a full-size driver.

## What's Actually in the Box

This is a bare tool. No battery, no charger, no case. You get the driver, a belt clip, and a bit holder. That's it. If you're already invested in the M12 ecosystem—and let's be honest, most people buying this are—that's fine. If you're starting fresh, factor in the cost of a starter kit or at least one 2.0Ah or 4.0Ah battery and a charger. The M12 Fuel platform is worth the entry fee, but it's a fee nonetheless.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline number here is 1,450 in-lbs of torque. That's not M18 territory, but it's genuinely impressive for a tool this size. I've driven 3-inch deck screws into pressure-treated lumber without pre-drilling, and it handled them with authority. The four-mode drive control (including a precise mode for lighter work) gives you real control when you're not just blasting screws in.

The build quality is quintessential Milwaukee. The rubber overmold grips well even with oily hands, the chuck holds bits firmly with no wobble, and the balance is excellent—this thing weighs just 2.3 pounds with a 2.0Ah battery attached. That weight matters when you're doing overhead work or driving dozens of screws in a row.

The Tri-LED lights are genuinely useful. They cast a wide, shadow-free beam that makes a real difference in dark corners. And the 20-minute auto-shutdown on the LEDs is a small detail that shows Milwaukee thought about battery preservation.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't an accident. The dominant theme in customer feedback is the size-to-power ratio. One contractor put it plainly: *"I've owned the M18 Fuel for years. This M12 gets grabbed first now because it's half the weight and does 90% of the work."* Another reviewer noted, *"The torque surprised me. I expected a toy. This is not a toy."*

The battery efficiency also gets consistent shoutouts. A 4.0Ah battery lasts a full day of light-to-moderate work, and even the 2.0Ah packs hold up surprisingly well.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. The 1/4" hex chuck means you're locked into hex-shank bits. For drill bits, you'll need a hex-to-round adapter, which adds length and can introduce wobble. If you regularly drill pilot holes with twist bits, this isn't your primary tool.

The bare tool pricing also stings if you're coming from another platform. And while the torque is impressive, it's not a substitute for an M18 when you're driving 6-inch lags into hardwood all day. The M12 will do it, but it'll work harder and drain batteries faster.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Cabinetry and finish carpenters:** The compact body gets you into places full-size drivers can't reach.
- **DIYers already in the M12 system:** This is the natural second tool to pair with a drill/driver.
- **Electricians and HVAC techs:** Lightweight, powerful, and fits in a tool bag without dominating it.
- **Anyone doing overhead work:** The weight difference is a genuine relief after an hour of screws above shoulder height.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this drive 3-inch screws?**
A: Yes, comfortably. It handles most general fastening tasks up to about 3.5 inches in softwood. For structural lags or hardwood, step up to an M18.

**Q: What battery should I get?**
A: A 2.0Ah for everyday work (it keeps the tool light). A 4.0Ah if you're doing longer sessions. Skip the 6.0Ah—it throws off the balance and adds weight you don't need.

**Q: Does it come with bits?**
A: No. It's a bare tool. You'll need your own 1/4" hex bits. The included bit holder on the belt clip is handy, but you're bringing your own bits.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're in the M12 ecosystem and want a compact driver that doesn't sacrifice performance. It's the best-in-class option for its size, and the 4.8-star rating reflects that.

**Skip it if:** You're starting fresh and need one tool to do everything. You'd be better served by an M18 combo kit that covers drilling and driving with more versatility.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The size-to-power ratio is genuinely exceptional, and the build quality is what you expect from Milwaukee. The bare-tool format and hex-only chuck keep it from a perfect score, but for its intended role, this is the compact impact driver to beat.
