If you spray paint, lacquer, or clear coat with any regularity, you already know the pain of a clogged exhaust filter. The fan sounds like it's dying, the booth pressure drops, and overspray starts settling on your freshly painted piece. The Paint Pockets GREEN 20x20 filters are designed to solve exactly that problem, and after looking at how they're built and what 235 buyers say (4.7 stars), they mostly do.

**What you actually get**

The box contains 40 individual 20x20x1-inch filters, each with a corrugated cardboard frame. That frame is the first thing you'll notice — it's not a flimsy fiberglass pad. The media itself is a green, multi-layer synthetic web folded into deep pleats that create small "pockets." That design is the whole point: more surface area means more places for overspray to land without blocking airflow.

One buyer put it well: "I was skeptical about the price versus standard fiberglass, but these last 3-4 times longer in my booth. They're basically the same cost per project in the end." That sentiment runs through the reviews — people are annoyed at the upfront cost but satisfied with the lifespan.

**Performance and build quality**

In real-world use, the GREEN rating means these are rated for particles down to 10 microns. That covers most solvent-based primers, enamels, and urethanes. The pocket design holds a surprising amount of paint before the filter needs replacing — I've seen reports of hobbyists getting 5-8 hours of actual spraying time before airflow drops noticeably. Compare that to flat fiberglass filters that start choking after 1-2 hours.

The cardboard frame does double duty. It keeps the filter rigid so it doesn't collapse into the fan housing, and it seals against the rack edges reasonably well. Some buyers note they need to trim a small lip on the frame to fit certain aftermarket booths, but standard 20x20 racks accept them without modification.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- Longevity: "I do 2-3 cars a month and change these every other project. Same as my old filters but I was changing those every single job."  
- Airflow: "My fan actually sounds quieter with these. The static pressure drop is way lower than the cheap pads."  
- The 40-pack value: "Bought the bulk pack to keep at the shop. Nobody complains about filter changes anymore."

**Where it falls short**

The GREEN rating is the entry-level efficiency tier in the Paint Pockets lineup. If you're spraying fine automotive clear coats with an HVLP gun at high pressure, you may see a light dusting of overspray on your exhaust duct over time. That's not a defect — it's the filter doing what it's rated for. If you need finer filtration, Paint Pockets sells WHITE and BLUE tiers that catch sub-5-micron particles, but they cost more per filter and restrict airflow more.

Also, these are not washable or reusable. Once loaded, they go in the trash. If you're a weekend warrior doing one project a year, a 40-pack will last you years — which is great for value but means you're storing 40 bulky filters.

**Who should buy this**

- **Professional spray booth operators** who want predictable filter life and minimal fan strain — this is your workhorse filter.  
- **Hobbyist painters** who do multiple projects per month and are tired of swapping cheap filters mid-job.  
- **Anyone with an expensive exhaust fan** — the low pressure drop means less motor wear over time.

Skip them if you only spray once or twice a year and don't mind replacing a $5 fiberglass pad each time. The upfront cost won't pay itself back at that usage rate.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these be cut to fit a smaller booth?**  
Yes, but don't. Cutting the media breaks the pocket structure and reduces efficiency. Buy the size that matches your rack.

**Q: How do I know when to replace them?**  
Watch airflow first, not visual loading. When you see paint buildup on the exhaust side of the plenum or the fan sound changes pitch, replace them.

**Q: Are these the same as the blue or white ones?**  
No. GREEN (10-micron) is the standard efficiency tier. WHITE (5-micron) and BLUE (2-micron) are for fine finishing work. Match the tier to your material.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You spray regularly and want filters that last multiple jobs, keep your fan happy, and don't need constant replacement.

**Skip it if:** You're a once-a-year sprayer or need sub-10-micron filtration for fine automotive finishes — in that case, step up to the WHITE tier.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid, well-engineered filters that cost more upfront but save money and frustration over time. Deducting half a star because the GREEN rating won't suit every application, and the bulk pack is overkill for casual users.
