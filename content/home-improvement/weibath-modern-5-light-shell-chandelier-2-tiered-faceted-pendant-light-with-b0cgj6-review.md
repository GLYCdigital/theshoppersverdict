Let’s be blunt: most chandeliers under $200 look like they came from a hotel liquidation sale — all brass plating and fake crystal. The Weibath Modern 5-Light Shell Chandelier, 2-Tiered Faceted Pendant Light is not that. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it’s earning its keep in real dining rooms. Here’s the breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the chandelier body, five faceted glass shades, mounting hardware, and clear instructions. The fixture measures roughly 25 inches in diameter and 23 inches in height — substantial, but not overwhelming for a standard 8-foot ceiling. The 2-tiered shell arrangement stacks five lights in two levels, which gives it that "custom designer piece" silhouette from across the room.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The frame is steel with a matte black finish that resists fingerprints — a detail you’ll appreciate after a month of dusting. The faceted glass shades are the star here. Unlike smooth globes that create hot spots, the facet pattern refracts light into a soft, even glow. One buyer noted, *"The light is warm and diffused, not blinding like my old fixture. Dinner guests actually complimented the ambiance."* That tracks with what I’d expect from the light dispersion design.

The 5-light layout uses E12 candelabra sockets. With 60-watt equivalent LED bulbs, you’ll get solid task lighting over a 6–8 foot dining table. Pair it with a dimmer switch and you can drop it to 20% for evening mood lighting without flicker. Several reviewers mentioned running it on dimmers without issues — worth confirming your switch is rated for LED before installation.

**Where it falls short**

The assembly is the weakest point. The 2-tiered structure requires threading the arms through the central column in a specific order. One reviewer called it *"a two-person puzzle"* — fair warning. Budget a solid hour, and have someone hold the upper tier while you tighten the set screws. It’s manageable, but not a 15-minute job.

Also, the E12 bulbs aren’t included. You’ll spend an extra $15–25 on a set of candelabra-base LEDs. Factor that into your total cost. And if your ceiling is sloped, the included mounting bracket works but may leave a slight gap on steeper angles — you’ll want a sloped-ceiling adapter for anything over 20 degrees.

**Who should buy this**

- **Dining room owners** replacing a dated flush mount or builder-grade fixture — this elevates the space instantly.
- **Renters or first-time buyers** who want a designer look at a sub-$200 price point.
- **Anyone with a dimmer switch** already installed — this fixture rewards you for it.

**Who should skip it**

- **DIY-averse buyers** — if you don’t enjoy assembling furniture, this will test your patience.
- **Low-ceiling rooms** under 7.5 feet — the drop is too tight; consider a flush mount instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this chandelier work with a sloped ceiling?**
A: Yes, but for angles steeper than 20 degrees, you’ll need a sloped-ceiling adapter (about $15). The included bracket is designed for flat ceilings.

**Q: What bulbs does it take, and are they included?**
A: It takes five E12 candelabra-base bulbs. None are included — you’ll need to buy them separately. LED versions are recommended for heat and energy savings.

**Q: Can I use it in a bedroom or hallway?**
A: Absolutely. The 25-inch diameter and 23-inch drop work well in stairwells, entryways, and larger bedrooms. Just verify your ceiling height first.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you want a statement piece that looks twice its price and you’re okay with a Saturday-morning assembly session. The faceted glass and 2-tiered design deliver genuine visual depth that flat-panel fixtures can’t touch.

**Skip it if** you need a quick swap with zero assembly fuss, or you can’t budget for the extra bulb purchase. But for the money, this is one of the better-designed chandeliers in its class — and 229 buyers largely agree.
