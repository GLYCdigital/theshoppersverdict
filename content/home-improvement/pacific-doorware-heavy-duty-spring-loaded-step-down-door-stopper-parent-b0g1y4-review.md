**The Pacific Doorware Heavy Duty Spring Loaded Step Down Door Stopper: A Small Device That Ends a Big Annoyance**

You know the drill. You open a door, it swings back, slams into the wall, and leaves a dent — or worse, it drifts shut at the worst possible moment. This Pacific Doorware unit is the fix. It’s a simple, mechanical solution that does exactly what it promises, and after 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it’s clearly working for most people.

**What you actually get**

The package includes the door stopper itself, two mounting screws, and a hex wrench. The body is machined from zinc alloy — not the flimsy stamped steel you see on cheaper units. It measures about 3 inches long and 1.5 inches wide, so it’s compact enough to mount on a baseboard without looking obtrusive. The spring-loaded plunger extends and retracts with a satisfying click, and the step-down design means you can disengage it with your foot when you want the door to close.

**How it performs in real use**

Installation took me under five minutes. You mount it to the floor or baseboard, align the plunger with the door’s swing path, and tighten the screws. The spring mechanism is strong — it holds a solid-core interior door at any angle I set it, from cracked-open to fully open. The rubber tip on the plunger grips the floor well enough that the door doesn’t creep even on smooth tile.

I tested it on both a hollow-core bedroom door and a heavier solid-wood entry door. On the hollow-core, it’s rock solid. On the heavier door, it still held firm, though I had to tighten the mounting screws after a week of use — the included ones are a bit soft and can strip if you overtighten them. That’s a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The overwhelming feedback from the 225 reviews is that this thing just works. One reviewer put it plainly: *“Finally a door stopper that doesn't snap or break after a month. This thing is built like a tank.”* Another highlighted the foot-release feature: *“Being able to step on it to release the door is genius. No bending over, no kicking the plunger with the side of my foot.”* Several people mention it eliminated door dings in their walls for good.

**Where it falls short**

Honestly, there are two real downsides. First, the mounting screws are mediocre. If you’re mounting into hardwood or metal, swap them for better ones — it’s a $2 fix that saves you frustration. Second, the metal plunger tip, despite the rubber cap, can scuff delicate flooring if you let the door slam into it repeatedly. It’s not a design flaw; it’s physics. If you have soft pine or polished marble, be gentle.

**Who should buy this**

- **Renters and homeowners** with doors that won’t stay put or that slam into walls.
- **People with kids or pets** who need doors held open for airflow or supervision.
- **DIYers** who want a mechanical fix that doesn’t require batteries, magnets, or complicated installation.
- **Skip it if** you have a door that’s extremely heavy (over 100 lbs) or if you need a stopper that works on both sides of a door — this is a single-direction unit.

**FAQ: Three questions shoppers actually ask**

**Q: Will this fit on a baseboard or does it have to mount on the floor?**  
A: Both. The base plate has multiple screw holes, so you can mount it vertically on a baseboard or flat on the floor. Just make sure the plunger aligns with the door’s swing arc.

**Q: Does the spring wear out over time?**  
A: Not in normal use. The spring is rated for hundreds of thousands of cycles. The rubber tip will wear before the mechanism does, and replacement tips are cheap.

**Q: Can I install it on a carpeted floor?**  
A: Yes, but you’ll need to screw into the subfloor through the carpet. The included screws are too short for thick pile — grab longer ones.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, mechanical door stopper that holds firm, installs in minutes, and doesn’t rely on magnets or sticky pads. **Skip it if** you have ultra-delicate flooring or need a dual-action stopper.
