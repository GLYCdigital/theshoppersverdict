**Bottom line: The Kobalt 80V 2.5Ah battery is the workhorse of the Kobalt cordless lineup — if you already own 80V tools. If you're starting fresh, the 2.5Ah capacity might leave you wanting more runtime.**

I've spent the past three weeks running this battery through a Kobalt 80V leaf blower, string trimmer, and chainsaw on a half-acre property. Here's what I found.

**What's in the box**

You get the battery pack itself — that's it. No charger included, no tool. If you're buying this as a replacement or spare, make sure you already own the Kobalt 80V rapid charger (sold separately). The battery measures roughly 6.5 x 3.5 x 3 inches and weighs 2.4 pounds. It's a chunky unit with a solid, grippy texture and a locking mechanism that clicks into tools with satisfying authority. No wobble, no play — the connection feels premium.

**Real-world performance**

The headline spec is 2.5 amp-hours at 80 volts, which translates to about 200 watt-hours. In practical terms, that means roughly 25-30 minutes of continuous high-speed operation on the 80V leaf blower, or about 40 minutes of string trimming at moderate throttle. I got 28 minutes of heavy leaf blowing on day one, and 22 minutes on day five after repeated charge cycles — some degradation is normal, but that's a noticeable drop worth tracking.

The power delivery is where this battery shines. Unlike cheaper packs that sag under load, the Kobalt 80V maintains consistent voltage until the final 10% of charge. My chainsaw cut through 8-inch oak rounds without stuttering, and the blower held full thrust right up until the low-battery warning kicked in. That's genuinely impressive engineering.

**What buyers praise**

Scrolling through the 220 Amazon ratings at 4.4 stars, a few themes dominate. One verified buyer wrote: "This battery outlasts my old 40V by double. I can finish the whole yard on one charge." Another noted: "Took a 4-foot drop off the ladder onto gravel. Scuffed the corner, still works perfectly." Durability and runtime are the consistent wins here.

**Where it falls short**

The 2.5Ah capacity is the compromise. If you're running a chainsaw or a hedge trimmer for serious work, you'll want the 4.0Ah or 6.0Ah versions — this one will leave you mid-project. The lack of an on-battery fuel gauge is also annoying; you're flying blind until the tool starts beeping. And at typical street pricing, this pack costs about 40% more per amp-hour than a comparable 40V battery. It only makes financial sense if you're already invested in the 80V platform.

**Who should buy this**

- **Kobalt 80V owners** who need a spare or replacement battery — this is the right call.
- **Light-to-moderate users** doing standard suburban yard work (half-acre or less).
- **Buyers who prioritize durability** — this pack is built like a brick.

**Skip it if:**

- You're starting a new cordless tool system from scratch — consider the 4.0Ah bundle deals instead.
- You need extended runtime for commercial or heavy residential use — go bigger.
- You don't already own the Kobalt 80V charger.

**FAQ**

*Q: Can I use this battery in Kobalt 40V tools?*  
No. The 80V and 40V systems use different connectors and voltages. They're not interchangeable.

*Q: How long does it take to charge?*  
With the Kobalt rapid charger, about 60 minutes to full. A standard charger takes closer to 90 minutes.

*Q: Does it come with a warranty?*  
Yes — Kobalt backs it with a 5-year limited warranty, which is solid for a battery. Keep your receipt.

**The Verdict: Buy it if you're in the Kobalt 80V ecosystem and need a reliable spare. Skip it if you're chasing maximum runtime or starting fresh — the 4.0Ah is worth the extra money for most users.**
