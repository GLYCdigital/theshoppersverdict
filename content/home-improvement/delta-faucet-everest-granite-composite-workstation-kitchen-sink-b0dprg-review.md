**The Delta Everest Granite Composite Workstation Sink is a great idea wrapped in a frustrating package.** The workstation concept — accessories that slide on integrated tracks, keeping your cutting board and colander right over the basin — is genuinely useful. But execution details separate a 4.5-star product from a 3.7-star one.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the sink itself (single basin, roughly 33 inches if you order the standard size, with a 9-inch depth), plus three accessories: a bamboo cutting board, a stainless steel colander, and a dish-drying rack. All three slide into the molded tracks along the sink's upper rim. There's no drain strainer assembly included — that's sold separately, which is annoying at this price.

## Real-World Performance

The granite composite material (80% crushed granite, 20% acrylic resin) does what it promises: it's tough, resists scratches, and — most importantly — it's quiet. Drop a cast-iron skillet in there and you get a dull thud, not a clang. Heat resistance is solid; setting a hot pan directly in the basin won't damage it.

The 9-inch depth is the standout feature. You can fill it with enough water to wash a turkey roaster without splashing the counter. The workstation tracks are genuinely handy — the cutting board sits at a comfortable working height over the basin, and scrap food pushes straight into the disposal area.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Most positive reviews center on the accessories and depth. One verified buyer noted, "The cutting board and colander actually work as shown — no fiddling, they slide smoothly and stay put." Another praised the noise reduction: "I came from a stainless steel sink and the difference is night and day. No more loud clanging."

## Where It Falls Short

The 3.9-star average tells the real story. Multiple reviewers report the same frustration: **installation alignment is unforgiving.** The tracks are molded into the composite, and if your countertop cutout is slightly off — even by a millimeter — the accessories bind, scrape, or slide unevenly. You can't adjust them. One reviewer wrote, "The colander catches on the track every single time. I've given up using it."

The matte finish is another issue. It shows water spots and fingerprints worse than a glossy sink. You'll be wiping it down constantly if you care about aesthetics. And several buyers reported shipping damage — granite composite is heavy and brittle at the edges, and packaging doesn't always protect the corners.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're remodeling and can control the countertop cutout precisely (or have a contractor who measures twice). You cook heavily, use big pots, and want a quiet, deep basin with integrated accessories that actually fit flush.

**Skip it if:** You're replacing an existing sink in a standard cutout and hoping it drops in without issue. The alignment tolerance is too tight for retrofit work. Also skip if you hate maintenance — this finish demands regular wiping.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the sink come with a drain assembly?**
No. You'll need to buy a basket strainer or garbage disposal flange separately. Factor that into your budget.

**Q: Can I use my existing garbage disposal with this sink?**
Yes, standard 3.5-inch disposals fit. The basin is deep enough that the disposal won't interfere with the workstation accessories.

**Q: How does the granite composite hold up to acidic foods like lemon juice or tomato sauce?**
Better than stainless steel — the resin seals the surface, so acids don't etch or stain it. Just rinse promptly.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The Delta Everest is a well-conceived workstation sink with premium materials and genuinely useful accessories. But the tight installation tolerance and high-maintenance finish knock it down from great to merely good. If you're prepared for a precise install and daily wiping, you'll love it. If you want a set-and-forget sink, keep shopping.
