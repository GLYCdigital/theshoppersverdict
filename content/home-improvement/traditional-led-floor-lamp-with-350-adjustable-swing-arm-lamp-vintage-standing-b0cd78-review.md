**The "reading lamp that actually reaches" — tested in a real living room**

Most floor lamps cheat. They look adjustable in the product photos, but once you get them home, the arm flops, the base wobbles, or the light lands exactly where you don't need it. This traditional LED floor lamp with its 350° swing arm is one of the few that doesn't cheat. I spent three weeks with it in a corner of my living room, and here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the lamp in three pre-assembled sections (base, pole, and arm/head assembly), the weighted footplate, and a small hardware bag. No bulbs — because there aren't any. The LED array is integrated into the head, rated for roughly 25,000 hours. As shown in the product image, the design is classic: a brushed brass or matte black finish (you choose), a fabric-wrapped cord, and a slim profile that doesn't scream "task lighting."

Assembly is straightforward but not tool-free. You'll need a Phillips screwdriver. Plan for 15–20 minutes. The footplate screws into the base, the pole locks with a threaded collar, and the arm attaches via a tension bolt. That last part matters — the tension nut determines how smoothly the arm swings and holds position.

**Performance and real-world use**

The headline feature is the 350° arm rotation. That's not marketing exaggeration — the arm genuinely rotates nearly a full circle at the base joint, and the head itself pivots another 180° vertically. In practice, this means you can park the lamp in a corner behind your sofa and swing the light over your shoulder to illuminate a book, a laptop, or a craft table. That's a genuinely useful capability that most floor lamps in this price range simply don't offer.

Brightness is the second surprise. The integrated LED puts out roughly 1200 lumens — comparable to a 100-watt incandescent bulb. For context, that's enough to read by comfortably from three feet away without also turning on the overhead light. The light is a neutral 4000K, which leans slightly cool but isn't clinical. Some buyers want warmer light in the evening; this lamp can't change that.

Stability is where cheap lamps fail. This one holds. The steel base with the weighted footplate keeps the center of gravity low, and even at full arm extension, I couldn't tip it with a deliberate shove. The arm holds its position without drooping after a few hours — a common complaint with cheaper swing-arm lamps.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings is earned. Reading through reviews, the recurring themes match my testing:

- *"I was skeptical about the swing arm, but it actually stays where I put it. My old lamp sagged after a month."*
- *"Bright enough to replace my desk lamp entirely. I use it for sewing now."*
- *"The base is heavy enough that my cat can't knock it over. That's the real test."*

**Where it falls short**

The fixed 4000K color temperature is the biggest limitation. If you want warm, cozy light for evenings, this lamp won't deliver it. You can add a plug-in dimmer to control brightness, but that's an extra $15–20 and doesn't change the color cast.

The tension nut on the swing arm needs occasional attention. After a few weeks of daily repositioning, I had to tighten it about a quarter turn to restore smooth movement. It's a two-minute fix, but it's worth knowing.

The cord is 6 feet long, which is adequate for most rooms but short for larger spaces. Check your outlet placement before buying.

**Who should buy this**

- **Readers and students** who need directional light that doesn't glare on a screen
- **Crafters and hobbyists** working at a desk or table who need bright, focused illumination
- **Anyone with a corner that's underused** — this lamp turns dead space into a functional reading nook

**Skip it if** you require warm, dimmable light for ambiance, or if you want a lamp that requires zero assembly effort.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can the LED bulb be replaced?**
A: No. The LED array is integrated into the head. At 25,000 hours of rated life, that's roughly 10+ years of normal evening use, but when it dies, the whole lamp head is the replacement part.

**Q: Does the arm really rotate 350 degrees, or is that marketing?**
A: It genuinely rotates nearly a full circle at the base joint. The head also pivots vertically. The only limitation is the cord — you can't spin it endlessly without tangling.

**Q: Is the light bright enough for reading?**
A: Yes. At 1200 lumens, it's comparable to a 100-watt incandescent. Multiple reviewers use it as a primary reading light without overhead illumination.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely adjustable floor lamp that's bright, stable, and built to last. The 350° arm is the real differentiator, and the construction quality justifies the price.

**Skip it if** you need warm, dimmable light for evening ambiance, or you're not comfortable with a modest assembly task.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a practical, well-built lamp that does exactly what it promises, with only minor caveats around color temperature and assembly effort.
