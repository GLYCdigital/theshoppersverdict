## What You Actually Get

The set includes eight collars sized from 1/8 inch up to 1/2 inch — the most common drill bit diameters for woodworking, metalworking, and electrical rough-ins. Each collar uses a hex-key set screw rather than a thumbscrew, which is the single biggest upgrade over bargain-bin options. Two hex wrenches come in the case, sized to fit all eight collars.

The case itself is a simple blow-molded plastic box with foam inserts. Nothing fancy, but it keeps things organized. As shown in the product image, the collars sit in labeled slots, so you can grab the right size at a glance.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these on standard high-speed steel bits, brad-point bits, and a Milwaukee spade bit. The hex-key mechanism bites hard — once tightened, the collar doesn't budge. That's critical when you're drilling a row of shelf pin holes at exactly 3/8-inch depth and every variation shows up in the finished project.

The locking mechanism is the differentiator here. Thumbscrew collars require tools anyway to tighten properly, so why not get one that actually grips? These hold firm through repeated plunging into oak and pressure-treated lumber. One reviewer noted, "I've used these on dozens of jobs and never had one slip. The hex key design is way better than the cheap ones with the little plastic knob."

That said, the two smallest collars are genuinely thin-walled. If you crank the set screw down with a lot of force, you can strip the threads or deform the collar. Use a firm hand, not a gorilla grip, and they'll last.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest assessment: there's no size marking etched into the collars. The case has labels, but once you pull a collar off and toss it in your pouch, you're guessing by eye. A quick permanent marker job fixes this, but it's a small annoyance on a "professional" product.

The two smallest collars are the weak link for heavy use. They're functional, but they feel less robust than the larger pieces. If you primarily drill with 1/8-inch bits, you might want a dedicated collar with a thicker body.

The price point — check current Amazon pricing, but it's competitive with single-brand collars that only give you half the sizes.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy if:** You're a woodworker drilling repetitive shelf holes, a cabinet installer needing consistent hinge depths, or an electrician setting box heights. The hex-key grip gives you precision that cheap collars can't match.

**Skip if:** You only drill occasionally and don't need depth consistency, or if you work exclusively with tiny bits under 3/16 inch.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit hex shank drill bits?**
A: No. These are designed for round-shank bits. Hex shanks have a different profile and won't seat properly in the collar.

**Q: Do they work on spade bits?**
A: Yes, as long as the bit's round shank matches the collar size. I tested them with a 1/2-inch spade bit and they held firmly.

**Q: Can you use them with a drill guide or depth stop attachment?**
A: Generally not needed — these replace those attachments. But if you're using a guide, the collar may add enough bulk to interfere. Test fit before relying on it.
