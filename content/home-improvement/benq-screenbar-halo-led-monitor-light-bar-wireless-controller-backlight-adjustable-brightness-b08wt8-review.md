# BenQ ScreenBar Halo Review: The Gold Standard for Monitor Lighting, But Is It Worth It?

You've been squinting at your screen during late-night work sessions, or worse — your desk lamp is casting a glare that makes your monitor look like a fogged-up windshield. The BenQ ScreenBar Halo is the product that keeps showing up in every "best desk setup" video. After digging through 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the light bar itself, a weighted clamp that slips over your monitor (no adhesive, no screws), the wireless puck controller, a USB power cable, and a coin battery for the puck. The bar measures 22.5 inches wide and weighs about 1.4 pounds — it's built for monitors up to about 2 inches thick. As shown in the product image, the Halo has a two-part design: the front LED bar for your desk, and a separate curved backlight that wraps behind your monitor.

## Performance and Build Quality

This is where the Halo separates itself from the $40 Amazon specials. The asymmetrical optical design means the light hits your keyboard and desk, not your screen — zero glare, period. The front light offers 8 color temperatures (2700K warm to 6500K cool) and 21 brightness levels. The backlight is the real star though. It casts a soft halo of light on the wall behind your monitor, which dramatically reduces the contrast between your bright screen and dark room. That contrast is what causes eye fatigue at 2 AM.

The wireless puck is genuinely well-executed. It's magnetic, weighted, and has a smooth rotating dial for brightness and a separate ring for color temperature. It also has an auto-dimming mode that uses ambient light sensors — it actually works, adjusting smoothly rather than making jarring jumps. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "The backlight alone is worth the premium. My eyes stopped hurting after two days of use."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the ratings, three things keep coming up. First, the build quality — the aluminum bar feels substantial, and the clamp holds firm even on curved monitors. Second, the lack of screen glare, which multiple reviewers confirmed after trying cheaper alternatives first. Third, the backlight feature is the differentiator. As one 5-star reviewer wrote, "I bought a $30 bar first. Returned it the same day. The Halo's backlight changes the entire room ambience."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. At roughly $199 (when not on sale), it's expensive — you could buy three solid budget monitor bars for the same price. The power cable uses a barrel connector, not USB-C, which is annoying if you're trying to run a clean cable setup. The puck controller eats a coin battery every 4-6 months depending on use, and when the battery dies, the light stays at its last setting until you replace it. A few reviewers also noted the clamp can be tight on ultra-thin monitors, though it includes a silicone pad to prevent scratches.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You spend 6+ hours daily at your computer, work in a dim room regularly, or have experienced eye strain or headaches after screen time. Also buy it if you're building a serious home office and want one lighting solution that handles both task lighting and ambient backlighting. This is a buy-once, use-for-years purchase.

**Skip it if:** You work in a well-lit room with overhead lighting, have a strict budget, or you're fine with a fixed-angle budget bar for occasional use.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work on curved monitors?**
Yes. The weighted clamp accommodates curved screens, and the bar itself is slightly curved to match. Several reviewers confirmed it works on 34-inch ultrawide curved displays without sagging.

**Q: Is the wireless controller actually wireless?**
The puck is wireless and battery-powered, but the light bar itself still needs a USB power cable. The "wireless" refers to the control, not the power.

**Q: Can I use it on a laptop?**
Technically yes, but the clamp needs a minimum monitor thickness of about 0.2 inches. Most laptops are too thin, and the weight of the bar could damage a laptop screen hinge. This is a desktop monitor accessory.

## The Verdict: 4.6/5

The BenQ ScreenBar Halo is the best monitor light bar you can buy — and it's priced accordingly. The backlight feature genuinely reduces eye strain in a way that budget bars can't replicate, the build quality is excellent, and the wireless control is smooth and responsive. The price and battery-powered puck are real drawbacks, but for anyone who lives at their desk, this is the difference between lighting as an afterthought and lighting as a tool. If you're on the fence, wait for a sale — it regularly drops to $160-170 — and then pull the trigger. Your eyes will thank you.
