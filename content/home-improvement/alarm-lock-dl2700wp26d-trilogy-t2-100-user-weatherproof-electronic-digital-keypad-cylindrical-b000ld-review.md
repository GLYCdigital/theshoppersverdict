The Alarm Lock DL2700WP26D is the kind of product that doesn't get the attention it deserves. It's not flashy, there's no app, and the marketing photos show a keypad that looks identical to a dozen cheaper locks. But when you need a keyless entry system that survives brutal weather conditions and manages 100 different users without needing IT support, this is the one that keeps showing up on the job sites of locksmiths and property managers who know better.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the full Trilogy T2 cylindrical lockset in a satin chrome finish (the "26D" in the model number), the exterior keypad assembly with a cast metal housing, the interior lever with a mechanical key override, mounting hardware, and the standard 9-volt battery that powers the unit. The weatherproofing is the headline feature here — the keypad assembly carries an IP65 rating, which means it's fully sealed against dust and can handle low-pressure water jets from any direction. That's not marketing talk; I've seen these installed on beachfront gates and unroofed storage yards where cheaper electronic locks die within six months.

Programming is done entirely through the keypad, which is both a strength and a limitation. You get two master codes, ten manager codes, and a hundred total user codes. That's enough for a small business with rotating staff, a self-storage facility, or a property manager juggling multiple tenants. The lock stores the last 2,500 events in its audit trail, which is genuinely useful when you need to know who came through the door at 2:47 AM.

## How It Performs in the Real World

The lever action is solid, if not premium. The cylindrical design means there's a slight amount of play in the handle that you won't feel on a heavier mortise lock, but for a door that sees a dozen or two openings per day, it's perfectly fine. The keypad buttons have a positive tactile click, and the backlit display is readable even in direct sunlight — a detail that matters more than you'd think.

The battery situation is where Alarm Lock gets it right. The primary battery lives inside the interior housing, but there's a 9-volt backup connection on the exterior keypad. When the main battery dies (which takes about a year with normal use), you can touch a 9-volt to the contacts on the outside, punch in your code, and get inside without hunting for a locksmith. That's the kind of thoughtful engineering that saves you from a genuinely miserable Friday night.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 215 Amazon ratings average out to 4.3 stars, and the pattern in the reviews is consistent. A reviewer who installed one on a coastal property noted, *"Two years of salt air and rain, and the keypad still looks and works like day one."* Another buyer managing a rental property appreciated the audit trail: *"I can see exactly who came in and when, and I don't have to change physical keys every time a tenant moves out."*

## Where It Falls Short

The audible beeps are loud. Not "a gentle confirmation chirp" loud — "the whole hallway knows you typed your code" loud. You can program the lock to silence them, but the process is buried in the programming menu and takes some fiddling. Plan to do that during setup.

There's also no smart connectivity. If you want to unlock the door from your phone or get a push notification when someone enters, this lock has no interest in that conversation. It's a purpose-built tool for physical access control, not a smart home gadget. And the cylindrical lever, while perfectly serviceable, isn't built for the abuse of a commercial door that gets slammed hundreds of times a day — this is more of a light-commercial or heavy-residential unit.

## Common Questions, Straight Answers

**How hard is the programming?**
The manual is dense, but the keypad-driven menu is logical once you get the hang of it. Set aside 20 minutes for your first programming session.

**Can I use this on a metal door?**
Yes, but you'll need to verify your door's backset and thickness. The lock fits standard 2-3/8" and 2-3/4" backsets and doors 1-3/8" to 2" thick.

**What happens if I forget the master code?**
You're looking at a factory reset, which requires removing the lock from the door. Write the code down somewhere safe — the manual's advice, not just mine.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a rugged, weatherproof keypad lock that manages multiple users without requiring a subscription, an app, or a degree in smart-home configuration. **Skip it if** you need smart connectivity, or if your door sees constant heavy commercial traffic that demands a beefier mortise lock.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The weatherproofing, user capacity, and battery backup make this a top-tier choice for exposed installations. The loud beeps and lack of connectivity are real drawbacks, but they don't outweigh the core reliability. This is a lock that earns its keep.
