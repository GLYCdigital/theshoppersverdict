If you've ever burned through a standard hammer drill trying to punch a 3/4-inch hole in a foundation wall, you know the difference between "hammer drill" and *rotary hammer* isn't marketing — it's physics. The WORKPRO 1-1/4-Inch SDS-Plus model is firmly in the latter category, and at its price point, it's quietly becoming the budget pick that DIYers and light contractors actually recommend to each other.

**What's in the box:** The drill body, a vibration-reducing side handle, a depth stop rod, a chuck key, and a basic blow-molded tray insert (no hard case). You also get a grease tube for the chuck — a detail that tells you WORKPRO expects this tool to last long enough to need maintenance. Notably absent: a second handle position for close-quarters work, and any kind of dust collection attachment.

**Performance and build quality:** The 8.5-amp motor delivers 4.5 joules of impact energy — enough to chew through rebar-laced concrete if you let the tool do the work. The SDS-Plus chuck accepts standard bits, and the three-mode selector (rotate, rotary hammer, and chip-breaking) is genuinely useful. Flip it to chip-breaking mode and you can use it as a light demolition tool for tile or thin masonry — a versatility that punches above its price class.

Real-world testing shows the vibration control is the standout feature. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I drilled 40 holes in a concrete retaining wall and my hands weren't numb afterward. My old DeWalt made me quit after 10."* That's the kind of feedback that matters when you're spending an afternoon on anchor bolts.

**The catches:** The dust shield over the SDS chuck is held on with a friction fit, and it will pop off if you drill at aggressive angles. It snaps right back, but it's a minor annoyance. More significantly, there's no case — if you're a site worker who throws tools in a gang box, budget for a bag. And the included side handle uses a plastic collar that can slip if you really torque it down; hand-tighten, then check it periodically.

**Who should buy this:** The homeowner tackling a concrete anchor project (foundation bolts, sill plates, fence posts). The weekend warrior installing a TV mount on brick or block. The small contractor who needs a backup rotary hammer that won't make them cry if it walks off a job site. It's also a smart pick for anyone who's been renting a rotary hammer from Home Depot — three rental fees and this thing has paid for itself.

**Skip it if:** You're a full-time framing or concrete contractor doing 8-hour days with 1-inch bits. The motor is good, but not Milwaukee Fuel good — it'll slow down on sustained heavy loads. Also skip if you need a true demolition hammer; this is a drill first, breaker second.

**FAQ:**

*Q: Does it accept standard SDS-Plus bits from other brands?*  
Yes — SDS-Plus is a universal standard. Bosch, DeWalt, Milwaukee bits all fit and lock in cleanly.

*Q: Can it drill rebar?*  
It can drill through rebar, but you'll feel it. Use a carbide-tipped bit and let the tool push — don't lean on it.

*Q: Is this too heavy for overhead work?*  
At 11.2 pounds, it's on par with most 1-1/8" rotary hammers. The anti-vibration handle makes overhead work more tolerable, but it's still a two-handed job.

**The Verdict:** The WORKPRO 1-1/4-Inch SDS-Plus Rotary Hammer Drill is the rare budget tool that doesn't feel like a compromise. It's not the most powerful, not the lightest, not the most refined — but it's an honest, capable unit that delivers pro-level features at a homeowner price.

- **Buy it if:** You need reliable concrete drilling without paying Bosch/Hilti prices, or you're tired of renting.
- **Skip it if:** You need a hard case, want a demolition hammer, or you're a production contractor.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Strong value, solid performance, minor ergonomic nits.
