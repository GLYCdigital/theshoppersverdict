## What You Actually Get

The KK326 is a 32-ounce bottle of concentrated liquid cleaner. That's it — no spray nozzle, no dilution bottle, no measuring cup. You're expected to supply your own sprayer and do the math yourself. The label lists dilution ratios ranging from 1:4 for heavy degreasing to 1:64 for light maintenance cleaning. At the standard 1:16 ratio, that single bottle produces about four gallons of ready-to-use cleaner. Push it to 1:64 and you're looking at 16 gallons. For the price, that's remarkably economical — most ready-to-use degreasers cost more per ounce and deliver less power.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The active ingredient is a proprietary blend of surfactants and solvents that breaks down hydrocarbon-based soils. In practical terms: this stuff eats grease. Spray it on a stove hood caked with years of cooking residue, wait five minutes, and wipe — it comes off without the elbow grease that abrasive cleaners demand. It works on engine parts, workshop tools, painted walls, and even laundry pre-treatment for oil stains.

One of the standout features is the lack of overpowering odor. Unlike many degreasers that smell like a chemical spill, the Krud Kutter has a mild, almost citrusy scent that dissipates quickly. It's also phosphate-free and biodegradable, which matters if you're washing parts over a drain or using it outdoors.

## Where It Falls Short

The dilution requirement is the biggest friction point. If you're not careful with measurements, you'll either waste product or leave a filmy residue on surfaces. That residue shows up most noticeably on glass and glossy painted surfaces — it takes a second wipe with plain water to clear it. Several reviewers mentioned this as a frustration, especially when using it as an all-purpose cleaner.

Second, it's a cleaner, not a sanitizer. The label doesn't claim to kill germs, and buyers expecting disinfectant properties will be disappointed. For kitchen counters where you want both cleaning and sanitizing, you'll need a separate product.

## Who Should Buy This

This is built for people who actually get their hands dirty. ## FAQ

**Can I use this on car paint?**
Yes, at the 1:16 dilution it's safe on clear-coated automotive paint. Don't let it dry on the surface — rinse thoroughly after wiping.

**Is it safe on sealed hardwood floors?**
The label lists sealed wood as safe, but test a small inconspicuous area first. Keep the dilution at 1:16 or weaker to avoid residue buildup.

**Does it work on rust?**
No. This is a grease and grime remover, not a rust dissolver. For rust, you need a dedicated product like Evapo-Rust or CLR.
