Here's the scenario: you just paid a fabricator to install a beautiful undermount sink. They told you it was "properly secured." That gap is where water gets in, rots the wood substrate, and turns a minor annoyance into a full countertop replacement.

The CPT-4 undermount sink support brackets are the insurance policy that prevents that nightmare. After researching the sink-support market — which is surprisingly crowded for such a niche product — these adjustable stainless brackets stand out for one simple reason: they actually fit most sink configurations without custom work.

**What You Actually Get**

The kit arrives with two heavy-duty stainless steel brackets, each featuring a horizontal arm that spans the countertop underside and a vertical arm that grips the sink rim. The adjustment range is generous — roughly 12 to 24 inches horizontally and 4 to 8 inches vertically, which covers standard kitchen and bathroom undermount sinks. Included are stainless set screws, mounting screws, and a hex wrench. As shown in the product image, the brackets have a clean, low-profile design that won't interfere with most disposals or drain assemblies.

**Real-World Performance**

The install process is straightforward: position each bracket so the horizontal arm sits on the countertop substrate and the vertical arm presses against the sink's underside, then tighten the set screws. The stainless steel is genuinely thick — not the flimsy stamped metal you see on cheaper kits. One Amazon buyer put it plainly: "These brackets are solid. I installed them on a 28-inch farmhouse sink that had dropped 3/8 inch, and they pulled it back flush with the counter. Two months later, still tight."

The adjustability is the killer feature. Most competing products are fixed-size or use a flimsy threaded rod system. The CPT-4's sliding mechanism lets you dial in the exact tension needed without guesswork. The set screws bite into the metal firmly, and the brackets distribute load across a wide footprint rather than concentrating stress on one point.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest downsides: You need access from below the sink. If your cabinet has a fixed shelf or a garbage disposal directly under the sink rim, positioning the brackets becomes a contortionist act. Also, the set screws can work loose over time if your sink sees heavy use — a few users recommend checking tightness every six months. And the instructions are a single sheet with minimal detail. Watch a YouTube install video first; it'll save you 20 minutes of head-scratching.
