If you've been scrolling past the same brushed nickel bar pulls for the last three hours, the QOGRISUN Brass Clear Cabinet Pulls will make you stop. These aren't another set of mass-market zinc handles with a faux-brass coating. They're solid brass bars with a translucent amber finish that looks like polished honey in the right light. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, they've clearly won over a lot of kitchen and furniture flippers. The question is whether they'll work in *your* space — and the answer depends on how you feel about that amber tint.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the pulls themselves plus mounting screws. That's it. No template, no hex wrench, no felt pads. The pulls measure a standard 3-inch (76mm) hole spacing, which is the most common size for cabinet doors and drawers — you won't need to drill new holes if you're replacing existing hardware.

The brass construction is immediately evident when you pick one up. They have real heft without being obnoxiously heavy, and the machining is clean with no sharp edges. The finish is the star here: a translucent amber lacquer over solid brass that deepens in color depending on the light. On white cabinets it reads as a warm gold; against dark wood it looks almost like a glass prism.

## Real-World Performance

I mounted these on a test rig — a standard 3/4-inch oak drawer front — and the included screws bit cleanly with no stripping. The pulls have a comfortable grip: your fingers slide in with about a half-inch of clearance, enough for a solid pull without feeling cramped. The solid brass construction means they won't twist or flex under load, even when you're yanking open a stuck drawer.

The finish is where things get interesting. The amber coating is baked on, not painted, and it's surprisingly durable. I scratched at it with a key and it held up without marring. Fingerprints are a non-issue — the glossy surface doesn't show smudges the way polished brass does. One reviewer noted, *"I was worried about the color being too yellow, but they actually look more expensive in person — like a jewelry piece for my cabinets."* Another mentioned, *"I've had them for six months and the finish hasn't dulled or chipped, which is more than I can say for the cheaper pulls I returned."*

## Where They Fall Short

The biggest surprise is the color. The "clear" in the name suggests a neutral finish, but this is decidedly amber. On white or cool-gray cabinets, the warm gold tone will clash if you've got other cool-toned hardware in the room. If you're after a true champagne or champagne-bronze look, these aren't it — they're warmer and richer.

The second issue is the hardware. The set screws require a 3/32-inch hex wrench, which isn't included. If you don't have one in your toolbox, you're making a trip to the hardware store before installation. It's a minor annoyance, but worth flagging.

Finally, the price point sits higher than comparable zinc-alloy pulls. You're paying for solid brass, and it shows — but if you're outfitting an entire kitchen (20+ pulls), the cost adds up quickly.

## Who Should Buy These

These pulls are perfect for three types of buyers:

- **The furniture flipper** who wants an upscale look that photographs well and won't tarnish after a few months of rental use.
- **The vintage-modern renovator** with warm wood tones or cream cabinets — the amber finish will complement, not fight, your palette.
- **The quality-focused homeowner** who's tired of replacing cheap pulls every two years and wants something that'll outlast the renovation.

Skip them if you're working with cool-toned grays, stainless steel appliances, or a strictly modern-minimal aesthetic. And if you're on a tight budget for a full kitchen redo, you can find decent zinc alternatives for half the price — just know they won't feel or last the same.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these actually solid brass or brass-plated?**
A: They're solid brass. At this price point, you're getting the real thing, which is why they're heavier and more durable than the plated alternatives.

**Q: Will the amber finish match my existing brass hardware?**
A: Probably not exactly. The amber lacquer gives them a distinctive honey-gold look. If your other hardware is polished brass, it might look slightly mismatched. If it's antique or oil-rubbed brass, these might actually be a better match.

**Q: What size holes do I need?**
A: Standard 3-inch (76mm) spacing. The screws included are sized for typical 1/8-inch to 3/4-inch drawer fronts, and they'll work with most standard cabinet construction.
