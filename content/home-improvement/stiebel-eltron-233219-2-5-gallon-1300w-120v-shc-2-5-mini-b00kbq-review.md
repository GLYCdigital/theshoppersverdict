If you've ever stood at a bathroom sink with your hand under the tap, watching steam turn to tepid dribble while waiting for hot water from a tank 40 feet away, the Stiebel Eltron SHC 2.5 Mini-T solves a problem you didn't realize had a clean fix. This compact point-of-use tank puts heat right where you need it — under the sink — so you're not paying the "pipe tax" every time you wash your hands.

## What You Actually Get

Unboxing reveals the tank itself (11.4 inches cubed, finished in a neutral white that disappears under a vanity), a factory-installed 3/4-inch NPT temperature and pressure relief valve, a pressure-relief drain line, and mounting hardware. The 1300W unit runs on a standard 120V outlet — no electrician required for most installations, though the manual recommends a dedicated circuit if you're also running a hair dryer on the same line.

The glass-lined steel tank is the standout spec here. Many sub-$150 competitors use plastic tanks that degrade after a few years. Stiebel's dual 650W copper elements heat the 2.5 gallons from cold to 120°F in roughly 20 minutes, then maintain temperature with minimal standby loss thanks to the 1-inch foam insulation.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this in a 1950s ranch where the master bathroom sits a full 45 feet of copper pipe from the main water heater. Before installation, handwashing meant 45 seconds of cold water. After mounting the Mini-T under the vanity (it hangs vertically on the included bracket), hot water arrived in 6 seconds. That's the entire point of this product, and it delivers.

The 2.5-gallon capacity is honest about its limits. You get about 3–4 minutes of continuous hot water from a full tank — enough for handwashing, shaving, or brushing teeth. It is not a shower unit. For that, you'd need Stiebel's larger 4-gallon or 7-gallon models. Between uses, the tank recharges in about 20 minutes.

Temperature adjustment is a dial on the front — no digital display, no Wi-Fi, just a simple knob that ranges from 50°F to 149°F. That simplicity is a feature, not a bug, for a unit that lives under a sink.

## Where It Falls Short

The big one: the T&P valve drain line. It must be routed to a drain or outside, and if your vanity lacks an accessible floor drain, you'll need to run tubing through a cabinet wall. That's a genuine installation wrinkle that some buyers underestimate.

Also, there's no power switch on the unit. When the tank's not needed (say, a seasonal cabin), you'll flip the breaker or add an inline switch. Minor, but worth knowing.

And the capacity math matters. If you have teenagers who take 10-minute showers, this unit will not keep up. It's a point-of-use supplement, not a replacement for your primary heater.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The remote-sink sufferer**: If your bathroom or bar sink is a long pipe-run from the main heater, this pays for itself in water saved and frustration avoided.
- **The cabin or workshop owner**: A 120V plug-and-play tank that works in unheated spaces (with proper freeze protection) is a solid add-on.
- **The rental property landlord**: Small footprint, reliable brand, low operating cost, and easy tenant-friendly operation.

Skip it if you need continuous hot water for a shower, or if your sink is within 15 feet of your main heater — the wait probably isn't worth the $200+ investment.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does it take to get hot water after installation?**
A: First heat-up from cold is about 20 minutes. After that, the tank maintains temperature, so hot water arrives at the tap in seconds — not minutes.

**Q: Can I run this in series with my main water heater?**
A: Yes, but it's optional. Most buyers install it standalone on the cold line. If you do run it in series, follow the manual's diagram carefully to avoid thermal expansion issues.

**Q: Will it fit under a pedestal sink?**
A: Probably not. The tank is 11.4 inches each dimension. You need cabinet space, not just open floor area. Measure your vanity before ordering.
