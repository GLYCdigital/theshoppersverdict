Let's cut through the listing photos. These kadision step lights are line-voltage units, which means you're wiring them directly to your 120V household circuit — no transformer, no low-voltage driver, no extra junction box buried behind drywall. That's the headline feature, and for anyone who's wrestled with a 12V landscape lighting system, it's a genuine timesaver.

## What's actually in the box

Each fixture ships with the LED step light housing, a clear glass lens with a brushed aluminum trim ring, and mounting screws. The housing is die-cast aluminum — not the thin stamped metal you see at this price point — and the gasket around the lens feels substantial. The junction box is integrated into the fixture body, which is why these need a 3-inch deep recess. The glass lens is tempered, rated for direct foot traffic, which matters more than you'd think for a product mounted low on stairs.

## Real-world performance

The 3000K/4000K/5000K selector switch is on the side of the housing. Set it before you mount the fixture — reaching it after installation is a two-hand job with a screwdriver. I'd recommend 3000K for indoor hallways and warm wood tones, 4000K for garages and utility spaces, and 5000K for outdoor security-adjacent lighting where you want maximum perceived brightness.

Dimmability is the spec that separates this from cheaper step lights. The kadision works with standard TRIAC and ELV dimmers, and buyers report smooth dimming down to about 10% brightness. One reviewer noted: *"Paired with a Lutron Caseta dimmer, these go from a subtle nightlight glow to full brightness with zero flicker. My electrician was surprised they weren't low-voltage units."* That tracks with my research — the internal driver handles phase-cut dimming well, but older leading-edge dimmers can produce a faint hum at mid-levels. If you're using a dimmer from before ~2015, budget for a replacement.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 229 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **Ease of wiring** — Direct 120V hookup means no transformer sizing, no voltage drop calculations, no daisy-chaining limits. As one electrician-buyer put it: *"Installed 12 of these on a rental property stairwell. Wired them in parallel on a single dimmer circuit. Took one afternoon."*

2. **Brightness and beam spread** — The 60-degree beam pattern throws light down the tread, not into your eyes. Multiple reviewers mention that guests don't trip, which is the entire point of stair lighting.

3. **Moisture resistance** — The IP65 rating holds up in practice. Buyers in Florida and the Pacific Northwest report no fogging or corrosion after a full rainy season.

## Where it falls short

The biggest complaint is the color-temperature switch placement. It's a tiny DIP switch on the driver, accessible only before mounting. If you install it, decide on 3000K, then decide you want 4000K later, you're pulling the whole fixture out of the wall.

The 3-inch minimum box depth is a real constraint. Many retrofit stairwells have shallow pancake boxes. You'll need to either deepen the box or cut new openings. That's a dealbreaker for some DIYers expecting a drop-in swap.

Finally, the trim ring is secured with small set screws. They can strip if you over-tighten. Use a properly sized hex key and snug them gently.

## Who should buy these

- **Homeowners with existing 120V stairwell wiring** — If you already have a switch and junction box near your stairs, these are a direct replacement.
- **Contractors doing new construction or major renovations** — The line-voltage design cuts labor costs versus low-voltage systems that require a transformer and separate runs.
- **Anyone with a Lutron or newer TRIAC dimmer** — You'll get the full dimming range without compatibility headaches.

**Skip these if** you're working with shallow junction boxes, you don't have a neutral wire available (though most 120V boxes do), or you need a fixture smaller than 4.5 inches in height.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these be used outdoors in freezing temperatures?**
A: Yes, the operating range is -4°F to 104°F. The aluminum housing dissipates heat well, and the sealed lens prevents condensation. Just ensure the junction box is rated for wet locations and the conduit connections are properly sealed.

**Q: Do I need a special dimmer?**
A: Use a TRIAC or ELV dimmer rated for LED loads. Lutron Caseta, Maestro, and most modern dimmers work fine. Avoid old magnetic low-voltage dimmers. If you hear a hum at 50% brightness, swap the dimmer, not the light.

**Q: What's the actual power draw?**
A: Each fixture draws 6 watts. Twelve fixtures on a circuit pull 72 watts total — roughly equivalent to a single old incandescent bulb. Expect negligible impact on your electric bill.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want line-voltage simplicity, dimmable output, and selectable color temperature in a durable, wet-rated package. This is the right choice for permanent stair lighting where you don't want to manage a low-voltage transformer.

**Skip it if** your junction boxes are shallower than 3 inches, or you're dead-set on changing color temperature after installation. For those cases, look at low-voltage puck lights instead.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The kadision step lights deliver exactly what the spec sheet promises, with a few installation quirks that are manageable for anyone comfortable with basic electrical work. At this price point, they're the smart pick for line-voltage step lighting.
