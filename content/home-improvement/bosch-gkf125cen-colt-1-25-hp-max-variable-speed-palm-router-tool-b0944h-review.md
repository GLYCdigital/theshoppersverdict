# Bosch GKF125CEN Colt 1.25 HP (Max) Variable-Speed Palm Router Review

If you've ever fought a screaming, underpowered trim router that bogs down in oak or dances across your workpiece, you know the struggle. The Bosch Colt GKF125CEN is the antidote. With 235 Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars, this palm router has earned a loyal following—and after testing it against cheaper competitors, I understand why. It's not perfect, but for trim work, edge profiling, and sign making, it punches well above its 3.4-pound weight.

## What's in the Box

The GKF125CEN arrives as a bare router—no case, no bits. You get the motor unit, an adjustable aluminum sub-base, a clear acrylic base plate, two wrenches, a 1/4-inch collet (installed), and a 1/2-inch collet in a small bag. The edge guide and dust extraction adapter are sold separately, which feels stingy at this price point, but the included centering tool for the sub-base is a thoughtful touch you won't find on DeWalt or Ryobi equivalents.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature here is the variable-speed dial—16,000 to 35,000 RPM—paired with electronic speed control. That's not marketing fluff. When I routed 3/4-inch Baltic birch with a 3/8-inch roundover bit, the motor held its speed audibly and visually. Cheaper routers groan and slow; the Colt just cuts. The soft-start prevents the jerky torque snap that makes precision work nerve-wracking, and the 5.4-amp motor handles hard maple without bogging.

The aluminum sub-base is a genuine upgrade over plastic. It's flat, rigid, and the micro-adjustment screw lets you dial in bit depth to within a millimeter—critical for consistent dovetail or hinge mortise work. The clear base offers decent visibility, though dust collection is an afterthought. Hook up a shop vac via the optional adapter and it works, but you'll still get chips on the workpiece.

Ergonomics are the weak spot. The motor housing is sleek, but the on/off switch sits low and requires a deliberate reach. Users with larger hands (I wear XL gloves) will find it awkward mid-cut. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: "The switch is in a terrible spot. I have to take my hand off the grip to turn it off." That's a fair critique for a tool designed for one-handed operation.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across hundreds of reviews, three themes dominate. First, power-to-size ratio. A self-described hobbyist wrote, "I've used this for cabinet hinges, edge routing, and even some inlay work—it's replaced my full-size router for 90% of tasks." Second, the variable speed gets repeated love: "The speed control makes it usable on plastic and aluminum, not just wood." Third, build quality. Multiple buyers noted the aluminum base and metal collet nut feel premium compared to the plastic-heavy competition.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the switch placement, the collet system is a minor annoyance. The 1/4-inch collet is press-fit into the spindle; swapping to 1/2-inch requires loosening a retaining screw and a bit of finagling. It's not hard, but it's not the tool-free swap Bosch advertises on some pricier models. Also, no plunge base included. If you need plunge capability for inlay or pocket cuts, you'll either buy the GKF125CEN kit (which includes the plunge base and case for roughly $40 more) or shell out separately.

## Who Should Buy This

The DIY furniture builder who wants clean edge profiles and hinge mortises without wrestling a 7-pound plunge router. The cabinet installer who needs a reliable trim router that fits in a tool bag. The sign maker who appreciates variable speed for acrylic and aluminum. Skip it if you need a dedicated plunge router for deep mortises or dovetail jigs—look at the Bosch 1617EVSP instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the GKF125CEN accept standard 1/4-inch shank router bits?**
A: Yes, the included collet handles all standard 1/4-inch shank bits. The 1/2-inch collet is included but requires swapping.

**Q: Can I use this for dovetail joinery?**
A: With a dovetail jig and careful setup, yes—the variable speed and depth adjustment make it workable. But for frequent dovetail work, a dedicated plunge router is more comfortable.

**Q: How loud is it at full speed?**
A: At 35,000 RPM, it's loud—around 90 dB. Use hearing protection. The soft-start helps, but there's no noise reduction at speed.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a compact, powerful trim router that won't bog down in hardwood and offers genuine speed control. **Skip it if** you need plunge capability out of the box or hate fumbling for switches.

The Bosch GKF125CEN Colt earns its 4.7-star rating with real performance, honest build quality, and a few frustrating quirks. At 4.6/5, it's the best palm router under $150—just budget for the edge guide and a dust adapter.
