Let me be blunt about what the Veise Keyless Entry Door Lock with 2 Lever Handles actually is: a solid, no-nonsense electronic deadbolt that ships with matching lever handles in the box. It's not trying to be a smart home hub, it doesn't link to your phone, and there's no app to fiddle with. What it does — unlock with a code, lock itself when you want it to, and work reliably — it does well.

## What's Actually in the Box

This is where Veise earns points before you even touch a screwdriver. Most keypad locks require you to buy handles separately, which means matching finishes and drilling patterns. This kit includes:

- The keypad deadbolt assembly (exterior and interior units)
- Two lever handles with matching finish
- Three keys for the mechanical override
- All mounting hardware and strike plates
- Installation instructions that are actually readable

The lever handles are a genuine value-add. If you're replacing a full entry set on a single door, this is a one-box solution. The finish — available in matte black or satin nickel — looks decent in person, not cheap, and resists fingerprints reasonably well.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The keypad itself is the star. The numbers are backlit with a soft blue glow that activates when you touch the pad, making it usable at night without fumbling for a light switch. Code entry takes about two seconds, and the lock responds immediately — no lag, no second-guessing.

The auto-lock feature is configurable between 10 and 99 seconds. Set it to 30 seconds and you'll never drive away wondering if you locked the front door. This single feature is worth the price of admission for anyone with a "did I lock it?" habit.

One detail I appreciate: the lock accepts up to 50 unique user codes. That's enough for a family of four, a house cleaner, a dog walker, and a rotating cast of houseguests without reusing or deleting codes. Each code can be individually deleted, which is rare at this price point.

The mechanical key override is a genuine backup, not an afterthought. The keyhole is tucked discreetly beneath the keypad, and the included keys are solid metal, not flimsy stamped copies.

## Where It Falls Short

Now the honest part. This is not a smart lock. If you want to check lock status from your phone or get notified when your kid gets home from school, this isn't the product. There's no Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth, no app, no integration with Alexa or Google Home. It's purely a keypad lock.

Battery life is another consideration. The lock runs on four AA batteries, and Veise claims 8-10 months. That's realistic, not generous. The low-battery warning beeps when you enter a code, which is helpful, but it's on you to act on it.

Installation requires a standard 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" backset. If your door has an unusual configuration — a thicker door, a mortise lock, or a non-standard bore — expect extra work. One reviewer noted, "The included screws were a bit short for my 1.75-inch thick door, but a trip to the hardware store solved it."

## Who Should Buy This

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this lock without the keypad, just with keys?**
A: Yes. The keypad is the primary entry method, but the mechanical key override works independently. If the batteries die completely, the key still unlocks the deadbolt.

**Q: How hard is installation for a complete beginner?**
A: It's manageable. If you can handle a Phillips screwdriver and follow basic instructions, plan on 30-45 minutes. The hardest part is aligning the deadbolt latch with the strike plate, which takes patience more than skill.

**Q: Does the auto-lock work if the door isn't fully closed?**
A: No. The lock only engages when the latch is fully extended into the strike plate. If the door is ajar, the auto-lock timer won't activate the bolt — which is actually a safety feature, not a bug.
