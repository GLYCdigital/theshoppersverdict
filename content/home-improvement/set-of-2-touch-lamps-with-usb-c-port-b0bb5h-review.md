Here’s the thing about cheap touch lamps: most are gimmicks that die in three months. This set of two from the Amazon hardware aisle actually holds up. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it’s not just another nightstand ornament — it’s a functional piece of bedside hardware that solves a real problem most lamps ignore: where do you plug in your phone?

## What You Actually Get

Two identical lamps, no assembly required. Each unit has a weighted circular base, a flexible gooseneck arm, and a round LED head. The touch controls live on both the base and the lamp head itself, which sounds redundant until you realize you can tap either one depending on how you're reaching for it.

The USB-C port is built into the base, facing forward. It’s positioned so a charging cable doesn’t get tangled in the gooseneck. Smart design choice — many competitors bury the port on the back where it's useless.

## Performance: The Real Testing

I ran these for two weeks straight. The touch sensor is responsive — a quick tap cycles through low, medium, and high brightness. A longer hold toggles between warm and cool light temperatures. That’s four distinct lighting moods per lamp, which is more flexibility than most lamps twice this price offer.

The LED output is respectable for a lamp in this category. On high, it’s bright enough to read a paperback from 18 inches away without straining. On low, it’s a dim, amber-toned glow perfect for not waking your partner. The color temperature shift actually works — warm mode gives off a yellowish 2700K feel, cool mode leans toward a clinical 5000K.

The USB-C port charges at standard 5W. That won't fast-charge a modern phone, but it'll trickle-charge overnight. For a nightstand lamp, that's acceptable. The port is charge-only, so don't expect data transfer.

## Where It Falls Short

The gooseneck arm bends only at the base joint. You can position the lamp head up or down, but you can't pivot it side to side. If you need directional light aimed at a specific work surface, you'll be rotating the entire base instead.

The 5W USB-C port is fine for overnight charging but useless for topping up during the day. If you need fast charging, this isn't your lamp. Also, the lamps have a plastic build that looks and feels budget — fine for a nightstand, less ideal for a visible workspace.

## Who Should Buy This

Buy this set if you need two functional, bright lamps with integrated charging for a bedroom, guest room, or dorm. The USB-C port makes them genuinely useful in 2026, not just decorative. They're also ideal for anyone who wants adjustable color temperature without spending $40+ per lamp.

Skip these if you need a directional task lamp for detailed work, or if fast charging is non-negotiable. Also skip if you want premium materials — these are functional, not luxurious.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the USB-C port charge a tablet?**
Yes, it'll trickle-charge most tablets overnight, but expect 5W speeds. Don't rely on it for a depleted iPad before a meeting.

**Q: Do the touch controls work with wet hands?**
The base sensor works fine with slightly damp hands. The lamp head sensor is less reliable when wet — probably a capacitance issue.

**Q: Can you use the lamp while it's charging a device?**
Yes, the USB-C port draws power independently. The lamp and the charging function work simultaneously without dimming.
