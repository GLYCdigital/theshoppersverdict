## Stop Basement Sweat Without Locking Moisture In

Most concrete sealers are liars. They paint a plastic film on your basement floor, look great for six months, then peel and flake when hydrostatic pressure pushes moisture up from below. RadonSeal Plus takes a different approach—it's a deeply penetrating sealer designed for below-grade concrete where the real water problems live. If you're tired of damp floors, musty smells, and efflorescence dusting your workshop tools, this is worth serious consideration. But at $254 for a 5-gallon pail, it's not an impulse buy.

## What's Actually in the Pail

You get 5 gallons of clear, water-based lithium silicate solution—enough to cover roughly 500–1,000 square feet depending on your concrete's porosity. Unlike surface sealers that sit on top, this reacts chemically with the free lime in concrete to form a crystalline structure inside the pores. The product image shows the standard industrial pail, but the magic is what you can't see: the active ingredients penetrating up to 6 inches deep.

Application is straightforward: spray, roll, or brush it on, then keep the surface damp for 20–30 minutes with a pump sprayer while it absorbs. One coat is usually enough. You'll need gloves, eye protection, and a low-pressure sprayer—none of which are included.

## Real-World Performance

Where this sealer shines is on interior basement floors and foundation walls that suffer from chronic dampness—not active flooding. It stops water vapor transmission, which is what causes that "sweaty" concrete feel and creates ideal conditions for mold. It also addresses efflorescence (those white powdery salt deposits) by blocking the moisture path that carries salts to the surface.

The breathability factor is the game-changer. Surface films can trap moisture inside the slab, leading to freeze-thaw damage in colder climates. RadonSeal's crystalline matrix allows vapor to escape while blocking liquid water. That's a massive advantage for attached garages, crawl spaces, and finished basements.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. This is a preventative and vapor-blocking treatment, not a structural repair. If you have active cracks or water seeping through joints, you'll need to patch those first with hydraulic cement or an epoxy injection—otherwise, you're sealing a sieve.

The price point is the other sticking point. At $254, this is better suited for whole basements and larger projects. For a single small room, you'd be better off finding a quart or gallon size, though those are harder to source. Also, the cure time is 72 hours before you can paint or put down flooring—plan your project timeline accordingly.

## FAQ

**Will RadonSeal Plus stop water from leaking through my basement wall?**
Only if that water is coming through the concrete itself as vapor or dampness. If you have visible water flowing through cracks or at the wall-floor joint, you need to repair those penetrations first. This seals the concrete matrix, not the gaps.

**Can I apply this over previously sealed or painted concrete?**
No. It needs to penetrate bare, uncoated concrete. You'll need to strip any existing sealers, paints, or coatings before application. A simple acid etch or mechanical grinding usually does the trick.

**How long does one application last?**
Because it becomes part of the concrete structure rather than a surface coating, it's essentially permanent. Most manufacturers and users report 20+ years of effectiveness, assuming the slab itself doesn't experience new structural damage.
