**The Review**

You know that moment when you're under a car, rusted bolt staring you down, and your cheap chrome socket rounds it off? That's the exact scenario the NEIKO 02440A was built to prevent. This is a 13-piece SAE impact socket set in 3/8-inch drive, and at a 4.8-star average from 240 buyers, it's clearly earning its keep in garages across the country. The question isn't whether it's a good set — it's whether it's the right set for *your* toolbox.

**What You Actually Get**

The set covers SAE sizes from 3/8-inch up to 7/8-inch, which is the practical range for most automotive and home repair work. Each socket is forged from chrome-vanadium steel — that's the same material used in pro-grade brands — and finished with a black phosphate coating that resists rust far better than the shiny chrome on standard sockets. The six-point design is critical here: it wraps completely around the fastener head rather than grabbing corners, which means less rounding and more torque transfer.

As shown in the product image, everything comes in a molded plastic case with each socket's size stamped clearly on both the socket and the case. No hunting for the right size mid-job.

**Real-World Performance**

The sockets handle impact driver abuse without cracking or deforming. The 3/8-inch drive is the sweet spot for DIYers — thick enough for lug nuts and suspension work, compact enough for tight engine bays. The black coating holds up well; it shows wear on the contact surfaces after heavy use, but that's cosmetic, not functional.

Buyers consistently mention the value proposition. One reviewer noted, *"I use these professionally and they've held up better than sets I paid three times as much for."* Another praised the clear size markings: *"No more squinting at tiny etched numbers. These are stamped deep and easy to read."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the gaps. First, this is SAE only — if you work on any import vehicle made after 1990, you're buying a metric set too. Second, the range skips 1/4-inch, so smaller fasteners aren't covered. Third, the case is utilitarian plastic; it'll survive a drop but won't win any beauty contests, and the latch feels like the weakest link in an otherwise solid package.
