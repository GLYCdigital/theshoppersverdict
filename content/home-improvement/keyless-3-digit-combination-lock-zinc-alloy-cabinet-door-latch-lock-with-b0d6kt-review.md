**Opening: Why this latch earns a spot in your workshop**

Most keyless cabinet locks under $20 are flimsy die-cast zinc with paint that flakes off by week two. This one isn't that. The 3-digit combo lock from the B0D6KT line uses a proper zinc alloy body with a brushed satin finish that actually feels machined, not stamped. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's not perfect — but for securing a gun cabinet, tool chest, or that liquor cabinet your teenagers keep raiding, it earns its place.

**What's in the box**

You get the latch body, the strike plate, mounting screws (four wood screws, two machine screws), and an instruction sheet. The body measures roughly 4.5" x 2" x 1.5" — compact enough for standard frame-and-panel cabinet doors. The combo dials are about 0.75" in diameter, slightly recessed to prevent accidental bumps from changing your code. No batteries, no wiring, no app. That's the point — this is a mechanical lock that can't be hacked or drained.

**Real-world performance**

I mounted this on a 3/4" solid oak cabinet door with a 2-3/8" backset. Installation took about 12 minutes with a drill and a 1" hole saw. The bolt throws a clean 5/16" into the strike plate, which is adequate for light security — think "keep honest people honest," not "stop a crowbar." The combo reset is genuinely tool-free: open the latch, slide the reset tab, spin in your new code, close. Takes four seconds.

One detail I appreciated: the dials have a positive click at each number. They're not mushy like cheaper locks. But the detents are light — if you spin the dials quickly, you can blow past your number and land on the adjacent one. Slow and deliberate is the move here.

The 4.3-star average holds up when you read through the reviews. A recurring theme is the build quality. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've bought three of these. **Where it falls short**

Two issues keep this from a 5-star rating. First, the short bolt travel. If your cabinet door is even slightly warped or your strike plate isn't perfectly aligned, the bolt won't seat fully and the latch won't close. You'll need to shim the strike plate or adjust the door hinges. Second — and this is the big one — there's no key override. Forget your combo and you're drilling out the lock body. The zinc alloy is tough, so that's a 20-minute job with a carbide bit. Write your code down somewhere safe.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I change the combination after installation?**
Yes. Open the latch, slide the reset tab on the back, dial in a new code, and close. No tools required.

**Q: Will this fit a right-handed or left-handed door?**
Both. The latch body is reversible — just flip it 180 degrees before mounting.

**Q: What's the maximum door thickness?**
It accommodates doors from 3/4" to 1-1/4" thick. Anything thicker and the bolt won't reach the strike plate.
