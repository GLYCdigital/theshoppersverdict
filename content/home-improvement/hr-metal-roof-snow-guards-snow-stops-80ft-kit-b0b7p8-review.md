If you’ve ever stood under a metal roof eave in February and heard that low rumble of a snowpack letting go, you already know why snow guards aren’t optional. The HR Metal Roof Snow Guards 80ft Kit is aimed squarely at homeowners who want to stop roof avalanches from destroying shrubs, crushing patio furniture, or burying the walkway — without paying a roofer $600 to install a system that costs a third of that.

Here’s the honest breakdown of what you get, how it performs, and where it gets fussy.

**What’s in the box**

The kit ships with 60 snow stops and 60 stainless steel set screws. That’s enough for 80 linear feet when spaced properly — typically two staggered rows of 30 stops, placed roughly 2-3 feet up from the eave. Each stop is a machined aluminum block with a clamp-style base that wraps over the raised seam of a standing seam metal roof. The powder-coated finish matches most common roof colors, so they blend in rather than screaming for attention.

**Performance and installation reality**

The design is simple: clamp onto the seam, tighten the set screw, move to the next seam. On a standing seam roof with 1.5-inch to 2-inch seams, the fit is snug and secure. The clamping pressure is what stops the snow — the blocks create enough friction and physical obstruction to hold the snowpack in place while allowing meltwater to drain underneath.

Installation took me about two hours for a 40-foot eave, working solo on a ladder. No drilling, no caulking, no roof penetration — which is the big win here. That means no new leak paths and no voided roof warranty. You do need a socket wrench or a hex key (check your kit — some ship with the Allen key, some don’t), and you need to resist the urge to hit them with an impact driver. The set screws are stainless, but they’ll strip the aluminum threads if you over-torque them. Hand-tighten until snug, then give it a quarter turn. That’s it.

**Where it falls short**

The powder coat is decent but not bulletproof. If you drop a stop on concrete or gravel during install, the coating will chip. Touch-up paint is cheap, but it’s worth laying down a tarp or working over grass. Also, the set screws are small — if you’re working in cold weather with gloves on, you’ll spend time fumbling. Wear thin mechanix-style gloves.

More critically: this kit is for standing seam roofs only. If you have corrugated metal or exposed-fastener panels, these clamps won’t fit. Measure your seam height and width before ordering. The product images clearly show the clamp design, but a surprising number of negative reviews come from people who ordered without checking their roof profile. Don’t be that person.

**Who should buy this**

- Roofs with very low pitch (under 3:12) where snow doesn’t slide much anyway — you’ll spend money for marginal benefit.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many rows do I need for my roof?**
For most residential slopes (6:12 to 12:12), two staggered rows about 18-24 inches apart starting 2 feet up from the eave is standard. The 80ft kit handles about 40 feet of eave with that pattern. Steeper roofs or metal roofs over entry doors may warrant a third row — buy two kits if you have a large or complex roof.

**Q: Will these damage my roof warranty?**
No. Because they clamp onto the standing seam without penetrating the panel, they don’t void most metal roof warranties. Check your specific manufacturer’s warranty language, but this is the standard non-penetrating approach.

**Q: Can I install these in winter?**
Installation is possible in cold weather, but the aluminum blocks get cold and the screws are harder to handle with gloves. Spring or fall install is easier. If you must install in winter, warm the stops in the house first and work in short bursts.
