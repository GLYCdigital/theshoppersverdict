## What you actually get

The unit is compact — about the size of a multimeter — with a rubberized grip and a flexible 15-inch gooseneck probe. The probe is the star here. It holds its shape when bent, so you can snake it behind a gas stove, around a water heater's burner assembly, or into the tight corners of an RV's propane compartment without contorting yourself.

It runs on 3 AAA batteries (not included), and there's a two-mode switch for LPG (propane/butane) versus natural gas (methane). The LED display shows relative concentration via a bar graph, and the beeper escalates from a slow tick to an urgent chirp as the leak gets worse. There's also a mute button for noisy environments — though you'll want the sound on for initial sweeps.

## Real-world performance

The sensitivity rating is 50 PPM for methane and 500 PPM for propane. In practice, that means this detector will find a leak you can't smell — most people can't detect natural gas odorant below ~1,000 PPM. The auto-calibration takes about 30 seconds and happens fresh every time you power on, which is smarter than the older units that drifted after a few uses.

One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"Found a leak at the regulator connection on my RV that the soap-and-water test missed. Saved me a big headache."* That matches my experience — the gooseneck probe lets you get right up against suspect fittings, and the escalating beep makes it obvious when you're closing in.

Where it falls short: this is not a meter. It gives you relative concentration, not a precise PPM readout. If you're a professional HVAC tech who needs to document exact leak rates for code compliance, you need a $150+ electronic leak detector with digital readout. This one's for finding the leak, not qualifying it.

## Honest downsides

The battery door is the biggest annoyance — it's secured with a Phillips screw. You'll need to keep a screwdriver handy or leave the door off entirely (not recommended). Also, there's no case included, so the probe tip is exposed when it's tossed in a tool bag. A 3/16" heat-shrink tube over the tip will protect it, but that's an owner modification.

The audible alarm shares a speaker with the beep — there's no separate loud buzzer for high concentrations. On a noisy job site, you might miss the escalation. And the sensitivity switch for LPG versus natural gas is a small slide toggle that's easy to bump accidentally.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this detect carbon monoxide?**
No. This detects combustible gases (methane, propane, butane) only. You need a separate CO detector for carbon monoxide.

**Q: How do I know if it's working?**
Test it against a gas stove burner — hold the probe near the unlit burner with the gas on briefly. The beep should escalate immediately. The auto-calibration also gives you confidence it's zeroed correctly.

**Q: Is it safe to use around gas appliances?**
Yes — the detector is non-sparking and the probe is plastic-tipped. Just follow standard practice: don't smoke, and ventilate the area if you do find a leak.
