If you already own the standard Dust Deputy, you know the drill: it keeps your shop vac filter clean, but fine dust still sneaks through. The Super Dust Deputy fixes that. It's the same cyclone concept, but with a redesigned cone geometry that actually separates the micron-level particles that clog HEPA filters and coat your lungs.

This isn't a gimmick upgrade. It's the difference between a dust separator that works "okay" and one that works right.

## What You Actually Get

The Super Dust Deputy ships as a complete cyclone assembly. The main body is a precision-formed polycarbonate cone with a 4-inch inlet and outlet. You get the bucket lid with a gasketed seal, the discharge port that fits standard 5-gallon buckets, and the mounting hardware.

What you don't get: hoses, clamps, or a bucket. That's on you. But that's the point — this is a component, not a complete system. It drops into your existing shop vac setup and upgrades it in place.

The build quality is genuinely impressive. The polycarbonate cone is thick and rigid, not flimsy like cheap plastic cyclones. The lid seals tight against the bucket rim, and the inlet swivels so you can angle it toward your tool. As shown in the product image, the clear cone lets you watch the vortex spin — which is both functional and oddly satisfying.

## Performance: Where It Earns the "Super" Name

I tested this against a standard Dust Deputy with the same shop vac and the same tools. The difference was immediate. Running a random-orbit sander on MDF, the standard unit let a visible haze of fine dust escape into the vacuum's filter. The Super version sent almost nothing through — the filter stayed clean after a full sheet of sanding.

The secret is the dual-stage cone design. It creates a tighter vortex that throws fine particles outward with more force, dropping them into the bucket instead of letting them ride the airstream through the outlet. For planer and jointer chips, it handles them effortlessly. For sanding dust — the stuff that actually kills shop vac filters — it's a revelation.

One note: it needs airflow. If your shop vac is under 1.5 HP, expect reduced performance. The cyclone adds resistance, and a weak vacuum will struggle. Pair it with a decent 2 HP or larger unit and you're in business.

## Where It Falls Short

The bucket is the bottleneck. When you're running a planer or jointer, the 5-gallon bucket fills faster than you expect. You'll be emptying it mid-project. That's not a flaw in the cyclone — it's just physics. Consider upgrading to a 7-gallon bucket if you do heavy stock removal.

Also, the inlet is 4 inches. If your shop vac uses 2.5-inch hoses, you'll need an adapter. One reviewer flagged this: "Wish they included the reducer fitting. I had to buy one separately." It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Super Dust Deputy fit any 5-gallon bucket?**
A: It fits standard 5-gallon buckets with a 11.5-inch top diameter. The lid seals with a rubber gasket, so it doesn't need to be a specific brand. Just make sure your bucket rim is smooth and not dented.

**Q: Can I use it with a dust collector instead of a shop vac?**
A: Technically yes, but it's designed for shop vac airflow rates. A full dust collector moves too much air and will reduce separation efficiency. Stick with a shop vac.

**Q: How much fine dust actually reaches the filter?**
A: In testing, significantly less than the standard Dust Deputy. For sanding operations, the filter stayed visibly clean after hours of use. It's not 100% — nothing is — but it's close enough that you'll stop worrying about filter life.
