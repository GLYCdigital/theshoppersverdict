## What You Actually Get

The box contains the assembled tongs — no assembly required, which is rare in this category. The tool measures roughly 28 inches from handle top to jaw tip, with a 12-inch jaw opening that closes down to about 2 inches. The handle is wrapped in a textured grip that stays put even with work gloves on. The jaws are forged from heat-treated carbon steel with a black oxide finish that resists rust better than the painted competitors.

The mechanics are simple: squeeze the handle, jaws open; release, and the weight of the log locks the grip. There's a locking latch near the handle pivot that keeps the jaws clamped when you set a log down — a small feature that prevents accidental drops when you're stacking.

## Performance and Real-World Use

This is where the HOTYELL earns its keep. The jaw teeth are aggressive — they bite into bark and hold. One reviewer noted, "I've used cheap tongs that slipped on wet oak. These dig in and don't let go." That matches the design: the jaws are angled with a self-tightening cam action, so the heavier the log, the harder the grip.

For splitting sessions, the 28-inch handle is the sweet spot. It keeps your lower back straight when lifting rounds off the ground, and the balance point lets you carry a 20-pound log with one hand without the handle tipping backward. The rated 330-pound capacity is generous — you'll hit the jaw spread limit before you hit the weight limit.

The locking mechanism is genuinely useful. When you're staging logs for the splitter, you can clamp a log, lock the jaws, and carry it without maintaining constant squeeze pressure. It's a small thing that makes a full cord of wood feel less like a workout.

## Where It Falls Short

The sharp jaw tips are the main complaint. They'll scratch concrete patios, garage floors, and wood decks if you drag the tongs while carrying. You need to lift the log clear of surfaces — not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

The jaw spread maxes out around 16 inches. For oversized rounds, you'll need to reposition the tongs or use a different tool. Also, the black oxide finish will wear at the contact points, which is cosmetic but inevitable with heavy use.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You process firewood regularly — even a few cords a season justifies the price. You have back issues or want to avoid bending. You split by hand or with a hydraulic splitter and need to move logs from pile to splitter to stack.

**Skip it if:** You only move a few logs a month, or you work exclusively with oversized rounds over 16 inches. In those cases, a cant hook or a larger pair of log tongs with a wider throat makes more sense.

## FAQ

**Will these work on wet or frozen logs?** Yes — the aggressive teeth grip bark even when wet. Frozen logs are fine, though you may need to initially seat the jaws harder to penetrate the surface ice.

**Can I use them one-handed?** For logs under 20 pounds, yes. Heavier logs require two hands for control, especially on uneven ground.

**Do they rust if left outside?** The black oxide finish offers some protection, but like any carbon steel tool, they'll develop surface rust if left in the elements. Wipe them down after use and store them dry.
