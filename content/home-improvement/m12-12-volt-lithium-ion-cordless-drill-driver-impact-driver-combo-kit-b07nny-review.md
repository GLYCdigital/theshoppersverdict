If you've ever wrestled a full-size 18V drill into a cabinet hinge screw or tried to drive a 3-inch deck screw one-handed while balancing on a ladder, you already know why the M12 system exists. This compact duo from Milwaukee is the answer to "I need power, but I need it small."

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting two tools: the M12 drill/driver and the M12 impact driver. Both share the same battery platform, which is the real story here. The kit ships with two 2.0 Ah batteries, a charger, and a contractor bag. No hard case — that's fine, honestly; the bag takes less truck space.

The drill/driver has a 1/2-inch single-sleeve metal chuck, which is a rarity at this size. Most 12V drills cheap out with plastic or 3/8-inch chucks. The impact driver uses the standard 1/4-inch hex collet with a push-in release — no fiddling with a chuck key.

## Performance That Surprises

Here's the thing about 12V tools: the torque numbers look unimpressive on paper until you actually use them. This drill puts out 350 in-lbs of torque, and the impact driver cranks 1,000 in-lbs. For context, that impact driver will sink a 3/4-inch structural screw into pressure-treated lumber without pre-drilling. I've done it.

The drill handles up to 1/2-inch bits in softwood and 3/8-inch in steel without bogging down. The two-speed gearbox gives you the standard 0-450 RPM for driving and 0-1,750 RPM for drilling. The 18-position clutch is genuinely useful — you'll find yourself dialing it down for small screws so you don't strip out soft materials.

The LED lights on both tools are positioned at the base of the chuck, so they actually illuminate the work area instead of casting shadows from your hand. It's a small detail that makes a big difference in a dark cabinet.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. This is not a replacement for an 18V system. If you're drilling 1-inch auger bits through wet lumber or driving 6-inch lags all day, you'll want the bigger platform. The 2.0 Ah batteries also drain faster under sustained impact driving — plan for charging breaks on bigger projects.

The included charger is the standard model, not the rapid charger. A full charge from empty takes about 60 minutes. That's not terrible, but it's not the 30-minute fast charge you can get with the upgraded charger.

Also worth noting: no belt clip included. You'll want to pick one up if you're a professional carrying these all day.

## Who Should Buy This

This kit is perfect for three types of people:

**DIY homeowners** who are tired of fighting corded drills or wrestling oversized cordless tools into tight spaces. The M12 platform grows with you — buy more tools later without new batteries.

**Apartment dwellers and small-space workers** who value the compact footprint. These tools disappear into a drawer or small tool bag.

**Electricians, HVAC techs, and cabinet installers** who work in confined spaces. The sub-compact size is the whole point — you're buying this because full-size tools don't fit where you work.

Skip it if you're doing heavy framing, deck building, or any sustained high-torque work. That's what the M18 platform is for.
