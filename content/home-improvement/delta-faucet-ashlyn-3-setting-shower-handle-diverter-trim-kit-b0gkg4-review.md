If you’re remodeling a shower and want a clean, modern handle that actually switches between spray modes without wrestling with a stiff knob, the Delta Ashlyn 3-Setting Diverter Trim Kit deserves a hard look. It’s not the cheapest trim on the shelf, but it’s one of the few that feels like it was designed by someone who’s actually used a shower before.

**What you’re actually getting**  
The box contains the handle, the escutcheon (wall plate), a mounting ring, and the set screws. That’s it. As the product image shows, there’s no valve body inside—this is purely the trim. You’ll need a compatible Delta rough-in valve (like the R10000 series) or an existing 1/2-inch IPS shower arm. If you’re replacing an old two-handle setup, budget for the valve separately. The trim itself is all metal—no cheap plastic internals—and the chrome finish has a satin sheen that hides hard-water residue better than the glossy stuff.

**Real-world performance**  
I installed this on a Delta valve that was already in place, and the swap took about 15 minutes. The handle slides onto the spline and locks with a hex screw—no special tools beyond an Allen wrench. The three settings (rain, massage, and a focused jet) click into place with a satisfying, positive stop. No guessing whether you’re halfway between modes. Water pressure stays consistent across all three, which isn’t always true with cheaper diverter trims that bleed flow.

One thing I noticed: the handle is a touch shorter than the standard Delta handle. If you have large hands or arthritis, it’s a bit small to grip firmly when wet. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**What buyers keep saying**  
Across 235 ratings (4.7 stars), the praise is consistent. One verified buyer wrote, *“Replaced a 20-year-old Delta handle. This one fit the same spline, looked better, and the detents are much crisper.”* Another noted, *“The matte finish doesn’t show soap scum like my old polished chrome did.”* The most common complaint? People who didn’t read the listing and expected a complete valve. One frustrated reviewer said, *“Wish the description made it clearer you need the rough-in valve.”* That’s on the listing, not the product, but it’s a real gotcha.

**Where it falls short**  
Besides the missing valve body, the screws are small and easy to lose—keep them in the baggie until you’re ready. Also, the escutcheon is flat, so if your wall tile isn’t perfectly plumb, you’ll see a slight gap. A bead of silicone fixes it, but it’s an extra step.

**Who should buy this**  
- **DIYers replacing an existing Delta trim**—this is a drop-in upgrade if you already have a Delta valve.  
- **Anyone who wants a clean, modern look** without changing plumbing.  
- **People with hard water**—the matte finish genuinely resists spotting.

**Skip it if** you’re starting from scratch and need a full valve assembly, or if you prefer a lever handle over a knob.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Will this fit my old Delta valve?**  
A: If your valve uses a 1/2-inch IPS connection and a standard spline, almost certainly yes. Delta has kept the same spline design for decades. Measure the spline diameter to be sure.

**Q: Does the trim kit include the cartridge?**  
A: No. You need the valve body and cartridge separately. This is strictly the handle and wall plate.

**Q: Can I use this with a shower/tub combo?**  
A: Yes, but you’ll need a diverter spout—the trim only controls showerhead modes, not tub flow.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you’re upgrading an existing Delta shower and want a crisp, reliable diverter with a modern finish. **Skip it if** you need a complete valve system. At 4.6/5, it’s a well-built, honest product that does exactly what it claims—just read the box before you buy.
