**Where It Falls Short**

Honest tradeoffs matter here. First, brightness: alabaster filters light significantly. With standard 40-watt equivalent bulbs, these read as mood lighting, not task illumination. Plan on 60-watt equivalent LED bulbs if you need to see clearly while shaving or applying makeup.

Second, the size discrepancy. The product photos make these look substantial, but they're compact. On a double vanity, two fixtures spaced three feet apart can look sparse. Measure your space and consider whether you need one sconce per sink or two flanking a single mirror.

Third, the natural stone factor cuts both ways. Each shade has unique veining, which is charming — but it also means one shade might be more translucent than the other. If you're a perfectionist, this inconsistency might bother you.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Can I use these outdoors or in a steamy bathroom?**
These are rated for damp locations, meaning they handle bathroom humidity fine. Not rated for direct outdoor exposure.

**Q: What bulb type works best?**
E12 candelabra base. For the softest glow, use frosted bulbs — clear bulbs show the filament through the stone. LED bulbs with a color temperature around 2700K preserve the warm alabaster tone best.

**Q: How do I clean the alabaster shades?**
Dry dusting with a soft cloth is your primary maintenance. Wipe gently with a slightly damp cloth for stubborn spots, then dry immediately. Avoid chemical cleaners — they'll dull the stone's natural finish.
