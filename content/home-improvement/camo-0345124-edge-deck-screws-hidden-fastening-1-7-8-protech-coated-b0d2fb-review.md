If you're laying down composite or hardwood decking and want a fastener-free surface, the CAMO Edge system has become the go-to for a reason. The 0345124 Pro screws are the consumable half of that equation — and at 1-7/8 inches, they're sized for the most common deck board thicknesses. But here's what you need to know before you commit.

## What you're actually getting

The box contains 500 ProTech-coated, #8 square-drive screws. The ProTech finish is a ceramic-epoxy blend that's specifically formulated to resist the corrosive environment of pressure-treated lumber. The head design is what makes the system work: it's offset, which lets the screw bite into the board's edge at an angle while the CAMO Marksman tool holds the board in place.

These are Type 17 points, which means they self-drill. On cedar, I didn't even need a pilot hole. On ipe or other tropical hardwoods, you'll still want to pre-drill regardless of what the marketing says.

## Real-world performance

The standout feature is the drive engagement. The square-drive socket in the head is deep and precise — the bit clicks in with authority, not that sloppy wobble you get from cheap screws. With a brushless impact driver on medium setting, I drove about 200 of these without a single stripped head or broken shaft.

The hidden fastening itself is solid. The screw pulls the board laterally into the joist while the tool maintains consistent spacing. The result is a deck surface with zero visible fasteners and a uniform 3/16-inch gap — exactly what you want for drainage and expansion.

One caveat: these screws are designed to sink at an angle into the board edge. If you're using them with anything thinner than 5/4 decking, the angle can cause the tip to surface on the top face. Stick with the intended thickness range.

## Where they fall short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the system lock-in. These screws are engineered specifically for CAMO's edge-fastening tools. You can drive them like regular screws, but then you're paying a premium for a feature you're not using.

Second, the loose packaging. They're not collated on a strip, so you're handling each screw individually. On a 400-square-foot deck, that's roughly 800 screws to load into the bit one at a time. It's not a dealbreaker, but it does slow you down versus a coil-fed system.

Third, the price. You're looking at roughly 15-20% more per fastener than quality structural screws. The hidden-fastener look is worth it for most people, but if you're on a tight budget and don't mind visible screws, you can save money elsewhere.

## Who should buy these

**The composite deck builder** — these are ideal for Trex, TimberTech, and similar materials where surface screws are unsightly and prone to mushrooming.

**The hardwood enthusiast** — if you're working with ipe, cumaru, or garapa, the ProTech coating and sharp point make a real difference in driving effort and fastener life.

**The renovation contractor** — if you're doing a deck every few weeks, the consistency and lack of stripped heads will save you time and callback headaches.

Skip them if you're doing a single DIY project with standard 2x4 framing and visible screws don't bother you. The tool investment won't pay off for one-time use.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these screws with the CAMO Edge Deck system?**
A: Yes — that's exactly what they're designed for. They work with both the Marksman Pro and the Edge Deck tool. The 1-7/8" length suits 5/4 deck boards and 1" composite boards.

**Q: Do I need to pre-drill with these?**
A: For softwoods like cedar, pine, and most composites, no. The Type 17 point self-drills cleanly. For dense tropical hardwoods, pre-drilling is recommended to prevent splitting and reduce drive torque.

**Q: Will they rust on a treated lumber deck?**
A: The ProTech coating is specifically tested against ACQ and other copper-based treatments. Under normal conditions, you won't see corrosion. In coastal salt air, no fastener is truly immune — but these hold up better than standard coated screws.
