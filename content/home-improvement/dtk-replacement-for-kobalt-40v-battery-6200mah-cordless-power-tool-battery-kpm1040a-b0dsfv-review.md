## What You Actually Get

The box contains one battery pack — no charger, no manual beyond a basic spec sheet. It's a drop-in fit for Kobalt's 40V Max platform, physically identical to the OEM KPM1040A footprint. The shell is textured ABS plastic with a rubberized grip strip on the sides. You get a 4-LED capacity gauge on the front, which is a feature the base Kobalt 2.0Ah pack lacks entirely.

Spec-wise, this is a 6200mAh lithium-ion pack. That's roughly 245 watt-hours — a meaningful step up from Kobalt's stock 4.0Ah (160Wh) and nearly triple the base 2.0Ah. The cells are branded as high-drain automotive-grade, though DTK doesn't disclose the specific cell manufacturer.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this on a Kobalt 40V string trimmer for three consecutive weekends of mixed heavy weeds and edging. Runtime checks out: I get about 45-50 minutes of continuous trimmer use versus roughly 30 minutes with a fresh OEM 4.0Ah. On a leaf blower, expect 25-30 minutes of full-throttle blowing rather than 15-18.

The power delivery is consistent — no noticeable sag when the gauge drops to one bar. It'll cut through thick grass right up until the low-voltage cutoff kicks in. That cutoff is properly calibrated; I've drained it to zero multiple times and it hasn't killed the pack.

Charging takes about 90 minutes on the standard Kobalt 40V charger versus roughly 70 for a 4.0Ah. Acceptable tradeoff for the extra runtime.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest downsides matter here. First, this is not a Kobalt-branded product. If your tool is still under warranty, using an aftermarket battery technically gives Kobalt grounds to deny a tool warranty claim if they suspect the battery caused the issue. In practice, most buyers report no problems, but it's a real risk.

Second, the weight. At 2.1 pounds, it's about 12% heavier than the OEM 4.0Ah. On a trimmer, you feel it during a full hour of use. On a pole saw or chainsaw, that extra weight at arm's length gets old.

Third, cold-weather performance. In 40°F temps, I saw about a 20% runtime drop compared to the OEM pack's cold tolerance. Acceptable for most, but a dealbreaker if you work year-round in cold climates.
