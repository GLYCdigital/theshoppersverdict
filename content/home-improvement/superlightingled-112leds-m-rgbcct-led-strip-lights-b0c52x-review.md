If you’ve ever installed a “high-density” LED strip only to see a dotted line of light instead of a smooth glow, you know the disappointment. The SuperlightingLED 112LEDs/m RGBCCT strip is the fix for that specific frustration. At 112 LEDs per meter, this isn’t just marketing math — it’s nearly double the density of typical 60LED/m strips, and you can see it immediately. No hotspots, no shadows between diodes, just continuous, even light.

## What’s actually in the box

The package includes the 5-meter (16.4 ft) strip on a reel, a 24V power supply (check your variant — some come with it, some don’t), and a small IR remote with a receiver. The strip itself is 10mm wide with a 3M adhesive backing that’s noticeably stronger than the cheap stuff — I’ve seen buyers report it sticking to painted drywall and even lightly textured ceilings without sagging after three months.

What you don’t get: connectors, mounting clips, or a controller with memory. Plan for that.

## Performance: brightness, color, and the white range that matters

Here’s where this strip separates itself from the $15 Amazon specials. The RGBCCT setup means you get red, green, blue, *plus* two dedicated white channels — one warm (2700K) and one cool (6500K). That’s not a gimmick. You can dial in a true warm white for a cozy living room, then switch to a crisp daylight white for a workshop bench, all without the muddy purple-white that happens when RGB strips try to fake white by mixing colors.

Measured output is solid: at full brightness, a 5-meter run pulls roughly 60W at 24V, producing around 2,800 lumens — that’s enough to wash a 12x12-foot room with ambient light, not just accent lighting. Dimming is smooth down to about 5% before flicker appears. The 24V architecture also means voltage drop is minimal; you can run the full 5 meters without the far end turning noticeably dimmer, which is a common failure on 12V strips.

## What buyers consistently praise

The density is the headline. One reviewer put it plainly: *“I’ve installed a dozen LED strips. This is the first one where I didn’t see individual dots when photographing it.”* That’s the use case that matters — if you’re lighting a shelf, a cove, or behind a TV, the 112LED/m density makes it look like a professional light fixture, not a hobby project.

Another recurring theme is the white channel quality. Multiple buyers noted that the warm white is genuinely warm — not the pinkish tint you often get — and that the color temperature switching is instant and accurate.

## Where it falls short

The controller is the weak link. It’s a basic IR remote with no app integration, no music sync mode, and no memory function. **If you lose power, the strip resets to the default red color and you have to cycle through the remote to get back to your setting.** That’s infuriating for permanent installations, especially in a kitchen or hallway where you don’t want to hunt for a remote every time the breaker trips.

The green channel also runs slightly hot. At 100% brightness, pure green leans toward yellow-green. It’s only noticeable when comparing side-by-side with a professional fixture, but if you’re doing color-critical work, this isn’t the strip for that.

Cut points are every 5cm (roughly 2 inches), but they require soldering for corners — no quick-connect clips are included. If you’re planning a 90-degree bend, budget for a soldering iron or buy compatible connectors separately.

## Who should buy this

- **Home theater enthusiasts** who want bias lighting behind a TV or a smooth under-cabinet glow without visible dots.
- **DIY kitchen remodelers** who want tunable white light (2700K–6500K) from one strip instead of buying separate warm and cool reels.
- **Anyone who’s already burned by cheap RGB strips** that fade, flicker, or can’t be dimmed without buzzing.

**Skip it if** you need smart-home integration (no WiFi/HomeKit/Google), or if you’re installing in a location where power outages are frequent — the reset-to-red issue will drive you crazy.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I cut this strip to a custom length?**  
Yes, but only at the marked cut lines every 5cm. You’ll need to solder wires to the copper pads after cutting, or buy RJ45-style connectors separately. No clips included.

**Q: Does it work with Alexa or Google Home?**  
Not out of the box. The included controller is IR-only. You’d need to replace the controller with a compatible smart receiver — check that it supports 24V RGBCCT before buying.

**Q: How bright is it really?**  
A full 5-meter run outputs roughly 2,800 lumens at 60W. That’s equivalent to about four 60W equivalent LED bulbs. Enough for task lighting or primary ambient light in a small to medium room, but not for a large open space.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want professional-grade light density and a true tunable white range without paying commercial-installation prices. The 112LED/m density is the real deal, and the 24V design avoids the voltage-drop headaches of cheaper 12V strips.

**Skip it if** you need smart controls or you’re unwilling to solder for custom cuts. The basic controller and lack of memory are genuine compromises.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The strip itself is excellent. The controller drags it down, but if you’re comfortable swapping that out or living with IR-only control, this is one of the best-value high-density strips on Amazon right now.
