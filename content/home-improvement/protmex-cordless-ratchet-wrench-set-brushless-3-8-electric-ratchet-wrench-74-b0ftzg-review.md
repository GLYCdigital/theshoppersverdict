Start here: If you've been eyeing a cordless ratchet but balked at Milwaukee or Snap-on pricing, the Protmex 3/8" brushless set is the budget-friendly alternative that actually delivers. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, it's not perfect — but for weekend mechanics and DIYers, it punches well above its price point.

**What's in the box**

The 74-piece kit is the real story. You get the ratchet itself, one 2.0Ah lithium battery, a charger, and a full socket set covering both SAE (5/16" to 3/4") and metric (8mm to 19mm). There are also extensions, a universal joint, and a compact carrying case with molded slots. As shown in the product image, the sockets are laser-etched with size markings that won't rub off after a few uses — a common complaint with cheaper sets. The ratchet body is a matte-finish composite with a rubberized grip, and the head is a standard 3/8" square drive, so it'll accept any existing sockets you already own.

**Performance and real-world use**

The brushless motor produces 60 Nm of max torque, which is the sweet spot for this class. It'll zip off brake caliper brackets, sway bar links, and even stubborn lug nuts if you've broken them loose first. The variable-speed trigger gives you decent control for lighter work — one reviewer noted, "I used it to swap out my alternator and it made short work of the bolts I couldn't reach with a regular ratchet. The head is a bit bulky but it got the job done."

Battery life is solid. Multiple buyers report getting through a full brake job — calipers, brackets, and slide pins — on a single charge. The brushless motor runs noticeably cooler than brushed alternatives, which matters when you're holding it under a dash for extended periods.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the standout. One verified purchase wrote: "I was skeptical at this price, but the socket set alone is worth what I paid. The ratchet has held up through three oil changes and a suspension refresh." Another noted the low vibration: "My hands don't go numb after using it for an hour like they did with my old cordless drill."

The included case also gets consistent props — it keeps everything organized, and the sockets stay put even when the case is tossed in a truck bed.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the limitations. The head length is 3.2 inches, which is longer than premium options from Milwaukee or Matco. You'll struggle with tight engine bay clearance — think alternator bolts on a transverse-mounted V6. Keep a manual ratchet handy for those spots.

The charger is also slow. At roughly 90 minutes for a full charge, it's not a tool you can drain and recharge quickly on a job site. Buy a second battery if you plan on heavy use. Finally, the torque is sufficient but not impressive — don't expect to break free rusted bolts without a breaker bar first.

**Who should buy this**

This set is ideal for three buyer types: DIY home mechanics doing brakes, suspension work, or fluid changes; RV and boat owners who need a compact tool for tight spaces; and anyone building their first tool collection who wants a cordless ratchet without a $400 investment. Skip it if you're a professional mechanic using a ratchet daily — the head length and charger will frustrate you, and you're better served by a premium brand.

**FAQ**

**Is the battery interchangeable with other Protmex tools?**  
Yes, Protmex uses a shared 20V battery platform across their cordless lineup. If you already own Protmex tools, this battery will work.

**Can this handle lug nuts?**  
It can remove lug nuts that were torqued to spec (80-100 ft-lbs), but you'll need to break them loose manually first. It's not a replacement for an impact wrench.

**Does the socket set skip any common sizes?**  
The kit covers 8mm to 19mm metric and 5/16" to 3/4" SAE. It skips the less common 18mm and 11/16", but for most modern vehicles, you're covered.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a capable cordless ratchet with a complete socket set at a fraction of the premium brand cost. The brushless motor, solid torque, and included kit make it a smart first purchase for DIYers.

**Skip it if** you're a working pro who needs maximum head clearance and fast charging — the slow charger and 3.2-inch head will cost you time on the job.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good value with honest tradeoffs. For the price, it's hard to beat.
