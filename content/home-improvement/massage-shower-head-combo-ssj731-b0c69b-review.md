Let me be direct: most shower heads under $40 are plastic junk that crack within a year. The Massage Shower Head Combo-SSJ731 isn't that — but it's not a high-end fixture either. It sits in a useful middle ground, and for most renters or budget-conscious homeowners, that's exactly where you want to be.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main shower head, a detachable handheld unit, a 5-foot stainless steel hose, a brass diverter valve, and Teflon tape. The chrome finish on the main body is genuine metal — not chrome-plated ABS — which is the first sign this isn't disposable. The face plate is silicone with 60+ nozzles, which means cleaning mineral buildup is as simple as wiping a finger across it. No soaking in vinegar required.

The combo design is the headline feature: you get a fixed rainfall-style head plus a handheld sprayer for rinsing kids, dogs, or hard-to-reach corners of the shower. The diverter switch is a simple lever that clicks between the two — no fiddly pull-knobs that strip out after a few months.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this against a $70 Delta unit. The SSJ731 holds its own on pressure. The massage mode — a concentrated jet stream — delivers noticeably more force than the standard spray, which matters if you're washing off after a workout or rinsing shampoo from thick hair. The six modes cycle through: rainfall, massage, mist, pulse, and two combination settings. The mist mode is weak, honestly, but you'll likely use it only for rinsing soap off a child's face.

The 4.3-star average from 215 ratings tracks with my experience. Buyers consistently mention the build quality and ease of installation. One reviewer noted: *"Took me 8 minutes to replace my old unit. The Teflon tape included saved me a trip to the hardware store."* Another said: *"The massage setting is legit — I use it on my shoulders after work and it's better than my handheld massager."*

## Where It Falls Short

The 5-foot hose is the biggest practical limitation. If your shower arm sits at standard height (around 72 inches), you'll manage fine. But if it's mounted high — or you're trying to use the handheld to rinse a bathtub from a standing position — you'll wish it were 6 or 7 feet. This is a common complaint in the reviews, and it's a valid one.

Second, the flow rate runs high. If you have low home water pressure (under 40 PSI), the restrictor isn't aggressive enough to compensate. You'll get a full flow but not a pressurized one. The internal flow restrictor is removable if you want maximum output, but that's a hack, not a solution.

Finally, the diverter lever is functional but feels slightly loose. It won't fail on you, but it lacks the crisp detent of a $100 fixture.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters and apartment dwellers**: Tool-free install means you can swap it back out when you move. Your security deposit stays safe.
- **Parents with young kids**: The handheld unit makes bath-time rinsing dramatically easier. The silicone nozzles won't scrape a toddler's scalp.
- **Anyone replacing a 5+ year old builder-grade head**: You'll notice a significant upgrade in both spray options and water coverage.

Skip it if you have a deep-soaking tub with a high-mounted arm, or if you're chasing luxury spa pressure — look at a $70+ model with a wider head.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my standard shower arm?**
A: Yes. It uses the universal 1/2-inch NPT threading that's standard on virtually all residential shower arms. The included Teflon tape handles the seal.

**Q: Can I use both the overhead and handheld at the same time?**
A: No. The diverter routes water to one or the other. This is by design — splitting flow would cut pressure in half on both.

**Q: How do I clean the nozzles if they clog?**
A: The silicone face is flexible. Rub your thumb across the nozzles while the water runs — mineral deposits pop out. No tools or chemicals needed.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a solid two-in-one shower head with real massage pressure and metal construction without paying premium prices.

**Skip it if** your shower arm is unusually high, you have chronic low water pressure, or you need a longer handheld hose.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good value that gets the fundamentals right. The hose length and flow-rate quirks keep it from greatness, but for the price, it's hard to beat. The 4.3 Amazon rating is earned, not inflated.
