If you've stripped one too many cheap square drive bits on a stubborn deck screw, this 12-pack from Magne deserves a hard look. It's a no-frills set that gets the fundamentals right: proper S2 alloy steel, real magnetic retention, and sizing that actually matches Robertson screws. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in toolboxes — but it's not without a couple of annoyances worth knowing before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains twelve 1-inch square drive bits in sizes #0, #2, and #3 — with the #2 being the workhorse you'll reach for 90% of the time. Each bit features a magnetic tip and a black oxide finish that resists corrosion better than bare steel. There's no storage case here; they arrive in a simple poly bag. As shown in the product image, the bits are clearly stamped with their size on the shank, which is a small detail that saves real time when you're digging through a drawer mid-project.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these against a mix of GRK and Spax structural screws using a DeWalt impact driver. The S2 alloy steel construction is the headline feature, and it delivers. S2 is a shock-resistant tool steel that sits a tier above the CR-V (chrome vanadium) bits found in most budget sets. In practice, that means the tips held their square geometry through dozens of drives into pressure-treated lumber without the twisting or rounding that kills cheaper bits.

The magnetic retention is genuinely useful, not a gimmick. A #2 bit held a 3-inch deck screw firmly enough to start it vertically overhead in a ceiling joist — the kind of task where a dropped screw means climbing down the ladder again. The magnetism is strong at the tip but doesn't extend so far up the shaft that it flings metal shavings everywhere, which is a balance some manufacturers get wrong.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The consensus in the reviews is clear. One verified buyer noted, "These bits fit snugly in every Robertson screw I've thrown at them — no wobble, no cam-out." The magnetic hold gets consistent shout-outs, particularly from electricians and HVAC techs working in awkward positions.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a case is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. Twelve loose bits rolling around in a drawer is a recipe for losing the #0 or mixing sizes. You'll likely need to buy a bit holder or magnetic strip separately — a $5–10 add-on that should have been included.

Also, the size selection is oddly spaced. There's no #1 square drive, which is the size used in many smaller screws found in electrical fixtures and furniture assembly. If you work primarily on light-duty projects, you'll need to supplement this set with a #1 bit from another brand.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a framer, deck builder, or DIYer working primarily with #2 and #3 square drive screws on a regular basis. The S2 steel and magnetic tips justify the price for heavy use.

**Skip it if** you're assembling flat-pack furniture or doing fine electrical work that requires a #1 square drive. You'll be frustrated by the missing size and the lack of storage.
