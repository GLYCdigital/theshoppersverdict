If you're chasing that Edison bulb aesthetic but refuse to pay the electric bill that comes with real incandescents, the Brightown Vintage LED is the compromise that actually works. At 6W with 750 lumens of output, it delivers genuine 60W-equivalent brightness — not the dim, decorative-only glow most "vintage" LEDs settle for. That's the headline. Here's the rest.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains six ST64-shaped bulbs, each measuring 4.6 inches tall with a 2.36-inch diameter. Standard E26 medium screw base — they drop into any US fixture without adapters. The filament is a real LED array designed to mimic old-style carbon filaments, and it shows. When lit, you get that warm, segmented glow pattern that reads as genuinely vintage, not like a cheap printed fake. Each bulb weighs about 1.4 ounces, so they won't sag in pendant fixtures.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these in a pair of gooseneck wall sconces and a ceiling fan light kit. The 2200K color temperature is *properly* amber — think candlelight with a touch more presence. Skin tones look flattering; food looks warm. If you're lighting a reading nook or workspace, this is the wrong bulb — you'd want something in the 2700K–3000K range. But for living rooms, dining areas, bars, and restaurant-style fixtures, the warmth is the point.

Dimming performance surprised me. One verified Amazon buyer noted, "I have them on a Lutron dimmer and they go down to about 10% with zero flicker." Another confirmed: "No buzz, no hum, just smooth dimming." That's not universal among budget LED filament bulbs — many flicker below 30%. Brightown got this right.

Heat is a non-issue. After four hours of continuous operation, the base was barely warm to the touch. That's the LED advantage over genuine Edison bulbs, which run hot enough to scorch your fingers and waste 90% of their energy as heat.

## Where It Falls Short

The size is the main gotcha. At 2.36 inches across, these are wider than standard A19 bulbs. In shallow flush-mount ceiling fixtures or enclosed globes under 5 inches in diameter, they'll either not fit or look crowded. Check your fixture dimensions before ordering.

The color temperature is also a commitment. 2200K is *warm*. If you're mixing these with 2700K bulbs in the same room, the mismatch will be obvious. And while they're dimmable, they need a compatible LED dimmer — older trailing-edge dimmers may still cause a slight buzz, though most buyers report no issues.

One more consideration: these are 60W-equivalent, not 100W. If you need serious task-level brightness, step up to Brightown's 100W-equivalent version.

## Who Should Buy These

Three buyer profiles fit best:

- **Restaurant, bar, and café owners** — that warm, inviting glow with minimal energy draw
- **Home décor enthusiasts** updating pendant lights, chandeliers, or exposed-bulb fixtures

Skip them if you need neutral white light, have tight-fixture clearance, or want a single bulb for a desk lamp.

## FAQ

**Will these work in enclosed fixtures?**
Yes, they're rated for enclosed fixtures. But given their size, measure your globe's interior diameter first — you need at least 2.5 inches of clearance.

**Do they actually save money vs. real Edison bulbs?**
Significantly. At 6W each, four bulbs running 5 hours daily cost roughly $1.50 per month at average US electricity rates. Equivalent incandescents would run about $10. They also last 15,000+ hours versus roughly 1,000 for incandescent.

**Can I use them on a standard dimmer switch?**
Yes, with a compatible LED dimmer. They perform best on newer LED-rated dimmers. Older standard dimmers may work but could produce slight flicker at low settings.
