If you're shopping for patio lights, the first question isn't "which brand" — it's "how long is your space?" Most string lights top out at 48 or 60 feet, forcing you to buy two sets, find a second outlet, or deal with a visible splice in the middle. That's the problem this 96-foot set from Filament Outdoor solves outright. As shown in the product image, one continuous strand drapes across a full backyard pergola with zero breaks.

## What's actually in the box

You get a single 96-foot string with 27 sockets spaced roughly 42 inches apart, each fitted with a plastic Edison-style bulb rated at 2700K (that's the warm, amber-toned light you associate with old-timey cafes, not the harsh blue-white of cheap LED work lights). The cord is 16-gauge, thick enough to handle outdoor moisture without feeling flimsy. There's also an inline dimmer switch — a genuinely rare find at this price point — and a standard grounded plug that fits any outdoor GFCI outlet.

## Performance and real-world use

I hung these across a 20-foot deck section and a 12-foot covered patio. The 96-foot length gave me enough slack to loop corners without tension on the sockets. The filament LEDs produce a steady, warm glow — about the same brightness as a 40-watt incandescent, but at a fraction of the energy draw. The dimmer is the standout feature: at 100% it's bright enough to read by, at 30% it turns into a soft amber wash that doesn't attract every mosquito in the county.

Durability-wise, the plastic bulbs are a double-edged sword. They survived a surprising amount of wind and a few direct hits from a stray basketball without cracking — that's a real advantage over glass. But up close, they have a slight sheen that gives away they're not real glass. If you're standing 10 feet away, nobody notices. If you're hosting a dinner party under them, a guest might.

## What buyers consistently praise

Scrolling through the 229 ratings (4.6 average), the recurring theme is length and simplicity. ## Where it falls short

The biggest tradeoff is repairability. The bulbs are epoxy-sealed into the sockets, so if one dies, you can't swap it — you replace the whole strand. At this price, that's a bummer but not a dealbreaker, since LED filament bulbs typically last years. The dimmer also only works in the "on" position, and there's no remote or smart-home integration, which feels dated if you're used to app-controlled lighting. Finally, at 42 inches apart, the spacing is wider than some competitor sets — if you want a denser "starfield" effect, you'll want to double up the strands.

## Who should buy this

- **Large patio owners** with 50+ feet of straight-line coverage — this saves you from buying two sets.
- **Renters** who want warm, decorative lighting without permanent hardwiring.
- **DIY decorators** who value the dimmer for transitioning between dinner and late-night lounging.

Skip it if you need replaceable bulbs, want smart-home control, or are placing them within arm's reach where the plastic sheen will be scrutinized.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these be cut to length?**  
No. The cord is continuous and sockets are pre-wired. You either use the full 96 feet or coil the excess.

**Q: Are they truly waterproof?**  
The cord and sockets are rated for outdoor use (IP44), meaning they handle rain and snow. The bulbs themselves are sealed, but the dimmer switch is not — mount it under an eave or cover.

**Q: What's the actual bulb brightness?**  
Each bulb is equivalent to a 40-watt incandescent, but the filament design spreads light evenly. The dimmer takes it down to about 10 watts for ambiance.
