Let me be blunt: replacing a torsion spring is one of the most dangerous DIY jobs in home maintenance. That coil stores enough energy to break bones — or worse. So when you're shopping for a replacement, you want precision and reliability, not guesswork.

The FUTI-US single torsion spring delivers exactly that, which is why it's earned a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings. But let's dig into whether it's the right fit for your specific door.

## What You're Actually Getting

Out of the box, you get one spring — no cones, no cables, no winding bars. That's it. The spring itself is the star: 0.225-inch wire gauge, 2-inch inner diameter, and a 25-inch coil length. That's the standard spec for most single-car residential garage doors weighing 150-250 pounds.

The spring arrives oiled and pre-stretched. That's not marketing fluff — pre-stretching means the spring is already at its working tension, so it doesn't need the break-in period that cheaper springs require. One buyer put it simply: "Worked perfectly, no squeaking, door balanced like new."

## Real-World Performance

The critical thing about torsion springs is cycle life. FUTI-US rates this at 10,000 cycles. That's roughly 5-7 years of normal use (opening and closing four times daily). That's mid-range — not the cheap 5,000-cycle springs you'll find for $20 less, but not the 20,000-cycle heavy-duty options either.

The spring balances the door smoothly. Multiple reviews confirm the door lifts easily and stays put at any height — that's the hallmark of correct spring tension. One reviewer noted, "I was skeptical about a single-pack spring, but it matched my old one exactly. Door works like new."

The oiled finish is a practical advantage. It reduces friction between coils, which cuts wear and keeps the spring quieter. That's why you'll see fewer complaints about rattling compared to dry springs.

## Where It Falls Short

The single-pack format is a double-edged sword. Garage door springs are sold in matched pairs for good reason — if one breaks, the other is usually close behind. Buying just one means you're likely to replace the second within a year. That's not a defect of this product; it's how springs work. But budget for a second one, or consider buying two now.

The painted cone ends chip easily during installation. Not a functional issue, but if you're particular about aesthetics in your garage, you'll notice it.

And again — no winding bars included. You absolutely need these for safe installation. Do not attempt this with screwdrivers or pliers. Buy a proper set of winding bars before you start.

## Who Should Buy This

This spring is ideal for the competent DIYer who's already replaced a torsion spring before. You know your door's exact spec, you have winding bars, and you understand the safety protocols — release tension slowly, use both bars, never loosen the set screws while the spring is loaded.

It's also perfect if you're replacing just one spring on a newer door. If the other spring is less than three years old, replacing only the broken one is financially smart.

Skip it if you're a first-timer. This isn't the product to learn on. Hire a professional if you've never done this job — the $150-$250 labor cost is cheap compared to an emergency room visit.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I know if this spring fits my garage door?**
Check the wire gauge, inner diameter, and coil length stamped on your current spring. This is 0.225" × 2" × 25". If your door weighs more than 250 pounds or is a double-car door, you need a different spec.

**Q: Can I replace just one spring instead of both?**
Yes, but only if the remaining spring is in good shape and less than three years old. Otherwise, replace both — they wear at the same rate.

**Q: What tools do I need for installation?**
Two solid winding bars (½-inch hex steel), a socket wrench, and a ladder. Never use screwdrivers or improvised tools — they'll slip and you'll get hurt.
