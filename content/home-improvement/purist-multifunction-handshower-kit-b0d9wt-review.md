The Purist Multifunction Handshower Kit sits in that awkward middle ground: not cheap enough to be a disposable impulse buy, not expensive enough to promise luxury. At 3.9 stars from 195 ratings, it's a solid "good but not great" product. Here's what you need to know before you buy.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the handshower head, a 5-foot stainless steel hose, a wall-mounted bracket, and the diverter valve that attaches to your existing shower arm. The kit is designed to replace a fixed showerhead or add a second handheld unit. The hose is genuinely kink-resistant, which matters more than most people realize — cheap hoses twist and restrict flow within months.

**Real-World Performance**

The star feature is the multifunction spray plate. There are five distinct modes: full spray, massage, jet, mist, and a "power wash" setting that concentrates the water into a narrow, high-velocity stream. That last one is surprisingly effective. As one buyer noted, "The jet mode is great for rinsing the shower walls and cleaning the dog — it actually has some force behind it." Another reviewer confirmed, "I use the power spray to clean the bathtub after bath time. It cuts through soap scum better than any spray cleaner I've tried."

But here's the catch: that power only exists if your home has adequate water pressure. Multiple reviewers reported the massage and mist modes feel weak. One customer put it plainly: "If you're on a well or have low pressure, skip the massage setting entirely. It's basically a trickle." That's not a defect — it's physics. The flow restrictor is doing its job, but it means this showerhead rewards homes with good plumbing and punishes those without it.

**Build Quality: The Honest Assessment**

The brushed nickel finish looks good and hides water spots well. The metal hose fittings and the diverter are solid. But the main body is plastic. It's not cheap-feeling plastic — it's got some heft to it — but it's not metal, and at this price point, some buyers expect metal. One reviewer summed it up: "The head feels durable enough, but you can tell it's plastic. It won't shatter if you drop it, but don't expect a premium feel."

Installation is genuinely simple. The diverter threads onto your existing shower arm, and the whole thing takes about 10 minutes with an adjustable wrench. The one recurring complaint? Leaks at the diverter connection. This is almost always user error — you need to wrap plumber's tape around the threads before tightening. If you skip that step, you'll get drips. It's not a design flaw, but it's worth knowing.

**Where It Falls Short**

- The wall bracket is flimsy. It holds the showerhead fine, but the plastic clip feels like it'll wear out after a year of daily use.
- The hose is 5 feet, which is standard, but if you have a large tub or want to reach a shower bench, you'll wish it were longer.
- The "mist" mode is basically useless for showering. It's fine as a novelty, but it doesn't clean or rinse effectively.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Pet owners** who need a powerful spray to rinse muddy dogs — the jet mode genuinely excels here.
- **Renters** who want an upgrade they can install and remove without permanent modifications.
- **Anyone with strong water pressure** who wants a multifunction option without spending $80+ on a premium brand.

**Skip It If**

- You have low water pressure. The restrictor will make every mode feel weak.
- You want a solid metal showerhead. This is plastic, period.
- You're looking for a rainfall experience. This is a handheld, not a fixed-head replacement.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the diverter let you use both the fixed showerhead and the handheld at the same time?**
A: No. The diverter is a two-way switch — it sends water either to your existing showerhead or the handheld. You can't run both simultaneously.

**Q: Will this fit my shower arm?**
A: It fits standard 1/2-inch NPT shower arms, which covers virtually all residential showers in the US and Canada. If you have a custom or European fitting, you'll need an adapter.

**Q: How do I stop the leak where the diverter meets the shower arm?**
A: Wrap Teflon plumber's tape around the shower arm threads — at least 4-5 wraps — before threading on the diverter. Tighten firmly with a wrench, but don't overdo it. This solves 95% of leak complaints.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a versatile handheld for cleaning, pets, or hair washing, and your water pressure is decent. **Skip it if** you want premium materials or have weak plumbing.

The Purist Multifunction Handshower Kit is a functional, capable product that doesn't overpromise. It's not the showerhead you brag about to friends, but it does its job — and the jet mode alone justifies the purchase for practical households. At 3.7/5, it's a reasonable value for what it delivers, with the caveat that the plastic body and pressure sensitivity keep it from greatness.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A capable mid-tier option that rewards practical buyers and punishes perfectionists.
