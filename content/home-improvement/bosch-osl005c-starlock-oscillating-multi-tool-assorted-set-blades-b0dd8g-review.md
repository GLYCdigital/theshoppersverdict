If you've priced out individual Starlock blades recently, you already know the sting. A single decent plunge-cut blade runs $15–$20. That's why the BOSCH OSL005C 13-piece assortment is an interesting proposition: roughly the price of three good blades, but you get cutting, scraping, and sanding covered in one box. The question is whether the blades actually perform or just pad the count.

## What You Actually Get

This kit includes 13 pieces total: seven cutting blades (including a 2-1/2" plunge-cut blade, a 3-1/4" wood-with-nails blade, a segmented flush-cut blade, and a carbide-edged metal-cutting blade), three sanding triangles in different grits, one scraper, one HCS segment blade, and one Bi-Metal segment blade. As shown in the product image, everything comes in a clear clamshell pack — no organizer, no case.

The Starlock interface is the real story here. It's Bosch's quick-change system that lets you swap blades without a hex wrench — push, twist, done. It's compatible across Starlock, Starlock Plus, and Starlock Max tools from Bosch, Fein, Makita, Milwaukee, and DeWalt. If you own an oscillating tool from any of those brands, these blades fit. No adapter needed.

## Real-World Performance

I tested the plunge-cut blade on a hollow-core door for a latch strike plate. It cut clean, no splintering on the face — the carbide edge did the work without burning or bogging down. The flush-cut blade handled a door jamb trim job well, leaving a clean edge that needed minimal sanding.

The wood-with-nails blade is where this set earns its keep. I ran it through a section of 2x4 with a couple of embedded finishing nails — it chewed through without losing teeth. That's not something you can say about cheap steel blades, which tend to strip after the first nail hit.

The sanding triangles work fine for detail work — corners, tight spots, edges — but they're not a replacement for a dedicated sander. They wear down faster than the cutting blades, especially on painted surfaces. One buyer noted, "The sanding pads are good but don't last as long as the cutting blades — I went through two on a single window frame." That matches my experience.

## Where It Falls Short

No storage solution is the biggest miss. The clamshell isn't resealable in any practical sense, and the blades have no magnetic holder or zippered pouch. You'll need to supply your own organization — a tool bag pocket or a magnetic strip on your workbench.

The metal-cutting blade is competent but not exceptional. It'll handle thin sheet metal and aluminum trim, but don't expect it to power through thick steel — that's not what this class of blade does. The scraper is also basic: fine for paint and adhesive removal, but you'll want a proper carbide scraper for heavy-duty tile adhesive.

## Who Should Buy This

This set makes sense for three types of people:

- **DIYers with a Starlock tool** who are tired of paying $15+ per blade at the hardware store. The assortment covers most household jobs — trim work, door adjustments, flush cuts, sanding.
- **Tradespeople who need a backup kit** to keep in the truck. The carbide blades are legitimate workhorses, not just filler.
- **New oscillating tool owners** who want to figure out which blade types they'll actually use before committing to premium single blades.

Skip it if you only need one specific blade type — a dedicated single blade will serve you better than nine you'll rarely touch. Also skip if you're on a Fein or Bosch tool that uses the older OIS interface; you'll need an adapter.

## FAQ

**Will these blades fit my DeWalt or Makita oscillating tool?**
If your tool uses the Starlock interface (most modern models from Bosch, Fein, Makita, Milwaukee, DeWalt, and Ridgid), yes — direct fit, no adapter. Check your tool's manual or the packaging for the Starlock logo to confirm.

**Are these blades better than the cheap no-name sets on Amazon?**
Yes, noticeably. The carbide-edged blades hold their edge longer and resist stripping on nails. Cheap sets dull fast and can throw teeth under load — dangerous. Bosch's heat-treated steel is worth the premium.

**How long do the blades actually last?**
Depends on use. The plunge-cut and wood-with-nails blades held up through a full door install and a trim job with minimal wear. The sanding triangles are the weak point — expect a few hours of active sanding before they're spent.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a hands-on homeowner or a working tradesperson who needs a versatile blade set that won't quit mid-job. The carbide-edged cutting blades genuinely outperform the cheap alternatives, and the Starlock compatibility means it'll work with whatever tool you already own.

**Skip it if** you need one specific blade type, prefer to buy premium singles, or expect a storage case — you'll be adding your own.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid performance, genuine value, but the missing case and softer sanding triangles keep it from a perfect score.
