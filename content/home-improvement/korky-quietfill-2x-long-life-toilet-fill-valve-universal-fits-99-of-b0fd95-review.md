A toilet that runs all night or hisses every time you flush is more than an annoyance — it's wasted water and money. The Korky QuietFILL 2X Long Life Fill Valve is designed to kill that problem at the source. With a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right valve for *your* toilet? Here's what you need to know before you buy.

**What's in the Box**

You get the complete fill valve assembly: the valve body, a locking collar, the refill tube that clips to your overflow pipe, and a threaded adapter for the tank's bottom opening. Korky claims this fits 99% of toilets, and the height adjusts from 9 to 14 inches — which covers both standard two-piece toilets and taller one-piece models. No shims, no cutting, no special tools beyond a pair of pliers. Installation runs about 20 minutes if you've done a fill valve before, maybe 40 if you're learning from a YouTube video.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The first thing you notice is the silence. The QuietFILL name isn't marketing fluff — the valve throttles water flow during the last portion of the fill cycle, so the tank fills fast at first, then slows to a whisper as it reaches capacity. This reduces the water hammer and high-pitched whine that plague cheaper valves.

The "2X Long Life" claim comes down to two specific parts: a silicone seal that resists hard-water mineral buildup and a stainless steel spring in the diaphragm. Both are the usual failure points on generic valves — the rubber gets stiff, the spring corrodes, and suddenly you're dealing with a constant dribble. Korky addressed both. That's a meaningful upgrade for anyone who's replaced a fill valve twice in five years.

The refill tube routes directly into the overflow tube, which is how it should be. This keeps the bowl refill separate from the tank fill and prevents the random "phantom flush" caused by a misaligned tube siphoning water.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the reviews, a few themes keep coming up. First is fit. As one homeowner put it: *"Replaced a Fluidmaster that was leaking after 4 years. This one went in without a fight and has been dead silent for 2 months now."* Another reviewer noted the obvious build quality difference: *"You can feel the heft. The plastic isn't cheap and flexible like the big-box store brands."* The quiet operation and the longevity of the silicone seal are the two features that get the most unprompted mentions.

**Where It Falls Short**

Nothing's perfect, and this valve has two real-world complaints. First, the base is slightly wider than some competitors. If your tank has a very narrow opening — common in older American Standard models from the 1970s and 1980s — you may need to angle the valve in at install. It fits, but it's snug.

Second, the locking collar needs a deliberate, firm twist to seat fully. Several reviewers reported leaks that traced back to the collar not being locked. It's a one-time fix — you just twist it harder until it clicks — but it's a moment of panic if you've already filled the tank and water starts pooling. Take the extra ten seconds to verify the click before you turn the water back on.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The "sick of replacing valves" homeowner** — If you've swapped a fill valve in the last three years, this is the upgrade you want. The silicone seal and steel spring will outlast the generic rubber-and-brass units.
- **Anyone with hard water** — Mineral scale is the #1 killer of toilet valves. The silicone seal resists that buildup far better than standard rubber.
- **Light DIYers** — The universal fit and no-cut adjustment make this a forgiving first-time project.

**Skip It If** — You have an unusually compact tank and even a small width increase is a dealbreaker. Measure first. Also, if you expect a "set and forget" install with zero attention to the locking collar, you might get frustrated.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my Kohler or Toto toilet?**
A: Most likely yes. The 9–14 inch height range covers the vast majority of residential toilets, and the base threads onto standard 2-inch tank openings. If you have an obscure European or vintage model, measure the tank opening height and width first.

**Q: Does it actually reduce noise?**
A: Yes, noticeably. The valve slows the fill rate in the final seconds, which kills the high-frequency whistle that cheap valves emit. It's not silent — you'll hear water moving — but it's a low, muffled sound instead of a screech.

**Q: How long will it last?**
A: Korky rates this at 2X the life of a standard valve, which translates to roughly 5–7 years under normal use. The silicone seal and stainless steel spring are the reason. Hard water will still shorten that, but less than with rubber seals.
