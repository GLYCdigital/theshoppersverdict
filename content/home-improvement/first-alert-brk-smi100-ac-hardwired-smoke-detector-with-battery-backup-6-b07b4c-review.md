Let’s cut through the noise: most hardwired smoke detectors are commodity junk. You screw them up, forget they exist, and hope they work when grease catches fire. The First Alert BRK SMI100-AC is not that. It’s a photoelectric-only detector that costs less than a pizza, yet it brings features normally reserved for $40 units. Here’s the real story.

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you’ll find the detector, a mounting bracket, a quick-disconnect wiring harness, two tamper-resistant locking pins, and a 9V battery already installed. No smoke test aerosol. No manual printed on recycled paper—it’s a small foldout card. The unit itself is 5.6 inches in diameter and 1.5 inches deep, which means it’ll sit flush on most ceilings without looking like a UFO landed.

The wiring harness is the standout. It plugs into the detector body with a firm click, and the bracket twists off with a quarter-turn. If you’ve ever fought with a seized alarm base at 2 AM, you’ll appreciate this design. The SMI100-AC is also interconnect-capable via a third wire, so you can link up to 18 units in a single home.

## Performance: The Photoelectric Tradeoff

This unit uses a photoelectric sensor, not ionization. That’s a deliberate choice. Photoelectric excels at detecting smoldering fires—think a cigarette in a couch or a faulty wire behind drywall—before flames erupt. In controlled tests, it reacts 15-30 minutes faster than ionization sensors on those slow-burn scenarios. The tradeoff: it’s slightly slower on fast-flaming fires (like a grease pan). For most homes, that’s the right priority. Kitchens and living rooms benefit most.

The 85-decibel alarm is genuinely loud. I tested it in a 1,200-square-foot ranch and could hear it clearly from the basement with the door shut. The test button works without tripping the actual sensor, which is more reliable than the “hold and pray” buttons on cheaper units.

## What Buyers Praise (and Grumble About)

With 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, the feedback is consistent. Multiple reviewers mention the same thing I noticed: installation is genuinely easy. One buyer wrote, “Replaced three old detectors in 20 minutes. The plug-in harness saved me from calling an electrician.” Another praised the battery backup: “Power went out for six hours and the alarm still worked when a neighbor burned toast.”

But there’s a recurring complaint: the low-battery chirp. It’s a piercing double-beep that starts at 2 AM, and the only way to silence it is to remove the unit or replace the battery. One reviewer said, “The chirp is so sharp my dog howls. I replaced the battery and it still chirped until I reset the unit.” That reset quirk—you must hold the test button for 10 seconds after swapping batteries—is not clearly documented in the card manual.

## Where It Falls Short

The SMI100-AC is not a combo detector. No carbon monoxide sensing. If you’re putting this in a hallway near bedrooms, you still need a separate CO alarm. That’s a common oversight in budget hardwired installs.

The plastic housing also feels thin. Not flimsy enough to crack during normal handling, but if you accidentally overtighten the mounting screws, you can strip the bracket threads. It’s a one-time install product—fine for homeowners, less ideal for landlords who swap units frequently.

Finally, the locking pins are a double-edged sword. They prevent theft and tampering, which is great for rentals, but if you lose the included removal tool (a small plastic key), you’ll be prying the unit off the ceiling with a screwdriver.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners replacing old hardwired units**: The harness makes it the easiest swap you’ll do all year.
- **Landlords with multiple properties**: The tamper pins and low cost (check current pricing) make it a smart bulk buy.
- **Anyone with a kitchen fire history**: The photoelectric sensor is forgiving of cooking smoke, reducing nuisance alarms.

**Skip it if** you need combined smoke/CO detection or if you have a large open floorplan where you’d benefit from a dual-sensor unit like the BRK 3120B (which costs roughly double).

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with existing hardwired systems?**
A: Yes, if your current detectors use the standard 3-wire setup (black, white, and red or yellow interconnect). It’s compatible with most BRK and First Alert systems, but not with older brands like Kidde without an adapter.

**Q: How long does the battery backup last?**
A: The included 9V alkaline battery lasts about a year under normal conditions. The unit will chirp for at least 7 days before going dead, giving you a warning window.

**Q: Can I use this in a mobile home?**
A: Yes, but verify your ceiling wiring is standard 120V AC. Some manufactured homes use 12V DC systems, which are not compatible.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, no-frills hardwired detector that installs in minutes and won’t false-alarm every time you sear a steak. It’s the smart choice for bedrooms and living areas where smoldering fires are the real risk.

**Skip it if** you need CO detection or want the peace of mind of dual-sensor coverage in a single unit.
