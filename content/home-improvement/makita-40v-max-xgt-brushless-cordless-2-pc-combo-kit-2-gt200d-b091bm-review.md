If you've been holding off on the Makita 40V XGT platform, waiting for the lineup to mature, this 2-piece combo kit is the signal you've been waiting for. The XGT system launched with a lot of fanfare and a hefty price tag, but now that the ecosystem has expanded, this hammer drill and impact driver pairing is the sweet spot for serious DIYers and professionals ready to ditch extension cords for good.

This kit, model GT200D / B091BM, bundles the XGT Hammer Driver-Drill (GHP01) and the XGT Impact Driver (GTD01) with two 2.5Ah batteries and a rapid charger. As shown in the product image, these are compact tools — the impact driver checks in at just 6.4 inches, which surprised me given the 40V power under the hood.

**What you actually get in the box**

Makita keeps this straightforward: the hammer drill, the impact driver, two 2.5Ah XGT batteries, the 40V rapid charger, and a tool bag. Some retailers ship the bag, some don't — verify before you order, because this is not a cheap kit and you want everything you're paying for.

**Performance that justifies the platform switch**

The GHP01 hammer drill is the star here. It delivers 1,100 in.lbs of max torque, which translates to real-world muscle. Driving 1-1/4" spade bits through dimensional lumber feels effortless, and the hammer mode handles masonry anchors without breaking a sweat. The variable speed trigger gives you genuine control for delicate work — I drove #8 screws into oak without stripping a single head.

The GTD01 impact driver is equally impressive. It's rated at 1,800 in.lbs of torque, which is class-leading for a compact driver. The key here is the form factor — at 6.4 inches and 3.4 pounds, it's shorter than many 18V drivers, making it far more maneuverable in cabinet work and overhead applications. The three-speed settings let you dial in precision for smaller fasteners, and the dual LED lights with afterglow are genuinely useful in dark corners.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The 2.5Ah batteries are fine for light duty, but if you're running the hammer drill continuously, you'll burn through both packs in a morning. The XGT platform doesn't accept 18V LXT batteries, so you're making a clean break — that's a significant investment if you're already deep in Makita's 18V ecosystem.

The price is the other elephant in the room. This kit commands a premium over comparable 18V offerings, and the value only makes sense if you're planning to expand your XGT collection. If you only need occasional drilling and driving, the 18V LXT line still makes more sense financially.

**Who should buy this**

This kit is for the serious DIYer tackling major renovations, the contractor who wants one battery platform for everything from a circular saw to a grinder, or anyone tired of swapping chargers and batteries across multiple voltage systems. It's also ideal for professionals who need corded-level performance without the cord. If you're a weekend warrior who drills three holes a month, save your money.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Can I use my existing Makita 18V batteries with these tools?*
No. The XGT system uses a completely different battery platform. This is a clean break — you'll need XGT batteries for all XGT tools.

*How long does the battery actually last?*
With the included 2.5Ah packs, expect roughly 100-150 screw drives or about 45 minutes of continuous drilling with the hammer drill. The rapid charger gets you back to full in about 28 minutes.

*Is the hammer drill actually powerful enough for masonry?*
Yes, for light to medium masonry work — anchors, small holes in brick or block. For heavy concrete drilling with large bits, you'll still want a rotary hammer.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're ready to commit to Makita's future platform, need corded-level performance in a cordless tool, and value compact design for tight spaces.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget, already invested in the 18V LXT system, or only need occasional use. The price premium is real, and the included batteries are only adequate.
