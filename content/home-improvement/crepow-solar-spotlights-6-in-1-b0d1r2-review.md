**The 6-in-1 gimmick is actually useful — here’s who should buy it**  

Most “multi-head” solar lights are a single plastic blob with three weak LEDs glued together. The CREPOW Solar Spotlights 6-in-1 is different. Each of the six heads rotates independently on two axes, so you can point one light up a tree trunk, two across a flower bed, and three along a walkway — all from a single pole. That flexibility is the real selling point, not the marketing buzzwords.  

**What’s actually in the box**  

You get two complete units (six heads total per unit). Each unit includes:  
- A 6-head light bar with 270° adjustable heads  
- A separate solar panel on a 10-foot cable  
- Ground stakes and wall-mounting brackets  
- A rechargeable 2000mAh battery (internal, not replaceable)  

The detachable panel is the feature that sets this apart from cheaper competition. As shown in the product image, you can mount the panel on your roof or a sunny fence post while the lights sit in a shaded garden bed — a huge advantage if your yard has patchy sun. The panel itself is a decent size (about 5.5 x 4 inches), which is larger than most in this price range.  

**Real-world performance: what 215 buyers found**  

With a 4.3-star average across 215 ratings, the consensus is solid but not glowing. Here’s what actually matters:  

**Brightness:** The 6-in-1 mode throws a warm, soft light — about 200 lumens total across all six heads. That’s plenty for accenting a porch, highlighting shrubs, or marking a path. It is *not* bright enough to illuminate a driveway for security. One reviewer put it plainly: *“Great for ambiance, useless for catching intruders.”* If you need security-grade output, look elsewhere.  

**Battery and runtime:** In summer, you’ll get a solid 7–8 hours from full charge. Come winter — or any stretch of cloudy days — that drops to 4–5 hours. The advertised 10-hour runtime is optimistic. Buyers in the Pacific Northwest and Northeast consistently reported shorter runtimes in overcast conditions. That’s physics, not a defect, but it’s worth knowing before you buy.  

**Build quality:** The housing is IP65-rated, and multiple long-term reviews confirm it survives rain, snow, and heat without fogging or water intrusion. The light heads feel sturdy, and the rotation mechanism holds position firmly — no sagging after months of adjustment. The weak point is the ground stake: it’s thin and bends easily in hard soil. Several reviewers noted this, and one suggested pre-drilling holes with a screwdriver — solid advice.  

**What buyers consistently praise**  

- **The 10-foot cable:** It’s actually useful. You can put the panel in the only sunny spot in your yard and the lights 10 feet away. That’s rare at this price.  
- **Installation ease:** No wiring, no tools beyond a screwdriver for the wall mount. Most buyers had them up in under 15 minutes.  
- **The 270° rotation:** One reviewer put it well: *“I’ve got one light pointing at the garage door, two at the hydrangeas, and three along the walkway. Never seen another solar light this flexible.”*  

**Where it falls short (be honest with yourself)**  

1. **Battery is non-replaceable.** When the 2000mAh cell degrades in 2–3 years, you’re buying new units. That’s a real environmental and financial downside.  
2. **The motion sensor mode is weak.** It has one, but the detection range is short (about 10 feet) and it’s slow to trigger. You’ll likely leave it in constant-on mode instead.  
3. **Color temperature is fixed.** It’s a warm white (3000K). If you want cool white or RGB, this isn’t the product.  

**Who should buy this**  

- **Homeowners with mixed sun/shade yards:** The detachable panel solves the “my lights are in the shade” problem better than anything else under $50.  
- **Gardeners who want accent lighting:** Use it to highlight a specimen tree, a water feature, or a border. The adjustable heads let you sculpt light like a cheap landscape designer.  
- **Renters:** No hardwiring, no permanent modifications. The wall mounts use two screws each, and the stakes are removable.  

**Skip it if** you need security-grade brightness, want replaceable batteries, or live somewhere with less than 4 hours of direct winter sun.  

**FAQ: Three questions shoppers actually ask**  

**Q: How long does the battery last in real use?**  
A: Expect 6–8 hours in summer, 4–5 in winter. The 10-hour claim only holds in ideal, full-sun, warm conditions.  

**Q: Can the lights be mounted on a wall?**  
A: Yes, the included brackets work for both vertical and horizontal surfaces. Just make sure you’re drilling into solid material — the brackets are solid plastic, not metal.  

**Q: Do the heads stay where you point them?**  
A: Yes, the friction hinges are tight. One reviewer noted they’ve held position through a full winter of storms without drooping.
