Let me save you the math. If you've priced out iRobot's genuine filters and brushes lately, you know a single 2-pack of filters runs you $20-plus. This 26-piece kit costs about the same and covers roughly six months of normal maintenance. That's the pitch. Here's whether it holds up.

## What's Actually in the Box

The kit breaks down into three categories: eight high-efficiency filters, eight rubber multi-surface brushes, eight three-pronged side brushes, plus two cleaning tools. For a Roomba i3 or i7 used daily in a home with pets, that's roughly two full filter changes per month for four months, or stretched longer if you're diligent about tapping out debris between swaps.

The rubber brushes are the star here. They're the same black, star-shaped design as OEM — not the older bristle style. That matters because rubber brushes don't tangle with hair the way bristles do. The side brushes are the standard three-arm configuration, and the included cleaning tool has the little hook for pulling hair off the brush axle.

## Fit and Real-World Performance

I tested these on a Roomba i7+. The rubber brushes click into the hexagonal drive pins without slop — no wobble, no grinding. Filter seating is the one friction point. The foam gasket around the filter edge is slightly thicker than OEM, so the first insertion takes a firm push. Once seated, they hold fine and the dust bin seals properly. One Amazon reviewer put it this way: "The filters are a little tight the first time, but after that they're fine. My robot hasn't complained once."

Suction and debris pickup are indistinguishable from the genuine parts in my testing. The robot's sensors didn't flag any airflow issues, which is the telltale sign of a cheap filter choking the system.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The value math dominates the feedback. With 220 ratings holding at 4.4 stars, the pattern is clear: people are tired of paying iRobot's premium for consumables. ## Where It Falls Short

Two honest complaints surface repeatedly. First, the side brush screws are made from softer metal than OEM. Use the included screwdriver gently — hand-tighten until snug and stop. Over-torquing will strip the heads. Second, the filter tightness I mentioned cuts both ways. If you have arthritis or grip issues, that first filter swap will frustrate you. It loosens up after a couple of installations, but the first one is genuinely stiff.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you own a Roomba i3, i3+, i4, i7, or i7+ and you're tired of paying $25 for two OEM filters. This is also a solid pick if you run your robot daily or have pets — you'll blow through filters fast enough that the bulk quantity makes sense.

**Skip it if** you have a different Roomba model (this doesn't fit the 600, 800, or 900 series), or if you're the type who only changes filters twice a year — the spare parts will sit around long enough that the gaskets might dry out.

## FAQ

**Will these work in my Roomba i3 and i7?**
Yes. The kit covers the i3, i3+, i4, i7, and i7+. If you own two compatible models, this one kit services both.

**How often should I replace the filters?**
iRobot recommends every 2 months with regular use. With pets, bump that to monthly. The kit gives you eight, so you're covered for 8-16 months depending on your household.

**Are these genuine iRobot parts?**
No, they're third-party compatible replacements. They match OEM dimensions and performance, but they aren't made by iRobot and don't carry the brand warranty.

## The Verdict

This kit doesn't reinvent the wheel — it just makes the wheel dramatically cheaper. The parts perform at OEM levels, the quantity covers real-world usage patterns, and the savings are undeniable. The tight filter fit and soft screws are minor annoyances, not dealbreakers. If you're maintaining a compatible Roomba, this is the kind of smart consumable purchase that keeps your robot running without bleeding your wallet dry.
