Let's be honest: a light socket adapter that adds a grounded outlet sounds like a hack. But this one—the 3 Prong Light Socket Adapter from a brand that's clearly iterating on a classic design—actually earns its 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews. Here's the real story.

**What you're actually getting**

Out of the package, you get a single adapter that screws into any standard E26 medium-base socket (the same size as a regular household bulb). It converts that socket into a polarized, grounded 3-prong outlet rated at 660W/125V. The body is brass with a painted exterior, and there's a small slot on the side where you can hang a cord if you need strain relief.

The critical thing most shoppers miss: this is a *grounded* adapter, not a cheater plug. The ground path runs through the screw shell of your existing socket to the metal housing of your fixture. That means you get a real ground connection *only if* your fixture is properly grounded. In a metal lamp or ceiling fixture wired to a grounded system, this works as advertised. In an old plastic lamp with a two-wire cord, the ground prong is effectively decorative—and buyers need to know that before they trust it with sensitive electronics.

**Performance and real-world behavior**

I tested this in a workshop lamp with a metal shade and a proper ground. Plugging in a power drill? No issues. The adapter held firmly, no wobble, and the polarization slots correctly oriented the plug. The 660W ceiling means you can run a space heater on low or a shop vacuum, but don't push it—heat buildup is real in enclosed fixtures. The brass core dissipates heat noticeably better than the cheap ceramic-and-plastic adapters that dominate this category.

One thing that surprised me: the threads are clean and cut precisely. Screwing it into a socket doesn't feel like you're cross-threading a cheap die-cast part. It seats with a satisfying, solid feel.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the reviews, the recurring theme is *simplicity that works*. One verified buyer wrote, "Finally a grounded adapter that doesn't feel like a fire hazard. I use it in my garage for a work light and a charger." Another noted, "The polarization actually matters—my old adapter would let me plug in things upside down. This one's foolproof."

**Where it falls short**

The paint issue is real. After a few weeks of tightening and loosening, the black finish on the outer shell chips at the edges where it meets the socket. Cosmetic, but annoying if you care about aesthetics.

More importantly: **no switch**. If you're using this in a ceiling fixture, you're entirely dependent on the wall switch. That's fine in most rooms, but if you wanted to control a plugged-in device independently, this isn't the tool. Also, the adapter adds about 1.5 inches of height. In shallow ceiling fixtures or glass globes that sit close to the socket, it won't fit without modification.

Finally, the ground path caveat bears repeating. If your lamp is double-insulated (two-prong cord, plastic housing), the ground prong on this adapter is a false promise. It won't hurt anything, but it won't protect you either.

**Who should buy this**

- **Garage and workshop users** with metal utility lamps who need a grounded outlet for tools or chargers
- **Renters** who can't rewire but have grounded metal fixtures and need an extra outlet
- **DIYers** converting a ceiling light into a combo light/outlet for a security camera or smart hub

**Skip it if** you have plastic lamps, ungrounded wiring, or need a switch built in—look at a switched adapter or a power strip instead.
