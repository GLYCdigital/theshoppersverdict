The MORVANO 60" x 32" acrylic shower base sits in a sweet spot most manufacturers ignore: it's pre-leveled, reinforced, and priced like a mid-tier pan. But before you pull the trigger on a bathroom remodel, let's get one thing straight — this isn't a luxury spa pan. It's a workhorse that does the job without drama.

## What's actually in the box

You get the pan itself, wrapped in protective film. That's it. No drain assembly, no leveling feet, no instructions beyond a basic setup sheet. As shown in the product image, the pan features a 3" threshold, a low center drain placement, and a textured floor pattern that runs the full length.

The dimensions are true: 60" long, 32" wide, 3" at the threshold. It's designed for a standard 60" alcove — measure your rough opening before ordering. The pan tapers slightly toward the drain, so the outer edges sit higher than the center, which is exactly what you want for proper water flow.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The reinforced acrylic is the headline here. At roughly 30 pounds, it's light enough for one person to wrestle into place, but the fiberglass backing gives it structural rigidity. I've seen cheaper pans flex in the middle — you step on them and feel the give. This one doesn't. Multiple buyers confirmed this, with one noting, "I'm 230 pounds and this pan doesn't budge. No flexing, no creaking. Solid."

The factory-leveled claim holds up. That's not marketing fluff — the bottom is machined flat with a mounting flange that sits directly on the subfloor. You don't need a mortar bed, which saves you a day of work and a bag of Quikrete. Just set it in place, check for level, and screw it down through the flange.

The textured floor is subtle but effective — small diamond patterns that provide grip without feeling like sandpaper on bare feet. It's a welcome improvement over the glossy pans that turn into ice rinks with soap.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings is backed by recurring themes. Leveling is the most common compliment. "I've installed a dozen shower pans," one reviewer wrote. "This is the only one that sat perfectly level without me shimming anything. Huge time saver."

Another frequent note: the pan doesn't scratch easily. Acrylic is softer than fiberglass, but the gel coat on this unit held up during installation — one buyer dragged a metal utility knife across it by accident and reported zero visible marks.

## Where it falls short

The drain flange is the biggest gotcha. It's not included, and you'll need to match it to your drain height. The pan's center drain opening is standard 3-1/2", but the flange depth varies, so measure carefully before buying. Expect to spend an extra $20–40 on a compatible kit.

The masking tape residue issue is real. The protective film leaves sticky residue along the edges that requires Goo Gone or mineral spirits to remove. It's cosmetic, not structural, but it's annoying when you're trying to finish a job cleanly.

Also note: the 3" threshold is shorter than many competition pans (some run 4-1/2"). If you're building a curbless shower or need wheelchair access, this won't work. It's designed for standard framed alcoves with a separate curb.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the 3" height include the tile flange?**
No. The 3" is the pan's total height at the threshold. The tile flange adds roughly 1-1/2" above that, so your finished curb will be around 4-1/2" tall. Plan your wall tile accordingly.

**Q: Can one person install this?**
Yes, but it's awkward. At 60" long and 30+ pounds, you'll want a second set of hands to maneuver it into the alcove without scratching the walls. Once it's in place, one person can easily level and secure it.

**Q: Does the textured floor make it harder to clean?**
Slightly. The texture is shallow enough that a squeegee and mild cleaner handle it fine. Avoid abrasive scrub pads — they'll dull the finish over time.
