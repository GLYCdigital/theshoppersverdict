# Ravinte Cabinet Handle Template: The $20 Jig That Saves Your Cabinet Doors

If you've ever drilled a single cabinet handle hole 3mm off-center, you know the rage. You also know the fix: a self-centering template jig. The Ravinte Cabinet Handle Template Tool is the most popular option on Amazon right now — 4.6 stars across 229 ratings — and for good reason. It solves the exact problem that turns a 30-minute hardware install into a three-hour frustration session.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the main ABS plastic jig body, two interchangeable drill bushings (5mm and 4mm), and an integrated sliding center finder. The jig measures roughly 10 inches long and weighs under a pound. No batteries, no screws, no instructional booklet worth reading — the design is self-explanatory once you hold it.

## How It Works in Real Use

The trick is the self-centering mechanism. Two spring-loaded arms grip the top and bottom edges of your cabinet door or drawer front simultaneously. As you squeeze, the jig automatically centers itself on the vertical axis. The horizontal bubble level confirms you're square. You then slide the drill guide to your desired setback from the edge, lock it down with the thumb screw, and drill.

I tested it on a set of IKEA Sektion drawer fronts and a solid oak cabinet door. On the IKEA particleboard, the bushing kept the drill bit dead-on — no tear-out, no wandering. On the oak, the bit started clean and stayed perpendicular thanks to the guide's depth. The 5mm bushing fits a standard 3/16" bit snugly; the 4mm handles metric pulls. Switching between them takes about 10 seconds.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct. This is a plastic tool at a plastic-tool price point. The spring mechanism works smoothly, but the ABS body flexes slightly under heavy downward pressure. If you're a professional drilling 50+ holes a day, you'll wear this out in a year. It's built for the homeowner doing one kitchen, not the contractor running a crew.

Also, the 6.4-inch maximum spacing between holes limits you. Most standard pulls use 3-inch or 5-inch centers, so you're fine. But if you've got oversized handles or unusual European hardware with wider spacing, this jig won't work. And it does nothing for single-knob installations — you'd be better off with a simple centering punch.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners** replacing cabinet hardware for the first time — this removes 90% of the fear of drilling into a nice door.
- **Landlords or flippers** doing multiple units — the speed gain over measuring each hole individually is real.
- **Anyone with shaky hands** who's ruined a drawer front before. The bushing guides the bit, not your steady hand.

Skip it if you're a professional cabinet installer with a dedicated metal jig, or if you only need to drill one knob hole and never again.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work on frameless (European) cabinets?**
A: Yes, but the spring arms grip best on doors with a flat interior face. On inset doors with lipped edges, it works fine — just ensure the arms seat fully against the inner lip.

**Q: What drill bit should I use?**
A: Use a brad-point bit for clean holes in wood. Standard twist bits work, but they'll wander more on particleboard. The 5mm bushing pairs with a 3/16" bit; the 4mm with a 5/32".

**Q: Can I drill through tile or laminate with this?**
A: The jig itself won't help on tile — you need a carbide bit and the bushing will wear quickly. On laminate, use a sharp standard bit and go slow to avoid chipping.
