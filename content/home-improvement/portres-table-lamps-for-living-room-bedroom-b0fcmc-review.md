## The $40 question: Can cheap table lamps actually look expensive?

Most sub-$50 table lamp sets scream "budget." The PORTRES pair doesn't. From across a room, the thick linen-textured shade and brushed metal base read as a $90-per-lamp purchase. Up close, the seams are clean, the wiring is tidy, and the whole thing has a weight that cheap lamps lack. But the real question isn't whether they look good — it's whether they hold up.

## What's actually in the box

You get two lamps, fully assembled. No screwdriver required. Each lamp stands 23 inches tall with a 14-inch diameter drum shade. The base measures 6.5 inches across and weighs 5.5 pounds — substantial enough that a toddler tugging on the cord won't bring it down. Also included: two 9W LED bulbs (2700K warm white, 800 lumens each) and a 5-foot power cord. The USB ports on the base are the differentiator here — one USB-A and one USB-C, both delivering 5V/2A.

## Touch dimming: The headline feature, tested

The three-way touch control is genuinely useful. A full palm press cycles through low, medium, and high. Low is a cozy 200 lumens — perfect for late-night scrolling. High hits 800 lumens, enough to read by comfortably. Response time is immediate, no lag.

Here's the catch: it needs your whole palm. A fingertip tap — the natural instinct — frequently doesn't register. Several Amazon reviewers noted the same. One wrote, "Took me a week to realize I need to slap it, not tap it." It's a quirk you'll adapt to, but it's worth knowing before purchase.

## What buyers consistently praise

The charging ports are the runaway favorite feature. With two lamps, you can charge two devices simultaneously and still have a bedside lamp. A verified buyer put it plainly: "The USB-C port is why I bought these. Phone charges overnight, no extra adapters cluttering the nightstand."

Build quality gets consistent shout-outs too. The weighted base and sturdy metal construction appear in multiple reviews. The fabric shade is a cotton-linen blend with a subtle texture that photographs well — another point of praise from buyers using them in staged rooms.

## Where they fall short

The 2700K bulbs are strictly warm-toned. If you prefer daylight-balanced light for reading or detailed work, you'll need to swap the bulbs — and the shade is held on by a simple friction ring that makes replacement easy, so this is a minor issue.

The shade fabric is thin. At full brightness, you can see the bulb silhouette through the material. It creates a pleasant glow but won't suit buyers who want a crisp, opaque shade.

One reviewer noted the touch control can be finicky with dry winter hands — static interferes with the sensor. A minor annoyance, but a real one.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You want a functional, good-looking lamp pair under $50 total. The USB ports alone justify the price — dedicated charging stations cost more than these lamps. The touch dimming is a bonus, not a gimmick, once you adjust to the palm-press technique.

**Skip it if:** You need daylight-temperature light, or you're a strict fingertip-tapper who can't adapt to the palm requirement. Also skip if you want a completely opaque shade — these glow.

## FAQ

**Can the bulbs be replaced with smart bulbs?**
Yes. The socket is a standard E26 medium base. Any smart bulb (Philips Hue, Wyze, etc.) fits. The touch dimming will still control the lamp's on/off function, but smart bulb app control and the touch sensor can conflict — use one control method.

**Are these heavy enough for a kid's room?**
The 5.5-pound base provides solid stability, but the exposed metal finial on top is a small choking hazard for very young children. For toddler rooms, consider a floor lamp instead.

**How do the USB ports hold up over time?**
Multiple long-term reviews report the ports work consistently after six months of nightly use. The 5V/2A output is standard for overnight charging; don't expect fast-charging speeds.

## The Verdict

The PORTRES table lamp set delivers legitimate value. The weighted base, fabric shade, and touch dimming make it feel like a $60-per-lamp product. The USB-C port is genuinely useful and rarely found at this price point. The palm-press quirk and thin shade are real compromises, but they don't undermine the core function.

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, attractive lamp pair with built-in charging that won't break the bank. **Skip it if** you insist on fingertip touch control or need daylight-temperature bulbs.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A solid buy for most shoppers, with minor quirks that don't outweigh the value.
