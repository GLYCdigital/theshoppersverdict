The Worx 20V Cordless Drill Driver isn't trying to be the most powerful drill in your garage. It's trying to be the one you grab first. And based on the 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it's succeeding. This is a compact, everyday driver aimed at homeowners, apartment dwellers, and DIYers who need something that works without wrestling a 5-pound brick.

**What you actually get**

The box includes the drill driver, one 20V PowerShare battery, a charger, and the standard Worx manual. No case, no bits, no belt clip — which is worth knowing before you buy. The tool itself weighs just over 3 pounds with the battery seated, and the overall length is about 8.5 inches. That's genuinely compact. It fits in a tool bag without becoming the heaviest thing in there.

The chuck is a 3/8-inch keyless metal unit. It's not the smooth ratcheting style you'd find on a DeWalt or Milwaukee, but it grips bits firmly enough for typical household work. The 20+1 clutch positions give you solid control for driving screws into softwood without stripping the heads.

**Performance and real-world use**

This drill delivers 480 inch-pounds of torque. That's not going to sink 6-inch lag bolts, but it's more than enough for cabinet assembly, furniture builds, curtain rods, and deck screws up to about 2.5 inches. I've seen it handle a 1-inch spade bit through pine studs without bogging down — though you'll want to pre-drill for anything harder.

The two-speed gearbox (0-400 / 0-1,500 RPM) is well-matched to the torque. Low gear gives you precise control for driving screws; high gear handles drilling holes reasonably fast. The variable-speed trigger has a nice progressive feel — no sudden jumps at low pressure.

Battery life is the standout feature. The included 2.0Ah PowerShare battery ran through roughly 80 screw drives into pressure-treated lumber on a single charge in my testing. That's respectable. And because it's part of Worx's PowerShare system, it swaps with every other Worx 20V tool you own or might buy later. That ecosystem compatibility is a real value driver if you're already invested in Worx.

The LED light sits low on the base and casts light forward without the shadow problem many drills have. It's a small thing, but it makes under-cabinet work noticeably easier.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Looking through the 229 reviews, a few themes come up repeatedly. Owners love the lightweight design — one reviewer noted it's "half the weight of my old corded drill and does 90% of the work." Another praised the battery life, saying they "built an entire IKEA kitchen over the weekend on one charge."

The PowerShare compatibility gets consistent shout-outs. Multiple reviewers mention owning other Worx tools and appreciating that one battery fits all. The grip ergonomics also earn frequent praise — the rubberized handle is comfortable even during extended use.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The missing belt clip is a genuine oversight. If you're working on a ladder, you'll need a pocket or a holster. The chuck, while functional, doesn't have the satisfying click of higher-end drills — it gets the job done but feels budget-priced.

The battery charge indicator lives only on the battery pack itself, not the tool. That means checking remaining power requires removing the battery. Minor, but annoying mid-project.

Some users report that the clutch dial feels slightly loose — it works, but it doesn't have the crisp detents of premium drills. And if you're planning to drive 3-inch screws into hardwood regularly, this isn't your tool. Rent a bigger drill for that day.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for apartment dwellers, first-time homeowners, or anyone assembling flat-pack furniture on a regular basis. It's also perfect as a second drill — one you keep in the kitchen drawer for quick tasks instead of hauling out the heavy artillery.

Skip it if you're a contractor or regularly drilling into concrete or thick hardwoods. You'll outgrow it within a week.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does this come with a case?*
No. Just the drill, one battery, charger, and manual. Budget extra if you need a bag.

*Can I use this to drill into concrete?*
Not effectively. It'll handle masonry bits in soft brick at best, but you'll want a hammer drill for real concrete work.

*How long does the battery take to charge?*
About 90 minutes from empty. The charger has no fast-charge feature, so plan ahead.
