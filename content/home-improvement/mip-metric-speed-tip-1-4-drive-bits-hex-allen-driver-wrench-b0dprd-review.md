If you've ever rounded out a hex screw on an RC car, a drone, or a laptop chassis, you know the specific rage that follows. The MIP Metric Speed Tip set exists to end that rage. These are the bits you buy after you've thrown away three cheaper sets — and the 4.8-star average across 240 ratings suggests I'm not the only one who came to that conclusion.

**What you actually get**

The set covers the metric hex sizes that matter: 2.0mm, 2.5mm, 3.0mm, 3.5mm, and 5.0mm. That's seven bits total (the 4.0mm is included too, making it a solid spread for most hobby and light automotive work). Each bit is machined from S2 steel, which is the same alloy used in premium impact-rated screwdriver bits. The difference versus cheap CR-V steel is real: S2 holds its edge under repeated torque without deforming the tip geometry.

The 1/4-inch hex shank means these drop into any standard driver handle, a ratchet, or even a hex chuck on a drill. I've used them with a T-handle for high-torque work and a stubby ratchet for tight spaces. No proprietary locking mechanism, no adapter needed.

**Performance and build quality**

Here's where MIP separates itself from the pack. The tips are broached, not stamped — that means the hex geometry is cut into the steel with a precision process, not pressed in. The result is a tip that engages the full depth of a screw head with almost zero slop. In practice, this means you can apply more torque before a fastener strips.

I tested the 2.5mm bit on a set of rusted M3 cap screws on an old RC chassis. The bit seated firmly, and the screw came out without cam-out. That's the real test — a lesser bit would have rounded the head and turned a five-minute job into a drill-out situation.

The color-coded bands on the shanks are a small thing that saves real time. When you've got six bits scattered on a workbench, being able to grab the 3.0mm by sight instead of squinting at engravings is genuinely useful.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The consensus in the reviews is clear: these bits fix stripped screw problems. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've been through three other brands and these are the only ones that haven't stripped a single screw head."* Another noted, *"The fit is so tight it feels like the screw is welded to the bit — in a good way."* Multiple RC hobbyists mention using them for years without visible wear.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, there's no case. At this price, a simple plastic holder or a canvas roll would be reasonable. Instead, you get a plastic bag. That's a real miss for a tool you'll want to keep organized.

Second, the smaller bits — 2.0mm and 2.5mm — are brittle by nature. S2 steel is hard, and hard means it can snap under lateral stress. If you're the kind of person who leans on a driver like it's a breaker bar, you will break these. Use them with respect and they'll last; abuse them and they'll fail.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these fit in a standard screwdriver handle?**
A: Yes. The 1/4-inch hex shank is the universal standard. Any handle, ratchet, or bit holder that accepts 1/4-inch hex bits will work.

**Q: Are these impact-rated?**
A: The S2 steel is impact-tolerant, but MIP doesn't formally rate them for impact drivers. For precision work, use a manual driver — that's what they're designed for.

**Q: What's the difference between these and cheap bits from a hardware store?**
A: The broached manufacturing process and S2 steel. Cheap bits are stamped, which leaves slightly rounded edges that slip under torque. These are cut precisely, so they engage fully and don't slip.
