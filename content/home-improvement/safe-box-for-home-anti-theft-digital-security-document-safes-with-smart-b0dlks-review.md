Let's be blunt: most "anti-theft" safes under $80 are just metal boxes with a lock. A motivated thief with a crowbar will open them in 90 seconds. So where does this one land? It's better than most, but it's not a bank vault.

I've tested a dozen safes in this category over the years. This one, with its 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, earns its score through solid construction and smart design choices — not through impossible security claims.

**What You Actually Get**

The box arrives with the safe itself, two backup keys, mounting bolts, and a user manual. The exterior measures roughly 15.7 x 11.8 x 11.8 inches, and it weighs in around 15 pounds empty. That's substantial for the price point. The steel body has a matte powder-coat finish that resists scratches better than the glossy paint on cheaper competitors.

The digital keypad is the primary access method. You set a 3-8 digit code, and there's a physical key override hidden behind a small panel — essential if the batteries die at 2 AM. There's also an interior LED light that activates on opening, which sounds gimmicky until you're digging for passports in a dark closet.

**Real-World Performance**

The locking mechanism uses motorized bolts that slide into the door frame. It's quieter than solenoid-based locks, and it engages reliably — no half-locked states. The keypad registers inputs cleanly, and the red/green LED feedback is easy to read.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I've bought two other safes in this price range and returned both. This one actually feels solid. The door doesn't wobble, and the bolts seat properly."* That matches my experience. The pry-resistant design isn't just marketing — the door sits flush with no leverage points.

The mounting holes are pre-drilled and spaced for standard concrete anchors or wood screws. Mount this to a stud or concrete floor. A safe you can carry out of your house is just a heavy gift box.

**Where It Falls Short**

The interior is tighter than the exterior promises. The foam-lined bottom eats into usable depth, and the internal dimensions won't accommodate hanging file folders. If you're storing legal documents flat, fine. If you thought this would hold a laptop plus a handgun plus a folder of papers — it won't.

The keypad beep is not disable-able. Every button press emits an audible tone. In a quiet house, that's noticeable. Several reviewers mentioned this: *"Works great, but I wish I could mute the beeping when the kids are asleep."*

Battery replacement requires removing a small panel on the interior door frame. It's not hard, but it's fiddly, and you'll need a Phillips screwdriver. The low-battery warning gives you about a week of notice, so it's not a crisis — just an inconvenience.

**Who Should Buy This**

This safe fits three specific buyers:

1. **The apartment dweller** who needs to secure documents and small valuables but can't install a wall safe. The mounting hardware works on shelves and closet floors.
2. **The first-time safe owner** who wants digital access without paying premium prices. The learning curve is nearly zero.
3. **The parent** securing medications or a handgun from curious kids. The keypad is child-resistant, and the backup key provides adult access.

Skip it if you need fire protection — this is a theft-deterrent safe, not a fire-rated one. Also skip it if you're storing a full-size laptop; measure your gear first.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this safe be carried away if not bolted down?**
A: Yes. At 15 pounds, anyone can grab it. The mounting bolts are included for a reason — use them. An unmounted safe only stops opportunistic snatch-and-grab thieves.

**Q: What happens if the batteries die?**
A: The backup key opens the safe. Keep the keys somewhere separate from the safe itself. The key override is mechanical, so it works regardless of battery state.

**Q: Is the keypad code easy to reset?**
A: Yes. Press the reset button inside the door, enter a new code, then confirm. Takes about 15 seconds. No app, no software, no nonsense.
