If you're tired of buying single-grit packs at full price and ending up with a drawer full of half-used sheets, the HSYMQ 24-piece assortment deserves a serious look. For roughly the cost of two or three specialty sheets at a big-box store, you get 24 sheets across 12 grits — from brutal 40-grit stripping to mirror-finish 3000-grit polishing. **What's actually in the box**  

The pack breaks down as two 5" x 5" sheets per grit: 40, 60, 80, 100, 120, 150, 180, 220, 320, 400, 600, and 3000. That's the full spectrum — fine enough to polish acrylic edges, coarse enough to strip paint off a door. The sheets are flat and well-cut, with no curling at the edges straight out of the package. **Real-world performance and build quality**  

I put these through the paces on three materials: red oak, pine, and a steel tabletop. The aluminum oxide grain delivers a consistent cut — no sudden "dead zones" where the grit gives out prematurely. The A-weight paper backing is the standout here; it's thick enough to handle aggressive pressure without tearing along fold lines, which is the #1 failure point on cheap sheets. On pine, the 80 and 100 grits loaded up with sawdust faster than premium brands, but a quick tap against the workbench cleared most of it. The 3000-grit sheet, often an afterthought in these packs, actually worked well for a satin finish on a polyurethane coat — a pleasant surprise at this price point.

**Where it falls short (and you should know this)**  

These are plain sheets — no PSA adhesive, no hook-and-loop backing. If you're using a random-orbit sander, you'll need to cut them to fit and use an adhesive spray, which adds cost and hassle. The 40 and 60 grits are aggressive but clog fast on softwoods; you'll want a sanding block or a quick vacuum session to extend their life. Also, the 5" x 5" size is smaller than standard 9" x 11" sheets — fine for hand sanding and small details, but you'll burn through more sheets on large flat surfaces like tabletops.

**FAQ: Three questions shoppers actually ask**  

*Q: Can these be used wet?*  
Yes, the paper handles wet sanding for the 600 and 3000 grits, but it won't last as long as wet/dry film. Keep it to short passes and let it dry between uses.

*Q: Do the sheets fit standard 5" random-orbit sanders?*  
No — they're plain sheets. You'll need to cut them to the sander's shape and use a spray adhesive. They're designed for hand sanding and small power tools with a sanding block.

*Q: Are the grits accurate to the label?*  
Yes, in my tests the 120 and 220 matched expected cut rates on oak. No mislabeled or wildly off-spec grits, which is a common issue in budget packs.
