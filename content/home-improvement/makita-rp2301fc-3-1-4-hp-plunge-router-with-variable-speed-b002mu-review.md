If you've ever watched a cheap router buck sideways on a plunge cut, you know the fear. The Makita RP2301FC attacks that problem with brute force and engineering — a 3-1/4 HP motor paired with soft start that eases the bit into the work instead of slamming it. This is the router you buy when you're done fighting your tools.

## What's Actually in the Box

Makita ships this as a plunge-only kit. Inside you get the router motor, plunge base, a 1/4" and 1/2" collet (both installed-ready), two wrenches, a guide bush, and the instruction manual. The RA1160 fixed base is NOT included — budget for it separately if you want both setups. The plunge base itself is a hefty aluminum casting with dual chrome-plated guide rods that slide through brass bushings, not plastic sleeves that wear out in a season.

## Performance: Smooth Power Where It Counts

The motor delivers 3-1/4 peak horsepower with a variable speed dial ranging from 8,000 to 24,000 RPM. In practice, you'll dial it down to 10-12K for large bits and let it rip at 20K+ for edge profiling. The soft start is the standout feature — it ramps up over roughly a second, so you don't get that violent torque twist when you pull the trigger. I ran a 1/2" straight bit through red oak at full depth, and the router tracked smooth with zero bogging.

Depth adjustment is where this thing shines. The turret stop gives you six positive stops, and the fine-adjustment knob lets you dial in thousandths of an inch. For dovetail jigs or repeated passes where you step down gradually, this precision is a genuine time-saver. The plunge action itself is buttery — no binding, no sticking — and the return springs bring the bit back up reliably every time.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 229 ratings holding a 4.6 average, owners hammer on the same points. One reviewer put it plainly: "I've owned routers from all the majors, and this is the smoothest plunge action I've ever felt. The soft start alone sold me." The electric brake draws frequent mentions too — it stops the bit in under two seconds, which matters when you're reaching for a workpiece and the cutter is still spinning. Several users running raised panel bits and lock miter joints reported zero bogging, which backs up the motor's real-world muscle.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the weight. At 20.9 pounds with the base, this is not a one-hand tool. Overhead work like trimming cabinet tops will fatigue you quickly — one owner said, "It's a beast. My arms are toast after an hour of overhead stuff." The stiff plunge springs also require deliberate downward pressure; if you have arthritis or weaker grip strength, this will wear on you.

The fixed-base-not-included situation is a genuine annoyance. At this price point, you'd expect both bases. Adding the RA1160 later costs another $150, which pushes the total well past what a comparable Triton or Bosch combo kit runs. For those who mostly work edge profiles on a table, the plunge-only configuration might be overkill.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a cabinet maker, furniture builder, or serious hobbyist doing heavy joinery — dovetails, raised panels, mortises. The precision depth control and smooth power justify the price.

**Skip it if** you're a weekend DIYer doing light edge routing, or if you primarily need a fixed-base router for table mounting. A lighter combo kit serves you better for less money.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the RP2301FC come with a fixed base?**
A: No. It ships with the plunge base only. The RA1160 fixed base is a separate purchase.

**Q: What's the maximum bit shank it accepts?**
A: Both 1/4" and 1/2" collets are included, so you can use any standard router bit.

**Q: Is the variable speed worth it?**
A: Yes, especially for large-diameter bits. Running a 2" raised panel bit at 24,000 RPM risks burning; dialing down to 10,000 RPM gives cleaner cuts and safer operation.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Makita RP2301FC is a professional-grade plunge router that delivers on its power rating with precision you can measure. It's heavy, pricey, and incomplete out of the box, but for serious woodworkers who need reliable, repeatable plunge cuts, it's one of the best tools in its class. Buy it if your projects demand accuracy; skip it if you need a lightweight all-rounder.
