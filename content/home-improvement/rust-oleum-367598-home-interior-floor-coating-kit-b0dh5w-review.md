Let's be straight about this: the Rust-Oleum 367598 Home Interior Floor Coating Kit can transform a tired concrete floor into something that looks almost like polished stone. But the difference between a showroom finish and a patchy mess comes down to one thing — how seriously you take the prep work.

This is a two-part epoxy system, not a simple paint. The kit includes the epoxy base, a decorative color chip blend, and the essential tools: a roller cover, tray, and mixing stick. As shown in the product image, it's designed to cover up to 250 square feet, which is right for a standard one-car garage or a medium basement. The color chips are the real selling point — they hide minor imperfections and give the floor that commercial-grade speckled look.

**What You Actually Get**

Open the box and you'll find the two epoxy cans, the chip bag, and the applicator set. That's it. No primer, no etching solution, no squeegee. For a kit marketed as "complete," the omission of an etching agent is notable — you'll need to buy a concrete etcher separately unless your floor is brand new and unsealed. The roller cover included is serviceable but thin; plan to swap it for a higher-quality 3/8-inch nap if you want a smoother finish.

**Performance and Durability**

When applied correctly, this coating is genuinely tough. The epoxy creates a chemical bond with the concrete that resists oil stains, gasoline, and mild solvents — things that would permanently mark bare concrete. Several reviewers noted it handles hot tire pickup well, which is the #1 killer of DIY floor coatings. One buyer put it simply: "Three years in, still looks like the day I rolled it. My only regret is not doing this sooner."

The two-part mix is forgiving if you follow the instructions to the letter. You get about 30 minutes of working time per batch, so plan to work in sections. The kit's coverage estimate of 250 square feet assumes a porous, properly etched surface. On smoother concrete, you'll stretch it further but get thinner coverage.

**Where It Falls Short**

Here's the honest part. The 4.3-star average hides a bimodal distribution — people either love it or hate it, and the haters almost always skipped prep. If your concrete has old paint, oil stains, or a glossy sealer, this coating will fail. It's not a question of if, but when. The cure time is also a commitment: 24 hours before foot traffic, 72 hours before you can park on it. That's a full weekend plus some, minimum.

The color chip distribution is another minor gripe. The bag contains varying chip sizes, and they have a tendency to clump when poured. You'll want to sprinkle them by hand in small batches rather than dumping. A few reviewers mentioned the finish looked "spotty" — that's user error, not product failure, but it's worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**

This kit is ideal for the homeowner who has a garage, basement, or utility room with bare, unsealed concrete that's in decent shape. If you're willing to spend a Saturday on prep — cleaning, etching, rinsing, drying — and another day on application, you'll get a professional-looking floor for a fraction of the cost of a pro install. Skip it if your floor has significant cracks, moisture issues, or existing coatings. It's also not the right choice for high-traffic commercial spaces; this is residential-grade epoxy.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this over painted concrete?* No. The epoxy needs to bond directly to bare, etched concrete. Paint will cause delamination.

*How long does the kit actually last?* With proper prep, expect 5–8 years in a garage. Basements with less UV exposure and traffic can go longer.

*Do I need to buy anything else?* Yes — a concrete etching solution and a decent roller. Budget an extra $20–25.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have a clean, bare concrete floor, you're patient with prep, and you want a durable, good-looking finish without hiring a contractor.

**Skip it if:** You want a weekend-only project, your floor has existing coatings or serious stains, or you're not willing to buy the separate etching solution.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Great value and performance when installed correctly, but the prep demands are real and the kit isn't as "complete" as the box suggests. For the price, it's still the best DIY epoxy option in this coverage class.
