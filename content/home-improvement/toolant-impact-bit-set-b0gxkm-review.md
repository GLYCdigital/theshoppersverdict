## What's in the box

The toolant Impact Bit Set ships with 40 pieces: a solid mix of Phillips, slotted, Torx, and hex bits, plus a magnetic holder and a few socket adapters. As shown in the product image, everything nests into a compact, clear-lid case roughly the size of a paperback book. The layout is thoughtful — each bit sits in its own molded slot with size markings printed underneath, so you're not digging through a jumbled pile mid-project.

The bits themselves are S2 alloy steel, which is the industry standard for impact-rated tools. The CNC-machined tips are precision-ground, not stamped, which makes a noticeable difference in how they seat into fastener heads.

## Real-world performance

I put these through the usual gauntlet: a DeWalt impact driver, an old stubborn deck, and a weekend of furniture assembly. The standout feature is the magnetic holder. It grips screws firmly enough that you can angle the driver downward without losing the fastener — a small thing that saves real time when you're working overhead or in tight cabinet spaces.

The bits hold up well under torque. I drove roughly 60 deck screws with a single Phillips #2 bit, and the tip showed only minor wear with no visible twisting or cam-out. That's the S2 steel doing its job. The Torx bits also seated cleanly into stripped-out screw heads that cheaper bits couldn't grip.

One thing I appreciate: the case. Unlike most bit sets where the lid pops open in your bag, this one has a positive latch that clicks shut and stays shut. The interior slots are snug enough that nothing rattles loose even after being tossed in a work truck.

## Where it falls short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The case hinge is the weakest link — it's functional but feels thin. If you're the type to throw tools loose into a drawer, expect the latch to eventually wear out. That's cosmetic, not functional, but worth knowing.

The bigger limitation is the socket selection. You get standard imperial sizes only (8mm through 13mm equivalents in SAE). If you work on metric-heavy vehicles or appliances, you'll need a separate set. This is a bit set, not a socket set — but the omission still limits its utility for automotive work.
