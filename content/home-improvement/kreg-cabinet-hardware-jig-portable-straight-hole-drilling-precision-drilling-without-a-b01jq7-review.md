If you've ever drilled a cabinet pull hole that came out crooked, you know the frustration. The hole walks, the bit wanders, and suddenly your "professional" cabinet installation has a 1/8-inch mistake staring at you every time you open the drawer. The Kreg Cabinet Hardware Jig is designed to eliminate that problem — and for the most part, it delivers.

## What You Actually Get

The jig is a compact, portable unit that clamps directly onto your drawer front or cabinet door. It's built from Kreg's typical glass-filled nylon — tough, lightweight, and clearly designed to survive job site abuse. The key feature is the self-centering guide bushing: you place the unit over your center mark, and the bushing automatically centers itself before you drill.

You get two drilling positions on the jig: one at 1-3/8 inches and another at 3-3/4 inches from the edge. These aren't random numbers — they match the standard mounting dimensions for most cabinet pulls and knobs. That means you can drill both holes for a pull without re-measuring, as long as you're working with standard spacing.

## The Self-Centering System in Practice

This is where the jig earns its keep. The bushing mechanism uses three centering fingers that grip the drill bit and force it into perfect alignment with your marked center point. I tested it on scrap oak, pine, and a melamine-coated MDF panel. Every single hole came out exactly where I marked it — no walking, no drift.

The integrated depth stop is another real feature, not a gimmick. If you're drilling into a 3/4-inch drawer front, you can set the stop so your bit doesn't punch through the back side. That's critical for cabinet doors where blow-through is visible. Several Amazon reviewers specifically mention this: "The depth stop saved me from ruining two new cabinet doors. I didn't realize how easy it was to drill too deep until I used this."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. The jig doesn't include a drill bit, which feels like an oversight at this price point. You'll need a 3/16-inch bit for most pulls — Kreg sells a separate accessory kit, but you shouldn't have to buy a drill guide and then realize you can't use it out of the box.

The clamping mechanism also has quirks. On narrow drawer fronts (under 2 inches wide) or heavily rounded edges, the clamp pads don't always seat flush. One reviewer noted: "Works great on flat surfaces, but I had to hold it in place on my shaker-style doors with rounded edges. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying."

There's also no bubble level built in. The jig keeps your bit perpendicular to the face, but it won't help you mark the horizontal line correctly. You still need to measure and mark your pull height accurately — the jig handles the drilling, not the layout.

## Who Should Buy This

**The DIY kitchen remodeler** — If you're hanging new doors or updating pulls on existing cabinets, this jig will save you from the most common installation error. It's cheaper than replacing a botched door.

**The rental property flipper** — Speed matters. The dual-position stops mean you can drill consistent pulls across an entire kitchen without re-measuring every hole. One reviewer said: "I did 14 drawers in under an hour. The first one took me five minutes; after that, it was drill, flip, drill, move."

**Skip it if** you're only drilling a single knob hole and already have steady hands. For one hole, a center punch and a sharp bit will do fine. But if you're doing more than a handful, the consistency is worth it.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with all drill bits?**
A: It accepts standard 1/4-inch hex shank bits and round shank bits up to 3/8-inch diameter. Your typical 3/16-inch pull bit works fine. Larger Forstner bits won't fit.

**Q: Can I use it on cabinet doors that are already installed?**
A: Yes, but you'll need clearance. The jig is about 4 inches deep, so you need that much space between the door face and any adjacent wall or appliance.

**Q: What's the maximum drawer front thickness it can handle?**
A: The depth stop adjusts up to about 1-1/8 inches. For thicker material, you can remove the stop entirely, but you'll lose the blow-through protection.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're installing pulls on more than a handful of cabinets, you value straight holes over saving $30, or you've ever had a drill bit walk on you and ruin a workpiece.

**Skip it if:** You're drilling one knob, you already own a drill press, or you're comfortable freehanding holes with a center punch.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect (the missing bit and fiddly clamping cost it a perfect score), but for the core job it promises — straight, centered, consistent hardware holes — it performs admirably. The Kreg Cabinet Hardware Jig is one of those tools that makes you wonder how you managed without it.
