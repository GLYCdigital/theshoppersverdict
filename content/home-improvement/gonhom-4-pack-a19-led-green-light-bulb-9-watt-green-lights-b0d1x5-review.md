If you're shopping for colored bulbs, you've probably seen the usual suspects: cheap plastic-coated LEDs that fade after a month, or party bulbs that look orange instead of red. The Gonhom 4-pack A19 LED green bulbs sit in a different lane. These are purpose-built colored bulbs with a frosted glass finish, and they deliver what they promise — consistent, saturated green light without the washed-out look you get from cheaper alternatives.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get four A19-shaped bulbs with standard E26 medium screw bases, so they fit almost any household fixture. Each bulb draws 9 watts and replaces a 60W incandescent in terms of light output. The frosted glass dome is the standout design choice here — it's not a painted coating that flakes off, but actual tinted glass that holds up to heat and handling.

The color is a deep, true green — think traffic light, not neon. The beam spread is wide (roughly 220 degrees), which makes these better for general room illumination than spot lighting.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested these in three scenarios: a standard desk lamp, a ceiling fixture with four bulbs, and an outdoor porch light. The color consistency across all four bulbs is excellent — no variation in hue or brightness. At 800 lumens, they're bright enough to light a small room with a green wash, but not so bright that they're unusable for ambient effects.

One thing that stands out is the build quality. The glass feels thicker than the cheap bulbs I've handled, and the base is machined metal — not the flimsy aluminum that strips threads. Heat management is solid too; after two hours of continuous use, the base was warm but not hot, which suggests the driver circuitry is doing its job.

Buyers on Amazon back this up. One reviewer noted, "Bought these for my haunted house and they're still going strong after two seasons of outdoor use," while another said, "The green is actually green — not yellow-green like other bulbs I've tried."

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest limitation is the lack of dimming capability. If you want to dial down the intensity for mood lighting or a subtle grow-room glow, you're out of luck. These run at full brightness or off.

Also, they're bright. Really bright. For nighttime use — say, a porch light or a bedroom lamp while winding down — the green output can feel intense. Several reviewers mentioned using these for nighttime photography or hunting and finding the output more powerful than anticipated.

Finally, there's no remote or smart-home integration. These are dumb bulbs — just screw them in and flip the switch. If you need app control or scheduling, look at smart RGB options instead.

## FAQ

**Are these bulbs safe for outdoor use?**  
Yes, they're rated for damp locations. The sealed glass and metal base hold up to humidity, but avoid direct, prolonged rain exposure — they're not fully waterproof.

**Will a 9W green LED replace my 60W incandescent?**  
For colored lighting, yes. You get roughly the same output, but it's green light, not white. If you need actual task lighting, these aren't a replacement for white bulbs.

**Do these work in enclosed fixtures?**  
Yes. The heat sink design handles enclosed spaces without overheating, which is more than I can say for some cheaper LEDs.
