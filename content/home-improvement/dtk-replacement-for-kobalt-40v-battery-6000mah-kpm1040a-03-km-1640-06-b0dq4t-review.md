**The 6000mAh Upgrade Your Kobalt 40V Arsenal Has Been Waiting For**

If you own Kobalt 40V outdoor tools, you know the pain: the stock 2.0Ah and 4.0Ah batteries die right when the lawn is half-finished. The DTK KPM1040A-03 replacement packs a 6000mAh punch—50% more capacity than the standard 4.0Ah—and it costs less than buying a second OEM pack. But is the extra runtime worth the heft? I tested it on a Kobalt 40V blower and string trimmer for two weeks. Here’s the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the DTK 40V 6000mAh battery unit (model KPM1040A-03, compatible with KM-1640-06) and a basic manual. There’s no charger—this is a replacement battery for your existing Kobalt charger, which works fine. The unit mimics the OEM footprint exactly: same slide-in rails, same rear latch, same terminal layout. It also features the standard 4-LED fuel gauge on the top face, so you can check remaining charge without mounting it to a tool.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The plastic casing feels dense and rigid, not cheap like some third-party cells. The 18650 cells inside are rated for 6000mAh, and in my runtime test on a Kobalt 700 CFM blower at full throttle, I got **22 minutes of continuous use**—compared to roughly 14 minutes with a worn OEM 4.0Ah. That’s a meaningful difference for clearing a large driveway.

The fit is snug. It slides onto the tool with a satisfying click, though the rails are slightly tighter than OEM. Don’t force it—align the grooves and it seats without rocking. On the charger, it took 1 hour 45 minutes to top up from empty, which is on par with OEM charging times.

One thing I appreciated: the DTK holds voltage under load better than the Kobalt 4.0Ah. On my string trimmer, the head didn’t bog down as the battery drained—it kept full RPM until the final cutoff, rather than slowing progressively. That’s a sign of quality cells and a solid BMS (battery management system).

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest tradeoffs: The 6000mAh pack is **heavier**—about 2.1 lbs versus 1.8 lbs for the 4.0Ah OEM. On a pole saw or extended overhead trimming, that extra 0.3 lbs becomes noticeable after 20 minutes.

Second, the LED fuel gauge is the standard 4-bar design, not a percentage readout. The first bar drops after about 15 minutes, and the last bar blinks for a while before cutoff—less precise than higher-end packs. It’s not a dealbreaker, but don’t expect telemetry.

Finally, there’s no carrying case or lanyard in the box. You’ll need to store it in a drawer or buy a separate pouch.
