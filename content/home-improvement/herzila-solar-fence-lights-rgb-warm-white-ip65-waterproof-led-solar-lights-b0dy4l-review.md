**Herzila Solar Fence Lights Review: RGB & Warm White, IP65 Waterproof — Worth It?**  

You’ve seen a dozen solar fence lights that promise “festive color” and deliver a flickering, sad blue glow by 9 PM. The Herzila Solar Fence Lights aren’t that. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, these small (4.1 x 2.2 x 1.3-inch) puck-style lights actually do what the listing claims — and a few things it doesn’t say clearly.  

Here’s what you’re really buying, and where the tradeoffs hide.  

**What’s in the box**  
Six light units, six mounting brackets, screws, and a tiny instruction sheet. No tools are needed beyond a Phillips screwdriver. Each light has a solar panel on top, a button on the front, and a built-in 600mAh battery. The IP65 rating means they’re dust-tight and survive hose-downs and rainstorms — not submersion, but they’ll handle a monsoon on your fence line.  

**Performance: The good, the meh, and the honest**  
The headline feature is the three-mode button: press once for RGB cycling (red→green→blue→purple, about a 5-second loop), twice for fixed warm white, three times for a static RGB color (it holds whichever color you stop on). That’s it — no remote, no app, no timer nonsense. For a fence light, this is actually a strength. You don’t want to fiddle with Bluetooth at 10 PM.  

Warm white mode is the sleeper hit. At 20 lumens per unit, it’s not a floodlight, but it throws enough light to mark a walkway or porch edge. One buyer put it plainly: “I bought these for the color, but I ended up leaving them on warm white — it looks like a proper little lantern, not a toy.”  

RGB mode is fun for about a week, then most people settle into white. That’s fine. The color transition is smooth, not strobe-like, and the LEDs are bright enough to be visible from 30 feet away.  

**Where it falls short**  
The battery is the weak link. In RGB mode, you’ll get 4–5 hours on a full sunny day’s charge. If your fence gets partial shade, expect closer to 3. That means lights will die around midnight in summer — fine for ambiance, but not for overnight security. The mounting brackets are also thinner than I’d like. They work fine on wood fences, but if you’re attaching to metal or vinyl, the screws strip easily. One reviewer noted: “The light is great, but the bracket cracked on the second screw turn. I used zip ties instead — worked fine.”  

Also, there’s no on/off switch. The button cycles modes, but you can’t fully power them down without covering the solar panel. If you live in a place with long summer days, they’ll charge and run every night whether you want them on or not.  

**Who should buy these**  
- **Fence and deck owners** who want subtle accent lighting without wiring.  
- **Renters** — the brackets screw in but are easy to remove, leaving only tiny holes.  
- **People who hate smart-home setups** — this is a button, not an ecosystem.  

Skip them if you need all-night illumination or if your fence is in deep shade. These are decorative accents, not security lights.  

**FAQ**  
**Q: How long do they last on a full charge?**  
A: 4–6 hours in warm white, 4–5 in RGB. Expect less in cloudy weather. They’ll recharge fully in 6–8 hours of direct sun.  

**Q: Can I use them indoors?**  
A: Technically yes, but the solar panel needs sun. They’re designed for outdoor mounting; indoor use will drain the battery fast without a charging source.  

**Q: Do they come in different colors?**  
A: The units are black plastic. The light color is what changes — RGB or warm white. No color options for the housing itself.  

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want cheap, reliable, no-fuss accent lighting for a fence, railing, or garden path. The warm white mode alone justifies the price, and the RGB is a fun bonus.  
**Skip it if** you need true all-night illumination or have a fully shaded mounting spot.  

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Great for the price, with honest limitations on battery life and bracket durability. For under $30, these punch well above their weight class.
