If you own a light fixture that takes G9 halogen bulbs, you already know the pain: they burn out at the worst possible moment, and the replacement aisle is full of confusing options. The YBEK G9 Halogen Bulb 120V 25W T4 Type 2 Pin Base is the kind of straightforward, no-drama replacement that gets the job done without making you think too hard about it. Here's the full breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The package contains 2, 4, or 6 bulbs depending on the listing option you select — each one a T4-size capsule measuring roughly 1.6 inches long with two looped pin contacts on the base. They're rated for 120V, pull 25W, and put out around 250 lumens of warm 2800K light. That's essentially identical to any generic G9 halogen you'd pull from a big-box store shelf. No fancy packaging, no branding theatrics — just glass capsules in a foam-lined box.

**Performance and real-world use**

Installation is a two-second job. The pins slide into standard G9 sockets cleanly — I've seen buyers note they fit "snug but not stuck" in several common fixture brands, which is the sweet spot. Push them in until they seat, and they stay put. No flickering, no buzzing, no intermittent contact issues that plague cheap G9 knockoffs.

The light quality is where these shine. At 2800K, they match the warm glow of older halogen fixtures almost perfectly. If you're replacing just one burned-out bulb in a multi-bulb chandelier, you won't get that jarring "one bulb looks bluish-white" mismatch that happens with some LED conversions. One reviewer put it exactly right: *"Replaced a dead bulb in my under-cabinet fixture — perfect color match, can't tell which one is new."* That's the practical benefit of staying with halogen rather than switching to LED.

Brightness is genuinely 25W — not the dim, half-hearted output you get from some off-brand bulbs that exaggerate specs. At close range, they're plenty for task lighting. From a ceiling fixture, they read as warm and ambient rather than blazing.

**Where it falls short**

Let's not sugarcoat it. Halogen is an old technology, and the downsides are baked in. These bulbs run hot — surface temperatures easily hit 300°F during operation. If your fixture has a tight glass enclosure or plastic components anywhere near the bulb, you'll want to verify clearance before running them for extended periods. I've seen a handful of complaints about bulbs overheating in enclosed fixtures; that's physics, not a defect, but it matters for your specific setup.

Lifespan is another honest limitation. YBEK rates these around 1,500–2,000 hours. That's typical for halogen — you'll get maybe 6–12 months of daily evening use. If you're tired of climbing a ladder to swap bulbs, a G9 LED is the better long-term play, even if the color temperature won't match perfectly.

**Who should buy this**

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these work in a dimmable fixture?**
A: Yes. Standard G9 halogens like these are inherently dimmable with most residential dimmer switches — just don't pair them with leading-edge dimmers designed for LEDs, which can cause flicker.

**Q: Can I use these in an enclosed light fixture?**
A: Technically yes, but be cautious. Halogen runs extremely hot, and enclosed fixtures trap heat. If your fixture is fully sealed with no venting, consider a lower-wattage bulb or an LED alternative to avoid premature burnout.

**Q: Do they fit all G9 sockets?**
A: The G9 base is standardized, so they'll fit any fixture designed for G9 bulbs. The pins align properly, and the capsule is compact enough for most glass shades.
