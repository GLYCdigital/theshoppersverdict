**The $38 Smart Lock That Actually Works — With One Big Caveat**

Here's the thing about budget smart locks: most are either glorified keypads with janky electronics, or they're so cheaply built you can feel the flex in the housing. The TMEZON Smart Door Lock is neither. It's a legitimate standalone fingerprint deadbolt that undercuts the competition by $60–100 — and for the most part, it earns its keep.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The package includes the exterior keypad assembly, interior thumb-turn unit, a standard ANSI/BHMA deadbolt latch (2-3/8" and 2-3/4" backset adjustable), strike plate, all mounting screws, and four AA batteries. Notably, there's **no physical key override** — if the batteries die and you haven't set up the 9V emergency power port on the bottom, you're calling a locksmith. That's a genuine design consideration you need to weigh.

The lock replaces your existing deadbolt with minimal fuss, but here's the catch: **it requires a 2-1/8" diameter bore hole** and a 1" crossbore. If your door was pre-drilled for a standard deadbolt, you're fine. If you're working with an older door, measure before you buy.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this lock for three weeks on a standard fiberglass entry door. Fingerprint recognition is genuinely quick — the capacitive sensor reads in roughly half a second on clean, dry fingers. It stored 20 fingerprints (the max) without issue, and the 50-code keypad memory handles the rest. The auto-lock feature (adjustable from 10 to 99 seconds) works as advertised, which is rare at any price.

The motor is surprisingly quiet — a soft whir rather than the clunky thunk you get from cheaper units. The interior thumb-turn is solid, not wobbly. Battery life is rated at 8–10 months with normal use, and the low-battery alarm gives you a decent heads-up before you're locked out.

**Where It Falls Short**

The fingerprint sensor has quirks. Wet fingers after rain or dishwashing? Expect failed reads. Dry winter skin? Same problem. You'll fall back on the keypad code — which is fine, but defeats the purpose if you bought this specifically for fingerprint convenience.

The bigger issue is the lack of smart connectivity. No Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth, no app, no integration with Alexa or Google Home. This is a **standalone smart lock**, not a connected one. If you want remote access or activity logs, look elsewhere — the August Wi-Fi Smart Lock or Schlage Encode are better (and much pricier) options.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Renters and landlords**: Easy to install, no permanent wiring, and you can reset codes between tenants. The price means you can outfit multiple properties without flinching.
- **Budget-conscious homeowners** who want keyless entry without the subscription fees of cloud-connected locks.
- **Anyone with a side or garage door** who wants convenience without overthinking it.

**Skip it if** you need app-based remote access, have very dry or sweaty hands that consistently fail fingerprint reads, or you're not comfortable with the no-physical-key fail-safe.

**FAQ**

**Does this lock fit a standard US door?** Yes — it fits 1-3/8" to 2" thick doors with a standard 2-1/8" bore hole. Measure your door's backset (2-3/8" or 2-3/4") and adjust the latch accordingly.

**What happens when the battery dies?** You'll get a low-battery warning beep for about two weeks. Once dead, use the 9V battery terminal on the bottom exterior to power it up temporarily. There's no key override.

**How many codes and fingerprints can I store?** Up to 20 fingerprints and 50 PIN codes. That's plenty for a family or small rental property.
