The TEENO Wood Router sits in the crowded budget trim router category, competing against big names like Makita and DeWalt. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 220+ verified owner experiences and the spec sheet.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the router body, a fixed base, an edge guide, a template guide adapter, two collet wrenches, and a 1/4-inch collet pre-installed. There's also a 1/8-inch collet included for smaller bits — a nice touch most budget routers skip. The body weighs 3.4 pounds, which puts it right in the featherweight class. You'll feel the difference after an hour of edge routing versus a 5-pound full-size unit.

## Performance: The Real Story

The variable speed dial is the standout feature here. It ranges from 10,000 to 30,000 RPM, and it actually holds speed under load. Multiple reviewers mention routing oak and maple without the motor bogging down — a common failure point on sub-$60 routers. One verified buyer wrote, "Ran a 1/2-inch roundover bit through white oak edging without hesitation. My old Skil would have stalled twice by now."

The aluminum base is flat and stays flat. That matters more than most shoppers realize — warped bases cause uneven cuts that ruin otherwise good work. The 6-hole pattern matches standard template guides, so you can swap in aftermarket bushings without adapter rings.

The LED light ring is genuinely useful. It surrounds the bit and illuminates the exact cut line, which helps when you're following a pencil mark or template edge. It's not gimmicky — you'll actually use it.

## Where It Falls Short

The collet wrench is the weakest link. It's thin stamped steel that rounds off under normal tightening torque. Buy a proper hex wrench or a collet nut socket before your first project. Several reviewers noted this exact issue.

Dust collection is mediocre. The port is small and clogs quickly with MDF or plywood debris. You'll need a shop vac with good suction, and even then, expect to stop and clear the port every few passes. This is a trim router, not a dust-management system, but it's worth knowing.

There's no soft-start. The motor kicks hard when you pull the trigger, which can cause a slight jump on plunge cuts. Experienced users won't care. Beginners should practice on scrap first.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a DIYer or cabinetmaker who needs a lightweight trim router for edge profiling, flush trimming, and laminate work. It's especially good if you work with hardwoods and want speed control without spending Makita money.

**Skip it if** you need a plunge base for mortising or you're a production shop routing 8 hours daily — you'll want a more robust unit with soft-start and better dust handling.

## Real Questions from Shoppers

**Q: Does it accept standard router bits?**
A: Yes, the 1/4-inch collet fits all standard shank bits. The included 1/8-inch collet handles smaller bits. It won't take 1/2-inch shank bits — that's typical for trim routers.

**Q: Is it loud?**
A: At 30,000 RPM, it's loud. Use hearing protection. The variable speed lets you drop to 10,000 RPM for quieter passes on softer materials, which helps in residential settings.

**Q: Can I use it with a router table?**
A: Yes, the base mounts to standard router table plates with the included screws. It works fine for small edge work, but the 3.4-pound weight means it's not ideal for heavy table use.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The TEENO Wood Router earns its 4.4-star rating with honest performance. It delivers solid variable-speed routing, a flat base, and useful features at a price that undercuts the big names. The thin wrench and weak dust port are real annoyances, but neither affects cut quality.

**Buy it if** you want a capable trim router for weekend projects and cabinet work without spending premium dollars.

**Skip it if** you need plunge capability or you're a pro who'll run it daily — spend more on a soft-start model with better dust management.

For the price, this is one of the better budget trim routers available. Just budget $8 for a replacement wrench.
