If you’ve ever fought with a countersink bit that chatters, skips, or leaves a ragged crater around your screw heads, this set is aimed squarely at you. The 5Pcs/15Pcs Countersink Drill Bit Set with Low Friction Depth stop isn’t a fancy brand name, but it solves the two biggest annoyances in countersinking: inconsistent depth and rough cuts. With a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, it’s clearly earning its keep in home shops. But is it worth your money? Let’s dig in.

**What’s in the bag**  
The set comes in two configurations: a 5-piece and a 15-piece. The 5-piece covers common sizes like #6, #8, #10, #12, and 1/4-inch — enough for most furniture builds and deck screws. The 15-piece adds spare bits and a wider range, useful if you burn through tips or work across many fastener sizes. Each bit is high-speed steel (HSS) with a six-flute design. That’s not marketing fluff — six flutes distribute cutting pressure evenly, which is why these bits don’t grab or bounce like cheap three-flute versions. The integrated depth stop is a metal collar that slides along the bit and locks with a hex wrench (included).

**Real-world performance**  
I tested the 5-piece set on pine, oak, and a scrap piece of 1/8-inch aluminum. In pine, the bit sank in cleanly and stopped exactly at the set depth — no overshoot, no torn fibers around the rim. Oak was the real test: hard, dense grain usually makes budget countersinks chatter. This one stayed steady, producing a smooth, even taper after about 15 holes. The aluminum was a pleasant surprise — the HSS edge held up without galling or binding, though I’d still use cutting fluid for longer runs.

The low-friction depth stop is the standout feature. Unlike spring-loaded stops that compress and drift, this collar locks rigidly. I set it for a 1/4-inch deep countersink on a hinge screw, and it held that depth across 20 holes without needing readjustment. That consistency is what separates this from a $8 generic kit.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Scanning the 220 reviews, the pattern is clear. One verified buyer wrote, “These cut like butter compared to the DeWalt set I had before. No wobble, no tear-out.” Another noted, “The depth stop actually works — I didn’t have to eyeball it anymore.” The six-flute design gets repeated shout-outs for staying quiet and vibration-free. Most users agree the bits arrive sharp and stay that way through moderate use — a few dozen holes in softwood or occasional hardwood.

**Where it falls short**  
No product is perfect, and this one has two honest flaws. First, the depth stop collar is a bit chunky on the smaller bits (the #6 and #8). If you’re countersinking in tight spots — like inside a cabinet face frame — the collar can crowd your drill chuck. It’s manageable but annoying. Second, there’s no storage case. The bits come loose in a plastic bag. At this price, a simple tin or plastic holder would have been a nice touch, and several reviewers mentioned losing bits in their toolbox. Also, the hex wrench is tiny — easy to misplace, so keep it with the bits.

**Who should buy this**  
This set is ideal for DIY furniture builders, cabinet installers, and anyone who wants screw heads flush without pilot-hole guesswork. The 5-piece is enough for most household projects; the 15-piece makes sense if you’re a weekend warrior who works across many fastener sizes or tends to lose bits. Skip it if you’re a professional doing high-volume production work — you’ll want a carbide-tipped set with a spring-loaded stop that self-adjusts, and this isn’t built for that abuse.

**FAQ**  

*Q: Will these fit a standard 3/8-inch drill chuck?*  
A: Yes, the hex shank is 1/4-inch, so it seats securely in any standard chuck. No wobble at high RPM.

*Q: Can I use these on metal?*  
A: Yes, mild steel and aluminum work fine, but go slow (under 1000 RPM) and use cutting fluid. Hardened steel will dull them fast.

*Q: How do I adjust the depth stop?*  
A: Loosen the hex screw on the collar, slide it to your desired depth, then tighten. Test on a scrap piece first — the collar holds firmly once locked.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want clean countersinks without the chatter and depth-guessing. The six-flute design and rigid stop deliver professional results at a budget price.  
**Skip it if** you need a compact tool for tight spaces or demand a carrying case — and if you’re a pro, invest in carbide.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid value, minor ergonomic quirks, and a missing case keep it from a perfect score, but for the price, this is a smart buy for any serious DIYer.
