## What You're Actually Getting

This is a melt-blown polypropylene cartridge — the industry standard for sediment filtration. The 5-micron rating means it traps particles down to 5 microns in size, which catches fine sand, rust flakes, and silt that coarser filters simply wave through. It's a 10-inch by 2.5-inch cartridge, the most common whole-house filter size on the market, so it drops into any standard housing. No proprietary locking mechanism, no special tools, no surprises.

The construction is dense and uniform. When you hold it, you can feel the difference between this and the flimsy spun filters at big-box stores — the BLUERISE has real structural integrity. It's designed to handle standard household flow rates (typically 5-10 GPM) without collapsing or creating channels where unfiltered water can slip through.

## Where It Falls Short

**No gasket included.** Most filter housings have an O-ring that seals the sump, but many replacements include a fresh one. This doesn't. If your existing O-ring is worn or cracked, you'll need to source one separately before installation. Not a dealbreaker, but a minor annoyance.

**Visual change-out indicators are unreliable.** The white outer layer picks up sediment quickly, so a filter that looks dirty at three weeks might actually still have weeks of life left. If you're the type who changes based on appearance rather than a schedule, you'll waste filters. Set a reminder instead.

**Not for heavy iron or manganese.** This is a sediment filter, not a water conditioner. If your water tests high for dissolved iron or has a rotten-egg smell, this won't fix it. Buy accordingly.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Well water users** dealing with sand, silt, or rust — this is your first line of defense
- **City water homes** with older pipes shedding sediment into the supply

Skip it if you need chemical reduction (chlorine taste, VOCs) — you'll want a carbon block filter instead. And if your water is already clean, a 20-micron filter will cost less and last longer.

## FAQ

**How often should I replace this filter?**
For typical city water, every 3-6 months. Well water with heavy sediment: every 1-2 months depending on load. Watch for a noticeable pressure drop at fixtures — that's your cue.

**Will this fit my housing?**
If your housing takes a standard 10" x 2.5" cartridge — the most common size in North America — yes. Check your current filter's dimensions before ordering. It won't fit the shorter 10" x 2.5" "big blue" housings that take 20" filters.

**Does it remove chlorine or improve taste?**
No. This is purely a sediment filter. It won't touch chlorine, chemicals, or dissolved minerals. You'd need a carbon block filter for those.
