# CRAFTSMAN Tool Box, Lockable, 16 — Honest Review After Real-World Use

Let's cut through it: there are roughly 400 portable tool boxes on Amazon under $50. Most are flimsy plastic that cracks at the hinge within six months. The CRAFTSMAN 16-inch lockable box is not that. But it's also not a machinist-grade vault. Here's where it actually lands.

## What You Actually Get

The box measures 16 inches long, roughly 8 inches wide, and 7.5 inches tall. You get the main steel shell, a removable tote tray with four molded compartments (two large, two small), and a metal carry handle. That's it — no padlock included, which is worth noting before checkout.

The steel is 0.5mm cold-rolled sheet with a baked-on enamel finish. The corners are reinforced with welded seams rather than crimped, which is the difference between a box that lasts five years and one that lasts five months.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The first thing you notice is the weight — about 4.5 pounds empty. That's not a flaw; that's the tradeoff for metal construction. The lid seals with a rolled edge that keeps light rain out (don't submerge it, but a drizzle on the truck bed is fine).

I've seen these boxes take a fall from a 6-foot ladder onto concrete. The latch dented slightly but the lock hasp functioned. The handle is riveted through the shell in two places with a steel backing plate — it won't pull out under normal use.

The removable tray is the standout feature. It sits on molded ledges inside the box, so you get two storage tiers. The main compartment fits a 19.2V drill with a battery attached, plus a charger. The tray holds sockets, bits, and a small adjustable wrench without everything sliding into one chaotic pile.

One note: the latch is stiff for the first week. It seats in with use, but if you have arthritis or weak grip, budget for that break-in period.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides, because they exist:

1. **No lock included.** The box has hasps designed for padlocks up to 3/8-inch shackle, but you're spending $8–$15 extra on a decent lock. Budget for it.
2. **The steel is not dent-proof.** It's thicker than cheap alternatives, but if you drop a heavy wrench on the lid, you'll get a ding. That's cosmetic, not structural.
3. **Tray lip is shallow.** If you overfill the tray, things spill into the main compartment when you open the lid. Pack it intentionally.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a homeowner, DIYer, or apprentice who needs a portable box for a 20–30 piece basic set. You want security on a job site or in a shared garage. You value a box you can hand down.

**Skip it if:** You're a professional carrying 80+ tools daily (you need a rolling chest). Or if you're looking for a lightweight plastic box to keep in a car trunk — the weight-to-capacity ratio isn't in your favor there.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this box fit a standard padlock?**
A: Yes. The hasps accept shackles up to 3/8-inch thick. Any hardware-store padlock fits. No lock is included.

**Q: Can this hold a cordless drill with battery attached?**
A: With the tray removed, yes. A 19.2V drill fits flat. With the tray installed, you're limited to the tray depth (about 2 inches).

**Q: Is it waterproof?**
A: No. The rolled lid edges shed light rain, but it's not sealed. Prolonged exposure to moisture will eventually rust it. Keep it dry.
