# LALISU Small Table Lamp Nightstand Lamp Linen Lampshade Bedr

If you're shopping for a small accent lamp, you've probably scrolled past dozens that look identical: fake silk shades, plastic bases, and a price tag that screams "temporary." The LALISU Small Table Lamp tries something different — a real linen shade over a wood base — and for the most part, it delivers. But with a 3.5-star average across 175 ratings, there's a split between buyers who love it and those who hit a quality snag. Here's the full picture.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box: the lamp comes with the linen shade already attached to the wood base (no assembly beyond inserting the bulb). You'll need your own E26 bulb — nothing included. The shade measures roughly 10 inches across, and the whole unit stands about 14 inches tall. That's a compact profile that fits comfortably on a nightstand, entryway console, or bookshelf without overwhelming the surface. The base is solid wood, not MDF or particleboard with a wood veneer — that's a genuine plus at this price tier.

## Performance and Build Quality

The linen shade isn't decorative only — it actually diffuses light well. With a standard 60W-equivalent LED, the glow is warm and even, no harsh hotspots. The wood base has a smooth finish, and the overall weight (about 2.5 pounds) keeps it stable enough that you won't knock it over reaching for your phone in the dark.

That said, the electrical components are where this lamp shows its budget roots. The cord is short — roughly 4.5 feet — and it exits straight out the bottom, which can complicate placement if your nightstand isn't flush against a wall outlet. A handful of reviewers reported a loose bulb socket that needed tightening or, in a few cases, replacement. It's not a widespread defect, but it's common enough that you should test the socket tension immediately after unboxing.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The aesthetic is the star. Multiple reviewers describe it as "much prettier in person" and "a great accent piece for a cozy bedroom." The linen texture and natural wood grain photograph well and look even better in real life. One buyer noted, "I bought three for different rooms — they all look consistent and well-made." Another appreciated the scale: "Finally a lamp that doesn't dwarf my small nightstand."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct. The light output is modest. With a 60W bulb, you'll get good ambiance but borderline-insufficient light for reading in bed. If that's your primary use case, look elsewhere or plan to pair it with a brighter floor lamp. The cord length is another practical annoyance — extension cords or power strips become part of the equation in most setups. And while the linen shade looks great, it's not removable for washing, so dust management means a lint roller or gentle vacuum attachment.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Accent-light seekers**: You want a warm, soft glow for a shelf, entryway, or console table — not task lighting.
- **Small-space dwellers**: Studio apartments or compact bedrooms where a full-size lamp is overkill.
- **Decor-focused shoppers**: The natural materials beat the plastic-and-polyester competition at this price.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the lampshade actually linen?**
A: Yes, it's a genuine linen-blend fabric, not a printed faux-linen poly. It has the natural texture and slight translucency you'd expect.

**Q: What bulb size does it take?**
A: Standard E26 medium base. Stick with LED — a 60W-equivalent gives good brightness without heat buildup against the fabric shade.

**Q: Can the shade be removed for cleaning?**
A: It's not designed to be removable. Spot-clean with a damp cloth or use a lint roller for dust. Don't submerge it.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a handsome, compact accent lamp with genuine linen and wood that elevates a corner of your room without breaking the bank. 

**Skip it if** you need reliable task lighting, have limited outlet access, or prefer a longer cord for flexible placement.

The LALISU Small Table Lamp earns a 3.7/5. It's a well-designed decorative piece with a few practical quirks. If you're okay with the tradeoffs — and most buyers seem to be — it's a solid purchase for the right spot.
