You've picked the hardest-working piece of hardware in your bathroom remodel, and it's not the toilet. A freestanding tub's drain and overflow assembly is what separates a spa-like soak from a frustrating leak hunt. The Woodbridge acrylic tub with its brushed nickel kit deserves a hard look — but let's talk about what's actually in the box and whether it's worth your Saturday afternoon.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The package ships with the acrylic freestanding tub body, the overflow assembly, and the drain kit with a brushed nickel finish. The tub itself measures in at the standard 59-inch length, which fits most master bath spaces without requiring demolition of your doorway. The acrylic construction uses a reinforced layer that resists the flex you feel on cheaper fiberglass units — push on the sidewall and it holds firm.

The drain hardware is where Woodbridge earns its keep. The overflow cover sits flush against the tub wall, and the drain flange threads cleanly into the waste pipe. The brushed nickel finish has a subtle satin sheen that won't show every water spot the way chrome does.

**Real-World Performance**

I've installed three of these over the last two years, and the standout feature is the 2-inch drain opening. When you pull the stopper, the water exits with authority — a full tub drains in under two minutes. That matters when you've overshot your soak time and the water's gone tepid.

The acrylic surface takes daily abuse well. Bath salts, essential oils, and the occasional dropped shampoo bottle leave no lasting marks. One buyer noted, *"After six months of daily use, the surface still looks brand new — no scratches, no dulling where my feet rest."* That tracks with what I've seen.

**Where It Falls Short**

The freestanding design is gorgeous until you need to service the plumbing. There's no access panel — if the drain connection develops a leak, you're either pulling the tub away from the wall or cutting into your floor. Plan your installation carefully. Have your plumber rough in the drain line in a position that allows the tub to sit flush without straining the connection.

The level-floor requirement is non-negotiable. On a slightly uneven floor, the tub will rock — not dangerously, but enough to feel unstable when you step in. Use a leveling compound before installation or budget for shims. One reviewer said, *"Had to return mine because my 100-year-old house floor wasn't flat enough. Not the tub's fault, but measure twice."*

**Who Should Buy This**

You're the right candidate if you're doing a full master bath renovation with a tiled floor you can level properly. You're comfortable coordinating with a plumber who understands freestanding tub drain rough-ins. You want a modern, clean look without paying boutique spa prices for the same acrylic material.

**Skip it if** you're replacing an existing tub in the same footprint, you have a tile floor you refuse to lift, or you need a built-in option with hidden plumbing access.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the drain kit fit standard plumbing connections?**
Yes. The kit uses a 1-1/2 inch slip-joint connection that mates with standard PVC drain pipe. Your plumber won't need special adapters or custom fabrication.

**Q: Is the brushed nickel finish durable?**
It's a PVD coating, not paint. That means it bonds at the molecular level and resists flaking. Clean it with mild soap and water — avoid abrasive scrubbers that will dull any finish.

**Q: Can one person install this?**
The tub weighs around 80 pounds. You can physically maneuver it, but the drain hookup under the tub while it's suspended is a two-person job unless you have a pipe wrench and patience. Get help.

**The Verdict**
