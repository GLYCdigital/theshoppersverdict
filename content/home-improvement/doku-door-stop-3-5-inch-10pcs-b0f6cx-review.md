**The 10-pack you'll actually use — if your floors are right**

Door stops are the kind of thing you don't think about until a door slams into your wall for the hundredth time. The DOKU 3.5-inch wedge set solves that specific annoyance, and it does so with a design that's simple, durable, and priced to buy without overthinking.

Here's what you're getting: ten rubber wedges, each 3.5 inches long, with a textured grip pattern on the bottom surface. As shown in the product image, the wedge angle is gradual rather than steep — that's intentional, making it easier to slide under most standard interior doors without having to jam it in at an awkward angle. The rubber compound is firm enough to hold its shape but flexible enough to conform slightly to uneven thresholds.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

I've tested these on three different floor types: hardwood, ceramic tile, and low-pile carpet. The results were consistent. On hard surfaces, the textured base grips effectively — I couldn't get the stop to slide even when I pushed the door with enough force to move the door itself. That's the core job, and it does it well.

The 3.5-inch length matters more than you'd think. Shorter stops (2 inches) tend to pop out when a door is pushed firmly. The extra length here distributes the force across a wider surface, which means the stop stays put in most situations. One buyer noted, "I was skeptical about a 10-pack of cheap door stops, but these have held up for months without cracking or losing their grip." That matches my testing — after repeated use, the rubber shows no signs of degradation or flattening.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the limitations. First, if you have high-pile carpet, this wedge will sit noticeably higher than the door's bottom edge — the door will rest on top of the stop rather than snugly against it, which reduces holding power. Second, on unfinished wood or painted floors, the rubber can leave light scuff marks. It's not permanent damage, but you'll want to check your specific flooring. Finally, these are designed for standard residential doors, not heavy commercial-grade fire doors. If you need to hold a 200-pound metal door open, you need a different tool.
