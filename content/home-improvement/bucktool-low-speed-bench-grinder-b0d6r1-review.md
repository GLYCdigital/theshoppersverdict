The Bucktool Low Speed Bench Grinder has carved out a reputation as a budget-friendly option for woodworkers and knife enthusiasts who need to sharpen without scorching their steel. With a 4.6-star average across 229 Amazon ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in home shops. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the grinder itself — which is the centerpiece — along with two 8-inch wheels (typically 60-grit and 120-grit grey aluminum oxide), adjustable tool rests, a water tray, and eye shields. The unit runs on a 3/4 HP motor that spins at a true 1,750 RPM. That low speed is the headline feature here.

As shown in the product image, the design is utilitarian — cast-iron base, steel wheel guards, and a straightforward layout that prioritizes function over looks. It's not pretty, but it means business.

## Performance and Build Quality

The low-speed operation is the differentiator. At 1,750 RPM, you're moving roughly half the speed of a standard 3,450 RPM grinder. This is critical when sharpening tools — high-speed grinding generates friction heat that can draw the temper out of a chisel edge. One buyer put it plainly: "I can hold a chisel to the wheel without turning it blue. My old 3450 RPM grinder ruined more edges than it sharpened." That's the core value proposition, and it delivers.

The 8-inch wheels provide a larger work surface and gentler hollow grind than 6-inch models. The cast-iron base keeps things stable — another reviewer noted, "This thing doesn't dance across my bench like my previous grinder did."

The motor handles constant use without bogging down, and the 1-inch arbor is the industry standard, meaning you can mount premium CBN wheels or white aluminum oxide wheels later if you outgrow the stock ones.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across reviews, the recurring themes are:

- **Low-speed sharpening without heat damage** — the primary reason people buy this over cheaper high-speed grinders
- **Quiet operation** — one buyer said, "It's noticeably quieter than my old Delta, which matters when I'm working in the garage at 6 AM"
- **Good value for the price point** — buyers consistently note you'd pay significantly more for a comparable Rikon or Jet model

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has honest tradeoffs.

**No work light.** This is the most common complaint. When you're sharpening a plane iron at a precise angle, you need to see the edge. The Bucktool doesn't include a light, and the eye shields are clear plastic that doesn't help much in dim conditions. Budget $15-20 for a magnetic LED work light.

**Stock wheels are serviceable, not exceptional.** The included grey wheels work fine for general grinding, but serious sharpening enthusiasts will swap them out quickly. One reviewer noted, "The wheels are okay for rough work, but I put a CBN wheel on the left arbor and it's a completely different machine." Factor that into your total cost.

**Tool rests require adjustment.** They're functional but not precision-grade. You'll want a jig system (like a Wolverine) for consistent bevel angles — the stock rests are fine for freehand work but won't hold a precise angle on their own.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Woodworkers** who sharpen chisels, plane irons, and lathe tools regularly — this is your machine
- **Knife makers and sharpeners** who need controlled, low-speed grinding for edge work
- **Anyone upgrading from a high-speed 6-inch grinder** who's tired of burning tool edges

Skip it if you only need rough grinding of yard tools or bolts — a high-speed grinder costs less and does that job fine.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use CBN wheels with this grinder?**
Yes. The 1-inch arbor and 8-inch wheel size accept standard aftermarket CBN wheels. Many buyers do exactly this upgrade.

**Q: Does it come with a work light?**
No. You'll need to add your own. This is the most common buyer complaint.

**Q: Is it suitable for sharpening lathe tools?**
Absolutely. The low speed is ideal for HSS and carbon steel lathe tools, and the 8-inch wheels provide a nice hollow grind.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're serious about sharpening and want low-speed operation without paying premium brand prices. **Skip it if** you need a light, prefer a 6-inch compact footprint, or only do rough grinding.
