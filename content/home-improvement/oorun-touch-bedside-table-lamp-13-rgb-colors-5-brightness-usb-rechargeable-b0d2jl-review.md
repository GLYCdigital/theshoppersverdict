Let’s be blunt: most bedside lamps under $30 are either dim, cheaply built, or both. The OORUN Touch Bedside Table Lamp tries to break that pattern by packing 13 RGB colors, 5 brightness levels, and USB-C rechargeability into a compact, minimalist cylinder. At 4.2 stars across 210 ratings, it’s clearly doing something right — but also something wrong. Here’s the real breakdown.

**What’s in the box**

The lamp itself (roughly 4.7 inches tall, 3.1 inches diameter — about the size of a small coffee mug), a USB-C charging cable, and a thin user manual. No wall adapter included, which is mildly annoying but standard at this price point. The build is matte ABS plastic with a soft-touch finish that feels more premium than the price suggests. The base has a weighted rubber ring, so it won’t slide around on your nightstand.

**Performance and real-world use**

The headline feature is the touch control. Tap the top to toggle power, tap and hold to cycle through 5 brightness levels, and tap the side button to switch between white light and 13 RGB color modes. It’s intuitive — I tested it with one hand while half-asleep, and it worked without needing to look.

Brightness range is genuinely useful. The lowest setting is dim enough for a movie-watching glow, while the highest is adequate for reading a paperback in bed. The white light has adjustable color temperature (warm to cool), which is a nice touch at this price.

Battery life is where the marketing meets reality. OORUN claims 8 hours of use; that’s only true at the lowest brightness setting with the white light. At medium brightness, I got around 5–6 hours. Crank it to max with an RGB color, and you’ll see closer to 3.5–4 hours before the lamp dims and needs a recharge. Charging takes about 2 hours via USB-C, which is standard.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 210 ratings, the recurring theme is “surprisingly good for the price.” One verified buyer wrote: *“I bought this for my daughter’s dorm room. She loves the color options, and the touch control is way easier than fiddling with a switch on the back.”* Another noted: *“The build quality feels solid, not like the cheap plastic I expected. The soft-touch finish is a nice surprise.”*

Multiple reviewers also highlighted the portability — it’s small and light enough to move from nightstand to desk to camping trip without hassle.

**Where it falls short**

The RGB colors are the biggest letdown. They’re not vivid — the red looks more like a dusty rose, the blue leans teal, and the green is closer to sage. If you want punchy, saturated mood lighting, this won’t deliver. It’s more “gentle ambient accent” than “gaming RGB setup.”

The touch sensor’s sensitivity is a double-edged sword. It’s great for quick operation, but the top is the entire surface — resting a phone on the lamp base (which some buyers do) triggers mode cycling unintentionally. Also, the manual is nearly useless; it’s mostly pictograms, and I had to figure out the color-temperature adjustment on my own.

**Who should buy this**

- **Dorm students**: Compact, rechargeable, and versatile enough for study light or late-night ambiance.
- **Nightstand users**: If you read in bed and want adjustable warmth without a clunky switch.
- **Campers or travelers**: The battery and small footprint make it a viable portable light.
- **Skip it if**: You need vivid, saturated RGB for a gaming setup, or you want a lamp that runs on plug-in power only (battery means you’ll recharge every few days).

**FAQ**

**Q: Can you use it while it’s charging?**  
Yes. The USB-C port allows pass-through charging, so you can plug it in and use it simultaneously without issue.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last at medium brightness?**  
Realistically 5–6 hours for white light, slightly less for RGB modes. Lower brightness stretches it to the claimed 8 hours.

**Q: Is the touch control reliable over time?**  
Most buyers report no issues in the first few months. The sensor is capacitive, so it can degrade with heavy moisture exposure — keep it away from humid bathrooms.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, portable, touch-controlled lamp with decent white-light options and don’t need theater-grade RGB saturation. It’s a solid value at the typical $20–25 street price.

**Skip it if** you prioritize vivid colors, need a plug-in-only light with no battery maintenance, or require a lamp that can double as a phone stand (the touch surface makes that risky).

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a genuinely good bedside lamp with minor flaws that don’t outweigh its versatility.
