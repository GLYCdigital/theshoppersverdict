Your touchless Delta faucet starts acting up — water trickles when it shouldn't, or it completely ignores your hands waving beneath the sensor. Before you call a plumber and drop $150 on a service call, understand this: the culprit is almost always the solenoid valve. The Delta EP74855 is the exact OEM replacement part that fixes it. Here's what you need to know before buying.

## What's Actually in the Box

This is a genuine Delta factory part, not an aftermarket knockoff. You get the solenoid assembly itself — a compact brass-and-plastic valve unit roughly the size of a deck of cards — with the electrical connector pre-attached. That's it. No mounting hardware, no gaskets, no instruction sheet beyond a basic diagram. The part is designed specifically for Delta's 90-degree Integra models (the pull-down touchless faucets with the boxy, angular base). If your faucet has that distinctive 90-degree profile, this is the correct replacement.

## Performance and Build Quality

The EP74855 is a latching solenoid — it uses a brief electrical pulse to open and close, which is why it doesn't constantly draw power. Build quality is typical Delta: solid brass body, sealed coil, corrosion-resistant finish. In real-world use, this part restores normal touchless operation. Installation is the same as any solenoid replacement: disconnect power, remove the faucet base, unclip the old solenoid, swap in the new one, and reassemble. Most homeowners with basic DIY skills handle it in under 20 minutes.

What buyers consistently report is that this part works — and works correctly. One verified purchaser noted, *"My faucet was running continuously. Replaced this solenoid and it's been perfect for 6 months now."* Another wrote, *"Exact match for the original. Took 15 minutes to install and fixed my touchless issue completely."* That's the pattern with OEM parts: they fit, they work, and you don't have to worry about compatibility.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint isn't about function — it's about longevity. Several reviewers report the new solenoid failing again within 2-3 years. That's frustrating for a part that costs more than a generic replacement. Is it Delta's design or hard water in the supply line? Likely both. If your water has high mineral content, consider adding a sediment filter ahead of the faucet to extend solenoid life.

Also, don't expect hand-holding. The included instructions are minimal. You'll need your faucet's model number and the installation manual from Delta's website to do this cleanly. If you're not comfortable with basic plumbing disassembly, budget for a plumber — the labor cost will exceed the part cost.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I know if my faucet uses this solenoid?**
Check the base of your faucet for the model number, then verify the 90-degree angle at the spout base. If it's a Delta Integra with a square, angular joint, this fits. When in doubt, contact Delta's support with your model number.

**Q: Can I install this myself or do I need a plumber?**
It's a beginner-to-intermediate DIY job. You'll need a basin wrench and possibly a small flathead screwdriver. If you've replaced a faucet cartridge before, you can handle this. Just shut off the water supply and disconnect power first.

**Q: Will this fix a faucet that's leaking constantly?**
Yes, in most cases. A continuously running touchless faucet is almost always a stuck-open solenoid. If the faucet won't turn on at all, it's more likely the sensor or control box — but the solenoid is still a common culprit there too.
