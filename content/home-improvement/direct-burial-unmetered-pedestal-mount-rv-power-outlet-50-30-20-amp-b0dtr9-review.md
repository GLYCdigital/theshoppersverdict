If you're wiring a permanent RV site, a temporary camp pad, or a barn outlet for your fifth wheel, this pedestal looks like the obvious choice at first glance. Three receptacles on one post, direct burial rated, and a price that undercuts the big box stores. But after digging through 210 ratings and the installation details, here's what you need to know before you bury this thing in the ground.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the assembled pedestal with a 50-amp 14-50R, a 30-amp TT-30R, and a 20-amp duplex GFCI receptacle all mounted on a powder-coated aluminum housing. There's a hinged cover over the 50 and 30 amp outlets — that's good, because those are the ones you'll use most. The 20-amp is a standard duplex with its own weatherproof flip cover.

The unit is rated for direct burial, meaning the enclosure itself is sealed well enough that you don't need to build a separate weatherproof box around it. The gaskets are thick and the screw-down faceplates compress against them properly. As shown in the product image, the whole thing mounts on a 3-inch schedule 40 pipe — you supply the pipe and the concrete.

## Real-World Performance

The wiring compartment is accessible from the back, which is the right call for a pedestal mount. You run your feeder up through the pipe, land it on the lugs, and button it up. The lugs accept up to 6 AWG copper — fine for a 50-amp feed at reasonable distances, but if you're running a long run that requires 4 AWG to handle voltage drop, you'll need to step down with a breaker or use a splice box. That's a real limitation worth planning around.

One buyer noted, "The build quality is solid for the price. I've had cheaper pedestals where the covers warped after one summer. This one has held up through two seasons of Texas heat and thunderstorms." That matches the 4.2-star average — most owners report the housing and gaskets holding up well.

The receptacles themselves are standard Leviton-grade units, not commercial spec, but they handle typical RV loads fine. The 50-amp outlet is a standard 14-50R that doesn't get hot under a full 50-amp draw from a Class A motorhome. The 30-amp TT-30R seats RV plugs snugly.

## What Buyers Praise

The three-in-one convenience is the biggest selling point. Multiple reviews mention powering a fifth wheel on the 50-amp while plugging a utility trailer or pressure washer into the 20-amp. One reviewer wrote: "I mounted this at my pole barn and it runs my 30-amp camper plus a battery charger and a work light simultaneously. No tripping, no heat. Exactly what I needed."

The direct burial rating is also a recurring theme in positive feedback. Buyers who installed it at remote hunting cabins and off-grid properties appreciated not having to build a separate enclosure or trench a second box. The aluminum housing is heavier than it looks and feels substantial.

## Where It Falls Short

The most common complaint is the small wiring compartment. If you're using 6 AWG THHN, you'll be fine, but don't expect room to spare. One frustrated customer described it this way: "Tight squeeze getting the wires landed. I ended up trimming the insulation back further than I wanted to make contact. Works fine, but it wasn't fun."

The lack of included mounting hardware is another gripe. The pedestal has pre-drilled holes for lag bolts, but you supply the bolts and the pipe. For a product you're burying in concrete, that's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

Some units ship with a misaligned faceplate on the 50-amp receptacle. It's a cosmetic issue that requires removing four screws to fix, but it's worth inspecting on arrival before you install it permanently.

## Who Should Buy This

This pedestal is ideal for a DIYer or property owner who needs a permanent, code-compliant RV hookup without paying electrician prices for a prefab unit. If you're comfortable with basic electrical work and your run is under 100 feet (so 6 AWG works), this is a solid value. It's also a good fit for a farm or workshop where you need a 50-amp outlet for a welder plus a 20-amp for tools.

Skip it if you need a 100-amp or dual-50-amp setup, or if you're not comfortable working with live feeder wires and need a fully pre-wired unit with breakers included.

## FAQ

**Do I need a separate GFCI breaker?**
Yes. This pedestal has no built-in breaker. You'll need a 50-amp double-pole GFCI breaker at your panel — add roughly $60-80 to your total cost.

**Can I bury the wire directly with this pedestal?**
The pedestal is direct burial rated, but the wire feeding it must also be rated for direct burial (UF-B or USE-2). Run it through conduit for additional protection if your trench has rocks.

**Will the 30-amp outlet power a standard travel trailer?**
Yes, the TT-30R is the standard receptacle for RV shore power on trailers up to 30 amps. Your trailer's cord will plug in directly.
