If you've ever fought with a cheap stapler that jams every third staple or won't sink a fastener flush into oak, you already know why the Bostitch medium heavy-duty crown stapler earns its 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews. This isn't a hobby-grade tool — it's the kind of stapler a trim carpenter reaches for at 7 AM on a Tuesday and doesn't think about again until lunch.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the stapler itself, a 360-degree adjustable exhaust port that you can redirect away from your face, and a no-mar pad on the nose to protect finished surfaces. No case, no oil, no belt hook — just the tool. That's worth knowing before you order, because you'll want a couple drops of pneumatic tool oil and a hook if you're working off a ladder.

**Performance that matches the price point**

This gun handles 16-gauge staples with a 7/16-inch crown, which is the sweet spot for attaching plywood cabinet backs, building crates, tackling upholstery, or running wainscoting. The depth adjustment wheel on the front lets you dial in flush or countersunk drives without pulling out a screwdriver — just twist and test on scrap. With 70-120 PSI operating range, it drives 1-1/2-inch staples through hardwood plywood without hesitation.

The dual trigger mechanism deserves specific mention. You get both sequential and contact (bump) firing modes. In sequential mode, you must press the nose against the workpiece before pulling the trigger — safer for precision work. In bump mode, you hold the trigger and bounce the nose along the work surface, which cranks out staples fast enough for production work. The lockout prevents dry-firing when the magazine is empty, which saves wear on the internal driver blade.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The jam release is the standout feature in customer feedback. One reviewer noted, "I've used this for years and it's jammed maybe twice. The tool-free jam release cleared both in about five seconds." Another buyer specifically mentioned running 10,000+ staples through it for furniture frames without rebuild kits. The magazine holds 150 staples, which means fewer reloads mid-run compared to smaller guns.

**Where it falls short**

The missing belt hook is a genuine oversight for a tool in this class. You'll be setting it down on ladders, scaffolding, or the edge of a workbench more often than you'd like. Also, the tool consumes air freely at higher pressures — a 1-gallon pancake compressor will keep up for intermittent work, but continuous framing or cabinet assembly will have you waiting on the compressor to catch up. It's also a dedicated crown stapler; it won't accept finish nails, so if you need both, you're buying two tools.

**Who should buy this**

- **Cabinet makers and furniture builders** who need reliable, flush-driven staples for backs and frames
- **Trim carpenters** who want a dedicated stapler that handles 7/16-inch crown staples from 1/2 to 1-1/2 inches without changing tools
- **DIYers with a compressor** doing upholstery, wainscoting, or crate building — the learning curve is minimal and the tool will outlast your project list

**Skip it if** you only need a nailer for finish work, or if you're on a strict budget and won't use it beyond a single weekend project.

**FAQ**

**Q: What size staples does this use?**  
A: 16-gauge, 7/16-inch crown staples, 1/2-inch to 1-1/2-inch length. Bostitch recommends their own brand, but quality aftermarket staples from Arrow or Hitachi work fine.

**Q: Will a small pancake compressor run this?**  
A: Yes, for intermittent work. At 90 PSI with 1-1/2-inch staples, it uses a fair amount of air. A 1-gallon compressor keeps up for trim work; continuous production runs need a 4-gallon or larger tank.

**Q: Does it jam often?**  
A: The tool-free external jam release makes clearing quick. In normal use with proper staples, jams are rare — most owners report single-digit jams over thousands of staples.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, professional-grade crown stapler that handles the full range of 7/16-inch staple lengths and won't leave you fighting jams mid-project. **Skip it if** you need a finish nailer instead, or you're only stapling cardboard — a manual stapler does that fine.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a solid, workhorse tool with one notable omission (the belt hook) and otherwise excellent reliability. At this price point, it's the stapler most pros would pick again.
