If you've been scrolling past the same generic drum shades and LED globes, the Sigaitwei Farmhouse Chandelier at least makes you stop. It's a rustic-industrial take on the classic wagon-wheel fixture — five exposed E26 sockets arranged around a central hub, framed by a dark steel ring and capped with natural pine wood slats. The look is specific: think reclaimed barnwood meets black pipe. And at a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it's clearly resonating with people who want character without commissioning a custom piece.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the chandelier body, five clear glass shades, a mounting plate with all hardware, and an adjustable chain measuring roughly 60 inches. The fixture measures about 23 inches in diameter and stands 12 inches tall at the body. You'll also find a 6-inch wire lead and an extra set of mounting screws — thoughtful touches for a product in this price bracket. Missing from the box: bulbs. The listing is clear about this, but it's worth repeating because the fixture's look depends heavily on what you screw in.

## Performance and Build Quality

Let's talk about the wood first, because that's what sells this piece. The pine slats are sanded smooth and finished with a clear matte coat that shows the grain. They're attached to a welded steel frame, not glued, which matters when you're hanging 15 pounds over a dining table. The matte black finish on the metal has a slight texture to it — it won't show fingerprints the way glossy black does.

Installation is genuinely manageable for a moderately handy person. The chain links are pre-split, so you can remove links with pliers to adjust drop height without cutting anything. The canopy is wide enough to cover a standard 4-inch junction box, and the mounting plate has slotted holes for alignment. One buyer noted, "I'm not handy at all and had this up in under 45 minutes with a friend holding the ladder." That tracks — the wiring is straightforward (black to black, white to white, ground to ground) and the fixture is light enough to handle solo if you're careful.

Light output depends entirely on your bulb choice. With five 60-watt equivalent LEDs, you'll get solid ambient light for a 6-8 foot dining table. The glass shades are open at the bottom, so light projects downward rather than being trapped. That's a plus for task lighting; it's also a minus if you wanted a soft, diffused glow.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the value-to-looks ratio. One reviewer wrote, "Looks way more expensive than it is — guests thought it was from a specialty store." Another mentioned the finish consistency: "No scratches, no dents, wood is actually nice, not that cheap plywood look." Multiple buyers also highlighted the dimming compatibility — with a standard dimmer switch and compatible bulbs, this fixture dims smoothly with no flicker down to about 20 percent.

## Where It Falls Short

The glass shades are the weak point. They're thin — not fragile, but they lack the weight of premium fixtures. The protective film on each shade is a genuine annoyance; it's cut tightly to the glass edges and leaves a sticky residue if you peel it wrong. Use rubbing alcohol to clean up any leftover adhesive.

The fixture also works best with smaller bulbs. Standard A19 bulbs look bulbous and overlap the shade openings. You'll want candelabra-style or vintage filament LEDs to get that exposed-bulb farmhouse look. That's an extra $15-20 you should budget for.

## Who Should Buy This

This chandelier suits three people specifically: the farmhouse-style renovator on a budget, the renter who wants a statement piece they can take to their next place, and the DIYer who wants a sloped-ceiling-compatible fixture without paying custom pricing. Skip it if you need a flush-mount light, want a truly industrial look with exposed Edison bulbs (the shades prevent that), or require a fixture with integrated LED that never needs bulb swaps.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this be installed on a vaulted ceiling?**
A: Yes. The chain mounts at a pivot point that accommodates slopes up to 45 degrees. The canopy tilts with the chain, so you don't need an angled adapter.

**Q: What bulbs look best in this fixture?**
A: Choose vintage-style filament LEDs in a candelabra shape — roughly 2 inches wide and 4 inches tall. They'll fit inside the shades without crowding and throw a warm 2700K glow that complements the wood tones.

**Q: Is the wood real or a veneer?**
A: It's solid pine, not a wrap or veneer. You can see end grain on the slats, and the finish is even on all edges.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a farmhouse statement piece that outperforms its price point in materials and finish, and you're willing to spend an extra 20 minutes and $20 on the right bulbs. **Skip it if** you need a flush-mount, hate fiddling with glass shade film, or want a purely industrial aesthetic without wood elements.

For the money, this is one of the better-built rustic chandeliers at this size. The wood is real, the steel is welded, and the sloped-ceiling compatibility is a genuine bonus. Just budget for bulbs and bring patience for the film removal.
