## Performance: What Actually Happens When You Use It

The self-leveling formula is the standout feature. You apply it, and it flows into the chip or crack rather than pooling on top. That matters — a repair that sits above the surface will catch dirt and look worse than the original damage.

The resin cures to a hard finish that sands cleanly. I've tested similar kits where the cured resin stays gummy or tears when sanded. This one doesn't. You can work it down to a smooth, polished surface that blends with the surrounding stone.

**The honest downside:** color matching is a genuine skill. The kit includes multiple colors, but natural stone — especially marble and quartzite — has veining and translucency that's hard to replicate. Expect to do two or three test mixes on a hidden spot before you get the blend right. Buyers who rush this step end up with a visible patch that's *almost* the right color — which is better than the chip, but not invisible.

## FAQ: Three Real Questions

**Q: How long does the repair actually last?**
A: Once cured, the resin is durable and holds up to normal countertop use — cutting, heat from warm pans, and cleaning products. It won't chip out like cheaper fillers. Expect years of service from a properly done repair.

**Q: Can I use this on a full crack across my countertop?**
A: Yes, but manage expectations. A hairline crack will be sealed and stabilized. A structural crack that's separating needs professional attention — this kit is for cosmetic and minor structural repairs, not load-bearing fixes.

**Q: Will the repaired area look identical to the surrounding stone?**
A: In most cases, it'll be close — not invisible. The key is blending colors carefully and sanding flush. For white or light gray stones, buyers report near-perfect results. For busy patterns with strong veining, expect a subtle difference up close.
