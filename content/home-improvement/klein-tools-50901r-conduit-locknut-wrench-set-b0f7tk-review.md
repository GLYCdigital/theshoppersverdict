If you've ever fought a rounded-off locknut in a cramped junction box with a pair of slip-joint pliers, you already know why this set exists. The Klein Tools 50901R Conduit Locknut Wrench Set is the kind of specialized tool that separates electricians who plan ahead from those who improvise. With a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep on belts nationwide.

**What You Actually Get**

The set includes three color-coded wrenches: red for 1/2-inch, black for 3/4-inch, and yellow for 1-inch conduit. Each measures roughly 6 inches long with a forged steel head and machined gripping teeth that bite into the locknut's notches. The handles are dipped in Klein's signature comfort grip, which matters when you're torquing down multiple fittings in a row.

Notably absent: any kind of hanging hole on the handle. Minor gripe, but if you're a pegboard person, you'll want to drill your own or use a pouch.

**How It Performs in the Field**

The machining quality is where Klein earns its reputation. The teeth on these wrenches are sharp and precisely cut, which means they engage locknuts cleanly rather than skating over the surface. On standard EMT compression fittings, the wrench seats fully and turns with satisfying authority. The 6-inch length gives you enough leverage for most jobs without becoming unwieldy in tight spots.

Where this set really shines is on rigid and IMC conduit, where locknuts sit recessed in hubs. The slim profile lets you reach in where a crescent wrench simply won't fit. One reviewer noted, "I've used these daily for six months on commercial jobs, and they've eliminated the need to carry three separate dedicated wrenches." Another verified buyer praised the grip: "The teeth haven't worn down after hundreds of connections — they still bite like new."

**Where It Falls Short**

The most common complaint is size coverage. At 1/2-inch through 1-inch, you're covered for residential and most light commercial work. But if you're running 1-1/4-inch or larger feeder conduit, you'll need to buy additional individual wrenches. That's not a flaw in this set, but it is a limitation to know before you buy.

A few users also noted the handles, while comfortable, can get slick if your hands are oily. Klein's dipped grip is better than bare steel, but it's not a full rubber overmold. Wear gloves if you're working with thread compound all day.

**Who Should Buy This**

This set is a no-brainer for:
- **Residential electricians** running EMT or flex in walls and crawl spaces
- **Commercial apprentices** who want the right tool before the journeyman asks for it
- **DIYers** doing a panel upgrade or adding a subpanel — one purchase covers the three most common conduit sizes
- **HVAC techs** who work with line-voltage control wiring where locknuts are standard

Skip it if you exclusively work with 2-inch-plus rigid conduit, or if you prefer a single adjustable wrench for everything (though you'll be fighting it in tight spots).

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Will these work on aluminum conduit locknuts?**
A: Yes. The machined teeth bite into softer aluminum cleanly without stripping the notches. Just apply even pressure and they'll seat firmly.

**Q: Are these wrenches compatible with standard steel locknuts only, or also with plastic/malleable types?**
A: They're designed for metal locknuts. Plastic fittings typically use snap-in or hand-tightened methods, so these won't be useful there.

**Q: Can I use these on compression fittings or just locknuts?**
A: Technically they're for locknuts, but the design works well on the hex surfaces of many compression fittings in a pinch. Just don't expect a perfect fit on every connector.

**The Verdict**
