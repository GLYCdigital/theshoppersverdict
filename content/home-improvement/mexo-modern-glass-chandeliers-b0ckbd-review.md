**The MEXO Modern Glass Chandelier has 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, and after digging through the feedback and spec sheet, here's what actually matters before you buy.**

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains one chandelier frame, six separate glass shades, mounting hardware, and a set of adjustable rods. No bulbs — that's a separate purchase. The frame is matte black steel, and the shades are clear glass with a slight smoky tint. As shown in the product image, the design is a mid-century-inspired globe cluster, not a full-on crystal statement piece. It reads modern, clean, and a little Scandinavian.

**Dimensions matter here.** The fixture spans roughly 24 inches wide and stands about 12 inches tall without the rods. With the included extensions, you can hang it anywhere from 20 to 60 inches below the ceiling. That range covers most dining tables, kitchen islands, and even stairwells. Measure your space before ordering — this thing is wider than it looks in photos.

## Real-World Performance

The glass shades are the star. They're thick — noticeably heavier than the cheap pressed-glass fixtures at big-box stores — and they diffuse light well. With six 40-watt equivalent LED candelabra bulbs, you get even illumination without harsh hotspots. One reviewer put it plainly: *"The light is soft and warm, exactly what I wanted over my dining table. No glare even when seated."*

Installation is doable for a confident DIYer but not a five-minute job. The wiring is standard black/white/ground, and the canopy mounts with a single plate. The adjustable rods connect with threaded collars — no tools needed for length changes. The catch: you'll want a second person to hold the frame while you attach the shades. Glass is glass, and one reviewer noted, *"I chipped the edge of one shade trying to install it solo. My fault, not the product's, but plan for help."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Light quality.** The combination of six bulbs and frosted glass means no dark corners. Multiple reviewers called it "bright but warm."
- **Build heft.** The steel frame and thick glass feel substantial. No wobble, no rattling.
- **Dimmability.** Works flawlessly with standard dimmer switches and LED bulbs. No flicker reported.

## Where It Falls Short

**The mounting hardware is the weakest link.** The canopy plate is thinner than I'd like for a fixture this weight. On drywall, use the included toggle bolts — don't trust the standard screws. A few reviewers mentioned needing to reinforce the box. Not a dealbreaker, but budget 30 extra minutes for mounting.

**Bulbs aren't included.** Six E12-base LEDs will cost you another $15–25 depending on brand. Annoying, but common at this price point.

**No smart-home integration.** This is a dumb fixture. If you want app control or voice commands, you'll need to pair it with smart bulbs yourself.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Dining room owners** wanting a focal point that doesn't scream "builder-grade."
- **Renters** — it's hardwired, but the adjustable drop makes it adaptable to different ceiling heights.
- **Anyone replacing an outdated ceiling fan light** — this fills the visual void without overwhelming a 10x10 room.

**Skip it if** you want a flush-mount fixture, need integrated smart features, or expect crystal-level sparkle. This is understated, not ornate.

## FAQ

**Q: What bulbs does it take?**  
A: Six E12 candelabra-base bulbs, max 40W equivalent each. LED recommended — they stay cool and last longer.

**Q: Can I install it on a sloped ceiling?**  
A: The canopy is flat, so you'd need an angled ceiling adapter. Not ideal for vaulted spaces.

**Q: How bright is it really?**  
A: With six 40W LEDs, you're looking at roughly 2,400 lumens. That's plenty for a 6-8 person dining table or a 5-foot island.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a well-built, mid-century modern fixture with warm, even light and a versatile drop range. **Skip it if** you're on a strict budget (remember the bulbs) or need something that mounts flush.

This is a solid 4.6-star product for the money — not flawless, but honest about its tradeoffs. The glass quality alone justifies the price.
