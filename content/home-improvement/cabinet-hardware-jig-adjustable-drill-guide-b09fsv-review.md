If you've ever installed cabinet pulls by measuring, marking, and praying your drill bit doesn't wander, you know the pain. One misaligned hole turns a $200 cabinet door into a frustrating redo. This adjustable drill jig aims to kill that problem permanently. After digging through 225 ratings and the mechanics of how it actually works, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Get

The jig arrives as a compact, self-contained unit — no assembly required. The body is a glass-reinforced nylon plastic, which sounds cheap but handles real torque better than you'd expect. The key components are two sliding arms with hardened steel drill bushings, a spring-loaded center punch, and a bubble level embedded in the top. It covers pull spacing from roughly 1 inch to 8 inches, which handles virtually every standard cabinet pull on the market. Knobs are just as easy — use the single center bushing.

As shown in the product image, there's also a clear acrylic base plate. That's not decorative — it lets you see exactly where the jig sits against your cabinet edge, so you can align it before committing the drill bit.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The self-centering mechanism is the star here. You press the jig against the door, the spring-loaded bushing automatically centers itself on the edge, and your drill bit goes in dead straight every time. For two-hole pulls, you set the spacing once, lock the arms down with the thumb screws, and every subsequent door takes about 30 seconds. No re-measuring, no layout mistakes.

The built-in bubble level is a genuine time-saver. Most drill guides make you eyeball leveling; this one gives you visual confirmation in two axes. Combined with the edge guide, it's hard to screw up even if you're hungover on a Saturday morning renovation.

One caveat: there's no depth stop. You're relying on your own judgment or a piece of tape on the bit. For thin cabinet doors, that's a real consideration. Several buyers mention this in their reviews, and it's worth noting before you buy.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme across positive reviews is accuracy. One buyer put it plainly: *"I installed 30 pulls in an afternoon with zero mistakes. Would have taken me all weekend before."* Another noted the spring-loaded punch leaves a pilot divot that keeps the bit from skating on slick laminate finishes. Build quality gets consistent nods too — people are surprised the plastic body holds up to repeated use without flexing.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a depth stop is the biggest functional gap. If you're working with 3/4-inch solid wood, no problem. If you're drilling into 1/2-inch MDF with a veneer face, you need to be careful. The plastic construction also means this isn't a throw-it-in-the-toolbox-and-forget-it tool. It's fine for a workshop or job-site bag, but don't expect it to survive being run over by a truck.

The thumbscrews that lock the sliding arms feel slightly undersized. They work, but if you're torquing them down hard repeatedly, they're the first thing that might strip. Treat them gently and they'll last.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY cabinet refacers**: If you're upgrading a kitchen or bathroom, this pays for itself in avoided mistakes on the first project.
- **Cabinet installers**: The speed gain on repetitive pulls is real. Set the spacing once, reproduce it perfectly across every drawer and door.
- **Anyone with shaky hands**: The self-centering mechanism compensates for imperfect aim. It's genuinely forgiving.

Skip it if you're a one-and-done weekend warrior doing a single cabinet door, or if you need a heavy-duty job-site tool that takes abuse. This is a precision instrument, not a demolition tool.

## FAQ

**Does it work with soft-close drawer slides and undermount hardware?**
Yes. The jig aligns to the face of the drawer, not the slides, so it doesn't interfere with any slide mechanism. Just make sure you account for slide clearance when setting your vertical position.

**Will it handle bar pulls longer than 8 inches?**
No. The max spacing is 8 inches center-to-center. For longer pulls, you'll need a different jig or manual layout. Check your pull specs before buying.

**Can it drill at an angle?**
No — it's designed for straight, perpendicular holes only. If you need angled drilling, this won't help you.
