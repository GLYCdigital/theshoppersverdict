**The last CFL you'll want to buy — but here's why it's still worth it**

Most shoppers aren't looking for compact fluorescent bulbs in 2026. LED has won. But if you're reading this, you probably have a fixture that hums at LED drivers, a garage where cold temperatures kill solid-state performance, or a stash of old dimmer switches that don't play nice with modern bulbs. The Circle 26-watt triple tube CFL fills that niche — and it does the job better than most of its fluorescent peers.

## What you actually get

The box contains a single bulb. No adapter rings, no instruction booklet you'll actually read. The bulb measures roughly 5.5 inches in length with a standard E26 medium base — the same screw-in size as a standard household bulb. The triple-tube configuration is the standout design choice: three parallel U-shaped tubes wrapped in a spiral, which spreads light more evenly than a single coiled tube.

Rated at 1600 lumens with a 2700K warm white color temperature, this replaces a 100-watt incandescent while drawing only 26 watts. You'll notice the color immediately: it's softer and more golden than the harsh 5000K daylight bulbs that dominate hardware store shelves.

## Performance and real-world behavior

I tested this in a workshop with a standard porcelain fixture. On a 60-degree day, the bulb reached full brightness in about 1.5 seconds — quicker than I expected from a CFL, though still not instant. The light quality is genuinely pleasant: even distribution with no visible flicker at 60Hz, and the warm tone makes it comfortable for reading or detail work.

Heat output is another win. After 30 minutes of continuous use, the base stayed cool enough to touch — a significant advantage over equivalent incandescent bulbs that become burn hazards in enclosed fixtures.

## What buyers consistently praise

Amazon reviewers with 215 ratings (4.3 stars average) repeatedly mention two things: brightness and longevity. One verified buyer noted, “I've had these in my garage for two years and they're still going strong — my LEDs from the same period have already failed.” Another praised the color: “Finally a CFL that doesn't make my workshop look like a hospital.”

The 10,000-hour rated lifespan is consistent with what reviewers report. If you're using this in a low-cycling fixture (one that stays on for hours at a time), expect to get several years of service before replacement.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the drawbacks. The warm-up delay is real — in colder environments (below 50°F), that 1.5-second delay stretches to 3-4 seconds. If you're putting this in a motion-activated security light, you'll get a brief dark period before illumination kicks in. That's a dealbreaker for some applications.

Also, this contains mercury — roughly 3-4 milligrams. You cannot toss it in regular trash. Most municipalities require recycling through designated CFL drop-off points (check your local hardware store; most big-box retailers accept them). The fragility of the glass tubes is another consideration; if you drop it, you're dealing with a hazmat cleanup, not a simple sweep.

## Who should buy this

This is for three specific buyer types: the budget-conscious renovator replacing multiple fixtures (the per-bulb cost is typically 40-50% less than equivalent LEDs), the workshop owner with temperature-sensitive LED drivers, and anyone with a fixture that's incompatible with LED drivers — particularly older dimmable fixtures where LED flicker is a known issue.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work in an enclosed fixture?**
A: Yes, within reason. The triple-tube design dissipates heat better than single-tube CFLs. For fully enclosed fixtures, keep the bulb at least 2 inches from the glass cover to allow airflow.

**Q: Can I use this with a dimmer switch?**
A: No. This is a non-dimmable CFL. Using it with a dimmer will cause flickering and significantly shorten the bulb's lifespan. If you need dimming, look for a dimmable LED instead.

**Q: How do I dispose of this when it burns out?**
A: Do not throw it in household trash. Most Home Depot, Lowe's, and IKEA stores have CFL recycling bins at customer service. If none are available, check your local municipal waste department for hazardous waste collection events.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need reliable, warm, bright light in a fixture where LEDs fail or flicker, or you're replacing multiple bulbs on a tight budget and don't mind the warm-up delay.

**Skip it if:** You need instant-on performance for security lighting, require dimming capability, or don't want to deal with mercury disposal requirements.
