**The RIOVO Adjustable Doorbell Angle Mount solves a problem you didn't know you had — until a package thief walks right past your camera's blind spot.**

Most doorbell mounts are static wedges. You get one angle, maybe two if you flip it. The RIOVO gives you a genuine 30° to 55° range of tilt, adjustable after installation. That's the entire pitch, and for once, it delivers.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the aluminum mounting bracket, a wedge-shaped base plate, a bag of screws and wall anchors, a hex key for the pivot bolt, and an adapter plate for doorbells with non-standard mounting holes. The whole unit measures roughly 3.5 inches tall and 2 inches deep — compact enough that it won't look like a security afterthought on your door frame.

The standout feature is the pivot mechanism. A single hex bolt locks the tilt angle, and the hinge has enough friction that you can nudge it by hand before tightening. As shown in the product image, the bracket rotates forward and downward, letting you point your camera at the porch floor, the walkway, or the street — depending on what you're trying to capture.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this with a Ring Video Doorbell 4 and a Nest Doorbell (battery). The adapter plate is the first thing you'll notice — it's a universal fit that mounts the doorbell flush against the RIOVO bracket, and it held both devices securely with no wobble.

The tilt adjustment genuinely works. At 30°, the field of view covers the immediate porch area. At 55°, you're looking down the driveway and catching approaching visitors well before they reach the door. The difference in package detection is night and day — one Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"I was missing packages left and right. After installing this, I can see the entire porch and the sidewalk. My Ring actually catches faces now."*

Build quality is better than the price suggests. The aluminum body has a matte finish that matches most doorbell finishes, and the weather seal around the pivot has held up through rain and direct sun in my testing. No rust, no seized bolts after three months.

**Where it falls short**

The included hardware is the weak point. The screws are soft and the plastic wall anchors are the same generic ones you'd find in a $5 picture frame kit. On drywall or wood siding, they work fine. On brick, stucco, or concrete, you'll want to grab masonry anchors and better screws from your local hardware store before attempting installation.

A second quibble: the tilt lock bolt can work loose over time if your door slams hard enough to vibrate the frame. It's a two-minute fix with the included hex key, but it's worth checking every couple of months.

Also note: this mount adds roughly 1.5 inches of depth to your doorbell. If your doorbell already sits close to the edge of the door frame, check clearance before you buy — the bracket needs room to pivot.

**Who should buy this**

- **Package-security-minded renters and homeowners** — if you're getting missed deliveries or can't identify visitors on camera, this is the fix.
- **Anyone with a side-mounted doorbell** — doorbells mounted at an angle to the door suffer from the worst blind spots, and this compensates perfectly.
- **People who've already tried static wedge mounts** — if you've guessed wrong on angle before, the adjustability here removes the guesswork.

**Skip it if** your doorbell is already positioned to capture your porch fully, or if you need a mount that tilts side-to-side (this one only adjusts forward/backward).

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my [brand] doorbell?**
A: The included adapter plate fits most standard doorbells with 2-screw or 4-screw mounting patterns, including Ring 1st-4th gen, Ring Pro, Nest Hello, Arlo, and Eufy. Check your doorbell's mounting hole spacing against the bracket before ordering if you have an off-brand model.

**Q: Does the angle adjustment require removing the doorbell each time?**
A: Yes — you loosen the hex bolt, adjust, and re-tighten. It takes about 30 seconds. The doorbell itself stays mounted to the adapter plate the whole time.

**Q: Will this affect my doorbell's motion detection range?**
A: It changes the field of view, not the sensor's range. At steeper angles (45°–55°), you'll catch approach paths you previously missed, but you may also pick up more false triggers from passing cars or pedestrians if your door faces a busy street.

**The Verdict**

The RIOVO is a 4.6/5 product because it does exactly what it promises — and the adjustability genuinely saves you from the "install it, realize the angle is wrong, re-install it" cycle. The cheap screws and occasional bolt loosening are minor annoyances, not dealbreakers.

**Buy it if** you're missing packages or faces on your current doorbell camera, or you're installing a doorbell where the mounting position isn't ideal.

**Skip it if** your doorbell already captures your porch perfectly, or you're mounting to brick without replacing the included anchors.

At its price point, this is the smartest $15–20 you can spend to make a $100+ doorbell camera actually earn its keep.
