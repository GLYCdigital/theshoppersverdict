**What You Actually Get**  
Eight individual straps, each 13 inches long and roughly 2 inches wide. The webbing is thick — think seatbelt material, not the flimsy hook-and-loop strips that come with cheap electronics. **Real-World Performance**  
I tested these on three common scenarios: a 100-foot 12-gauge contractor cord, a 25-foot 14-gauge household cord, and a handful of smaller appliance cords. The 13-inch length is the sweet spot — it wraps around a 12-inch coil of thick 12-gauge cord with about an inch of overlap, which gives you room to cinch it down tight. The Velcro held firm even when I shook the bundle aggressively. No slippage, no unpeeling.

The heavy-duty webbing also does something subtle but important: it distributes pressure evenly. With thinner straps, I've seen cords get crimped or flattened at the pinch point. These don't — the 2-inch width spreads the load so your cord jacket stays round and flexible.

**Where It Falls Short**  
Honest talk: the aggressive Velcro is a double-edged sword. If you're wrapping a cord with a thin rubber jacket, the hook side can snag and leave little pulls in the material. It won't damage the cord, but it's annoying if you're fussy about your gear. Also, these are pure straps — there's no loop, hook, or handle for hanging. If you want to put them on a pegboard, you'll need to thread the strap through a hook or buy separate hangers.

And for smaller cords — think phone chargers or 6-foot appliance cables — the 13-inch length is overkill. You'll have a lot of excess strap flapping around. This is a tool for *extension cords*, not everyday cable management.

**FAQ**  

**Q: Will these fit a 100-foot 12-gauge extension cord?**  
A: Yes, but you'll need to coil it to about a 10-12 inch diameter first. The 13-inch strap wraps around with room to spare. For thicker 10-gauge cords, coil a bit looser — the webbing will still close, but it'll be snug.

**Q: Do these stretch or lose grip over time?**  
A: No. The webbing is non-elastic, and the Velcro maintains its hold through hundreds of cycles in my testing. Just make sure you're pressing the full length of the Velcro together, not just the ends.

**Q: Can I write on them to label cords?**  
A: The straps have a smooth woven surface. A permanent marker works but can fade with handling. Better to use a label maker or zip-tie tags if you need identification.
