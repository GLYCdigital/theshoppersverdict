## A porch light that actually looks like it costs more than $70

Most sub-$100 outdoor sconces are flimsy plastic pretending to be metal. The LONEDRUID Outdoor Wall Light Fixtures break that pattern. At $69.99 for a pair, these are die-cast aluminum with tempered glass — not the thin-gauge stamped steel that rusts through in two winters.

I've installed a lot of exterior lighting, and the first thing I noticed pulling these out of the box was the weight. These aren't hollow shells. The matte black finish is baked on, not painted, which means it won't flake off in UV light or peel when you pressure-wash the siding.

## What's in the box

You get two complete wall lanterns, each with the mounting plate, hardware kit, and gasket pre-installed. The instructions are basic but adequate — if you've hung a light before, you won't need them. If you haven't, the wiring is straightforward: black to black, white to white, ground to ground. The mounting plate slots into the fixture with a hinge, which is a small detail that makes a big difference when you're balancing on a ladder with one hand.

One thing to know upfront: **bulbs are not included.** You need two E26-base bulbs (one per fixture). Most buyers grab LED A19s or vintage Edison bulbs. Go with LED — these are rated for damp locations, and LED bulbs last far longer in enclosed fixtures where heat builds up.

## Real-world performance

The seeded glass is the smartest design choice here. It diffuses the light so you get a warm, even glow rather than a harsh spotlight. One reviewer put it well: *"I was worried the seeded glass would be too dim, but it throws plenty of light on my porch — my Ring camera catches everything clearly at night."* That matches my experience — the diffusion cuts glare without sacrificing usable illumination.

The fixtures are rated IP65 waterproof, which in practical terms means they'll handle direct rain, snow, and the kind of humidity that wrecks cheaper lights. The gasket seals tight against the wall, so you won't get water pooling behind the fixture — a common failure point on cheaper sconces that leads to rusted screws and bug invasions.

## Where it falls short

The glass door swings down on a hinge held by a small thumbscrew. It's convenient for swapping bulbs, but the screw is easy to overtighten. Go gentle — snug is enough. Also, the clear glass option (if you choose it over seeded) will show every speck of dust and every dead moth. Seeded glass hides that but cuts maybe 10% of the output. Trade-off worth knowing.

## Who should buy this

- **Homeowners modernizing a 70s split-level** — the clean lines update dated exterior trim without looking trendy
- **Anyone replacing rusted builder-grade fixtures** — the die-cast aluminum will outlast the house
- **People who hate ladder time** — the hinged door and tool-free access mean bulb changes are a 30-second job

**Skip it if** you need a fixture with a built-in motion sensor or dusk-to-dawn photocell. This is a plain, well-built sconce — no smart features, no automation. Also skip if you're on a tight budget; $70 for two lights is mid-range, and you'll spend another $10–15 on bulbs.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these be installed pointing downward only, or can I mount them upside down?**
A: They're designed for downward light. Mounting upside down defeats the weatherproofing — the gasket seals from the top. Just install as intended.

**Q: What size bulbs fit?**
A: Standard E26 medium base. A19 or A60 LED bulbs work perfectly. Stick to 60W equivalent or less; anything hotter risks cooking the wiring over time in an enclosed fixture.

**Q: Will these hold up in coastal areas with salt air?**
A: Yes. The die-cast aluminum and baked finish handle salt spray far better than galvanized steel. Rinse them with a hose occasionally if you're within a mile of the ocean.

**Buy it if** you want a durable, good-looking porch light that doesn't scream "builder's special" and won't need replacing in three years. **Skip it if** you need smart features or want something with a more ornate, traditional look — this leans modern farmhouse.
