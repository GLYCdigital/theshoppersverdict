Here's the thing about commercial-style spring faucets: most look the part but collapse under pressure. The FORIOUS version, however, has earned a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings for a reason — it actually functions like the restaurant-grade piece it mimics.

**What you get in the box**

The package includes the faucet body, a single-lever handle assembly, the integrated soap dispenser pump with reservoir, mounting hardware, and braided supply lines. The finish is a brushed stainless steel that resists fingerprints better than polished chrome alternatives. The spring measures roughly 8 inches in length, giving you a solid 15-inch reach from the base to the spout head — enough to fill tall stockpots without contorting.

**Performance and build quality**

The stainless steel spring is the star here. Cheaper versions use thin coils that sag after a few months; this one holds its arc firmly. The single-handle lever operates with a smooth, hydraulic-like glide — no jerky resistance, no temperature spikes. The 360-degree swivel base lets you swing the spout across both basins without lifting a finger.

The soap dispenser is the headline feature, and it works — with caveats. The pump delivers a consistent dollop of liquid, and the reservoir holds a generous 22 ounces, meaning fewer refill trips. But as one verified buyer noted: *"The soap pump is great with Dawn, but my thicker organic soap clogged it within a week. Stick to thin dish liquid and you're fine."* That's consistent with what other reviewers report.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Owners repeatedly highlight the installation ease. The deck-plate design covers standard 1- or 3-hole configurations, and the included hardware is solid brass, not the zinc alloy you see at this price point. Another frequently mentioned plus: the spout's aeration. One reviewer wrote, *"The water stream is strong but doesn't splash everywhere — it's the quietest faucet I've owned."*

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about the tradeoffs. The fixed spring head means no pull-down sprayer — if you routinely spray down produce or blast stuck-on residue, this will frustrate you. The coil design also collects water spots, especially in hard-water areas. And the soap dispenser's pump, while functional, feels slightly less durable than the faucet itself — treat it gently.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home cooks who want commercial aesthetics without commercial prices** — this delivers the pro-kitchen look for a fraction of restaurant-supply cost.
- **Small-space kitchens** — the integrated soap dispenser removes a countertop bottle, reclaiming ~6 inches of workspace.
- **DIY installers** — the straightforward mounting and included hardware make this a Saturday-afternoon project.

Skip it if you need a pull-down sprayer or if your household uses thick, natural soaps exclusively.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the soap dispenser work with hand soap or only dish soap?**
A: Thin liquids only. The pump mechanism struggles with viscosities thicker than standard dish soap. Hand soap typically works if it's runny enough.

**Q: What's the actual spout height clearance?**
A: Roughly 11 inches from the deck to the spring's apex — accommodates standard 9-inch stockpots with room to spare.

**Q: Will the spring weaken over time?**
A: The stainless steel coil maintains tension well. Some reviewers report minor droop after 12+ months of heavy use, but it's cosmetic — water flow isn't affected.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want restaurant-style performance and the convenience of a built-in soap dispenser without dropping $300+. **Skip it if** you need a pull-down sprayer or prefer a minimalist, non-spring design.

At 4.6 stars, this is one of the better value plays in commercial-style faucets — just pair it with the right soap and wipe the coils down weekly.
