If you've ever stood in the hardware aisle staring at a pegboard full of single-purpose mounting brackets, the TAYMAC ML500 is the product that makes you wonder why nobody thought of this sooner. This single-gang mount packs 16 different mounting configurations into one steel plate roughly the size of a standard electrical box cover. It's the kind of tool that either becomes your new go-to or sits in a drawer because you didn't think through how you'd actually use it. Here's what you need to know before you buy.

**What You Actually Get**

The ML500 is a 16-gauge steel plate designed to fit standard single-gang electrical boxes (the 2" x 4" opening you find in most residential walls). It's powder-coated in a matte finish that resists scratches and corrosion. The plate features a series of pre-drilled holes arranged in patterns that accommodate everything from conduit clamps and cable straps to small equipment mounts and shelf brackets.

You get the mounting plate itself—that's it. No screws, no anchors, no instructions printed on the packaging. If you're the type who expects a complete kit, factor in the cost of hardware. For most pros, this isn't a dealbreaker; you likely have a box of #8 and #10 screws already. But for a homeowner doing a weekend project, it's an extra trip to the store.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The 16-gauge steel feels appropriately substantial. It's not flimsy like the thin stamped brackets you sometimes find in big-box stores. The powder coating is evenly applied and should hold up to years of use in a garage, workshop, or utility closet. I've seen some cheaper multi-tools in this category where the holes are misaligned or the plate warps when tightened—the ML500 doesn't have those issues. The holes are cleanly punched, and the plate sits flat against a standard box.

That said, there's a learning curve. With 16 configurations, the plate is busy. When you're working overhead or in a tight crawlspace, finding the right hole pattern by feel is not intuitive. You'll likely want to study the layout before you start, or keep the product page up on your phone for reference. A printed diagram on the packaging would have been helpful—this is a minor annoyance, but a real one.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The versatility is the headline feature, and buyers agree. One reviewer noted, "This replaced four separate brackets I was carrying in my tool bag. I can mount conduit, cable, and small fixtures with one plate." Another highlighted the build quality: "Feels much more substantial than the cheap plastic alternatives. The steel won't crack or bend."

Electricians and low-voltage installers seem to be the core audience. For anyone running conduit or securing cables in commercial or residential settings, the ML500 consolidates a lot of what you'd otherwise need in a single compact package. The single-gang form factor means it works with boxes you already have in place, which is a practical advantage over universal mounts that require more complex installation.

**Where It Falls Short**

The lack of included hardware is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. When a product's entire pitch is "one tool for many jobs," shipping it without the screws needed for those jobs feels like an oversight. You'll need to know what you're mounting and have the right fasteners on hand.

The other issue is the learning curve mentioned earlier. With 16 options, the plate is visually busy. Several buyers noted they had to "trial and error" to find the right hole pattern for their application. This isn't a dealbreaker, but it does slow you down on the first few uses.

**Who Should Buy This**

The ML500 is ideal for:
- **Electricians and low-voltage installers** who work with conduit, cable, and mounting brackets daily
- **Homeowners with active workshop or garage projects** who want one versatile solution instead of a drawer of single-purpose brackets
- **DIYers retrofitting existing single-gang boxes** who need a flexible mounting solution without cutting new holes

Skip it if you need one specific bracket for one specific job—a purpose-built bracket will be simpler and cheaper. Also skip it if you're looking for a complete kit with hardware included.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Will this fit a standard 2-gang box?**
No. The ML500 is designed for single-gang boxes only. For a 2-gang application, you'd need two plates.

**Q: Can this support heavy equipment, like a small shelf or tool holder?**
It's rated for light-to-moderate loads. The steel is sturdy, but the mounting box's holding capacity is the limiting factor. For anything heavy, use appropriate anchors or structural mounting.

**Q: What screw sizes does it accept?**
The plate accommodates #6, #8, and #10 screws, but you'll need to supply them. The hole patterns are designed for standard electrical box screws plus common mounting hardware.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a professional who wants to consolidate your mounting hardware or a serious DIYer who values versatility over specialization. The build quality justifies the price, and the 16 configurations genuinely replace multiple tools.

**Skip it if** you need a single-purpose bracket for a one-off job, or if you don't want to source your own hardware.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful tool with a real design flaw (missing hardware) and a minor usability issue (busy layout). The steel is solid, the concept is sound, and for the right user, this earns a permanent spot in the tool bag.
