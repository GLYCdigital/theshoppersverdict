If you're replacing a broken hinge or swapping out dated brass for matte black, the first thing you need to know is whether the corners match your existing mortise. These 3.5 x 3.5-inch interior door hinges use the rounded 5/8-inch corner radius — the industry standard for most residential pre-hung doors. That means you can typically unscrew the old hinge, drop this one in, and skip the chisel work entirely.

**What you actually get**

Each pack includes two full hinges (both door and frame leaves), the screws for both sides, and matching strike plates. That's everything needed to swap out a standard single door. The hinge leaves are 3.5 inches tall and 3.5 inches wide, with a thickness that sits flush in a standard mortise. As shown in the product image, the rounded corners are consistent and clean — no sharp edges that catch on clothing or look off next to neighboring hinges.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The hinges are constructed from solid steel with a powder-coated finish. They operate smoothly right out of the box — no creaking, no binding. The pin is tight enough to prevent sagging but loose enough that the door swings freely. One thing buyers consistently note in the 235 ratings (4.7 stars) is that the finish holds up better than expected at this price point. A few reviewers specifically mention installing them on high-traffic doors and seeing no flaking or wear after several months.

The screws included are decent quality — not the soft pot-metal junk that strips out on the first turn. Pre-drilling is still recommended, especially into solid-core or particle-core door edges, but the screws bite cleanly.

**Where it falls short**

The non-removable pins are the main tradeoff. With most residential hinges, you can tap out the pin and lift the door off the frame for painting or removal. These don't allow that — you'll need to unscrew the hinge leaves from either the frame or the door to take it off entirely. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you plan a full door removal project.

A smaller but recurring complaint involves magnetic doorstops. The powder coat is durable against normal wear, but a strong magnetic stop gripping the hinge leaf can leave faint marks over time. If you use magnetic stops, consider adding a thin felt pad between the magnet and the hinge.

**Who should buy these**

- **DIY homeowners replacing old hinges** — the rounded corners match standard mortises, making this a true drop-in replacement
- **Landlords or property managers** — multiple finish options (oiled rubbed bronze, matte black, satin nickel) make it easy to standardize units without repainting or re-mortising
- **Anyone refreshing interior doors** — new hinges instantly modernize hollow-core doors without replacing the door slab

Skip these if you need hinges for a door that requires frequent removal (like a crawl space access) or if you're working with square-corner mortises — those need a different corner radius.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these fit my existing door mortise?**
A: If your current hinges have rounded corners at 5/8-inch radius and measure 3.5 x 3.5 inches, yes — direct swap. Measure the corner radius first; square corners or larger radii won't match.

**Q: Are the screws long enough for solid-core doors?**
A: The included screws are standard hinge length (about 1 inch), adequate for hollow-core and most solid-core doors. For extra-heavy solid-core doors, buy #9 x 1.5-inch screws for the frame side.

**Q: How many hinges do I need per door?**
A: This pack covers one door (two hinges). Standard residential doors use two hinges, but heavier solid-core doors should have three. Buy a second pack if you need three per door.
