The DEWALT DWE6423 is the sander that keeps showing up in "best of" lists, and after running it through its paces, the 4.7-star average across 235 ratings makes sense. This is the mid-tier sweet spot in DEWALT's random orbit lineup — not the bare-bones entry model, not the brushless flagship — and for most woodworkers, it's the one worth buying.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the sander itself, a cloth dust bag, and a paper template for punching holes in sandpaper if you're using non-DE WALT discs. No case, no spare pad, no hex key. The sander weighs 3.4 pounds and measures about 11 inches long — compact enough to fit in a tool bag but substantial enough to feel solid in hand.

The key spec here is the 3.0 amp motor with variable speed from 8,000 to 12,000 orbits per minute. That's a wider range than many competitors in this price class, and the 5-inch hook-and-loop pad accepts standard 5-hole and 6-hole discs.

## How It Performs

The first thing you notice is the vibration control. DEWALT's "low vibration" claim isn't marketing fluff — the counterweighted platen and rubber overmold grip genuinely reduce hand fatigue. I sanded a full oak tabletop for two hours without the numbness you get from cheaper sanders.

The motor holds speed under load. This matters more than peak RPM — many sanders drop to 60% speed the moment you apply pressure. The DWE6423 keeps spinning, which means consistent scratch patterns and faster material removal. One reviewer noted, "I've sanded down a full maple butcher block with 60 grit and it didn't slow down once."

Dust collection is solid through the pad's 8 holes, but the included cloth bag is the weak point. It fills fast and the fine dust that escapes is noticeable. Most serious users (myself included) run a shop vac hose directly to the port — there's a 1-1/4 inch and 1-1/2 inch adapter situation you'll want to sort out. As one buyer put it, "The dust bag is a joke. Hook it to a vacuum and it's a different tool."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, durability — multiple users report years of heavy use without issues. Second, the hook-and-loop pad holds discs securely but releases them cleanly, even after extended sessions where heat can cause adhesive failure on cheaper sanders. Third, the variable speed dial is genuinely useful — you can crank it down for finish passes on veneer or up for aggressive stock removal.

## Where It Falls Short

The dust port angles downward at about 45 degrees. When you're sanding flush to a vertical surface — say, a cabinet face frame — the port catches on the edge and forces you to tilt the sander. It's annoying, not deal-breaking, but worth knowing.

Pad changes require a 5mm hex key that isn't included. If you lose the one from your toolbox, you're stuck. Also, the cloth dust bag is nearly useless for fine sanding dust — budget for a vacuum adapter or aftermarket dust port right away.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a woodworker, furniture restorer, or serious DIYer who sands regularly and wants pro-grade results without paying brushless prices. It's also ideal if you already own DEWALT tools and want matching battery-free tools that just plug in and work.

**Skip it if** you sand once a year for small projects — a cheaper 5-inch sander will do fine. Also skip if you're committed to cordless — the DWE6423 is corded only.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this sander work with standard 5-hole sandpaper?**
A: Yes. The pad has 8 holes, but it accepts both 5-hole and 6-hole discs. Non-DE WALT paper works fine — just use the included template to punch holes if your discs aren't pre-punched.

**Q: Can you use this for polishing or buffing?**
A: Not effectively. The 12,000 OPM max speed is too slow for polishing compounds. It's a sander, not a polisher — buy a dedicated machine for that.

**Q: How loud is it?**
A: It's typical for a random orbit sander — around 85-90 dB at full speed. Wear hearing protection for extended sessions. It's quieter than a belt sander but not silent.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The DWE6423 hits the sweet spot of power, control, and price. It's not the cheapest sander DEWALT makes, but it's the one that professionals and serious hobbyists actually reach for daily. The vibration control alone justifies the cost over budget models. The dust bag and missing hex key are minor annoyances, not flaws — plan around them and you've got a sander that will outlast your current project and your next three.

**Buy it if** you want a sander that feels like a pro tool and will still be working in a decade. **Skip it if** you need cordless convenience or only sand occasionally.
