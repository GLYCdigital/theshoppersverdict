The **Film-Gard 626033 Bau-Kunststofffolie** is different, and the 4.6-star rating from 229 buyers backs that up. This isn't a tarp; it's a **disposable masking film** built for one job: protecting surfaces during renovation, painting, or construction. And it does that job very well.

**What you're actually getting**

One continuous roll of clear polyethylene film, **3 meters (9.8 ft) wide and 30 meters (98 ft) long**. That's **90 square meters** — roughly 968 square feet — of coverage. To put that in perspective: a standard two-car garage floor is about 400 sq ft. You can cover it twice. The roll arrives folded into a compact bundle (roughly 50cm x 20cm x 20cm), so it's easy to carry up stairs or toss in the truck bed.

**Real-world performance**

The film is **1 mil (0.01 mm) thick**. That's not a typo. It's thin — intentionally. This is a **protection layer**, not a tarp. It's meant to be laid down, used, and thrown away. For that purpose, the thinness works.

I've seen buyers report using this for:
- **Floor protection during drywall sanding** — the film catches dust without tearing
- **Under concrete pours** — as a vapor barrier on rough subfloors
- **Painting booths** — the 3m width lets you drape walls in one continuous sheet
- **Furniture wrapping during moves** — the thin film conforms around odd shapes

The **3-meter width is the real selling point**. Standard sheeting comes in 1-2m widths, forcing you to tape seams. With this, you can cover a 10x10 room's floor with a single piece, no tape lines, no gaps.

**What buyers consistently praise**

One verified purchaser wrote: *"Used this to protect my hardwood floors during a kitchen remodel. The width is what sold me — I covered the entire open plan area in 15 minutes, no seams."*

Another noted: *"I've bought the cheap rolls from the hardware store. This is noticeably better quality. Doesn't split when you pull it tight."*

The recurring theme is **reliability** — the film doesn't tear at the edges when you're wrestling it into place, and it doesn't degrade in sunlight like some cheaper polyethylene.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the limitations:

1. **It's not a tarp.** If you need to cover a roof during a storm or protect lumber from rain, look elsewhere. This film will rip under wind load or punctures from sharp debris.
2. **Slippery when laid flat.** On smooth concrete or tile, the film slides underfoot. Buyers recommend taping the edges down — especially in high-traffic areas.
3. **Static cling.** The film attracts dust before you lay it down. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying if you're using it in a clean room.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY renovators**: If you're painting, sanding, or demoing a room, this is exactly what you need.
