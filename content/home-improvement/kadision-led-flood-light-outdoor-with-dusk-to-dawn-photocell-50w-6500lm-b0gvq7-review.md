**The kadision 50W LED flood light doesn't try to be clever. It just floods your yard with 6,500 lumens of clean white light the moment the sun goes down — and shuts itself off at dawn. No app, no motion sensor, no Wi-Fi. For a homeowner who wants reliable, hands-off illumination, that simplicity is exactly the point.**

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the flood light fixture (fully assembled), a mounting bracket with three screws and anchors, and a wire connector set. The unit itself measures roughly 9.8 x 7.5 x 2.4 inches — compact for a 50W fixture. It's built from die-cast aluminum with a tempered glass lens, and the photocell sensor sits prominently on the front face. The light head pivots on a single axis, letting you tilt it up or down about 90 degrees.

Wiring is straightforward: three wires (black, white, green) for standard 120V hookup. If you're replacing an existing flood light, this is a 15-minute swap. If you're wiring fresh, you'll need a junction box — the included bracket mounts directly over a standard 3.5-inch box.

## Performance: The Brightness Is Real

Here's where the kadision earns its keep. At 50W, it pulls about 0.42 amps and pushes out 6,500 lumens at 6500K daylight color. That's roughly 130 lumens per watt — efficient for the price range. In practical terms, one unit easily covers a two-car driveway or a 40-foot backyard with overlapping light pools.

The light distribution is wide and even, thanks to the array of 48 individual LED chips behind the frosted lens. There's no harsh hotspot directly below the fixture, which is a common complaint with cheaper floodlights. The 6500K temperature is crisp and bright — great for security and task lighting, but it's not cozy. If you want warm ambient light for entertaining, this isn't it.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The photocell is the star. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Installed it three weeks ago and haven't touched it since. Comes on right at dusk and goes off at sunrise. Exactly what I wanted."* Another noted, *"I've had cheap floodlights that flicker or stay on all day. This one has been flawless through rain and heat."*

Build quality gets consistent shout-outs too. Multiple buyers mention the aluminum housing feels premium compared to the plastic-bodied units at similar price points. The tempered glass lens doesn't yellow or haze, which keeps light output consistent over months of sun exposure.

## Where It Falls Short

The fixed bracket is the biggest limitation. You can tilt the head up and down, but there's no horizontal swivel. If your mounting surface isn't facing the direction you need, you'll have to install an angled adapter or choose a different mounting spot. Plan your placement carefully.

A handful of reviewers also noted the photocell has a slight delay on overcast days — it might take an extra 5–10 minutes to trigger when the sky is gray but not fully dark. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you want lights on exactly at sunset regardless of weather.

And there's no motion sensor. This is always-on from dusk to dawn. If you want motion-triggered activation, look at kadision's PIR model instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this need a special switch or smart home hub?**
No. Hardwire it to standard 120V, and the photocell handles everything. No hub, no app, no special switch required.

**Q: How bright is 6,500 lumens, really?**
For reference, a standard 60W incandescent is about 800 lumens. This is roughly eight times that. One unit comfortably lights a full driveway or a medium backyard.

**Q: Will the photocell work through a covered porch or under an eave?**
Yes, but it needs ambient light exposure to sense dusk and dawn. If it's deeply recessed or shaded by a tree, the sensor may trigger late or early. Mount it where it has a clear view of the sky.
