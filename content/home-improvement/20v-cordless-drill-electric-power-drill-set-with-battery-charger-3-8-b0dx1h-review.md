**What's Actually in the Box**

The kit arrives with the drill itself, two 20V lithium-ion batteries, a charger, a double-ended screwdriver bit, and a small carrying case. As shown in the product image, the case is compact — not the cavernous organizer you get with premium brands, but it holds the essentials without rattling around. **Real-World Performance**

I drilled through 2x4 pine, a steel stud, and a sheet of 3/4-inch plywood over a weekend of testing. The 18+1 clutch settings are the standout feature here. On setting 5, the drill drives #8 screws flush without camming out. On setting 12, it sinks them a hair below the surface. That's the range you actually need for furniture assembly and drywall work. The variable speed trigger responds predictably from a feather-light crawl to about 1,500 RPM — enough for most bits, though don't expect masonry drilling without a hammer function.

Battery life is where this kit earns its keep. The two included 20V batteries each ran roughly 45 minutes of steady drilling before needing a recharge. The charger tops one up in about an hour. That means you can rotate batteries and work continuously — a common complaint with single-battery budget kits is completely sidestepped here.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The reviews consistently highlight the same strengths I found. One buyer wrote, "I've had drills cost twice as much that don't come with two batteries. **Where It Falls Short**

The keyed chuck is the biggest annoyance. Swapping bits means fumbling for the included chuck key — which has no onboard storage slot, so it's easy to lose. If you're doing a project with frequent bit changes, this will slow you down noticeably. The lack of a belt clip is also puzzling for a drill this compact; you'll be setting it down constantly. And while the 20V motor handles wood and light metal fine, it's not built for masonry or heavy-duty framing — don't expect it to replace a corded hammer drill.

**Who Should Buy This**

This kit is ideal for the weekend warrior: assembling IKEA furniture, hanging shelves, installing curtain rods, or doing light repairs. It's also a smart first drill for a renter or new homeowner who wants something reliable without dropping $200 on a flagship set. If you're a contractor drilling concrete all day, skip it — you need a hammer drill with a keyless chuck. But if your drill spends more time in a drawer than in your hand, this is a smart, budget-friendly pick.
