## What's actually in the box

This is a two-filter system: a washable pre-filter (the fine mesh screen that catches hair and dust bunnies), a True HEPA filter rated H13-grade, and an activated carbon deodorization filter. The "2 Year Supply" designation means you get **two complete HEPA + carbon sets** — enough for four filter changes if you follow the standard 6-month replacement cycle. That's the full package, not a single filter with a cheeky label.

As shown in the product image, the HEPA filter has the same accordion-style pleated media and rigid cardboard frame as the OEM part. ## Real-world performance: Does it actually clean the air?

Here's where this filter surprises. Multiple buyers tested it with standalone particle counters and reported **PM2.5 readings dropping from hazardous levels (150+ µg/m³) to below 10 µg/m³ within 30-45 minutes** in medium-sized rooms. That's performance statistically indistinguishable from the OEM filter.

The H13 HEPA media is rated to capture 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns — the industry standard for "True HEPA." For context, that covers dust mites, pollen, mold spores, pet dander, and most smoke particles. The carbon filter handles VOCs and odors, though it's thinner than the OEM version. One buyer noted, "The carbon layer is noticeably less dense, but for my allergies and dust, it's been fine."

Where the build quality shows is in the frame rigidity. The OEM filter has a slightly stiffer cardboard frame that compresses more evenly against the purifier's housing. This third-party version is adequate but slightly more flexible. Most units seal fine; a few buyers reported minor air bypass if the filter wasn't seated perfectly. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Where it falls short

Let's be blunt about the tradeoffs. The carbon filter is the weakest link. For everyday odors — cooking, pets, general living — it works. But if you're a heavy smoker or frequently burn food, you'll likely need to replace the carbon filter more often than the 6-month schedule. The HEPA itself will last fine; the carbon will exhaust faster.

The missing gasket is the other real concern. The OEM filter has a thin foam gasket around the edge that creates a positive seal. This one doesn't. In most units, the housing's plastic lip compresses the frame enough to seal properly. But if your Coway is older and the plastic has warped slightly, you might get a bit of unfiltered air bypass. Check the seal after installation by running your hand around the filter door — if you feel airflow, you have a problem.
