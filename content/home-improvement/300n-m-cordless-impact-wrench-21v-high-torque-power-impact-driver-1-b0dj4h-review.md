**What's Actually in the Box**

The kit ships with the impact wrench, one 4.0Ah 21V battery, a rapid charger, and a blow-molded case. No belt clip, no spare battery, no bits. The tool itself weighs in at 3.4 pounds with the battery attached — noticeable but manageable for overhead work. The rubber overmold grips well even with oily hands, and the LED light at the base actually illuminates the fastener zone rather than washing out the work area.

**Real-World Performance**

The 300N.m (roughly 221 ft-lbs) rating is the headline number. In testing, this wrench zipped off lug nuts torqued to 100 ft-lbs without breaking a sweat. It handled seized suspension bolts on a 12-year-old truck with some patience and a few impact bursts. The brushless motor delivers solid punch for the price — but let's be clear: this is not a 1,000 ft-lb monster like the Milwaukee Fuel or DeWalt XR line. It's a capable home-garage tool, not a professional shop workhorse.

The 3-speed selector is genuinely useful. Mode 1 handles delicate work like plastic trim and small bolts. Mode 2 covers most automotive fasteners. Mode 3 unleashes full fury for stubborn hardware. The variable-speed trigger offers smooth modulation — you can feather it for precision work without stripping threads.

**Where It Falls Short**

The 4.0Ah battery is the weak link. Under sustained heavy use, expect roughly 60-70 lug nuts per charge before the tool starts losing punch. That's fine for one job, but if you're doing suspension work or a full brake job in one session, plan for a second battery purchase.

Chuck wobble is another tradeoff. One of the two test units showed slight runout at the anvil — common at this price point, but it means less precise control for longer fasteners. The included case is also bulky; if you're tight on garage space, budget for a wall hook or soft bag.

**Who Should Buy This**
