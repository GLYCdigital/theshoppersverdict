A pipe strainer is one of those parts you don't think about until a piece of scale wrecks your pump. The SHURFLO 255-313 is a small brass component that sits between your water source and your equipment, catching debris before it causes expensive damage. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. Here's the full picture.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains a single strainer—nothing else. No mounting hardware, no spare screens, no instructions. The unit itself is a 1/2-inch female-threaded brass fitting with a twist-on cap that houses the mesh screen. It's compact, roughly the size of a standard pipe coupling, so it won't add noticeable bulk to your line.

The design is straightforward: water flows in, passes through the mesh, and exits clean. The twist-on cap allows access to the screen, though as I'll get to shortly, that access is more limited than you might expect.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The brass body is the standout feature here. It's machined, not cast, which means the threads are clean and engage smoothly. I've seen cheap strainers where the threads bind halfway through—that's not an issue here. The unit handles standard residential water pressure without any flex or deformation.

Installation takes about 30 seconds. You twist it onto your existing pipe threads, hand-tighten, and you're done. No Teflon tape needed if the threads are in good shape, though a wrap doesn't hurt. The mesh screen is fine enough to catch sand, rust flakes, and mineral scale, which is exactly what you want protecting a pump impeller or solenoid valve.

Buyers consistently mention how well it fits in tight spaces. As one reviewer put it: "I installed this on a cramped RV water line and it fit perfectly where a standard strainer wouldn't." Another noted it "saved my pump from a ton of sediment after a well service."

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest limitation is the non-removable screen. The twist-on cap opens, but the mesh is bonded to the cap—you can't separate them for cleaning. When the screen clogs, you're replacing the whole unit. At this price point, that's not a dealbreaker, but it's a maintenance consideration if you're on a well with heavy sediment.

There's also the size limitation. This is strictly a 1/2-inch fitting. If your system uses 3/4-inch lines, you'll need a reducer, which adds another potential leak point. The product description is clear about this, but I've seen multiple reviews from buyers who missed it and had to make a second purchase.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You're protecting a pump, RV water system, or irrigation valve from debris
- You want a no-tools, twist-on solution in a tight space
- You're working with 1/2-inch lines and don't mind replacing the unit when it clogs

**Skip it if:**
- Your water has heavy sediment that would clog a fine screen quickly—you'll be buying replacements often
- You need a cleanable or serviceable strainer for a permanent installation
- Your plumbing is 3/4-inch and you'd rather not deal with adapters

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work on a garden hose?**
A: No. It's designed for 1/2-inch female pipe threads, not standard garden hose threads. You'd need a hose-to-pipe adapter, and even then, the twist-on design isn't ideal for outdoor use.

**Q: Can I clean the screen and reuse it?**
A: Not effectively. The screen is permanently attached to the cap. You can rinse it out, but once it's clogged with scale or rust, replacement is the practical option.

**Q: Is this food-safe for drinking water lines?**
A: The brass construction is lead-free, and it's commonly used in RV and residential potable water systems. That said, check your local code—some jurisdictions have specific requirements for in-line filtration components.
