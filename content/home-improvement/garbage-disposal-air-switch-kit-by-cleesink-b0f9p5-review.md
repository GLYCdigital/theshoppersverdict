Let me be blunt: if you're still reaching under the sink to flip a wall switch every time you grind food scraps, you're doing it wrong. The CLEESINK Garbage Disposal Air Switch Kit is the $30-ish fix that makes your disposal actually convenient — and at 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, it's clearly working for most people. But it's not perfect, and I'll tell you exactly where it stumbles.

**What's in the box**  
You get the chrome push button (the part that sits on your countertop), a 1.6-meter air tube, the power control module, and mounting hardware. Notably missing: an outlet cord. The module has bare wires for hardwiring, so if you're not comfortable with basic electrical work, budget for an appliance cord or call someone who is. The button itself is the star — brushed stainless, 2.4 inches wide, and it looks like it belongs on a $500 faucet, not a $30 kit.

**Installation reality check**  
The concept is simple: the button pushes air through the tube, which triggers the module to cut power to the disposal. No electrical wires near water — that's the safety win here. The actual install took me about 40 minutes, and that included drilling a 1.3-inch hole in my granite countertop. The kit includes a hole saw adapter, but if you've got quartz or stone, go slow with water cooling or you'll burn the bit.

The adhesive mount that comes with it? It's fine for laminate or tile, but I'd skip it for stone. One reviewer put it plainly: *"The double-sided tape gave up after two weeks on my granite — the drill mount is the way to go."* That's the reality. The button itself is solid, but the adhesive is the weak link.

**Performance in daily use**  
Here's where this kit earns its keep. The button has a satisfying click, and the air tube is long enough that I never felt tension on the countertop mount. It's rated for 15A/120V, which covers standard 1/2 to 1 HP disposals without issue. I've had mine for three months and the response is instant — press, the disposal hums, release, it stops. No lag, no ghost triggering.

The safety angle is worth repeating: because there's no electrical current at the sink, you can't get shocked if water splashes. That's a genuinely better design than old-school toggle switches. Multiple buyers mention this exact benefit, like this one: *"I feel safer knowing there's no live switch near my wet hands."*

**Where it falls short**  
The missing power cord is my biggest gripe. For a product aimed at DIYers, making me source a separate cord or hardwire it adds friction. Also, the air tube connector is plastic — it works, but I'd be gentler with it than I'd like. And if your sink has a soap dispenser or sprayer already in the countertop, you might not have a spare hole. That's a planning issue, not a product flaw, but worth measuring before you buy.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Renters** who can't rewire a wall switch but can drill a small countertop hole (with permission, obviously)  
- **Families** with kids — the no-electricity-near-water design is a genuine safety upgrade  
- **Anyone** replacing a broken wall switch and tired of the old setup  

**Skip it if** you have zero countertop space, no spare hole, or you're not comfortable drilling into stone. Also skip it if you want a plug-and-play experience — you'll need to supply the cord.

**FAQ**  
*Q: Does this work with all disposals?*  
A: It works with any standard 120V disposal up to 1 HP. Check your disposal's amp rating — if it's above 15A, this won't handle it.

*Q: Can I install this without an electrician?*  
A: The air-side is totally DIY. The power module requires connecting two wires — if you've changed an outlet before, you're fine. If not, get help.

*Q: Will the button fit my existing countertop hole?*  
A: It needs a 1.3-inch hole. If you have a soap dispenser hole of the same size, you can swap it out. Otherwise, you'll drill a new one.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5**  
**Buy it if** you want a clean, safe, and genuinely convenient way to control your disposal. The brushed steel button is premium, the air-tube design is smart, and the safety benefit is real.  
**Skip it if** you can't drill a countertop hole or want a fully plug-and-play kit without sourcing a cord.

For the price and the 4.8-star track record, this is the air switch kit I'd buy again. Just order the drill mount version if you've got stone countertops, and grab a cord while you're at it.
