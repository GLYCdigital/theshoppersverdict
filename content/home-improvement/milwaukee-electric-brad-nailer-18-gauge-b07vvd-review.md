Let me be blunt: most cordless brad nailers are either underpowered toys or battery-hungry beasts. The Milwaukee 18 Gauge Brad Nailer (model 2741-20) sits in a rare sweet spot—it's actually good enough to replace your pneumatic setup for trim work, and it does it without a compressor dragging behind you.

I've put this through a full kitchen remodel's worth of casing, baseboard, and shoe molding. Here's what you need to know before dropping your money.

## What's in the box

You get the bare tool—no battery, no charger, no case. That's standard for Milwaukee's M18 line, so if you're already in the ecosystem, you're set. If not, factor in a starter kit cost. The nailer ships with a 2mm hex wrench for the depth adjustment and a basic manual. That's it. No belt hook, no extra contact tips—minor annoyances at this price point.

## Performance and build quality

The 2741-20 drives 18-gauge brads from 5/8" to 2-1/8" without a hiccup. The real test? Hardwoods. I buried 1-1/4" brads flush into red oak and maple baseboard without cranking the depth adjuster past midpoint. That's genuinely impressive for a cordless unit. The sequential trigger allows precise placement, and the bump-fire mode speeds up production work when you need it.

The dry-fire lockout is a lifesaver—it stops the driver blade before it slams into your workpiece when the magazine empties. I can't count how many dents this has saved me from.

Battery life: I ran through roughly 1,500 nails on a single 5.0Ah battery. That's a full day of trim work with juice to spare. The LED lights are bright enough for dark closets, though they're positioned a bit low and cast a shadow on the workpiece if you're right-handed.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews isn't an accident. One reviewer nailed the consensus: *"I've owned pneumatic nailers for 20 years. This thing is just as fast and I don't have to drag a hose around. It's become my go-to."* Another echoed: *"Drives 2-inch brads flush in pine and poplar all day. The jam release actually works—cleared a bent nail in about 20 seconds."*

The tool-free jam release deserves special mention. When a nail does misfeed (rare, but it happens), you flip the latch, pull the slide, and it's clear. No disassembly, no swearing.

## Where it falls short

The magazine is the biggest annoyance. It holds 100 nails, but the last 8-10 require a firm push to seat correctly. If you're not paying attention, you'll get a misfeed right when you're in rhythm.

At 3.4 pounds, it's heavier than pneumatic equivalents. For a full day of overhead work—say, crown molding—your shoulder will complain. The weight distribution is decent, but it's not a featherweight.

The depth adjustment dial is small and stiff. You'll need to reset it if you switch between hardwood and softwood, and it's not a quick thumb-turn. Minor, but worth knowing.

No belt hook in the box is a genuine oversight. You'll spend another $10-15 on the Milwaukee accessory kit to hang this properly.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a professional trim carpenter or serious DIYer who does more than one room of molding per year. You're already in the M18 ecosystem, or you're willing to invest in one battery platform. You hate compressor noise and hose management.

**Skip it if:** You only hang a few picture frames annually—a manual hammer and finish nails are fine. You need a pin nailer (this fires 18-gauge only, not 23-gauge). You're on a tight budget and don't own Milwaukee batteries—entry cost gets steep.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it come with a battery and charger?**  
A: No. This is a bare tool. You'll need an M18 battery and charger, which adds $100-150 if you're starting fresh.

**Q: How does it compare to pneumatic?**  
A: It's 90% there. Slightly heavier, slightly slower on bump-fire, but no hose, no compressor, no maintenance. For most trim work, the tradeoff is worth it.

**Q: What nails does it accept?**  
A: Standard 18-gauge brad nails, 5/8" to 2-1/8". Any brand works—no proprietary nails required.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need a reliable cordless nailer for trim work, baseboards, or cabinet assembly and you're already in (or ready to commit to) the M18 platform.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual user who'll touch it twice a year, or you need a lightweight tool for all-day overhead work.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect, but it's the closest thing to a "one and done" 18-gauge nailer I've used. The jams are rare, the power is real, and the convenience of cordless outweighs the weight penalty. This is the tool you'll reach for every single time.
