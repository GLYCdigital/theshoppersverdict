## The ST64 shape is finally done right — mostly

Walk into any home improvement store and you'll find a dozen "vintage Edison" LED bulbs that look like cheap plastic props. The TOBUSA ST64 4-pack is not that. These are a legitimate 64mm teardrop profile with a real exposed filament array, and at normal viewing distance they're nearly indistinguishable from the incandescent originals they're replacing.

At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, this is one of the better-reviewed ST64 options on Amazon. Here's what you're actually getting.

## What's in the box

Four ST64 LED bulbs, each with E26 medium base, rated at 60W equivalent while drawing only 6W. The clear glass lets you see the full filament structure — four vertical bars with a zigzag pattern that mimics the old carbon filament look. Box dimensions are standard for a 4-pack: roughly 8 x 4 x 3 inches, so it ships in a regular padded envelope without issue.

## Real-world performance

The 2700K color temperature is genuinely warm — not the sterile yellow-white you get from cheaper "warm white" LEDs. Color rendering is decent for a budget filament bulb; skin tones look natural in bathroom vanity fixtures and dining room pendants. The 6W draw means a four-bulb fixture runs at just 24W total, which is a real savings over 4 x 60W incandescent.

Heat is the surprise win. After two hours in an enclosed pendant, the glass was warm but not hot — you can touch it briefly without pulling back. That's a meaningful safety improvement over incandescent, especially in fixtures close to ceilings or with fabric shades.

## What buyers consistently praise

The most repeated positive in the reviews is the appearance. One buyer put it plainly: *"These look like the $8-per-bulb Edison bulbs from the lighting store, but I paid a fraction of that."* Another noted: *"Installed in my kitchen island pendants and honestly can't tell they're LED until I touch them."* The consistency across the four bulbs in the pack gets mentioned too — no mismatched color temperatures or brightness levels.

## Where it falls short

Two issues surface repeatedly in the negative reviews. First, the unfrosted filament bars create glare. If you install these in a bare socket at eye level — think wall sconces or a desk lamp — the exposed LEDs are bright enough to be uncomfortable. They're best in pendants, ceiling fixtures, or anywhere the bulb is above eye line.

Second, dimmer compatibility is hit-or-miss. Several buyers report buzzing on older Lutron rotary dimmers, especially when dimmed below 30%. Newer LED-compatible dimmers handle them fine, but if you have older dimmer switches in your home, budget for replacing them or skip these.

A: No. There's no weatherproofing rating listed. Keep them in covered, dry locations only.

**Q: Are they actually dimmable?**
