The set ships with a single 15.4-inch offset extension wrench built around a 1/2-inch drive. That's it—no accessories, no case, no cheater pipe. The offset is what makes it useful: the drive end is angled off-axis from the socket end, which lets you rotate a ratchet or breaker bar in a plane that clears obstructions. As shown in the product image, it's a forged steel bar with a chrome satin finish, not a stamped piece of sheet metal. The drive end has a spring-loaded ball detent to hold sockets securely.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The offset is fixed at roughly 15 degrees. That's great for some applications, useless for others. If you need a 30-degree or 45-degree angle, you're out of luck—you'll need a universal joint adapter, which defeats the purpose. Also, because it's a single piece with no pass-through, deeply recessed bolts that also require an angle (like some transmission bellhousing bolts) will still defeat it. And the 15.4-inch length, while useful, can be unwieldy in tight engine bays—you sometimes have to angle the ratchet awkwardly just to swing it.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with an impact wrench?**
A: It's designed for hand tools—ratchets and breaker bars. The steel is strong, but the offset design isn't rated for impact torque and could stress the drive end. Stick to hand use.

**Q: What size sockets does it fit?**
A: It's a 1/2-inch drive, so it fits any standard 1/2-inch drive socket, from 10mm up to 24mm or larger. You'll need to supply your own sockets.

**Q: Can I use this with a torque wrench?**
A: Yes, for final torque, but the offset can slightly affect the torque reading on some wrench designs. Use it for removal and rough tightening, then finish with a straight extension for accuracy.
