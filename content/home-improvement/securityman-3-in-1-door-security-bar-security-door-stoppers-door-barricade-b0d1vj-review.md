Let me be blunt about why I picked this up in the first place: I travel a lot, and hotel chain locks have never fully convinced me. The SECURITYMAN 3-in-1 isn't a gadget — it's a hunk of steel that physically refuses to let a door open. Here's what 225 other buyers and I discovered after actually using it.

**What you actually get**

The package contains one steel bar, roughly 12 inches long when fully extended, with a rubber-padded foot that wedges under the door and a curved bracket that hooks around a doorknob. There's no assembly, no screws, no batteries. The three positions are labeled directly on the unit: door stop (for inward-opening doors, placed at the bottom), door jam (wedged between the door edge and frame), and handle brace (looped over a round knob with the base planted on the floor). The adjustability comes from a telescoping shaft that locks at multiple lengths, so it adapts to door gaps from about 1/4 inch to over an inch.

**How it holds up in real use**

I tested this on my front door (standard 36-inch interior door with a 3/4-inch gap), a sliding glass door track, and a hotel room door in a mid-century building with a heavy metal frame. On the home door, the door stop position is rock solid — I put my full weight against the door and the bar didn't budge, flex, or slide. The rubber foot grips hardwood and tile without leaving marks, which was my first concern. On carpet, it gripped even better.

The handle brace position is clever but has a real limitation: it only works with round doorknobs. My home office has a lever handle, so that position was useless there. In the hotel, the round knob worked perfectly — the bar looped over the knob and the base held firm on the floor, creating a solid mechanical block that would require serious force to defeat. Multiple Amazon reviewers noted the same versatility, with one writing, "I used it in a rental cabin and a hotel, and it gave me peace of mind I didn't have with the flimsy chain lock."

**Where it falls short**

The biggest miss is the lever-handle incompatibility. Modern hotels and many newer homes use levers, and if that's what you have, you lose a third of the product's functionality. Also, on a particularly thick commercial door with a wide gap, I noticed the wedge position needed re-seating after a few hard pushes — the bar didn't fail, but it shifted slightly. That's a minor annoyance, not a safety flaw. And if you're expecting a loud alarm or a motion sensor, look elsewhere — this is a purely passive barrier. One reviewer summed it up: "It's not a security system, it's a physical deterrent. For that, it works."

**Who should buy this**

- **Frequent travelers** who stay in hotels, Airbnbs, or rentals with questionable locks — this fits in a carry-on and works on most standard doors.
- **Apartment dwellers** who want an extra layer of defense on a rental door they can't modify with permanent hardware.
- **Anyone with round doorknobs** who wants a simple, no-install barrier for a home office, bedroom, or basement door.

Skip it if you have lever handles throughout your home and no travel needs — you'd only be using one position, and there are cheaper single-purpose door jams for that.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work on a sliding glass door?**  
A: Yes, the door stop position works on sliding tracks. Place the bar vertically in the track and it blocks the panel from sliding. It won't lock the door, but it prevents it from opening more than an inch or two.

**Q: Does it damage the door or floor?**  
A: No. The rubber padding on both the foot and the handle bracket prevents scratches on wood, tile, and metal. On carpet, it leaves no lasting marks.

**Q: How much force does it take to defeat?**  
A: In my test, a full-body shoulder push didn't budge it. It's not Fort Knox — a determined attacker with a crowbar could pry the door frame — but it will stop casual attempts and slow down anyone serious.

**The Verdict**

The SECURITYMAN 3-in-1 is a well-built, genuinely useful security tool that earns its 4.5-star rating. It's not perfect — the lever-handle gap is real — but for the price, it delivers more versatility than a basic door wedge and does so with solid construction. 

**Buy it if** you travel, rent, or want a non-permanent security upgrade for a knob-handle door.  
**Skip it if** you exclusively have lever handles and never travel.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A reliable, honest piece of hardware that does exactly what it promises, with one notable design limitation.
