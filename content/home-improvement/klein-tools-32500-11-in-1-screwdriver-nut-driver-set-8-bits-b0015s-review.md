Every electrician I know has a Klein 11-in-1 in their pouch. The 32500 is the reason why. This isn't a novelty multi-tool that tries to do everything poorly — it's a workhorse with eight swappable bits and two nut drivers built into the handle. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, the reputation is earned. But it's not perfect for everyone, so let's break down what you're actually getting.

## What's in the Handle

The 32500 packs eight bits inside the hollow handle: #1 and #2 Phillips, 3/16-inch and 1/4-inch slotted, #1 and #2 square recess, and T10 and T15 Torx. The handle base doubles as a 3/8-inch nut driver, and the shaft flips over to reveal a 1/4-inch nut driver. That gives you 11 total functions in a tool that's about 7 inches long.

The bit storage is genuinely clever. As shown in the product image, the bits are color-coded by type, and the translucent handle lets you see which bit is loaded without pulling it out. That's a small touch, but it saves time when you're on a ladder with a wire nut in one hand.

## Real-World Performance

The magnetic shaft is the standout feature. It holds #2 Phillips screws securely enough to start them in drywall or electrical boxes without fumbling. One reviewer noted, "The magnet is strong enough to hold a screw vertically, which is rare in multi-bit drivers." That matches my experience — most competing multi-bit drivers from Milwaukee or DeWalt have weaker magnets that drop screws at the worst moment.

The cushioned grip has three zones with different textures. It's genuinely comfortable for sustained use, though the handle diameter is noticeably thicker than a standard Klein cabinet-tip screwdriver. If you have smaller hands or work in tight electrical boxes, this can feel clumsy. The tradeoff is torque — the thicker handle gives you more leverage for stubborn screws.

The 3/8-inch nut driver on the handle base is a double-edged sword. It's perfect for ground screws and hex-head bolts in panels, but it's not the size most homeowners need. The 1/4-inch driver on the shaft flip side is more universally useful. You'll likely carry a separate 5/16-inch nut driver anyway, which partially defeats the "11-in-1" convenience.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The durability is the recurring theme in reviews. Multiple buyers mention dropping it from ladders or using it daily for years without bit wear or handle cracking. One reviewer put it simply: "I've used this daily for three years as an electrician. The bits still fit snugly, and the magnet hasn't weakened." Another praised the warranty: "Klein replaced a cracked handle with zero questions — just sent a photo and got a new one."

The bit retention is also solid. A common complaint with cheap multi-bit drivers is bits falling out mid-task. That doesn't happen here. The internal spring mechanism holds bits firmly, and the color-coding means you always know what's loaded.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the handle thickness and the less-common 3/8-inch driver size, there are two nitpicks. The bits are shorter than dedicated drivers, which can be an issue in deep recesses — you may not reach screws set far back in fixtures. Also, the hollow handle design means you can't use the butt end as a hammer, which some older Klein models allowed. Minor, but worth noting if you're accustomed to that feature.

The price is higher than generic multi-bit drivers, but not outrageous. You're paying for Klein's alloy steel and the lifetime warranty. If you're a weekend DIYer, a $10 Husky will do the job. If you're an electrician or do serious electrical work, this is the right investment.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're an electrician, HVAC tech, or serious DIYer who works with electrical boxes, panels, and appliances daily. The magnetic retention, bit variety, and durability justify the cost.

**Skip it if** you're a casual home user who needs a basic screwdriver for furniture assembly. The 3/8-inch nut driver and thicker handle won't serve you well, and you'll be fine with a cheaper option.

## FAQ

**Q: Are the bits replaceable if they wear out?**
A: Yes. Klein sells replacement bit sets (model 32515) that fit this driver. The bits are standard 1-inch hex, so any 1/4-inch hex bit will work in a pinch.

**Q: Does the magnetic tip interfere with electronics?**
A: No. The magnetic tip is strong enough for screws but won't damage circuit boards. It's safe for terminating wires and working on outlets.

**Q: Is this the same as the Klein 32500HD?**
A: No. The HD version has a shatter-resistant handle and different bit selection. The 32500 is the standard version with the cushioned grip. For most users, the standard is sufficient.

## The Verdict

The Klein 32500 is the reference standard for multi-bit drivers, and it earns that position with a 4.6/5 rating in my book. It's not perfect — the handle is chunky, and the 3/8-inch nut driver is niche — but the magnetic shaft, durable construction, and lifetime warranty make it the best choice for anyone doing regular electrical work.

**Buy it if** you're a professional or serious DIYer who needs a reliable, everyday driver. **Skip it if** you're a casual user or need more nut driver sizes. For the right buyer, this is the last multi-bit screwdriver you'll ever buy.
