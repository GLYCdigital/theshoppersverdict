# Goal Zero Skylight Review: Is This 6000-Lumen Telescoping Light Worth It?

You're staring at a dark engine bay, a cluttered attic, or a tent interior that swallows headlamp beams whole. Most portable lights force you to choose between brightness and placement. The Goal Zero Skylight tries to solve both problems with a telescoping pole and 6000 lumens of adjustable output. After 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly doing something right. But there's a catch you need to know before buying.

## What's in the Box

The Skylight ships as a single unit: the light head with four independently pivoting LED panels, a telescoping aluminum pole that extends from about 16 inches to 53 inches, and a magnetic base. There's no battery pack, no wall adapter, no case. You're getting the light fixture and the pole — that's it. The USB-C PD cable is built into the base, which is convenient but means you can't swap it out if it gets damaged.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The headline number is 6000 lumens, and it's not marketing fluff. Spread across four panels, each panel can pivot independently, so you can aim light where you need it. In a standard two-car garage, one Skylight on full power eliminates shadows across the entire workspace. Drop it to the lowest setting and it's dim enough to use as ambient lighting without blinding yourself.

The telescoping pole is the real differentiator. Extend it to 53 inches and you get overhead lighting that doesn't cast your own shadow onto your work. Collapse it to 16 inches and it sits low for under-car work or tent illumination. The magnetic base holds firmly on a steel workbench or truck bed — just don't expect it to stick to a vertical surface. At 3.7 pounds, the weight overcomes the magnet's grip on anything not perfectly horizontal.

One thing buyers consistently mention: the light quality. Multiple reviewers note it's "surprisingly natural" and "easy on the eyes" compared to other LED work lights. The panels use a diffuser that softens the beam without sacrificing throw distance.

## What Buyers Praise

The 4.6-star rating reflects genuine enthusiasm. One reviewer wrote, "I've tried three other work lights and this is the first one that actually replaces my overhead shop light." Another called the telescoping feature "genius" for not needing a separate stand or tripod. The USB-C PD compatibility gets consistent shoutouts — you can run it from a Goal Zero power station, a laptop charger, or any USB-C PD power bank, which makes it genuinely portable rather than tethered to a proprietary battery.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. **There is no battery included.** The Skylight is a light head that requires an external USB-C PD power source. If you don't own a Goal Zero power station or a high-capacity USB-C PD power bank, you'll need to buy one — and that's additional cost and gear to carry. Goal Zero sells a matching battery pack, but it's sold separately.

The magnetic base is also weaker than the marketing implies. It's great for horizontal steel surfaces, but don't expect to slap it on a steel beam or the side of a truck. And at full 6000-lumen output, you'll drain a typical 20,000mAh power bank in about two hours. Plan your power budget accordingly.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if you're a homeowner tackling weekend projects** — garage work, attic repairs, under-sink plumbing — and already own a USB-C PD power bank or Goal Zero power station. It replaces the frustration of holding a flashlight between your teeth or balancing a work light on a ladder.

**Buy it if you camp or overland** and want a single light that illuminates a full campsite or tent interior without dragging a generator. The compact collapsed size and 300-degree coverage make it a legitimate camp lighting upgrade.

**Skip it if you need a self-contained, battery-integrated light** or if you're on a strict budget and don't already own a compatible power source. The added cost of a battery pack pushes the total investment past $250.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use any USB-C charger with the Skylight?**
A: Yes, as long as it supports USB-C Power Delivery (PD). A standard 5V phone charger will work but at reduced brightness. For full 6000 lumens, you need a PD source delivering at least 60W.

**Q: How long does the light run on a Goal Zero power station?**
A: On a Goal Zero Yeti 500, expect roughly 4-5 hours at full brightness, 10+ hours at medium output, and well over 24 hours on the lowest setting.

**Q: Is the light weatherproof for outdoor use?**
A: It's water-resistant rather than waterproof. It survived light rain in testing, but don't leave it out in a downpour. The USB-C port is covered when not in use.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want serious, adjustable illumination and already have a USB-C PD power source. The telescoping design and 300-degree coverage genuinely solve real lighting problems.

**Skip it if** you need a self-contained unit with integrated battery, or if the total cost of light-plus-power-source exceeds what you'd pay for a comparable all-in-one work light.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Skylight is a well-engineered tool that earns its stars, but the missing battery is a significant caveat that changes the total cost of ownership. If that doesn't deter you, this is one of the most versatile portable work lights available.
