The BLANCO Precis 27" is a sink that disappears into your countertop and lets the rest of your kitchen do the talking. As shown in the product image, it's a clean, rectangular basin with rounded corners — nothing flashy, just function. But function in a sink means different things to different people. After digging through 215 ratings (4.3 stars) and the spec sheet, here's what actually matters before you drop the cash.

**What's in the box**

You get the sink itself (27" x 18 1/8" x 9" deep), a stainless steel bottom grid that protects the basin floor, and a basket strainer drain assembly. That's it. No mounting clips, no template, no cutout diagram printed on the box. If you're a contractor or fabricator, you already know the drill. If you're a weekend warrior, budget extra time to source the undermount clips and figure out your countertop material's requirements.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

This is a 16-gauge, 304-grade stainless steel sink — the sweet spot between the flimsy 18-gauge budget options and the bank-breaking 14-gauge commercial units. The brushed satin finish is directional, so it hides the inevitable scratches and water spots far better than a mirror polish. I've seen reviewers call it "forgiving" and "low-maintenance," which tracks.

The 9-inch depth is the standout feature. You can stack a full dinner service for six and still have room to rinse a sheet pan without soaking your shirt. The corner radius is tight enough to maximize usable floor space but not so sharp that you're scrubbing crusted food out of a 90-degree angle with a toothbrush.

Sound damping is handled by SoundGuard — a thick undercoating plus a separate pad layer. One reviewer noted, "I can run the disposal at full blast while my wife watches TV in the next room and she doesn't flinch." That's the kind of specific praise you want to hear.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- The finish: "Three months in, still looks brand new. No spots, no scratches."
- The depth: "Finally a sink where the sprayer doesn't bounce water back at me."
- The included grid: "Actually fits flush — not a flimsy afterthought."

**Where it falls short**

The undermount installation is the biggest hurdle. This sink doesn't clamp to the countertop from above; it's meant to be attached underneath with epoxy and clips. On granite or quartz, that's a professional job. Get it wrong and you'll have a sagging gap that collects crumbs and looks terrible.

Also, the basin floor has a subtle slope to the drain, but the corners are slightly elevated. Water pools there unless you aim the sprayer deliberately. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker — but you should know before you're standing there chasing a puddle with a sponge.

Finally, there are no faucet holes. That's typical for a single-bowl undermount, but first-time buyers sometimes miss it and end up scrambling for a deck-mount faucet. Plan ahead.

**Who should buy this**

- **The serious home cook** who needs depth for big pots and isn't afraid of a professional install.
- **The remodeler** pairing this with quartz or granite — the clean undermount look is worth the effort.
- **The noise-sensitive** who cooks while others sleep or watch TV.

Skip it if you're on a laminate countertop (undermount on laminate is rarely worth the hassle) or you want a drop-in replacement for a weekend project.

**FAQ**

**Is 16-gauge steel actually better than 18-gauge?** Yes. Lower gauge = thicker metal. 16-gauge is noticeably more rigid and less likely to dent if you drop a cast iron skillet. 18-gauge can "oil-can" or flex under heavy loads.

**Will this fit in a 30-inch base cabinet?** The sink is 27" wide, and the recommended cabinet size is 30" minimum. It fits, but you'll have less room for plumbing access on the sides.

**Does the bottom grid scratch the sink?** The grid has rubber feet that grip the basin floor, so it stays put without metal-on-metal contact. No scratches reported.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a deep, quiet, well-finished workhorse sink and you're prepared for a proper undermount installation. **Skip it if** you need a drop-in or you're not ready to source mounting hardware and pay a fabricator. At 4.2/5, this is a top-tier choice for a permanent kitchen upgrade — just don't expect it to be a plug-and-play afternoon project.
