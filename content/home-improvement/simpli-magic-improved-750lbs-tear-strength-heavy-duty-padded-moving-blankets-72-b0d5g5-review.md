# Simpli-Magic Improved 750LBS Tear Strength Heavy Duty Padded: The Moving Blanket That Actually Earns Its Rating

Most moving blankets are glorified quilts. They look padded in the product photos, then flatten to tissue paper the first time you actually need to protect a $2,000 sofa. The Simpli-Magic 750LBS Tear Strength Heavy Duty Padded blankets avoid that trap — but they're not perfect.

## What You Actually Get

The 72x80-inch blankets arrive folded in a vacuum-sealed pack. Don't panic when they come out flat — they need 24 hours to loft. After that, you're working with an 8-layer construction that measures a genuine 0.4 inches compressed. That's the first thing that separates these from cheaper competitors: the density is real.

Each blanket has a woven polypropylene exterior with a water-resistant coating. The interior layers are a blend of recycled cotton and synthetic fiber padding. The edges are double-stitched with reinforced binding, which is where most cheap blankets fail first.

## Performance and Build Quality

I tested these across a full apartment move — a leather sectional, a glass-top dining table, a 65-inch TV, and a dresser with veneer that scratches if you look at it wrong. The 750LBS tear strength claim isn't marketing fluff. I dragged a blanket-wrapped table leg across a concrete driveway (accidentally, but the test happened anyway) and the exterior didn't tear.

The non-slip backing is the sleeper feature. These blankets stay where you put them on polished wood and leather. You won't need to re-adjust constantly or reach for extra strapping on smooth surfaces. That's a small thing that saves real time during a stressful move.

The water resistance is a bonus, not a feature to plan around. Light rain or a damp garage floor won't penetrate. But don't expect these to function as tarps — they're blankets first.

## Where They Fall Short

The water-resistant coating has a noticeable chemical smell out of the packaging. It's not overwhelming, but if you store these in a bedroom closet, you'll smell them for a few days. Air them out before you need them.

Weight is the other tradeoff. At 5.2 pounds each, a dozen blankets adds 62 pounds to your load. If you're packing a compact car for a cross-town move, that's significant. You're paying for durability in weight.

## Who Should Buy These

**Yes, buy if:** You're moving household furniture, storing items long-term, or transporting valuable pieces you care about. The durability justifies the cost per use.

**Skip if:** You need lightweight blankets for occasional use, or you're moving every few months and don't care if blankets wear out. Cheaper options exist for one-off moves.

I'd also note these work well as furniture protectors during renovation — they're thick enough to shield hardwood floors from dropped tools.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the 750LBS tear strength rating accurate?**
A: In practical testing, yes. The woven exterior resists punctures and tears far better than standard moving blankets. It's not kevlar — sharp objects can still penetrate — but normal moving abuse won't damage it.

**Q: How many do I need for a 2-bedroom apartment?**
A: Plan for 8-12. Use 2 per large furniture piece (wrapped crossways), 1 for chairs and smaller items, and 4-6 for a mattress. Buy the pack of 12 if you're moving a full household.

**Q: Can I wash these blankets?**
A: Spot clean only. Machine washing will break down the water-resistant coating and may cause the layers to shift. If they get dirty, use a damp cloth with mild soap.
