If you've ever finished a crimp only to watch the connector pop off because two wires didn't seat deep enough, you know the frustration. This all-in-one RJ45 kit directly targets that problem with pass-through connector compatibility — and after 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clear the approach works. Here's the honest breakdown before you spend your money.

## What's Actually in the Kit

The package includes the crimper itself, a wire stripper built into the tool, and a cutter. You're not getting a full networking toolkit — no cable tester, no boot assortment, no spare connectors. What you get is the core tool plus the mechanism that makes it worth owning. The crimper accepts 8P8C (RJ45) and 6P6C/6P4C (RJ11/RJ12) dies, so it covers both network and phone cable runs.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The all-metal frame with cushioned grips gives this a reassuring heft. It's not a flimsy $12 special — the ratchet mechanism engages cleanly and won't release until the crimp cycle completes. That's the single biggest quality-of-life improvement over cheap crimpers: you physically cannot produce a half-crimped connector. One reviewer put it simply: "Finally a crimper that works every time. My first 10 connectors were all perfect."

The built-in stripper deserves specific praise. It's adjustable for cable diameter, and the blade sits at the correct depth to score the jacket without nicking the twisted pairs underneath. For Cat5e and Cat6 solid-core cable, this works as intended. The cutter blade slices through cable cleanly, though it's not designed for heavy-duty repeated use on thick outdoor-rated cable.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The pass-through design is the standout feature. Wires slide through the connector and out the front, letting you visually confirm each conductor is in the correct position before crimping. This eliminates the most common DIY failure point. As one 5-star reviewer noted, "I've made about 30 cables and every single one tested perfectly. The pass-through connectors make it idiot-proof."

The ratchet mechanism also earns repeated mention. Multiple reviewers describe the "click" when the crimp finishes as satisfying and confidence-inspiring. The tool also handles shielded connectors reasonably well, though the die doesn't grip them quite as snugly as unshielded ones.

## Where It Falls Short

The most significant limitation: this crimper is designed primarily for pass-through connectors. Standard RJ45 plugs (the ones with the wire channels closed at the front) don't seat properly in the die. If you have a box of standard connectors already, you'll need to buy pass-through plugs to match this tool.

The strain relief boot installation is fiddly. The die doesn't compress the boot flush with the connector body — you'll need to push the boot forward manually before crimping, and it's easy to misalign. It works, but it's not the smooth one-step process the marketing implies.

Also, the stripper blades aren't replaceable. After heavy use — we're talking hundreds of terminations — they'll dull, and you'll need to replace the entire tool rather than just swapping blades.

## Who Should Buy This

**Network installers and IT pros** who terminate cables daily will appreciate the ratchet consistency and pass-through efficiency. **DIY home network builders** running Cat6 drops through walls will find this pays for itself in fewer wasted connectors. **Students or hobbyists** learning to terminate cable will benefit from the visual confirmation of wire placement.

Skip it if you're terminating only a handful of cables with standard connectors you already own — buy pass-through plugs to match, or stick with a traditional crimper.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with Cat6 and Cat6a cable?**
A: Yes, for solid-core Cat6. Cat6a with thicker 23AWG conductors may require stripping the jacket carefully, but the die handles the connector size fine. It won't work with shielded Cat6a connectors — the die isn't sized for the thicker metal bodies.

**Q: What connectors do I need to buy separately?**
A: Pass-through RJ45 connectors (also called "EZ" or "feed-through" plugs). A pack of 50 runs about $10–15. Standard connectors won't fit the die properly.

**Q: Does it include a cable tester?**
A: No. The kit is just the crimper, stripper, and cutter. A basic network tester runs $15–20 and is worth adding if you're doing more than a few connections.
