## What’s in the Box

Three identical 4-inch cup brushes, each with a 5/8-11 threaded arbor. That’s the standard mount for nearly every angle grinder on the market — DeWalt, Milwaukee, Makita, Ryobi, you name it. No adapters, no fuss, just thread it on and go. The brushes use crimped steel wire, which is the right choice for heavy-duty cleaning: it’s stiffer than knot-style wire but more forgiving on the surface than you’d expect.

Each brush is rated for a max RPM of 12,500, which covers virtually all consumer and prosumer grinders. The cup shape concentrates the bristles at the rim, giving you a flatter working profile than a wheel brush — ideal for cleaning flat surfaces, pipe exteriors, and weld seams without gouging.

## Performance: Where It Shines

I tested these on three jobs: rust removal on a steel workbench, paint stripping on an old fence gate, and cleaning up slag around a weld. The first thing I noticed is the aggression — these brushes don’t just polish rust, they chew through it. The crimped wire bites hard, and the 4-inch diameter keeps the brush balanced without that annoying vibration you get from cheaper off-brand wheels.

The pack’s real strength is endurance. Each brush held up for roughly 20–25 minutes of continuous heavy use before I noticed any significant wire loss. That’s about twice what I’ve gotten from bargain-bin singles. With three in the pack, you’re looking at over an hour of serious grinding time before you’re reaching for a refill.

## Where They Fall Short

Let’s be straight about the downsides. First, these are carbon steel brushes — do **not** use them on stainless steel. The carbon steel particles can embed in the surface and cause rust spots later. If you’re working with stainless, you need a dedicated stainless brush.

Second, the wire shedding I mentioned. It tapers off after break-in, but the first use is messy. Budget for cleanup time.

Finally, these aren’t the best choice for delicate work. If you’re looking for a light surface prep on thin sheet metal, the crimped wire is too aggressive — you’ll want a finer abrasive pad instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my grinder?**  
A: If your grinder has a standard 5/8-11 spindle — which is almost every angle grinder sold in the US — yes. Just thread it on and tighten with a wrench.

**Q: Can I use these on wood?**  
A: Technically yes, but don’t. The wire will tear up wood grain and leave metal fragments embedded. Stick to metal surfaces.

**Q: How do I know when to replace the brush?**  
A: When the wire length drops below half of what it was new, or when you start seeing excessive vibration, it’s done. Worn brushes also lose their aggression — you’ll feel it.
