# 10-Sheet Long Hexagon Peel and Stick Kitchen Backsplash Tile Review

You've seen the rental-apartment hack videos. Peel and stick backsplash. Ten minutes. No grout. No mess. But when you're staring down 10 square feet of adhesive PVC, the real question is whether it actually looks like tile or like a shower curtain pretending to be tile.

The 10-sheet Long Hexagon set from this manufacturer lands somewhere in the middle—closer to real tile than most peel-and-stick options, but with enough caveats that you need to know what you're buying before you commit.

## What You Actually Get

Ten 12-inch square sheets of hexagon-pattern PVC, each pre-cut with that long, elongated hexagon shape that gives a more modern, scaled look than the standard honeycomb. The 3D surface depth is the standout feature here—the raised pattern measures roughly 1.5mm, which means it catches light differently throughout the day. At arm's length, it genuinely reads as a metallic tile backsplash. The brushed silver finish shown in the product images is accurate to what arrives.

Each sheet is scored at the grout lines, so you can tear along the seams rather than cutting every piece. That works well for straight runs but gets fiddly around outlets and corners.

## Real-World Performance

The adhesive is the make-or-break on any peel-and-stick product, and this one performs adequately—with conditions. On clean, smooth drywall with flat paint, the sheets grab firmly and stay put. After 60 days behind a kitchen sink, the edges remain sealed with no lifting or curling where they were pressed down properly.

Here's the catch: the adhesive is not forgiving. On textured walls, heavily painted surfaces, or anything with even minor imperfections, the sheets will not lay flat. Multiple buyers report the same issue—one reviewer noted, "I didn't realize my walls had orange peel texture until I tried to install these. The bumps show through and the tile won't stick in the low spots." Surface prep is the entire game with this product. Clean with rubbing alcohol, fill any divots, and sand smooth. Skip that, and you'll be peeling them off in a week.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The visual impact earns near-universal approval. The 3D pattern gives depth that flat vinyl tiles completely lack. As one verified purchase put it: "Guests genuinely thought this was real metal tile. My contractor brother couldn't tell until he touched it." That's the experience you want from a peel-and-stick product.

The waterproof claim holds up in testing. The PVC material handles steam, splatter, and occasional scrubbing with no warping or discoloration. The 10-sheet quantity covers roughly 10 square feet—enough for a standard range backsplash or a compact bathroom vanity area.

## Where It Falls Short

Seam visibility is the most common complaint. The long hexagon pattern must align exactly between sheets to maintain the visual flow. Get off by even a millimeter, and you'll see a distinct line. This requires careful measuring and patient alignment—not the "slap it up in ten minutes" experience the marketing suggests.

The adhesive also leaves residue if you need to reposition. Once a sheet touches the wall, it's committed. Pulling it up damages the backing and leaves sticky marks that require adhesive remover to clean.

## Who Should Buy This

This product suits renters wanting a temporary upgrade, DIYers with smooth, flat walls, and anyone seeking the look of metal tile without the $400 material cost of real tile and installation. Skip it if you have textured walls, if you're a perfectionist who notices misaligned patterns, or if you need to cover more than 10 square feet without visible seams.

## FAQ

**Will this work over existing tile?**
Not well. The adhesive needs a smooth, flat surface to bond properly. Grout lines and tile texture create air pockets that cause the sheets to lift at the edges.

**Can I cut it to fit around outlets?**
Yes, with a sharp utility knife and a straightedge. Score the back, snap, and finish the cut from the front. It cuts cleanly without cracking or chipping.

**How do I remove it without damaging walls?**
Heat with a hairdryer for 30 seconds per section, then peel slowly at a 45-degree angle. Adhesive residue comes off with rubbing alcohol or Goo Gone.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have smooth, clean walls and want a convincing metal-tile look for under $50 without the commitment of real tile. The 3D depth and waterproof performance genuinely deliver.

**Skip it if:** Your walls have any texture, you lack patience for pattern alignment, or you need seamless coverage across a large area. This is a precision-install product marketed as a quick fix—approach it accordingly.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid performance earned through proper installation. The remaining 0.8 points are the seam-matching difficulty and adhesive sensitivity to wall condition.
