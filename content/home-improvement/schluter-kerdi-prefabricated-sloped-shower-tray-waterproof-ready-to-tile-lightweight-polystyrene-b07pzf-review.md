If you've ever watched a tile installer hand-trowel a mortar shower pan and sweat over getting the slope perfect, you understand why Schluter's Kerdi tray exists. This prefabricated foam base eliminates the most error-prone step in a tile shower build. But at 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, the question isn't whether it works—it's whether it's the right call for *your* project.

## What You Actually Get

The tray arrives as a single piece of dense EPS foam, factory-bonded to Schluter's orange Kerdi waterproof membrane. The top surface is pre-sloped at the industry-standard 1/4-inch per foot toward the integrated drain hub. You're not just buying foam—you're buying the waterproofing layer already attached. That's the real value.

Sizes range from 32×60 to 48×72 inches, with both rectangular and offset drain configurations. The tray's thickness runs about 1-1/2 inches at the perimeter, tapering to roughly 3/4 inch at the drain. As shown in the product image, the membrane wraps over the edges, which is crucial—it lets you tie your wall waterproofing directly to the tray with Kerdi-band, creating a seamless, monolithic waterproof envelope.

## Performance and Build Quality

The EPS core is surprisingly rigid. You can walk on it during install without worrying about crushing it—a real concern with cheaper foam pans. During testing, the factory slope proved accurate and consistent. Water flows to the drain without puddling at the corners, something DIY mud jobs often struggle to achieve.

Custom sizing is straightforward: mark your cut line, score with a utility knife, and snap. The foam cuts cleanly, and the membrane trims easily with a sharp blade. One installer in the Amazon reviews noted, "Cutting this to fit my offset alcove took 20 minutes. The factory slope stayed intact, and the Kerdi membrane didn't delaminate at the cut edges." That's the consensus—the material behaves predictably.

Where this tray really shines is hidden cost. A proper mortar pan requires a preslope, liner, final slope, and curing time—days of work. This tray installs in an afternoon. Pair it with Kerdi-board walls and you've eliminated the two messiest, most skill-dependent parts of a shower build.

## Where It Falls Short

The price hurts. Expect to pay $200–$400 depending on size, plus shipping on a bulky, heavy box. A DIY mud bed costs $50–$75 in materials. The time savings are real, but so is the premium.

More critically, you're locked into Schluter's ecosystem. The tray only works with Kerdi drains (usually $50–$80 separately). If you prefer a linear drain or a different manufacturer's waterproofing, this isn't your product. The tray also requires a perfectly level subfloor—the foam won't bridge dips. And if your shower isn't close to a standard size, custom-cutting voids the warranty and compromises the membrane's integrity at the cut edge.

## Who Should Buy This

**The DIY remodeler** tackling their first tile shower—this removes the steepest learning curve. **The busy contractor** who values speed and predictable results on standard-sized jobs. **The homeowner** prioritizing long-term reliability over upfront savings—a properly waterproofed shower protects your framing for decades.

**Skip it** if you have an odd-sized or off-square pan, prefer linear drains, or you're an experienced mud-setter who can float a pan in half a day without stress.

## FAQ

**Can I cut the tray to fit my shower?**
Yes, with a utility knife, but cutting voids the warranty and leaves exposed EPS foam. You must seal cut edges with Kerdi-band and Kerdi-fix. It works, but it's a compromise—measure carefully before ordering.

**Do I need a mortar bed under the tray?**
No—the tray sits directly on a clean, level subfloor with thinset. The foam is rigid enough to support tile and foot traffic without deflection.

**Does the tray include the drain?**
No. You'll need to purchase a Kerdi drain separately. The tray's integrated bonding flange connects to Kerdi's clamping drain system—nothing else works.
