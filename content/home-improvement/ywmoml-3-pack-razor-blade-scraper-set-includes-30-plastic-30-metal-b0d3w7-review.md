You've probably got one of two problems: either you're scraping paint off glass and ruining the glass with a cheap metal scraper, or you're scrubbing stickers off surfaces with your fingernails because you're scared of gouging them. The Ywmoml 3-Pack Razor Blade Scraper Set is aimed squarely at both scenarios — and it mostly hits the mark.

## What's Actually in the Box

This set gives you three full scraper handles plus 60 blades: 30 plastic and 30 metal. The handles are stainless steel with a rubberized overmold on the grip area. Each one measures about 6 inches long, which is compact enough to slip into a tool bag but substantial enough for real work. The blade release mechanism is a simple push-button on the back — no tools required, no fumbling with screws.

The plastic blades are the headline feature here. They're designed to cut through adhesives, paint overspray, and gunk without scratching the surface underneath. The metal blades are your standard razor-sharp utility blades for tougher jobs.

## How It Performs in the Real World

I put these through three common scenarios: removing a bumper sticker from a car window, scraping dried paint drips off a glass picture frame, and cleaning adhesive residue off a ceramic cooktop.

The plastic blade handled the bumper sticker cleanly. The adhesive came off in strips without leaving micro-scratches on the glass. On the cooktop, it worked exactly as advertised — no gouges, no swirl marks. The metal blade made quick work of the paint drips on the frame, though you need a steady hand to avoid nicking the glass if you're at an angle.

The rubberized grip is genuinely comfortable. After 20 minutes of continuous scraping on a kitchen backsplash, my hand wasn't cramping — a real issue with cheaper all-plastic scrapers that have hard handles.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The versatility of having both blade types is the most common compliment in the 220 ratings. One reviewer noted, "The plastic blades saved my glass shower doors — I'd been using a metal scraper and leaving scratches. These took off the soap scum without a mark." Another said, "I've bought single scrapers for $8 that didn't come with a third of the blades this set includes."

## Where It Falls Short

The plastic blades dull faster than you'd expect on anything abrasive. If you try to scrape paint off concrete or brick, they'll wear out quickly — you'll burn through several blades on a single project. That's fine if you're working on glass or tile, but don't expect them to be a replacement for a metal scraper on rough surfaces.

The included storage box is weak cardboard. It falls apart within a few uses, and the blades end up loose in your drawer. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying if you like organized tool storage. Also, the blade ejection mechanism isn't spring-loaded — you push and hold to release, which takes two hands if you're swapping blades mid-project.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal if you're a renter doing move-out cleanup, a homeowner tackling sticker removal or paint touch-ups, or someone who does regular detailing on cars or boats. The plastic blades are a genuine safety net for delicate surfaces — worth the price just for the peace of mind.

Skip it if you're a professional contractor who needs a heavy-duty scraper for daily demolition work. The plastic blades won't hold up to that kind of abuse, and you'd be better off with a single premium metal scraper.

## FAQ

**Can the plastic blades really not scratch glass?**
They're made from a softer polymer than glass, so they physically can't scratch it under normal pressure. They'll still remove adhesive and paint. Just don't press so hard you flex the blade into the frame edge.

**How do I switch between plastic and metal blades?**
Push the button on the back of the handle, slide the old blade out, insert the new one, and release. Takes about 3 seconds per swap.

**Are these blades standard size?**
Yes — they fit standard 1.5-inch utility razor blades, so you can find replacements at any hardware store if you run through the included 60.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need a versatile scraper for home use, especially on glass, tile, or painted surfaces where you don't want scratches. The 60-blade count makes this exceptional value for the price.

**Skip it if:** You're a pro needing daily heavy-duty scraping on concrete or masonry — the plastic blades won't survive that workload.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Great value, smart design, and the plastic blades genuinely work. Docked slightly for the flimsy storage box and plastic blade durability on rough surfaces. For the average homeowner, this is the scraper set to grab.
