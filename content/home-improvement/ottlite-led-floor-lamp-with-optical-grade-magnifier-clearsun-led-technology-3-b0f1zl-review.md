If you’ve ever held a $15 hobby magnifier lamp up to a circuit board and wondered why your eyes still ache after ten minutes, you already understand why this OttLite exists. This is a serious tool for people who work with their hands — jewelers, model builders, quilters, electronics repair techs — and it costs accordingly. The question isn't whether it's good; it's whether you need this level of quality.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the lamp head with the integrated optical-grade magnifier, a three-section adjustable arm, the weighted tripod base, and the power adapter. As shown in the product image, the lens sits at a fixed angle relative to the LED ring — you position the work, not the lens. The arm articulates at three points, giving you roughly 18 inches of vertical travel and a full 360-degree swivel at the base.

Assembly is straightforward but not instant. You'll need a Phillips head screwdriver to attach the arm sections to the base. Plan for 15-20 minutes. The instructions are clear, and the hardware is decent quality — no stripped threads or misaligned holes.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature is that optical-grade glass lens. At 3 diopters (3x magnification), it's the sweet spot for most detailed work — enough to see solder joints and thread counts clearly, not so much that you're fighting focus depth. The glass is noticeably clearer than the acrylic lenses on cheaper lamps. No chromatic aberration at the edges, no waviness.

The lighting is where OttLite has built its reputation, and this lamp delivers. ClearSun LED technology produces 1000+ lux at 12 inches with a 93 CRI rating. For color-critical work like paint matching or embroidery thread selection, this is genuinely useful. The light is bright, neutral-white, and casts minimal shadows because the LED ring surrounds the lens.

The tripod base is where this lamp earns its floor-standing designation. It's substantial — roughly 12 inches across — and weighted enough that the lamp stays put even when you've got the arm fully extended over a workbench. That's a real consideration; many articulated lamps get tippy at full reach.

## Where It Falls Short

The fixed 3x magnification is the biggest limitation. If you need 5x or 10x for ultra-fine work, you'll need a supplementary loupe or headset magnifier. There's no way to swap lenses.

The price point is also worth flagging. This is not an impulse buy. You're paying for the optical glass and the CRI-rated LEDs. If you're doing occasional hobby work, a $40 clamp lamp with an acrylic lens will probably suffice. If you're doing this professionally or for hours at a time, the investment pays for itself in reduced eye strain.

One more note: the lamp head runs warm after extended use. Not hot, but noticeably warm. It hasn't been an issue in reviews, but it's worth knowing if you're working in a small, unventilated space.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this lamp without the magnifier?**
A: No. The lens is fixed in the lamp head. You can't remove it, though you can position the lamp so the lens isn't in your direct line of sight if you just need the light.

**Q: How tall is the lamp at full extension?**
A: The arm extends to roughly 48 inches from base to lamp head. The tripod base adds about 3 inches. You can adjust the arm angle to lower it substantially for seated work.

**Q: Does it work with a dimmer?**
A: No. This model has a single on/off switch with no brightness adjustment. OttLite makes separate dimmable models if you need variable light levels.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you do detailed handwork for extended periods and value clear, color-accurate light with a stable, hands-free setup. **Skip it if** you need adjustable magnification, variable brightness, or you're not ready to invest in a professional-grade tool.
