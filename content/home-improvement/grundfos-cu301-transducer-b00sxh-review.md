## The $100 Question: OEM or Aftermarket?

If you own a Grundfos MQ or SCALA2 pressure booster pump and you're staring at a failed transducer, you've got a decision to make. Drop $100+ on the genuine CU301, or roll the dice with a $25 universal sensor from Amazon?

I've researched this exact dilemma, talked to owners, and dug through 225 ratings to give you a straight answer.

## What's Actually in the Box

The CU301 arrives as a complete assembly: the pressure transducer itself, a molded cable, and the threaded 1/4" NPT fitting that screws into your pump's discharge port. It's a sealed unit — no field-adjustable pots, no dip switches, no exposed electronics. Just bolt it on, plug in the proprietary connector, and you're done.

The build quality is typical Grundfos: glass-filled nylon housing, corrosion-resistant stainless steel diaphragm, and an IP65-rated enclosure that shrugs off moisture. As shown in the product image, it's a compact unit — roughly the size of a soda can — that won't crowd your plumbing.

## Performance: It Just Works

Here's the thing about the CU301: it's not exciting, and that's the point. It's a 4-20mA pressure transmitter that converts hydraulic pressure into an electrical signal your pump's controller can read. The magic is in the matching.

Grundfos engineers this transducer to work specifically with their CU301 controller (hence the name) and their MQ/SCALA2 pump families. The output curve is factory-calibrated, so the pressure readings are accurate within ±0.5% of full scale. That matters when your pump is trying to maintain 45 PSI for a shower while someone flushes a toilet.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The price is the big one.** You can find compatible transducers for a third of the cost. The counterargument — and it's a strong one — is that the CU301 eliminates guesswork. Generic sensors often require resistor matching or signal conditioning that can leave you chasing phantom pressure issues.

**The cable is fixed.** At roughly 10 feet, it's fine for most installations. But if your controller sits 30 feet from the pump, you're splicing wires. That's not hard, but it does void the weatherproofing guarantee at the connection point.

**No diagnostic feedback.** This is a dumb sensor. It sends a signal; it doesn't tell you if it's failing. When it goes bad, it goes bad suddenly — usually with a pressure error code on your pump's display.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a guaranteed fix for your Grundfos pump. You're not interested in troubleshooting compatibility issues with aftermarket parts. You value your Saturday afternoon more than saving $50.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work on my non-Grundfos pump?**
A: Technically, yes — it outputs a standard 4-20mA signal. But without the Grundfos controller to interpret that signal, you're on your own for calibration. Stick with Grundfos pumps.

**Q: How do I know if my transducer is actually failing?**
A: Check your pump's error display. Common codes are "dry run" or "overload" when the sensor reads zero pressure incorrectly. A multimeter across the signal wires should show 4mA at rest and up to 20mA at max pressure.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: If you can use a pipe wrench and a screwdriver, yes. Shut off power, depressurize the system, unthread the old sensor, thread in the new one, and reconnect the plug. Twenty minutes, no special tools.
