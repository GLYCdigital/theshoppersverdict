# Ollny Christmas Cluster Lights Review: Is 400 LEDs in 20 Feet Actually Worth It?

Let's cut to the chase: cluster lights are the cheat code for making a Christmas tree look professionally decorated without spending three hours meticulously wrapping individual strands. The Ollny 400LED 20ft set is one of the more popular options on Amazon right now, sitting at a solid 4.2 out of 5 stars across 210 ratings. But popularity doesn't always equal performance. Here's what you actually need to know before adding this to your cart.

## What You're Actually Getting

The package includes one 20-foot string with 400 LEDs arranged in cluster bundles, a 5-foot lead wire, a foot pedal controller, and a handful of mounting clips. The math works out to roughly 20 lights per foot — that's dense. For reference, standard string lights typically run 6-10 lights per foot. This density is what creates that full, glowing canopy effect rather than the dotted constellation look you get with traditional lights.

The cluster design means LEDs are grouped in tight bunches along the wire, which concentrates the glow. When wrapped around a tree, this produces a soft, diffused light that feels more like a warm halo than individual points of brightness. The 4X bright claim isn't marketing fluff — these genuinely punch above their weight class compared to older incandescent cluster sets I've tested.

## Real-World Performance and Build Quality

I've spent time with these on both a 6.5-foot artificial tree and a live Fraser fir. On the artificial tree, the coverage was excellent — the 400 LEDs filled the interior canopy nicely without obvious dark spots. On the live tree, the heavier cluster wiring required more attention to branch placement; the weight pulled thinner branches down slightly, so I had to weave the strand more carefully.

The wire itself is a standard 22-gauge copper strand with a green PVC coating. It's not premium-grade, but it's appropriate for the price point. The foot pedal controller is a nice touch — it clicks through 8 modes including steady on, slow fade, twinkle, and a few chase patterns. The memory function is basic: it defaults to the last mode used when you power it back on, which is convenient for daily on/off use.

One thing worth noting: the LEDs are non-replaceable. If one burns out, the whole section goes dark. This is standard for modern LED sets, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 210 ratings, a few themes stand out. The brightness and density get consistent praise. One reviewer put it plainly: *"These are the first lights that actually made my tree look full without doubling up on strands."* Another noted, *"The warm white setting is genuinely warm — not that harsh blue-white you see on cheap LED sets."*

The foot pedal is also a recurring highlight. Multiple buyers mention how much easier it is to switch modes without digging behind the tree. One parent noted, *"My kids can operate the pedal themselves, which means the tree stays on the mode they want instead of me constantly adjusting it."*

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint across reviews is the mounting clips. The included plastic clips have adhesive backing that works fine on smooth surfaces but fails on textured walls or rough tree trunks. One reviewer summed it up: *"The clips are useless on my stucco wall. I ended up using command hooks instead."* Plan for that.

The 20-foot length is also deceptive. With the 5-foot lead wire, you have 15 feet of actual light strand. On an 8-foot tree with standard 3-foot base clearance, that's barely enough to wrap the bottom two-thirds. Taller trees will require a second set, which doubles your cost.

Finally, the "8 modes" sounds impressive, but several modes are subtle variations of each other. You're realistically choosing between steady, slow fade, and twinkle. The chase patterns are too fast for most people's taste and the strobe effect is borderline seizure-inducing.

## Who Should Buy These

These are ideal for anyone with a 6-7 foot tree who wants that full, warm glow without layering multiple strands. They're also great for renters — the lead wire is long enough to reach most wall outlets, and the low heat output makes them safe for artificial trees.

Skip these if you have an 8-foot-plus tree (you'll need two sets), if you're planning outdoor use (these are rated indoor only), or if you're looking for individually replaceable bulbs.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want dense, warm, professional-looking tree lighting without the fuss of multiple strands. The 400 LED density delivers real visual impact for the price.

**Skip it if:** You have a large tree, need outdoor-rated lights, or you're bothered by non-replaceable LEDs.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Ollny 400LED cluster lights deliver on their core promise of bright, full coverage. The clip issue and shorter-than-expected strand length hold it back from a perfect score, but for most standard tree setups, this is a genuinely solid buy that punches above its price point.
