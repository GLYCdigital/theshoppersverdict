**Bottom Line:** If you're remodeling a bathroom and want that resort-style waterfall fill without paying resort prices, the NIITAWH Roman Tub Faucet delivers serious visual impact for the money. But there are a couple of installation realities you need to know before you commit.

## What You're Actually Getting

This is a 3-hole Roman tub faucet with a wide waterfall spout and a detachable hand shower. The box contains the main faucet body, the hand shower with hose, the spout, mounting hardware, and deck plates. As shown in the product image, it's a floor-mount or deck-mount design — you'll need 8 inches of center-to-center spacing between the outer holes, which is the standard Roman tub configuration.

The finish options are brushed nickel or chrome, both with a brushed texture that hides water spots better than polished surfaces. The hand shower docks on the side of the faucet body, which keeps the deck clean when not in use.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The waterfall spout is the headline here, and it doesn't disappoint. Instead of a narrow stream, you get a wide, even sheet of water that fills the tub more like a natural cascade than a traditional spout. One reviewer noted, “The waterfall effect is genuinely beautiful — my kids fight over who gets to take a bath now.” The spout is wide enough to look proportional on a standard 60-inch tub but not so wide it splashes onto the floor.

The diverter valve is a pleasant surprise at this price point. It switches between the waterfall and the hand shower with a clear, positive click, and it holds its position without creeping back. Several buyers mentioned they were worried about cheap plastic internals, but the valve feels solid in daily use.

The hand shower itself puts out a decent spray — not luxury-rain-shower pressure, but perfectly adequate for rinsing the tub, washing hair, or cleaning up after the dog. The hose is metal-braided, which should hold up better than the plastic hoses on cheaper units.

## Where It Falls Short

The hand shower hose measures 59 inches. That's roughly 10-15 inches shorter than what you'll find on pricier Roman faucets. If your tub is deep or wide, you may find yourself stretching to reach the far end. It's workable, but it's a compromise.

The bigger issue is installation. This faucet does **not** include a pressure-balancing valve. If you're replacing an existing Roman tub faucet, you may already have one in the wall — but if this is new construction or a remodel where you're running new supply lines, you'll need to purchase one separately to meet plumbing code in most areas. That's an extra $40-80 and an extra trip to the hardware store. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's something you should budget for.

Also worth noting: the drain assembly is not included. You'll need to buy a separate tub drain, which adds to the total cost.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're upgrading a master bathroom and want the waterfall aesthetic without dropping $500+ on a big-name brand. It's also a solid choice if you're comfortable with basic DIY plumbing or have a contractor handling the install.

**Skip it if** you have an unusually large or deep tub that needs a longer hand shower hose, or if you're not prepared to source the pressure-balancing valve and drain separately.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this faucet work with a standard 3-hole Roman tub setup?**
A: Yes, it's designed for 8-inch center-to-center spacing. The included deck plates also cover up extra holes if you're replacing an older 4-hole configuration.

**Q: Is the waterfall spout loud?**
A: It produces a gentle, continuous sheet of water — quieter than a traditional aerated stream. Some buyers actually commented on how relaxing the sound is.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: If you have basic plumbing experience and access under the tub, yes. If not, budget for a plumber. The lack of an included pressure-balancing valve means you'll need to integrate one into your supply lines.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The NIITAWH Roman Tub Faucet earns its stars on looks, build quality, and that genuinely impressive waterfall flow. The brass construction and smooth diverter feel like they belong on a more expensive fixture. The compromises — shorter hose, missing pressure-balance valve and drain — are real but manageable if you know about them going in.

For the price, this is one of the better values in the Roman tub faucet category. Just plan your install accordingly and you'll be soaking in style without soaking your wallet.

**Buy it if** you want the waterfall look and don't mind sourcing two extra parts. **Skip it if** you need maximum hand shower reach or want a true plug-and-play install.
