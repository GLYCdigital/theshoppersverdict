Let's be blunt: most camping lanterns are either blindingly bright for 20 minutes or dim enough to read by for a week. The MalloMe Camping Lantern tries to split that difference, and for the most part, it actually pulls it off.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box: the lantern itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a small carabiner for hanging. No wall adapter, no batteries, no carry pouch. The unit weighs 10.6 ounces and measures 5.1 inches tall and 3.4 inches wide — roughly the size of a tall soda can. The body is a matte-finish plastic with a rubberized top that houses the controls: one button for power, one for mode cycling.

## Build and Performance

The standout feature here is the 360-degree light diffusion. As shown in the product image, the frosted globe spreads light evenly in every direction — no harsh hotspot on the table, no dark shadow under the handle. On the highest setting, it outputs 300 lumens, which is genuinely enough to light a 4-person tent or a small campsite table for card games.

The USB-C charging is a quiet win. It takes about 3.5 hours to fully charge the 2000mAh battery, and you can top it off from a power bank or car charger. That's a small thing that makes a big difference when you're packing for a trip and don't want to carry three different cables.

## What Buyers Keep Pointing Out

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings isn't inflated. Common themes in positive reviews:

- *"I bought two for hurricane prep. They've held up through two storms and still charge fine."*
- *"The light quality is warm, not that harsh blue-white LED look. My kids actually sleep with it on."*
- *"The magnet on the bottom is stronger than I expected. Sticks to the trailer frame without slipping."*

That last point is worth emphasizing — the base has a built-in magnet, which makes it useful for attaching to metal surfaces in garages, sheds, or during car repairs. It's a feature that doesn't appear in the marketing photos but buyers consistently mention.

## Where It Falls Short

The battery life at full brightness is the honest weak spot. Six hours sounds fine until you're on night three of a power outage and you're rationing light. The low setting stretches it to 40+ hours, but then you're reading at roughly candlelight level.

The bottom flashlight mode is also a miss. It's 80 lumens, casts a narrow beam, and drains the battery faster than you'd expect. You'll likely use it once, realize your phone's flashlight is better, and never touch it again.

And there's no battery percentage readout — just a three-LED meter that gives you a rough idea. It's not a dealbreaker, but for a product marketed for emergencies, knowing exactly how much juice you have left matters.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a weekend camper, you want a dependable light for power outages, or you need something that hangs, stands, sticks, and charges with modern cables. It's also a strong pick for RV owners and anyone who works in garages or sheds.

**Skip it if:** You're a through-hiker counting every gram, you need a lantern that runs for a full week off-grid, or you want something that doubles as a serious task light. This is a general-purpose lantern, not a specialty tool.

## FAQ

**Is the MalloMe lantern waterproof?** 
It's IPX4 rated — splash-proof. Rain and spills are fine. Submerging it in a creek is not. The charging port is covered by a rubber flap, but don't test its limits.

**Can it charge my phone?** 
No. Despite the USB-C port, it's input-only. The battery is 2000mAh, which is about half a phone charge — MalloMe wisely didn't build in reverse charging.

**How does it compare to a propane lantern?** 
It's not as bright (300 lumens vs. 800+ for a dual-mantle propane), but it's safer, quieter, and you don't have to carry fuel. For tent use, the LED is the better call.

## The Verdict

The MalloMe Camping Lantern earns its 4.7-star rating honestly. It's well-built, thoughtfully designed, and does exactly what a portable lantern should do — provide reliable, even light without fuss. The battery life and the gimmicky flashlight mode keep it from a perfect score, but for $25-ish (check current pricing), it's one of the better values in this category.

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, versatile lantern for camping, emergencies, or garage work. **Skip it if** you need multi-day battery endurance or a serious task light.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely useful tool with a couple of minor quirks you can live with.
