You’ve priced out a frameless glass shower door, and now the hinges are staring you down. The Alise Glass Shower Door Hinges promise 90- and 180-degree swing flexibility, but do they hold up to steam, daily slams, and the occasional elbow bump? I’ve installed a set on a custom 3/8" glass panel and put them through a month of real showers. Here’s the story.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains two hinges, a full set of stainless Allen bolts, rubber washers for the glass, and an Allen wrench that’s just barely usable. No mounting template, no paper guide — just the hardware. The hinges are heavy, which is a good sign: each one weighs about 1.2 lbs and is milled from 304 stainless steel, not the zinc alloy you see on cheaper sets. The finish is a brushed satin that doesn’t show fingerprints as badly as polished chrome.

**Real-world performance**  
Mounting requires drilling into tempered glass — hire a pro if you haven’t done this before. Once installed, the swing action is smooth. The 90-degree stop is firm; the 180-degree stop is genuinely useful if you want to fold the door flat against the wall for cleaning. The rubber washers seat tightly against the glass, and I had zero leaks along the hinge line after a week of high-pressure showers. The pivot pins are stainless, not plastic, so they won’t corrode over time.

One thing that surprised me: the included Allen wrench is too short for good leverage. If you over-tighten the bolts, you can strip the soft stainless threads on the hinge body. Use a proper hex driver and torque to about 15 ft-lbs, not the “grip it and rip it” approach.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Scrolling through the 220 ratings, the 4.4-star average holds up. One verified buyer wrote: *“Fit my 3/8” glass perfectly. The 180-degree feature is great for cleaning behind the door.”* Another mentioned: *“Installation took 30 minutes once I drilled the glass. Solid feel, no wobble.”* The durability feedback is consistent — no one reporting rust or finish peeling after months of use.

**Where it falls short**  
The biggest gripe? No installation template. You’ll need to measure twice, mark the glass, and drill blind. That’s fine for a pro, but DIYers without a diamond hole saw will struggle. Also, the finish is a magnet for hard water spots — you’ll see white marks unless you wipe down after every shower. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Another nuance: the 180-degree stop works best when the hinge is mounted on the wall side. If you mount it on the glass side (reversed), the swing radius changes and the door can hit the adjacent tile. Plan your pivot direction before drilling.

**Who should buy this**  
- **DIY renovators** with existing glass panels who want to upgrade from a pivot to a full-swing door.  
- **Contractors** installing custom frameless showers where clients want the fold-flat cleaning option.  
- **Anyone with 3/8" to 1/2" tempered glass** — the hinge clamps securely without needing shims.  

Skip it if you expect a plug-and-play kit with drilling guides, or if you need a hinge that hides water spots completely.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Will these fit my 1/4" glass shower door?**  
A: No. They’re rated for 3/8" to 1/2" glass only. Thinner glass will crack under the clamping force.

**Q: Can I use these on a steam shower enclosure?**  
A: Yes, the stainless and rubber gaskets handle humidity fine. Just keep the pivot pins lubricated with silicone spray every few months.

**Q: Do they come with the glass drilling bit?**  
A: No. You’ll need a separate diamond-tipped hole saw (1/2" size) and a pilot drill.
