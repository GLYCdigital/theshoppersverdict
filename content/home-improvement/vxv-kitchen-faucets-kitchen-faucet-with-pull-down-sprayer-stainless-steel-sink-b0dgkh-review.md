**The Short Version: A $100-ish Faucet That Doesn't Embarrass Itself Next to $300 Brands**

Most kitchen faucets under $150 are a gamble. You're either getting a faucet that looks good for three months then drips, or one that's built like a tank but ugly enough to make you regret the remodel. The VXV pull-down sits in a sweet spot: it's got the features you'd expect from a Delta or Moen at half the price, and the build quality backs up the claims. That 4.4-star average across 220 ratings isn't from bots — it's from people who've actually installed this thing.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the faucet body, the pull-down spray head with a 60-inch nylon hose, a metal deck plate (for 3-hole sinks), and the supply lines. You'll need to supply your own thread seal tape — that's not included, which is mildly annoying but standard at this price point.

The unit ships fully assembled for the most part. The spray head clicks into the body with a satisfying detent, and the hose retracts smoothly. The brushed stainless finish is consistent across all visible surfaces — no two-tone mismatch like you see on cheaper faucets. As shown in the product image, the high-arc gooseneck clears a stock pot without issue, and the sprayer sits flush against the body when docked.

**Real-World Performance**

The three-function sprayer is the headline feature, and it works. The stream mode delivers a solid, aerated flow that doesn't splash back at you. The spray mode has real pressure — it'll rinse a blender pitcher clean without needing to scrub. The pause button is genuinely useful for filling a pot on the counter while you prep; no more juggling the sprayer while you grab a lid.

The 60-inch hose is the standout spec. Most budget faucets give you 48-55 inches, and that extra reach matters when you have a deep farmhouse sink or a dual-basin setup. The hose retracts with a smooth, weighted pull-back — not the violent snap you get on some cheap models.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The reviews cluster around two themes: install ease and the sprayer's build quality. One verified buyer wrote, *"Took me 45 minutes to swap out my old faucet. The braided lines are long enough to reach my shutoff valves without extra adapters."* Another noted, *"I've had Delta and Moen — this sprayer feels just as solid. The metal head doesn't wobble like my old plastic one."*

That's the recurring sentiment: people are surprised the sprayer doesn't feel cheap. The metal head and weighted hose retraction are the details that separate this from the $60 specials on Amazon.

**Where It Falls Short**

The finish is the biggest tradeoff. Brushed stainless looks great in the showroom, but it's a fingerprint and water-spot magnet. If you have hard water, you'll be wiping this down weekly. A brushed nickel finish would have been more forgiving — that's not available on this model.

The installation instructions are minimal. You get line drawings, not step-by-step photos. If you're handy, it's a non-issue. If you're a first-timer, budget an extra hour to watch a YouTube tutorial on deck plate installation and supply line routing. The braided supply lines are pre-attached, which saves a step, but the connections under the sink are tight if you have a garbage disposal in the way.

One more note: the pause button on the sprayer head doesn't hold indefinitely. After about 10 seconds of pause, it releases back to stream. Not a dealbreaker, but if you were hoping to pause the flow while you reach for a dish on the other side of the counter, it won't stay paused.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **DIY homeowners replacing a leaky builder-grade faucet** — the install is straightforward, and you'll get a solid upgrade without calling a plumber.
- **Anyone with a deep or double-basin sink** — the 60-inch hose is the differentiator here.
- **Renters or budget remodels** — you get the look and function of a premium faucet without the premium price tag.

**Skip This If**

- You need a commercial-style faucet with a spring arm — this is a residential unit, not a pro-grade tool.
- You have extremely hard water and don't want to wipe down the finish regularly.
- You want a touchless or motion-sensor model — this is fully manual.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the sprayer stay docked when you use the stream mode?**
A: Yes. The spray head locks into the body with a detent, and the stream mode comes out the main spout. You only pull the head down when you need the spray.

**Q: Will this fit a 3-hole sink?**
A: Yes, the deck plate covers the extra holes. It's a standard 3-hole setup with 8-inch centers, which covers most residential sinks.

**Q: Do the supply lines reach standard shutoff valves?**
A: Generally, yes. The braided lines are about 24 inches, which covers most under-sink setups. If your shutoffs are unusually far, you'll need extension kits.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a functional, good-looking faucet with real pull-down reach and don't want to pay Delta or Moen prices. The metal spray head and 60-inch hose make it feel like a $200+ faucet.

**Skip it if** you're a perfectionist about finishes or need a commercial-grade unit. The water-spot issue and basic instructions are real annoyances, but they don't undermine the core function.
