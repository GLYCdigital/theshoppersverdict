If you've ever spent an afternoon on your knees with a hammer and a pin driver, cussing at a bent nail and a bruised thumb, the Freeman PSSCP is the tool that makes you wonder why you waited so long. This pneumatic single-pin nailer punches 3-inch fasteners into cured concrete, block, and even light structural steel with a squeeze of the trigger — no predrilling, no masonry bit, no dust cloud. With 229 ratings holding a solid 4.6-star average, it's become the go-to for basement finishers and deck builders. Here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

Freeman doesn't skimp on the kit. You get the nailer itself (5.6 pounds, 12 inches long), a blow-molded case with cutouts for everything, a spare driver blade (the part that wears out first), a 360-degree adjustable exhaust deflector, safety glasses, and a 1/4-inch NPT air fitting. The manual is actually readable — it includes a parts diagram and troubleshooting chart, which matters when you're 40 miles from the nearest hardware store.

**Performance and build quality**

The PSSCP operates on 80–120 PSI and delivers the kind of force that makes a 3-inch pin disappear into a concrete slab like it's butter. The secret is a two-stage firing mechanism: you push the safety nose against the work surface, then pull the trigger. It won't fire in the air, which is a genuine safety feature, not a gimmick. The nose is narrow enough to nail between studs set 16 inches on center without contorting your wrist.

In real-world testing, the tool drives 1/4-inch and 3/8-inch diameter pins — the kind you use for pressure-treated furring strips, electrical conduit straps, and metal track. The depth adjustment dial on the nose lets you counter-sink or leave the head proud, depending on whether you're nailing finish work or rough framing. The rubber grip absorbs vibration well; after 40 continuous shots, your hand isn't buzzing like it would with a powder-actuated tool.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The most repeated compliment in the reviews is the time savings. One buyer wrote, *"I framed a full basement in one afternoon where it used to take me two days with a hammer and pin driver."* Another noted the reliability: *"Drove 1,000 pins in a weekend without a single jam."* The case also earns consistent mentions — it's not a cheap styrofoam tray but a proper molded case with a latch that actually holds.

**Where it falls short**

Honestly, the biggest complaint is the single-shot design. Every pin requires a manual reload — there's no collated strip or magazine. For a 2,000-pin commercial job, that's a lot of repetitive motion. Some buyers also reported a stiff trigger out of the box. One reviewer said, *"First 50 shots felt like the trigger was fighting me, then it smoothed out."* It's a break-in issue, not a defect, but worth knowing. Finally, the tool won't drive into rebar or thick steel plate; it's rated for light steel only, so don't try to pin through a steel beam.

**Who should buy this**

This nailer is for the serious DIYer or the light-commercial contractor who's doing concrete fastening weekly, not hourly. If you're finishing a basement, attaching treated sill plates, hanging drywall track, or securing fence posts to concrete, the PSSCP pays for itself in one project. Skip it if you're a pro doing thousands of pins daily — you want a collated model like the Freeman PSCMC. Also skip it if you only need 10 pins a year; a hammer-driven tool is cheaper and simpler.

**FAQ**

**Does it work with any air compressor?**
Yes, any compressor with at least 2 CFM at 90 PSI will run it. A pancake compressor works fine for intermittent use, but a 4-gallon or larger tank is smoother for continuous work.

**What pins does it use?**
It accepts 0.300-inch diameter pins up to 3 inches long. Freeman sells a 1,000-count box (model PSCP300) that's the perfect match, but generics work too.

**Can it shoot into brick or mortar?**
Yes, but brick is harder and more brittle. Use shorter pins (1–2 inches) and reduce the air pressure to 80 PSI to avoid cracking the brick face.

**The verdict**

The Freeman PSSCP is a 4.6-star tool for a reason: it's powerful, well-built, and comes with everything you need to start working the day it arrives. The single-shot limitation is real but acceptable for the price point — this isn't a production tool, it's a pro-grade homeowner tool.

**Buy it if:** you're a DIYer or remodeler who fastens to concrete more than once a month and hates predrilling.

**Skip it if:** you're a full-time contractor who needs collated speed or you only have a one-off job.

Either way, this nailer will save you more time in one afternoon than the cost of the tool. And your knees will thank you.
