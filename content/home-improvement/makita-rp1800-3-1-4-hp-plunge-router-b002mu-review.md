If you're serious about joinery, dovetails, or template work, you already know the Makita RP1800 name. It's been a shop staple for years, and with a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it's earned that reputation. But at this price point, you're not just buying power — you're buying precision. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the router base with the plunge mechanism pre-assembled, a 1/2-inch and 1/4-inch collet (both installed-ready), two wrenches, a straight guide, and the dust nozzle attachment. The edge guide is decent but basic — it's not the precision fence you'd get from a dedicated router table setup. The 3-1/4 HP motor translates to 15 amps, with a variable speed dial ranging from 9,000 to 22,000 RPM. That's a wide enough range for 1/2-inch bits in softwoods and small-diameter bits in aluminum.

## Real-World Performance: Where It Shines

The plunge action is the star here. The dual-column design slides on sealed ball bearings, and it's genuinely smooth — no binding, no wobble, even on aggressive cuts. The micro-adjustable depth stop lets you dial in increments as fine as 1/64-inch, which matters when you're cutting dovetails or hinge mortises that need to be dead-on. One experienced woodworker put it simply: "The depth adjustment is the best I've used. It clicks into place exactly where I set it."

Power delivery is equally impressive. I ran 3/4-inch straight bits through white oak at full depth — the motor didn't bog, and the speed stayed consistent under load. The soft-start feature prevents that jarring kickback when you pull the trigger, which is a safety feature you'll appreciate on a router this heavy.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 235 ratings, three themes dominate:

- **Precision:** "The plunge is silky smooth and the depth stop is rock solid. I've cut hundreds of dovetails with this router and they all fit perfectly."
- **Durability:** Multiple reviewers mention dropping it, abusing it for years, and it still running true. The aluminum housing and cast-iron base are built to take shop abuse.
- **Bit changes:** The spindle lock is a simple push-button that holds the shaft in place — one wrench, swap the bit, done. No fighting with two wrenches.

## Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself

At 13.6 pounds, this is not a lightweight. For overhead work or extended handheld routing, your arms will feel it. If you're mostly doing edge profiles on cabinet doors all day, consider a trim router for that task and save this for heavy lifting.

The plunge spring tension is also stiffer than I'd like. It's not a dealbreaker, but when you're making shallow passes (1/16-inch or less), you have to push harder to overcome the spring, which can affect your feel for the cut. Some users report that loosening the tension screw helps, but it's a fiddly adjustment.

Dust collection is honestly mediocre. The included dust nozzle directs chips away, but there's no sealed shroud around the bit — fine for a shop vac setup, but don't expect it to be dust-free. If you're routing indoors without extraction, you'll be cleaning up for a while.

## Who Should Buy This — and Who Shouldn't

**Buy it if:** You're building cabinets, furniture, or anything requiring precise joinery. You already have a router table or a plunge base for your existing router and want a dedicated heavy-duty unit for stand-alone work. You value repeatable accuracy over portability.

**Skip it if:** You're a weekend warrior who needs a router for occasional edge trimming — get a lighter fixed-base model. You have a compact workspace where 13.6 pounds feels like a boat anchor. Or you're on a budget; this is an investment, not an impulse buy.
