Let’s cut through the noise: the VIANIS Outdoor Lamp Post Light V86013P is a solid, no-nonsense fixture for homeowners who want curb appeal without paying landscape-lighting boutique prices. With 215 ratings averaging 4.3 out of 5, it’s clearly doing something right. But it’s not perfect, and I’ll tell you exactly where it stumbles.

## What you actually get

The box contains the lamp post head assembly, a mounting plate, and hardware. That’s it — no post. You’re buying the fixture head that mounts on top of a standard 3-inch post (the mounting plate fits posts up to about 3 inches in diameter). You’ll also need to supply your own bulb, but that’s actually a feature: the standard E26 base means you can drop in an LED, halogen, or even a smart bulb if you’re feeling fancy.

As shown in the product image, the fixture sports a traditional lantern silhouette with clear glass panels and a matte black finish. It’s a classic look that won’t fight your home’s architecture — whether you’ve got a craftsman bungalow or a modern farmhouse.

## Build quality and real-world performance

Here’s where the VIANIS earns its keep. The housing is cast aluminum, not the stamped sheet metal you find on budget fixtures. That matters. Cast aluminum resists corrosion and handles temperature swings without warping. The finish is baked on, and buyers consistently report it holds up through rain, snow, and brutal summer sun without peeling or fading.

One buyer noted: *"Installed in October, and it’s been through a Michigan winter and spring storms. No rust, no fading, glass still clear."* That tracks with the materials — this is built to last a decade, not a season.

The built-in dusk-to-dawn photocell is a genuine convenience. It clicks on as the light fades and shuts off at sunrise. No timers, no smart home hub, no fiddling. One reviewer summed it up: *"Set it and forget it. I haven’t touched it since the day I installed it."*

Light output depends entirely on your bulb. With a standard 60-watt equivalent LED, you get solid ambient illumination for a walkway or front yard. It’s not a floodlight — it’s meant to define space and add warmth, not blast a security beam.

## Where it falls short

Let me be direct about the downsides.

**The photocell sensor placement is the biggest gripe.** It sits on the side of the fixture, which means it can be triggered by reflections off a white garage wall or a nearby porch light. If your post is close to the house, you may find the light staying on longer than it should. A few reviewers reported this exact issue. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s worth knowing before you mount it in a tight spot.

**Installation is not plug-and-play.** You’ll need basic wiring knowledge — matching wires, grounding, and mounting the plate level. The instructions are sparse. If you’re not comfortable with electrical work, budget for an electrician. This is not a "hang it and hope" product.

**The clear glass is a bug magnet.** At night, the light draws every moth within a block, and the clear panels mean you’ll see the aftermath. You’ll be cleaning the inside of the glass more often than you’d like. If that bothers you, look for a fixture with seeded or frosted glass.

## Who should buy this

- **The DIY homeowner** who wants a quality post light without paying a landscaper’s markup. If you’ve done basic electrical work before, this is a manageable weekend project.
- **The curb-appeal seeker** replacing a tired, rusted fixture. The classic lantern design upgrades your front yard instantly.
- **The "set it and forget it" type** who wants dusk-to-dawn lighting without smart home complexity.

**Skip it if** you need a security-grade floodlight, if your post is in a spot where the sensor will get confused, or if you want zero maintenance — the clear glass will need periodic cleaning.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this come with a post?**
No. You’re buying the fixture head and mounting hardware. It fits standard 3-inch posts, but you’ll need to supply the post itself.

**Q: What bulb should I use?**
Any E26 base bulb. An LED in the 800-1100 lumen range with a warm white (2700-3000K) color temperature gives the best classic look. Avoid CFLs — they can be finicky with photocell sensors.

**Q: Can I use it with a dimmer switch?**
No. The photocell sensor controls the light, and it’s not designed for dimmer compatibility. If you want dimming, this isn’t the fixture.
