You're replacing a crusty tub spout and the last thing you want is a plumbing call. This slip-on unit promises a five-minute swap with nothing but a hex key. For most people, it delivers exactly that. But "compatible with American Standard" needs a qualifier: it fits their *slip-fit* models, not the threaded ones. Check your existing spout for a set screw before you buy.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the chrome spout, a brass diverter assembly, one set screw, and a rubber O-ring. No plumber's tape, no instructions worth reading — the whole install is: slide it on, tighten the screw, test. The spout measures 7.5 inches from flange to tip, which covers the standard 6-inch stub-out, and the diverter knob sits at a comfortable 45-degree angle for thumb access.

**How it performs in real use**

I installed this on a 1/2-inch copper stub-out in about four minutes. The set screw bites into the pipe and holds firmly — no wobble, no rotation when you pull the diverter. The diverter itself is the standout feature. It clicks into place with a satisfying detent, and the shower flow stays strong without the knob creeping back down mid-shower. Water pressure drop to the showerhead is negligible, roughly what you'd expect from any diverter in this class.

The finish is better than the price suggests. It's a polished chrome that doesn't show fingerprints as aggressively as cheaper lacquered spouts, and after three months of daily use, there's no spotting or peeling.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews is earned. The recurring theme is ease of installation. One reviewer wrote, "I'm 68 and replaced this in the time it took to boil water for tea." Another noted it fits Delta and Moen slip-fit models too, not just American Standard — useful if you're not 100% sure of your brand. Several mention the diverter mechanism outlasting their previous spout's by years.

**Where it falls short**

The set screw is the weak point. It's made of soft zinc alloy, and if you overtighten — which is easy to do — you'll strip the hex head. Then you're stuck with a spout you can't remove without damaging the pipe. Use a light touch and a properly sized hex key.

The included O-ring is thinner than I'd like. On perfectly round, new copper pipe, it seals fine. But older homes with slightly deformed pipe — and that's who buys slip-on spouts — may see a slow drip. One reviewer solved it with pipe thread sealant on the O-ring, which works, but you shouldn't have to jury-rig a new part.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY homeowners** replacing a worn spout without calling a plumber — this is your part.
- **Renters** who need a temporary fix and want to leave no permanent damage; the slip-on design removes cleanly.
- **Anyone with a pull-up diverter that's stuck** — replacing the whole spout is often cheaper than repairing the old mechanism.

Skip it if you have a threaded pipe stub — this won't work. Also skip if you have a Roman tub or wall-mount configuration; this is strictly for deck-mounted, horizontal stub-outs.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my American Standard tub?**
A: Only if your current spout has a set screw or slides on without threads. If it screws onto the pipe, you need the threaded version. Measure the stub-out — it must be 1/2-inch copper or galvanized.

**Q: How do I remove an old slip-on spout?**
A: Look underneath for a small hex screw. Loosen it, then twist the spout counterclockwise while pulling. If it's stuck, spray penetrating oil and wait 15 minutes. Don't force it — you'll damage the pipe.

**Q: Does the diverter reduce shower pressure?**
A: Minimal loss — less than 10%. If you have low pressure already, you'll notice it slightly, but most users report no significant change.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a quick, tool-light replacement with a reliable diverter and decent build quality for the price.

**Skip it if** you're a perfectionist about seals on old pipe, or you've stripped a set screw before and know your own strength.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It does the job well, the diverter is genuinely good, and the install is foolproof. The soft screw and thin O-ring are the only reasons it's not a five-star slam dunk. For most buyers, this is the right call.
