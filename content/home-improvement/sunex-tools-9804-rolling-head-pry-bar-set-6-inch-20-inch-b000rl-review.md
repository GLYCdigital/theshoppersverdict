# SUNEX TOOLS 9804 Rolling Head Pry Bar Set: The 20-Inch Workhorse You Didn't Know You Needed  

If you've ever fought a stubborn nail with a flimsy pry bar that bends or slips, you'll appreciate what this set does differently. The SUNEX TOOLS 9804 isn't just a bunch of steel bars tossed in a pouch — it's a thoughtfully designed system that solves the most annoying part of demolition work: keeping the tip where you want it.  

## What You Actually Get  

The set includes six bars: 6", 10", 12", 16", 18", and 20". That's not just length variety — each bar has a different tip shape and angle, so you're covered for everything from popping baseboards to tearing apart pallets. The 20-inch bar is the standout: it's got the leverage to handle framing nails and the reach to work behind drywall without wrecking your knuckles.  

The rolling head is the real innovation. Instead of a fixed head that forces you to angle the whole bar, the head rotates independently. That means you can keep the handle flat against the surface while the tip digs in at the perfect angle. It sounds minor, but it's a game-changer when you're working against a stud or in a tight corner.  

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance  

These bars are made from heat-treated alloy steel — not the cheap stamped stuff you find at the big-box store. The black oxide finish isn't just for looks; it adds corrosion resistance and reduces glare. In practice, the bars feel solid in hand, with no flex even on the 20-inch model when you're putting serious weight on it.  

I've used these for everything from pulling nails out of pressure-treated lumber to prying off old siding. The tips hold their edge well — no mushrooming after a month of regular use. The rolling head stays smooth, too, which is important because a gritty head will bind up and defeat the whole purpose.  

One thing that impressed me: the 16-inch bar has a notch that's actually useful for pulling nails without marring the surface. It's not just a decorative cutout — it grips the nail shank cleanly.

## Where It Falls Short  

Let's be honest about the downsides. The 6-inch bar lacks a nail puller notch, which is annoying if you do fine trim work — you'll need a dedicated mini pry bar for that. The handles are bare steel, not rubberized. In cold weather or with sweaty gloves, grip becomes an issue. A quick wrap of electric tape or a set of grip sleeves solves it, but it's a minor design oversight.  

Also, there's no storage pouch or roll included. For a set this size, that's a miss — you'll need to find your own way to keep them organized in a toolbox or drawer.
