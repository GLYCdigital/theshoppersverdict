A utility sink is one of those purchases you don't think about until you're scrubbing paint rollers in the kitchen sink or washing dog gear in the bathtub. This stainless steel freestanding model with pre-plumbed hot and cold water pipes is aimed squarely at garages, basements, and workshops — and for the most part, it delivers.

**What You Actually Get**

The unit arrives mostly assembled. The basin, legs, and cross-bracing are already put together; you attach the four legs, install the faucet (included), and connect the supply lines that are already threaded through the frame. The stainless basin measures roughly 22 x 18 inches with a 10-inch depth — big enough for a five-gallon bucket, small enough to tuck into a corner.

The standout feature is the integrated water supply. Instead of buying separate angle stops and supply hoses, this sink comes with copper pipes pre-soldered into the frame, terminating in standard 1/2-inch NPT threads. You connect your home's plumbing directly to those threads. It's a thoughtful design that cuts installation time significantly.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The 16-gauge stainless basin is the highlight. It doesn't flex when you lean on it, and the brushed finish hides water spots and scratches far better than cheaper 20-gauge alternatives. The rolled front edge is comfortable to lean against, and the integrated backsplash — about 4 inches tall — keeps splashes off your drywall.

Buyers consistently mention how solid the sink feels once assembled. As one verified purchaser put it: "I've had mine in the garage for eight months. Washed engine parts, paint brushes, and muddy boots in it. The basin still looks new, and the frame hasn't wobbled once."

But there's a recurring weak point: the drain kit. Multiple reviewers noted the plastic drain assembly feels cheap and can crack if overtightened. One buyer wrote, "The sink itself is great, but the drain strainer is junk. I replaced it with a metal one from the hardware store for $8." Budget for that swap upfront.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the drain, the legs have no adjustable feet. On a perfectly level floor, you're fine. In most garages — which have sloped floors for water runoff — you'll need shims or rubber pads under one or two legs to stop rocking. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's an extra step that cheaper sinks with leveling feet don't require.

The faucet is functional but basic. It's a single-handle chrome model with a swivel spout. Water pressure is fine, but don't expect premium feel — the handle has a slightly loose action. If you have hard water, plan on descaling the aerator every few months.

**Who Should Buy This**

This sink is ideal for homeowners who need a dedicated workhorse sink and want hot water without running a separate tank. The pre-plumbed design makes it a strong choice for DIYers comfortable with basic copper or PEX connections. It's also well-suited for hobbyists who regularly clean tools, fishing gear, or gardening equipment and want to keep that mess out of the house.

Skip it if you need a portable unit — this needs a permanent water connection. Also skip it if you're on a tight budget; you can find cheaper plastic utility sinks, but they won't hold up to heavy use the way stainless steel does.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Do I need a plumber to install this?**
If you have basic plumbing skills and access to existing 1/2-inch supply lines with shut-off valves, you can install this yourself in under an hour. If you're tapping into new lines or your home has non-standard plumbing, hire a pro.

**Does the sink come with the faucet?**
Yes, a single-handle faucet is included, along with supply lines already connected to the sink's frame. You just hook the other end to your home's water supply.

**Can this be used outdoors?**
The stainless basin can handle outdoor conditions, but the frame is not powder-coated for extreme weather. It's best used in a covered, semi-enclosed space like a garage or barn.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a heavy-duty utility sink with hot and cold water that's genuinely ready to connect. The 16-gauge basin, included faucet, and pre-plumbed supply lines make this a solid value for the price.

**Skip it if** you're not comfortable with basic plumbing work, or if you expect everything to be perfect out of the box — you'll want to budget for a replacement drain kit and possibly shims.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-built workhorse with a couple of cheap components that are easy and inexpensive to upgrade.
