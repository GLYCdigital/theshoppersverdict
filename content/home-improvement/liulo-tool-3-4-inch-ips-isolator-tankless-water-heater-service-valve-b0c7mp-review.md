Let me be blunt: most tankless water heater service valves are overpriced brass paperweights. The LIULO TOOL 3/4-inch IPS Isolator set is different — it actually does what the marketing claims, and it does it without requiring a plumbing degree.

## What You Actually Get

The kit ships with two full-port 3/4-inch IPS isolation valves (one for hot, one for cold), each with a 1/4-turn ball valve, integrated drain port, and a threaded cap. The bodies are lead-free brass — you can verify the "LF" stamp right on the casting. Handles are color-coded red and blue, which sounds gimmicky until you're contorted under a sink at 6 AM trying to remember which line is which.

Also in the box: mounting screws and plastic anchors. Use them at your own risk — more on that below.

## Real-World Performance

I installed these on a Rinnai RU199iN and immediately noticed the difference versus the cheap ball valves they replaced. The full-port design matters: at 6 GPM (typical for two simultaneous showers), there's zero measurable pressure drop. The 1/4-turn action is crisp — a quarter rotation goes from fully open to fully closed with a positive stop. No quarter-turn slop like you get with cheaper valves.

The drain ports are where this kit earns its keep. Annual descaling with vinegar or citric acid used to mean disconnecting hard lines and praying you didn't cross-thread the compression fittings. With these, you screw in a standard garden hose adapter, open the port, and run the pump. The 1/4-turn port valve holds tight — no weeping, no drips, even at 80 PSI line pressure.

I tested the shutoff under load: closed the cold-side valve while the unit was running, and it sealed immediately with no hammering. The PTFE seat handles the 140°F+ water temps without deforming.

## Where It Falls Short

The mounting screws are genuinely bad. They're soft zinc-plated steel that will strip if you look at them wrong. Budget five minutes and two bucks for stainless #8 x 1-inch screws and wall anchors.

The lack of an integrated pressure relief port is a legitimate design gap. If your jurisdiction requires a T&P relief valve on the service line (many do), you'll need to add a tee fitting — which partially defeats the purpose of a kit that's supposed to simplify your plumbing.

Also worth noting: these are IPS (iron pipe size) threaded connections, not PEX or compression. If you're on PEX, you'll need adapter fittings, which adds cost and potential leak points.

## Who Should Buy This

**DIY homeowners** with basic plumbing skills will find these transformative for annual maintenance. **Plumbers** looking for a reliable service valve that won't fail mid-job will appreciate the build quality. **Anyone with a tankless unit over 3 years old** who's never flushed it — this kit makes the task so easy there's no excuse.

**Skip it if** you need PEX connections, require a built-in pressure relief port, or you're on a tight budget — generic 3/4-inch ball valves cost half as much if you're not doing regular descaling.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my Navien/Noritz/Rinnai?**

**Q: Can I descale without removing the valves?**

**Q: Are these safe for potable water?**
A: Yes. The lead-free brass meets NSF 61 standards, and the handles indicate hot/cold clearly to prevent cross-connection mistakes.
