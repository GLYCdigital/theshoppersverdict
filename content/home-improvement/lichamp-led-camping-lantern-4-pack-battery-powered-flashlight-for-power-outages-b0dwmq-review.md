**The 4-Pack Answer to Every Blackout, Campout, and Garage Project**

Here's the thing about emergency lighting: you never realize how few lanterns you own until the power goes out at 10 PM and you're fumbling through a drawer of dead flashlights. The Lichamp LED Camping Lantern 4-Pack solves that problem permanently — you get four working lights for roughly the price of one premium single lantern. That math alone makes this worth a serious look.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains four identical lanterns, each measuring about 7 inches tall with a 4-inch diameter base. They're built from a matte-finish ABS plastic that feels denser than the cheap stuff you'll find at dollar stores. Each unit has a collapsible metal hook on top — fold it up for hanging from a tent ridgepole or tree branch, fold it down to create a flush surface for tabletop use. The base is flat and stable, no wobble on uneven ground.

Each lantern takes 3 D-cell batteries. That's 12 D-cells for the full set — a real consideration if you're buying batteries fresh. I'd recommend grabbing a bulk pack on your next Amazon order because these things chew through power only if you leave them on high, but more on that in a moment.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The light itself comes from a ring of 24 LEDs arranged around the top of the lantern body, which creates a 360-degree glow rather than a directional beam. This is the right design choice for ambient lighting — you're not trying to spot a deer, you're trying to see the whole tent or garage bay.

Brightness is controlled by a simple push-button switch on the side: one press for high (300 lumens), another for low (about 100 lumens), a third for the flashing emergency mode, and a fourth to shut it off. The button has a satisfying click and is easy to operate even with thick gloves — a detail that matters when you're setting up camp in cold weather.

Runtime is where this thing earns its keep. On low mode, Lichamp claims up to 100 hours from fresh D-cells. That's four straight days of continuous light. On high, you're looking at roughly 15-20 hours, which is still respectable for a weekend trip. The tradeoff is clear: use low for reading and hanging out, save high for tasks that need real brightness.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. Twelve D-cell batteries is a real upfront cost — expect to spend $15-20 on a bulk pack if you don't already have them. There's no rechargeable version, so you're committed to disposable batteries long-term. If you're a frequent camper who'd rather own one premium rechargeable lantern, this isn't that product.

The 300-lumen high mode is also modest by modern standards. It's plenty for a tent, a power outage, or lighting up a workbench, but it won't replace a dedicated work light for detailed projects. And the color temperature is a cool white — great for visibility, less cozy for reading by than a warm amber glow.

**Who Should Buy This**

This 4-pack is perfect for three specific buyers: the homeowner who wants a dedicated emergency light for every bedroom and the basement; the weekend camper who'd rather scatter four lanterns around camp than carry one expensive light; and anyone who's tired of buying single lanterns that fail after a season. If you need one ultra-bright light for spotlight tasks, look elsewhere. If you need dependable ambient light in multiple places at once, this is your set.

**FAQ**

*Q: How long do the batteries actually last?*
A: On low mode, expect 80-100 hours from quality alkaline D-cells. On high, figure 15-20 hours. In cold weather (below freezing), cut those numbers by about 25%.

*Q: Can I use rechargeable D-cells?*
A: Yes. Standard NiMH rechargeable D-cells work fine, though runtime will be shorter than alkaline cells — typically 60-70% of the stated maximum.

*Q: Are these waterproof?*
A: They're weather-resistant with a rubber gasket around the battery compartment, but not submersible. Light rain is fine; dropping one in a lake is not.
