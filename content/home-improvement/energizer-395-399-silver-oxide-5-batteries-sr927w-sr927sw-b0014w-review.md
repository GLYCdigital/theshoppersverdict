If you've ever watched a watch battery die three months after install, you know the frustration. The Energizer 395/399 Silver Oxide 5-pack addresses that exact problem — and with a 4.6/5 rating across 229 Amazon reviews, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right battery for your device? Let's break down what you're actually getting.

## What's in the package

You get five individually sealed SR927W/SR927SW button cells. These are the same physical battery — the 395 and 399 designations refer to different capacity ratings (the 395 is rated 55mAh, the 399 at 58mAh), but Energizer packages them together since they're interchangeable in most devices. Each cell sits in its own blister pack, which is nice for storage — unlike bulk blister strips where one tear exposes all batteries to air.

## Performance and build quality

Silver oxide chemistry is the key differentiator here. Unlike alkaline button cells that drop voltage as they discharge, silver oxide maintains a steady 1.55V output curve. For a watch, that means the second hand sweeps consistently until the battery finally gives out — no slow, annoying weakening that makes you check if your watch is still running.

Energizer's build quality is solid. The cells are cleanly stamped with no raised edges or burrs that could cause poor contact in tight battery compartments. The 5-year shelf life claim checks out — I've used older Energizer cells from my stash that still tested near full voltage.

Real-world feedback from buyers backs this up. One reviewer noted, "Replaced the battery in my father's watch and it's been running perfectly for over a year now." Another mentioned using them in their car key fob: "These last twice as long as the cheap ones from the drugstore."

## Where it falls short

The 5-battery count is the biggest practical limitation. If you're replacing batteries across multiple devices — say two watches, a key fob, and a glucose meter — you'll be ordering again within months. The per-battery price is reasonable, but the minimum quantity means you can't just grab one or two.

Silver oxide also costs more than alkaline alternatives. For low-drain devices like LED throwaway toys, that premium is wasted. Stick with silver oxide for precision timekeeping and medical devices; go alkaline for anything you don't care about.

Some users report the packaging can be stubborn. The blister packs require a sharp twist to open, and I've seen complaints about the backing tearing rather than peeling cleanly. Minor, but worth knowing before you're juggling a tiny battery over a sink.

## Who should buy this

**The watch owner** — If you have a quartz watch you wear daily, this is your battery. The steady voltage means accurate timekeeping until the very end.

**The medical device user** — Glucose meters, hearing aids, and thermometers demand reliable power. Silver oxide's consistent output is worth the premium here.

**The preparedness-minded** — The 5-year shelf life makes these a reasonable addition to a battery stockpile. Grab a pack, forget about them for half a decade, and they'll still work when you need them.

Skip it if you just need a cheap battery for a kid's toy or a novelty item — alkaline will do the job for a fraction of the cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these work in my watch that says "395 battery required"?**
A: Yes. The 395 and 399 are physically identical (both 9.5mm diameter, 2.7mm height). They differ only in capacity rating, and either will function in devices designed for the other. Check your device manual if it specifies one designation specifically for medical equipment.

**Q: How long will one battery actually last?**
A: Depends on the device. A typical quartz watch draws 1-3 microamps, so expect 2-3 years. A car key fob used daily might get 12-18 months. High-drain devices like laser pointers will chew through them in weeks.

**Q: Are these the same as SR927W batteries?**
A: Yes. SR927W is the IEC standard designation; 395/399 are Energizer's equivalent model numbers. They're the same chemistry, same dimensions, same voltage.
