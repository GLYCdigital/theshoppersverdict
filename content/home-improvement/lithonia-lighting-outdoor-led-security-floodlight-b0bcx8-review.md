Let's be blunt: most residential floodlights are junk. Thin aluminum that bends in your hand, "1200 lumens" that somehow illuminate less than a flashlight, and motion sensors that either ignore your porch pirate or trigger on every raccoon in the county. The Lithonia Lighting Outdoor LED Security Floodlight — the model shown in the product image with twin adjustable heads — tries to be the exception. For the most part, it succeeds.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the assembled fixture with two independently adjustable LED heads, a mounting bracket, wire nuts, and mounting screws. No bulbs — you don't need them, the LEDs are integrated and rated for roughly 50,000 hours. That's about 11 years at 12 hours per night. The housing is die-cast aluminum with a tempered glass lens, and it's gasketed against moisture. This is the same build quality Lithonia sells to commercial contractors, and it shows.

**Performance and real-world use**

Here's where this thing separates from the $30 specials at the big-box store. At 3000+ lumens with a 5000K daylight color temperature, this floodlight doesn't just "light up" your driveway — it makes it look like a football stadium. One reviewer put it plainly: *"This thing is BRIGHT. My neighbor asked if I installed a streetlight."* That's the consensus across the 220 ratings. People consistently describe the output as commercial-grade, and they're right.

The motion sensor is the weak point. It works — it detects motion up to 70 feet away and the heads swivel so you can aim them independently. But the detection zone is a fixed 180 degrees. You cannot adjust sensitivity or narrow the field. If your fixture faces a street with regular traffic, every passing car will trigger it. Several buyers noted this exact issue: *"Great light, but the sensor picks up cars from the road and it turns on constantly."* You can set it to stay on for 1, 5, or 10 minutes after triggering, but you can't stop the false triggers without repositioning the whole unit.

The other gap: no photocell. This is a motion-only fixture unless you leave the switch on permanently, which defeats the purpose of a security light. If you want dusk-to-dawn operation, you'll need to pair it with an external timer or buy a different model.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the sensor limitations, installation is a real project. This is hardwired, requires a junction box, and you'll need basic electrical knowledge — or an electrician. If you're replacing an existing floodlight, it's a straightforward swap. If you're starting from scratch, budget for professional installation.

The integrated LEDs also mean no replaceable bulbs. When they eventually die in a decade, the whole fixture goes. That's industry-standard for modern LED fixtures, but it's worth knowing going in.

**Who should buy this**

- **Homeowners with a real security concern**: If you want your backyard or driveway properly lit, this delivers output that cheap fixtures can't touch.
- **DIYers comfortable with wiring**: The install is manageable if you've done basic electrical work before.
- **Anyone replacing a failing floodlight**: The mounting pattern is standard, so it's a direct swap.

Skip it if you're a renter, if your fixture faces a busy street (the sensor will drive you insane), or if you specifically need dusk-to-dawn automatic operation.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with a standard light switch?**
Yes. It's hardwired into your existing junction box and controlled by whatever switch is already on that circuit. The motion sensor handles activation, but you'll need the switch in the "on" position.

**Q: How bright is 3000 lumens, really?**
Bright enough to clearly illuminate a two-car driveway and the surrounding yard. For comparison, a typical 60W incandescent produces about 800 lumens. This is roughly four of those, focused into two adjustable heads.

**Q: Will it survive rain and snow?**
Yes. The housing is IP65-rated — that means fully dust-tight and protected against water jets. The gasketed lens and aluminum housing are built for outdoor exposure.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want genuine, commercial-grade illumination for your property and don't mind a hardwired install with a slightly inflexible motion sensor.

**Skip it if** you need a plug-in fixture, you want dusk-to-dawn functionality, or your mounting location faces a road with regular traffic.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The output and build quality are genuinely excellent. The sensor limitations and hardwired-only design keep it from being perfect, but for the right buyer, this is a set-and-forget security solution that will outlast three cheap fixtures.
