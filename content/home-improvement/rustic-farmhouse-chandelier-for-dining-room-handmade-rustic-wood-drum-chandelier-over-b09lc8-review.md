If you've been scrolling past the same brushed-nickel drum shades and generic glass globes, this handmade rustic chandelier is the first thing that actually looks different. And not in a gimmicky way — it's a legit wood drum fixture that brings real texture to a dining room.

With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. Here's what you need to know before you buy.

## What you actually get

The package includes the chandelier assembly, mounting hardware, and a hanging kit with adjustable rods. The fixture measures roughly 24 inches in diameter and 10 inches tall — substantial enough to anchor a standard 6-8 person dining table without overwhelming the room.

The construction is where this thing earns its keep. It's real wood, not veneer over MDF. The slats are individually placed around the drum, and you can see the natural grain variation in the finish. As shown in the product image, the Edison-style bulbs visible through the slats create that warm, lodge-like glow that farmhouse styling is chasing.

## Performance and real-world use

Light output is warm and diffused. The drum design softens the bulbs so you don't get harsh spots on your table settings. It takes five E12 candelabra-base bulbs (not included, which is a minor annoyance).

Installation is a two-person job, mostly because of the weight. At around 20 pounds, this isn't a flimsy piece. The instructions are straightforward, but you'll want a helper to hold the fixture while you wire it.

One thing buyers consistently mention: the finish is darker in person than in photos. One reviewer put it plainly: *"The wood is richer and more brown than the light oak color in the pictures. I was initially surprised, but it actually looks better in my space."* Another said, *"Quality is excellent. Very heavy and well-made. The rustic look is exactly what I wanted."*

## Where it comes up short

The bulb situation is the biggest friction point. E12 bulbs are easy to accidentally swap for the more common E26 medium base. Read the specs carefully before ordering bulbs. Also, there's no dimmer built in — you'll need dimmable LED bulbs and a compatible switch if that matters to you.

The darker finish is worth repeating. If you're matching a specific light oak or whitewashed palette, this may not be your fixture. It leans toward walnut/chestnut tones.

A few buyers noted the slats collect dust between them. It's not a dealbreaker, but a microfiber duster will become your friend.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if** you're furnishing a farmhouse, cabin, or transitional dining room and want a genuine wood statement piece without paying custom artisan pricing. It also works well over kitchen islands and in entryways with high ceilings, since the adjustable rods give you flexibility.

**Skip it if** you need precise finish matching, prefer modern minimalist styles, or want plug-and-play installation without reading bulb specs carefully.

## FAQ

**Q: What bulbs does this chandelier require?**
A: Five E12 candelabra-base bulbs. The fixture doesn't include them. Most buyers use LED Edison-style bulbs for that vintage amber glow, but any E12 bulb works.

**Q: Can this chandelier be installed on a sloped or vaulted ceiling?**
A: Yes, the mounting bracket accommodates sloped ceilings up to roughly 45 degrees. The hanging rods adjust to handle the angle.

**Q: Is it compatible with a dimmer switch?**
A: The fixture itself is dimmer-compatible, but you need dimmable LED bulbs and a compatible dimmer switch. Standard non-dimmable bulbs will flicker.
