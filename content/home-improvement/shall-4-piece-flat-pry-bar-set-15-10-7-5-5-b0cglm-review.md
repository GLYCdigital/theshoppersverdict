Let's be honest: most pry bar sets under $25 are junk. They bend on the first stubborn nail, the tips snap off, and you end up throwing them in a drawer next to the bent screwdrivers. The SHALL 4-piece set, at $20.49, isn't that set — but it's not a $60 pro-grade kit either. Here's where it actually lands after real-world use.

## What You Actually Get

Four flat pry bars: 15", 10", 7.5", and 5.5". All are made from heat-treated chrome vanadium steel with a black oxide finish to resist rust. The two larger bars have the standard flattened, angled tip for prying; the smaller ones work better as scrapers, gasket removers, or trim lifters. The 15" bar includes a magnetic tip — a genuinely useful touch that holds nails and screws so they don't scatter across your floor. As shown in the product image, the set comes in a clear plastic storage sleeve, but there's no wall hanger or pouch. Plan your own storage.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Struggles

The flat design is the standout feature. Unlike a curved crowbar, these slide under baseboards, door casings, and pallet boards with minimal surface damage. I used the 10" bar to pull hundreds of finish nails from reclaimed lumber, and the tip held its edge without bending. The 15" bar handled prying apart nailed 2x4s without flexing — that's genuinely impressive at this price point.

The magnetic tip on the 15" bar works, but it's not a strong magnet. It holds a nail or screw, not a handful of them. The smaller bars are excellent for scraping paint, removing caulk, or popping off electrical outlet covers without gouging the drywall.

The dipped rubber grips are comfortable for short jobs, but they're not textured or contoured. After 20 minutes of heavy prying, they get slick. Wear gloves for demo work.

## Where It Falls Short

First, no strike cap. If you need to hammer a pry bar under a stubborn board, these aren't the right tools — the handles will mushroom and deform. Use a dedicated wrecking bar for that.

Second, the black oxide finish isn't a coating that lasts forever. It wears off at the tips with heavy use, which is normal but means you'll need to oil them occasionally to prevent rust.

Third, the smallest 5.5" bar is less useful than it sounds. It's a bit too thick for fine electronics work and a bit too short for real prying. It's fine, but it's not the standout in the set.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners** doing trim, flooring, or furniture repairs — the 15" and 10" bars will handle 90% of your needs
- **Flooring installers** who need to pull up old planks and nails without destroying subfloor
- **Pallet upcyclers** who want a flat bar that won't chew up wood

Skip it if you need a heavy demolition bar for framing tear-outs, or if you want a strike cap for hammering.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these bend under heavy use?**
A: The 15" and 10" bars are heat-treated chrome vanadium steel and held up to prying apart nailed lumber in testing. The 7.5" and 5.5" are thinner, so use them for lighter work like scraping and trim removal.

**Q: Can I hammer on these?**
A: No. There's no strike cap. Tapping lightly is fine, but repeated hammering will deform the handle end.

**Q: Are the magnetic tips strong enough to hold screws?**
A: Yes, for one or two at a time. It's a convenience feature, not a magnetic tray replacement.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, affordable pry bar set that handles trim, flooring, and light demolition without breaking the bank. **Skip it if** you're a pro doing daily heavy framing work — you'll want a strike-cap wrecking bar.
