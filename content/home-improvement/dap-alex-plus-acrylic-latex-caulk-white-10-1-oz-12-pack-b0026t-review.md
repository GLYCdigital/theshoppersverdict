## What's in the Box

You get **twelve 10.1-ounce cartridges** of white acrylic latex caulk. That's roughly 120 linear feet of sealant at a standard 1/4-inch bead — enough for a few rooms of trim or a whole house of baseboards. Each tube requires you to puncture the inner seal and cut the nozzle tip at your desired angle. It's a minor setup, but with twelve tubes, you'll want a nail or caulk tool ready on day one.

The white color is a dead-on match for most stock trim and works fine under paint. If you need a tinted or clear sealant, this isn't it.

## Performance and Build Quality

This is where Alex Plus earns its reputation. The caulk goes on smooth and buttery, unlike the stringy mess you get from cheaper acrylics. It tools beautifully with a wet finger or a caulk finishing tool, leaving a clean, recessed bead that hides imperfections.

The standout feature is **paintability**. You can coat it with latex or oil-based paint in just 30 minutes — no primer needed. Shoppers consistently confirm this in reviews, with one noting, *"Painted right over it the same day, no issues at all."* That's a massive time-saver compared to silicone, which often rejects paint and requires masking.

It also flexes with up to **25% joint movement**. That means seasonal expansion and contraction in your home won't crack the seal. For interior trim, doors, and windows, this is exactly the durability you want. Cleanup is plain water — no solvents, no acetone, no lingering chemical smell.

## What Buyers Praise

Beyond paintability, the recurring themes in reviews are **ease of use** and **value**. One reviewer said, *"Great value for the price. I used it for all my baseboards and had plenty left over."* Another highlighted the consistency: *"Smooths out nicely and doesn't shrink or crack like cheaper brands."* The 12-pack economics are obvious — you're paying roughly half the per-tube cost of single cartridges.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: **this is not an exterior or wet-area caulk.** Acrylic latex will break down with prolonged water exposure and UV light. Don't use it in a shower, around a sink, or on exterior window frames. For those, you need a silicone or polyurethane product. Some reviewers learned this the hard way, reporting mold or failure in bathrooms. That's not a defect — it's a misuse of the material.

Also, the 30-minute paint time assumes dry, moderate conditions. In humid basements or cold garages, give it a few hours. And puncturing twelve tubes is mildly annoying, but it's a one-time cost you'll forget once the job's done.

## Who Should Buy This

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this caulk in a bathroom?**
A: Only in dry areas like around a vanity or baseboards. Do not use it in the shower or directly on wet surfaces — it will fail. Use a silicone-based caulk there.

**Q: How long before I can paint over it?**
A: Typically 30 minutes for latex paint, but wait 2-3 hours in humid conditions. Oil-based paints require a longer wait — check the label.

**Q: Does this caulk shrink?**
A: Minimal shrinkage, far less than cheaper acrylics. Tool it well and you'll get a tight seal that holds for years.
