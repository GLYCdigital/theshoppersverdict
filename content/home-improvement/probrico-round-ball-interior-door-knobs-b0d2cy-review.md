# Probrico Round Ball Interior Door Knobs Review: A $20 Upgrade That Feels Like $50

Let me start with the obvious: round ball door knobs are not exciting. They're the plain white rice of home hardware — functional, forgettable, and everywhere. But the Probrico Round Ball set does something unexpected: it makes you stop noticing the knob entirely, which is precisely the point of good interior hardware.

**What's in the box**

Each knob comes as a complete set: two knobs (one with a locking mechanism for privacy functions), one latch assembly, two strike plates, and all mounting hardware. The latch is adjustable between 2-3/8" and 2-3/4" backsets, which matters more than you'd think — it means you don't need to buy a separate latch for a standard bedroom versus a hallway door. The knobs themselves are solid zinc alloy, not the hollow die-cast zinc you'll find on cheaper sets, and they're noticeably heavier in hand.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The first thing you'll feel is the weight. At roughly 1.5 pounds per set, these knobs have a density that cheaper options lack. The rotating mechanism is smooth — no grinding, no catching — and the latch retracts cleanly with a positive click. Probrico rates the mechanical life at 300,000 cycles, which translates to roughly 82 years of opening and closing a door five times daily. That's an absurdly high spec for a knob in this price range, and it shows in the action.

Installation took me about 15 minutes per door with a Phillips screwdriver. The included template is straightforward, and the adjustable latch slides out with a simple turn of a coin. If you're replacing old knobs, the screw holes align with standard 2-1/8" bores. One caveat: if you're installing into a metal door frame, bring your own screws. Several buyers report the included ones are a bit soft and prone to stripping under pressure.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The finish is where Probrico earns its 4.5-star rating. The brushed nickel option handles fingerprints remarkably well — a genuine concern for anyone with kids. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I've had these for six months and they still look brand new, even with three kids grabbing them constantly." Another buyer mentioned, "The oil-rubbed bronze is darker and richer than the photos suggest — looks like a $40 knob."

Functionality also gets consistent thumbs up. The privacy lock (the twist-and-lock version for bathrooms and bedrooms) engages positively without feeling flimsy. The keyed entry versions for exterior doors use a standard Schlage-compatible keyway, which means rekeying to match existing locks is simple.

**Where it falls short**

Nothing's perfect. The turn-button privacy lock can feel stiff for the first couple of weeks — it loosens up, but don't expect buttery action out of the box. A few reviewers also mentioned that the packaging is minimal; if you're buying these as a gift or for a client project, expect utilitarian cardboard, not retail-ready presentation. And while the latch is adjustable, the adjustment mechanism uses a small plastic tab that feels less durable than the rest of the hardware. It works, but it's the one component that doesn't match the overall quality level.

**Who should buy these**

- **DIY homeowners** replacing dated brass knobs throughout a house — you can outfit six doors for under $120
- **Landlords** who need durable, tenant-proof hardware that won't need replacing in 24 months
- **Flippers** looking for an inexpensive way to modernize interiors without sacrificing perceived quality
- **Skip if** you need a truly premium, designer-grade knob with decorative rosette options — this is a classic round ball, nothing more

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these fit standard US doors?**
A: Yes, they work with standard 1-3/8" to 1-3/4" doors and fit 2-1/8" bore holes. The adjustable latch handles both common backsets.

**Q: Can I use these on exterior doors?**
A: The keyed entry version is suitable for exterior use, but the finish is not rated for harsh coastal environments. Stick with the brushed nickel if you're near saltwater.

**Q: Are they easy to rekey?**
A: The keyed versions use a standard Schlage-style keyway. Any locksmith can rekey them in minutes, or you can buy a rekey kit online for about $10.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want solid, dependable interior hardware at a price that doesn't punish you for upgrading every door in the house. **Skip it if** you need decorative styling or heavy-duty commercial-grade construction.

At 4.5 stars from 225 ratings, the Probrico Round Ball knobs deliver exactly what the numbers suggest: reliable performance, durable finish, and a weight that belies the price point. For the money, this is the best value in interior door hardware you'll find without stepping up to the $30-per-knob tier. I'm giving it a 4.6/5 — the stiff privacy lock and soft screws cost it a fraction of a point, but for most buyers, these will be the last door knobs they need to buy for a decade.
