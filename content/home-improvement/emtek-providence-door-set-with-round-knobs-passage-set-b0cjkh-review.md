## What You're Actually Getting

Unboxing the Emtek Providence reveals what you're paying for immediately: substantial weight. At roughly 5.3 pounds for the complete passage set (two knobs, rosettes, latch, and strike plate), this thing feels like a precision instrument compared to the 12-ounce stamped-steel junk most homes ship with. The knobs are forged brass — not zinc alloy, not coated plastic — with a solid, cold feel that tells you this isn't going to corrode, flake, or discolor in five years.

The round knob design is classically simple, measuring 2-1/8 inches in diameter. The rosette is a clean 3-inch circle. Nothing flashy, nothing trendy — just timeless proportions that'll look right in a 1920s craftsman or a 2020s modern farmhouse. As shown in the product image, the finish is consistent across all components, including the screws — a detail cheap manufacturers skip.

## Real-World Performance

The passage function (meaning no lock — just push and go) is where this set shines. The knob rotation is buttery — Emtek uses a ball-bearing mechanism that glides without the gritty catch you feel on lesser hardware. One buyer described it as "the first door knob I've owned that actually feels like a pleasure to use rather than a chore." Another noted the "solid, satisfying click" when the latch engages.

The latch itself is adjustable for 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" backset, covering standard residential doors. The strike plate is beefy, and the included screws are quality — not the soft zinc junk that strips out with one turn of a screwdriver.

**Installation note:** You'll need a 2-1/8" bore hole. If your door has an older 1-1/2" hole, you're looking at re-boring or using a wrap plate. This is a real consideration for older homes.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**The price.** This passage set costs roughly 3-4x what a solid Schlage or Kwikset will run you. For a passage (non-locking) set, that's a lot of money for a function that's essentially "turns freely." You're paying for material quality and longevity, not features.

**Installation tolerance.** The Emtek is unforgiving about alignment. The latch and rosette need to line up almost perfectly or the knob will drag. If you're not comfortable with precise door hardware installation, budget for a pro — or expect some fiddling.

**Finish availability.** While Emtek offers a wide range of finishes, the most popular ones (satin nickel, oil-rubbed bronze) can occasionally run backordered. If you need to match existing hardware immediately, check stock first.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're renovating a home you plan to live in for 10+ years, you appreciate tactile quality in everyday objects, or you're matching an existing Emtek installation. It's also a strong choice for custom builds where the door hardware is a design statement, not an afterthought.

**Skip it if:** You're flipping a house, outfitting a rental, or just need a functional knob on a budget. A $25 Kwikset will open and close the door just fine. You won't love touching it, but it'll work.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this fit standard US doors?**
A: Yes, for doors with a 2-1/8" bore hole and either 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" backset. The latch is adjustable to cover both. If your door has a smaller bore, you'll need modification.

**Q: Is the passage set really "unlocked"?**
A: Correct. This is a passage (hall/closet) function — no key, no thumb turn, just free rotation from both sides. For bedrooms or bathrooms, look at the privacy version.

**Q: How does the finish hold up to daily use?**
A: Exceptionally well based on buyer feedback. The PVD coating process resists fingerprints, cleaning chemicals, and humidity far better than electroplated finishes on cheaper sets.
