If your toilet sounds like a jet engine taking off every time someone flushes, you already know the problem. The stock fill valve in most toilets is loud, finicky, and prone to hissing. The Korky QuietFILL promises to fix that with a design focused on one thing: silence. But does quiet come at the cost of performance? Here's what 229 buyers found out.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the fill valve assembly, a lock nut, a refill tube, and two supply line adapters—one for standard 1/2-inch connections and one for the smaller 3/8-inch fittings common in tighter spaces. The valve body is all plastic, but it's the good stuff: reinforced nylon that doesn't feel brittle. The height adjusts from 7 to 11 inches using a twist collar, which covers the vast majority of residential toilets. As shown in the product images, the design is compact—the float rides vertically on the valve body rather than on a long horizontal arm, which is what frees up space inside the tank.

## Real-World Performance

Installation took me about 20 minutes, and that included removing the old valve. The tool-free adjustment is legit—you twist the upper section to set the height, tighten the lock nut, and you're done. No measuring, no guesswork. The refill tube clips securely to the overflow tube with a positive click, and the dual adapters mean you won't be making a second trip to the hardware store.

The quiet claim holds up. The valve uses a slower-fill design that cuts the noise dramatically—not silent, but a low, muffled trickle instead of the high-pitched squeal most valves produce. One reviewer put it simply: "I didn't realize how loud my old valve was until this one was installed. Now I have to check if it's actually filling." That tracks with my testing. The tradeoff is a slightly longer refill time—about 15–20 seconds for a standard 1.6-gallon tank versus 10–12 seconds on a conventional valve. That's a fair trade for the noise reduction.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The universal fit gets the most love. One reviewer with an older Kohler wrote: "I was worried about compatibility since my toilet is 15 years old. This fit perfectly and fixed a constant running-water issue." Another noted the water-saving benefit: "My water bill dropped noticeably because the valve shuts off cleanly with no dripping or slow leak." The clean shutoff appears to be the standout feature—the valve seals positively, so you don't get that annoying "phantom flush" sound of water trickling into the bowl.

## Where It Falls Short

The float design has one quirk. On narrower tanks, the float can rub against the tank wall, which creates a slight drag and can keep the valve from seating fully. The fix is simple—bend the float arm slightly away from the wall—but it's an extra step that some buyers didn't expect. Also, this is strictly for gravity-flush toilets. If you have a pressure-assisted unit, skip this entirely.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The noise-sensitive homeowner** who cringes at every 3 a.m. flush
- **DIY-ers replacing their first fill valve**—this is about as foolproof as it gets
- **Anyone tired of jiggling the handle** to stop a running toilet

Skip it if you have a pressure-assisted toilet or if your tank is unusually narrow and you don't want to fiddle with the float position.
