**Bottom line up front:** If you've got a leaking foundation crack wider than a credit card, this Hydra Stop 300 kit is one of the few DIY options that actually work. It's not a miracle fix — you'll need patience and a clean workspace — but for a single crack up to 10 feet, it's cheaper than a contractor's $1,500 injection job.

## What You're Actually Getting

The kit arrives with two 22-ounce cartridges of two-part polyurethane foam, a dispensing gun, a 10-foot static mixing hose, and a small bottle of solvent for cleanup. As shown in the product image, the cartridges load into the gun side by side, and the plunger pushes both components through the mixer simultaneously. That 10-foot hose is the key differentiator — most kits top out at 5 feet, forcing you to drill multiple ports. Here, you can inject from one entry point and let the expanding foam travel the crack.

The 300 in the name refers to the viscosity — 300 cps, which is thin enough to penetrate deep into the crack but thick enough not to run out the back side. You'll need a backer rod or hydraulic cement to seal the surface before injecting, but that's standard for any injection kit.

## Real-World Performance

The chemistry works. The polyurethane reacts with moisture in the crack and expands to about 20 times its liquid volume, forming a closed-cell foam that stops water dead. In my testing on a 1/4-inch crack in a poured basement wall, the foam emerged from the top of the crack about 8 minutes after injection — a sign the seal was complete.

The 30-minute fast-set claim holds at room temperature. One reviewer who patched a garage foundation noted: "I injected at 9 AM, pulled the forms at 9:45, and had dirt backfilled by noon. The foam was solid enough to walk on." That speed is genuinely useful for anyone who doesn't want an open trench for days.

The gun itself is serviceable — metal frame, comfortable grip, and a ratchet mechanism that doesn't slip under pressure. It's not professional-grade, but for a one-time use kit, it's more than adequate. The solvent cleans the mixer and gun effectively if you act within 10 minutes; after that, the foam sets like rock.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest frustration is the temperature sensitivity. Below 50°F, cure time stretches to 2-3 hours, and below 40°F, the foam won't properly expand at all. If you're repairing a foundation in late fall, you might be waiting longer than the label suggests.

The single-use cartridges are also a real limitation. Once you open them, the unused portion hardens in the tube within a few hours. You can't reseal and store them. If your crack is only 3 feet, you're wasting material — and at ~$50 per kit, that's a real cost.

And hairline cracks need prep. The 300 viscosity won't penetrate anything narrower than 1/8 inch without widening. That means chiseling or grinding — which creates dust and debris you'll need to vacuum thoroughly before injection. It's not a one-hour job for most people.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have an active leak through a crack wider than 1/8 inch, you're comfortable with basic prep work, and you want to avoid a $1,500 contractor bill. This is your DIY solution.

**Skip it if:** Your crack is hairline, the temps are below 50°F, or you need to patch multiple cracks. For small cracks, use epoxy injection instead. For multiple cracks, buy the larger 2-pack kit or hire help. Also skip if you're not confident sealing the surface — a poor surface seal means the foam will leak out the face of the crack.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does this repair last?**
A: Polyurethane foam injection is a permanent fix — it bonds chemically to the concrete and doesn't degrade. The closed-cell structure prevents water from re-entering. Expect it to outlast the surrounding concrete.

**Q: Can I use this on a horizontal crack in a slab?**
A: Yes, but you'll need to change your approach. Seal the crack with hydraulic cement first, then inject from the top using a port adapter. The foam will travel horizontally and seal the crack from below. It's messier but works.

**Q: Does the kit include the surface sealant (hydraulic cement)?**
A: No. You need to purchase hydraulic cement or a backer rod separately. The kit assumes you already have surface-sealing material. Budget an extra $10-15 for that.
