## The JULLISON 4-Inch Selectable LED: A Smart Fixture That Respects Your Time

You’ve got a dark hallway, a closet that eats light bulbs, or a laundry room where your hands are always full. The JULLISON 4-inch LED motion sensor ceiling light solves that with one tidy package. But as with anything that promises “set it and forget it,” the devil is in the details. Here’s the full story after digging into specs, user feedback, and the realities of installation.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the complete fixture: a 4-inch IC-rated housing, a frosted lens trim ring, and a motion sensor module pre-wired for 120V. There’s no bulb to buy—the LEDs are integrated, rated for 50,000 hours. You also get a small dial on the housing face that lets you choose among five color temperatures (2700K, 3000K, 3500K, 4000K, 5000K) *before* you push it into the ceiling. That’s a genuinely useful feature for matching existing trim in a room with mixed lighting.

As shown in the product image, the sensor is a small dome protruding slightly from the trim—it’s not invisible, but it’s subtle enough in most residential settings. The fixture is wet-rated and IC-rated, meaning it can sit against insulation and handle damp locations like a covered porch.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The motion sensor fires up the light within about half a second—fast enough that you don’t feel like you’re standing in the dark. The default time delay is 30 seconds, but you can adjust it to 1, 3, or 5 minutes via a small switch on the sensor module. There’s also a daylight sensor that keeps the light off when ambient light is sufficient, which is a nice battery-saver for spaces with windows.

One thing buyers consistently note is the quality of the light itself. A verified reviewer put it simply: *“The 4000K setting is perfect for my garage—bright but not harsh, and the sensor catches me from about 12 feet away.”* Another said, *“Install took 15 minutes. The CCT dial is genius—I matched my hallway perfectly.”* That aligns with the 4.6-star average across 229 ratings.

The brightness is solid—roughly 650 lumens at 4000K—which is plenty for a 4-inch fixture in a hallway or closet. It’s not a room’s primary light source, but it’s not meant to be.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has two real quirks. First, the motion sensor’s detection range is fixed. You can’t adjust sensitivity or field of view—only the time delay. If you mount it too high (above 8 feet) or in a spot with a narrow angle, you might find yourself waving your arms to trigger it. One buyer noted: *“Works great in my 7-foot hallway, but in my taller stairwell, I have to step right under it.”*

Second, there’s no dimming. It’s full brightness or off. If you wanted a subtle nightlight effect, this isn’t it. The sensor’s daylight override also means you can’t force it on manually—if you want light during the day, you’ll need to cover the sensor or accept the darkness.

Finally, the junction box fit matters. The housing is designed for a standard 4-inch round box, but if your box sits even slightly recessed, the trim ring won’t sit flush against the ceiling. A few reviewers mentioned needing to shim the box—worth checking before you commit.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners with dark closets, pantries, or laundry rooms** who want hands-free lighting without running new wiring.
- **DIYers** comfortable with basic electrical work (the wiring is simple: line, neutral, ground).
- **Landlords or property managers** upgrading units on a budget—this fixture is a cheap, reliable way to add modern convenience.
- **Anyone replacing old 4-inch recessed cans** who wants to skip the bulb-and-trim dance.

Skip it if you need dimming, have unusually tall ceilings (above 9 feet), or expect to adjust the sensor’s range.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this in a shower or outdoor covered area?**  
A: Yes. It’s wet-rated, so it can handle humidity and direct moisture. For outdoor use, it must be under a covered eave—not fully exposed to rain.

**Q: Does it work with a standard light switch?**  
A: Yes, it connects to a standard 120V circuit. The sensor overrides the switch—leave the switch on and the sensor controls the light. If you want to bypass the sensor, you can’t easily; it’s integrated.

**Q: How do I change the color temperature after installation?**  
A: You don’t—unless you pull the whole fixture down. The CCT dial is on the housing, so it must be set before you push it into the ceiling. Choose carefully the first time.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a no-fuss, motion-activated light for a closet, hallway, or utility space, and you care about matching color temperature without buying five different fixtures. **Skip it if** you need dimming, adjustable detection range, or a fixture that will work in ceilings taller than 8 feet.
