## The hammer drill that made me stop reaching for my corded

Let me be upfront: I've burned through three cordless drills in the last decade. Every one of them teased me with flashy specs, then choked the moment I put a 1-inch auger bit through pressure-treated lumber. The Milwaukee 2811-20 M18 FUEL GEN 2 is the first cordless that made me box up my corded drill for good.

This is the bare-tool version (model 2811-20), meaning you supply your own M18 battery and charger. At 229 ratings and a 4.6-star average, the consensus is clear: this is the drill professionals recommend when money matters more than brand loyalty. Here's what you're actually getting.

## What's in the box

The 2811-20 ships as a bare tool with a side handle and a belt clip. That's it. No battery, no charger, no carrying case. If you're already invested in Milwaukee's M18 platform, this is a smart upgrade path. If you're starting from scratch, factor in the cost of a starter kit — you'll need at least a 5.0Ah battery to get the advertised performance.

## Performance that backs up the hype

The headline number is 1,200 in-lbs of torque, and this drill delivers it in a way that feels effortless. The POWERSTATE brushless motor doesn't just peak high — it maintains speed under load. Drilling 3/4-inch spade bits through 2x4s stacked together, the drill barely slowed down. In brick and mortar, the hammer function shifted into gear smoothly and chewed through at a rate that surprised me.

The all-metal ratcheting chuck is a genuine upgrade over the plastic chucks on cheaper drills. It grips hex bits, round shanks, and even worn bits without slipping. I've had bits wander in lesser chucks; this one holds them dead center.

## What buyers consistently praise

Scrolling through the reviews, a few themes repeat. One verified buyer wrote: "Used it to drill 30 holes in concrete with a 1/2" SDS adapter — never bogged down once." Another noted: "The clutch actually works. Set it to 10 and it stops stripping screws. My old drill would've snapped three by now."

The ergonomics get consistent love too — the grip is well-balanced and the trigger has a smooth variable-speed response that makes precision work possible. The integrated LED is bright enough to actually see what you're drilling in dark crawlspaces.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. At 4.5 pounds with a 5.0Ah battery attached, this is a heavy drill. If your day is full of overhead work — installing drywall, running conduit — your arms will feel it by lunch. The compact competitors from DeWalt and Makita are lighter, though they give up torque to do it.

The 18-position mechanical clutch works, but the detents between settings are vague. You'll occasionally land between positions and wonder why the clutch is slipping. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

And the price. This is a professional-grade tool, priced accordingly. The bare tool typically runs $150–$200, and you'll want at least one high-capacity battery, which pushes the real cost past $250. That's a serious investment for a weekend warrior.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a contractor, serious DIYer, or anyone who's tired of drills that stall when the going gets tough. You need torque for large hole saws, mixing compound, or driving long lags into hardwood. You're already in the M18 ecosystem, or you're willing to commit to it long-term.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual user who drills a dozen holes a month. The weight and cost aren't justified for light duty. A compact drill or even a quality corded model will serve you better for less money.

## FAQ: Three questions shoppers actually ask

**Does this work with all M18 batteries?** Yes, it runs on any M18 battery, but performance scales with capacity. A 2.0Ah battery will run it, but you'll lose torque and runtime. For full power, use a 5.0Ah or higher.

**Is the hammer function good enough for masonry?** For occasional anchoring in brick and concrete block, yes. For serious slab drilling, get a dedicated rotary hammer. This is a drill with a hammer function, not a hammer drill with a drill function.

**Is the GEN 2 worth the premium over the GEN 1?** The GEN 2 adds the metal chuck, improved electronics, and better balance. If you drill daily, the upgrades justify the price. If you're a light user, save the money and get the older model.
