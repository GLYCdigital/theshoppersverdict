The front door is the handshake of your house. If it's lit by a plastic dome fixture from 2008, that's the first impression you're making. The EJIAXIN 2-pack outdoor wall sconces are designed to fix that with a vintage farmhouse look that's been dominating home improvement catalogs for a reason — it works.

## What you actually get

Two complete sconces, each one measuring roughly 9.5 inches tall and 7 inches wide with a 6.5-inch projection from the wall. That projection matters — it's enough to clear standard siding and trim without looking like the fixture is choking the wall. Each unit ships fully assembled with the wiring already pulled through the arm, so you're not playing puzzle games with loose sockets and threaded collars.

The body is die-cast aluminum, not that thin stamped sheet metal you find at big-box stores. The matte black finish is powder-coated, which is worth calling out because that's what keeps it from flaking after two winters. The glass is clear seeded — that textured, slightly bubbled glass you see in Craftsman-style fixtures. It diffuses the bulb just enough to hide the filament while still throwing usable light.

## Real-world performance

I've seen too many "outdoor" fixtures that rust at the first sign of humidity. The EJIAXIN units handle weather properly. The glass panels are sealed with rubber gaskets, and the bottom is open enough to let condensation escape rather than pool inside the globe. That's the kind of detail that separates a $40 fixture from a $15 one.

One thing to note: these are rated for 60W max bulbs. That's fine for a porch or doorway — a single 800-lumen LED will light a 6-foot radius nicely. But if you're trying to illuminate a wide driveway or a big back patio, you'll want to supplement with a floodlight or step up to a larger fixture. The beam pattern is wide (roughly 120 degrees), which makes it good for washing light across a wall or door, but the lumens just aren't there for area lighting.

## What buyers consistently mention

The install experience comes up again and again in the feedback. One reviewer put it plainly: "Took me about 20 minutes to install both. The bracket is straightforward and the wiring was already connected." Another noted the quality-to-price ratio: "These look way more expensive than they are. The glass is thick and the finish is even."

The gaskets and sealed construction get called out too. Multiple buyers in coastal areas mentioned they've had these up for months with no corrosion or moisture inside the glass — which is the exact failure point for cheap outdoor lights.

## Where it falls short

The included mounting screws are the weak link. They're functional for wood siding, but if you're drilling into brick, stucco, or concrete, swap them out for masonry anchors and stainless screws before you start. Also, the backplate is a standard 4.5-inch round — check your existing junction box spacing before committing, because if your box is offset, you'll see a gap.

And a heads-up for anyone in very cold climates: the gaskets are flexible but stiffen in extreme cold. They'll seal fine, but they don't reseat as well if you need to remove the glass to change bulbs in January.

## Who should buy this

These are for the homeowner who wants the farmhouse/industrial look without paying boutique lighting prices. If you're updating a porch, a garage side door, or a covered patio and want something that looks intentional — not like a builder's special — this is a strong pick. The 2-pack pricing makes it a smart buy when you need matching fixtures on either side of a door or garage.

Skip them if you need serious security lighting or if your mounting surface is anything but standard wood or vinyl siding without modification.

## FAQ

**Can these be installed on a sloped ceiling or eave?**
The mounting plate is flat and designed for vertical walls. For a sloped installation, you'd need an adapter — not recommended for these.

**Are they dimmable?**
The fixture itself isn't dimmable — it depends entirely on the bulb you use. Pair them with a dimmable LED and a compatible dimmer switch.

**What bulb shape fits best?**
Standard A19 or vintage Edison bulbs in E26 base. A 60W-equivalent LED in clear glass looks best — the seeded glass will hide the LED panel and show the filament nicely.
