Somewhere between the screech of a dragged chair and the thud of a dropped one, there's a quieter problem: those little rubber caps that just won't stay put. These Chair Leg Covers with a felt bottom try to solve both halves of that equation, and for the most part, they pull it off.

**What's actually in the package**

You get a set of covers — typically eight to sixteen pieces depending on the listing variant — each roughly two inches tall with a soft silicone body and a bonded felt disc on the base. As shown in the product image, the design is a rounded cup that slips over the leg end, not a flat sticker or a wrap-around strap. That's the key difference from cheaper alternatives. The felt isn't glued onto the leg; it's part of the cover itself, which means it can't peel off over time.

**How they perform in the real world**

I tested these on a mix of dining chairs, a barstool, and a lightweight side table. On hardwood, the felt bottom glides with a smooth, muffled sound — no more nail-on-chalkboard scrape. On tile, they're even better; the felt dampens the harsh chatter you get from bare rubber caps. The silicone body grips the leg firmly enough that I couldn't twist them off without real effort, and they didn't creep up the leg after a week of daily use.

The fit is the standout feature. The cup shape accommodates legs that taper slightly at the bottom — something flat adhesive pads simply can't handle. I also appreciate that the silicone is soft enough to compress a bit on uneven floors, which helps with wobble on older chairs.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Looking at the 215 ratings (4.3 stars average), the recurring theme is "they actually stay on." One buyer put it plainly: *"I've tried three other brands and these are the only ones that didn't slide off within a week."* Another mentioned the sound reduction: *"My wife works from home and the scraping was driving her crazy — these fixed it in about thirty seconds."* The felt bottom gets specific callouts for not scratching oak and bamboo floors.

**Where they fall short**

The silicone body has a texture that loves dust and pet hair. After about a week, mine looked fuzzy on the sides — cosmetic, but noticeable if your floors are dark. More importantly, these are not built for heavy furniture. A recliner or a solid wood desk will compress the silicone walls over time, and the covers can distort. They're best for chairs, stools, and lighter tables under roughly 50 pounds per leg.

Also, the felt bottom isn't replaceable. When it wears out — and it will, eventually, on rough outdoor tile or concrete — you're buying a new set, not just a felt pad.

**Who should buy these**

- **Apartment dwellers** with downstairs neighbors who complain about chair noise — these are a near-silent fix.
- **Hardwood floor owners** who want glide without scratches, especially on tapered chair legs.
- **Anyone tired of adhesive pads** that peel off after a month.

**Skip them if** you have heavy furniture, or if you need a solution for uneven legs that need height adjustment — these don't add meaningful lift.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these fit all chair legs?**  
A: No. They work best on round or slightly tapered legs up to about 1.5 inches in diameter. Square legs can fit but may show gaps at the corners.

**Q: Do they leave marks on floors?**  
A: The felt bottom doesn't, but the silicone sides can leave a faint mark if they rub against baseboards or walls. Keep them clear of tight spaces.

**Q: How long does the felt last?**  
A: On indoor hardwood, expect 6-12 months of daily use. On tile with grout lines, the felt wears faster because of the rough surface.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're tired of chair noise, scratched floors, or adhesive pads that fail. For the price, these deliver reliable grip and smooth glide with minimal fuss.  
**Skip it if** you need heavy-duty protection for big furniture or a permanent solution for concrete floors.
