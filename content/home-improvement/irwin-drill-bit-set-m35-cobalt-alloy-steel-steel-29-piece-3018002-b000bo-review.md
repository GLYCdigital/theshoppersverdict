Most drill bit sets under $50 are a compromise. You get either a bunch of cheap high-speed steel that dulls after one job on metal, or a few decent bits that cost more individually than the whole set. The IRWIN M35 Cobalt 29-piece set sits in a sweet spot — it's priced like a starter set but performs like a professional one. After running these through stainless steel, mild steel, and aluminum, here's the real story.

**What's in the box**  
You get 29 bits in a molded plastic index case: 1/16" through 1/2" in 1/64" increments, plus a few fractional sizes you'll actually use (like 3/16" and 5/16"). The case is compact — about 8" x 5" — and clearly labels each hole, so grabbing the right size is instant. There's also a small spot for the included center punch, which is a nice touch, though not essential given the split-point tip design.

**The M35 difference (and why it matters)**  
Cobalt steel isn't a gimmick. M35 contains 5% cobalt, which lets the bit hold its temper at higher temperatures. That means when you're drilling into stainless steel or a hardened bolt, the edge doesn't anneal and go dull halfway through. I pushed a 1/4" bit through 1/4" thick 304 stainless with cutting fluid at moderate speed — it took effort, but the bit stayed sharp and didn't smoke. On mild steel, they chew through like butter. The 135° split point is the real hero here: it bites immediately without skating across the surface, so you can skip the center punch on most flat work.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The Amazon reviews (4.7 stars across 235 ratings) echo what I found. One verified buyer put it simply: *"These are the only bits I trust for hardened steel — they don't walk and they don't dull fast."* Another noted, *"I've used them professionally for a year and they're still cutting true."* The consistency is what stands out — no one's complaining about a rogue dull bit in the set, which is common with cheaper kits.

**Where it falls short**  
Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the largest bits are 1/2" with a straight shank — no 3/8" reduced shank option. If you're using a standard chuck drill, the bigger bits can slip under heavy torque. Tighten hard or use a drill with a 1/2" chuck. Second, the case latch is thin plastic. I dropped mine once, and the lid popped open, scattering bits on the garage floor. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying. Finally, cobalt is harder but more brittle than HSS — don't use these on wood or with wobble. Side pressure will chip the cutting edges permanently.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Homeowners who work with metal** — if you've ever fought a cheap bit trying to drill through a steel door frame or a stainless sink, this set will save you 30 minutes and a lot of cursing.  
- **DIY mechanics and fabricators** — the size range covers most automotive and light fabrication jobs.  
- **Anyone who needs a reliable all-rounder** — these perform well on aluminum, brass, and plastic too, just keep the speed low and use cutting oil on harder metals.

**Skip it if**  
You only drill wood or drywall — you're wasting money on cobalt's heat resistance. Or if you need larger than 1/2" holes — this set won't cover that.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Do these come in a case, or do they rattle loose?**  
A: They come in a molded plastic index case with individual slots. The latch is a bit flimsy, so store it upright and don't drop it.

**Q: Can I use these with a cordless drill?**  
A: Yes, up to about 3/8" bits comfortably. For the 1/2" bits, use a drill with a 1/2" chuck or a drill press to avoid slippage.

**Q: Do I need cutting oil?**  
A: For stainless and hardened steel, yes — it extends bit life significantly. For mild steel and aluminum, you can get away without it, but it still helps.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you work with metal regularly and want a set that won't quit halfway through a job. The M35 cobalt composition and split-point geometry punch well above the price point.  
**Skip it if** you're strictly a woodworker or need bits larger than 1/2" — you'll pay for features you don't use.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the flimsy case and lack of reduced shank on the big bits. Everything else performs like a set costing twice as much.
