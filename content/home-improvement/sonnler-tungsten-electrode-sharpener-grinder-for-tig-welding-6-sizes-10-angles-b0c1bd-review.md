If you've ever hand-ground a tungsten on a bench grinder, you know the struggle: inconsistent angles, wavy flats, and that annoying habit of grinding the wrong direction. The SONNLER Tungsten Electrode Sharpener aims to fix all that for $69.99 — about the cost of a decent night out and far less than a dedicated tungsten grinder from brands like Sharpie or Piranha. But does a compact benchtop unit actually deliver? After digging through 210 Amazon ratings (4.2 stars) and examining the specs, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the grinder unit, six collets (1.0mm, 1.6mm, 2.0mm, 2.4mm, 3.0mm, and 3.2mm), a hex wrench for collet changes, and a small diamond grinding wheel that's pre-installed. The unit measures roughly 7 inches long and weighs just over 3 pounds — small enough to clamp in a bench vise or bolt to a workbench. The collet system is the star here: you drop the tungsten through the correct collet, tighten the cap, and rotate the tool to grind. No chucks, no adapters, no guesswork.

## Performance and Build Quality

The angle dial offers 10 settings from 15° to 45°, which covers the vast majority of TIG applications. For AC aluminum welding, you'll want the wider 30–45° angles; for DC steel and stainless, the sharper 15–20° settings work better. The dial clicks into place with positive detents, so you know exactly where you are.

The diamond wheel spins at a respectable speed and produces a surprisingly clean taper. One buyer noted, *"The tip comes out looking like it was ground on a $500 machine — perfectly concentric and smooth."* That's the core value proposition: consistency. You can sharpen 20 electrodes in a row and every one will have the same geometry. That's impossible with hand grinding.

The motor is the weak point. It's not a brushless unit, and at full speed it produces a high-pitched whine that gets old fast. Another reviewer put it bluntly: *"It's noisy. I wear earplugs when I use it, and I still hear it."* The vibration is manageable but noticeable — you'll want to bolt it down, not just set it on a table.

## Where It Falls Short

**Small wheel, slow removal.** The grinding wheel is about 2 inches in diameter. If you're touching up a clean electrode, that's fine — 10 seconds and you're done. But if you're trying to reshape a badly contaminated or melted tip, you'll be at it for a while. One customer said, *"It took me a good three minutes to grind down a badly balled-up 3/32 electrode. My bench grinder would have done it in 20 seconds."*

**No dust collection.** The unit has a small debris tray, but it's not sealed. Fine tungsten dust will escape into your shop. Tungsten dust is toxic, so you should wear a respirator or at minimum use this near a shop vac. This is a real oversight for the price.

**Collet changes are fiddly.** Swapping collets requires loosening the cap, pulling out the old collet, and inserting the new one. It's not hard, but it's not tool-less either. If you switch between 1/16" and 3/32" electrodes frequently, you'll be doing this dance often.

## Who Should Buy This

**The hobbyist TIG welder** who's tired of inconsistent hand-ground tips and wants repeatable results without dropping $300+ on a dedicated machine. **The mobile welder** who needs a compact, portable sharpener that fits in a toolbox. **The fabrication shop** that wants to standardize tip geometry across multiple welders.

**Skip it** if you're a production welder who grinds dozens of electrodes daily — the small wheel and slow removal rate will frustrate you. Also skip it if you primarily weld with 1.0mm or smaller tungsten, as those thin rods can flex during grinding and produce uneven tapers.

## FAQ

**Can I use this with a variable speed drill instead of plugging it in?**
No. The SONNLER has a built-in 12V motor and requires the included AC adapter. It's not designed to be driven externally.

**Does it work with pure tungsten for AC welding?**
Yes, but pure tungsten is softer and will load the wheel faster. You'll need to clean the wheel more often. For AC, consider using it with zirconiated or rare-earth tungsten instead.

**How do I clean the diamond wheel?**
Use a dressing stick (not included) or a fine-grit silicon carbide stone. Run the wheel for a few seconds against the dressing stick to remove embedded tungsten particles. Don't use water — the wheel is dry-use only.

## The Verdict

**Skip it if** you need heavy material removal, work in a dust-sensitive environment, or grind more than a few electrodes per day.
