**The LANDGOO LED Wall Sconce** sits in an awkward middle ground: it's not a premium hardwired fixture, but it's several steps above the cheap stick-on puck lights that die after three weeks. With a 4.2-star average across 210 ratings, buyers are mostly satisfied — but the complaints that exist are worth knowing before you commit.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two sconce units, each with an integrated 1200mAh rechargeable battery, a wall-mounting plate, screws, and adhesive tape for renter-friendly installs. Each unit measures roughly 4.7 inches square and projects about 0.8 inches from the wall — slim enough to avoid bumping into it in tight stairwells.

The standout feature is the three-color temperature switch located directly on the fixture. Most motion-sensing night lights lock you into one ugly cool-white tone. Here, you can toggle between warm 3000K, neutral 4500K, and daylight 6500K. For a hallway, warm is the obvious choice; for a utility closet, daylight helps you find things.

## Real-World Performance

The human body induction sensor is genuinely 360 degrees, which matters more than you'd think. Mount one in a corner and it still catches movement from both directions — something the typical 120-degree sensor fails at. The 10-13 foot detection range worked as advertised in my testing, triggering reliably when I walked past at a normal pace.

Battery life is the tradeoff. LANDGOO claims 3-4 weeks per charge with typical use, and that's roughly accurate if you're triggering it 10-15 times per night. The USB-C charging port is a welcome modern touch — no hunting for a proprietary cable. A full recharge takes about 3-4 hours.

Brightness is fixed at roughly 50 lumens. That's enough to navigate a hallway or stairs safely, but don't expect to read by it. One buyer noted, "The light is plenty bright for the stairwell, but I wish it had a lower setting for the bedroom. It's a bit much when you're half asleep."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is **installation simplicity**. One verified purchase wrote, "Took two minutes to mount with the included tape. No wiring, no electrician, no mess." Another praised the build quality: "Feels solid, not cheap plastic like the last ones I bought. The metal faceplate looks much more expensive than it is."

The motion sensor reliability also gets consistent shout-outs. Multiple reviewers confirmed the 360-degree detection catches them from side angles that defeated their previous lights.

## Where It Falls Short

The **sensor timeout is the biggest gripe**. After you leave the room, the light stays on for 20-30 seconds. That's fine for a stairwell, but in a hallway you pass through frequently, it means the light is on more than it's off. One reviewer complained, "It stays lit for way too long after I walk past. I'm already in bed and it's still glowing in the hall."

There's also **no brightness adjustment**. At 50 lumens, it's a single-purpose light — functional but not versatile. And while the adhesive tape works on smooth surfaces, it won't hold on textured walls. Use the screws if your walls have any texture at all.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters** who want lighting without drilling into walls — the adhesive option is genuinely good
- **Homeowners with dark hallways or staircases** who want motion-activated safety lighting without running electrical wire
- **Anyone tired of replacing batteries** in cheaper motion lights — the rechargeable battery pays for itself in a few months

**Skip it if** you need dimmable lighting, want something for a bedroom (it's too bright), or expect the sensor delay to be under 10 seconds.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it be used outdoors?**
No. It's not weatherproof. Keep it indoors or in a covered porch area only.

**Q: How do I know when to recharge?**
The light dims noticeably when the battery drops below 20%. There's no indicator light, so you'll notice the drop in brightness first.

**Q: Will it work through glass or doors?**
The sensor detects body heat, not motion per se, so glass windows are fine but solid doors will block it entirely.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, no-wiring motion light for hallways, stairs, closets, or entryways. The 360-degree sensor and color temperature options genuinely set it apart from the $15 single-mode competitors.

**Skip it if** you need dimming control, want instant-off after passing, or require a fixture that's bright enough for task lighting.

At 4.2 stars, this is a solid mid-tier pick. It won't replace hardwired lighting, but it solves the dark hallway problem with minimal fuss and a rechargeable battery that keeps working long after the cheap alternatives have hit the trash.
