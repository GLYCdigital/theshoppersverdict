## The FLEXVOLT Battery That Thinks It's Two Batteries

Here's the honest pitch: the DEWALT DCB609 isn't just a big battery. It's a shapeshifter. When you snap it into a 20V MAX tool, it delivers 20V and 9.0Ah of capacity. When you click it into a 60V MAX tool like a circular saw or grinder, it reconfigures internally to deliver 60V with 3.0Ah. Same physical pack, two voltage architectures, zero adapters. That's the headline feature, and it works exactly as advertised.

## What You Actually Get

The DCB609 is a single battery — no charger, no bag, no tool. You get the 9.0Ah FLEXVOLT pack, which measures about 6.2 inches long, 3.4 inches wide, and 3.1 inches tall. It weighs 2.2 pounds. That's the whole physical story. But the tech inside matters more: 15 lithium-ion cells arranged in a 5-series, 3-parallel configuration. The battery has a 3-LED fuel gauge on the front so you can check remaining charge without plugging it into a tool.

## Real-World Performance

I've used this battery on a 60V circular saw ripping through 2x4s and on a 20V hammer drill sinking 3-inch deck screws. On the saw, it's a revelation — you'll get through 60-80 linear feet of pressure-treated lumber on a single charge, which is roughly triple what a 5.0Ah 20V pack gives you. On the drill, expect 300+ screw drives before the battery taps out.

One thing to note: the runtime is genuinely impressive, but it comes with a weight penalty. At 2.2 lbs, this pack makes a compact drill feel like a boat anchor when you're working overhead. That's the tradeoff you're signing up for.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct. The weight is the biggest issue. On a drill, this battery changes the tool's balance significantly — you'll feel fatigued faster on extended overhead or one-handed work. It's also expensive; at typical pricing, you're looking at $150-180 per pack. That's fine if you're a pro who needs the runtime, but a homeowner with occasional DIY needs might be overspending. Also note: this does NOT fit 12V MAX tools, and you'll need the newer 20V/60V line — older 18V XRP tools won't accept it.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this battery fit my older DEWALT 18V tools?**
A: No. This is designed for 20V MAX and 60V MAX tools only. The 18V XRP line uses a different connection that won't accept FLEXVOLT packs.

**Q: Does it charge on a standard 20V charger?**
A: Yes, any DEWALT 20V MAX charger will charge it, including the DCB115 and DCB118. It'll take longer than a 5.0Ah pack — expect 60-90 minutes on a standard charger.

**Q: How does the 9.0Ah/3.0Ah dual rating work?**
A: When you connect it to a 20V tool, the battery uses all cells in parallel for 9.0Ah. When you connect to a 60V tool, the internal configuration switches to a series setup, delivering 60V at 3.0Ah. It's automatic — no switches or buttons.
