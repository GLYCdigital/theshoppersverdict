If you build cabinets, shelving, or furniture frames, you already know Kreg’s name. The KPHJ320-22 is their mid-tier portable jig, and it hits a sweet spot between the bare-bones Mini and the auto-clamping K5. After 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, the consensus is clear: this is the one to buy if you want pro-level pocket holes without the $150 price tag.

**What you actually get**  
Out of the box: the jig body, a 2-inch offset face attachment, a 3/8-inch hex key, and a sample screw. That’s it. No clamp, no drill bit, no stop collar. You’re paying for the jig itself — the metal clamp and Kreg’s stepped drill bit (part #KPH-BIT) are sold separately. Factor that into your budget if you’re starting from scratch.

The body is cast aluminum with hardened steel drill guides. That’s not marketing fluff — steel guides mean the bit doesn’t wallow out the hole after 500 uses, which is the failure point on cheaper plastic jigs. The 2-inch offset face is the key upgrade here: it lets you drill into stock up to 1.5 inches thick (like 2x4s or glued-up panels) without flipping the jig or using spacers. You just set the depth collar, slide the face over the edge, and drill. It’s a real time-saver, and it’s the reason this version exists over the older K4.

**Performance and real-world use**  
I ran a dozen joints through it on pine, oak, and plywood. The drill guides are tight — no bit wobble, no tear-out on the backside when you use a sharp bit. The integrated dust port connects to a 1-1/4-inch or 2-1/2-inch shop vac hose and catches maybe 80% of the shavings. That matters more than you think when you’re on your knees inside a cabinet carcase.

The fixed 3-hole pattern (two holes at the standard 3/4-inch spacing, plus one offset) is fine for face frames and edge joints. But if you’re joining narrow trim (under 2 inches), you’ll only use one or two holes, and you can’t adjust the pattern. That’s the tradeoff for the price — the K5 lets you move the guide block, but it costs twice as much.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The reviews are overwhelmingly positive, and the recurring themes are durability and accuracy. One buyer wrote: *“I’ve built three full kitchens with this jig and it’s still dead-on square. My buddy’s cheap Harbor Freight jig is garbage after one project.”* Another noted: *“The 2-inch offset is genius — I can drill into 2x4s without any setup. Saves me 20 minutes per project.”* Multiple reviewers mention that the steel guides keep the bit from drifting, which is the #1 complaint on knockoff brands.

**Where it falls short**  
The missing clamp is the biggest gripe. You can’t use the jig one-handed — you need to hold it against the workpiece while drilling, and without the Kreg clamp (about $20), that’s awkward and slightly dangerous. Buy the clamp at the same time. Also, the depth collar on the drill bit must be set precisely; if you’re off by even 1/16 inch, you’ll either blow through the back or leave a shallow hole that splits the wood. It’s a skill issue, not a jig issue, but be prepared for a learning curve.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Cabinetmakers and furniture builders** who want a portable jig that stays accurate for years.  
- **DIYers working with 2x4s or thick hardwood** who need the 2-inch offset capability.  
- **Anyone who owns a shop vac** — the dust port makes cleanup a non-issue.  

**Skip it if** you only join thin stock (under 1 inch) and want the cheapest option — the Kreg Mini at $30 will do that job. Or if you need variable hole spacing, step up to the K5.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Does this work with 1x3 boards?**  
A: Yes, but you’ll only use two of the three holes. The 2-inch offset face is designed for thicker stock, so for 3/4-inch material, you can use the standard face (included) for a cleaner fit.

**Q: Can I use it with a right-angle drill?**  
A: No. The jig requires a drill that fits in the guides at 90 degrees. A standard 3/8-inch or 1/2-inch cordless drill works fine; a right-angle attachment won’t align.

**Q: Is the 2-inch face worth the extra cost over the K4?**  
A: If you work with 2x4s, glued-up panels, or any stock over 1 inch thick, yes. The K4 requires you to flip the jig and re-clamp, which is slow and error-prone. If you only use 3/4-inch plywood, you won’t notice the difference.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a rugged, accurate pocket-hole jig that handles thick stock and won’t need replacing. **Skip it if** you’re on a tight budget and only join thin boards — the Kreg Mini is a better value there.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It loses a half-star for the separate clamp purchase, but the build quality and 2-inch offset capability make it the best mid-range pocket-hole jig on the market.
