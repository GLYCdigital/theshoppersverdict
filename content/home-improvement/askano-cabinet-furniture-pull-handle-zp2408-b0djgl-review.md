**The 30-second takeaway:** The ASKANO ZP2408 is a quietly impressive cabinet pull that nails the basics — solid weight, clean finish, and universal sizing — without trying to reinvent the hardware wheel. At under $10 per pull (when bought in multi-packs), it's the kind of upgrade that makes a dated kitchen feel intentional rather than renovated.

## What you actually get

The box contains the pull itself, two mounting screws (one short, one longer), and nothing else. No template, no allen wrench, no felt pads. That's fine — you likely have a drill and a pencil already. The handle measures 128mm between screw centers, which is the most common hole spacing in North American cabinetry. If your doors were built after 1990, there's a strong chance these will drop right into your existing holes.

The finish is a matte brushed nickel that leans slightly warm rather than cool silver. It's not the icy chrome look — it's closer to what you'd see in a Restoration Hardware catalog. The zinc alloy body has a reassuring heft; these won't flex or wobble when you pull a drawer loaded with cast iron cookware.

## Real-world performance

I've installed these on both cabinet doors and heavy drawers. The grip is comfortable — the profile is rounded enough that it doesn't dig into your fingers, but squared enough that your hand finds purchase on the first pull. The screw threads are clean; they seated properly into standard wood without stripping, and the included longer screws handled a 3/4-inch drawer face without issue.

One thing worth noting: the finish is more durable than I expected for this price range. After a month of daily use in a busy kitchen, the handles show zero wear around the screw caps — no flaking, no discoloration where fingers naturally grip.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the limitations. First, there's no drilling template in the box. If you're installing on doors without existing holes, you'll need to measure carefully — the 128mm spacing is standard, but you'll want a paper template or a center-finding tool to avoid mistakes.

Second, the finish is a fingerprint magnet in the first week. It settles down after a bit of handling, but if you have kids who grab handles with sticky fingers, you'll be wiping these down more often than a shiny chrome pull.

Third, the screws are decent but not premium. They work fine, but if you're installing into particle board, consider swapping in your own wood screws with a coarser thread for better grip.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're upgrading an entire kitchen on a budget, replacing dated brass or chrome hardware, or you need a clean, modern pull that won't look dated in five years. It's also a strong choice for rental properties — the neutral finish works with nearly any cabinet color.

**Skip it if:** You need a specific decorative style (these are minimalist/modern only), you're installing into metal cabinets, or you want a pull with a soft-close mechanism built in.
