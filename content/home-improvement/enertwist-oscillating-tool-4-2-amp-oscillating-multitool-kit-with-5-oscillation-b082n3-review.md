The ENERTWIST 4.2 Amp Oscillating Multitool Kit undercuts that dramatically while promising 18,000 oscillations per minute and 25 accessories in the box. ## What's Actually in the Box

This isn't one of those kits where the accessories are filler. As shown in the product image, you get the tool body, a storage case, and a substantial accessory set: wood-cutting blades (including a plunge-cut blade), a bi-metal blade for metal, a segment saw blade, a scraper, and a range of sanding pads in multiple grits. That's roughly 25 pieces total.

The tool itself has a variable speed dial that ranges from 10,000 to 18,000 OPM, a 4.2-amp motor, and a quick-change accessory system that accepts both OIS and Starlock-compatible blades. The body is rubber-overmolded for grip, and the power cord is a standard 6-foot length — enough for most workbench or floor-level jobs without dragging an extension cord.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The 4.2-amp motor is the standout here. For context, many tools in this price bracket ship with 2.5 to 3 amps and struggle on anything beyond light sanding. The ENERTWIST has enough torque to make flush cuts through 2x4s and plunge cuts into drywall without bogging down, provided you let the tool work at its pace rather than forcing it.

The variable speed dial matters more than you might think. For sanding, you'll want to drop it to the lower range — around 10,000 to 12,000 OPM — to avoid gouging the surface. For cutting, crank it up. Buyers consistently mention that the speed control is responsive and actually useful, not just a gimmick.

The quick-change lever is solid. It locks accessories firmly with no wobble, which is critical for clean cuts. Some budget tools have sloppy accessory retention that causes the blade to wander; that's not an issue here based on the feedback.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

One buyer noted, "I've used it to cut door jambs, trim, and PVC pipe — it handles everything I've thrown at it." Another said, "The case alone is worth it. Everything has a spot, and it's easy to grab and go." Multiple reviewers mention that the included blades are sharper than expected for a budget kit, and the universal compatibility means you can swap in higher-end Fein or Bosch blades later without buying a different tool.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the vibration. At 18,000 OPM, this tool buzzes — noticeably. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're doing extended sanding sessions or precision work for hours, your hand will fatigue faster than with a higher-end model with better vibration dampening. One reviewer said, "If you're a pro using it daily, spend more. For weekend projects, this is more than enough."

The included sanding pads are mediocre. They work, but they shed quickly on hardwood. Plan to replace them with higher-quality third-party paper almost immediately. Also, the tool is louder than premium models — nothing offensive, but you'll want hearing protection for extended use.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the homeowner, DIYer, or weekend warrior who needs a versatile multitool for occasional projects: cutting baseboards, trimming drywall, sanding tight corners, scraping old caulk. It's also a great second tool for someone who already owns a premium multitool but wants a backup or a dedicated sanding unit without dropping another $150.

Skip it if you're a professional using a multitool daily for hours. The vibration, noise, and accessory quality will push you toward a premium brand. Also skip it if you need cordless operation — this is corded only.
