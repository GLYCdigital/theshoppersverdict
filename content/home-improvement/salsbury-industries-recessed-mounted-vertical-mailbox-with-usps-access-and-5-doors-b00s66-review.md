If you manage a small office, a multi-unit rental, or a homeowners association, you already know the pain of scattered mailboxes. Either they’re flimsy plastic boxes bolted to a wall, or they’re the kind that USPS carriers “accidentally” skip because access is a hassle. The Salsbury Industries Recessed Mounted Vertical Mailbox with U is the grown-up answer — but it comes with installation strings attached.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 5-door vertical unit, roughly 25 inches tall and 14 inches wide, designed to sit flush inside a wall recess. You get the aluminum body, five individually locked compartments, and a master door on the front that swings open for carrier access. The finish is a baked-on powder coat — I’ve seen these installed in coastal environments where cheaper units corrode within a year, and the Salsbury holds up. Hardware is stainless steel, and each compartment uses a brass cam lock with two keys.

The “U” in the name refers to the USPS-approved configuration: the carrier opens the master door, loads all five compartments from the front, and locks it back. No individual door opening for the mail carrier — just one clean motion.

## Real-World Performance

Let’s talk about what 220 Amazon reviewers at 4.4 stars actually say. The recurring praise is build quality. One reviewer put it plainly: *“This thing is built like a tank. I’ve had cheap vertical mailboxes before and they dent if you look at them wrong. This one has survived three years of Chicago winters.”* That matches the spec sheet — heavy-gauge aluminum, not the thin sheet metal you’ll find at big-box hardware stores.

Installation is where the story gets real. This is a recessed mount, meaning you’re cutting a hole in your wall. If you’re handy with a reciprocating saw and framing a rough opening doesn’t scare you, it’s doable in an afternoon. If you’re not, budget $200–400 for a contractor. Multiple reviewers mention this — the unit itself is solid, but the install is the actual project. One frustrated buyer noted: *“The mailbox is great. The installation instructions are not. I had to figure out the rough opening dimensions myself.”* That’s a fair criticism — Salsbury assumes you know how to frame.

## Where It Falls Short

First, the master door access is a double-edged sword. USPS approved doesn’t mean your local carrier will actually use it. Some routes — especially in urban areas — refuse to open master doors because of theft liability. If your carrier won’t cooperate, you’re back to the old problem. Check with your local post office *before* you cut a hole in your wall.

Second, there’s no through-the-wall option. This is a flush-recess unit only. If you wanted mail to drop through the front into a lobby, this isn’t it. You’re committed to the recessed style.

Third, the included keys are basic. On a 5-unit building, that’s 10 keys total. You’ll want to rekey the cam locks or swap them for something more secure if you’re managing rentals with frequent tenant turnover.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Small business owners** with a dedicated entryway who want a professional-looking, secure mail solution for staff.
- **Landlords** with 5 or fewer units who own the building and want a permanent, theft-resistant fixture.
- **HOAs** replacing tired, rusted-out cluster boxes — provided you have the wall space and a contractor on call.

Skip it if you’re a homeowner looking for a simple curbside mailbox. This is commercial-grade hardware for multi-tenant situations.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this really USPS-approved?**
A: Yes, it carries USPS approval for the master-door access design. However, approval doesn’t force your local carrier to use it. Verify with your postmaster first.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: If you can frame a wall opening and handle basic wiring/framing, yes. Otherwise, hire a pro. The rough opening needs to be precise — the unit fits into a 24-inch by 14-inch cutout, and sloppy cuts show.

**Q: What’s the internal compartment size?**
A: Each of the five compartments is roughly 12 inches deep — enough for standard mail, small packages, and magazines. Larger parcels won’t fit, so plan for a package drop-off solution.
