The gooseneck arm is the star here. It's adjustable, which sounds like a gimmick until you're trying to angle light over a garage door or onto a specific walkway. The arm holds its position firmly once tightened — no sagging or drooping after months of weather exposure.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest gap: no photocell or motion sensor. For an outdoor fixture in 2026, that's a miss. You'll need a smart bulb or an external timer if you want dusk-to-dawn automation. It's an easy workaround, but it's an extra cost and an extra step.

The bulb isn't included either. The vintage Edison bulbs in the product photos look great, but they're sold separately — budget another $10–20 per fixture if you want that exact look.

Installation is straightforward but assumes a modern junction box. The mounting plate requires a standard 4-inch box. If your home was built before the 1980s and has smaller boxes, you'll need an adapter plate. Not a dealbreaker, but know it before you start.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this with a dimmer switch?**
Yes, as long as you use dimmable bulbs. The fixture itself has no dimming built in, but standard E26 bulbs and a compatible wall dimmer work fine.

**Q: Is it truly weatherproof?**
The housing is rated for wet locations and the gasket seals the glass well. It's held up in heavy rain and snow in customer reports. The exposed screws on the mounting plate are the weak point — a dab of silicone caulk during install prevents any moisture intrusion.

**Q: Does the arm hold position after adjustment?**
Yes. The joint uses a friction lock that holds firmly once tightened. Multiple reviewers confirmed no drooping over months of use.
