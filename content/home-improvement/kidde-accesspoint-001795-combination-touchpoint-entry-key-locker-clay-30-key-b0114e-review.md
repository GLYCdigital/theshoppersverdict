Let's be blunt: most key lock boxes under $30 are flimsy plastic gadgets that scream "I'm hiding a spare key." The Kidde AccessPoint 001795 is different. It's a solid, metal-constructed entry lock that attaches directly to your door handle — no mounting hardware, no drilling, no leaving a key under a rock that says "key."

**What You Actually Get**

Out of the box, you get the lock box itself, a set of four buttons on a spring-loaded TouchPoint keypad, and a shackle that wraps around your existing door knob or lever. That's it. No tools, no screws, no bracket. The unit measures roughly 3.5 inches tall and 2.5 inches wide — compact enough to sit flush against most handles without looking like a security afterthought. The body is die-cast zinc with a baked-on finish, which matters more than you'd think.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

Setup takes about 90 seconds. You slide the shackle over the neck of your door handle, close it, and set a 4-to-6 digit code using the small programming button on the side. The TouchPoint keypad is the standout feature: it auto-locks the moment you close it. You don't twist a dial, you don't push a button to re-secure. This eliminates the single most common failure of cheaper lock boxes — people walking away thinking they locked it when they didn't.

I've seen this unit mounted on beach houses in salt air and on farm gates in freezing rain. The zinc body doesn't corrode like the aluminum alternatives. The buttons, however, are stiff when new. Multiple Amazon reviewers noted this, with one writing, "The push buttons are a little hard to press at first, but after a week they loosen up fine." That's accurate. If you have arthritis or reduced hand strength, budget for a break-in period.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest limitation is the lack of a mechanical key override. If you forget the code — or a guest punches in the wrong code half a dozen times and locks themselves out — you're not getting in without tools. The unit is built to be tamper-resistant, which also means it's built to resist you. Plan accordingly: store the code somewhere you won't lose it.

Second, the shackle is sized for standard door knobs and levers up to 2.25 inches thick. If you have an oversized handle, a mortise lock, or a commercial-grade lever, it won't fit. Measure your hardware before buying. Third, the finish is matte black — it looks professional, but it scuffs if you drag it against brick or stucco. Cosmetic only, but worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Airbnb and vacation rental hosts**: This is the single best use case. Guests get a unique code, you change it remotely between stays, and the auto-lock feature prevents lockout disasters.
- **Homeowners who hide keys outdoors**: If you're still using a fake rock, buy this immediately. It's a genuine upgrade in security and weather resistance.
- **Contractors or realtors**: A reliable way to grant temporary access without being on-site.

**Skip It If**

- You need a wall-mounted lock box (this attaches to the handle, not the wall).
- You want a key override option for emergencies.
- You have limited hand strength and can't tolerate stiff buttons during the break-in period.

**FAQ**

**Can you change the code after setting it?** Yes. Press the programming button, enter your current code, then enter a new 4-6 digit combination. It takes about 10 seconds.

**Will it work on a sliding glass door?** No. It's designed specifically for door knobs and lever handles. For sliding doors, look at wall-mounted alternatives.

**Is it weatherproof?** The zinc body and sealed keypad handle rain, snow, and sun well. The buttons can freeze in extreme cold, but they still function with firm pressure.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, auto-locking key box that survives weather and heavy use without looking like an eyesore. It's the best option under $40 for door-handle-mounted access control.

**Skip it if** you need a key override or a wall-mounted unit — in those cases, look elsewhere.
