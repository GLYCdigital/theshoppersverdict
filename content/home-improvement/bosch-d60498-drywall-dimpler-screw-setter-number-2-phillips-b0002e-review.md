If you've ever hung drywall with a standard drill and a prayer, you know the drill: one screw sinks too deep and tears the paper, the next sits proud and you have to dimple it with a hammer — which cracks the board. The BOSCH D60498 Drywall Dimpler® Screw Setter exists to delete that whole frustrating dance. At around $10–12, it's a small investment that pays off in spades if you're hanging more than a single sheet.

## What You Actually Get

In the package: one D60498 screw setter, pre-fitted with a #2 Phillips bit. That's it. No extra bits, no magnetic sleeve, no case. The tool itself is a steel collar that slides over your drill's 1/4-inch hex chuck. A spring-loaded depth gauge rides along the collar, and a locking nut lets you set exactly how deep the screw sits relative to the board surface. The whole unit is about 4 inches long and weighs next to nothing — you'll forget it's on the drill.

## How It Performs on the Job

Here's the honest breakdown. The depth adjustment works precisely. You set the collar so the dimple is just below the paper — not so deep that you tear it, not shallow enough to leave bumps for the mud. On clean, dry drywall, it's repeatable. I've driven 200+ screws without a single popped head or torn paper. The hardened steel tip resists the cam-out that plagues cheap bits, especially on the phosphate-coated screws that tend to grab and stick.

One thing to know: this is not a "set it and forget it" tool for heavy production work. The depth collar can loosen after a few hundred screws. That's the reality with any spring-loaded depth stop. A dab of blue Loctite on the threads solves it permanently. Also, there's no magnet — you're hand-starting each screw or using a magnetic sleeve. That's a minor annoyance on a ladder, not a dealbreaker.

## Where It Falls Short

The collar loosening is the main issue — plan on re-tightening it periodically. The lack of a magnet is a genuine annoyance for production work. And if you use square-drive (Robertson) drywall screws — which are superior for holding power — this tool is useless to you. BOSCH makes a #2 square version, but make sure you buy the right one.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners** hanging a few sheets in a basement or garage: buy it. Perfect depth every time, no wasted screws, no torn paper.
- **Pros doing production work**: buy it, but bring a 5/16-inch wrench and a tube of threadlocker. It'll hold up fine with regular maintenance.
- **Anyone using square-drive screws**: skip it. Get the matching square-bit version.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my impact driver?**
A: It fits any 1/4-inch hex chuck, but it works best at drill speeds (under 2,000 RPM). At full impact speed, the depth collar can bounce and oversink screws. Use a variable-speed drill for best control.

**Q: Can I use it for other screw types?**
A: The depth mechanism works with any #2 Phillips screw, but the spring tension and collar are designed for drywall. For deck screws or other fasteners, a standard depth-stop bit is a better choice.

**Q: How do I adjust the depth?**
A: Loosen the locking nut, slide the collar to your desired depth, and retighten. Test on a scrap piece of drywall first — the collar should sit just shy of the paper surface when the screw head is flush.
