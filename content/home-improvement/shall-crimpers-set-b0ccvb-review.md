**Is the SHALL Crimpers Set the last crimping tool you'll need to buy?**

If you've ever fought with a cheap pair of crimpers that crush terminals sideways or leave you with loose connections that pull apart, you know the value of a proper ratcheting tool. The SHALL Crimpers Set promises pro-grade results at a DIY-friendly price point, and with 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in toolboxes. But is it genuinely worth the money, or is this just another Amazon special that looks better in photos than in practice? Here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box**

The SHALL set arrives in a molded hard case with a surprisingly complete lineup. You get the main ratcheting crimping tool, eight interchangeable die sets, a pair of wire strippers, and a cutting tool. As shown in the product image, the dies are clearly labeled and organized in the case's foam insert, so you won't be digging around trying to identify which jaw is which. The kit covers insulated connectors (22-10 AWG), non-insulated terminals, and open-barrel contacts — the three most common crimp types used in automotive, marine, and home electrical work.

**Performance and build quality**

The star of the show is the ratcheting mechanism. Unlike squeeze-and-hope crimpers, this tool locks onto the connector and won't release until the crimp is fully compressed. That's a game-changer for consistency. I've seen plenty of $15 crimpers that require Hulk-level grip strength to get a proper connection; the SHALL's ratcheting action spreads the effort across multiple squeezes, so even smaller hands can produce solid, professional-grade crimps.

The dies are machined, not stamped, which matters for the crimp profile. Stamped dies tend to deform over time and produce inconsistent results, but the machined steel jaws here hold their shape. The wire strippers are functional and include a crimper for butt splices, though they're not the main event — they're a convenience add-on, not a replacement for dedicated strippers.

One design note: changing dies requires loosening the pivot bolt on the side of the tool. It's not difficult — just a quarter-turn with the included wrench — but it's slower than quick-release systems where you pop a button and swap jaws. If you're switching between connector types frequently, this adds a few seconds to each change. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the reviews, a few themes stand out. Multiple buyers who do automotive wiring specifically call out the crimp quality. One reviewer noted, "I've used these on battery cables and trailer wiring — the crimps are solid and don't pull apart like my old tool." Another mentioned the ratcheting action specifically: "Finally, a crimper that doesn't hurt my hands after doing a dozen connectors."

The included case also gets consistent love. "Everything stays organized, and the case is compact enough to toss in my truck's toolbox," one buyer wrote. For a kit that includes multiple die sets, organization matters — loose dies are how tools get lost.

**Where it falls short**

The case latches are the weak point. Several reviewers report cracked hinges after months of regular use, particularly if the case gets tossed around a job site. It's not a functional issue with the crimping itself, but if you're rough on your tools, plan on the case eventually needing a rubber band or a replacement.

The pivot bolt die-change system, while functional, is also a minor annoyance during high-volume work. If you're doing production runs where you're switching between wire gauges constantly, a quick-change system would serve you better. For a homeowner or weekend mechanic, it's a non-issue.

**Who should buy this**

This kit is ideal for the DIYer who does enough electrical work that cheap crimpers are a frustration, but not enough to justify dropping $150+ on professional-grade tools. Automotive enthusiasts, RV owners, and boaters who need reliable connections in harsh environments will get real value here. It's also a solid choice for apprentices or hobbyists building out their first serious tool kit.

If you're a professional electrician doing production work day in and day out, you'll probably want something with faster die changes and a better case. But for 90% of buyers, this set hits the sweet spot.

**FAQ**

**Does this work on coax or RJ45 connectors?**
No. This is designed for wire terminals and connectors — insulated, non-insulated, and open-barrel styles. For network or coax crimping, you'll need a separate tool.

**Can I use it on 12 AWG house wiring?**
Yes, the dies cover up to 10 AWG, so standard 12 AWG Romex is well within range. Just make sure you're using the correct die for the connector type.

**Is the ratcheting mechanism adjustable?**
The ratchet tension is fixed, but the dies are interchangeable to accommodate different wire gauges. For most users, the standard tension produces correct crimps without adjustment.

**The Verdict**

**Skip it if** you're a pro who needs rapid die changes, or you only crimp a connector once a year — in which case a $10 basic crimper will do.
