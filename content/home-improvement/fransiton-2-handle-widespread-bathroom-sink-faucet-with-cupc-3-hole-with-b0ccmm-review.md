## What You Actually Get

The box contains the faucet body, two handles with metal levers, and the spout. That's it. No supply lines, no drain assembly. For a 3-hole widespread setup, you're looking at roughly $15–25 extra for braided supply hoses and another $10–20 if you need a pop-up drain. Factor that into your total cost before checkout — it's the most common surprise in the reviews.

The finish is brushed nickel (also available in chrome and matte black), and the body is solid brass with a cUPC certification stamped on it. That certification matters: it means the faucet meets Uniform Plumbing Code standards for lead content and pressure performance, which isn't something you get from every no-name import at this price point.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The ceramic disc cartridges are the standout feature here. They give you a smooth quarter-turn operation — hot and cold handles rotate with a satisfying, precise click rather than the mushy feel you get from rubber washers. This is the kind of mechanism that holds up for years without dripping. Multiple buyers note the handles feel "solid and heavy" in hand, which tracks with the brass construction.

The spout reaches 6.5 inches from the base, which is adequate for most drop-in and vessel sinks. Water flow is steady at 1.2 GPM — compliant with California and federal water restrictions, but still strong enough for handwashing and toothbrushing without feeling weak.

One consistent praise point in the reviews: the installation. The pre-attached mounting nuts and clear instructions make this a DIY-friendly project. One reviewer put it simply: "Took me 30 minutes to replace my old faucet. Everything lined up perfectly." The adjustable centers (8–16 inches) mean it fits standard 3-hole configurations without modification.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest gripe in the reviews is the missing parts. Supply lines aren't included, which is unusual at this price point — most competitors in the $60–100 range throw in braided hoses. You'll also need to buy a drain separately, which adds to the total cost and feels like nickel-and-diming.

The finish is another point of contention. While it looks good out of the box, several buyers report water spots and fingerprints showing up quickly on the brushed nickel. It's not a defect — it's the nature of the finish — but if you hate wiping down fixtures, consider the matte black option instead.

Finally, the handles are on the smaller side. If you have large hands or limited grip strength, the levers can feel a bit fiddly to operate. Not a dealbreaker, but worth noting.
