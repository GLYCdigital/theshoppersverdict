Curb appeal is one of those things you don't think about until a visitor drives past your house three times looking for numbers that have faded into the siding. The ADDRESSES OF DISTINCTION Oak Mailbox & Post Kit in black solves that problem with something that looks less like a mailbox and more like a piece of furniture for your front yard. With a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. Here's what you actually need to know before handing over your money.

## What You Actually Get

The kit includes the black powder-coated mailbox, a matching aluminum post, a mounting bracket, pre-cut address numbers, and all the hardware needed for assembly. As shown in the product image, the design is clean—a rounded oak-grain-textured body on a straight post with a contrasting black finish. The address numbers mount on the side, which means they're visible from the street even when the mailbox door is open. That's a small detail, but it matters when delivery drivers are scanning for your house number at 7 a.m.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The post is aluminum, not steel. That's a deliberate choice—aluminum won't rust or corrode the way steel does after a few winters of road salt spray. One buyer noted, "Three years in and it still looks like the day I installed it. The finish has held up through Michigan winters without peeling or fading." That tracks with the powder-coated surface, which is far more durable than sprayed-on paint.

The mounting bracket is beefy—thick enough that you won't worry about the mailbox flexing in high winds. Assembly is straightforward but not a five-minute job. You'll need a drill, a level, and if you're setting the post in concrete, a masonry bit. Budget 45 to 60 minutes if you're handy, longer if you're not. One reviewer warned, "The instructions are minimal. If you've never set a post before, watch a YouTube video first." Fair advice.

## Where It Falls Short

The mailbox body is smaller than a standard rural mailbox. That means magazines, small boxes, and most Amazon deliveries will fit, but anything larger than a shoebox won't. One buyer put it bluntly: "If you order big items regularly, this will frustrate you. The mail carrier left a note saying packages wouldn't fit." Consider your delivery habits before committing.

The address numbers are plastic, not metal. They look fine out of the box, but they're the most likely component to fade or crack over time. Several reviewers mentioned replacing them within a couple of years. Cheap and easy to swap, but worth noting.

## Who Should Buy This

This kit is ideal for homeowners who want a distinctive, polished look without paying a contractor for a custom installation. It's a strong fit for new builds, renovation projects, or anyone replacing a rusted-out mailbox that's past its prime. If you live in an area with heavy snow or coastal salt air, the aluminum post makes it a smarter choice than steel alternatives.

Skip it if you get frequent large deliveries, prefer a traditional oversized mailbox, or want a zero-assembly option. This is a project, not a plug-and-play purchase.

## FAQ

**Can the post be set in gravel instead of concrete?**
Technically yes, but concrete is recommended. The aluminum post is lightweight, and without a solid base, it can shift in soft ground or freeze-thaw cycles. Use at least one 50-pound bag of concrete mix for stability.

**Are the address numbers customizable?**
The kit includes numbers for the address you specify at purchase. You can't rearrange them, so double-check your house number before ordering. Replacement numbers are available separately if you need them.

**Will the black finish fade in direct sun?**
Powder coating resists UV damage far better than paint. Most buyers report no noticeable fading after two to three years, even in full sun exposure.
