Don't let the ceiling-hugging design fool you. The LiftMaster RJO 98032 is a serious piece of hardware that solves a real problem — but it comes with a catch that's driving some owners up the wall. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 180+ verified buyer experiences.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the wall-mount power unit itself, a battery backup (pre-installed in the housing), mounting hardware, and the standard two 3-button remote controls. Notably absent: safety sensor kit. For a unit at this premium price point, that omission stings. You'll need to source photo eyes separately unless you're retrofitting an existing system.

The jackshaft design mounts on the wall beside your garage door, connecting directly to the torsion bar. This is the same layout used in commercial applications, and it does exactly what it promises — reclaims all that overhead ceiling space a traditional trolley opener consumes.

## Performance and Build Quality

The DC motor delivers genuinely quiet, smooth operation. Acceleration and deceleration are controlled and gentle — no jerky start-stop that rattles the door tracks. The soft-start/soft-stop feature is immediately noticeable and appreciated.

Battery backup integration is the standout feature. When power drops, the opener automatically switches to battery mode. Multiple buyers report it working flawlessly during outages, with enough juice for 10-20 cycles depending on door weight. That's real peace of mind.

But here's where the RJO 98032 splits opinion. The built-in WiFi and camera functionality — a major selling point — is inconsistent. Set up the myQ app and you'll get push notifications, remote open/close, and camera views when it works. The problem is that "when." Reviewers consistently report dropped connections, app sync failures, and setup headaches. One buyer summarized: *"Great opener, terrible app integration. Works 70% of the time, which isn't good enough for a connected device."*

## What Owners Praise

- **Space savings**: Genuinely transformative for garages with storage racks or anyone parking a lifted truck. Multiple owners call this the primary reason they chose it.
- **Battery backup reliability**: Several reviewers detail power outages where the door kept working seamlessly.
- **Quiet operation**: Consistently described as quieter than belt-drive systems, especially during opening cycles.

## Where It Falls Short

- **WiFi reliability is the elephant in the room**. Roughly a quarter of written reviews mention connection issues — some resolved after router reconfiguration, others never fully fixed. If you're buying this for smart-home integration, set expectations accordingly.
- **Installation difficulty is underestimated**. This isn't a weekend DIY for most homeowners. The torsion bar must be properly aligned, spring tension matters significantly, and non-standard doors often require additional mounting kits. Several reviewers report needing professional installation after struggling — factor in $150-$250 for a pro if you're not mechanically confident.
- **Missing safety sensors** at this price point feel like nickel-and-diming.
- **The integrated camera** is basic — no pan/tilt, and some users report poor night vision quality.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're replacing a ceiling-mounted opener in a garage with limited headroom, you want battery backup for outage resilience, and you're okay with a possibly finicky app experience. Ideal for owners of lifted trucks, SUVs with roof racks, or anyone wanting attic storage access.

**Skip it if** you're a smart-home enthusiast who needs rock-solid app reliability, or if you want a straightforward DIY install. For those cases, a traditional belt-drive opener with a separate battery backup might serve you better at lower cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with all garage doors?**
A: No. Standard residential sectional doors with torsion bars — yes. One-piece doors, low-headroom tracks, or doors with extension springs will need extra parts or won't work at all. Check your door type before buying.

**Q: Is the battery backup easy to replace?**
A: Yes, it's a user-serviceable battery unit that slides out. Replacement batteries run about $60-$80. Expect 3-5 years of life depending on usage.

**Q: Can I bypass WiFi and just use the remotes?**
A: Absolutely. The remotes work independently of WiFi. The connectivity features are additive — if the app gives you trouble, the door still operates normally.

## The Verdict

The LiftMaster RJO 98032 delivers where it matters most — mechanical performance, quiet operation, and that invaluable battery backup. The space-saving design is genuinely excellent. The WiFi inconsistency and missing sensors hold it back from greatness.

**Buy it if**: Your garage layout demands a wall-mount opener and you value outage protection. You're prepared for professional installation.

**Skip it if**: You need flawless smart-home integration, or you're expecting a plug-and-play DIY project.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A mechanically excellent product with smart features that don't always live up to the billing.
