**The 60-inch ceiling fan market is crowded with flimsy options that move more noise than air. The YITAHOME 60 Inch 5-Blade model isn't one of them.** 

With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this fan has earned its place in the conversation for anyone cooling a great room, open-concept living area, or oversized master bedroom. I've dug through the specs, the build, and what real owners are saying. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box:** You get the fan motor housing, five blades, an integrated 24W LED light kit, a remote control with receiver, and all mounting hardware. The canopy and downrod are included, so you don't need to source extra parts for a standard flat or angled ceiling. The blades have a sleek, matte finish that resists fingerprints — a small detail, but one that matters in a high-traffic living space.

**Build quality and real-world performance:** This is a DC motor fan, which is the right call at this price point. It's rated for rooms up to 400 square feet, and at 60 inches, the blade sweep genuinely covers that space. Owners consistently mention how much air it moves. One reviewer noted, *"I was skeptical about a 60-inch fan at this price, but it pushes air across my 20x20 living room with zero wobble."* The integrated light is dimmable and offers three color temperatures (3000K warm, 4000K neutral, 6000K daylight), which is a flexibility you rarely get without buying a separate smart bulb. The remote controls both fan speed (6 speeds) and light dimming, plus a reverse function for winter use.

**Where it falls short:** The installation is the main friction point. At roughly 18 pounds, this isn't a heavy fan, but the canopy alignment is finicky. Multiple buyers mention needing a second set of hands. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Took two of us an hour and a half. The hardest part was getting the canopy to seat flush."* If you're not comfortable with basic electrical work, budget for a pro. Also, the remote is infrared, not RF. That means it needs line-of-sight to the receiver. You can't hide the remote in a drawer and use it from another room. A wall-mounted control is available but sold separately — a small cost, but worth knowing upfront.

**Who should buy this:** 
- **The large-room owner** who needs serious airflow without a noisy, wobbly fan. 
- **The style-conscious buyer** who wants a modern, low-profile look that doesn't dominate the room.
- **The energy-conscious shopper** — the DC motor draws significantly less power than AC motors at the same speed, which adds up on your electric bill over a season.

**Who should skip it:** 
- **Single-person DIY installers** who don't have a helper available. 
- **Anyone needing a second remote or wall control** — factor in the extra cost.
- **Low-ceiling rooms under 8 feet** — the downrod is standard length, and you'll want clearance.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Is this fan quiet enough for a bedroom?** 
Yes, at lower speeds it's nearly silent. At top speed, you'll hear air movement, but the motor hum is minimal. Several owners use it in bedrooms with no complaints.

**Q: Does the light work without the fan running?** 
Yes, the light and fan functions are independent. You can run the light alone, the fan alone, or both together.

**Q: Can this fan be installed on a sloped ceiling?** 
The included hardware supports up to a 15-degree angled ceiling. For steeper slopes, you'll need a separate angled-mount adapter.

**The Verdict:**

**Buy it if** you need a large-format fan with integrated lighting that actually cools a big space without sounding like a helicopter. The DC motor, dimmable light, and 60-inch sweep justify the price.

**Skip it if** you're installing solo, or you want a remote that works through walls without line-of-sight.
