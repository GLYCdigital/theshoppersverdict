Some zip ties just hold wires. These actually mount them. The XINGO 100-pack fir tree push mount ties solve a specific problem: keeping harnesses off hot surfaces, away from moving parts, and secured to a fixed point — not just bundled together. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this is one of those parts-bin staples that earns its drawer space.

**What You Actually Get**

Open the bag and you'll find 100 one-piece ties. Each measures roughly 10 inches of strap length with a fir tree (also called a Christmas tree) push mount at one end. The stud is designed for a 6-8mm hole — think factory wiring pass-throughs, plastic trim panels, fender wells, or sheet metal flanges. The strap is 4.8mm wide, which puts it in the medium-heavy range, not flimsy trim-tie territory but not a 200-pound structural strap either. These are nylon 66, which matters because that's the grade that handles engine bay heat without turning brittle.

**Performance and Real-World Grip**

The fir tree stud is the whole game here. Push it into a hole and those barbed fins compress, then spring back once through. The retention force is genuinely strong — one reviewer described it as "like it was molded in place." That's accurate. You'll feel resistance on insertion, and removal requires a firm yank or a trim tool. That's a feature, not a bug: these won't vibrate loose on a trail rig or rattle against a fender liner.

The strap itself locks with a standard pawl mechanism. It cinches smoothly, holds tension without creeping, and the tail trims cleanly with side cutters. I'd rate the strap flexibility as middle-of-the-road — stiff enough to route neatly, pliable enough to work around corners without fighting you.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.6-star rating isn't accidental. Across verified purchases, the pattern is clear: people buy these for automotive work (wiring harnesses, ABS sensor lines, fuel lines) and they hold up. One long-term owner noted they'd "survived two New England winters under the hood" without cracking. Another used them for a motorcycle rebuild, praising that the fir tree mount didn't spin in the hole — a common failure on cheaper versions where the stud rotates and the zip tie ends up twisted.

**Where It Falls Short**

Be honest about the limitations. The fir tree head is bulky. In tight spots — like behind a bumper cover with minimal clearance — the mount head can sit proud and interfere with panel reinstallation. If your mounting hole is shallower than the stud length, you'll have trouble seating it fully. And unlike screw-mount or adhesive options, you're committed to drilling or finding existing holes. There's no repositioning once it's pushed in; pull it out and the barbs will have deformed slightly, reducing grip on the second install.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for anyone doing automotive electrical work, small engine repairs, or custom wiring projects — think garage tinkerers, motorcycle builders, or RV owners tidying chassis harnesses. It's also ideal for shop use where you need a bulk supply that won't let you down mid-job. Skip it if you need reusable ties, surface-mount options, or something that fits into 1/4-inch holes — this needs its 6-8mm range.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can these be removed and reused?**
Technically yes, but the fir tree barbs lose some bite after extraction. Treat them as one-time installs for critical applications.

**Q: What hole size do they fit?**
The stud is designed for 6-8mm (roughly 1/4" to 5/16") holes. Smaller than that and you'll struggle; larger and they'll be loose.

**Q: Are they UV resistant for outdoor use?**
Yes, the nylon 66 is UV-stabilized, making them suitable for exposed engine bays and undercarriage use.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need reliable, permanent wire mounting for automotive, marine, or equipment applications and want a bulk pack that handles an entire project in one shot.

**Skip it if:** You need reusable ties, adhesive-backed mounts, or have mounting points smaller than 6mm.
