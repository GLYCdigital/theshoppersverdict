Utility sinks get abused. They wash paintbrushes, muddy boots, mop heads, and whatever else you drag in from the garage. The JS Jackson Supplies Dual Handle Pull Out Utility-Sink Faucet is built for that punishment — mostly. Here's what 210 Amazon ratings at 4.2 stars actually tell you, and what they don't.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the faucet body, dual handles, pull-out sprayer head, deck plate, and braided stainless supply lines. The metal construction is the headline here — this isn't a plastic-bodied faucet that'll crack when you knock a bucket into it. The sprayer head has a 360-degree swivel, which matters more than you'd think when you're trying to rinse the back corner of a deep laundry sink.

The pull-out hose extends roughly 24 inches. That's enough for most utility sink setups, though if you have a massive commercial-style basin, you might find yourself stretching.

## Real-World Performance

The dual handle setup gives you independent hot and cold control. That sounds basic, but for a utility faucet it's actually a differentiator — single-handle models can be finicky about temperature. One reviewer noted, "I can set the hot side where I want it and just adjust cold with one hand while holding a hose with the other."

Water pressure is strong — maybe too strong. There's no flow restrictor, so full-blast output can splash out of shallow sinks. If your utility sink is a shallow basin, plan to run it at partial pressure or you'll be mopping the floor.

The sprayer has two modes — stream and spray. The spray mode is genuinely useful for rinsing caked-on mud off tools. One buyer said, "The sprayer actually has some bite to it. It stripped mud off my garden trowel in seconds."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The build quality gets the most love. Multiple reviewers used the word "solid" without prompting. From the reviews: "I've bought three cheap utility faucets in the last two years. This one actually feels like it'll last." The braided supply lines are another recurring positive — they're not the thin, kink-prone vinyl hoses you see on budget faucets.

The 360-degree swivel is also a consistent winner. Being able to spin the spout and sprayer without moving the whole faucet makes a real difference in a cramped laundry room or workshop corner.

## Where It Falls Short

The deck plate is the most common complaint. It's sized for standard 3-hole sinks with 6-inch centers, but several buyers found it didn't cover their existing holes. Measure your sink's hole spacing before you buy — this is the #1 reason for returns.

The hose kinking issue comes up a few times. The pull-out hose is braided, which is good, but if you don't route it carefully during installation, it can bind. Take the time to feed it through cleanly, and you'll avoid the frustration others hit.

One more thing: no flow restrictor means you're getting full line pressure. Good for cleaning, bad for splashing. It's a tradeoff, not a defect.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners with a laundry room or mudroom sink** — this will handle everything you throw at it
- **Garage workshop users** — the sprayer power is a genuine asset for cleaning tools
- **Anyone replacing a cheap faucet that already cracked** — the metal body is a real upgrade

**Skip it if** you have a non-standard sink hole layout, or if you need a faucet with a built-in flow restrictor for a shallow basin.

A: It fits standard 6-inch center spacing. If your sink has wider or irregular hole spacing, the deck plate may not cover everything. Measure first.

**Q: Is the sprayer hose long enough for a deep sink?**

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: Yes, if you're comfortable with basic plumbing. The braided lines make hookup straightforward. The main challenge is routing the pull-out hose without kinks — take your time with that step.
