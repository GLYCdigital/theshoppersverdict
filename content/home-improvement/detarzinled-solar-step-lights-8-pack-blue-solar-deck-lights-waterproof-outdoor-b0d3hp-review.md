## Where They Fall Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**The charging angle is the weak point.** If your steps face north or sit under a porch overhang, these will charge slowly and dim early. Several reviewers reported shorter runtime with indirect sun exposure. This isn't a defect — it's physics — but it's worth planning around.

**The blue light is a taste choice.** It looks sharp on modern homes but can feel cold against warm-toned wood or brick. If you're on the fence, the warm white version is the safer bet for traditional exteriors.

**Plastic housing, not metal.** They feel solid, but they're not armored. A direct hit from a lawnmower or heavy foot traffic could crack the casing. Mount them on risers, not tread surfaces, to avoid stomping damage.

## FAQ

**Q: How long do these actually last on a full charge?**
Expect 6-8 hours in summer. Less in cloudy weather or with partial sun exposure. The battery is replaceable, which extends the unit's life beyond typical disposable solar lights.

**Q: Will these work in freezing winter temperatures?**
Lithium batteries lose capacity in the cold. They'll still function but with reduced runtime. If you get hard freezes, consider removing them or accepting diminished performance from November through February.

**Q: Do the blue LEDs attract bugs?**
Less than warm white lights. Blue wavelengths are less attractive to most flying insects, so this is actually a bonus for patio seating areas.
