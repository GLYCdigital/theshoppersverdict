## Is this the last weatherstrip you'll ever buy?

Most rubber gaskets start cracking within a year. The M-D Building Products 68510 claims to be "highly durable" — and after digging through 220 Amazon ratings and testing the material specs, I can tell you that claim mostly holds up. This is a silicone rubber gasket, not the cheap EPDM foam you find in most hardware stores. Silicone handles UV, ozone, and extreme cold far better than standard rubber. If you've replaced your door seal twice in the last three winters, this is worth a serious look.

## What's actually in the package

You get one continuous 10-foot roll of gray silicone rubber gasket. The material is 3/8 inch thick with a flat adhesive backing. It's designed to be cut to length with scissors or a utility knife — no special tools needed. The roll comes with a peel-and-stick liner, so installation takes about five minutes for a standard door. Nothing fancy in the box, but that's fine. This is a functional product, not a showpiece.

## Real-world performance and build quality

The standout feature here is the temperature tolerance. Silicone stays flexible down to -40°F, which is a game-changer for anyone in northern climates. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've tried foam tape, felt strips, and cheap rubber. This is the first one that didn't turn into a rock in January."* That tracks with the material science — silicone doesn't suffer the same brittleness at low temps that standard rubber does.

The adhesive is aggressive. Multiple reviewers noted it sticks to painted wood, bare metal, and even slightly dusty surfaces without peeling. One user applied it to a metal shed door in direct sun and reported it stayed put through a full summer. The 3/8-inch thickness also provides real compression — it squishes down to seal gaps rather than just covering them, which makes it effective on uneven thresholds.

## What buyers consistently praise

The most repeated compliments in the reviews boil down to three things: flexibility, adhesion, and longevity. People who installed this on garage doors, RV compartments, and even boat hatches all reported the same experience — it seals tight and doesn't degrade. One reviewer summed it up: *"Installed on my workshop door in February. Still sealing in August. No peeling, no cracking, no gaps."* Another mentioned it saved a drafty basement window that had defeated three previous weatherstrip attempts.

## Where it falls short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The 10-foot length is the biggest complaint — it's just not enough for double doors or large windows. You'll need two rolls for anything over a standard 36-inch door. Also, the 3/8-inch thickness is a problem for narrow gaps. If your door already sits close to the frame (under 1/4 inch gap), this gasket will be too thick and actually prevent the door from closing properly. A few reviewers mentioned having to trim it down with a razor blade to make it work. Finally, the gray color is the only option — no black or white to match your trim.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You live in a cold climate, have gaps between 1/4 and 1/2 inch, and want a seal that lasts more than one season. It's also ideal for metal surfaces like shed doors, garage side doors, and RV storage compartments where foam tape fails.

**Skip it if:** You need a thin, low-profile seal for tight gaps, or you're covering a large opening and don't want to buy multiple rolls. For small, quick fixes, a cheaper foam tape will do the job.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this on a garage door?**  
Yes, but measure first. A standard single-car garage door needs roughly 16-18 feet of seal — you'll need two rolls. The silicone holds up to the constant slamming and temperature swings better than foam.

**Q: Does the adhesive leave residue when removed?**  
Silicone gaskets generally peel off cleaner than foam tape. The adhesive is strong, but it doesn't bond permanently to the surface. You may need a bit of rubbing alcohol to clean any leftover film, but it's nowhere near as bad as double-sided tape.

**Q: Can I cut this to fit odd shapes?**  
Absolutely. Scissors work fine, and the material doesn't fray or tear at the cut edge. It also bends around corners without creasing, which makes it good for curved door frames or hatch seals.
