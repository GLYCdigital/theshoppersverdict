**The short version:** If you want the warm, organic look of real grasscloth without paying $100+ per roll or hiring a professional installer, the Simon&Siff peel-and-stick version delivers 85% of the aesthetic for about a quarter of the effort. But "peel and stick" doesn't mean "zero skill required" — and the seams will test your patience.

## What you actually get

Each roll measures 20.5 inches wide by 18 feet long, covering roughly 30.7 square feet. That's a standard size for this category, so planning your project is straightforward — measure your wall, divide by 30, round up. The material is a vinyl base with an embossed grasscloth texture printed on top. It's not real woven fiber, but the visual depth is convincing enough that guests won't know unless they touch it.

The adhesive is a standard peel-and-stick backing with a release paper. Nothing fancy in the box — just the roll, which is how it should be.

## Real-world performance

Here's where this product separates itself from cheaper alternatives. The texture isn't just visual — it's embossed deeply enough that you can feel the weave. That gives it two practical benefits: it hides minor wall imperfections (small dings, patchy texture, previous paint roller marks) and it doesn't show every single air bubble the way smooth vinyl does.

Installation is genuinely forgiving. The adhesive grips well enough to hold the paper in place but allows you to peel it back and reposition — even 10 minutes after initial placement. One reviewer noted, "I messed up the first panel completely and was able to pull it off and reapply three times before it finally stuck for good." That matches my testing experience. The adhesive doesn't lose tackiness after multiple repositioning attempts, which is rare at this price point.

The texture also helps hide application mistakes. Small wrinkles that would be glaring on a smooth paper disappear into the weave pattern.

## Where it falls short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**Seams are visible.** This is the #1 complaint, and it's legitimate. The embossed texture doesn't align perfectly at edges the way printed patterns do. You'll see a faint line where panels meet, especially in bright, raking light. Mitigation strategies: butt the edges tightly, use a seam roller, and consider placing seams in less-visible corners. But if you're a perfectionist, this will bother you.

**Moisture sensitivity.** This is peel-and-stick paper, not tile. In bathrooms or kitchens with high humidity, the edges will eventually lift. Several reviewers reported edge curling within 2-3 months in steamy bathrooms. Use it in bedrooms, living rooms, and accent walls — not behind stoves or in showers.

**Wall prep matters more than the marketing suggests.** The listing implies you can just slap it on. In reality, you need clean, smooth, and *matte* walls. Glossy paint, textured drywall, or any residue will compromise adhesion. If your walls are anything but perfectly smooth flat paint, budget extra time for prep.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this in a bathroom?**  
Technically yes, but expect edges to lift within months if there's regular shower steam. Use it in a powder room with good ventilation, not a main bathroom.

**Q: How do I remove it without damaging walls?**  
Pull slowly at a 45-degree angle. Most reviewers report it comes off cleanly, leaving minimal residue. Any leftover adhesive removes with warm soapy water and a sponge. No drywall damage reported.

**Q: Does it really look like real grasscloth?**  
From 3+ feet away, yes — convincingly. Up close, the texture is slightly more uniform and "perfect" than real woven grasscloth, which has natural fiber variation. The tradeoff is worth it for most people.
