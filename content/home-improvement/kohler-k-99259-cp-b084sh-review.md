If you’ve priced bathroom faucets lately, you know the gap between a $40 builder-grade special and a $150+ Kohler is more than just the logo. The K-99259-CP sits firmly in the “buy once, cry once” territory. Here’s the real question: does it justify the premium over the cheap stuff? After digging through 204 ratings and the spec sheet, here’s what you need to know.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box, you get the faucet body, two lever handles, supply lines (already attached—thankfully), and mounting hardware. The polished chrome finish is the star here. As shown in the product image, the design is clean and traditional without feeling dated. It’ll blend with everything from farmhouse to contemporary bathrooms.

Not included: the drain assembly. That’s a $30–$60 add-on depending on whether you go with Kohler’s matching unit or a generic pop-up drain. Annoying, but common at this price point.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

This is where Kohler earns its reputation. The body is solid brass, not the zinc alloy you’ll find on cheaper competitors. That matters for two reasons: it won’t corrode from the inside, and it feels substantial when you operate the handles. The ceramic disc valves are the real upgrade—they deliver a smooth quarter-turn action and don’t develop drips over time like rubber washers do.

The polished chrome is forgiving in practice. Multiple buyers noted it wipes clean easily and doesn’t show every water drop the way brushed nickel does. One reviewer summed it up: *“I’ve had mine for six months, and it still looks brand new with just a weekly wipe-down.”* The spray pattern is consistent—no sputtering or uneven flow, even at lower water pressure.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The handle action gets repeated mentions. It’s not the flimsy, loose-feeling operation you get on budget faucets. The lever handles have a satisfying, precise detent at the temperature stops. For anyone with arthritis or limited hand strength, the ADA-compliant levers are a genuine benefit, not a marketing checkbox.

Finish durability is the other standout. *“Two years in, no pitting, no peeling, no spots,”* reported one long-term owner. That’s the polished chrome doing its job—it’s a harder, more durable finish than most PVD coatings on cheaper faucets.

## Where It Falls Short

The missing drain assembly is the most common complaint, and it’s legitimate. When you’re spending this much, the expectation is a complete kit. Budget an extra $40 if you’re starting from scratch.

Installation is straightforward but has one gotcha: the deck thickness requirement. If your vanity top is thinner than 1-1/4 inches—many prefab vanities are—you’ll need a shim kit or the mounting threads won’t engage properly. That’s an extra trip to the hardware store.

The lever handles are also wider than some compact faucets. On a 4-inch centerset configuration, they’re fine, but if your sink is tight against a backsplash, measure before you buy.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The replace-it-once homeowner**: If you’re tired of fixing drips every year, the ceramic valves are worth the price alone.
- **Anyone with mobility concerns**: The lever handles are genuinely easy to operate, even with wet, soapy hands.
- **Traditional bathroom renovations**: The design plays well with classic tile, white fixtures, and wood vanities.

Skip it if you’re on a strict budget, need a faucet with a built-in drain, or have an unusually thin vanity top without a shim kit ready.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the polished chrome finish prone to water spots?**
A: Less than you’d think. Polished chrome actually sheds water better than brushed finishes. A quick wipe with a microfiber cloth keeps it spotless, and it won’t show fingerprints like stainless steel.

**Q: Does this faucet work with a tankless water heater?**
A: Yes. The ceramic valves don’t add significant flow restriction, and the supply lines are standard 3/8-inch compression fittings. Just make sure your water pressure is above 20 PSI.

**Q: What’s the actual flow rate?**
A: 1.2 gallons per minute—a WaterSense-certified rate that’s noticeably less than older 2.2 GPM faucets. You won’t notice the difference for handwashing, but it’s worth knowing if you plan to fill pots or basins from the tap.
