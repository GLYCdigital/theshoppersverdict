If you're cutting cabochons seriously, you already know the pain of switching wheels between grits mid-project. The CabKing 6-wheel system eliminates that shuffle entirely. It's not cheap, and it's not perfect, but after 240 Amazon ratings averaging 4.8 stars, this machine has earned its reputation as the workhorse of hobbyist lapidary.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the assembled head unit (motor, arbor, and splash hood), six diamond-impregnated wheels mounted in sequence from 80-grit shaping through 14,000-grit final polish, a recirculating water pump, and the necessary hosing. The base is a separate steel frame you bolt together—expect 30-45 minutes of setup. The 6" wheels run on a 1/2 HP sealed motor that handles continuous duty without overheating. As shown in the product image, the splash hood covers the top half of the wheels; the exposed lower portion is where you do the work.

**Performance and real-world use**

The genius here is workflow. You move from rough shaping on the 80-grit wheel to preform on 220, then 600, 1200, 3000, and 14,000 for the final polish. No wheel changes, no re-truing mid-project. The water system drips continuously onto each wheel face, keeping both stone and diamond surface cool. One reviewer put it plainly: "I went from taking two hours to cab a stone to forty-five minutes. The 14,000-grit wheel gives a mirror finish that needs no additional cerium oxide."

The motor has real torque. Hard materials like agate, jasper, and petrified wood don't stall it. The variable speed control (though not digital) gives you enough range to slow down for delicate work on the finer wheels.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.8-star rating isn't an accident. Recurring themes in verified reviews: the finish quality straight off the final wheel, the time savings versus single-wheel machines, and the quiet operation. One long-term owner noted, "Two years of weekly use and still on the original wheels. I've cabbed over 300 stones." Another mentioned the sealed bearing design—no grit getting into the motor, which is the #1 killer of cheaper machines.

**Where it falls short**

The splash control is the weak point. The hood covers the top, but water sprays forward and sideways. Reviewers consistently mention wet shirts and slippery floors. You'll want a rubber mat and probably a shop apron. The manual is minimal—one buyer called it "a photocopied afterthought." Setup isn't hard, but the instructions assume you've run a lapidary machine before. The 6" wheel size also means you're limited to cabochons roughly 2 inches or smaller; larger slabs need a bigger machine.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the serious hobbyist or small-production cabber. If you're cutting stones weekly and want to move from "I can make cabs" to "I can make 20 cabs in an afternoon," this is your machine. Beginners should probably start with a flat lap or a cheaper single-wheel unit to confirm the hobby sticks. And if you're only cutting the occasional stone, the price tag is hard to justify.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this for faceting?* No. It's for cabochons—domed stones. Faceting requires a completely different machine with an adjustable angle arm.

*Are replacement wheels expensive?* The diamond wheels are the main consumable. They last 1-3 years depending on use, and replacements run $40-60 each. The 14,000-grit wheel wears fastest.

*Does it need special electrical hookup?* No. Standard 110V household outlet. It draws about 6 amps.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You cut cabochons regularly and want professional results without a $3,000 shop setup. The workflow efficiency alone justifies the cost for active hobbyists.

**Skip it if:** You're new to lapidary, only cut occasionally, or need to work with stones larger than 2 inches. The splash issue also makes it a poor choice for carpeted or finished spaces.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The CabKing earns its keep. It's not flawless, but for the price, there's no better 6-wheel cabbing machine on the market. The 240 buyers who rated it 4.8 stars aren't wrong.
