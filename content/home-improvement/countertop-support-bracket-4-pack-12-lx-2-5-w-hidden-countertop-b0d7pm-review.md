**What you actually get**

Four steel brackets, each measuring 12 inches long and 2.5 inches wide. The design is a flat L-shape that slides under the countertop and mounts to the cabinet side or wall stud. The "hidden" part means the bracket face sits flush against the underside of your countertop — no lip, no visible ledge, no obtrusive metal line running across your cabinet face. As shown in the product image, the finish is matte black, which disappears under most overhangs.

Each bracket comes with mounting screws. That's it — no template, no shims, no instructions beyond what’s on the listing page. For the price of a 4-pack, you’re getting roughly what you’d pay for two single brackets at a hardware store, so the value math works out.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The steel is 2.5 inches wide at the mounting face, which gives you a solid footprint against the cabinet frame. The 12-inch depth is the sweet spot for standard 24-inch deep countertops with a typical 12-inch overhang — that's the exact scenario where you need support without a visible leg or corbel. The brackets are rated to handle the weight of standard laminate, quartz, and mid-weight natural stone installations when anchored into solid framing.

Where this bracket genuinely shines is the hidden design. Unlike corbels or angled support arms, there's no decorative metal visible from the front. If you have a clean, modern kitchen or a minimalist breakfast bar, that matters. One reviewer put it well: *"I didn’t want a bracket that looked like it belonged in a barn. **Where it falls short — honest talk**

The included screws are functional but nothing special. For a heavy stone countertop, I’d swap them for 2-inch #10 or #12 wood screws into studs or cabinet frames. If you’re mounting into engineered wood or MDF cabinet sides, you’ll want to pre-drill and possibly use anchors — the brackets themselves are strong, but the screws are the weak link if you rush.

Second, there’s no installation template. For a product this simple, that’s not a dealbreaker, but it means you’re measuring and marking yourself. A single misaligned hole can leave a bracket sitting proud or crooked. Use a level, mark your pilot holes, and you’ll be fine. But if you’re not comfortable with a tape measure, budget for a helper or a cheap positioning guide.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the DIYer finishing a kitchen island, a basement bar, or a butcher block desk with an overhang. It’s also ideal if you have an existing countertop showing flex and you want a clean, invisible fix. If you have a decorative, furniture-style kitchen with exposed corbels, this isn't the aesthetic you want — look at ornamental brackets instead. And if you’re supporting a full-depth stone slab with more than a 14-inch overhang, this isn’t enough — you need a heavier-duty system or cabinet legs.
