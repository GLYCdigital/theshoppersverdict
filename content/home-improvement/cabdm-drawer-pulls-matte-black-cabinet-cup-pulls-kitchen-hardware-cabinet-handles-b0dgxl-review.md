Let me be blunt: most budget cabinet hardware is junk. Thin stamped metal, finish that flakes off in six months, screws that strip the moment you look at them. The CABDM matte black cup pulls aren't that. But they're not premium hardware either — and knowing the difference before you order saves you a return trip.

Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the pulls themselves (count depends on the pack size you choose), mounting screws, and — surprisingly — a paper drilling template. That template matters more than you'd think. Cup pulls have two screw holes spaced a specific distance apart, and if you're replacing existing hardware, you need the spacing to match your current holes. CABDM offers 3-inch (76mm), 5-inch (128mm), and 6.4-inch (163mm) center-to-center options. Measure your existing holes before ordering. This is the single most common mistake I see in buyer reviews — people order the wrong size and then complain the holes don't line up.

The pulls are zinc alloy, not aluminum or plastic. That gives them real heft — roughly 80-100 grams per pull depending on size. You can feel the difference when you pick one up.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The matte black finish is the headline feature here. It's applied evenly with no bare spots or drips along the edges, and the texture has a slight grip to it rather than being slick. On the 4.3-star average across 215 ratings, the finish is the most consistently praised element.

One buyer noted: "The black is actually matte, not that weird shiny charcoal you get with cheap pulls. They match my matte black faucet perfectly."

Installation is straightforward — the included template keeps your holes aligned, and the screws are the standard #8 size that fits most drawer fronts. You'll want a drill with a 3/16-inch bit for pilot holes, then a Phillips driver for the screws. Go slow on the final tightening. The zinc alloy is solid, but the coating can chip if you crank down hard. Hand-tighten plus a quarter turn is the sweet spot.

Here's where it gets real: the grip. The cup design means you hook your fingers underneath to pull. On the 3-inch size, that opening is about 1.1 inches deep. If you have larger hands or wear work gloves in the garage, this feels cramped. Multiple reviewers mention this specifically — "fine for kitchen cabinets, but I wouldn't use these on heavy workshop drawers." For kitchen use, the depth is adequate for two fingers.

## Where It Falls Short

The coating durability is the main concern. It's not baked-on powder coat; it's a painted finish. Dropping a pull on concrete or scraping it against metal will leave marks. One reviewer noted: "One of mine hit the tile floor during installation and now has a visible chip on the corner." Handle with care during install.

The other issue: no rubber or felt backing pads included. Metal-on-wood contact over years of use can leave wear marks on your drawer fronts. A $3 pack of felt pads solves this, but it's an extra step.

## Who Should Buy These

- **DIY kitchen remodelers** replacing dated brass or silver pulls — the matte black updates the whole look instantly
- **Bathroom vanity upgrades** where you want a modern, non-trendy finish that won't look dated in two years
- **Rental property owners** who need durable, neutral hardware that photographs well for listings

**Skip them if** you're installing on heavy pull-out drawers (like pot drawers) that get constant aggressive use — you'll want bar pulls with a full grip, not cup pulls, regardless of brand.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit standard kitchen cabinet holes?**
A: Only if you measure first. Standard cup pull spacing is typically 3-inch or 5-inch center-to-center. Measure from the center of one existing screw hole to the center of the other. If you're drilling new holes, the included template makes it easy.

**Q: How do I clean them without damaging the finish?**
A: Mild soap and water on a soft cloth. Avoid abrasive cleaners and anything with citrus or ammonia — those will dull the matte finish over time.

**Q: Do the screws work on 3/4-inch drawer fronts?**
A: Yes, the included screws are long enough for 3/4-inch material. For thicker fronts (like 1-inch shaker panels), you'll need longer screws from a hardware store.
