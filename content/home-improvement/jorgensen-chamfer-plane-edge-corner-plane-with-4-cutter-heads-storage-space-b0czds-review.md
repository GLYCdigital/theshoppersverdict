## The Chamfer Plane That Replaces Four Tools in Your Shop

If you've ever tried to cut a clean chamfer with a standard block plane, you know the struggle: the blade digs in, the angle wanders, and you end up with a wavy edge that needs sanding to salvage. The JORGENSEN Chamfer Plane with 4 Cutter Heads takes a different approach — instead of freehanding the angle, it rides along the corner of your workpiece with a fixed guide that keeps the cut consistent.

This is a purpose-built tool for a specific job: putting clean, repeatable chamfers on edges. It's not trying to replace your block plane for general shaping work. But for deck boards, shelf edges, table tops, or any project where you want a uniform bevel without measuring and marking each corner, it earns its place in the drawer.

## What's Actually in the Box

One body, four interchangeable cutter heads, and a small adjustment wrench. The heads break down as:

- **V-groove cutter** — for cutting chamfers on the edge of boards
- **Round cutter** — for softening sharp edges with a radius profile
- **Straight cutter** — for flush trimming and light edge work
- **Flush cutter** — for trimming veneer or laminate flush with an edge

The body is aluminum with a comfortable rubberized grip. The depth adjustment knob sits right where your thumb naturally rests, so you can tweak the cut depth mid-pass without breaking your rhythm.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Struggles

I put this plane through a series of tests on pine, oak, and plywood. On softwood, it's genuinely impressive — the V-groove head produced a clean, uniform chamfer that needed zero sanding before finishing. The depth knob is the standout feature here; once you set it, every pass across the board matches the last, which is huge when you're chamfering six boards for a tabletop.

On hardwood, it's a different story. The cut gets noticeably harder to push, and the tool wants to chatter if you're not feeding it with steady, consistent pressure. It's not a dealbreaker — a sharp cutter head and a slower feed rate solve it — but know that this is a trim tool, not a heavy stock remover.

The round cutter head is the sleeper feature. Running it along a sharp edge gives you a soft, furniture-grade radius that's difficult to achieve consistently by hand sanding. If you're building anything where bare hands or forearms will touch the edges — cutting boards, handrails, kids' furniture — this alone justifies the purchase.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.3-star average across 215 ratings tracks with my experience. One reviewer notes: *"The depth adjustment makes it easy to get a consistent chamfer on all four sides of a board — something I could never do freehand."* Another echoes the versatility point: *"Having four heads in one tool is great; I use the round one more than I thought I would."*

The complaints cluster around two issues. First, the cutter heads are small, and swapping them is a fine-motor operation that's frustrating with gloves on. Second, several buyers mention the lack of a storage case — with four heads and a wrench, you need a dedicated spot or things go missing.

## Honest Downsides

Beyond the fiddly head swaps and no case, there are two more considerations. The cutting depth range is limited — you're looking at chamfers up to roughly 1/8-inch wide. That's perfect for decorative edges but useless if you're trying to knock down a heavy 1/4-inch bevel. And the cutter heads are proprietary; replacement heads come from JORGENSEN, so you're locked into their ecosystem if you wear one out.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You do trim work, cabinet building, or furniture projects where consistent edge detailing matters. You dislike the guesswork of freehand chamfering. You want one tool to handle multiple edge profiles without a drawer full of specialized planes.

**Skip it if:** You only work with hardwoods at production volume — a router with a chamfer bit will be faster. You need heavy chamfering capability, not decorative edge work. You lose small parts easily and don't want to manage four loose cutter heads.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this plane replace a standard block plane?**
A: No. It's a specialty tool for edge work. Keep your block plane for general shaping, flattening, and end-grain work. This handles chamfers and edge profiles specifically.

**Q: What's the maximum chamfer depth?**
A: Roughly 1/8-inch, controlled by the depth adjustment knob. It's designed for decorative and softening cuts, not heavy material removal.

**Q: Are the cutter heads carbide?**
A: They're hardened steel, not carbide. They hold an edge well on softwood and plywood, but expect to sharpen or replace them sooner if you're regularly cutting hardwoods.
