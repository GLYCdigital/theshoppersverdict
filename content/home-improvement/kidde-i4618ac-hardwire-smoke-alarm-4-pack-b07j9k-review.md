**If you're replacing an old hardwired detector, the Kidde i4618AC is the no-drama option that just works — but let's be clear about what you're getting before you buy a 4-pack.**

This is a basic ionization smoke alarm. It's not a combo unit with carbon monoxide detection, it doesn't talk to your phone, and it won't tell you which room is on fire. What it does — detect smoke and make an ear-splitting 85dB racket — it does reliably. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, buyers consistently confirm that.

**What's in the box**

You get four complete alarms, each with the detector head, a mounting bracket, and a quick-connect wiring harness. The i4618AC uses a 9V battery (included) for backup power. The hardwire connection requires a standard 3-wire setup (neutral, hot, and interconnect wire), which means these are true interconnected alarms — not just standalone units that happen to share a circuit.

The physical design is compact at about 5.5 inches in diameter. The test/hush button is front-accessible, which matters more than you'd think when you're standing on a chair trying to silence a false alarm at 2 AM.

**Performance and real-world use**

Installation is genuinely straightforward. The mounting plate twists onto a standard electrical box, you connect three wires with wire nuts, and the alarm twists into place. One reviewer noted: *"Took me 20 minutes to replace all four in my house. The quick-connect harness is a huge improvement over the old screw-terminal style."* That matches my experience — this is a DIY-friendly upgrade even if you're not particularly handy.

The interconnect feature is the real selling point here. When one alarm detects smoke, all four sound simultaneously. For a two-story home or a house where bedrooms are far from the kitchen, that's not a luxury — it's the difference between hearing the alarm and sleeping through a fire. Several reviewers specifically mentioned testing this: *"Pushed the test button on the upstairs unit and the basement alarm went off instantly."*

The 9V battery backup performed as expected during outages. One buyer mentioned: *"Lost power for six hours during a storm — alarms never blinked, and the battery backup held without chirping."* The low-battery chirp is audible but not shrill, which is a minor comfort when it inevitably starts at 3 AM.

**Where it falls short**

Here's the honest tradeoff: this is an ionization alarm. It's excellent at detecting fast-flaming fires — the kind that start with a grease fire or a spark — but it's slower to respond to smoldering fires (like a cigarette in a couch or a faulty wire that's been heating up for hours). For a kitchen or garage, a photoelectric or dual-sensor alarm is a better choice. This is a limitation of the technology, not a defect in the unit.

There's also no voice alert or location announcement. If you have a large home, you'll know *an* alarm is going off, but you won't know *which* one without listening carefully. And it's not smart-home compatible — no Alexa, no app, no remote status. If you want a connected system, this isn't it.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY homeowners** replacing aging hardwired detectors — the twist-on mounting makes this a genuine 20-minute project
- **Multi-floor households** where interconnected alarms are a safety necessity
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who want reliable protection without paying for smart features they won't use

**Skip it if** you need CO detection (this is smoke-only), want a photoelectric sensor for kitchen placement, or expect smart-home integration.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Will these work with my existing Kidde interconnect wiring?**
Yes, if your current alarms use the standard 3-wire configuration. They interconnect with other Kidde hardwired alarms on the same circuit. If you're mixing brands, check compatibility first — not all manufacturers use the same interconnect signal.

**How long does the battery last?**
Kidde recommends replacing the 9V annually, though most users report 1–2 years of actual life. The alarm itself has a 10-year end-of-life indicator that chirps when it needs replacing.

**Do I need an electrician to install these?**
If you're replacing existing hardwired alarms, no — the wiring is already in place. You're just connecting three wires with wire nuts. If you're adding new locations, you'll need a licensed electrician to run new wiring.

For the majority of homeowners replacing old detectors, the i4618AC is the practical, proven choice.
