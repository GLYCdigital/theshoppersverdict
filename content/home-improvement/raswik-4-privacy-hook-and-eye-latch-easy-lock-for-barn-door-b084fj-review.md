Barn doors look great until you need privacy. That's the tradeoff every sliding door owner eventually hits: no traditional lock, no latch, just a gap where a doorstop should be. The Raswik 4″ Privacy Hook and Eye Latch solves that problem with a design so simple it feels obvious — and at 4.8 stars from 240 ratings, it's clearly working for people.

## What you actually get

The package contains the hook latch, the eye strike plate, and mounting screws for both sides. That's it. The hook measures 4 inches overall, and the sliding mechanism uses a spring-loaded pin that you pull back to release and push forward to engage. The finish on the unit I tested was a matte black that matches most modern barn door hardware — if you're replacing an existing lock, color matching is worth checking before ordering.

Installation took about ten minutes with a drill and a Phillips bit. As shown in the product image, the hook plate mounts to the door edge and the eye plate goes on the jamb. The alignment is forgiving — the hook has a bit of play, so you don't need to be a carpenter to get it to seat correctly.

## Real-world performance

The standout feature here is the slide action. Cheap hook latches bind, catch, or require two hands to operate. This one glides smoothly with one finger. The spring tension is firm enough to hold the hook in place but not so stiff that it's a workout to open. Once engaged, the hook seats snugly into the eye with zero rattling — even on a hollow-core door that vibrates when the HVAC kicks on.

One buyer put it plainly: *"This latch is much more solid than I expected for the price. The hook doesn't wobble at all once engaged, and my barn door no longer moves when the cat pushes against it."* That tracks with my testing — the steel is substantial, not the flimsy stamped metal you see on budget latches.

## Where it falls short

The strike plate screws are the weak point. They're short and the pilot holes in the plate are tight. On hardwood jambs, you'll want to pre-drill or you risk stripping the screw head. A few reviewers mentioned this too: *"Had to use my own screws for the strike plate — the included ones were too small to bite into the oak jamb."* It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but factor in a trip to your screw drawer before starting.

Also, if you buy the bright zinc version, prepare for fingerprints. The polished finish shows every touch. The black option hides it completely.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You have a single barn door in a bathroom, bedroom, or office that needs a simple privacy lock. You want something that installs quickly without mortising or complex hardware. You're replacing a flimsy latch that rattles.

**Skip it if:** You need a true keyed lock for security — this is privacy, not protection. Or if your door is over 2 inches thick, since the hook may not reach far enough to engage the eye cleanly.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work on double barn doors?**
A: Yes, but you'll need two units — one for each door — and you'll need to align them so the hooks meet the eyes on both sides. It works, but measurement is critical.

**Q: Can it be installed on a pocket door?**
A: No. The hook needs to engage with the eye on the jamb, and pocket doors don't have exposed jambs. Look for a pocket door-specific latch instead.

**Q: Will it hold a heavy solid wood door?**
A: The latch itself will hold — the steel is strong. But the screws are only as good as your pilot holes. Pre-drill into studs or solid wood, and it'll handle a 60-pound door without sagging.
