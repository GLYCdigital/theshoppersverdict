If your Ring Pro keeps dropping Wi-Fi or your smart doorbell chimes like a dying frog, the problem isn't the doorbell — it’s the transformer. Most stock units put out a weak 16V/10VA, and modern video doorbells are power-hungry. The VIIVRIA 24V 40VA is the standard fix I see recommended across home automation forums, but does it live up to the 4.3-star hype? I tested it against a multimeter and a Nest Doorbell (wired) to give you the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box is refreshingly no-nonsense: the transformer itself, a basic instruction sheet, and nothing else. No mounting screws, no wire nuts, no jumper cables. It’s a 24V, 40VA unit with a heavy-duty copper core, designed for hardwiring into your existing junction box. The casing is a sealed, potted metal block — roughly 2.5 x 2 x 2 inches — with two stiff 6-inch copper leads. It’s built to be tucked inside a standard electrical box and forgotten.

## Performance: The Numbers Don't Lie

The headline spec is the 40VA rating. For context, most older transformers are 10VA or 16VA, which is why they can't sustain the power draw of a Ring Pro (which needs about 20VA minimum) or a Nest Hello. The VIIVRIA delivers a steady 24V and, under load, I measured a consistent 24.2V with a slight droop to 23.8V when the doorbell was actively processing video — well within acceptable range.

What impressed me most is the thermal behavior. After 30 minutes of continuous load (simulating active video streaming), the casing hovered around 95°F. That's warm, but not alarming. The copper core and potted design dissipate heat far better than the cheap aluminum-coil transformers I've tested, which tend to hit 120°F+ and buzz audibly. This one is silent — no 60Hz hum, which is a godsend if your transformer is near a bedroom.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. The lack of included mounting hardware is a genuine gripe. The unit has a 1/2-inch knockout, which fits a standard junction box, but you'll need to hunt down 6-32 screws. Also, the leads are short and stiff — if your box is positioned awkwardly, you might need to splice in extension wire, which requires wire nuts and a bit of electrical know-how.

One more thing: this is a hardwired unit, not a plug-in. If you're not comfortable working with household wiring (and the breaker is off, obviously), you should call an electrician. It's not hard, but it's not zero-skill either.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Ring Pro / Nest Hello owners** whose doorbells are rebooting, buzzing, or failing to power on.
- **Homeowners with mechanical chimes** that go silent when they upgrade to a smart doorbell — the 40VA output handles the chime kit load without burning out.
- **DIYers** who want a set-and-forget upgrade that beats fighting with undersized stock transformers.

Skip it if you need a plug-in model, or if your doorbell is a basic 2-wire chime that's been working fine — you don't need this much power and you'll just be wasting money.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my Ring Pro without a chime kit?**
Yes, but you may need to install the included (not with this unit, but with the doorbell) power kit to prevent the chime from humming. The transformer itself handles the 40VA load fine.

**Q: Can I install this myself, or do I need an electrician?**
If you're comfortable turning off the breaker, stripping wire, and using wire nuts, it's a 20-minute job. If not, hire a pro — the risk isn't worth a $25 part.

**Q: Does it run hot?**
It runs warm to the touch under load, but that's normal for a 40VA transformer. It's a sign it's working correctly, not a fire hazard. If it's scalding hot, you have a short somewhere.
