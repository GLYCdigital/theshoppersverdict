## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains a single E26-base bulb rated at 15 watts (roughly 120–150 lumens equivalent). It's a standard incandescent-style bulb, not LED, which matters for salt lamps: the gentle heat from incandescent bulbs helps warm the salt and release the negative ions that people buy salt lamps for in the first place. The bulb measures about 4.2 inches long and 2.4 inches in diameter—slightly larger than a standard A19 bulb, so it fits most salt lamp sockets but may stick out in shallow, caged fixtures.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The standout feature here is the dimming range. I tested this with a modern LED-compatible dimmer switch, and it dropped smoothly from full brightness down to a faint ember-like glow—exactly what you want for a night light. At the lowest setting, it's warm and subtle, casting just enough light to navigate a dark room without waking anyone. No flicker, no hum, no buzzing at the low end.

The heat output is worth noting: after 30 minutes at full brightness, the bulb surface measured around 110°F—warm enough to heat the salt but not hot enough to burn if you brush against it. That's a meaningful improvement over cheaper bulbs that run scalding hot.

If you have a dimmer switch from before 2015, you may experience this. Second, 15 watts is noticeably brighter than the 5W–7W bulbs most salt lamps ship with. For small lamps (under 5 pounds), it can be overwhelming—you may need to run it dimmed most of the time. Third, there's no color temperature choice. It's fixed warm white, which is fine for most, but if you wanted a neutral or cool tone, this isn't it.

## FAQ

**Will this fit my salt lamp?**
Most salt lamps use a standard E26 socket, and this bulb uses that base. It's slightly larger than a typical A19 bulb, so measure your lamp's opening — if it's a tight cage design, it may not fit.

**Does it actually produce negative ions?**
Yes, the heat from the incandescent filament warms the salt crystal, which is what triggers ion release. This is one reason to choose incandescent over LED for salt lamps.

**Can I use this with a smart dimmer?**
It works with most modern smart dimmers that are rated for incandescent loads. If you have a smart dimmer, it should handle this fine — just avoid setting it to a very low percentage if you notice flicker.
