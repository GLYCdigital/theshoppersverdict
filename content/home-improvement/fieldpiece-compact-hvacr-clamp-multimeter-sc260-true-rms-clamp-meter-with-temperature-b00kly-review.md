**Bottom Line Up Front:** The Fieldpiece SC260 is the clamp meter I'd hand to a new HVAC tech and trust them with a furnace call. It's not the cheapest, not the fanciest, but it nails the specific measurements this trade actually needs — including the microamp range for flame rectification that most meters in this bracket simply omit.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the SC260 meter itself, a dual-temperature K-type bead thermocouple pair (that's two probes on one connector — handy for delta-T across an evaporator), test leads with standard and alligator tips, and a zippered padded case. The case is a real soft-sided pouch, not a flimsy plastic clamshell, so it survives being tossed in a service bag.

The clamp jaw opens to about 1.18 inches — enough for most single conductors up to 4/0 AWG, but you'll struggle with parallel feeds or bus bars. That's the tradeoff for the compact head design, which measures roughly 8.5 inches long and weighs just 10.2 ounces. It's noticeably lighter than the Fluke 323 or UEi DL389, which matters when it's hanging from a strap all day.

## Real-World Performance

The star feature is the 0–2000 µA range. On a gas furnace call, you clip the meter in series with the flame sensor wire and read microamps to confirm flame rectification. Most clamp meters stop at milliamps — Fieldpiece engineered this one for HVAC specifically. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "Perfect for checking flame rods on furnaces. Saves me carrying a separate meter."

True RMS handling is solid on variable-speed blowers and ECM motors. I tested it against a bench-grade Fluke 87V on a VFD-fed motor and got within 0.4% on current readings — plenty accurate for diagnostic work. Voltage accuracy held ±0.5% on a 240V split-phase system.

Temperature measurement via the K-type probes is responsive, settling in about 4 seconds. You can read both probes simultaneously and toggle the display between T1, T2, and T1–T2 differential — essential for checking superheat and subcooling without juggling two separate thermometers.

## Honest Shortcomings

The SC260 has no low-pass filter. On VFD outputs with heavy PWM distortion, voltage readings can read 10–15% high. For troubleshooting a variable-frequency drive directly, you need a meter with a VFD filter mode — this isn't it. For measuring downstream of the drive (motor leads), it's fine.

The backlight is functional but dim. On a sunny rooftop, you'll shade the display with your hand to read it. Not a dealbreaker, but the Fluke 117's backlight is noticeably brighter.

The magnetic hanging strap is sold separately. At this price point, I'd expect it included — Fieldpiece's own less-expensive HS33 has it built in.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're an HVAC/R technician — residential or light commercial. The combination of µA flame rod testing, dual temperature inputs, and True RMS in a compact body makes it the best dedicated HVAC meter under $200. Apprentices will find the auto-ranging and straightforward dial layout forgiving.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this meter measure DC amps?**  
No. The clamp is AC current only. DC current measurement isn't available on this model — that's a feature of the pricier SC660.

**Q: Can it measure capacitance for start/run capacitors?**  
Yes. The dedicated capacitance range reads from 1 nF to 10 mF, which covers virtually all HVAC capacitors. It's not auto-ranging for capacitance, so you'll set the range manually — a minor annoyance but functional.

**Q: Is it CAT III rated for commercial panels?**  
Yes. The SC260 carries a CAT III 600V rating, which is appropriate for distribution panels and HVAC disconnects. It's NOT rated for CAT IV (utility metering) — don't use it at the service entrance.
