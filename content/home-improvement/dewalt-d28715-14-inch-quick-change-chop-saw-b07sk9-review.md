If you're still cutting angle iron with a grinder and a prayer, the DEWALT D28715 is the upgrade that pays for itself by the end of the first week. This is the chop saw that metal fabricators, welders, and serious DIYers have been running for years — and the 4.6-star average across 229 ratings backs up the reputation. But it's not a precision tool, and it's not for everyone. Here's the real breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the saw itself, a 14-inch abrasive cutting wheel, a hex key, and the quick-change retaining nut. No stand, no clamp, no carrying case — that's all aftermarket. The saw sits on a heavy steel base with rubber feet, and the head locks down for transport. The 41.5-pound weight tells you this isn't a toy; it's a shop-bound machine with a factory-mounted rear handle that makes carrying it from the truck to the bench manageable, if not comfortable.

**Performance where it counts**

The 15-amp motor spins the 14-inch wheel at a loud, aggressive 4,200 RPM. It chews through 1/2-inch solid steel rebar, 3-inch channel, and 2-inch square tube in a single pass. The quick-change feature is the headline — a lever releases the spindle lock and the wheel comes off with a quarter-turn, no wrenches. If you're switching between abrasive wheels and a dry-cut blade, this saves you minutes every swap, and over a long week that's real time back.

Cut capacity is solid: 9.5 inches deep and 5 inches tall at 90 degrees, dropping to 6.5 and 3.5 inches at 45 degrees miter. The fence adjusts for miter angles with a positive stop at 45 degrees, but it's a thumbscrew operation, not a detent system — fine for fabrication rough cuts, not for finish trim.

**What owners actually say**

Buyers consistently praise the power-to-price ratio. One contractor noted, "I've cut hundreds of pieces of 3/8-inch angle iron and it's never bogged down." Another said the quick-change feature "makes this worth the extra money over the cheaper models." The motor holds speed under load, which is the difference between a clean cut and a stalled mess.

**Where it falls short**

The sparks are biblical. Without a built-in spark deflector, hot debris showers everywhere — you'll want a fire-resistant blanket nearby and eye protection always. The base clamp slots are narrow, so if you're using 3/8-inch bolts to secure it to a stand, you'll be drilling or grinding the holes wider. And while the quick-change is excellent, the abrasive wheel itself leaves burrs that need a file or flap disc before assembly — that's the nature of abrasive cutting, not a defect.

**Who should buy this**

Buy it if you're a welder, metal fabricator, or serious DIYer cutting steel, aluminum, or stainless regularly. It's also the right pick for a small shop that needs one dependable cutoff solution without spending $800 on a cold saw. Skip it if you're a finish carpenter needing miter accuracy on wood, or if you only cut a few pieces a month — a portable bandsaw might serve you better without the spark storm.

**FAQ**

**Does it cut aluminum cleanly?**
It cuts it, but abrasive wheels leave a rough edge and can load up on softer alloys. Use a dedicated aluminum-cutting abrasive wheel and go slow. For clean miters on aluminum, a cold saw is the better tool.

**Can I replace the abrasive wheel with a dry-cut blade?**
Yes, the spindle is 1-inch arbor with a 5/8-inch adapter available. The quick-change system works with both. Just remember dry-cut blades spin at the same RPM and are louder, but leave a cleaner edge.

**Will it fit on a standard miter saw stand?**
The base bolt pattern is standard for most universal stands, but the narrow slots may need washers. Many owners mount it directly to a portable workbench or a dedicated chop saw stand.
