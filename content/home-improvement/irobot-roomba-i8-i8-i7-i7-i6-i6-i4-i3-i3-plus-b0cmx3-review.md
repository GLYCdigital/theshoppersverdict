If you're staring at iRobot's confusing lineup of i-series, J-series, and E-series models wondering which one actually deserves your money, you're not alone. This listing covers the entire mid-range Roomba family — i8, i7, i6, i4, i3, J7, E5, E6 — and with a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, the consensus is clear: these are solid machines. But they're not all created equal, and some are dramatically better values than others.

## What You're Actually Getting

Every model in this lineup shares iRobot's core DNA: a round robot with dual rubber multi-surface brushes (no hair-wrap tangles), a 3-stage cleaning system, and dirt Detect sensors that concentrate on high-traffic zones. The differences come down to three things: suction power, navigation smarts, and whether you get the self-emptying "Plus" dock.

The **i3 and i4** are your budget entries — single-pass navigation, no mapping, just random-but-effective coverage. The **i6, i7, and i8** step up to Imprint Smart Mapping, which learns your floor plan and lets you send the bot to specific rooms. The **J7** adds obstacle detection that actually avoids pet waste and charging cables. The **E5 and E6** are the older generation — still decent, but lacking the mapping smarts of the i-series.

## Real-World Performance

The suction story is where this lineup separates itself from budget robovacs. The i7 and i8 deliver 10x the suction of the original Roomba 600 series — that's genuine deep-cleaning power for carpeted homes. One verified buyer noted: *"Pulled up pet hair my Dyson cordless kept missing in the Berber carpet. First run filled the bin completely."* That's the experience you're paying for.

Battery life runs 75-90 minutes depending on model, which handles most apartments and average houses in one pass. Recharge-and-resume kicks in for larger homes — the bot returns to base, tops up, and finishes where it left off.

The self-emptying dock on Plus models is the standout feature. The base holds up to 60 days of debris in an allergen-sealed bag. For anyone with allergies or who simply hates touching vacuum dust, this is worth the upgrade cost alone.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the navigation is dated. iRobot still uses vSLAM camera-based mapping while competitors like Roborock have moved to lidar. This means slower initial mapping, occasional "lost robot" moments, and the i3/i4 models bumping around randomly rather than methodically.

The non-Plus models have a genuinely small dustbin — expect to empty after every cleaning cycle in homes with pets. That's annoying for a $300+ machine.

Parts pricing hurts too. Genuine iRobot filters, side brushes, and self-emptying bags add up fast. Aftermarket options exist but vary wildly in quality. Budget $60-100 annually in consumables.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy the i7+ or i8+ if** you have wall-to-wall carpet, multiple pets, and want true hands-off operation. The self-emptying dock plus mapping is worth every penny.

**Buy the i3 or i4 if** you have mostly hard floors, a small space, and want iRobot reliability on a budget.

**Skip the E5/E6** unless you find them heavily discounted — the i3 outperforms them at similar prices.

**Consider the J7 if** you have pet accidents or a cluttered floor with cables — its obstacle avoidance prevents the nightmare of a robot smearing disaster across your floors.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need the self-emptying Plus model?**
A: Only if you vacuum daily or have pets. The standard bin fills fast — one husky owner reported emptying mid-cycle. If you clean weekly, the standard model is fine.

**Q: Will these work on dark rugs or black floors?**
A: Yes — the cliff sensors handle drop-offs well, and dual rubber brushes adapt to surface changes. Some older Roombas struggled with dark surfaces; these don't.

**Q: How often must I replace brushes and filters?**
A: Filters monthly, side brushes every 3-4 months, main brushes every 6-12 months depending on hair volume. Set a calendar reminder — clogged filters dramatically reduce suction.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, well-supported robot vacuum for carpeted homes, especially the i7+ or i8+ with self-emptying. **Skip it if** you're on a tight budget or have a mostly hard-floor home — cheaper lidar-based competitors offer better navigation value.

The 4.6-star rating holds up. These aren't the flashiest or smartest robovacs on the market, but they're proven, reliable, and backed by a company that's been at this game for two decades. That counts for something when your robot is navigating under couches at 2 AM.
