Let's cut to the chase: the BOSCH GWX18V-8N is one of the few cordless grinders that doesn't make you feel like you're compromising. With 8,500 RPM from a brushless motor and the proprietary X-LOCK tool-free disc change system, this 4-1/2-inch grinder targets pros who need speed and tradespeople already invested in Bosch's 18V platform. But it's not without quirks — and you need to know them before you click buy.

**What's Actually in the Box**

This is the bare tool, so don't expect batteries or a charger in the box. You get the grinder itself, a standard guard, an auxiliary handle, and a wrench. That's it. The X-LOCK system means the wrench is mostly for the guard, not the disc — more on that in a moment. If you're new to Bosch's 18V line, the GWX18V-8N is sold as a kit with a 4.0Ah battery and charger for roughly $100 more, but the price fluctuates. Factor that into your budget.

**Performance: The Real Deal**

The brushless motor is the star here. It generates 8,500 RPM under load, which places it right next to corded grinders in real-world cutting speed. In my testing on 1/4-inch steel plate, it chewed through with minimal bogging — something most cordless grinders can't claim. The Constant Speed Electronics (CSE) circuitry keeps the RPM steady even as the battery drains, so your last cut feels as strong as your first.

Thermal management is also impressive. Bosch designed the air vents to pull cool air through the motor and exhaust it away from your hands. After a 10-minute continuous grinding session with a 4.0Ah battery, the housing was warm but not uncomfortable — a major improvement over older brushed models that turned into hand warmers.

The X-LOCK system is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade. You press the disc, it clicks, you're done. No wrench, no spindle lock, no fumbling. Removing a disc is just as fast — flip the lever, the disc pops off. If you're swapping between grinding and cutting wheels multiple times a day, this saves real time. As one buyer put it, "The X-LOCK is a game changer. I used to dread changing wheels on my old grinder; now it's literally a two-second job."

**Build Quality and Ergonomics**

The GWX18V-8N measures 11.2 inches from nose to tail and weighs 3.5 pounds without a battery. That's lighter than most competitors in this class. The slim grip circumference — just under 7 inches around — makes it comfortable for smaller hands, and the auxiliary handle can mount on either side in three positions. The guard is tool-free adjustable, which pairs well with the X-LOCK system for rapid job changes.

One thing I appreciate: the paddle switch. It's a dead-man style — you have to hold it to run. That's a safety feature you want on a tool that spins a grinding wheel at 141 revolutions per second. Some buyers prefer a slide switch for continuous use, but the paddle is the safer choice and Bosch got the tension right.

**Where It Falls Short**

The X-LOCK system is a double-edged sword. Those proprietary discs cost about 20-30% more than universal 7/8-inch arbor wheels, and your local hardware store might not stock them. Amazon has a decent selection, but if you're in a rural area, plan ahead. There's no adapter for standard discs — once you're X-LOCK, you're X-LOCK.

No brake is the other notable omission. After you release the trigger, the wheel free-spins for what feels like an eternity — about 5-6 seconds. On a busy job site, that's time you're standing there waiting. Many competing grinders in this price range (like DeWalt's DCG412) include an electronic brake. Bosch omitted it here, presumably to keep costs down, but it's a real annoyance.

Battery life is also a consideration. With a 4.0Ah battery, you'll get roughly 15-20 minutes of continuous heavy grinding. That's typical for the class, but you'll want at least two batteries if you're doing serious fabrication work. The 8.0Ah High Capacity batteries extend runtime substantially, but they add weight and cost.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Bosch 18V ecosystem users**: If you already own Bosch batteries and chargers, this is a no-brainer addition. The bare tool price is competitive, and the performance justifies it.
- **Metal fabricators and welders**: The power and X-LOCK speed will make you more efficient. The brushless motor handles continuous duty better than brushed alternatives.
- **DIYers with a Bosch investment**: If you're committed to the platform, this is a great grinder. But if you're starting from scratch, weigh the X-LOCK disc cost against a universal-arbor model.

**Skip It If**

- You're not in the Bosch ecosystem and don't want to be. The X-LOCK disc premium and proprietary system lock you in.
- You need a brake. If you're doing precision cuts where spin-down time matters, look at DeWalt or Makita options with electronic brakes.
- You're a casual user who grinds twice a year. A corded grinder at half the price will serve you fine.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can I use standard 4-1/2-inch discs with the X-LOCK system?**
A: No. X-LOCK requires Bosch-specific discs. There's no adapter for universal 7/8-inch arbor wheels. Bosch makes a broad range of X-LOCK discs, but your selection is limited to their catalog and a few third-party manufacturers.

**Q: Does it come with a battery and charger?**
A: This listing is for the bare tool only. Bosch sells a kit version (GWX18V-8N20) that includes a 4.0Ah battery and charger, but you'll pay more. If you're new to Bosch 18V, the kit is the better value.

**Q: How long does the battery last on a full charge?**
A: With a 4.0Ah battery, expect 15-20 minutes of continuous heavy grinding or roughly 30 minutes of intermittent cutting. A 6.0Ah or 8.0Ah battery roughly doubles that but adds about a pound of weight.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if**: You're in the Bosch 18V ecosystem, do regular metalwork, and value tool-free disc changes. The power-to-weight ratio is excellent, and the brushless motor holds up under sustained use.

**Skip it if**: You want a brake, dislike proprietary consumables, or aren't already invested in Bosch batteries.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A strong performer held back by the X-LOCK premium and no-brake design. For the right buyer, it's a daily-driver workhorse. For everyone else, it's a well-engineered tool with a few frustrating compromises.
