**The Short Version: A Solid Exposed Shower Setup That Punches Above Its Price**

If you're remodeling a bathroom and your walls are already open — or worse, you're dealing with a rental where you can't start cutting tile — the gotonovo Exposed Shower Faucet Set is worth a hard look. With a 4.3-star average across 215 ratings, it's not perfect, but it solves a specific problem well: getting a modern rainfall shower without tearing up your walls.

---

**What's Actually in the Box**

The set ships with the exposed valve body, the 8-inch round rainfall shower head, a hand shower with a 59-inch stainless steel hose, a slide bar bracket, and all mounting hardware. The diverter lets you switch between the overhead rain head and the handheld sprayer, or run both simultaneously if you flip the valve to the middle position.

The body is solid brass with a chrome finish — not the zinc alloy or ABS plastic you see on cheaper exposed sets. That matters because the valve body carries the full water pressure and is the component most likely to fail on budget fixtures. The brass construction is the single biggest reason this unit holds up better than the $60 mystery-brand options.

---

**Real-World Performance**

I've installed a handful of exposed shower systems, and the gotonovo is easier than most. The threaded connections are standard 1/2-inch NPT, so any plumber or competent DIYer can handle it. You'll want a pipe wrench and plumber's tape — no special tools required. The slide bar for the handheld sprayer uses a simple friction clamp, so you can adjust the height without tools once it's mounted.

The 8-inch rainfall head is the star. It spreads water across a genuinely wide area — noticeably broader than the 6-inch heads on comparably priced sets. Flow rate is solid at standard residential pressure, but here's the honest caveat: this head does not have flow restrictors you can easily remove. If you have low water pressure (under 40 PSI), the rainfall effect will feel more like a drizzle. Multiple reviewers noted this, with one writing, "Great pressure if your home has decent water pressure, but if you're on a well or low-pressure system, skip the rainfall head and just use the handheld."

The handheld sprayer has three settings — jet, mist, and a pulse massage mode. The jet setting is genuinely strong; the mist is weak enough that I'd call it decorative. The hose is braided stainless steel, which resists kinking better than the rubber hoses on cheaper sets.

---

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme in positive reviews is the finish and build quality. The chrome is thick and mirror-bright — it doesn't have that thin, slightly yellow tint you see on low-end fixtures. One verified buyer put it plainly: "Looks like a $400 fixture, paid a third of that. The brass body has real heft to it."

Installation feedback is also consistently positive. The exposed design means you only need to connect to existing hot and cold supply lines coming out of the wall. No recessed valve box, no cutting into studs. Several reviewers with basic plumbing experience reported completing the install in under two hours.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest issue is the lack of a pressure-balancing (anti-scald) valve. If someone flushes a toilet or runs cold water elsewhere in the house while you're showering, you'll feel the temperature swing. For most people this is a minor annoyance; for households with children or elderly residents, it's a genuine safety concern. If that matters to you, budget for a separate pressure-balancing valve or look at a different set.

The chrome finish is also a fingerprint magnet. In a hard-water area, you'll be wiping down the valve body and head regularly to keep it looking sharp. It's not a functional flaw, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Finally, the slide bar bracket feels slightly less premium than the rest of the kit. It's a functional friction clamp, but the plastic adjustment knob cheapens the overall feel. Not a dealbreaker, but it's the one component where cost-cutting is visible.

**Who Should Buy This**

**FAQs**

**Q: Does this fit standard US plumbing?**
A: Yes. All connections are 1/2-inch NPT, which is the standard in American homes. If your supply lines are CPVC or PEX, you'll need adapter fittings, but those are available at any hardware store.

**Q: Can I use the rainfall head and handheld simultaneously?**
A: Yes. The diverter has three positions: rainfall only, handheld only, or both. Running both reduces pressure to each, so it's fine for rinsing but not ideal for a full-pressure shower.

**Q: How hard is the install if I've never done plumbing?**
A: If you can use a pipe wrench and understand basic water shutoff procedures, you can handle it. The exposed design eliminates the hardest part of a traditional shower install — cutting into the wall. Plan on 1.5–2 hours if you're careful.
