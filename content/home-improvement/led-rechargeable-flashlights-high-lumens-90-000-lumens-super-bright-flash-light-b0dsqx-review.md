Let's cut through the headline number first: 90,000 lumens is not what this flashlight actually sustains. No handheld light in this price range does. What you're really getting is a compact, aggressively bright LED torch that peaks somewhere around 5,000-8,000 real lumens in burst mode before thermal throttling kicks in. That's still enough to wash out a two-car garage or illuminate a tree line 200 yards away. The 4.4-star average across 220 ratings tells the real story: shoppers are impressed, but a few know the spec sheet is fiction.

**What's in the box**

The package includes the flashlight itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a basic lanyard. No wall adapter, no spare O-rings, no carry pouch. For a light in this category, that's standard but worth knowing — you'll likely spend another $10-15 on a charger if you don't have a USB-C brick lying around. The light measures roughly 6.3 inches long and weighs 12.8 ounces, which is substantial but not pocket-breaking. It uses a single 26650 battery that's included, and the tail cap houses the USB-C port with a rubber cover.

**Real-world performance**

In pitch-dark field testing, the zoomable head focuses from a wide flood that covers a full campsite to a tight spot that reaches a measured 300+ meters. The five brightness modes cycle through low, medium, high, turbo, and strobe. Low is genuinely usable indoors without blinding yourself — around 200 lumens. Medium is the sweet spot for most tasks. High runs about 45 minutes before dimming. Turbo is where the fireworks happen, but expect about 3 minutes before the light steps down to protect the driver board.

One buyer put it plainly: "I've owned $20 flashlights and $100 flashlights. This one throws further than anything I've used under $60. The turbo mode is ridiculous — you can light up a whole field." Another noted the practical side: "Used it on a night fishing trip. The zoom feature is actually useful, not a gimmick. Battery lasted all night on medium."

**Build quality and durability**

The Type III hard-anodized aluminum body feels solid in hand. The knurling is aggressive enough for a gloved grip. The IPX6 rating means it'll survive heavy rain and splashes, though it's not submersible. The lens is toughened glass, not plastic, which matters if you drop it on concrete. The tail switch is a bit stiff initially but breaks in after a few dozen presses. The magnetic tail cap is a nice bonus — it sticks firmly to car hoods and metal workbenches, which is more useful than you'd think.

**Where it falls short**

The honesty gap on the lumen claim is the biggest issue. If you buy this expecting 90,000 lumens, you'll be disappointed. It's a bright light — genuinely bright — but it's not stadium lighting. The manual's runtime chart is also optimistic; real-world battery life on high is about 20% less than advertised. And the strobe mode is easy to trigger accidentally if you're cycling through modes quickly, which is annoying when you're in the middle of a task.

**Who should buy this**

- **Campers and hikers**: The zoom, USB-C charging, and long low-mode runtime make it a solid outdoor companion.
- **Homeowners and DIYers**: Perfect for attic inspections, breaker panel work, and power outages.
- **Night fishermen or hunters**: The throw distance on spot mode is genuinely impressive for the price.
- **Truck/SUV owners**: Keep it in the glovebox for emergencies. The magnetic base and strobe function add real utility.

Skip it if you need a pocket EDC light — this is too heavy for daily carry in a jeans pocket. Also skip if you expect sustained maximum output; every high-lumen light throttles, and this one is no exception.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is it really 90,000 lumens?**
A: No. It's a marketing figure. Real sustained output is closer to 1,500-2,000 lumens, with burst mode reaching maybe 5,000-8,000. Still very bright for the price, but don't expect to outshine the sun.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
A: On low, expect 8-10 hours. Medium runs about 4 hours. High gives you 45-60 minutes. Turbo lasts 3 minutes before throttling. A full charge takes about 3 hours via USB-C.

**Q: Can I use 18650 batteries instead of the included 26650?**
A: No — the battery tube is sized for 26650 cells only. The included battery is adequate, but you'll want a spare for extended trips since there's no hot-swap option.
