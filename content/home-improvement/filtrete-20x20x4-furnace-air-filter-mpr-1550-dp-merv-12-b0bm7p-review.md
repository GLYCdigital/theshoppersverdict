# Filtrete 20x20x4 Furnace Air Filter MPR 1550 DP MERV 12

Most homeowners grab whatever 1-inch filter is on sale, swap it every three months, and call it done. That's fine if you're renting. If you own your system, you're leaving performance on the table—and paying for it in dust, allergens, and premature blower wear.

The Filtrete 20x20x4 MPR 1550 takes a different approach: a 4-inch-deep pleated media filter that filters harder and lasts longer than anything in the 1-inch category. After digging through specs, owner reports, and the 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, here's the real story.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains one filter. That's it. No gaskets, no adapter frames, no instructions—if you've changed a filter before, you know what to do. The 20x20x4 dimensions are nominal, so measure your existing slot before ordering; actual size runs about 19.5 x 19.5 x 3.5 inches, which is standard for 4-inch media cabinets.

The construction is where this differentiates itself. The frame is rigid cardboard that won't warp when you slide it in, and the pleated media is dense—significantly more surface area than a comparable 1-inch filter. That density is why it's rated MPR 1550 (Filtrete's own scale) and independently verified at MERV 12, which means it catches particles down to 0.3–1.0 microns: smoke, smog, pollen, dust mite debris, and most airborne bacteria.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the 4-inch depth earns its keep. Because the pleats are deep, the filter has roughly 2–3x the surface area of a 1-inch MERV 12 filter. That translates to lower pressure drop—meaning your blower doesn't work as hard—and a service life of up to 12 months. Owners consistently confirm this. One reviewer noted, "I replaced my 1-inch filters every 60 days. This thing went a full year and still looked like it had life left when I pulled it."

The filtration itself is legit. Multiple buyers with allergies and pets reported noticeable improvement in dust accumulation and sneezing within days. Another common thread in reviews: the filter holds its structural integrity over time. No bowing, no media collapse, no air bypassing through gaps.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

First, airflow restriction. A MERV 12 filter is dense, and even with the 4-inch depth, it creates more static pressure than a cheap fiberglass filter. If your furnace is older, or your return duct is undersized, you could see reduced airflow and increased blower strain. This isn't a filter for every system—it's for systems with proper duct sizing.

Second, price. The 4-inch filters cost more upfront than 1-inch disposables. But spread over 12 months versus 3–4 changes of 1-inch filters, the cost-per-month is actually comparable. The catch: you have to commit to the longer change interval to realize the savings. If you forget it's in there and run it 18 months, you're doing more harm than good.

Third, the cardboard frame. While it holds up fine in normal use, it's not moisture-resistant. If you have a condensate leak or high humidity in your mechanical room, the frame can soften. Keep it dry.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a 4-inch filter cabinet, you care about indoor air quality (allergies, asthma, pets, or wildfire season), and your HVAC system has adequate duct sizing. This is also a smart pick for landlords or property managers who want set-and-forget performance for a year.

**Skip it if:** You have a 1-inch filter slot (this won't fit—you'd need the 1-inch version), an older furnace with a weak blower, or you're on a tight budget and don't mind changing filters every 60–90 days.

## FAQ

**Q: How often do I actually need to change this filter?**
A: Filtrete recommends every 12 months for the 4-inch depth. Check monthly during peak seasons—if it looks gray and clogged at 8 months, change it early.

**Q: Will this fit my existing system?**
A: Measure the slot. If you currently use a 20x20x4, it'll fit. If you use a 20x20x1, this is too thick—order the 1-inch version instead.

**Q: Is MERV 12 overkill for a normal home?**
A: Not if you have allergies, pets, or smoke concerns. For basic dust control, a MERV 8–11 might suffice, but you'll notice the difference with this filter in air quality.
