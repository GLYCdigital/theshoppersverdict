## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The first thing you notice is the weight. This isn't hollow zinc or chrome-plated plastic — it's solid die-cast metal that feels substantial in your hand. The grip is ergonomically shaped with a slight curve that fits your palm naturally when sliding the door open.

The locking mechanism is where this set earns its keep. The latch engages with a positive, solid click that tells you it's fully seated.

One detail I appreciate: the handle assembly uses a through-bolt design rather than wood screws into the door edge. That means the handle won't loosen over time with constant use — a common failure point on cheaper sets.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the limitations. The 1-inch lock cylinder bore is the biggest gotcha — if your existing hardware uses a smaller hole (common on older doors), you'll need a hole saw and a steady hand to enlarge it. That's not a dealbreaker, but it does mean this isn't a pure "unscrew and replace" job for every door.

The included Phillips-head screws are functional but unremarkable. For a metal-frame installation or if you live in a high-humidity area, upgrading to stainless steel hardware is worth the $3 investment.

Finally, the keys are basic — no restricted keyway or anti-pick pins. This is a privacy lock, not a security solution. If you're looking for high-security protection for a back door, pair this with a separate secondary lock.
