### Performance: Real-World Muscle

This isn't a toy. The brushless motor delivers serious torque for its size. In practical terms, it'll sink 3" deck screws all day without bogging down, and it has the guts to drive larger lags with pre-drilled holes. The variable speed trigger offers excellent control for lighter tasks like cabinet assembly, while the 4-mode drive control (including a self-tapping mode and a "slow start" mode for precision) gives you flexibility that cheaper drivers simply lack.

Battery life is where this driver shines. On a 2.0Ah M12 battery, you'll get through a full day of light-to-moderate work. With a 4.0Ah or 6.0Ah pack, it's a weekend warrior's dream. The LED light positioned above the battery pack is bright and well-placed, casting light directly on the work surface without being blocked by the bit or your hand.

### Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest: no tool is perfect. The most common complaints center on the **lack of a belt clip in the box**—it's an accessory you'll likely need to purchase separately. For a tool designed for portability, this feels like a miss. Additionally, the 1/4" hex drive limits you to hex-shank bits. If you need to drive sockets, you'll need an adapter, which adds length and can reduce torque transmission. Finally, while the M12 battery platform is fantastic, if you're a Milwaukee user with only M18 tools, you'll be buying into a second battery system.

### FAQ: Real Questions, Straight Answers

**Q: Does this work with standard 1/4" hex bits?**
A: Yes, it accepts all standard 1" hex-shank bits. The friction ring holds them securely, though you'll want a strong magnetized bit for overhead work.

**Q: How does it compare to the M18 Fuel version?**
A: The M18 Fuel delivers significantly more torque (up to 1,800 in-lbs vs. the M12's ~350 in-lbs), but it's heavier and bulkier. For 90% of driving tasks, the M12 is more than adequate and far more maneuverable.

**Q: Is the 4-mode drive control actually useful?**
A: Yes. The "slow start" mode is perfect for avoiding cam-outs on delicate materials, and the self-tapping mode is a godsend for metal studs. It's not a gimmick.
