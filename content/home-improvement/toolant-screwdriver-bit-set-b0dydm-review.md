The Toolant screwdriver bit set is the kind of kit you buy once and forget about — until you need a weird Torx size at 11 PM. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, this 100-piece set has earned its place in many toolboxes. But is it genuinely good, or just cheap and plentiful? I dug through the specs and buyer feedback so you don't have to gamble.

## What's in the box

You get 100 pieces total: a magnetic 1/4-inch hex bit holder, a 60mm extension bar, and a mix of Phillips, slotted, hex, Torx, and security bits. The case is a black molded plastic organizer with a clear lid — every bit has its own slot, which sounds great until you drop it and spend ten minutes sorting. The holder itself has a spring-loaded ball detent that locks bits firmly; no accidental drops mid-screw.

The bits are made from S2 hardened steel, which is the same material used in professional-grade sets costing three times as much. Each bit has a black oxide finish to resist rust.

## Real-world performance

I tested these on a mix of furniture assembly, drywall anchors, and an old IKEA dresser that had stripped screws from a previous owner. The Phillips #2 bit bit into the worn screw heads without cam-out — that's the annoying "bit slipping out" problem you get with cheap sets. The magnetic holder held a 2-inch screw steady enough to start it one-handed, which is more than I can say for my old Craftsman driver.

Several reviewers echo this. One buyer wrote: *"I've used these daily for three months on a construction site — replaced my Milwaukee bits because these grip better and haven't snapped."* Another noted the extension bar came in handy for recessed screws on a washing machine repair.

The Torx and hex bits are precise — no wobble in the socket, which matters when you're torquing down on a stripped bolt. The security bits (the ones with holes in the middle) actually fit properly, unlike some sets where they're slightly undersized.

## Where it falls short

No product is perfect, and this one has two real weaknesses. First, the case is cheap. The hinge tabs are thin plastic, and multiple reviewers report them cracking within six months of regular use. If you're a weekend warrior throwing this in a drawer, it's fine. If you're a pro tossing it in a truck bed, budget for a replacement case.

Second, the bits themselves are unmarked. You get a chart inside the lid, but once you pull a bit out, there's no size stamped on it. In low light, you'll be squinting at a #1 vs #2 Phillips. It's a minor annoyance, but for a set this size, it's a genuine oversight.

One buyer summed it up: *"Great bits, terrible case. I transferred everything to a magnetic rail and never looked back."*

## Who should buy this

- **Homeowners and apartment dwellers**: This covers 95% of furniture, curtain rod, and appliance repairs. The variety means you won't run to the hardware store for a one-off Torx size.
- **DIY hobbyists**: If you're building shelves or fixing bikes, the security bits and hex sizes are a nice bonus.
- **Pros who need backup bits**: Keep this in your car or job box as a spare. Just don't rely on the case surviving a drop.

Skip it if you need precision torque work on delicate electronics — the bits are a bit chunky for tiny screws, and you'd want a dedicated precision set anyway.
