If you've ever shaved or applied makeup under a single overhead light, you know the problem: your face is half in shadow, and you're tilting your chin at awkward angles just to see what you're doing. The Deyidn three-light vanity fixture attacks that problem directly. It spreads light across the mirror's width instead of dumping it from one point, and at roughly $40-60 depending on the finish you pick, it's priced like a smart upgrade rather than a renovation.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the fixture body (pre-wired), three milky white glass shades, a mounting plate, wire nuts, and the small set screws that hold the shades in place. The metal construction is a matte black finish in the standard version — clean lines, no fussy ornamentation, about 24 inches wide and 5.5 inches tall. It's designed for a standard 4-inch round or octagonal junction box, and it's rated for damp locations, so it's safe directly above a shower or in a steamy bathroom.

**Installation and build quality**

This is a hardwired fixture, not a plug-in. If you're comfortable swapping a light fixture, you'll manage this in 30-45 minutes. The mounting plate arrives pre-attached to the fixture body, which is a small but meaningful time-saver — you're not juggling three separate pieces while standing on a stool. The wire connections are straightforward: black to black, white to white, green or bare copper to ground. The instructions are basic but sufficient, and the mounting screws align easily with standard boxes.

The glass shades are the standout feature. They're thick enough to feel substantial but not so heavy that they stress the mounts. The milky finish does real work — it softens the light output significantly. If you use 60-watt equivalent LED bulbs, you get even, glare-free illumination across the mirror. One buyer noted, "The frosted glass makes a huge difference — no squinting when I do my makeup." That tracks with our testing.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 215 ratings (4.3 stars average), three themes come up repeatedly:

- **Light distribution** — People consistently mention that the three-light design kills shadows. A reviewer with a 30-inch mirror said, "Finally, both sides of my face are lit evenly."
- **Aesthetic versatility** — The matte black finish pairs well with both modern and transitional bathroom styles. It doesn't scream "builder grade."
- **Value for money** — Multiple reviewers compared it favorably to fixtures at big-box stores costing double. One wrote, "Same look as the $120 fixture at Home Depot, but this was $50."

**Where it falls short**

The bulbs-not-included situation is worth flagging. It's common with vanity fixtures, but it's still an extra $15-25 and a second trip to the store. More importantly, the set screws that hold the glass shades are small and easy to overtighten. A few reviewers reported cracked glass from exactly that — go slow, hand-tighten only.

The other limitation is the backplate. It's sized for a standard 4-inch junction box. If your bathroom has an older, larger retrofit box or a pancake box, you'll need a trim plate or some creative shimming. Check what's behind your current fixture before ordering.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY homeowners** replacing a dated fluorescent bar or single sconce — this is a straightforward swap with clear instructions.
- **Renters or flippers** who want maximum visual impact per dollar — the matte black and milky glass look far more expensive than they are.
- **Anyone with a 24-36 inch mirror** — the three-light spread is ideal for that range. If your mirror is smaller than 20 inches, consider the two-light version instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this be dimmed?**
A: Yes, but only if you use dimmable LED bulbs and a compatible dimmer switch. The fixture itself has no dimmer built in.

**Q: Is it okay in a steamy bathroom?**
A: Yes — it's damp-rated, meaning it handles humidity and steam fine. It's not rated for direct water contact, so don't mount it inside the shower.

**Q: What bulb type works best?**
A: LED A19 or globe bulbs in 2700-3000K (warm white) look best with the milky shades. Avoid clear bulbs — they defeat the diffusing effect.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want even, shadow-free bathroom lighting without spending $150+ on a designer fixture. **Skip it if** you're not comfortable with basic wiring, or if your junction box is an unusual size.

This is a solid 4.2-star product — genuinely good light quality, clean looks, and a price that makes sense. Just budget for bulbs, handle the glass gently, and check your junction box first. For most bathrooms, it's the right call.
