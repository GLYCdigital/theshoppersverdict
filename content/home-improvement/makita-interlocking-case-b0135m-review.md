## Is this just another plastic box, or does it actually earn its keep?

Most tool cases promise organization and deliver a flimsy shell that cracks by month two. The Makita Interlocking Case (model B0135M) is different. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this isn't a product people feel neutral about—they either swear by it or wish they'd bought the rolling version.

I've spent time with this case, and here's the honest breakdown.

## What's in the box

You get the case itself—no tools included, obviously. The main unit features a removable top organizer tray with adjustable dividers, plus a deep bottom compartment. The lid has a gasket seal that keeps dust out (though I wouldn't trust it in a downpour for extended periods). The interlocking lugs on the top and bottom are the headline feature, and they work as advertised.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The ABS polymer shell is genuinely tough. I've seen these things thrown into truck beds, dropped off ladders, and left in freezing garages. The reinforced corners take the abuse. The latches are metal-reinforced plastic—they won't snap off like the cheap all-plastic ones on lesser cases.

The interlocking mechanism is the standout. Cases click together with a satisfying positive engagement, and they don't wobble when stacked. Side-mounted handles on each case make carrying a stack of two or three manageable, though it gets heavy fast.

One thing that surprised me: the interior dimensions are actually usable. The bottom compartment fits a full-size drill/driver combo with batteries and charger, and the top tray holds bits, drivers, and small fasteners without everything jumbling together.

## What buyers consistently praise

The stackability is the recurring theme. One verified buyer put it plainly: "The interlocking feature is no gimmick—these things lock together rock solid. I've got four stacked in my van and they haven't shifted in 6 months of daily driving." Another noted the durability: "Dropped mine off a 6-foot ladder onto concrete. Case survived, impact driver survived. That's all I needed to know."

The modular flexibility also gets love—buyers mix and match with the larger Makita cases for a custom setup.

## Where it falls short

Let's be real about the downsides:

**No wheels.** If you're hauling more than two stacked cases any distance, your arms will feel it. Makita sells a rolling cart base separately, but that's an extra purchase.

**Empty case bulk.** These things are chunky. If you're not actively using them, they eat garage space. They don't nest or collapse.

**The gasket isn't waterproof.** Dust-resistant, yes. Submersion-proof, absolutely not. Don't leave sensitive tools in one that's been sitting in a flooded truck bed.

## Who should buy this

- **Tradespeople** who need modular, stackable storage that survives daily job-site abuse
- **Homeowners** with a growing tool collection who want organized, protected storage without spending Snap-on money
- **Van/vehicle organizers** who need cases that stay put during transit

Skip it if you need wheels, if you're storing lightweight items that don't need heavy protection, or if you're on a tight budget—cheaper non-interlocking cases exist that'll hold a drill fine.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit in a standard truck bed or van shelf?**
A: The external dimensions are roughly 16.5" x 14" x 7.5" per case. Measure your space—they stack neatly but don't expect them to slide into narrow under-seat storage.

**Q: Can I use foam inserts for custom tool fitting?**
A: Yes. The bottom compartment accepts standard pick-and-pluck foam. Several buyers do this for delicate measuring tools or optics.

**Q: Do the latches hold up over time?**
A: Generally yes. The metal-reinforced design handles repetitive opening/closing well. The main reported failure point is the handle if you overload the case with heavy tools.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need rugged, stackable storage that keeps tools organized and protected on the job site or in your vehicle. The interlocking system genuinely works, and the build quality justifies the price over cheaper alternatives.

**Skip it if** you need a wheeled system, you're only storing a single tool (a soft bag is lighter and cheaper), or you expect waterproof performance.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect (wheels, anyone?), but for what it does—rugged, modular, stackable tool storage—it's one of the best in its class. The 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars back that up.
