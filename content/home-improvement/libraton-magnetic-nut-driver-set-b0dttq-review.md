**What You Actually Get**

The set ships with the most common metric and SAE sizes — you're looking at roughly 4mm through 13mm and 5/32" through 1/2", depending on the exact configuration you order. Each driver has a 1/4-inch hex shank, which means they work with any standard impact driver, drill chuck, or ratcheting bit holder. The shafts are made from chrome vanadium steel, which is the same alloy used in professional-grade sockets. The magnetic tips are full-length magnets, not just a thin coating, which is a detail that matters when you're fishing a bolt out of a dark crevice.

**Performance and Build Quality**

I've seen the common failure modes for nut drivers: tips that lose magnetism after a week, shafts that twist under load, and sizing marks that wear off. LIBRATON addresses all three. The laser-etched sizing on the shaft won't rub off, and the color-coded bands on the handle give you a quick visual reference without reading anything. The magnetism is strong enough to hold a nut sideways — a detail multiple buyers mention in their reviews.

One buyer put it simply: *"These actually hold the nut. I've had sets where the magnet is so weak the fastener falls off before you get it to the bolt. Not these."*

The knurled handles provide a solid grip even with oily hands, and the overall length gives you decent reach without being unwieldy. The steel feels properly heat-treated — no flex when you're torquing down a moderately tight fastener.

**Where It Falls Short**

Nothing is perfect, and this set has two real limitations. First, there's no storage case. Buyers have noted this repeatedly — one wrote, *"Great drivers, but they come loose in a bag. Spend five bucks on a magnetic rail."* That's a fair criticism; if you're the type who loses tools, factor in an organizer.

Second, the 1/4-inch hex shank is a torque ceiling. This is not a breaker-bar replacement. If you're trying to loosen a stubborn, rusted bolt, you'll round it off or twist the driver before you break it free. This set is designed for assembly, disassembly of lightly torqued fasteners, and quick work — not heavy-duty repair.
