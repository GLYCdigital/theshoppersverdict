If you've been scrolling through farmhouse chandeliers and wondering why similar six-light fixtures cost three times as much, the Lynnoland is the answer to that question. At under $100 on most days, this is the kind of budget-friendly lighting that actually looks like you paid more — but only if you go in knowing what you're getting.

**What's in the box**

The chandelier arrives mostly pre-assembled. You get the main body with six arms already attached, the canopy, a mounting plate, and the hanging chain and cord. The glass shades come individually wrapped — I counted six — and they're the seeded glass style that diffuses light nicely. The instruction manual is basic but adequate; it's a straightforward install that took me about 45 minutes working alone.

**Build quality and installation**

The steel construction is solid for the price. The matte black finish is applied evenly, and the arms feel sturdy, not flimsy like some budget fixtures. The chain is a standard 59 inches, which gives you flexibility on ceiling height. One thing I appreciated: the wiring is color-coded and the ground wire is clearly marked, so even a first-timer with a voltage tester won't feel lost.

The main assembly task is screwing in the six glass shades, which is easy. The harder part? Getting the canopy flush against the ceiling. Like many budget chandeliers, the mounting plate is functional but not pretty — if your electrical box is slightly recessed or off-center, you'll want to double-check the alignment before tightening everything down.

**Performance and light output**

Here's where this chandelier shines. The six arms are spaced evenly, and with clear bulbs installed, the light distribution is genuinely even — no dark spots on your table. The open-bottom design means downward light is strong, which is exactly what you want over a dining table. With six 60-watt equivalent LED bulbs, this thing throws serious brightness; I'd recommend dimmable bulbs and a dimmer switch if your dining room doubles as a workspace.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 229 ratings, a few themes come up repeatedly. The value is the big one — buyers consistently note how this looks like a $200+ fixture. One reviewer put it plainly: "I was honestly shocked at the quality for the price — looks expensive, was not." Another common theme is the adjustable height: "Perfect for our 9-foot ceilings — we had plenty of chain left over." **Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The glass shades are the weakest point — they're thin and could chip if you're not careful during installation. Two buyers mentioned receiving a chipped shade and needing to contact customer service for a replacement (which they said was handled quickly, but it's still a hassle). Also, the fixture is rated for 60-watt bulbs, which is plenty, but the sockets themselves feel slightly less premium than the rest of the build. And as mentioned, bulbs aren't included — that's a hidden cost to budget for.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for three types of shoppers: first-time homeowners furnishing a dining room on a budget, anyone doing a quick rental update without investing in permanent fixtures, and farmhouse-style lovers who want the look without the designer markup. It's also a solid pick for a breakfast nook or kitchen island if you want the same aesthetic in a smaller footprint.

Skip it if you're looking for a statement piece with crystal accents or modern brass finishes — this is firmly in rustic-industrial territory, and the seeded glass won't match ultra-modern decor.

**FAQ**

*Can I install this on a sloped or vaulted ceiling?*  
The included mounting hardware is designed for flat ceilings. For sloped ceilings, you'd need an angled ceiling adapter kit — doable, but it's extra parts and some DIY know-how.

*What bulb type works best?*  
Go with 60-watt equivalent LED Edison bulbs in clear glass. The amber glow pairs perfectly with the seeded shades and gives that warm farmhouse ambiance. Avoid frosted bulbs — they dull the visual effect.

*How far down should this hang above a dining table?*  
Standard rule of thumb: 30–36 inches from the table surface. With the adjustable chain, you can dial it in between roughly 20 and 60 inches from the ceiling, covering most standard setups.
