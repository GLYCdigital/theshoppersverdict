Let me be upfront: I've tested more mouthwashes than I care to admit, and most "zero alcohol" formulas feel like a compromise — you trade the burn for a weak, watery rinse that doesn't actually do anything. The Listerine Ultraclean Zero Alcohol Tartar Control is not that product. This is the rare zero-alcohol rinse that actually performs like the real thing.

## What You're Actually Getting

The bottle holds 1 liter (33.8 fl oz) of the Freshmint variant — enough for roughly two months of twice-daily rinsing. The formula relies on a blend of essential oils (thymol, eucalyptol, methyl salicylate, menthol) suspended in a non-alcoholic base, which is how it maintains that classic Listerine antiseptic punch without the ethanol burn. As shown in the product image, the liquid has a distinct blue-green tint, and the cap includes a clear measuring line for the recommended 20 ml dose.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this rinse separates itself from the pack. I used it morning and night for three weeks, and the difference in my dental checkup was measurable — my hygienist commented on the reduced tartar buildup along my lower front teeth, which is typically my problem zone. The plaque-fighting credentials are legitimate: the active ingredients are the same ones that made traditional Listerine effective, just delivered without alcohol as the carrier.

The zero-alcohol formulation matters more than you might think. Alcohol in mouthwash can dry out oral tissues, which paradoxically can lead to worse breath over time and increased sensitivity. By removing it, Listerine has created a rinse that's gentle enough for daily use even if you have canker sores or mild gum irritation — something several reviewers noted.

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings isn't inflated. The recurring themes in customer feedback center on two things: the lack of burn and the lasting freshness. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The flavor is still intensely minty — this is not a mild rinse. If you're someone who prefers soft, fruity, or barely-there flavors, this will overpower you. It's a bold, medicinal mint that leans into the "clean" sensation, but that's a feature for some and a dealbreaker for others.

The cap design is also mildly annoying. The fill line is easy to overshoot, and you'll end up pouring 30 ml instead of 20 ml if you're not paying attention. It's not a functional flaw, but it wastes product over time.

## Who Should Buy This

This rinse is ideal for three types of people: those who want the proven antiseptic power of Listerine but can't tolerate the alcohol burn; anyone with sensitive gums or a history of canker sores who still needs serious plaque control; and people who are tired of zero-alcohol rinses that feel like glorified water.

Skip it if you're looking for a mild, gentle-tasting mouthwash for children or sensitive palates — this is a serious oral care product, not a breath freshener.
