**Bottom line up front:** The Buqikma short almond nail tips are a solid value for DIY acrylic users who want salon-level coverage without the salon markup. At roughly 240 tips per case, you're getting enough inventory to practice, mess up, and start over — which is exactly what most home nail techs need. But they're not press-ons, and they're not for beginners who want instant results.

## What's actually in the box

You get a clear plastic case with 10 compartments, each holding 24 tips — that's 240 total. Sizes run from 0 (smallest) to 9, and the short almond shape measures about 15mm from cuticle to tip at the largest size. That's noticeably shorter than the "short" offerings from brands like Aprés or Gelish, which tend to run closer to 18-20mm. If you've got naturally short nail beds, these will look like a natural extension rather than a dramatic enhancement.

The tips themselves are made from a semi-transparent ABS material. They're stiffer than cheap polycarbonate tips but have enough flex to bend slightly without snapping — important when you're applying them with acrylic and need to press them into the natural nail curve.

## How they perform in real use

I've used these for three different full sets, and the standout feature is consistency. Every tip in the case has the same thickness at the cuticle edge, which matters more than you'd think — uneven tips create lifting points where acrylic separates from the nail. I measured a few with calipers and got a uniform 0.5mm at the base, tapering to about 0.2mm at the free edge.

The sidewalls are straight and parallel, not flared like some cheap tips that look deformed. This makes fitting easier because you're not fighting a warped base. Filing the tip to blend into the acrylic takes about half the time I've spent with other budget brands.

One thing buyers consistently mention in reviews is how well these hold shape under acrylic. One verified reviewer wrote: *"I've been through three brands of tips and these are the first that didn't warp when I applied monomer. The short length also means they don't bend when I'm doing chores."* Another noted: *"Perfect for my client who works in food service — short enough to pass health code but still looks like a professional set."*

## Where they fall short

The glossy finish is the biggest annoyance. These tips arrive with a shiny coating that needs to be buffed off with a 180-grit file before acrylic or gel will grip properly. If you skip this step, you'll get lifting within a week. It's a 10-minute extra step, but it's easy to forget when you're in the flow of doing a set.

The size chart on the case is printed in tiny text that's genuinely hard to read in dim lighting. I ended up photographing it with my phone and zooming in. The clear plastic sizer strip that comes inside is more practical — lay it over your natural nails to find your size before you start.

Also worth noting: these are bare tips. No adhesive tabs, no glue included. If you ordered these expecting press-ons, you'll be disappointed. These are strictly for acrylic or gel overlay application.

## Who should buy these

**You should buy these if:** You do your own acrylics at home and want a bulk supply of reliable, consistent tips. You prefer a shorter length that doesn't interfere with daily tasks. You're a nail tech student who needs practice inventory without breaking the bank.

**Skip these if:** You want press-on nails with adhesive included. You need longer tips for dramatic stiletto or coffin shapes. You're a complete beginner who hasn't used acrylic before — the learning curve with liquid monomer and powder is steep enough without adding tip-fitting variables.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these fit natural nails that curve downward?**
A: The tips are fairly flat across the base, so if your natural nails have a pronounced C-curve, you'll need to pinch the tip slightly while the acrylic sets. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**Q: How many uses do you get from one case?**
A: If you're doing your own nails, a case lasts roughly 8-10 full sets, accounting for the occasional tip that snaps during fitting. For a professional doing clients, expect 4-5 sets since you'll use multiple sizes per hand.

**Q: Are these reusable?**
A: If you soak off the acrylic carefully without damaging the tip, yes. But at this price point, most users don't bother — it's cheaper to grab a fresh tip than to risk contamination or weakening from acetone.
