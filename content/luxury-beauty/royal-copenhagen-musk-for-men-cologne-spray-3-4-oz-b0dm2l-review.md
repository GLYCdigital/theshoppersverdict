The opening is a clean, powdery musk with a subtle hint of citrus that fades within ten minutes. What remains is the heart: a warm, skin-hugging musk with soft sandalwood and a whisper of amber. It's not a loud fragrance. It doesn't announce your arrival from across the room. Instead, it rewards proximity — the kind of scent someone notices when they lean in for a hug.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honesty time. Projection is the weak point. After that first hour, this sits close to the skin. If you want a fragrance that fills a room, keep scrolling. This is an intimate scent — someone will smell it when they're within arm's reach, not across the bar.

The bottle is also unapologetically utilitarian. It's plastic, it's light, and it doesn't look premium on a dresser. If you care about presentation, buy a decant and put it in a nicer atomizer.

Finally, the musk note is old-school. It's not the sweet, candy-like musk of modern designer releases. It's a classic, slightly soapy, barbershop-adjacent scent. If you only know modern sweet fragrances, this might read as "grandpa" to you. That's not a flaw — it's a character trait — but know what you're buying.
