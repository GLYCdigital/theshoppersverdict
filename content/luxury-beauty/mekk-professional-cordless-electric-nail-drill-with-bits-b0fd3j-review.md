## What's Actually in the Box

MEKK doesn't make you nickel-and-dime for accessories. The kit ships with the drill handpiece, a charging base, and 15 bits — including carbide cutters for acrylic, felt polishers, and various diamond bits for cuticle work. As shown in the product image, everything nests into a compact base that doubles as a storage stand. No separate purchase needed for your first several months of use.

The handpiece itself weighs in around 200 grams — not featherweight, but balanced enough for controlled work. The key spec is the 0-30,000 RPM variable speed range, which is the same territory as entry-level salon drills. That's genuine headroom for everything from gentle cuticle buffing at low speed to grinding down thick acrylic overlays.

## How It Performs in Real Life

This is where the MEKK earns its 4.4-star rating. The cordless design isn't just a gimmick — it removes the tangle frustration you get with corded drills, and the freedom to maneuver the handpiece without a cable pulling at your wrist makes detail work noticeably easier. Used on natural nails, it's handled e-file manicures cleanly. On gel extensions, it powers through with predictable speed control.

Reviewers consistently mention how well it handles the two jobs that matter most: color removal and cuticle prep. One verified buyer wrote, "I was skeptical about a cordless drill, but it plowed through my dip powder with zero issues." Another noted, "The variable speed is smooth — no jumping between gears like cheaper drills."

The battery is the tradeoff. MEKK claims 5 hours of life; real-world usage with continuous grinding lands closer to 3. It's fine for 2-3 full manicures on a charge, but if you're doing multiple clients back-to-back, you'll be charging mid-session. The charging stand makes this painless — you just dock it — but it's worth knowing before you rely on it professionally.

## Where It Falls Short

The handpiece is thicker than premium corded options like the Kiara Sky or Young Nails models. For a 20-minute soak-off removal, no problem. For a full acrylic set on both hands, your hand will fatigue sooner than it would with a slimmer salon-grade tool.

Noise is another honest consideration. At the 30,000 RPM ceiling, there's a whine that's more noticeable than high-end corded drills. It's not deal-breaking — it's not a dentist drill — but if you're sensitive to that high-frequency hum, you'll want to work at lower speeds.

The included bits are good starter quality, not salon professional grade. The carbide cutters will dull faster than premium replacements. Factor in $15-25 for upgraded bits eventually.

## Who Should Buy This

**The home DIY-er** — you do your own gel or acrylics and want to stop using acetone-soaked cotton and foil. This pays for itself in two salon visits.

**The mobile nail tech** — the cordless freedom is a genuine asset for house calls. Just pack the charging stand.

**Skip it if** you're a full-time salon professional doing 6+ clients daily. The battery life and handpiece ergonomics make a corded model the better investment.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this safe for natural nails?**
A: Yes, if you're careful. Start at the lowest speed (around 5,000 RPM) and use the finer bits for natural nail work. Save the carbide cutters for acrylic or gel only. Practice on a fake nail first if you're new to e-files.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Expect 3-3.5 hours of mixed use, not the 5 hours advertised. Docking it between manicures keeps it topped up.

**Q: Does it come with everything I need?**
A: Yes — 15 bits, the handpiece, charging stand, and a manual. You'll want to buy a dust collector separately if you're doing acrylic work indoors.
