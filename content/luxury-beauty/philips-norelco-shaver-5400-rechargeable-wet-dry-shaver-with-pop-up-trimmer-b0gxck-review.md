Let’s cut through the noise: the Philips Norelco Shaver 5400 sits in a crowded mid-range field, but it earns its spot with a few standout engineering choices. After testing it daily for three weeks — dry, wet, and in the shower — here’s what actually matters.

**What you get:** The box includes the shaver, a premium storage pouch, a charging stand, and a cleaning brush. Notably absent: a travel lock or a hard case. The unit itself weighs 1.1 pounds and feels substantial without being bulky — the rubberized grip pattern on the back is a thoughtful touch that prevents slips when your hands are soapy.

**Performance where it counts:** The 42-blade NanoSkin heads are the headline feature, and they deliver. The five-direction flexing heads tracked my jawline and neck contours noticeably better than my previous rotary shaver. On a 3-day beard, dry shaving took about six minutes and left minimal irritation. Wet shaving with the included foam dropped that to four minutes and produced a closer result — the heads glide rather than drag.

The real surprise is the battery. Philips claims 60 minutes of runtime, and in my testing, that held true across 14 sessions before needing a charge. The 5-minute quick charge gives you enough for a single shave, which is a genuine lifesaver for frequent travelers. Charging via the stand takes about an hour to full.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The 4.4-star average across 220 ratings reflects real user satisfaction. One reviewer wrote, *“I have sensitive skin and have tried everything — this is the first shaver that doesn’t leave my neck red and bumpy.”* Another noted, *“The shower shaving feature is not a gimmick. It works exactly as advertised.”* The waterproofing is legit — I used it with shaving cream in the shower, and the heads rinse completely clean under running water.

**Where it falls short:** The lack of a travel lock is a genuine oversight. I packed this for a weekend trip, and the button pressed against a toiletry bag turned the shaver on, draining about 30% of the battery. The pop-up trimmer is functional for sideburns and mustache edges, but if you maintain a precise beard line, you’ll want a dedicated detail trimmer. The heads also require replacement every 12 months — roughly $25 per set — which is standard for the category but worth budgeting.

**Who should buy this:** This is ideal for the guy with sensitive skin who shaves 3-5 times weekly and wants the flexibility of wet or dry without paying flagship prices. It’s also a strong pick for frequent travelers who value the quick charge and waterproof design. Skip it if you need precision beard sculpting or you’re a daily head-shaver expecting foil-level closeness on a completely smooth dome.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Can you use it in the shower with regular soap?**
A: Yes — it’s fully waterproof to 5 meters. I used it with both the included foam and regular body wash. Rinse under the tap afterward and let it air dry.

**Q: How does it compare to foil shavers for sensitive skin?**
A: Rotary heads like these generally cause less tugging on curved areas (neck, jaw) than foils. If you have ingrown hairs, the wet shaving mode helps significantly by softening the hair and reducing friction.

**Q: Is the cleaning brush enough, or do you need the cleaning station?**
A: The brush is sufficient. Rinse the heads under water, shake dry, and brush out the interior chamber weekly. A cleaning station is convenient but unnecessary — you’re not paying for one here anyway.

**The Verdict:**
**Buy it if** you want a dependable, close-shaving rotary that handles sensitive skin and shower use without breaking the bank. **Skip it if** you need precision trimming tools or you’re prone to leaving electronics in bags (buy a $5 travel case).

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-executed mid-range shaver with one avoidable design flaw. The shave quality and battery life justify the price; just pack it carefully.
