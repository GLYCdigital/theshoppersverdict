You know that moment when you press a powder over your foundation and it instantly looks… powdery? The bareMinerals Original Mineral Veil Talc-Free Pressed Setting Powder avoids that trap. This is the kind of setting powder that disappears into the skin while quietly doing its job — blurring pores, taming shine, and locking makeup in place without that dry, cakey mask.

Here's what you actually get: a sleek, compact press (0.21 oz) that clicks shut securely. Inside is a finely-milled translucent powder with a mirror and a thin puff. It's talc-free, which matters if your skin reacts to talc or you're tired of powders that flash back white in photos. The pressed format is a genuine upgrade from the loose version for anyone who travels or throws their makeup bag in a tote — no mushroom clouds of powder on your clothes.

**Performance and real-world wear**

The texture is where this powder earns its keep. It's whisper-fine, almost silky, and blends into foundation without disturbing it. I applied it with a fluffy brush over both liquid and cream foundations, and it set both without patchiness. The finish is natural matte — not flat, not dewy, just "my skin but better." Shine control held around four to six hours on my combination skin before a light blot was needed. For reference, that's comparable to the Laura Mercier powder but with less of that heavy, velvet-matte feel.

It also plays well over bare skin — a quick dusting solo gives a soft-focus effect that photographs beautifully. No white cast, no chalky residue.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scanning the 229 Amazon ratings at 4.6 stars, a few themes repeat. One reviewer said, "Finally a powder that doesn't make my dry patches look worse." **Where it falls short**

Honest tradeoffs exist. First, it's translucent only — there's no shade range, so if you're looking for a powder to add coverage or color-correct, skip it. Second, the included puff is flimsy; it applies unevenly and picks up too much product. Plan to use your own brush. Third, the price per ounce stings. At roughly $30 for 0.21 oz, you're paying for the brand and the talc-free formulation. If you go through powder quickly, that's a real cost.

**Who should buy this**

- **Sensitive-skin folks** who've had reactions to talc-based powders
- **Travelers** who hate loose powder explosions in their luggage
- **Photo-shoot or event wearers** who need a flashback-free setting finish
- **Dry-to-combination skin** that flakes with heavier matte powders

Skip it if you want a powder with buildable coverage, need oil control for a 12-hour shift, or you're on a strict drugstore budget.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work for oily skin?**
A: It controls shine for a few hours but isn't a heavy-duty oil sponge. Oily skin will need touch-ups, especially in summer. It won't clog pores, which is a plus.

**Q: How do you apply it for best results?**
A: Ditch the puff. Use a fluffy powder brush, tap off excess, and press or roll it into the skin rather than sweeping. Sweeping can disturb your foundation.

**Q: Is it really talc-free?**
A: Yes, the formula is talc-free and uses minerals instead. That's why it doesn't flash back in photos or cause that heavy feeling.
