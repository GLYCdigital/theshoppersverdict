Let's be blunt: most body washes under $10 a bottle either smell like a chemistry experiment or leave your skin feeling like you just washed with dish soap. The Dial Advanced Clean Gold line sits in a weird middle ground — it's not trying to be a $30 luxury product, but it's clearly not the blue gel your dad has used since 1998. After two weeks of daily use, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you're actually getting**

The 4-pack ships as four 16-fluid-ounce bottles. That's 64 ounces total, which at typical use (about 1.5 pumps per shower) lasts most people two to three months. The packaging is a gold-tinted opaque bottle — it looks more premium on a shower caddy than the standard Dial white bottles, though it's still clearly drugstore. No pump included, which is a genuine annoyance. The flip-top cap requires a two-handed squeeze, and you'll inevitably lose product down the drain.

The formula itself is where this earns its keep. The "Advanced Clean" technology uses a combination of mild surfactants — mostly sodium laureth sulfate with cocamidopropyl betaine as the secondary cleanser. That's a standard combo, but the gold infusion (actual colloidal gold particles, not just a marketing label) adds a smoothing agent that most comparably priced washes skip entirely.

**How it performs in the real world**

The lather is the standout feature. It's dense and creamy — closer to what you'd expect from a body butter-based wash than a $4 drugstore product. It spreads easily and doesn't require half the bottle to cover your whole body. Rinsing is clean; there's no slippery film left behind, but your skin doesn't feel squeaky either. That's a hard balance to hit, and Dial nails it.

I have dry, eczema-prone skin, and this is the first non-premium body wash I've used that doesn't trigger a flare-up. The gold particles seem to do real work here — my legs stayed soft through a full workday, which is rare even for brands like Dove or Olay. Multiple Amazon reviewers echo this. One buyer noted, "I have sensitive skin and this didn't cause any irritation, which is rare for me." Another said, "The scent is light but pleasant — it's the softness that won me over."

**Where it falls short**

The fragrance is the weakest link. It's described as "gold" — which apparently means a soft, slightly sweet, almost powdery scent that's pleasant in the bottle but evaporates within an hour of your shower. If you want a body wash that leaves your bathroom smelling like a spa or your skin carrying scent all day, this isn't it. That's a deliberate tradeoff for the sensitive-skin formula, but worth knowing.

The flip cap, as mentioned, is a real design flaw. And while the 4-pack is great value, you're committing to 64 ounces of the same product. If you hate the scent after the first bottle, you're stuck with three more.

**Who should buy this**

This is perfect for the person who wants luxury-feeling skin without the luxury price tag. It's ideal for:

- **Sensitive-skin sufferers** who've been burned by heavily fragranced washes
- **Dry-skin folks** in winter climates who need extra moisture without a heavy body butter
- **Families on a budget** — 64 ounces for under $15 beats most single premium bottles
- **Anyone tired of the "clean but tight" feeling** of cheap body washes

Skip it if you're a fragrance-first buyer, or if you need a pump dispenser for ease of use.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the "gold" actually in the formula, or just marketing?**
A: It's real — colloidal gold particles are listed in the ingredients. They act as a skin-conditioning agent, which explains the smoothness. It's not a gimmick, though the amount is small.

**Q: Can I use this on my face?**
A: Technically you could, but I wouldn't. The surfactant system is still a body wash formulation — it's gentler than most, but facial skin is more delicate. Stick to a dedicated face cleanser.

**Q: How does this compare to Dial's regular body wash?**
A: The Advanced Clean line is noticeably richer. The standard Dial is thinner, less moisturizing, and has a stronger (harsher) scent. This is a clear upgrade for about $1 more per bottle.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a budget-friendly body wash that performs like a mid-tier premium product. **Skip it if** you're chasing a long-lasting fragrance or need pump-top convenience.

At a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, this isn't a fluke — it's a genuinely well-formulated product that undercuts its competition on value. The gold gimmick actually works, the lather is excellent, and the price per ounce is hard to beat. It's not a luxury product, but it makes you feel like you're using one. That's a fair trade for the few compromises.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — docking half a point for the cap design and the fleeting scent.
