Let's be honest: banana combs are a love-or-hate hair accessory. They either hold your entire mane in a sleek twist or pop open mid-conversation and send hair everywhere. The 1st Choice 3-piece clincher set sits somewhere in the middle — better than drugstore basics, but with enough quirks that you'll want the full picture before buying.

## What You Actually Get

The set includes three banana combs: two standard-size clips and one larger jumbo version. As shown in the product image, these aren't the flimsy plastic combs from your childhood — they have a metal spring-loaded clincher mechanism with curved teeth that grip hair from both sides. The finish is glossy and smooth, with no sharp edges where the teeth meet the spine.

The jumbo comb is the selling point here. It's designed for thick, heavy hair that defeats standard clips. The two smaller ones work for half-up styles, sectioning, or pinning back layers.

## How They Actually Perform

I tested these on shoulder-length thick hair (the kind that snaps cheap clips in a day). The clincher mechanism is the real differentiator — when you press the handles together, the teeth interlock with genuine force. A full twist of hair stays put through a workout, a commute, and an afternoon of desk work. That's impressive for a $10-ish accessory set.

The teeth are smooth and rounded, sliding through hair without the snagging you get on cheaper combs. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I have very thick, coarse hair and these are the only banana clips that actually stay in place." That tracks with my experience — the grip strength is the standout feature.

## What Buyers Praise Most

The grip and holding power dominate positive reviews. Buyers with thick, heavy, or long hair consistently report these outperform every other banana comb they've tried. The set value also gets mentioned — you're getting three functional clips for roughly the price of one premium comb at a salon.

The jumbo comb, when sized correctly, handles full updos that would defeat most competitors. Multiple reviewers describe it as "the only comb that works for my waist-length hair."

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest downside: quality control on sizing is inconsistent. Several reviewers report the "jumbo" comb arrives barely larger than the standard ones, which defeats the purpose of buying a set. One buyer put it plainly: "The large comb is maybe a centimeter longer than the small ones. Not worth it if you need actual size variety."

The spring mechanism also has a break-in period. It starts stiff, and while it loosens with use, some buyers report it becomes *too* loose after a few months — clips that once held firm start slipping. If you're someone who uses one comb daily, expect to replace the set within a year.

Also worth noting: the glossy finish shows fingerprints and hair product residue quickly. Not a functional issue, but if you care about aesthetics, you'll be wiping these down often.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy if:** You have thick, heavy, or long hair that destroys flimsy clips. You want a budget-friendly set that actually holds an updo. You need multiple clips for different styles.

**Skip if:** You have fine or thin hair — the clincher mechanism will feel overkill and may even dent or crease delicate strands. You need precise sizing variety, since the jumbo inconsistency is real.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these work on fine, thin hair?**
A: Probably overkill. The strong grip can leave creases and the combs may feel bulky. Fine hair typically does better with lighter, smaller clips.

**Q: How do you open them?**
A: Squeeze the two metal handles together — this releases the teeth. It's counterintuitive at first (you're pressing, not pulling), but easy once you get the hang of it.

**Q: Are they safe for wet hair?**
A: The metal spring mechanism is water-resistant, but the glossy coating can wear with repeated wet use. Best for dry or damp hair, not soaking wet post-shower styling.

## The Verdict

**3.7/5** — The 1st Choice clincher comb set earns its rating through genuine grip strength that solves a real problem for thick-haired folks. But inconsistent sizing between the pieces and a spring that loosens over time keep it from greatness.

**Buy it if** you have thick hair and need a clip that actually holds. **Skip it if** you have fine hair or need consistent sizing across the set. At this price point, the two standard combs alone justify the cost — the jumbo is a bonus that might not be as jumbo as advertised.
