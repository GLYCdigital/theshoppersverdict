## What You're Actually Getting

The brush features a unique spiral bristle arrangement — not the standard rows you see on a Wet Brush or Tangle Teezer. The bristles are arranged in a continuous corkscrew pattern around a cushioned pad, with flexible, ball-tipped pins that bend rather than fight your hair. The handle is ergonomically curved with a rubberized grip, and the whole unit weighs about 4 ounces — light enough to toss in a gym bag without noticing it.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The spiral design is the headline feature, and it genuinely does what it claims. Because the bristles hit your hair at an angle as you glide the brush through, they work through tangles progressively rather than all at once. On dry, day-two hair with a few stubborn knots at the nape of my neck, the SHINLEA worked through them in about three passes with noticeably less pulling than my usual paddle brush.

Wet hair is where this brush earns its keep. Post-conditioner tangles that usually take me five minutes of careful work cleared in under two. The flexible bristles flex significantly when they hit a knot, reducing the force transmitted to your scalp and hair shaft.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I have very thick, curly hair that tangles if I look at it wrong. This is the first brush that doesn't make me dread detangling. ## Where It Falls Short

The spiral pattern has a real design flaw: it traps loose hairs between the bristle rows. You'll need to clean it regularly with a comb or the included cleaning tool, and if you shed heavily, you'll be doing this every few days. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Also, if your hair is very fine or fragile, the bristle pressure might feel too aggressive. A few reviewers mentioned it worked better on medium-to-thick textures. And while it's marketed for all hair types, I'd call it optimal for wavy to curly hair rather than baby-fine straight hair.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this on wet hair without damaging it?**
A: Yes, but don't go full-force. The bristles are flexible enough for wet use, but you should still apply conditioner and work from the ends upward. It's gentler than most brushes, but no brush makes wet hair invincible.

**Q: How do I clean the spiral bristles?**
A: Use the pointed end of a comb or a brush-cleaning tool to lift out trapped hairs. The spiral pattern holds onto loose strands more than a standard paddle brush, so expect to clean it weekly.

**Q: Does it work on extensions or weaves?**
A: Yes, but proceed carefully. The flexible bristles work well on extensions, but you'll want to brush from mid-length down and use a detangling spray first.
