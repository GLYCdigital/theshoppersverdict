## The $9 Concealer That Beats My $34 One

Here’s the thing about L.A. Girl HD Pro.Conceal: it’s not supposed to be this good. A drugstore concealer with a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings? I was skeptical. Then I used it on a model with deep-set under-eye circles and a red, broken-out chin — and had to double-check the price tag.

This isn’t a “good for the price” product. It’s a genuinely excellent concealer that happens to cost less than a fancy coffee drink.

**What you actually get:** A slim 0.28 fl oz tube with a doe-foot applicator (standard, but precise enough for spot concealing). The texture is liquid but not runny — think melted mousse. It spreads like a dream for about 45 seconds, then sets to a natural matte finish. No powder needed if you have normal-to-dry skin; oily folks might want a light dusting.

**Performance, no fluff:** One swipe covers dark circles at about 80%. Two swipes? Full coverage that still looks like skin, not spackle. I tested it on a 12-hour workday with no primer, no setting spray — there was minor creasing under my eyes at hour 9, but nothing a quick pat with a fingertip didn’t fix. On blemishes, it stayed put through a gym session and a post-workout smoothie. Impressive.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The shade range is the star. As one verified reviewer put it, *“I’ve been mixing foundation for years because nothing matched my olive undertone. This is the first concealer I’ve used straight from the tube.”* Another noted, *“The coverage is so full I skip foundation entirely now — just concealer, blush, and I’m done.”* Multiple reviewers mention the same thing I noticed: this outperforms concealers at 3-4x the price.

**Where it falls short — honestly:** That quick-dry formula is a double-edged sword. If you blend one eye fully before moving to the other, you’ll see patchiness where it set. Work in small sections. Also, the shade names are numbers (GC974, etc.) — no helpful “Fair with Pink Undertones” labels. You’ll need to do homework to match online. And if you have very dry, flaky skin, the matte finish can emphasize texture unless you moisturize first.

**Who should buy this:**
- **The minimal-makeup person** who wants one product to handle dark circles, blemishes, and redness without a full face.
- **The budget-conscious beauty lover** tired of paying $30+ for similar performance.
- **The shade-match struggler** — this range has genuinely diverse undertones, including olive, neutral, and deep shades that aren’t just “darker” versions of the same tone.
- **Skip it if** you prefer dewy, luminous finishes or need a hydrating formula for severely dry skin.

**FAQ shoppers actually ask:**

*Does it crease?*  
Under the eyes, expect minor creasing after 6-8 hours, especially if you don’t set it. On blemishes, no creasing at all.

*Is it good for dry skin?*  
It’s workable, but you need moisturizer or eye cream underneath. The matte finish will cling to dry patches if you skip prep.

*How do I choose my shade?*  
Look at the last letter in the shade number — N for neutral, W for warm, C for cool. Then match to the depth number. When in doubt, buy two shades close to your match; they’re cheap enough to blend.

**The Verdict:**

**Buy it if:** You want full, natural-looking coverage that survives long days without breaking the bank, and you’re willing to spend 10 minutes learning the blend-fast technique.

**Skip it if:** You need a luminous, hydrating formula or prefer the forgiveness of a slower-setting concealer.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It would earn a perfect 5 if the shade names were more intuitive and the working time were slightly longer. As is, this is the best value in the concealer aisle right now, and it earns that 4.7-star Amazon rating honestly.
