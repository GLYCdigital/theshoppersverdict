You know that moment when you're sectioning hair for a blowout, a deep conditioner, or a quick updo, and your clips just... give up? They slide out, snap shut too early, or leave dents in your strands. The RED by Kiss 80-piece set is designed to eliminate that frustration — and for the most part, it delivers. ## What You Actually Get

Open the package and you'll find 80 slide-in clips, all measuring 1.75 inches. They come in a mix of colors — typically black, brown, and clear — which helps you color-code sections if you're doing a detailed styling job. Each clip features a metal body with a spring-loaded jaw. The metal isn't flimsy; it has a solid weight that feels reassuring in your hand, unlike the cheap plastic alternatives that snap after a week.

As shown in the product image, these are the classic duckbill-style clips, but with a slimmer profile that makes them easier to slide into place without disturbing already-styled sections.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these on everything from freshly washed, slippery fine hair to thick, coarse, day-old hair. The grip is the standout feature. The metal spring applies consistent pressure across the entire jaw, so you don't get that annoying "one side holds, the other side gapes" problem. They stayed put through a full blow-dry session, which is more than I can say for cheaper drugstore clips.

The smooth edges deserve a mention. There's no rough burr or sharp seam where the metal folds over, so you won't snag or tear strands when sliding them in or out. For anyone with fragile or color-treated hair, this matters more than you'd think.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The 1.75-inch size is compact. If you have very thick, long hair, you'll find these struggle to encompass a large section. You'll need to work with smaller sections than you might with a 2.5-inch jumbo clip.

The spring tension inconsistency is real. In my set of 80, about six clips had noticeably weaker springs — they'd hold fine hair but slipped on thicker sections. It's a minor percentage, but if you're relying on every single clip for a full styling job, it's worth checking each one when they arrive.

Also, the color coating can chip off the metal over time, especially if you drop them on hard surfaces. It's purely cosmetic, but if you're particular about appearance, it's worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a hairstylist, a frequent DIY styler, or someone who sections hair for treatments and wants a bulk supply that won't quit. The 80-piece count makes it ideal for pros or anyone who loses clips constantly.

**Skip it if:** You only need two or three clips for a quick twist-up, or if you have extremely thick hair that requires jumbo-sized clips. For those use cases, a smaller pack of larger clips would serve you better.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these clips heat-resistant?**
A: The metal body handles heat well, but the colored coating can discolor if you use them directly against hot tools like curling irons. They're fine for blow-drying, but avoid direct contact with heated plates.

**Q: Can I use these on wet hair without rusting?**
A: Yes. The metal is coated and won't rust from regular use on damp hair. I've used them on soaking-wet sections during wash days with zero issues.

**Q: How do these compare to plastic clips?**
A: They grip significantly better because the metal spring doesn't lose tension as quickly as plastic hinges. They also last longer — plastic clips tend to crack at the pivot point within a few months.
