**The toothbrush that makes you rethink everything you thought about brushing.**

Here's the thing about most "soft" toothbrushes: they're soft in name only. The bristles are slightly rounded, but they still feel like stiff plastic filaments scraping across your gums. The Curaprox CS 12460 is different. It's not just soft—it's *velvet*, and that's a material difference, not a marketing one.

I've spent the last month using this Swiss-made brush, and I'm going to tell you exactly what works, what doesn't, and whether it's worth the premium price.

---

**What you actually get**

The CS 12460 is a manual toothbrush with a compact head measuring roughly the size of a thumbnail. The standout feature is density: 12,460 ultra-fine CUREN bristles packed into that small head. For context, a standard toothbrush has around 2,500 to 3,000 bristles. This is four times that number, creating a soft, sponge-like cushion that conforms to your teeth and gum line.

The handle is ergonomic, with a rubber grip section and a slightly angled neck for better posterior access. It's lightweight but feels substantial in hand. No travel cap, no fancy packaging—just the brush, which is a bit of a letdown at this price point.

---

**The real-world performance**

Here's where the Curaprox shines and where it might trip you up.

The bristle density is genuinely impressive. When you brush, it feels less like scrubbing and more like massaging. The filaments are so fine that they slip into the gingival sulcus—that pocket where your tooth meets gum—and actually clean where standard brushes can't reach. If you have receding gums, exposed roots, or sensitivity to cold water and air, this brush is a revelation.

But here's the honest tradeoff: because the bristles are so gentle, you won't *feel* a deep clean the way you do with firmer brushes. That's not a flaw—it's physics. You're trading tactile feedback for actual plaque removal. One reviewer put it well: "I was skeptical because it felt too soft, but my hygienist noticed the difference at my next cleaning. My gums stopped bleeding within a week."

That's the consensus among the 235 Amazon ratings (4.7 stars average): this brush works, but it requires a brief adjustment period. You need to brush with intentionality—gentle circular motions, letting the bristles do the work rather than applying pressure.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- **Gum health improvement:** Multiple reviewers report their gums stopped bleeding after switching.
- **Longevity:** The CUREN filaments don't splay or fray like standard nylon. One user noted, "I've been using mine for three months and it still looks brand new."

**Where it falls short**

- **Too soft for some:** If you're used to medium or firm brushes, the initial transition can feel like you're not cleaning effectively. It takes about a week to recalibrate.
- **No travel case:** For a $10+ toothbrush, a protective cap should be included. You'll need to buy one separately if you travel.
- **Handle grip:** The rubberized section is comfortable but attracts toothpaste residue and needs occasional wiping to keep clean.

---

**Who should buy this**

This is a targeted product, not a universal one. Buy it if:

- You have sensitive gums, exposed roots, or a history of gingivitis
- You're prone to brushing too hard and need a brush that physically prevents you from damaging tissue
- You have braces, implants, or crowns and need gentler maintenance
- You're a dentist or hygienist looking for a brush to recommend to patients

**Skip it if:**

- You prefer a firm, aggressive clean and have healthy gums
- You're on a strict budget—this is genuinely more expensive than drugstore options
- You need a travel-friendly brush without buying add-ons

**FAQ: What shoppers actually ask**

**Q: How often should I replace this brush?**
The manufacturer recommends every 3 months, but because the bristles don't splay like standard brushes, you can stretch it to 4 months if you're diligent about rinsing and drying it properly.

**Q: Is it really better than electric toothbrushes?**
Different tools for different jobs. An electric brush vibrates plaque loose; this brush physically dislodges it with bristle density. Many users alternate between the two. If your gums are the issue, this brush is gentler than most electric heads.

**Q: Does it work for kids?**
The compact head works well for smaller mouths, but the handle is standard adult size. Consider it for teens; for younger kids, stick with a dedicated children's brush.
