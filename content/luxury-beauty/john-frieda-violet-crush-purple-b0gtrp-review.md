Let's be blunt: most purple shampoos are either too weak to actually tone brass or so pigmented they leave your blonde looking lavender. The John Frieda Violet Crush sits in that rare sweet spot — and the 4.6-star average across 229 ratings confirms it's not just me saying that.

## What You're Actually Getting

The bottle is a standard 8.5 fl oz squeeze tube with a flip-top cap — nothing fancy, but functional. The product itself is where the attention went. The violet pigment is suspended in a pearlescent, lavender-toned base that lathers into a rich, creamy foam. As shown in the product image, the formula has that distinct purple hue that signals serious toning power — this isn't a clear shampoo with a drop of dye.

## Performance: The Real Test

I tested this on my own balayage (level 8-9 blonde with noticeable brass at the mids-to-ends). After one wash with a three-minute hold, the orange-gold cast was visibly neutralized — not fully gone, but a solid 60-70% improvement. The second wash took it to a cool, champagne-toned blonde that didn't look flat or ashy.

The formula is sulfate-free, which matters if you've invested in salon color. It lathered well even without sulfates, and I didn't need to double-shampoo to feel clean. My hair felt conditioned afterward — not squeaky or straw-like, which is a common complaint with clarifying purple shampoos.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The consensus across reviews centers on two things: effectiveness and gentleness. One buyer wrote, *"Finally a purple shampoo that doesn't turn my silver hair blue,"* while another praised it for *"actually toning without drying out my highlighted ends."* Multiple reviewers noted it outperformed more expensive salon brands they'd used previously.

## Where It Falls Short

The thicker consistency is a double-edged sword. On fine hair, it's easy to over-apply, and if you don't rinse thoroughly, you'll notice a slight residue at the roots. The instructions say leave it on for 2-3 minutes — respect that. I left it for five minutes once on my most porous ends and got a subtle violet tint that took two regular washes to fade.

Also, if you have very light platinum or silver hair, this may be more pigmented than you need. You'll want to dilute it with your regular shampoo or reduce the hold time to 60 seconds.

## FAQ

**How often should I use this?**
Start with 1-2 times per week. If your brass is stubborn, every other wash for the first week, then back off to once weekly to maintain. Overuse leads to that purple tint I mentioned.

**Will it stain my shower or hands?**
The formula rinses clean from skin and tile with minimal residue. I didn't experience staining on my white shower wall, but I also rinse promptly after washing.

**Is it safe for keratin-treated hair?**
Yes — the sulfate-free formula won't strip keratin treatments. Just keep the hold time on the shorter end to avoid over-toning.
