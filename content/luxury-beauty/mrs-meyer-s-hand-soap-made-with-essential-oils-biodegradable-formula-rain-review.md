You're probably here because you've seen this soap in a thousand "favorites" roundups, or you caught a whiff of it at a friend's sink and couldn't stop thinking about it. The Rain Water scent is the sleeper hit of the Mrs. Meyer's lineup — not as loud as Lavender, not as zesty as Lemon, but somehow the one people repurchase for years.

Let's get the practical stuff out of the way first. You're getting three 12.5-fluid-ounce pump bottles. That's roughly 37 ounces total, which for typical household use (hand washing maybe 8-10 times a day) translates to about two to three months per kitchen or bathroom. The pump mechanism is solid — no dripping, no jamming, and it dispenses a workable dime-sized dollop rather than a watery stream.

**The scent is the star, but it's a supporting actor, not the lead.**

Rain Water is marketed as a fresh, clean aquatic scent. In practice, it smells like what rain actually smells like after it hits warm pavement — petrichor, if you want the technical term. It's subtle. You'll catch it when you lift your hands to your face, but it won't perfume your entire bathroom. One Amazon reviewer put it well: "It doesn't smell like chemicals or artificial 'ocean breeze.' It just smells clean, like a window left open after a storm." Another noted, "My husband, who hates scented products, actually asked me to keep buying this one."

**Performance wise, this soap does its job without drama.**

The formula is built around plant-derived cleaning agents and aloe vera, plus essential oils for the fragrance. It produces a rich, creamy lather that actually feels like it's doing something — not the thin, disappointing foam you get from some natural brands. It rinses off completely without leaving that filmy residue or the tight, stripped feeling you associate with harsh antibacterial soaps. Your hands feel clean and soft, not parched.

Because it's a biodegradable formula free of parabens, phthalates, and artificial colors, it's also a reasonable choice for households trying to reduce chemical load — though I'd stop short of calling it "clean beauty" in the strictest sense. The fragrance still comes with the caveat that essential oil blends can irritate extremely sensitive skin. If you have eczema or known fragrance sensitivities, patch test before you commit.

**Where it falls short — and it's worth being honest here.**

The price is the biggest sticking point. At $31.30 for the three-pack, you're paying roughly $10.43 per bottle. That's two to three times what you'd spend on a comparable drugstore hand soap like Softsoap or Dial. You're paying for the Mrs. Meyer's name, the garden-inspired aesthetic, and the genuinely nice scent. For many people, that's worth it — the brand has near-cult status for a reason. But if you go through hand soap quickly or are outfitting multiple bathrooms, the cost adds up.

The Rain Water scent is also polarizing in its subtlety. If you're the type who wants your hand soap to announce itself — the Peppermint and Lavender varieties are much bolder — this one might underwhelm. It's a "close your eyes and imagine a clean spring morning" kind of scent, not a "smells like a spa" experience.

**Who should actually buy this?**

If you're someone who washes their hands frequently (healthcare workers, parents, home cooks) and wants a soap that won't wreck your skin, this is a solid investment. The aloe and plant-derived cleansers make it noticeably gentler than standard supermarket soaps. It's also a great choice for a guest bathroom — the Rain Water scent is inoffensive enough to please almost anyone, and the classic Mrs. Meyer's packaging looks intentionally styled rather than cluttered.

If you're on a tight budget, or you're the kind of person who buys hand soap in bulk at warehouse clubs, this isn't the product for you. You're paying for the experience as much as the soap itself.

**Frequently asked questions:**

**Is Mrs. Meyer's hand soap actually biodegradable?**
The formula is biodegradable per the brand's claims, meaning the plant-derived surfactants break down in standard wastewater treatment conditions. It doesn't mean you can dump it in a lake with a clean conscience, but it's a more environmentally thoughtful formula than many conventional options.

**Does Rain Water smell like other "ocean" or "aqua" soaps?**
No — and this is the key distinction. Most aquatic-scented products smell like a synthetic cocktail of melon, cucumber, and "blue." Rain Water is much drier and earthier, closer to the smell of wet stone or fresh soil after a storm. It's noticeably more natural and less perfumey.

**How long does one bottle last?**
With average use — maybe 10 hand washes a day in a busy household — a single 12.5-ounce bottle lasts roughly four to six weeks. The gel consistency means you need less per wash than watery soaps.

**The Verdict:**

Buy it if: You value a genuinely pleasant, understated scent, want a soap that doesn't dry out your hands, and appreciate a brand with a consistent track record (4.8 stars across 16,000+ ratings doesn't happen by accident).

Skip it if: Price per ounce matters more to you than the experience, or you prefer bold, assertive fragrances in your personal care products.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting a half-star for the premium pricing and the scent's subtlety, which won't suit everyone. Everything else — lather, gentleness, packaging, reliability — earns its keep.
