**The eco-friendly floss pick that doesn't feel like a sacrifice**

Most biodegradable floss picks are a compromise. Bamboo handles splinter. PLA plastic snaps. The string shreds. You end up with a drawer full of guilt and a mouth full of frustration. These picks from Natural Dental solve most of that — and at $9.99 for 75, they're priced competitively with the plastic crap you're trying to replace.

**What you actually get**

A recyclable paper box containing 75 individually molded picks. The handle is made from PLA (plant-based polylactic acid, typically derived from corn), which is the key differentiator here. Unlike bamboo, it won't crack or splinter. Unlike conventional polypropylene, it breaks down in commercial composting facilities. The floss itself is coated in candelilla wax — a plant-based alternative to petroleum-derived paraffin — and infused with peppermint oil for flavor.

As shown in the product image, the picks have a tapered, ergonomic shape with a textured grip section. They're slim enough to reach back molars without feeling like you're wrestling your own jaw.

**Real-world performance**

I floss twice daily, and I put these through a month of use. The handle stiffness is the standout feature. It flexes just enough to absorb pressure without bending permanently — a common failure point in cheaper bioplastics. The string is smooth and slides between tight contacts, though it's not quite as slick as Glide's PTFE-coated stuff. If you have seriously crowded teeth, you'll need to work the pick gently rather than forcing it.

The mint flavor is subtle and natural — think peppermint tea, not chewing gum. It dissipates in about 30 seconds and leaves a clean, non-medicinal taste.

**Where it falls short**

The pick's head is slightly bulkier than conventional brands. If you have very narrow spaces between teeth, you may need to angle it more aggressively. And the string tension is a touch loose compared to Glide picks; aggressive flossers will need to adapt their technique.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I compost these at home?**  
A: PLA requires commercial composting conditions (high heat, controlled moisture). Home compost piles typically won't break them down fast enough. They're biodegradable, not backyard-compostable.

**Q: Are they safe for braces or dental work?**  
A: Yes, the smooth PLA handle won't scratch brackets, and the wax-coated string is gentle on restorations. Just don't snap the string against wires.

**Q: How does the count compare to regular picks?**  
A: Most plastic picks come in 150-300 count boxes. At 75, this is smaller, but the material quality justifies the difference for most buyers.
