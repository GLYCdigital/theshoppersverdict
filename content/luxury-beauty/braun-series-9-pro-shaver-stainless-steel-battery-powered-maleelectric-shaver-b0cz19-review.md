# Braun Series 9 Pro+ Shaver: The Premium Electric Shave, Deconstructed

Let's cut through the marketing. The Braun Series 9 Pro+ is positioned as the pinnacle of electric shaving — a $300+ stainless steel instrument promising a shave that rivals a blade. After combing through 220 ratings and digging into how this thing actually performs, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the shaver itself (the stainless steel "M" variant you're looking at), a premium charging stand, a leather-like travel case, a cleaning brush, and the SmartCare center if you buy the full kit. The unit weighs in at roughly 6.5 ounces — noticeably heavier than the plastic Series 7, which translates to a feel of quality in hand. The 5-blade system (four cutting elements plus a skin guard) sits behind a flexible head that pivots in 10 directions.

Battery life is rated at 60 minutes of shaving per full charge — about 3 weeks of daily use. A 5-minute quick charge gives you one full shave, which is a genuinely useful feature for travelers.

## Real-World Shaving Performance

Here's where the Series 9 Pro+ separates itself from cheaper Braun models. The headline feature is AutoSense — a chip that reads hair density 160 times per minute and adjusts power accordingly. In practice, this means it doesn't bog down on a week of growth, and it doesn't buzz aggressively on a 24-hour shadow. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've used electrics for 15 years. This is the first one that doesn't pull on my neck."*

The 5-blade system handles two key complaints about electric shavers: closeness and irritation. The shave isn't literally blade-close — anyone claiming that is lying — but it's about 90% there. On a clean-shaven morning, you'll feel slight stubble if you rub against the grain. For most men, this is acceptable. The real win is zero razor burn, even on sensitive skin.

## Build Quality and Maintenance

The stainless steel body is the star. It feels like a precision instrument, not a bathroom gadget. The IPX7 waterproof rating means you can shave in the shower, and the head rinses clean under running water in seconds. The included cleaning station uses an alcohol-based solution that both cleans and lubricates the foils — but it's loud (imagine a small printer) and the refill cartridges run $20 a pop.

The honest downside: replacement heads are expensive. A new shaver head and foil combo runs $60-80, and Braun recommends replacing it annually. Over 5 years of ownership, that's the real cost of admission. Several Amazon reviewers flagged this, with one noting: *"Great shave, but my old Panasonic cost half as much to maintain."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the flaws. First, no USB-C charging. In 2026, a flagship shaver with a proprietary charging cable is mildly infuriating — especially for travel where you're already carrying USB-C for everything else. Second, the cleaning station is borderline unnecessary. It's bulky on a countertop, and hand-rinsing achieves 90% of the cleanliness with none of the noise. Third, the LED display is basic: battery level and a cleaning indicator. For this price, a more informative interface would be nice.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have coarse or dense facial hair that's destroyed cheaper electrics. You want a premium daily shaver that handles both 5 o'clock shadow and occasional multi-day growth. You value build quality and don't mind paying for replacement heads annually.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget — the Series 7 at roughly half the price delivers 85% of the performance. You need a blade-close shave for professional reasons (stick with a manual razor). You're a frequent traveler who hates proprietary chargers.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Braun Series 9 Pro+ is the best electric shaver most people will ever own. It's expensive, the maintenance costs sting, and the missing USB-C port is a legitimate annoyance. But if you want a premium, irritation-free daily shave that holds up for years, this is the benchmark. It's a luxury purchase — just know what you're signing up for.

**Buy it if** you've tried cheaper electrics and been disappointed. **Skip it if** you're hoping for a miracle — no electric razor matches a blade, and this is simply the closest you'll get.
