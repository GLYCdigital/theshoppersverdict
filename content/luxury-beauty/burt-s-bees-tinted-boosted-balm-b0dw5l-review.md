If you've ever slapped on a "tinted balm" that turned out to be a waxy crayon with zero pigment, you know the struggle. Burt's Bees Tinted Boosted Balm isn't that. It's the rare hybrid that actually delivers on both promises — serious hydration and a visible, flattering wash of color. With 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in makeup bags. But is it *your* next lip purchase? Here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The balm comes in a standard twist-up tube (0.15 oz — about what you'd expect from a premium lip product). The formula leans on responsibly sourced shea butter, jojoba oil, and sunflower seed oil for the moisture base, with a blend of fruit and plant waxes giving it that smooth, gliding texture. There's no minty sting or overpowering scent — just a faint, clean vanilla-adjacent aroma that disappears quickly.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this across a full workday, in dry office air and through a lunch that involved a messy sandwich. The hydration claim holds: lips felt conditioned for hours after application, not just while the balm was sitting on top. The texture is thicker than a gloss but lighter than a lipstick — think a creamy balm-stain hybrid. It melts in without that slippery, hair-sticking-to-lips feeling.

The tint is where this shines. One swipe gives a sheer wash; two or three swipes build to a medium, polished color. It won't give you full-coverage drama, but it's perfect for the "my lips but better" aesthetic. It also doesn't settle into lip lines — a common fail point for tinted balms — and leaves a soft, natural sheen rather than a high-gloss lacquer.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

Scrolling through the Amazon feedback, a few themes pop up repeatedly. One verified buyer notes: *"Finally a tinted balm that doesn't dry my lips out by noon. The color is subtle but noticeable — I get compliments."* Another echoes the longevity: *"Lasts through coffee, which is more than I can say for my $35 lipstick."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Be honest about the limitations. The shade range skews neutral — pinks, roses, and a muted berry dominate. If you want a bold red or a deep, vampy plum, this isn't your product. Darker shades in the lineup can also cling to dry patches, so exfoliate first or stick to the lighter end of the spectrum. And while the balm stays hydrating, the *tint* fades after about 2–3 hours, especially if you're eating or drinking. You'll reapply more often than you would a lipstick — but the tube is slim enough that it's not a chore.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The minimalist** who wants one product that does skincare + color without a 5-step routine.
- **The clean-beauty shopper** who checks ingredient lists obsessively (this passes the test).
- **The dry-lip sufferer** who's given up on lipsticks that crack by noon.
- **The low-maintenance commuter** who wants a polished look without a mirror.

Skip it if you need dramatic, long-wearing pigment or a wide shade range for bold looks.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this actually moisturizing, or just a tinted lipstick in disguise?**  
A: Genuinely hydrating. Shea butter and jojoba oil are the base, not afterthoughts. Most reviewers report lips feeling softer after wear, not drier.

**Q: How does it compare to the classic Burt's Bees Beeswax Lip Balm?**  
A: The Boosted Balm is thicker, more pigmented, and has a more emollient feel. The classic balm is lighter and more of a basic protectant; this is a step up in both color and conditioning.

**Q: Does it have SPF?**  
A: No SPF in this line. If sun protection is a must, layer a separate SPF balm underneath or look to Burt's Bees' dedicated SPF products.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a clean, hydrating lip product with a dependable, pretty tint that works for everyday wear. **Skip it if** you need bold color payoff, long-wearing pigment, or a wider shade range.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A solid, honest performer that earns its praise. It's not revolutionary, but it does exactly what it promises, and that's increasingly rare.
