## Where It Falls Short

Now the honest part. If your hair is **severely tangled**, this brush will not save you. Boar bristles are smooth by design; they don't have the aggressive teeth needed to rip through knots. You'll still need a detangling comb for wet hair, full stop. That's not a flaw — it's the nature of the material — but it's a limitation worth knowing before you buy.

Second, the **initial shedding**. Natural boar bristles loosen during the first week of use. It's normal, but if you're the type to panic over a few bristles in your hair, brace yourself. It stops after break-in.

Finally, the price. This is a premium set, and it's priced like one. If you're on a strict budget, you can find cheaper single brushes, but you'll likely sacrifice bristle density and handle quality.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this brush work on wet hair?**
A: No. Boar bristles are for dry hair only. Using it on wet hair will cause breakage and excessive shedding. Detangle wet hair with a wide-tooth comb first.

**Q: How often should I clean it?**
A: Once a week. Remove hair from the bristles, then wash with lukewarm water and a drop of gentle shampoo. Dry bristle-side down to prevent water from loosening the glue.

**Q: Is this suitable for curly hair?**
A: If you want to stretch curls and add shine, yes — but use it sparingly. Over-brushing curly hair will create frizz. Use it only on dry, styled hair for polish.
