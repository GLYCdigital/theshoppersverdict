Let's be direct: glitter is a pain in the ass to buy online. You get 12 colors, five are basically the same gold, and the "fine" glitter clumps like wet sand. That's not the case here.

The LEOBRO 32-color set has earned its 4.7-star average from 235 ratings for one simple reason: it respects the craft. These are true fine-mill particles, roughly 0.1mm, which means they suspend evenly in resin instead of sinking to the bottom or floating in clumps on top. The particle size is consistent too — no random chunky flakes ruining a smooth pour.

**What you actually get**

Thirty-two individual jars at 0.7g each. That sounds small, and it is, but fine glitter is dense. A little goes a long way. The range covers metallics (gold, silver, bronze), holographic shifts, neons, and some genuinely surprising shades like deep teal and burgundy that most budget sets skip. Every color in the lineup is distinct — no three variations of "champagne" padding the count.

The jars themselves deserve a mention. They have sealed twist tops with sifter holes, which means you can shake out a controlled dusting or pop the lid for heavier coverage. I've knocked these over mid-project more times than I'll admit and lost maybe a gram total. That's unheard of in the glitter world.

**How it performs**

In resin, this is where the set shines. The fine grind means it disperses evenly without that "sparkle on top, nothing underneath" problem. One reviewer noted, *"I've used this in deep pour epoxy and it stayed suspended perfectly — no settling after 24 hours."* Another said, *"The holographic shades are unreal in UV resin earrings. Photos don't do them justice."* For nail art, the particle size works well under top coat without that gritty texture coarser glitters leave behind.

Polymer clay users report good results too — the fine particles condition into the clay without creating structural weak points. And for card making or scrapbooking, the sifter jars make precise application genuinely easy.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the limitations. The lighter shades — white, pastel pink, pale yellow — struggle with opacity. Over a dark resin base or black paper, they disappear almost entirely. You need to layer them or use a white base coat first. That's a real constraint, not a nitpick.

The jar size is the second issue. At 0.7g each, if you're doing regular production runs (selling jewelry, making ornaments in bulk), you'll burn through the popular colors fast. This is a sampler set for versatility, not a bulk purchase. Buy a larger single-color jar once you find your workhorse shades.

One more thing: the lids can be stubborn to open initially. The seal is tight by design, but I saw a few complaints about needing pliers. A quick twist with a rubber band solves it.

**Who should buy this**

This set is ideal for three groups: resin beginners who want to experiment with color options before committing to bulk jars, nail techs and hobbyists who need variety for different client looks, and mixed-media artists who want a comprehensive palette without buying 32 separate products. It's less ideal for high-volume production or anyone working exclusively with dark base materials.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Is this glitter safe for cosmetics or body use?*
No. The listing explicitly states it's for crafts, not cosmetics. Don't use it on skin or nails if you're applying it directly to the body — the fine particles can irritate.

*Does it work in all resin types?*
Yes, both epoxy and UV resin. The particle size is consistent enough to suspend in both. Just stir thoroughly to avoid air bubbles trapping the glitter.

*How do the holographic colors actually look?*
They shift across the spectrum depending on light angle. In direct light, you get a rainbow effect; in soft indoor light, they read as subtle shimmer. More impressive in person than in product photos.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a comprehensive fine glitter collection for resin, nail art, or card making without paying premium per-jar prices. The color variety is legitimately useful, and the particle quality is consistent.

**Skip it if:** You need bulk quantities, work mostly with dark bases, or expect cosmetic-grade safety. This is a craft product, and it's priced and sized accordingly.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A versatile, well-executed set that delivers on its promise. The opacity issues with light shades and small jar sizes keep it from a perfect score, but for the craft market, this is genuinely one of the better glitter investments you'll make this year.
