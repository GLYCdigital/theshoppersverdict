If you've been eyeballing salon-grade e-files but balked at $150+ price tags, the MelodySusie Electric Nail Drill Machine deserves your attention. This compact portable unit packs enough torque for gel removal, acrylic filing, and natural nail shaping — all in a package smaller than a hair dryer. After digging through 220 Amazon ratings and the technical specs, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The kit includes the drill handpiece, a control box with a speed dial, six bits (tungsten carbide and ceramic), a drill stand, dust brush, and a carrying case. As shown in the product image, the handpiece is slim and lightweight — about the size of a thick pen. The control box has a built-in display showing RPM, and the whole thing runs on a standard AC adapter.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 20,000 RPM motor is genuinely capable. Reviewers consistently note it chews through acrylic and gel polish without the motor straining or overheating. One buyer put it plainly: *"I've done 20+ full sets of acrylics with this and it hasn't slowed down."* The torque holds up even when you press a bit into harder material — a common weakness in cheaper e-files that stall under pressure.

Vibration is the standout surprise. At mid-range speeds, the handpiece stays smooth enough for detailed work on natural nails without the "buzzing" that makes your hands go numb. That's rare at this price point.

The bits included are solid. The carbide cutters handle removal work, while the ceramic bits give a finer finish for shaping. You won't need to buy replacements immediately, though you'll eventually want a finer grit set for cuticle work.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Ease of use for beginners**: Multiple reviewers mention being able to switch from manual filing to the e-file within minutes
- **Quiet operation**: One reviewer noted it's *"much quieter than my previous drill — I can use it while my kids sleep"*
- **Durability**: Several long-term owners report 6–12 months of regular use without issues

## Where It Falls Short

No foot pedal is the biggest frustration. You're constantly reaching for the dial to adjust speed mid-manicure, which breaks your flow. If you're doing both hands, you'll be contorting to adjust settings every few minutes.

The forward-only rotation is a real limitation for cuticle work on your dominant hand. Reverse rotation helps when working at awkward angles, and its absence means you'll need to reposition more often.

A few reviewers also mentioned the bits can get hot during extended use on acrylic — take breaks or use a lower speed to avoid burning the client (or yourself).

## Who Should Buy This

**Home DIY nail enthusiasts** who do their own gels, dip powder, or acrylics and want to speed up removal and prep. Also great for **beginners wanting to learn e-file technique** without dropping $200+ on a professional unit.

**Skip it if** you're a licensed nail tech doing 5+ clients daily — you'll want a foot pedal, reverse rotation, and a stronger motor for production work. Budget another $100 for a pro-grade tool.

Yes, at low speeds (5,000–8,000 RPM) with the ceramic bits, it works well for gentle buffing and shaping. Just keep the pressure light.

**Q: How loud is it?**

**Q: Does it come with everything needed to start?**
Yes — drill, 6 bits, stand, dust brush, and case. You'll only need to supply nail files and polishing products.
