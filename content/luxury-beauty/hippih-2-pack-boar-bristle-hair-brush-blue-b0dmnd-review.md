Let’s be honest about the boar bristle brush hype: a good one can transform dull, frizzy hair into a glossy, polished mane. A bad one just yanks your hair and sheds bristles everywhere. The HIPPIH 2-pack sits right in the middle — and for the price, it leans heavily toward the good side.

**What you actually get**

Two identical brushes in the blue finish shown in the product image. Each has a solid wooden handle (not hollow plastic, which surprised me at this price point), a cushioned pad, and densely packed boar bristles. The bristles are genuine — they have that slightly irregular, natural taper you don't get with synthetic nylon. The handle is about 8.5 inches long, making it easy to maneuver, and the cushion has just enough give to contour to your scalp without feeling flimsy.

**Performance and real-world use**

Boar bristles work by distributing your scalp's natural oils from root to tip — that's what creates the shine. The HIPPIH brushes do this effectively on medium-thickness, straight-to-wavy hair. I used one daily for three weeks: it smoothed flyaways, added visible gloss by day four, and helped stretch time between washes.

For detangling, it's decent but not exceptional. The bristles are firm enough to work through minor knots, but if you have thick or curly hair, this brush will fight you. It's a finishing brush, not a detangling tool — use a wide-tooth comb first, then run this through to smooth and polish.

The 2-pack logic makes sense: keep one at your desk or in your bag, one at your vanity. Or alternate them to let the bristles "rest" — which genuinely extends their lifespan.

**Where it falls short**

The shedding is real. During the first two weeks, I lost bristles every time I brushed — not alarming amounts, but enough to notice. This is common with natural bristle brushes (the fibers are fixed into the cushion, and loose ones work free during initial use), but it's worth expecting. It does settle down after consistent use.

The handle, while ergonomic, is not lacquered or sealed as thoroughly as premium brands. Don't leave it in a damp bathroom — it can absorb moisture and warp over time. Keep it dry.

And the big one: **this brush will not work for everyone.** If you have thick, coarse, curly, or coily hair, the all-boar bristle design is too stiff and won't penetrate deeply enough to distribute oils effectively. You'd need a mixed brush (boar + nylon) for that. This is a fine-to-medium hair tool.

**Who should buy this**

- **Fine to medium hair** — if you want to add shine and reduce frizz without product buildup, this is your brush.
- **Oily scalps** — daily brushing distributes oils and reduces the greasy look between washes.

**Skip it if** you have thick, textured, or curly hair, or if you need a brush that detangles aggressively.

**FAQ**

**Q: How do I clean a boar bristle brush?**
A: Remove loose hair with your fingers or a comb. Once a week, wash the bristles with a drop of mild shampoo in lukewarm water, rinse bristle-side down, and dry flat overnight. Never soak the wooden handle.

**Q: Will this work on wet hair?**
A: No. Boar bristles are for dry or nearly dry hair. Using on wet hair causes breakage and will destroy the bristles quickly.

**Q: How long do these brushes last?**
A: With proper care, 1–2 years. The bristles soften slightly with use, which is normal. If the cushion starts to split or bristles fall out in clumps (not singles), replace them.
