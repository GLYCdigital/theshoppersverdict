# Morphe Face The Beat Makeup Brushes: A 5-Piece Set That Actually Earns Its Spot

Most brush sets under $30 are a gamble. You get one brush that works, two that shed, and a couple that feel like they were assembled from a craft store clearance bin. The Morphe Face The Beat 5-piece set is not that gamble. With a 4.6-star average across 229 Amazon ratings, this kit has earned real staying power — and after digging into what buyers actually say, it's clear why.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting five brushes that cover the essentials: a large powder brush (the M120), a dense foundation brush (the M136), a concealer brush (the M156), a blush brush (the M164), and a fluffy eye blender (the M508). The handles are a matte black with the Morphe logo stamped on each ferrule — nothing flashy, but they feel solid in the hand.

The bristles are synthetic and cruelty-free, which matters if you're avoiding animal hair. They're also incredibly soft — several reviewers compared the powder brush to "buffing with a cloud," which tracks with my read on the density. These aren't scratchy bargain-bin bristles; they're the same material Morphe uses in their higher-end lines.

## How They Perform in Real Life

The foundation brush (M136) is the workhorse here. It's dense and flat-topped, designed for buffing liquid or cream foundation into the skin. Buyers consistently note that it doesn't soak up product the way cheaper synthetic brushes do — one reviewer said, "I use half the foundation I used to because this brush actually deposits product instead of eating it." That's the difference between a $10 brush and a $25 brush, and it's worth repeating.

The powder brush is large enough to set your whole face in two or three sweeps, but it's not so fluffy that it kicks up powder everywhere. The concealer brush is small and precise — good for under-eye coverage and spot-correcting. The blush brush has a slight taper that makes it easy to place color on the apples of your cheeks without over-spreading. And the eye blender is a solid utility brush for crease work or blending out transition shades.

One thing to note: the foundation brush is dense. If you're using a very liquidy foundation, you'll want to work in small sections and blend quickly, or you'll see streaks. A couple of reviewers mentioned this, and it's a fair criticism — but it's also a fixable technique issue, not a fatal flaw.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 229 ratings, the recurring themes are softness, value, and even application. One reviewer wrote, "I've spent $60 on a single MAC brush that didn't feel this good." Another noted that after three months of daily use and weekly washing, "they still look brand new — no shedding, no bent bristles."

The value angle is the strongest argument here. For the price of roughly one mid-range brush, you're getting five that perform at or above that level. That's a compelling math problem for anyone building a kit from scratch.

## Where It Falls Short

The shedding issue is real, though not severe. Several buyers reported losing a few bristles during the first wash — that's normal for synthetic brushes as the glue settles, and it stops after the second or third wash. If you're obsessive about zero shedding, wait it out.

There's also no case or pouch included. For travel, you'll need to find your own storage solution, which feels like a missed opportunity at this price point. And the handles, while comfortable, are on the longer side — if you have a small makeup bag, they may not fit vertically.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Beginners** who need a complete face routine without dropping $100+ on individual brushes

- **Travelers** who don't mind sourcing their own brush roll and want a compact, reliable kit
- **Skip it if** you're a pro who needs specialty brushes (angled liners, precise crease brushes) — this set covers basics only

## FAQ

**Q: Are these brushes good for cream products like concealer and cream blush?**
A: Yes — the dense synthetic bristles work well with cream formulas. The foundation brush handles cream blush and contour fine, though you'll want to blend quickly to avoid streaks.

**Q: How do these compare to Morphe's more expensive lines like the X collection?**
A: The Face The Beat set uses the same synthetic fiber technology but with simpler handles. Performance is comparable for basic application; you're paying less for the lack of fancy packaging, not lower quality bristles.

**Q: Do I need to wash these before first use?**
A: Yes — wash with a gentle brush cleanser or baby shampoo, let them dry flat, and you'll remove any manufacturing residue and loose bristles from the first cycle.
