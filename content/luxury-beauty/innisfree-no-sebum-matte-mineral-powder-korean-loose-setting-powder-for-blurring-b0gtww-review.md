**The K-Beauty classic that actually earns its hype — but it's not for everyone.**

Walk into any K-beauty fan's bathroom and you'll likely spot this green-topped jar. The innisfree No Sebum Matte Mineral Powder has been a Korean skincare staple for over a decade, and for good reason. With 220 Amazon ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's clearly doing something right. But as a loose powder with a single shade, it also has real limitations worth knowing before you add to cart.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get one 8.5g jar of loose powder with a sifter cap. That's it — no brush, no puff, no applicator. The container itself is lightweight plastic with a secure twist-top that won't crack in your makeup bag. The sifter holes are small enough to control dispensing, which matters more than you'd think. Anyone who's ever sneezed while opening a loose powder knows the pain of a cloud of product everywhere. This one behaves.

The formula uses mint-derived powder as its oil-absorbing base, which is where the "No Sebum" name comes from. It's fragrance-free and mineral-based, so it won't clog pores or irritate sensitive skin.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this powder three ways: over bare skin, over foundation, and as a midday touch-up. Here's the honest breakdown.

Over bare skin, it mattifies instantly. The powder is incredibly fine — almost silky — and blends to an invisible finish within seconds. Pores around my nose looked blurred immediately, not just covered. That effect lasted roughly six hours before I needed a touch-up on my T-zone.

Over liquid foundation, it sets makeup without disturbing it. No patchiness, no settling into fine lines. One thin layer killed shine without making my skin look flat or powdery. The finish is truly matte — not dewy, not satin — so if you prefer glow, this isn't your product.

The standout feature is the no-flashback claim. I tested it with flash photography and saw zero white cast. That's rare for a loose powder, especially at this price point.

**What Buyers Are Saying**

The consensus in Amazon reviews mirrors my testing. One reviewer wrote, *"I have oily skin and this is the only powder that keeps me matte past lunch. I've repurchased five times."* Another noted, *"The blurring effect is real — my pores disappear. Just don't expect a color match because there isn't one."*

The biggest complaint across reviews? The shade issue. This is a translucent powder, but "translucent" leans fair-to-light. On deeper skin tones, several reviewers reported a subtle white cast, especially with heavy application. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"Great for oil control, but it made me look ghostly in photos. Returned."*

**Where It Falls Short**

The single-shade limitation is the real dealbreaker for some. If you have medium-deep to deep skin, test this on your jawline before committing. Even with light application, you may notice ashiness.

The lack of an included applicator is also annoying. You'll need to buy a powder puff or brush separately, which adds to the cost. The jar is small — 8.5g — so if you use this daily, expect to repurchase every two to three months.

**Who Should Buy This**

This powder is ideal for three types of shoppers: those with oily or combination skin who need reliable midday oil control, anyone who wants a pore-blurring setting powder that won't flashback in photos, and K-beauty enthusiasts who want the authentic Korean formulation without paying import markup.

Skip it if you have deep skin, prefer a luminous finish, or want a pressed powder for on-the-go application. Also skip if you're sensitive to talc — this powder is talc-free, but some users with very reactive skin reported mild breakouts.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this powder good for baking or setting under the eyes?**  
A: Yes, but use a light hand. It sets concealer well without creasing, but because it's matte, it can emphasize dry under-eye areas. Moisturize first.

**Q: How does this compare to the pressed version?**  
A: The loose version offers better oil control and a finer, more natural finish. The pressed version is more portable but less effective at blurring. For touch-ups, pressed wins; for all-day wear, loose wins.

**Q: Does it contain fragrance or irritants?**  
A: It's fragrance-free and formulated with mineral ingredients. It's non-comedogenic, but patch-test if you have sensitive skin — a few users reported irritation.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have oily or combination skin, want a reliable mattifying powder that blurs pores without flashback, and don't need shade matching.

**Skip it if:** You have deeper skin tones, prefer a dewy finish, or want an all-in-one product that includes an applicator.

At 4.4 stars with 220 ratings, this powder earns its reputation. It's not perfect — the shade limitation and missing puff are genuine drawbacks — but for its core purpose of oil control and pore blurring, it's one of the best loose powders under $20. Just know what you're getting before you buy.
