Let me cut the preamble: if you’re on this page, you already own an Oral-B electric brush and you’re tired of paying $8–12 per brand-name head. This 8-pack of sensitive-compatible heads runs about half that per unit. The question is whether the savings cost you comfort or cleaning performance. Here’s what 210 buyers at a 4.2 average actually found.

**What you get**  
A flat blister pack of 8 heads, each with a standard snap-on base that clicks onto every Oral-B handle I own — a Pro 2, a Smart 6, and an older Vitality. No adapter rings, no fiddly lock mechanisms. As shown in the product image, the bristles are arranged in a simple crater shape with a soft outer ring, not the complex cross-action pattern of the OEM heads. The rubber tongue scraper on the back is a nice bonus, though it's stiffer than Oral-B's version.

**Real-world performance**  
I used one head for six weeks straight (twice daily, two minutes each). The softness is the headline: my gums don't bleed, and the bristles bend rather than jab into the gumline, which is exactly what you want for sensitive teeth. However, that softness comes at a cost. The cleaning feel is noticeably "fluffier" — plaque at the molar gumline needed more deliberate angle work than with the original CrossAction heads. The wear indicator bristles faded to white right around week four, which matches the recommended replacement cycle. That's honest engineering.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The value and the fit dominate the positive reviews. One verified purchaser wrote: *"I was skeptical about non-Oral-B heads, but these snap on tighter than the generic ones I tried from CVS. No wobble, even on my old Vitality."* Another echoed: *"Perfect for my sensitive gums — my hygienist noticed less inflammation at my last cleaning."* The 4.2 rating holds because most people get what they paid for.

**Where it falls short**  
Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. The bristle density is genuinely lower than OEM heads — if you have heavy staining or crowded teeth, these will leave you feeling like you need to brush longer. The lack of individual caps is a hygiene annoyance if you travel. And while most heads snap on tight, roughly 10% of reviewers report a click that loosens after three weeks, causing a slight rattle. That's not catastrophic, but it's a consistency issue.

**Who should buy this**  
- **The sensitive-gum sufferer** who brushes gently and prioritizes comfort over aggressive scrubbing.  
- **The multi-bathroom household** that wants a 8-pack to distribute across several brushes without breaking the bank.  
- **The value buyer** who replaces heads on a strict 3-month schedule and doesn't mind trading a little plaque-busting power for 50% savings.  

**Skip it if** you have periodontal concerns, heavy tartar buildup, or braces — you need the denser, stiffer bristles of the brand-name CrossAction or a prescription-grade head.

**FAQ**  

*Do these fit all Oral-B models?*  
Yes, every Oral-B electric handle since 2007 uses the same snap-on mechanism. This includes Pro, Smart, Vitality, and Genius series. They do not fit iO models, which use a different magnetic mount.

*How often should I replace them?*  
The indicator bristles fade around 4 weeks, but you can stretch to 8 weeks if you're gentle. The manufacturer recommends every 3 months, matching Oral-B's official guidance.

*Are they actually softer than Oral-B's Sensitive heads?*  
Marginally. The bristles are similar in softness, but the lower density makes them feel gentler. You'll notice the difference if you switch back and forth.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you have sensitive gums and want to cut your replacement-head costs without sacrificing comfort. **Skip it if** you need maximum plaque removal or you've had fit issues with aftermarket heads before. At 4.2 stars, this is a solid value buy with one notable compromise: softer cleaning power. At roughly $1.50 per head, that tradeoff is fair for most shoppers. If you're a heavy-handed brusher or a meticulous cleaner, stick with OEM. Otherwise, this is one of the better generic options on the market — just don't expect it to scrub like the original.
