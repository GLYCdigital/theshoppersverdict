Let’s be honest: most nail files are an afterthought. You grab whatever’s at the drugstore checkout, file a few rough edges, and toss it in a drawer. The Tammy Taylor 180 Grit Emery Boards are not that. These are the files your nail tech actually uses — the ones that make you wonder why you ever settled for the flimsy foam ones that disintegrate after three uses.

### What You Actually Get

The pack contains 100 individual emery boards, each measuring approximately 7 inches long and 1 inch wide — the standard professional size. The 180 grit rating is the sweet spot for natural nails: coarse enough to shape and shorten efficiently, but fine enough that you won’t feel like you’re sanding down a deck. Each board has the same 180 grit on both sides, so there’s no guessing which side does what.

### Performance and Build Quality

Here’s where these files separate themselves from the pack. The grit is *consistent* — not just at the start, but across the entire surface and through multiple uses. I filed a full manicure (both hands, shaping and smoothing) on a single board, and it still had bite left. Cheaper files lose their abrasiveness after one or two nails; these hold up for several sessions.

The construction matters too. The cardboard core is dense and rigid — not the hollow, crushable kind you find on budget files. It won’t bend or snap mid-file, which is crucial when you’re applying pressure to shape a stubborn corner. One caveat: keep them dry. The core absorbs moisture, so don’t leave them on a damp counter or use them with wet hands.

### Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. First, there’s no finer grit side. If you want to buff and shine the nail edge after shaping, you’ll need a separate block or a higher-grit file. Some users found the 180 grit slightly aggressive for very thin, brittle nails — if that’s you, consider a 220 or 240 grit instead.

The cardboard core, while sturdy, isn’t waterproof. Leaving them in a humid bathroom can cause warping. Store them in a dry drawer or container.

### Who Should Buy These

**You, if you:** Do your own manicures regularly, are a professional nail tech, or want a bulk supply that lasts months. These are also great for families — with 100 boards, everyone gets their own.

**Not for you, if you:** Only file your nails occasionally (once a month or less) and don’t want to store a bulk pack. In that case, a smaller pack of higher-end files makes more sense.

### FAQ

**Q: How long does one board actually last?**
A: For a standard at-home manicure, expect 3–5 uses before the grit noticeably dulls. For shaping only (not shortening), it can stretch to 6–8 uses.

**Q: Are these safe for acrylic or gel nails?**
A: Not ideal. The 180 grit is designed for natural nails. For acrylics or hard gels, you’ll want a coarser 100–150 grit file.

**Q: Can I disinfect and reuse these between clients?**
A: Yes, but only with a dry method like UV sterilization. Wet disinfectants will ruin the cardboard core.
