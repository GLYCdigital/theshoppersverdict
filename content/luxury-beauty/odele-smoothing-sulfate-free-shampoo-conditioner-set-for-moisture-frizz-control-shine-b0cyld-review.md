**The $25 Question: Can a "clean" shampoo actually smooth hair?**

Most sulfate-free shampoos feel like a compromise. You trade lather for dryness, or you get slip but zero cleansing. The Odele Smoothing Set tries to give you both — and for the most part, it pulls it off.

As shown in the product image, you're getting two minimalist 13.5 oz bottles — shampoo and conditioner — in a frosted matte finish that looks far more expensive than the price point. But the real question is whether the formula delivers. After testing and digging through 215 ratings, here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box**

Two full-size bottles. No samples, no travel sizes, no fluff. The shampoo is a clear gel that lathers surprisingly well for a sulfate-free formula — a common complaint with "clean" shampoos is that they feel like washing with water, but this one generates enough foam to feel like you're actually cleaning your scalp. The conditioner is a rich, butter-like cream with shea butter and jojoba oil as headliners.

**Performance and real-world results**

The smoothing claim holds up for most hair types. The shampoo cleanses without stripping — you know that "squeaky clean" feeling? You won't get it here, and that's a good thing. Your scalp feels clean, not tight. The conditioner does the heavy lifting: it detangles well and visibly reduces frizz on air-dried hair.

Where it shines: humidity. One reviewer noted, "I live in Florida and this is the first shampoo that keeps my hair from puffing up by noon." Another mentioned it "tamed my wavy frizz without making my hair greasy."

But here's the catch: the conditioner is *thick*. If you have fine hair, you'll want to use a nickel-sized amount, not a quarter-sized one. Multiple reviews mention needing to rinse thoroughly to avoid buildup. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a learning curve.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- **Frizz control that lasts** — not just a temporary "just washed" effect
- **Clean ingredient list** — no sulfates, parabens, phthalates, or dyes
- **Cruelty-free and vegan** — Leaping Bunny certified, which matters to a growing number of shoppers
- **Scent is subtle** — a light botanical blend that doesn't clash with your perfume or styling products

**Where it falls short**

The thick conditioner is the most consistent complaint. Fine-haired users report it weighs hair down, especially if they apply it to their roots. The fix is easy — apply only to the mid-lengths and ends — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Also, the smoothing effect isn't a miracle worker. If you have coarse, tightly curled hair, this will soften and manage frizz, but it won't give you a salon-grade sleek blowout. It's a maintenance product, not a transformation product.

**Who should buy this**

- **Wavy-to-curly haired people** fighting frizz and humidity
- **Color-treated hair** — the sulfate-free formula won't strip your color
- **Clean beauty shoppers** who want efficacy without harsh ingredients
- **Sensitive scalps** — the fragrance is mild and the formula is gentle

**Skip it if** you have very fine, limp hair and need volume, or if you prefer strongly scented hair products.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this set good for color-treated hair?**
A: Yes. The sulfate-free formula won't strip your color, and the smoothing ingredients help keep hair healthy between color treatments. Just use the conditioner sparingly if your hair is fine.

**Q: How does the shampoo lather without sulfates?**
A: It uses a blend of gentle surfactants (like sodium coco-sulfate derived from coconut) that create a moderate lather. It's not as foamy as traditional shampoos, but it's noticeably better than many sulfate-free options.

**Q: Can I use the conditioner as a leave-in?**
A: Technically yes, but I wouldn't. It's thick enough that it can weigh hair down if not rinsed. If you want a leave-in, apply a tiny amount to damp ends only.
