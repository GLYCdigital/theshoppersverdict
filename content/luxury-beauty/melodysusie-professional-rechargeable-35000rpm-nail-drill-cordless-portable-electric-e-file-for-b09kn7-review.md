Let me save you the guesswork: The MelodySusie Professional Rechargeable 35000RPM Nail Drill is the rare cordless e-file that doesn't feel like a compromise. It won't replace a $400 Kupa in a full-time salon, but for home users, mobile techs, and anyone tired of wrestling with cords, this is the one to beat at its price point.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the drill handpiece, a charging base, six bits (carbide and felt varieties), a hex key, and a user manual. The handpiece itself is surprisingly svelte — about 6.5 inches long and weighing under 8 ounces. That matters when you're holding it for 45 minutes straight; hand fatigue is real, and this one won't cramp your grip.

The LED screen on the base shows your current RPM and direction (forward/reverse). It's not a gimmick — being able to see exactly 12,000 RPM versus 18,000 RPM makes a tangible difference when you're switching between removing gel polish and shaping a free edge.

## Real-World Performance

The brushless motor is the star here. At 35,000 RPM, it chews through acrylic and hard gel without the bogging-down you get from cheaper corded drills. The swivel head rotates 360 degrees, which means you can angle the handpiece naturally rather than twisting your wrist into awkward positions.

Battery life is genuinely impressive. In my testing, a full charge handled two complete manicure sets (including soak-off gel removal and full shaping) with juice to spare. The USB-C charging base is convenient, though 3 hours for a full charge is on the slower side — this isn't a "plug in for 15 minutes before a client" situation.

Noise-wise, it sits in the acceptable range for a 35K RPM drill. It's not silent, but it's noticeably quieter than the $40 corded units that sound like a dentist's office. Your neighbors won't hear it through walls.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The cordless freedom is the #1 recurring theme in Amazon reviews. One verified buyer put it well: *"I can do my own nails at my kitchen table without worrying about outlets or the cord pulling the drill out of my hand."* Another tech praised the power: *"I've used $150 drills that didn't have this much torque. It eats through acrylics like butter."*

Multiple reviewers mention the swivel head as a deciding factor — it's a feature usually reserved for much pricier models.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the bits. The six included accessories are okay for beginners, but the carbide bits dull faster than pro-grade options. If you're doing regular acrylic work, budget for a set of better bits within your first month. That's an extra $15-25, so factor it into your total cost.

The reverse function is noticeably weaker than forward rotation. It's fine for light cuticle work, but don't expect the same aggressive removal in reverse. Also, there's no foot pedal. For many home users that's fine, but if you're used to pedal control from a salon, you'll miss it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home users doing their own gel/acrylic nails** — this replaces multiple salon visits in a month
- **Mobile nail techs** who need cordless flexibility at clients' homes
- **Beginners** who want room to grow without buying a second drill later

Skip it if you're a high-volume salon professional doing 8+ clients daily — you'll want a corded workhorse with a pedal and stronger reverse torque.

## FAQ

**Is this drill good for beginners?**
Yes, but start on the lowest speed (around 5,000 RPM) and practice on a practice finger or fake nail first. The learning curve is manageable, and the variable speed control helps you ease in.

**How long does the battery actually last?**
Realistically 2.5 to 3 hours of continuous use. That translates to 2-3 full manicure sets, or about a week of personal use for most people.

**Does it work on natural nails?**
Yes, at low speeds with the fine bit. But be careful — 35,000 RPM is powerful. Use the lightest touch on natural nails and keep the speed under 10,000 RPM.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want cordless freedom, real power, and don't want to spend $300+ on a salon-grade drill.

**Skip it if:** You're a full-time professional who needs a foot pedal and aggressive reverse, or you're on a tight budget and can't afford quality replacement bits.
