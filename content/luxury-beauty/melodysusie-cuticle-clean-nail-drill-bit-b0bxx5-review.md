If you've ever watched a nail tech whip through cuticle work in seconds and wondered why your own e-file bit leaves you with ragged edges and burning friction, the culprit isn't your technique — it's likely your bit. The MelodySusie Cuticle Clean Nail Drill Bit is a $9 upgrade that solves the most common complaint I see from at-home manicurists: bits that clog, dull quickly, or chew up the nail plate.

**What you actually get**

This is a single tungsten steel bit with a standard 3/32-inch shank, which fits virtually every consumer e-file on the market — MelodySusie's own drills, Makartt, LONOVEER, and most salon-grade models. The cutting head is shaped like an inverted cone with a rounded tip, designed specifically for the proximal nail fold area where cuticle accumulates. It's not a multi-pack, not a kit — just the one bit, which is worth noting if you're expecting a full set at this price point.

**Performance and build quality**

The tungsten steel material is the headline feature here, and it matters. Cheaper carbide bits tend to go dull after 10–15 uses, which is when you start noticing more pressure needed and more heat generated. This bit holds its cutting edge significantly longer — most users report months of regular use before noticing any performance drop. The flute design is also genuinely well-thought-out: it channels removed cuticle away from the work area rather than letting it pack into the grooves, which means less stopping to clean and less friction buildup.

In real-world use, this bit excels at removing dead cuticle and lifting the eponychium without biting into live tissue. The rounded tip gives you a margin for error that sharper cone bits don't offer. As one verified buyer noted: *"I was terrified of e-filing after a bad experience with a cheap bit that gouged my nail. This one is so much smoother — it just glides along the cuticle line and takes off the dead skin without catching."*

**Where it falls short**

The honest downside: this bit is *aggressive* on natural nails if you're not careful. The tungsten steel doesn't flex, so if you press down hard or use a high speed, you will over-file. Start at the lowest RPM setting and work up — this is not a bit for heavy-handed beginners. Also, there's no protective cap or case included. At $9, that's not a dealbreaker, but you'll want to store it in a separate container to avoid nicking the cutting edge against other tools.
