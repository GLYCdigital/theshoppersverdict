**Is a $20 nail file actually worth it?** I've tested dozens of files over the years, from $2 drugstore boards to $40 glass options, and the 3 Swords Germany sapphire file occupies a strange middle ground: it's not the cheapest, not the fanciest, but it might be the last one you ever buy.

## What You Actually Get

The 3 Swords file is a single-piece stainless steel tool, roughly 7 inches long, with sapphire crystal grit fused to both sides. One side is coarse (around 180 grit), the other fine (around 320 grit). The steel is 1.5mm thick — noticeably sturdier than the flimsy metal files you'll find in drugstore sets. As shown in the product image, it has a slight taper at both ends, which makes reaching the corners of nails much easier than with a flat rectangular file.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where this file separates itself from the pack. That sapphire coating isn't painted on — it's a crystalline layer fused to the metal at high temperature. The practical difference? **It files in both directions without shredding your nail edge.** Most cheap metal files only work in one direction and leave frays. This one smooths the edge whether you push or pull.

I tested it on natural nails, gel overlays, and acrylic tips. On natural nails, the coarse side removes length efficiently — about 10 strokes to take off 2mm. The fine side leaves a glass-like edge that needs almost no buffing afterward. On acrylic, it cut through faster than my electric drill's coarse bit, which surprised me.

The balance is genuinely good. The file weighs about 30 grams, with the weight distributed toward the center, so your hand doesn't cramp during longer sessions. After three months of weekly use, the grit shows zero wear. That's the real value proposition: **a $20 file that lasts decades versus $20 of disposable boards that last months.**

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the downsides are minor but real. **The lack of a protective case is the biggest annoyance** — this file is hard enough to scratch glass, so it will absolutely scratch other tools in your drawer. A simple sleeve would cost them 50 cents to include.

The fine grit side is still coarser than a glass file's finest surface. For people with extremely thin, peeling nails, the coarse side can feel aggressive — you'll want to start with the fine side and be gentle. Also, if you're expecting a completely silent filing experience, the metal-on-nail sound is similar to any other file — not louder, but not whisper-quiet either.
