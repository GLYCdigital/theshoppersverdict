If you do nail art more than once a month, you already know the frustration: cheap liner brushes that splay after two uses, or single-tip pens that force you to switch tools mid-design. The Artdone 5-piece copper liner set sits at a sweet spot — it's not the cheapest option, but it's built well enough that you won't be replacing it every season. Here's the real breakdown after handling these brushes and combing through what other buyers report.

## What you actually get

Five brushes, all with copper-colored aluminum handles and black nylon bristles. The tips range from an ultra-fine 00 liner (think hairline details, spiderweb patterns) to a slightly wider striper for filling in bold lines or creating French tips. The handles are lightweight but solid — no hollow-feeling plastic here. As shown in the product image, the set presents neatly, though there's no storage case, so keep that in mind for travel.

## Performance and real-world use

The bristles are the star. They're firm enough to push gel polish or acrylic paint without bending, but flexible enough to glide smoothly around cuticles. I tested them with both regular lacquer and gel — cleanup was straightforward with acetone, and the tips didn't splay after multiple wipes. One buyer noted, "The fine tip is actually fine — not a chisel in disguise like some other sets." That tracks with my experience: the 00 liner holds a sharp point consistently, which is the #1 thing you want from a detailing brush.

The copper coating on the handles is durable, not just decorative. It doesn't chip or flake after repeated cleaning. That said, the shorter handle length (~4.5 inches) takes some adjustment if you're used to full-length brushes. It's fine for most hand positions, but if you have larger fingers, you might find yourself gripping closer to the ferrule than you'd like.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 210 ratings (4.2 average), the recurring theme is value for precision work. Multiple reviewers mention using these for stamping cleanup — the fine tip catches excess polish along the cuticle line without gouging the skin. Another common compliment: "These hold up better than brushes twice the price." That aligns with the nylon bristle quality — they resist fraying and maintain their shape session after session.

Beginners also report success with these, which is notable because liner brushes typically have a learning curve. The variety of tips means you can practice with the wider striper before attempting micro-details with the 00.

## Where it falls short

Honest tradeoffs, because they exist. First: no caps. The fine tips are delicate — if you toss these in a drawer or bag unprotected, you risk bending a tip. It's a minor omission, but for a "luxury" positioning, protective caps feel like a should-have. Second, the short handles mentioned above. Third, while the nylon bristles are sturdy, they're not ideal for heavy acrylic application — these are liners, not mixing brushes. Don't expect them to double as all-purpose tools.

## Who should buy this

- **Nail art hobbyists** who want a dedicated liner set without paying salon-supply prices
- **Beginners** who need multiple tip sizes to learn with — the variety reduces frustration
- **Stamping users** who need a precise cleanup brush that won't shred after acetone exposure

Skip it if you need full-length handles, prefer capped brushes, or you're a professional who already owns high-end kolinsky liners — this set won't replace those.
