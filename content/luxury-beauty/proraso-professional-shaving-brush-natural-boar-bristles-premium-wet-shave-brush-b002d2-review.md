**What You Actually Get**

The box contains exactly one thing: a brush with a dense knot of natural boar bristles seated in a classic resin handle. The handle is simple — no chrome, no faux-wood grain, just a functional, grippable black resin that won't slip when your hands are soapy. The bristle loft is roughly 50mm, with a knot diameter around 24mm. That's a mid-sized brush, not a gigantic lather bomb, but substantial enough to build a creamy lather in a bowl or directly on your face.

The bristles arrive stiff. Very stiff. This is normal for boar — they need a break-in period of 10-15 shaves before they soften and split at the tips. If you've only ever used synthetic brushes, this will feel like scrubbing your face with a wire brush for the first week. That's the tradeoff for what boar does best: holding water and releasing it slowly into the soap, which creates a richer, more cushioning lather than most synthetics can manage.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The handle is solid and weighted well — it won't roll off your sink or feel cheap in hand. The bristles are packed tightly, and after the break-in period, they develop that coveted "gel-like" tip feel that boar enthusiasts chase. The brush excels at face lathering, especially with hard soaps like Proraso's own green tub or Mitchell's Wool Fat. It loads soap quickly and releases it evenly across the face.

One thing that stands out: the water retention. Boar bristles hold significantly more water than synthetic fibers, which means you'll want to shake it out thoroughly before loading soap, or you'll get a watery, airy lather. Once you learn that trick, the brush produces thick, yogurt-consistency lather that stays on your face without drying out mid-pass.

**Where It Falls Short**

The break-in period is the elephant in the room. If you're not prepared to deal with a stiff, prickly brush for the first couple of weeks, you'll be disappointed. It also sheds a few bristles initially — that's normal, but it can alarm first-time boar users. The handle, while functional, is utilitarian. If you want something that looks premium on your counter, this isn't it. And unlike synthetic brushes that dry in hours, boar bristles take 24+ hours to fully dry, so you'll want to store it in a ventilated area to prevent odor buildup.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this brush work for bowl lathering, or only face lathering?**  
A: Both, but it shines at face lathering. The stiff backbone makes it easy to work lather into your beard. For bowl lathering, it works fine — just load more soap than you think you need.

**Q: How long does the break-in period actually take?**  
A: Plan on 10-15 shaves. You'll notice the tips starting to split and soften around the 5-6 shave mark, but full softness comes closer to two weeks of regular use.

**Q: Will it shed bristles?**  
A: Yes, initially. Expect 5-10 loose bristles in your lather during the first few weeks. If it continues shedding heavily after a month, that's a defect and you should return it.
