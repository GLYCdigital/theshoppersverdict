Let me be blunt: you don't need to spend $180 on Skinceuticals CE Ferulic. The SeoulCeuticals 20% Vitamin C serum delivers the same core actives — 20% L-ascorbic acid, ferulic acid, and vitamin E — for $19.98. With 27,650 Amazon ratings holding a 4.4-star average, this is one of the most-reviewed vitamin C serums on the platform. But is it a true dupe, or just a cheap copy? I tested it for three weeks and dug through hundreds of verified reviews to give you the full picture.

**What's in the bottle**

The 1oz (30ml) amber glass dropper bottle contains a watery, pale-gold liquid. The formula stacks 20% L-ascorbic acid (the gold-standard vitamin C form) with ferulic acid to stabilize it and vitamin E for antioxidant synergy. Two molecular weights of hyaluronic acid are included — this matters because high-strength vitamin C is notoriously drying, and the HA helps pull moisture back into the skin. As shown in the product image, the packaging is functional rather than fancy: no pump, just a standard dropper. That's fine, but you'll want to keep the cap tight since L-ascorbic acid oxidizes quickly when exposed to air.

**Real-world performance**

I applied this every morning on clean, dry skin before moisturizer. The texture is thin and absorbs in about 60 seconds — no greasy residue. By week two, I noticed my post-acne marks faded noticeably faster, and my skin had that "glass skin" glow Korean beauty promises. The hyaluronic acid does its job; I didn't experience the flakiness I've had with other 20% vitamin C serums.

But here's the honest tradeoff: the smell. Ferulic acid has a distinct odor that most people describe as "hot dog water." It dissipates after 10–15 minutes, but it's there every morning. One top reviewer wrote, *"The smell is bad. Like processed meat. But it fades and my skin looks amazing, so I keep buying it."* That sentiment echoes across hundreds of reviews — people tolerate the scent because the results justify it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The most common theme in verified reviews is the price-to-performance ratio. One 5-star review stated: *"I've used Skinceuticals for years. This is 90% as effective at 10% of the price. My dark spots are fading just as fast."* Another reviewer noted: *"My dermatologist asked what I was using because my skin looked brighter at my follow-up."* The brightening effect on hyperpigmentation and post-acne marks is the most frequently cited benefit, followed by improved skin texture within 2–3 weeks of daily use.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the smell, there are two real issues. First, 20% L-ascorbic acid is a high concentration — if you've never used vitamin C, this will likely sting and may cause purging. Several reviewers with sensitive skin reported redness and breakouts in the first week. Second, the watery consistency can pill under thick moisturizers or sunscreen. Apply it to damp skin and wait two minutes before layering.

Also note: because this is L-ascorbic acid (not a stabilized derivative), it oxidizes faster than pricier formulations. You'll notice the liquid turning darker yellow-orange around the 3–4 month mark. That's normal, but it means this isn't a "buy two and stock up" product — buy one, use it daily, replace it.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for three types of shoppers: anyone currently paying $100+ for CE Ferulic serums and wanting a budget alternative; those with hyperpigmentation, sun spots, or post-acne scarring looking for a proven brightening agent; and vitamin C veterans who know their skin tolerates high concentrations. Skip it if you have sensitive or reactive skin, are new to vitamin C, or can't tolerate strong cosmetic scents.

**FAQ**

*How long does one bottle last?* With daily morning use (4–5 drops), expect 2–3 months. The 1oz size is standard for vitamin C serums.

*Can I use this with retinol?* Yes, but not at the same time. Use vitamin C in the morning and retinol at night. Never layer them together.

*Is it really comparable to Skinceuticals?* The active ingredients are nearly identical in concentration. The delivery system and pH buffering aren't as refined, which is why it's cheaper — but for most people, results are comparable.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want clinically-proven brightening actives without the luxury markup. This is the best value vitamin C serum I've tested under $25.

**Skip it if:** Your skin reacts to strong actives, or you can't get past the ferulic acid smell. Sensitive skin types should start with a 10% formulation instead.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Deducting half a star for the unpleasant scent and another fraction for the oxidation lifespan. Otherwise, this delivers results that punch far above its price point.
