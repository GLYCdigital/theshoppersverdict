Here's the thing about cleansing oils: most either strip your skin like paint thinner or leave a greasy film that makes you wonder if you actually washed your face. Palmer's Cocoa Butter Skin Therapy Cleansing Facial Oil sits in that sweet middle spot — and with 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's earning real loyalty.

**What you're actually getting**

The bottle delivers 5.1 fluid ounces of amber-tinted oil with a pump dispenser — thank goodness, because pour-top oils are a mess waiting to happen. The formula leads with sweet almond oil, followed by mineral oil, and then the star: fair trade certified cocoa butter. That's the Palmer's signature, and it's not just for the smell. Cocoa butter is rich in fatty acids that help maintain the skin barrier, which matters when you're using an oil cleanser.

**How it performs in real life**

I tested this against the two things that usually defeat cleansers: waterproof mascara and a full face of matte foundation. Massaged it in dry for about 30 seconds, added water, and it emulsified into a milky texture that rinsed off clean. The waterproof mascara came off without the aggressive rubbing that causes crepey eyelid skin. That's the win here — this oil breaks down stubborn makeup without dragging your skin.

Where it gets interesting: the after-feel. Most oil cleansers leave one of two extremes — squeaky-tight or slick-greasy. This one lands in between. Your skin feels clean but cushioned, almost like you've already applied moisturizer. That's the cocoa butter doing its job. Dry skin types will genuinely appreciate this. Oily skin types should still consider it, but you'll want to follow with a water-based cleanser to keep things balanced.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.6-star rating isn't an accident. Across the 229 reviews, the recurring themes are makeup removal power and skin softness. One verified buyer wrote, *"I've spent $40+ on cleansing balms that don't work this well. This removes everything and my face feels like I just left the spa."* Another noted, *"The pump makes it easy to control how much you use — a bottle lasts me almost three months."* That's a practical detail worth repeating: a little goes a long way, and the pump prevents over-pouring.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the scent. It's unmistakably cocoa butter — sweet, warm, and fairly strong. If you're sensitive to fragrance or prefer unscented skincare, this might trigger irritation or just be too much first thing in the morning. Second, it's not a one-step cleanse. You'll need to follow with a regular face wash or at least a warm, damp cloth to ensure no residue lingers. Skipping that step can lead to clogged pores, especially for acne-prone skin. Third, the mineral oil base means it's not the most "clean beauty" formula out there — if you avoid petroleum derivatives, this isn't for you.

**Who should buy this**

- Dry or combination skin that feels tight after cleansing — this will change your routine
- Anyone who wears heavy makeup daily and wants a gentle first-step remover
- Budget-conscious shoppers who want results without the $40+ price tag of prestige cleansing oils
- Cocoa butter lovers who already use Palmer's body products and want the same scent in a facial formula

Skip it if you have fragrance allergies, prefer fully "clean" ingredient lists, or want a single-step cleanser with zero follow-up needed.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this on my eyes?*
Yes, it removes waterproof mascara effectively. Keep your eyes closed when emulsifying to avoid any blurriness, and rinse thoroughly.

*Will this break me out if I have oily skin?*
Potentially, if you don't double-cleanse. The residual oil can clog pores. Pair it with a foaming cleanser afterward and you should be fine.

*How much should I use per wash?*
Two to three pumps is plenty for the whole face. One pump will struggle with full makeup coverage.

**The verdict**

Palmer's Cocoa Butter Skin Therapy Cleansing Facial Oil delivers what it promises: effective makeup removal with real skin-conditioning benefits. It's not perfect — the scent is divisive and it requires a second cleanse — but at this price point, it outperforms many prestige oils I've tested. 

**Buy it if** you want gentle, effective makeup removal that leaves your skin feeling moisturized rather than stripped. **Skip it if** you're fragrance-sensitive or looking for a single-step cleansing solution.
