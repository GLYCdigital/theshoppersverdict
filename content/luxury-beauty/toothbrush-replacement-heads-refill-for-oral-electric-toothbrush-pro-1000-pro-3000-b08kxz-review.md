You get eight brush heads in a clear plastic clamshell. ## Fit and Real-World Performance

The critical question with any third-party head is whether it clicks on properly. Loose heads rattle during brushing, defeat the oscillating action, and generally feel cheap. These don't. The connection is tight — I had to give a firm twist to seat them, and once locked, there's no play. The brush head sits flush against the handle exactly like the OEM version.

Bristle quality holds up over a standard three-month replacement cycle. The filaments are firm without being abrasive, and the tip is rounded enough that gum irritation isn't an issue. One thing I noticed: the bristles splay slightly faster than genuine Oral-B heads — around week ten versus week twelve — but at this price, swapping a month early still leaves you ahead on cost.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of color-fading indicator bristles is the most legitimate gripe. Oral-B's OEM heads have blue bristles that fade to white over three months — a built-in replacement reminder. You don't get that here, so you'll need to track your own timeline. If you're the type who brushes until the bristles look like a frayed mop, that's a hygiene issue, not a product one.

The other tradeoff is the bristle lifespan I mentioned. These aren't as durable as the genuine article. They're not bad — they're just not OEM-level. For the price difference, most buyers accept that.
