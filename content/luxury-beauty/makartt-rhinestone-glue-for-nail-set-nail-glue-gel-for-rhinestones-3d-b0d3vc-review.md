If you've ever tried to place a 3mm rhinestone with regular nail glue, you know the pain: the gem slides, the glue floods, and half your crystals end up stuck to your fingers instead of your nails. The Makartt Rhinestone Glue exists to fix exactly that problem. At 4.3 stars from 215 Amazon ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but is it the right pick for your nail routine?

## What You Actually Get

The set includes a 7ml (about 0.24 fl oz) bottle of rhinestone glue gel with a fine needle applicator tip. That tip is the whole game — it lets you place a single precise drop exactly where you need it, rather than squeezing out a blob and hoping for the best. As shown in the product image, the bottle is compact and designed for control, not bulk. There's no brush, no dropper — just the needle tip and the tube.

## Performance: The Real-World Test

The gel consistency is noticeably thicker than standard nail glue. That thickness is a feature, not a bug — it creates an instant "grab" that holds rhinestones in place while you position them, which is crucial for 3D gems that like to roll off wet glue. You'll want to apply a small dot, press the gem in, and hold for about 10-15 seconds before moving on.

Here's the honest caveat: this is not a one-and-done product. The glue needs proper drying time, and if you're working with larger crystals or heavy embellishments, a UV/LED lamp speeds things up significantly. Multiple reviewers note that without lamp curing, you need to let the glue set longer between stones.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The precision and hold are the consistent themes across positive reviews. One reviewer said, "This is the only glue I've found that doesn't make my rhinestones slide around while I'm placing them. The needle tip is perfect for tiny gems." Another noted, "I did a full set of 3D flowers and crystals and nothing has fallen off in two weeks."

That durability matters — for a category that often sees gems popping off within days, the Makartt glue delivers on its core promise of staying power.

## Where It Falls Short

The 7ml size is the biggest practical complaint. If you're doing intricate, full-coverage nail art regularly, you'll burn through this tube quickly. Some reviewers also mention the cap can get clogged if you don't clean the tip after each use — a small maintenance annoyance, but worth knowing.

The other consideration: this is a thicker gel, not a thin liquid adhesive. If you're looking for a brush-on base coat type glue for flat foil or micro-glitter, this isn't it. It's specifically formulated for dimensional gems and 3D embellishments.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Nail artists and enthusiasts** who regularly place rhinestones, pearls, or 3D charms and need precision placement
- **DIY manicurists** tired of gems falling off after a day or two
- **Anyone working with tiny crystals** (1mm-3mm range) where standard glue application gets messy

Skip it if you only occasionally add a single stone to a manicure — you can get away with regular nail glue for that. Also skip if you need a massive quantity for professional volume; the 7ml tube won't last long under heavy use.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work on acrylic and gel nails?**
A: Yes, it bonds well to both. Several reviewers use it over cured gel polish and acrylic overlays without issues. Just make sure your nail surface is dry and free of oils before applying.

**Q: How long does it take to dry?**
A: It starts gripping within seconds, but full curing takes a few minutes. Under a UV/LED lamp, it sets much faster — around 30-60 seconds. Without a lamp, allow 5-10 minutes before doing anything strenuous with your hands.

**Q: Can I use this for press-on nails or just rhinestones?**
A: It's designed for gems and embellishments, though some users report success using it to attach small press-ons. For full press-on nails, a dedicated nail glue is a better choice — the gel consistency isn't ideal for that purpose.
