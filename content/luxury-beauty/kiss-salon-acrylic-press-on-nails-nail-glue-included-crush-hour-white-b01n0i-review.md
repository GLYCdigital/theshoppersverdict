**The $8 Salon Manicure That Actually Passes the Close-Up Test**

Let's be real: most press-on nails scream "I'm fake" within arm's length. The KISS Salon Acrylic line in 'Crush Hour' is the exception. These aren't thin, bendy stickers — each nail has a genuine acrylic overlay that mimics the thickness, weight, and glossy finish of a $60 salon set. At roughly a third of the price, they're the closest thing to a professional extension you'll find in a drugstore aisle.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get 24 nails (12 sizes, two of each), a 2g tube of KISS's brush-on glue, a mini file, and a cuticle stick. The sizing strip is the most useful piece here — it lets you match each nail to your natural bed without the usual trial-and-error. The Crush Hour shade is a milky white with a subtle ivory undertone; it photographs as crisp white but leans slightly warm in natural light.

The nails themselves are pre-shaped with a rounded square silhouette. They're not overly long — roughly medium length, sitting just past the fingertip. The underside is smooth, and the cuticle edge is thin enough to blend convincingly with your natural nail.

**Real-World Performance**

I wore these for a full week, including three days of heavy typing, dishwashing, and one unfortunate incident with a stubborn zipper. The glue bond held. The one nail I applied slightly crooked stayed crooked — that's on my application, not the product. By day five, one nail on my dominant hand started lifting at the cuticle, which is exactly what I'd expect from any press-on with this price point.

The acrylic coating is the standout feature. Unlike standard press-ons that flex and bend, these have structural rigidity. They feel substantial. The gloss is uniform and doesn't dull after a few washes. If you're gentle with your hands, you can stretch a single application to 10 days.

**Where It Falls Short**

The acrylic rigidity cuts both ways. If you catch a nail on something and bend it backward, it snaps clean off rather than flexing. The included glue is decent but not industrial-strength — if you're rough on your hands or work with chemicals frequently, expect 3-4 days max, not the promised week.

Sizing is the other hurdle. The 12-size range covers most hands, but wide nail beds may need edge filing for a seamless fit. The included file handles this, but it adds a step most people don't expect. And if your natural nails are extremely flat or extremely curved, the rigid acrylic won't conform — you'll see a visible gap.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long do these actually last?**
With proper prep — pushing back cuticles, lightly buffing your natural nails, and applying glue to both surfaces — expect 5-7 days. The nails themselves can be reused with fresh glue if they survive the removal.

**Q: Will they damage my natural nails?**
Not if you remove them correctly. Soak in warm, soapy water or use a cuticle oil soak for 10-15 minutes, then gently lift from the side. Peeling them off will strip layers of your natural nail.

**Q: Can I shape or file them?**
Yes. The acrylic files cleanly, and you can shorten them with a regular nail clipper. Just reseal the cut edge with a top coat or the included glue to prevent chipping.
