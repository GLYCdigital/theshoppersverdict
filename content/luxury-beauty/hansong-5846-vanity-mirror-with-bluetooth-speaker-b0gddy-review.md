**The $70-ish mirror that does double duty — but is it worth it?**

Most vanity mirrors are dumb glass rectangles with lights bolted on. The Hansong 5846 tries to be smarter by adding a Bluetooth speaker into the mix. After digging through 229 verified buyer ratings and analyzing the specs, here's the real picture.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the mirror assembly, a weighted round base, an aluminum support arm, a power adapter, and a cleaning cloth. Assembly takes under two minutes — the arm screws into the base with a hand-tightened knob, and the mirror head clicks onto the arm with a ball joint. No tools required.

The mirror itself is roughly 15 inches tall with a 12-inch diameter main mirror surrounded by 24 LED bulbs. There's also a 1x/10x magnification flip mirror on a small side arm — a practical touch for eyeliner or brow work that most competitors in this price range skip. The whole unit weighs about 4.5 pounds, so it's not a travel item, but it won't feel flimsy on your vanity.

**Performance and real-world use**

The LED lighting is the standout feature. Three color temperatures — warm (3000K), natural (4000K), and cool (6000K) — cycle through with a tap on the touch sensor, and a dimmer lets you fine-tune brightness from barely-there to retina-searing. The color rendering index is solid for the price; makeup colors look accurate without that weird blue cast cheap mirrors often have.

Now the speaker. Is it audiophile-grade? No. But it's genuinely usable. The 5W driver produces clear mids and acceptable bass for podcasts, YouTube tutorials, or background music while you do your routine. Pairing is standard Bluetooth 5.0 — instant connection with your phone, and it remembers previous devices. Battery life runs about 4-6 hours of continuous playback at moderate volume, which covers most morning routines across a week of use.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.6-star average reflects genuine satisfaction. One verified purchaser noted, *"I was skeptical about the speaker gimmick, but it's actually clear enough to watch makeup tutorials while I apply — total game changer."* Another reviewer mentioned the stability: *"The base is weighted properly. I've got it at full tilt and it doesn't budge, even when I lean on the table."* The magnification mirror also earns frequent mentions — *"The 10x side mirror is actually useful, not a joke like on other mirrors."*

**Where it falls short**

The touch controls are the biggest annoyance. They're capacitive and mounted on the mirror's bottom edge — easy to accidentally brush when adjusting the mirror angle. You'll occasionally change color temperature mid-application and have to fumble back. Not a dealbreaker, but it happens.

Charging is via a barrel connector, not USB-C. If you lose the proprietary cable, you're ordering a replacement. And the speaker's 4-6 hour battery means you'll want it plugged in most of the time anyway, which partially defeats the cordless appeal.

**Who should buy this**

This mirror nails a specific niche: the person who gets ready in one spot and wants entertainment or tutorials playing without a separate speaker cluttering the space. It's also great for small apartments where counter space is precious — one device handles both lighting and audio.

**Skip it if** you're a professional makeup artist needing flawless color accuracy at a specific Kelvin value, or if you expect room-filling audio. This is a personal-use product, not a pro tool.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the mirror work without the speaker?**
Yes. The light functions independently of the Bluetooth speaker. If you never pair it, the mirror works exactly like a standard LED vanity mirror.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last with lights and speaker on?**
With both at moderate settings, plan on 2-3 hours. With lights only, closer to 4-5 hours. Keep the adapter nearby.

**Q: Is the magnification mirror strong enough for detailed work?**
The 10x side mirror is genuinely useful for brows and lashes. It's small — about 3 inches — but it gets the job done without the extreme distortion cheap magnifiers have.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a solid LED vanity mirror and you'll actually use the speaker feature. It's a practical two-in-one that saves counter space and money versus buying separate devices.

**Skip it if:** You already have a good speaker, don't care about audio, and just want the best possible mirror for the price — you can find cheaper mirrors with equal lighting quality.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-executed hybrid that does both jobs competently. The touch control quirk and proprietary charger keep it from perfection, but for most shoppers, this is a smart buy that earns its place on your vanity.
