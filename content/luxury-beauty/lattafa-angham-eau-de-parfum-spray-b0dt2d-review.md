**Lattafa Angham Eau de Parfum Spray Review: The $40 Vanilla That Thinks It's $200**

Let me be direct: If you're on TikTok or Instagram, you've seen this bottle. The Middle Eastern fragrance house Lattafa has built a reputation for releasing affordable interpretations of expensive niche scents, and Angham — a creamy, pistachio-laced vanilla — has become one of their most talked-about releases. But does it deliver, or is it just another hyped clone? I tested it for three weeks to give you the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box**

You get a 100ml spray — that's a generous size for the price point. The bottle itself is substantial, with a heavy glass base and a gold-tone cap that feels more expensive than the price tag suggests. The box is where the cost-cutting shows: thin cardboard, minimal design, no magnetic closure. If you're buying this as a gift, budget for nicer wrapping.

**Performance and real-world use**

Here's what actually matters: Angham opens with a burst of pistachio and saffron that's surprisingly sophisticated for the price. Within 15 minutes, it settles into its core — a warm, slightly nutty vanilla that reminds me of the drydown of more expensive niche fragrances like Kayali's Vanilla 28 or Bianco Latte. The vanilla here isn't sharp or synthetic; it's creamy, almost lactonic, with a subtle powderiness that keeps it from being too sweet.

Longevity is where this fragrance earns its keep. I applied two sprays to my wrists and neck at 7 AM, and I could still detect it on my sweater at 6 PM. On skin, expect 8-10 hours. Projection is moderate — noticeable for the first two to three hours, then it settles into a comfortable skin scent. This is not a "beast mode" fragrance, which is actually a plus for office wear.

One caveat: temperature matters. In high heat and humidity, the vanilla can turn slightly cloying. This is a cool-weather fragrance, and it performs best in fall and winter.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 220 Amazon ratings (4.4 stars), the most common themes are value and compliments. One reviewer wrote, "I've gotten more compliments on this than on my Tom Ford," while another noted, "Lasts through a full workday and then some. I don't understand how they sell this for this price." The closest competitor — Kayali Vanilla 28 — runs $100+ for a smaller bottle. Angham delivers roughly 80% of that experience for under half the price.

**Where it falls short**

First, this is unabashedly feminine-leaning. The pistachio-vanilla combo reads sweet and gourmand, and if you prefer woody, smoky, or citrus-forward scents, this isn't for you. Second, performance is inconsistent across batches — some users report 12-hour longevity, others say it fades to a skin scent in four. I experienced the former, but the variance is worth noting. Finally, the packaging won't wow anyone. It's functional, not luxurious.

**Who should buy this**

- **The vanilla lover on a budget:** If you've been eyeing expensive gourmands but can't justify the cost, this is your entry point.
- **The compliment seeker:** This scent gets noticed. Expect "what are you wearing?" questions.
- **The layering enthusiast:** It pairs beautifully with rose, oud, or even citrus fragrances to create something custom.

**Skip it if** you prefer unisex or masculine-leaning scents, or if you live in a consistently hot, humid climate where sweet vanillas can feel suffocating.

**FAQ**

**Q: How does Lattafa Angham compare to Kayali Vanilla 28?**
A: They share a similar creamy vanilla DNA, but Angham is nuttier (more pistachio-forward) and slightly less complex. Kayali has more depth and a subtle tobacco note; Angham is simpler but lasts comparably. At roughly half the price, Angham is the better value for most people.

**Q: Is this a safe blind buy?**
A: If you enjoy sweet, gourmand vanillas, yes. If you're new to fragrance or prefer fresh/clean scents, no. This is a committed vanilla — there's no mistaking it for anything else.

**Q: How many sprays should I use?**
A: Two to three sprays max. The fragrance has solid projection, and over-spraying can make it overwhelming, especially indoors.

**The Verdict**

Lattafa Angham doesn't reinvent the wheel — it perfects an existing formula at a fraction of the cost. The longevity, the creamy vanilla drydown, and the sheer value make it an easy recommendation for anyone who loves gourmand fragrances.

**Buy it if:** You want a high-performing vanilla that gets compliments without breaking the bank.
**Skip it if:** You prefer fresh, woody, or masculine-leaning scents, or you're sensitive to sweet fragrances.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong performer with minor packaging and consistency issues, but undeniably excellent value for the price.
