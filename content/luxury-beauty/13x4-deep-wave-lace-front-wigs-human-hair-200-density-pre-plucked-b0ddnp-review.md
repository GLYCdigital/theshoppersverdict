**What you actually get**

The box contains the wig itself, a wig cap, and a few bobby pins — nothing fancy, no styling tools or edge control. The wig is 100% human hair at 200% density, which is the headline feature here. As shown in the product image, the deep wave pattern is tight and defined at the root, loosening slightly toward the ends. The 13x4 lace frontal gives you 13 inches across the forehead and 4 inches back, which covers the full hairline without needing a full lace unit.

The hair length lands around 22-24 inches when stretched, which is consistent with most listings in this category. The cap construction is wefted with a decent number of combs — three total (two sides, one back) — plus an adjustable strap. Nothing groundbreaking, but functional.

**Performance and real-world use**

The 200% density is the star here. This wig is *thick*. If you're coming from a 130% or 150% density wig, the difference is immediately noticeable. There's no need to tease or add clip-ins to get volume — it's all there. The deep wave pattern holds well with mousse or a light gel, and the curl pattern stays defined for 3-4 days between refreshes.

Buyers consistently praise the thickness and the hair quality. One reviewer noted, *"The density is no joke — I've bought '200%' wigs before that were clearly 150%. This one is actually thick."* Another said, *"The deep wave pattern is beautiful and the hair is soft, not dry or straw-like like some cheap units."*

The pre-plucked hairline is genuinely well done. The baby hairs are already laid, and the knots are bleached enough that you can do a quick install without heavy concealer — though lighter skin tones will need less work than deeper tones.

**Where it falls short**

The lace tint is the most common complaint. Multiple reviewers mention the lace running too light for deeper skin tones, forcing them to tint or dye the lace before their first wear. That's an extra step and an extra product you weren't planning for. One buyer wrote, *"The lace is way too light for my skin tone. I had to buy a tint spray to make it work."*

There's also a reported chemical smell out of the box. This isn't unusual for human hair wigs, but it's more pronounced here. Plan for 2-3 washes with a clarifying shampoo and a good conditioner before install. Skip this step and you'll be smelling it all day.

Shedding is the third issue. Some buyers report more than normal shedding in the first week, particularly around the lace edges and parting area. It's not catastrophic — no one's reporting bald spots — but it's enough to notice and mention.

**Who should buy this**

This wig suits two buyer types: the first is someone who already knows how to tint lace and doesn't mind a bit of prep work. The second is someone with a medium-to-light skin tone who can wear the lace as-is. If either describes you, this is a solid value for the density and hair quality.

Skip it if you're a first-time wig wearer who wants a ready-to-go unit, or if you have a deeper skin tone and aren't comfortable customizing lace.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the hair really 200% density?**  
Yes, based on multiple buyer reviews and the visual weight of the unit, this is genuinely thick hair. If anything, some buyers found it almost *too* thick and had to thin it out at the nape.

**Q: How long does the deep wave pattern last?**  
With proper care — mousse, finger coiling, and avoiding heavy brushing when dry — the pattern holds for 4-6 weeks before needing a re-style. It's not a heat-trained pattern, so don't expect it to stay perfect without maintenance.

**Q: Does the wig shed a lot?**  
Some buyers report moderate shedding in the first week, mostly around the lace edges. After that initial period, shedding normalizes. This is common with 200% density wigs because of the sheer volume of hair.
