## The 2-in-1 that actually pulls double duty

Most combo nail tools are a compromise — a drill that vibrates like a jackhammer or a UV lamp that barely cures. This cordless drill with integrated UV lamp is the rare exception that does both jobs competently. After two weeks of testing on acrylics, hard gels, and natural nails, here's the honest breakdown.

## What's actually in the box

You get the drill handpiece, a charging base that doubles as the UV lamp, six carbide and diamond bits, a nail dust brush, and a USB-C charging cable. The bits cover the essentials — a large barrel bit for acrylic removal, a fine cone for cuticle work, and a small ball bit for corners. No sanding bands included, which is worth noting since they wear out fastest.

The handpiece is surprisingly light at 4.2 ounces. It balances well in hand, and the swivel chuck accepts standard 3/32" bits, so you're not locked into proprietary accessories.

## Performance: Where it shines and where it stumbles

**The drill itself is the star.** The brushless motor delivers consistent torque even at low speeds — that's rare at this price point. At 10,000 RPM it's gentle enough for removing gel polish without touching the natural nail. Crank it to 30,000 RPM and it chews through acrylic extensions without bogging down. Speed control is a sliding trigger on the side, which takes a few sessions to master but offers finer control than preset buttons.

The UV lamp is a different story. It's built into the charging base, which sounds convenient but means the curing surface is small. As shown in the product image, you place your fingers flat under the lamp rather than inserting them into a tunnel-style dryer. It cures gel polish fully in 60 seconds — I tested with a standard LED/UV hybrid gel and got a proper cure. But you'll need to reposition each hand in sections, adding a minute or two per manicure.

**Battery life is the biggest tradeoff.** Cordless freedom is great, but you'll get roughly 2.5 hours of actual drilling time before the green light blinks for a charge. A full recharge takes about 90 minutes. For a professional doing back-to-back clients, that's limiting. For home use or a side business, it's workable.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.2-star rating across 210 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. One reviewer wrote, *"I've owned three nail drills over the years and this is the first one that doesn't leave my hand numb after 20 minutes."* Another noted, *"The fact that I can move around my client's hand without dragging a cord is a game changer."*

The noise level also earns frequent mention — it's noticeably quieter than comparable corded drills, which matters if you're working in a small space or with anxious clients.

## Where it falls short — honestly

Beyond battery life, the included bits are mediocre. The carbide bits work fine but dull faster than replacement-grade options. Budget for a quality bit set if you're doing regular acrylic work.

The UV lamp's small footprint is also a genuine limitation. If you primarily do gel manicures rather than acrylics, a separate full-hand lamp will serve you better. The integrated lamp is a convenience feature, not a professional curing station.

Finally, there's no carrying case. For a tool marketed as portable, that's an oversight. You'll want to buy a padded pouch to protect the handpiece.

## Who should buy this

**The home enthusiast** who does their own nails and wants salon-quality tools without the salon price tag. The cordless design and compact footprint make it ideal for a bedroom vanity or small apartment.

**The mobile nail tech** who works at clients' homes or does events. The lack of a cord is genuinely liberating, and the UV lamp means one less device to carry.

**Skip it if** you're a full-time salon professional doing 6+ clients daily — the battery won't last, and you'll want a heavier-duty corded model with a larger curing lamp.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work on dip powder nails?**
A: Yes, but use the coarse barrel bit at medium speed. The drill generates enough torque to file down dip powder, though it's slower than acrylic removal.

**Q: Can you use it while charging?**
A: No. The drill must be docked in the base to charge, and it won't operate while connected. Plan your charging around your work sessions.

**Q: What's the actual RPM range?**
A: The dial spans roughly 5,000 to 30,000 RPM. The lowest setting is gentle enough for natural nail buffing, and the highest handles acrylics and hard gels.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely portable 2-in-1 that handles both drilling and curing without sacrificing quality. The motor is smooth, the speed control is precise, and the convenience factor is real.

**Skip it if** you need all-day battery life or cure full hands in one go — those specific needs require separate professional tools.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-executed combo tool with honest limitations. For the right user, it's a clear upgrade from corded budget drills.
