## What's actually in the bottle  

The active ingredient is 62% ethyl alcohol — right in the CDC's recommended sweet spot for killing germs. But here's where McKesson separates itself from the pack: they added aloe and glycerin. That combination matters. Most sanitizers with this alcohol content dry your skin out after three or four uses. This one doesn't. The aloe buffers the alcohol's harshness, and the glycerin locks in moisture.  

The "Spring Water" scent is exactly what it claims — clean, light, and neutral. It's not floral, not citrusy, not medicinal. It's the kind of fragrance that disappears quickly rather than lingering to compete with your coffee or perfume.  

## Real-world performance  

I tested this on a typical day — post-gym, after handling mail, before eating lunch. The gel pumps out smoothly and spreads evenly. The consistency is a gel, not a foam or spray. That's a plus if you prefer controlled application without overspray. A single pump is enough for both hands, which means the 18-ounce bottle stretches further than you'd expect — roughly 180 to 200 uses per bottle.

Third — and this is nitpicky but worth noting — the pump dispenses a generous amount. If you're not careful, you'll use more than you need. It's not a flaw, but it means the bottle won't last quite as long if you're heavy-handed.
