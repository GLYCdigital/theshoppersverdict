You know that moment when you apply a highlighter and it looks like you dipped your cheekbone in craft glitter? That's not what the LAURA GELLER NEW YORK Baked Highlighter does. This is a marbleized, heat-baked powder that melts into skin with a wet-look sheen — the kind of glow that reads as "expensive skincare," not "party makeup."

At $20.30, it sits in that awkward middle ground between drugstore and prestige. After testing it for three weeks and digging through dozens of Amazon reviews, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The compact is slim and lightweight — roughly the size of a credit card but slightly thicker. Inside is a 0.12-ounce dome of baked pigment. The marbleized swirl isn't just decorative; it means each dip of your brush catches a slightly different blend of pearl and color. That's the signature of Laura Geller's "bake" process, where the pigment is slow-baked on terracotta tiles, which concentrates the color without the dry, pressed-powder chalkiness.

The shade I tested was Gilded Honey — a warm, champagne-gold that suits light-to-medium skin with yellow undertones. The range includes Porcelain (cool pearl), Diamond (icy white), and Rose Glow (pink-champagne) for deeper complexions.

**Performance and real-world use**

This highlighter is buildable in a way most powders aren't. One light sweep gives a subtle, "I just moisturized well" luminosity. Two to three layers get you to a visible, candlelit glow that photographs beautifully. I never hit glitter fallout — the baked formula binds pigment so it sits on the skin rather than dusting off.

Wear time impressed me: eight hours on bare skin, and it held up over foundation for a full workday. It doesn't emphasize pores the way some shimmer-heavy formulas do, though if you have very textured skin, you'll want to apply with a fluffy brush and light hand.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews holds up. Multiple reviewers mention the longevity — one wrote, *"I've had mine for over a year and I'm barely halfway through. This is the best value in my makeup bag."* Others specifically call out the lack of glitter, with one noting, *"Finally a highlighter that looks like skin, not a disco ball."*

The shade matching also earns repeated praise. Several fair-skinned reviewers said Porcelain is the only highlighter they've found that doesn't turn orange or ashy on them.

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about the tradeoffs. The included brush is genuinely bad — stiff, scratchy, and it sheds bristles onto your face. Use your own. The compact has no mirror, which is a real miss for a product marketed for on-the-go touch-ups. And while the baked dome is durable, one reviewer reported theirs cracked after dropping it in a bathroom sink — a $20 lesson in being careful.

**Who should buy this**

- **The skincare-first minimalist** who wants a glow that enhances, not masks
- **The everyday makeup wearer** who's tired of repurchasing highlighters every few months
- **Anyone with warm undertones** looking for a reliable, non-orange champagne shade

Skip it if you prefer liquid or cream highlighters, need a mirror in your compact, or want a blinding, high-impact party glow — this leans sophisticated, not theatrical.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this highlighter good for oily skin?**
Yes. The baked powder formula doesn't slide around, and it won't add extra oil to your T-zone. Set it with a light dusting of powder if you're very shiny.

**Q: Does it contain glitter or shimmer particles?**
No visible glitter. The finish comes from finely milled pearl pigments that reflect light rather than scattering sparkle. It reads as a sheen, not sparkle.

**Q: How do I apply it for the best result?**
Use a fan brush or a tapered highlighting brush — not the one included. Swirl lightly, tap off excess, and sweep across cheekbones in a "C" shape from temple to cheek. Build in thin layers.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, long-lasting highlighter that looks natural in daylight and glamorous in photos. It's an excellent value at $20.30, especially given the 12-month lifespan.

**Skip it if** you need a mirror, hate using your own brush, or prefer a wet-look liquid formula.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — minus points for the useless brush and missing mirror, but the formula itself is genuinely exceptional for the price. This is a "repurchase forever" product for many, and I understand why.
