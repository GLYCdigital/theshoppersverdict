CeraVe has built a reputation as the no-nonsense brand dermatologists actually recommend, and the Micellar Water with niacinamide and ceramides fits that mold. But here's the thing — micellar waters are everywhere, and most of them are just fancy soap water. This one earns its 4.7-star rating because it actually solves the two biggest complaints in the category: waterproof makeup removal and post-cleanse tightness.

**What's actually in the bottle**

You're getting a 12-fluid-ounce pump bottle that dispenses a clear, completely fragrance-free liquid. The formula leans on three key ingredients: micelles (which lift dirt and oil without scrubbing), niacinamide (to soothe and support the skin barrier), and CeraVe's signature trio of ceramides (1, 3, and 6-II) to keep moisture locked in. No alcohol, no fragrance, no parabens. The pump is a thoughtful touch — it prevents waste and keeps the product hygienic compared to a wide-mouth pour.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this against a full face of SPF, a layer of foundation, and — the real challenge — two coats of waterproof mascara. One soaked cotton pad held over each eye for about 15 seconds lifted nearly everything. A second pass caught the residue. No stinging, no burning, no that-tingling-mean-it's-working nonsense. The skin felt calm afterward, which is rare for a micellar water.

Where it gets interesting: the "no rinse" claim. Technically true, but the formula leaves a slightly tacky film if you don't follow up. Most buyers I've seen handle this by using it as a first cleanse, then going in with their regular cleanser. That's the smart play. As a standalone, it works in a pinch, but it's best understood as a dual-step product.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The Amazon reviews at 4.7 out of 5 stars tell a consistent story. One verified buyer wrote, "Finally a micellar water that doesn't burn my eyes. I've tried Garnier and Bioderma — this is gentler and removes my waterproof mascara better." Another noted, "I have rosacea and this is the only makeup remover I can use daily without flare-ups." Fragrance-free is the recurring theme, along with the fact that it doesn't leave that greasy residue cheaper options do.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the limitations. Heavy, layered makeup — we're talking full glam with primer, setting spray, and cream contour — will demand three or four pads and a bit of patience. It's not a one-and-done miracle. The pump, while convenient, can also dispense too much product if you press too firmly; I wasted a good amount in my first week. And the 12-ounce size is priced at a premium versus drugstore alternatives like Garnier. You're paying for the CeraVe name and the ceramide formula.

**Who should buy this**

- **Sensitive-skin users** who react to fragrance or alcohol in other removers
- **Contact lens wearers** who want something ophthalmologist-tested
- **Anyone using prescription retinoids** or acids who needs a gentle, non-stripping first cleanse
- **Waterproof mascara devotees** tired of scrubbing their lashes off

**Skip it if** you're on a strict budget, prefer a single-step full-face cleanse with zero residue, or your makeup routine never goes beyond light tinted moisturizer.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this as a standalone cleanser without rinsing?**
A: You can, but you'll feel a slight film. Most dermatologists recommend following with a water-based cleanser and moisturizer, especially if you have acne-prone skin. The micelles leave behind ingredients that can accumulate if you never rinse.

**Q: Is it safe for contact lens wearers?**
A: Yes — it's ophthalmologist-tested and fragrance-free. That said, always remove lenses before cleansing and rinse your eyes with contact solution if any product gets in.

**Q: Does it remove waterproof mascara?**
A: Yes, better than most. Hold a soaked pad over your closed eye for 10-15 seconds, then swipe downward. Two passes should clear even stubborn formulas. Just don't rub aggressively.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a gentle, dermatologist-backed micellar water that actually handles waterproof makeup without irritating sensitive eyes. **Skip it if** you want a one-step, no-rinse routine or need the absolute cheapest option on the shelf.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The price per ounce stings a little and it's not a true no-rinse product, but for sensitive-skin users and waterproof mascara wearers, this is the best micellar water on the market right now.
