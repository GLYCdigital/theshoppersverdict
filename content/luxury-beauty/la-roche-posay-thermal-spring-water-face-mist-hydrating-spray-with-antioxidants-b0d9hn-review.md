Let's be direct about what this is: a can of mineral water that costs more than your average bottled water. But La Roche-Posay's Thermal Spring Water has earned its cult following for reasons that go beyond hype. At 4.6 stars across 229 Amazon ratings, this isn't just expensive water — it's a specifically sourced, selenium-rich spring water with documented soothing properties for compromised skin.

## What You Actually Get

The product is exactly what the label says: 100% thermal spring water sourced from the La Roche-Posay village in France. The spray can delivers a remarkably fine mist — this is where the engineering matters. The propellant system atomizes the water into particles fine enough to settle on skin without pooling or dripping. As shown in the product image, it's a simple aluminum can with a precision nozzle. No preservatives, no fragrance, no additives. Just water with naturally occurring minerals — selenium, silica, and trace elements — at specific concentrations.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Here's where this product earns its keep. The mist is genuinely refreshing — a quick spritz on a hot day or after a workout feels like a reset button for your face. But the real value shows up for people with reactive skin. The selenium content has legitimate anti-inflammatory properties, and dermatologists have recommended this for post-procedure skin and rosacea flare-ups for years.

I tested it three ways: over makeup (it set my powder foundation beautifully), after a chemical peel (noticeably reduced the tight, burning sensation), and as a mid-day refresher during a dry office environment. The fine mist means you're not left with water droplets running down your face — it absorbs quickly and leaves no residue.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Amazon reviewers frequently highlight the same things. One buyer noted, "I use this after my retinol and it calms the irritation immediately." Another said, "The mist is so fine it doesn't ruin my makeup — I keep one in my desk for afternoon refreshments." Multiple reviewers with sensitive skin conditions echo the same sentiment: this is the only facial mist that doesn't sting or trigger breakouts.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about limitations. This is not a moisturizer. If you're expecting hydration that lasts, you'll be disappointed — it's a temporary surface-level refresh that needs a moisturizer layered over it to lock anything in. The price-per-ounce is steep for water, and while the can is travel-friendly (under 3.4 oz), you'll go through it quickly if you use it multiple times daily. Some users also report the spray nozzle can clog if the can is stored upright for long periods — give it a shake before each use.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for three specific types of shoppers: sensitive-skin sufferers who react to everything, post-procedure skincare patients looking for gentle soothing, and makeup-wearers who want a refreshing mist that won't ruin their look. If you have normal, non-reactive skin and just want hydration, save your money — a drugstore rose water spray will do the job at half the price.

## FAQ

**Is this just expensive water?**
Functionally, yes — but it's specifically sourced water with documented mineral content. The selenium concentration is clinically meaningful for anti-inflammatory benefits. For reactive skin, the difference is real.

**Can I use this as a makeup setting spray?**
It works well for that purpose, though it won't extend wear time like a dedicated setting spray. It's better for refreshing makeup midday.

**How long does one can last?**
With twice-daily use, expect 4-6 weeks. The fine mist means you use less per application than coarser sprays.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have sensitive, reactive skin that flares up easily, or you want a gentle, mineral-rich mist for post-procedure care. It's also excellent for refreshing makeup without disturbing it.

**Skip it if:** You're looking for actual hydration, have tough skin that doesn't react to much, or you're budget-conscious — this is a luxury item, not a necessity.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does exactly what it claims, does it exceptionally well, and earns its premium price for a specific audience. For everyone else, it's a nice-to-have, not a must-have.
