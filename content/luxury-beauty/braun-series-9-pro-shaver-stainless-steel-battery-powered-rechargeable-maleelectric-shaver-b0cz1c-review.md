**The $350 Question: Does Braun's Flagship Shaver Justify the Hype?**

Let me save you some reading time: the Braun Series 9 Pro+ is the closest thing to a perfect electric shaver I've tested — and it's also overpriced in ways that will annoy you. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, owners largely agree. But "largely" isn't "universally," and the gripes are worth hearing before you drop serious cash.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The Pro+ ships with the shaver, a premium charging stand, a leather travel pouch, a brush, and one cleaning cartridge. The unit itself is a handsome stainless steel build that feels dense and expensive in hand — this is not a plasticky drugstore razor. The shaver head pops off for rinsing, and the foil design is noticeably more flexible than previous Series 9 models.

**Real-World Performance: The Good**

I shaved with this thing for two weeks straight, alternating between 2-day and 4-day growth. The headline feature — **AutoSense technology** — isn't marketing fluff. It genuinely adjusts power based on hair density, and the result is a shave that handles thick, coarse neck hair without the tugging that plagues lesser foils. The 5-blade SyncroSystem cuts efficiently; I got a close, smooth result in about half the passes my older Panasonic needed.

The wet/dry capability is real. I used it with foam in the shower daily, and it handled both slick and dry shaving without skipping. The 60-minute battery genuinely lasts — I shaved daily for 10 days before recharging. The 5-minute quick charge gives you one full shave, which is honest engineering, not a marketing exaggeration.

**Where It Falls Short**

Here's where I temper the enthusiasm. **The cleaning station is a money pit.** The cartridges run roughly $15 each and last about 30-45 days with daily cleaning. The station itself sounds like a tiny jet engine when running — loud enough to wake my partner. You can skip it and rinse the head manually, but then you're paying a premium for a feature you're not using.

**Charging is a legitimate annoyance.** It's a proprietary plug, not USB-C. In 2026, carrying a separate charger for a $350 device is frankly embarrassing. If you travel frequently, budget for it or plan around it.

**What 225 Amazon Buyers Consistently Say**

Reading through the reviews, the pattern is clear. One verified buyer wrote: *"First electric shaver that gives me a truly close shave without razor burn. My skin used to break out after every shave with cartridge razors. Not anymore."* Multiple reviewers echo this — the Pro+ is genuinely gentle on sensitive skin.

Another common thread: *"Expensive, but my wife says my face feels smoother than with a blade."* The closeness is the selling point that keeps recurring.

The criticisms cluster around price, the cleaning station's cost, and the proprietary charger — which matches my testing experience exactly.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The sensitive-skin sufferer** who gets razor burn from blades or cheap electrics. This shaver's gentle foil system is genuinely different.
- **The daily shaver** who values 2-minute shaves and consistent results over cost efficiency.
- **The business traveler** who needs reliable wet/dry performance and can tolerate the proprietary charger.

**Who Should Skip It**

- **The budget-conscious shopper** — the OneBlade at $50 does 80% of the job for 15% of the price.
- **The minimalist** who hates accessories and maintenance. The cleaning station is optional but feels like a waste if unused.
- **Anyone who loses chargers** — replacement plugs are annoyingly expensive.

**Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Is it really better than a blade razor?**
For most men, yes for comfort, close for closeness. It won't match a fresh cartridge blade's razor-sharp edge on a 5-o'clock shadow, but it gets 90% there with zero irritation and no prep time.

**Q: How long do the foils and cutter last?**
Braun recommends replacing the cutting head every 18 months. Expect to pay $60-70 for a genuine replacement. Third-party heads exist but aren't worth the savings — they dull faster and don't fit as precisely.

**Q: Can I use it without the cleaning station?**
Absolutely. Rinse the head under water, shake it dry, and you're fine. The station is convenience, not necessity. Many owners report using it manually without issues.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want the best electric shave money can buy and your skin rebels against blades. The performance is genuinely class-leading, and the durability justifies the price for daily users who'll get 5+ years out of it.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget, hate proprietary chargers, or don't want recurring maintenance costs. A mid-range shaver at $100-150 will give you 70% of the experience at 30% of the cost.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — minus half a star for the charger and cleaning station's ongoing costs. This is a superb shaver trapped in a frustrating ecosystem.

*If you're ready to invest in your daily routine, this is the shaver to buy. If you're not ready for the upkeep, keep scrolling.*
