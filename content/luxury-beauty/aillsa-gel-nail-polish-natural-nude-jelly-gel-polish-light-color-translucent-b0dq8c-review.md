I've tested enough gel polishes to know that "jelly" formulas are a gamble. Most are either so sheer they look like tinted top coat after three coats, or so thick they self-level into a lumpy mess. The AILLSA Natural Nude Jelly in Light C sits squarely in the middle — and that's actually a good thing.

**What's in the bottle**

You get the standard 15ml (0.5 fl oz) gel polish in a slim glass bottle with a black cap. The brush is tapered with rounded edges — nothing fancy, but it fans out nicely and holds enough product for one full nail. No lamp, no base or top coat included, so factor those into your cost if you're starting from scratch.

**The formula story**

This is a translucent jelly, not a cream. That means it's designed to show your natural nail through. The Light C shade is described as "natural nude," but here's the honest truth: it pulls noticeably pink on the nail. If you're warm-toned or have olive skin, test this on a bare nail before committing to a full manicure. Cool-toned skin? You'll love how it brightens your nail beds without looking washed out.

In practice, the viscosity is on the thinner side — think nail lacquer rather than thick builder gel. That's a double-edged sword. Thin gel means fewer bubbles and easier self-leveling, but it also means you need to wipe the brush on the bottle rim before applying. Skip that step and you'll get product flooding your cuticles, which then cures into a mess you'll have to file off. I learned this the hard way.

**Curing and wear**

Under a 36W LED lamp, two minutes of curing per coat is plenty. No tacky layer issues, no soft spots. Three coats gets you to full coverage — the first coat will look like a sheer pink wash, the second adds depth, and the third gives you that glossy "your nails but better" finish. The third coat is where most people stop; four starts to look milky and defeats the jelly purpose.

Wear time is solid for a jelly formula. I got 10 days without chipping, which is about what I expect from mid-range gel polishes. One reviewer noted, "I've worn this for two weeks with zero lifting, but I'm careful to cap the free edge." Another said, "The color is gorgeous but it's thinner than I expected — my first manicure had pooling issues until I got the hang of it." Both tracks match my experience.

**Where it falls short**

The shade accuracy issue is the biggest complaint across the 210 ratings. The product images show a warm, beige-leaning nude. What you actually get is a rosy pink with cool undertones. It's pretty, don't get me wrong — but if you bought this specifically for a neutral beige, you'll be disappointed.

The thin formula also means it's not beginner-friendly. If you've never worked with jelly gels before, practice on a few nails first. And unlike some budget gel polishes, this doesn't forgive sloppy application. You'll need to be deliberate with each coat.

**Who should buy this**

- **Cool-toned nail enthusiasts** who want a sheer, buildable nude that flatters rather than hides
- **Jelly gel veterans** who know how to work with thin formulas and want a reliable mid-range option
- **Anyone who prefers natural, "clean girl" manicures** over opaque, high-impact colors

Skip it if you're warm-toned, prefer opaque coverage, or want a polish that's forgiving of application mistakes.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many coats do I need for full coverage?**
A: Three coats gets you the full jelly effect. Two is a sheer wash; four starts to look milky and loses the translucency.

**Q: Does this require a special lamp?**
A: No. Standard 36W LED lamps work fine — 60 seconds per coat. UV lamps take about 2 minutes.

**Q: Will this work over dark or stained nails?**
A: No. Jelly formulas are transparent by design. If your nails aren't clean and even-toned, the imperfections will show through. Use a nude base coat first if needed.

**The Verdict**

The AILLSA Natural Nude Jelly in Light C is a solid performer with one notable flaw: the shade runs pinker than advertised. If that works for your skin tone, you're getting a well-behaved, self-leveling jelly that cures properly and wears well. If you're looking for a true beige nude, look elsewhere.

**Buy it if** you're cool-toned, comfortable with thin gel formulas, and want a buildable sheer nude.
**Skip it if** you need a warm neutral or you're new to gel application — the learning curve is real.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Deducting points for the shade mismatch and the unforgiving thin formula, but the finish and wear time genuinely impress.
