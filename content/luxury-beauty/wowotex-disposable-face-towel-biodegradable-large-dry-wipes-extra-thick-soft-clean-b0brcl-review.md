**What You Actually Get**

The box contains 100 extra-thick dry wipes, each measuring a full 8x8 inches. That size matters — cheaper brands shrink to 6x6, forcing you to use two. **Performance in Real Use**

Here's where these shine. Wet one under the tap and it transforms into something closer to a soft cotton pad than a paper towel. It holds water without disintegrating, and it doesn't shred or leave lint on your face. The embossed side gives gentle exfoliation; the smooth side is for sensitive days. I've used them for double cleansing, toning, and removing clay masks — they handled all three without breaking apart.

One buyer summed it up: *"I was skeptical about paper towels on my face, but these are genuinely soft when wet. **Where They Fall Short**

The honest downsides. Dry, these feel like thick tissue — not plush cotton. If you're used to luxury bamboo towels, the texture takes getting used to. They also require thorough wetting; patting them dry on your face without pre-wetting can feel abrasive on reactive skin.

The sustainability math deserves scrutiny. Yes, they're biodegradable and plant-based — buyers have confirmed they break down in compost. But "disposable" is still disposable. If you use 4-5 daily for a year, that's over 1,400 single-use wipes. For some, the hygiene and convenience tradeoff is worth it. For others, a washable microfiber cloth is the greener — if less convenient — alternative.

**The Price Reality**

At roughly $20-25 per box of 100, that's about 20-25 cents per wipe. That's affordable for luxury skincare users, but it's a recurring subscription-style cost. You're paying for convenience and hygiene — not for something you'll own.
