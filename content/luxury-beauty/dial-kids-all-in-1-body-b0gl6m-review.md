**The Honest Lowdown on Dial Kids All-in-1 Body +**

Let’s be blunt: most kids’ bath products are either overpriced soap water or so heavily fragranced they might as well be perfume. Dial’s Kids All-in-1 Body + tries to split the difference — and for most families, it actually lands. With a 4.6-star average from 229 ratings, this isn’t a cult favorite by accident. But is it the right pick for *your* kid? I dug into the formula, the packaging, and what real parents are saying to give you the full picture.

**What You’re Actually Getting**

This is a 2-in-1 shampoo and body wash, so one bottle replaces two. The pump dispenses a gel-like liquid that lathers up nicely — a little goes a long way. As shown in the product image, the bottle is squat and kid-friendly, but the pump is the real story. It’s designed for one-handed use, which matters when you’re holding a slippery toddler with the other hand. That said, the pump mechanism can jam if the bottle tips over mid-shower. A few reviewers mentioned this, so it’s not a fluke — keep it upright and you’ll be fine.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The formula is where Dial earns its keep. It’s sulfate-free and uses a mild surfactant blend that doesn’t strip natural oils. In practice, that means it rinses clean without leaving that squeaky-dry feeling. The tear-free claim holds up — I didn’t see a single complaint about stinging eyes, even from parents who have to wrestle their kids into the rinse position. The lather is rich enough to actually clean but light enough that you’re not scrubbing for five minutes to get it out.

What surprised me most was the scent. It’s a soft, clean, almost powdery fragrance — not the artificial candy explosion most kids’ products go for. Some parents loved that; others wished it was stronger. If your kid is picky about smells, this is a safe neutral, but don’t expect a bubblegum party.

**What Parents Are Saying (The Good and the Gritty)**

The 4.6 rating isn’t inflated. One parent said, *“Finally a wash that doesn’t make my daughter scream during bath time. Rinses out fast, no tears, and the pump actually works one-handed.”* That tracks with my research — the tear-free claim and the quick-rinse factor were the two most-praised points.

But there’s a split. A handful of reviewers with kids who have eczema or very sensitive skin reported mild redness after a few uses. Dial didn’t market this as a dermatologist-tested eczema wash, so that’s not a dealbreaker for most — but if your child has reactive skin, you’ll want to patch-test first. Another common gripe: the pump can shoot out a bit more product than needed, which wastes soap if you’re not careful.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Parents of toddlers and young kids (ages 2-8)** who need a no-fuss, tear-free option that cuts bath time down to minutes.
- **Families who prefer mild scents** — this won’t overpower a small bathroom or trigger scent-sensitive allergies.
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want a solid 2-in-1 without paying boutique prices for the same ingredients.

**Skip It If**

- **Your child has diagnosed eczema or extremely sensitive skin** — you’re better off with a dedicated hypoallergenic wash like Cetaphil or Vanicream.
- **You want a strong, fun fragrance** — this is subtle, and kids who love “smelling like fruit” will be disappointed.
- **You need a pump that survives being tossed in a bath bag** — the mechanism is decent, not indestructible.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Is this safe for a newborn?**  
A: Dial doesn’t specify an age range, but the formula is mild enough for most babies over 6 months. For newborns, stick to a fragrance-free, pediatrician-approved wash. For older infants, this is fine.

**Q: Does it really work as both shampoo and body wash?**  
A: Yes. It cleans hair and skin equally well. Hair comes out soft without conditioner, and body skin doesn’t feel stripped. It’s not a 3-in-1 (no conditioner), so manage expectations.

**Q: How does it compare to Johnson’s or Aveeno?**  
A: It’s less watery than Johnson’s and more affordable than Aveeno’s baby line. The scent is milder than both. If you want a middle ground between cheap and premium, this is it.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, tear-free 2-in-1 that works for most kids and won’t break the bank.  
**Skip it if** your child has sensitive skin or you’re looking for a fragrance-forward product.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It’s not perfect (the pump and sensitive-skin concerns are real), but for the majority of families, this is the bath-time workhorse you’ll reach for every night.
