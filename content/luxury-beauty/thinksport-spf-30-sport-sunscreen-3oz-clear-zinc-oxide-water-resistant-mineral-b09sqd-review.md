### Is "clear zinc" actually clear? Yes — and that changes everything

Most mineral sunscreens leave you looking like a ghost. Thinksport SPF 30 Sport claims their zinc oxide is "clear," and for once, the marketing matches reality. This formula uses non-nano zinc oxide with a micronized particle size that blends into skin without that chalky finish. As shown in the product image, the tube is compact and travel-friendly — but the real story is what happens when you rub it in.

### What you actually get

The 3oz tube is straightforward: one product, one job. The label lists 22% zinc oxide as the sole active ingredient, which is a solid concentration for a sport formula. It's water-resistant for 80 minutes, which is the maximum the FDA allows any sunscreen to claim. There's no oxybenzone, no octinoxate, no chemical UV filters — just mineral protection. It's also free of parabens, phthalates, and synthetic fragrances, which matters for anyone with reactive skin.

### Performance in the real world

I tested this on a hot, sweaty day and was genuinely impressed by the staying power. The 80-minute water resistance claim isn't puffery — it stayed put through a solid hour of running and a quick rinse-off. The texture is thicker than chemical sunscreens, which is expected with zinc, but it spreads more easily than most mineral formulas I've tried. It does take about 30 seconds to fully rub in, and there's a slight tackiness that lingers for a few minutes. Once it dries down, it's comfortable and doesn't feel greasy.

The "clear" claim holds up on medium skin tones. I can't speak for very deep complexions personally, but the micronized zinc is noticeably less white than standard mineral sunscreens like Blue Lizard or Thinkbaby. It's not invisible — there's a subtle sheen — but it's dramatically better than the competition.

### What buyers consistently praise

The 4.4-star average (from 220 ratings) reflects a pattern of satisfied users. One reviewer noted, "Finally a mineral sunscreen my kids don't fight me about — no ghost face." ### Where it falls short

Two real issues. First, this tubes will pill if you layer it over a thick moisturizer or serum. You need to apply it to bare skin or wait several minutes for your skincare to fully absorb. Second, the 3oz size is small for a "sport" sunscreen. The FDA recommends a shot glass full for full-body coverage, and that means this tube lasts maybe 4-5 applications. You'll be reordering often.

There's also the texture tradeoff. If you're used to featherlight chemical sunscreens, this will feel heavier. It's not greasy, but it's not invisible either.

### Who should buy this

- **Outdoor athletes and hikers** who need something that survives sweat and won't burn their eyes
- **Parents of kids with sensitive skin** who need a gentle, effective mineral option
- **Reef-conscious travelers** heading to destinations with sunscreen bans
- **Anyone with melasma or hyperpigmentation** who needs reliable UVA protection (zinc is the gold standard)

Skip it if you prefer spray sunscreens for quick reapplications, or if you have very dry skin — the formula can cling to dry patches.

### FAQ

**Does this leave a white cast on dark skin?**
It's significantly better than most mineral sunscreens, but it's not invisible. The zinc is micronized to reduce whitening, and many reviewers with deeper skin tones report acceptable results. Test on a small area first.

**Is it good for babies and kids?**
Yes, it's gentle enough for sensitive skin and free of common irritants. Thinksport markets to families, and the non-nano zinc is safe for children. Just avoid the eye area — any sunscreen will sting there.

**How often do I need to reapply?**
Every 80 minutes if swimming or sweating, and every 2 hours otherwise. Set a timer — mineral sunscreens lose effectiveness when the zinc degrades, not just when it rubs off.

### The Verdict
