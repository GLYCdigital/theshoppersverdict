Let's be honest about bath salts: most are just magnesium sulfate with a few drops of fragrance oil and a marketing budget. Dr Teal's asks $18 for a 5-pound bag, which puts it at a premium over generic drugstore Epsom salt. The question is whether the upgrade is real or just packaging. After testing this across a standard bathtub and a deeper soaking tub, here's what actually matters.

**What you're getting**

The box contains one 5-pound bag of Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate USP grade) infused with lavender essential oil. The bag itself is a thick mylar-style pouch with a zip closure that's actually functional — not one of those seals that gives up after two uses. The product image shows the classic Dr Teal's lavender labeling, and what arrives matches that exactly. No surprises, no filler.

**Dissolving and performance**

This is where Dr Teal's earns its keep. The salt dissolves fast — genuinely fast. In water at 102°F, a full cup disappears within two to three minutes with minimal stirring. That matters because undissolved Epsom salt sitting on the tub floor is the fastest way to turn a relaxing bath into a grit-and-grind session against your backside. I tested the cheaper store-brand stuff side by side, and the difference was obvious: the generic brand left a visible sediment layer; Dr Teal's didn't.

The magnesium sulfate concentration is standard USP grade, so you're getting the same muscle-relaxing properties as any competitor. What's different is the scent delivery. The lavender is present but restrained — it reads as a spa aroma rather than a candle aisle assault. If you're sensitive to strong fragrances, this is a safe bet.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't inflated. The recurring themes in customer feedback are dissolving speed and skin feel. One verified buyer wrote, "I've tried every Epsom salt brand at Target and this is the only one that doesn't leave me itchy afterward." Another noted, "The scent actually lasts through a 30-minute soak, which is rare." Both match my experience — my skin felt conditioned, not stripped, and the lavender lingered just enough to notice without being overwhelming.

**Where it falls short**

The packaging is the weak point. The 5-pound bag is unwieldy, and the zip closure sits perpendicular to the pour spout, which means you're either tilting the whole thing awkwardly or scooping with a cup. I spilled salt on the bathroom floor twice before I learned to decant it into a glass jar. That's a minor annoyance, but at this price point, a better-designed pour spout shouldn't be too much to ask.

The scent also fades fast in larger tubs. In a standard 40-gallon bath, one cup is plenty. In a garden tub or soaking tub, you'll need two to three cups to get the same aromatherapy effect. That cuts into the value proposition — you're going through this bag faster than the label suggests.

**Who should buy this**

- **Bath enthusiasts** who take 20+ minute soaks and want a reliable, fast-dissolving salt that won't leave a mess
- **Sensitive-skin folks** who've reacted to cheaper brands or heavily fragranced bath products
- **Anyone buying a gift** — the presentation is solid and the scent is universally pleasant

**Skip it if** you're on a strict budget and just want magnesium for muscle recovery. The generic stuff at half the price does the mineral work fine. You're paying for the scent quality and the dissolving performance here.

**FAQ**

**Q: How much should I use per bath?**
A: One cup for a standard tub, two if you want stronger scent or have a deeper tub. Don't exceed 3 cups — it can leave a filmy residue on your skin.

**Q: Does it stain tubs or leave a ring?**
A: No, but if you have hard water, you'll still get a mineral ring from the water itself, not the salt. Clean the tub after soaking regardless.

**Q: Is this safe for daily use?**
A: Yes, the magnesium sulfate concentration is gentle enough for daily bathing, but you don't need it every day. Three to four times a week is plenty for muscle recovery.
