The Wet Brush Original has been a drugstore staple for over a decade, but with 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, it's clearly still earning its place in shower caddies. I tested this against three hair types—fine straight, thick wavy, and curly—to see if the hype holds up.

**What you actually get**

The package contains one brush with the signature flexible IntelliFlex bristles and a cushioned pad. The handle has a rubberized grip with slight contouring that sits comfortably in your palm. Bristles are arranged in an oval pattern, which is wider than the brand's compact version. It's lightweight—about 3.2 ounces—so it won't strain your wrist during long detangling sessions.

**Real-world performance**

On wet hair with conditioner still in, this brush lives up to its name. The bristles bend rather than fight tangles, which means you can start at the ends and work upward without the usual snagging. I timed detangling on my thick, wavy hair: roughly 90 seconds from tangled mess to brushable, versus nearly four minutes with a standard paddle brush.

For fine hair, the flexible bristles are gentle enough to use on damp strands without that harsh scraping sensation. One buyer noted, "I have fine hair that knots instantly, and this is the only brush that doesn't rip through it. I can actually brush my daughter's hair without her crying." That sentiment echoes across multiple reviews—parents of kids with sensitive scalps particularly praise it.

**Where it falls short**

The bristles' flexibility is also the main weakness. On very thick, tightly coiled hair, you'll need to apply more pressure to get through dense curl patterns—and that's when the bristle base can crack. A handful of reviewers report the pad separating from the handle after a few months of heavy use. It's not universal, but it's frequent enough to mention.

Also, don't expect this to double as a smoothing brush for blowouts. The flexible bristles don't provide enough tension to straighten or polish hair. You'll need a separate round brush or paddle brush for that.

**Who should buy this**

- **Parents of young kids**: The gentle bristles make hair brushing less of a battle. Multiple reviewers mention using it on toddlers and elementary-age children.
- **People with fine or medium hair**: You'll see the most benefit—effective detangling without breakage.
- **Anyone prone to tangles after swimming or washing**: It works exceptionally well on wet, conditioner-laden hair.

**Skip it if** your hair is extremely thick and coarse, or if you're looking for a multi-purpose styling tool. Consider the Wet Brush Epic Pro instead, which has longer, sturdier bristles for denser hair.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this on dry hair?*  
Yes, but it's less effective. The bristles work best on wet or damp hair with conditioner or detangler. On dry hair, it still removes tangles but may cause more static.

*Does it work on extensions or wigs?*  
It's safe on human hair extensions and wigs, but be gentle near the wefts. The flexible bristles won't snag or pull at the attachment points.

*How do you clean it?*  
Rinse under warm water to remove hair and product buildup. For deeper cleaning, use a mild shampoo and let it air dry bristle-side down.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, gentle detangler for everyday use on normal to fine hair. It's affordable, effective, and lives up to the 4.7-star average.

**Skip it if** you have very thick, coarse curls that need firmer bristles, or if you need a brush that can also handle heat styling.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Wet Brush Original does its core job exceptionally well. The durability concerns on denser hair types keep it from a perfect score, but for most shoppers, this is the detangler to beat.
