Let’s be honest: hand soap is rarely a purchase you get excited about. But Softsoap’s Milk and Honey formula has quietly earned a 4.8-star average across 240 Amazon ratings — and that kind of consistency means something. After testing this 7.5-ounce pump bottle at my kitchen sink for three weeks, here’s the real story.

**What you actually get**

The 7.5-fluid-ounce bottle is compact — about the height of a coffee mug — with a standard pump that dispenses a generous single dose. The liquid itself is opaque and slightly thick, closer to a lotion than a typical gel soap. The label promises moisturizing benefits from milk proteins and honey, and the ingredient list backs that up with glycerin and other humectants placed reasonably high.

**Performance and real-world feel**

This is where the product earns its keep. One pump produces a dense, creamy lather that spreads easily across both hands. It rinses off completely in about five seconds — no squeaky-clean tightness, no lingering slipperiness. After drying, my hands felt noticeably softer than with my usual antibacterial soap, and that effect held through several washes without re-applying lotion.

The scent is the star for many buyers. It’s warm, slightly sweet, and reminiscent of a mild oatmeal bath — not cloying or artificial. One reviewer put it simply: “The smell is amazing and not overpowering.” Another noted, “My hands don’t crack like they did with other soaps.” That tracks with my experience: even after a dozen washes in a day, the moisture barrier held up better than with budget soaps.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the reviews, three themes repeat: the scent, the lather, and the non-drying feel. Buyers frequently describe it as “a spa experience at home” and note that guests comment on it. The pump mechanism also deserves credit — it doesn't clog or dribble, which sounds minor until you’ve wrestled with a sticky dispenser at 6 a.m.

**Where it falls short**

The 7.5-ounce size is the biggest practical drawback. For a household of two adults washing hands regularly, this bottle lasted about three weeks. You’ll be reordering more often than you’d like, and the per-ounce cost is higher than buying the larger refill sizes Softsoap offers. If you’re primarily buying for a powder room used by guests, the small bottle is fine. For a busy family kitchen, budget for refills.

Also, the scent profile won’t suit everyone. It’s decidedly sweet and creamy — if you prefer eucalyptus, citrus, or unscented soaps, this will feel heavy and cloying by the second week.

**Who should buy this**

- **The dry-hand sufferer**: If winter washing leaves your knuckles raw, this is a genuinely effective solution.
- **The guest-bathroom host**: The warm scent and attractive bottle (cream label, gold accents) look intentional, not generic.
- **The scent-sensitive minimalist**: One pump fills a small bathroom with a soft fragrance that fades — no air freshener needed.

**Skip it if** you need a heavy-duty antibacterial soap for a medical or food-service setting, or if you prefer crisp, clean fragrances over sweet, creamy ones.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this soap antibacterial?**
No — it’s a moisturizing formula, not labeled antibacterial. If that matters for your household, look elsewhere.

**Q: Does the scent linger on hands after washing?**
Lightly, for about 10-15 minutes. It’s pleasant, not perfumey.

**Q: Can I use it as a body wash?**
Technically yes, but it’s formulated for hands. The moisturizing profile is similar to a gentle body wash, but you’ll run through the bottle faster.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a hand soap that moisturizes genuinely, smells warm and inviting, and looks good on the counter. **Skip it if** you need maximum value per ounce or prefer unscented/antibacterial options.

This is a solid everyday upgrade for under $5. The 4.8-star rating holds up — it’s not the cheapest soap, but it’s one of the most pleasant to use. **4.6/5 stars.**
