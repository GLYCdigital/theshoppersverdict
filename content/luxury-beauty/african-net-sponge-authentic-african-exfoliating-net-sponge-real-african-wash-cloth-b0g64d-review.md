## What You Actually Get

The package contains one continuous tube of stretchy nylon netting — roughly 60 inches long when fully opened. ## The Real-World Performance

Here's the honest physics of this thing: nylon mesh + friction = dead skin removal. But it's not just any friction. The individual strands create a gentle "sawing" action across your skin that lifts dead cells without the abrasive grit of salt scrubs.

I tested it against a popular Italian exfoliating mitt and a Korean Italy towel. The African Net Sponge sits between them in aggressiveness — more effective than the mitt, less punishing than the Italy towel. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the texture is genuinely aggressive. If you have sensitive skin, eczema, or fresh tattoos, this will sting. Start with barely any pressure and work up — your skin needs to build tolerance.

Second, the darker color options bled dye during the first two washes. I recommend washing it alone in cold water before first use.

Third, this is nylon. It's plastic mesh. If you're trying to reduce synthetic materials in your beauty routine, this isn't a natural fiber alternative. It does, however, last 6-12 months versus the 3-4 weeks a loofah survives before turning into a bacteria farm.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should I use it?**
Start with 2-3 times per week. Daily use is fine for most people, but let your skin tell you — if it feels tight or stingy afterward, reduce frequency.

**Q: Does it really get the back?**
Yes. The long tube design lets you hold both ends and pull it across your back like a towel. This is the single best feature for acne-prone back skin.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
Rinse thoroughly after each use and hang to dry. Once a week, soak in warm water with a drop of dish soap to remove body oil buildup. Replace every 4-6 months or when it starts stretching out.
