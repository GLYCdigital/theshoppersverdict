## What's Actually in the Box

StyleCraft keeps the packaging honest. You get the Rogue clipper body, one adjustable blade (zero-gap ready), a magnetic charging stand, AC adapter, blade oil, cleaning brush, and a second taper lever guard. No gimmicky travel case, no "free" accessories you'll never touch. The matte gunmetal finish on the full aluminum chassis looks even better in person than the product images suggest — it's got a subtle texture that resists fingerprints, which matters when you're doing back-to-back cuts.

## Performance: Where the 9V Magnetic Motor Shines

The headline feature is the 9V magnetic motor, and it's not marketing fluff. Traditional magnetic motors (the kind in $40 Wahl clippers) lose torque when you hit dense hair. The Rogue maintains consistent speed through thick, coarse textures. At 14 ounces, it's noticeably lighter than rotary-powered competitors like the Wahl Senior, which reduces hand fatigue during long sessions.

The blade system is the real standout. The Rogue ships zero-gapped from the factory — meaning the cutting blade sits flush against the stationary blade for the tightest possible cut. Out of the box, I was getting crisp fade lines without any adjustment. The taper lever gives solid range from a #0000 close cut up to about a #1.5, which covers most fade work. For general cutting, it pairs well with guard attachments, though you'll want a separate trimmer for detail work around ears and necklines.

Battery life checks in at roughly 90-120 minutes per charge depending on hair density. That's enough for a full day of barbering or several weeks of personal cuts. The magnetic charging stand is convenient — drop it on, and you're charging without fumbling with cables.

## Where It Falls Short

The battery indicator is the weakest link. It shows green when charged and red when low — nothing in between. You can't tell if you have 20 minutes or 80 minutes left, which is a genuine problem for professionals who need to plan their day. The included maintenance supplies are also minimal; the blade oil bottle is tiny, and the brush is barely adequate. Budget for proper cleaning supplies within your first month of ownership.

The motor runs warm during extended use — not hot enough to be uncomfortable, but noticeable if you're cutting multiple heads in a row. It's a tradeoff for the power output, and most users won't find it deal-breaking.
