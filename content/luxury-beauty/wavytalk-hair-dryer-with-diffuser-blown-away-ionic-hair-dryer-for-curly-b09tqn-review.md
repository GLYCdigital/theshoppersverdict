Let's cut through the noise. The Wavytalk Blown Away Ionic Hair Dryer isn't a Dyson Supersonic — but at a fraction of the price, it's aiming for the same category of performance. The question is whether it actually lands the blowout.

I've spent three weeks with this dryer on 2A waves, 3B curls, and a thick mane that normally takes 25 minutes to dry. Here's what matters.

**What you actually get**

The box includes the dryer body, three magnetic attachments (a wide-tooth diffuser, a concentrator nozzle, and a flyaway-smoothing nozzle), plus a storage bag and a cleaning brush. The diffuser is the star here — it's deep enough to cup curls without crushing them, and the prongs are flexible rather than stiff plastic.

The dryer itself weighs 1.2 pounds, which sounds light on paper. In hand, it's balanced reasonably well, though the motor sits high so there's a slight forward tilt.

**Performance: fast, but watch the heat**

The 110,000 RPM brushless motor is the real deal. On my thick hair, dry time dropped from 25 minutes to about 11. That's significant — less time under heat means less damage, period.

The ionic function (200 million negative ions per second) does help with frizz. One reviewer noted, *"My curls have never looked this defined after air drying — the diffuser actually distributes air evenly instead of blasting one spot."* I agree. Curl clumps stayed intact, and the finished result had real shine rather than that dull, heat-stressed look.

The temperature control is where it gets tricky. There are three heat settings and two speeds, but even the low heat setting runs noticeably warm. For anyone with fine or color-treated hair, you'll be holding the cold shot button more than you'd like. It works, but it's clunky.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The magnetic attachments get a lot of love. They snap on with a satisfying click and stay put mid-dry — no hunting for a cool-down period to swap nozzles. Owners of curly hair specifically call out the diffuser, with one writing, *"Finally a diffuser that doesn't just push my curls flat. I get volume at the root and definition at the ends."*

Noise level is also genuinely low for this class of dryer. It's not silent, but you can hold a conversation over it — a nice change from the jet-engine whine of cheaper models.

**Where it falls short**

The heat issue is the biggest real-world complaint. Multiple reviewers mention that even the lowest setting feels hot. This is a genuine limitation for fine or fragile hair.

Also, the cord is only 8 feet long. In most bathrooms, that means you're tethered to the outlet closest to the mirror. Minor, but annoying when you're trying to angle the dryer.

Finally, the "cool" button requires constant pressure — there's no lock-on toggle. For a dryer in this price tier, that's a missed convenience.

**Who should buy this**

- **Curly and wavy hair owners** who want defined, frizz-free results without dropping $400 on a premium dryer
- **Thick hair sufferers** tired of 20+ minute drying marathons
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want 80% of the Dyson experience at 30% of the price

**Skip it if** you have extremely fine, fragile hair that can't handle warmth — or if you don't care about attachments and just need a basic dryer.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this dryer actually good for curly hair?**
A: Yes, genuinely. The diffuser is deep and the ionic tech helps define curls. Use the low heat setting with the cool shot for best results.

**Q: How loud is it compared to a standard dryer?**
A: Noticeably quieter — roughly the volume of a stand mixer on low. Conversation is possible.

**Q: Does the diffuser fit other dryers?**
A: No, it's magnetic and specifically sized for this model. The attachments don't work with other brands.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have thick or curly hair and want salon-quality drying at home without the luxury markup. **Skip it if** your hair is fine and heat-sensitive — you'll be fighting the temperature settings the whole time.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely impressive dryer with real performance chops, held back only by heat control that runs hot and a cool shot button that requires a finger of commitment.
