Here's the thing about hair claws: most are either too flimsy to hold anything or so aggressive they give you a headache by noon. The Morhom 6-piece set sits in a rare sweet spot — and 225 Amazon reviewers mostly agree, averaging 4.5 stars.

I tested these across a week of gym sessions, work-from-home video calls, and one particularly humid outdoor dinner. Here's what actually matters.

**What you get**

Six medium-sized claws at 1.3 inches each. They arrive in a mix of neutral and soft tones (the exact colorway varies by listing), with a glossy, tortoiseshell-adjacent finish. Each clip has interlocking teeth that grip from both sides rather than just clamping down. The plastic feels dense — not the hollow, brittle kind that snaps on day three.

**How they perform**

The spring tension is the headline feature. These clips close with a firm snap and hold that position. I have thick, wavy hair that laughs at most drugstore claws — these stayed put through a 40-minute run and a full workday of head-tilting calls. One reviewer put it plainly: "Finally a claw that doesn't slide out by lunch. I've bought three more packs since."

The rounded teeth are a genuine plus. No snagging, no scalp scratching, no painful removal. You can twist your hair up, clamp down, and forget about it. That's the entire job, and these do it well.

The medium size is the tradeoff. At 1.3 inches, these work best for half-up styles, ponytail twists, and shorter buns. If you've got waist-length hair that you like to pile into a massive top knot, you'll need to use two clips or size up. A few reviewers noted this exact limitation: "Cute and sturdy, but my thick, long hair needs the large size."

**Where they fall short**

The glossy finish is the main culprit for fine, straight hair. If your hair is silky-smooth, the clip can slowly migrate south over a few hours — the grip teeth do their job, but the polished surface doesn't add friction. A quick texturizing spray or a rougher hair texture solves it, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Also, six identical clips is a lot of one style. You get variety in color, but not in shape or size. If you wanted a mix of small and medium for different looks, this isn't that set.

**Who should buy this**

- **The gym-goer** who needs hair to stay off their neck without constant re-adjusting
- **The half-up enthusiast** who wants a few clips to rotate through outfits
- **Anyone tired of metal clips** that pull hair or plastic ones that crack after a week

**FAQ**

**Will these hold thick hair?** Yes, if you're doing a twist or half-up style. For full buns with very long hair, you'll need the large size — the medium just can't span that much hair.

**Do they hurt after hours of wear?** No. The rounded teeth and even pressure distribution mean no hot spots, even after a full workday.

**Are they durable?** The spring mechanism is the weak point on any claw. These feel solid — no creaking or loosening after a month of daily use — but drop them on tile and you might crack the plastic.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want reliable, comfortable medium claws that actually grip. **Skip it if** you have very long, thick hair needing a large-size clip, or if your hair is so fine that glossy surfaces don't hold.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A practical, well-made set that nails the basics. The size limitation is the only real reason to look elsewhere, and it's a solvable one.
