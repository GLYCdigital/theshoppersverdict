If you fish for stocked trout, bluegill, or crappie, you already know the struggle: standard PowerBait sinks into muck, and live bait dies in your pocket. Berkley's Floating Mice Tails fixes the first problem with a simple trick — the tail floats. Whether that earns a permanent spot in your tackle box depends on how you fish.

**What you actually get**

One sealed package contains roughly a dozen soft tails in a blend of pink, chartreuse, and natural worm colors. Each tail measures about 2.5 inches — small enough for panfish but substantial enough to entice a 16-inch rainbow. As shown in the product image, the packaging is the familiar clamshell with the PowerBait logo, and the tails are pre-scented with that signature garlic-crawfish smell that's been catching fish for decades.

**How it performs on the water**

The floating action is the headline feature, and it delivers. Rig one on a size 8 or 10 baitholder hook with a split shot 12 inches up the line, and the tail suspends vertically — quivering like an injured creature. That presentation gets slammed by aggressive bluegill and stocked rainbows. One verified buyer noted, "I caught 14 trout in two hours using these under a bobber. The float keeps it in the strike zone instead of buried in the rocks."

On a slow retrieve, the tail kicks with a subtle side-to-side wobble. That's the PowerBait scent doing its work — fish hold on longer before dropping the bait, which gives you an extra second to set the hook.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The scent durability gets the most shout-outs. Unlike live worms that go limp after one fish, these tails hold up through multiple catches. One reviewer commented, "Used one tail for five crappie before it finally tore. The scent stayed strong the whole time." The floating action also solves a specific problem for bank fishermen — you can fish weed lines and drop-offs without losing bait to bottom snags.

**Where it falls short**

The softness that makes the action so lifelike is also the weakness. Aggressive strikes from bigger fish tear the tail in half. Several reviewers mentioned burning through a full package in a single afternoon of heavy fishing. And if you're fishing moving water, the scent dissipates faster than in still ponds — expect to swap tails every 15 to 20 minutes to maintain effectiveness.

**Who should buy this**

- **Bank anglers targeting stocked trout** — the floating action keeps bait visible and accessible without constant re-rigging
- **Panfish hunters using slip-bobber rigs** — perfect match for bluegill, crappie, and perch
- **Kids learning to fish** — forgiving bait that stays on the hook and gets bites quickly

Skip it if you primarily fish strong current, need a durable bait for repeated casts into heavy cover, or prefer fishing bottom with weight — the floating action works against you in those scenarios.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use these with a Carolina rig?**
A: Technically yes, but the floating action will lift your bait off the bottom. You're better off with a slip-bobber or a simple split-shot rig to let the tail rise naturally.

**Q: Do they work for bass?**
A: Occasionally — smallmouth will hit them on rivers — but these are designed for panfish and trout. Use a larger soft plastic if bass is your target.

**Q: How long does the scent last?**
A: Expect 3–5 fish or about 20 minutes in still water. In current, cut that time in half.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, scent-packed panfish bait that stays in the strike zone and catches fish without fuss.

**Skip it if** you need durability for heavy cover or fish exclusively in fast-moving rivers.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The floating action and scent delivery are genuinely effective, but the fragile body keeps it from a perfect score. At this price point, it's a solid value for anyone chasing panfish or stocked trout.
