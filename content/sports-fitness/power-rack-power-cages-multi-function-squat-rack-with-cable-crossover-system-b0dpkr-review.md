If you're shopping for a power rack that also gives you cable work, the Power Rack Power Cages Multi-Function Squat Rack with Cable is trying to be your entire home gym in one footprint. The question is whether it delivers enough quality to justify the space — or whether you'd be better off with a bare-bones cage and a separate cable tower.

Here's the honest breakdown after digging through the specs and what 210 buyers are saying.

**What you're actually getting**

This is a 2x2-inch, 11-gauge steel rack with a 72-inch height and roughly 48 inches of depth — compact enough for a garage corner or spare bedroom. The package includes the rack frame, J-hooks, safety bars, a pull-up bar, and the headline feature: a dual-pulley cable crossover system with a lat pulldown bar and low-row handles.

The cable system is what sets this apart from a $300 basic squat stand. You get a functional trainer-style setup for chest flies, tricep pushdowns, lat pulldowns, and cable rows without buying a second machine. That's a legit value play — a standalone functional trainer from a major brand runs $800 to $1,500.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The frame is solid for the price point. The 11-gauge steel is the same thickness used in racks from Titan and comparable to entry-level REP units. Buyers consistently mention the rack feels stable during heavy squats — one reviewer noted, "I'm 230 pounds and repping 315 on squats, and this thing doesn't budge." The J-hooks have a decent plastic coating that protects your bar, and the safety bars are rated adequately for most home lifters.

The weak point is the cable system. It works, but it's not buttery smooth. The 3.5mm cables run through basic pulleys that produce a slight grinding sensation on heavier loads. You won't mistake this for a gym-grade functional trainer. For moderate weight (under 150 pounds on the stack), it's fine. If you're planning heavy cable rows at 200+ pounds, you'll feel the friction.

Assembly is the other real hurdle. Multiple reviewers mention it took 3–4 hours with two people. The hardware is organized in bags, but they're not labeled by assembly step. Lay everything out and watch the manual twice before starting.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The space-saving design gets the most love. One verified purchase wrote: "I live in an apartment and this replaced my squat stand, pull-up bar, and cable machine all in one. My wife uses it for cable work, I use it for squats. It's the best money I've spent on home gym equipment." Another said the lat pulldown bar "actually feels decent for a rack attachment."

The value proposition is real. Getting a squat rack, pull-up bar, and cable crossover for roughly the price of a mid-range rack alone is why this sits at 4.2 stars.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the cable smoothness and assembly time, the pull-up bar is fixed at a set height — no multi-grip options, no adjustment. If you're over 6 feet tall, you'll be doing bent-knee pull-ups. The cable crossover arms don't have independent height adjustment on both sides; you adjust them together, which limits some exercises.

Also, the weight stack is not included. This is a plate-loaded system, meaning you need your own Olympic plates for both the rack and the cables. That's worth factoring into your budget if you're starting from scratch.

**Who should buy this**

This rack is ideal for:
- **Apartment or garage lifters** with limited floor space who want compound lifts and cable accessories in one unit
- **Lifters up to intermediate level** (squatting under 400 pounds) who don't need commercial-grade smoothness
- **Home gyms on a budget** that would otherwise need to buy a rack and a cable machine separately

Skip it if you're a serious powerlifter pushing heavy weight on cables, or if you have a dedicated room where you can buy separate, specialized equipment.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Is the cable system smooth enough for daily use?**
A: Yes for most exercises under 150 pounds. It's not commercial-grade, but it's functional. If you're doing light-to-moderate cable work, you won't be disappointed.

**Q: What's the max weight capacity?**
A: The rack frame is rated to hold 800 pounds (bar + plates). The cable system is rated around 300 pounds per side. Stay within those and you're fine.

**Q: Does it come with a weight stack?**
A: No. It's plate-loaded, so you need your own Olympic plates. Budget for that if you're new to home gyms.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a rack, pull-up bar, and cable crossover in one compact unit without spending $2,000+. For the price, the versatility is unmatched, and the frame is solid enough for most home lifters.

**Skip it if:** You need buttery-smooth cables for heavy work, or you're over 6'2" and need adjustable pull-up bar heights.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful multi-station rack held back by cable friction and assembly complexity, but a strong value for space-conscious lifters.
