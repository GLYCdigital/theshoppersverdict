If you've ever watched your golf cart lean into a turn like a boat in a swell, you already know the problem: a narrow track width makes these things feel tippy. The 10L0L 2-inch wheel spacer kit is the most straightforward fix for that, and with 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, it's clearly working for a lot of owners. But "straightforward" doesn't mean "zero effort" — here's what you need to know before you buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

The kit includes four 2-inch spacers (two per axle), each precision-machined from 6061-T6 aluminum with a black anodized finish that won't flake off after a season of sun and rain. You also get eight extended lug nuts — these aren't cheap afterthoughts, they're hardened steel with the correct 1/2-20 thread pitch for most American carts. Each spacer is hub-centric, meaning it centers on the hub lip rather than relying on the lug nuts to hold alignment. That's the difference between a smooth ride at 20 mph and a wheel that wobbles like a shopping cart.

## Real-World Performance

Installation is where the 10L0L kit earns its keep. The spacers bolt directly onto your existing hub, then your wheel mounts on top. Most 4-lug Club Car and EZGO owners report a 30-minute job with basic hand tools. The kit's design also clears factory calipers on disc-brake models — a common clearance issue with cheaper spacers that push the wheel out but grind against brake components.

One buyer noted: "Installed on my 2018 EZGO RXV, zero vibration at full speed, and the cart feels planted on hills now." Another mentioned: "Used these to fit 12-inch wheels on a lifted Club Car — no rubbing, even with four passengers."

The stability improvement is the headline feature. Widening the track by 4 inches total (2 per side) meaningfully reduces body roll. If your course has sloped fairways or you carry a cooler and two passengers, this is a genuine safety upgrade, not just a cosmetic one.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the fitment caveats. The listing claims "fits most," but that's doing heavy lifting. On some Club Car DS models from the mid-2000s, the factory studs are slightly too long and need trimming — about 1/4 inch — before the spacer seats flush. Not hard with a grinder, but it's not the pure bolt-on experience the listing implies. Double-check your hub's stud length before starting.

Also, understand the steering tradeoff. Adding 2 inches of offset per side increases the scrub radius, which means slightly heavier steering at parking-lot speeds and more feedback through the wheel over bumps. It's manageable, but if you have a stock steering system with worn bushings, you'll feel the difference.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Lifted cart owners** running larger tires who need extra clearance from the suspension — this kit is a must-have to prevent rubbing.
- **Two-seat cart owners** who regularly carry passengers or cargo and want to kill that tippy feeling.
- **Anyone upgrading to custom wheels** who wants a proper stance without buying new axles.

Skip it if you have a factory-stock cart you only drive solo on flat, paved paths — the stock width is fine, and you'll be paying for stability you won't use.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my Yamaha Drive?**
A: Yes, the 5-lug pattern (5x4.5") is standard on Yamaha Drive models. Verify your lug pattern first — it's stamped on the hub — but this covers the vast majority of carts built after 2007.

**Q: Do I need a lift kit to use these?**
A: No. Spacers work on stock-height carts. They widen the stance but don't raise anything. If you're adding them to clear larger tires, that's when a lift becomes relevant.

**Q: Are the included lug nuts strong enough for highway speeds?**
A: They're rated for the application, and the hardened steel is overkill for golf cart use. Torque them to 80-90 ft-lbs and re-check after your first 25 miles; that's the standard practice for any wheel hardware.
