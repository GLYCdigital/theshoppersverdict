If you’ve gripped a sweaty pickleball paddle mid-rally, you know the feeling: the handle slips, your shot goes soft, and you lose confidence. JOOLA’s replacement grip tape aims to fix that with a moisture-wicking, tacky surface that’s become a favorite among the 229 Amazon reviewers who’ve rated it 4.6 out of 5. But is it actually better than the stock grip on your paddle, or just another roll of sticky plastic? I tested it over three weeks of outdoor and indoor play to give you the straight scoop.

**What’s in the pack**  
You get three rolls of grip tape, each measuring roughly 1.1mm thick and about 3.5 feet long, plus three strips of finishing tape. That’s enough for two full paddle re-wraps (or one if you like a thick, layered grip). The material is a polyurethane blend—not the cheap rubber you find on bargain rolls. As shown in the product image, the surface has a subtle texture pattern that’s designed to channel sweat away from your palm rather than letting it pool.

**How it performs on the court**  
The first thing you notice is the tackiness. It’s grippy without being sticky—your hand doesn’t feel glued to the handle, but the paddle won’t twist on a hard swing. In 90-degree humidity, my palm stayed dry for a full two-hour session, which is better than the standard factory grip on most JOOLA paddles. The 1.1mm thickness is the sweet spot: it adds just enough cushion to soften impact on off-center hits without muting the feedback you need for dinking and resets.

One thing that surprised me: the grip doesn’t absorb impact noise as much as thicker wraps, so you’ll still hear the “pop” of the ball—something purists actually prefer. The finishing tape adheres well and doesn’t peel after a week of play, which is a complaint I’ve seen with cheaper brands.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Scrolling through the reviews, a few themes come up again and again. One verified buyer wrote: *“The best grip I’ve used on my Joola Perseus—feels like a custom wrap, not a generic replacement.”* Another noted: *“I’ve bought three packs now; the tackiness lasts longer than Gamma or Wilson overgrips.”* Multiple reviewers mention the value—three rolls for the price of two from competitors—and the fact that it fits standard 4.25-inch circumference handles without bunching or wrinkling.

**Where it falls short**  
Let’s be honest: no grip is perfect. The length is the biggest issue. If you have a paddle with an extended handle (like the JOOLA Ben Johns Hyperion), one roll won’t cover the full length plus a figure-eight wrap at the base. You’ll need to stretch it thin or use a second roll, which eats into the value. Also, the initial tackiness—which feels fantastic on day one—dulls after about two weeks of daily play. It doesn’t become slippery, but it loses that “new grip” feel. If you play four times a week, budget for a re-wrap every month.

**Who should buy this**  
This grip is ideal for intermediate to advanced players who care about feel and moisture control, especially if you play outdoors in summer or in non-air-conditioned courts. Beginners with a stock paddle will notice an immediate upgrade in control, but casual players who only play once a week might find the re-wrap cycle annoying. If you have a paddle with a non-standard handle shape (like a flared or oversized grip), measure first—the 3.5-foot length may not suffice.

**FAQ: What shoppers actually ask**  
*Q: Does this fit my JOOLA paddle?*  
Yes, it fits all standard 4.25-inch round or octagonal handles. For Ben Johns Hyperion models with longer handles, you may need to overlap slightly or use a second roll.

*Q: How does it compare to overgrips?*  
Overgrips are thinner (0.5mm) and meant to be replaced often. This is a replacement grip—thicker and more durable—so it changes the paddle’s feel more noticeably.

*Q: Can I wash it?*  
No. It’s designed for one-time use. Washing will degrade the adhesive and tackiness.

**The Verdict**  
JOOLA’s grip tape delivers exactly what it promises: reliable moisture control and a tacky, comfortable feel that improves your paddle’s handling. It’s not the cheapest option, but the three-pack makes it a solid value if you play regularly. The short length and fading tackiness are real tradeoffs, but for most players, this is a worthy upgrade over stock grips.

**Buy it if:** You play 2+ times per week, sweat a lot, or want a firmer, more connected feel than stock grips offer.  
**Skip it if:** You have an oversized handle, play casually once a month, or prefer ultra-thin overgrips for maximum paddle feel.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A strong, reliable choice with minor length and longevity caveats.
