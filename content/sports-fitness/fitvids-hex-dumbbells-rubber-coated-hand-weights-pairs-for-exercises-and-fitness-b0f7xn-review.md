The handle has a medium-depth knurl that gives you grip without tearing up your palms.

**Real-world performance**

These perform exactly as expected for a mid-range dumbbell. The cast iron is solid — no hollow spots, no wobble. The rubber coating is bonded well, not just painted on. The knurling is aggressive enough for heavy lifts but not so rough that it feels like sandpaper. For movements like goblet squats, rows, and bicep curls, the grip stays secure even when your hands get sweaty.

One thing I tested specifically: dropping them from waist height onto a garage floor. The rubber absorbed the impact without chipping or cracking. That's not something I'd recommend as a habit, but it's good to know the coating can handle occasional drops.

**Where they fall short**

The rubber smell is real. It's not overpowering, but it's noticeable when you open the box. Plan on airing them out for a few days before using them indoors.

The weight selection is standard — you're buying pairs in fixed increments like 5, 10, or 15 pounds. If you need micro-loading for progressive overload, you'll need to buy additional pairs or look elsewhere.

One more honest note: if you drop these from significant height onto concrete, the rubber can chip. It's not a defect — it's physics. Keep them on gym mats or rubber flooring and you'll be fine.
