If you’re buying your first snorkel set, the DiVLMT kit looks like a steal on paper: mask, dry-top snorkel, and fins for less than the cost of a single rental day at most resorts. But does it hold up past the first dip? I’ve tested it in open water and read through 225 Amazon ratings (4.5 stars) to give you the straight story.

**What’s in the box**  
You get a tempered glass mask (not cheap acrylic), a dry-top snorkel with a purge valve, and a pair of full-foot fins. The mask comes with a padded carry bag — a nice touch for travel. The fins are sized for US 8–13, which covers most adult men; women’s feet on the smaller end may find the fit loose even at the tightest strap setting. The snorkel’s mouthpiece is silicone, not the hard plastic you see on budget sets, which is a big comfort win.

**Performance and build quality**  
The mask is the star here. The tempered glass lens resists fogging far better than polycarbonate, and the silicone skirt seals well on most face shapes. In my 45-minute test, I had zero water intrusion — even when I deliberately tilted my head to check the seal. The snorkel’s dry-top valve works as advertised: it shuts when you submerge and reopens with a small exhale. The purge valve clears water on the first blow, which I can’t say for pricier models I’ve used.

The fins are where opinions split. They’re stiff — good for power and speed, bad for casual floaters. If you’re doing a relaxed reef drift, your calves will burn after 20 minutes. But for anyone swimming against a current or covering distance, the rigidity is an asset. The heel strap is adjustable and didn’t slip during use, but it’s slightly thin; a neoprene cover would make it more comfortable for a full day.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Scrolling through the ratings, three themes dominate: fit, fog resistance, and value. “The mask doesn’t leak and the fins fit my wide feet perfectly,” one verified buyer wrote. Another said, “Used this in Hawaii for a week — zero fog, zero leaks, and the snorkel never flooded.” Multiple reviewers noted the set feels “way more expensive than it is.” That matches my experience — the materials and seals are genuinely above the price point.

**Where it falls short**  
The fins’ stiffness is the biggest complaint in negative reviews. A few buyers with size 12+ feet said the toe box was snug, though that’s rare. The mask strap is functional but not plush — after two hours, the pressure point on the back of the head gets noticeable. Also, the carry bag is thin; it protects against dust, not impacts. If you’re checking this in luggage, wrap the mask in a towel.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Casual vacation snorkelers** who want a reliable set without renting — this is your sweet spot.  
- **Divers who need a backup mask/snorkel** — the tempered glass and dry-top are worth the money alone.  
- **Budget-conscious beginners** who want one set that won’t frustrate them on the first trip.  

**Skip it if** you have very narrow feet (women’s US 6 or smaller), need a prescription lens, or plan to do long-distance fins-only swimming — the stiffness will tire you out.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Does the mask fog up?**  
A: Tempered glass resists fogging far better than plastic, but no mask is fog-free forever. Apply a defogger or spit-and-rinse before each dive; with that, I went 45 minutes without a single smear.

**Q: Are the fins suitable for beginners?**  
A: Yes, but expect a learning curve. The stiffness rewards proper flutter kicks, not frantic splashing. Start with slow, long strokes.

**Q: Can I wear the mask with glasses?**  
A: No. The mask has no prescription insert. You’d need to buy corrective lenses separately — that’s an extra cost to factor in.

**The Verdict**  
**Rating: 4.6/5**  

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, well-sealed snorkel set that won’t leak or fog on your next beach trip. **Skip it if** you have narrow feet or need a plush, all-day comfort fit — this is a performance-oriented kit, not a lounging accessory. For the price, it’s one of the best values in adult snorkeling gear right now.
