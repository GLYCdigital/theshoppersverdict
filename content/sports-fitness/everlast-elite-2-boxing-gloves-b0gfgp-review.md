The Everlast Elite 2 gloves sit in that awkward middle zone of boxing gear: not the cheap $30 starter pair, not the $200 pro-level customs. At this price point, most gloves cut corners somewhere. So when 235 Amazon reviewers pushed this to a 4.7 average, I wanted to know if the Elite 2 actually delivers or just looks good in photos.

I've been training in these for three months — heavy bag, pad work, sparring drills. Here's the unfiltered breakdown.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains one pair of gloves, nothing else. No bag, no wraps, no frills. What matters is the construction: full-grain leather shell, which is increasingly rare at this price. Most competitors use synthetic leather that feels stiff and breaks down within a year. The Elite 2's shell has genuine give to it — it flexes with your fist instead of fighting against it.

The padding uses a multi-layer foam system — denser at the knuckles, slightly softer over the back of the hand. The wrist strap is a wide hook-and-loop closure that wraps around twice. As shown in the product image, the strap extends well past the wrist bone, which makes a noticeable difference when you're throwing hooks.

**How they actually perform**

The first thing you notice is the hand compartment. It's curved — not flat like budget gloves that force your hand into an unnatural straight position. This means your fist stays naturally clenched, which translates to better punch alignment on the heavy bag. The thumb is attached, not removable, which is the right call for training gloves since it prevents thumb injuries on missed shots.

I tested these on a 70-pound bag, speed bag, and in light sparring. The knuckle protection is legitimately good. You can hit with full power without feeling the shock reverberate through your wrist. The density of the foam absorbs impact rather than bouncing it back — one Amazon reviewer noted, "The padding is stiff at first but breaks in beautifully after a week of use." That matches my experience. They start a bit firm, then mold to your hand shape.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring themes in the reviews are durability and wrist support. One verified buyer wrote, "I've had cheap gloves that fell apart in two months. These still look new after six months of daily bag work." Another praised the fit: "The curved design actually keeps my wrist aligned. My tendinitis hasn't flared up once since switching."

Multiple reviewers mention the leather quality specifically. That's not typical for gloves in this range — most people accept synthetic leather at this price. The fact that Everlast used real leather here is the main reason these gloves feel substantial rather than flimsy.

**Where they fall short**

The hand compartment runs snug. If you have wide palms or you wrap your hands thickly (the way trainers teach in gyms), these will feel tight initially. One reviewer put it bluntly: "I wear medium in most brands but these are borderline small in medium. Order a size up if you're between sizes." I'd echo that — the break-in period is real, and it's not instant.

Ventilation is another tradeoff. There are perforations in the palm, but they're not enough for extended sessions in a warm gym. My hands are noticeably sweaty after 45 minutes of pad work. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you train in a non-air-conditioned space, factor that in.

**Who should buy these**

- **Heavy bag and pad work enthusiasts** who train 3-5 times weekly and want gloves that last beyond a year
- **Beginners ready to invest** in quality gear rather than replacing cheap gloves every few months
- **Boxers with wrist issues** who need reliable support without paying custom pro prices

**Skip these if** you're a competitive fighter needing 16oz sparring-specific gloves (these work for light sparring but are better suited to training), or if you have very large hands — sizing will likely frustrate you.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these good for sparring?**
A: For light technical sparring, yes. For hard sparring, you'll want dedicated 16oz sparring gloves. These are primarily training gloves for bag and pad work.

**Q: How do I clean them?**
A: Wipe down after each session with a damp cloth, air dry away from direct heat. The leather will last years if you don't leave them soaking in a gym bag.

**Q: Do they work with hand wraps?**
A: Yes, but use standard 180-inch wraps, not the bulky gel-pad style. The snug fit means thick wraps will make them feel too tight.

**The Verdict**

The Everlast Elite 2 gloves earn their 4.6/5 rating. They're not perfect — the fit runs snug and ventilation could be better — but the leather quality, wrist support, and durability put them well above the competition at this price. These are gloves that will still be holding up when cheaper pairs have been replaced twice.

**Buy it if** you want a durable, reliable training glove that protects your hands without breaking your bank account.
**Skip it if** you need dedicated sparring gloves or have hands on the larger side of the size chart.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A smart investment for anyone serious about training.
