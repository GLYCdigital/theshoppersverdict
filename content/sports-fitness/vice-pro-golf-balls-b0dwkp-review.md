Vice has spent years positioning itself as the "smart golfer's tour ball" — direct-to-consumer pricing, no retail markup, and specs that stack up against the $50-per-dozen premium options. The Vice Pro is the brand's flagship three-piece urethane offering, and at a street price that typically lands 30-40% below Titleist's premium line, it deserves a hard look from anyone who refuses to lose a $5 ball every other hole.

## What You're Actually Getting

The Vice Pro is a three-piece ball with a cast urethane cover — the same construction family as the Pro V1 and TaylorMade TP5. The compression rating sits at 76, which puts it in the "soft-ish but not marshmallow" zone. You're getting a 336-dimple pattern designed to promote a penetrating flight, and the ball comes in White, Lime, or Red matte finishes.

As shown in the product image, the matte coatings are Vice's signature — they're not just cosmetic. The Lime and Red versions are significantly easier to track in the air and on the ground, which is a genuine advantage in fall or on overcast days. The box includes a dozen balls, and the branding is clean and modern without being obnoxious.

## On-Course Performance

Off the driver, the Vice Pro launches with a mid trajectory and modest spin — exactly what most single-digit and low-teens handicappers want. The feel is firm but responsive, not dead or boardy. Around the greens is where this ball earns its keep: the urethane cover grips wedges aggressively, and you can confidently play flop shots and one-hop-stops into firm greens.

One thing that surprised me in testing: the putter feel. At 76 compression, the ball comes off the face with a crisp, muted click that gives solid feedback without feeling harsh. It's noticeably softer than the Vice Pro Plus (which runs 95 compression) and better suited for players who prefer a touch-responsive putter feel.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating across 240+ reviews is telling. The recurring theme in customer feedback is value without compromise. One verified buyer noted: "I've played Pro V1s for a decade. Switched to these and honestly can't tell the difference — except my wallet can." Another reviewer highlighted durability: "Played 18 holes with one ball, hit trees, cart path, the works. It's scuffed but still playable."

The matte finish gets its own share of love. Multiple reviewers mention how much easier it is to spot the Lime version in the rough or against cloudy skies. That's a practical benefit that saves you time and money over the course of a season.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The matte cover is softer than glossy urethane — it scuffs more readily on wedge grooves and cart paths. One reviewer put it plainly: "The matte finish picks up scuffs faster than my previous balls. Doesn't affect performance, but it's noticeable."

The other limitation is swing speed. If you're bombing drives at 110+ mph, the 76 compression can feel slightly mushy off the driver and may produce a bit more spin than you want. The Vice Pro Plus or Pro Soft would serve that profile better. This ball is optimized for the 85-105 mph range — which covers the vast majority of recreational golfers.

## Who Should Buy This

The Vice Pro is ideal for three buyer profiles: the mid-handicapper who wants tour-level spin without the tour-level price tag; the golfer who loses 2-3 balls per round and refuses to pay $5 each for the privilege; and the player who values visibility and wants a matte finish that's easy to track. If you're a high swing speed player or someone who prefers a rock-hard feel, look elsewhere.

## FAQ

**Is the Vice Pro as good as a Pro V1?**
In terms of spin around greens, feel, and durability, it's very close. The Pro V1 has a slight edge in wind performance and high-swing-speed stability, but for most players the difference is negligible — and the price difference is not.

**How long do matte golf balls last?**
With typical play, you'll get 3-5 rounds before the finish starts showing significant wear. The ball remains fully playable, but the matte coating will fade and scuff on the cover.

**Do these balls work for beginners?**
They're better suited for mid-to-low handicappers. Beginners with slower swing speeds (under 80 mph) won't compress the ball enough to unlock its spin benefits — a two-piece ball would serve them better.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want tour-level urethane performance at a price that doesn't punish your golf budget. The Vice Pro delivers 90% of the Pro V1 experience for roughly 60% of the cost.

**Skip it if:** You swing over 110 mph, prefer a glossy cover, or you're a high-handicapper who needs maximum forgiveness rather than spin control.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A legitimate premium ball that undercuts the establishment on price. The scuffing and swing-speed limitations keep it from perfection, but for the vast majority of golfers, this is the smartest ball money in golf right now.
