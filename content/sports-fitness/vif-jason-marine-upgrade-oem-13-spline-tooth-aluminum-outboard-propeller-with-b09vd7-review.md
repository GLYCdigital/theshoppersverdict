## Performance and Build Quality

The 13-spline hub is machined to OEM tolerances, so it slides onto the shaft with the same snug fit as factory parts. The aluminum alloy is aircraft-grade 5083, which is lighter than stainless steel — that translates to quicker hole shots and less rotational mass on mid-range engines. During testing, the prop tracked straight at WOT with minimal ventilation, even in tight turns.

The blades are CNC-balanced, which you'll notice in the absence of vibration at cruising speed. For a $60–80 prop, the finish quality is surprisingly good: no casting flash, no burrs on the blade edges, and the paint holds up better than most budget props I've tested. One Amazon reviewer noted, "Bolt pattern matched my Mercury 90 exactly — no filing, no shimming, just bolted on and went."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. Aluminum is softer than stainless, so if you run in shallow water with rocky bottoms or submerged timber, you'll ding the blades. That's not a defect — it's physics. The prop is designed to sacrifice itself to protect your lower unit, which is exactly what you want in a budget prop.

The other limitation is pitch selection. VIF offers the standard sizes (13.25x17, 13.75x15, etc.), but if you need a custom pitch for a heavily loaded boat or high-altitude running, you'll have to look elsewhere. Also, a few buyers reported needing to file the hub bore slightly for older Mercury gearcases — that's rare, but worth knowing if you're running a pre-2000 motor.
