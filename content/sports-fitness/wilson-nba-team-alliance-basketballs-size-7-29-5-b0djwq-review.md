If you're shopping for a basketball that actually plays like the real thing, the WILSON NBA Team Alliance is one of the few balls under $100 that gets it right. This isn't the $30 rubber ball you grab for driveway pickup — it's the composite leather game ball WILSON builds for competitive indoor play, and it shows from the first dribble.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the ball itself, inflated to roughly 8 PSI, and that's it. No pump, no carrying bag, no accessories. At 29.5 inches and 22 ounces, it's a true size 7 — the official NBA spec. The cover is WILSON's proprietary composite leather with the deep-channel design that gives it that pro-level feel. The branding is clean and understated: the NBA logo on one side, WILSON on the other, with team graphics depending on which version you grab.

**Performance and real-world feel**

Here's the honest truth: this ball needs a break-in period. Out of the box, the composite leather is stiff and the grip is surprisingly slick. Don't panic — that's normal. After about two hours of shooting and dribbling, the channels soften and the ball starts gripping your fingers the way you want. By hour three, it feels like a different ball entirely.

Once broken in, the performance is excellent. The channels are deep enough to give you real finger placement, and the ball tracks true on passes — no wobble, no dead spots. I tested it in a dusty community gym where cheaper balls turn into slippery rocks by the second quarter. The Team Alliance held up noticeably better, retaining grip far longer than synthetic rubber alternatives.

One thing I appreciate: the weight distribution is spot on. Some budget balls feel top-heavy or lopsided in the air. This one flies true, which makes a real difference on long-range shots and full-court passes.

**Where it falls short**

The break-in period is the biggest friction point. If you're buying this for a kid's first game tomorrow, it'll feel stiff and unforgiving. Plan to break it in before game day.

More importantly, this is not an outdoor ball. Composite leather on asphalt or concrete will scuff and wear within a few sessions. If you're playing on blacktop, get a rubber indoor/outdoor ball instead — you'll save money and get better longevity.
