**Bottom line up front:** If you’re a soccer parent, a weekend-warrior athlete, or a coach running small-group sessions, this 20-foot agility ladder with four adjustable hurdles is one of the better value kits under $40. It won’t survive years of daily pro-level punishment, but for 3–4 sessions a week, it delivers exactly what you need: a flat, snag-free ladder and hurdles that don’t collapse mid-drill.

## What’s in the bag

The set includes a 20-foot ladder with 12 flat rungs, 4 adjustable agility hurdles, 8 ground stakes, and a drawstring carry bag. The rungs are made of a stiff nylon webbing — not the flimsy plastic clips you see on $15 Amazon specials. As shown in the product image, the hurdles feature a snap-lock height adjustment with three settings: 6, 9, and 12 inches. Total packed weight is around 3.5 pounds, so it’s genuinely throw-in-the-trunk portable.

## Performance and build quality

The ladder’s flat rung design is the standout feature. I’ve tested ladders with round PVC rungs that roll underfoot — these don’t. On grass, the included stakes keep the ladder drum-tight; on turf or a gym floor, the rungs grip well enough that you won’t need to re-align them between sets. The 20-foot length gives you 12 workable rungs, which is the sweet spot for most footwork drills — you can do full 10-rung patterns without running out of runway.

The hurdles are where this kit separates from cheaper alternatives. The snap-lock mechanism clicks positively at each height, and I couldn’t get them to collapse during high-knee drills or lateral hops. The base is wide enough that they don’t tip on grass, but on a slick basketball court, they’ll slide if you clip them hard. Use the stakes outdoors; indoors, accept that they’ll drift a few inches over a 15-minute session.

One genuine weak point: the ladder’s end straps. They’re thin webbing, and after roughly 15 sessions on concrete, one of mine started fraying where the stake loops through. It hasn’t failed completely, but it’s the first thing that will go. The ladder itself — the rungs and side rails — shows no wear after two months of weekly use.

## What buyers consistently praise

Scrolling through the 215 ratings, three themes dominate. First, **setup speed**: “Took me 30 seconds to lay out and stake down. ## Where it falls short

Beyond the strap durability issue, the hurdles are the limiting factor for advanced athletes. The 12-inch max height is fine for youth players and general conditioning, but if you’re a college-level athlete needing 18–24-inch hurdle work, this isn’t the set for you. Also, the carry bag is thin — it’s fine for storage, but don’t expect it to survive being checked as luggage.

## Who should buy this

- **Soccer and football parents** whose kids train 2–4 times per week. The kit covers footwork, agility, and plyometric basics without breaking the bank.
- **High school coaches** running small-group stations. The flat rungs work on grass, turf, and gym floors, and the hurdles add variety.
- **Home gym owners** who want a compact, portable agility setup for warm-ups or finishers.

Skip it if you’re a professional athlete needing competition-grade hurdles, or if you train exclusively on concrete and expect the straps to last a full season.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the ladder work on grass without stakes?**
It works, but it will bunch up during lateral drills. The stakes are included and take 30 seconds to install — use them.

**Q: Can the hurdles be used for box jumps?**
No. They’re for stepping over and high-knee drills, not landing on. Max height is 12 inches, and the plastic won’t support body weight.

**Q: Is this suitable for kids under 10?**
Yes. The rungs are close enough together (about 15 inches) for smaller strides, and the hurdles at 6 inches are a good intro height.
