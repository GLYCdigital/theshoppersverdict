**The Xflyee Electronic Whistles 4 Pack is the quiet revolution in sports officiating — if you can get past the learning curve of going electric.**

Let me be straight with you: I've blown my share of traditional metal whistles, and they work. But they also leave your ears ringing, your lips chapped, and your patience thin when you're trying to get a gym full of kids to stop mid-drill. The Xflyee pack is designed to replace that hassle with a push-button, three-tone electronic system that hits 120 decibels — roughly the same volume as a chainsaw or a rock concert.

**What's actually in the box**

You get four complete whistles. Each one ships with a CR2032 battery pre-installed, a lanyard, and a detachable carabiner clip. The whistle body itself is compact — about 2.5 inches long and 1 inch wide — and weighs next to nothing at 1.2 ounces. As shown in the product image, the matte finish plastic housing has a single large button on the top and a small LED indicator that flashes when you're in alarm mode. There's no assembly required: pop the battery tab, attach the lanyard, and you're live.

**Performance and build quality in real-world use**

I tested these across three settings: outdoor soccer practice, a high school basketball scrimmage, and a rainy-day flag football game. The three tones are genuinely useful. Tone 1 is a continuous blast — your standard "stop everything" whistle. Tone 2 is an intermittent chirp, which works well for calling rotations or starting drills without shattering eardrums. Tone 3 is a piercing alarm that's unmistakably urgent, ideal for emergencies or getting attention across a large field.

The 120dB claim holds up. On the soccer field, the whistle cut through wind and distant chatter without me having to inhale deeply or reposition the mouthpiece. That's the real advantage over acoustic whistles — you get maximum volume with zero breath control. One Amazon reviewer noted: *"I coach three teams and use these for all of them. The battery has lasted two months of daily practice. The kids actually respond faster because the sound is so sharp."* Another said: *"Bought these for a swim team. Waterproof claim is real — dropped one in the pool during a relay and it kept working."*

Build quality is solid for the price point. The plastic is thick enough to survive being stepped on, and the IPX5 waterproof rating held up in my rain test. The button has a satisfying click, though it's not a hair-trigger — you need a deliberate press. That's a feature when you're worried about accidental activation in a pocket, but it's a minor annoyance when you're juggling a clipboard and trying to blow a quick foul call.

**Where it falls short**

The button placement is the biggest gripe among buyers. It's recessed slightly to prevent accidental presses, which means gloved hands — think winter outdoor sports or football coaches — need to remove a glove or press hard. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Wish the button was bigger. I have to look down to make sure I'm hitting it in a hurry."* That's a fair criticism.

Battery life also varies. The continuous alarm tone drains the CR2032 noticeably faster than the intermittent chirp. In my testing, heavy use (30+ minutes of cumulative blowing per session) got me about three weeks per battery. Light use stretches to two months. Spare batteries are cheap and included replacements are easy to find, but it's worth buying a 10-pack of CR2032s alongside this.

**Who should buy this**

- **Coaches** who blow whistles 50+ times per session and want to save their voice and hearing
- **Referees and umpires** who need consistent volume regardless of crowd noise
- **Swim instructors** who need a waterproof option that won't die from pool exposure

Skip it if you're a traditionalist who loves the feel of a metal pea whistle, or if you need a one-handed whistle that can be blown while carrying gear — the button requires a free hand.
