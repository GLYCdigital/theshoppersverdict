**Vice Golf's Boost set is a bold swing at the beginner market — but it's not for everyone. Here's who should buy it, who should skip it, and what the 185 Amazon raters are actually saying.**

Vice is a brand built on direct-to-consumer balls, and they've brought that same no-frills pricing to clubs. The Boost Complete Set (right-handed) packs 12 clubs into a 4.5 lb stand bag, which is a solid value proposition on paper. But as the 3.7-star average suggests, the real-world experience is more nuanced.

**What's in the box**  
You're getting a 460cc driver, a 3-wood, a 5-hybrid, 6-iron through pitching wedge, and a blade-style putter — 12 clubs total. The bag is a highlight: it's a 6-way top with full-length dividers, padded double straps, and a rain hood. That's more bag than you'll find in most $400–$500 boxed sets. The grips are standard-size Vice-branded rubber, and the shafts are steel for irons, graphite for woods and hybrid.

**Performance and build quality**  
The driver has an oversized face and a shallow profile — forgiving on low-heel strikes, which is exactly where beginners miss. The 5-hybrid is the star of the set: it launches high and straight, which most reviewers confirm. The irons are deep cavity-backs with a wide sole, which helps turf interaction for choppy swings. But here's the catch: **everything is stiff flex**. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I'm 58 and swing at 85 mph — these felt like boards. I had to swing out of my shoes to get any height."* Another buyer noted: *"The putter looked great but weighed less than my phone — took a few rounds to get used to the touch."*

**Where it falls short**  
No headcovers for the 3-wood or hybrid is a genuine oversight — your clubs will clank against each other in the bag. The putter is also polarizing: some love the clean look, others say it lacks a proper alignment line. And the stiff shafts are non-negotiable — if you're not swinging above 90 mph, you'll struggle to launch the ball. That's a significant limitation for a set marketed as "complete" for all skill levels.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Athletic beginners** (ages 25–45) with decent strength and a fast swing — you'll get real distance and forgiveness.  
- **Golfers upgrading from a used starter set** who want a modern bag with full-length dividers.  
- **Lefties?** This is right-handed only, so southpaws, look elsewhere.  

**Who should skip it**  
- **Seniors or anyone with a moderate swing speed** — the stiff shafts will sap your distance and enjoyment.  
- **High-handicappers who want a mallet putter** — the blade requires a more precise stroke.  
- **Players expecting premium feel** — these are entry-level clubs, and they feel like it.

**FAQ**  

*Are the shafts really stiff flex?*  
Yes, all clubs are stiff. There's no regular or senior option. If you're over 50 or swing under 90 mph, this is a dealbreaker.

*Does it come with a bag that fits on a cart?*  
The stand bag has a 6-way top and a pass-through for cart straps, so it works on most push and riding carts. The legs retract easily.

*Is the putter any good?*  
It's a 345g blade with a soft insert. It's serviceable but not forgiving. Most buyers say it's the weakest club in the set.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you're a younger beginner with a quick swing, want a complete set with a genuinely good bag, and don't mind stiff shafts.  
**Skip it if** you're a senior, a high-handicapper with a slower tempo, or you expect headcovers for all your woods.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A great value for the right buyer, but the shaft flex and missing headcovers keep it from being a universal recommendation.
