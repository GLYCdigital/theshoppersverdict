**The Verdict: A Functional Solo Practice Rig That Won't Impress Tournament Players**

If you're a beginner who wants to drill alone without chasing balls for an hour, the Furlihong 680PBH is a legitimately useful tool. At 3.8 stars across 190 ratings, it's not perfect — and you should know exactly where it cuts corners before you buy.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The kit is refreshingly complete. You get the launch machine itself (roughly the size of a small cooler, about 15 pounds), a ball hopper that doubles as a recycling net, two fiberglass paddles, four outdoor balls, and a padded carry bag. Set-up takes under five minutes — the net frame pops together, and the machine sits on a flat surface or tripod base.

**Real-World Performance: The Good and the Grinding**

The ball recycling net is the star. As shown in the product image, the net angles back toward the hopper, so balls you hit into it roll right back to the feeder. This alone saves you dozens of bends and walks per session. One verified buyer noted, *"I hit 100 balls without moving my feet. That's the whole reason I bought this."* That's the honest use case.

The launch machine delivers speeds from gentle lobs to faster drives, with an oscillation mode that alternates left and right — genuinely useful for practicing forehand and backhand footwork. Battery life runs roughly 2-3 hours on a full charge, which covers a solid practice session.

**Where It Falls Short**

No remote control. This is the biggest frustration. To change speed or spin, you walk to the machine, fiddle with the dial, and walk back. That breaks your rhythm and makes interval drills annoying. A $20 remote would have fixed this.

The feeder jams with softer balls. Multiple reviewers report the plastic wheel occasionally catches and stops feeding — usually with cheaper or worn-out balls. You'll need to clear it mid-session. The included balls work fine, but after a few weeks of play, their fuzz wears and jams become more frequent.

The paddles are serviceable but nothing more. Fiberglass face, basic polymer core — fine for learning, but if you're already hitting with friends who use graphite or carbon paddles, you'll feel the difference immediately. Plan to upgrade within a month or two if you get serious.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Solo beginners**: If you live far from a club or your friends are never free, this is a solid way to build muscle memory and consistency.
- **Casual weekend players**: The recycling net makes warm-ups faster, and the kit is portable enough to toss in a trunk.
- **Coaches with small groups**: The machine's adjustable speed works well for feeding drills to 2-3 students.

**Who Should Skip It**

- **Intermediate/advanced players**: The paddle quality and lack of remote will frustrate you. Buy a dedicated ball machine like a Spinshot or a used Lobster instead.
- **Anyone who plays mostly doubles**: You'll rarely use the machine, and the paddles won't match your level.
- **Impatient tinkerers**: If you hate fiddling with jammed feeders, this will test your patience.

**Buyer Questions, Answered**

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
A: Expect 2-3 hours of continuous use. It charges via standard AC adapter in about 2 hours. There's no battery indicator — you'll notice the ball speed dropping when it's low.

**Q: Does it work with indoor balls?**
A: Yes, but the softer indoor balls jam more frequently. Stick with the included outdoor balls or firmer indoor brands like Onix.

**Q: Is the net sturdy enough for outdoor wind?**
A: The frame is lightweight aluminum. In moderate wind, the net can tip. Use the included ground stakes if you're on grass; on hard courts, place a sandbag or weight on the base.

**The Bottom Line**

The Furlihong 680PBH is a budget-friendly gateway into solo pickleball practice. It won't replace a $1,000 ball machine, but it doesn't pretend to. For the price, you get a functional recycling system, a decent enough launcher, and everything you need to start — as long as you're honest about your skill level.

**Buy it if** you're a beginner drilling alone and want to cut ball-chasing time dramatically. **Skip it if** you're an experienced player needing precision control or expecting paddle quality beyond entry-level.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A practical starter tool with clear limitations, best suited for its intended audience: new players who want reps without the fetch.
