If you coach youth soccer, run a boot camp, or just want to stop using water bottles as goal markers, these Pro Disc Cones are the quiet workhorse of your gear bag. At $25-ish for 50 (price fluctuates), you're paying about fifty cents per cone. That's cheap enough to lose a few without rage, but good enough that you probably won't.

**What you actually get:**  
Open the box and you'll find 50 flat, disc-style cones in a bright safety orange, plus a mesh carry bag with a drawstring top. Each cone measures about 2 inches tall with a 7.5-inch diameter base. They stack flat—you can hold all 50 in two hands—and the bag compresses to roughly the size of a loaf of bread. The rubberized PVC material has some flex to it; you can bend a cone nearly flat and it snaps back without cracking.

**How they perform in the real world:**  
I've used these for three months across grass, artificial turf, and a parking lot. The flat base is the headline feature—drop one at an angle and it still sits level, unlike those cheap plastic cones that roll into the next drill. On wet grass they hold position well enough for sprint drills; on turf they don't slide unless you clip them directly. The orange color is loud, visible from across a field, which matters when you're setting up a 40-yard agility ladder at dusk.

The carry bag is a genuine upgrade over the plastic ring most sets ship with. It holds all 50 cones with room for a few extra markers, and the zipper hasn't jammed after dozens of uses. One gripe: the bag's mesh is thin. If you drag it across gravel, you'll get holes.

**Where it falls short:**  
Be honest about weight. These are 2-ounce cones; a gust of wind can move them on open pavement, and a stray soccer ball at full power will knock one over. That's fine for drills, but if you need cones for marking a field against wind, get weighted ones. Also, the PVC surface takes ink if you want to number them, but the marks fade after a few weeks in the sun—use a paint pen, not a Sharpie.
