## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. The 18-inch length is shorter than some competitors — many straps run 24 inches. If you have larger wrists or prefer to wrap the tail around the bar multiple times for extra security, you’ll find the length limiting.

The padding, while great for comfort, adds bulk. If you also wear wrist wraps for pressing movements, you’ll need to adjust how they fit together. And for Olympic lifts like cleans or snatches that require quick bar release, these straps are too stiff — they’re designed for pulling movements, not dynamic work.

One more thing: the hook-and-loop closure can loosen slightly during very heavy sets if you don’t pull it tight enough at the start. It’s a 30-second fix, but worth knowing.

## FAQ

**Are these legal for powerlifting competitions?**
Most federations allow lifting straps for deadlift assistance work, but competition rules vary. Check your specific federation's rulebook — some ban straps during the actual deadlift event.

**How do I wrap them properly?**
Loop the strap around your wrist with the padded side against your skin. Thread the tail through the loop, pull tight, then wrap the remaining length around the bar. Pull the bar to tension the strap before lifting.

**Can I use these for pull-ups?**
Technically yes, but they’re not ideal. The padding helps, but the cotton webbing is thick and can be awkward on a pull-up bar. If you need grip assistance for pull-ups, consider dedicated pull-up grips instead.
