The CRBN X Series Power paddle doesn't waste time with subtlety. From the first hit, you know exactly what this paddle is built for: aggressive, offensive play from the baseline and mid-court. It's the rare paddle that delivers on its name — power is the headline feature, but the real story is how much control CRBN managed to pack alongside it.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the paddle itself. That's it. No cover, no edge tape, no paddle sleeve. At this price point, that stings a bit — most competitors in the $200+ range at least toss in a basic cover. The paddle itself, though, is immediately impressive: a T700 carbon fiber face with a textured peel-ply surface, a polypropylene honeycomb core, and that distinctive thermoformed edge that creates the "unibody" feel.

As shown in the product image, the X Series has a clean, understated look — black face, subtle branding, no loud graphics. It measures a standard 16.5 inches long with a 7.5-inch wide face, and the 5.5-inch handle gives you room for a two-handed backhand without feeling cramped.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The thermoformed construction is the key differentiator here. Unlike traditional paddles where the face is glued to the core, thermoforming fuses everything into a single piece. The result is a larger sweet spot and a more consistent response across the entire face. You'll notice it most on off-center hits — instead of losing power or control, the ball still comes off clean.

The power is real. CRBN specs this at a swing weight that rewards players who generate their own pace — you can drive through the ball with authority from the baseline, and overheads feel genuinely explosive. But here's the surprising part: the carbon fiber surface gives you enough spin and touch to keep the ball in play. Drop shots from the kitchen line feel controllable, and you can shape serves with real intent.

One buyer put it well: "I've played with the Joola and the Selkirk, and this paddle hits harder with better feel. It took me a couple games to adjust, but now I don't want to play with anything else."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in the 229 ratings is the power-to-control ratio. Multiple reviewers note that the paddle rewards clean mechanics — if you swing properly, the ball rockets off the face; if you're sloppy, it doesn't save you. That's honest feedback for a power paddle.

The spin potential gets frequent shoutouts too. The textured carbon face grips the ball noticeably better than smooth fiberglass or graphite surfaces, giving you sharper slice serves and more aggressive topspin drives.

Durability reports are strong so far. The foam-injected edge guard has held up well in reviews, with no reports of edge chipping or face delamination — a common failure point on cheaper paddles.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. The weight distribution runs head-heavy, which is part of why it generates so much power — but it means slower hand speed at the net. If you're a quick-hands player who relies on fast resets and dink battles, this paddle will work against you.

The price is the other barrier. This is a premium paddle, and it's priced accordingly. For players at the 3.0 level or below, this is overkill — you'd be better served by a $100–150 paddle that's more forgiving.

Finally, the grip. It's tacky and comfortable, but some players have noted it wears down faster than expected. Plan on replacing it within a few months of regular play.

## Who Should Buy This

The X Series Power is built for the 3.5+ player who wants to add offensive firepower to their game. If you're a baseline player who likes to drive the ball, if you're working on developing a serve that puts opponents on their heels, or if you're a tournament player who needs a paddle that can keep up with aggressive play — this is worth your money.

Skip it if you're a new player still developing mechanics, or if you prioritize quick hands at the net over raw power.

## FAQ

**Is this paddle USAPA-approved for tournament play?**
Yes. The CRBN X Series is on the USA Pickleball approved paddle list and is legal for tournament competition.

**How does it compare to the CRBN 1 or CRBN 2?**
The X Series Power has a higher swing weight and more pop than the standard CRBN 1. If you want the carbon face with more control and a lighter feel, the 1 is a better fit. The X is for players who want maximum offensive capability.

**Does the textured face affect how the ball comes off?**
Yes, and that's a good thing. The peel-ply texture grabs the ball, giving you more spin on serves and drives. The tradeoff is a slightly different sound and feel — some players love it, and it takes a session or two to get used to.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a 3.5+ player who wants legitimate, playable power without giving up spin and control. The thermoformed construction is legitimately good, and the paddle holds its own against the Joolas and Selkirks of the world at a slightly better price.

**Skip it if:** You're newer to the game, you prioritize net speed over baseline power, or you're not ready to invest in a premium paddle without a cover included.
