If you're chasing tuna, yellowtail, or oversized stripers, you already know the BG name. Daiwa's BG series has been the budget saltwater workhorse for years — the reel that punches above its price point until it simply can't. The 2023 BG SW (23BG SW) is the next generation, and it's not just a paint job. I've been running the 8000 size for three months of pier, jetty, and kayak fishing. Here's the real story.

**What's in the box:** The reel itself, a right-hand retrieve handle (no left-hand option on most sizes — check that before buying), and a reel bag that's actually decent. No spare spool, no tools. Daiwa keeps the packaging minimal, which is fine because you're paying for the machinery, not the box.

**Build and performance:** The headline feature is the Air Metal rotor and Zaion V body — that's the same carbon composite used in the much pricier Zillion line. It's stiff, light, and doesn't flex when you're cranking against a fish that's decided it doesn't want to come in. The 8000 size weighs in around 21 ounces, which is reasonable for a reel that can hold 300 yards of 50-pound braid.

The ATD (Automatic Tournament Drag) is smooth — genuinely smooth. I set the drag at 15 pounds for schoolie tuna and it never stuttered, even when a fish made a sudden run. The carbon drag washers handle heat well; after a 20-minute fight with a 40-pound yellowfin, the drag was warm but not smoking, and the retrieve stayed buttery.

What buyers consistently mention in the 225 Amazon ratings is the sealing. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"I've dunked this reel in the surf more times than I care to admit, and it's still spinning like day one."* The Magsealed line roller and main shaft are the key upgrades over the old BG. That said, this is not a fully sealed reel — the body itself isn't Magsealed. If you plan to kayak and flip the reel in the water regularly, you'll want something from the Saltist or Certate line instead.

**Where it falls short:** The handle knob. It's smaller than I'd like — with cold, wet hands or gloves, it's awkward to grip firmly. It's a minor annoyance, but when you're fighting a fish at dusk, it matters. Also, the size range is a trap. The 4000 and 5000 are light-duty inshore reels (perfect for flounder, redfish, small stripers). The 8000, 10000, and 18000 are heavy offshore machines. There's no real middle ground — if you want a do-everything reel, the 6000 (which doesn't exist in this lineup) would be ideal. Pick your size carefully.

**Who should buy this:**

- **The surf caster** who wants a reliable 4000-6000 size that won't corrode after one season
- **The kayak angler** targeting stripers or redfish who needs a sealed-enough reel that can take a splash
- **The budget-conscious offshore angler** who wants 80% of a Saltist's performance for 60% of the price

Skip it if you're a hardcore kayak angler who regularly flips your reel upside down in the water — get a fully sealed model. Or if you need a left-hand retrieve — Daiwa's left-hand options in the BG SW lineup are limited and often out of stock.

**FAQ:**

*Is the 23BG SW worth the price over the older BG?* Yes, if you fish saltwater regularly. The Magsealed line roller and main shaft are the difference between a reel that lasts three seasons and one that lasts ten. Freshwater-only anglers won't notice the difference — save the money.

*Which size should I get?* Match it to your target. 4000-5000 for inshore (stripers, redfish, flounder). 8000-10000 for schoolie tuna, yellowtail, and jigging. 18000 only if you're serious about big tuna or shark fishing from a boat.

*Can I use this reel for freshwater?* Absolutely, but it's overkill. The heavier build and saltwater sealing add weight you don't need for bass or trout.

**The Verdict:**

The 23BG SW is the best value in Daiwa's saltwater lineup right now. It's not perfect — the handle knob is mediocre and it's not fully sealed — but at this price point, nothing else offers this level of drag smoothness and corrosion resistance.

**Buy it if** you want a dependable saltwater reel that won't break the bank and you're willing to rinse it after each trip.
**Skip it if** you need a fully submersible reel or left-hand retrieve.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuine upgrade over the old BG, with only minor ergonomic gripes keeping it from a perfect score.
