If you've ever gripped a premium tour ball and thought, "I'm not good enough for this," the Callaway Supersoft is the ball that says otherwise. It's the best-selling soft feel ball on the market for a reason, and the 2021 version refined the formula that made the original a cult favorite.

**What You Actually Get**

The 2021 Supersoft comes in a standard 12-pack. You're getting a two-piece ball with a 38-compression core — that's dramatically softer than the 90+ compression you'll find in tour-level balls like the Pro V1. The cover is Callaway's proprietary Trionomer material, which is their softer alternative to urethane. As shown in the product image, the hex aerodynamic pattern covers the entire surface, with dimples designed to reduce drag and promote a higher launch.

**Performance and Feel**

Here's where the Supersoft earns its reputation. Off the driver, the ball launches high with noticeably low spin. For a mid-handicapper who fights a slice, that's a game-changer. The low spin rate means less side spin, which translates to straighter shots. You won't get the same workability as a tour ball, but you'll find more fairways.

The feel is the headline feature. On putts and chips, the Supersoft feels almost marshmallow-like — but in a controlled way. You get a muted, soft response that gives you confidence on delicate shots around the green. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "These balls feel amazing off the putter. I can finally gauge my lag putts." Another noted, "First time I've played a full round without losing a ball. They fly straight and feel great."

The durability surprised me. Soft balls often scuff after one cart-path encounter, but the Trionomer cover held up well. Most buyers report getting multiple rounds out of a single ball, which makes the price point even more attractive.

**Where It Falls Short**

This isn't a ball for everyone. If your driver swing speed is over 100 mph, the Supersoft will feel like a marshmallow off the clubface. You'll lose distance because the ball compresses too much, and you'll see excess spin that can balloon your shots. Low handicappers who like to shape shots will find the Supersoft too one-dimensional — it goes straight, period. That's the tradeoff for forgiveness.

The color options are also a minor frustration. The white balls are almost always in stock, but the yellow and orange versions sell out quickly. If you're a fan of high-visibility balls, you'll need to act fast when they're available.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Mid-to-high handicappers (15-25):** You'll gain distance and straighter flight immediately
- **Seniors and players with moderate swing speeds:** The soft compression maximizes distance for slower swings
- **Beginners:** The forgiveness and soft feel build confidence without the premium price tag
- **Anyone who prioritizes feel over spin:** If you want to feel your putts, this is your ball

**Skip It If** — You're a low handicapper needing spin control around greens, or you swing over 100 mph with the driver.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these actually go further than a tour ball?**
A: For most amateur swing speeds (under 95 mph), yes. The low compression and high launch angle maximize distance for slower swings. For players with faster swings, a firmer ball will go further.

**Q: Are they good for beginners?**
A: Absolutely. The straight flight and soft feel make the game more enjoyable. You won't lose balls as often, and the price point won't hurt when you do.

**Q: How do they compare to the Wilson Duo Soft?**
A: Both are ultra-soft balls, but the Supersoft has a slightly firmer feel and better durability. The hexagonal dimple pattern also gives it a more penetrating flight. Worth the small price premium.

**The Verdict**

The Callaway Supersoft 2021 delivers exactly what it promises: soft feel, straight flight, and forgiveness. It's not a tour ball, and it doesn't pretend to be. But for the vast majority of golfers — especially those who play for fun and shoot in the 80s or 90s — this is the best ball you can buy for the money.

**Buy it if** you want straighter shots, softer feel, and a ball that'll last more than one round. **Skip it if** you're a low handicapper needing spin control or a high-speed swinger chasing distance.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting points only because it's not suited for elite players. For everyone else, it's a hole-in-one.
