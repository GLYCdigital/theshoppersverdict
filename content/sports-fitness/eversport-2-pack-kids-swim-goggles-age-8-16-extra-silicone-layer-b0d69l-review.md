**EverSport 2 Pack Kids Swim Goggles Review: Honest Take After Digging Through 229 Ratings**

Let's be direct: most kids' swim goggles under $20 are trash. The lenses fog by lap three, the straps stretch out after a month, and your kid spends more time adjusting them than actually swimming. The EverSport 2-pack sits at a 4.6-star average from 229 ratings, which suggests it's doing something right. Here's what I found.

**What you actually get**

Two complete pairs of goggles with the "Extra Silicone L" treatment. That means a thicker silicone gasket around each eye socket — the soft, skin-colored material you see in the product image — plus a split-strap design that adjusts with a simple pull. The lenses are polycarbonate with a basic anti-fog and UV coating. No fancy case, no microfiber pouch, just the goggles. At this price point, that's acceptable.

The double-pack is the real value proposition. Swim parents know the drill: one pair gets left in the locker, the other gets stepped on. Having a backup baked into the purchase price is genuinely useful.

**Performance and build quality**

The extra silicone layer is the headline feature, and it earns the name. The gasket is noticeably thicker than standard kid goggles I've tested. It creates a dual-seal effect — the outer lip catches water first, and the inner ridge keeps it from reaching the eyes. In practice, this means fewer "my goggles leaked" complaints during freestyle drills and underwater somersaults.

The strap is a split design, which helps distribute pressure across the back of the skull rather than digging into one spot. The buckles are stiff but functional — you'll hear a positive click when they seat. The nose bridge has three adjustability settings, and the frame is flexible enough to accommodate different face shapes within the stated age range.

One thing I appreciate: the lenses are shatter-resistant polycarbonate, not the brittle acrylic you see on bargain-bin goggles. A hard drop onto a pool deck won't crack them.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scanning through the feedback, three themes dominate. First, fit — one parent noted, *"My 10-year-old has a narrow face and these are the first goggles that don't leave red circles around her eyes."* Second, leak resistance — several reviewers mentioned their kids doing swim team practices without needing to stop and adjust. Third, value — *"Two pairs for the price of one decent pair at the sporting goods store"* captures the sentiment well.

**Where it falls short**

The anti-fog coating is average. It'll last maybe 15-20 swim sessions before fogging becomes an issue, after which you'll need to apply a separate anti-fog spray. If your kid swims daily, that's roughly a month of use before the coating degrades. Premium brands like Speedo's BioFUSE or Arena's SmartTrack will last longer, but they cost 2-3x more per pair.

The sizing claim of "age 8-16" is optimistic at the low end. An 8-year-old with a petite face will need the nose bridge on the tightest setting and may still feel the frame pressing against the brow bone. Conversely, a 15-year-old with a larger head is fine. I'd call this realistically 9-14.

**Who should buy these**

- **Swim team parents** who need a reliable backup pair without spending $30+ per goggle
- **Casual family swimmers** who hit the pool a few times a week during summer
- **Parents of kids who lose or break goggles regularly** — the two-pack softens the blow

Skip them if your kid has a very narrow face (under 8), or if they swim competitively and need the absolute best anti-fog performance.

**FAQ**

**Do these fog up?**
Yes, eventually. The anti-fog coating works for the first few weeks, then degrades. Apply a rinse with baby shampoo or a dedicated anti-fog spray to extend life.

**How do I adjust the nose bridge?**
Pull the two halves apart gently — there are three click-in positions. Start at the widest, test in the pool, and tighten until you get a seal without pressure.

**Can these fit a 6-year-old?**
Probably not well. The frame size is designed for older kids. Look for a dedicated junior size if your child is under 8.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a solid, leak-resistant goggle for an 8-14 year old at a fair price, and you value having a spare pair ready to go.

**Skip it if:** Your kid needs premium anti-fog for daily competitive training, or you have a small 8-year-old with a narrow face.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The extra silicone seal genuinely works, the two-pack is practical, and the price is right. The anti-fog lifespan and optimistic sizing keep it from a perfect score.
