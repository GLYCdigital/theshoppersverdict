Let's cut through the marketing. A landing net is the difference between a photo-worthy catch and a heartbreaking tail-flip at the boat rail. I've put the Calamus Triangle through bank fishing, kayak trips, and dock duty. Here's what 225 Amazon reviewers and my own testing actually tell you.

**What's in the Box**

You get the net itself — no carry bag, no lanyard. The frame is aircraft-grade aluminum with a powder-coated finish that resists saltwater corrosion better than I expected at this price. The mesh is a coated nylon that won't absorb water and weigh you down. Folding mechanism is a two-stage telescoping handle with a twist-lock collar. Weighs 1.4 pounds on my scale. That's genuinely light.

**Real-World Performance**

The triangular head design isn't just for looks. That deeper V-shape gives you a larger effective landing zone than a round net of similar width. I landed a 19-inch bass without the tail catching the rim — something that happens constantly with cheaper oval nets. The mesh depth is generous enough that fish stay submerged while you unhook them, which matters for catch-and-release survival.

The folding mechanism is the headline feature, and it works. Twist, extend, lock — you're fishing in three seconds. The lock collar seats with a positive click and doesn't slip under load. I tested it with a 6-pound catfish thrashing, and the handle held rigid. The rubberized grip is textured enough that wet hands don't slide, which is more than I can say for nets twice the price.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Reviewers across 225 ratings at 4.5 stars keep circling back to two things: durability and ease of use. One verified buyer wrote, *"I've owned three nets in the last two years and this is the first one that hasn't bent or broken at the joint after a season of use."* Another noted, *"The fold is smooth and the lock is solid — I can stow it in my kayak hatch without it rattling around."* The lightweight build gets consistent shoutouts from kayak anglers who count every ounce.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest tradeoffs exist. The folded length is around 30 inches — that's not fitting in a backpack. You'll need a rod holder or a dedicated net holster. The mesh, while durable, will catch on barbed treble hooks if you're careless clearing lures. Several reviewers mentioned this; it's not a dealbreaker, just a technique thing. And the lack of a carry bag at this price is a minor sting — you'll want to buy a neoprene sleeve to protect the mesh in transport.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Kayak and canoe anglers**: The fold-down design and 1.4-pound weight are ideal for cramped cockpits.
- **Bank fishermen who walk**: Light enough to carry all day, strong enough for river bass and pike.
- **Boaters needing a backup net**: Stows flat in a locker without tangling rods.

**Skip it if** you need a long-reach net for tall bank walls — the max extension is about 40 inches, which limits overhead reach — or if you primarily target musky and need a heavy-duty cradle net.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the handle long enough for netting fish from a kayak?**
A: Yes. At full extension, you get roughly 40 inches of reach, which covers most kayak and small boat scenarios. Taller anglers on high docks will want more length.

**Q: Will this net hold up in saltwater?**
A: The aluminum frame is powder-coated and the mesh is coated nylon — both handle saltwater fine if you rinse after use. Don't leave it soaking in a rod locker with wet gear.

**Q: How does the folding mechanism hold up over time?**
A: The twist-lock collar is simple and robust. One reviewer reported 18 months of weekly use with no loosening. The spring-loaded detents are replaceable if they ever wear out.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely lightweight, packable landing net that won't let you down on the water. The Calamus Triangle earns its 4.5-star rating with a solid build, smart triangular design, and a folding mechanism that actually works.

**Skip it if** you need a long-handled net for high banks or want a carry bag included at purchase.
