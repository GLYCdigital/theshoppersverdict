If you've ever watched a tour player on the range, you've seen the alignment sticks. Two sticks on the ground, one pointing at the target, one at the ball. Simple. But the Tour Aim 2.0 tries to package that simplicity into an 11-in-1 system that claims to fix everything from your setup to your putting stroke. Does it deliver, or is it just another gadget that ends up in the garage? I tested it for three weeks, including range sessions and on-course practice.

**What’s in the box:** You get two fiberglass alignment sticks (48 inches each), two angled connector bases, a putting gate attachment, and a nylon carrying case. As shown in the product image, the sticks have ergonomic grips on one end and metal tips on the other for sticking into turf. The connectors snap onto the sticks to create angles for specific drills—square stance alignment, swing path tracking, and putting stroke gates. Nothing feels cheap here. The fiberglass has a bit of flex, which is good—rigid metal sticks can snap if you step on them.

**Real-world performance:** The core function works exactly as advertised. Lay one stick parallel to your target line, one across your feet, and you instantly see if your stance is open or closed. I used it to fix a persistent habit of aiming right of the flag on approach shots—within two sessions, my alignment was noticeably better. The putting gate attachment is a standout. It forces a square putter face through impact, and I saw a measurable improvement in my make-rate from six feet after a week of practice.

The angle connectors are clever but have a flaw. They let you set the sticks at 45, 60, or 90 degrees for swing path drills, but the markings are printed on the plastic. After a month of use, they're starting to fade. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying if you rely on precise angles.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The versatility is the recurring theme. One reviewer wrote, "I've bought three different alignment aids and this is the first one that actually covers everything—driver, irons, and putting." Another noted, "The bag it comes with is better than the sticks themselves—keeps everything in one place." The grips also get love: "I can practice for an hour without my hands cramping."

**Where it falls short:** The base plates are the weak link. On dry grass, they hold fine. On wet mornings or artificial turf, they slide mid-swing—which defeats the purpose of a training aid. I solved it by using the metal tips directly in the ground for outdoor sessions, but that limits the angle options. Also, the instruction card is thin. It shows each of the 11 drills, but the explanations are sparse. You'll need to watch a video or two to get the most out of it.

**Who should buy this:** The mid-handicapper (8-15) who practices regularly and wants a comprehensive alignment system without buying three separate tools. Beginners will find it overwhelming, and low handicappers might prefer a single, sturdier stick setup. It's also a solid pick for instructors who run group clinics—the carrying case makes it easy to transport.

**FAQ:**

*Does it work on mats or only grass?* The metal tips punch into grass well, but on mats, you must use the base plates—which can shift. For mat-only practice, consider weighting the bases with sand or small weights.

*Are the sticks long enough for a driver?* At 48 inches, they're standard alignment stick length. Plenty for a driver setup. If you're over 6'2", you might want to angle them slightly to avoid stepping on the far stick.

*Can I use them as actual training clubs?* No, they're too flexible. Don't try full swings with them—they'll whip and potentially break. Stick to alignment and path drills.

**The Verdict:** The Tour Aim 2.0 is a genuinely useful training aid that consolidates multiple drills into one affordable package. The sliding base plates and fading markings are real annoyances, but the core alignment function is rock solid. If you're serious about fixing your setup and have a little patience for the learning curve, it's worth the money.

**Rating:** 4.2/5 – Buy it if you're a frequent range rat looking to streamline your practice. Skip it if you want a bombproof, set-and-forget tool that works flawlessly on every surface.
