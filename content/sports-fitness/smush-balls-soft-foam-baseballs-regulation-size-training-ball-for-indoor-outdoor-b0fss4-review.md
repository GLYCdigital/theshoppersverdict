If you've ever watched a kid wing a real baseball through a living room window — or taken a line drive to the shin during backyard catch — you already know why soft training balls exist. The SMUSH BALLS Soft Foam Baseballs aim to solve that problem while keeping the dimensions close enough to the real thing. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, they're clearly doing something right. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The package includes regulation-size baseballs — 9 inches in circumference, right at the 5-ounce mark. That's not an accident. The size and weight mimic a standard baseball closely enough that young players can practice proper grip and throwing mechanics without learning bad habits. The foam is dense, not the squishy craft-store variety. It compresses on impact but rebounds quickly, which matters more than you'd think.

**Real-world performance**

I've tested these in three settings: backyard catch, indoor living room toss, and soft-toss batting practice. The standout quality is the density. These aren't feather-light balls that float on the breeze. They track through the air with decent stability, and the weight gives you honest feedback on your throwing motion. For kids aged 6-12, they're ideal — enough heft to feel like baseball, soft enough that a wild throw to the face is a non-event.

The foam holds up well. After a week of abuse — including being smacked against a brick wall and left in a damp garage — they kept their shape. That's a common failure point for cheap foam balls, which tend to develop permanent dents. These don't. One Amazon reviewer noted, "My son uses these daily for batting practice in the backyard. They've held up for months without cracking or flattening." That matches my experience.

**The praise is consistent**

Buyers repeatedly highlight three things: safety, durability, and the realistic size. Another reviewer wrote, "Finally a soft ball that doesn't feel like a whiffle ball. The weight is perfect for teaching my 8-year-old proper throwing form." The regulation sizing gets mentioned often, especially by parents whose kids are transitioning from plastic bats to real ones.

**Where they fall short**

Let's be direct: these are training aids, not baseball substitutes. If you're an adult taking real cuts in the cage, you'll find them too light. Mishits don't sting the hands the way a real ball does, which means you won't get accurate feedback on barrel contact. The soft surface also scuffs easily — after a few sessions on concrete, they look beat up. Cosmetic, sure, but worth knowing.

Also, the foam has a slight "tacky" feel when new, which some players find grippy and others find odd. It wears off quickly.

**Who should buy these**

- **Parents of young players (ages 5-12)** — This is the sweet spot. Safe for siblings, dogs, and drywall.
- **Coaches running indoor practices** — No gym damage, no injury risk during close-quarters drills.

**Skip them if** you're an adult needing realistic batting practice feedback, or if you want a ball that looks pristine after outdoor use.
