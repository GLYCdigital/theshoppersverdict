Let's cut through the noise: most golf gloves are either expensive leather that dies after three rounds of sweat, or cheap synthetics that stretch out by the turn. The CaddyDaddy Claw PRO and Claw MAX sit in a different lane — they're built for golfers who play in wet conditions, grip the club too hard, or are tired of replacing gloves every month.

I've spent time with both models and dug through the buyer feedback (215 ratings, 4.3 stars) to give you the straight story.

**What's actually in the box**

You get one glove per package. The Claw PRO is the standard-profile option — a close-fitting, all-around glove with a textured synthetic palm. The Claw MAX is the beefier sibling: it adds an extended cuff that covers more of your wrist and extra padding along the palm and fingers. Both use 100% synthetic materials, which is the entire point — no leather to stretch, no leather to rot.

**Real-world performance**

The synthetic palm is the headline feature. Traditional cabretta leather gloves are grippy when dry but turn slippery and soggy in humidity or light rain. The Claw's textured pattern holds up in damp conditions — several reviewers noted they kept their grip through misty morning rounds where leather gloves would've been useless. One buyer put it simply: "Played 18 in steady drizzle. Glove never slipped once."

The durability story is strong too. Multiple reviewers reported getting 20-30 rounds out of a single Claw glove, versus the 5-10 they'd squeeze from leather. Because it's synthetic, it won't shrink when it dries after a sweaty round — a common death sentence for leather gloves left in a hot car.

The Claw MAX's extra padding does genuine work on mishits. If you've got arthritis, tendonitis, or just play a lot of range balls, the vibration dampening is noticeable. It won't fix your slice, but it makes a thin shot sting less.

**Where it falls short**

Sizing is the biggest complaint — and it's consistent. Multiple reviewers warn the gloves run small. One said, "I normally wear a Large in every glove made. This Large felt like a Medium." If you're between sizes, go up. The MAX's thicker padding also eats into your feel on finesse shots around the green — you'll trade a bit of touch for the extra protection. And on 95°F days, the synthetic material traps heat more than leather. Breathable, but not breathable *like leather*.

**Who should buy this**

- **The wet-weather golfer** — Pacific Northwest, UK, or anyone who plays early morning dew rounds. This glove is the answer leather can't provide.
- **The high-handicapper who grips too hard** — you're destroying leather gloves in 5 rounds. The Claw will last you 4-5x longer.
- **Golfers with hand pain** — specifically the Claw MAX. The padding is legit.

Skip it if you're a low-handicapper who demands maximum feel on every shot and plays mostly in dry, mild conditions. Leather still wins there.

**FAQ**

**Q: How does the sizing compare to FootJoy or Titleist gloves?**
A: The Claw runs about half a size small. Most buyers recommend ordering one size up from your usual. If you're between sizes, definitely go larger.

**Q: Can you wash these gloves?**
A: Yes — hand wash with mild soap and air dry. Unlike leather, they won't stiffen or shrink. Just don't machine dry them.

**Q: Is the Claw MAX much hotter than the PRO?**
A: Yes, noticeably. The extra padding and cuff trap more heat. The PRO is the better choice for summer play; the MAX shines in cooler weather or for extra hand protection.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** you're tired of replacing leather gloves every few rounds, you play in damp conditions, or you want extra hand protection without spending premium glove money.

**Skip it if:** you're a feel player who lives in a dry climate and can't stand any bulk between your hand and the grip.

The Claw PRO and MAX aren't perfect — sizing is fiddly, and synthetic will never match leather's pure tactile feedback. But for the price and the durability, they're a smart buy for most recreational golfers. The 4.3-star rating holds up.
