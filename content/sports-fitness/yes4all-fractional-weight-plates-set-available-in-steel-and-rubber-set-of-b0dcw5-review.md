If you've been stuck at the same squat or bench weight for three weeks, fractional plates are the unlock. The Yes4All set gives you 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1 lb increments per pair — enough to break plateaus without jumping 5 lbs and failing rep five. But with a 3.9-star average across 195 ratings, this set has a split personality. Here's the real breakdown.

**What's in the box**

You get four pairs of plates (eight total) in either steel or rubber-coated steel. The steel set is bare metal with a brushed finish; the rubber version wraps each plate in a protective coating. Total weight is roughly 2.5 lbs per pair when you combine all increments — enough to add 5 lbs to your bar over a full cycle. The plates have a standard 1-inch center hole, so they'll fit most Olympic barbells and dumbbell handles.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The steel plates are thin — around 3-4 mm for the lightest pair. That's a genuine advantage: you can stack micro-loading plates alongside your working weight without pushing your collars to the edge. The rubber-coated set is slightly thicker but still manageable.

Accuracy matters more than finish here. Several buyers confirmed the weights are true to spec — a 0.25 lb plate actually weighs 0.25 lb. That's not universal in this category; some budget sets run heavy or light by 10-15%. Yes4All got this right.

The rubber coating does two things well: it keeps plates from scratching your bar sleeves and deadens the clank when you set them down. It also reeks. Multiple reviewers noted a strong chemical smell that lingers for days. If you train in a small apartment or garage with poor ventilation, plan to air them out before your first session.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The most common positive thread in reviews is that these plates deliver exactly what they promise for the price. One reviewer wrote, "These are perfect for my home gym. I was stuck at 185 on bench for three weeks; adding 0.5 lbs per session got me to 205." Another noted, "The steel ones are slimmer than I expected, which is great because my barbell sleeves are short."

The value angle is real — you're paying less than most competitors for the same increment range, and the steel option undercuts rubber-coated sets from Rogue or Titan by a wide margin.

**Where it falls short**

The rubber smell is the headline con. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real inconvenience. One reviewer described it as "overpowering plastic smell that made my whole garage reek for a week."

Fit tolerances are inconsistent. Some buyers report the center hole is slightly undersized, making plates a struggle to slide onto the bar — especially if you have a knurled sleeve. Others say they're loose enough to wobble. That variability is frustrating when you're swapping plates mid-workout.

Steel plates are bare metal, so they're prone to surface rust if you train in a humid basement or garage without climate control. A light coat of 3-in-1 oil or keeping them in a sealed bin mitigates this, but it's maintenance you shouldn't need at this price point.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home gym lifters stuck at a plateau** — if you're grinding out the same weight and need 0.25-1 lb jumps, this is the cheapest reliable fix.
- **Programmers running linear progression** — these plates let you add small loads weekly without skipping ahead.
- **Lifters with short barbell sleeves** — the steel set's slim profile is a genuine advantage over thicker urethane options.

**Skip it if**

- **You want a premium feel** — the rubber coating and finish aren't Rogue-tier. They're functional, not fancy.
- **You train in a humid space** — the steel set will rust without regular care.
- **You're sensitive to chemical smells** — the rubber version needs off-gassing time.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these fit standard 1-inch dumbbells?**
A: Yes, the 1-inch center hole fits most standard dumbbell handles and Olympic barbells. Some buyers report a tight fit on certain bars, but it's within normal tolerance for this price range.

**Q: Are the weights accurate?**

**Q: Steel or rubber?**
A: Steel if you want the thinnest profile and don't mind occasional rust care. Rubber if you want floor protection and quieter sets — just budget a few days for the smell to fade.

**The Verdict**
