# GRANDUP Basketball Backpack with Ball and Shoes Compartment Review: The Court Bag That Finally Gets It

Let’s be honest about the pre-game ritual. You’re juggling a basketball, a pair of stinky sneakers, a water bottle, and a change of clothes. Most backpacks force you into a compromise — either the ball goes in a mesh side pocket (and flops around) or the shoes go in a plastic bag inside your main compartment (and everything still smells like them). The GRANDUP basketball backpack takes a different approach: it gives each piece of gear its own dedicated home.

As shown in the product image, this isn’t a generic gym duffel pretending to be a backpack. It’s a purpose-built vertical carryall with a drop-down bottom section that swallows a regulation size-7 basketball. The design solves the two biggest complaints gym-goers have with standard backpacks — and it does it without looking like you’re hauling a tactical military pack.

## What You Actually Get

The GRANDUP arrives as a single backpack with a main top compartment, a zippered bottom ball section, a front pocket for accessories, and two mesh side pockets that fit water bottles or a smaller ball. The shoe compartment sits at the back of the main area, separated by a fabric divider with ventilation holes. It’s constructed from a water-repellent polyester that shrugs off light rain and gym floor grime.

Build quality is the first thing you notice. The zippers are chunky and glide smoothly — no catching or jamming, which is rare at this price point. The stitching on the stress points (where the shoulder straps meet the bag) is reinforced, and the base of the ball compartment has a padded bottom so the ball doesn’t slam against the ground when you set the bag down. The straps are padded with a breathable mesh that doesn’t soak through with sweat on a hot day.

## Real-World Performance

I loaded this bag with a size-7 Spalding, a pair of size-11 high-tops, a hoodie, shorts, a t-shirt, and a 32-ounce water bottle. The ball dropped into the bottom compartment with room to spare, and the zipper closed cleanly. The shoes went into their dedicated pocket, and here’s the thing — they didn’t touch anything else. That divider works. After a sweaty session, I tossed the shoes back in without wiping them down, and the ventilation kept the smell contained.

The main compartment is where you need to be strategic. It’s 18 inches tall, but the shoe section eats into that space. A hoodie plus full practice gear fits, but it’s snug. If you’re carrying a packed lunch, a towel, and a change of clothes, you’ll need to roll your gear tightly or leave the hoodie at home. This is a tradeoff of the design — you get the ball and shoe separation, but you sacrifice main-compartment volume.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 240 Amazon ratings (4.8 stars), the recurring theme is that this bag solves a specific problem. One reviewer noted: *“Finally a bag that keeps my ball from rubbing against my clean clothes. ## Where It Falls Short

The main compartment size is the most common criticism. If you’re a serious gym rat who carries a full outfit, a towel, toiletries, and a protein shaker, this bag will feel cramped. It’s better suited for players who travel light — ball, shoes, one change of clothes, water.

There’s also no laptop sleeve. For students who go straight from class to the court, that’s a real miss. You can put a laptop in the main compartment, but it’ll be sandwiched between your gear with no padding. And while the water-repellent fabric handles light rain, it’s not a waterproof bag — don’t leave it out in a downpour.

## Who Should Buy This

**The weekly rec-league player** who drives to the gym and wants to keep the trunk clean. **The high school or college athlete** who bikes or walks to practice and needs both hands free. **The parent** who’s tired of buying a new bag every season because the seams blew out — this one is built to last.

**Skip it if** you’re a minimalist who carries only a ball and keys, or if you need to haul a laptop plus full gym gear in one bag. Look at a dedicated duffel with a separate shoe pocket instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the bottom compartment fit a size-7 basketball?**
A: Yes, comfortably. It’s sized for regulation-size balls (29.5 inches circumference). Volleyballs and soccer balls also fit, but they’ll rattle around a bit more.

**Q: Can the ball compartment be used for something else?**
A: Absolutely. If you’re not carrying a ball, it works as a dirty-clothes section or extra storage for a second pair of shoes. The divider zips, so you have flexibility.

**Q: Is the backpack suitable for everyday carry, not just basketball?**
A: It works as a general gym bag or travel daypack, but the ball compartment is a permanent feature. You can’t remove it, so it takes up space even when empty. For pure EDC, a standard backpack is more practical.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you’re tired of your basketball rolling around your trunk or your shoes ruining your clean clothes. This is a well-built, thoughtfully designed bag that does exactly what it promises — at a price that undercuts most competitors with similar features.

**Skip it if** you need max main-compartment capacity or a laptop sleeve. The GRANDUP makes a deliberate tradeoff: dedicated ball and shoe storage at the expense of general volume. For its intended purpose — getting you and your gear to the court cleanly — it earns a strong recommendation.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a purpose-built solution that respects the problem it solves.
