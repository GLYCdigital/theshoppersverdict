**Is the Fitvids 100 lb set a bargain or a gamble? At $200–$260 (price fluctuates), you're getting five pairs of rubber hex dumbbells plus a rack. That's tempting. But with a 3.4-star average across 170 ratings, the real story is more complicated than the price tag suggests.**

## What you actually get

The box contains five pairs of rubber-coated hex dumbbells — 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 lbs each — plus a black A-frame storage rack. That's 100 lbs total, and it's a complete home gym starter kit for curls, presses, rows, and lateral raises.

The rubber coating is the highlight. It's thick, grippy, and genuinely odorless. One reviewer noted, "I was expecting that nasty chemical smell like my old vinyl set. These have zero odor." The knurling underneath is decent — not aggressive, but enough to hold onto when your palms get sweaty.

## Build quality: the split personality

Here's where the ratings tell two different stories. The majority of positive reviews focus on value. "Can't beat the price for a full set with a rack," one buyer wrote. "I've had mine for 6 months, dropped them on concrete, and they've held up fine."

But the negative reviews cluster around one consistent issue: quality control on the rubber coating and casting. Multiple buyers report chips and cracks appearing within weeks. One detailed review described it: "The 20 lb pair arrived with a visible seam line on the rubber. By week three, chunks were flaking off the hex ends." Another mentioned the 25 lb pair being "noticeably heavier on one side" — a casting irregularity that's rare with machined dumbbells from brands like Yes4All or CAP.

The rack is a mixed bag too. The steel frame is solid once assembled — it holds the full set without swaying. But assembly is the weak point. Several reviewers mention stripped bolts and misaligned holes. One said bluntly: "Took 45 minutes of frustration because the holes didn't line up. I had to re-drill two of them." If you're not handy with tools, budget for that headache.

## Where it falls short

The 25 lb pair is the problem child. The hex heads are oversized compared to standard dumbbells, which makes them awkward for exercises requiring a narrow grip — tricep extensions, skull crushers, or close-grip presses. You'll feel the bulk. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real ergonomic flaw.

Also, this set tops out at 25 lbs per dumbbell. If you're past the beginner phase for lower-body movements like goblet squats or Romanian deadlifts, you'll outgrow it fast. This is a starter set, not a long-term investment for progressive overload.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if** you're building your first home gym on a budget, you're doing primarily upper-body and accessory work, and you don't need to go heavier than 25 lbs per hand. The value is undeniable — you're getting five pairs and a rack for roughly the cost of one pair of adjustable dumbbells.

**Skip it if** you're an experienced lifter who needs heavier loads, you're picky about finish quality, or you hate assembly projects. The casting inconsistencies and rack assembly issues are too frequent to ignore at this rating level.

## FAQ

**Q: Do the dumbbells smell like rubber?**
A: No. The rubber coating is odorless out of the box. This is a common complaint with cheaper sets, but Fitvids got this right.

**Q: Can I drop these on the floor?**
A: The rubber protects your floors from metal-on-floor damage, but the coating can chip on hard impacts. Be careful — several buyers reported chunks cracking off when dropped.

**Q: Is the rack worth it?**
A: Yes, if you get one with aligned holes. It's sturdy steel, not plastic. But if you're not comfortable with basic DIY, consider buying the dumbbells alone and using a shelf.

## The verdict

**Rating: 3.3/5**

The Fitvids 100 lb set is a classic "you get what you pay for" scenario. At its best, it's an unbeatable value for beginners — a complete, functional set with a solid rack. At its worst, you'll deal with chipping rubber, casting quirks, and an aggravating assembly process.

If you're a casual lifter who wants a complete home setup without breaking the bank, this set will probably serve you fine. If you're serious about training and want equipment that'll last a decade, spend the extra money on machined dumbbells from a brand with better quality control. Your call.
