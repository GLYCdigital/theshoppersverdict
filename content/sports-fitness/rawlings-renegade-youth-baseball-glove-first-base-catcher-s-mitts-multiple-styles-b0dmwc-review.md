If your kid just got handed the first base or catcher position, you're staring down a specialized mitt that costs twice what a normal glove does. The Rawlings Renegade youth series is one of the few options that doesn't treat position players like an afterthought. Here's what you're actually getting for the money.

## What's in the box

The Renegade arrives in a plain Rawlings box with the mitt, a hang tag, and nothing else. No ball, no conditioning oil, no break-in tool. That's fine — you'll want to buy a decent glove conditioner separately anyway. The mitt itself is built on a full-grain leather shell with a padded palm liner and a basket web on the first base version. The catcher's mitt uses a conventional closed web with extra heel padding.

Sizing runs 11.5 inches for the first base mitt and 32.5 inches for the catcher's version, which puts both squarely in the 9-12 year old range. If your kid is on the smaller side of that spectrum, the adjustable Velcro strap matters more than the listed size — it cinches down tight enough to keep the mitt from spinning on a hand that's still growing.

## Build quality and real-world performance

Rawlings spec'd this with their "Soft Touch" synthetic lining and a conventional back. The leather is legitimately full-grain, not the bonded split leather you see on budget gloves. That means it holds its shape when a hard grounder hits the pocket, and it doesn't pancake after a season of sitting in a garage between games.

The first base mitt's basket web is the standout feature. It creates a deep pocket that's forgiving on high throws and short hops. A 10-year-old who's still learning to scoop will appreciate the margin for error. The catcher's mitt, by contrast, is stiffer at the hinge. Young catchers will need to work the glove open and closed repeatedly before it moves naturally.

Break-in is the real tradeoff here. Multiple buyers note it takes dedicated work — glove oil, a mallet, and repetitive closing — for two to three weeks before the mitt feels game-ready. That's not a flaw; it's the price of using real leather instead of synthetic mesh. But if you're buying this the night before the first game, you're going to have a frustrated kid.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings holds up when you read the actual feedback. Parents repeatedly mention that the mitt holds up better than expected for the price point. One reviewer noted their son "uses it daily in practice and games, and it's still in great shape after a full spring season." Another called out the wrist strap specifically, saying it "keeps the glove secure on small hands without needing to add padding."

The basket web gets consistent love from first basemen's parents, with one describing it as "a vacuum for bad throws." That's the kind of specific, experience-based feedback that matters more than any spec sheet.

## Where it falls short

Honest downsides: the catcher's mitt version is genuinely stiff. If your kid is under 10 or has average hand strength, they'll fight the glove for the first month. Also, the synthetic lining doesn't breathe as well as premium leather interiors — expect sweaty hands in summer tournaments.

There's also a color consistency issue. The tan version looks great in product photos, but some buyers report the leather arriving lighter or darker than expected. Cosmetic, not functional, but worth knowing.

## Who should buy this

- **The 9-12 year old first baseman** who needs a real mitt, not a converted outfielder's glove. The basket web and deep pocket are worth it.
- **The rec-league catcher** who plays once or twice a week and needs something that won't fall apart by July.
- **Parents who want one mitt to last** two seasons minimum. The full-grain leather will outlast the growth spurt.

Skip it if your kid is under 8 — the stiffness will frustrate them. Skip it if you need something game-ready this weekend without break-in time.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does the break-in actually take?**
A: Plan on 10-15 sessions of 15-20 minutes each. Use glove oil, a mallet, and have your kid close it around a ball while watching TV. Two to three weeks of daily work gets it game-ready.

**Q: Is this mitt too big for a 9-year-old?**
A: The 11.5-inch first base mitt fits most 9-12 year olds. The adjustable Velcro strap helps smaller hands. For the catcher's mitt, older or stronger kids will handle the stiffness better.

**Q: Does it come with a ball or conditioning oil?**
A: No. You'll need to buy glove oil separately. Rawlings Glovolium works fine and costs about $8.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** your young infielder or catcher needs a real position-specific mitt that will survive a season or two of actual use. The full-grain leather and deep pocket justify the price over cheaper synthetic options.

**Skip it if** you need a glove ready for tomorrow's game, or if your kid is under 9 and won't have the hand strength to manage the break-in period.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A solid, honest position-specific mitt that rewards patience with durability. Just budget for the break-in time.
