Let's cut through the noise: most folding mats under $100 are glorified yoga mats with extra foam. The RitFit Upgraded Folding Exercise Mat is not that. At 2 inches thick with a dense cross-linked foam core, this is the mat you buy when your tailbone and wrists are begging for mercy after burpees on a hardwood floor.

## What You Actually Get

The mat measures 6 feet long by 3 feet wide — enough room for a 5'10" adult to stretch out fully without hanging off the edge. It arrives folded into thirds, and the fold lines are the weak point on many competitors. RitFit addressed this with a hinged design that lays flat within a minute of unboxing; the creases soften noticeably after a few sessions. Two integrated carry handles on the outer panels make transport manageable, though at 15 pounds this isn't a grab-and-go accessory.

The covering is a textured PVC outer layer that grips both your skin and your floor. On hardwood and tile, the mat stays put during dynamic movements — no slide, no creep.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this mat for three weeks across various workouts: HIIT circuits, dumbbell floor work, stretching, and the occasional ab rollouts. The 2-inch thickness is the headline feature, and it delivers. During kneeling exercises, my knees felt the difference immediately — no pressure points, no need to double up with a towel. At 185 pounds, I couldn't bottom out the foam during any movement, including dropping to my knees from a standing position.

The surface texture deserves mention. It's slightly tacky but not sticky — sweaty palms don't slip during planks, but you can still slide your feet for glute bridges. The foam density is also worth noting: it's firm, not squishy. If you want a sink-in-soft surface for meditation or restorative yoga, this isn't it. But for active training, the firmness provides better stability during balance work and prevents joint instability.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The firmness that makes this mat great for strength work makes it less comfortable for lying on your back for extended stretching — you'll feel the floor beneath the foam. The PVC surface can get slippery if you're doing hot yoga (though it's fine for standard workouts). And the 15-pound weight, while manageable, rules out casual portability. This is a home mat, not a throw-it-in-the-car-and-go mat.

## FAQ

**Will the fold lines flatten out?**
Yes, but not instantly. Expect visible creases for the first 3-5 sessions. After two weeks of regular use, they're barely noticeable.

**Can I do high-impact moves like box jumps on it?**
No. This is a floor mat for bodyweight and floor work — it's not designed for landing from height. Stick to exercises where your feet stay below hip height.

**How do I clean it?**
Wipe with a damp cloth and mild soap. Avoid harsh chemicals or abrasive scrubbers, which can damage the PVC surface.
