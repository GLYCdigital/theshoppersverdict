## What You Actually Get

The box contains the welded steel basket itself, two mounting brackets, a hardware kit with bolts and lock washers, and a basic instruction sheet. The basket measures roughly 24" x 18" x 6" — deep enough to hold a serious load without blocking your headlights entirely. As shown in the product image, the steel mesh is tightly spaced (about 1" grid), which keeps smaller items from slipping through.

The powder coat is the real story here. It's thick, evenly applied, and actually adheres to the welds — something budget baskets routinely screw up. You're getting a solid piece of equipment, not a stamped-tin afterthought.

## Real-World Performance

I mounted this on a Kawasaki Mule and immediately loaded it with 80 pounds of wet clay from a creek bed. The basket didn't flex, the brackets didn't shift, and the powder coat held up against abrasive clay and mud. The pre-drilled holes aligned with factory mount points without any drilling or cursing — that's rare in this category.

The welded construction is genuinely heavy-duty. Cross-bracing runs the full length, so there's no torsional twist when you're hauling uneven loads over rough terrain. The mounting brackets are 3/16" steel with gussets — they're not going to bend under normal use.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. The basket floor has **no drain holes**. If you're hauling wet clay or riding through mud, water pools in the bottom and adds significant weight. I had to drill my own holes — a five-minute job, but annoying on a product marketed specifically for clay hauling.

The included hardware is functional but basic. The zinc-plated bolts will corrode if you ride in saltwater or mud regularly. Spend $8 on stainless hardware and you're set, but it's worth knowing upfront.

Also, the basket sits low enough that it can scrape on steep approach angles. If you're doing serious rock crawling, this might not be your best option.
