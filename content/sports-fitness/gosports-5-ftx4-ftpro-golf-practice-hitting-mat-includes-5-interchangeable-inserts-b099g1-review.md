# GoSports 5x4 ftPRO Golf Practice Hitting Mat Review: Five Inserts, Zero Excuses

Most hitting mats under $200 are glorified door mats with a rubber backing. The GoSports 5x4 ftPRO is not that. This is a 5-foot by 4-foot, 2.5-inch-thick practice station built for golfers who actually want to improve—not just beat balls into a net and wonder why their wrist hurts.

The headline feature is obvious from the product image: five interchangeable turf inserts. You get standard fairway lie, tight lie, rough, a tee slot, and a chipping surface. That's the entire short game and full swing covered on one mat. But does the execution match the concept? After digging through 220 ratings and the specs, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main 5x4 foam base, five separate turf inserts, and a set of practice tees. The base is dense, high-density foam—not the flimsy EVA stuff you'll find on cheaper mats. It measures a true 60 inches by 48 inches, giving you about 20 square feet of hitting surface. That's enough room to set up properly and swing without the mental tax of "don't miss the mat."

The inserts lock into cutouts in the base. Each one is a different turf density, which actually changes how the club interacts with the ground. The rough insert grabs the clubhead like real thick grass. The tight lie simulates that hard, baked-out fairway feel. It's a smart design—when one section wears out, you flip in a fresh one instead of buying a whole new mat.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the GoSports ftPRO earns its 4.4-star rating. The thickness matters. At 2.5 inches, this mat absorbs the blow of steep swings without transferring shock to your elbows and wrists. Multiple reviewers mentioned this exact point—one wrote, "I've hit 500 balls on this and my joints feel fine, unlike my old 1-inch mat that felt like hitting off concrete."

The mat stays put. No sliding, no creeping across your garage floor or patio. The dense foam grips well enough that you won't need to stake it down for normal practice sessions.

The chipping insert is genuinely useful. It's a shorter, denser turf that lets you work on pitch shots and bump-and-runs. For a home practice setup, that's a feature you rarely find at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

The tee holder is the main annoyance. It's a small rubber grommet-style opening, but tees sit at an awkward angle unless you press them in hard. One reviewer complained, "The tee holes are too tight—I have to hammer the tee in, and it still wobbles." It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a design flaw you'll deal with every session.

The inserts can also shift slightly if you don't press them all the way into the base. During a full swing, you'll occasionally feel a tiny movement. Not dangerous, but it breaks your focus.

And at roughly 25 pounds, this is not a travel mat. It's a garage, backyard, or office setup. If you want something to throw in the trunk for the range, look at a lighter option.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The home practicer:** If you have a net or simulator setup, this mat is the foundation you want. The interchangeable inserts mean it lasts 3-4x longer than fixed-turf mats.
- **The elbow-sensitive golfer:** The foam base genuinely protects joints. If you've had wrist or elbow issues, this is worth the investment.
- **The short-game obsessive:** The chipping insert alone justifies the price for players focused on scoring inside 50 yards.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this mat damage my garage floor?**
No. The foam base is non-marring and won't scuff concrete or epoxy floors. It also doesn't shift during swings.

**Q: Can I use real tees with the tee insert?**
Yes, but the holes are tight. Press firmly and they'll seat properly. Some users recommend using a screwdriver to widen the holes slightly—it's a 2-minute fix.

**Q: Does the mat hold up outdoors in direct sun?**
The turf is UV-resistant, but the foam base can degrade with prolonged sun exposure. If you're leaving it outside, cover it or store it after sessions.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a durable, joint-friendly practice mat with realistic lie variety and don't mind the slight tee holder annoyance.

**Skip it if:** You need a lightweight, portable mat for travel, or you're on a strict budget—this is an investment piece.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The GoSports 5x4 ftPRO is one of the best-value hitting mats in its class. The interchangeable insert system is genuinely innovative, the build quality is solid, and it holds up to real use. The tee holder quirk and occasional insert shift keep it from perfection, but for most golfers, this is the last practice mat you'll need to buy.
