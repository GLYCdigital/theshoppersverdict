## What’s Actually in the Box

You get a **12x15-inch aluminum frame** with a glossy black chrome finish, one pre-cut team decal, a clear protective cover (not glass — it’s lightweight acrylic), and a hanging kit with screws and anchors. The frame itself has a nice weight to it — not flimsy, not heavy. The decal is the star: it’s die-cut to match your school’s logo, and the adhesive backing is strong enough to stay put once applied.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The black chrome finish is the selling point here. It looks more expensive than it is — the reflective surface catches light nicely without being gaudy. Unlike cheaper plastic frames, this aluminum body won’t warp or yellow over time. I’ve seen similar products crack at the corners within months; this one feels like it’ll survive a move or two.

Applying the decal takes patience. The instructions are clear: clean the surface, peel slowly, and use a credit card to smooth out air bubbles. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: *“Took me 10 minutes to get it perfect — just go slow and it looks factory-made.”* Another noted, *“The decal is thinner than I expected, but once it’s on, you can’t tell.”* Fair warning: if you mess up and try to peel it off, the adhesive won’t forgive you. Order a backup decal if you’re a perfectionist.

Across the 229 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **Looks premium for the price.** The chrome frame gets constant compliments — one reviewer said, *“People think I paid $50 for this at a team store.”*

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the biggest gripe is the decal system. Unlike a printed insert where you just slide in a paper, this is a permanent adhesive application. If you ever want to change teams (or you bought the wrong school by accident), you’re out of luck — removing the decal will leave residue and likely damage the chrome finish. Also, the acrylic cover scratches easier than glass. One reviewer noted, *“Dusting with a dry cloth left micro-scratches — use a microfiber cloth only.”*

Another minor issue: team availability. Rico covers major programs (Alabama, Ohio State, Michigan, etc.), but if you’re a fan of a smaller Division II school, you’re probably out of luck.
