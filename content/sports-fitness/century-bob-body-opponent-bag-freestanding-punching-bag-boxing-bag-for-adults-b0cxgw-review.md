Let me be direct: the Century BOB is the freestanding bag that every other freestanding bag pretends to be. It's been the gold standard for home striking training for over a decade, and this latest version keeps that reputation intact. But it's not perfect, and you should know exactly what you're getting before you drop the money.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box, you get the BOB head-and-torso unit, the heavy-duty polyurethane base, an adjustable height column, and an air pump. Assembly takes about 20 minutes — you attach the torso to the column, lock in the height, then fill the base. The head and shoulders are molded from a firm but forgiving vinyl that's designed to absorb repeated punishment without breaking down.

The height adjustment is the killer feature here. You can set the striking surface anywhere from 5'3" to 6'3", which means this bag works for everyone from a 5-foot beginner to a 6-foot-plus advanced striker. As shown in the product image, the realistic head-and-shoulder silhouette forces you to aim — something a cylindrical heavy bag never does.

## Performance and Build Quality

This is where BOB separates from the cheap competition. The 300-pound base capacity is no joke. Fill it with sand (water works but sand is better) and this thing does not move. I've seen reviewers throw full-power body hooks and head kicks for hours without the base sliding more than an inch.

The real-world training benefit is target precision. Because BOB has a defined head and torso, you're forced to work angles, slip and counter, and practice accuracy rather than just swinging wild. Fighters and boxing coaches consistently praise it for footwork drills and combination work. One reviewer put it simply: *"It's the closest thing to sparring a partner without getting hit back."*

The construction holds up. The vinyl skin takes thousands of strikes without cracking, and the foam core retains its shape. Multiple long-term owners report using it daily for 2-3 years with zero structural failure.

## Where It Falls Short

The setup is genuinely annoying. The base weighs over 100 pounds empty, and filling it with sand means hauling multiple 50-pound bags or running a hose to it. Plan for a solid hour of setup and possibly a second person.

The neck joint is the weak point. With repeated head strikes, the bolt connecting the head to the torso can loosen. It's an easy fix — just re-tighten with a wrench — but you'll do it more often than you'd like if you're working head shots regularly.

Also, this is not a silent piece of equipment. The thud of strikes resonates through the base. If you live in an apartment, your downstairs neighbors will file a complaint. Basement or garage training only.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a boxer, Muay Thai practitioner, or MMA fighter who wants precision striking practice at home. You have space — this thing needs a 5x5 foot footprint minimum — and you're willing to put in the setup effort.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual fitness user who just wants to throw some punches for cardio. You'll be better served by a cheaper, lighter freestanding bag or a wall-mounted option. And if you're under 5'3", the lowest setting still forces you to reach up slightly.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to use gloves?**
A: Yes, absolutely. The vinyl face will tear if you hit it bare-handed. Use 12-16 oz boxing gloves or MMA gloves. This is non-negotiable.

**Q: Sand or water in the base?**
A: Sand. It's heavier and doesn't slosh, so the bag stays more stable. Water works but the base will shift more with hard kicks. Expect to use about 6-7 bags of play sand.

**Q: Can this damage flooring?**
A: The base is smooth rubber and won't scratch most floors, but the weight can leave indentations in softer surfaces like laminate or hardwood. Put a rubber mat underneath to be safe.

## The Verdict

**4.6/5 — Buy it if you're serious about striking.** This is the best freestanding bag on the market for realistic target practice, and it's built to last years. The setup hassle and neck joint maintenance are real tradeoffs, but they're minor against the training value. If you're committed to improving your striking at home, BOB is worth every penny.
