**The Problem with Most Wrist Weights: They Slide.** Here's why the Fragraim set actually stays put.

Most wrist weights on Amazon are basically sand-filled tubes with velcro. You strap them on, start walking, and within five minutes they've migrated toward your elbow. The Fragraim Wrist Weights solve this with a simple but effective addition: a **thumb loop** that anchors the weight to your hand. It sounds minor, but it changes the entire experience.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes two 1lb weights (one per wrist), each with a neoprene shell, an adjustable velcro strap, and that signature thumb loop. The build quality is better than the price suggests — the stitching is reinforced at stress points, and the neoprene has a smooth outer finish that won't snag on workout clothes. As shown in the product image, the thumb loop is integrated into the strap rather than being a flimsy afterthought. You also get a small mesh storage pouch, which is more useful than you'd think for keeping the velcro from grabbing everything in your gym bag.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

I tested these during 30-minute brisk walks, light jogging, and arm-focused cardio routines. The thumb loop does what it claims — the weights stay locked in position even when you're swinging your arms aggressively. The adjustable strap cinches down securely, and the soft inner lining prevents the chafing that plagues cheaper neoprene weights.

At 1lb per wrist, these are **not** for strength training. You won't build muscle with these. What they excel at is adding resistance to cardio — increasing calorie burn, engaging your core during walks, and adding challenge to arm movements. For physical therapy or post-injury rehab, the light weight is actually ideal.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest downsides: the velcro is strong — almost too strong. It grabs onto fleece, knits, and any looped fabric. Keep them in the pouch when not in use. Also, if you're hoping to progress your resistance, you'll need to buy a separate, heavier set. There's no way to add weight to these. And while the thumb loop is great for most activities, some users doing yoga or floor work found it slightly restrictive during palm-down positions.
