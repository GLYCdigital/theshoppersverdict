## Where the PlayBolt Set Stumbles

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**Inflation is a chore.** You'll spend five solid minutes per ball with a hand pump. If you don't own a pump with a ball needle, factor that $8-10 purchase into your total cost. Some reviewers noted that using a compressor or gas station air hose can overinflate quickly — there's no pressure gauge on the valve.

**The leak risk is real if you're careless.** Overinflate past 3 PSI and the valve stem can deform, causing a slow leak that leaves you with a saggy, lopsided ball within days. One reviewer complained, *"Two of the six lost air within a week. I think I overinflated them, but there's no way to tell without a separate gauge."*

**No storage solution.** The balls don't come with a mesh bag or carry case. If you need to keep them organized between uses, you're buying a separate bag or bin.

## Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Do these need a special pump?**
A: Any standard ball pump with a needle attachment works. The valves are universal size. Don't use a compressor without a pressure regulator — you'll blow past the safe PSI range instantly.

**Q: How long do they hold air?**
A: Properly inflated to 2-3 PSI, expect to top off every 1-2 weeks with regular use. If a ball goes flat within days, you've either overinflated it or have a defective valve — contact PlayBolt customer service.

**Q: Are these safe for indoor use?**
A: They'll work on hardwood or tile, but they'll scuff floors and knock over anything fragile. The bounce is strong enough to clear a coffee table. Outdoor use is where they shine.
