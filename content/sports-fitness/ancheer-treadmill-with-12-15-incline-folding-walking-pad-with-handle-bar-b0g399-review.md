## What You Actually Get

The box contains the assembled treadmill frame, the handle bar (which you attach with four bolts — about 10 minutes of work), a safety key, and a lubrication bottle. The folding mechanism is genuinely well-executed: the deck lifts and locks vertically, and the transport wheels let you roll it to a closet or under a bed. As shown in the product image, the handle bar version gives you something to grip during incline work — a meaningful upgrade over the bar-less models in this price band.

The walking surface measures roughly 40" x 16". That's compact, which is the tradeoff for the fold-flat design. Your stride length matters here — if you're over 6 feet, you'll want to test the deck length before committing.

---

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Doesn't

The headline feature is the incline: 12% and 15% options. That's not a gimmick. At 12% you're doing serious glute and hamstring work; at 15% you're essentially doing hill repeats. Most sub-$500 treadmills cap at 8% or skip incline entirely. This is the ANCHEER's strongest argument.

Speed tops out at around 4 mph in walking mode and slightly higher in jogging mode — but don't buy this expecting interval sprint training. The motor is rated for walking and light jogging, and pushing it beyond that will strain the belt drive. One buyer noted: *"The incline at 15% makes me sweat like I ran a 5K, but I can't run on it — it's a walking machine."* That's the honest truth.

The belt is adequately cushioned for walking, though it's not plush like a $2,000 NordicTrack. At 15% incline, the deck stays stable — no wobble, no creaking. The motor handles the incline transitions smoothly, which is impressive at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest with yourself before buying:

- **Speed ceiling.** If you're a runner, this is not your machine. It's a walking pad with an incline, not a running treadmill.
- **Deck width.** At 16 inches, it's functional but not spacious. People with wider stances may feel constrained.
- **No Bluetooth/app integration.** You get a basic LED display showing speed, time, distance, and incline. That's it. If you want synced workouts, look elsewhere.
- **Assembly is minimal but real.** The handle bar attachment requires a Phillips head screwdriver and some patience. It's not hard, but it's not zero-effort.
