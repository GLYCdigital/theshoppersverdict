## What's in the Box

You get two identical dip bar arms, each with a drop-in bracket that slides over your rack's uprights. The key spec here is compatibility: they fit 2"x2" and 3"x3" posts with 5/8" or 1" hardware holes. The mounting system uses a spring-loaded pull pin that locks into your existing rack holes—no wrenches, no bolts, no fiddling. The grips themselves are 30mm diameter steel with a medium knurl. That's roughly the same thickness as a standard Olympic barbell, which is good for wrist comfort during weighted dips.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The steel is powder-coated matte black, and the welds look clean—no slag, no rough edges. The UHMW (ultra-high molecular weight) plastic inserts on the bracket are the standout design choice. They do two things: they prevent metal-on-metal contact with your rack's finish, and they make installation glide smoothly. I've seen cheaper attachments that rely on rubber pads that peel off after six months. This won't happen here.

In use, the dip bars feel rock-solid. I mounted them on a 3"x3" REP rack with 5/8" holes, and the pull pin seated with a satisfying click. During weighted dips with a 45-pound plate hanging from a belt, there was zero lateral wobble. The knurling is aggressive enough to grip but not so sharp that it shreds your hands—think medium-grade, like a Rogue Ohio bar.

One thing to note: the grip width is fixed. The arms angle slightly inward, which works well for most lifters, but if you have unusually wide shoulders or prefer a neutral-width dip stance, you'll feel a bit cramped. There's no adjustment mechanism.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, there are no rubber end caps on the grip tips. If you leave the bars installed and walk past the rack, the open steel ends are at shin height. That's a bruise waiting to happen. Second, the fixed width is a real limitation. If you're 6'2" or taller, you might find the stance narrower than you'd like. Third, the mounting bracket is designed for vertical posts—if your rack has angled uprights (some budget racks do), the fit could be loose.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my rack?**
Check your upright dimensions and hole size. They work on 2"x2" or 3"x3" posts with 5/8" or 1" holes. If your rack uses 2"x3" posts (some older models), these won't fit.

**Q: Can I use these for muscle-ups or kipping?**
Technically yes, but the fixed width and lack of end caps make them risky for dynamic movements. Stick to strict dips.

**Q: How much weight can they hold?**
The steel construction handles 300+ pounds without issue, based on user reports. The limiting factor is your rack's weight rating, not these bars.
