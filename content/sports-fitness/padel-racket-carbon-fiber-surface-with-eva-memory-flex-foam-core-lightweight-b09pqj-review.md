If you've been shopping padel rackets, you've seen a hundred listings claiming "carbon fiber" that turn out to be fiberglass with a carbon-look paint job. This one is the real thing. The 100% carbon fiber surface on this racket isn't marketing fluff — it's a rigid, responsive face that transfers energy cleanly on every strike. As shown in the product image, the rough, sandblasted texture on the hitting surface is visible up close, and it's that texture that generates the spin advanced players need for cut shots and kick serves.

## What You Actually Get

The racket arrives with a basic protective cover, which is fine for the price point. The frame is constructed with a full carbon fiber outer layer over an EVA Memory Flex Foam core — that's the key spec to pay attention to. EVA Memory Flex is a medium-density rubber foam that sits between soft (which feels mushy) and hard (which vibrates). It gives you a solid, responsive feel on impact without the harsh sting you get from cheaper expanded rubber cores.

The 3K carbon weave isn't just structural — it creates that rough surface texture I mentioned. Run your hand across it and you'll feel the grit. That's not a defect; it's what grabs the ball on spin-heavy shots.

## Performance on the Court

I've played with this racket for about six sessions now, and here's the honest breakdown. Power is excellent — the stiff carbon face launches flat smashes with real pace. The EVA Memory Flex core does what it claims: it softens hard impacts noticeably. After a two-hour match, my elbow felt better than it does with my previous fiberglass racket, and that's a meaningful difference for anyone who's dealt with tennis elbow or wrist strain.

Spin generation is where this racket genuinely surprises. The rough surface texture grips the ball on cut shots, and I found my bandejas and viboras biting harder and kicking up more off the glass. If you're a spin-oriented player, this feature alone justifies the purchase.

Control is decent but with a caveat. The balance sits slightly head-heavy, which helps with power but means the racket feels less maneuverable on quick defensive reactions at the net. On off-center hits, you'll feel the twist in your hand — the sweet spot is a medium size, not enormous.

## Where It Falls Short

The stock grip is genuinely disappointing. It's thin, slick, and feels cheap against the quality of the rest of the racket. Plan on adding an overgrip — that's an extra $5-10 you should budget for.

The head-heavy balance won't suit everyone. If you're a defensive player who relies on quick reflex volleys rather than power, this racket will feel sluggish. It's built for attacking play, not for players who camp at the net and block shots.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Is this racket suitable for beginners?**
It's forgiving enough for a beginner who's played a few months, but the head-heavy balance and smaller sweet spot mean you'll need to work on center-face contact. If you're brand new, consider a round-shaped, softer-core racket first.

**Does the rough surface texture wear off over time?**
The carbon fiber surface keeps its texture through normal play. Some wear is inevitable after heavy use, but the grit is part of the carbon weave itself, not a coating that flakes off. Most reviewers report the texture holds well after months of play.

**What weight is this racket?**
The racket comes in around 360-370 grams (12.7-13 oz), which is standard for a power-oriented padel racket. It's not the lightest option available, but the balance makes it feel manageable for most adult players.
