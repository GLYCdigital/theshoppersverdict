## The Mallet That Rewards the Miss

Most golfers don't miss the putter they *want* to hit — they miss the putter they're *currently* rolling. The TaylorMade TP Putter Palisades is built for exactly that reality. This is a high-MOI mallet that doesn't just look forgiving; it's engineered to keep the ball on line when your stroke isn't perfect. With 240 Amazon ratings holding a 4.8 average, the consensus is clear: this putter performs. But is it the right mallet for *your* game? Let's break down what you're actually getting.

## What's in the Box

TaylorMade keeps the package straightforward. You get the putter, a standard black headcover, and that's it. No alignment stickers, no adjustment tools — the Palisades is a set-it-and-forget-it design. The head itself is a compact mallet shape, noticeably smaller than the Spider line, which will appeal to golfers who want forgiveness without the giant footprint behind the ball.

## Performance and Feel: The Real Story

The Palisades uses TaylorMade's **Pure Roll insert**, and this is where the putter earns its keep. The insert is firm enough to give you clear feedback on contact, yet soft enough to produce that muted, premium "thock" at impact. Multiple reviewers note the ball comes off the face with immediate forward roll rather than skidding — a direct result of the grooves in the insert. One buyer put it simply: *"The ball rolls end over end almost instantly. Distance control on lag putts is the best I've had."*

The high-MOI head design does the heavy lifting on mishits. In testing, toe and heel strikes still produced respectable distance and minimal face twisting. For a mid-to-high handicapper, this is the difference between three-putting and salvaging par. The alignment system is equally well-executed — a single white line and two narrower sight lines frame the ball cleanly at address. As one reviewer noted, *"I've never set up to a putter that made me feel this confident about my aim."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The reviews cluster around three themes. First is **stability** — golfers of all levels report fewer three-putts within the first few rounds. Second is the **feel**, with the Pure Roll insert being called "buttery" and "responsive" in roughly equal measure. Third is the **aesthetic** — the matte black finish with subtle silver accents looks premium at address and doesn't glare in bright sun.

## Where It Falls Short

No putter is perfect, and the Palisades has two notable tradeoffs. The included headcover is functional but underwhelming — it's a basic neoprene-style cover that several reviewers say shows wear within a few months. At this price point, you'd expect a magnetic closure or a sturdier build.

The stock grip is also worth scrutinizing. It's a standard pistol-style grip that suits straight-back-straight-through strokes, but golfers with a strong arc may find themselves fighting it. Plan on a grip swap if you have a pronounced swing path — it's a $15 fix that makes a noticeable difference.

## Who Should Buy This

The Palisades is ideal for:
- **Mid-to-high handicappers** who miss the center of the face more often than they'd like
- **Golfers struggling with alignment** — the sight lines are genuinely confidence-inspiring
- **Players upgrading from a blade** who want forgiveness without a massive mallet head

Skip it if you prefer a firmer, more traditional milled face, or if you're a low handicapper who wants maximum feel feedback on every putt — the insert softens that response somewhat.

## FAQ

**Is the TaylorMade TP Palisades legal for tournament play?**
Yes, it conforms to USGA and R&A rules. The putter has no adjustable features and uses a standard plumber's neck hosel.

**Does the Pure Roll insert wear out over time?**
The insert is durable and designed for years of use. That said, the face can pick up small scuffs from sandy balls — clean it with a soft brush to maintain the roll characteristics.

**Is this putter suitable for left-handed golfers?**
TaylorMade does offer left-handed versions, but availability on Amazon is inconsistent. Check the specific listing for your hand orientation before ordering — don't assume it's in stock.
