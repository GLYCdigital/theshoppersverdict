Let’s be direct: most Swiss bars under $150 are either too flimsy for serious pressing or have grips spaced like a child's toy. The Yes4All Multi Grip Barbell sits in a weird middle ground — it’s not the premium Rogue version, but it’s not junk either. Here’s what 204 buyers at a 4.1 average are actually getting.

## What's in the box

You get the bar itself — that’s it. No collars, no carry bag, no instructional pamphlet. The bar arrives fully assembled, wrapped in foam, with the rotating sleeves already greased. As shown in the product image, the American Bar profile has three grip pairs: wide, medium, and narrow, all positioned at a 15-degree neutral angle. Total weight is around 25 pounds for the 1-inch version (the 2-inch Olympic model comes in closer to 30). The shaft is 47 inches long, so it fits most standard power racks without issue.

## Build and real-world performance

Let’s talk metal. The main shaft is 30mm steel with a medium aggressive knurl — think "gritty but not cheese-grater." It’s a noticeable upgrade over the slick, polished finishes on cheaper multi-grip bars. The handles themselves are 25mm diameter with a lighter knurl. That’s fine for pressing, but if you’re doing heavy Meadows rows or upright pulls, you’ll want chalk.

The rotating sleeves are bushing-based, not bearing-based. They spin adequately for bench press and squats, but they won’t spin like a competition bar during Olympic lifts. For a bar marketed as a bench/row tool, that’s acceptable.

A key design detail: the grip spacing. The narrow grips sit at 12 inches apart, medium at 17, wide at 22. On a 47-inch bar, that wide setting is about as far out as you’d want — it gives you a proper chest-blasting press without the extreme shoulder rotation of a standard straight bar.

## What buyers consistently praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is versatility. One buyer put it plainly: *"I bought this for pressing and ended up using it for rows, pull-ups, and even landmine work. It replaced two pieces of equipment."* That’s the core value — it’s a Swiss bar and a cable attachment in one, so if you have a cable tower, you can clip a carabiner to the center ring and do triceps pushdowns or face pulls without switching tools.

The knurling quality on the main shaft also gets consistent shout-outs. Multiple reviewers noted it’s *"much grippier than expected for the price"* — a fair point, since most budget bars in this range are polished to a mirror finish.

## Where it falls short

Here’s the honest stuff. First, the handle knurling is light — almost passive. If you’re pulling heavy (over 225 pounds), your grip will fatigue before your back does. Plan for straps.

Second, the 1-inch version is a trap for new lifters. It only fits standard 1-inch plates, not the 2-inch Olympic plates most home gyms own. If you already have Olympic plates, spend the extra $15 for the 2-inch version. Don’t buy adapters; they wobble.

Third, weld quality is functional but inconsistent. The Y-joints where handles meet the shaft are solid, but you’ll see minor splatter and uneven grinding in places. It’s cosmetic, not structural — but inspect your unit before loading heavy.

## Who should buy this

- **Home gym owners on a budget** who want pressing variety without dropping $300+ on a Rogue or Kabuki bar.
- **Lifters with shoulder or wrist issues** — the neutral grip is a genuine relief for pressing.
- **Anyone with a cable tower** who wants a multi-purpose attachment.

Skip it if you’re a competitive powerlifter needing a precise, competition-grade Swiss bar, or if you demand flawless welds and deep knurling at every contact point.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my Olympic barbell plates?**
Only if you buy the 2-inch version. The base model takes 1-inch standard plates. Check the listing carefully — the product title says "A" but the dropdown has both sizes.

**Q: Can I use this for bench press safely?**
Yes. The neutral grip reduces shoulder stress and many users report pressing heavier with less joint pain. Use collars on the sleeves — they’re smooth and plates can slide without them.

**Q: Does the knurling wear off with heavy use?**
The main shaft knurling holds up well — it’s cut, not rolled. The handle knurling is shallow but hasn’t worn slick in most long-term reviews. Chalk helps.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, budget-friendly Swiss bar for pressing, rows, and cable work — and you already own standard plates or choose the Olympic version.

**Skip it if** you need competition-grade build quality, or if your grip strength is already a limiting factor in your lifts.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It won’t be the last bar you buy, but it’s a damn good first one.
