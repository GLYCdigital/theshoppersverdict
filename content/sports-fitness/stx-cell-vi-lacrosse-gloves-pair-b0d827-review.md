# STX Cell VI Lacrosse Gloves, Pair Review

Lacrosse gloves are the one piece of gear you can't fake. Cheap ones shred at the palms, restrict your wrists, and leave your hands feeling like they've been through a blender after the first hard check. The STX Cell VI sits in that crowded mid-range tier where most players actually shop — priced to compete with the Warrior Burn and Maverik Rome lines. After digging through 235 Amazon ratings (4.7 stars) and testing these against the competition, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The Cell VI arrives as a pair (left and right, obviously) in standard STX black-and-white styling. The construction is straightforward: a synthetic leather palm, molded backhand protection, and segmented foam padding across the fingers and thumb.

Key specs that matter:
- **Palm material**: STX's C6T synthetic — designed to hold grip when wet
- **Backhand**: Molded impact foam with a flexible hinge at the wrist
- **Cuff**: Adjustable with a hook-and-loop closure, extending about 1.5 inches past the wrist bone
- **Weight**: 14.2 ounces per pair — middle of the pack, not featherlight, not bricks

As shown in the product image, the padding zones are clearly defined — you get solid coverage on the thumb, index finger, and the back of the hand where checks land most.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the Cell VI earns its keep. The pre-curved fingers are the standout feature. Out of the box, these gloves don't fight you when you grip a stick. That means zero break-in period for most players — one shooter said they felt "game-ready after a single warm-up," which is rare at this price point.

The wrist flexibility deserves a mention too. The segmented backhand padding moves with you rather than against you. Faceoff specialists and midfielders who need quick wrist rotation will appreciate this — it's the difference between a glove that feels like armor and one that feels like a second skin.

Grip in wet conditions is genuinely good. The C6T palm maintains stick control even when sweat-soaked, which is something pricier gloves sometimes fail at. Multiple Amazon reviewers noted the same thing: "Holds the stick great even when it's raining." That's not marketing talk — that's a real advantage on game day.

**Durability check**: The palm material holds up well against friction from ground balls and repetitive scooping. One reviewer reported "two full seasons of HS ball" before needing replacement. But the thread on the thumb seam shows wear around the 30-game mark — worth monitoring if you play a physical style.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest flaws, because every glove has them:

- **Sizing runs small**. Multiple buyers flagged this. If you're between sizes, go up. A medium fits like a snug large in other brands. One reviewer summed it up: "I normally wear medium in Warrior — these felt tight. Ordered large next time."
- **Ventilation is mediocre**. The backhand padding traps heat. Summer tournaments or indoor box leagues will leave your hands sweaty. Not a dealbreaker, but the Maverick Rome vents better.
- **The cuff rides up**. After extended wear, the adjustable strap can loosen slightly, requiring a re-tighten mid-game. Minor, but annoying when you're in the middle of a shift.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy the Cell VI if:**
- You're a high school or college player who needs reliable protection without dropping $200+
- You play attack or midfield and need wrist mobility for quick stick moves
- You're replacing a pair that fell apart after one season — these last

## FAQ

**Q: Do these run true to size?**
A: No — they run about a half-size small. If you're between sizes, order up. Check the size chart carefully before buying.

**Q: Are these good for faceoff specialists?**
A: Yes, the wrist flexibility and pre-curved fingers work well for clamping and quick releases. The palm grip holds up in wet conditions too.

**Q: How do they compare to the Warrior Burn gloves?**
A: The Cell VI has better palm durability and a more comfortable out-of-box fit. The Burn has slightly better ventilation. Both are solid mid-range options — pick based on your priority.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** ⭐⭐⭐⭐½

The STX Cell VI is one of the better value picks in the mid-range glove market. The pre-curved construction, tacky palm, and wrist mobility make it a legitimate choice for players who actually use their hands — not just padding for them. The sizing quirk and average ventilation keep it from a perfect score, but at this price point, you'd be hard-pressed to find a better all-around glove.

**Buy it if** you want a glove that's game-ready immediately, holds up through a full season, and won't break the bank.
