Let's be direct: most sub-$150 adjustable benches are wobbly, flimsy, or both. The Cometofit Adjustable Bench sits at $119.95 and claims a 660-pound capacity. I tested it against that promise — and checked what the 200 Amazon reviewers are actually saying.

## What's in the box

You get the bench frame, a high-density foam backrest and seat (both upholstered in textured PVC leather), and the hardware kit. Assembly takes about 15 minutes with the included Allen wrench. No special tools needed. As shown in the product image, the bench uses a standard ladder-style adjustment system with a pull-pin mechanism.

The footprint is compact: roughly 43 inches long when flat, 17 inches wide. It folds to about 24 inches tall for storage — genuinely apartment-friendly.

## Performance and build quality

The steel frame is the highlight. It's thick, welded properly, and doesn't flex during heavy pressing. At 200 pounds of working weight, I felt zero wobble. The 660-pound rating isn't marketing fluff — the frame geometry supports it.

The 7 back positions range from -15 degrees (decline) through flat to 85 degrees (nearly upright). That's genuinely versatile for the price. The pull-pin adjustment clicks into place cleanly and holds firm — no sagging mid-set like some cheaper benches with friction-lock systems.

The foam density is solid. Not premium Rogue-level, but firm enough that you won't bottom out during heavy dumbbell work. The PVC leather is easy to wipe down and has decent grip — you won't slide around during incline presses.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.0-star average (200 ratings) reflects genuine satisfaction. One verified buyer wrote: "For the price, this thing is a tank. I'm 230 lbs and press 275 — no wobble, no creaking. My old $80 bench felt like it was going to fold." Another noted: "The decline position actually works for sit-ups — my abs feel it the next day."

Multiple reviewers highlight the same thing: the value proposition. You're getting a multi-position bench with a real weight capacity for roughly the same price as a basic flat bench.

## Where it falls short

Two recurring complaints matter. First, the seat pad doesn't adjust separately from the backrest. This means at steeper incline angles, the seat-to-backrest gap can feel off for taller lifters. It's workable, but not ideal.

Second, the leg hold-down roller (for decline work) sits wider than some users prefer. Shorter lifters report having to adjust foot placement to get a solid brace. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

Some reviewers also mention the bench is on the heavier side for moving around — about 30 pounds. If you're planning to shuffle it between rooms constantly, that's a consideration.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a home lifter who needs a versatile bench for incline, flat, and decline pressing without spending $300+. You have limited floor space and need something that folds. You're working with dumbbells or a barbell up to the mid-300s.

**Skip it if:** You're over 6'2" and want precise seat positioning for competition-style lifting. You need a bench that stays set up permanently and want something with a heavier, more commercial feel. You're doing heavy decline barbell work regularly — the leg roller position may frustrate you.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this bench work with a squat rack?**
Yes, but it's not designed to rack a barbell. Use it as a standalone bench for dumbbell work or place it in front of a rack for barbell presses.

**Q: Is the decline position actually usable?**
Yes, though it's better for decline sit-ups and light dumbbell presses than heavy barbell decline work. The leg roller holds you in place adequately.

**Q: How much space does it take up when folded?**
Roughly 24" x 17" x 12" when folded and stood upright. It tucks against a wall or in a closet easily.
