Let’s cut through the noise: most kids’ boxing gloves on Amazon are either cheap vinyl toys that fall apart in a month or overbuilt adult gloves that swamp a child’s hands. The RDX Kids Boxing Gloves sit in a rare middle ground — real leather construction at a price that won’t make you wince. At $32.99, these are worth a hard look for any parent whose kid has caught the fight bug.

**What you actually get**

The gloves arrive as a single pair — no wraps, no bag. That’s fine, because the money went into the build. The outer shell is Maya Hide leather, which is RDX’s branded synthetic leather with a surprisingly premium feel. It’s not the buttery-soft genuine leather you’d find on $150 Winning gloves, but it’s a significant step up from the PU vinyl that coats most sub-$40 kids’ gloves. The 8oz pair I tested measured about 9 inches from cuff to knuckle tip — a proper fit for ages 7-10 depending on hand size.

**Real-world performance**

The padding is the star here. RDX uses a multi-layer foam setup — a firmer outer layer that absorbs impact and a softer inner layer that cushions the hand. During heavy bag sessions, knuckle protection felt solid without turning the gloves into stiff clubs. That matters for sparring, where you want feedback but not pain. The wrist support is where RDX actually beats many adult gloves: a wide hook-and-loop strap with an elastic cinch system that lets kids tighten their own gloves. No more waiting for a coach to tie laces.

The Maya Hide leather does what it promises. After three months of twice-weekly bag work and pad drills, the gloves show scuffs but zero cracking, peeling, or seam separation. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: “Bought these for my 8-year-old who hits the bag like a maniac. Six months in, still holding up perfectly.” That durability narrative repeats across the 240 ratings.

**Where they stumble**

Sizing is the biggest headache. RDX’s size chart runs small — multiple reviews note that the 8oz fits more like a 6oz. One parent wrote, “My 10-year-old is average size and these were tight with hand wraps on. Order up.” If you’re between sizes, go bigger. There’s no thumb attachment loop either, which is a genuine safety concern for younger kids still learning to keep their thumbs tucked. That’s a design oversight at this price point.

Breathability is also mediocre. The Maya Hide doesn’t ventilate well, so after 30 minutes of sparring, hands get sweaty. Not a dealbreaker, but expect to air them out between rounds.

**Who should buy these**

These gloves nail three specific buyer profiles:

- **The young bag warrior** (ages 8-12): The leather shell and foam stack will survive aggressive bag work that destroys vinyl gloves.
- **The budget-conscious coach**: At $33, you can outfit a whole youth class without sacrificing safety.
- **The Muay Thai parent**: The wrist support and cuff design translate well to clinch work and pad drills.

Skip them if you have a child under 7 — the smallest size still runs large — or if you need competition-grade gloves for sanctioned bouts. These are training tools, not fight-night gear.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these gloves require hand wraps?**  
A: Not mandatory, but recommended for bag work. The padding is solid, but wraps add wrist stability and absorb sweat. If your kid skips wraps, the gloves still protect knuckles adequately for light sparring.

**Q: How do I clean the Maya Hide leather?**  
A: Wipe down with a damp cloth after each use and let them air dry. Avoid leather conditioners — they can break down the synthetic fibers. Never machine wash.

**Q: Are these suitable for actual sparring or just bag work?**  
A: Both, but with a caveat. The foam density is forgiving enough for controlled sparring with a partner wearing headgear. For hard, competitive sparring, step up to 10oz or 12oz with more padding.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, properly protective training glove for a kid who’s serious about boxing or Muay Thai without dropping $80+. **Skip it if** your child is on the young/small end of the size range or you need competition-grade gear.

At 4.6/5, the RDX Kids Boxing Gloves earn their rating. They’re not perfect — the sizing quirk and missing thumb loop are real flaws — but for the price, they deliver leather-grade durability and wrist support that punches well above their weight class. Your kid’s hands will thank you.
