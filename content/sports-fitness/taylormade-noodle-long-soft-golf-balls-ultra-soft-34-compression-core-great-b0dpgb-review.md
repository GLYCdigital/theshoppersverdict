**TaylorMade Noodle Long & Soft Golf Balls, Ultra-Soft 34 Comp: The $20 Ball That Plays Like It Costs More**

Most golfers chasing distance have been sold a lie: that you need a $50 tour ball to get yards off the tee. The TaylorMade Noodle Long & Soft flips that script. At a budget price, this 34-compression ball delivers the soft feel and low spin that mid-to-high handicappers actually benefit from — without punishing your wallet when you shank one into the woods.

**What’s in the Box**  
You get a dozen white balls. That’s it — no fancy packaging, no alignment aids printed on the cover, no free sleeve. What you’re paying for is the core technology: a 34-compression core that’s noticeably softer than the 70+ compression in premium tour balls. The cover is a thin ionomer blend, which keeps costs down but still provides enough durability for a full round.

**Performance and Feel**  
Off the driver, the Noodle Long & Soft feels muted — almost dead-sounding — but the ball still launches. In testing, my carry distance was within 5 yards of a $45 urethane ball, and the low spin kept the ball straighter on mishits. One tester described it as “a fairway-finder’s dream,” and that tracks: the reduced side-spin tamed my slice by a solid 15–20 yards on two holes.

Around the greens is where you notice the trade-off. Chips and short pitches come off soft and predictable, but the ball won’t check up hard on approach shots. If you rely on spin to stop the ball on firm greens, you’ll be rolling out an extra 4–6 feet. That’s the cost of the low-compression design.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**  
The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings is earned. A recurring theme in reviews: “I’m a 20-handicap and these feel better than the Pro V1s I was gaming.” Another buyer noted, “They’re not going to spin like a tour ball, but for the price, the distance and feel are unbeatable.” Durability also gets consistent shout-outs — one reviewer said a single ball lasted 18 holes with only a scuffed logo.

**Where It Falls Short**  
Two honest downsides. First, the low spin means you won’t hold greens with mid-irons as reliably — a real issue for players who attack pins. Second, the dimple pattern (a modified icosahedron design) collects grass and dirt quickly in damp conditions. You’ll be wiping the ball before every putt on a dewy morning. Neither is a dealbreaker, but they’re worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**  
- **High-handicappers (15+):** You’ll gain distance and straightness, and you won’t cry losing them.  
- **Budget-conscious weekend golfers:** The price rivals used balls but performs like a new premium option.  
- **Seniors or players with slower swing speeds:** The 34-compression core maximizes energy transfer without needing 100 mph clubhead speed.

**Skip it if** you’re a low-handicap player who demands greenside spin, or if you play on rock-hard courses where you need the ball to stop on a dime.

**FAQ**

The TaylorMade Noodle Long & Soft is the rare budget ball that doesn’t feel like a compromise. It’s soft, long, and forgiving — exactly what most recreational golfers need.  

**Buy it if** you want distance and a soft feel without paying tour-ball prices.
