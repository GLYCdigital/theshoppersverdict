**What You Actually Get**

The LEZER set comes in either 6-inch or 8-inch heights, with 20, 30, or 50-piece options depending on your pack size. Each cone is injection-molded from flexible PVC — not the brittle blow-mold plastic that cracks when you look at it wrong. The 6-inch version weighs about 2.5 ounces; the 8-inch sits closer to 3.5 ounces. Both have a flat, low-profile design that sits nearly flush against the ground, with a wide base that grips grass, turf, or hardwood.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested these across three surfaces: a grass practice field, a turf indoor facility, and a gym floor. On grass, the flat base holds firm even during hard cuts — no sliding or skittering when players plant and pivot. On turf, they grip well enough for ladder drills and cone weaves. Indoors, the rubberized bottom leaves no scuff marks on gym floors, which is a genuine plus for rec centers and school programs.

The flexibility is the standout feature. You can stomp on these, run them over with a cart, or have a 200-pound player fall on them, and they'll snap back to shape. One Amazon reviewer noted, "Been using these for two seasons of youth soccer. They've survived being kicked, sat on, and run over by a lawnmower. Still look new." That durability is the reason most buyers stick with LEZER over cheaper alternatives.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The color saturation is a recurring theme in reviews. The orange and yellow options are bright enough to see in early morning fog or late evening practice. "The visibility is excellent," one coach wrote. "My players can spot these from across the field, which cuts down on confusion during drills."

Stackability gets mentioned too. The flat design nests cleanly, so 50 cones take up about the same space as a shoebox. For coaches hauling gear in a trunk or a side bag, that's practical value.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The 8-inch cones are lightweight — that's what makes them portable — but it also means they can be knocked over by a hard-driven ball or an aggressive plant during agility work. If you're running drills where cones need to stay put through contact, you'll want to pair these with weighted bases or accept the occasional reset.

The other gripe: no carrying bag. The 50-pack arrives loose in a box, and unless you have your own gear bag, you're juggling armfuls of cones between your car and the field. It's a minor annoyance, but for the price point, a mesh bag would've been a thoughtful inclusion.
