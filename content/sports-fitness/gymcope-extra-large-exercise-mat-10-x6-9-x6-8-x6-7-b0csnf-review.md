# GymCope Extra Large Exercise Mat: The Floor-Saving Solution Most Brands Ignore

Most exercise mats are designed for people who are 5'6" and weigh 140 pounds. If you're taller, broader, or simply want a dedicated workout zone in your living room, you've probably felt the frustration of your hands or feet sliding off the edge mid-plank. The GymCope Extra Large Exercise Mat exists specifically to solve that problem — and with 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it appears to be doing it well.

## What You're Actually Getting

The GymCope comes in six sizes: 6'x4', 7'x5', 8'x6', 9'x6', and 10'x6' (the listing also includes a 6' variant). The material is NBR (nitrile butadiene rubber) foam, which is the same closed-cell foam used in most high-end gym flooring tiles. Thickness runs between 6mm and 10mm depending on the size you choose — the larger mats get the thicker padding.

Out of the box, you get the mat itself, a carry strap (on most sizes), and nothing else. No repair kit, no instructional booklet, no gimmicks. That's fine — the mat is the product, and the mat is what matters.

## Real-World Performance

**Comfort: This is where GymCope earns its keep.** On hardwood or tile, the NBR foam absorbs impact noticeably better than a standard PVC yoga mat. If you're doing core work, stretching, or bodyweight exercises, your spine and knees will thank you. The textured surface provides decent grip even when you're sweating through a cardio session.

**Build quality: Solid, with one caveat.** The foam density is consistent across the mat — no thin spots or uneven compression. The edges are cleanly cut and don't fray. However, the surface is not shoe-friendly. If you plan to do HIIT or bootcamp workouts in trainers, you'll see scuff marks. This is a barefoot or sock mat.

**Storage is the real tradeoff.** A 10'x6' mat rolled up is roughly 6 feet long and 8 inches in diameter. It will not fit in a standard yoga mat bag, and it's heavy enough (15+ lbs for the largest size) that you won't want to haul it around. This is a "set it up and leave it down" product for a dedicated home gym space, not a portable option.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the **chemical smell** is real. NBR foam off-gasses, and multiple reviewers reported a strong odor that took several days to dissipate. If you're sensitive to smells, plan to unroll it in a ventilated area before using it.

Second, the **surface attracts dust and pet hair** like a magnet. You'll need to wipe it down regularly, and a lint roller becomes essential if you have pets.

Third, the **price-to-size ratio** — while the smaller sizes are very reasonable, the 10'x6' pushes into premium territory. If you only need a 6'x4' mat, you're overpaying for capacity you won't use.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Tall exercisers (6'+)**: Standard mats simply don't cover you. The 9' or 10' lengths are game-changers.
- **Home gym owners with hard floors**: The thickness genuinely protects joints during floor work.
- **Couples or duos**: The 8'x6' and larger sizes allow two people to work out simultaneously.

**Skip it if** you need portability, you're a shoed HIIT enthusiast, or you have a tiny storage space.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
