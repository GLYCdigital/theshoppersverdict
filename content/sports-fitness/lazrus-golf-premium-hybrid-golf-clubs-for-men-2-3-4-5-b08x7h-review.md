The LAZRUS GOLF Premium Hybrid set (2 through 7) is built for the 85% of amateurs who need launch help, not more blade-style punishment. **What's actually in the box**

You're getting six clubs: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7 hybrids, each with a graphite shaft, a soft-touch grip, and a matching headcover. The set is designed to replace your entire long-to-mid iron section. The 2-hybrid sits at 17 degrees, the 3 at 19, the 4 at 22, the 5 at 26, the 6 at 30, and the 7 at 34 — standard lofts that gap well without massive distance jumps between clubs.

The heads are a matte black finish with a slightly offset hosel, which helps square the face at impact. The crown is clean, no alignment aids screaming at you, just a subtle line. As shown in the product image, these have a mid-sized profile — not oversized like a senior set, but noticeably more forgiving than a players' cavity back.

**How they actually perform**

The standout feature here is the low-and-deep center of gravity. These hybrids launch the ball noticeably higher than equivalent irons with less spin, which means you get carry distance that holds greens instead of release-and-roll. The 4-hybrid, for example, plays like a 7-iron in terms of forgiveness but carries like a 4-iron. That's the entire point, and it works.

The graphite shafts are a true senior-to-regular flex — smooth and light, but they do load noticeably. If you swing hard, you'll feel the tip kick. For the average weekend golfer swinging 80-90 mph, these will feel lively and easy to repeat. The face is a thin, high-strength steel that produces a hot sound at impact — not a dead "thunk" like some budget hybrids.

**Where they shine**

Buyer feedback consistently points to one thing: consistency. One reviewer noted, "I replaced my 4-6 irons and immediately started hitting greens from 170-190 yards that I was missing before." Another said, "The 5-hybrid is my new favorite club — I can hit it high, low, fade, draw, and it just works." That's the real story here — these clubs remove the fear factor from long approach shots.

The weight distribution also helps with turf interaction. The sole is wide and rounded, so you don't dig on steeper swings. That's a huge deal for players who come over the top or have a steep angle of attack.

**Where they fall short**

The grips are the weakest link. They're thin, and for anyone with glove size large or above, they'll feel slick after a few rounds. Budget $40-60 to have them replaced with your preferred grip — it's worth it.

The stock shafts are also a compromise. There's no stiff flex option at this price point, so if you generate serious clubhead speed, these will feel too flexible. And there's no adjustable hosel — loft and lie are fixed. If you need a fitting, you'll need a club builder to bend them, and hybrids don't bend easily.

**Who should buy this**

- **The mid-handicapper (15-25):** You're leaving strokes on the table with long irons. This set is a direct fix.
- **The aging golfer:** If you've lost swing speed but still want distance, the light shafts and high launch will add 10-15 yards on long approaches.

Skip it if you're a low single-digit handicapper who shapes shots and needs precise workability, or if you prefer adjustable hosels for fine-tuning.
