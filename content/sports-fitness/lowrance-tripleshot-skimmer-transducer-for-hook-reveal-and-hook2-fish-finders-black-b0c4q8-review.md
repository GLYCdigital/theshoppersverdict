Let me save you the headache I went through: that fish finder you bought with your boat is only as good as the transducer bolted to your transom. The Lowrance TripleShot Skimmer is the upgrade that turns a decent Hook Reveal unit into a serious structure-finding machine — but it's not without quirks.

**What You Actually Get**

In the box: the TripleShot Skimmer transducer, a 20-foot cable, and a standard transom mount bracket with hardware. The unit itself measures about 7 inches long and houses three elements — 2D CHIRP sonar, DownScan, and SideScan. The cable terminates in a 12-pin Lowrance connector that plugs directly into Hook Reveal and Hook2 5x/7x displays. No adapters, no firmware gymnastics. It's genuinely plug-and-play, which is rare in the marine electronics world.

**Performance: Where It Shines**

I mounted this on a 17-foot aluminum bass boat with a Hook Reveal 7. At 5 mph, the SideScan image quality is genuinely impressive for the price. You can pick out submerged brush piles, rock ledges, and even individual bait balls at 50-80 feet to each side. The DownScan gives you a clean, near-photographic view of bottom composition — you'll immediately tell hard bottom from mud or grass.

The 2D CHIRP element is solid for traditional anglers. Target separation at 50 feet is respectable, and it holds bottom contact reliably at 25 mph. The 455 kHz and 800 kHz frequencies let you toggle between wide coverage and finer detail depending on water depth.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The 4.4-star average across 220 ratings isn't luck. A recurring theme in reviews: "I was shocked at the detail for the money." Another user noted, "Installed in 20 minutes and immediately saw fish-holding structure I'd been motoring over for years." Multiple reviewers confirm the cable length is sufficient for most transom-to-console runs on boats up to 20 feet.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest limitation: SideScan needs speed. Below 3 mph, the image degrades into mush. If you primarily slow-troll or fish anchored in current, you're paying for a feature you can't use. The bracket is also the weak link — several reviewers mention vibration at planing speeds. I'd recommend adding a stainless steel mounting plate or using the optional trolling motor mount if your boat runs fast.

Also worth noting: this transducer lacks a built-in temperature sensor. If you're upgrading from an HDI transducer, you'll lose water temp readouts on your display. It's a minor annoyance, but one Lowrance could have easily avoided.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the weekend angler with a Hook Reveal or Hook2 who fishes lakes, reservoirs, and protected bays. If you're hunting bass around submerged timber, chasing walleye along breaklines, or mapping out crappie brush piles, the TripleShot will pay for itself in the first season. It's not for offshore trollers (no 50/200 kHz wide-angle element) or for kayakers (too bulky, needs speed).

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work with my Hook Reveal 5?**
A: Yes, all Hook Reveal and Hook2 5x/7x models with the 12-pin connector work. If your unit has an 8-pin socket, you need an adapter cable.

**Q: Can I mount this on a trolling motor?**
A: You can, but the included bracket is designed for transoms. Lowrance sells a separate trolling motor mount kit. SideScan still needs forward motion to work well.

**Q: Does it interfere with existing sonar?**
A: Unlikely. The 2D element runs at 455/800 kHz, so it won't conflict with other units on standard 50/200 kHz frequencies.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You own a Hook Reveal or Hook2, fish structure-oriented species, and want side imaging without dropping $500+ on a new unit.

**Skip it if:** You fish at trolling speeds under 3 mph, need water temp readouts, or run your boat above 30 mph regularly.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The imaging performance far exceeds the price point, but the speed requirement and flimsy bracket keep it from being a perfect upgrade. For the majority of inland anglers, this is the single best cost-per-feature upgrade you can make to your existing Lowrance setup.
