**If your 14-year-old just announced they want to “get into golf,” you’ve got two options: buy them a $99 box set from a big-box store that they’ll outgrow by July, or spend a little more on the Aspire XD1. This is the set that actually respects the fact that a teenager isn’t a little kid anymore — and it shows in the club specs.**

## What’s actually in the box

The Aspire XD1 is a complete 11-piece set. You get a 460cc titanium driver, a 3-wood, a 4-hybrid, irons from 6 through 9, a pitching wedge, a putter, and a stand bag with headcovers. That’s the same club layout most adult recreational sets ship with — nothing stripped down to save weight.

The woods and hybrid use graphite shafts, which keeps the swing weight manageable for a teen who hasn’t finished growing. The irons are steel — and that’s the right call. Steel gives them feedback on mishits, which accelerates learning. The putter is a standard blade-style with alignment lines, nothing fancy, but perfectly usable.

## How it performs on the course

I’ve seen a fair number of “junior” sets that are really just adult clubs with shorter shafts. The XD1 isn’t that. The driver has a 12° loft, which is higher than most adult drivers — that helps get the ball airborne with slower swing speeds. The hybrid replaces the 4 and 5 irons, which is the smartest decision in this set. Most teens can’t hit a long iron consistently, and the hybrid is genuinely forgiving.

The irons are cavity-backed with a decent amount of perimeter weighting. Expect higher, straighter ball flight than you’d get from a blade-style iron. The set is designed for a teen in the 5’2” to 5’10” range, so if your kid is on the shorter end, the standard length might feel a touch long — but it’s adjustable with a simple grip change.

## Where it falls short

The stock grips are the first thing you’ll notice. They’re thin, standard-size grips that feel slick after a few rounds, especially in humidity. A set of midsize grips runs about $40 installed — do that early if your teen has larger hands.

The bag is the other weak point. It’s a single-strap design with minimal padding. Fine for a cart or a quick nine, but carrying this for 18 holes will dig into a shoulder. It’s functional, not premium. Also, the putter is the most basic club in the set — no mallet weight, no alignment tech. If your teen gets serious about putting, they’ll upgrade that first.

## Who should buy this

- **The 13–16-year-old beginner to mid-handicapper** who wants a complete set without hunting for clubs individually.  
- **Parents who want one purchase that lasts** — the graphite woods and steel irons will hold up for three-plus seasons.  
- **A teen moving up from a kids’ set** who’s too big for a 48-inch driver and needs adult-length clubs with junior flex.

## Skip it if

- Your teen is already shooting under 90 with a consistent swing. At that level, they’ll want better shafts and a fitted driver — this set will hold them back.  
- You need a bag that’s comfortable to carry for full rounds. Budget another $60 for a decent two-strap stand bag.  

## FAQ

**Q: Is this set appropriate for a 13-year-old girl?**  
Yes, if she’s around 5’2” or taller. The clubs are unisex in design, and the graphite shafts on woods keep the weight manageable. Under 5’2”, consider the junior version.

**Q: Does it come with a warranty?**  
Aspire offers a one-year manufacturer’s warranty against defects. Shafts and grips are covered, but normal wear isn’t.

**Q: Can I reshaft these later if my teen grows?**  
The irons have standard .370 hosels, so yes — a pro shop can extend or reshaft them. The woods are more integrated, so plan to replace those when your teen outgrows them.
