You know that sound — the one that makes your downstairs neighbors hate you. If you're deadlifting or doing Olympic lifts at home, the Yes4All Silencer Drop Pads are your first line of defense against cracked concrete, dented subfloors, and noise complaints. At 4.5 stars from 225 ratings, this is one of the most-reviewed drop pad options on Amazon. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

Each pad measures roughly 24" x 24" and is 2 inches thick. The construction is two-layer: a dense foam core wrapped in a heavy-duty PVC shell. The product image shows the reinforced stitching along the edges and the integrated carry handles — those handles are real, not decorative. They're stitched into the PVC and make moving a 15-20 lb pad around your gym space much easier than wrestling with bare foam blocks.

You buy them individually or in pairs. For deadlifts, you need two — one under each plate. For cleans or snatches, four is ideal if you're dropping from overhead.

**Real-World Performance**

Here's what the silencer actually does well: it kills the sharp impact noise. Multiple buyers describe the difference as night and day. One reviewer noted, *"My downstairs neighbor stopped complaining after the first session. The thud is now a dull thump instead of a crack."* That's the dual-layer construction working — the foam compresses on impact rather than bouncing the bar back at you like some cheaper rubber mats do.

The PVC shell is genuinely durable. Chalk, sweat, and the inevitable scrapes from steel knurling wipe right off. Buyers who've owned these for 6+ months report the surface holds up without cracking or peeling at the seams.

**Where It Falls Short**

Be honest about the 2-inch thickness. For most lifters working up to 315 lbs, these are fine. But if you're dropping 405+ from lockout height, you'll feel the floor through the pad. One strong deadlifter put it plainly: *"I hit 455 and felt the concrete flex. These are for moderate weight, not max-effort drops."*

The PVC surface can also be slick. If you're loading unevenly — say, dropping one side slightly before the other — the plates can slide on impact. It's not dangerous, but it's something to be aware of if you're coming down at an angle.

And yes, there's a smell. New PVC outgasses a chemical odor that takes a few days to fade. It's not toxic, but it's noticeable in a small apartment gym.

**Skip These If:**

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I drop a loaded barbell on these from overhead?**
A: Yes, for moderate weights. The foam absorbs the impact, but the 2-inch thickness means heavy loads (405 lbs+) from overhead height will compress fully. For high-volume overhead work, consider stacking two pads per side.

**Q: Do I need one or two pads for deadlifts?**
A: Two — one under each plate. The bar itself is supported by the plates, so you only need protection where the plates land.

**Q: Will these work on carpet?**
A: Yes, but the pads will compress differently. On thick carpet, they may feel softer and less stable. Hard floors are the ideal surface.
