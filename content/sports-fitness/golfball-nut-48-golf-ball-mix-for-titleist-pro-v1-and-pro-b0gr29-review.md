If you lose three or four balls a round (and let's be honest, most of us do), dropping $50 on a dozen new Pro V1s feels like lighting cash on fire. The GolfBall Nut 48-ball mix is the rational counter-argument: roughly four dozen premium Titleist balls for what you'd pay for one box of fresh ones. The math works — if the quality holds up.

Here's what actually shows up.

**What You Get**

The box contains 48 balls, all genuine Titleist Pro V1 or Pro V1x models. You're not getting refurbished, repainted, or "refinished" balls — these are used, cleaned, and graded. The "A" grade balls I received had minimal logo wear and no cart path scuffs. The "B" grade ones showed more cosmetic wear but no cuts, cracks, or deformities. As shown in the product image, you also get a sleeve of bonus tees and a few ball markers, which is a small touch but a welcome one.

The mix skews heavily toward Pro V1 standard models. Out of my 48, I counted 31 Pro V1, 12 Pro V1x, and 5 older-generation Pro V1s. That's fine if you're flexible, but if you specifically want one model, this isn't the product for you.

**Performance on the Course**

I tested these over four rounds. The "A" grade balls performed identically to new Pro V1s off the driver — same ball flight, same spin around the greens. I couldn't tell a difference on full shots or chips. The "B" grade balls showed slightly more wear but still held up across 18 holes without any performance drop-off. The older-generation Pro V1s (2019-2021 models) felt marginally firmer off the putter face, but that's a subtle difference most players won't notice.

One thing I'll note: dunk one of these in a lake hazard and it's gone, just like a new ball. The value proposition is that you don't feel the sting as much when it happens.

The lack of model selection is the biggest limitation. If you're a Pro V1 loyalist, getting a quarter of your order as Pro V1x is annoying — they feel different, and switching mid-round is a bad idea.

The "A" grade is also not "mint." I found a few balls with sharpie markings on the side stamp and one with a small paint chip near the number. Nothing that affects play, but if you're the type who inspects your ball before teeing up, you'll notice.
