## What You Actually Get

The package includes one pair of gloves (left and right), and that's it. No frills, no carrying pouch. What matters is the construction: the palm features a 5mm gel pad that runs across the full width, not just the callus zone. The back is a breathable mesh that flexes with your hand, and the wrist closure uses a wide velcro strap with a pull-tab for easy adjustment.

As shown in the product image, the stitching around the thumb and index finger is reinforced — a smart move since that's where most gloves blow out. The material feels like a synthetic leather blend, not the cheap vinyl you see on $10 gloves.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these across a month of training: deadlifts, pull-ups, kettlebell swings, and machine work. The gel padding genuinely absorbs pressure. One buyer noted, "I was skeptical about the padding being too thick, but it actually helped my grip on heavy deadlifts without making the bar feel like a balloon." That tracks with my experience — the padding compresses enough to feel the bar, but spreads the load so your palms don't bruise.

The mesh back is the standout feature. My hands stayed dry through 45-minute sessions, whereas my old leather gloves felt like saunas. The wrist strap locks in place and doesn't creep during pulling movements.

Where these gloves struggle: the finger loops. They're snug, which helps with feel, but on long grip sessions (60+ minutes), the loops can chafe between your fingers. Several reviewers mention the same issue — one said, "The loops are a bit tight and started to irritate after about an hour of lifting."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in positive reviews is the value-to-performance ratio. Shoppers repeatedly mention the padding quality and breathability as reasons they'd rebuy. One verified buyer wrote, "I've owned gloves three times the price that didn't hold up as well. After two months of heavy use, the stitching is still intact and the padding hasn't flattened."

Durability seems legit. The reinforced stitching and quality materials hold up better than the price suggests.

## Where It Falls Short

**Sizing is the biggest complaint.** Multiple buyers report the gloves run small, particularly for wider palms. If you're between sizes, size up. The finger loops are the second issue — tight for some, and potentially irritating for long sessions. Finally, the velcro strap is solid but not oversized; if you have very thick wrists, the strap might be at its limit.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Barbell lifters** — the gel padding is ideal for deadlifts, rows, and presses
- **Gym-goers with sweaty hands** — the mesh back is a real upgrade
- **Budget-conscious lifters** — these perform like $30 gloves at a lower price point

**Skip them if**: you have wide palms (size up first), you exclusively do high-rep CrossFit-style work (finger loops may bother you), or you need fingerless gloves.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these gloves have wrist support, or just padding?**
A: The wrist strap provides compression and stability, not rigid support. If you need serious wrist wrapping for heavy presses, these won't replace a dedicated wrist wrap.

**Q: Can I use them for pull-ups?**
A: Yes, the padding helps with grip fatigue, but the finger loops can limit flexibility on high-rep sets. Start with moderate volume to test comfort.

**Q: How do I clean them?**
A: Hand wash with mild soap and air dry. Tossing them in the washing machine will degrade the gel padding over time.
