## What You Actually Get

The box contains one pair of fingerless gloves (available in sizes S through XXL) with a few features that matter more than the price tag suggests. The padding is a 5mm gel layer concentrated across the palm and lower fingers — this isn’t the uniform foam you see on bargain gloves. It’s shaped to match where a barbell or dumbbell actually rests, which makes a noticeable difference during pressing movements.

The pull-on loops at the wrist are a small detail that pays off daily. You won’t wrestle with these after a set of heavy rows. The wrist strap uses a hook-and-loop closure that holds snugly without digging in, and the silicone gripper pattern across the palm provides extra traction on slick chrome bars.

## Real-World Performance

I’ve tested these across three workout styles: powerlifting-style barbell work, dumbbell circuits, and pull-ups with an assist band. The padding does its job — palm fatigue during heavy rack pulls was noticeably reduced compared to bare-handed work. The gripper dots hold up well; even with sweaty palms on a humid day, the bar didn’t slip during deadlifts.

The build quality is where these gloves surprise. The double-stitching at the thumb crotch — the first place cheap gloves fail — held up through months of weekly use. One reviewer noted, “After 6 months of CrossFit, the stitching is still intact and the padding hasn’t flattened out.” That’s consistent with what I found.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in positive reviews is the padding-to-feel ratio. Multiple buyers described it as “just the right amount of cushion” — enough to protect without killing your grip strength. Another recurring compliment is the breathability. The mesh backing between the fingers and across the knuckle area keeps hands from becoming swampy mid-workout.

## Where They Fall Short

Being fingerless means your upper knuckles remain exposed. If you do heavy barbell work, you’ll still develop calluses on the middle knuckles — that’s just physics. One reviewer complained the “gloves stop too short, leaving the top of my fingers rubbing against the bar.” Fair point, but that’s inherent to the fingerless design, not a defect.

The sizing is the bigger issue. These run small for wide hands. If you’re between sizes, size up. Several reviews mention ordering a large and finding it snug, which is worth factoring into your decision.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You’re a lifter doing moderate-to-heavy dumbbell and barbell work who wants palm protection without losing fingertip dexterity for adjustments. They’re also excellent for gym-goers who use equipment shared with strangers — these gloves prevent direct skin contact with sweaty bars.

**Skip these if:** You exclusively do heavy Olympic lifting where maximum bar feel is critical, or if you have extra-wide hands — the sizing issues will frustrate you. Also skip if you need full-finger coverage for rope climbs or strongman-style work.

## FAQ

**Do these gloves help with grip strength?**
No. They protect your palms, but the fingerless design means your grip works the same as bare-handed. If you need grip assistance, look for wrist wraps instead.

**Can I use them for pull-ups?**
Yes, but the padding can actually make gripping a pull-up bar slightly more difficult because it adds thickness. They work fine, just be aware the bar feel is different.

**How do I wash them?**
Hand wash with mild soap and air dry. Machine washing will eventually degrade the silicone grippers and compress the padding.
