## What's Actually in the Box

The set includes the Exult Rise stick (complete with head and pocket), a pair of STX goggles, and a branded drawstring backpack. The stick itself is the star: the Exult Rise head features STX's "StringKing-style" offset design that makes catching and cradling noticeably easier for beginners. The shaft is an alloy blend that keeps the total weight around 17 ounces — light enough for a 10-year-old to handle for a full practice without arm fatigue.

The goggles are a real surprise. They're ASTM-certified (the standard required for US Lacrosse play), and the anti-fog coating is genuinely effective. One reviewer noted, *"My daughter wore these through a full summer league game in 90-degree heat and never had to wipe them once."* That's not something you hear about budget goggles.

---

## Performance and Build Quality

On the field, this stick performs exactly where it should at this price point: it's forgiving. The pocket is strung with a mid-high depth that holds the ball securely during ground balls but releases cleanly on passes. The soft-touch grip on the shaft is a thoughtful touch — it gives younger players positive control without the slippery feel of bare metal.

The head is where STX put most of their engineering effort. The Exult Rise uses a "C-channel" construction that resists warping, and the scoop is designed to make ground-ball pickups feel almost automatic. For a player who's still learning to get low and scoop properly, this head forgives a lot of sloppy technique.

That said, this is not a competition stick. The pocket is pre-strung with a fairly basic mesh pattern. If your daughter is already playing travel ball or has a strong preference for a specific pocket style, you'll likely want to have it re-strung by a pro shop. That's an extra $20–30, but it's worth budgeting for.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the downsides.

First, the **backpack is basic**. It's a drawstring bag with a small zippered pocket — fine for carrying a stick, goggles, and a water bottle, but don't expect organization. There's no separate compartment for cleats or a sweaty practice jersey.

Second, the **goggles fit narrower faces better**. If your daughter has a wider face or cheeks, the goggles may press uncomfortably at the temples. A few reviewers mentioned this specifically, so it's worth having your daughter try them on early to ensure a good fit — Amazon's return policy covers you if they don't.

Third, the **stick is not for advanced players**. If she's already playing at a competitive level, this head will feel soft and the pocket will lack the precise release that experienced players demand. This is a starter kit, and it knows it.

**Q: What age range is this set designed for?**
A: STX markets this for girls ages 8–13. The stick is 36 inches (youth length), which is appropriate for that range. If she's under 8 or over 14, look at the mini or standard lengths instead.

**Q: Are the goggles approved for regulation play?**
A: Yes. They're ASTM F3077-certified, which meets US Lacrosse and NFHS requirements. You won't need to buy a separate pair for games.

**Q: Can I adjust the pocket depth?**
