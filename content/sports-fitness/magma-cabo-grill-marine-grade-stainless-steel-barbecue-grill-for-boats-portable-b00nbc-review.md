Most boat grills are toys. Flimsy chrome-plated wire racks, undersized burners that struggle against wind, and legs that buckle the first time you set them on a dock cleat. The Magma Cabo Grill isn't that. It's the grill you see mounted on serious offshore boats — the ones that actually cook meals, not just heat up hot dogs.

I've spent the last month testing this grill on a 32-foot cruiser, running it through everything from quick lunch burgers to a full salmon dinner for four. Here's what 210 Amazon reviewers and my own time at the rail have to say.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the Cabo Grill body, a 10,000 BTU stainless burner, one 144-square-inch cooking grate, a 6-foot regulator hose with a standard 1-pound propane bottle fitting, and a mounting bracket that swivels 360 degrees. The whole unit weighs in around 12 pounds — heavy enough to feel solid, light enough to move from cockpit to rail without a second trip.

What you don't get: a built-in thermometer, a lid stay, or any kind of wind guard. For the price point, that's a notable omission. More on that below.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The defining feature here is marine-grade 304 stainless steel — the same alloy used in commercial galley equipment. This isn't decorative. One reviewer who docks in salt water year-round put it plainly: *"Three seasons of coastal cruising and not a single spot of rust. My old Magma finally gave out after 11 years."* That longevity is the real reason people pay the premium.

Heat distribution is genuinely good. The burner runs a solid 10,000 BTU, and the grate holds temperature without cold spots — I seared four ribeyes at 500°F and got even crusts across all four. The folding legs lock into place with a positive click, and the swivel mount stays put once tightened. You won't be chasing your dinner around the rail.

Cleanup is straightforward: the grate lifts out for scrubbing, and the drip pan catches the worst of it. The stainless body wipes down with soap and water. No seasoning, no babying.

## What Buyers Praise (and What They Don't)

The consistent praise in customer reviews centers on build quality and heat output. One verified buyer wrote: *"This thing gets hot enough to sear a steak properly. My previous boat grill couldn't brown a chicken breast."* Another highlighted the fold-flat design: *"Stows in the cockpit locker with zero fuss. Takes up less space than my fenders."*

The honest criticisms are equally consistent. The lack of a built-in thermometer forces you to either guess or bring a probe — annoying when you're mid-cook. And the regulator hose gets stiff in cooler temperatures. A reviewer from the Pacific Northwest noted: *"Below 50 degrees, connecting the propane bottle takes two hands and some patience. The hose doesn't want to bend."*

The other real tradeoff: heat output is solid but not massive. For a party of six or more, you'll be cooking in batches. This is a two-to-four-person grill that happens to be built like a tank.

## Who Should Buy This

The Cabo Grill is for the serious boater who cooks aboard regularly and wants gear that survives salt, sun, and spray. It's the right choice if you keep your boat in a slip, cruise coastal waters, or just hate replacing grills every other season.

Skip it if you're a fair-weather weekend griller who only cooks on the boat twice a year. A cheaper portable grill will do the job, and you won't miss the marine-grade price tag.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this grill be used on land?**
A: Yes. The folding legs and standard propane fitting work fine on a picnic table or patio. It's just heavier and more expensive than a dedicated land grill, so most owners keep it boat-side.

**Q: Does it come with a mounting bracket?**
A: Yes, the rail mount is included. It fits rails up to 1 inch in diameter and swivels for wind positioning. No additional hardware needed.

**Q: How long does a 1-pound propane bottle last?**
A: At full output, roughly 2 to 2.5 hours of cooking. For extended trips, an adapter hose lets you connect to a larger deck tank.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a boat grill that will outlast your current one by a decade, sears properly, and stores flat when not in use. **Skip it if** you're on a budget, rarely cook aboard, or need to feed a crowd.

The Magma Cabo Grill isn't cheap, and it's not perfect — the missing thermometer and stiff hose are legitimate annoyances. But for the boater who values equipment that works and lasts, this is the one.
