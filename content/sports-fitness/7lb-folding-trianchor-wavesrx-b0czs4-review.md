**The 7LB Folding Trianchor from WavesRx solves a problem most athletes don't realize they have until they're at a park with a resistance band and no way to anchor it.**

If you've ever tried to do banded rows outdoors, you know the struggle: wrapping a band around a tree trunk that's too wide, or a fence post that's too flimsy, or a friend who's getting tired of being your human anchor. That's the gap this product fills.

Here's what the actual shopping experience looks like. The unit arrives fully assembled — no tools, no setup. It's a triangular steel frame with three stakes that fold into a compact shape. When closed, it measures about 8 inches by 6 inches by 2 inches thick. That's smaller than a hardcover novel. It weighs 7 pounds, which is substantial enough to feel solid in hand but light enough to toss in a duffel.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains just the Trianchor itself. No case, no bands, no accessories. Keep that in mind — if you don't already own resistance bands, you'll need to buy those separately. The steel construction is powder-coated, and the three stake points are welded, not bolted. That's a good sign for durability. One buyer noted, "I've hammered this thing into hard-packed dirt and it hasn't bent or warped."

## Real-World Performance

The Trianchor's job is simple: stay put when you pull on it. And it does that well on the surfaces it's designed for. The three-point stance distributes load evenly, so when you attach a band and pull, the anchor doesn't tip or slide. For sled pushes on grass, it held firm through repeated heavy pushes. For banded sprints, it stayed planted.

The folding mechanism is the standout feature. It's genuinely tool-free — you rotate the three arms into position and they lock. No pins to lose, no screws to tighten. This matters more than you'd think. A lot of "portable" anchors on the market require assembly at the park, which kills the convenience factor. This one doesn't.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest tradeoff: this is not an indoor tool. The stakes need soft ground. On concrete, asphalt, or artificial turf, the points will not penetrate, and the anchor will slide when you pull hard. If you're thinking about using this in a garage gym with a rubber mat — don't. It won't work.

The lack of a carry case is also a minor annoyance. The folded unit has sharp-ish edges at the stake tips, so tossing it unprotected into a car trunk risks scratching surfaces. A simple nylon pouch would have made this a near-perfect product.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if**: You train outdoors regularly — park workouts, beach sessions, backyard conditioning. You travel and want a reliable anchor that fits in a carry-on. You use resistance bands for rowing, pressing, or sprint work and are tired of improvising anchors.

**Skip it if**: You train exclusively indoors on hard floors. You need an anchor for heavy sled work on pavement. You expect a complete kit with bands included.

## FAQ

**Can this hold a 200-pound person doing heavy banded work?**
Yes, on soft ground. The tri-anchor design distributes load across three points, which prevents tipping. Multiple verified buyers report using it at 200+ pounds without movement. On hard surfaces, it will slide — that's a limitation of the design, not a defect.

**Does it fold down enough for travel?**
It folds to about 8x6x2 inches and weighs 7 pounds. It fits in a backpack or carry-on. No case is included, so wrap it in a towel or clothing to protect your bag from the stake tips.

**What surfaces does it work on?**
Grass, dirt, sand, and soft soil. It will not penetrate concrete, asphalt, or artificial turf. For those surfaces, you'd need a weighted anchor or a different solution entirely.
