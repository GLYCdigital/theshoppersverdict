If you've ever tried to play 4-square with a regular volleyball net, you know the problem: half the court is unusable, the ball rolls into the street, and someone's always arguing over the "real" boundaries.

The ZEALFEEL 4 Square set solves that by creating four actual courts with a cross-shaped net system. Instead of one net and a lot of improvisation, you get a proper arena that turns a backyard into a mini tournament venue. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's clearly working for most buyers—but let's get into the specifics.

## What's actually in the box

The set includes four net panels that connect at the center, eight fiberglass poles, ground stakes, and a carrying bag. The poles are the backbone here—they're not the flimsy hollow aluminum you see on cheap badminton sets. They're sturdy enough to hold tension across all four quadrants without bowing.

Assembly takes about 10 minutes the first time. The nets slide onto the poles with sleeve pockets, and the center connector locks the whole structure together. As shown in the product image, the setup is symmetrical—four equal playing squares around a central hub.

## Performance and real-world use

The headline feature is the adjustability. The poles have three height settings, which matters more than you'd think. Kids at 3.5 feet play a completely different game than adults at full height. I've seen families set this up at cookouts where grandparents and grandkids take turns—being able to drop the net lower for the younger players keeps the game moving instead of stalling.

The center poles do a solid job of holding the net taut. On grass with the included stakes, it handles moderate wind without collapsing. The fiberglass poles flex rather than snap, which is the right call for a product that's going to take hits from stray balls and clumsy players.

One thing I appreciate: the net is actually visible. Some budget 4-square sets use thin webbing that disappears in sunlight. This one has proper mesh that you can see clearly from across the yard, which cuts down on "was that in or out?" disputes.

## What buyers consistently praise

The dominant theme in reviews is the "instant party" factor. One verified buyer wrote: *"Bought this for a family reunion and it was the most-used item all weekend. Eight kids cycled through for hours without fighting."* Another mentioned using it for PE classes: *"My students ask for it every Friday. It's held up to weekly use for three months."*

The portability also gets consistent mentions. It breaks down into the included bag, and at roughly 15 pounds total, it's easy to toss in a trunk for park trips or beach days (with the sand-stake caveat below).

## Where it falls short

The height adjustment mechanism is functional but not elegant. You're loosening wing nuts on each pole, sliding the inner section, and re-tightening. It's a two-minute job, but if you're planning to switch between kids and adults multiple times in one afternoon, it gets tedious.

The carry bag is the weakest link. It's thin nylon with a drawstring—fine for storage, but the pole ends can poke through if you pack it carelessly. One reviewer noted their bag ripped after the third trip. It won't affect the game, but it's worth knowing.

Finally, the stakes are designed for grass. On sand or hard court, you'll need to improvise—sand stakes or sandbags are a must for beach play.

## Who should buy this

This is ideal for three specific groups:

- **Families with kids aged 6–14** who want a backyard game that actually engages multiple children at once
- **PE teachers and camp counselors** who need a durable, portable activity that works for different age groups
- **Tailgaters and park regulars** who want something more organized than a frisbee toss

It's less ideal for serious volleyball players—this is a casual game set, not a regulation net. And if you're exclusively a beach player, factor in the extra cost of sand stakes.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a backyard game that gets everyone involved—kids, adults, mixed ages—without the chaos of a traditional net setup. The adjustability is a genuine differentiator, and the build quality justifies the price for a product that will survive a full summer of use.
