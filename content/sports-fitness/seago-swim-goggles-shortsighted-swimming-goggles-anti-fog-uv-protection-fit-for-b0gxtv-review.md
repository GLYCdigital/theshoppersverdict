If you’re nearsighted and tired of squinting at the lane line or swimming blind without your glasses, the Seago Shortsighted Swim Goggles are a practical, budget-friendly fix. They’re not perfect — no sub-$30 goggle is — but they solve the one problem that matters most: you can actually see where you’re going.

**What’s in the box**  
You get the goggles themselves, a soft microfiber pouch (which doubles as an anti-fog protector if you remember to use it), and a hard plastic case that survives being thrown in a gym bag. The lenses come in a range of standard diopters — from -1.5 up to -8.0 — so you can match your prescription without paying for custom lenses. The frame is a standard one-size shape, with a split silicone strap that adjusts at both temples.

**Performance and build quality**  
The first thing you notice is the seal. The silicone gasket is soft and conforms to most face shapes without the “suction cup” headache you get from cheaper goggles. I swam 2,000 meters with these and didn’t get the raccoon-eye marks that usually appear after 20 minutes. The lenses are polycarbonate with a scratch-resistant coating, and the anti-fog treatment actually works — for a while. Expect about 20–25 sessions before fog starts creeping in around the edges. That’s normal for this price point; just carry the included pouch and wipe the lenses with a drop of baby shampoo when it happens.

UV protection is listed at 100% UVA/UVB, and while that’s accurate, it’s not polarized. If you swim in an outdoor pool midday, you’ll still get glare off the water surface. Fine for lap swimming, less ideal for open water on bright days.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The most common theme in the 220 ratings is clarity. One reviewer wrote, *“I’ve bought three pairs of ‘prescription’ goggles before and they were all blurry. These are actually sharp — I can read the pace clock from the other end of the pool.”* Another noted the strap doesn’t slip during starts: *“I do flip turns every lap and these don’t budge. No water leaks in, either.”* The fit gets repeat mentions — multiple people with narrow faces say the seal holds without overtightening.

**Where it falls short**  
The nose bridge is fixed, not adjustable. If you have a wide nasal bridge, you’ll feel pressure after 30 minutes. There’s no spare nose piece in the box, so you’re stuck with what you get. The anti-fog coating also degrades faster if you touch the inside of the lens — which people do, constantly, despite the warning sticker. And the UV protection, while present, isn’t rated for extended sun exposure; it’s fine for a 45-minute workout, but don’t treat these as your open-water racing goggles.

**Who should buy these**  
- **Recreational lap swimmers** who need corrective vision and swim 2–3 times a week.  
- **Triathletes** looking for a backup pair that won’t break the bank.  
- **Parents** buying for teens who keep losing or scratching their goggles.  

**Skip them if** you have a very wide or narrow face (fixed bridge), if you need polarized lenses for bright outdoor swimming, or if your prescription exceeds -8.0 — you’ll need custom lenses elsewhere.

**FAQ**  

*Q: Do these fit over contact lenses?*  
A: Yes, but if you’re wearing contacts, you don’t need the prescription lens. The non-prescription version is cheaper and lighter.  

*Q: How do I stop them from fogging?*  
A: Rinse with fresh water after every swim, let them air dry fully, and never wipe the inside with a towel. When fog appears, apply a drop of baby shampoo, spread it thin, rinse, and air dry. That buys you another month.  

*Q: Are the lenses actual glass?*  
A: No, they’re polycarbonate — lighter and shatter-resistant, which you want near your eyes. They scratch if you drop them on concrete, so use the included case.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you need affordable, functional vision correction for pool swimming and don’t demand pro-level optics. **Skip it if** you need an adjustable bridge or polarized lenses for extreme conditions.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, no-nonsense goggle that does exactly what it claims. The fixed nose bridge and non-polarized lenses keep it from a perfect score, but for the price, it’s one of the better prescription options on Amazon right now.
