The box contains one single-sided banner flag measuring a true 3 feet by 5 feet. The print is screen-applied to 110-denier polyester — the industry standard for this price tier. You get the official NFL shield and team logo, plus the team name in bold lettering across the face. **Real-world performance**

The critical question for any outdoor flag is simple: does it survive a Tuesday thunderstorm? The construction here is better than the $10 gas-station specials. The fly-end hem is double-stitched with a reinforced edge — the most common failure point on cheap flags — and buyers consistently report the stitching holds. One reviewer noted, "Hung in late August, still sharp after 40+ mph wind gusts in October." The screen printing is another strength; colors stay saturated without the cracking you see on lower-quality transfers.

The tradeoff is the fabric weight. This is a lightweight polyester, not a heavy woven nylon. That means it flies (literally) in light breezes — great for visibility — but in sustained coastal or high-desert wind, it will wear faster than a $40 heavyweight flag. If you live in Chicago or coastal Florida, expect 8–12 months of life before fraying starts. In moderate climates, buyers report 2+ seasons.

**Where it falls short**

The single-sided print is the biggest caveat. The front is bold and correct; the back is a mirror image with muted colors. If your flag will be viewed from both sides — say, mounted perpendicular to the street — the reverse side looks noticeably washed out. This is standard at this price point, but worth knowing before you buy.

Also, the mounting hardware is not included. You'll need a flagpole, halyard clips, or a house mount bracket. Factor in $15–30 extra if you don't already have a pole.
