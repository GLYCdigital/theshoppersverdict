# Precise Complete Teen Golf Club Set for Ages 13–16 – Inc

**The Short Version:** Most "junior" golf sets are either toys or adult clubs with shorter shafts bolted on. The Precise Complete Teen Set actually respects the fact that a 14-year-old swinging 85 mph needs different equipment than a 40-year-old weekend warrior. For families with a teen who's serious (or getting serious) about golf, this is the set to beat under $300 — but it's not without compromises.

---

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find a genuine 12-club configuration:

- **Driver:** 460cc titanium head with graphite shaft
- **Fairway woods:** 3-wood and 5-wood
- **Irons:** 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and pitching wedge
- **Putter:** Heel-toe weighted mallet
- **Stand bag:** With dual shoulder straps and rain hood

Missing from the box: headcovers (more on that below). The clubs themselves are sized specifically for teens in the 5'1" to 5'8" range — which is the actual age group this targets. The driver measures 43 inches, roughly two inches shorter than a standard men's driver, with lighter overall swing weights throughout the set.

## Build Quality and Performance

Let's be direct: this isn't tour-grade equipment. But it's genuinely solid for the price point. The titanium driver face is hot enough that a decent teen swing will launch the ball with respectable distance — you're not losing 30 yards to an adult set. The graphite shafts are forgiving on mishits, and the perimeter-weighted irons (cavity backs, essentially) make thin contact playable.

Where this set truly earns its keep is the progression. Your teen can learn on this set for two or three seasons before they outgrow it — and by then, they'll know exactly what they want in a "real" upgrade. That's worth real money.

Parents consistently report the same thing: their kids hit the ball farther and straighter with these than with hand-me-down adult clubs. One reviewer noted, *"My 15-year-old son gained 20 yards off the tee the first time he used this set. The lighter shafts made an immediate difference."*

Another common quote: *"We bought a $500 set last year and it was too heavy. This one actually fits him. He's playing 9 holes every weekend now."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws.

**The bag is the weak link.** The stand mechanism works, but the zippers feel like they came from a $30 luggage set. If your teen is rough on gear — and most are — expect the side pocket zipper to start catching within a season. The straps are padded but not ergonomically shaped; fine for a driving range session, less comfortable for a full 18-hole carry.

**No headcovers.** For a set in this price range, this feels like an oversight. The titanium driver is the most expensive component in the box, and without a cover, it'll inevitably get banged around in the trunk. You can buy a three-pack of generic covers for $15 on Amazon — do that immediately.

**The putter is basic.** It's a decent mallet shape, but the face insert is minimal and the feel is dead-ish. For a teen who's actually putting for bogey or better, this will be the first club they'll want to replace.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- Your teen is 13–16, roughly 5'1"–5'8", and growing into the game
- You want a complete set that won't need upgrading for 2–3 seasons
- Your budget is under $300 and you'd rather spend on lessons than equipment

**Skip it if:**
- Your teen is already 5'9" or taller — you'll want standard adult clubs
- They're already shooting in the 80s — at that point, individual fitted clubs make sense
- You need a bag that'll survive airline travel or daily cart abuse

## FAQ

**Q: Will this set work for a 13-year-old who's barely 5 feet tall?**
A: Probably not ideal. The driver is 43 inches, which suits someone closer to 5'4". For a smaller teen, look at the youth version of this set or have the clubs cut down by a pro shop.

**Q: Are these clubs legal for high school tournament play?**
A: Yes. The driver is conforming (460cc, within COR limits) and the set meets USGA equipment standards. No concerns for junior league or high school competition.

**Q: How long will these last before my teen needs new clubs?**
A: Expect 2–3 seasons of regular play. The shafts and heads will hold up; the grips will wear out first, and you'll likely replace the bag before anything else. If your teen grows more than 3 inches, you'll need to reassess length.
