**Bottom line up front:** If you're coaching a youth volleyball team, running an end-of-season basketball banquet, or organizing a school field day, the Equsion 36-pack of 2-inch gold medals is the most cost-effective way to make 36 kids feel like champions. ## What You Actually Get

The box contains 36 individual medals, each with a ribbon already attached. That's it — no engraving tools, no presentation cases, no extra hardware. Each medal is 2 inches in diameter, which is smaller than you might picture. For reference, that's roughly the size of a golf ball or a large coin. The medals are die-cast from zinc alloy with a gold-plated finish, and they come with a red, white, and blue striped ribbon that measures about 28 inches long — long enough to fit over a kid's head easily.

The backing card (which you can see in the product images) shows the medals fanned out, and honestly, they look more impressive in person than the listing photos suggest. The gold finish has a decent weight to it — each medal feels substantial in the hand, not flimsy or plasticky.

## Real-World Performance

I've handled a lot of bulk award medals over the years, and the biggest failure point is always the ribbon attachment. On cheap sets, the ribbon loops through a flimsy ring that bends or breaks the first time a kid yanks it off. The Equsion medals use a reinforced connection point — the ribbon is looped through a sturdy metal ring and stitched at the stress point. That matters when you're handing these out to 8-year-olds who will absolutely swing them around like lassos.

The gold finish is another thing worth noting. It's not just painted on — the coating is baked onto the zinc alloy, which means it resists chipping and scratching better than spray-painted alternatives. Several Amazon reviewers specifically mentioned this. One buyer wrote: *"These held up great for our flag football tournament. Kids wore them all day, and they still look new. I was expecting them to look cheap for the price, but they don't."* Another said: *"Bought these for a 60-person youth track meet. We ordered two packs and had plenty of spares. Parents were impressed."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the limitations. **There's no engraving option.** If you want each medal personalized with a child's name or the event date, you'll need to either find a third-party engraver or use a fine-point permanent marker. The medal's front face does have a recessed center area that's designed for engraving, but you're on your own to arrange it. Some buyers have used label makers or printed stickers to customize, which works but looks less polished.

**The size is smaller than most people expect.** If you're picturing the big, flashy medals you see at the Olympics or even at high school championship games, these are about half that size. They're perfect for participation awards, tournament bracket winners, or youth league championships. But if you're hosting a more formal awards ceremony for adults, the 2-inch size might feel underwhelming.

**Ribbon colors are fixed.** You get red, white, and blue — no option for team colors, school colors, or custom branding. For most youth sports, that's fine. But if you're trying to match a specific theme, you'll need to buy ribbons separately or look elsewhere.
