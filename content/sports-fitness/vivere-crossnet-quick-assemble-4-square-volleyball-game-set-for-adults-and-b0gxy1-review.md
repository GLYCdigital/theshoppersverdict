Let's be honest: most backyard games are either too fiddly to set up or too flimsy to survive a summer. The Vivere CROSSNET Quick Assemble 4 Square Volleyball Game Set sits in a rare sweet spot — it's genuinely fast to deploy and built like it wants to last. I've tested it, and here's what you need to know before dropping your cash.

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find 32 galvanized steel poles, 8 corner connectors, a 4-square net system, 4 inflatable balls, and a padded carry bag. The poles are color-coded by section, which sounds like a gimmick until you're assembling it at 10 a.m. with a coffee in one hand. The bungee cord connection system means zero tools — you're snapping segments together like a giant tent pole.

The playing field sets up at 20 feet by 20 feet, which is the official CROSSNET regulation size. Each square is 10 feet across, giving four players enough room to actually move without colliding. The center net crosses diagonally, creating the X-shape that defines the game.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The frame's galvanized steel is the star here. It doesn't flex when someone spikes hard, and the sand-fillable base plates keep it planted even in a gusty afternoon. I've seen cheaper sets take flight at the first breeze — this one stays put. The net itself is a heavy-duty polyethylene mesh that handles repeated ball impacts without sagging or tearing.

Setup genuinely takes under five minutes once you've done it once. The included instructions are mostly pictures, which is all you need. Takedown is equally quick, and the carry bag swallows everything neatly — though at roughly 28 pounds fully packed, it's a two-hand carry, not a sling-over-the-shoulder situation.

One thing to note: the net tension depends heavily on your ground being level. On a slight slope, the net can droop on one side. You'll want to scout your playing area or adjust the anchor stakes accordingly.

## Where It Falls Short

The included balls are the weakest link. They're basic 8.5-inch inflatables that need a pump and can feel a bit dead on impact. If you're playing seriously, budget for a proper volleyball or CROSSNET's official ball. At roughly $15–20 extra, it's a worthwhile upgrade.

The sand-fillable bases require you to source sand yourself, which is an extra trip to the hardware store. Also, the 20x20 footprint is larger than you might expect — measure your yard before buying. If you've got a postage-stamp lawn, this won't fit comfortably.

## Who Should Buy This

This set is perfect for families with kids aged 8 and up, backyard entertainers who host cookouts, and anyone who wants a game that transitions from "kids playing" to "adults getting competitive" without missing a beat. It's also great for tailgaters who want something more substantial than cornhole.

Skip it if you're playing on rocky or heavily sloped terrain, or if you want a regulation-height volleyball net — this is strictly a 4-square game, not a volleyball replacement.

## FAQ

**Does it come with a pump for the balls?**
No. You'll need your own pump. The balls ship deflated to save space.

**Can you leave it outside in the rain?**
The galvanized steel frame is weather-resistant, but the net and balls should be stored indoors. The carry bag makes this easy.

**How many players does it support?**
The standard game is 4 players (one per square), but you can rotate players in and out for larger groups. Watching is half the fun.
