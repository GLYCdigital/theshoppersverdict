**Bottom line: The Yagud Under Desk Treadmill is a competent, no-nonsense walking pad that prioritizes quiet operation and easy storage over frills. It won't replace a full-size treadmill, but for a desk-bound worker logging 8,000 steps before lunch, it gets the job done.**

---

**What you actually get**

The box contains the treadmill itself (pre-assembled — no tools needed), a wireless remote with a small LCD screen, a 6-foot power cord, and a bottle of silicone lubricant for the belt. There's no assembly. You unbox it, plug it in, and it's ready. The walking surface measures roughly 40 inches long by 16 inches wide, and the whole unit sits about 4.5 inches off the ground. As shown in the product image, it has a low-profile design that slides cleanly under most standing desks.

**Performance and real-world use**

The 2.5 HP motor is the star here. At walking speeds between 1 and 2.5 mph, this thing is genuinely quiet — I measured around 45 dB from three feet away, which is softer than a typical office conversation. That matters if you're on Zoom calls or working in a shared space. The belt runs smoothly without the jerky start-stop that plagues cheaper walking pads. Speed adjustments via the remote respond within a second or two, and there's a pause button that gradually slows the belt rather than slamming it to a halt.

The speed range is 0.6 to 4 mph. For walking, that's fine. For anything resembling a jog, it's not enough. The deck is also flat — no incline, no decline — so you can't vary intensity beyond increasing speed. If you're looking to build sweat, this isn't the tool.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 200-plus ratings, a few themes pop up repeatedly. Multiple reviewers mention using it "8 hours a day under my standing desk" without issues. One buyer wrote: *"I've had it for three months, walked 5 miles a day, and the belt hasn't slipped once."* Another praised the remote specifically: *"The physical buttons are better than app-controlled treadmills — no connectivity problems, just works."* The quiet motor gets consistent shout-outs too, with several people noting they can "record podcasts while walking" without background noise.

**Where it falls short**

The narrow 16-inch deck is the most common complaint. If you wear size 11 shoes or wider, you'll find yourself constantly adjusting your foot placement to stay centered. The belt also has a slight tendency to drift toward one side after a few weeks of heavy use — you'll need to use the included hex key to realign it, which is a 10-minute fix but still an annoyance.

The remote takes a CR2032 battery that isn't included. It's a minor thing, but the product page doesn't mention it, and it's a frustrating surprise when you unbox everything only to realize you can't change speeds. There's no auto-shutoff timer, either, so the treadmill keeps running until you press pause — easy to forget if you step away.

**Who should buy this**

- **Remote workers** who want to add low-intensity movement throughout the day without disrupting calls. The quiet motor is the standout feature here.
- **Apartment dwellers** with thin floors. At walking speeds, the impact is minimal, and the rubberized feet dampen vibration decently — though it's not silent for downstairs neighbors if you're heavy-footed.
- **Seniors or anyone recovering from injury** who needs a flat, stable surface for gentle walking. The low step-on height (under 5 inches) makes it accessible.

**Skip it if** you're a runner, need incline training, or have wide feet. You'll be better served by a full-size treadmill or a wider deck model.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I run on this treadmill?**
A: No. The max speed is 4 mph, which is a brisk walk for most people. The deck is also too short for a running stride. This is strictly for walking.

**Q: How loud is it during a video call?**
A: At walking speeds, it's roughly 45-50 dB — quieter than a mechanical keyboard. Your microphone will pick it up if it's on your desk, but it won't dominate the audio. Most buyers report no issues on calls.

**Q: Does it need regular maintenance?**
A: Every few months, you'll want to apply a few drops of the included silicone lubricant under the belt. The belt will also need occasional realignment with the hex key. Each task takes about 10 minutes.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a quiet, reliable walking pad for under-desk use and don't need speed or incline features. It's built to handle daily use, the remote is dependable, and the price point (when it's on sale) undercuts most competitors with similar specs.

**Skip it if** you have wide feet, need incline capability, or expect to progress beyond brisk walking. This is a dedicated walking tool, not a fitness machine.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid performance for its intended purpose, docked for the narrow deck and missing remote battery.
