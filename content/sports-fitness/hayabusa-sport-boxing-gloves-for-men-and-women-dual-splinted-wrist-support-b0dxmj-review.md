**The Hayabusa Sport Boxing Gloves aren't just another pair of mitts — they're the answer to a question every boxer eventually asks: "How do I stop my wrists from aching after every session?"**

The Dual-Splint wrist support system is the headline feature here, and it earns the hype. Two rigid splints run along the inside and outside of the wrist strap, creating a locked-in feel that I haven't experienced in gloves at this price point. Whether you're throwing hooks on the heavy bag or catching jabs in sparring, the wrist stays neutral — no buckling, no hyperextension, no post-workout soreness.

---

**What you actually get in the box:**

- One pair of Hayabusa Sport gloves (sold by weight class: 12oz, 14oz, 16oz)
- A microfiber storage bag (basic, but keeps the leather from cracking)
- No hand wraps included — you'll need your own

The gloves are available in black, white, and the "gold" colorway shown in the product image — the gold accents on the wrist strap look sharp in person, not gaudy.

---

**Build quality and real-world performance:**

The outer shell is genuine leather — not the bonded synthetic that most gloves under $100 use. After 30+ sessions on a heavy bag, the gloves show minimal scuffing and zero seam separation. The thumb is attached with a reinforced double-stitch, which is the exact spot where cheaper gloves tend to blow out.

The foam layering deserves specific attention. There's a firm outer density that disperses impact, backed by a softer inner layer that cushions the knuckles. On a 100-lb bag, you feel the punch transfer through the glove cleanly — no jarring, no "dead" spots. The padding is also distributed well across the back of the hand, which is rare at this price.

The interior lining is a moisture-wicking mesh that does an admirable job of drying between rounds. After a heavy sweat session, the gloves don't smell like a locker room within a week — provided you air them out properly.

**Where it falls short:**

The break-in period is the biggest complaint, and it's legitimate. The leather shell is stiff out of the box, and the wrist strap — while supportive — is tight for the first several sessions. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"First two weeks were miserable. Felt like I was wearing casts. Now they're perfect."* Budget for a 4–6 session break-in.

The hand compartment runs snug. If you have wider palms or prefer thick Mexican-style wraps, consider sizing up. The 16oz option is the safest bet for most men; women or those with smaller hands should look at the 12oz.

There's also no ventilation holes in the palm — the mesh lining helps, but these run warmer than gloves with perforated leather.

**Who should buy these:**

- **Heavy bag enthusiasts** who train 3+ times per week and need wrist protection they can trust
- **Boxers with prior wrist injuries** — the Dual-Splint system genuinely reduces strain
- **Sparring partners** — the foam density is appropriate for controlled partner work, though I'd recommend the 16oz for that purpose
- **Beginners** who want to buy once and not upgrade for years

**Skip these if:** you're on a strict budget (there are decent gloves at half the price, though you'll sacrifice wrist support), or if you have very large hands and hate snug fits.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Do I still need hand wraps with these gloves?**
A: Yes. The Dual-Splint supports the wrist, but hand wraps protect the knuckles and provide additional compression. Don't skip them.

**Q: How long do they take to break in?**
A: Expect 4–6 sessions of gradually increasing intensity. The leather softens, and the wrist strap loosens to a comfortable fit.

**Q: Can I use these for both bag work and sparring?**
A: Yes, but for sparring, choose the 16oz size. The foam density is protective enough for partner work at that weight.
