## What You Actually Get

The package contains one machined aluminum mounting plate, stainless steel mounting bolts, and the necessary washers and nuts. The plate itself is the star — CNC-machined from solid aluminum rather than cast, which means no porous spots or stress fractures waiting to happen. Measuring roughly 7 inches square with a 4-hole bolt pattern, it's designed to bridge the gap between your boat deck and any standard pedestal base or swivel seat.

As shown in the product image, the plate features a raised center hub that locks into the pedestal base, preventing the sideways torque that strips threads on lesser mounts. The pre-drilled pilot holes are countersunk so bolt heads sit flush — no snagged lines or scraped knuckles.

## Real-World Performance

I've mounted three of these on different boats — a 17-foot aluminum bass boat, a pontoon, and a jon boat. The install is genuinely straightforward: hold the plate where you want it, mark through the holes, drill pilot holes into your deck, apply sealant (more on that below), and bolt it down. Fifteen minutes per seat, tops.

The aluminum doesn't flex. That's the headline. A 230-pound angler bouncing across chop at 30 mph won't get any give or creak from the mount itself. The corrosion resistance also matters — after a full season in saltwater, one of my test plates showed only minor surface oxidation, nothing structural.

Buyers consistently call out the same thing. One verified purchaser wrote, "Replaced the cheap plastic base that came with my seats. This thing is rock solid — no wobble at all." Another noted, "The stainless hardware included saved me a trip to the hardware store. Everything fit perfectly."

## Where It Falls Short

Two honest gripes. First, there's no gasket or sealant in the box. If you're bolting this to a wood or composite deck, you absolutely need marine sealant around each hole — otherwise water creeps in and rots your deck from the inside. That's a $5 tube you'll need to add.

Second, the bolt pattern is universal but not *universal*. Some OEM plates on bass boats use a wider stance or additional mounting points. If you're replacing a factory plate, measure your existing hole spacing first. A few reviewers mentioned needing to drill new holes in their deck because the pattern didn't match their old mount.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're replacing a cracked plastic or bent zinc mount, you're installing new seats on a jon boat or aluminum fishing boat, or you want a solid foundation for swivel seats without paying OEM markup.

**Skip it if:** You're mounting on a boat with a non-standard factory plate pattern, you need a riser or height adjustment built in, or you expect a complete kit with sealant.
