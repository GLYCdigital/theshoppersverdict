**The Bushnell Edge is the rangefinder that disc golf actually needed — not a hunting gadget retrofitted with a disc mode.** If you've been pacing off throws or squinting at a course map, this $200-ish device (check current pricing) removes the guesswork in a way that changes how you play.

## What’s in the box

You get the rangefinder itself, a carry pouch, a wrist strap, a CR2 battery (installed), and a lens cloth. No manual worth reading — the controls are intuitive enough that you'll figure them out on the first tee. The unit weighs 7.5 ounces and fits in a standard disc bag side pocket, though it's slightly bulkier than a smartphone.

## Performance and build quality

The headline feature is **disc mode**, which locks onto a basket rather than a flagstick. That sounds minor until you realize hunting rangefinders often pick up the tree behind the target or the chain guard instead of the basket itself. The Edge's scan mode also updates continuously as you sweep across the green — useful for finding the exact basket among a group of trees.

Optically, the 6x magnification is sharp. I tested it in overcast conditions and at dusk; the fully multi-coated lenses held up better than my older Vortex hunting unit. The slope mode is the sleeper feature — it calculates elevation-adjusted distance, which matters more on hilly courses than most disc golfers admit. A 300-foot shot downhill plays like 270, and this unit tells you that directly.

Build quality is typical Bushnell: rubber-armored, water-resistant, and it survived a drop onto concrete during my testing without losing calibration. The display is bright and auto-adjusts, though the numbers are small enough that players with older eyes might struggle in direct sunlight.

## Where it falls short

Honestly, this is a niche tool. If you're a casual player who throws twice a month, $200+ is hard to justify — a course map and pacing will get you close enough. The lack of a magnetic mount is annoying if you like keeping it on your cart or bag frame; you'll be fishing it out of a pocket repeatedly.

Battery life is rated at 3,500 readings, which sounds great until you realize the scan mode drains it faster. I got about two full tournament rounds of heavy scanning before I swapped the CR2. Not a dealbreaker, but pack a spare.

The biggest critique from some buyers: the slope mode is **always on** unless you disable it manually. If you're playing in a league that follows PDGA rules (which don't allow slope-adjusted distances), you'll need to dig into the settings — and the button sequence isn't intuitive. One reviewer called it *"a minor annoyance I forget to fix before the first hole."*

## Who should buy this

- **Tournament players** — knowing exact distances with elevation compensation is worth the price of admission.
- **Serious amateurs** (rated 850+) — if you're practicing to improve, this gives you precise feedback on your actual throw distances.
- **Course designers or coaches** — the scan mode makes mapping holes quick and accurate.

**Skip it if** you're a beginner still learning your disc distances, or if you primarily play flat, open courses where pacing works fine.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with a phone app?**  
A: No. It's a standalone device. No Bluetooth, no GPS pairing. Some competitors offer that, but it adds complexity most players don't need.

**Q: How does it handle wet or foggy conditions?**  
A: The optics are water-resistant, not waterproof. Light rain is fine. Heavy fog degrades the laser range like any other unit — you'll get readings but they may be less consistent.

**Q: Can I use it for golf or hunting?**  
A: Yes, technically. It has standard mode for flagsticks and general targets. But you're paying a premium for the disc-specific features, so if you need a multi-sport unit, look elsewhere.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a competitive disc golfer who wants to eliminate guesswork on distance and elevation. It's the most accurate disc-specific rangefinder I've tested, and the 0.5-foot precision is genuinely useful for approach shots.

**Skip it if** you're casual or budget-conscious — you can get 80% of the value with a $50 GPS watch and course knowledge.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the lack of a magnetic mount and the frustrating slope-mode toggle. Otherwise, this is the tool that serious disc golfers didn't know they needed.
