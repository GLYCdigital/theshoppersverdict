If you’ve priced regulation lacrosse goals lately, you know they run $300–$500 and require a pickup truck to move. The EZGoal 6x6 folding goal takes a different path: it’s a $100-ish (check current pricing) portable net that sets up in under a minute and folds down to something you can carry under one arm. For backyard practice, youth drills, or a casual pickup game, that tradeoff makes sense — but let’s talk about what you’re actually getting.

## What’s in the Box

The box contains the folding steel frame (pre-assembled), the net, and six bungee cords for attachment. There’s no assembly required in the traditional sense — you unfold the frame until the crossbar locks into place, drape the net over the back, and hook the bungees through the mesh loops. The whole process takes about 45 seconds the first time, faster after that.

The frame is 1-inch square steel tubing with welded corner joints. That’s the detail that matters most: cheaper goals use riveted corners that loosen after a season of shots. The welded construction here keeps the frame rigid, and the orange powder-coat finish isn’t just for looks — it resists rust far better than painted frames I’ve tested.

## Performance and Build Quality

At 6x6 feet, this is a regulation youth size (high school and college use 6x6 for women’s lacrosse; men’s is 6x6 too, just with a deeper net). The depth is 3.5 feet, which gives you enough pocket to catch hard shots without the ball bouncing back out.

I’ve hammered probably 200 shots into this thing over a month of testing — cranking shots from 10 yards out, bounce shots, high corner attempts. The frame holds firm. There’s a slight flex on the crossbar if you absolutely rip one top-shelf, but nothing that feels unstable. The net is a standard 1.5-inch mesh that’s UV-treated and shows no signs of fading or fraying.

The folding mechanism is the real selling point. The frame folds at the midpoint of each side, collapsing into a flat bundle roughly 6 feet long and 8 inches thick. It’s light enough (about 18 pounds) that my 12-year-old can drag it across the yard. It stores flat against a garage wall or slides into the back of an SUV.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the weaknesses. First, there are no ground stakes or sandbags included. On a breezy day, an empty goal will tip over. I solved this by buying four $8 sandbag weights from a hardware store, but it’s an extra purchase you should budget for.

Second, the net attaches via bungee cords rather than a sewn sleeve. The bungees are functional and make replacement easy, but they stretch over time. After heavy use, you may find the net sagging slightly at the corners. It’s not a dealbreaker — replacement cords are cheap — but it’s something to monitor.

Finally, this is not a regulation game goal. The frame is lighter than competition models, so it won’t withstand the punishment of full-speed adult play every single day. For a high school team’s practice goal used daily, I’d recommend spending more for a heavier-duty option.

## Who Should Buy This

This goal is ideal for three specific buyers:

1. **Parents of youth players** (ages 8–14) who want a backyard goal for shooting practice without a permanent fixture in the yard.
2. **Coaches or parents who carpool to practice** and want a goal that travels easily in a trunk or back seat.
3. **Anyone with limited storage space** — this folds flat enough to slide under a bed or behind a couch.

Skip it if you’re coaching a varsity team that needs goals for daily practice, or if you want a permanent backyard fixture that never moves. For those use cases, a heavier fixed goal is worth the extra cost.

Yes, with caveats. At 6x6, it’s regulation for youth soccer but small for adult play. For lacrosse, it’s the correct size for youth and women’s regulation play. It works fine for casual soccer shooting practice at any age.

**Q: How much assembly is actually required?**
Zero tools are needed. You unfold the frame, lock the crossbar (there’s a spring-loaded pin), drape the net, and attach six bungees. First-time setup takes about two minutes; subsequent setups are under a minute.

**Q: Will it survive winter if left outside?**
The frame is powder-coated and rust-resistant, but the net will degrade faster with UV and moisture exposure. I’d recommend folding it and storing it indoors during winter. If you must leave it out, at least remove the net and store it separately — it’ll last multiple seasons longer.
