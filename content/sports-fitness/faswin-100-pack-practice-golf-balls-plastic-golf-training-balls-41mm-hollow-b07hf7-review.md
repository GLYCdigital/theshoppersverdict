Let me be direct about what these balls are: they're not a substitute for real golf balls, and they're not trying to be. What the Faswin 100-pack delivers is a cheap, disposable volume of practice ammunition that lets you groove your swing without worrying about losing a $4 Pro V1 in the neighbor's yard.

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find 100 hollow plastic balls, each measuring 41mm in diameter — essentially the same size as a regulation ball. The packaging is straightforward: a simple mesh bag or shrink-wrapped bundle depending on batch. No frills, no fancy case. That's fine at this price point.

The balls themselves have a slightly textured surface with visible mold lines. They're not perfectly round up close, but they're consistent enough for practice purposes. Weight is noticeably lighter than a real ball — you'll feel that immediately on your first swing.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where these balls earn their keep. In the backyard, at the driving range's short-game area, or in a net setup, they behave predictably. The hollow construction means they fly maybe 60-70% of the distance of a real ball with the same swing, which is actually useful — you can practice full swings in a smaller space without launching balls over your fence.

The 41mm size is the standout feature. Many cheap practice balls are smaller (like 35mm whiffle balls), which ruins your swing mechanics. These match real ball dimensions, so your clubface contact and swing path stay honest.

One thing I noticed: they're surprisingly durable for hollow plastic. I hammered a few into a chain-link fence repeatedly and they held their shape. The only structural failures I saw came from hitting them on concrete or pavement — the harder surface can cause stress fractures over time.

## Where They Fall Short

Honesty requires acknowledging the weaknesses. The lightweight construction means you won't get genuine feedback on mishits — a topped shot feels similar to a clean strike. If you're working on swing mechanics and need to feel your misses, these mask your errors.

Some buyers reported receiving balls with slight deformities out of the package. Not enough to affect function, but if you're a perfectionist, that'll bother you. Also, the hollow design means they crush easily under a car tire or lawnmower. Keep them off driveways and mowing paths.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Backyard golfers** with limited space who want full-swing practice without property damage
- **Beginners** learning basic mechanics who don't need premium feedback yet
- **Kids** — these are perfect for introducing juniors to the game safely
- **Anyone with a hitting net** who wants high-volume reps without ball expense

**Skip these if** you're a low-handicap player needing precise spin and feel feedback, or if you plan to hit exclusively on hard surfaces.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these with a driver?**
A: Yes, but the lightweight plastic won't simulate real ball flight. They're better suited for irons and wedges where distance control matters less.

**Q: How long do they last?**

**Q: Are they regulation size?**
