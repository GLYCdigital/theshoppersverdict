**Rawlings Colorsync Hooded Jacket: The Training Layer That Actually Works**

Most athletic hoodies are either paper-thin shells that rip after one season or heavyweight sweat traps that leave you drenched by the third set. The Rawlings Colorsync Long Sleeve Hooded Jacket sits in a rare middle ground — it's built like baseball training gear should be, with enough structure to handle real movement without cooking you alive.

**What You're Actually Getting**

This is a full-zip hooded jacket in Rawlings' Colorsync fabric, which is their moisture-wicking polyester blend designed for practice and game-day warmups. The cut is athletic — narrower through the waist, roomier through the shoulders and chest. It's not a baggy streetwear hoodie, and it's not compression-tight either. The hood is a standard three-piece construction, and the cuffs have integrated thumb holes.

**Real-World Performance**

I wore this through batting practice, indoor gym sessions, and a few cool-weather runs. The fabric breathes noticeably better than a cotton-blend hoodie — sweat evaporates instead of pooling. The athletic cut earns its keep: full arm extension during swings doesn't pull the hem up or restrict the shoulders.

The hood deserves specific credit. It's cut deep enough to stay on your head during movement, which sounds minor until you've spent a session fighting a hood that slides back mid-rep. The zipper is a YKK-style unit that runs smooth and hasn't snagged in months of use.

The honest downsides matter here. **Sizing is the biggest complaint.** This runs snug. If you're between sizes, go up — especially if you have a wider chest or carry more mass through the midsection. Several reviewers mentioned having to exchange for a size up.

The **thumb holes are narrow** — functional for average hands, but tight if you've got larger hands. And there's **no interior pocket**, which is a genuine miss for a training jacket. Your phone goes in the exterior zip pockets or it doesn't come with you.
