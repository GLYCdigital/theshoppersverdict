**The Rigel 600lbs 6-in-1 Bench: One Station to Rule Your Home Gym, or a Compromise in Every Direction?**

Most "6-in-1" fitness contraptions are junk pretending to be versatile. The Rigel 600lbs adjustable Olympic weight bench set is not that. It's a genuinely functional multi-station that trades a bit of perfection in each movement for one compact footprint. After digging through 210 ratings (4.2 stars), here's what you need to know before you buy.

**What's in the Box — and What You're Actually Building**

You get the main adjustable bench (8 backrest positions, 4 seat angles), a squat/rack stand system, Olympic-length barbell rests, preacher curl pad, leg developer with adjustable rollers, lat pulldown cable attachment, and a landmine attachment. The frame is 2x2" commercial-gauge steel with a 600lb weight capacity rating — that's the bench itself, not the cable station, so be clear on that.

As shown in the product image, the setup is more compact than separate units. You're looking at roughly 6 feet long and 4 feet wide when fully assembled. It's not a rack you'll bolt to the floor, but at 110 pounds it's hefty enough not to slide around during heavy pressing.

**Real-World Performance: Where It Shines**

The bench press function is the star. The backrest locks solidly at all angles — no wobble at the 300-pound mark. One verified buyer said, *"I've loaded 405 on the squat stands and the frame didn't flex. The welds are clean and the padding is firmer than my old Bowflex."* The preacher curl station is genuinely usable, not an afterthought — the pad height adjusts to fit most arm lengths.

The leg developer (that's the leg curl/extension attachment) uses a weight plate post rather than a cable stack. That means you load plates directly — more friction than a gym machine, but smoother than most budget options. For a home user doing 3 sets of leg curls, it's completely fine.

**Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself**

The lat pulldown is the weakest link. It's a low pulley system with a straight bar, and the cable routing creates noticeable friction. You'll get maybe 70% of what a dedicated cable tower delivers. It works for light-to-moderate lat work, but don't expect a smooth 200-pound pulldown.

Assembly is the biggest complaint across verified reviews. Multiple buyers mention the instruction manual's exploded diagrams are hard to follow, and hardware bags aren't labeled. Budget 2-3 hours with a socket set and patience. One reviewer summed it up: *"Great bench once it's together, but I almost returned it during assembly."*

The J-hooks for squatting are shallow. If you're re-racking heavy weights, you need to center the bar carefully. This is worth noting if you lift alone without a spotter.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home gym starters** with limited space who want bench press, squat stands, and accessories without buying three separate machines
- **Lifters up to intermediate level** (working sets under 400lbs) who don't need competition-grade precision
- **Apartment or garage users** who value footprint over perfection in every exercise

**Skip it if** you're an advanced powerlifter who needs a dedicated rack with safeties, or if you primarily want a cable machine — buy a separate functional trainer instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the 600lb rating mean I can squat 600lbs on the stands?**
A: Yes, the frame and J-hooks are rated for 600lbs total, but the shallow hooks mean you should use a spotter or safety arms for anything over 300lbs. The bench itself is rated to 600lbs for incline/decline pressing.

**Q: Can I do decline bench presses on this?**
A: Yes — the backrest goes to a -20° decline position. The leg lock rollers hold you in place, and it works well for weighted decline work.

**Q: Is the lat pulldown attachment worth using?**
A: It's functional for moderate weight (up to about 150lbs smoothly). You'll feel cable friction at higher loads. It's a bonus feature, not a replacement for a proper cable station.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a well-built, space-saving multi-station that handles serious bench pressing and squatting without the $1,500 price tag of separate units. **Skip it if** you need a flawless cable machine or competition-grade rack precision.

At 4.2 stars from 210 ratings, the Rigel earns its score — solid steel, honest capacity, and one genuine weakness (the cable system). For most home lifters, this is a smart, practical investment that'll survive years of abuse.
