If you've ever bought an "adult small" glove for a kid and watched it bunch up like a deflated balloon, you know the struggle. The Footjoy Junior Golf Glove exists specifically to fix that problem — and for the most part, it does exactly what it promises.

Here's what you actually get in the package: a single white glove, sized for junior hands, with a cabretta leather palm and synthetic mesh backing. Footjoy didn't just shrink down an adult design — the proportions are genuinely different. The fingers are shorter relative to the palm, and the overall cut is narrower, which matters when you're fitting a 10-year-old who's between sizes.

**Real-World Performance**

The cabretta leather palm is the headline feature here, and it earns its keep. It's the same material Footjoy uses in their adult Tour models, and it provides that tacky, confident grip young players need when they're still developing their swing mechanics. One parent reviewer noted, "My son has gone through three cheap gloves this season. This one has lasted two months of weekly practice and still has life left." That durability is consistent with what I've seen — the leather holds up well against junior swing speeds, which don't generate the same heat and friction as adult swings.

The mesh paneling between the fingers is a thoughtful touch. Kids sweat more than adults relative to their size, and a soggy glove is a slippery glove. The breathability here keeps that from becoming a problem, even on hot summer range sessions.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

Across the 220 ratings, the most consistent theme is fit accuracy. Parents repeatedly mention that junior sizing actually means junior sizing — no more rolling up cuffs or dealing with excess material at the fingertips. One reviewer put it simply: "Finally, a glove that fits my 9-year-old without looking like he's wearing his dad's gear."

The grip quality also gets frequent shout-outs. Multiple parents report their kids have more control over the club, which translates to better contact and increased confidence at the tee box.

**Where It Falls Short**

The white leather is the obvious tradeoff. It looks sharp out of the box, but junior golfers tend to drag their hands on the ground during practice swings, and this glove shows every bit of that. Budget for regular cleaning or accept that it'll look well-worn by mid-season.

Also worth noting: this is a left-hand glove only (for right-handed golfers). If your junior player is a lefty, you'll need to hunt for a specialized version, which often costs more and has fewer size options.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is the right pick for junior golfers who are past the "first glove" stage and playing regularly — think weekly lessons, practice sessions, or junior league play. The cabretta leather gives them a legitimate performance advantage over entry-level synthetic gloves, and the proper fit means they can develop good grip habits without compensating for ill-fitting equipment.

It's also a smart buy for parents tired of replacing cheap gloves every few weeks. The durability here isn't miraculous, but it's clearly better than the $10 synthetic options that fall apart after a handful of rounds.

**FAQ**

**Q: How does the sizing run compared to adult gloves?**
A: True to junior sizing. If your child wears a youth medium in most brands, this will fit similarly. When in doubt, size up — the leather stretches slightly with use.

**Q: Is this glove suitable for left-handed golfers?**
A: No. This is a left-hand glove designed for right-handed players. Left-handed juniors need to look for a right-hand glove version.

**Q: How do I clean the white leather without damaging it?**
A: Use a damp cloth with mild soap, gently wipe the leather, then air dry away from direct heat. Avoid machine washing — it'll break down the leather fibers.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** your junior golfer is playing regularly and needs a glove that fits properly and lasts more than a few weeks. The cabretta leather and true junior sizing justify the price over bargain-bin alternatives.

**Skip it if** your kid only plays a few times a year, or if you're not prepared to deal with white leather that shows dirt quickly. In those cases, a cheaper synthetic glove will serve you just fine.
