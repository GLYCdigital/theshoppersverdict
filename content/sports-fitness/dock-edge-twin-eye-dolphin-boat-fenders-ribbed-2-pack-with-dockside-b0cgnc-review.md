Let me cut to the chase: if you dock a 20-foot bowrider or a pontoon in a slip with any current, you already know the pain of watching your gel coat kiss a concrete piling. The Dock Edge Twin Eye Dolphin fenders are a solid, no-drama answer — with a couple of caveats worth your attention before you add to cart.

## What's Actually in the Package

You get two ribbed vinyl fenders, each measuring 6.5 inches in diameter by 26 inches long. That's a mid-size fender — not the stubby little bumpers that barely reach your rub rail, and not the massive commercial-grade cylinders that take up half your cockpit. Each fender has molded eyes at both ends, which is the "Twin Eye" selling point. No grommets to corrode, no metal hardware to scratch your deck. You'll need to supply your own line; Dock Edge doesn't include rope, so factor that into your budget.

## Real-World Performance

The ribbed surface isn't cosmetic. Those vertical ridges create a cushion of air between the fender and your hull, which measurably reduces the squeaking and grinding you get with smooth fenders when the boat rocks. One reviewer put it plainly: *"These things took a beating on a windy weekend at the lake and my hull came out with zero marks. The ribs really do help."* That tracks with what I'd expect from the material — it's a thick, marine-grade vinyl that feels substantial without being rock-hard.

The twin-eye design earns its keep the first time you dock in a crosswind. Hang it vertically for standard side protection, or run a line through both eyes for horizontal mounting along a swim platform or transom. That flexibility means one set handles most docking scenarios without you carrying four different sizes.

Inflation is straightforward but slightly annoying. The valve is a standard push-pin style, and you'll want a pump with a needle adapter — blowing these up by mouth takes forever and leaves you lightheaded. Several owners mention this in reviews: *"Bring a pump. The valve works fine, but hand-pumping is a workout."* Worth knowing before you're at the dock with a flat fender and no adapter.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 235 ratings at 4.7 stars, the recurring themes are durability and value. Owners with pontoons and aluminum fishing boats particularly like that these fenders don't mark or scuff their rails. The molded eyes — often the first failure point on cheaper fenders — have held up well in sustained use. One long-term owner notes: *"Two seasons of weekend docking, still holding air, no cracks in the eyes. I've paid three times as much for worse."*

## Where It Falls Short

Size is the big one. The 6.5x26-inch dimensions are ideal for boats in the 16-to-24-foot range. If you're docking a 28-foot cruiser or anything with a tall freeboard, these will sit too high or too low depending on your cleat placement — you'll need to buy two sets and stagger them. That's not a defect, but it's a planning consideration.

The inflation valve is a minor frustration. It's positioned flush with the fender surface, which is good for not snagging, but it makes attaching a pump adapter fiddly. And if you overinflate, there's no easy relief valve — you'll push in the pin and try to control the release. Not a dealbreaker, just something to know.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these fenders horizontally?**
A: Yes — that's the point of the twin-eye design. Run a line through both eyes and position them along your gunwale or transom. Just make sure the line is snug enough to prevent sliding.

**Q: Will these fit a 22-foot pontoon?**
A: Generally yes, though you'll want them positioned at the mid-ship and stern where the pontoons meet the deck. For a tritoon, you may need three fenders for full coverage.

**Q: How much air pressure should I use?**
A: Inflate until the fender is firm but still has a slight give when you squeeze it. Overinflating makes them rigid and less effective at absorbing impact. Underinflating means the hull can contact the dock through the fender.
