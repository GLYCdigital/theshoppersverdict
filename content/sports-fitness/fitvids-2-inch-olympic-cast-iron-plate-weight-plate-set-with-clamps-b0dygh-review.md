Let's cut through the noise. If you're shopping for Olympic plates under $2 per pound, you're looking at three options: used junk from Craigslist, rubber-coated stuff that smells like a tire fire, or bare cast iron like the Fitvids set. I've trained on all three, and here's the honest breakdown of what the Fitvids 2-inch plates deliver for the money.

**What you actually get**

The set ships with machined cast iron plates in the standard diameter for Olympic bars (2-inch center hole). The construction is straightforward — no chrome, no urethane coating, just solid gray iron. The center holes are cleanly machined with no sharp edges that'll shred your barbell's knurling, and the plates slide on smooth without the annoying wobble you get on cheaper stamped versions. As shown in the product image, the plates have a slightly raised lip around the outer edge, which gives them a more finished look than flat discs and makes them easier to grip when loading.

**Real-world performance**

I loaded these up for deadlifts, squats, and presses over several weeks. The fit on a standard 20kg Olympic bar is snug but not tight — they slide on easily and sit flush against each other. The included spring clamps are actually usable, which surprised me. Most budget collars are flimsy wire that bends after a week; these hold firm enough that I didn't have plates shifting during heavy sets.

The bare iron surface does have a tradeoff. It's grippy when your hands are chalked, which is good. But it's also porous — leave these in a damp garage and you'll see surface rust within a month. A quick wipe with 3-in-1 oil every couple of weeks handles it, but if you want zero maintenance, look elsewhere.

**What buyers consistently mention**

The 4.2-star rating across 210 reviews tracks with what I found. Owners repeatedly mention the value proposition — you're getting functional Olympic plates without the boutique price tag. One reviewer noted, "These are heavy and solid, exactly what I expected for the price. No complaints about the quality." Another mentioned, "The clamps are better than I thought they'd be — actually hold the plates in place." The consistent praise centers on durability and the no-nonsense design.

**Where it falls short**

The weight tolerance is the biggest honest caveat. These aren't calibrated plates — expect a few ounces to a pound variance from what's stamped. For general strength training, it won't matter. If you're chasing a specific competition weight or doing precise progressive overload, it will.

The second issue is noise and floor damage. These are bare iron — dropping them on a concrete garage floor will chip the plates and mar your floor. You need a deadlift platform or rubber mats. The third thing: no storage hole in the center of the plates, which makes them awkward to carry more than one or two at a time.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home gym owners on a budget** who need functional plates without paying premium for brand names
- **Strength training beginners** who want a full set without investing in calibrated competition plates
- **Garage lifters** who already have rubber flooring and don't mind a little iron patina

**Skip it if** you're a competitive powerlifter needing exact weights, or you train in an apartment where noise is a dealbreaker.
