You've seen the videos: heels elevated, knees tracking forward, butt hitting the grass. The UNMERA Squat Wedge Block 2PCS is a set of two angled plastic ramps designed to do exactly that — fix your squat depth without requiring a double-jointed ankle. At 4.4 stars from 220 buyers, it's a solid mid-tier pick in a crowded market of plywood scraps and flimsy foam. But is it worth your gym bag space? Let's break it down.

## What You Actually Get

Two identical wedge blocks, each roughly 11 inches long and 5.5 inches wide at the base. The height ramps from about 1.5 inches at the low end to 3.5 inches at the peak, giving you a natural slope rather than a flat block. The top surface features a raised grip texture that's aggressive enough to hold your shoes but not so rough it tears up your favorite lifting sneakers. Flip them over and there's a rubberized bottom layer — this is the non-slip claim in action, and it works.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The plastic is dense and rigid. I've seen cheaper wedges that flex and bow under a loaded barbell; these don't. The UNMERA blocks held firm with 315 pounds of back squat — no creaking, no deformation, no sliding on a rubber gym mat. The dual-angle aspect is the standout feature: you can place your heels on the lower slope for a subtle 15-degree elevation or push further up for a steeper 20-degree ramp. That's genuinely useful if you're alternating between squat mechanics work and calf-stretching mobility drills.

One thing the product images don't fully convey: the surface texture is *effective* but not soft. On barefoot or sock-footed work, the grip pattern can dig into your heels after twenty-plus reps. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're doing high-rep goblet squats without shoes, you'll feel it.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The consensus in the reviews is consistent. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"These actually stay put. I was skeptical about the non-slip claim, but they don't move even on my smooth garage floor."* Another mentioned the dual-angle versatility: *"I use the steeper side for ankle mobility before lifting and the lower side for my actual squats. Two tools in one."* The durability praise is also recurring — multiple reviewers note the rigid plastic hasn't cracked or chipped after months of dropped dumbbells and barbell reracks.

## Where It Falls Short

The non-slip base is excellent on hard surfaces, but it's less effective on textured or rubberized gym mats. The rubber grip can't bite into a bumpy surface as well, so you may need to re-position between sets on some commercial gym flooring. Also, the pair is sold as a set of two, which is great for a single lifter — but if you're training with a partner or supersetting two stations, you'll need to buy two sets. That doubles the cost and feels like a missed opportunity to offer a four-pack.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The home gym lifter** with smooth concrete or wood floors who wants to fix squat depth without buying expensive lifting shoes
- **The mobility-focused athlete** doing ankle dorsiflexion drills, calf stretches, or overhead squat progressions
- **The beginner** learning proper squat mechanics — the wedge forces better knee tracking and reduces the compensation of leaning forward

Skip it if you already own lifting shoes with an elevated heel, or if you exclusively train on thick, cushioned rubber mats where the grip loses effectiveness.

## FAQ

**Will these work for benching or overhead pressing?**  
Not really — they're angled for lower-body work. Use them for squats, lunges, split squats, or calf stretches. For pressing, you'd want a flat board.

**Can I use them barefoot?**  
You can, but the grip texture gets uncomfortable on bare skin after a few high-rep sets. Wear socks or lifting shoes for longer sessions.

**How much weight can they handle?**  
The plastic is rated well above typical home gym loads. Multiple reviewers report using them with 300+ pounds without flex or failure. That's more than enough for most lifters.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a reliable, non-slip heel elevation tool for squat depth or ankle mobility and you train on hard flooring. **Skip it if** you already have heeled lifting shoes, or if your gym floor is thick rubber where the grip loses its bite.
