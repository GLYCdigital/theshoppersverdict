## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. EVA foam is a soft material, and it will show wear. Nail indentations from gripping the blocks during difficult poses are common, and scuff marks appear on the edges after a few months of regular use. These are purely cosmetic issues — the blocks still function perfectly — but if you're particular about your gear looking pristine, this might bother you.

More significantly, these blocks aren't ideal for advanced practitioners who use blocks for heavy weight-bearing work. If you're doing advanced arm balances or using blocks as a prop for deep hip openers, the slight give in the foam might feel less stable than cork or wooden blocks. One reviewer mentioned, "I prefer cork for restorative work where I'm lying on the block for long periods, but these are my go-to for vinyasa classes."
