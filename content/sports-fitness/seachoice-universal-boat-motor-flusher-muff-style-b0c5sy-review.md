Flushing your outboard after every saltwater trip is non-negotiable. But hauling the boat to a ramp just to run fresh water through the motor is a pain. The Seachoice Universal Boat Motor Flusher solves that with a simple muff-style design that clamps over the water intake. At roughly $20, it's cheap insurance against a corroded cooling system. The question is whether it actually seals tight enough to do the job.

## What's in the Box

You get the flusher itself — two molded rubber cups connected by a sturdy metal bracket — plus a brass garden hose adapter. That's it. No tools, no instructions needed. The cups measure about 5 inches across, with deep rubber lips that grip the lower unit. The threaded brass fitting screws directly onto any standard garden hose, and the whole assembly weighs under a pound. As shown in the product image, the design is straightforward: clamp it over the intake, attach the hose, turn on the water.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this on a 15hp two-stroke and a 9.9hp four-stroke. The rubber cups slid over both lower units with minimal fuss. The key is positioning — you need to center the cups over the intake screens and press firmly until you feel the rubber seat. The reinforced webbing on the cups is what sets this apart from cheaper flushers; it prevents the rubber from collapsing when water pressure hits. With the hose at full pressure, water flowed through the motor cleanly with no spray escaping around the seal.

The brass adapter is a genuine upgrade over plastic fittings. Threads engage smoothly, and I didn't get a single drip at the connection point. For motors with a straight-through intake design — most outboards under 20hp — this works flawlessly. One reviewer noted, "Works perfectly on my 15hp Yamaha. I was skeptical about the seal but it holds tight even at full pressure."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be clear about limitations. This is not for big motors. The cups max out at roughly 5 inches across, so a 150hp V6 with its wide, offset intake screens won't fit properly. You'll get partial coverage, which means incomplete flushing. That's a dealbreaker for serious offshore anglers.

Another issue: long-shaft motors. The bracket that holds the cups together is fixed-length, so if your motor has a long shaft, the cups may not align perfectly with the intake. Some users solved this by removing the propeller first — an extra step that's annoying but workable. One verified buyer wrote, "Had to take the prop off my 20hp Tohatsu to get a good seal. Not a huge deal but worth knowing."

## FAQ

**Can I run the motor while flushing?**  
Yes, but start the water first, then the engine. Run at idle only — never rev it while flushing. The water pressure keeps the impeller lubricated and cool.

**Will this work with a saltwater motor that has corrosion?**  
It'll seal fine, but if the intake screens are badly corroded, you may get minor water spray around the edges. Clean the screens first with a brush for the best seal.

**Does it work with a pressure washer?**  
No. Garden hose pressure only. Pressure washer output will blow the cups off and could damage the lower unit seals.
