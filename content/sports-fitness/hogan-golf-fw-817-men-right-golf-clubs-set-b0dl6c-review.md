The Hogan name carries weight in golf. Ben Hogan built his reputation on precision and shot-making, so when a complete set rolls out under that banner for under $300, skeptics are right to ask: did they cut corners, or is this the rare value play that actually delivers? After digging through 235 Amazon ratings and the specs, here's the real picture.

**What's actually in the box**

The FW-817 is a 10-piece set built for right-handed men. You get a driver, 3-wood, 4-hybrid, 6-iron through pitching wedge (that's five irons), a mallet putter, and a stand bag. Missing from that lineup: sand wedge and lob wedge. For anyone who's ever faced a greenside bunker with a pitching wedge, you already know that's a gap you'll want to fill.

The driver features a 460cc head — the maximum legal size — paired with a graphite shaft. The hybrid replaces the 4- and 5-irons you'd expect in a traditional set, which is honestly the smartest design choice here. High-handicappers routinely struggle with long irons; the hybrid's wider sole and lower center of gravity make those shots far more forgiving.

**How it actually performs**

The pitch from Hogan is "game improvement" technology, and the design backs it up. The driver's oversized face has a generous sweet spot, which means off-center hits don't punish you with the violent twisting you'd get from a blade-style driver. Multiple reviewers noted they picked up distance on mishits compared to their old sets — one buyer wrote, "I'm 58 and haven't played in 15 years. First round back I was hitting drives 220 yards straight down the middle."

The iron set is perimeter-weighted and cavity-backed, which is exactly what you want if your swing isn't repeatable yet. Those wide soles also help prevent digging on fat shots, a common miss for beginners. The putter is a standard mallet with alignment lines — nothing fancy, but it does the job.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 235 ratings, three themes dominate. First, forgiveness. Reviewers repeatedly mention that mishits stay playable. Second, the hybrid. "The 4-hybrid alone is worth the price," one verified purchaser wrote. "I used to top my 4-iron every time. This thing gets the ball airborne and my confidence back." Third, value — buyers consistently note this performs like sets costing twice as much.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of a sand wedge is the most obvious gap. You'll need to budget another $40–60 for a basic SW to handle bunkers and chips around the green. The graphite shafts in the irons are regular flex — fine for most amateurs, but if you swing fast, they'll feel soft and potentially produce higher, ballooning ball flight. And while the stand bag works, it's not a premium piece: no rain hood, minimal padding on the strap, and the pockets are functional but basic.

**Who should buy this**

This set is ideal for three specific buyers: beginners who want one purchase to cover the bag and clubs without assembling a set piecemeal; mid-handicappers upgrading from a decades-old starter set; and casual weekend golfers who play a few times a season and want reliable, forgiving equipment. It's not the right call for low-handicappers who need precise workability or specific shaft flexes — you'll want to build a custom set instead.

**FAQ**

*Does this set come with a bag?*
Yes, a lightweight stand bag with a single strap and several pockets is included. It's not a cart bag — it's designed to carry.

*What flex are the shafts?*
All shafts are regular flex (R-flex) graphite. If you have a particularly fast or slow swing, you may want to account for that — but for most adult male golfers, R-flex is the right starting point.

*Is the set suitable for tall or short golfers?*
The standard length clubs are designed for men around 5'8" to 6'0". If you're significantly outside that range, you'll likely need length adjustments, which will cost extra.

**The Verdict**

The Hogan FW-817 is a solid, honest set that doesn't pretend to be something it isn't. It's forgiving, complete enough to play a full round immediately, and priced fairly for what you get. The missing wedges and basic bag are the tradeoffs you accept at this price point.

**Buy it if:** You're a beginner or high-handicapper wanting a forgiving, all-in-one set that won't break the bank. **Skip it if:** You're a stronger player needing specific shaft flexes or a low-handicapper requiring workable shot shaping.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a legitimate value set with a couple of honest gaps.
