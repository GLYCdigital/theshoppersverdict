Here's the truth about the Roylvan Under Desk Elliptical. If you've been eyeing those pedal machines to burn a few extra hundred calories while answering emails, this one has a legitimate twist worth your attention — it's electric. That changes the game in a specific way.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the elliptical base unit, a wired remote control, and a power adapter. Assembly is minimal — you're not building a Peloton. The unit measures about 20 inches long by 17 inches wide, which is compact enough to slide under a standard 29-inch desk. The pedals have textured surfaces and adjustable straps to keep your feet from slipping, which matters more than you'd think when you're wearing socks.

The electric feature is the headline. Unlike manual under-desk ellipticals that only move when you push, the Roylvan's motor drives the pedals for you. You set the speed with the remote, and the pedals keep rotating continuously. That means you can literally zone out on a spreadsheet and your legs are still moving. It's passive activity, but it's activity.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

In practice, this machine works best for low-intensity movement. The motor speed ranges from a slow, almost therapeutic glide to a brisk walking-pace rotation. You can also reverse direction, which targets different muscle groups on your calves and hamstrings. The max speed is not going to get your heart rate up — be honest about that expectation — but it will prevent that stiff-legged feeling after a four-hour video call marathon.

The build quality is acceptable for the price tier. The frame is steel with a powder-coated finish, and the base has rubber grips that keep it from sliding on hardwood or carpet. One buyer noted, "I was skeptical about the motor burning out, but I've used it daily for two months and it's still running smooth." That aligns with what most owners report.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The convenience factor is the recurring theme in positive reviews. People love that they can multitask. A verified purchase said, "I get about 8,000 extra steps a day without leaving my chair. My legs don't feel like jelly after work anymore." Another reviewer highlighted the remote: "Being able to adjust speed without reaching under the desk is a small thing that makes a big difference."

The noise level also gets mentioned frequently. It's not silent, but it's quieter than most treadmills and doesn't disrupt phone calls. The motor produces a low hum that fades into background noise within a few minutes.

**Where It Falls Short**

Now the honest downsides. First, the "quiet" claim is oversold. The motor emits a consistent whir that's noticeable in a silent room. If you're on video calls without a headset, your coworkers will likely hear it. It's not loud enough to be obnoxious, but it's not stealth.

Second, there's no real resistance adjustment. The motor controls the speed, but you can't dial up tension to make it more challenging. This is a movement machine, not a workout machine. If you're expecting a calorie-torching session, you'll be disappointed.

Third, the pedals are relatively narrow. Users with larger feet (size 11+) have reported their heels sometimes slip off the edges despite the straps. Consider that if you're a bigger-framed person.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the desk-bound professional who sits 8+ hours daily and wants to add non-disruptive movement. It's also suitable for seniors or anyone recovering from lower-body injuries who needs gentle, motor-assisted range-of-motion exercise. It's NOT for fitness enthusiasts seeking cardiovascular conditioning or muscle building.

**FAQ**

*Can you use it with a standing desk?*
Yes, but you'll need to stand closer to the desk than you normally would. The machine is low-profile, and the motor pulls your feet back toward the base, so you'll need to adjust your stance. Most standing desk users report it works fine, just takes a few minutes to find the right position.

*How loud is it actually?*
Measured from a seated position, it's around 45-50 decibels — comparable to a quiet conversation or a refrigerator hum. On a phone call, the other party will likely hear it if you're not using a headset with noise cancellation.

*Does it require assembly?*
Minimal. You plug in the power adapter and attach the remote wire. No tools needed.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an effortless way to keep your legs moving during work hours and value the electric assist that keeps you consistent. **Skip it if** you're looking for a real workout or need absolute silence during calls.

The Roylvan does exactly what it claims — it's a portable, electric leg exerciser that fits under your desk. It won't transform your fitness, but it will keep your blood flowing during those marathon work sessions. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, most buyers agree it's a solid desk companion.
