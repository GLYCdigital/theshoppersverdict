# Pyramid Deluxe Double Roller: Two Balls, One Bag, Zero Excuses

If you're still hauling balls in a flimsy tote with a broken zipper, stop. The Pyramid Deluxe Double Roller is the upgrade your back and your bowling game have been waiting for. This isn't a novelty bag — it's a serious piece of gear for league bowlers who carry two balls and refuse to look like they're wrestling luggage through the parking lot.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the bag itself — no balls, no shoes, no surprises. It's a true double-roller with a dedicated large shoe compartment, not the standard "stuff your shoes in with the balls and hope" design. The main compartment holds two 15-pound bowling balls in padded, individual cradles that keep them from banging into each other during transit. That's a small detail that matters more than you'd think — nothing worse than hearing your favorite ball chip against its sibling on every speed bump.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The 1680D polyester shell is the same material used in high-end luggage, and it shows. After three months of weekly league use, mine shows zero fraying, the stitching on the handles hasn't pulled, and the telescoping handle locks crisply every time. The roller wheels are smooth on concrete and tile — they're not skateboard wheels, but they're a solid step above the cheap plastic rollers you'll find on budget bags.

The shoe compartment is genuinely large. I wear size 13 shoes and have room left for a towel, a rosin bag, and a small tape roll. That's a rare luxury — most double rollers I've tested force you to cram shoes into the main pocket or carry them separately.

One clever touch: the bag stands upright on its own, even with both balls loaded. That means you can set it down in the concourse without it flopping over and rolling into someone's ankles. Small win, but real.

## Where It Falls Short

It's not perfect, and you should know the tradeoffs before buying.

**Weight when loaded** — Two 15-pound balls, shoes, and accessories put you at roughly 21-23 pounds. The bag handles it fine, but lifting it into a tall SUV trunk requires a bit of a heave. There's no bottom grip handle to grab from the low side, so you'll be gripping the main handle or the side handles. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you have back issues.

**Shoe compartment zipper** — It's robust, but if you overstuff it — I'm talking cramming boots or oversized shoes past the designed capacity — the zipper can catch and snag. It won't break, but it'll test your patience at 7 AM before a tournament.

**No backpack straps** — Some competitors offer convertible backpack straps for stairs and bleachers. This bag is roller-only, so if your home alley has a massive stairwell with no ramp, you'll be carrying it by the handle.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a league bowler or tournament player who carries two balls, wants a dedicated shoe compartment, and values durability over flash. It's also ideal for seniors or anyone who'd rather roll than carry — the wheels and telescoping handle genuinely reduce strain.

**Skip it if** you only bowl once a month with one ball, or if you need a backpack-style bag for venues with no ramp access. Also skip if you're a minimalist — this bag is built for two balls and the gear that goes with them, not a single ball and a water bottle.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit a 16-pound ball?**
A: Yes, but it's tight. The cradles are designed for 15-pound balls, which are the most common size. A 16-pound ball fits but makes the bag heavier and slightly harder to zip closed. For 14-pound and below, you'll have extra room.

**Q: Can I fit both bowling shoes and dress shoes in the shoe compartment?**
A: The large compartment fits one pair of bowling shoes plus a towel and accessories. Two pairs of shoes is a squeeze — you'd need to use the side accessory pockets for anything extra.

**Q: Is the bag airline-checkable?**
A: Yes, it meets standard checked-luggage dimensions. Just make sure your balls are under the airline's weight limit per bag, and consider using the included padding as extra protection. It's not a hard case, so don't expect it to survive being thrown like a suitcase.
