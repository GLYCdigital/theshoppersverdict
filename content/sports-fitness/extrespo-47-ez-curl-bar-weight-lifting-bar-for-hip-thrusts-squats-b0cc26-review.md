If you've ever tried hip thrusts with a bare Olympic bar, you know the drill: bruising your hips, fighting the bar as it rolls, and improvising with a torn yoga mat. The ExtreSpo 47" EZ Curl Bar is designed to kill that problem in one move. It's a compact, padded, multi-purpose bar aimed squarely at glute work, but it pulls double duty for curls and light pressing. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, it's clearly clicking with a lot of home lifters. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The bar is 47 inches long, which is the sweet spot for home gyms — it fits between standard power racks and doesn't require a massive footprint. It features a thick, high-density foam pad in the center that's roughly 12 inches wide, which is what makes hip thrusts actually comfortable. The bar itself is 1-inch diameter, so it accepts standard weight plates (the ones with the smaller 1-inch holes), not Olympic plates.

It ships with two rotating sleeves for plates, secured by snap rings. Assembly takes about a minute. There's also a mild knurling on the grip areas — not aggressive, but enough to keep the bar from slipping when your hands get sweaty.

## Performance and Build Quality

The standout feature is the pad. It's dense enough to distribute pressure across your hips without feeling like you're lying on a rock, but firm enough that you don't sink in and lose stability. Multiple buyers specifically called this out — one reviewer wrote, "No more bruises on my hips after heavy sets," and another noted, "The pad stays put — I don't have to adjust it between reps." That's a big deal, because cheaper bars with slip-on pads shift constantly during sets.

The rotating sleeves are a genuine upgrade over fixed-sleeve bars. During curls and skull crushers, they let the plates rotate naturally, which reduces torque on your wrists and elbows. For hip thrusts, the smooth rotation means the bar doesn't bind when you're driving through your glutes.

The 47-inch length is also a practical win. It fits comfortably in a corner of a bedroom or small apartment gym, and it's light enough (around 15 pounds) to move around easily. That said, the 1-inch sleeve diameter is the biggest limitation. If you already own Olympic plates (the ones with the 2-inch holes), this bar won't work without an adapter sleeve — and those adapters can wobble under load.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate:

1. **The pad works.** Multiple buyers called out that they no longer need a separate hip thrust pad or a towel. One review said, "I've used this for 3 months, 3x a week, and the pad hasn't flattened or cracked."
2. **It's versatile.** People aren't just using it for hip thrusts. I saw mentions of bicep curls, tricep extensions, bent-over rows, and even light shoulder presses. The angled grip positions are genuinely comfortable for curl variations.
3. **Solid build for the price.** Buyers described the welds as clean and the snap rings as secure. One reviewer noted, "I've loaded 180 pounds on this and it doesn't flex or feel sketchy."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

The 1-inch sleeve diameter is the dealbreaker for some. If you've invested in Olympic plates, you'll need to buy adapters, and those add $15–20 and can introduce a bit of play. The weight capacity is also lower — realistically, this bar is built for up to 200–250 pounds, not heavy powerlifting loads. If you're hip thrusting 300+ pounds, this isn't your bar.

The knurling is mild. That's fine for hip thrusts and curls, but if you want a bar for heavy rows or deadlifts, you'll find the grip lacking. Also, the foam pad absorbs some sweat, so you'll want to wipe it down after workouts — a few reviewers noted the pad can get a bit funky if you don't air it out.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a home gym lifter who does hip thrusts and wants a dedicated bar that doesn't bruise your hips. You use standard 1-inch plates, or you're willing to buy adapters. You want one bar that handles glute work, curls, and light pressing without needing a full rack of specialty bars.

**Skip it if:** You're lifting heavy (300+ pounds) and need Olympic loading. You already have a fully equipped gym with a padded hip thrust machine. Or you prefer a bare barbell with a separate pad for maximum versatility.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this bar fit Olympic plates?**
No. The sleeves are 1-inch diameter. You'll need a 1-inch to 2-inch adapter, which can be purchased separately, but be aware that adapters can reduce stability under heavy loads.

**Q: How much weight can it hold?**
Most buyers report loading 150–200 pounds without issues. The bar is rated for moderate loads — think glute work, curls, and light presses. It's not built for heavy deadlifting or 300-pound hip thrusts.

**Q: Is the pad removable?**
Yes, the foam pad slides off, which makes it easier to clean. Some buyers have also swapped it for a thicker pad if they wanted more cushion, though the stock pad works well for most.
