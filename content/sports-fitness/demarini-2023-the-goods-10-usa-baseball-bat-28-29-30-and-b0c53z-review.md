**The Good, The Goods, and The Honest Truth**

If you've shopped youth bats lately, you know the USA Baseball stamp is the great equalizer — and the great frustrator. It limits performance to keep the game fair, which means most USA bats feel dead. The DeMarini 2023 The Goods (-10) is one of the few exceptions that actually delivers on its name. But it's not without compromise.

**What's in the Box**

You get the bat itself. Nothing more. No bat bag, no grip tape, no warranty card that matters — just the alloy one-piece with DeMarini's signature "The Goods" branding down the barrel. As shown in the product image, it's a clean, aggressive look: matte finish, bold graphics, and a tapered end cap. Available in 28", 29", and 30" lengths, all with a -10 drop weight. That means a 28-inch bat weighs 18 ounces — light enough for younger players to swing with control.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The barrel is a single-piece alloy construction, which is the right call for this price point and age group. One-piece bats transfer more energy to the ball than two-piece designs, and you feel it. Multiple buyers describe the pop as "hot out of the wrapper" — one parent noted, "My son hit farther his first game than he did all last season with his composite." That's the alloy advantage: no break-in period, just instant feedback.

The -10 drop makes it a contact hitter's dream. It's balanced, not end-loaded, so players can get the barrel through the zone quickly. For 8- to 11-year-olds still developing bat speed, this is the sweet spot. The grip is DeMarini's standard tacky tape, and the tapered knob helps reduce hand sting — a small detail that matters when kids are swinging 50 times a practice.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the power-to-weight ratio. Buyers specifically call out the "stiff feel" and "no vibration on mishits" as major wins. Parents also appreciate that it doesn't require the typical 100-200 swing break-in period that composite bats demand. It's ready on day one.

**Where It Falls Short**

Here's the honest part. Alloy dents. It's physics. Several reviewers report dents after hitting "off-speed pitching machine balls" or "batting cage balls that were cold and hard." This isn't a defect — it's the nature of alloy barrels. If your player is facing live pitching only and you're not feeding them rock-hard cage balls, it'll last. But if you're a cage rat family, budget for a shorter lifespan.

Also, the USA stamp is a ceiling. If your kid is a 12-year-old crushing the ball and moving toward travel ball with USSSA rules, this bat will feel limiting within a season. It's built for the recreational and early-competitive levels, not the elite showcase circuit.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The 8-11 year old transitioning from tee-ball to coach-pitch or machine-pitch.** The -10 weight is forgiving, and the pop gives them confidence.
- **Parents who want immediate performance without a break-in period.** No babysitting this bat — it works from swing one.
- **Players who prioritize bat speed over raw power.** If your kid is small for their age, the balanced swing weight is a game-changer.

**Skip It If**

- **Your player is already swinging a -8 or -5.** They'll find this too light and sacrifice power.
- **You're in a cold-weather climate and mostly use hard cage balls.** Alloy + cold = dent risk.
- **You're shopping for USSSA/travel ball.** Get a USSSA-stamped bat instead; the USA version will hold them back.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this bat need breaking in?**
A: No. It's one-piece alloy, so it performs at full strength from the first swing. Composite bats need 100+ swings; this doesn't.

**Q: Is the -10 drop too light for a 10-year-old?**
A: Depends on the kid. If they're average size and still developing bat speed, -10 is ideal. If they're already hitting with authority, consider a -8 for more mass behind the ball.

**Q: How does it handle cold weather?**
A: Poorly, like most alloy bats. Keep it above 60°F when possible. Cold balls and cold bats increase dent risk significantly.
