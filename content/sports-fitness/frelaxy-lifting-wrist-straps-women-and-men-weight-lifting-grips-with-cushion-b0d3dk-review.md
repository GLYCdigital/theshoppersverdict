**Frelaxy Lifting Wrist Straps Review: Grip Support That Actually Holds Up**

If you've ever felt the bar slipping mid deadlift or your grip failing before your back does, you know the frustration. The Frelaxy Lifting Wrist Straps are a budget-friendly answer to that problem—and with a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, they're clearly doing something right. But are they the right pair for *your* training style? Let's break it down.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes two straps—one for each hand—constructed from a heavy-duty nylon webbing. The standout feature is the integrated neoprene wrist pad, which runs the full width of the strap. This isn't a flimsy strip of foam; it's a substantial cushion that sits between the bar and your wrist, addressing the number one complaint with cheap straps: digging and bruising.

The loop end is stitched with a reinforced bar-tack, and the tail is long enough to wrap securely around standard and Olympic barbells. There's no velcro here—these are traditional wrap-around straps, which means they rely on friction and technique rather than a quick-release buckle.

## Performance and Build Quality

In real-world use, these straps feel more expensive than they are. The nylon has a slight stiffness out of the box but breaks in within a couple of sessions. The stitching is uniform and tight—no loose threads or premature fraying reported across the review base.

One of the most consistent points of praise is the grip security. As one buyer put it: *"I was skeptical at $15, but these held my 315lb deadlift without slipping once. The cushion is a game-changer for my bony wrists."* Another reviewer noted, *"Used them for heavy rows and shrugs—the padding made all the difference. No numbness like my old leather straps."*

The neoprene pad deserves special mention. It's roughly 1/4-inch thick and flexible enough to conform to your wrist without restricting blood flow. Even during high-rep accessory work, testers reported minimal irritation.

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be honest: nothing's perfect. The biggest complaint centers on the learning curve. If you've only ever used lifting hooks or figure-8 straps, the traditional loop-and-wrap technique takes a few tries to master. A handful of reviewers mentioned the tail being slightly *too* long, requiring an extra tuck to keep it from flapping on lighter sets.

The fabric can also get slick if your hands sweat heavily, though this is mitigated by chalk. And while the stitching is solid, the loop itself is not reinforced with extra leather layers like some premium straps—so if you're routinely pulling over 400 pounds, you may want a heavier-duty option.

## Who Should Buy These

- **The home gym lifter**: If you're building out a garage setup on a budget, these give you 90% of the performance of $30 straps for half the price.
- **The sensitive-wrist lifter**: If you've avoided straps because of wrist pain, the neoprene pad is the standout feature here.
- **The deadlift/row specialist**: Anyone doing heavy pulling where grip is the limiting factor will benefit immediately.

**Skip them if**: You're a competitive powerlifter maxing out beyond 500 pounds regularly, or if you prefer the convenience of quick-release velcro straps for supersets.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these legal for powerlifting competitions?**
A: Most federations allow traditional wrap straps, but check your specific federation's rules. The long tail and non-quick-release design are typically compliant.

**Q: How do I put them on?**
A: Loop the sewn end around the bar, slip your hand through the loop, then wrap the long tail around your wrist and tuck it under. Practice a few light reps to get the tension right.

**Q: Will these fit smaller wrists?**
A: Yes—the strap wraps around itself, so it adjusts to any wrist size. The pad is wide enough to stay in place even on narrower wrists.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want reliable, comfortable grip support without spending premium money. The cushioning alone justifies the purchase for anyone who's dealt with bruised forearms.

**Skip it if** you need competition-grade durability for elite-level loads or prefer velcro convenience.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A solid, well-executed product that nails the fundamentals and earns its place in any gym bag.
