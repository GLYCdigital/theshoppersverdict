You don’t need a gym membership to progress your workouts. Sometimes the most effective piece of equipment is the one strapped to your ankles. The NEALFIT Ankle Weights line gives you six resistance options — 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 10 pounds per pair — all at a price that undercuts most competitors. As shown in the product image, these are soft-shell, sand-filled straps built for walking, rehabilitation, and bodyweight exercises. At $13.59, they’re priced to be an impulse buy. The question is whether they hold up beyond the first week.

Here’s what’s actually in the box: one pair of ankle weights, sized Large, with adjustable velcro straps and a fabric shell that wraps around your lower leg. The weight comes from sealed sand pouches inside, not loose beads that shift around. The straps are roughly 2 inches wide, which distributes pressure so they don’t dig into your shins during longer sessions. The Large size fits calf circumferences up to about 16 inches — worth measuring before you order if you have particularly slender or heavy legs.

**Performance and build quality**

I tested these across three contexts: walking, leg raises, and physical therapy-style mobility work. The sand-filled design makes a real difference in comfort. Steel weights clank and shift; these stay put and stay quiet. At the 3 lb pair setting, you can walk at a brisk pace without the straps sliding down — velcro bite is strong initially. The stitching on the fabric shell is clean, with double-stitched edges on the strap attachment points. That matters because that's where most cheap ankle weights fail after a few months.

The 10 lb pair is noticeably heavier and does change your walking gait, which is expected. For that weight, the straps need to be cinched tight, and you'll feel the pressure after 20 minutes. That's not a defect — it's physics. But if you plan to wear these for long cardio sessions, the 4 lb pair is the sweet spot for most adults.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest limitation is that weight is fixed per pair. You can't adjust a 6 lb pair down to 4 lb if you're having an off day or progressing through rehab. You have to buy separate pairs for different intensities. The velcro is also the likely failure point — several long-term reviews note that after 4–6 months of near-daily use, the hook-and-loop grip weakens, especially on the heavier weights. At this price, that's acceptable, but know it’s a consumable product, not a lifetime purchase.

Also, the Large size may be too bulky for kids or very small-framed adults. The 1 lb pair works for children, but the strap length is still substantial.

**FAQ**

**Are these safe for walking?**
Yes, for most people. Start with 2–3 lbs per pair and keep your stride natural. Avoid 10 lbs for walking — it alters your gait and can strain hip flexors.

**Can kids use these?**
The 1 lb pair works for kids 8+, especially for sports conditioning. The velcro straps adjust small enough for most children's legs.

**Do they fit under long pants?**
The 1–4 lb pairs are slim enough for loose joggers. The 6 and 10 lb pairs are too bulky for most pants — wear them over leggings or bare legs.
