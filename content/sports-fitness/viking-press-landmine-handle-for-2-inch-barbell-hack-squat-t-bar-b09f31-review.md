**The $40 question: Is this landmine attachment a game-changer for your home gym, or just another piece of steel collecting dust in the corner?**

I've tested enough landmine handles to know most are variations on the same theme — a plate that slides over the bar with a single handle welded on. The Viking Press Landmine Handle tries something different with its dual upright grips and rotating collar. Here's what you actually need to know before buying.

**What you get in the package**

The box contains the handle assembly itself — no hardware, no instructions, no mounting plate. It's designed to slide directly onto a 2-inch barbell sleeve. The unit weighs roughly 5 pounds and measures about 12 inches from the collar to the top of the handles. The construction is welded steel with a black powder-coat finish that resists chipping reasonably well.

The key design feature is the rotating collar. Unlike fixed landmine attachments, this one pivots freely on the bar, which changes the feel of every exercise dramatically. The dual vertical handles sit side by side, spaced about 4 inches apart — narrow enough for a natural pressing motion but wide enough to grip comfortably.

**Real-world performance**

I ran this through a full training cycle: viking presses, hack squats, T-bar rows, and landmine rotations. The rotating collar is the standout feature. When you press overhead, the bar rotates naturally instead of forcing your wrists into an awkward angle. That's a genuine ergonomic win that fixed handles can't match.

The hack squat position works well too. Load the bar, stand on the plate, grip the handles, and squat. The vertical handle orientation lets you pull the weight up from behind you without the bar digging into your back. The neutral grip keeps your elbows in a comfortable position throughout the movement.

For T-bar rows, the handle shines. The rotation lets the bar track naturally as you pull, and the dual grip gives you options for how wide you want to hold. You can grip both handles together or spread your hands across them for a slightly wider stance.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The rotation mechanism gets the most love. One verified buyer noted, "The fact that it rotates makes pressing movements feel much more natural than my old fixed handle." Another mentioned the build quality: "This thing is solid. I've loaded 315 on it for hack squats and it doesn't flex at all."

The versatility is also a recurring theme. Multiple reviewers mention using it for viking press, hack squat, and various row variations without needing different equipment. The neutral grip position gets consistent praise for shoulder-friendly pressing.

**Where it falls short**

The fit is the biggest issue. The collar is designed for a standard 2-inch barbell, but if your bar has a thicker sleeve or aggressive knurling near the end, the handle won't slide on smoothly. Some buyers report having to file the collar or use force to get it seated. That's a frustrating first experience.

The fixed handle spacing is another limitation. At roughly 4 inches apart, broader-shouldered lifters may find the grip too narrow for comfortable pressing. This isn't adjustable, so what you see is what you get.

Several reviewers also report a gritty or sticky rotation out of the box. A quick shot of WD-40 or silicone spray fixes it, but it's an extra step you shouldn't have to deal with on a new product.

**Who should buy this**

This handle makes sense for home gym owners who want to expand their landmine exercise library without buying multiple attachments. It's particularly good for lifters who struggle with wrist pain during overhead pressing, since the rotation eliminates that awkward joint angle.

Skip it if you're a competitive powerlifter who needs precise, fixed positioning for specific movements, or if you have a non-standard barbell that might not fit the collar.

**Three questions shoppers ask**

*Does this fit a standard Olympic barbell?* Yes, it fits a standard 2-inch Olympic bar. But if your bar has a thicker sleeve or heavy knurling near the end, it may be tight. Measure your bar's end diameter before buying.

*Can I use this for landmine rows?* Absolutely. The dual handles work great for T-bar and landmine rows. The rotation actually helps the bar track more naturally during the pull.

*Is it worth the price over a basic landmine handle?* If you plan to do pressing and squatting movements, yes — the rotation is worth the extra cost. If you only need it for rows, a fixed handle will do the job for less.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a versatile landmine attachment that handles pressing, squatting, and rowing movements with a more natural rotation. The build quality is solid, and the ergonomic benefits are real.

**Skip it if:** You have a non-standard barbell, need adjustable grip width, or don't want to deal with potential fit or lubrication issues out of the box.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A genuinely useful attachment with smart design, held back by fit inconsistencies and fixed handle spacing. Worth considering if you know your barbell will work with it.
