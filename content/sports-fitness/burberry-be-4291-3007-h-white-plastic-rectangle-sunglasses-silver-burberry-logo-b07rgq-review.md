Let's cut straight to it: these Burberry BE 4291 sunglasses are not your average designer shades. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, they've clearly won over a lot of buyers. But are they worth the premium for anyone who actually sweats? Here's the real deal.

**What's in the box**

You get the sunglasses themselves, a Burberry-branded microfiber pouch, and a cleaning cloth. No hard case. No documentation beyond the authenticity card. For a designer piece, that's a bit thin — if you're a gym-bag tosser, buy a hard case separately or accept scratches will happen.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The frame is injection-molded plastic with a glossy white finish. It's not the flimsy acetate you find on $20 sunglasses — the hinges are metal, the arms snap open with a reassuring click, and the whole thing flexes without creaking. The white plastic resists yellowing remarkably well; several long-term reviewers noted the frame still looks crisp after a year of summer use.

The lenses are Category 3 with UV400 protection. That means they block 100% of UVA and UVB rays — essential for anyone doing outdoor cardio or water sports. One buyer wrote, "I've worn these through a full marathon in July and my eyes never felt strained." That's the kind of endorsement that matters.

At 24 grams, these are genuinely light. You'll forget they're on your face during a long ride or hike. The rectangle shape offers solid coverage without that wrap-around sporty look — you can wear these to breakfast after your workout and not look like a cyclist.

**Where it falls short**

The size S designation is no joke. The frame width is about 135mm, and the temple arms apply noticeable pressure on wider heads. Multiple reviewers flagged this. One said: "Love the style but after two hours of driving, my temples ached." If you've ever struggled with "one size fits all" sunglasses, size up or try a different model.

The lenses are not polarized. For driving or water sports, that's a legitimate drawback — glare off pavement or lake water will still hit you. At this price point, you'd expect polarization. It's the single biggest functional miss here.

**Who should buy these**

The weekend runner who wants style without sacrificing eye protection. The traveler who needs one pair that works from trailhead to trattoria. The brand-conscious buyer who wants Burberry quality without the oversized celebrity shades look.

Skip these if you have a wide face, if polarized lenses are non-negotiable for your activities, or if you need a hard case for transport.

**FAQ**

*Are these real Burberry?*
Yes. The BE 4291 is a current production model. The logo branding is embossed on the temples, and the authenticity card plus serial number match the frame etching.

*Do these fit small faces?*
The S sizing is accurate. They suit narrow-to-average faces well. If you usually wear medium or large in other brands, these will likely be too tight.

*Can I replace the lenses?*
Yes — the lenses pop out of the plastic frame. Burberry offers replacements, but third-party optical shops can also fit prescription or polarized lenses if you want to upgrade.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a lightweight, genuine designer pair for outdoor activities, have a narrow-to-average face, and prioritize UV protection over polarization.

**Skip it if:** You need polarized lenses, have a wide head, or expect a hard case at this price.

These are excellent sunglasses with one major functional gap (no polarization) and one fit limitation (size S runs narrow). For the right buyer, they're a 4.6-star product. For everyone else, weigh those cons carefully.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Style, build, and UV protection are top-tier. The missing polarization and snug fit keep it from perfection.
