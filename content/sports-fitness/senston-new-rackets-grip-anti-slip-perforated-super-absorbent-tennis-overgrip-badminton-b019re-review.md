If you've ever had a racket slip mid-swing during a humid summer match, you know the panic. The Senston New Rackets Grip is a budget-friendly overgrip that aims to solve exactly that problem with a perforated, moisture-wicking design. At a 4.2-star average across 210 ratings, it's clearly working for most players — but let's dig into whether it's the right fix for your game.

**What You Actually Get**

The packaging is no-frills: a set of overgrips (typically 3-6 per pack depending on the listing), each pre-cut to standard racket length. The material is a polyurethane blend with a visible perforation pattern across the entire surface — that's the "Super Absorben" claim in action. The grip measures roughly 1.8mm thick, which is on the slimmer side of overgrip territory, and the finish is slightly tacky out of the wrapper rather than slick.

**Performance and Real-World Feel**

Installing this grip is straightforward, though you'll want to be deliberate about the spiral overlap. The lack of a built-in adhesive strip is my first honest gripe — the finish tape included is functional, but if you're aggressive with your grip pressure, you may want to add a thin strip of electrical tape at the top for extra security.

On the court, the perforations do exactly what they're designed to do. They create tiny channels that let moisture escape rather than pooling under your palm. In a 90-minute session with moderate sweating, the grip maintains its tackiness noticeably better than a standard smooth overgrip. The slim profile also means you preserve the bevel edges of your handle — important for players who rely on precise grip changes for spin and placement.

**What Buyers Are Saying**

The praise in the Amazon reviews consistently centers on two things: feel and value. One verified buyer notes, "These grips feel like they cost three times more than they do. The tack is perfect out of the package." Another tennis player mentions, "I use these on my badminton racket too — the absorbency holds up even in a hot gym."

The criticism is equally consistent. Multiple reviewers flag durability as the weak point. One user states plainly, "Great for two weeks, then the perforation layer starts to shred on the edges." Another notes that heavy hitters with high grip pressure may find the material compresses faster than pricier options like Tourna Grip or Wilson Pro Overgrip.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond durability, there's a tradeoff with the perforation design itself. While it excels at absorbing moisture, the perforated texture can feel slightly abrasive during the first hour of use — a minor annoyance if you have sensitive skin. Additionally, if you play in dry, cool conditions, the moisture-wicking advantage is largely moot, and you'd be better served by a tackier smooth grip.

**Who Should Buy This**

This grip is ideal for:
- **Club-level players** who want a consistent feel without paying premium prices
- **Badminton or squash players** who go through grips quickly and need a cost-effective rotation
- **Recreational players** in humid climates who struggle with sweaty hands mid-match

**Skip it if:**
- You're a tournament-level player logging 6+ hours weekly — you'll likely want the durability of a premium grip
- You prefer a cushioned, shock-absorbing feel — this grip is slim and direct, not plush
