If you train grappling sports, you already know the struggle: solo drilling feels empty. You're shrimping across a mat with no resistance, practicing your shot form against air, and hoping muscle memory sticks. SQUID BANDS were designed to fix exactly that problem. As shown in the product image, these aren't your average resistance bands — they're built with combat sports in mind, with anchor points and tension curves that mimic an actual opponent's resistance.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the band system itself, with interchangeable resistance levels. The construction is noticeably more substantial than the $15 door-anchor bands you'll find at a big-box store. The latex is 1.5mm thick, and the stitching at the connection points is reinforced — this matters because grappling drills put lateral stress on bands, not just straight-line tension like a bicep curl.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these for two weeks across three disciplines: wrestling shots, BJJ guard retention, and judo grip fighting. The band's real value shows during high-rep drilling. Running 50 penetration steps with resistance forces you to stay low and explosive — something shadow drilling simply can't replicate. The anchor system holds firm on both a wrestling mat edge and a home door frame, though you'll want a solid anchor point for the heavy band.

The tension curve is what surprised me most. Unlike standard bands that get exponentially harder at peak stretch, SQUID BANDS maintain more consistent resistance through the movement's mid-range. That's critical for shrimping drills where you need resistance through the hip escape, not just at the end range of motion.

## Where It Falls Short

The heavy band is genuinely heavy. Smaller athletes — under 150 pounds — will find it overwhelming for certain drills and may need to stick with the medium option. There's also no instructional material included beyond basic setup. If you're new to solo grappling drills, you'll spend time on YouTube figuring out how to use them effectively. And while the price is reasonable for the build quality, it's not a budget impulse buy.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a wrestler, BJJ practitioner, judoka, or MMA fighter who wants productive solo training sessions. You drill at home without a partner and need realistic resistance. You're an intermediate to advanced athlete who knows your drills and just needs the tool.

**Skip it if:** You're brand new and haven't learned basic movements yet. You only train with a partner regularly and don't drill solo. Or you're looking for a general-purpose fitness band — there are cheaper options for that.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these for strength training too?**
A: Technically yes, but they're designed for speed and technique work, not hypertrophy. Stick to grappling-specific drills.

**Q: How do I know which resistance level to buy?**
A: If you're under 150 lbs, start with light or medium. Over 180 lbs, the heavy band will serve you well. The medium is a safe middle ground.

**Q: Will these damage my door frame?**
A: The anchor system includes protective padding, but repeated use on a hollow door frame can leave marks. Use a solid anchor or mat edge when possible.
