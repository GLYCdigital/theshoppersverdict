Let’s be honest about putters: most of them don’t actually fix your stroke. The Cleveland Golf HB Soft 2 (Model 1) is different — not because it magically makes you a better putter, but because it forgives the mistakes you already make. With a 4.6/5 rating across 229 Amazon reviews, this is one of the most consistently praised mallet-style putters under $150. Here’s the real story.

## What you actually get

The box contains the putter (33, 34, or 35-inch shaft options), a Cleveland headcover, and that’s it — no adjustment tools, no extra weights. The Model 1 is a face-balanced mallet with a plumber’s neck hosel, designed for players with a straight-back-straight-through stroke. It uses Cleveland’s “HB Soft” insert — a milled face that’s 20% softer than their previous generation — paired with a two-piece alignment system: a white sightline that runs the full length of the crown and a contrasting dark cavity.

The head itself is a hybrid between a blade and a full mallet. It’s not the massive spaceship look you get from some Odyssey or Spider models, but it’s notably larger than a classic Anser-style blade. Weight comes in around 365 grams — heavier than the standard 340-350 gram blade putter, which is intentional for stability.

## Performance on the greens

I tested this putter for three rounds on medium-speed municipal greens and one round on a private course with stimpmeter readings around 11. The first thing you notice is the feel — it’s genuinely soft, almost muted at impact, but not dead. Balls come off the face with a consistent roll that holds its line well. The milling pattern does what it claims: it reduces skid on short putts, which is where most amateurs lose strokes.

The alignment system is the standout feature. The white stripe against the dark cavity creates a strong visual contrast that makes squaring the face feel intuitive. I found myself lining up putts noticeably faster — maybe two seconds less per putt — and that adds up over 18 holes.

Where the HB Soft 2 really shines is on mis-hits. The higher MOI (moment of inertia) means off-center strikes toward the toe or heel lose far less distance than they would on a blade. One reviewer put it well: *“I’m a high-handicapper who three-putts way too much. This putter turned my 30-foot lag putts into tap-ins. The feel is buttery and the alignment line is stupidly easy to see.”* Another noted: *“Bought this to replace a Scotty I’d had for years. Honestly, I’m not missing the Scotty.”*

## Where it falls short

The head weight is the biggest adjustment. Coming from a lighter blade, my first few rounds had me leaving putts short — I was subconsciously decelerating because the head felt heavy through the stroke. It took about two rounds to recalibrate. If you’re a player who prefers a light, responsive head for feel-based distance control, this may not be your putter.

The stock grip is also a point of contention. It’s a standard pistol-style grip that’s fine but forgettable. Multiple reviewers mention swapping it out immediately — *“Grip felt cheap, replaced it with a SuperStroke and it’s perfect now.”* Factor in an extra $15-20 if you plan to do the same.

## Who should buy this

- **Mid-to-high handicappers** who struggle with consistency on short putts and need forgiveness
- **Straight-back-straight-through strokers** — this putter is built for you, not for players with a strong arc
- **Budget-conscious golfers** who want premium feel without the $300+ price tag of big-brand equivalents

## Who should skip it

- **Players who love a heavy, blade-style feel** — this is lighter and more forgiving but not the same experience
- **Strong arc putters** — the face-balanced design will fight your natural stroke
- **Anyone who hates white alignment aids** — the stripe is prominent and not going anywhere

## FAQ

**Q: Is this putter good for beginners?**
A: Yes, particularly if you’re still developing a consistent stroke. The forgiveness on mis-hits and the clear alignment aid make it easier to build good habits. The heavier head might take a few rounds to get used to, but it’s not a dealbreaker.

**Q: Does the Model 1 come in left-handed?**
A: Yes, Cleveland offers left-handed versions in the HB Soft 2 line, though availability can vary on Amazon. Check the product options before ordering.

**Q: How does it compare to the previous HB Soft putter?**
A: The “2” generation has a softer face insert and improved alignment contrast. The head shape is similar, but the feel is noticeably more refined. If you already own the original, the upgrade is minor; if you’re coming from something older, this is a clear step up.
