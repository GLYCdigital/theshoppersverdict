**The Verdict:** The leikefitness Exercise Step Platform is a legitimately versatile piece of home cardio gear that earns its 4.6-star rating through smart design and genuine value. It's not a commercial-grade step bench, but for home users who want step aerobics plus light strength training without buying three separate pieces of equipment, this is a smart buy.

---

**What you actually get**

Open the box and you'll find the step platform itself (about 29 inches long, 13.5 inches wide), four risers that click into the base, two resistance cords with handles, and a pair of 1.5-pound detachable dumbbells that snap into the platform's sides when not in use. The whole thing weighs roughly 15 pounds, so it's easy to stash under a couch or in a closet between workouts.

The height adjustment is the headline feature. You get three positions — 4, 6, and 8 inches — by stacking the locking risers. Unlike cheaper steps where risers wobble or pop out mid-workout, these click in with a satisfying snap and stay put. One reviewer noted, *"I'm 5'9" and 190 lbs, and I've done 40-minute HIIT sessions at the highest setting without any shifting."* That tracks with the build quality.

**Performance and real-world use**

The surface texture is grippy without being abrasive. Sweaty feet stay planted, and the rubber pads underneath keep the platform from sliding on hardwood or tile. At the 4-inch setting, it's approachable for beginners or post-injury returners. At 8 inches, it's a legit cardio challenge — your quads and glutes will feel it within minutes.

The multifunctional angle is where this earns its keep. The two resistance cords attach to the platform base and give you rows, chest presses, and overhead presses while stepping. The detachable dumbbells are light (1.5 lbs each), so don't expect to build serious muscle with them — but they're perfect for adding resistance to high-rep moves or keeping your arms engaged during stepping intervals. One buyer said, *"I use this for step aerobics videos and the bands for upper body supersets. It's replaced my gym membership for cardio days."*

The platform holds up to 400 pounds, which is solid for a home step in this price range. The surface is slightly cushioned but firm enough to feel stable — no concerning flex even at max height.

**Where it falls short**

At the 8-inch setting, the platform depth is the limiting factor. If you have larger feet (size 11+) or are tall, the 13.5-inch depth gives you less margin for error on fast choreography. You'll need to be deliberate about foot placement, especially during step aerobics routines that involve lateral movements.

The resistance cords are functional but basic. The anchor points are plastic and the bands themselves are light-to-medium resistance. If you're already doing strength training with real dumbbells, you'll find these underwhelming. They're a bonus, not a replacement.

Assembly takes about two minutes — the risers snap in without tools, and the cords attach via loops. No complaints there.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home step aerobics enthusiasts** who follow YouTube or DVD workouts and need a stable, adjustable platform
- **Beginner to intermediate fitness users** who want one piece of equipment for cardio, light strength, and recovery work
- **Small-space exercisers** who need something compact that stores easily and does double duty

**Skip it if** you're a serious strength trainer needing heavy resistance, or if you're over 6'2" and need a wider stepping surface.

**FAQ**

*Q: Does the platform feel stable on carpet?*
A: The rubberized feet grip carpet well, but the platform can sink slightly into plush carpet, making the heights less precise. For best stability, use it on a hard, flat surface.

*Q: Can I use this with a step aerobics video that requires a specific height?*
A: Yes — the three height settings (4/6/8 inches) cover the standard heights used in most step workout programs. Just check your video's requirements; most use 6 inches as a default.

*Q: Are the dumbbells worth using, or are they gimmicks?*
A: At 1.5 pounds each, they're light. But they're genuinely useful for adding arm work during step routines or for exercises like tricep extensions and lateral raises where you want form over load. Not a gimmick, just not a strength tool.

**The Bottom Line**

**Buy it if** you want a stable, adjustable step platform that also throws in resistance bands and light dumbbells for a complete home cardio session. **Skip it if** you need a wide commercial-grade step or serious resistance training — this is a home fitness tool, not a gym replacement.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The leikefitness Exercise Step Platform delivers what it promises: a sturdy, multifunctional aerobic deck that's well-built, easy to store, and genuinely versatile. The minor width limitation at max height is the only real tradeoff.
