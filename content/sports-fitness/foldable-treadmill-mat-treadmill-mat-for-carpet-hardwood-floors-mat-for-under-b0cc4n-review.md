### What You Actually Get

The package is straightforward: one mat, no tools, no instructions needed. It arrives rolled tightly, and it lays flat within a few hours (give it overnight if it's been in a cold warehouse). The dimensions are generous enough for most folding treadmills — roughly 6 feet long and 3 feet wide, which covers the footprint of popular models like the WalkingPad or compact Horizon units. The material is a dense, textured PVC/rubber blend, about 3mm thick. It's not a gym-floor thick pad, but it's not supposed to be.

### Performance: Does It Actually Protect Your Floor?

Here's the honest breakdown for the two main floor types:

**On hardwood:** This is where the mat earns its keep. The textured underside grips the floor firmly — no sliding, even during a brisk walking pace. The 3mm thickness won't absorb heavy impact from running, but it *will* prevent the dreaded black scuff marks and the vibration-induced scratches that happen when a treadmill's feet shift over weeks of use. One buyer noted, *"I was skeptical because it's thin, but it's dense. My floors have zero marks after three months."* That tracks.

**On carpet:** This is a slightly different story. The mat creates a stable platform, and it does protect the carpet from indentations left by the treadmill's front wheels. However, it does *not* magically eliminate the "treadmill on carpet" wobble. If your carpet is plush or high-pile, the mat will still flex slightly. For low-pile commercial carpet, it's excellent. For thick home carpet, it's better than nothing but not a miracle worker.

### What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in the feedback is **odor**. Specifically, the *lack* of it. Many cheap rubber mats smell like a tire factory for weeks. Multiple reviewers explicitly mentioned this mat was "odor-free out of the box" or had a "very faint smell that vanished in a day." That's a huge win for an indoor product.

The **foldability** is also legit. It creases but doesn't crack, and you can roll it or fold it into a compact square for storage. It's light enough to move easily — about 5-6 pounds — which makes it practical if you store your treadmill upright.

### Where It Falls Short (Be Honest)

**1. It's not a noise insulator.** If you bought this hoping to silence a loud treadmill for downstairs neighbors, you'll be disappointed. The mat dampens vibration transfer to the floor, but the motor and belt noise will still travel through the structure. It's a floor protector, not a soundproofing solution.

**2. The edges can curl initially.** Several buyers mentioned the corners curling for the first week. It does flatten out, but if you're placing it under a treadmill immediately, the front edge might catch on the treadmill's wheels as you walk. Weigh it down or give it a day to relax.

**3. Not for heavy running.** At 3mm, this is optimized for walking and light jogging on folding treadmills. If you have a full-size, 300-pound commercial treadmill, you need a thicker, more robust pad. This one will compress and wear unevenly under heavy impact.

**Q: Is it truly non-slip on hardwood?**
