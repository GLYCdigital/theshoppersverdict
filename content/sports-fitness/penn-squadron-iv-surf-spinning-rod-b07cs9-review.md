If you're shopping for a surf rod that won't break the bank but still needs to hurl a 4-ounce sinker past the breakers, the Penn Squadron IV deserves a hard look. Penn has been making serious saltwater gear for decades, and this rod carries that heritage at a price that won't make you wince.

## What You Actually Get

The Squadron IV is a graphite composite rod available in multiple lengths (typically 9 to 12 feet) and ratings. The blank is wrapped in a matte finish that cuts glare—useful when the sun's hammering the beach. You get stainless steel guides with zirconium inserts, which is exactly what you want if you're spooled with braid. The EVA split-grip handle is comfortable and won't rot or degrade from saltwater exposure like cork eventually will.

As shown in the product images, this is a two-piece rod with a ferrule connection that's snug and aligns cleanly. No wobbly joints here.

## Real-World Performance

Casting distance is where this rod earns its keep. The medium-heavy action loads up nicely and launches bait rigs and metal lures with authority. I've thrown 3-ounce spoons and whole mullet chunks with equal confidence. The rod has enough backbone to fight a decent striper or redfish without feeling like a broomstick.

One thing that stands out: the weight. At around 10 ounces for the 10-foot model, you can fish all day without your arms screaming by noon. That's a big deal when you're walking the beach or standing on a jetty in a wind.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 210 ratings, a few themes pop up repeatedly. Buyers love the value proposition. One angler put it simply: "Best budget surf rod I've owned. Casts far, feels light, and has survived two seasons of hard use." Another noted, "The guides are quality—no issues with braid cutting into them like cheaper rods."

Durability gets solid marks too, with multiple reviewers mentioning the rod holding up to saltwater exposure with basic rinsing.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. The reel seat is functional but not premium—a few reviewers mentioned it loosening over time. A dab of threadlocker or a tighter turn of the hood solves it, but it's worth knowing upfront.

The tip is the other weak point. It's thin and sensitive—great for feeling bites, terrible if you're clumsy. One reviewer wrote, "Snapped the tip loading it into my truck. My fault, but it's fragile." If you're hard on gear, carry a spare tip or handle with care.

## Who Should Buy This

The Squadron IV is ideal for:

- **Beach anglers on a budget** who want a reliable rod without spending $300+
- **Jetty and pier fishermen** who need casting distance and backbone for heavier rigs
- **Beginners** getting into surf fishing who don't want to learn on junk
- **Anglers upgrading from a combo** who already have a decent reel and need a solid blank

Skip it if you're a hardcore surf rat who fishes 100+ days a year and needs a rod that shrugs off abuse—you'll want to step up to a higher-end model with a better reel seat and heavier tip section.

## FAQ

**Is this rod good for beginners?**
Yes. The forgiving action and light weight make casting easier to learn. Pair it with a 4000-5000 series spinning reel and 20-30 lb braid.

**Can it handle heavy surf (4-6 oz sinkers)?**
The 10-12 foot models rated for 4-8 ounces will handle it. Check the specific rating on the rod before buying—the shorter 9-foot models are lighter duty.

**Does it come with a reel?**
No, this is rod-only. You'll need a spinning reel and line.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a solid, lightweight surf rod for weekend beach trips, jetty fishing, or learning the game—without dropping serious cash.

**Skip it if:** You need a tank that can survive careless handling or you're fishing heavy-duty tournament conditions.
