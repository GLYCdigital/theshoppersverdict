**TYR Swimple Metallized Kids' Mirrored Goggles Review: Small Face, Big Performance**

Finding goggles that actually stay sealed on a child’s face without leaving raccoon eyes is a battle every swim parent knows. The TYR Swimple Metallized Kids’ Mirrored Goggles attempt to solve that with a compact frame, soft silicone gasket, and a mirrored lens that costs less than $20. After combing through 235 Amazon ratings (4.7 stars) and the feedback embedded in them, here’s the straight story.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes the goggles, a two-tone silicone strap, and a standard hard-shell case — no microfiber pouch, which is a minor miss. The lens is polycarbonate with a metallized mirror finish available in several colorways (the blue shown in the product image is the most popular). The frame is low-profile, designed specifically for smaller faces. TYR lists this as “Training/Recreation,” which tells you it’s built for lessons and pool play, not competitive racing.

**Real-World Performance**

The mirrored coating is the headline feature, and it delivers. “My daughter can finally see in the outdoor pool without squinting,” one parent noted. The mirror cuts surface glare effectively while still allowing enough light penetration that kids don’t feel like they’re swimming in a cave — a common issue with heavily tinted lenses. For indoor pools, the lens is fine but unremarkable.

The seal is where these goggles surprise. The soft silicone gasket conforms well to the orbital bone, and the four-way split strap distributes pressure evenly. Multiple reviewers with kids aged 5–8 reported zero leaks during swim lessons and cannonball sessions. That’s genuinely rare at this price point.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

- **Comfort**: “My 7-year-old forgot she was wearing them after 30 minutes.”
- **Fit on narrow faces**: Parents of kids who couldn’t wear Speedo Junior or Arena youth goggles found these sealed properly.
- **Strap security**: The split design stays put through flip turns and water slides — no mid-session adjustments.

**Where It Falls Short**

The anti-fog coating is the weak link. Several long-term reviewers reported fogging after 3–4 weeks of twice-weekly use. That’s not unusual for sub-$20 goggles, but it’s worth knowing. Also, the sizing is genuinely small. If your child is 10 or older or has a larger head, skip this and get the TYR Youth size. One reviewer noted the strap was fully extended on their 9-year-old.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Parents of kids aged 4–9** in swim lessons who need a reliable, comfortable seal
- **Outdoor pool families** dealing with bright sun glare
- **Budget-minded buyers** who want mirrored performance without paying Speedo or Arena prices

**Skip if** your kid is 10+, needs prescription lenses, or swims competitively multiple times per week — you’ll want a higher-end anti-fog treatment.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these suitable for kids with sensitive eyes?**
A: The silicone gasket is hypoallergenic and gentle, but if your child has severe light sensitivity, the mirrored lens may not be dark enough. Consider a fully tinted lens instead.

**Q: How do I extend the anti-fog life?**
A: Rinse with cold water after each use and never wipe the inside lens with your fingers. Use a defogger spray (like Jaws Quick Spit) once the factory coating starts failing.

**Q: Do they come in sizes?**
A: TYR makes a Youth version that runs slightly larger. If your child is between sizes, size up — the Swimple runs small.

**The Verdict**

The TYR Swimple Metallized Kids’ Mirrored Goggles earn a 4.6/5 because they nail the two things that matter most for young swimmers: fit and glare protection. The anti-fog lifespan is the tradeoff you accept at this price. For a kid in swim lessons or summer pool days, these are a smart, affordable choice.

**Buy it if** you need dependable, comfortable goggles for a young child at an outdoor pool.
**Skip it if** your child is near double-digit age or swims so often that fogging will drive you crazy — invest in a higher-end pair instead.
