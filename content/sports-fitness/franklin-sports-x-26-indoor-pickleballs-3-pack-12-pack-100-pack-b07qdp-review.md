Let's be blunt: most pickleballs under $20 feel like hollow Easter eggs that crack on the first hard serve. The Franklin Sports X-26 isn't that. With 210 ratings averaging 4.2 stars, it's the budget indoor ball that serious players actually debate — mostly because it punches well above its price point, but not without a couple of quirks.

**What you're actually getting**

The X-26 is a 26-hole indoor ball, and that hole count is the whole story. Fewer, larger holes means less air resistance — which translates to faster, flatter trajectory and less of that annoying parachute wobble you get from 40-hole balls. As shown in the product image, the holes are precision-drilled, not punched, which keeps the edges clean. That matters: rough hole edges create drag and inconsistent bounce. The plastic is a touch thicker than typical budget balls, and at 2.87 inches diameter with a 0.91-ounce weight, it meets USA Pickleball specs for sanctioned play.

You can grab a 3-pack to test, a 12-pack for league nights, or a 100-pack if you're running a club or giving lessons. The 12-pack is the sweet spot for most regular players — roughly two months of play if you're not slamming them into walls.

**How it actually performs**

Indoor hard courts are where this ball lives. The harder plastic gives a crisp, predictable bounce that holds true even on slightly worn gym floors. Off the paddle, it feels firmer than the soft, grippy feel of a DuraFast 40 — more like a slightly toned-down version of the Onix Pure Indoor. Control players will appreciate the consistency; power hitters will find it a touch hot off the face.

The 26-hole design genuinely reduces wind drag, but here's the catch: this is an *indoor* ball. Take it outside on a breezy day and it'll dance like a butterfly on espresso. That's not a flaw — it's physics. Buy it for indoor use only.

**What buyers keep saying**

The praise is consistent. One verified buyer noted: *"These are the best value indoor balls I've found. They last longer than the cheap 40-hole ones and play much truer."* Another echoed the durability angle: *"Played four sessions with one ball and it's still round, no cracks."* Multiple reviewers specifically call out the ball's resistance to cracking on hard drives — a common failure point for budget options.

**Where it stumbles**

The firmness cuts both ways. If you're used to softer indoor balls, the X-26 can feel a bit "clicky" off the paddle and slightly unforgiving on dinks. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real adjustment period. The second gripe is packaging inconsistency — a few 12-pack buyers reported receiving balls loose in a box rather than in a tube, which risks warping in transit. It's not widespread, but it's worth checking on arrival.

**Who should buy this**

- **League and tournament players** who need USA Pickleball-approved balls without paying premium prices for a 3-pack that vanishes in a week
- **Recreational indoor players** who want a ball that won't crack on the first aggressive serve
- **Club organizers or coaches** buying in bulk — the 100-pack is a legit cost-saver

**Skip it if** you primarily play outdoors (buy a dedicated outdoor ball), or if you're a soft-touch player who absolutely needs that cushioned feel of a premium DuraFast.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does one ball typically last?**

**Q: Can I use these outdoors?**
A: Technically yes, but they're not designed for it. Even light wind will affect their flight significantly. Stick to indoor courts.
