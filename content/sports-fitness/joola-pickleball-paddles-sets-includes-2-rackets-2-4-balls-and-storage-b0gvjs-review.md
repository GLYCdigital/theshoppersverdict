**What's actually in the box**

This isn't one of those flimsy "everything included" kits that falls apart in a month. You get two JOOLA paddles, four outdoor balls, and a padded carry case. The paddles themselves are the headline — they feature a carbon fiber face laminated over a polymer honeycomb core. **Build quality and on-court performance**

The first thing you'll notice is the weight — these come in around 7.5 to 8 ounces, which is right in the sweet spot for recreational and intermediate players. The balance point is slightly head-heavy, giving you extra momentum on drives without feeling like you're swinging a sledgehammer. The carbon face delivers consistent ball release on spin shots, and the polymer core does a solid job of deadening the "ping" you get from cheaper paddles.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I've played with $100+ paddles and these feel 80% as good for half the price. The grip is comfortable and I didn't get any wrist fatigue after a two-hour session."* That tracks — the grip is a cushioned, perforated wrap that does absorb a fair amount of vibration on off-center hits.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The edge guard is on the thinner side. If you're the type of player who regularly scrapes the paddle edge along the court to scoop up low balls, you'll start seeing scuff marks sooner than you would on a reinforced model like a Selkirk or a premium JOOLA. It won't affect performance, but it will affect how new it looks.

Also, the handle measures about 4.5 inches. That's standard for most recreational paddles, but if you have larger hands or prefer a two-handed backhand, you'll find yourself choking up more than you'd like. This isn't a dealbreaker for most, but it's worth knowing before you buy.
