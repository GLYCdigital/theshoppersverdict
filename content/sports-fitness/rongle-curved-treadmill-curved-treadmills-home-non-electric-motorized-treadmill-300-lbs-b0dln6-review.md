# Is This $700 Curved Treadmill a Smart Buy or a Wallet Trap?

A curved treadmill for under $700 sounds like a steal. The RongLe Curved Treadmill promises the same self-powered design used in high-end gyms like Assault Fitness and Woodway — at a fraction of the price. But with a 2.3/5 average across 150 Amazon ratings, the math on this one doesn't add up for most shoppers. Here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box arrives with the assembled frame, a curved running deck, and hardware. There's no motor, no console, no incline adjustment — just you and a curved belt that you push with your feet. The deck is roughly 40 inches long with a 16-inch-wide running surface, and the claimed weight capacity is 300 pounds.

**Assembly takes about an hour** with two people. The frame is welded steel, and the curved track uses a series of rollers rather than a traditional flat belt. It's a genuinely different mechanical system than a motorized treadmill.

## Performance and Build Quality

The curved design forces you to run on the balls of your feet, which promotes better posture and reduces impact on knees compared to heel-striking on a flat deck. That's the theory, and it works — when the machine functions properly.

**The problems start with the belt.** Multiple reviewers report the belt slipping or catching, especially after 20-30 hours of use. One buyer wrote: *"The belt started making a grinding noise after two weeks. Tried adjusting the tension per the manual, but it never ran smoothly again."* Another noted: *"The rollers feel cheap — I can see them flexing when I run at speed."*

The resistance curve is also steeper than premium curved treadmills. On an Assault Runner, the belt moves freely once you get it going. On the RongLe, you're fighting friction the entire time. That's not necessarily bad for a workout, but it makes interval training frustrating — you'll exhaust your calves before you hit your target heart rate.

## What Buyers Actually Say (The Good and the Ugly)

The 2.3-star rating isn't from one bad batch. It's a pattern. Here's what consistently comes up:

**Praise:**
- *"The workout is intense. I sweat more in 15 minutes than 30 on my old motorized treadmill."*
- *"No plug needed is great for my apartment — I just push it against the wall when done."*

**Complaints:**
- Belt tension issues requiring constant adjustment
- Loud clicking from the rollers during use
- Frame wobble at speeds above 8 mph
- Customer service unresponsive — multiple reports of emails going unanswered for weeks
- One reviewer's unit arrived with a bent frame rail, and replacement parts took 6 weeks

## Where It Falls Short

The RongLe's biggest flaw is consistency. When it works, it delivers a solid, no-frills curved running experience. When it doesn't — and the review data suggests it fails often — you're stuck with a 100+ pound paperweight and no support.

**The learning curve is real.** If you've never used a curved treadmill, plan for 2-3 weeks of adjustment. Your Achilles tendons and calves will be sore. That's true of all curved treadmills, but the RongLe's heavy resistance amplifies the difficulty.

There's also zero data tracking. No Bluetooth, no heart rate monitor, no app. You're buying a purely mechanical device in 2026.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're an experienced runner who likes the curved-deck training style, you're on a tight budget, and you're comfortable doing your own maintenance. If you can handle basic belt adjustments and don't mind a loud-ish machine, this could work.

**Skip it if:** You're a beginner, you want a quiet workout, you need incline options, or you expect customer support to be there when something breaks. Also skip it if you weigh over 220 pounds — the frame flex reported by heavier users is concerning.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this treadmill good for walking?**
A: No. Curved treadmills are designed for running. Walking on one feels awkward — the curve makes your foot strike unnatural at slow speeds.

**Q: How loud is the RongLe curved treadmill?**
A: Quieter than a motorized treadmill, but not silent. The roller mechanism produces a rhythmic clicking that's noticeable in quiet rooms. You'll hear it through apartment floors.

**Q: Does it require any electrical outlet?**
A: Correct, it's completely non-electric. No plug, no power cord, no electricity costs. That's a genuine advantage if you want to run anywhere.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.3/5**

The RongLe curved treadmill is a gamble. The concept is sound — self-powered curved running is an excellent workout — and the price is tempting. But with 150 ratings averaging 2.3 stars, the quality control issues are too frequent to ignore. You're rolling the dice on whether you get a functional unit or a lemon.

**Buy it if** you're a budget-conscious runner who wants curved-deck training and accepts the risk of DIY fixes. **Skip it if** you want reliability, customer support, or a machine that works out of the box. For most shoppers, spending $200-300 more on a reputable brand like ProForm's non-motorized options or saving for an Assault Fitness model is the smarter long-term investment.
