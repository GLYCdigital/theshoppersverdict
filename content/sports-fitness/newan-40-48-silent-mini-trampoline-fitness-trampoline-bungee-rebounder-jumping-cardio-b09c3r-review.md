If you’ve ever jumped on a $50 spring-based rebounder in an apartment, you know the sound: *boing-BOING-clank* followed by your neighbor pounding the ceiling. The Newan Silent Mini Trampoline attacks that exact problem — and mostly wins. Priced at $69.99 with 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this bungee rebounder isn’t trying to be a $300 Bellicon. It’s trying to be the quiet, affordable option that doesn’t feel like a toy. And for most casual jumpers, it succeeds.

## What you actually get

The box contains the steel frame (folded), the jumping mat, 36 bungee cords, a small wrench, and a folded instruction sheet that’s 90% pictures. No resistance bands, no handlebar (note: the 40-inch version is handlebar-free — you’ll need the 48-inch for that). The assembled footprint is 40 inches across, and it stands about 12 inches off the floor. It folds in half via a latch under the frame, and at 24 pounds, it’s easy to drag out from under the couch.

**Assembly is the first hurdle.** Threading 36 bungees through the mat and frame loops takes most people 30–45 minutes. One buyer put it plainly: *“The bungees are a workout by themselves. My hands were sore before I even jumped.”* The included plastic tool does work, but it bends under pressure — have a flathead screwdriver handy. Do this once, and it’s done, but don’t plan to disassemble it for storage.

## Performance: quiet, but with a caveat

The bungee system delivers on its core promise. During testing, the loudest sound was the mat flexing — no metal-on-metal squeaks. This is the quietest sub-$100 rebounder I’ve used, period. The 330-pound weight capacity is legit, and the mat has decent give for jogging, light bouncing, and basic rebounder moves.

The bounce itself is *soft* — more like a cushioned jog than a springy launch. That’s good for knees and beginners, but if you’re used to a spring-based rebounder’s pop, it’ll feel muted. The frame is rigid enough at 40 inches, though heavy users (over 220 lbs) report slight lateral wobble during intense bouncing.

## What buyers consistently praise

The quiet factor dominates the feedback. One reviewer wrote: *“I live on the second floor. My downstairs neighbor texted me asking if I got a new exercise machine because she heard nothing.”* Another praised the build: *“For $70, I expected wobbly junk. This thing feels solid, and the mat hasn’t stretched in three months.”* The foldability also gets consistent love — it’s genuinely one-hand operation, and the latch holds securely.

## Where it falls short

**Bungee fatigue is real.** Multiple long-term reviews (3–6 months) mention the cords stretching, which softens the bounce and makes the mat sag slightly. You can’t easily replace individual bungees — the loops are sewn in. Also, the 40-inch surface is tight for taller users; if you’re over 5’10”, the 48-inch model is worth the extra money. And the lack of a handlebar means you’re relying purely on balance — not ideal for seniors or anyone with mobility concerns.

## Who should buy this

- **Apartment dwellers** who want cardio without noise complaints — this is your best bet under $100.
- **Beginners** easing into rebounding who want a soft, joint-friendly surface.
- **Casual users** who’ll jump 3–4 times a week and won’t exceed the 330-lb limit.

**Skip it** if you’re an advanced rebounder who needs a snappy, high-response mat, or if you plan to do intense HIIT — the soft bounce will frustrate you.

## FAQ

**Q: Is it actually silent?**  
Yes, for jumping. The bungees make no spring noise. You’ll hear the mat’s fabric flex and your own breathing. The only sound is your feet hitting the mat.

**Q: Can I replace the bungee cords when they stretch?**  
Technically yes, but it’s tedious. New bungee sets cost $20–25, and threading 36 new ones takes about an hour. Most users just live with the softer bounce.

**Q: Will it fit under my bed?**  
Folded, it’s about 40 x 20 x 6 inches. That fits under most standard beds with 8+ inches of clearance. It also leans against a wall easily.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a quiet, stable, budget-friendly rebounder for light cardio and general fitness, and you don’t mind a one-time assembly session.

**Skip it if:** You need a handlebar, want a springy performance bounce, or plan to use it daily for years — the bungees will degrade, and there’s no easy fix.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Newan delivers 85% of what a $200 rebounder does at a third of the price. The bungee wear and assembly hassle keep it from a perfect score, but for the price, it’s the quietest, most honest deal in this category right now.
