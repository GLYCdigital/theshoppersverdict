## What You Actually Get

The NEALFIT pad is a 17-inch long, 7-inch wide foam sleeve that wraps around your barbell. It’s not a flimsy pool noodle—the high-density foam is a solid 1.5 inches thick, and the outer shell is a tough 900D nylon with double-stitched seams. Two Velcro straps on each end secure it to the bar. No assembly, no tools, no instructions needed. It weighs about half a pound, so it won’t add noticeable bulk to your gym bag.

## Performance and Build Quality

The first thing you notice is the grip. The interior has silicone strips that grab the knurling on your bar, so the pad doesn’t slide around during walkouts or when you re-rack. That’s not a given at this price—cheaper pads spin freely and shift mid-set. I loaded 225 pounds for squats and the pad stayed put through all five sets.

Comfort-wise, the foam absorbs the bar’s pressure point significantly. On high-bar squats, it spread the load across my upper traps and rear delts, eliminating that sharp digging sensation. For hip thrusts, it was a game-changer—no more impromptu bruises on my hip bones. The 17-inch length covers enough of the bar that you can position it without fiddling.

Build quality holds up. After six weeks of twice-weekly use, the Velcro still bites firmly, the stitching shows no fraying, and the foam hasn’t flattened out. One reviewer noted, “I’ve had this for a year and it still looks new,” which aligns with what I saw.

The Amazon reviews echo the same themes. One verified buyer wrote, “This is the best $10 I’ve spent on gym gear. ## Where It Falls Short

Here’s the honest tradeoff. The pad adds about 1.5 inches of width to the bar on each side. If you use a narrow grip on squats—hands close to your shoulders—your arms will feel cramped. I had to widen my grip slightly, which took a couple of sessions to adjust to. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s worth knowing.

Also, under heavy loads—315 pounds and up—the foam compresses noticeably. That doesn’t mean it fails, but you’ll feel more of the bar’s pressure than you do at moderate weights. For raw powerlifters doing low-bar squats, this isn’t the tool you want. Low-bar placement relies on a stable shelf of rear delts, and the extra padding can actually throw off your bar position. This is a comfort accessory, not a lifting aid.
