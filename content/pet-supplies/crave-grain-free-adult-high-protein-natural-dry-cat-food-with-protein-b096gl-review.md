If your cat treats every meal like a negotiation, you've probably cycled through a dozen food brands already. CRAVE's Grain Free Adult High Protein formula has earned a 4.6-star average from 229 Amazon ratings — but that number alone doesn't tell you whether it's right for *your* cat. Here's what the packaging doesn't say.

## What You're Actually Buying

The 10-pound bag (also available in 5-pound and 24-pound sizes) features chicken as the first ingredient, followed by chicken meal and turkey meal. That's a solid protein-forward lineup — no vague "meat by-products" hiding in the top spots. The guaranteed analysis shows 40% crude protein minimum, which puts it firmly in the high-protein camp alongside premium brands that cost twice as much.

What's *not* in the bag matters too: no corn, wheat, soy, or artificial flavors. If your cat has demonstrated grain sensitivities, this formula sidesteps the usual suspects.

## Real-World Performance

The kibble itself is small — roughly a quarter-inch in diameter — which works well for both kittens and senior cats with dental issues. One reviewer noted, "My 14-year-old tabby who lost teeth last year manages these easily. She's actually gained back the weight she lost on her old food."

Multiple verified purchasers mention their cats became noticeably more energetic within two weeks of switching. That aligns with the higher protein-to-carb ratio. Cats are obligate carnivores; they process animal protein far more efficiently than plant-based fillers.

The 4.6-star average isn't without context. Of the 229 ratings, roughly 80% are five-star, but there's a consistent thread in the critical reviews: stomach upset during transition. One reviewer wrote, "My cat loved the taste but threw up twice in the first week. We had to mix it 50/50 with her old food for ten days before she adjusted." That's not a defect — it's a reality of switching any high-protein food cold turkey. But you should plan for a gradual transition.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs:

**Pea protein and tapioca starch** appear further down the ingredient list. These are common in grain-free kibble as structural binders, but they're not ideal for cats prone to weight gain. If your cat is already overweight, this food's calorie density (around 3,700 kcal/kg) could work against you.

**Inconsistent kibble size** is a minor but recurring complaint. A few reviewers noted that some bags produce more powder at the bottom than others. Not a dealbreaker — just don't expect surgical precision in every batch.

**The smell.** Every cat food has an aroma, but this one leans toward "intensely meaty." If you're sensitive to pet food odors, you'll want an airtight container immediately.

## Who Should Buy This — And Who Shouldn't

**Buy it if:** Your cat is a healthy adult with no known food allergies, you want a high-protein grain-free formula, and you'd rather spend $35–$40 per 10-pound bag than $55+ on premium brands with similar macros.

**Skip it if:** Your cat has a confirmed sensitivity to peas or legumes (some cats develop allergic reactions to these proteins), or if your vet has recommended a low-protein diet for kidney or liver concerns. This food is *not* suitable for cats with certain medical conditions.

## Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Is this good for kittens?**
No — CRAVE makes a separate kitten formula with higher fat and DHA. This adult formula won't hurt a kitten in a pinch, but it's not nutritionally optimized for growth.

**Q: How does it compare to Blue Buffalo Wilderness?**
Both are grain-free high-protein foods with similar macros. CRAVE typically runs $5–$8 less per bag and has a simpler ingredient list with fewer plant extracts. Blue Buffalo has slightly more fiber, which can help with hairball control.

**Q: Can I mix this with wet food?**
Yes, and many reviewers do exactly that. Just account for the extra calories — a 10-pound cat needs roughly 200–250 calories daily total, wet or dry.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

CRAVE Grain Free Adult High Protein delivers exactly what it promises: a protein-forward, grain-free kibble that most cats genuinely enjoy. The 40% protein content at this price point is a legitimate value — you'd pay 40–60% more for comparable nutrition from boutique brands.

**Buy it if** you want high-protein nutrition without the luxury-brand markup and your cat has a normal digestive system. **Skip it if** your cat has known legume sensitivities or a medical condition requiring restricted protein.

The 4.6-star rating reflects a product that satisfies the majority of cats and owners — with a clear caveat that transitions need to be gradual. For most households, this is a smart, budget-conscious choice that prioritizes what cats actually need: animal protein, without the filler.
