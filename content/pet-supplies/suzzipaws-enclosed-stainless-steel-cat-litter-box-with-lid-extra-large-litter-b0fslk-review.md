If you've ever deep-cleaned a plastic litter box and noticed that faint ammonia smell *never* quite disappears, you already know the problem. Plastic is porous. It traps odors, scratches easily, and harbors bacteria no matter how hard you scrub. The Suzzipaws Enclosed Stainless Steel Litter Box with Lid addresses that core issue with a material upgrade, and at $79.99, it's priced like a serious piece of pet gear. But does it justify the jump from a $30 plastic hooded box? Mostly yes — with a few caveats worth knowing before you buy.

**What You Actually Get**

The box ships with the stainless steel base, a matching lid, a swing door, and a carbon filter insert. As shown in the product image, the design is clean and modern — no fake wood grain or pastel plastic here. The steel base is the headline feature: it's one continuous piece with no seams where urine can seep into crevices. The lid snaps on securely but lifts off entirely for cleaning. The entry is a front-facing opening with a clear swing door that cats push through. For a large cat, the interior is genuinely roomy — roughly 22 inches long and 17 inches wide, which accommodates most cats up to 15 pounds without cramping.

**Real-World Performance**

Start with the odor claim, because that's why most people pay extra. Stainless steel doesn't absorb urine the way plastic does, so the base won't develop that permanent stink after six months of use. The carbon filter in the lid helps trap ammonia odors before they hit your nostrils. In practice, daily scooping plus the filter means the box stays neutral-smelling between cleanouts. One verified buyer noted, "I was skeptical about spending this much, but the smell difference vs. my old plastic box is night and day. Even the room around it doesn't smell."

Litter scatter is also better controlled than an open box. The hood contains kicked litter, and the swing door catches some of what would otherwise fly out. That said, it's not a miracle worker — long-haired cats or enthusiastic diggers will still track some litter out the opening. The door itself is a minor annoyance: some cats take days to adjust to pushing through it. It's removable if your cat refuses, but you'll need a screwdriver to take it off, which feels like an unnecessary hassle.

Cleaning is where this box shines for the owner. Because the lid lifts off completely, you can dump the entire base straight into a trash bag without bending over or scraping out corners. The smooth steel surface also rinses clean under a faucet or with a hose — no scrubbing grooves. Multiple reviewers mentioned this as the main reason they'd never go back to plastic. One wrote, "I can actually wash this thing without gagging. The steel doesn't hold the smell after a rinse."

**Where It Falls Short**

The price is the first hurdle. $79.99 is three to four times what a decent hooded plastic box costs. If your cat is a finicky door-hater or you're on a tight budget, that premium stings. The swing door is the most polarizing feature — while most cats adapt within a week, some refuse entirely, and removing it voids some of the odor-containment benefit. Also, the steel base is heavier than plastic. It's less likely to tip when a cat jumps in, but lifting it for cleaning with a full load of litter is a two-hand job. Finally, the carbon filter is a consumable — you'll need to replace it every few months, and replacement filters aren't always in stock.

**Who Should Buy This**

This box is for owners who are tired of replacing plastic boxes every year because they've absorbed odors. It's also ideal for multi-cat households where odor control is a constant battle, or for anyone with a sensitive nose who wants a box that actually cleans up. Skip it if you're on a tight budget, if your cat is extremely shy about new enclosed spaces, or if you prefer disposable-style solutions.

**FAQ**

*Is the swing door removable?* Yes, but it requires a screwdriver to detach. Removing it makes the box easier for hesitant cats but reduces odor containment.

*Does stainless steel really control odor better than plastic?* Yes. Steel is non-porous, so urine doesn't soak into the material itself. The base won't develop permanent odor even after months of use.

*What size litter scoop fits this box?* Standard scoopers work fine. The base is wide enough that you can reach all corners without scraping your knuckles on the sides.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're done replacing stinky plastic boxes every year and want a durable, easy-to-clean solution that genuinely controls odors.

**Skip it if:** You're price-sensitive, or your cat refuses covered boxes and you don't want to deal with door removal.
