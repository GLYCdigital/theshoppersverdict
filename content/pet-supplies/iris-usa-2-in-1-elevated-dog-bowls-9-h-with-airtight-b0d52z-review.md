## What You Actually Get

The box contains the assembled stand (9 inches tall), two 2-quart stainless steel bowls, and a 6-quart storage bin that sits in the base. The bowls are the real selling point — heavy-gauge stainless with rolled rims, not the flimsy stamped metal you see on budget feeders. The base has a cutout that holds the storage bin flush, so it doesn't look like an afterthought.

As shown in the product image, the bins and bowls are separate pieces. The stand itself is polypropylene plastic — not bamboo or wood. That's a plus for hygiene and a minus for aesthetics, depending on your kitchen vibe.

## Real-World Performance

The 9-inch height is the sweet spot for my 55-pound Lab mix. She no longer hunches to eat, and the slight angle helps her swallow more comfortably. For a Great Dane or a Chihuahua, this height won't work — measure your dog's shoulder height first.

The airtight lid is the star feature. I tested it with a fresh bag of kibble, and three weeks later it still had that just-opened crunch. One buyer put it plainly: *"The seal actually works — no more stale food or ants finding their way in."* The lid has a gasket and two locking tabs that click with satisfying authority.

The stainless bowls are dishwasher-safe, which is non-negotiable for me. The stand wipes clean with a damp cloth. No crevices where crumbs hide.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**The plastic stand is lighter than it looks.** On hardwood or tile, a determined 70-pound dog can nudge the whole unit across the floor mid-meal. There's no rubber base or grippy feet. A $3 pack of adhesive furniture pads solves this, but you shouldn't have to buy extras.

**The storage bin opening is narrower than the bin itself.** When you get down to the last cup of kibble, you'll be tilting the bin at an awkward angle to scoop it out. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying for daily use.

**The bowls sit in recessed wells but aren't locked in place.** An enthusiastic eater — the kind who flips bowls — can occasionally pop one loose. It hasn't happened to me, but a few reviewers mention it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Medium-to-large breed owners** (dogs 25–70 lbs) who want proper eating posture
- **Single-dog households** — the 6-quart bin holds roughly 6–7 pounds of kibble, enough for 2–3 weeks for a medium dog

## FAQ

**Q: Can the bowls go in the dishwasher?**
A: Yes, both stainless bowls are dishwasher-safe. The plastic stand and storage bin should be hand-washed — the heat can warp the lid seal.

**Q: How much kibble fits in the storage container?**
A: 6 quarts, which is roughly 5–7 pounds of dry food depending on kibble density. For a 50-pound dog eating 3 cups daily, that's about two weeks of food.

**Q: Is the 9-inch height right for my dog?**
A: Measure from the floor to the bottom of your dog's chest while standing. The bowl rim should be roughly level with that point. For most medium breeds, 9 inches works; for taller dogs, look for a 12-inch model.
