## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect. The lavender scent, while effective at masking odor, is strong when you first open the package. If you're sensitive to fragrances or have a dog with allergies, this could be an issue. One reviewer mentions: *"The smell hits you when you open the bag. It fades after a few minutes, but it's intense initially."* 

There's also no wetness indicator strip — you'll need to check manually or swap on a schedule. And if you leave a pad down for more than 24 hours, the edges start to curl slightly, which can catch a dog's paw. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy if:** You have a large breed (50+ lbs), a senior dog with incontinence, or you're potty training a puppy and want maximum coverage with minimal floor cleanup. The 30x36 size is ideal for crates and x-pens where standard pads leave gaps.

**Skip if:** You have a small dog that uses a corner of the pad — the extra size is wasted money. Also skip if anyone in your household is fragrance-sensitive or if your dog has a history of chewing pads (the scent could increase attraction).

## FAQ

**How often should I change these pads?**
For a single dog, every 12-24 hours depending on output. If saturated, change immediately — the leak-proof layer holds, but bacteria build-up happens fast.

**Can these be used in a crate?**
Yes, the 28x34in fits standard large crates (36x24in and up). The 30x36in is better for 42-inch crates or x-pens.

**Do these attract dogs to pee on them?**
The lavender scent isn't an attractant — it's purely for odor control. If you need a training aid, look for pads with a specific urine-attractant formula.
