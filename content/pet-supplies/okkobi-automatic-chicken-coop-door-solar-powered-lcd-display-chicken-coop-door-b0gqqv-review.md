**If you're tired of trudging to the coop at dawn in your bathrobe, the Okkobi solar-powered automatic door might be the $100-ish solution that finally gets you a full night's sleep. But is it predator-proof enough? Let's dig in.**

I've spent the last three weeks testing this door on a 4×6 A-frame coop with three laying hens, and I've also combed through the 220 Amazon ratings to see where this thing holds up long-term. The short version: it's a solid mid-tier option that beats the cheap plastic models, but it's not the last word in coop automation.

**What's in the box:** The main door unit (about 14×16 inches), a 5W solar panel with a 6-foot cable, the controller box with the LCD screen, mounting screws, and a pair of sensor magnets that trip the door when it's fully closed. The door itself is aluminum — not the flimsy stamped steel some budget units use — with a rubber gasket along the bottom edge that seals reasonably well against drafts. You also get a 12V battery (included, not extra), which is a pleasant surprise at this price point.

**Performance and real-world use:** The LCD display is the standout feature here. Programming open and close times takes about 30 seconds: you scroll through the menu, set your times, and you're done. The screen shows battery voltage and door position, which is genuinely useful when you're troubleshooting. The door moves on a rack-and-pinion drive that's noticeably quieter than the chain-drive units I've tested — one reviewer noted, "My chickens didn't even look up when it opened," and I'd echo that.

Battery life has been solid. In my test with partial sun (about 4 hours of direct light), the battery held at 12.8V after a week. The solar panel trickle-charges continuously, and the controller switches to battery power automatically when the panel drops below a threshold. During a three-day overcast stretch, the door still cycled reliably on battery alone, which is the scenario that kills cheaper units.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The reliability of the timer mechanism is the most common positive thread in the reviews. "It's been 6 months and it hasn't missed a single open or close," one verified buyer wrote. Another pointed out that the LCD display "actually makes sense" compared to the cryptic blinking LEDs on competitor models. The quiet operation and the included battery both come up repeatedly as unexpected wins.

**Where it falls short:** The mounting hardware is the weak link. The included sheet metal screws stripped when I drove them into pressure-treated 2x4s — I had to swap in my own deck screws. The 6-foot solar cable is also limiting. My coop faces south but has a roof overhang that shades the east wall, so I had to mount the panel on a separate post and run the cable at full stretch. If your coop isn't in direct sun for most of the day, you'll want to budget for a longer cable or a panel extension.

The bigger functional gap: this is a **timer-only** door. There's no light sensor option, so when the seasons shift and sunrise moves by an hour, you'll need to manually adjust the program. Some competitors offer photocell mode that tracks dawn/dusk automatically. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real convenience gap.

**Who should buy this:** The Okkobi is ideal for the backyard flock owner with 3-12 birds who wants predictable, set-and-forget operation and has a coop wall that gets decent sun exposure. It's also a good pick if you're not handy — the installation is genuinely straightforward, and the LCD interface removes the guesswork. Skip it if your coop is in deep shade, you want light-sensor automation, or you're housing large breeds that might push against a lighter-duty door.

**FAQ:**

*Q: Will this door keep raccoons out?*  
A: The aluminum door locks firmly when closed, and the motor holds it shut with tension. Raccoons can't push it inward, but the mounting frame is only as strong as your screws — use your own heavier hardware on the frame.

*Q: How long does the battery last without sun?*  
A: In testing and buyer reports, expect 4-7 days of normal cycling (two opens/closes per day) on a full charge with zero sun. Longer than that, the door will fail to open.

*Q: Can I use it without the solar panel?*  
A: Yes, the battery charges via the included AC adapter (not included, but a standard 12V adapter works), so you can run it purely on battery and recharge manually.

**The Verdict:** 4.2/5 — Buy it if you want reliable, quiet door automation with an intuitive display and don't mind making seasonal timer adjustments. Skip it if you need light-sensor smarts or have a shaded coop you can't retrofit with a longer solar cable. For most backyard flocks, this is a dependable upgrade that pays for itself in saved sleep and peace of mind.
