## Is This Actually a Carnivore Diet in a Kibble Bag?

Most "high protein" dog foods are marketing sleight-of-hand — they slap a protein percentage on the front and backfill the recipe with peas, lentils, and potato starch. Go! Solutions Carnivore doesn't do that. As shown in the product image, the guaranteed analysis reads 38% protein, but the real story is the ingredient order: deboned chicken, chicken meal, turkey meal, salmon meal, deboned turkey. That's five animal proteins before a single plant ingredient appears.

The 4.2-star rating across 210 reviews reflects a product that delivers on its core promise but isn't without friction. Here's what you actually need to know before spending your money.

## What's in the Bag

You get a 25lb (or 12lb) bag of dark, dense kibble roughly the size of a nickel. The pieces are crunchy with a noticeable meaty aroma — not the faint "baked biscuit" smell of budget kibble. The formula includes omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids from salmon, plus chelated minerals for absorption. Notably absent: grain, potato, tapioca, carrageenan, and artificial preservatives.

The resealable zipper is the cheapest-feeling component. Multiple reviewers report it tearing off within the first week. Plan to transfer this to a dedicated food storage container from day one.

## Real-World Performance

The protein density does what it claims. Active dogs, working breeds, and puppies with high energy needs consistently show better muscle tone and shinier coats on this formula. One verified buyer wrote: *"My GSD was skin and bones on a popular vet brand. Switched to this and within six weeks he filled out properly — no more rib show."* Another noted: *"My picky husky actually finishes her bowl now. She's never done that with any other kibble."*

The palatability is a genuine strength. The salmon meal gives it an odor that most dogs find irresistible — useful if you're currently coaxing a reluctant eater or dealing with a senior dog whose appetite has dropped.

## Where It Falls Short

The digestive adjustment is the most consistent complaint in the review data. Dogs switching from lower-protein, grain-inclusive foods often experience loose stools for 5–10 days, even with a proper 7-day transition. If your dog has a sensitive stomach, this is a real consideration — not a dealbreaker, but you'll want to mix it with pumpkin or a probiotic during the switch.

The price point is the second friction point. At roughly $2.50–$3.00 per pound, it undercuts boutique brands like Orijen or Acana but still costs meaningfully more than Kirkland or 4Health. You're paying for the clean ingredient profile and the animal-first ratio — that premium is justified only if your dog actually thrives on it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Active and working dogs** — hunting breeds, agility dogs, or any dog burning serious calories daily
- **Picky eaters** — the salmon-forward aroma solves "I'm not eating that" standoffs
- **Owners who distrust plant-heavy kibble** — if you've read the first five ingredients on most supermarket brands and felt uneasy, this is a cleaner alternative

**Skip it if** your dog has a confirmed grain allergy *and* a sensitive GI tract — you'll fight the transition period harder than the benefit is worth. Also skip if you're on a strict budget, because this isn't a value brand.

## FAQ

**Will this fix my dog's itchy skin?**
It can help if the itching stems from grain sensitivity. But if the allergen is chicken (a common trigger), the chicken meal in this formula could make things worse. If itching persists after 4–6 weeks, ask your vet about a limited-ingredient diet.

**Is 38% protein safe for a senior dog?**
Usually yes, but senior dogs with kidney issues need reduced protein. This is not the right food for a dog with diagnosed renal disease — talk to your vet first.

**Can puppies eat this?**
The formula supports growth, but the kibble size is large for small breeds. For medium and large breed puppies under one year, it's workable. Small breeds should get a smaller-kibble formula.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you have an active, picky, or underweight dog that needs a genuinely protein-first diet and you're willing to manage a transition week and pay a premium for clean ingredients.

**Skip it if** your dog has a chicken sensitivity, a fragile digestive system, or you want maximum value per pound — there are cheaper foods that meet basic nutrition without the finesse.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The formula is excellent and the palatability is elite, but the flimsy bag closure and digestive adjustment period knock off points. When it works, it works beautifully. When it doesn't, you'll know within two weeks.
