If you've got a small backyard pond that's turned into pea soup, the **Pennington Aquagarden Inpond** is one of those rare all-in-one solutions that actually does what it says. It's not a powerhouse, and it won't fix a 2,000-gallon koi pond, but for a decorative water feature or modest fish pond under 500 gallons, this pump-filter-UV combo is a solid, space-saving pick. Here's the real story after digging through 210 ratings and the spec sheet.

**What's in the box:** You get the submersible pump, a foam filter cartridge, the UV clarifier chamber, a 16-foot power cord, and a small LED spotlight that mounts on the pump housing. The whole unit sits on the pond floor — no external plumbing, no separate filter box. The pump draws water through the foam, passes it through the UV chamber, and pushes it back out through a fountain head or a hose adapter. The included fountain attachment gives you a decent spray pattern, but the real value is the UV bulb, which zaps single-celled algae that cause green water.

**Performance and build quality:** The pump moves about 130 gallons per hour at zero lift — enough for a gentle fountain or a small waterfall up to 3 feet high. The UV clarifier is rated for ponds up to 500 gallons, and most buyers report noticeable clarity within a week. As one reviewer put it: *"My pond went from murky green to clear in four days. I was skeptical, but the UV really works."* The build is all-black plastic with a sealed motor housing; it's not heavy-duty, but it's fine for seasonal use. The LED spotlight is a nice touch — it's bright enough to make the water shimmer at night, and it runs off the same power cord, so no extra wiring.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The ease of setup is the #1 recurring theme. You literally drop it in, plug it in, and it runs. The UV bulb is replaceable (though you'll need to source a compatible T5 bulb), and the foam filter rinses clean under a hose. Many owners with small preformed ponds or whiskey-barrel water features love that it's one unit — no tangled tubes, no separate pump in a skimmer box.

**Where it falls short — honest tradeoffs:** The flow rate is the big limitation. If you have fish, you'll want more aeration and filtration than this provides. The filter foam is small — about the size of a sponge — and it clogs quickly if you have leaves or debris. You'll be cleaning it every 2–3 weeks in summer, and there's no bypass valve, so you have to shut the pump down to clean it. The UV bulb also needs replacing every 12–18 months (roughly $20–25), which adds to the long-term cost. A few reviewers noted the pump is a bit noisy right out of the box, though it quiets down after a few hours of running.

**Who should buy this:**  
- **The casual pond owner** with a preformed pond or a stock tank under 300 gallons.  
- **The patio water-feature person** who wants a fountain, filter, and UV in one compact package.  
- **The first-time pond keeper** who wants something simple and doesn't want to piece together separate components.  

**Skip it if:** You have a larger koi pond, heavy fish loads, or want a waterfall pump with serious lift. This is a clarifier and decorative pump, not a full biological filtration system.

**FAQ:**  
**Q: Can I use it in winter?**  
A: No. The pump and UV chamber are not freeze-proof. Bring it indoors when temps drop below freezing.

**Q: How often do I need to replace the UV bulb?**  
A: About every 12–18 months. The clarifier stops working when the bulb dies, but the pump still runs.

**Q: Will it work with a waterfall?**  
A: Yes, but only up to 3 feet of lift. For higher falls, you'll lose most of the flow.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you have a small pond or water feature under 500 gallons and want one plug-and-play unit that clears green water and adds a fountain. **Skip it if** you need serious filtration for fish or a high-lift waterfall pump.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's not perfect, but for the price, it delivers clear water and simple operation. The 4.2-star average across 210 ratings is earned: most people are happy, and the complaints are mostly about the filter size and flow limits, which are fair tradeoffs for this size and price point.
