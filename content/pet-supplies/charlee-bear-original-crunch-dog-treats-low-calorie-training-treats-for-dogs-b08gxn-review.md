**If you've ever calculated how many calories you're actually feeding your dog during a training session, you already know the problem.** Most commercial treats pack 15-25 calories per piece, which means a 20-minute obedience session with a highly motivated pup can quietly add 100+ calories to their daily intake. For a 15-pound dog, that's nearly 10% of their daily caloric needs — in treats alone.

Charlee Bear Original Crunch treats solve that math problem. At just 3 calories per piece, these bite-sized, liver-flavored morsels let you reward generously without turning your training sessions into an accidental bulk-up program.

## What You're Actually Getting

The bag contains small, bone-shaped treats roughly the size of a quarter — thin, crunchy, and light. As shown in the product image, the bag features a cheerful golden retriever, but don't let the marketing fool you: these treats are formulated for dogs of all sizes. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: whole grain corn, soybean meal, animal fat preserved with mixed tocopherols (vitamin E), liver meal, and salt. That's it. No artificial colors, no corn syrup, no questionable preservatives.

## Real-World Performance

The crunch factor is the standout feature here. Dogs seem to genuinely enjoy the snap and texture — one reviewer noted, "My picky shih tzu goes crazy for these. She hears the bag crinkle and comes running from across the house." The liver flavor is present but not overpowering, which makes them versatile enough for both training rewards and casual treat-giving.

Where these really shine is in high-repetition training scenarios. Because they're small and low-calorie, you can reward your dog 10-15 times in a single session without guilt. They're also easy to break into even smaller pieces for micro-rewards during precision work like heel position or stays.

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the crumb factor is real. The crunchy texture means they shatter when dropped, so expect to find "treat dust" in your training area. Second, the pieces are genuinely small — for large breeds like German Shepherds or Labs, one piece might feel less like a reward and more like a tease. You'll likely need to double up for bigger dogs.

The packaging also lacks a resealable closure. Once opened, you'll want to transfer them to an airtight container or use a clip. Left in the bag, they can absorb moisture and lose their signature crunch.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You have a small-to-medium breed, you're doing any kind of formal training that requires frequent rewards, your dog is on a weight management plan, or you just want a guilt-free way to show affection throughout the day.

**Skip these if:** You have a giant breed that needs substantial rewards to feel motivated, your dog prefers soft/chewy textures, or you're looking for a treat that doubles as a dental chew.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these good for dogs with allergies?**
A: They contain corn and soy, so they're not ideal for dogs with grain or soy sensitivities. However, they're free of common allergens like chicken, beef, and dairy, making them a decent option for dogs with protein allergies.

**Q: How many can I give my dog per day?**
A: The bag recommends limiting treats to 10% of daily caloric intake. For a 20-pound dog eating about 400 calories daily, that's roughly 13 treats. For training sessions, you can safely exceed this occasionally, but track it for weight management.

**Q: Are these suitable for puppies?**
A: Yes, the small size and crunchy texture make them appropriate for puppies over 8 weeks old. They're easy to chew and won't pose a choking hazard.
