If your dog drinks like a fire hose aimed at the floor, you've already mopped up enough frustration to justify this purchase. The Gardner Pet Stainless Steel No Spill Water Bowl (Small) is a 10-ounce solution to a very specific problem: dogs that splash more water than they swallow. 

The trick is a floating disc that rests on the water's surface. As your dog laps, the disc moves with the water level instead of letting it slosh over the rim. In my testing with a 14-pound terrier mix that normally creates a puddle within two seconds of drinking, the difference was dramatic — maybe a few drops on the floor instead of a soaked kitchen corner.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains three pieces: a brushed stainless steel bowl, the floating disc, and a silicone seal ring. The bowl measures about 6 inches in diameter and stands 2.5 inches tall. You'll also find printed care instructions that actually matter — the disc needs to be snapped onto the rim correctly or it won't float properly.

The stainless steel is 18/8 grade, which is the same quality you'd expect from a premium human cookware brand. It's magnetic (so it won't slide on a steel crate tray), and the brushed finish hides water spots and paw prints far better than shiny bowls do.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this bowl either earns its keep or disappoints, depending on your dog's drinking style. The no-spill mechanism works best with dogs that lap normally. For dogs that plunge their whole muzzle in and blow bubbles — think bulldogs, pugs, or overly enthusiastic retrievers — the disc reduces splashing by maybe 60-70% rather than eliminating it. That's still a win, just not a miracle.

The bowl's weight is a pleasant surprise. At 1.2 pounds empty, it has enough heft that my terrier can't slide it across the floor. But here's the catch: there's no rubber base pad, so on smooth tile or hardwood, the bowl itself stays put but the water still gets flung out during aggressive drinking sessions.

## Where It Falls Short

The small size is the biggest limitation. At 10 ounces, this is strictly for dogs under 15 pounds. A 30-pound dog will drain it in two gulps, and then the disc sits uselessly at the bottom. There's a larger version, but if you're reading this for a medium or large breed, the small won't cut it.

The floating disc also requires a specific water level to work optimally. If you only fill it halfway, the disc sits too low to catch the splash. Fill it to the indicated line, and you're golden — but that's an extra step some owners won't bother with.

## Who Should Buy This

This bowl is perfect for three types of owners: those with small-breed messy drinkers, anyone with a senior dog prone to slipping on wet floors, and people who crate their dogs and hate cleaning wet crate trays. If you have a medium or large dog, skip the small version and look for the bigger Gardner model — or accept that you'll be refilling this constantly.

## FAQ

**How often do I need to refill the small bowl?**
For a 5-10 pound dog, twice a day is typical. For a 15-pound dog, plan on three refills daily. This is a hydration bowl, not a reservoir.

**Can the floating disc be removed for cleaning?**
Yes, and it should be. The disc pops off the rim with a gentle twist. Both pieces are dishwasher-safe on the top rack.

**Will the bowl rust if left outdoors?**
No. The 18/8 stainless steel is rust-proof, but the silicone seal can degrade in direct sunlight over a year or two. Keep it in the shade if using outdoors.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have a small dog (under 15 lbs) that makes a mess drinking, or you're tired of wiping down floors every time your pet takes a sip. The floating disc design is genuinely effective, not a gimmick.

**Skip it if:** You have a medium or large breed, a dog that needs to drink large volumes quickly, or you're expecting 100% spill elimination. This reduces mess by 80-90%, not completely.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A smart, well-built solution for small-dog owners with messy drinkers. The size limitation and the water-level sensitivity keep it from a perfect score, but for its intended purpose, it works exactly as advertised.
