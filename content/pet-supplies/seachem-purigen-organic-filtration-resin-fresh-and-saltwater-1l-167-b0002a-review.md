If you're chasing that "liquid glass" look for your aquarium — where the water is so clear it looks like the fish are floating in air — Seachem Purigen is the closest thing to a cheat code in the hobby. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, this synthetic resin has built a cult following among reef keepers and planted tank enthusiasts. But at roughly double the price of activated carbon, is it actually worth it? Here's the honest breakdown.

**What you're actually getting**

The 1L package contains roughly a half-pound of spherical, beige resin beads pre-packed in a sealed mesh bag. You drop the entire bag into your filter — no measuring, no rinsing required. The resin works by adsorbing organic compounds that cause yellowing, odor, and protein buildup. Unlike carbon, which stops working after a few days, Purigen keeps pulling organics for weeks. As shown in the product image, the beads darken from cream to deep brown as they load up, giving you a visual indicator of when it's time to regenerate.

**Real-world performance**

In practice, Purigen excels at two things: clarity and stability. Aquarists report visible water clarity improvements within 24 hours, with yellow tannin staining gone in 2–3 days. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I run it in a 75-gallon with heavy bioload and the water looks like it's not even there. Carbon never did this for me." Another noted, "My skimmer pulls less gunk now because Purigen is doing the heavy lifting first."

The standout feature is regeneration. When the beads darken, you soak them in a 1:1 bleach-to-water solution for 24 hours, then treat with Seachem Prime, and they're ready for another cycle. Seachem claims up to 10 reuses per batch. At that rate, the cost-per-gallon of filtration drops below carbon.

**Where it falls short**

Purigen is not a replacement for biological filtration. It does nothing for ammonia, nitrite, or nitrate — those are your bacteria's job. It also ignores phosphate, so algae-prone tanks will still need GFO or other phosphate removers. The resin clogs if you skip weekly rinsing; the bag traps fine particulate matter, and a dirty bag means bypass flow and wasted media. Finally, the regeneration process is fiddly — bleach residue that isn't fully neutralized will wreck your tank, so you must follow the instructions precisely.

**Who should buy this**

Get Purigen if you keep discus, angelfish, or any species that demand pristine water. It's also ideal for reef tanks where carbon's trace-element stripping is a concern — Purigen doesn't touch calcium, magnesium, or alkalinity. Skip it if you're on a tight budget, have a low-bioload tank that stays clear anyway, or you're not willing to maintain a rinse-and-regenerate schedule.

**FAQ**

*How long does a 1L bag last before needing regeneration?*
Under normal bioload, expect 4–6 weeks. Heavily stocked tanks may need regeneration every 2–3 weeks. The color change from cream to dark brown is your cue.

*Can I use Purigen with activated carbon together?*
Yes, but it's generally redundant. Carbon handles chemical pollutants and odors; Purigen handles organics. For most tanks, Purigen alone is sufficient.

*Is it safe for shrimp and snails?*
Yes. Purigen doesn't leach copper or other inverts-toxic compounds. Reef-safe and plant-safe.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want crystal-clear water with less maintenance than carbon, and you're willing to learn the regeneration routine. **Skip it if** you want a zero-effort filtration add-on or need nutrient control beyond organics.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The price stings upfront, but the clarity results and 10x regeneration make it a long-term value winner. Just don't expect it to fix what your filter bacteria are supposed to handle.
