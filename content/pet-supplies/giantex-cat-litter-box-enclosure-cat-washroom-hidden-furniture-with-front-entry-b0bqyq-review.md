Let’s be honest about what a litter box enclosure really is: it’s a compromise. You want your cat to have a private, comfortable bathroom, and you also want your living room to not look like a cat bathroom. The Giantex Cat Litter Box Enclosure tries to be both, and for the most part, it succeeds — with a few caveats you should know before you buy.

**What you actually get**

Out of the box, you get a flat-pack cabinet measuring roughly 23.6" L x 15.7" W x 23.6" H. The exterior is a wood-grain laminate that looks remarkably like real furniture from across the room. As shown in the product image, it’s designed to pass as a side table or nightstand — and honestly, it does. The front entry is a flip-down door that gives your cat access without you having to move the whole unit.

Inside, there’s a removable tray that fits standard 19–21 inch litter boxes. You also get a small carbon filter that slides into a slot near the top, plus a door stopper to keep the front flap from slamming shut. Assembly is straightforward: pre-drilled holes, labeled parts, and maybe 15–20 minutes of your time with a Phillips screwdriver. No power tools needed.

**Real-world performance**

The build quality is better than I expected at this price point. The MDF panels are dense, not flimsy, and the water-resistant coating actually matters — litter dust and the occasional miss wipe off easily with a damp cloth. The hinges on the front door feel solid, not like they’ll snap after a month of daily use.

But here’s where the honest talk comes in. The interior space is the limiting factor. If you have a Maine Coon, a chunky orange tabby, or any cat over 12 pounds, this enclosure will feel cramped. Your cat can get in and out fine, but turning around inside a hooded litter box becomes a tight squeeze. Several Amazon reviewers mention this exact issue. One wrote, *“My 14 lb cat uses it, but I can tell she’s not thrilled about the lack of room. She does her business and gets out fast.”* Another said, *“Perfect for my 9 lb kitten, but I’d size up if you have a bigger cat.”*

Odor control is decent but not miraculous. The carbon filter helps, but it’s thin — expect to replace it every 3–4 weeks to keep smells from lingering near the cabinet. The front door does a good job of containing litter scatter, but you’ll still find a few pellets near the entrance if your cat is an enthusiastic digger.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 225 ratings (4.5 stars), the recurring theme is that this enclosure genuinely looks like furniture. One reviewer put it well: *“I’ve had guests ask where the litter box is. They didn’t believe it was inside the nightstand.”* Another praised the ease of cleaning: *“The top lifts off completely, which makes scooping and deep cleaning way easier than I expected.”* The flat top is also a practical bonus — it holds a plant, a lamp, or a stack of books without issue.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the size constraints, the front door design has a minor flaw. The door swings down, which means you need about 18 inches of clearance in front of the unit. If you’re tucking this into a tight corner, that can be a problem. Also, the included litter mat is essentially a thin plastic sheet — you’ll want to replace it with something more absorbent.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers** who need to hide litter boxes in shared spaces
- **Owners of small-to-medium cats** (under 12 lbs)
- **Anyone who wants a functional side table** that also solves the litter box problem
- **Not for multi-cat households** — one enclosure, one box, one cat max

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit a standard hooded litter box?**
A: It fits boxes up to about 20" L x 15" W. A standard uncovered box works great. A hooded box will fit, but your cat needs to be on the smaller side to maneuver comfortably.

**Q: How do you clean it?**
A: The top lifts off entirely, giving you full access. The interior tray slides out for quick cleaning, and the surfaces wipe down easily with a pet-safe cleaner.

**Q: Does it actually control odor?**
A: The carbon filter helps with mild odors, but it’s not a substitute for regular scooping. Replace the filter monthly and scoop daily for best results.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have a small-to-medium cat and want to hide the litter box in plain sight without sacrificing aesthetics. It’s well-built, looks like actual furniture, and makes cleaning manageable.

**Skip it if** you have a large breed cat, multiple cats, or you’re expecting the carbon filter to handle odor on its own. This is a good product with honest limitations.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A solid, attractive solution for most cat owners, held back only by its modest interior dimensions.
