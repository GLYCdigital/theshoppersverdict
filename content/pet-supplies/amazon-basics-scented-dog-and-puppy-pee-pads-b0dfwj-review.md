## What You Actually Get

Each pad measures 22 x 22 inches — a solid sweet spot for small-to-medium breeds, puppies, and senior dogs. The box ships with a generous count (typically 50–100 depending on the size you select), and the pads arrive tightly compressed. The top layer is a soft, quilted non-woven fabric that feels surprisingly comfortable under paw. Beneath that sits a 5-layer construction: the absorbent core is fluff pulp blended with super-absorbent polymer — the same stuff in disposable diapers — which is what does the heavy lifting.

There’s also a built-in attractant in the center. That’s not marketing fluff; it genuinely helps redirect puppies who think the living room rug is a toilet.

## Real-World Performance

Here’s where the scented claim gets interesting. The fragrance is a light, fresh scent — think baby powder crossed with laundry detergent. It’s not overpowering in a well-ventilated room, but it is noticeable if you get within a foot or two. The real win: it does neutralize ammonia odors between changes. One buyer noted, *“I was skeptical about the scent, but it genuinely keeps the room from smelling like a kennel between cleanups.”* That tracks with how the formula works — it’s not just perfume, it’s an odor-neutralizing agent.

Absorbency is the standout feature. The polymer core locks liquid away fast. Multiple reviewers mention using these for senior dogs with incontinence, and the pads held up through the night *if* the dog wasn’t a heavy drinker. The leak-proof plastic backing does its job — no floor stains reported in the majority of reviews.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides. The pads are thinner than premium options like Simple Solution or Wee-Wee. For a large breed or a dog that floods the pad in one go, you’ll want to change it more frequently. One frustrated reviewer wrote, *“My 70-pound lab mix soaked through these in about 4 hours. These are fine for puppies, not for big dogs.”* That’s a fair criticism — these are optimized for small-to-medium dogs and puppies, not 70-pounders.

The scent, while effective at odor control, isn’t for everyone. A small minority of buyers reported their dogs refused to step on the pads initially, likely due to the fragrance. It usually resolves within a day or two, but if your dog is picky, the unscented version is the safer bet.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these be used in a crate or pen?**
Yes, but place them under a waterproof liner if you’re dealing with a chewer. The plastic backing is durable but not indestructible — a determined puppy will shred it.

**Q: How often should I change them?**
For puppies, every 2–3 accidents. For adults, every 4–6 hours or when the surface feels damp to the touch. Don’t wait for the pad to look saturated — the polymer core can hold a lot, but the surface will feel wet before it leaks.

**Q: Do these actually attract dogs to pee on them?**
The built-in attractant helps, but it’s not magic. It works best when paired with training. Put the pad where your dog already eliminates, praise them when they use it, and the scent will reinforce the behavior.
