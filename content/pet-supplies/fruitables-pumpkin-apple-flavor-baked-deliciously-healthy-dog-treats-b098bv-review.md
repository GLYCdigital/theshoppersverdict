**If you've read the ingredient list on most commercial dog treats, you know the drill: corn, wheat, by-products, artificial colors. Fruitables Pumpkin & Apple Baked treats flip that script with a first ingredient that's actually a vegetable.** These crunchy little bites have racked up a 4.6-star average across 229 Amazon ratings, and after digging into what buyers actually say, the praise is mostly deserved.

## What's Actually in the Bag

You're getting a 5-ounce pouch of small, square-shaped baked treats. Each one clocks in at just 3 calories — that's a genuinely useful number for training, especially if you've got a pup who needs 20 reps per session. The ingredient list leads with pumpkin, followed by apples, oats, and flaxseed. No wheat, no corn, no soy, no artificial preservatives. The texture is hard and crunchy, more like a biscuit than a soft chew.

## Real-World Performance

The crunch factor is where this treat earns its keep. Multiple reviewers note their dogs with dental sensitivities still manage these fine, and the hardness helps with plaque control. One buyer put it plainly: *"My picky shih tzu devours these. She's never been a treat dog, but she does tricks for these now."* Another long-term customer mentioned using them for a senior dog with a sensitive stomach — pumpkin is famously gentle on digestion, and the simple formula seems to agree with most pups.

The flavor is where opinions split. The pumpkin-apple combo smells faintly like baked goods, but it's not overpowering. Some dogs are absolutely obsessed; others, not so much. One reviewer noted their dog *"turned her nose up at first, then came around after a few days."* If your dog is a hardcore meat-flavor loyalist, this might be a tougher sell. But for dogs who like variety or have food sensitivities, this is a solid option.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The bag is small — 5 ounces disappears fast with a 60-pound dog. Several reviewers flagged this as their main complaint, with one writing, *"Great treats but I wish the bag was bigger for the price."* Also, the standard pouch doesn't have a resealable zipper. If you don't transfer the treats to an airtight container, they'll go soft within a week. That's a frustrating oversight for a product positioned as premium.

Finally, these are crunchy treats, not training treats in the soft-and-tiny sense. If you need something that breaks into tiny pieces easily, you'll be snapping these by hand. They do break cleanly, but it's an extra step during training sessions.

## Who Should Buy These

**Get these if:** You have a dog with food sensitivities, you're training with a calorie-conscious treat, or you want something without the usual filler ingredients. They're especially great for small-to-medium breeds that don't need a massive reward to stay motivated.

**Skip these if:** Your dog only responds to meaty, stinky treats, or you have a large breed that will blow through a 5-ounce bag in two days. You'll be reordering constantly, and the per-treat cost adds up.

## FAQs

**Q: Are these good for dogs with allergies?**
A: The formula avoids the big three triggers — corn, wheat, and soy — and uses pumpkin and oats instead. Many reviewers with sensitive-stomach dogs report no issues. But always check with your vet if your dog has known allergies.

**Q: How many can I give my dog per day?**
A: At 3 calories each, the math is forgiving. A 20-pound dog can handle 10-15 treats daily as part of a balanced diet. Start with a few to see how your dog's stomach reacts, then adjust.

**Q: Are these hard or soft treats?**
A: They're distinctly crunchy. This makes them good for dental health but not ideal if you need to hide medication or want a soft reward for senior dogs with dental issues.
