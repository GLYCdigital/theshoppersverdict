Let me be upfront: if you're looking for a healthy daily snack for your rabbit, Vitakraft Drops aren't it. But if you need a high-value training reward or a way to win over a picky bunny, these little yogurt drops earn their 4.6-star rating.

**What's in the tub**

You get a plastic tub with a flip-top lid containing small, round yogurt-based drops. They measure roughly the size of a pea — smaller than the marketing photos imply. The texture is soft enough to pinch in half with your fingers, which matters more than you'd think. Many rabbit treats on the market are rock-hard pellets that require a hammer to break into training portions. These split cleanly.

The tub reseals properly, which is worth calling out because moisture is the enemy of yogurt treats. Leave most treat containers half-open and you'll find a brick of fused-together candy within a week. The Vitakraft seal holds up in practice — several buyers noted their drops stayed soft and separate for months.

**How it performs in real life**

The primary use case here is training and bonding. Rabbits are food-motivated but cautious; a novel, sweet-smelling treat gets their attention fast. The drops have a strong vanilla-yogurt scent that carries, so your rabbit will know you have them before you're even close.

For training, the size is actually a benefit. One drop is about three to four small rewards, which means a single tub lasts a long time if you're using it for clicker training or litter box reinforcement. Buyers consistently mention using these for nail trims and vet visits — the high-value status makes rabbits tolerate handling they'd otherwise resist.

One owner put it plainly: *"My bunny is obsessed. She does tricks she's never done before just to get these. I break one into three pieces and she's satisfied."*

Another noted the practical side: *"Finally a treat my picky lionhead will eat. The tub keeps them fresh and they're soft enough to break up for training."*

**Where it falls short**

The sugar content is the honest concern here. Yogurt drops are essentially candy for rabbits. The first ingredient is sugar, and the nutrition profile reflects that. For a healthy adult rabbit on unlimited hay, a drop or two daily is fine. But rabbits with GI sensitivity, obesity, or dental issues shouldn't get these at all.

There's also the portion surprise. The tub looks fuller in the product photos than it actually is — the drops settle and there's empty space at the top. It's not deceptive, but several reviewers commented they expected more volume for the price. Given that you'll likely break each drop into pieces, the value works out fine, but manage your expectations on quantity.

Finally, not every rabbit likes these. Most do — the 4.6 rating confirms that — but a minority of owners report their bunnies sniff and walk away. If your rabbit is extremely picky about novel foods, buy the smallest size first rather than committing to a multi-pack.

**Who should buy this**

- **New rabbit owners** who need a bonding tool — these make hand-feeding easy and positive.
- **Owners with stubborn rabbits** who refuse standard treats during nail trims or grooming.
- **Training-focused owners** doing clicker work who need a high-value, easily portioned reward.

**Skip it if** your rabbit has health issues that require a low-sugar diet, or if you're looking for a daily supplement disguised as a treat. This is a reward, not nutrition.

**FAQ**

*Can baby rabbits eat these?*
Wait until your rabbit is at least 12 weeks old and fully weaned. Their digestive systems are delicate, and the sugar load is unnecessary. Stick to hay and fresh greens for young rabbits.

*How many drops per day is safe?*
One or two drops maximum for an adult rabbit, and that's generous. If you're training, break one drop into multiple pieces and spread them out. These are concentrated sugar — think of them like chocolate chips for humans.

*Do these work for other small animals?*
The label says rabbits, but guinea pig and chinchilla owners report success using them as occasional treats. If you have multiple species, check with your vet first — sugar tolerance varies significantly between small animals.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a reliable high-value treat for training, bonding, or grooming. The soft texture, resealable packaging, and portion control make it one of the better yogurt-drop options on the market.

**Skip it if** your rabbit has dietary restrictions, or you want a treat that contributes nutritional value. This is pure reward — and there's nothing wrong with that, as long as you treat it like candy, not food.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Effective at what it does, honest about what it isn't.
