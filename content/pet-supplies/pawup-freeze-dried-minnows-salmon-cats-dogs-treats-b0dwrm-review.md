**The short version:** If your cat turns their nose up at every treat you've tried, or your dog needs a high-value reward that isn't a greasy meat stick, PAWUP's freeze-dried minnows and salmon are worth a shot. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, it's not a perfect product — but it's a genuinely useful one for pets who need protein-dense, single-ingredient motivation.

**What you actually get**  
A pouch of freeze-dried whole minnows and separate salmon pieces. The minnows are roughly the size of your pinky nail — small, light, and brittle. The salmon comes in bite-sized flakes and chunks. Both are 100% freeze-dried with no added salt, oils, or binders. As shown in the product image, the pouch is straightforward: a clear bag with minimal branding, which honestly tracks with the "no-frills ingredient" positioning. Some batches include a zipper seal; others don't — that's a real inconsistency multiple buyers flagged.

**Performance and real-world use**  
The texture is the standout feature. Freeze-drying preserves the crunch, so minnows snap cleanly rather than turning into chewy mush. That crunch provides mild dental abrasion — think of it like a tiny, fishy biscuit. For cats, the minnows are about the right size for a single crunch. For small dogs, they work as a high-value training reward that doesn't crumble into a mess on your carpet (though the powder at the bottom of the bag is a real thing — it happens).

The salmon pieces are softer and more flaky, which makes them easier to break into smaller bits for training sessions. One buyer noted: *"My cat is a senior with missing teeth, and she can eat the salmon without any trouble. The minnows she has to crunch, so I only give her those as a treat, not a meal topper."* That's a fair split — max crunch for dental benefits, softer salmon for older pets.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The picky-eater factor is the most repeated win. Over and over, reviewers mention pets who ignore other treats but go after these. One customer wrote: *"I've tried every expensive treat at PetSmart. My cat ignores all of them. She heard this bag crinkle from the other room and came running."* The smell is strong — fishy, but not rotting-fish offensive — which seems to be exactly what triggers the food drive in stubborn pets.

The single-ingredient aspect also matters for owners with allergy-sensitive pets. No grains, no chicken byproduct, no artificial colors. If your pet has a poultry sensitivity, these are a safe swap.

**Where it falls short**  
The fragility is the biggest con. Minnows arrive whole, but if the bag gets jostled in shipping (or your dog gets excited and knocks it off the counter), you'll have a pouch of fish dust. Multiple ratings mention this. It's not a dealbreaker — the dust still works as a topper on wet food — but if you wanted whole, intact minnows for training, expect some breakage.

The no-resealable-zipper inconsistency is annoying. When you get a batch without a zipper, the freeze-dried fish absorbs moisture from the air and can soften within a week. You'll need a clip or a separate airtight container. Not a flaw in the treat itself, but a packaging oversight that matters for a moisture-sensitive product.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Cat owners with picky eaters** — this is the primary use case. If your cat turns down everything else, try this.  
- **Small-dog owners doing training** — the minnows are the right size for quick rewards, and the salmon breaks into tiny bits for multiple reps.  
- **Pet owners managing allergy diets** — single-ingredient treats are a clean way to reward without triggering sensitivities.  

**Who should skip it**  
- **Large dogs** — minnows are too small to be rewarding for a 70-pound dog; they'll swallow them without noticing.  
- **Owners who need intact, uniform treats** — if breakage bothers you, the lack of consistency will frustrate you.  
- **Pets with kidney issues** — fish-based treats are naturally higher in phosphorus; check with your vet if this applies.

**FAQ**  

**Can I use these as a meal topper?**  
Yes, crumble them over wet food. The powder from broken minnows actually works well for this.

**How many calories per treat?**  
The manufacturer doesn't list per-piece calorie counts, but freeze-dried fish is low-calorie. A single minnow is roughly 1-2 calories; a salmon piece is around 3-5. Safe for daily training use in moderation.

**Are these safe for puppies and kittens?**  
Yes, if they're weaned and eating solid food. The minnows are brittle enough that even a small puppy can crunch them. Just supervise the first few times to ensure they chew rather than gulp.

**The verdict**  
**Buy it if:** your pet is a picky eater, you want a single-ingredient treat with no fillers, or you need a crunchy reward that won't leave greasy residue on your hands.  
**Skip it if:** you have a large dog, need perfectly intact treats, or have a pet with kidney concerns.
