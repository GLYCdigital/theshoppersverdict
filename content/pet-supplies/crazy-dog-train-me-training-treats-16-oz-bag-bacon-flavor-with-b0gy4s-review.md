Let me be direct: most "training treats" are just tiny versions of regular treats, overpriced and under-effective. The Crazy Dog Train-Me! Bacon Flavor treats are different — and after looking at the specs, the 4.7-star rating from 235 buyers makes sense.

## What You Actually Get

The 16-ounce bag delivers roughly 800–900 pieces depending on how generously the factory fills it. Each treat is about the size of a pea — maybe 3/8 inch across. That's the sweet spot for training: small enough that your dog swallows and refocuses quickly, but not so tiny you can't grab them mid-session.

The texture is soft and almost doughy. You can pinch one in half without crumbling. That matters more than you'd think — dogs with missing teeth or puppies with baby teeth don't struggle with these, and you won't be scraping rock-hard crumbs off your living room carpet.

## How They Perform in Real Training

Here's the critical metric: does your dog actually care about these? Based on the ingredient list and buyer feedback, yes. The bacon flavor comes through strongly — one reviewer noted their "super picky golden retriever" who "turns her nose up at most treats" would "do backflips for these." Another mentioned using them for leash reactivity work, where the high-value reward is essential.

At roughly 2 calories per piece, you can reward every correct behavior without guilt. For a 50-pound dog, you'd need to give over 100 pieces to make a meaningful dent in daily calories. That's a genuine advantage over using cut-up hot dogs or cheese, which add up fast.

The soft texture also means fast eating — no crunching, no lingering. Your dog's eyes go back to you within seconds, which keeps training momentum going.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Looking across the reviews, three themes dominate:

**Scent payoff:** The bacon smell is noticeable when you open the bag but not overwhelming for humans. Dogs, however, seem to detect it instantly. Multiple reviewers described their dogs "going crazy" at the sound of the bag opening.

**Portion control:** The small size gets repeated praise from owners doing obedience classes or agility training where frequent rewards are necessary.

**Value:** At this price point for 16 ounces, you're getting far more pieces per dollar than most packaged training treats from bigger brands.

## Honest Shortcomings

The ingredient list won't impress clean-label shoppers. Chicken by-product meal and artificial smoke flavor are in there. If you're committed to limited-ingredient or all-natural diets only, this isn't your product.

The resealable zipper on the bag is functional but weak. Several reviewers noted the treats got stale after a few weeks. Transfer them to a Mason jar or airtight container when you open the bag — it's annoying but solves the problem.

Also worth noting: if your dog has a chicken allergy, skip this entirely. Despite being "bacon flavor," chicken by-product is the primary protein source.

## Who Should Buy This

**Get these if:** You're actively training — obedience, tricks, agility, or behavioral work — and need a high-value, low-calorie reward your dog will actually work for. They're also solid for puppies going through socialization and basic commands.

**Skip these if:** You need a durable chew or long-lasting treat, you're avoiding chicken-based ingredients entirely, or you want something for casual "just because" giving rather than training sessions.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these for everyday treats, not just training?**
A: Sure, but the pieces are small — most dogs will want 5–10 to feel like they got a treat. They're designed for frequent, small rewards, so they work better as training currency than as a "good boy" snack.

**Q: Are they okay for puppies under 6 months?**
A: Yes. The soft texture is actually ideal for teething puppies. Just adjust the quantity — the 2-calorie count makes portion control easy.

**Q: How long does an open bag last?**
A: If you reseal properly, 4–6 weeks. If you use an airtight container, longer. The treats will lose moisture and harden over time, which affects palatability more than safety.
