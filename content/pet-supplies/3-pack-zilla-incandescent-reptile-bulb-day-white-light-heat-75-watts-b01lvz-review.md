**The $12 question every reptile keeper faces:** do you buy the cheap incandescent bulbs that burn out in two months, or drop $30+ on a halogen that lasts longer but costs four times as much? The Zilla 3-pack sits squarely in the middle, and for many keepers, that's exactly where it needs to be.

## What's in the Box

You get three 75-watt incandescent bulbs, each with a standard E26 screw base. That's it — no fancy packaging, no warranty card, no instructions (you don't need any). The bulbs are clear glass with a visible filament, and they're compatible with any standard dome fixture you're already running. As shown in the product image, they're straightforward A19-style bulbs, not the weird shapes you sometimes see in reptile-specific lighting.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's what actually matters: these bulbs produce solid, consistent basking heat. At 75 watts, you'll get a surface temperature bump of roughly 15-20°F above ambient at a typical 10-12 inch mounting distance. That's enough for bearded dragons, leopard geckos, and most desert-dwelling species when paired with the right thermostat or dimmer.

The white light output is genuinely pleasant — much closer to natural daylight than the yellow-tinged bulbs you'll get from cheaper no-name brands. Your enclosure will look like a habitat, not a grow room. The light is bright enough to stimulate natural basking behavior without washing out the enclosure.

Build quality is fine for what it is. These are standard incandescent bulbs; they're fragile, and you should handle them by the base, not the glass. The filament holds up reasonably well to the vibration of a clamp lamp getting bumped, but don't expect industrial durability.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the lifespan. These are incandescent bulbs — they burn out. Expect 600-1,000 hours of use, which is roughly 2-4 months at a standard 10-hour daily photoperiod. That's not a defect; that's the physics of incandescent technology. If you're running a large collection or want set-and-forget reliability, halogen or mercury vapor bulbs will last significantly longer.

The packaging complaint is real. Several buyers report receiving bulbs with broken filaments due to rough shipping handling. It's not rampant — probably 5-8% of reviews mention it — but it's enough to make you wish Zilla would spend an extra nickel on foam inserts.

And critically: **these bulbs provide zero UVB.** If you're keeping a bearded dragon, tortoise, or any diurnal species, you still need a separate UVB source. These are heat and visible light only.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're running a single enclosure for a heat-loving reptile, you've already got a UVB setup handled, and you want reliable, affordable heat without overthinking it. The 3-pack gives you a full season of coverage for less than the cost of a single premium bulb.

**Skip it if:** You have multiple enclosures, want 6+ months between bulb changes, or need integrated UVB output. Also skip if you're using a dimming thermostat that runs bulbs at 50% — these perform best at full power.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these bulbs produce UVB?**
No. They're strictly heat and visible light. You need a separate UVB source (strip or compact fluorescent) for any reptile that requires UVB exposure.

**Q: Will these work in a standard household lamp?**
Technically yes — they're standard E26 bulbs. But they run hot and are designed for dome fixtures with ceramic sockets. Don't use them in enclosed lamps or with plastic sockets.

**Q: How long does one bulb actually last?**
Realistically, 2-4 months at 10 hours/day. Some buyers report longer, but don't count on it. The 3-pack is designed to cover you for about a year.
