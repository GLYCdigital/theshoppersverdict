**The Short Version:** If you've got a small-breed dog that demolishes soft treats in 30 seconds flat, these Pawant chicken-wrapped rawhide bones hit a sweet spot — they're substantial enough to occupy a determined chewer without being so hard that tiny teeth struggle. At 4.5 stars from 225 ratings, the consensus is clear: these are a solid value for small dogs who need a longer-lasting chew.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The bag contains rawhide bones wrapped in a layer of chicken that's baked onto the surface. As shown in the product image, each bone is knotted at both ends — that's not decorative. Those knots give your dog something to grip and work on, which extends chew time considerably versus a flat strip.

The sizing matters here. These are specifically cut for small breeds — think Chihuahuas, Yorkies, Maltese, and small terriers. They're roughly finger-length, which means they're not appropriate for medium or large dogs (they'd swallow them whole, and that's a vet visit waiting to happen). For the intended audience, though, the size is well-calibrated.

**Real-World Performance**

The chicken wrapping isn't just a gimmick. It serves two purposes: it adds flavor that most dogs find irresistible, and it gives the bone a softer outer layer that's gentler on puppy teeth and senior gums. Several reviewers noted their dogs who typically ignore plain rawhide will work on these until the chicken layer is gone and then keep going.

One reviewer put it plainly: *"My picky shih tzu ignores most treats. She actually chewed this for 20 minutes straight. That never happens."* Another mentioned that the bones kept their 8-month-old puppy occupied during crate training without the puppy losing interest after the first few minutes.

The chew time lands somewhere between a soft training treat (45 seconds) and a hard nylon bone (weeks). Most small dogs get 15-30 minutes of active chewing from one bone, which is a decent mental workout for a breed that needs stimulation.

**Where These Fall Short — Be Honest With Yourself**

Rawhide is rawhide. Despite the chicken wrap, the core is still hide that's not fully digestible. If you have an aggressive chewer who bites off chunks and swallows them whole, these are not the right choice — you'll want a digestible alternative like bully sticks or sweet potato chews. The product description says for dogs 5-20 lbs, and that's worth respecting.

There's also the consistency issue. A few reviewers mentioned receiving bones where the chicken coverage was thin or patchy, leaving bare rawhide exposed at the knots. It doesn't affect safety, but it does reduce the flavor appeal — and if your dog is picky, they might lose interest faster.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Small-breed owners** whose dogs are moderate chewers (not power chewers) and need something that lasts longer than a biscuit
- **Puppy owners** looking for a teething chew that's softer than hard bones but more substantial than soft chews
- **Owners of picky small dogs** who've tried plain rawhide and gotten the "I'm not touching that" stare

**Skip These If:** You have a large breed, a dog with a history of swallowing treats without chewing, or you're strictly avoiding rawhide products for safety reasons. There are better options for those scenarios.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does one bone last for a small dog?**  
A: Expect 15-30 minutes of active chewing for most small breeds. Puppies with sharper teeth may go through them faster.

**Q: Are these safe for puppies?**  
A: Generally yes, for puppies old enough to have adult teeth coming in (4+ months). The chicken wrap softens the rawhide, but always supervise — no rawhide treat is fully safe for unsupervised chewing.

**Q: Do these smell bad like regular rawhide?**  
A: The chicken wrap masks most of the traditional rawhide odor. There's a mild meaty scent, but it's significantly less offensive than plain rawhide.
