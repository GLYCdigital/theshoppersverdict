If you're tired of cracked, dirty eggs and hens fighting over one prime nesting spot, this six-compartment metal box is worth a serious look. It solves two of the most annoying coop problems at once: egg cleanliness and hen competition. But as with most mid-priced coop accessories, there are assembly quirks and design tradeoffs you need to know before you commit.

## What You Actually Get

The box measures roughly 24 inches wide by 18 inches deep — each of the six compartments is about 9 inches square, which is snug but workable for standard breeds like Rhode Island Reds or Australorps. Heavier breeds like Orpingtons might find it tight, so measure your girls before ordering.

The unit ships flat with six dividers, a back panel, and the roll-out tray system. The galvanized steel panels are pre-drilled and punch-cut. You get the nesting box hardware — bolts, nuts, and washers — but no mounting screws for attaching it to wood, so plan a trip to your hardware bin.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The galvanized coating is the standout feature here. After months of exposure to coop conditions — ammonia from droppings, humidity, and regular pressure-washing — the panels hold up without rust spots. Buyers consistently confirm this. As one verified owner put it: "I've had mine through two rainy Pacific Northwest winters and it looks as good as day one."

The roll-out mechanism is genuinely clever. Compartments are angled slightly backward, and eggs roll gently into the collection tray at the front. This serves double duty: eggs stay cleaner because hens don't sit on them, and you don't have to reach deep into the box to retrieve eggs. Multiple reviewers noted this dramatically reduced egg pecking in their flocks — hens never see the eggs sitting in the nest, so the behavior doesn't develop.

That said, the tray is shallow — about 1.5 inches deep. Hens that aggressively kick bedding around can occasionally push an egg over the lip. A thin layer of straw in the roll-out tray cushions the drop and solves this.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest complaint, and it's legitimate. The pre-drilled holes occasionally misalign by a millimeter or two, requiring you to wrestle bolts through with a screwdriver as a pry bar. Allow 45 minutes to an hour, and keep a socket wrench handy — a Phillips head will make you miserable.

The 9-inch compartments also limit breed compatibility. Bantams and standard layers are fine, but if you keep Jersey Giants or Brahmas, they'll struggle to turn around in there. That's not a defect, but it's a real constraint for backyard keepers with mixed flocks. One reviewer noted: "My Buff Orpingtons squeeze in fine, but my friend's Brahmas wouldn't fit at all."

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for the backyard chicken keeper with 6 to 12 hens who wants clean eggs without daily deep-cleaning. The roll-out system is a genuine time-saver — you'll spend less time scrubbing manure off eggs and more time actually collecting them.

It's also a strong pick for first-time flock owners who want to establish good habits early. Because the eggs roll away immediately, young hens don't develop egg-eating behaviors.

Skip it if you have extra-large breeds, want a floor-standing unit (this is wall-mount only), or you're not comfortable with basic tool work.

## FAQ

**Does this work for a floor-mounted setup?**
No. It's designed for wall mounting. You could rig legs, but that voids the intended design and makes the roll-out tray ineffective.

**How many hens per compartment?**
One per compartment is ideal, but two small bantams will share comfortably. Standard breeds should get their own space to prevent egg breakage.

**Can I pressure wash it?**
Yes. The galvanized coating handles it fine. Just dry it thoroughly before reinstalling to prevent moisture buildup in the tray seams.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want cleaner eggs, less hen violence at laying time, and a box that will outlast your current flock. **Skip it if** you have extra-large breeds, hate assembly projects, or need a free-standing unit.

At a 4.0 average from 200 ratings, this is a solid, well-reviewed product with a clear design philosophy: make egg collection easier and cleaner. The assembly frustration is real, but the daily payoff — reaching into a clean tray instead of a manure-caked nest — makes that one-time headache worth it.
