**Real-World Performance**

The kibble size is small and flat—ideal for small to medium breeds, though larger dogs will still manage fine. The texture is dry and crunchy, which helps with dental health, but it’s not so hard that senior dogs struggle. The salmon smell is noticeable when you open the bag, but it’s not overpowering; most dogs seem genuinely excited at feeding time, which is a good sign for palatability.

In terms of digestion, this is where the formula shines. The combination of sweet potato and pumpkin provides soluble fiber that helps firm up stools. Multiple reviewers note their dogs transitioned to this food with minimal stomach upset, which is rare for a grain-free formula. One verified buyer wrote: *“My 9-year-old lab had constant loose stools on other brands. Within a week on this, she’s solid and her coat is shinier.”* That tracks with the omega-3s from salmon, which support skin and coat health.

**Where It Falls Short**

No product is perfect, and this one has tradeoffs. First, the protein content is moderate—around 24% crude protein—which is fine for most adult dogs but might be insufficient for highly active working breeds or puppies. Second, the kibble is small; if you have a giant breed, they may gulp it without chewing, which increases the risk of bloat. You’ll need to monitor feeding speed. Third, some reviewers mention the price has crept up over the past year, making it pricier than comparable grain-free options. If you’re on a tight budget, that could be a sticking point.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Owners of dogs with chicken or grain sensitivities** — This formula avoids both, making it a safe first try.
- **Small to medium breed households** — The kibble size and protein level are a good match.
- **Dogs with sensitive stomachs** — The pumpkin and sweet potato combo is gentle and effective.

Skip it if you have a puppy under 1 year old (they need higher protein for growth) or a very active sporting breed that requires a performance formula with 28%+ protein.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this food suitable for dogs with a fish allergy?**
A: No—salmon is the primary protein source. If your dog has any fish sensitivity, avoid this formula and look for a turkey or venison-based option instead.

**Q: Can I mix this with wet food?**
A: Yes, but keep the ratio consistent. Mixing can change calorie intake, so start with 25% wet food and adjust based on your dog’s weight and stool quality.

**Q: Does this cause gas in dogs?**
A: Some dogs experience mild gas during the first week as their gut adjusts to the new fiber. It typically subsides after 7–10 days. If it persists, reduce the portion size slightly.
