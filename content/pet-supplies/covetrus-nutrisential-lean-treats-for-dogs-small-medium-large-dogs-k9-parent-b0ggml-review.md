The texture is where these treats shine. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The pieces are genuinely small. If you have a Labrador or German Shepherd, you'll find yourself dispensing three or four per cue just to make it feel rewarding. That's fine for training, but it means a 6-ounce bag won't last long with a big dog.

Second, the flavor is mild. Dogs accustomed to high-fat, heavily flavored treats might sniff these and walk away. One buyer reported, "My beagle turned her nose up at first — I had to mix them with her regular treats to get her interested." Picky eaters exist, and this isn't the treat that will convert them.

Finally, the bag has no resealable zipper. For a product designed for frequent training use, that's a frustrating omission. Plan on a chip clip or transferring to an airtight container.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You're serious about training and your dog's weight. This includes owners of overweight dogs on veterinary weight-loss programs, agility or obedience competitors who run through dozens of treats per session, and anyone with a small-to-medium breed who wants a guilt-free reward.
