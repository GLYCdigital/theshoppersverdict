## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The small breed size is still too large for toy breeds under 5 pounds — a Yorkie or Chihuahua will struggle to get a proper grip. You'll need to supervise closely or look for a smaller alternative.

The calorie math matters too. Each bone runs roughly 55-60 calories, which for a 10-pound dog is about 8-10% of their daily caloric needs. If you're handing out one of these daily on top of regular meals, you're setting up your pup for weight gain. Factor it into their food portion or limit frequency.

Some picky eaters lose interest once the flavorful outer layer is gone. The inner core is less palatable, and I've seen reviews from owners whose dogs abandoned half-chewed bones after the first session. If your dog is finicky, start with a single bone before committing to a bulk pack.

## FAQ

**How long does one bone actually last?**
For a typical small breed, expect 20-40 minutes of active chewing across one or two sessions. Aggressive chewers might finish one in 15 minutes; casual chewers might stretch it over several days.

**Are these safe for puppies?**
Purina recommends these for dogs 6 months and older. Puppies younger than that have developing teeth and jaws that may not handle the density well. Always supervise any chew session.

**Can I give these to a dog with a sensitive stomach?**
The wheat flour base could be problematic for dogs with grain sensitivities. If your dog has known food allergies, check with your vet first. Otherwise, the simple ingredient list causes fewer issues than most rawhide products.
