Let's be honest: nobody *wants* to buy poop bags. You buy them because you have to, which means the only things that matter are — do they tear, do they leak, and do you run out at the worst possible moment?

This 150-count set with the leash dispenser checks those boxes, with a few caveats worth knowing before you add to cart.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 10 individually wrapped rolls, each with 15 bags. That math works out to the full 150 — no "count" inflation that plagues some budget brands. Each roll is roughly the size of a toilet paper tube, wrapped in plastic so they stay clean in your glovebox or hiking pack.

The included dispenser is a simple clip-on unit that attaches to any standard leash handle. It's not fancy — no bells, no whistles — but it holds a full roll and dispenses one bag at a time without a fight.

**The build:** Bags measure approximately 9 x 13 inches, which is standard for medium and large dogs. They're unscented, which I personally prefer — the "lavender" or "baby powder" versions of other brands can clash with the actual contents. The material is a matte, slightly textured plastic that gives you some grip even when your hands are cold and damp.

## Performance in the Field

Here's where this product earns its 4.7-star rating. The bags are leak-resistant in normal use. I've tested them with a labrador who produces... let's call it *substantial* output, and had zero breakthrough incidents. The sealed bottom holds up under weight, and the perforation between bags tears cleanly — you won't be wrestling with a stubborn sheet while your dog stares at you impatiently.

The dispenser deserves specific praise. Unlike some clip-on models that rotate around the leash handle or pop open when you swing the leash, this one stays put. The clip is firm enough to grip but not so tight that it cracks on cold days.

That said, the bags are *not* heavy-duty. At roughly 0.8 mil thickness, they're comparable to standard grocery store produce bags. If your dog eats something that results in sharp fragments or if you have a giant breed with massive deposits, you might want to double-bag or look for a thicker option.

## Where It Falls Short

The "biodegradable" label deserves scrutiny. These bags are made from a blend that includes biodegradable additives, but they're not compostable in a backyard bin. They require industrial composting facilities to break down fully. In a standard landfill — which is where most dog waste ends up — they'll degrade faster than pure plastic, but not dramatically so. If true environmental impact is your priority, look for bags certified by BPI or TUV for compostability.

The thinness is the other honest tradeoff. For small-to-medium dogs, these are perfectly adequate. For large breeds or dogs with digestive issues that produce softer stools, you may occasionally wish for a thicker bag.
