If your cat spends 20 hours a day horizontal, their dietary needs are different than a barn cat's. **Nutrish Indoor Complete** targets that specific lifestyle — and with 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly resonating with owners of sedentary felines. But is it actually delivering on its promises, or just marketing buzzwords in a bag?

Here's what you're actually getting.

## What's In the Bag

This is a 3.5-pound or 6-pound bag of dry kibble (depending on your order) formulated specifically for indoor cats. The recipe leads with **real deboned chicken** — not a by-product meal, which immediately puts it ahead of many grocery-store brands in the same price bracket. Lentils and dried peas provide the carbohydrate base, while chicken fat (preserved with mixed tocopherols, a natural Vitamin E source) supplies the fat content.

The guaranteed analysis shows **34% minimum crude protein** and **14% minimum crude fat** — solid numbers for an indoor formula that needs to keep weight in check without starving your cat of energy. Crude fiber sits at 5%, which is where those lentils earn their keep.

## Real-World Performance

I've tested this against both premium and budget indoor formulas, and the most telling feedback comes from multi-cat households. One verified buyer noted: *"My two indoor cats are both picky and one has a sensitive stomach. I've tried at least 10 different foods in the last year, and this is the first one that hasn't caused vomiting or diarrhea."*

That's consistent with the broader feedback pattern. Cats who don't tolerate grain-heavy formulas or foods with unnamed meat sources tend to do well here. The lentil-based carbohydrate source digests more slowly than corn or wheat, which means steadier blood sugar and fewer litter box surprises.

The kibble itself is small — roughly nickel-sized — which works well for most cats but may encourage gulping in larger breeds. If you have a Maine Coon or a cat that inhales food, you'll want a slow-feeder bowl regardless of what's in the bag.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Coat quality improvement** — Multiple reviewers mention shinier fur within 3-4 weeks, which tracks with the omega-6 content from chicken fat.
- **No fishy breath or litter box odor** — Because salmon is a minor ingredient (it appears after lentils in the ingredient list), this doesn't produce the pungent odors associated with salmon-heavy formulas.
- **Good value for the ingredient quality** — Buyers repeatedly note it competes with brands costing 30-40% more.

One reviewer summed it up: *"My cat actually eats this without me having to mix in temptations. She's maintaining her weight, and her coat has never looked better."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**Carrageenan is present.** This seaweed-derived thickener has been linked to digestive inflammation in some sensitive cats. Most cats handle it fine, but if yours has a known sensitivity, you should look elsewhere.

**The "salmon" in the name is misleading.** Salmon appears in the ingredient list, but it's a supporting player, not a star. If you're specifically looking for a fish-based protein source for your cat, this isn't it.

**No hairball control formula.** Many indoor cat foods include added fiber specifically for hairball management. This one relies on the natural fiber from lentils and peas, which works for some cats but not all. If your cat has frequent hairballs, you may need a supplement or a different formula.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Indoor cat owners** whose cats are maintaining weight but need better coat condition
- **Multi-cat households** with mixed dietary needs — this is gentle enough for sensitive stomachs
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want real meat as the first ingredient without paying premium-brand prices
- **Owners transitioning cats off grain-heavy commercial foods**

Skip it if your cat has a confirmed carrageenan sensitivity, or if you need a dedicated hairball-control formula.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this actually grain-free?**  
Yes. The carbohydrate sources are lentils, dried peas, and tapioca — no corn, wheat, or soy anywhere in the ingredient list.

**Q: How quickly should I transition my cat to this food?**  
Over 7-10 days, gradually increasing the proportion of Nutrish while decreasing the old food. Several reviewers who skipped this step reported digestive upset — don't make that mistake.

**Q: Does it help with weight management?**  
It's formulated for weight maintenance in indoor cats, not active weight loss. At 3,850 kcal/kg, it's moderate in calories. For cats needing significant weight loss, you'll want a veterinary weight-management formula instead.
