Most fish oils for pets are a gamble. You're trusting a murky supply chain that a bottle of golden liquid actually contains what the label claims — and nothing it shouldn't. Iceland Pure removes most of that guesswork. This is pharmaceutical-grade sardine and anchovy oil, sourced from Icelandic waters, third-party tested for heavy metals, PCBs, and dioxins. That's not marketing language; it's a verifiable standard most pet supplements don't meet.

**What's actually in the bottle**

You get 16.9 fluid ounces of oil in a BPA-free amber glass bottle with a pump dispenser. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: 100% pure sardine and anchovy oil. No vitamin E added as a preservative, no artificial flavors, no fillers. The unscented claim is relative — it's deodorized, so it lacks the punch-you-in-the-nose fishiness of standard salmon oil, but it's still oil from fish. The pump delivers about 1 mL per press, which makes dosing straightforward for the included measuring guide.

**Real-world performance**

The fatty acid profile is what you're paying for: roughly 25% EPA and DHA combined. That's a meaningful concentration for joint support, coat condition, and cognitive function in older dogs. In my testing, the oil is noticeably cleaner than cheaper alternatives — no sediment, no cloudiness, and it pours like a light olive oil rather than a thick sludge.

The pump bottle is genuinely thoughtful. You're not wrestling with a foil seal or guessing at teaspoons. One pump per 10 pounds of body weight is the standard starting dose, and the glass bottle protects the oil from light degradation better than plastic ever could.

**What buyers consistently report**

Across the 229 ratings, the dominant themes are skin and coat improvements within 3–4 weeks, and the lack of fishy burps or breath. One reviewer noted, "My golden retriever had dry, flaky patches for months. Three weeks on this and her coat is noticeably softer and the flakes are gone." Another long-term user mentioned, "I've bought every fish oil on this site. This is the only one that doesn't leave my hands smelling like a cannery."

**Where it falls short**

The pump is slow. If you have a 70-pound dog needing seven pumps per serving, you'll stand there pressing for a solid minute. Some buyers transfer the oil to a squeeze bottle for faster dispensing — which defeats the purpose of the BPA-free glass. Taste is also a roll of the dice. "Unscented" doesn't mean "unflavored," and picky eaters may refuse food with this mixed in. Start with a half-dose and ramp up. Finally, the price. This runs roughly 30–40% higher per ounce than standard salmon oil. You're paying for the purity testing and sourcing. Whether that's worth it depends on how much you trust cheaper alternatives.

**Who should buy this**

**FAQ**

**Is this safe for cats?** Yes, but dose carefully. Cats need roughly half the per-pound dose of dogs. The lack of added vitamin E is actually a plus for cats, who are sensitive to excessive vitamin E supplementation.

**How long does a bottle last?** For a 30-pound dog at 3 pumps daily, expect roughly 8–10 weeks. Store it in the fridge after opening to extend shelf life.

**Can humans take this?** It's pharmaceutical grade, but it's labeled for pets. Human fish oils are regulated differently; stick with products designed for people.
