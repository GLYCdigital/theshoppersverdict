Look, most cat trees under $100 are glorified cardboard with carpet stapled to them. They wobble, they shed, and your cat ignores them within a week. The Hey-brother Cat Tree with Large Hammock is not that product — but it's not perfect either. Here's what 229 buyers (averaging 4.6 stars) actually found when they unboxed this multi-level tower.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains a two-tier climbing tower with a spacious top perch, a generously sized hammock suspended beneath it, and two sisal-wrapped scratching posts. The base measures roughly 16 inches square, and the total height lands around 38 inches — tall enough to satisfy climbing instincts without dominating a small apartment. The materials are a cut above the typical budget fare: dense particleboard wrapped in plush faux fur, with sisal rope that's actually tightly wound rather than loosely spiraled.

Assembly is genuinely tool-free. The parts arrive numbered, the bolts are pre-inserted into the correct holes, and the Allen wrench included actually fits properly. Most buyers report completing setup in 10-15 minutes without needing a second person. One reviewer noted, "I've assembled three cat trees in my life and this was by far the easiest — everything lined up perfectly."

**Real-World Performance**

The standout feature here is the hammock, and it earns the "large" in the product name. It's deep enough for a 12-pound cat to stretch out fully, and the fabric is a breathable mesh that doesn't trap heat. Owners of heavier cats consistently report it holds up without the sagging-to-the-floor problem that plagues cheaper hammocks.

The scratching posts are where this tree separates itself from the $40 Amazon specials. The sisal is dense and rough enough to actually satisfy a cat's need to dig in, and multiple buyers mention their cats prefer these posts over their furniture — which is the whole point. One reviewer wrote, "My cat destroyed two cheap trees before this one. Three months in, the sisal still looks nearly new."

Stability is decent for the price point. The base is solid and the weight distribution is well-balanced, but if you have a cat that launches itself from high surfaces, you'll want to use the included wall anchor strap. That's not a dealbreaker — it's standard for trees in this category — but it's worth knowing before you set it up in a high-traffic area.

**Where It Falls Short**

The base platform is narrower than I'd like for a tree this tall. A 15-pound cat landing hard on the top perch will make the whole unit shift noticeably. The wall anchor solves this, but it's an extra step that cheaper trees don't always require.

The faux fur sheds. It's not catastrophic — a few passes with a lint roller after assembly handles most of it — but if you have a cat with allergies or you're sensitive to loose fibers, budget for that cleanup.

The hammock is attached with clips that are functional but not elegant. They hold fine, but they're visible and can catch on a cat's claws if they're particularly vigorous about getting in and out.

**Who Should Buy This**

This tree is ideal for single-cat households or two-cat homes where both cats are under 15 pounds. It's perfect for apartment dwellers who need vertical space without a massive footprint, and for owners who want a tree that won't disintegrate after six months of use. It's also a strong choice for first-time cat owners who want a reliable, no-fuss product without overpaying.

Skip it if you have a Maine Coon or a particularly beefy cat — the perch and hammock will fit, but the base stability will leave you wanting. Also skip it if you need a tree that looks like furniture; this is clearly a cat product, not a design statement.

**FAQ**

*Is this stable enough for a 15-pound cat?* With the wall anchor installed, yes. Without it, you'll notice wobble when they jump down from the top perch. Use the anchor.

*How hard is assembly really?* Ten to fifteen minutes, no tools. The parts are labeled, and the instructions are actually clear — rare for this category.

*Does the hammock hold up to daily use?* Multiple reviewers report three-plus months of daily use with no sagging or tearing. The mesh is surprisingly durable.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, well-built cat tree under $100 that your cat will actually use, with a hammock that holds up and scratching posts that survive real claw work.

**Skip it if** you have a very heavy cat, need furniture-grade aesthetics, or can't be bothered to use the wall anchor.

The Hey-brother Cat Tree with Large Hammock is a legitimately good product at its price point. It's not perfect — the base could be wider and the fur sheds a bit — but it delivers where it matters: durability, function, and a hammock your cat will actually claim as their throne.
