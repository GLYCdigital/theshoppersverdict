**The EHEIM Classic is the Toyota Corolla of aquarium filters: boring, unglamorous, and utterly dependable.** If you want flashy smart features or tool-free disconnects, look elsewhere. But if you want a filter that quietly runs for a decade, this German workhorse deserves a hard look.

## What's Actually in the Box

EHEIM packs everything you need to start filtering immediately: the canister body, pump head, two lengths of hose, intake/outtake tubes with strainer and spray bar, and — notably — three media types already loaded in. You get EHEIM MechPro for mechanical filtration, SubstratPro for biological colonization, and activated carbon for chemical polishing. That's a genuine value add; most competitors sell you an empty box and make you drop another $30-40 on media.

## Performance and Build Quality

The Classic line has been essentially unchanged for 40 years, and that's the point. The canister is thick, UV-stabilized plastic that feels like it could survive a car running over it. The pump head seals with four metal clamps that create a positive, leak-resistant seal — no fiddly O-ring alignment issues common with cheaper filters.

Flow rate is honest. The 2213 model (for tanks up to 60 gallons) moves 116 gallons per hour at zero head pressure, and about half that once you factor in hoses and media. That's adequate for most community tanks, though you'll want the larger 2215 or 2217 for heavily stocked setups.

The self-priming pump is a genuine convenience. Press the red button a few times and the filter pulls water from the tank automatically — no siphoning, no filling the canister by hand, no swearing. It's a feature I wish every canister filter had.

## What Owners Keep Telling Me

Scrolling through the 204 ratings, a pattern emerges: these filters last. One reviewer wrote, *"I've had mine running for 8 years. Replaced the impeller once. Still going strong."* Another noted, *"Set it up, forgot about it. Water stays crystal clear."* The biological media is frequently praised for jump-starting the nitrogen cycle in new tanks.

## Where It Falls Short

**The hoses are the weak point.** The standard EHEIM hoses are stiff, and the clamps use a screw-down design that's genuinely annoying when you want to remove the filter for cleaning. Modern competitors like Fluval use lever-operated quick disconnects that shut off water flow automatically. With the EHEIM, you'll drain water on the floor at least once. Plan for it.

**No media baskets.** The Classic uses loose media stacked in layers, separated only by perforated plates. This means when you replace carbon, you're digging through SubstratPro by hand. It also risks channeling — water finding the path of least resistance through the media rather than flowing evenly.

**It's noisy during prime.** The pump makes a gurgling sound for a few minutes after startup until air purges. That's normal but startling the first time.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a set-and-forget aquarist who values reliability over convenience. It's ideal for planted tanks, discus setups, or any aquarium where consistent biological filtration matters more than quick maintenance. The included media makes it a smart first canister filter for beginners who want one box, nothing else to buy.

**Skip it if** you hate maintenance enough to pay a premium for quick-release fittings, or if you're stocking a tank over 75 gallons — you'll need the bigger, pricier models anyway.

## FAQ

**Q: How often do I need to clean the media?**
Every 4-6 weeks, rinse mechanical media in dechlorinated water. Replace carbon monthly, biological media only when it crumbles.

**Q: Will this work with my 10-gallon tank?**
The 2211 model is rated for tanks up to 30 gallons. The 2213 works best for 20-60 gallons. Match the model to your tank volume.

**Q: Is the pump silent?**
After priming, yes — a low hum you'll only notice in a quiet room. The gurgle during startup fades within minutes.

## The Verdict
