**The Dakota 283 Crate: The Budget Kennel That Actually Holds Up**

Most economy kennels under $60 are flimsy wire tents that bend if your dog leans on them. The Dakota 283 Crate—part of the Canine Rough and Tough line—doesn't fall into that trap. It's not fancy, but it's built with a seriousness that the price tag doesn't suggest.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the welded wire frame (already assembled into panels), a one-piece ABS plastic floor pan, and the hardware pack. Assembly takes about 10 minutes with a Phillips screwdriver—no Allen wrenches or headaches. The included pan is the standout component: it's thick, rigid plastic that won't crack under clawing, and it slides out for hosing down after muddy days.

**Build Quality: Solid, With One Caveat**

The wire is 3.8mm gauge—thicker than most economy crates. The frame is fully welded at the corners, not just spot-welded, which makes a real difference when you have a determined chewer or a dog that throws its weight against the door. The door latch is a two-step sliding bolt that's genuinely harder for clever paws to operate.

That said, the wire spacing runs 1.5 inches. That's a significant gap for small breeds or puppies. A determined chihuahua or a 10-week-old lab pup can squeeze through or get a paw caught. This is a crate for medium to large dogs—think 30 pounds and up.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The recurring theme in verified reviews is "surprisingly sturdy for the price." One owner of a 70-pound lab mix wrote: *"My dog has destroyed two metal crates from big-box stores. This one has survived three months of daily use, including his anxious chewing on the door bars. Not a single bend."*

Another common praise point is the folding mechanism. The crate collapses flat in under a minute—no tools—which makes it practical for road trips or storing in a closet between uses. Multiple reviewers noted how quiet it is, too: *"No rattling, no squeaking when my dog moves around at night."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The floor pan is functional but not luxurious—it's a flat plastic sheet with no lip to catch urine drips. If you have an incontinent senior dog or a puppy going through house training, you'll want a pee pad underneath or a second pan to rotate. 

The pan also shifts slightly on hard floors when your dog does a full-body shake or runs in place. It doesn't slide out completely, but it's enough to be mildly annoying. A few users mentioned adding non-slip furniture pads under the pan to fix this.

The door only opens on the front—no side access. If you plan to tuck this crate into a corner or against a wall, you'll need to pull it out to let your dog in and out easily.

**Who Should Buy This**

This crate suits three buyer types: 
- **Budget-conscious owners with medium-to-large dogs** who need a durable crate without paying $150+ for premium brands.
- **Travelers** who want a crate that folds flat and survives being thrown in the trunk.
- **Owners of chewers** who have already gone through one flimsy crate and need something that resists bending.

Skip it if you have a small breed, a puppy under 12 pounds, or a dog with separation anxiety that requires a heavy-duty escape-proof model. For those cases, look at a 1-inch wire spacing or a reinforced aluminum crate.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Is this crate safe for a dog that likes to chew on metal?**
A: The welded construction and thicker wire hold up far better than typical economy crates, but no wire crate is chew-proof. If your dog is an aggressive biter that targets bars specifically, you're better off with a heavy-duty or impact-resistant crate.

**Q: Does it fit in a standard SUV trunk?**
A: The 36-inch and 42-inch sizes fold down to roughly 3-4 inches thick. It fits flat in most sedan trunks and SUV cargo areas, though it's long—measure your trunk opening first.

**Q: How hard is cleaning the pan?**
A: The ABS plastic is non-porous. Mild soap and water, or a rinse with a hose, removes messes quickly. It doesn't absorb odors like some softer plastics do.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need an affordable, genuinely sturdy crate for a medium-to-large dog that will see daily use. The Dakota 283 outperforms its price class on build quality and folds flat when you need the space back.

**Skip it if** you have a small dog under 30 pounds (the wire spacing is a safety concern), need a crate with side access, or expect a pan that catches every drop of liquid.

This isn't a showpiece. It's a workhorse that respects your wallet. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, the Dakota 283 earns its reputation as the economy crate that doesn't embarrass you in front of your vet.
