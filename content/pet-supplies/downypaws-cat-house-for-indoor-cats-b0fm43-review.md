**The Short Version: A Cozy Cave That Cats Actually Use**

Cats are picky. You know it, I know it, and DownyPaws clearly knows it. This cat house isn't just another cardboard box with a hole cut in it — it's a properly engineered hideaway that most indoor cats will adopt within days. Here's the full breakdown.

---

**What You're Actually Getting**

The box contains the cat house itself (fully assembled — no tools, no wrestling with instructions), plus a thick, plush inner cushion that's about 1.5 inches of dense padding. The house measures roughly 18" x 18" x 16" tall, which gives a 10–12 lb cat plenty of room to curl up without feeling lost in a cavern.

The exterior is a soft, felt-like fabric bonded to a rigid inner frame. The construction is three layers: the soft outer shell, a structural middle layer that holds its shape, and the plush interior lining. The entry hole is 7 inches in diameter — big enough for most adult cats, small enough to keep the interior feeling enclosed and secure.

---

**Real-World Performance**

I've tested this with multiple cats, and the behavioral response is the real metric. Most cats investigate it within the first hour, and the majority start napping inside by day two. That's not an accident — the enclosed, cave-like design taps into a cat's natural instinct to seek out safe, confined sleeping spots.

The cushion is the standout feature. It's removable and machine-washable, and it actually holds up. The stitching doesn't unravel, and the padding doesn't clump into sad lumps after a few wash cycles. That's rare in this price range.

Build quality is solid. The house holds its shape when a cat sits on top of it — and they will, because cats treat any elevated surface as a throne. The base is flat and stable, so it doesn't wobble on hardwood or carpet.

**Where It Falls Short**

The base isn't waterproof. If your cat has an accident or knocks over a water bowl nearby, moisture seeps through to whatever's underneath. Put it on a hard floor or a washable rug, not on your white carpet.

There's also a noticeable chemical smell straight out of the box. It's not overwhelming, but it's there — and it takes a couple of days to air out. If your cat is sensitive to new scents, let it air in a ventilated room before introducing it.

One more thing: the fabric attracts fur like a magnet. It's not a dealbreaker, but you'll want a lint roller nearby if you keep it in a high-traffic area.
