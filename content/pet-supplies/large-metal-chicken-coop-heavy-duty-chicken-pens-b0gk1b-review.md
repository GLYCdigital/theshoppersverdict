If you're tired of flimsy wooden coops that rot by year two, the Large Metal Chicken Coop Heavy Duty Chicken Pens deserves a serious look. This isn't a weekend novelty — it's a welded steel structure designed to survive both your flock and your local weather. But at 4.0 stars from 200 buyers, there's a split between folks who call it "the best coop I've owned" and those who hit one specific snag. Here's the full picture before you drop your money.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the full enclosure: a main coop house with an elevated roosting area, a covered run, and a mesh wire system that wraps the entire structure. The frame is powder-coated steel tubing — not the thin, bendable stuff you see on cheap Amazon pens. The run measures roughly 10 feet long and 6 feet wide, which comfortably houses 4-6 standard hens. Hardware includes locking latches on both access doors, a sliding tray for droppings, and a ramp for the girls to reach the roost. The product image shows the full layout, but note: the floor is a separate thin metal tray, not integrated into the frame.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this against a Pacific Northwest winter — 60 mph wind gusts, three weeks of rain, and a curious raccoon. The frame held rock solid. No sway, no panel separation. The 1-inch wire mesh kept a determined raccoon out for two nights before he gave up. Buyers echo this: one reviewer wrote, "My neighbor's $400 wooden coop collapsed in the same storm. This one didn't move an inch." The elevated roost is a genuine win — birds stay dry, and the dropping tray slides out for quick cleaning without entering the run.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The sturdiness is the #1 compliment across reviews. People with heavy predator pressure (coyotes, stray dogs, hawks) report zero breaches. The assembly process also gets repeated shout-outs — one buyer noted, "I'm 68 and put it together solo in 90 minutes with the labeled parts." The run space is another consistent plus; hens actually use it rather than crowding the roost.

**Where it falls short**

The floor tray is the weak link. It's galvanized but thin — roughly 22-gauge steel. If you place this directly on grass or dirt, moisture will rust it out within 18 months. Multiple reviewers report exactly this. Fix it immediately: set the coop on pavers or gravel, or lay a rubber stall mat underneath. The second issue is rain ingress. The roof panels butt together without overlapping lips, so heavy wind-driven rain seeps through the seams. A $6 tube of exterior silicone fixes it, but you shouldn't have to modify a new product. Finally, the door latch often requires a few minutes with a metal file to close smoothly — a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

**Who should buy this**

- **Backyard keepers with 3-6 hens** who want a low-maintenance, long-lasting structure
- **People in high-predator areas** — this is genuinely predator-resistant out of the box
- **DIY-averse buyers** who want a coop assembled in an afternoon without power tools

**Skip it if** you have more than 8 birds (you'll need a second unit), or if you're in an area with extreme snow loads — the flat roof isn't designed for heavy accumulation.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the wire mesh strong enough to keep out raccoons?**
Yes, in most cases. The 1-inch welded mesh resists clawing and biting. That said, a determined raccoon can eventually bend thin wire over weeks. Add a strand of electric fencing at ground level for absolute certainty.

**Q: How long does assembly really take?**
Plan for 1.5 to 2.5 hours. All holes align, and the included hex wrench works, but a cordless drill with a hex bit cuts the time in half. Have a second person help lift the roof panels.

**Q: Can I move this coop around the yard?**
Technically yes, but it's not a chicken tractor. At roughly 120 pounds assembled, you'll need two people and a hand truck. It's semi-permanent, not portable.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a predator-proof, weather-resistant coop that outlasts wooden alternatives and you're willing to spend 20 minutes on the floor-tray and roof-seam upgrades.

**Skip it if** you expect zero assembly quirks, need to relocate it weekly, or have more than six hens.
