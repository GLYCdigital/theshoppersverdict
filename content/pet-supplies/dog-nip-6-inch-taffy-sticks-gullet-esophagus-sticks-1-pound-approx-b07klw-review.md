**What You Actually Get**

The product ships as a 1-pound bag of dried gullet esophagus sticks, each roughly 6 inches long. As shown in the product image, these are tan, slightly curled tubes — not uniform cylinders. **Performance and Real-World Use**

The texture is the standout feature. Gullet is dense but slightly spongy — it gives under pressure rather than splintering. That makes it genuinely safer than bone for aggressive chewers who crack teeth. When a dog gnaws on it, small pieces soften and break off gradually, which makes it more digestible than rawhide.

The tradeoff? For the hardest chewers, these aren't marathon chews. Several reviews note a determined Lab or Pit can finish one in 20-30 minutes. That's not a defect — it's physics. But if you need something to occupy a dog for an hour, adjust expectations.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

- **Digestibility:** Repeated feedback confirms these break down easily and don't cause the digestive upset that rawhide or even some bully sticks trigger.
- **Low odor:** This is the most common compliment. Compared to bully sticks, gullet has a much milder, less offensive smell.

**Where It Falls Short**

The inconsistency in stick size is the most frequent complaint. A 1-pound bag doesn't guarantee a specific count, and some sticks are notably thinner than others. For the price, you might feel shortchanged if you get fewer, smaller pieces.

And the packaging — a simple bag with no resealable zipper — isn't great if you're storing multiple sticks for weeks. Keep them in an airtight container or they'll dry out further and get brittle.
