If your dog is a power chewer who demolishes flimsy treats in ninety seconds flat, the Best Pet Supplies GigaBite Beef Esophagus is worth a hard look. These are not your average jerky strips. They're thick, dense tubes of dried beef gullet — the esophagus muscle — that force your dog to actually work for their reward. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, the consensus is clear: most dogs love them, and most owners appreciate the value. But there are tradeoffs worth knowing before you buy.

**What You're Actually Getting**

Open the bag and you'll find roughly a dozen or more long, tan-brown tubes that look like dehydrated squid tentacles. They're stiff at first, almost brittle on the outside, but they have a leathery core that softens as saliva works into it. The texture is the whole point here. Unlike rawhide — which can splinter and cause blockages — beef esophagus is a natural muscle tissue that breaks down into softer, chewable pieces rather than sharp shards. It's also a single-ingredient product: just beef esophagus. No binders, no glycerin, no artificial smoke flavoring.

**The Real-World Performance**

Here's where this treat earns its keep. My own lab mix went through a standard bully stick in about eleven minutes. A GigaBite esophagus chew kept her occupied for nearly forty-five minutes on the first go, and she went back for the softer remnants later. The texture also makes it a decent option for anxious chewers who need a prolonged activity to settle down.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The recurring theme in the reviews is the value-to-durability ratio. **Where It Falls Short — Honestly**

The odor is the biggest complaint, and it's legitimate. These are dried animal parts, and they smell like it — a strong, barnyard-like aroma that intensifies when your dog is actively chewing. If you're sensitive to smells or plan to give these in the car or on the couch, reconsider. The greasy residue is another issue. The natural fats leach out during chewing and can leave dark, oily stains on light-colored carpets. Give these on a washable surface or outdoors.

The second issue is the texture curve. While the treat softens as it's chewed, the initial hardness can be too much for senior dogs or those with dental problems. Several reviewers noted their older dogs lost interest after a few minutes because the chew was too tough to start. If your dog has significant dental issues, skip these and look for softer alternatives.

**Who Should Buy These**

**Skip These If**

- Your dog is a senior with dental problems or missing teeth.

- Your dog is a "gulper" who swallows treats whole rather than chewing. The size and density could pose a choking risk for that style of eater.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Are these safe for puppies?**
For puppies over six months with adult teeth coming in, yes, with supervision. Younger puppies with baby teeth will struggle with the density. Always monitor any long-lasting chew, regardless of age.

**How long does one chew last?**
Depends on your dog's chewing style. Aggressive chewers get 20-40 minutes; moderate chewers can stretch it to an hour or more. Some buyers reported their gentle chewers made one stick last across multiple sessions.

**Do these splinter like rawhide?**
No. That's the main advantage over rawhide. The esophagus muscle softens and shreds into pliable pieces rather than breaking into hard shards. Still, no treat is 100% risk-free — supervise the first few uses to see how your dog handles them.
