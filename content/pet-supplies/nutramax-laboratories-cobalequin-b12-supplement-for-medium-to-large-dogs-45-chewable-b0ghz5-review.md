## What You're Actually Getting

The bottle contains 45 chewable tablets designed specifically for medium to large dogs (roughly 25-75 pounds). Each chewable delivers 1,000 mcg of methylcobalamin — not the cheaper cyanocobalamin found in most grocery store brands. That distinction matters: methylcobalamin is the naturally occurring, bioactive form of B12 that your dog's body can use immediately without conversion.

The tablets are chicken-liver flavored. That's not a marketing gimmick — it's practical. Anyone who's tried to force a pill down a reluctant dog's throat knows the value of a chewable that reads as a treat.

## Real-World Performance

Dogs with B12 deficiency typically show gastrointestinal symptoms and lethargy. Owners report noticeable changes in both areas, though patience is required. One reviewer noted: "My senior German Shepherd had chronic diarrhea for months. After three weeks on Cobalequin, her stools firmed up and she started acting like a puppy again."

The absorption factor matters here. Because methylcobalamin bypasses the need for intrinsic factor in the gut (which is often compromised in dogs with GI issues), it works even when the digestive system isn't functioning optimally. That's why vets specifically recommend this form over standard B12.

## FAQ

**Q: How long until I see results?**
Most owners report improvements in energy and digestion within 2-4 weeks of daily dosing. Full restoration of B12 levels typically takes 4-6 weeks. Don't expect overnight miracles.

**Q: Can I give this to a small dog?**
This specific product is formulated for medium to large breeds. Nutramax makes a separate small dog version with adjusted dosing. Check with your vet for the correct product and dosage.

**Q: Is it safe with other medications?**
B12 is water-soluble, and excess is excreted in urine, making it generally very safe. However, always confirm with your vet if your dog is on metformin, proton pump inhibitors, or other meds that can interfere with B12 absorption.
