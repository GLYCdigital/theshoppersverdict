If you've got a tall dog and a living room that doesn't need to look like a kennel, the Keyluv 71''H x 47.2''W Large Dog Crate Furniture is probably on your radar. Here's the real story after digging through 210 ratings and owner experiences.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the two-story crate frame, engineered wood side panels, the wire mesh door, two storage shelves, and all the cam-lock fittings. The footprint is 47.2 inches wide by 27.5 inches deep — that's a substantial piece of furniture, not a corner crate. The two-tier design gives you a 35.5-inch lower den and an upper level with a ramp or platform divider, plus the top surface becomes two flat storage shelves.

As shown in the product image, the whole thing reads as a sideboard or console table when the door is closed. The dark walnut finish on the engineered wood is convincing enough that guests won't immediately clock it as a dog cage.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The frame is a hybrid: engineered wood panels bolted to a steel wire structure. The panels have a smooth, water-resistant coating that wipes clean with a damp cloth — a small mercy when muddy paws are involved. The door latch is a two-point locking mechanism that holds securely but isn't child-proof; a determined toddler can figure it out.

The 71-inch height is the headline feature. Owners with Great Danes, Irish Wolfhounds, and mastiffs consistently report their dogs can stand, turn around, and stretch without hitting the ceiling. That's a genuine differentiator — most "large" crates top out around 42 inches.

One thing to know: the included wire floor pan is functional but not luxurious. Several reviewers noted it flexes under heavier dogs (100+ pounds), so ordering a custom-fit plastic tray or a thick memory foam crate pad is worth the extra $30-$50.

## What Owners Praise

The furniture factor is the most repeated compliment. One reviewer wrote, *"I've had this in my dining room for six months and guests ask where we keep the dog. They don't realize the sideboard is her crate."* Another noted, *"The storage on top is actually usable — I keep her food bin and bowls up there, and it looks intentional."* The two-shelf top is a smart use of vertical space that most crate furniture misses.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest friction point. The cam-lock system requires aligning panels precisely, and the instructions are mostly diagrams with minimal text. Multiple owners reported 90-minute builds with two people. Budget for that.

The wire floor pan issue is real. It's thin gauge and can shift during use. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's an obvious cost-cutting decision on an otherwise solid product.

Also, this is heavy — around 90 pounds assembled. You're not going to fold it up and stash it in a closet. If you move frequently, this is a commitment.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Tall-breed owners** who've been squeezing their dog into standard 42-inch crates — this is the fix
- **Design-conscious households** that want a crate that doubles as a console table
- **Owners with space for a permanent furniture piece** who don't need portability

Skip it if you have a small-to-medium dog (overkill), live in a rental where you'll move annually, or hate multi-step assembly.

A: Yes, for sleeping and lounging. The 71-inch height and 47-inch width give a 130-pound dog room to stand and turn. Just upgrade the floor pan for comfort.

**Q: Is the two-story design safe for older or arthritic dogs?**
A: The upper level requires climbing the ramp. For healthy dogs, it's fine. For seniors or dogs with joint issues, keep them in the lower den and use the upper level for storage.

**Q: Does the door have a double-lock for escape artists?**
A: The two-point latch is secure for most dogs, but a determined chewer can work at the corners. If your dog is a known crate-escapee, add a carabiner clip as backup.
