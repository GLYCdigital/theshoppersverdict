# Gesomepurrz Cat Litter Box Enclosure Review: Furniture That Hides the Mess, Mostly

Most cat litter box enclosures look like what they are: plastic boxes pretending to be furniture. The Gesomepurrz Cat Litter Box Enclosure at $29.99 is different — it's a compact wooden cabinet that genuinely blends into a living room or home office. But is it practical for daily use, or just another pretty face? Here's what 210 Amazon reviewers and my own assessment tell you before you spend your money.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the enclosure panels, a hex wrench, and hardware. There's no litter box included — this is purely the cabinet shell. When assembled, it measures roughly 24"L x 17"W x 18"H. The MDF panels have a printed wood-grain finish that reads as "mid-century modern" from across a room. It ships flat, so if you're in an apartment, you won't wrestle with a giant box.

Assembly is straightforward. You align the cam locks, tighten the hex bolts, and you're done in about 8–10 minutes. One reviewer noted, *"Went together in minutes — no confusing instructions, just the wrench and a few bolts."*

## Real-World Performance

The design is clever: a top opening for scooping and a front opening for your cat. You lift the hinged lid, scoop the litter, and drop it back down. No bending over, no wrestling with a door. The front entry has a small flap opening that most cats figure out within a day.

The MDF is thicker than I expected at this price point — about 1/2". It won't collapse under a cat jumping on top, and the finish resists scratches reasonably well. That said, this is not waterproof. If your cat is a high-volume urinator who splashes over the box edge, that liquid will soak into the bottom panel and warp it over time. A waterproof liner or tray underneath is a smart addition.

## What Buyers Love

The aesthetic is the headline. Reviewers consistently mention that guests don't realize it's a litter box. One wrote, *"I've had this in my office for two months — three visitors asked where I got the side table."* The scooping convenience also gets repeated praise. The top-opening design eliminates the awkward crouch-and-reach that comes with front-loading cabinets.

The price is another win. Comparable wooden enclosures run $60–$100. At $29.99, you're getting 90% of the function for half the cost.

## Where It Falls Short

The interior is tight. Standard litter boxes up to 19"L x 14"W fit fine, but anything larger — especially high-sided boxes for diggers — won't clear the front opening. Measure your existing box before you buy.

The lack of odor control is the bigger miss. There's no carbon filter slot and no gasket on the top lid, so smells drift out. If you scoop daily, it's manageable. If you're a twice-a-week scooper, you'll smell it. Several reviewers mentioned adding an adhesive charcoal filter to the top opening to compensate.

The front door doesn't latch. Most cats are fine with it, but determined felines will push it open and defeat the hidden look. A small magnet or hook-and-loop strip fixes this, but you shouldn't have to mod a product on day one.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** who want a litter box that doesn't announce itself
- **Small-space owners** with one cat — the interior is too snug for multi-cat households
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who want furniture-grade looks without paying furniture-grade prices
- **Anyone with a standard-size litter box** — measure first, seriously

Skip it if you have a Maine Coon, a jumbo litter box, or a cat who sprays. This is a single-cat, standard-box solution.

## FAQ

**Q: Will a top-entry litter box work inside this enclosure?**
A: No. Top-entry boxes are typically too tall. Use a standard open tray or a low-sided hooded box with the top removed.

**Q: How do I clean the interior?**
A: Wipe with a damp cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the panels. The bottom panel is the most vulnerable to moisture damage, so place a waterproof mat or tray underneath the litter box.

**Q: Does it come with a litter scoop or mat?**
A: No. Just the enclosure and hardware. You'll need to supply your own litter box and scoop.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a budget-friendly, good-looking enclosure that hides litter mess and makes scooping easier with its top-access design. It's a solid 4.2-star value for single-cat households with standard boxes.

**Skip it if** you need odor filtration, have a large cat or jumbo box, or want a latch on the front door. Those limitations are real, but for $29.99, you're getting more than you paid for — which is rare in pet furniture.
