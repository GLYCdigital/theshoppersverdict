Let’s be blunt: most dog treats on Amazon are 30% mystery meat and 70% marketing. The Fresh Is Best Turkey Heart Fillets are the opposite — one ingredient, zero filler, and a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings that mostly holds up under scrutiny. If you’re shopping for a high-value training reward or a topper for a picky eater, this is worth a hard look. But it’s not perfect, and I’ll get to that.

**What you actually get**

The bag contains freeze-dried raw turkey hearts, sliced into fillet-style strips. No grain, no glycerin, no artificial flavoring, no “natural turkey flavor” (which is code for processed junk). Just turkey heart. The freeze-drying process removes moisture while preserving enzymes and nutrients that get destroyed in high-heat baking. As shown in the product image, the pieces are irregular, reddish-brown strips — expect some variation in size and thickness between pieces.

Depending on your dog’s size, you’ll get roughly 30-50 pieces per 3-ounce bag. That’s the tradeoff: premium single-ingredient treats cost more per ounce than standard biscuits, and you’ll notice it on the price tag.

**Real-world performance**

The texture is where these shine. Unlike rock-hard freeze-dried liver cubes that make you worry about cracked teeth, the turkey heart fillets are soft and chewy. They tear easily — a huge plus for puppies, senior dogs, or any dog that inhales treats without chewing. One Amazon reviewer noted, *“My 14-year-old chihuahua with missing teeth can eat these without issue. She finishes them faster than any other treat I’ve tried.”* That tracks with the 4.4-star rating; dental-friendly texture is a recurring theme in positive reviews.

Nutritionally, turkey heart is lean protein with naturally occurring taurine, which supports cardiac health — a thoughtful choice for breeds prone to heart issues. The single-ingredient nature also makes these a solid option for dogs with food sensitivities, since you can rule out any hidden triggers.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Owners repeatedly mention three things: the soft texture, the clean ingredient list, and the versatility. The fillets work as training treats, medication disguisers, or crumbled over kibble for picky eaters. Multiple reviewers use them as a high-reward treat during vet visits or grooming. **Where it falls short**

Let’s be honest about the downsides. First, the smell. This is organ meat — it smells like organ meat. If you’re sensitive to that, you’ll notice it every time you open the bag. It’s not overwhelming, but it’s not a pleasant bakery scent either. Second, the size inconsistency. Some pieces are thin and small; others are thick and substantial. For toy breeds, you’ll likely need to break pieces in half or crumble them. Third, the price. At roughly $0.60-$0.80 per ounce (depending on current pricing), these are a splurge compared to standard treats. You’re paying for quality, but budget-conscious owners may find the cost hard to justify for daily use.
