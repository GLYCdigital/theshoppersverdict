If you've owned a guinea pig for more than a week, you know the hay problem. It ends up everywhere — trampled into bedding, scattered across the floor, and wasted by the handful. This hanging hay feeder bag attacks that problem directly, and with a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, it's clearly working for most owners. But is it the right feeder for your setup? Let's dig into the details.

**What You Actually Get**

The package contains one hanging feeder bag constructed from Oxford cloth — the same dense, woven material you'd find on quality luggage — with reinforced stitching along the seams. The bag features a wide top opening that stays rigid enough to accept a handful of hay without collapsing, and a mesh front panel that allows your pet to pull strands through while keeping the bulk contained. Two metal hooks attach to the back, designed to loop over standard cage bars.

As shown in the product image, the feeder measures roughly 10 inches across and 8 inches tall — compact enough for a 2x4 C&C cage or a standard mid-size rabbit hutch, but still large enough to hold a solid day's worth of hay for one or two guinea pigs.

**Real-World Performance**

The mesh front is the smartest design choice here. Guinea pigs and rabbits naturally want to forage and pull — that's instinctual behavior. The mesh lets them work for their food without creating a mess. In practice, owners report that hay waste drops dramatically compared to floor feeding or open bins. One reviewer noted, "My piggies used to trample half their hay into the bedding. Now I'm throwing away maybe a quarter of what I used to."

The Oxford cloth holds up better than the canvas or nylon bags I've tested from cheaper brands. Guinea pigs chew — constantly — and the stitching on this unit survives the attention. The hooks are functional, though not the strongest point of the design. They're thin enough to bend if you're rough with them, so seat them carefully on the cage bars.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme across positive reviews is cleanliness. Multiple owners specifically call out how this feeder keeps hay off the cage floor, which means less frequent full bedding changes and less odor. One long-term buyer wrote, "I've had mine for four months and it still looks new. My guinea pigs love pulling hay through the mesh — it keeps them entertained."

The wide mouth opening gets consistent shout-outs too. You can refill it one-handed without unclipping the feeder from the cage, which matters when you're doing morning hay refills before coffee.

**Where It Falls Short**

The mesh holes are small enough that fine hay particles and dust accumulate inside the bag. You'll need to shake it out or give it a rinse every few days to keep airflow moving — otherwise, the hay at the bottom can get stale and your pet will start ignoring it.

The hooks are the second weak point. On standard wire cages they work fine, but if you have thicker bars or a wooden enclosure, you'll need to rig your own attachment method. A few reviewers mentioned replacing the hooks with zip ties or carabiners for a more secure hang.

**Who Should Buy This**

This feeder suits guinea pig owners with 1–3 pigs, or a single dwarf rabbit, who are tired of daily hay cleanup. It's also a solid pick if you're going on a weekend trip and want hay to stay accessible without becoming bedding. Skip it if you have heavy chewers who destroy fabric — you'll want a metal or ceramic option instead. And if you have more than three pigs, you'll likely need two of these to keep up with demand.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will my guinea pig get stuck in the mesh?**
A: No. The mesh openings are roughly half an inch across — large enough for hay strands, too small for a paw or head. Multiple reviewers with baby guinea pigs confirm no issues.

**Q: Can this work for chinchillas or rats?**
A: Yes, with a caveat. Rats can chew through softer fabrics, but the Oxford cloth here is dense enough that most owners report success. Chinchillas will also use it well, though they may be messier about pulling hay out.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
A: Shake out loose debris daily. For deeper cleaning, hand wash with mild soap and warm water, then air dry. Avoid the washing machine — the hooks will dent the drum and the agitation can stress the seams.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're tired of wasting hay and cleaning it out of bedding daily. This feeder solves that problem at a reasonable price with durable materials and a design that respects your pet's foraging instincts.

**Skip it if** you have an aggressive chewer who destroys fabric items, or if you need something for more than three small animals.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The hay waste reduction alone justifies the purchase. Just budget two minutes every few days to shake out the dust, and you'll be satisfied.
