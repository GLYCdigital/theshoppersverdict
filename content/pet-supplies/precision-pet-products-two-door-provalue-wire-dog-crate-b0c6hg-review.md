Let me be straight with you: there are roughly 47,000 wire dog crates on Amazon, and most of them are the same Chinese-made box with different logos stamped on. The Precision Pet Two Door Provalue isn't revolutionary — but it's better built than most in its price range, and the dual-door layout solves a real problem. Here's the breakdown after digging through 229 ratings and owner feedback.

**What You Actually Get**

The crate ships flat in a box with the frame pre-assembled in panels. You get the wire frame, a removable plastic pan, and a metal divider panel for puppy training. Assembly is genuinely tool-free — the panels click together at the corners, and the whole thing collapses flat when you need to store it. The divider slides in on tracks and locks with the same latch system, so you can shrink the interior space for housebreaking without buying a second crate.

The two doors are the headline feature. The front door is the standard swing-open, and the side door runs nearly the full length of the crate. That matters more than you'd think: slide the crate against a wall and the side door still gives you full access for cleaning or coaxing a reluctant dog inside. It also means you can position the crate in corners or under tables where a front-only door would make it useless.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The wire is 11-gauge steel — thicker than the cheap 12-gauge stuff on bargain crates. The frame holds its shape without wobble, and the doors swing on smooth hinges that don't bind. The finish is a baked-on black epoxy that resists scratching better than the painted alternatives. Owners consistently mention the durability:

> "We've had this for two years with a 70-pound lab mix who thinks the crate is a chew toy. The frame is solid, the doors still align perfectly."

> "Assembly took me literally four minutes. I was expecting a puzzle."

The pan is the weak point. It's a thin plastic tray that slides out for cleaning — fine for a 30-pound dog, but larger breeds can flex it, and repeated scratching will eventually crack it. Replacement pans are cheap, but you shouldn't need to buy one in the first six months.

**Where It Falls Short**

The slide-bolt latches on both doors are the weakest component. They work, but they're light-gauge metal that doesn't inspire confidence with a determined chewer. Multiple reviews mention dogs learning to rattle the latch open:

> "My beagle figured out the side door latch in three days. Had to add carabiners."

If you have an escape-artist dog, budget for aftermarket latches or look at a heavy-duty crate with locking mechanisms. The crate also has no carrying handle — it's strictly a stationary home crate, not a travel option.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Puppy owners**: The included divider makes this a two-stage crate. Buy the size your dog will reach as an adult, use the divider while they're small, remove it as they grow. Saves you buying two crates.
- **People with limited floor space**: The side door lets you tuck this against a wall or into a corner without sacrificing access.
- **Owners who move the crate occasionally**: It folds flat in seconds and stores under a bed or in a closet.

**Skip it if** you have a power chewer over 60 pounds, a dog that's learned to work slide latches, or you need a crate for airline travel — this isn't airline-approved.

**FAQ**

**Q: What size should I get for my dog?**
Measure your dog from nose to tail and add 4-6 inches for the length, and from floor to top of head while sitting for height. The 36-inch fits most medium breeds (25-40 lbs), the 42-inch suits large breeds (40-70 lbs), and the 48-inch works for giant breeds up to 90 lbs. When in doubt, size up — the divider handles the small-dog phase.

**Q: Is the pan really dishwasher safe?**
The manufacturer says yes, but hand-washing is smarter. The pan is thin plastic and the heat of a dishwasher cycle can warp it over time. A hose or bathtub rinse with mild soap is all it needs.

**Q: Does the crate fold down completely flat?**
Yes — the panels detach at the corners and stack flat. The whole thing fits in a car trunk with room to spare. Reassembly takes about the same time as setup: under five minutes.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, functional wire crate with the flexibility of two doors and a useful divider — especially for puppies or tight spaces. The build quality beats most competitors at this price point, and the folding design is genuinely convenient.

**Skip it if** you have a large, determined chewer or a dog that's already proven it can work simple latches. The thin pan and basic locks are the budget compromises here, and they'll show on a dog that tests them.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-rounded crate that nails the basics and earns its rating through smart design. The latch and pan issues keep it from a perfect score, but for most owners, this is the right crate.
