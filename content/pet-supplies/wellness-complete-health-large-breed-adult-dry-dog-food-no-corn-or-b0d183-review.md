## Is This the Right Kibble for Your 70-Pound Companion?

Large breed dogs have specific nutritional needs that standard adult formulas don't always address. That's the gap Wellness Complete Health Large Breed Adult aims to fill — and with 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly connecting with owners. But does the formula live up to the hype? I dug into the ingredient panel, analyzed owner feedback, and compared it against the competition. Here's what you need to know before adding it to your cart.

## What's Actually in the Bag

The recipe leads with deboned chicken, followed by chicken meal — a solid one-two protein punch that puts real animal protein at the forefront. At 22% minimum crude protein and 12% fat, it's calibrated for adult large breeds rather than puppies or seniors. The kibble size is notably larger than standard formulas, which encourages chewing and slows down fast eaters — a thoughtful detail for gulpers.

What sets this apart from budget options is the joint support package. Glucosamine and chondroitin are included at meaningful levels, not just trace amounts for label decoration. Omega fatty acids from salmon oil support skin and coat health, while prebiotic fiber aids digestion. The calorie count sits around 350 kcal per cup, which helps maintain healthy weight — critical for breeds like Labs and German Shepherds that are prone to obesity.

## What Owners Are Saying

The praise is consistent across the reviews. One owner of a 3-year-old Golden Retriever noted, "My dog's coat has never looked better, and his stools are firm and regular." Another reviewer with a Great Dane mix mentioned, "The joint support is why I switched — my vet approved the ingredients immediately."

But there are recurring complaints worth flagging. Picky eaters are a real issue. Several owners report their dogs initially turned their noses up at the kibble, requiring a slow transition period of 7-10 days. One reviewer bluntly stated, "My Lab ate the first bowl, then refused it for two days. We mixed it with wet food to get her to finish the bag." If you have a notoriously finicky eater, buy the smallest bag first as a test.

## Where It Falls Short

The price is the most obvious drawback. Per-pound cost runs roughly 30-40% higher than comparable large-breed formulas from Purina One or Iams. You're paying for the ingredient transparency and the absence of corn, wheat, and soy — and for many owners, that's worth it. But if budget is your primary constraint, this will strain it.

Also note: this is a grain-inclusive formula. Despite the "No" in the product title referring to corn, wheat, and soy, it does contain grains like barley and brown rice. Dogs with confirmed grain allergies need a different formula entirely.

## Who Should Buy This

This food is ideal for owners of active large breeds — think German Shepherds, Labs, Rottweilers, and similar working or sporting dogs. It's particularly well-suited if you're already concerned about joint health and want a food that supports it without needing separate supplements. It's also a strong choice if you prefer foods without corn and soy fillers but aren't committed to a grain-free diet.

Skip it if you have a picky eater, a dog with grain allergies, or a tight monthly pet food budget.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should I feed my large breed dog?**
A: The bag recommends 2.5 to 4.5 cups daily for dogs weighing 50-100 pounds, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level and body condition — the feeding chart is a starting point, not gospel.

**Q: Is this suitable for large breed puppies?**
A: No. This is specifically an adult formula. Wellness makes a separate Large Breed Puppy recipe with different calcium and phosphorus ratios for proper bone development.

**Q: Will this help with my dog's hip dysplasia?**
A: The glucosamine and chondroitin support joint health, but they can't reverse existing structural issues. Think of it as prevention and maintenance, not a cure.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have an adult large breed dog and want a high-quality, joint-supporting formula with real meat as the first ingredient, and you're willing to pay a premium for it.

**Skip it if:** Your dog is a picky eater, has grain sensitivities, or you need a more budget-friendly option.
