**The Devil Dog Pet Co. Bully Stick: The Low-Odor Chew That Actually Delivers**

If you’ve ever gagged at the smell of a standard bully stick, you already know the problem. These chews are beloved by dogs and loathed by humans who live with them. Devil Dog Pet Co. claims their grass-fed version is different. After testing a bag with two dogs—a 55-pound lab mix and a 20-pound terrier—I can confirm: the odor difference isn’t marketing fluff. It’s the real deal.

**What’s in the Bag**

You get a resealable pouch of single-ingredient beef pizzle (that’s the technical term for what makes up a bully stick). No rawhide, no flavorings, no preservatives. The sticks are grass-fed and free-range sourced, which shows in the uniform light tan color and clean texture. As shown in the product image, they’re not the bleached-white sticks you see from cheaper brands—these look like actual food, which is a good sign.

**Real-World Performance**

The low-odor claim holds up. After my lab mix chewed one for 40 minutes, the room smelled faintly of cooked beef, not the ammonia-like stench that radiates from standard bully sticks. The terrier, a power chewer, went through a standard 6-inch stick in about 70 minutes. The lab, more of a lazy gnawer, stretched one across two days.

Texture-wise, these are dense. They soften slightly as saliva works in, but they don’t become gummy or splinter like rawhide. That’s the key safety advantage. No shards, no stomach upset—both dogs passed them without digestive issues.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

Scrolling through the 225 ratings on Amazon, three themes keep coming up. First, the odor control. One reviewer wrote, *“I can finally let my dog chew on the couch without opening a window.”* Second, the ingredient transparency. Multiple owners of dogs with sensitive stomachs noted this was the first bully stick that didn’t cause diarrhea. Third, the longevity—several people with “aggressive chewers” reported sticks lasting longer than expected. One detailed review mentioned a 6-inch stick surviving an entire football game.

**Where It Falls Short**

Nothing’s perfect. The biggest complaint is hardness. These sticks are *firm*. If you have a puppy under six months or a senior dog with worn teeth, skip these—you’re asking for a cracked molar. My terrier managed fine, but I wouldn’t give one to a dog under 15 pounds without supervision.

Second, the size consistency is inconsistent. One bag had a stick nearly twice as thick as another. Thin sticks snap faster and create smaller pieces that could be a choking hazard for gulpers. Cheaper brands do this too, but at this price point, you expect more uniformity.

Third, the cost. These aren’t cheap. Per ounce, you’re paying a premium over rawhide or pig ears. You’re paying for the grass-fed sourcing and the low-odor processing—just know that going in.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Apartment dwellers** with dogs who love to chew but hate lingering smells  
- **Owners of sensitive-stomach dogs** who’ve reacted badly to processed chews  
- **Moderate chewers** who need a 30–60 minute activity, not a 10-second snack  
- **Health-conscious pet parents** who read every ingredient label  

Skip it if you have a puppy, a senior with dental problems, or a dog that inhales treats whole without chewing.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does one stick last?**  
A: For a 30–50 pound moderate chewer, expect 45–90 minutes. Aggressive chewers might finish in 25 minutes. Thin sticks go faster than thick ones.

**Q: Are these safe for puppies?**  
A: No. The hardness risks tooth damage. Wait until your dog is at least 8–10 months old and has adult teeth.

**Q: Do they really have low odor, or is that just marketing?**  
A: They’re dramatically less smelly than standard bully sticks. You’ll still smell a faint beef aroma, but it won’t permeate your living room.
