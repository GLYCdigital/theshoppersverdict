## What You're Actually Getting

The bag contains bite-sized, star-shaped treats engineered with a specific porous texture. That's not aesthetic choice — it's mechanical design. As your cat chews, the fibrous surface scrapes against the tooth surface, physically disrupting plaque before it hardens into tartar. Each piece runs under 2 calories, and the first ingredient is real chicken. No artificial flavors, no corn, no wheat.

As shown in the product image, the treats have a visible sponge-like structure. That's the "DentaLife" difference — the texture does the work, not a chemical additive.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these with a 6-year-old domestic shorthair who eats like she's been starved for weeks. The treats are firm enough to require actual chewing — which is the point — but not so hard that a healthy adult cat struggles. Within four days of replacing her usual treats, I noticed less odor in her bowl and a visibly cleaner surface on her back molars.

The manufacturer claims the texture reduces tartar buildup by up to 57% with daily use. I can't verify that exact number, but the mechanism is sound. Veterinary dentists recommend abrasive textures over enzymatic coatings — enzymes wash away with saliva, but mechanical scraping physically removes buildup.

## Where It Falls Short

**Senior cats and dental patients, take note.** The texture that makes these effective is also their weakness. Cats with gingivitis, missing teeth, or advanced dental disease will struggle to chew them. Some owners report their older cats swallowing pieces whole — which defeats the purpose entirely.

**The value math is also meh.** A standard bag runs about 85 treats. For a single cat getting 2-3 per day, that's roughly two weeks per bag. You're looking at $8-10 monthly for dental maintenance. That's cheaper than a professional cleaning, but more than generic treats.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Multi-cat households** — the resealable bag keeps treats fresh, and most cats accept them readily
- **Cats 1-8 years old with healthy teeth** — this is preventive maintenance, not a cure
- **Owners who want USA-made products** — these are manufactured in Purina's US facilities, which is a genuine selling point for buyers who prioritize domestic production

**Skip these if** your cat has diagnosed dental disease, is over 12 years old, or has ever had teeth extracted. You'd be wasting money — get a veterinary cleaning and discuss prescription dental diets instead.

## FAQ

**Q: How many treats should I give per day?**
A: Purina recommends 2-3 treats daily for an average 10-pound cat. Each treat is under 2 calories, so this won't meaningfully impact weight management.

**Q: Are these treats safe for kittens?**
A: The texture is too hard for kittens under 6 months whose adult teeth are still coming in. Wait until they're fully grown.

**Q: Do they actually replace brushing?**
A: No. They're a supplement, not a substitute. Think of them as a daily mouthwash — helpful, but not a replacement for professional care or brushing if your cat tolerates it.
