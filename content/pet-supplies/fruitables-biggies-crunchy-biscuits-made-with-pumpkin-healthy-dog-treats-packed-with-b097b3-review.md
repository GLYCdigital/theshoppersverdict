Let me be blunt: most "healthy" dog treats are marketing fluff wrapped in a misleading label. But Fruitables Biggies Crunchy Biscuits with Pumpkin actually live up to the claim — and with a 4.6/5 rating across 229 reviews, I'm not the only one convinced.

**What You're Actually Getting**

These are large-format biscuits (about 3 inches across) designed for medium and big dogs. The bag is straightforward — no flashy packaging, just a functional resealable pouch. Each biscuit comes in at 15 calories, which matters more than you'd think when you're training a 70-pound dog who needs multiple rewards per session. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: pumpkin, oats, and a few other whole-food components. No corn, no wheat, no by-products, no artificial colors.

**Real-World Performance**

The crunch factor is the standout feature here. These biscuits are genuinely hard — not the crumbly, falls-apart texture of cheaper treats. That density means two things: your dog actually has to chew (which buys you a few minutes of quiet), and the mechanical scraping action does help with dental hygiene. Several reviewers noted their vets commented on cleaner teeth after switching to these as their daily training treat.

The pumpkin flavor is mild but dogs seem to love it. One reviewer said, "My picky Golden Retriever spits out most treats. She devours these like they're steak." That sentiment echoes throughout the reviews — even finicky eaters tend to accept these.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.6-star rating isn't accidental. Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, the calorie count: owners of overweight dogs appreciate being able to train without blowing the diet. Second, the ingredient transparency — no hidden junk. Third, the size-to-value ratio: these biscuits are substantial, so a bag lasts noticeably longer than smaller treats.

**Where It Falls Short**

No product is perfect, and these have two honest drawbacks. The pumpkin fiber content can cause loose stools in dogs that aren't used to it. If your dog has a sensitive stomach, introduce these gradually rather than handing over a handful on day one. Second, the biscuits are brittle enough that shipping can break them. Multiple reviewers reported opening the bag to find a crumb pile instead of intact biscuits. The taste is unaffected, but if presentation matters to you, that's a real consideration.

**Who Should Buy These**

These biscuits are ideal for three specific buyer profiles:

1. **The weight-conscious owner** with a dog that needs to drop a few pounds but still needs training rewards
2. **The ingredient-label reader** who avoids by-products and artificial additives
3. **The large-dog owner** tired of buying tiny treats that disappear in one gulp and are gone in seconds

If you have a small dog, look elsewhere — these are genuinely too big for most breeds under 15 pounds.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can I break these into smaller pieces for a small dog?**
Yes, they snap cleanly, but honestly, you'd be better off buying the smaller Fruitables size. Breaking them works in a pinch, though.

**Q: How many can I give my dog per day?**
The bag recommends adjusting based on your dog's weight and activity level. At 15 calories each, five biscuits is roughly 75 calories — about 5% of a 50-pound dog's daily needs. That's a reasonable training budget.

**Q: Are these grain-free?**
No. They contain oats. If your dog needs grain-free for medical reasons, these aren't for you. If you're avoiding grains because of marketing hype, these are fine.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely healthy, low-calorie training treat that your dog will actually eat and that won't wreck your dog's waistline or your budget.

**Skip it if:** You have a small dog, your dog needs grain-free specifically, or you can't tolerate getting some broken biscuits in the bag.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — the broken-biscuit shipping issue and the fiber adjustment period keep it from a perfect score, but as a daily training treat, this is one of the best values on Amazon right now.
