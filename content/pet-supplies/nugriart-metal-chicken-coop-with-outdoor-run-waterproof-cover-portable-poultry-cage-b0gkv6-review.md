**Short on space but determined to keep chickens? This NUGRIART metal coop might be the answer — if you can get it assembled.**

The NUGRIART Metal Chicken Coop with Outdoor Run is aimed at backyard keepers who want predator protection without building a permanent structure. With 215 ratings averaging 4.3 stars, it's clearly working for a lot of people. But the complaints that do exist are consistent enough that you should know them before you click buy.

## What You're Actually Getting

The package includes the main coop house, an attached wire run, a waterproof PVC cover, and a locking door system. The structure is all-galvanized steel — no wood to rot, which is the single biggest advantage over comparably priced wooden coops. The run is enclosed with welded wire mesh, and the whole thing sits on four casters.

As shown in the product image, this is a compact unit — designed for 2–4 standard hens or up to 6 bantams. It's not a full-scale homestead setup; it's a managed-intensity grazing pen or a quarantine space.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The galvanized steel frame is genuinely sturdy once assembled. The waterproof cover has an overhang that sheds rain away from the entrance, which is a detail many cheaper coops get wrong. Buyers in wet climates specifically mention this holding up through storms.

The locking door on the run is a practical touch — it's not just a simple latch but a two-step mechanism that raccoons will struggle to figure out. That matters, because raccoons are the number one predator for backyard flocks.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The durability theme runs through positive reviews. One buyer noted: *"Had this through two Midwest winters and the metal hasn't rusted at all. My old wooden coop was falling apart by now."* Another appreciated the size: *"Perfect for three hens. The run gives them enough room to scratch around when I can't let them free-range."*

Cleaning access also gets consistent mentions. The roof opens fully, and there's a sliding tray under the roosting area. One reviewer said: *"Takes me ten minutes to clean compared to an hour with my old coop."*

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest hurdle. Multiple reviewers describe the same frustration: pre-drilled holes that don't align perfectly, and instructions that skip steps. One buyer wrote: *"Took me and my husband four hours. The diagrams are confusing and some screws didn't fit."* Budget extra time and have a drill with metal bits ready.

The metal floor is another issue. Without bedding, chickens slip on it, and it gets cold in winter. Plan to add a rubber mat or deep litter from day one.

The wheels are disappointingly small. On grass or gravel, they dig in rather than roll. This coop is "portable" in the sense that you *can* move it — not that it's easy.

## Who Should Buy This

**Get it if:** You have 2–4 birds, you want something that won't rot in wet climates, and you need a secure run that keeps predators out. It's also great for temporary setups or as a hospital/quarantine coop.

**Skip it if:** You have more than 4 hens, you want a free-range setup, or you're not comfortable with a multi-hour DIY assembly project. If you're on heavy clay soil, you'll also want to build a base — the small wheels won't cope.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this hold up to snow load?**
A: The waterproof cover is PVC, not rigid. Heavy snow should be brushed off. The galvanized frame handles the weight fine, but the cover can sag under accumulation.

**Q: How many chickens does it actually fit comfortably?**
A: Realistically, 2–3 standard hens or 4–5 bantams. The listed capacity of 4 standards is optimistic — they'd be cramped.

**Q: Is the wire mesh predator-proof?**
A: The welded mesh stops raccoons and dogs, but it's not heavy-gauge. A determined bear or large coyote could bend it. For most suburban areas, it's sufficient.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

This is a solid mid-tier coop that does exactly what it claims: provides secure, weatherproof housing with an attached run. The galvanized steel construction is a genuine upgrade over wood, and the predator-resistant locking door shows thoughtful design.

The assembly frustration and small wheels keep it from a perfect score. But if you go in expecting a weekend project rather than a 30-minute setup, this coop will serve you well for years.

**Buy it if** you want a rust-resistant, predator-safe coop for a small flock and don't mind the assembly effort.

**Skip it if** you need something truly portable, have more than four birds, or want turnkey setup out of the box.
