**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, because the catnip is exposed to air through the fabric (rather than sealed in a mylar pouch like single-use toys), the potency fades noticeably faster. You'll be refilling every 7–10 days for maximum effect, not every month. That's fine if you buy catnip in bulk, but it's an ongoing cost some shoppers don't anticipate.

Second, there's no odor-proof layer. The fabric absorbs the catnip oil permanently. After a few weeks, the toy smells like old catnip even when empty. That's not a dealbreaker — cats actually seem to like the lingering scent — but it's worth knowing if you're sensitive to smells.

Third, the velcro closure is the weak point. It's secure enough for normal play, but a determined cat can eventually work it open. One buyer mentioned, *"My cat figured out the velcro after two weeks and pulled all the nip out on the carpet."* If you have a particularly clever cat, you'll want to supervise or reinforce the closure.

**FAQ**

**Q: How much catnip do I add per refill?**
A: About a tablespoon is plenty. Overstuffing makes the toy too stiff to kick comfortably and wastes nip. Start with less — you can always add more.

**Q: Can I use fresh catnip from my garden?**
A: Yes, but dry it first. Fresh catnip has lower nepetalactone concentration and can go moldy inside the sealed pouch.

**Q: Are these safe for aggressive chewers?**
A: The fabric resists puncturing, but no fabric toy is indestructible. If your cat shreds toys regularly, inspect the seams weekly and discard at the first sign of damage to prevent ingestion.
