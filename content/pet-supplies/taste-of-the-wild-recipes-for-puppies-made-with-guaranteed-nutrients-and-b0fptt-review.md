# Taste of the Wild Recipes for Puppies, Made with Guaranteed

**A high-protein, novel-protein puppy food that actually walks the walk on ingredient quality — but it's not for every pup.**

You've seen the bags at the pet store, the one with the wolf on the front promising "ancestral diet" nutrition. Taste of the Wild has built a loyal following on the premise that dogs deserve meat-first, grain-conscious food without the boutique price tag. This puppy recipe — featuring roasted bison and venison — is their entry point for young dogs. After digging through 229 ratings and the full ingredient panel, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The bag delivers a 5-pound or 14-pound (depending on your choice) kibble formulated for puppies up to 12-18 months, and pregnant or nursing mothers. The guaranteed analysis shows **32% crude protein and 20% fat** — that's a solidly high-protein ratio for growing muscles and sustained energy. The first three ingredients are roasted bison, lamb meal, and venison meal. No chicken by-product meal, no corn, no wheat, no soy. That's a genuinely clean opening lineup.

As shown in the product image, the kibble pieces are small and round — roughly a quarter-inch in diameter. That matters more than most new puppy owners realize. Large-breed kibble can be a choking hazard or simply too tough for a 10-week-old teething jaw. This size works for Chihuahuas and Labs alike.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The star feature here is the guaranteed probiotics — specifically *Bacillus coagulans* — which is a hardy strain that survives stomach acid and actually reaches the gut. For puppies transitioning from mother's milk to solid food, digestive upset is the #1 complaint I see in puppy food reviews. This formula is designed to head that off at the pass.

Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids are also included at meaningful levels — DHA for brain development, EPA for joint health. For a breed like a Golden Retriever or German Shepherd prone to hip dysplasia, starting with this kind of nutritional support early is a legitimate advantage.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star average isn't inflated. Here's what keeps coming up in the feedback:

> "My GSD puppy has a sensitive stomach and we tried four other brands before this one. No more loose stools, and his coat is noticeably softer within two weeks."

That's a recurring theme — owners of breeds with notoriously finicky digestion (German Shepherds, French Bulldogs, Labs) reporting that the probiotic blend actually does what it promises.

Another frequent compliment is the value equation: "This is half the price of the vet-recommended brand and my pup eats it more eagerly." Taste of the Wild typically lands in the mid-range on price per pound, but the novel proteins (bison, venison) are usually a premium upcharge with other brands. You're getting unusual meat sources at a mainstream price point.

## Where It Falls Short

Not every puppy is a bison fan. The gamey flavor profile is distinctive — I've seen multiple reviews where owners note their puppy refused the first few meals. If your pup is already a picky eater, this may take a slow transition (mixing 25% new food with old over a week) or it may be a flat-out no-go.

There's also no grain-free version of this puppy recipe. If your vet has recommended grain-free for allergies, you'll need to look at Taste of the Wild's adult line or another brand entirely. And practical gripe: the bag lacks a resealable zipper. That's a small thing, but when you're scooping kibble daily, it's genuinely annoying.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The budget-conscious owner who wants premium ingredients** — you're getting bison and venison without paying boutique prices.
- **Owners of large-breed puppies** — the joint and brain support is worth the investment early.
- **Anyone dealing with puppy diarrhea** — the probiotics are the real deal for settling stomachs.

Skip it if your puppy is already a picky eater with a preference for chicken, or if your vet has specifically advised grain-free.

## FAQ: 3 Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I feed this to my adult dog?**
A: The formula is calorie-dense and higher in fat than adult maintenance food. For a healthy adult, you'd need to reduce portions significantly. It's not formulated for all-life-stages, so stick to puppy-specific food until 12-18 months.

**Q: Is the bison and venison sourced in the USA?**
A: Taste of the Wild states ingredients are sourced globally with final manufacturing in the USA. They don't disclose exact sourcing locations per batch, which is standard for the industry but worth knowing if you're strict about domestic-only sourcing.

**Q: How fast should I transition my puppy onto this?**
A: Over 7-10 days. Start with 25% new food, increase by 25% every 2-3 days. The probiotics help, but a sudden switch can still cause loose stools.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a high-protein, novel-protein puppy food with proven digestive support at a fair price, and your pup isn't picky about gamey flavors.

**Skip it if** your puppy has a known sensitivity to bison/venison, you need grain-free, or you're on an ultra-tight budget (this isn't the cheapest bag on the shelf).

**Rating: 4.6/5** — This is a genuinely solid puppy food that delivers on its ingredient promises. The picky-eater risk and lack of a resealable bag are the only reasons it doesn't hit 5 stars. For most families raising a healthy, energetic puppy, this is a smart choice.
