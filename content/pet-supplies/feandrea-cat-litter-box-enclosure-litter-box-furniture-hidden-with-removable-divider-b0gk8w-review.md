If you're tired of looking at a plastic tub full of clumping litter every time you walk into your living room, the Feandrea Cat Litter Box Enclosure is the kind of product that makes you wonder why you didn't buy it sooner. But before you hit "Add to Cart," let's be clear about what this piece of furniture does — and doesn't — do well.

**What you're actually getting**

This isn't a flimsy fabric tent. The Feandrea enclosure is built from engineered wood with a smooth, matte finish that genuinely looks like a mid-century sideboard from across the room. As shown in the product image, it features a clean rectangular design with a single side-entry hole and a flip-top lid for easy access. The whole unit measures roughly 23.6" L x 15.7" W x 19.7" H — compact enough for an apartment corner but substantial enough to pass as real furniture.

The standout feature is the **removable divider**. You can run it as one open interior space for a large cat, or insert the divider to create two stacked compartments — one for the litter tray and one for supplies. For multi-cat households, this flexibility is genuinely useful, though you'll need two standard-size litter boxes (not jumbo ones) to make the double setup work.

**Performance and real-world use**

The top surface holds up to 110 pounds, which means you can actually set a lamp, books, or a small plant on it without worrying about collapse. The MDF surface is sealed and smooth — cat urine and litter dust wipe off without soaking in. That's a significant upgrade over cheaper particle-board enclosures that swell and warp after a few months of use.

The flip-top lid is hinged and stays open on its own, which makes scooping far less awkward than reaching through a front door. One buyer noted, *"I can scoop without moving the whole unit. The lid stays up, and I'm not crouching on the floor like a weirdo."* Another praised the odor control: *"I was skeptical, but the charcoal filter in the back actually does cut down on the smell. Not completely, but noticeably."*

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the assembly. This is not a tool-free build. You'll need a Phillips screwdriver, and plan on 30–40 minutes of your evening. The instructions are clear but the cam locks require a firm hand — several reviewers mentioned stripped screws if you overtighten.

The side entry hole is fixed on one side only. You cannot reverse the door panel to suit your room layout, which limits placement options. Also, the interior height is about 16 inches — a tall, extra-large hooded litter box won't fit inside. You'll need a standard open tray or a low-sided box.

Finally, the price point sits higher than basic litter box covers. You're paying for the furniture look, and if that's not a priority, you can solve the problem for $30 less with a neoprene tent.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers** who need to hide the litter box in a shared living space — this looks like furniture, not a cat toilet.
- **Multi-cat households** that want the double-stack option for small spaces.
- **Design-conscious owners** who are tired of plastic domes clashing with their decor.

**Skip it if** you have a large breed cat (Maine Coon, ragdoll) that needs a jumbo box, or if you want tool-free assembly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it really control odor?**
A: The included charcoal filter helps, but it's not magic. You'll still need to scoop daily. The enclosure does contain the smell far better than an open tray, but it won't eliminate it entirely.

**Q: Will my cat actually use it?**
A: Most cats adapt within a few days. Keep the interior spacious (remove the divider for one cat) and leave the entry flap open initially. Some cats dislike enclosed spaces, so have a backup plan if yours is skittish.

**Q: Can I fit a top-entry litter box inside?**
A: No. The interior height is roughly 16 inches, so a top-entry box won't clear the lid. Stick with a standard open tray.

**The Verdict**

The Feandrea Cat Litter Box Enclosure earns a solid **4.2 out of 5**. It's genuinely well-made, versatile, and attractive enough to pass as real furniture. The assembly hassle and the fixed side-entry orientation are the main compromises.

**Buy it if** you want to hide the litter box in a living space and you're willing to spend 40 minutes with a screwdriver.

**Skip it if** you have a giant breed or need a tool-free setup.

For what it costs, you're getting a durable, functional piece that solves a real problem — not another piece of disposable pet junk.
