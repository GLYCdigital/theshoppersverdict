**Bottom Line Up Front:** The YITAHOME 62-inch flight cage is a genuinely spacious home for cockatiels, conures, and similarly sized birds — but it's not the universal cage the marketing suggests. Here's who should buy it, who should skip it, and what 225 buyers are actually saying.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the cage body, a rolling stand with four locking casters, three plastic food/water cups, two wooden perches, and a removable metal grate and tray. As shown in the product image, the cage is tall and rectangular — roughly 62 inches high, about 30 inches wide, and 20 inches deep. That's enough room for a pair of cockatiels to flap without clipping wing feathers, which is the whole point of a flight cage.

The metal is powder-coated, not painted, which matters. Powder coating resists chipping when birds chew on bars — and they will chew. The wire is sturdy enough that a determined conure won't bend it, though it's not parrot-proof for macaws or large cockatoos.

## Real-World Performance

The standout feature is the door configuration. Four large front doors swing open fully, plus there's a top opening. That means you can reach any corner of the cage without contorting your arm — something smaller cages make you do constantly. One buyer noted, "The doors are huge, which makes cleaning and getting my conure out so much easier than my old cage." That sentiment appears repeatedly in the reviews.

The rolling stand is a genuine upgrade over fixed-leg cages. Locking casters hold the cage steady, but when unlocked, you can wheel it to a sunny window or different room without dismantling anything. Given the cage's weight (about 40 pounds assembled), this is practical, not gimmicky.

The removable tray and grate simplify cleaning — you slide them out, scrape, and slide back. No wrestling with the whole cage. Buyers consistently praise this: "Tray slides out smoothly and is easy to wipe down. Takes me 10 minutes to clean now versus half an hour before."

## What Buyers Praise

Across 225 ratings (4.5 stars average), the recurring positives are:
- **Genuine flight space** — "My cockatiel actually flies across the cage now instead of hopping perch to perch"

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the bar spacing. At roughly half an inch, this cage is **not safe for budgies, finches, canaries, or lovebirds**. They can squeeze through or get their heads stuck. Multiple reviews mention this as a reason for returns or exchanges. If you own small birds, this is a hard pass.

The included plastic food cups are the weakest component. Several buyers note the clips don't grip the bars securely, and cups pop off when birds land on them. Plan to budget $15-25 for metal cups or stainless bowls with proper mounting hardware.

Assembly is doable but not fun. One reviewer said, "Took my husband and I about an hour. The instructions are mostly pictures, and a couple of steps were confusing until we figured them out." Not a dealbreaker, but set aside time and have a second pair of hands.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you own cockatiels, conures, quakers, or similar small-to-medium parrots and want them to have actual flight space. It's also a strong choice for owners who've struggled with hard-to-clean cages — the tray and door design genuinely simplify daily maintenance.

**Skip it if** you have budgies, finches, or lovebirds — bar spacing is a safety risk. Also skip if you need a cage for a large parrot like an African grey or amazon; this isn't built for their beak strength.

## FAQ

**Is the bar spacing safe for budgies?**
No. At roughly 0.5 inches, budgies can squeeze through or trap their heads. This cage is designed for medium-sized birds like cockatiels and conures, not small birds.

**Can one person assemble this alone?**
Technically yes, but it's frustrating. The cage panels are large and awkward to align solo. A second person makes it a 45-minute job instead of a frustrating hour-plus.

**Does the rolling stand actually hold steady?**
Yes, when the casters are locked. Unlocked, the cage moves smoothly on hard floors. On thick carpet, the wheels can drag slightly — nothing that prevents movement, but it's worth knowing.
