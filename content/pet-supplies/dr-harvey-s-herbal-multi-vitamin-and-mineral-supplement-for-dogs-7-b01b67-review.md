# Dr. Harvey's Herbal Multi-Vitamin: Does Your Dog Actually Need This Green Powder?

Let's be direct: most dog vitamins on Amazon are overpriced pee. Synthetic vitamins in a chewable that get absorbed at maybe 20% efficiency. Dr. Harvey's is different — and that difference is exactly why it has 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars. But different doesn't automatically mean right for your dog. Let's break down what's actually in this green powder and whether it earns a spot in your feeding routine.

## What You're Actually Getting

The product image shows a straightforward 12-ounce plastic jar filled with a fine, dark-green powder. You're not getting a scoop in the lid — you'll want to keep a measuring spoon handy. The recommended serving is 1 scoop (about 5 grams) per 50 pounds of body weight, mixed into food once daily.

The ingredient list is where this supplement separates itself from the pack. You're looking at a whole-food base of kelp, alfalfa, dandelion, parsley, and ginger — plus chelated minerals like zinc and iron that bind to amino acids for better absorption. No artificial colors, no corn syrup, no mystery "meat meal." It's human-grade, which matters because the FDA doesn't regulate pet supplements — you're trusting the company's sourcing standards.

## Real-World Performance

The most consistent feedback across buyer reviews centers on three improvements: coat quality, energy, and digestion. One verified purchaser noted, *"My 9-year-old lab's coat went from dull and flaky to shiny within three weeks. She also seems to have more spring in her step on our morning walks."* Another long-term user reported, *"I've used this for two years with my German Shepherd. Her digestion issues cleared up and she stopped eating grass constantly."*

The powder mixes cleanly into wet food and even dissolves reasonably well into kibble that's been lightly moistened. It doesn't clump the way some rice-bran-based supplements do.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the smell. This is an herbal product, and it smells like it — earthy, slightly bitter, with a noticeable kelp note. Most dogs don't care once it's mixed into food, but some picky eaters will turn their noses up at first. One reviewer wrote, *"My Shih Tzu refused to eat for two days. I had to mix it with canned pumpkin and warm water to get her to accept it."* If you have a notoriously finicky eater, budget for a transition period.

There's also a practical issue for small-dog households. The serving size is small — a 15-pound dog needs less than a third of a scoop daily. At that rate, a 12-ounce jar lasts several months. The herbal ingredients do lose potency over time, and the manufacturer recommends using within 90 days of opening. You may end up tossing a partially-used jar.

## Who Should Buy This

Dr. Harvey's makes sense for three specific buyer profiles:

- **The whole-food purist** — someone feeding fresh or raw food who wants to fill nutritional gaps without synthetic additives.
- **The senior dog owner** — dogs 7+ years old often show the most noticeable coat and mobility improvements.
- **The "tried everything" owner** — if your dog has chronic skin issues or dull coat that hasn't responded to other supplements.

Skip it if you're on a tight budget, have a dog with known allergies to kelp or seaweed, or need a quick "sprinkle and done" solution for a picky eater.

## FAQ

**Can I give this to a puppy?**
The manufacturer recommends it for dogs 6 months and older. For puppies, consult your vet first — their calcium-to-phosphorus ratios need careful management during growth.

**Does it replace a complete diet?**
No. This is a supplement, not a meal replacement. It fills micronutrient gaps but doesn't provide protein, fat, or calories.

**How long until I see results?**
Most buyers report visible coat changes in 2–4 weeks. Energy and digestion improvements typically show in 4–6 weeks.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a whole-food, human-grade multivitamin with proven results for coat and energy — and you're willing to commit to daily consistency.

**Skip it if** your dog is a picky eater, you have a tiny breed that won't finish the jar before expiration, or you're looking for a chewable convenience format.

At 4.6/5, Dr. Harvey's earns its rating through ingredient transparency and measurable results. It's not the cheapest option, but for dogs that respond well, it's one of the most effective whole-food supplements on the market.
