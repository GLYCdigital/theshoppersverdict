## What You're Actually Getting

This is a one-piece, high-sided stainless steel pan — no hood, no filter, no frills. The dimensions are generous for a single-cat household or a small multi-cat setup. The high sides are the standout design feature, running roughly 8 to 9 inches tall. That's enough to catch most litter scatter from enthusiastic diggers and redirect male cats who like to spray.

You get the pan, and that's it. No scoop, no mat, no liner. That's fine — you already own those. What matters is that the stainless steel is thick enough not to flex or warp under a full load of litter. This isn't a flimsy sheet-metal tray; it has actual structural weight to it.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The core advantage here is material science. Stainless steel is non-porous. Urine doesn't soak into the surface, so odors don't linger the way they do with plastic. One reviewer put it plainly: "I replaced a plastic box that smelled no matter what I did. This steel box doesn't hold any smell at all. A quick rinse and it's like new." That matches the broader consensus in the ratings — odor control is the #1 reason buyers switched.

Cleaning is genuinely easier. You can use hot water, bleach solutions, or enzyme cleaners without worrying about degrading the material. Plastic boxes eventually get scratched and stained; this won't. For people with multiple cats, that's a meaningful upgrade in hygiene.

The high sides do their job. Multiple reviewers mention owning "kickers" or "diggers" — cats that launch litter across the room. The tall walls catch most of it. That said, if you have a cat that backs up to the edge and pees over the rim, no box fixes that. This one minimizes it, but doesn't eliminate it.

## Where It Falls Short

This isn't a perfect product, and you should know the tradeoffs before buying.

**It's heavy.** With a full 15-20 pounds of litter, moving this box for a complete change is a two-hand job. The lack of handles or rim grips makes it worse. You're basically gripping smooth steel edges. If you have arthritis or back issues, this will be a genuine annoyance.

**It's cold and loud.** Metal conducts temperature. In a cool room, the surface is noticeably cold on a cat's paws. Some cats don't care; some do. Similarly, digging sounds louder than on plastic. One reviewer noted their cat was "suspicious of the new surface" for a few days before adjusting.

**No splash guard for the back.** The sides are high, but if your cat is a "high pee-er" who aims over the back edge, you'll still get mess on the wall. A hooded box or a taller back panel would solve this, but this pan doesn't have one.
