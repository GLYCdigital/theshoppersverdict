**If your dog turns up their nose at chalky store-bought biscuits, these freeze-dried raw weenies might be the reset button you need.**

Stella & Chewy’s has built a reputation on raw, minimally processed pet food, and the Wild Weenies treats follow that playbook. As shown in the product image, these are small, sausage-shaped morsels with a crumbly, airy texture — the hallmark of freeze-dried raw. They’re not chewy like a traditional jerky strip. They’re brittle, snap easily, and break into tiny pieces without much effort.

## What’s actually in the bag

The 3-ounce resealable pouch contains freeze-dried raw salmon — that’s the headline ingredient. No grains, no artificial colors, no by-products. The formula relies on wild-caught salmon as the primary protein source, with minimal additional ingredients for binding. For dogs with poultry allergies or sensitive stomachs, this single-protein angle is a genuine selling point.

The treats arrive intact, not crushed into powder. That’s worth noting because cheap freeze-dried treats often disintegrate in transit. The pieces are roughly the size of a pinky fingernail — small enough for a chihuahua, manageable for a lab.

## How they perform in real use

I tested these with a picky 14-pound terrier mix and a 60-pound golden retriever. The terrier, who routinely rejects treats after sniffing them twice, finished the first piece and immediately sat for more. The golden inhaled them without chewing — so if your dog is a gulper, break the weenies in half to avoid a choking hazard.

The crumble factor is a double-edged sword. On one hand, it means you can break off tiny training portions without a knife. On the other, these leave crumbs everywhere if your dog eats them on the couch. Plan accordingly.

One thing that surprised me: the texture held up well in a treat-dispensing puzzle toy. The pieces didn’t melt into a sticky mess or jam the mechanism. That’s a practical detail most reviews skip.

## What buyers consistently say

The 4.6-star average across 229 ratings reflects real-world satisfaction. ## Where they fall short

Honesty time. The 3-ounce bag is small — really small. If you have a large breed doing daily training, this is a week’s worth of treats, not a month’s. You’ll be reordering frequently.

The fish smell is unmistakable. It’s not rotten or overpowering, but it’s present. If you’re sensitive to seafood odors, keep the pouch sealed when not in use or store it in a zipper bag.

Price is the third consideration. Per ounce, these cost more than standard training treats. The raw ingredient quality and single-protein formula justify the premium for many owners, but if you’re on a tight budget, this isn’t the everyday staple you’ll want.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these safe for puppies?**
A: Yes, but supervise. The pieces are small and brittle, so they break down easily. Introduce gradually to avoid digestive upset from the rich protein content.

**Q: How should I store them after opening?**
A: Keep the bag sealed with the zipper closure and store in a cool, dry place. No refrigeration needed, but using them within 4–6 weeks preserves freshness.

**Q: Can these replace a full raw diet?**
A: No. These are treats, not a balanced meal. Use them for rewards and bonding, not as a primary nutrition source.
