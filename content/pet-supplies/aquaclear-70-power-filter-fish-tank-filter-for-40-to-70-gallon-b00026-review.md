If you've kept fish for more than a month, you've heard the name AquaClear. The 70 model is the middle child of the lineup — rated for 40- to 70-gallon tanks — and it's been a staple in the hobby for decades. But does it still hold up against modern canister filters and cheap hang-on-backs? I've spent time with this unit, and I've combed through buyer feedback across 215 Amazon ratings. Here's what you actually need to know.

## What's in the box

You get the filter body, the motorhead, a media basket, one blue foam pad, a bag of BioMax (biological media), a fine filter pad, and the intake tube with strainer. Notably absent: any activated carbon. That's a deliberate choice by AquaClear — they expect you to buy their proprietary carbon packets separately, which is a minor annoyance but not a dealbreaker. The unit measures roughly 12 inches long and 7 inches tall, so it will hang over the back rim of most tanks without blocking the lid.

## Real-world performance

The AquaClear's claim to fame is its vertical media basket. Water flows up through the foam, then the chemical layer, then the biological media, before spilling back into the tank. This is genuinely better than the horizontal cartridge systems on most HOB filters — it keeps oxygenated water moving through the bacteria colony, which is why this filter is so good at establishing and maintaining a cycle. The foam is coarse and reusable; a rinse in old tank water every few weeks brings it back to life. Owners consistently report crystal-clear water within days of installation. One reviewer with a 60-gallon cichlid tank wrote: *"I was skeptical because it's not a canister, but my nitrates dropped and the water is the clearest it's been in years."*

The adjustable flow control is a genuine selling point. Turn it down for a betta or a community tank with long-finned fish; crank it up for goldfish or African cichlids. The self-priming motor also means you never need to suction-start the siphon — plug it in, and it figures itself out. That's a small convenience that saves real frustration.

## What buyers consistently praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is reliability and low maintenance. The reusable foam means you're not shelling out for replacement cartridges every month — a cost that sneaks up on you with cheaper filters. The motor is quiet, and the flow is strong enough to create surface agitation for gas exchange. Multiple reviewers noted it's survived power outages and years of continuous use without skipping a beat. One long-term owner said: *"I've had mine running for six years across three different tanks. Only replaced the impeller once. It just works."*

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the flaws. First, the media basket seating is finicky. If you don't push it down until you hear a click, the filter will hum loudly and the flow will drop to a trickle. This is the #1 complaint in negative reviews, and it's easy to miss if you're rushing. Second, the included BioMax is a small packet — you'll want to add more for a well-stocked tank. Third, on the upper end of its rating (70 gallons), this filter is underpowered for anything more than light stocking. Most hobbyists with 75-gallon tanks run two of these units or step up to the 110. The flow rate is rated at 300 GPH, but that's before media resistance; real-world output is closer to 200 GPH.

## Who should buy this

- **Hobbyists with 40–55 gallon community tanks** — perfect match, plenty of flow, easy maintenance.
- **Breeders or quarantine tanks** — the reusable media and self-priming design make it ideal for quick setup.
- **Anyone tired of replacing cartridges** — the foam pays for itself within a year compared to cartridge-based HOBs.
- **Skip it if** you have a heavily stocked 70-gallon tank or need silent operation in a bedroom (the hum is minor but present).

## FAQ

**Q: Does this filter come with carbon?**  
No. You'll need to buy AquaClear's activated carbon insert separately, or use your own media in the basket. The basket is flexible — you can add filter floss, Purigen, or ceramic rings.

**Q: Can I use it on a tank smaller than 40 gallons?**  
Technically yes, but the flow is strong. Use the adjustable flow dial to lower it, and consider it only for 20-gallon tanks or larger with fish that tolerate current.

**Q: How often do I need to clean the foam?**  
Every 3–4 weeks. Rinse it in a bucket of old tank water — never tap water, as chlorine kills the beneficial bacteria. Replace the foam only when it starts to fall apart, which is typically 1–2 years.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a durable, low-cost-per-year filter that will keep your water clear and your fish healthy in a 40–55 gallon setup. **Skip it if** you have a 70-gallon tank with a heavy bioload, or if you want truly silent operation — in both cases, look at a canister filter or the AquaClear 110.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, proven workhorse with a couple of quirks that don't outweigh its long-term value. For most hobbyists, this is the best HOB filter you can buy at this price point.
