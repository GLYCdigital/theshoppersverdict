Let's be direct about pet fountains: most are plastic, and plastic is the problem. It scratches, harbors bacteria in those microscopic grooves, and leaches a taste many cats flat-out reject. That's why the iPettie Tritone stands out — it's actual fired ceramic, not a plastic shell pretending to be stone.

As shown in the product image, this is a three-tier waterfall design. Water cascades from the top basin down through two intermediate levels into the 84-ounce reservoir. That's nearly a full gallon, which for a single cat means refilling every three to four days. For two or three cats, you're still only filling twice a week.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the ceramic fountain body (three stacking tiers), the submersible pump with adjustable flow control, two replacement filters, a length of tubing, and the pump's AC adapter. The pump sits in the bottom reservoir, hidden from view. Assembly is intuitive — tiers stack without tools, and the water flows by gravity plus the pump's lift.

The ceramic finish is smooth and glazed, which is the entire point. Unlike plastic, it won't develop that slippery biofilm as quickly, and when it does need cleaning, you can scrub it with hot water and mild soap without worrying about scratching the surface. No dishwasher though — hand wash only.

## Real-World Performance

The pump is rated under 30dB, and in practice, it's genuinely quiet. Owners consistently mention the lack of motor hum. One reviewer put it plainly: "My cat drinks from it constantly now, and I can't hear it from the next room. My old plastic fountain sounded like a fish tank." The adjustable flow knob lets you dial down the waterfall to a gentle trickle if your cat prefers less splashing.

Filtration uses a dual-layer system — a foam pre-filter catches hair and debris, and a carbon layer handles odor and taste. It works, but here's the honest tradeoff: the filters are iPettie-specific. Third-party alternatives exist but vary wildly in fit. Budget for the official replacements roughly every three to four weeks.

## What Buyers Praise

Across 225 ratings at 4.5 stars, the dominant theme is cats switching from plastic to ceramic. Multiple owners report their finicky felines suddenly drinking more water — a meaningful win for urinary health. The weight of the ceramic base also earns consistent praise; it doesn't slide across the floor when a 15-pound cat leans on it. The 84-ounce capacity gets specific callouts from multi-pet households who appreciate not refilling twice daily.

## Where It Falls Short

The ceramic body is heavy — about six pounds empty, closer to ten with water. That's a feature for stability but a genuine pain when you're lugging it to the sink for the weekly deep clean. More importantly, it's fragile. Ceramic that survives shipping can still crack if you're clumsy during washing. One reviewer noted, "I've broken two of these cleaning them. It's my fault, but it's also an expensive mistake."

The three-tier design also means more surfaces to clean. Each tier needs wiping down, and the pump housing requires regular disassembly to prevent fur buildup. It's not a five-minute chore. If you want a fountain you can rinse and ignore, this isn't it — this demands proper maintenance.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the owner who's already tried plastic fountains and watched their cat refuse to drink. It's for households with multiple cats who need the capacity, and for anyone concerned about long-term plastic exposure in their pet's water. It's also a solid choice for light sleepers — the quiet pump won't keep you up.

Skip it if you're looking for a low-maintenance gadget you can toss in the dishwasher, or if your cat is a notorious knocker-over of heavy objects. And if you're on a tight budget, factor in the ongoing filter cost.

## FAQ

**Is the pump actually quiet enough for a bedroom?**
Yes. The 30dB rating is accurate — it's quieter than most phone chargers. The water trickle is the only audible sound, and the flow adjuster can drop that to near-silence.

**How often do I really need to clean it?**
Weekly is the honest answer. The carbon filter needs replacing every 3–4 weeks, but the ceramic tiers and pump need a proper wipe-down weekly. Skipping this leads to the slimy buildup that defeats the ceramic advantage.

**Will this work for small dogs too?**
Yes, though the top tier is shallow. Brachycephalic breeds (pugs, bulldogs) may find the top basin awkward but can drink from the lower tiers easily.
