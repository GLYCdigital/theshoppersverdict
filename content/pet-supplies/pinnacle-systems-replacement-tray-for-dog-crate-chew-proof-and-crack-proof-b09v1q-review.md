## What You Actually Get

The box contains a single molded plastic tray — no hardware, no assembly. It's designed as a direct replacement for the standard pans that ship with Midwest, Petmate, and similar wire crates. The key differentiator is the material: a high-density polyethylene blend with reinforced ribbing along the underside. The rim is noticeably thicker than the flimsy trays that come stock with most crates — roughly double the wall thickness at the edges where chewers usually attack first.

Dimensions vary by crate size, so measure your crate floor before ordering. The fit is snug, not loose — it sits flush with no gaps for paws or noses to wedge under.

## Real-World Performance

The "Chew-Pro" name isn't just branding. The reinforced rim is the first line of defense, and the underside ribbing adds structural rigidity that prevents the tray from flexing and cracking when a determined dog works on it. The surface is smooth and non-porous, which means no trapped odors and no scratches that harbor bacteria.

For cleaning, this is the easiest tray I've seen. A quick hose-down or wipe with a disinfectant spray handles most messes. It's also compatible with crate pads and liners — the smooth surface doesn't create uneven pressure points that would make bedding lumpy.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint is the fit issue. This is not a universal tray. If your crate isn't a standard Midwest or Petmate model, you'll need to measure carefully — and even then, some buyers reported the tray being slightly smaller than their crate floor, leaving a gap that collects debris.

The glossy surface is slippery. Older dogs with hip issues or puppies still finding their footing may struggle to stand comfortably. A textured surface or non-slip backing would have been a nice touch, though it's not a dealbreaker for most.

Finally, the price. This runs notably higher than basic replacement trays — typically 30-50% more. You're paying for the durability, and it's worth it if you're replacing trays every few months. But if your dog isn't a chewer, a cheaper basic tray does the job.
