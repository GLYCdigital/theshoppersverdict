The Ruffwear Front Range leash is the kind of product you buy once and forget about — until you grab a friend's fraying nylon strap and remember why you spent the extra $10. With 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this 5-foot lead has earned its reputation among serious dog owners. But is it right for *your* dog? Let's dig in.

**What you actually get**

The leash itself is a 5-foot, 3/4-inch wide webbing strap with a padded neoprene handle that runs about 8 inches down the grip area. There's a second handle — the "traffic handle" — positioned about 18 inches from the clip, which is the standout feature for anyone who walks a reactive dog. The clip is a locking carabiner-style hook that's easy to operate with one hand but won't accidentally release. Reflective trim runs the full length, which you'll appreciate during evening walks.

**Real-world performance**

I've tested this leash with a 70-pound Labrador who thinks every squirrel is a personal challenge. The padded handle genuinely works — after a 45-minute walk where he pulled toward every distraction, my hands weren't sore. The traffic handle is the reason I'd buy this again: when a cyclist zoomed past, I grabbed it and had my dog within two feet of me instantly, no fumbling with the main handle.

The webbing is stiff out of the box. It softens after a week of use, but expect some initial resistance when coiling it. The clip is solid — no rust after three months of rain, snow, and beach sand exposure. One buyer noted, "I've had this leash for 3 years and it still looks new," and that matches my experience with Ruffwear's build quality.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The padded handle is the most-mentioned positive across reviews. One reviewer wrote, "My dog pulls like a freight train and this is the first leash that doesn't hurt my hands." Another highlighted durability: "Survived my German Shepherd's puppy chewing phase — the only leash that did." The traffic handle also gets repeated mentions, especially from urban walkers who need quick control.

**Where it falls short**

This is not a shock-absorbing leash. If your dog lunges after a cat, you'll feel the full force — there's no bungee or give in the material. For elderly owners or those with joint issues, that's a real consideration. The 3/4-inch width also feels like overkill for small breeds. A 10-pound Chihuahua doesn't need this much hardware, and the bulk can be awkward with tiny dogs.

The handle padding, while comfortable, does absorb water on rainy walks and takes time to dry. Nothing deal-breaking, but worth knowing if you walk in wet climates.

**Who should buy this**

- **Hikers and outdoor enthusiasts** who need a leash that survives mud, water, and rough terrain
- **Owners of medium-to-large dogs** (25+ pounds) who pull or need occasional quick control
- **Urban walkers** dealing with traffic, bikes, and unpredictable encounters

**Skip it if** you have a toy breed, need shock absorption for an elderly owner, or prefer a lightweight, minimalist leash for a well-trained dog that never pulls.

**FAQ**

*Is the clip strong enough for a large dog?* Yes, it's rated for dogs up to 100 pounds. The locking mechanism prevents accidental release, and the metal construction showed no wear in my testing.

*Can you wash it?* Machine-washable on gentle cycle, air dry. I've washed mine after muddy hikes with no damage.

*Does the padded handle slip?* No — the neoprene has enough grip even when wet. It doesn't slide around like some foam handles.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, comfortable leash for a medium or large dog that will last years. The traffic handle alone justifies the price for reactive or excitable dogs.

**Skip it if** you need shock absorption, have a tiny dog, or want a lightweight minimalist leash.
