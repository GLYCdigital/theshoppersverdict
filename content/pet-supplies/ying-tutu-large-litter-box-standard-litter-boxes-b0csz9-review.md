Let's skip the fluff. You're here because you're tired of scrubbing urine off your bathroom floor or watching your 18-pound Maine Coon squeeze into a box meant for a Chihuahua. The Ying Tutu Large Litter Box is a serious contender, but it's not perfect. Here's what 229 buyers (and the physics of cat behavior) actually tell us.

## What You Actually Get

The box arrives fully assembled — no screwdriver required, no panic-inducing instruction sheet. As shown in the product image, it's a one-piece, open-top plastic tray with a notably high back wall and a lower front lip. Dimensions run roughly 22.5 inches long by 17.5 inches wide, with the back wall standing about 6.5 inches tall. That's not "jumbo" territory, but it's comfortably larger than the standard 18-inch boxes that most pet stores push.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The plastic is smooth, thick enough to resist cracking, and — critically — non-porous. That last point matters more than most shoppers realize. Porous plastic absorbs ammonia odors permanently; this one doesn't. Multiple reviewers noted that a simple wipe-down with warm soapy water takes care of even week-old clumps.

The geometry is the real selling point, though. Cats that prefer to back up against a wall while peeing (common in males and seniors) get full coverage from that tall rear panel. The front lip sits low enough that a 12-week-old kitten or a cat with arthritis can step in without launching themselves like a gymnast. One reviewer put it plainly: *"My 15-year-old cat with hip problems can finally get in and out without yowling. That alone is worth the price."*

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. There's no lid, no rim guard, and no anti-scatter shield. If your cat is a digger — the type that excavates like it's hunting buried treasure — you'll still find litter granules on the floor. The smooth walls mean there's no texture to slow down kicking.

Also, the side walls are moderately flexible. If you overload it with dense, heavy clay litter, the base can bow slightly over time. It won't crack, but it's a reminder that this is a well-made standard box, not a fortress. Use a lightweight clumping litter and you'll never notice.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Senior cat owners**: The low entry is a genuine game-changer for cats with arthritis or mobility issues.
- **Owners of male cats**: That tall back wall is a lifesaver against urine spray.
- **Budget-conscious shoppers**: This undercuts most "jumbo" boxes by a significant margin while delivering comparable protection.

**Skip it if** you have a cat that launches litter like a toddler in a sandbox, or if you need a covered box for privacy. This is an open tray, period.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this box big enough for a Maine Coon or a 20-pound cat?**
A: Yes, for most. The 22.5-inch length gives a large cat room to turn around and dig. If your cat is genuinely gigantic (25+ pounds), you'll want a dedicated jumbo box, but for the average large cat, this fits well.

**Q: Does the plastic stain or hold onto odors?**
A: No. The non-porous surface resists staining, and a regular wipe-down with a mild cleaner keeps it odor-free. Avoid abrasive scrubbers, which can scratch the surface and create hiding spots for bacteria.

**Q: Can this be used with a litter mat?**
A: Absolutely. The low front lip sits flush against most mats, which helps catch tracking litter before it spreads across your floors.
