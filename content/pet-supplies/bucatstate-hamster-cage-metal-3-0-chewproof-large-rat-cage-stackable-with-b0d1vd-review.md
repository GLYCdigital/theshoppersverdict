Let's be blunt: most "chewproof" cages are a marketing lie. A determined Syrian hamster can gnaw through thin galvanized wire in a night, and cheap plastic connectors crack within a month. The BUCATSTATE Metal 3.0 is one of the few cages that actually earns the label — but it's not without compromises.

What you're getting is a modular metal system. The base unit measures roughly 30 x 18 x 18 inches, with a powder-coated steel frame and welded wire mesh that's noticeably thicker than the flimsy bars on typical pet-store cages. The deep plastic base tray is a standout — it's a solid 4 inches deep, which means you can dump in 3+ inches of aspen or paper bedding for burrowing species. That's a genuine upgrade over the shallow pans on most cages in this price bracket.

Assembly is where the frustration starts. The panels lock together using tension clips that require serious hand strength to seat properly. Several Amazon reviewers noted the same issue. One wrote, *"I needed my husband's help to get the clips in — my hands were raw after 20 minutes."* That's not a dealbreaker, but budget time for a frustrating 30-45 minute build. The included instructions are mostly diagrams, so you'll be figuring out orientation by trial and error.

Once it's together, the structure is genuinely solid. The cage doesn't wobble, the door latches are secure, and the metal bars have a smooth powder coat that won't snag fur or scratch your floor when you slide the cage. The 0.8mm wire is a real selling point — it's roughly 30% thicker than standard cages, and it shows. If you've got a determined chewer, this will hold.

Now, the honest limitations. Bar spacing is a critical spec, and BUCATSTATE doesn't publish it clearly. Based on measurements from owners, it's approximately 0.5 inches. That's perfectly safe for adult rats, Syrian hamsters, and adult mice. It is NOT suitable for mouse pups, dwarf hamster babies, or any rodent under 4 weeks old — they can squeeze through. If you're breeding or adopting youngsters, you need a different enclosure.

The second real issue: no interior furniture. This is a bare metal box. There are no shelves, ramps, or platforms included, and the wire walls make attaching traditional plastic accessories tricky. You'll want to budget for aftermarket platforms, hammocks, and climbing structures — which adds $20-40 to your total cost. This is a shell, not a complete habitat.

Where this cage shines is the stackable design. The top panel removes completely, and the metal frame includes interlocking tabs that let you stack a second unit vertically. For rat owners (who need significant vertical space), this is a legitimate plus — you can create a two-story habitat without buying a giant all-in-one cage that's impossible to clean.

For cleaning, the deep tray lifts out entirely. That's the single best feature for daily maintenance. You can swap bedding in under two minutes without dismantling anything. Given that hamsters and rats both need regular spot-cleaning, this alone justifies the price for many owners.

**Who should buy this:**

- Rat owners with 2-3 adults who need a chewproof, stackable habitat
- Syrian or Chinese hamster owners tired of replacing gnawed plastic cages
- Anyone who prioritizes easy cleaning over aesthetic appeal

**Who should skip it:**

- Owners of mouse pups or dwarf hamster babies (bar spacing is unsafe)
- Anyone who wants a turnkey habitat without buying extra accessories
- People with arthritis or hand strength issues — assembly is genuinely difficult

**Frequently asked questions:**

**Q: Can a Syrian hamster really not chew through this?**
A: The wire is thick enough that most hamsters give up after a few attempts. The plastic base tray is still vulnerable along the top rim, so watch that area. No cage is 100% chewproof, but this is as close as it gets under $100.

**Q: How many rats can fit in one unit?**
A: Two adult male rats comfortably, or three females. For a proper colony of four or more, you'll want to stack a second unit.

**Q: Is the tray truly leak-proof for litter training?**
A: Yes, but only if you don't overfill. The tray has sealed corners, but if you pack bedding above the rim, urine can seep through the seam where the tray meets the wire.

**The Verdict:**

**Buy it if** you need a genuinely chewproof cage for adult rodents and don't mind a frustrating assembly session. It's a solid, durable shell that will outlast cheaper alternatives by years.

**Skip it if** you have baby rodents, want a furnished habitat out of the box, or lack the hand strength for assembly.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The build quality and deep tray earn the score. The assembly pain and missing accessories dock points. For the right owner, this is a 5-star cage.
