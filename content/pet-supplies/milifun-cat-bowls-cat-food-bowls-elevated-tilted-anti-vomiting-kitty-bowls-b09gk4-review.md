If your cat throws up after meals more often than you'd like, you've probably tried every trick in the book. Raised bowls. Slow feeders. Different food brands. The MILIFUN Cat Bowls take a slightly different approach — they combine elevation with a tilted angle to address both the physical posture and the speed-eating problem at once. After digging through 215 ratings and the mechanics of how this bowl works, here's the real picture.

## What You Actually Get

The set includes two stainless steel bowls seated in a plastic base with a non-skid silicone bottom. The tilt is approximately 15 degrees, which angles the food toward your cat so they don't have to strain their neck as much to reach the bottom of the dish. Each bowl holds about 8 ounces — enough for a single meal for most adult cats, though larger breeds might need a refill.

The stainless steel inserts lift out for easy cleaning, and both the bowls and the base are dishwasher-safe. That's a practical detail worth noting because plastic cat bowls are notorious for developing micro-scratches that trap bacteria and cause chin acne. Steel doesn't have that problem.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The tilted design does two things: it reduces whisker fatigue (that sensitive whisker-triggered stress some cats get from deep bowls) and it slows down gulping. When food sits at an angle, your cat has to work around the incline, which naturally paces their eating. Multiple reviewers noted their cats stopped vomiting within a few days of switching. One buyer commented, "My Persian used to scarf and barf every single morning. After two weeks with these bowls, it's down to maybe once a week." That's a meaningful improvement.

The non-skid base deserves a mention. Some cats are aggressive eaters and push their bowls across the floor. The silicone grip on this unit holds firm on tile and hardwood. One reviewer with a rambunctious 6-month-old kitten said, "She can't slide these bowls around anymore. They stay put."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. The bowls are not adjustable. The fixed height is roughly 4 inches off the ground, which works well for average adult cats but is too high for kittens and possibly too low for very large breeds like Maine Coons. If you have multiple cats of different sizes, you're looking at buying two sets.

The footprint is another consideration. At about 12 inches long and 6 inches wide, this takes up counter space. If you're tight on kitchen real estate, it's noticeable. Also, the plastic base — while sturdy — is not what I'd call premium. It does the job, but it's functional rather than beautiful.

A handful of reviewers mentioned the bowls are shallower than expected. If your cat is a messy eater who flings kibble, the angled design can sometimes send pieces flying over the edge. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** Your cat vomits after meals, eats too fast, or shows signs of whisker stress. This is also a solid choice if you're looking for an easy-to-clean stainless steel option that won't harbor bacteria. It's particularly good for senior cats who benefit from reduced neck strain.

**Skip it if:** You have kittens (they'll need a lower bowl), you need height adjustability for multiple cats of different sizes, or you're looking for something aesthetically premium to match your kitchen. This is a functional tool, not a design piece.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this actually stop my cat from vomiting?**
A: It helps with regurgitation caused by eating too fast or poor posture. It won't fix vomiting caused by food allergies, hairballs, or medical conditions. If the problem persists after two weeks, see your vet.

**Q: Can I put these in the dishwasher?**
A: Yes. Both the steel bowls and the plastic base are dishwasher-safe. Just place them on the top rack to be safe.

**Q: Is the height suitable for all cats?**
A: No. The fixed height works for average adult cats but is too high for kittens and likely too low for giant breeds. Measure your cat's shoulder height before buying.
