If you're tired of racing home before dusk to shut the coop door — or worse, doing the 6 a.m. waddle out in pajamas to let the girls out — an automatic door is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade. The Sun Energise Automatic Chicken Coop Door aims to solve that with a programmable timer, a light sensor, and a price point that undercuts the big names. After digging through 210 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the door unit, a 12V power adapter, a control box with a backlit LCD, mounting screws, and the manual. The door itself is aluminum with a powder-coated finish — it feels more substantial than the flimsy plastic panels on cheaper units. The control box is where the magic happens: you can set open/close times in 1-minute increments, or flip to light-sensor mode and let sunrise/sunset trigger the mechanism.

One thing buyers consistently note: the setup is genuinely simple. As one verified purchaser put it, *"I'm not handy at all and had this mounted in about 20 minutes. The instructions are actually readable."* That's rare in this category.

## Performance and Build Quality

The motor is quiet and smooth, which matters if your coop is near the house. The anti-pinch sensor is the standout feature — it reverses the door if it meets resistance. Multiple reviewers report it working exactly as intended when a curious hen stuck her head in at the wrong moment. That's a safety feature worth paying for, because a door that crushes a bird is a door that costs you a lot more than it saved.

The light sensor mode is genuinely useful for the changing seasons — no need to reprogram as daylight shifts. But it has a quirk: the sensor needs a clear view of the sky. If your coop has a deep overhang or is under heavy tree cover, the door may open late or close early. Several reviewers hit this exact issue. The timer mode sidesteps it, but you'll need to adjust it monthly as the seasons shift.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The reliability is the headline. With 4.2 stars across 210 ratings, the consensus is that this door does its job day after day. Buyers specifically mention:

- **The battery backup option** — it runs on 12V, so you can wire it to a small solar setup or deep-cycle battery. Off-grid users love this.
- **The anti-pinch mechanism** — again, this is the feature that separates it from budget competitors.
- **Customer service responsiveness** — several reviewers noted quick replacements when units arrived with minor cosmetic damage.

## Where It Falls Short

The mounting hardware is the weak link. The included screws are soft and strip easily when you're driving them into dense wood. Plan to use your own deck screws from the start — it'll save you a headache.

The light sensor sensitivity is the other real gripe. As one reviewer put it, *"Works great in timer mode, but the sensor mode opens the door way too early on cloudy mornings. I switched to timer and never looked back."* If you're buying this specifically for the light sensor, set your expectations accordingly — it's a convenience feature, not a precision instrument.

Also note: the control box is not weatherproof. It needs to be mounted inside the coop or under cover. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you install.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a standard backyard coop, want the safety of anti-pinch detection, and want the flexibility of both timer and light-sensor modes. It's ideal for the hobbyist with 3–10 birds who wants reliable automation without spending $200+.

**Skip it if:** Your coop has a deeply recessed door opening (the sensor will struggle), you need a fully weatherproof outdoor unit, or you want smartphone app control — this is a manual-program unit, no Wi-Fi here.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this door handle predators trying to push it open?**
The door locks in the closed position with a mechanical latch. It won't stop a determined bear, but it will hold against raccoons, foxes, and dogs. The aluminum construction is sturdy enough for typical backyard threats.

**Q: How long does the battery last on a full charge?**
Buyers report 3–5 days on a single charge with normal use (two open/close cycles daily). With a small 12V solar panel and battery setup, you can run it indefinitely off-grid.

**Q: Will it work with an existing coop or does it need a specific frame?**
It's designed for standard 2x4 or 2x6 framed openings. The mounting plate is adjustable, but you'll want to measure your opening before ordering — it's meant for doors roughly 12" wide by 14" tall.
