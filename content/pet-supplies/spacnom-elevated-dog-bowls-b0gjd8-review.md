Elevated dog bowls are one of those products that sound like a gimmick until you own a senior dog with arthritis or a Great Dane who eats like his bowl is on fire. The Spacnom Elevated Dog Bowls — currently sitting at 4.7 stars across 235 ratings — have carved out a solid reputation in this crowded category. But is the hype justified? ## What You Actually Get

The Spacnom comes in two configurations: a fixed-height model (8 inches for small breeds) and an adjustable version that extends from 10 to 14 inches. Both ship with two stainless steel bowls — the standard capacity is roughly 4.5 cups per bowl, which handles most kibble portions comfortably. The frame is powder-coated steel tubing, and the bowls sit in a double-walled housing that keeps them secure.

As shown in the product image, the design is clean and modern — no garish branding, just a matte black frame that doesn't look out of place in a kitchen. Assembly takes about five minutes with the included hardware, though you'll want your own screwdriver (more on that below).

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The standout feature here is stability. The legs splay outward at a wider angle than many competitors, and each foot has a thick rubber pad. One buyer noted: *"My 70-pound lab mix jumps on these bowls like they're a toy, and they haven't budged an inch. The floor is tile, and I was sure they'd slide."* That tracks — the rubber feet provide serious grip, and the frame's weight distribution keeps the center of gravity low.

The stainless steel bowls are the right call. They're double-walled (so they don't transfer cold or heat aggressively), pop out for dishwasher cleaning, and don't develop that plasticky film that cheaper feeders accumulate. Another recurring theme in reviews is the height adjustability: *"We have a corgi and a shepherd mix. The adjustable stand let me set the right height for each without buying two different feeders."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 235 ratings, a few themes dominate:

- **Ease of cleaning** — The removable bowls are the most-cited win. No crevices where kibble dust and drool hide.
- **Arthritis relief** — Multiple reviewers mention senior dogs with joint issues eating more comfortably. One wrote: *"My 11-year-old golden retriever has stopped straining her neck. She actually finishes her meals now."*
- **Value for money** — At this price point, buyers consistently say it outperforms feeders costing twice as much.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and the Spacnom has a few honest flaws.

The assembly experience is mildly annoying. The included Allen wrench is undersized, and tightening the bolts to full torque without stripping them requires patience. Use your own Phillips screwdriver and you'll be fine, but it's a friction point.

The silicone ring that seats the bowls can trap moisture. If you don't dry the bowls completely before snapping them back in, you'll get a thin film of water under the rim. It's not a mold risk if you clean regularly, but it's a detail that could've been engineered better.

Finally, the 4.5-cup capacity is fine for most dogs, but if you have a 100-pound lab who inhales six cups a day, the bowl depth might feel shallow. You'll be refilling more often than you'd like.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Senior dog owners** — The elevated angle genuinely reduces neck and joint strain.

- **Messy eaters** — The stable base and deep bowls cut down on spilled kibble and water splashes.

Skip it if you have a giant breed needing more than 5 cups per meal, or if you can't tolerate minor assembly friction.

## FAQ

A: Yes, the stainless steel bowls are dishwasher-safe. The frame and silicone rings are hand-wash only.

**Q: How much weight can the stand hold?**
A: The frame is rated for dogs up to 90 pounds. The bowls themselves hold about 4.5 cups each of dry food.
