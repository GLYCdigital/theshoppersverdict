## The Budget Pate That Actually Delivers

Most cat owners know the drill: you buy a case of wet food, your cat sniffs it once, and walks away. The Friskies Poultry Platter Pate is one of the few exceptions — a 4.6-star product with 229 ratings that most cats seem to genuinely enjoy. But is it right for *your* cat? Let's dig in.

## What You're Actually Getting

This is a 24-pack of 5.5-ounce cans (that's 132 ounces total, or roughly 8.25 pounds of food). The Poultry Platter variety includes chicken, turkey, and chicken giblets flavors — all in the classic Friskies pate style. The cans are standard pull-tab, easy to open, and stack cleanly in a pantry shelf.

As shown in the product image, the pate is smooth and uniform — no chunks, no gravy, just a dense meatloaf-style texture. That matters more than you'd think.

## Real-World Performance

The pate texture is the star here. Because it's so smooth, you can easily mash it with a fork and add warm water to create a slurry — a trick that works wonders for senior cats with dental problems or kittens transitioning from milk. One reviewer noted, "My 16-year-old cat with only two teeth left eats this without any trouble. I add a little water and she licks the bowl clean."

Nutritionally, real poultry is the first ingredient, and there are no artificial colors or carrageenan (a common thickener that some owners avoid). It's not a premium food — the second and third ingredients are meat by-products and poultry by-products — but for the price point, it's honest, solid nutrition.

The 5.5-ounce can size is the sweet spot. It's enough for one adult cat per day (split into two meals) or one generous serving for a larger cat. The 3-ounce cans are convenient for single meals but cost significantly more per ounce.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The consistency of quality across 24 cans is a recurring theme. One verified purchase wrote: "Every single can is the same — no watery cans, no dried-out pate, no weird jelly liquid on top. Just consistent food my cats inhale." Another owner of three cats mentioned: "I've bought this case three times in a row. All three cats eat it, even my picky one."

The value proposition is clear: for multi-cat households or owners feeding multiple times daily, this case format beats buying individual cans at big-box stores.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, there are two real downsides. First, the smell. This is a fish-forward poultry blend, and it's potent. It's not appetizing to humans — expect the kitchen to smell for a bit after feeding time. Second, the pate texture is polarizing. Cats that prefer shreds or gravy-style food may outright reject this. If your cat has never eaten pate, buy a single can first before committing to 24 of them.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Multi-cat households** looking to cut costs without sacrificing quality
- **Owners of senior cats** with dental issues who need soft, mashable food
- **Anyone tired of inconsistent can-to-can quality** from cheaper brands
- **Kitten owners** transitioning from milk to solids (with added water)

**Skip this if** your cat refuses pate textures or you prefer grain-free formulas.

**Q: Can I mix this with dry food?**
Absolutely. Many buyers use this as a wet-food topper over kibble to increase moisture intake — especially useful for cats prone to urinary issues.

## The Verdict

**Skip it if** your cat demands shreds or gravy textures, or if you specifically need grain-free formulas.
