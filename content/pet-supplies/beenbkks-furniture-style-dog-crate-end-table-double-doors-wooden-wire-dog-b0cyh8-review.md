Let’s be honest: most “furniture-style” dog crates are just wire cages with a cheap wooden skirt slapped on. The beeNbkks Furniture Style Dog Crate End Table with double doors is not that. But it’s also not perfect. Here’s what 225 buyers at 4.5 stars are actually saying — and what they’re not.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the crate frame, engineered wood panels for the top and sides, a removable plastic tray, two wire mesh doors, and all hardware. No tools included — you’ll need a Phillips head screwdriver and about 45 minutes of patience. The assembled footprint for the 30-inch model is roughly 30" L x 19.5" W x 24.5" H. The tabletop is rated to hold up to 220 pounds, which means you can set a lamp, books, or a drink on it without worrying about collapse.

The double doors are the headline feature. One opens on the front; the other swings from the side. That side door is genuinely useful if you’re sliding the crate against a wall or into a corner — you can still reach your dog without dragging the whole unit out. Both doors use sliding latch locks that actually engage with a solid click. No flimsy wire hooks that bend after a week.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The engineered wood panels have a matte finish that looks convincingly like real furniture from a few feet away. It’s not solid oak, but it won’t embarrass you in a living room either. The wire mesh is welded, not spot-tacked, so it holds up to scratching. The removable tray slides out from the front for cleaning — but here’s the catch: the bottom isn’t fully enclosed. When the tray is out, you can see through the gaps. That’s fine for cleanup, but it means small messes can leak onto your floor if the tray shifts.

Multiple buyers mention the assembly friction. One reviewer wrote, “The instructions are mostly pictures and some holes didn’t line up perfectly. I had to re-drill two spots.” Another said, “It took me an hour, but once it was together it felt solid.” That tracks — the build quality is good once assembled, but the process is the weakest link.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The furniture look is the top compliment. One 5-star review said, “My guests genuinely thought it was a side table until my dog walked out of it.” Another praised the side door: “Being able to open it from the side when it’s tucked in the corner is a game-changer.” The 220-pound tabletop rating gets frequent mention — people are actually using it as a real end table, not a crate that happens to have a flat top.

**Where it falls short**

The sizing is the biggest complaint. The 30-inch model is listed for dogs up to 55 pounds, but several buyers with 50-pound dogs said it was too tight. One wrote, “My 45-pound lab mix fits, but he can’t fully stretch out.” If your dog is between sizes, go up one. Also, the plastic tray is thin — one reviewer said it cracked after a few months of heavy use. And the wood finish is prone to scratches from dog nails if they paw at the doors.

**Who should buy this**

- Apartment dwellers who need a crate that doesn’t scream “dog prison” when guests visit
- Owners with medium dogs (under 45 pounds) who want a functional side table, not just a crate
- Anyone with space constraints — the side door is a genuine convenience in tight rooms

Skip it if you have a large breed, a heavy chewer who’ll attack the wood frame, or zero patience for furniture assembly.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this as an actual end table?* Yes — the top holds up to 220 pounds. A lamp, books, or drinks are fine. Just use coasters; the finish is matte and can stain.

*Does the side door work well against a wall?* Yes, that’s its purpose. It swings open fully, giving you access without pulling the crate out. Just leave 2–3 inches of clearance for the hinge.

*What size dog fits the 30-inch model?* Realistically, up to 40–45 pounds. If your dog is 50+ pounds, size up. Measure your dog’s length from nose to tail base before ordering.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a crate that doubles as actual furniture and your dog is under 45 pounds. The double doors, solid build, and real tabletop make it worth the assembly hassle.

**Skip it if** you have a larger dog, need a crate that’s fully enclosed at the bottom, or want something ready in 10 minutes out of the box.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely useful piece of furniture that happens to be a crate, held back only by fiddly assembly and optimistic sizing. For medium-dog owners who care about aesthetics, this is the best option in its class.
