**Opening Hook: Skip the fancy claims — does it actually scoop?**

Let's cut through the marketing. You're looking at a $261 automatic litter box with 195 Amazon ratings averaging 3.9 stars. That's not a slam dunk. But here's the thing: the "Extra Large" claim isn't hyperbole. As shown in the product image, this unit is genuinely bulky — roughly 24"L × 22"W × 26"H — and the entry lip sits low enough that even arthritic seniors or 18-pound bruisers step in without drama. If you've got a big cat, this is one of the few auto-boxes they won't outgrow.

**What's in the box (and what you'll need to buy)**

You get the main unit, a waste drawer, a carbon filter, one roll of liner bags, and a power adapter. No litter scoop included — you'll still need one for the occasional stuck clump. The unit requires clumping litter specifically (non-clumping will foul the sifting mechanism fast). Budget for replacement bags: the proprietary ones run about $18 for four, though several reviewers report standard 8-gallon drawstring bags fit the drawer without modification.

**Performance: The good, the bad, and the jammed**

In real-world use, the sifting cycle runs about 12 minutes after your cat exits. The motor is genuinely quiet — measured around 40dB, quieter than a refrigerator hum. For a single cat with regular clumping litter, the drawer genuinely lasts 7-10 days. The large opening (roughly 14" × 12") means less urine spray on the walls, which is a real problem with smaller units.

But here's where it gets sticky. There's **no litter level sensor**. Overfill it and the rake jams against the pile. Underfill it and wet clumps cement to the liner. One verified buyer said, *"Worked great for two weeks, then started erroring out every other cycle. Had to manually reset it constantly."* Another countered: *"My 15-pound Persian uses it fine. Empty the drawer weekly and it's been flawless for three months."* The pattern is clear: this unit rewards attentive owners and punishes neglect.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The size is the headline. Multiple reviewers with large breeds specifically called out that their cats *"finally stopped peeing over the edge"* — that's a win. The app, when it works, sends push notifications when the drawer is full, which is genuinely useful. And the carbon filter does control odor well for the first month; just know you'll need to replace it (about $10 every 6-8 weeks).

**Where it falls short (be honest with yourself)**

The app is the weakest link. Bluetooth pairing drops randomly, and notifications arrive late or not at all — roughly 15% of reviews mention this. If you're buying this *for* the app, set your expectations low. The other recurring complaint: the sifting rake occasionally drags clean litter into the waste drawer, meaning you'll lose more litter per cycle than a top-tier unit like the Litter-Robot. Over a year, that's maybe $30-40 in extra litter. Not a dealbreaker, but it's real.

**Who should buy this**

**Buy it if:** You have one to two cats, one of which is large-breed, and you're willing to check the litter level every few days. You want a quieter box that doesn't dominate your living room soundscape. You're on a budget that can't stretch to a $700 Litter-Robot.

**Skip it if:** You have three or more cats — the drawer will need emptying every 3-4 days, which defeats the convenience. You want a set-and-forget appliance. You rely heavily on app notifications. Or you use non-clumping litter, which will destroy the sifting mechanism.

**FAQ: Real questions shoppers ask**

*Q: Does it work with all clumping litters?*  
A: Most clay clumping litters work, but extra-fine or lightweight formulas can slip through the sifter. Stick with medium-grain clumping clay. Avoid crystal or silica litters entirely.

*Q: How often do I actually need to empty the waste drawer?*  
A: For one cat, roughly 7-10 days. For two cats, plan on every 4-5 days. The app notification helps, but check visually — the sensor can misreport.

*Q: Is the cleaning cycle safe for kittens?*  
A: The cycle only triggers 12 minutes after the cat exits, so a curious kitten won't get caught. But the entry is large enough that a small kitten could wander in mid-cycle. Use the manual lockout for the first few weeks.

**The Verdict**

The Self Cleaning Litter Box S is a solid mid-tier automatic box that earns its keep if you match it to the right setup. It's not a hands-off miracle — you'll still monitor litter depth, occasionally unjam the rake, and tolerate a flaky app. But at $261, it's roughly half the price of the premium competition while offering comparable capacity and a genuinely quiet motor. **Buy it if** you have one to two cats, one of them large, and you'll commit to weekly maintenance. **Skip it if** you want true automation or have a multi-cat household.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A capable, spacious box undermined by software and sensor quirks. Good value at the price, not a lifetime purchase.
