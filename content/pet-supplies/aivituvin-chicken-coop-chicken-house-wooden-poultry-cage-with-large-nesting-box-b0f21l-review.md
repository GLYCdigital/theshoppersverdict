**The Short Version**

If you're looking for a backyard coop that actually shelters three hens without breaking your budget, the Aivituvin is worth a hard look. At its price point, it's a solid mid-tier option — better built than the flimsy Amazon specials, but not in the same league as a $1,000+ heirloom coop. At 3.8 stars from 190 ratings, the consensus is clear: good coop, frustrating assembly, and a few design quirks you'll want to know about before you click buy.

---

**What You Actually Get**

The box is heavy — expect to haul around 60 pounds of pre-drilled fir wood panels, hardware, and corrugated roofing. The coop measures roughly 70 inches long by 26 inches deep by 40 inches tall at the peak, which gives you about 10-12 square feet of floor space in the main run. That's genuinely adequate for 2-3 standard hens, or 4 if you're willing to let them free-range during the day.

As shown in the product image, the design features a covered run section with wire mesh on three sides, a solid-walled roosting area, and the standout feature: a large nesting box with a hinged lid. The nesting area is roughly 20 inches square — big enough for multiple hens to share without egg-thieving squabbles.

---

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The fir wood is pressure-treated and coated with a weather-resistant paint. After six months of Pacific Northwest rain, mine shows no warping or rot. The corrugated roof overhangs enough to keep the nesting box dry during downpours.

The sliding dropping tray is a genuine improvement over ground-level coops — you can pull it out and scrape it clean in under two minutes. The side access door to the nesting box is another thoughtful touch; no more reaching through the pop hole.

But here's the honest tradeoff: the wire mesh is 0.5mm galvanized wire. It'll stop raccoons from reaching in, but determined predators can bend it. Several owners echo this: *"Had a raccoon bend the mesh near the latch. It didn't get in, but I reinforced it with hardware cloth anyway."* If you live in an area with aggressive predators, budget $20 for extra hardware cloth and a better latch.

---

**What Owners Consistently Praise**

The nesting box is the MVP. One reviewer put it plainly: *"My hens actually use it. The lid makes egg collection stupidly easy."* Another noted: *"For the price, the wood is thicker than I expected. It's held up through two winters."*

The size is also a recurring win. Multiple owners mention fitting 3-4 hens comfortably, with one saying: *"I've got three Buff Orpingtons in here — they're cozy but not cramped."*

---

**Where It Falls Short**

Assembly is the #1 complaint — and it's legitimate. The instructions are mostly exploded diagrams with no written steps. Plan for 3-4 hours if you're handy, 5+ if you're not. Bring a cordless drill; the pre-drilled holes align decently, but you'll want a screwdriver bit to avoid stripping the softwood.

The ramp is steeper than ideal — about 45 degrees. Heavy hens and chicks struggle with it. Most owners add traction strips or lower the angle with a brick underneath. It's a minor fix but annoying.

The latch on the main door is a simple hook-and-eye — flimsy for the price. Upgrade it immediately if you have raccoons or curious dogs.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Urban/suburban owners** who need something compact that fits against a fence or shed

**FAQ**

**Q: How many chickens does this actually hold?**
A: Comfortably 2-3 standard breeds. Four bantams or small breeds like Silkies can work, but you'll want daily free-range time. Overcrowding leads to pecking and dirty eggs.

**Q: Does it need to be on a level surface?**
A: Strongly recommended. The legs are adjustable by about an inch, but significant slopes will stress the joints. Place it on pavers or a wooden platform for stability.

**Q: Is the wood treated against mites and rot?**
A: The paint provides basic weather protection, but it's not treated against pests. Apply a food-safe wood sealant annually and dust the roosting areas with poultry-grade diatomaceous earth for mite prevention.
