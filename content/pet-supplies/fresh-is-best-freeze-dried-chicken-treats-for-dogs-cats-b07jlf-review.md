**The Short Version**

Most freeze-dried treats are either rock-hard nuggets your senior dog refuses to chew or greasy strips that stain your couch. Fresh Is Best takes a different path: airy, brittle morsels of pure chicken breast that shatter between your fingers. It's the closest thing to homemade you'll find in a bag, and that simplicity is exactly why it works — or doesn't, depending on what you're looking for.

**What You Actually Get**

One bag of freeze-dried chicken breast pieces. That's it. No grains, no glycerin, no "natural flavors" hiding artificial anything. The ingredient list is literally one word: chicken. As shown in the product image, the pieces are irregular, off-white chunks that look like what they are — dehydrated meat, not processed kibble bits. The bag holds roughly 3 ounces, and because freeze-drying removes about 70% of the water weight, that's more actual protein than you'd expect from a same-size jerky bag.

**Real-World Performance**

The texture is the headline here. These treats are porous and fragile — you can crumble them between two fingers into a powder that's perfect for sprinkling over picky eaters' food. That's a genuine advantage for training sessions where you need tiny, low-calorie rewards. One buyer noted, *"I break one piece into 10+ training tidbits. A single bag lasts me two weeks of daily clicker work."* Another reviewer with a senior cat said, *"My 14-year-old with no teeth can gum these easily. They dissolve fast."*

For cats, the small crumbles work well as a topper or hand-fed treat. For dogs, most owners report enthusiasm — the smell is mild but distinctly meaty, and the freeze-drying preserves the natural flavor without the salty punch of many commercial treats.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the packaging first: the bag has no resealable zipper. Once opened, you're committed to either finishing it quickly or finding your own clip. Moisture is the enemy of freeze-dried products, and a humid day can turn crisp pieces into chewy, less-appealing chunks.

The size inconsistency is the other notable gripe. Some bags arrive with a fair amount of powder and broken shards at the bottom. It's not wasted — you can use the dust as a food topper — but if you're expecting uniform, photo-ready pieces, you'll be mildly annoyed. One reviewer captured it well: *"Great product, but half the bag was crumbs. My dog didn't care. I sort of did."*

Finally, the price. At roughly $0.50–$0.70 per ounce of pure protein, it costs more per pound than whole chicken breast at the grocery store. You're paying for convenience and the freeze-drying process, not for value. If you're feeding a 70-pound dog who needs a handful of treats per training session, you'll blow through a bag in days.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Training-focused owners**: The crumble-ability makes portion control effortless.
- **Multi-pet households**: One bag serves both dog and cat, simplifying your treat drawer.
- **Picky or senior pets**: The soft texture and pure chicken flavor win over most reluctant eaters.
- **Owners avoiding additives**: If you read every label and flinch at "animal digest," this is as clean as it gets.

**Skip It If**

You need a resealable, grab-and-go bag for outdoor adventures, or you're on a tight budget and need maximum volume per dollar. Also, if your dog prefers chewy, jerky-style treats, the brittle texture might not satisfy.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these be rehydrated for extra picky eaters?**
A: Yes. Add a teaspoon of warm water and let them sit for 2–3 minutes. They soften into a paste-like consistency that works well for sick pets or those with dental issues.

**Q: Are these safe for puppies and kittens?**
A: Generally yes — the single-ingredient recipe is gentle on developing digestive systems. But introduce slowly and break pieces into smaller bits to prevent choking, since the pieces can be larger than a puppy's mouth.

**Q: How should I store them after opening?**
A: Transfer to an airtight container or use a bag clip. Keep them in a cool, dry place. Avoid the refrigerator — the moisture will degrade the texture. For long-term storage, they freeze well for up to 6 months.
