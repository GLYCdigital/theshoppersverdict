Let's be honest about the GUTINNEEN Rabbit Hutch: it's a budget-priced two-story cage that tries to do a lot — indoor coziness, outdoor versatility, portability, and easy cleaning. At 3.9 stars from 195 ratings, it's polarizing. Some owners swear by it for dwarf breeds; others returned it within a week. Here's the real breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The hutch arrives flat-packed with the usual assortment of screws, panels, and a plastic ramp connecting the two levels. The main structure is fir wood with a galvanized wire mesh front and sides, plus a solid roof. The lower level has a pull-out plastic tray — this is your cleaning lifeline. The whole thing sits on four locking caster wheels, which is the standout feature.

Dimensions matter here: this is not a spacious mansion. The interior footprint is roughly 36" L x 20" W, and the upper level is smaller because the ramp cuts into it. Total height is around 40". That's adequate for a single dwarf or small breed rabbit (under 4 lbs) as long as they get daily free-roam time. A Flemish Giant or even a medium-sized lop will feel cramped fast.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The fir wood is lightweight — which helps with portability but means it's not built for heavy weather. If you plan to keep this outdoors permanently, you'll want to treat the wood with a pet-safe sealant first. Several buyers noted the roof doesn't overhang enough to keep rain out of the upper level during storms. One reviewer put it plainly: "It's fine on a covered porch, but don't trust it as a standalone outdoor hutch in heavy rain."

Assembly took most buyers 45–60 minutes. The instructions are minimal line drawings, and the pre-drilled holes don't always line up perfectly. A cordless drill helps; a manual screwdriver will test your patience. Plan for some head-scratching.

The ramp is plastic with small cross-bars, and young or senior rabbits can struggle with the angle. If you have a baby bunny, consider adding a towel or carpet strip on the ramp for traction — that's a common buyer fix.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The wheels are the most-loved feature. "I roll my rabbit's cage from the living room to the porch and back daily — it's so much easier than lifting a heavy hutch," one verified buyer wrote. The pull-out tray also earns consistent praise: "Cleaning takes five minutes, no scrubbing corners."

The two-level design is a hit with owners of active dwarf breeds who want vertical space without a large floor footprint. For apartment dwellers, the compact size is a genuine plus.

**Where It Falls Short**

The wire floor is the biggest concern. It's a galvanized mesh that's too open for rabbit feet — prolonged exposure can cause sore hocks. This is non-negotiable: you must add solid resting boards, fleece pads, or a layer of cardboard covered with bedding over at least part of each level. Several reviewers flagged this after their rabbit developed irritated feet.

The wood is also thin — about 0.4 inches thick — so it won't withstand aggressive chewing. If your rabbit is a dedicated chewer, they'll gnaw through the door frame eventually. Reinforce corners with metal corner brackets if you have a chewer.

The upper level door is small, making it awkward to reach in and grab a reluctant rabbit. And the plastic tray doesn't fit perfectly flush — some bedding can slip underneath into the gap.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Apartment dwellers** with a small single rabbit who need a compact, movable cage — this fits a corner and rolls out for cleaning.
- **Owners who move between indoor and outdoor** settings daily and want a lightweight option.
- **First-time rabbit owners** on a budget who understand they'll need to add solid flooring and possibly seal the wood.

**Skip It If:**

- You have a medium or large breed rabbit (over 5 lbs) — it's too small.
- You need a truly weatherproof outdoor hutch — this isn't it.
- You're not willing to modify the wire floor — your rabbit's feet will pay the price.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this hutch safe for a baby rabbit?**
A: Yes, but the ramp angle is steep for babies. Add traction material on the ramp and keep a solid mat on the wire floor. Also, check that your baby can't squeeze through the wire gaps — they're about 0.5", which is fine for most kits, but verify.

**Q: Can I keep this outdoors year-round?**
A: Not without modification. The wood needs sealing, and the roof doesn't fully protect the upper level from rain. Use it on a covered porch or under a patio overhang — don't leave it exposed to the elements.

**Q: How many rabbits can fit comfortably?**
A: One dwarf or small breed rabbit, comfortably. Two bonded small rabbits could work, but you'd need to remove the ramp's upper-level divider to give them space — and they'd still need daily free-roam time.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have a small rabbit, a modest budget, and you're willing to invest 30 minutes of modifications (solid floor mats, sealing the wood) to make it safe and durable.

**Skip it if:** You want a zero-fuss, weatherproof hutch or you have a larger breed.

The GUTINNEEN is a decent value at the price point, but it's a "starter hutch" — not a forever home. With proper modifications and realistic expectations, it'll serve a single small rabbit well for a year or two. Just don't expect it to survive as a permanent outdoor structure.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — Good bones, needs buyer effort.
