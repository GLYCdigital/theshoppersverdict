## Is KOHA Pure Shred Actually "Pure"? Here's What 215 Buyers Discovered

Most wet dog foods are a slurry of mystery meat, thickeners, and enough salt to make a pretzel blush. KOHA Pure Shred takes a different route: single-source protein, minimal ingredients, and a texture that actually looks like food. With a 4.3-star average across 215 ratings, it's clearly working for many dogs — but it's not without tradeoffs.

---

## What's Actually in the Pouch

KOHA Pure Shred comes in 12.5-ounce pouches (not cans), available in beef, chicken, turkey, and lamb. The ingredient deck is refreshingly short: the named protein, water sufficient for processing, and a few supplements like taurine and vitamin E. No carrageenan, no guar gum, no artificial colors.

The shredded texture is the headline feature — it's real meat strips in broth, not a pâté. As shown in the product image, this looks closer to human-grade stew than typical dog food. For dogs who turn their nose up at mush, this alone can be a game-changer.

## Real-World Performance: What Buyers Keep Saying

The praise clusters around two themes: **picky eaters** and **sensitive stomachs**. One verified buyer wrote: *"My 11-year-old shih tzu refuses everything else. She licks the bowl clean with KOHA — I've reordered four times."* Another noted: *"Finally a wet food that doesn't give my golden retriever diarrhea. The ingredient list is shorter than my own protein bars."*

The broth-to-meat ratio is generous, which helps with hydration — a real bonus for dogs on dry kibble. Several reviewers specifically mentioned using the broth as a topper for finicky dogs, stretching the value further.

---

## Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself

**The cost math is the biggest hurdle.** At roughly $3–4 per pouch, a 70-pound Labrador needs two pouches per meal to meet caloric needs. That's $6–8 a day just for food. This is a premium product, and the price reflects it. If you have a large breed, budget accordingly or use it as a topper rather than a full meal.

**The smell is polarizing.** The beef liver variant has a strong, gamey odor that some dogs love and some owners find off-putting. One reviewer admitted: *"I had to hold my breath opening the pouch. My dog doesn't care, but I almost do."* If you're scent-sensitive, start with the chicken or turkey.

**Pouch format isn't resealable.** Once opened, you need to refrigerate and use within 2–3 days. For single-dog households, that's fine. For multi-dog homes, you'll go through it fast — which circles back to cost.

## Who Should Buy This (and Who Shouldn't)

**Buy it if:** You have a picky eater, a senior dog with dental problems, or a pup with food sensitivities. The limited ingredient list makes it a solid elimination diet candidate. Also great for dogs who need extra hydration.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget with a large breed, or your dog is perfectly happy with a standard pâté. There's no functional advantage over cheaper options for non-picky eaters.

## FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I mix this with kibble?**  
Yes, and many buyers do. The broth softens kibble and adds moisture. Start with a quarter pouch and adjust — too much broth can cause loose stools in sensitive dogs.

**Q: Is this complete and balanced for all life stages?**  
KOHA formulates this as complete nutrition for adult maintenance. For puppies or pregnant/nursing dogs, check with your vet — the calorie density is moderate, not growth-specific.

**Q: How does it compare to The Honest Kitchen or Open Farm?**  
KOHA is simpler on ingredients but less varied in recipes. It's cheaper than Open Farm's stews but pricier than standard grocery brands. The shredded texture is more distinctive than either competitor's pâté lines.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — This earns its score through ingredient transparency and proven appeal to picky eaters. The texture is genuinely different, and the short ingredient list gives peace of mind. But the cost-per-calorie math and the pungent liver variety keep it from a perfect rating.
