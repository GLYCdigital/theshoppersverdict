Let’s be straight about the NICREW C10 24/7: it’s not a high-end Chihiros or Twinstar. It’s a budget LED that tries to do a lot — and mostly succeeds. At 4.3 stars from 215 ratings, it’s clearly earning its keep in the aquarium community. But the "24/7" feature is the headline, and whether that’s a gimmick or a game-changer depends entirely on your setup.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the light bar itself, two adjustable mounting brackets, a power adapter, and a slim remote control. The unit measures roughly 23 inches for the standard size (they offer multiple lengths), with an aluminum heat sink that stays cool even after 10 hours running. The brackets slide to fit tank walls up to 0.5 inches thick — fine for standard 10-gallon and 20-gallon tanks, but if you’ve got a rimmed aquarium with thicker glass, you’ll need to rig something. The remote is small and functional, not fancy, but it covers all modes without needing to fiddle with buttons on the light itself.

**The 24/7 cycle — the real story**

Here’s where the C10 shines. The 24/7 mode automatically shifts color temperature and intensity throughout the day: cool bright white at noon, warm amber at dusk, dim blue moonlight at night. It’s genuinely seamless, and for planted tanks with low-light species like Anubias or Java fern, it provides a natural photoperiod without you touching a timer. One reviewer put it well: *"I set it once and forgot about it. My fish seem less stressed, and the gradual dimming at night is beautiful."*

But here’s the catch: if power flickers or you unplug the light, the cycle resets to the start time. There’s no internal memory. For a $40-60 light, that’s a frustrating omission — you’ll need to re-sync the cycle manually after outages. If you’re not home when it happens, your fish get a weird schedule for a day.

**Build quality and performance**

The chassis feels solid — aluminum, not cheap plastic. The LEDs are bright enough for low-to-medium light plants, but don’t expect high-light carpeting plants like dwarf baby tears to thrive. This is a fish-focused light with plant benefits, not a horticultural powerhouse. The 6500K spectrum makes red and blue fish pop nicely, and the moonlight mode is genuinely calming at night — a nice touch for viewing nocturnal behavior.

The remote works up to about 6 feet away, which is fine for most stands. One buyer noted: *"The remote is a bit finicky — you have to point it directly at the sensor, but once you learn that, it's fine."* That tracks with my experience.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the timer-reset issue, the mounting system is the weakest link. The brackets are simple L-shaped clips with thumbscrews. They work, but they don’t sit perfectly flush on all tanks — slight wobble is possible if your glass isn’t exactly parallel. Also, there’s no way to suspend this light; it’s strictly rim-mount. For rimless tanks, it looks clean, but for standard framed tanks, the clips can interfere with the plastic rim.

**Who should buy this**

- **Beginner aquarists** wanting a set-and-forget lighting solution for a community tank.
- **Planted-tank newcomers** with low-light plant species who don’t want to shell out $150+ for a high-end fixture.
- **Fishkeepers with consistent power** who won’t be tripped up by the reset issue.

**Skip it if** you’re running a high-tech CO2 planted tank demanding intense light, or if you need a light that remembers its schedule through outages.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the 24/7 mode work without the remote?**  
A: Yes. The cycle runs automatically once set, but you need the remote to initially select the mode and set the start time.

**Q: Will this grow carpeting plants?**  
A: Not well. It’s fine for low-light species, but high-light plants will stretch and look leggy.

**Q: Is it safe for shrimp and nano fish?**  
A: Absolutely. The moonlight mode is gentle, and the gradual transitions avoid startling skittish species.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, attractive light with a genuinely useful 24/7 cycle for a low-tech tank. **Skip it if** you demand timer memory or high photosynthetic output. At 4.2/5, it’s a solid value for the price — just know its limits before you buy.
