Let's be honest about mushroom cultivation: the monotub is where most grows either succeed or turn into a science experiment gone wrong. The Grower's Select 28Q Cube attempts to remove the variables — pre-drilled holes, included filters, and a clear body — and for the most part, it delivers exactly what the 4.7-star average suggests.

## What you actually get

The box contains the 28-quart tub, a latching lid, two small discs of stick-on synthetic filter material, and zero instructions. That last part matters. If you're a first-time grower expecting hand-holding, you'll be disappointed — but the tub itself is self-explanatory if you've watched even one monotub tutorial.

The plastic is polypropylene, not the flimsy acrylic you see on cheaper chambers. It's rigid enough to hold a 5-pound grain spawn block without bowing, yet translucent enough that you can spot early pinning without cracking the lid and disturbing your humidity gradient.

## Performance in real use

The pre-drilled holes are positioned about 3 inches from the bottom on all four sides — the classic "low FAE" configuration. The included filter disks adhere cleanly and cover the holes completely. In practice, this creates a passive airflow loop: CO₂ settles and exits low, fresh oxygen enters without you touching the tub. One reviewer noted, *"I set it and forgot it for 10 days. Came back to a full pinset with zero intervention."* That tracks with my testing.

The lid latch is functional but not airtight — which is actually a feature. Too many tubs seal completely and suffocate the mycelium. Here, the slight gap allows pressure equalization without letting in contaminants, as long as you're not in a dusty basement.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 235 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **Durability** — *"Dropped mine on concrete. Cracked the floor tile, not the tub."*
2. **Filter disk adhesion** — *"Survived three full flushes and two soaks without peeling."*
3. **The clear walls** — Being able to see early contam (green trich spots) before they spread is the single biggest advantage over opaque tubs.

## Where it falls short

The filters are the weak point. After repeated wiping with 70% isopropyl, the adhesive degrades. You'll need replacement disks by flush three — budget for that or learn to cut your own from filter patch material.

Second, the plastic is a fingerprint and water-spot magnet. If you care about aesthetics for photos, this tub will annoy you. Functionally irrelevant, but worth knowing.

Third, there's no rubber gasket on the bottom. Stacking two tubs for incubation is possible but slippery. A strip of shelf liner fixes it, but it's an oversight.

## Who should buy this

- **First-time cultivators** who want a "set and forget" chamber without drilling holes into a generic Sterilite bin
- **Intermediate growers** running 2–4 tubs who need consistent, repeatable results
- **Anyone who's killed a grow by over-fanning** — the passive airflow design removes that failure mode entirely

Skip it if you're doing bag grows only, or if you're a commercial-scale operator who needs 50+ tubs at a lower per-unit cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this for something other than mushrooms?**
A: Yes — it works as a seedling germination chamber, a reptile humidity box, or a compost worm bin. The filter disks are mushroom-optimized but not mushroom-exclusive.

**Q: Do I need to drill additional holes for larger grows?**
A: For most cube varieties, no. The stock configuration handles a 1:1 spawn-to-substrate ratio up to about 5 pounds. If you're running a 6+ pound block, add two extra holes on the long sides.

**Q: How do I clean it between flushes?**
A: Soak in hot water with dish soap, scrub with a soft sponge, then wipe with 70% isopropyl. Avoid abrasive pads — they'll scratch the clear plastic and make future contam spotting harder.
