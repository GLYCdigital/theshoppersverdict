If you're shopping for a rabbit cage, you already know the drill: most options are either too small, too flimsy, or built like a prison cell. The GDLF Two Floors 58" Wooden Hutch tries to split the difference — and for the most part, it lands on the right side of the fence.

**What's actually in the box**

You get a two-story pine wood hutch measuring 58 inches long, 23.5 inches deep, and 37.5 inches tall. The bottom floor has a removable plastic tray for easy cleaning, while the upper level connects via a wooden ramp with traction strips. The whole thing sits on four locking casters. Hardware, tools, and a clearly diagrammed (if not wordy) instruction manual are included. No bedding, food bowls, or accessories — this is just the structure.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The pine frame is thicker than most cages in this price range — roughly 0.6-inch panels — and the manufacturer applies a waterproof coating that actually holds up. I've seen multiple reports of rabbits chewing on the corners without the wood splintering, which is a common failure point on cheaper hutches. The wire mesh is welded, not spot-welded like some budget options, so it resists bending when a rabbit kicks or pushes against it.

The casters are a standout feature. Locking wheels on all four corners mean you can roll this from a sunny corner to a shaded spot without waking the rabbit. The locks engage firmly, so it won't roll during normal activity.

That said, the upper-level wire floor is a genuine concern. Rabbits with thin fur on their feet — think Rex breeds or young kits — can develop sore hocks from prolonged standing on the grate. The fix is simple: add a fleece liner or a wooden resting board on the upper level. Several reviewers mentioned this exact workaround, and it's worth budgeting for.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The size gets the most love. Owners with two dwarf rabbits report both can stretch out fully on the bottom floor without crowding. One reviewer noted, "My pair of Netherland dwarfs have plenty of room to run laps between levels — it's the first cage they haven't tried to escape." Another praised the durability: "Six months in, daily cleaning, and the wood still looks new. My previous cage was falling apart by now."

The cleaning trays also earn points. They slide out from the front, so you don't have to reach through the door or disassemble anything to scoop. For a 58-inch cage, that's a convenience you'll appreciate every single day.

**Where it falls short**

Assembly is the biggest hurdle. Expect a solid two hours with a screwdriver and patience. Several reviewers noted that pre-drilled holes didn't always align perfectly with the brackets, requiring a bit of persuasion. The instructions are mostly exploded diagrams with minimal text — if you're not handy, you might want a second set of hands.

The trays are shallow. A rabbit that drinks heavily or knocks over a water bowl can flood the tray quickly, and the wood frame doesn't have a plastic lip to contain spills. You'll want a heavy ceramic bowl or a bottle-style waterer to minimize mess.

Finally, the ramp. It's functional, but the traction strips are adhesive and can peel off over time. A few reviewers replaced them with carpet tape. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

This hutch suits indoor owners with one medium rabbit (like a Holland Lop or Mini Rex) or a bonded pair of dwarfs. It's also a solid choice if you need mobility — the wheels make it genuinely easy to relocate. Skip it if you have a large breed like a Flemish Giant; the 23.5-inch depth will feel cramped. And if you're not comfortable with a multi-hour assembly project, factor in the cost of hiring a handyman or buying a pre-assembled option.

**FAQ**

*Q: Can this hutch be used outdoors?*
A: The wood is waterproof-coated, but it's not weatherproof. Rain, humidity, and direct sun will degrade it over time. This is an indoor cage.

*Q: Is the wire floor safe for rabbit feet?*
A: It's safe for most rabbits, but sensitive breeds need a liner or resting board. Don't skip this if your rabbit is prone to sore hocks.

*Q: How many rabbits can fit comfortably?*
A: Two dwarf breeds or one medium rabbit. A single large breed will feel confined.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a spacious, attractive indoor hutch that rolls easily and holds up to daily use. **Skip it if** you're not prepared for a lengthy assembly or you have a large-breed rabbit.
