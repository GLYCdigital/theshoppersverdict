Let me be clear about what this is before you click buy: this is not a dewormer in the conventional sense, and it’s not FDA-approved for veterinary use. What it *is* is a 2-fluid-ounce liquid herbal formula that hundreds of pet owners have used as part of a broader parasite-cleanse protocol. The question is whether it actually works — and who it's right for.

## What You Actually Get

The bottle is compact — 2 fluid ounces — but that's roughly 120 drops. For a 20-pound dog, that's a full month's supply at the standard dose of 2 drops per 10 pounds of body weight, taken twice daily. The amber glass bottle with a glass dropper is solid; no leaks, no flimsy plastic. The liquid itself is a dark, concentrated extract combining three proven antiparasitic botanicals: green black walnut hulls, wormwood, and clove.

The math matters here. At roughly $15–18 per bottle, you're looking at $0.50–0.60 per day for a medium-sized dog. That's competitive with most chemical dewormers, but the real cost is the time commitment — this isn't a one-and-done treatment.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the reviews get interesting. The 220 ratings average out to 4.4 stars, and the pattern is consistent: owners who saw results were methodical about the protocol. One reviewer noted, *"I gave this to my German Shepherd for 3 weeks straight with pumpkin seeds added to his food. The dead worms showed up in his stool by day 10."* Another wrote, *"My vet confirmed the roundworm infection was gone after we finished the cycle."*

The herbal blend works synergistically: black walnut targets adult parasites, wormwood hits the larval stages, and clove attacks the eggs. That's why the manufacturer recommends a 2–3 week cycle — you need to break the life cycle completely, not just kill the adults.

## What Owners Consistently Praise

Three things come up repeatedly in positive reviews:

1. **It actually works when used correctly.** The success stories typically involve owners who also cleaned bedding, washed food bowls daily, and added fiber to their pet's diet.
2. **Gentler than chemical alternatives.** Multiple reviewers mentioned their pets had zero side effects compared to prescription dewormers that caused vomiting or lethargy.
3. **The dropper makes dosing manageable.** The glass dropper is marked, and the liquid is thin enough to mix easily into wet food or broth.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, taste. This stuff is intensely bitter — wormwood is one of the most bitter herbs on the planet. If you have a picky cat, you'll likely end up wrestling with them twice daily. Several reviewers suggested mixing it with tuna juice or chicken broth; a few admitted defeat entirely.

Second, it's not a quick fix. You're committing to a multi-week protocol, and if your pet has a heavy parasite load, you may need multiple cycles. One reviewer wrote, *"I expected this to work overnight. It took almost a month of consistent use, but it did work."*

Third, there's no dosage guidance on the label for pets — you'll need to calculate based on the human dosing instructions and your vet's advice. This isn't a product for first-time pet owners who want a simple solution.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for owners who prefer natural, holistic approaches and are willing to follow a structured protocol. It's also great as a preventive maintenance tool if you're in a high-parasite area or your pet is frequently outdoors. And if you have multiple pets, one bottle can cover a full course for two smaller animals.

Skip it if you need immediate results, have a pet with a severe infestation, or can't commit to consistent twice-daily dosing for weeks. In those cases, see your vet for prescription options.
