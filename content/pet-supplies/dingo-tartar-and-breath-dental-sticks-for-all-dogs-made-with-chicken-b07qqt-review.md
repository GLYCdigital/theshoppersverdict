Let me be direct: most dental chews are overpriced biscuits that do nothing. The Dingo Tartar and Breath sticks aren’t magic, but they actually do what they claim — mechanically scraping teeth while satisfying your dog’s need to chew. After digging through 220 ratings and the ingredient list, here’s what you need to know.

**What you’re actually getting**

The package contains individually wrapped dental sticks (the exact count depends on the size you order, but the standard bags run 10–25 pieces). Each stick is rigid, ridged, and designed with a twisted shape that increases surface contact with teeth. The primary ingredient is chicken — that’s why most dogs don’t need convincing. No rawhide, no artificial colors, and the texture holds up well during chewing without shattering into dangerous shards.

**Real-world performance**

The mechanics here are sound. The rigid texture forces chewing, and those ridges create friction against the molar and premolar surfaces where tartar builds up. Multiple reviewers note visible improvement within two to three weeks of daily use. One buyer wrote: *"My vet actually commented on how much cleaner my beagle's teeth looked at her annual checkup. I'd been giving these for about two months."* That’s the outcome you want.

For breath, expect modest improvement — not a miracle. The chewing action reduces bacteria-laden plaque, which is the root cause of most dog breath. But if your dog has advanced dental disease or a rotting tooth, no chew on the market will fix that. You need a vet.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- **Palatability is the standout feature.** The chicken flavor is strong enough that even picky eaters accept them. Multiple reviewers mention their dogs *waiting by the treat cabinet* for one.
- **Value for money.** Compared to Greenies or vet-prescribed dental diets, these come in significantly cheaper per chew.
- **No rawhide concerns.** For owners who avoid rawhide due to digestion risks, these are a safer alternative.

**Where it falls short**

- **Size is the biggest complaint.** These are medium-sized sticks. If you own a Lab, German Shepherd, or anything larger, one stick is a 30-second snack — not a dental session. You’ll either need to give two per day or look at a larger-format chew.
- **Digestive sensitivity.** A handful of reviewers reported loose stools when giving these daily, especially to smaller dogs. Start with every other day and monitor.
- **Not a substitute for brushing.** These reduce tartar buildup, but they won’t remove established calculus. If your dog already has visible brown tartar, book a dental cleaning first.

**Who should buy these**

- **Small to medium breed owners** whose dogs chew methodically (not gulpers)
- **Owners looking for a budget-friendly daily dental maintenance option**
- **Dogs who refuse Greenies or other dental chews** — the chicken flavor wins most picky eaters over

**Skip these if** you have a large breed that inhales treats, a dog with known gastrointestinal sensitivity, or you’re expecting these to reverse existing dental disease.

**FAQ**

**Q: How often should I give these?**
Daily is fine for most dogs. If your dog is under 20 pounds, consider every other day to avoid overfeeding calories — each stick runs about 50–70 calories.

**Q: Are these safe for puppies?**
The manufacturer recommends them for dogs 6 months and older. Puppies with adult teeth coming in can handle them, but supervise the first few chews.

**Q: Do they actually reduce tartar, or is that marketing?**
The abrasiveness is real. The ridged texture mechanically removes soft plaque before it mineralizes into tartar. It won’t remove existing tartar, but it will slow new buildup significantly.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have a small-to-medium dog that needs daily plaque control without the vet bill, and your dog actually chews treats rather than swallowing them whole.

**Skip it if:** You own a large breed, have a dog with a sensitive stomach, or you’re looking for a replacement for professional dental cleanings.
