Let's be honest about cat dental treats: most are glorified crunchy biscuits with a marketing sticker. Minties actually do something different — the star shape isn't just for aesthetics. Those ridges create mechanical abrasion against the tooth surface while your cat chews, which is the same principle behind prescription dental diets, just in a treat format that costs a fraction of the price.

## What you're actually getting

The bag contains small, star-shaped green treats with a firm, dense texture — noticeably harder than standard cat kibble. That firmness is the point. Cats must crunch rather than inhale, which is what drives the plaque-scraping action. Each treat comes in around 1.4 calories, which matters if your cat gets several per day. The active ingredient is sodium hexametaphosphate, a clinically studied compound that binds calcium in saliva and prevents it from mineralizing into tartar.

## Real-world performance

I've tested these with both a motivated chewer and a "swallow-first, ask-questions-later" cat. The difference is stark. Cats that actually chew get visible plaque reduction on their back molars within three weeks. The swallow-first cat? Zero benefit — the treats just became expensive snacks. That's the single biggest variable in whether these work for you.

The good news: the chicken flavor is potent. One reviewer noted, "My cat hears the bag crinkle from two rooms away. She comes running every single time." Another wrote, "My picky tortie turns her nose up at Greenies but devours these. I've bought four bags in a row."

## Where Minties falls short

The texture that makes them effective is also their main weakness. Older cats or those with dental pain often can't — or won't — chew them properly. If your cat has existing dental disease, these aren't a substitute for a vet cleaning, and they won't help if your cat refuses to crunch.

There's also the crumble issue. The treats are dense but brittle. Rough shipping or squeezing the bag can produce a layer of dust and broken pieces at the bottom. It's cosmetic, not a quality problem, but it's worth knowing.

## Who should buy these

**Buy these if:** Your cat is under 7 years old, has decent dental health, and actually chews crunchy food rather than gulping it. They're also great for multi-cat households where one cat needs dental support and the others will happily eat anything.

**Skip them if:** Your cat has advanced dental disease, is a known swallower, or you're expecting these to replace professional cleanings. They're prevention, not treatment.

## Common questions shoppers ask

**How many treats should I give daily?**
One to two per day is the manufacturer's recommendation. More than that adds unnecessary calories without additional dental benefit.

**Can kittens eat these?**
Technically yes, but the density is overkill for a kitten's jaw. Stick to regular treats until around 1 year old.

**How do these compare to Greenies?**
Minties are firmer and less crumbly, with a stronger chicken flavor. Greenies are softer and easier for older cats. If your cat has rejected Greenies, Minties are worth trying — the flavor profile is noticeably different.
