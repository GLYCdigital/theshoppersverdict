If you've ever replaced a plastic litter box every six months because it started smelling no matter how thoroughly you scrubbed it, you already understand why the KITCATY Stainless Steel Litter Box has earned a 4.7-star average from 235 buyers. ## What You Actually Get

The KITCATY is a generously proportioned open pan — roughly 24 inches long, 18 inches wide, and 7 inches deep. That depth is the headline feature. Most standard pans run 5–6 inches deep, which means litter scatter becomes a daily ritual. The extra inch might not sound like much, but for cats that dig to China before doing their business, it's the difference between sweeping the floor twice a day and once every few days.

The pan arrives with a rolled rim — a subtle design element that matters more than you'd think. It prevents the edge from digging into your palms when you carry it, and it adds rigidity so the pan doesn't bow or flex when full. The steel itself is brushed silver, which resists showing scratches and paw prints. No included scoop, no mat, no liner — just the pan. That's fine; those add-ons are usually junk anyway.

## Performance and Build Quality

Stainless steel's advantage isn't just odor resistance — it's also easier to clean. Clumps release from the surface without the scraping and scrubbing that plastic demands. You can use enzymatic cleaners, diluted bleach, or even run it through a rinse cycle without worrying about chemical absorption. The material is naturally antimicrobial compared to plastic, which is a meaningful benefit for multi-cat households.

Buyers consistently highlight the odor control. One reviewer noted, "I was skeptical about spending this much on a litter box, but the smell difference is night and day. My old plastic box smelled even after washing; this doesn't." Another long-term owner mentioned the durability: "Had it for eight months now and it looks brand new. My previous plastic pans were stained and scratched within weeks."

The size is genuinely XL. Large-breed cats — Maine Coons, Ragdolls, or just chunky domestic shorthairs — have room to turn around and position themselves comfortably. Senior cats with mobility issues also benefit from the low entry point combined with high walls; they can step in easily but don't have to worry about spraying over the edge.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. This is an open-top pan, which means zero odor containment beyond what the material itself provides. If your cat produces particularly pungent waste, you'll still smell it until you scoop. Covered boxes trap odors better (and also trap dust and ammonia inside, which is its own problem). Some cats also prefer privacy — if yours is skittish, they may refuse to use an exposed pan.

The size is a double-edged sword. Yes, it's great for big cats, but it also demands real floor space. If you're trying to squeeze it into a half-bath or a laundry room corner, you'll likely find it doesn't fit. You need roughly 24×18 inches of clear floor area, plus room for the cat to approach and exit comfortably.

There's also the price consideration. Stainless steel costs more than plastic — there's no way around that. And because it's open-top, you're paying a premium for a pan that doesn't offer the privacy features some competing stainless models provide (some have hoods, albeit at a higher price point).

## Who Should Buy This

**Get it if:** You're tired of replacing plastic boxes every 6–12 months, you have a large-breed cat, or you're sensitive to litter box odors and want a material that doesn't retain them.

**Skip it if:** Your cat demands a covered box, your space is tight, or you're on a strict budget and would rather buy three plastic pans over the same period.

## FAQ

**Q: Is stainless steel actually better at controlling odor than plastic?**
Yes, measurably. Plastic is porous and absorbs ammonia and bacteria over time. Stainless steel is non-porous, so odors don't penetrate the surface. It won't eliminate the smell of fresh waste, but it won't accumulate and amplify it either.

**Q: Will this fit in a standard litter box enclosure or furniture?**
Probably not. Most litter box cabinets are designed for pans around 22×16 inches. At 24×18, this pan is larger than typical enclosures. Measure your space before buying.

**Q: How often do I need to deep-clean it?**
Weekly washing with mild soap and water is sufficient for most households. Because the surface is non-porous, you can use stronger disinfectants more frequently without degrading the material — something you can't do with plastic.
