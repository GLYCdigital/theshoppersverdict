# HOOBRO Dog Crate Furniture for 2 Dogs, 63" Large Double Dog — An Honest Deep Dive

Two dogs, one room, zero floor space left. That's the problem this 63-inch HOOBRO unit tries to solve — and for most households, it genuinely does. But "double dog crate furniture" covers a lot of ground, so let's cut through the marketing and talk about what you're actually getting.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package arrives flat-packed in a heavy 70+ lb box. Inside you'll find:

- Two side-by-side wire crates (each 23.6" L x 21.7" W x 22.4" H)
- A particleboard cabinet frame with a water-resistant laminate finish
- Two sliding front doors with spring-loaded locking latches
- Two removable plastic pull-out trays
- All hardware, Allen wrenches, and an instruction booklet

The unit measures 63" wide x 21.7" deep x 33.9" tall when assembled. The top surface is 63" x 15.7" and rated to hold up to 110 lbs — so yes, you can set a lamp, books, or a small TV on it without worry. The photos in the listing show it styled as a sideboard, and that's not a bait-and-switch; it genuinely looks like furniture, not a cage.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The frame is 0.6" thick particleboard with a laminated wood-grain finish (dark brown or black options). It's not heirloom oak, but it's sturdier than most crates in this price bracket. The wire crates are welded steel with a rust-resistant coating, and the sliding doors glide smoothly on the tracks once aligned properly.

The dual locking latches on each door are a thoughtful touch — one on the top, one on the bottom — so even a determined escape artist has to figure out two mechanisms. The pull-out trays are legitimately useful; you can slide them out, wipe them down, and slide them back without dismantling anything.

**What buyers consistently praise:**

One verified purchaser noted: *"I was skeptical about the particleboard, but after 4 months with two 40-lb labs who treat it like a wrestling ring, it's still solid. No wobble, no sagging."*

Another: *"The top really does hold my 50-inch TV. I didn't believe the weight rating, but it hasn't budged."*

The most common positive thread: it solves the "two crates take up half my living room" problem in one clean unit.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations.

**Assembly is the biggest friction point.** Multiple reviewers report 45–60 minute build times with 40+ screws. A few noted that the pre-drilled holes on the upper cabinet section didn't align perfectly, requiring a bit of persuasion. Not a dealbreaker, but budget the time and have a cordless drill handy.

**The "large" label is optimistic.** Each crate interior is roughly 23.6" long. That's comfortable for a beagle, a corgi, or a small-to-medium lab. But if you have a 60+ lb dog that's 30" from nose to tail, they will not stretch out fully. Measure your dog before you buy — this is not for giant breeds.

**The laminate surface scratches.** Sliding a heavy ceramic planter or metal decor across the top will leave marks. Use a protective mat if you're putting anything abrasive up there.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Multi-dog households** with small-to-medium breeds (under 50 lbs) who need separate crate spaces but lack floor space
- **Owners who hate the "cage look"** — this genuinely blends into living room furniture
- **People who want a functional side table** — the 110-lb top rating is real, and it's a solid TV stand height

**Skip it if:** you have large/giant breeds, you want something you can assemble in 15 minutes, or you need a fully enclosed crate for a dog that's a serious chewer (the wire is sturdy but not indestructible).

## FAQ

**Q: Can I remove the divider between the two crates to make one large space?**
A: No. The crates are separate wire enclosures with a solid wood panel between them. If you need one large crate for a single big dog, look elsewhere.

**Q: Does the top really hold 110 lbs?**
A: Yes, according to the manufacturer and confirmed by multiple buyers who report placing TVs and heavy decor on it. The reinforced top is one of the best features.

**Q: Is it safe for a dog that likes to chew?**
A: The wire is welded and coated, so it resists moderate chewing. But particleboard corners are vulnerable if your dog targets the frame. For aggressive chewers, a fully steel crate is safer.
