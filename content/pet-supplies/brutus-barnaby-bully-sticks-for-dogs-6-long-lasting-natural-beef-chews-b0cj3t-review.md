**The 6-inch bully stick is the Goldilocks of dog chews** — long enough to keep a determined chewer busy, short enough to avoid the awkward "last two inches" fumbling that ends with a swallowed nub. BRUTUS & BARNABY's offering hits that sweet spot, and after digging through 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, the consensus is clear: this is a solid, single-ingredient chew that most dogs demolish with enthusiasm.

## What you actually get

The package contains 6-inch bully sticks made from 100% beef pizzle — that's the muscle, not the organ, so the protein content is high and the fat is minimal. There are no rawhide bases, no artificial smoke flavoring sprayed on to mask quality issues, and no binding agents holding scraps together. What you see is what your dog gets: a dried, single-source protein chew.

These are thick enough to satisfy medium and large breeds. A 40-pound border collie will work through one in roughly 30–45 minutes depending on persistence; a 70-pound lab might finish in 20. The texture starts rock-hard and gradually softens as saliva breaks it down, which is exactly what you want for dental benefits.

## Real-world performance

The standout feature buyers consistently mention is the **odor control**. One reviewer noted, "I've tried three other brands and my living room smelled like a barn for days. These have a faint smell up close but nothing that lingers." That tracks with the manufacturing process — BRUTUS & BARNABY slow-roasts rather than smoke-dries, which cuts the pungent ammonia-like smell that turns many owners off bully sticks entirely.

Durability is good but not exceptional. The sticks hold up well against moderate chewers, but multiple reviews flag that power chewers can snap them into smaller chunks. One customer wrote, "My German Shepherd cracked one in half in about ten minutes and I had to take the pieces away before she swallowed them." That's not a defect — it's physics. No dried meat product survives a determined 90-pound jaw indefinitely.

## What buyers consistently praise

- **Digestibility**: Multiple reviewers with sensitive-stomach dogs report no vomiting or diarrhea, which is the gold standard for any chew.
- **Clean ingredients**: The single-ingredient label matters to owners who've been burned by "natural" chews that secretly contain hide glue or glycerin.
- **Value for moderate chewers**: If your dog isn't a demolition expert, a single stick delivers solid engagement time for the price.

## Where it falls short

The **price per ounce** is the biggest hurdle. These run noticeably more expensive than rawhide rolls or compressed bone chews, and if your dog goes through two or three per week, the cost adds up fast. There's also no variety in thickness — every stick in the bag is roughly the same diameter, so if your dog prefers a thicker or thinner chew, you're stuck with what's offered.

The **snapping risk** for aggressive chewers deserves emphasis. While no bully stick is indestructible, thinner sticks like these can fracture into sharp-edged shards. If your dog gulps rather than gnaws, supervision isn't optional — it's mandatory.

## Who should buy this

- **Owners of moderate chewers** (15–60 lbs) who want a clean, digestible chew that lasts 20–40 minutes
- **Households sensitive to odor** — this is one of the least offensive bully sticks on the market
- **Dogs with poultry allergies** who need a beef-based alternative to chicken jerky or duck chews

**Skip it if** you have a power chewer who destroys everything in under five minutes, or if you're on a tight budget and need the cheapest chew per hour of engagement.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does one stick actually last?**
A: For a typical medium breed, expect 25–45 minutes of active chewing. Small dogs might stretch it to an hour; heavy chewers can finish in 15–20. The last inch softens into a rubbery nub that some dogs swallow whole — take it away when it gets small.

**Q: Are these safe for puppies?**
A: Yes, for puppies over 6 months with adult teeth. Younger puppies may struggle to break down the density, and the hard texture can be tough on developing jaws. Supervise any puppy's first chew session.

**Q: Do these smell as bad as other bully sticks?**
A: No. The slow-roasting process keeps the odor significantly lower than smoke-dried alternatives. You'll notice a mild scent when the bag opens, but it dissipates quickly and doesn't cling to furniture.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a clean, digestible, reasonably long-lasting chew without the barnyard odor that plagues most bully sticks. **Skip it if** you've got a power chewer who needs industrial-strength bones or you're watching every penny of the pet budget.
