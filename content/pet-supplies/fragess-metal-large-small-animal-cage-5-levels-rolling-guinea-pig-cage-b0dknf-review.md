**Is this the vertical cage solution your small pet actually needs?**

You've seen the cramped wire cages at big-box stores. You know the ones — barely bigger than a shoebox, with a single shelf and a wheel that squeaks at 2 AM. The Fragess 5-Level cage is not that. This is a genuine vertical habitat that changes how your small animal uses space.

---

**What's Actually in the Box**

Fragess ships this as a flat-pack project. You get five wire levels, four ramps with safety ledges, a powder-coated metal base tray, two locking casters and two standard ones, plus the hardware bag. The assembly manual is mostly diagrams — you'll want the exploded view handy.

The footprint is compact — roughly 24 inches by 18 inches — but the height is where it shines at nearly 40 inches tall. That's five full floors of exploration space for a guinea pig, rat, or chinchilla that would otherwise be pacing a single-level enclosure.

---

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The powder-coated metal frame is the standout feature. Unlike cheaper cages that rust after a few months of urine exposure, this finish holds up. The wire spacing is tight enough to prevent escape but open enough for ventilation — critical for guinea pigs prone to respiratory issues.

The locking casters are a thoughtful touch. Two wheels lock independently, so the cage stays put when you're cleaning or when kids are nearby. Rolling it between rooms is smooth, even with a full bedding load.

Cleaning is where this cage earns its keep. Each level lifts out independently, so you can spot-clean one floor without dismantling the whole structure. The base tray slides out for deep cleans. That said, the tray's shallow depth is the cage's weakest link — wire floors above mean droppings fall through, but bedding will scatter with enthusiastic diggers.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the limitations. Assembly is the biggest hurdle — the included Allen wrench is genuinely poor quality, and you'll want your own tools. Budget 45-60 minutes and recruit a second person for the upper levels.

The ramp spacing is a real consideration for larger guinea pigs. If you've got a 700g+ adult, those ramps get tight. This cage is better suited to rats, ferrets, or smaller guinea pigs. Chinchillas will love it — they're natural climbers.

The shallow tray means you'll be sweeping up bedding more often than with a deeper base. Consider a litter box on the bottom level to minimize scatter.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Rat owners** — this is near-ideal rat real estate. Vertical space, good ventilation, easy cleaning.
- **Apartment dwellers** — the compact footprint maximizes vertical space.
- **Smaller guinea pig owners** — under 600g, your pigs will navigate the ramps fine.
- **Skip it if** you have large breed guinea pigs or expect a no-assembly, plug-and-play setup.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I add a wheel inside?**
A: Yes, but keep it on the bottom level. The upper floors have wire mesh that can catch small wheel spokes.

**Q: How often should I deep clean?**
A: With 2 small animals, spot-clean daily and do a full tray swap every 5-7 days. The removable levels make this manageable.

**Q: Will a ferret escape?**
A: Ferrets are escape artists. The wire spacing is secure, but the door latch is simple — add a carabiner if you own a determined ferret.
