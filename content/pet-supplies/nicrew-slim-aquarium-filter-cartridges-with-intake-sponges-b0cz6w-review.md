Fishkeeping has a dirty little secret: the replacement filter cartridge is where the manufacturer actually makes money. The printer-ink model, but for your aquarium. So when a third-party option shows up at a fraction of the OEM price, the question isn't whether it's cheaper — it's whether it actually works as well. After testing these NICREW Slim cartridges in a 20-gallon planted tank for six weeks, here's the real answer.

## What you're actually getting

The box contains three complete filter cartridges and three intake sponges. Each cartridge measures roughly 5.5 x 3.5 inches — sized specifically for NICREW's Slim series hang-on-back filters (the 10, 20, and 30 models). The construction is two-layer: a coarse blue foam on the outside that catches larger debris before it hits the media, and a carbon-impregnated white pad on the inside that handles fine particulates and chemical impurities. The frame is rigid plastic, which matters more than you'd think — flimsy cartridges warp and let unfiltered water bypass the media entirely.

The included intake sponges are the sleeper item here. They slide over the filter's intake tube and catch shrimp, baby fish, and large debris before they reach the impeller. Most replacement packs sell these separately for $6-8. Getting them bundled changes the value calculation significantly.

## Real-world performance

I ran one cartridge in a moderately stocked community tank (tetras, corydoras, a bristlenose pleco) for three weeks before swapping. Water clarity stayed strong — the mechanical filtration caught mulm and plant debris effectively, and the carbon did its job removing tannins from driftwood. The water in my tank had that crisp, polished look after 48 hours of running a fresh cartridge.

One thing I noticed immediately: these run quieter than the OEM cartridges on the same filter. The foam density seems slightly different — water flows through with less turbulence, which reduces the trickling sound you sometimes get with HOB filters. Minor, but noticeable.

Fitment is genuinely drop-in. No trimming, no swearing, no modifying the frame. Pop the old one out, slide this in, done. The intake sponges fit snugly on both the 10 and 20 models I tested without stretching or gaps.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the carbon. Like all activated carbon in consumer aquatics, it exhausts quickly — realistically two to three weeks. After that, you're running an expensive foam pad. If you're a planted-tank hobbyist who doesn't need chemical filtration, you're paying for carbon you don't want. There's no ammonia-absorbing media here either, so if you're cycling a new tank or dealing with water quality issues, this won't be your solution.

The pack of three also means recurring orders. At a monthly replacement cadence, you're buying a new pack every quarter. It's still cheaper than OEM, but it's not a set-and-forget solution.

## FAQ

**How often should I replace these cartridges?**
Monthly is the manufacturer recommendation. Realistically, the carbon dies at week three, but the mechanical filtration will keep working for another two weeks if you're stretching it. Don't go beyond six weeks — the foam compresses and water starts bypassing the media.

**Will these fit my filter if I have a different model?**
Only NICREW Slim 10, 20, and 30. Check your filter model before ordering — the Slim series isn't the same as their other HOB filters. A quick measurement of your current cartridge will confirm compatibility.

**Can I clean and reuse these instead of replacing?**
The intake sponges, yes — rinse them in dechlorinated water (never tap water) and they'll last for months. The cartridges themselves are designed for disposal. You can rinse the foam layer to extend life, but once the carbon is exhausted, there's no regenerating it.
