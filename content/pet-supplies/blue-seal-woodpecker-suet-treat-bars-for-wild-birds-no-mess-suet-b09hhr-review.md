If you've been feeding suet for a while, you know the struggle: cheap cakes melt into a greasy puddle by July, or they're so crumbly the squirrels treat them like a buffet. The Blue Seal Woodpecker Suet Treat Bars take a different approach — these are dense, no-mesh blocks designed specifically for woodpeckers, and after 229 ratings sitting at a 4.6 average, they're clearly doing something right.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains two suet bars, each sized to fit standard suet cages (roughly 4.5 x 4.5 inches). Unlike traditional suet cakes wrapped in mesh, these come in a plastic tray with no covering — which is the whole point of the "no mess" claim. The ingredient list leans heavy on peanuts and sunflower seeds, both proven woodpecker attractants, mixed with rendered beef suet for that high-fat energy birds need in winter and during nesting season.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this product separates itself from the discount-bin suet. The bars are **firm**. Not crumbly, not greasy — dense enough that you can handle them without coating your hands in tallow. That density translates to slower consumption, meaning one bar lasts noticeably longer than standard cakes. In summer heat, the no-melt formulation holds its shape; it softens but doesn't drip through the cage onto your deck.

As for actually attracting woodpeckers? That's the core question, and buyer feedback is consistent. One reviewer noted, *"Downy woodpeckers found this within hours — I've had it up for a week and they're hitting it daily."* Another confirmed, *"The red-bellied woodpeckers ignore everything else and go straight for these."* The peanut content is doing heavy lifting here; if you've had trouble getting woodpeckers to visit, this is a legitimate fix.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The no-mesh design gets the most love. Traditional suet wrappers create litter as birds peck through, and they're a nightmare to remove. These bars eliminate that entirely — birds eat every scrap, and when the bar is gone, the cage is clean. Multiple reviewers mention how much less waste they see compared to mesh-wrapped cakes.

The durability also earns repeat mentions. One buyer wrote, *"These don't fall apart in the rain like other suet. I've had one up for two weeks in Florida humidity and it's still intact."* That's a strong endorsement for southern buyers who've watched cheaper suet dissolve.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has three honest drawbacks. First, the bars come in a rigid plastic tray that requires some careful handling — a few reviewers report the bars cracking when they try to pop them out. It's not a dealbreaker, but you'll want to flex the tray gently.

Second, **squirrels will absolutely find these**. The peanut content is a magnet, and without a caged feeder, you'll watch your bars disappear in an afternoon. If squirrels are a problem in your yard, budget for a squirrel-proof cage before ordering.

Third, the no-mesh design means no protection from the elements. In heavy rain or extreme humidity, the outer layer can soften before birds get through it. It won't melt into a puddle, but it's not immune to weather.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for you if you're specifically targeting woodpeckers and want a low-maintenance option that won't create feeder litter. It's ideal for:
- **Backyard birders** who've tried generic suet and gotten mostly starlings

Skip it if you have a serious squirrel problem without a protective cage, or if you prefer mesh-wrapped cakes for their weather resistance.
