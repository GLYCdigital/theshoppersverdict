**Opening Hook**

Most metal coops under $500 are glorified wire cages with a plywood box stapled on. The Guiferland Large Metal Chicken Coop isn't that. This is a fully galvanized walk-in run with an integrated hen house — and after digging through 200 ratings and the spec sheet, I can tell you it's a serious step up from the flimsy options flooding Amazon. But "serious" also means "serious assembly," and that's where you need to go in with open eyes.

**What You Actually Get**

The box arrives heavy — roughly 90 pounds of pre-cut galvanized steel panels, welded wire mesh sections, and hardware. You get the main coop house with nesting boxes, a ramp, the enclosed run (approximately 8 ft long x 6 ft wide x 6 ft tall at peak), and a sliding door between the house and run. The run floor is integrated into the frame, which is a big deal: many competitors sell open-bottom runs that predators can tunnel under. The mesh is fused onto the panels, not stapled, which explains why buyers consistently report it surviving windstorms that flattened their neighbors' wooden coops.

**Real-World Performance and Build Quality**

The galvanized coating is the star here. One reviewer who's owned it for eight months noted, "No rust spots after a full winter of rain and snow — my old wooden coop was rotting by now." The walk-in height is the feature you'll appreciate daily. Cleaning the run, filling the feeder, and collecting eggs without crawling is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade.

The hen house itself is compact but functional: two nesting boxes with an accessible rear door, plus a roosting bar. It comfortably houses 4–6 standard hens, or up to 8 bantams. Ventilation is decent — there are mesh panels near the top of the house that provide airflow without drafts at roost level.

**Where It Falls Short (Honestly)**

Assembly is the biggest complaint across the reviews. Multiple buyers describe the same experience: "Took my husband and I about four hours. The instructions are basically pictures, and some bolts didn't line up perfectly." Plan for a full afternoon with two people, a cordless drill, and patience.

The wire gauge is adequate for foxes and stray dogs, but it's not heavy-duty predator-proof. If you have persistent raccoons or coyotes in your area, you'll want to reinforce the base perimeter with pavers or buried hardware cloth. And several reviewers mention the included tarp for the run roof doesn't cover the full span — you'll want to add your own waterproofing if you live in a wet climate.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Suburban backyard keepers** who want a permanent, low-maintenance run that doesn't look like an eyesore
- **Anyone tired of replacing rotting wooden coops** every 2–3 years — galvanized steel will outlast them
- **Flock owners with 4–6 hens** who want walk-in convenience for daily chores
- **Not ideal for:** complete beginners who want a 30-minute setup, or anyone with heavy predator pressure expecting fortress-level security

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask**

**Q: Can one person assemble this?**
Technically yes, but you'll struggle. The panels are large and awkward. Two people make it a 3–4 hour job. One person will take 6+ hours and a lot of cursing.

**Q: Is the run truly predator-proof?**
It stops digging predators because the floor is integrated. But the mesh can be bent by determined raccoons. Add a skirt of hardware cloth around the base if predators are a known issue in your area.

**Q: Does it come with everything needed, including tools?**
All hardware is included, but you'll need your own drill, a socket set, and possibly self-tapping screws for the roof peak. No tools ship in the box.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a galvanized, rust-resistant coop that will outlast wooden alternatives, you have 4–6 hens, and you're willing to invest an afternoon in assembly with a helper.

**Skip it if:** You need a quick weekend project, you're on a tight budget, or you face serious predator pressure that demands heavy-gauge reinforced housing.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Deducting half a star for the frustrating assembly experience and thin tarp. The core structure, however, is genuinely well-built for the price, and buyers who push through the setup overwhelmingly report satisfaction. At 4.0 stars across 200 ratings, this is a solid, honest performer — just budget your time accordingly.
