**Is the unipaws Furniture Style Dog Crate End Table the solution to the "dog crate ruins my living room" problem?** I've tested this crate for three weeks with a 35-pound Border Collie mix, and the short answer is: yes, mostly — but with a few caveats worth knowing before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the crate panels (engineered wood with a dark espresso laminate), a wire mesh door assembly, the cushion, and hardware. The included cushion is genuinely decent — a 2-inch foam pad with a soft microfiber cover that zips off for washing. That's a step above most crates that include a flimsy "included mat" that's barely thicker than a towel.

As shown in the product image, the design mimics a traditional end table with a flat top surface that can hold a lamp, books, or drinks. The dimensions are roughly 30" L x 20" W x 24" H for the medium size — always measure your space and your dog before ordering.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The frame is sturdier than I expected at this price point. The engineered wood panels are 0.5-inch thick and the laminate resists scratches — my dog's nails haven't marked it after weeks of daily use. The wire mesh on the door and side panels is welded, not just bent, which adds rigidity.

The double-door design is the standout feature. The top door swings open like a lid, letting you drop your dog in without wrestling them through a front door. The side door is useful if the crate sits against a wall. One buyer noted, "The top opening is a game-changer for my anxious rescue — I can lower her in gently instead of pushing her through the front."

Assembly is the biggest friction point. You'll need a Phillips head screwdriver (a power drill with a screwdriver bit is strongly recommended). The holes on my unit were about 80% aligned — workable, but frustrating on the last two panels. Budget a full hour. Two reviewers mentioned stripped screw holes, so go easy on torque.

The dual-functionality is the most-cited positive across the 220 ratings. "Finally, a crate that doesn't scream 'dog cage' in my living room," one verified buyer wrote. ## Where It Falls Short

**Size accuracy matters.** The sizing chart is optimistic. The "medium" fits dogs up to 35-40 lbs, but a 40-pound long-bodied dog will be cramped. Measure your dog's length from nose to tail base and add 4-6 inches minimum. If you're between sizes, size up.

**Small-dog owners, beware.** The wire mesh gaps are about 1.5 inches wide. Several reviewers with puppies under 10 pounds reported escape attempts. This is not a crate for tiny breeds or determined chewers.

**No portability.** At roughly 28 pounds, this is a set-it-and-forget-it piece. If you need a crate to move between rooms or travel with, look elsewhere.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** who want their crate to double as actual furniture
- **Homeowners** whose crate sits in a visible main living area
- **Owners of medium-to-large dogs** (30-60 lbs depending on size option) who are already crate-trained
- **People who value aesthetics** over maximum portability

**Skip it if** you have a puppy under 15 lbs, need a travel crate, or hate assembling furniture.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the cushion washable?**
Yes — the cover zips off and is machine-washable on cold. The foam insert is not washable, so use the cover as a barrier.

**Q: Can I use this for a puppy?**
Technically yes, but the mesh gaps make it risky for puppies under 10-12 lbs. Wait until they're bigger or choose the smaller size option with tighter spacing.

**Q: Does the top table surface hold weight?**
The flat top supports up to 30 lbs safely — enough for a lamp, small plants, or a drink tray. Don't sit on it.
