Let me be blunt: most commercial dog treats are 90% filler and 10% apology. The Three Dog Bakery Grain-Free "Woof" treats are not that. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this soft-baked biscuit has earned its reputation — but it's not perfect, and here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The 10-ounce bag arrives with roughly 40-50 small, bone-shaped biscuits. Each treat is roughly the size of a quarter — small enough for training sessions but substantial enough that one or two feels like a real reward. The texture is the standout: soft-baked and slightly crumbly, similar to a fresh oatmeal cookie. As shown in the product image, these are uniform, golden-brown biscuits with visible peanut butter flecks throughout.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: peanut butter, oats (gluten-free), tapioca starch, and a few natural preservatives. No corn, no wheat, no soy, no artificial colors. For a grain-free treat, that's a solid lineup.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these with three dogs across different life stages — a 10-month-old golden retriever puppy, a 6-year-old border collie mix, and a 12-year-old beagle with missing teeth. The soft texture was a universal win. The senior beagle could easily break the biscuit with her gums, while the puppy demolished them in seconds. The collie, normally a picky eater, went through the entire bag in two days.

The treats hold up reasonably well in a training pouch. They don't melt into a greasy mess like some soft treats, and they don't shatter into crumbs when you reach for one. That said, the outer edges do dry out slightly if the bag is left open for more than a few days — reseal the pouch properly, or you'll get harder biscuits by week two.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The praise is consistent across reviews. One verified buyer noted, *"My dog is allergic to chicken and beef, and these are one of the few treats she can eat without itching. She goes crazy for them."* Another owner of a senior dog wrote, *"Finally a treat my 14-year-old dachshund can actually chew. He's missing most of his teeth and these break apart perfectly."* The peanut butter flavor is the clear crowd-pleaser — multiple reviewers mentioned their dogs doing tricks unprompted just to earn another piece.

## Where It Falls Short

The peanut butter is both the strength and the weakness. If your dog has a peanut allergy — and that's more common than most owners realize — these treats are off the table. Several reviews noted stomach upset in dogs with sensitive digestion. There's no novel-protein alternative in this line, so pets with multiple allergies may need to look elsewhere.

The other issue is portion control. These treats are small and tasty, and your dog *will* give you the "one more" eyes. At roughly 15 calories per biscuit, they're not a diet disaster, but they're not a low-calorie training treat either. If your dog is prone to weight gain, factor these into their daily calorie count.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You have a senior dog or a pup with dental sensitivities who needs a soft treat that's still satisfying. You want a grain-free option with recognizable ingredients. Your dog loves peanut butter and you want a treat that works for both training and casual rewarding.

**Skip them if:** Your dog has a known peanut allergy or a very sensitive stomach. You need a crunchy dental-cleaning treat — this won't scrape plaque. You're looking for a low-calorie option for a weight-managed dog.

## FAQ

**Are these treats actually grain-free?**
Yes. The primary ingredients are peanut butter, gluten-free oats, and tapioca starch. No wheat, corn, or soy. However, if your dog has celiac-like sensitivities, check with your vet — oats can sometimes trigger reactions in highly sensitive dogs.

**How many treats are in a 10-ounce bag?**
Roughly 40-50 biscuits, depending on batch size. Each treat is about the size of a quarter and around 15 calories.

**Can puppies eat these?**
Yes, for puppies over 8 weeks old. The soft texture is safe for teething pups, and the ingredients are gentle. Just adjust portion size — a single treat is plenty for a small puppy.
