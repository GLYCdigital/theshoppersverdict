## What You’re Actually Getting

Fresh News comes as compressed, pellet-like paper particles that expand when you fluff them into the cage. A standard bag covers a decent footprint, but here’s the honest math: plan on laying 2–3 inches deep for proper odor control. That’s not a flaw — it’s how paper bedding works. ## Real-World Performance

The absorbency claim isn’t marketing fluff. Paper fibers soak up moisture roughly three times better than wood shavings by volume, and that translates into tangible differences: you’ll change the cage less frequently, and the ammonia smell from urine takes noticeably longer to build. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *“I switched from aspen to this for my rabbit and stopped having to fully clean the litter box every other day. It clumps enough to scoop, which I didn’t expect from paper.”*

The dust factor matters more than most new small-pet owners realize. Respiratory infections are a leading health issue in rabbits and guinea pigs, often triggered by dusty wood bedding. At 99.7% dust-free, Fresh News eliminates that variable. If you have a sensitive animal or keep your cage in a bedroom, this is the single strongest argument for choosing this product over cheaper alternatives.

## Honest Shortcomings

Paper bedding isn’t perfect, and you should know the tradeoffs before buying.

First, it’s **lightweight**. If your guinea pig loves to dig or burrow, you’ll find paper particles scattered outside the cage. A high-sided litter pan or cage skirt solves this, but it’s an extra consideration.

Second, **cost per volume runs higher** than kiln-dried pine. For a single rabbit or pair of guinea pigs, the absorbency savings justify the price. If you’re running a small animal rescue with ten cages, you’ll feel the difference in your wallet.

Third, and this surprises some buyers: **you need to lay it thick.** Skimping on depth means the bedding saturates faster and odor control drops off. This isn’t a product flaw — it’s physics.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you own rabbits, guinea pigs, chinchillas, or hamsters and you care about respiratory health. This is also the right choice if your small pet lives in a bedroom or common area where dust and odor matter. Owners of animals with urinary issues will appreciate the absorbency.

**Skip it if** you have a large multi-cage setup on a tight budget, or if your pet is a heavy digger and you refuse to use a covered litter pan. In those cases, kiln-dried pine offers better economics and weight.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this safe for rabbits that eat their bedding?**
A: It’s non-toxic and made from recycled paper, so incidental ingestion isn’t an emergency. But if your rabbit is actively eating bedding regularly, that’s a sign of a dietary deficiency — talk to your vet.

**Q: How often should I change the cage?**
A: With a 2–3 inch layer, spot-cleaning daily and a full change every 5–7 days works for most single-animal setups. You’ll extend that by a couple days compared to wood shavings.

**Q: Does it work in litter boxes?**
A: Yes, and many buyers use it specifically for litter training. Urine clumps enough to scoop cleanly, which wood shavings don’t do.
