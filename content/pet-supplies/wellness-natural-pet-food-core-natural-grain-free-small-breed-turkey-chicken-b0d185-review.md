Small breed dogs have small stomachs, fast metabolisms, and big opinions about their food. The Wellness CORE Natural Grain-Free Small Breed formula with turkey and chicken is trying to hit a specific sweet spot: high-protein nutrition in a kibble small enough that a 6-pound Yorkie doesn't have to work overtime to chew it. With 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the consensus is mostly positive. Here's what you're actually getting.

**What's in the bag**

This is a grain-free recipe built around deboned turkey and deboned chicken as the first two ingredients — that's real meat, not meat meal, leading the label. It also includes turkey meal for concentrated protein, plus flaxseed, salmon oil, and blueberries for omega fatty acids and antioxidants. The guaranteed analysis comes in around 36% protein and 17% fat, which is appropriate for energetic small breeds that burn through calories quickly.

As shown in the product image, the kibble is genuinely tiny — roughly the size of a peppercorn. That's a deliberate design choice. Small breed dogs, especially seniors with dental issues, struggle with oversized kibble designed for Labs and German Shepherds. This size reduces choking risk and makes mealtime less frustrating for picky chewers.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The manufacturing comes from Wellness, a brand with a solid track record for quality control and recall history compared to many competitors. The kibble itself has a consistent shape and texture — not overly greasy, which means less residue on your dog's bowl and fewer upset stomachs from sudden fat spikes.

The probiotic inclusion (specifically dried Bacillus coagulans) is worth calling out. Many premium foods list probiotics but don't guarantee them. Wellness CORE does, and that's a meaningful difference for small dogs with sensitive digestion.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Looking through the ratings, three themes keep coming up. First, picky eaters actually eat this. One reviewer noted, "My 11-year-old Chihuahua mix has turned his nose up at every brand we've tried. He cleaned his bowl with this." That's not a rare story — multiple owners of toy breeds report the same.

Second, the small kibble size gets specific praise. "Finally a food my Maltese can actually chew without gagging," reads one verified purchase review. For small breed owners, this isn't a minor detail — it's the difference between a dog that eats and one that doesn't.

Third, coat and energy improvements show up within a few weeks. The omega fatty acids from salmon oil and flaxseed appear to deliver visible results, with several reviewers mentioning glossier coats and better energy levels.

**Where it falls short**

The price is the obvious sticking point. This is a premium grain-free formula, and the cost per pound reflects that. If you have a 15-pound dog, you'll go through a bag in about three weeks at the recommended feeding amounts. That's a real line item in your monthly budget.

The grain-free positioning is also worth questioning. Unless your dog has a confirmed grain allergy, the science doesn't clearly favor grain-free diets — and some dogs with sensitive stomachs actually do better with rice or oats. If your dog has never shown grain sensitivity, you might save money with Wellness' grain-inclusive line instead.

Finally, the bag lacks a resealable zipper. That's a minor annoyance, but kibble freshness matters, especially with the added probiotics. You'll want a clip or an airtight container.

**Who should buy this**

This is best for owners of toy and small breed dogs — think Chihuahuas, Maltese, Shih Tzus, Miniature Poodles, and small terriers — who are dealing with picky eating or dental challenges. It's also a strong option if you want a protein-forward, grain-free diet without fillers like corn, wheat, or soy.

Skip it if your dog has no grain sensitivity and your budget is tight — the grain-inclusive formulas from the same brand deliver similar nutrition at a lower cost.

**FAQ**

*Is this food good for small breed puppies?*  
Yes, but check the feeding guidelines. It's formulated for all life stages, including puppies. Just adjust portions based on your puppy's weight and activity level.

*Can this cause digestive upset?*  
Any diet change can. Transition over 7-10 days, mixing increasing amounts of the new food with the old. The added probiotics help, but they're not a substitute for a gradual switch.

*Is grain-free actually better for small dogs?*  
Only if your dog has a diagnosed grain allergy or intolerance, which is relatively rare. For most dogs, grain-free is a preference, not a medical necessity. Don't pay extra for it unless you've seen a benefit.
