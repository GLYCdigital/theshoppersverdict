**If you're shopping for a cat cage that doesn't look like a temporary prison, the COZIWOW Large Wooden Cat Cage deserves a hard look.** At 4.2 stars from 210 Amazon ratings, it's not perfect — but for multi-cat households or owners who need to confine a cat safely (post-surgery, new kitten integration, or just to protect furniture), this is one of the better-built options I've seen under $200.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the wooden frame pieces, plywood panels, perches, two condo boxes, and a removable plastic bottom tray. The finished cage stands roughly 5 feet tall with a footprint around 36" × 24" — meaning it's a piece of furniture, not a cage you tuck away. The front door swings wide for full access, and there's a side door for secondary entry. The two condos are enclosed boxes with circular openings, giving cats that den-like hiding spot they naturally crave.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The pine wood frame is genuinely sturdy — this isn't the flimsy particleboard you see in budget cages. One buyer noted, *"I was surprised at how solid this feels once assembled. My 15-pound Maine Coon jumps from the top perch and the whole thing barely wobbles."* I believe it. The plywood panels are thicker than typical, and the perches are screwed into the frame rather than resting on plastic clips.

The three-level layout works well in practice. Cats get a ground floor, a mid-level condo, and a top perch with sightlines. For households with multiple cats, that vertical separation reduces territorial stress. The removable bottom tray is a genuinely thoughtful touch — slide it out, hose it down, done.

## Where It Falls Short

**Assembly is the biggest hurdle.** Multiple reviewers mention it takes 1–2 hours with two people. The included hex key is cheap, and the pre-drilled holes sometimes don't align perfectly. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"Took me three hours solo. Bring a drill and a friend."* That's fair criticism.

**The wood scratches.** If your cat is a determined clawer, the pine will show damage quickly. It's a cage, not heirloom furniture, but buyers should temper expectations about longevity of the finish.

**The footprint is real.** At roughly 3 feet by 2 feet, this isn't a corner nook cage. Measure your space before ordering — it needs breathing room.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Multi-cat households** needing separate spaces without stacking cheap wire crates
- **Post-surgery or recovering cats** that need confinement but deserve more than a carrier
- **Owners who want a cage that doesn't scream "cage"** — the wood aesthetic passes as furniture in a living room
- **Kitten owners** who need safe nighttime containment with room to play

**Skip it if:** you have a single small cat that only needs occasional confinement (a carrier or pop-up pen suffices), you live in a small apartment, or you're not comfortable with a 2-hour assembly project.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I put a litter box inside?**
Yes. The base floor fits a standard 15" × 19" litter pan with room to spare. Just know the tray is shallow, so use a pan with high sides or you'll get litter scatter.

**Q: Will this hold a large breed cat?**
The weight capacity is solid — buyers report 15+ pound cats using the top perch without issue. The perches are screwed into the frame, not clipped, so they hold. Just check the entry hole size; it's about 8 inches, which is fine for most adult cats.

**Q: Is the wood treated or safe for chewing?**
It's unfinished pine, which is safe if your cat chews (no toxic coatings). That's also why it scratches easily — there's no protective sealant. You can apply a pet-safe wood sealant if you want more durability.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a sturdy, multi-level enclosure that functions as furniture and can handle multiple cats. **Skip it if** you want quick assembly, have limited floor space, or need a scratch-proof surface.
