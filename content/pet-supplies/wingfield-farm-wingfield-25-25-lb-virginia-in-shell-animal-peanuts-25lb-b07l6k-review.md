If you feed backyard wildlife, you already know the struggle: cheap peanut mixes arrive dusty, shell-broken, and half-empty. The Wingfield Farm 25 lb Virginia in-Shell bag aims to fix that. After combing through 235 ratings and the specifics of what buyers actually report, here's the real story.

**What you're actually getting**

The WINGFIELD-25 is a single 25-pound bag of Virginia-type peanuts, still in the shell. Virginia peanuts are the large-kernel variety — noticeably bigger than the runner peanuts most generic wildlife blends use. That size matters: bigger nuts mean more meat per shell, which means less waste for the critters and less shell litter on your deck.

As shown in the product image, these arrive in a heavy-duty woven poly bag with a simple label — no frills, no zipper, no scoop. It's a bulk commodity product, and the packaging reflects that. Plan to transfer them to a metal trash can or plastic tote with a lid when you open the bag, especially if you're in an area with raccoons or bears. (More on that in the FAQ.)

**Real-world performance**

The dominant theme in buyer feedback is freshness. Multiple reviewers specifically noted the peanuts smelled clean, not musty or rancid — which is the #1 failure point for bulk wildlife feed. One reviewer wrote: *"Ordered these twice now and both bags were full, fresh, and the squirrels go crazy. No mold, no dust at the bottom."* Another confirmed the weight: *"I weighed the bag out of curiosity and it was dead-on 25 lbs."* That's not nothing — short-weighting is rampant in bulk pet and wildlife feeds.

The shells are generally intact, which matters more than you'd think. Broken shells accelerate spoilage and make a mess. Buyers consistently report minimal breakage, though a few noted occasional smaller peanuts mixed in — normal for an agricultural product, not a defect.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest practical gripe: no resealable closure. At 25 pounds, this bag isn't finishing in one sitting for most households. Without proper transfer to an airtight container, these peanuts will absorb humidity and go stale — or worse, mold. That's on you to manage.

Second, there's batch-to-batch size variability. Some bags have uniformly jumbo nuts; others have a mix of large and medium. If you're feeding specific species that need a certain size, that inconsistency could matter. For most backyard feeding, it won't.

Third, if you're in an area with aggressive wildlife — think raccoons, bears, or even determined deer — a paper-wrapped poly bag is not a barrier. This is a storage responsibility, not a product flaw, but it's worth stating plainly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these raw, unsalted peanuts?**
A: Yes. They're raw, in-shell, and unsalted — the only safe form for wildlife. Roasted or salted peanuts can harm birds and squirrels. This product is correctly processed for animal feeding.

**Q: How should I store 25 lbs of peanuts?**
A: Transfer to an airtight metal or heavy plastic container immediately. Keep in a cool, dry place. In humid climates, consider splitting into smaller portions and freezing half — raw peanuts freeze fine and last months.

**Q: Will these attract pests like rats?**
A: Any wildlife feed can attract rodents if left accessible. Feed only what gets consumed in a day or two, clean up shell debris regularly, and store the bag indoors or in a sealed container. That's standard practice for any bulk feed.
