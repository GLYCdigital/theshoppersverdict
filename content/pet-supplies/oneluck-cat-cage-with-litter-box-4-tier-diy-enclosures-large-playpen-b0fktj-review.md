**The Oneluck 4-Tier Cat Cage: Vertical Space That Actually Works — With a Few Caveats**

Most cat cages are glorified wire boxes. The Oneluck 4-Tier DIY Enclosure is different — it thinks vertically. If you've got a kitten who needs supervised time, a recovering surgical patient, or a multi-cat household where someone needs a timeout, this 4-foot-tall tower gives them room to climb, perch, and observe without feeling trapped. But is it worth the assembly headache? Let's break it down.

**What You Actually Get**

Unboxing reveals a stack of wire mesh panels, connector clips, a molded plastic litter tray base, and a fabric hammock. No tools required — the panels snap together with included connectors. The footprint is roughly 24" x 24", but the height is where it earns its keep. As shown in the product image, the four tiers create distinct zones: the litter tray base, two mid-level platforms, and a top perch with the hammock. You're getting about 60 inches of climbable vertical territory in a compact square footprint.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The wire mesh is powder-coated, which matters — bare metal rusts when urine splashes or when you wipe down the panels weekly. The connectors are plastic, and they're the weak point. They hold fine under normal use, but if you're rough during assembly or have a cat that body-slams the walls during play, check the connections monthly. One buyer noted, *"The clips feel cheap but they've held up for 4 months with two rambunctious kittens."* That tracks with the 4.0-star average — it's not premium, but it's functional.

The integrated litter box tray is the standout feature. It slides out from the base without dismantling the cage, which is a godsend for daily scooping. The tray is sized for standard clumping litter and has a low-entry lip that even arthritic cats can step over. However, the wire mesh floor above the tray means litter tracking is inevitable — you'll find granules scattered around the base within days.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The modular DIY design gets the most love. Multiple reviewers mention rearranging the panels to create a wider single-level enclosure for a postpartum queen and her kittens, then later reconfiguring it back to a tower. The hammock is also a hit — cats gravitate to it immediately. One verified purchase put it simply: *"My cat chose the hammock over the $80 cat tree I bought her. Go figure."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Assembly is the biggest hurdle. The instructions are picture-based with minimal labeling, and the panels don't have obvious orientation markers. Budget 45–60 minutes and have a second person hold panels while you clip. The plastic connectors also make the whole structure slightly wobbly — it won't collapse, but it doesn't feel rock-solid if your cat launches from the top tier.

The wire floor is the other issue. For kittens and adult cats with healthy paws, it's fine. For seniors or cats with sensitive pads, it's uncomfortable. Buy a few fleece mats or cut cardboard to size for the platforms. Also note: the cage is tall but not wide. Large breeds like Maine Coons will find the 24" square footprint cramped for anything but sleeping.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Kitten owners** who need a safe space for supervised play while they work
- **Multi-cat households** needing a temporary separation zone during introductions
- **Post-surgery recovery** — the vertical design keeps a healing cat comfortable without full confinement
- **Small apartment dwellers** whose cats need vertical territory without a bulky cat tree

**Skip it if** you have a senior cat with mobility issues, or if you want a permanent enclosure — the plastic connectors will eventually wear out with daily disassembly/reassembly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I put a large litter box inside?**
The included tray is about 20" x 15" — standard size. A jumbo hooded box won't fit. You can remove the tray and place a larger box on the floor, but the cage walls sit flush to the ground, so you'd need to elevate the cage slightly.

**Q: Does it fold flat for storage?**
Yes. Fully disassembled, the panels stack into a flat bundle about 24" x 24" x 4". The connectors store in a small bag. Plan 15–20 minutes for disassembly.

**Q: Is the hammock machine washable?**
The fabric hammock detaches and is machine washable on cold, gentle cycle. Air dry only — the clips on the corners will melt in a dryer.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a versatile, space-efficient enclosure for kittens or active adult cats and don't mind a bit of assembly frustration. The integrated litter tray and modular flexibility make it a solid value at this price point.

**Skip it if** you're housing a senior cat, need furniture-grade stability, or want something you'll never have to tighten again. This is a good product, not a great one — and for most buyers, that's enough.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Recommended with caveats.
