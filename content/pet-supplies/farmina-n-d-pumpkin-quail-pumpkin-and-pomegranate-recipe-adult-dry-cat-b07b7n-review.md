If your cat has been scratching endlessly, throwing up after meals, or turning their nose up at yet another chicken-based kibble, the Farmina N&D Pumpkin Quail recipe might be the reset button you've been looking for. This is not your average grocery store cat food — it's a low-glycemic, novel-protein formula from an Italian brand that treats feline nutrition like a science, not an afterthought.

**What's Actually in the Bag**

You're getting a 5.5-pound or 14-ounce bag of grain-free kibble that's 92% animal-based. The headline ingredient is deboned quail — an uncommon protein source that's a game-changer for cats with food intolerances. The remaining 8% includes pumpkin, pomegranate, and a vitamin-mineral complex. No corn, no wheat, no rice, no tapioca starch. As shown in the product image, the kibble itself is small, round, and brown — easy for most adult cats to chew, though the texture is denser than typical extruded kibble.

The guaranteed analysis reads well: 44% crude protein, 20% fat, and just 1.8% fiber. Compare that to your standard grocery-store kibble at 30-34% protein and you'll see why this formula is priced at a premium.

**Real-World Performance**

I've seen this food work wonders for cats with chronic digestive issues. One Amazon reviewer wrote, "My cat has IBD and this is one of the few foods she can eat without vomiting." Another noted, "The quail protein made a huge difference for my cat's itchy skin — the vet recommended it."

The low-glycemic carbohydrate profile matters too. Cats are obligate carnivores, and this formula keeps carb content around 18% — much lower than the 30-40% you'll find in most commercial kibble. Owners consistently report better litter box odor control and firmer stools after a full transition.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest — this food has a noticeable fishy scent from the herring oil and cod liver oil. Some cats love it; others will walk away. One reviewer admitted, "My cat took three days to even try it." The smell isn't overwhelming, but it's distinct.

The price is the bigger hurdle. You're paying boutique-brand money, and there's no way around it. If you have multiple cats, this becomes a significant monthly expense. Also, if your cat is currently eating high-carb kibble, expect a messy transition week — the sudden shift to 44% protein can cause loose stools. Transition over 10-14 days, not three.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the cat owner whose pet has unexplained skin issues, chronic vomiting, or persistent soft stools that standard vet diets haven't fixed. It's also ideal for picky eaters who've gone off chicken or turkey. If your cat thrives on whatever you put in front of them, you can save money elsewhere — but if you're troubleshooting a health issue, this is worth the investment.

**FAQ**

*Is this food good for kittens?* No — this is formulated for adult cats. Farmina has a separate kitten line with higher calcium and phosphorus for growth.

*Does it contain any grains?* None. It's completely grain-free, with pumpkin and pomegranate as the primary carbohydrate sources.

*How much should I feed my 10-pound cat?* Roughly 1/2 to 3/4 cup daily, split into two meals. Adjust based on your cat's activity level and body condition.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** Your cat has food sensitivities, you want a genuinely low-carb formula, or you're willing to pay for premium ingredients.

**Skip it if:** Your cat is fine on standard food, you're on a tight budget, or your cat is extremely sensitive to smell.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A thoughtfully formulated, novel-protein kibble that delivers on its promises, held back only by its price and strong fishy aroma. If your cat tolerates the scent, this could be the food that finally solves their problems.
