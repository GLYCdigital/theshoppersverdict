Let me tell you something that might surprise you: your cat doesn't need kibble. And if you've been reading ingredient labels lately, you already suspect it. Open Farm's Freeze Dried Raw Cat Morsels is the brand that finally made me feel okay about saying that out loud — but it's not without its caveats.

## What You're Actually Paying For

Open Farm isn't just another freeze-dried raw option. The brand's entire pitch revolves around traceability — every batch has a lot code you can punch into their website to see exactly which farm your meat came from. That's not marketing fluff; that's accountability you don't get from most pet food companies. The morsels themselves are freeze-dried raw, meaning they're never cooked, and the protein (salmon, beef, chicken, or turkey depending on the recipe) is the first ingredient. Organs, bone, and produce round out the formula — no peas, no lentils, no potato fillers that pad cheaper brands.

## Real-World Performance

I've fed these to my two cats for three weeks. The first thing you'll notice: the morsels are small, dense, and break apart easily. That's actually a feature — you can crumble them over kibble as a topper or rehydrate them with warm water for a full meal. Rehydrated, they smell like actual meat, not fishy kibble dust. My cats went from sniffing to inhaling within two days, though my older cat needed a five-day transition where I mixed increasing amounts with her usual wet food.

The 220 Amazon ratings at 4.4 stars mostly reflect what I experienced. One reviewer wrote, "My picky Bengal is obsessed — she licks the bowl clean and I love that I can see exactly where the salmon came from." Another noted, "These are the only freeze-dried treats that don't give my cat digestive issues." That tracks — the single-protein recipes and minimal ingredient list make it gentler on sensitive stomachs.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. At roughly $1.50–$2.00 per ounce, this is expensive — period. If you're feeding a 10-pound cat as a full meal, a 3.5oz bag lasts maybe four days. That's a $15+ weekly habit. Most buyers use it as a topper, which stretches the bag to two weeks, but at that price point you should know what you're getting into.

The texture is also a legitimate hurdle. These morsels are hard and dense, not crumbly like some freeze-dried options. Cats who prefer pâté or shredded textures may reject them outright. One Amazon reviewer hit this exact issue: "My cat turned up her nose for a week. She finally eats it, but only if I crumble it finely over her wet food." If your cat is texture-sensitive, budget for a transition period.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the pet parent who's already done the research and wants the cleanest option without going full DIY raw. If you're feeding raw or gently cooked food and need a travel-friendly backup, this works. If you have a picky eater who thrives on toppers, this is a premium upgrade over standard treats. But if you're on a tight budget or your cat is a texture snob, start with a single bag before committing.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this a complete meal or just a topper?**
A: It's nutritionally complete and balanced for adult cats — AAFCO-approved for all life stages. You can feed it as a full meal, but most buyers use it as a topper due to cost. Rehydrate with warm water for a meal.

**Q: How does the traceability actually work?**
A: Each bag has a lot code on the back. Enter it on Open Farm's website and you'll see the farm, harvest date, and sourcing details for the protein in that specific batch. It's not a gimmick — it's genuinely useful.

**Q: Does it need refrigeration?**
A: No. Freeze-dried means it's shelf-stable until opened. Once the bag is open, keep it sealed in a cool, dry place and use within 30 days. Rehydrated portions need refrigeration if not eaten immediately.

## The Verdict
