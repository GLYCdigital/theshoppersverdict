If your dog has a sensitive stomach or itchy skin that you've been chasing with trial-and-error diets, the Natural Balance Limited Ingredient Diet deserves a serious look. This is one of those foods that does exactly what it says on the bag — no marketing gymnastics, no exotic ingredient laundry list. At 4.5 stars from 225 ratings, it's clearly resonating with owners who've been down the "mystery allergy" road before.

## What you actually get

The formula I'm reviewing is the salmon and brown rice recipe — a single animal protein source paired with one carbohydrate. That's the entire point of a limited ingredient diet: fewer components means fewer chances for your dog to react. The bag comes in standard sizes, and the kibble itself is a modest-sized piece that works well for most medium dogs. As shown in the product image, the formula is straightforward: salmon first, then brown rice, oatmeal, and flaxseed.

## Performance and real-world use

Here's where this food earns its keep. The salmon provides high-quality protein while delivering omega-3 fatty acids that directly address the two most common reasons owners switch to LID foods: skin issues and digestive upset. Multiple buyers report noticeable improvements within two to three weeks. One reviewer noted, "My dog had chronic ear infections from food allergies and this cleared it up in about a month." Another mentioned, "Finally a food my picky Boston Terrier will eat consistently."

The formula includes vitamins and minerals to meet AAFCO standards for all life stages, so it works for puppies through seniors. The brown rice and oatmeal provide gentle, soluble fiber that helps firm up stool without the inflammatory response some dogs get from corn or wheat.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. This is not a grain-free food. If your vet has specifically recommended grain-free for a confirmed wheat allergy, this won't fit. The brown rice is a solid carbohydrate source, but it's still a grain.

The kibble size is another consideration. For large-breed owners, the pieces run on the smaller side. One Amazon reviewer pointed out, "Great food but my Great Dane just inhales it — I wish they made a larger kibble for big dogs." That's a fair complaint if you've got a 100-pound chewer who doesn't slow down to chew.

And the price. You're paying a premium over standard grocery brands — typically 20-30% more per pound. For a 30-pound bag, that adds up over a month. This is a specialty diet, and the price reflects that.

## Who should buy this

This food is for owners who've identified — or suspect — a specific food sensitivity in their dog. If your dog has chronic itching, recurring ear infections, soft stools, or excessive gas, this is a logical first step before jumping to prescription diets. It's also a solid choice for dogs who are simply picky eaters and need a palatable, simple formula.

Skip it if your dog has a confirmed grain allergy, if you're on a tight budget, or if your dog is a large breed that needs a bigger kibble to slow down eating.

## FAQ

**Q: How quickly will I see improvement in my dog's skin or digestion?**
A: Most owners report visible changes within 2-4 weeks. Skin issues may take longer — up to 8 weeks — as the old food fully clears the system. Be patient and don't switch back and forth.

**Q: Is this food suitable for puppies?**
A: Yes. It's formulated to meet AAFCO nutrient profiles for all life stages, including growth. The salmon provides DHA for brain and eye development.

**Q: Can I mix this with other foods?**
A: You can, but it defeats the purpose. If you're doing an elimination diet, this food needs to be fed exclusively for at least 8 weeks to get meaningful results.
