If your test kit reads 40+ ppm nitrates no matter how often you water-change, you're not alone. API's NITRA-ZORB Size 6 pouch is one of the few chemical filtration media that actually targets nitrate — not just ammonia or nitrite — and it does so without turning your canister into a science experiment. But "works" and "works for long" are two different things, and this pouch has a clear expiration date.

Here's what you get in the box: one white mesh pouch, roughly the size of a sandwich bag, filled with a proprietary ion-exchange resin. The Size 6 designation matters — it's built for canisters with a flow rate up to 400 GPH, which covers the Fluval 407, FX4/FX6, and most Sunsun or Oase units. Smaller than that? You're oversizing. Larger? You'll need two pouches. The mesh is durable enough to survive repeated handling, but don't expect it to hold up if you're cramming it into a tight media basket with coarse filter pads.

Performance-wise, the math is real but time-limited. The resin exchanges nitrate ions for chloride, which is why you'll see a measurable drop in 2–3 days in a moderately stocked tank. One reviewer noted, *"I went from 50 ppm to 20 ppm in four days in a 75-gallon with heavy bioload. It's not magic, but it bought me time between water changes."* That's the honest use case — it's a stopgap, not a permanent solution.

Where it falls short: the resin saturates faster than you'd hope. Most buyers report meaningful nitrate reduction for roughly 3–4 weeks, then a slow creep back to baseline. Unlike purigen, you can't recharge this with bleach. Once it's spent, it's trash. At the price per pouch, that's a recurring cost you need to budget for. Also, a smaller subset of users flagged a pH drop of 0.3–0.5 in soft water tanks. If you keep discus, wild apistos, or Caridina shrimp, test your alkalinity before committing.

Who should buy this? Two groups: (1) The overstocked community tank owner who can't do 50% water changes weekly and needs a buffer, and (2) the planted tank hobbyist fighting chronic nitrate buildup from ferts. Who should skip it? If you already do consistent water changes and your nitrates sit under 20 ppm, this is unnecessary expense. It's also not a fix for a broken nitrogen cycle — ammonia and nitrite spikes won't be touched by this pouch.

**FAQ**

*How long does API NITRA-ZORB actually last?*  
Realistically, 3–5 weeks depending on bioload. Heavily stocked tanks hit saturation closer to 3 weeks. You'll know it's done when nitrate readings start climbing again.

*Can I use this in a hang-on-back filter?*  
Technically yes, but Size 6 is designed for canister flow rates. In an HOB, the water bypasses the media too quickly for efficient exchange. Stick with Size 3 or 4 for HOBs.

*Will it remove phosphate or other organics?*  
No. This is specifically nitrate exchange. Your phosphate issues need a separate media like PhosGuard or rowaphos.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're fighting chronic nitrates between water changes and run a canister filter. It's effective, easy to install, and genuinely moves the needle for a month.

**Skip it if** you can't stomach the replacement cost every 4 weeks, have soft acidic water, or expect a permanent fix. This is a tool, not a cure.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It does exactly what it claims for a limited window. The short lifespan and pH caveat keep it from a perfect score, but for the frustrated hobbyist, it's a legitimate band-aid.
