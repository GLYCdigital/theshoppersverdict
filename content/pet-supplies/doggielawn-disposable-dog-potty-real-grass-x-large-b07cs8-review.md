**Bottom line up front:** If your dog refuses pee pads, hates fake grass, and you need a temporary or apartment-friendly solution, the DoggieLawn X-Large delivers exactly what it promises: real grass that dogs actually use. But this is a subscription-priced convenience, not a budget fix. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 200+ owner experiences.

## What You Actually Get

The box arrives with a black plastic tray (36" x 24" x 4") containing a slab of **live, rooted sod**—not artificial turf, not recycled paper. The grass is grown hydroponically, so there's no soil mess to track through your apartment. The tray has a perforated drain layer that separates the grass from the liquid reservoir underneath. You set it on the floor, and that's it.

The X-Large is genuinely big—about the footprint of a standard door mat but thicker. It fits a 70-pound Lab comfortably, though a Great Dane will hang off the edges.

## Real-World Performance

The biggest selling point is behavioral: dogs smell living grass and immediately understand the assignment. One verified buyer noted, *"My 11-year-old rescue who NEVER used pee pads walked onto this and went in 30 seconds. It's like a switch flipped."* That's consistent with dozens of reviews—the real grass eliminates the training hurdle that fake grass and pads create.

The drainage system works. Urine filters through the sod into the tray below, keeping the grass surface relatively dry between uses. But here's the catch: you need to dump the reservoir every 2–3 days for a single dog, or daily for multiple dogs. If you forget, the tray gets heavy and sloshy, and the smell becomes noticeable by day 10–12.

**Longevity is the real variable.** The manufacturer claims 4–5 weeks. In practice, most owners report 2–3 weeks before the grass yellows, thins, and starts to smell. Climate matters—hot, dry rooms kill it faster. Some buyers stretch it to a month by watering the grass daily (yes, you water a pee pad—it's alive).

## Where It Falls Short

The cost math is brutal. At roughly $50–$60 per tray, a month of indoor potty use runs $100–$120. That's cheaper than a dog walker for some, but far pricier than pee pads or a reusable fake-grass system.

Leakage is a real complaint. Dogs that lift a leg near the edge will send urine down the side of the tray onto your floor. Several reviewers reported tray cracks within the first week, though others said their tray lasted 5–6 weeks. The drain holes can clog with grass clippings over time, slowing drainage and creating puddles.

Odor control is adequate, not excellent. The grass masks urine smell initially, but by week two, ammonia builds up. You'll want to rinse the grass with water periodically—this helps but also shortens the grass's life.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a puppy in apartment training, a senior dog with mobility issues who can't make it outside, or you're recovering from surgery and need a temporary solution. It's also a lifesaver for showing dogs or traveling with pets in RVs.

**Skip it if:** You're looking for a long-term, cost-effective indoor potty solution. For ongoing use, a reusable fake-grass tray with replaceable pads will pay for itself in two months. Also skip if you have a heavy chewer—the tray is thin plastic, not indestructible.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does one tray actually last?**
A: Plan on 2–3 weeks for a single dog. Some push it to 4–5 weeks with daily watering and diligent reservoir dumping, but the grass quality noticeably declines after week three.

**Q: Can I use this on a balcony or patio?**
A: Yes, and it actually lasts longer outdoors in mild weather. But rain will flood the reservoir, and direct sun will bake the grass quickly.

**Q: Does it smell like a barn?**
A: No—the grass itself has a fresh, earthy scent initially. The urine smell develops in the reservoir, not the grass. Dump it every 2 days and you'll stay ahead of the odor.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The DoggieLawn X-Large is a premium convenience product that works exactly as advertised. It's the closest thing to real outdoor potty training for indoor dogs, and for specific situations—apartments, recovery, travel—it's genuinely worth the money. The drawbacks are the recurring cost, the leak risk at the edges, and the inevitable odor management. If you need this for a few weeks, it's a five-star solution. If you need it for months, look elsewhere.

**Buy it if:** You need a temporary, highly effective indoor potty solution and don't mind the price.
**Skip it if:** You want an economical long-term setup or have a dog that's prone to destructive chewing.
