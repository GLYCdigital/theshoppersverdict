**If you've been fighting squirrels at your bird feeder all winter, this cylinder is the ceasefire you've been looking for.** The Mr. Bird Flaming Hot Feast Large Wild Bird Seed Cylinder (4L) takes a simple approach — coat the seeds in capsaicin, the same compound that makes chili peppers hot, and let birds enjoy the feast while mammals run for water. It works, but there are some setup quirks you need to know about before you buy.

**What you actually get**

The box contains one densely compressed cylinder, roughly the size of a large coffee can. The 4L size is substantial — expect it to last two to four weeks with moderate backyard traffic. The seed blend inside includes sunflower seeds, millet, peanuts, and cracked corn, all bound together with a heat-treated binder that holds its shape. As shown in the product image, the cylinder has a rough, textured surface that gives birds good footing while they cling and peck.

**How it performs in the real world**

The capsaicin coating does exactly what it claims. Squirrels take one nibble and move on — the heat is immediate and unpleasant for mammals, but birds lack the receptors that detect capsaicin, so they eat it happily. One reviewer put it plainly: "The squirrels tried it once and never came back. The cardinals didn't even pause." That's the core value proposition, and it holds up.

The cylinder's density is also a genuine plus. Rain and humidity don't turn it into a soggy mess the way loose seed blends do. It stays solid, which means less waste and fewer trips to refill. Smaller birds like chickadees and nuthatches cling to the surface easily, while larger birds like cardinals and blue jays perch on top and work their way down.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The bird diversity is the most-cited positive across the 210 ratings. One seasoned birder noted, "I've had woodpeckers, nuthatches, chickadees, and even a pair of mourning doves picking at the fallen bits. The variety is better than any loose mix I've tried." The squirrel deterrence gets consistent mention too — it's not a gimmick, it genuinely works.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest issue is compatibility. This is not a drop-in product for a standard tube feeder. The cylinder is designed for a dedicated cylinder feeder with a central spike or a platform feeder with a hold-down mechanism. If you don't have either, you'll need to buy one — factor that into your total cost.

Some long-term users also note that once birds have pecked away the outer layer, the remaining core gets harder and less appealing. Smaller birds may lose interest before the cylinder is fully consumed, leaving you with a half-eaten block that's mostly ignored. It's not a dealbreaker, but it means you'll waste some product.

**Who should buy this**

- Backyard birders who are losing the war against squirrels and want a hands-off solution
- Anyone who wants a low-maintenance feeder that doesn't need daily refilling during peak bird activity
- People in wet or humid climates who are tired of moldy loose seed

**Skip it if** you only have a standard tube feeder and don't want to buy additional hardware, or if you're looking for a budget option — this cylinder costs more per pound than loose seed blends.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does the heat hurt birds?* No. Birds don't have the taste receptors that detect capsaicin, so they eat it without any discomfort. It's specifically designed this way.

*Will this work in a standard tube feeder?* Not well. The cylinder is too large for most tube feeder openings. You need a cylinder feeder with a center spike or a tray feeder.

*How long does it last?* With average backyard traffic (10-15 birds per day), expect two to three weeks. Heavy bird activity can go through it faster, but the dense construction slows consumption versus loose seed.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** squirrel-proofing is your priority and you're willing to invest in the right feeder setup. **Skip it if** you're not prepared to add a cylinder feeder to your setup or you want the absolute cheapest seed per pound.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It does what it promises, and the bird diversity is genuinely impressive. The feeder compatibility issue and the hardening core keep it from a perfect score, but for squirrel-plagued backyards, this is a solid, effective solution.
