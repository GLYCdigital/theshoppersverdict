Let’s be blunt: most “smart” pet feeders are a headache. They drop WiFi, their apps feel like they were coded in 2012, and they’re often just a kibble dispenser with a Bluetooth chip. The iPettie FW54 tries to be different by combining a feeder and a water fountain in one unit while supporting both 2.4GHz and 5GHz WiFi bands. That 5GHz support alone puts it ahead of 80% of the competition, which still forces you to dig through router settings to create a legacy 2.4GHz network. But does the rest hold up? Here’s what 210 Amazon reviewers and my own research reveal.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main unit with two side-by-side compartments — one for kibble, one for water — plus a stainless steel bowl for each, a water pump, tubing, and the power adapter. The feeder holds roughly 4 liters of dry food, which is decent for one cat for two to three weeks. The water reservoir is where the design gets tight: it’s about 1.5 liters, so for a single cat that’s manageable, but two cats will drain it in under 24 hours.

The build is solid ABS plastic with a matte finish that doesn’t show paw prints as badly as glossy models. The stainless steel bowls are the standout feature here — they pop out for dishwasher cleaning, and they’re a genuine upgrade over the plastic bowls most competitors ship. If you’ve dealt with feline chin acne, you know why this matters.

## Real-World Performance

The WiFi setup is the make-or-break for any smart feeder, and the FW54 mostly delivers. Users report the 5GHz connection holds steady once paired, with app controls for portion sizes (from 1/8 cup up to 2 cups per serving) and scheduling up to 6 meals daily. The app sends push notifications when food is dispensed and when the water level runs low.

One thing that stands out in the feedback: the portion accuracy is surprisingly consistent. A reviewer noted, “My 14-pound cat stopped begging between meals because the portions are actually the same every time.” That’s not something you can say about every feeder in this price range.

The water fountain is the weaker half. The pump is quiet — noticeably quieter than the Catit or PetSafe fountains — but the filtration is basic. It uses a simple sponge filter rather than a carbon block, so odor control is average. If your pet is picky about water taste, you’ll want to change the filter weekly, not biweekly as the manual suggests.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the dual-function convenience. “I was skeptical about a 2-in-1, but it replaces two clunky machines on my kitchen counter,” one verified purchase reads. Another reviewer highlighted the app reliability: “I’ve had it for three months and it hasn’t dropped off my WiFi once — my old PetSafe did that weekly.”

The 5GHz support gets specific callouts. Several reviewers mention they bought this specifically because their Eero or Google Nest mesh networks don’t expose a 2.4GHz band easily. That’s a smart purchase reason.

## Where It Falls Short

The water capacity is the biggest compromise. At 1.5 liters, it’s fine for one small pet, but you’ll refill daily with multiple animals. The app setup also trips up some users — a few report needing three or four attempts to pair the device initially. It’s not a dealbreaker, but expect some frustration on day one.

There’s no battery backup. If your power goes out, so does your pet’s dinner. For a device marketed as “smart home” compatible, the lack of a battery option feels like a cost-cutting miss. Also, the app is functional but basic — no camera integration, no voice commands, no analytics on eating habits. It does what it needs to, nothing more.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You’re a single-pet household with a modern mesh WiFi network, you’re tired of cluttered countertops with separate feeder and fountain units, and you want stainless steel bowls that don’t harbor bacteria.

**Skip it if:** You have multiple pets (the water reservoir will frustrate you), you need offline backup power, or you want advanced smart features like camera monitoring. For those cases, look at separate dedicated units.

## FAQ

**Does it work with Alexa or Google Home?**
No. The FW54 uses its own app only — no voice assistant integration. If that’s a must-have, look elsewhere.

**Can I use it for wet food?**
Technically yes, but the auger mechanism is designed for dry kibble. Wet food will gum up the dispensing mechanism quickly. Stick to dry.

**How often do I need to clean it?**
The food bowl and water bowl are dishwasher-safe and should go in daily. The water pump needs a vinegar rinse every two weeks, and the food hopper should be wiped down monthly to prevent oil buildup.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The iPettie FW54 nails the fundamentals — reliable 5GHz WiFi, consistent portions, and a genuinely useful 2-in-1 design. The water capacity and missing battery backup are real limitations, but for a single-pet household looking to declutter, this is one of the better values in the smart feeder category. It’s not perfect, but it’s honest, functional hardware that does exactly what it promises.
