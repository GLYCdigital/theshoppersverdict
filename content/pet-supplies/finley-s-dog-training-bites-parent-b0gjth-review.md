If you’ve ever fumbled with a crumbly, dry training treat while your dog stares at you with laser focus, you know the struggle. Finley’s Dog Training Bites aim to solve that exact problem with a soft, pliable formula designed for rapid-fire reward sessions. After combing through 229 ratings and the product specs, here’s the real story for anyone considering these bites.

**What You Actually Get**

The bag contains small, bite-sized morsels that are roughly the size of a pea. The texture is the headline feature: they’re soft enough to tear apart with your fingers but dense enough to hold their shape in a treat pouch. You won’t find a crumb pile at the bottom of the bag, which is a small but meaningful quality-of-life win. The ingredient list is refreshingly straightforward—real meat is the first ingredient, followed by a limited set of binders and natural preservatives like mixed tocopherols (vitamin E). No corn, soy, or artificial anything.

**Performance in the Real World**

The scent is the first thing you’ll notice. These bites have a potent, savory aroma that dogs find nearly irresistible. In practical terms, this means your dog’s ears perk up the second you open the bag, even if they’re across the room. One verified buyer noted, *“My GSD will do anything for these — even ignore the neighbor’s cat, which is a miracle.”* Another owner of a notoriously picky Shiba Inu commented, *“First treat my dog actually gets excited about. We’ve tried four other brands.”* That level of engagement is critical for training, especially if you’re working on recall or loose-leash walking in high-distraction areas.

The softness also makes them safe for puppies and senior dogs with dental sensitivities. You won’t hear the crunching that can cause a dog to pause mid-command. They break cleanly, so you can dispense a third of a bite for a quick “yes!” without losing the rest to the floor.

**Where It Falls Short**

No product is perfect, and Finley’s has two notable drawbacks. First, the packaging is a miss. The bag is a simple tear-open design with no zipper seal. Unless you have a chip clip or a separate airtight container, the treats will start to dry out after a few days, becoming tougher and less appealing. Given that the texture is the core selling point, this feels like an oversight.

Second, the calorie density is worth tracking. At about 3.5 kcal per bite, they’re not the lowest-calorie option on the market. For a 60-pound lab, that’s a non-issue. For a 12-pound Chihuahua, you’ll need to break each bite in half to avoid accidentally doubling their daily treat allowance during a long training session.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Puppy owners** who need a high-value, soft reward that won’t tax a developing digestive system.
- **Owners of picky eaters** who’ve tried milk-bone-style biscuits and gotten the side-eye.
- **Trainers working on focus** in noisy environments where a bland treat won’t cut it.

**Skip it if** you have a dog with severe food allergies to common proteins (check the specific protein variant on the label) or if you’re looking for a shelf-stable treat that can survive a week in a hot car—these are best kept cool and sealed.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many pieces are in a bag?**
A: It varies by weight, but the standard 6-ounce bag contains roughly 150-180 pieces, depending on batch size. Plan for about 3-4 weeks of daily training for an average dog.

**Q: Can these be used as a meal topper?**
A: Technically yes, but their small size and high palatability make them better suited for training rewards than a full meal supplement. They’re not nutritionally complete as a food source.

**Q: Do they work for dogs with sensitive stomachs?**
A: The limited ingredient list helps, but every dog is different. The most common complaint among the handful of negative reviews was mild digestive upset—always introduce new treats gradually.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a training treat that your dog will actually work for, with a clean ingredient list and a texture that makes dispensing easy. **Skip it if** you can’t tolerate non-resealable packaging or you need a near-zero-calorie option for a small breed.

At 4.6 stars, Finley’s earns its rating. It’s a well-executed, honest product that delivers on its core promise. Just buy a chippy clip with it.
