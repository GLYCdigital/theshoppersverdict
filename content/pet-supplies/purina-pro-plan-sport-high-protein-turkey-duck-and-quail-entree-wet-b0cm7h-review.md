**A high-protein wet food that actually respects the "sport" in its name — but you'll pay for the privilege.**

Most "high protein" dog foods pad their numbers with pea protein or other plant concentrates. Purina Pro Plan Sport doesn't do that. The first three ingredients here are turkey, duck, and quail — actual meat, in that order. At 34% crude protein from animal sources alone, this is one of the most protein-dense wet foods you can buy for under $3 per can.

Here's what you're actually getting, and whether your dog needs it.

## What's in the can

Each 13-ounce can contains a pâté-style entree that's dense enough to hold its shape when scooped. The ingredient list is refreshingly short for a Purina product: turkey, duck, quail, liver, and a vitamin/mineral premix. No corn, wheat, soy, or artificial colors. The 18% fat content provides the calorie density working dogs need, but it's a double-edged sword — more on that below.

The texture is worth noting. As shown in the product image, this isn't a gravy-heavy stew. It's a firm, meatloaf-like pâté that breaks into chunks rather than smearing. That matters if you have a dog that eats fast — this food actually requires some chewing.

## What 240 ratings at 4.8 stars tell you

The consensus from verified buyers is that this food works for dogs that need serious fuel. One reviewer noted, *"My German Shorthaired Pointer hunts 4-5 days a week and has never maintained weight this well on any other wet food."* Another owner of a senior husky reported, *"She was losing muscle tone before we switched. Three months on this and her vet commented on the improvement."*

The most repeated praise is about coat condition. Multiple reviews mention shinier coats and less shedding within two to three weeks — likely the result of the omega fatty acids from the duck and quail.

## Where it falls short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**Price.** At roughly $3.50 per 13-ounce can, this costs about 40% more than Purina's standard Pro Plan wet foods. If you have a 70-pound dog eating two cans daily, that's over $200 per month. This is a performance food, priced accordingly.

**Fat content.** The 18% fat is great for working dogs but problematic for couch potatoes. One reviewer mentioned their beagle gained weight on this formula because they didn't adjust portions. If your dog isn't truly active, this is the wrong food.

**Aroma.** The three-poultry blend produces a strong, gamey smell that's more intense than single-protein foods. A few reviewers noted their dogs needed a day or two to accept the scent.

## Who should buy this

This food makes sense for three specific groups:

- **Working and hunting dogs** that burn 1,000+ calories daily
- **Active breeds** (pointers, retrievers, shepherds) that struggle to maintain muscle mass
- **Multi-dog households** with puppies and adults — this formula meets AAFCO standards for all life stages, so you only need one food

Skip it if your dog is sedentary, overweight, or has a known sensitivity to rich foods.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I mix this with dry food?**
A: Yes, and many owners do. It's calorie-dense enough that you'll need to reduce dry food by roughly 50% to avoid overfeeding. Start with a 50/50 ratio and adjust based on your dog's weight over two weeks.

**Q: Is this good for dogs with chicken allergies?**
A: Generally, yes. There's no chicken in this formula, which is why many allergy-prone dogs do well on it. But turkey and duck are still poultry, so dogs with broad poultry sensitivities may still react. Watch for itching or digestive issues in the first week.

**Q: How long does an open can last?**
A: Refrigerated, 3-4 days maximum. The high protein content means it spoils faster than lower-protein foods. Portion it into airtight containers immediately, and don't leave it sitting out for more than 30 minutes.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** Your dog is genuinely active, needs muscle support, or you want a single food that works for every dog in your house regardless of age.

**Skip it if:** Your dog is a lap dog, you're on a tight budget, or you need a low-fat formula for weight management.

This is the rare wet food that delivers on its performance claims. The price is steep, but for dogs that truly need this level of nutrition, it's hard to beat.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses points only on price and the fat content that makes it unsuitable for sedentary dogs.
