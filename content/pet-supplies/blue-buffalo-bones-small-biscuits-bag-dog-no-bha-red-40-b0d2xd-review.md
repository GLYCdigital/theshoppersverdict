**The short version:** If you've got a small dog and you're tired of flipping over treat bags to find Red 40, BHA, or corn syrup in the ingredient list, these Blue Buffalo biscuits are a legitimately clean option. But they're not perfect — the packaging has a real flaw, and the price per treat stings a bit. Here's the full breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The bag holds 12 ounces of small bone-shaped biscuits. As shown in the product image, these are roughly the size of a human thumb — about 1.5 inches long and a quarter-inch thick. That's genuinely small-dog territory. A 10-pound Chihuahua or Yorkie can crunch these comfortably; a 40-pound beagle will swallow them whole without chewing, so size your dog accordingly.

The ingredient list is where Blue Buffalo earns its premium. Deboned chicken is the first ingredient, followed by whole grains, flaxseed, and fish oil. No BHA, no Red 40, no corn, wheat, or soy. For a mainstream brand sold at this price point, that's a cleaner label than most competitors in the biscuit aisle.

**Real-world performance**

The texture delivers what most buyers want from a crunchy treat. One reviewer noted, "My picky Maltipoo actually chews these instead of inhaling them — the crunch seems to satisfy something." That's consistent with what I've seen: the biscuits are hard enough to provide light dental scraping but not so rock-hard that senior dogs struggle. They crumble rather than shatter, which reduces choking risk for tiny mouths.

Calorie-wise, each biscuit runs about 10-12 calories. At 8-10 treats per day (the feeding guideline on the bag), that's roughly 100 calories — significant for a 10-pound dog, so adjust meal portions accordingly.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The clean ingredient list is the #1 reason people stick with these. Multiple Amazon reviews mention switching from grocery-store brands specifically because of Red 40 concerns. As one verified buyer put it: "Finally a biscuit I don't feel guilty giving my puppy three times a day." The small size also gets repeated shout-outs — owners of toy breeds consistently mention that other "small breed" treats are still too big.

**Where it falls short**

The packaging is the biggest genuine complaint. The bag has no zipper closure, and buyers report breakage during shipping. One review noted, "Half the bag was crumbs by the time it arrived." That's frustrating at this price point. Transfer to an airtight container immediately, or you'll be dealing with stale, crumbly biscuits within a week.

The price is the second issue. At roughly $0.30-$0.40 per treat, you're paying a significant premium over comparable grain-inclusive biscuits. You're paying for the Blue Buffalo brand name and the clean label — that's a legitimate value proposition, but it's worth knowing what you're buying.

**Who should buy this**

- **Toy breed owners** who need genuinely small, crunchy treats for training or daily rewards
- **Ingredient-conscious buyers** who want a mainstream brand with no artificial colors or preservatives
- **Puppy owners** in the teething phase who need a firm but not rock-hard chew

**Skip it if** you have a large breed (these are too small to be satisfying), you need a soft treat for a senior dog with dental issues, or you're on a tight budget — store brands with comparable ingredients run cheaper.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these safe for puppies?**
A: Yes, generally. The biscuits are firm but crumble easily. Start with half a treat for dogs under 6 months and monitor chewing. The clean ingredient list makes them a better choice than many puppy-specific treats.

**Q: How long does the bag last?**
A: For a 10-pound dog at 8 treats per day, about 2-3 weeks. For a 20-pound dog, closer to 1-2 weeks. Factor that into the cost per serving.

**Q: Do these actually help with dental health?**
A: The crunchy texture provides mild mechanical scraping, similar to most dental biscuits. They're not a substitute for brushing, but they're better than soft treats for tartar control.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a clean-label, genuinely small crunchy treat for a toy or small breed and don't mind paying a premium for ingredient transparency.

**Skip it if:** You need a budget option, have a large dog, or want a resealable package that survives shipping intact.
