## Is CRAVE Indoor Cat Food Actually Worth It? Here's the Real Story

If you've got an indoor-only cat, you already know the struggle: they're less active, they groom more, and they tend to pack on pounds if you're not careful. CRAVE's Grain Free Indoor formula is aimed squarely at that problem. But does it deliver, or is it just another overpriced bag of kibble with a "high-protein" sticker on the front?

I dug through the specs, the ingredient panel, and what real buyers are saying across 229 Amazon ratings (averaging 4.6 stars). Here's what actually matters.

## What's in the Bag (and What's Not)

Open the bag and you'll notice the kibble is small — about the size of a pea — which is ideal for indoor cats who tend to gulp their food. The first ingredient is real chicken, not a vague "poultry by-product" or meal. That's a genuinely good sign at this price tier.

The protein content sits at 40%, which is notably higher than the 30-32% you typically see in standard indoor formulas. It's also grain-free — no corn, wheat, or soy. Instead, you get pea fiber and dried beet pulp, which are there to help with hairball control. Indoor cats groom more because they're bored, so that fiber matters more than you'd think.

One thing to note: this is a dry food only. No wet food option in this specific formula, so if your cat is a wet-food snob, you'll need to supplement separately.

## Real-World Performance: What Cat Owners Actually Report

The feedback across Amazon is remarkably consistent. The most common praise centers on coat condition and energy. One buyer noted, *"My two indoor cats have shinier coats and more energy than when they were on the grocery store brand. They also stopped throwing up hairballs every week."* That's a pattern I see repeated — owners who switched from lower-protein indoor formulas report fewer hairballs and better weight management within 4-6 weeks.

Another recurring theme: picky cats actually eat it. *"I have a notoriously fussy Maine Coon who turns her nose up at everything. She cleaned her bowl the first night,"* reported a verified purchase. That's not universal, but it's encouraging for a grain-free formula, which some cats find less palatable.

The 40% protein is doing real work here. Indoor cats lose muscle mass faster than outdoor cats because they move less. Higher protein helps preserve that lean tissue, which in turn keeps their metabolism firing. Multiple reviewers specifically mentioned their vet approved the switch for overweight indoor cats.

## Where It Falls Short (Honestly)

No product is perfect, and this one has two real drawbacks.

First, the kibble is small and hard. If your cat is a senior with dental issues or a kitten with a tiny mouth, they might struggle. Several reviewers noted their older cats needed the food softened with water. That's a workaround, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Second, the transition period is real. A handful of reviewers reported digestive upset — loose stools, vomiting — when they switched too quickly. That's true of any high-protein food, but this formula is potent enough that you absolutely need to mix it with the old food over 7-10 days. Don't skip that step.

There's also no grain-free guarantee against cross-contamination, which is standard for non-certified brands. If your cat has a severe grain allergy, you might want a dedicated limited-ingredient brand instead.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a healthy adult indoor cat (1-7 years old) who needs weight management, struggles with hairballs, or is bored with lower-protein foods. It's also a strong pick if you're looking for a clean ingredient list without paying premium-brand prices.

**Skip it if:** You have a senior cat with dental problems, a kitten under 12 months, or a cat with a confirmed grain allergy who needs a fully limited-ingredient diet. Also skip if your cat is already at a healthy weight and very sedentary — the high calorie density might cause weight gain if you're not portion-controlling.
