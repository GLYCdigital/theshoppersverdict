If your cat scarfs down dinner and then hacks it back up on your rug 10 minutes later, you've probably already Googled "elevated cat feeder" and found a hundred options ranging from $15 plastic junk to $80 designer wood. The CatGuru Food Table sits in the middle of that range, and based on 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, it's earning its place on kitchen floors across the country. But is it actually worth the money, or are buyers just relieved their cat stopped throwing up? Let's dig in.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The CatGuru arrives fully assembled — no hex keys, no "some assembly required" frustration. You get the bamboo table itself, two 8-ounce stainless steel bowls, and non-slip silicone pads on the bottom of each leg. The table measures roughly 15 inches long by 8 inches wide, with a 4.5-inch raised platform. That height is the sweet spot for most adult cats, putting the food at chest level rather than forcing them to crouch down flat on the floor.

The stainless bowls are the real standout here. They're deep enough to hold a full can of wet food plus kibble, but not so cavernous that a cat's whiskers hit the sides. One reviewer noted, *"My senior cat used to vomit almost daily from eating too fast. I've had this for three weeks and zero throw-up incidents."* That's the core promise of any elevated feeder, and the CatGuru delivers on it.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The bamboo construction is noticeably sturdier than the MDF-and-screw options at the same price point. It doesn't wobble when a 12-pound cat jumps up mid-meal, and the silicone feet grip tile and hardwood without sliding. The surface is sealed with a food-safe finish, which matters if you're like me and occasionally spill water while refilling.

Here's where I need to be honest: the bowl cutouts don't have any rubber gasket around the edge. Crumbs and wet food residue will work their way under the bowl lip, and you'll need a toothpick or a damp cloth to get them out. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a design oversight you'll deal with weekly. Also, despite the sealed finish, you should not soak this table in the sink. Hand-wipe it. Bamboo and standing water don't mix, and one buyer learned that the hard way after running it under the faucet.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The overwhelming theme in positive reviews is that this thing *works* for problem eaters. Multiple buyers with senior cats, flat-faced breeds like Persians, and chronic vomiters reported improvement within days. One review mentioned, *"My vet recommended raising my cat's bowls to help with regurgitation. This is much nicer than the stack of books I was using before."* Another common thread: the bowls are the right size. Not too deep, not too shallow, and they don't have that cheap metal smell some stainless bowls have straight out of the box.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest limitation is bowl compatibility. The cutouts are designed for the included 5.5-inch bowls, and if you want to swap in your own ceramic or glass bowls, you're out of luck unless they match that exact diameter. Also, if you have a larger breed like a Maine Coon, 4.5 inches might be a bit low — they'd still be bending slightly. And the bamboo, while attractive, will show water rings and claw scratches over time if you have a cat that paws at their food.

**Who Should Buy This**

Get the CatGuru if you have a fast eater, a senior cat with arthritis, a flat-faced breed, or a cat that vomits shortly after meals. It's also a solid pick if you care about aesthetics — this looks like actual furniture, not a plastic pet dish. Skip it if you have a giant breed that needs a taller stand, or if you're planning to use your own bowls rather than the included stainless ones.

**FAQ**

*Q: Is the height adjustable?*  
No, it's fixed at 4.5 inches. If your cat is unusually tall or short, measure your cat's chest height from floor to elbow and compare.

*Q: Can the bowls go in the dishwasher?*  
Yes, the stainless bowls are dishwasher-safe. The wooden table is not — hand-wipe it only.

*Q: Will this help my cat stop throwing up?*  
It helps with regurgitation caused by eating too fast or poor posture. It won't fix underlying medical issues, so if vomiting persists after two weeks, see your vet.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy It**

The CatGuru Food Table does exactly what it promises: raises your cat's food to a comfortable height, reduces scarf-and-barfs, and looks good doing it. The minor annoyances — crumbs under the bowl lip, hand-wash-only wood, fixed bowl size — are tradeoffs for the solid build and the fact that it actually solves a real problem. For the price, you're getting a well-made product that your cat will use twice a day, every day, for years.

**Buy it if** your cat eats too fast, has mobility issues, or needs a more ergonomic eating position. **Skip it if** you need a fully adjustable stand or insist on using your own oversized ceramic bowls.

*Note: Reviewers who had issues mostly cited either the fixed height or wanting to use different bowls. Neither is a flaw in the product itself — just know what you're buying.*
