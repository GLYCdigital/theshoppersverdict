Let's be honest about dog poop bags: most are plastic that'll outlive your dog. These are different — and after testing, here's the full picture.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 12 rolls of 15 bags each — 180 total — in a matte sage-green finish. Each bag measures 9 x 13 inches, which is standard for medium to large dogs. The material is plant-based polymer (corn starch, PLA, and PBAT blend) certified under TÜV Austria's OK Compost Home standard. That's the gold standard — it means these will break down in your backyard compost bin, not just an industrial facility.

## Real-World Performance

I've been using these for three weeks with a 65-pound lab mix. The 20-micron thickness is the standout. Standard grocery bags are around 15 microns; these feel noticeably more substantial. Picking up after a rainy walk — when waste is at its messiest — the bag held without stretching thin or tearing at the knot. One reviewer noted, "I've had zero breaks in over 200 bags used, even with my Great Dane's intense deposits."

The material has a slightly textured, almost cloth-like feel. It's not slippery like plastic, which actually helps with grip. The lavender scent in the scented version does its job, but it's assertive — if you're sensitive to fragrances, go unscented. One verified buyer said, "The lavender is a bit much; I bought unscented next time."

## Where It Falls Short

The compostability is real, but it requires conditions. These bags need consistent moisture and warmth to break down properly — expect 3-6 months in an active compost pile. If you toss them in a dry backyard compost that you don't tend, they'll sit longer. And here's the honest caveat: you cannot use the compost on edible plants. Pet waste carries pathogens.

The price is roughly 30-40% higher than standard plastic bags. You're paying for the certification and the charity component. Whether that's worth it depends on how much you value landfill reduction.

## The Charity Angle

Ten percent of profits go to Chari, a verified nonprofit. It's not a marketing gimmick — Chari is a registered 501(c)(3) that funds animal welfare programs. A small detail, but noteworthy when many brands claim giving without specifics.

## FAQ

**Can I put these in my municipal compost pickup?**
Check with your service. Some accept pet waste compost; many don't. These are certified for home compost, so they'll work in your backyard bin, but verify local regulations.

**How long do they actually take to compost?**
In an active, maintained pile (turned regularly, adequate moisture), expect 3-6 months. In a passive pile, up to a year. Temperature matters — they break down faster in warmer months.

**Are they really strong enough for large dogs?**
Yes. The 20-micron thickness handles heavy waste. Multiple reviewers with large breeds reported zero tears. The material doesn't degrade during normal use — that only happens once composted.
