Somewhere between "plastic pan that permanently smells like regret" and "robot box that costs as much as a used sedan" sits the Furkid Stainless Steel Cat Litter Box. At a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But does the extra cost over a standard plastic hooded box actually buy you a better experience? After digging through owner feedback and the spec sheet, here's the straight answer.

**What You Actually Get**

The Furkid arrives as a two-piece system: a welded stainless steel base and a plastic lid with a charcoal filter slot. The XL dimensions listed are roughly 22 inches long, 17 inches wide, and 16 inches tall at the peak of the hood. That's a genuine extra-large footprint — not marketing math. A standard 18-inch pan is cramped for a 15-pound cat; this gives them room to circle, dig, and turn without hitting the walls. The kit includes the lid, filter, and a scoop. Nothing else, no assembly drama.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

Stainless steel is the point here. Unlike plastic, it's non-porous, so urine doesn't soak into microscopic scratches and permanently bond with the material. One owner noted, *"I've had plastic boxes I had to throw away after a year because they stank no matter what I cleaned them with. This one wipes out with a paper towel and there's zero odor left."* That matches the physics — steel doesn't absorb, it releases. The charcoal filter in the lid pulls double duty, cutting ammonia smells between cleanings.

The steel gauge feels solid, not flimsy. It doesn't flex when you lift it with a full load of clumping litter. The entry lip is low enough for senior cats or kittens, but here's the catch: it's not a high-back design. An enthusiastic digger — especially a Maine Coon — can still fling litter over that front edge. You'll want a mat.

The lid, however, is the weak link. It sits on the base but doesn't latch. If your cat likes to jump on top of the box (and many do), the lid can shift or pop off. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before purchase.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The recurring themes in positive reviews are odor control and cleanup ease. A verified purchase put it plainly: *"I was skeptical about the price, but after three months, no smell in a small apartment. I'll never buy plastic again."* Multiple owners with multiple cats report the charcoal filter lasts about a month before swapping, and replacements are cheap. Large-breed owners specifically call out the XL sizing as the reason their cat actually uses the box instead of protesting on the floor.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the lid issue, the filter door on top is a bit flimsy — it's held in place by friction and can pop out if you bump the box while scooping. Also, this is not a discreet box. It looks like a functional piece of pet equipment, not home decor. If you wanted something that blends into a living room, this isn't it.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Multi-cat households** where odor control matters in tight spaces
- **Owners of large breeds** (Maine Coons, Ragdolls) who need the XL footprint
- **Anyone tired of replacing plastic boxes** every 12–18 months due to odor absorption
- **Senior cats** that need a low entry lip but still want privacy from the lid

**Skip It If**

- Your cat is a litter-flinger. You'll be sweeping daily without a high-sided guard.
- You need a fully sealed, latchable enclosure. This isn't that.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the stainless steel really prevent odor better than plastic?**
Yes. Plastic absorbs urine into micro-scratches over time, creating permanent odor. Steel is non-porous — odors wipe off completely. The charcoal filter handles the gaseous ammonia smell between cleanings.

**Q: Is the XL size right for a 20-pound cat?**
Yes. The interior floor space gives large cats room to turn and dig. If your cat is over 20 pounds, measure your space — this is already one of the larger covered boxes on the market.

**Q: How often do I need to replace the charcoal filter?**
Most owners report replacing it every 3–4 weeks. It's a standard-sized filter, and replacements run about $10 for a multi-pack.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're tired of plastic pans that hold onto smells and you have the space (and budget) for a premium box. The steel base genuinely solves the odor problem, and the XL size keeps bigger cats happy.

**Skip it if** you need a fully sealed, latchable unit or your cat is a chronic litter-launcher.
