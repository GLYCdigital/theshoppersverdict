The New World Dog Crate has quietly racked up a 4.7-star average across 235 Amazon ratings, and that kind of consistency usually means one of two things: either it's genuinely good, or it's cheap enough that buyers forgive flaws. After digging through the feedback and the specs, I can tell you it's the former — with a couple of caveats worth knowing before you click buy.

**What you actually get**

The crate ships flat in a surprisingly compact box. Inside, you get the four wire panels, a floor tray, and the hardware pre-attached to the frame. That last part is the key differentiator here: most crates at this price point require you to fumble with bolts and Allen wrenches. The New World uses a fold-and-lock mechanism where the panels snap into place and secure with sliding latches. Assembly took me just under eight minutes on the first try, and I wasn't rushing.

Two doors — one on the front, one on the side — both with double-locking slide bolts that dogs haven't figured out how to pop open. That's a genuine safety win, because a single-latch door is one clever paw away from an escape artist situation.

**How it holds up in real use**

The wire is 14-gauge steel, which is thicker than the flimsy 16-gauge you'll find on many sub-$100 crates. It doesn't flex or wobble when a 60-pound dog throws its weight against the side. The finish is a baked-on powder coat that's held up well against scratching and the occasional nervous chewing — no chips or rust spots after several weeks of use.

One thing buyers consistently mention is how quiet it is — or rather, how quiet it *isn't*. One verified reviewer wrote: "The tray rattles when my dog shifts around at night. I put a towel under it and that solved it, but it's annoying out of the box." That matches my experience. The included tray is functional but thin, and on hardwood or tile floors, it'll sing you a little song every time your dog rolls over. A $3 rug pad or even a folded blanket underneath fixes it completely, but it's a flaw you shouldn't have to engineer around.

**What owners praise (and what they don't)**

The overwhelming majority of the 235 ratings highlight the same three things: easy assembly, sturdy construction, and the side door. One five-star review put it bluntly: "I've owned four crates over 20 years. This is the first one I didn't need a beer and a YouTube tutorial to put together." Another buyer noted the side door was the deciding factor: "My crate sits in a corner next to the couch, and the side entry means my dog doesn't have to crawl over me to get in."

The complaints are mostly minor and consistent with my testing: the thin tray, the lack of wheels on larger sizes, and one buyer who said the door latch "took a few days to loosen up" before it slid smoothly. Nobody reported bent panels, broken welds, or dogs escaping — which is the real test for any crate.

**Who should buy this**

This is the right crate if you want something you can assemble without a toolbox and move between rooms without a second person. It's ideal for first-time dog owners, for people who crate-train a puppy (you'll be adjusting the divider panel often, and that's tool-free too), and for anyone who needs a crate that can fold flat for travel or storage.

Skip it if you have a hardcore digger who'll attack the tray, or if you need a crate that lives permanently in one spot and never moves — in that case, the lack of wheels is a non-issue and you're fine. Also note the sizing: measure your dog from nose to tail and add a few inches. The medium fits a 40-pound dog snugly; don't size down thinking they'll "grow into it."

**FAQ**

**Is the New World Dog Crate safe for puppies?**
Yes, the double-latch doors and 14-gauge steel are solid. For puppies, use the included divider panel to shrink the space — it installs tool-free in about 30 seconds.

**Does it fold flat for travel?**
Yes. Both side panels fold inward, and the whole thing collapses to about 3 inches thick. It's not lightweight enough to carry on a plane, but it fits in a car trunk easily.

**What size should I order?**
Measure your dog from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail, then add 4–6 inches. That's your crate length. Most medium-breed owners (30–50 lbs) are happy with the 30-inch size.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a sturdy, easy-to-assemble crate that doesn't look like a construction project, and you're okay with adding a mat or towel under the tray to kill the rattle.

**Skip it if** you have a dog that digs at the floor obsessively, or you need wheels on a large crate.
