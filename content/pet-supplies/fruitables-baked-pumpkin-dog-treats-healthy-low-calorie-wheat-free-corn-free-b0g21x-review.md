If you've ever stood in the pet aisle squinting at calorie counts, wondering if that "training treat" is actually just a tiny biscuit that'll blow your dog's daily budget, these Fruitables Baked Pumpkin treats deserve a hard look. At 3 calories per piece, they're one of the few treats on Amazon where the low-calorie claim actually checks out.

## What's Actually in the Bag

You get a 5-ounce resealable pouch of small, bone-shaped treats. The ingredient list opens with pumpkin — not corn, not wheat, not "meat by-product" — followed by oat flour and a few natural additions like molasses and cinnamon. No artificial colors, no preservatives, no corn syrup. For a treat that costs less than most premium biscuits, the ingredient quality punches above its weight.

The texture is soft-baked, which makes them easy to break into smaller pieces for training. That's a double-edged sword — more on that in a moment.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these with a 60-pound lab mix who inhales anything remotely edible, and a 12-pound dachshund who turns her nose up at 80% of what crosses her bowl. The lab went through a training session like they were filet mignon. The dachshund? She ate them, but without her usual enthusiasm — the pumpkin flavor is subtle, not intense.

The soft-baked texture is genuinely useful. You can snap a treat in half without a crumb explosion, which makes portion control for overweight dogs much easier. And because they're not rock-hard, senior dogs with dental issues handle them fine.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't inflated. A recurring theme in the feedback is how well these work for dogs on weight-management plans. One reviewer put it plainly: *"My vet recommended these for my lab who needs to lose 10 pounds. She thinks she's getting a treat, I know she's getting 3 calories. Win-win."* Another noted, *"Finally a treat my picky shih tzu will eat that doesn't give her gas. The pumpkin seems to actually settle her stomach."*

Digestive benefits came up repeatedly — several owners mentioned that the pumpkin helps with occasional loose stools. That's a nice bonus if your dog has a sensitive stomach.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the crumble factor. These treats are soft and slightly crumbly — expect a layer of dust at the bottom of the pouch by the time you're halfway through. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's messier than a hard biscuit.

Second, the freshness window. The resealable pouch works, but only if you actually press the seal closed every time. Leave it open overnight and the treats stiffen and lose their appeal. If your household has kids who forget to close bags, transfer these to an airtight container.

Third, the flavor is mild. If your dog is a food-motivated fiend who'll eat anything, great. If you have a truly picky eater, the subtle pumpkin taste might not cut it. A few reviewers noted their dogs needed a couple of tries before accepting them.

## Who Should Buy These

These are ideal for: dogs on calorie-restricted diets who still need training rewards, puppies (the 3-calorie size is perfect for frequent reinforcement), senior dogs with dental issues, and owners who want a wheat-free option without paying boutique prices.

Skip them if: your dog demands intensely flavored treats, you need something crunchy for dental health, or you want a single treat that'll keep a large dog busy for more than two seconds.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**How many treats can I give my dog per day?**
The bag suggests 1-2 treats per 10 pounds of body weight daily. At 3 calories each, even a generous training session won't wreck your dog's daily intake — but adjust meal portions if you're going through half the bag in a day.

**Are these actually wheat-free?**
Yes. The recipe uses oat flour instead of wheat, and there's no corn or soy either. That makes them a reasonable option for dogs with grain sensitivities, though oat flour isn't gluten-free if your dog has celiac-level sensitivity.

**Can puppies eat these?**
Yes, and the small size makes them excellent for puppy training. Just supervise — the soft texture means they don't require much chewing, so a fast eater could inhale them without proper chewing.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a genuinely low-calorie training treat with clean ingredients your dog will actually eat. These deliver on their promises — the calorie count is honest, the ingredient list is respectable, and the price is fair.

**Skip it if** you have an extremely picky dog, need a crunchy dental treat, or can't tolerate a bit of crumb dust in the bag.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — docked half a star for the crumble factor and mild flavor. Otherwise, these are a solid, trustworthy choice for health-conscious pet parents.
