**This coop has the bones of a great product, but the execution leaves room for frustration.**

At 3.8 stars across 190 ratings, this wooden hen house for 2-4 chickens sits squarely in "decent budget option with caveats" territory. I've spent the last week tearing down the specs, digging through buyer feedback, and comparing it against the competition. Here's what you need to know before dropping money on this thing.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes a pre-cut fir wood coop with a sloped asphalt roof, a separate run area, two nesting boxes with hinged lids, a roosting bar, and a plastic pull-out tray for easy cleaning. The coop itself measures roughly 4 feet wide by 2.5 feet deep, with the run adding another 4 feet of enclosed space. It's compact — fine for two standard hens or four bantams, but don't push it.

The plywood is thicker than most coops in this price bracket, and the exterior has a protective treatment that handles rain reasonably well. One buyer noted, "Survived two heavy storms without a single leak inside the nesting area," which aligns with what I'd expect from the materials list.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The cleaning tray is the standout feature. Multiple reviews highlight how the slide-out plastic tray makes weekly cleanouts a 10-minute job instead of a wrestling match. One verified purchase put it plainly: "The pull-out tray is genius. I just slide it, scrape, and done."

The nesting boxes are also well-designed — hinged lids mean you collect eggs without entering the coop, and the boxes are sized correctly for standard hens. The roosting bar is positioned above the tray, which keeps droppings contained.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: assembly is a headache. This is the most consistent complaint across the 190 ratings. The pre-drilled holes frequently don't align, and several buyers report needing to re-drill or modify parts to get the structure square. One reviewer summarized the experience: "Took 6 hours with two people. The instructions are basically pictures with no detail, and two panels didn't fit without force."

The run is also a genuine weak point. The included wire mesh has gaps large enough for a determined raccoon or weasel to squeeze through. Multiple buyers report predator attacks — one devastating review describes losing three hens to a raccoon that tore through the run mesh. If you buy this coop, budget an additional $30-50 for hardware cloth and a roll of zip ties to reinforce every seam.

## Who Should Buy This

This coop works best for:

- **First-time chicken keepers** with 2-3 hens who want an affordable starter coop and are willing to put in assembly sweat equity
- **Backyard hobbyists** in mild climates who plan to reinforce the run anyway
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who understand they're getting a "good bones" product, not a turnkey fortress

Skip it if you have heavy predator pressure, want something that assembles in under two hours, or keep more than four full-size hens.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Is this coop truly predator-proof out of the box?**
A: No. The mesh on the run is too wide for raccoons and weasels. Plan to replace or reinforce it with 1/2-inch hardware cloth. The coop itself is solid wood and secure when latched, but the run is the weak point.

**Q: How many chickens can realistically fit?**
A: Two standard hens comfortably, three if they're docile breeds, four only if they're bantams. The specs say 4, but that's pushing it — overcrowding leads to pecking and respiratory issues.

**Q: Does the weatherproofing hold up?**
A: The exterior treatment handles rain and sun well for the first year, but several long-term reviewers recommend applying an additional wood sealant before the first winter. The roof is solid, but the run floor will need cleaning and eventual replacement.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're handy, have modest hen numbers, and want a wooden coop that won't fall apart after one season. The cleaning tray and nesting box design are genuinely good, and the fir construction beats the particleboard garbage at the same price point.

**Skip it if** you're not willing to reinforce the run, you hate complicated assembly, or you live in an area with raccoons, foxes, or stray dogs.

This is a 3.7-star product — not a disaster, not a gem. It's a solid foundation that rewards buyers who put in the extra work. For first-time keepers who want a project and a coop that'll last 3-5 years with proper care, it's a reasonable pick. Just budget for the predator-proofing upgrades and a weekend for assembly.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — Good bones, frustrating assembly, and an under-built run hold it back from greatness.
