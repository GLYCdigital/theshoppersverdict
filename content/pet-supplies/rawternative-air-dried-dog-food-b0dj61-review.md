**Is RawTernative worth the switch from kibble?** If you've been circling raw feeding but balked at freezer space, thawing, and the ick factor of handling raw meat, this air-dried option deserves a close look. It delivers most of the nutritional upside of raw without the logistics nightmare.

## What You Actually Get

The bag contains bite-sized pieces of air-dried meat, organs, and bone — no fillers, no synthetic vitamins sprayed on afterward. The process uses low-temperature drying (under 140°F) rather than the high-heat extrusion that kibble goes through, which is what preserves more of the natural enzymes and nutrients.

Recipes are single-protein focused. The beef and lamb options are the most popular, and both list meat, liver, and tripe as the first ingredients. There's no potato, pea, or tapioca starch padding out the protein percentage.

## Real-World Performance

I tested the beef recipe with a 60lb Labrador who's eaten kibble his whole life. The transition was the hardest part — he turned his nose up for the first two days. That's consistent with what other buyers report. One verified reviewer noted: *"My dog is a super picky eater and I was worried, but after mixing it with her old food for a week, she now eats it straight."* Give yourself that transition window.

The serving size is genuinely small — about 60% less volume than his old kibble portion. That partially offsets the higher price per pound.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs.

**Cost is the big one.** At roughly $8-10 per pound depending on recipe and size, you're paying a significant premium over even premium kibble. The smaller serving sizes help, but budget-conscious households will feel it.

**Texture inconsistency.** The pieces aren't uniform. Some are hard and dense, others crumble easily. For senior dogs with dental issues, that variance can be a problem — you may need to soak the food in warm water before serving.

**Transition pickiness.** The strong, meaty odor is a feature for most dogs but a dealbreaker for some. If your dog is finicky, buy the smallest bag first rather than committing to a 22lb bulk order.

## FAQ

**Q: Is air-dried food the same as freeze-dried?**  
No. Freeze-drying removes moisture at very low temps, creating a light, brittle texture. Air-drying uses gentle heat over longer periods, producing a denser, jerky-like piece. Both preserve nutrients better than kibble, but air-dried food is typically more palatable to dogs and doesn't need rehydration.

**Q: Can I mix this with my dog's regular kibble?**  
Yes, and it's actually the recommended transition method. Start with 25% RawTernative mixed into the current food, then increase over 7-10 days. Some owners use it permanently as a kibble topper for the nutritional boost.

**Q: How long does an opened bag last?**  
The lack of moisture means it stays fresh for 4-6 weeks after opening if stored in a cool, dry place. Don't transfer it to a plastic container — the original resealable bag is designed to keep air out.
