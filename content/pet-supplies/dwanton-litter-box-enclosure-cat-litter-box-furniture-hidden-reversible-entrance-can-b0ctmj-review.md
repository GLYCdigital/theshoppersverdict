Let me be direct with you: most litter box enclosures look like a plastic bin someone dressed up in a wood costume. The Dwanton Litter Box Enclosure is different — it actually looks like a piece of furniture. As shown in the product image, this cabinet-style unit features a clean wood-grain finish that could pass as a side table or media console in a small apartment.

But does it hold up where it matters — containing mess, controlling odor, and giving your cat a space they'll actually use? I dug through the 220 ratings and the 4.4-star average to find out.

## What You Actually Get

The Dwanton enclosure is a freestanding cabinet made from engineered wood (MDF) with a laminated wood-grain exterior. It measures roughly 23 inches wide, 23 inches deep, and 24 inches tall — compact enough for a laundry room corner or bathroom, but spacious enough inside for a standard 18-inch litter pan.

The standout feature is the **reversible entrance panel**. You can mount the entry hole on either side of the unit, which means you can position the enclosure against a wall or in a corner and still orient the opening the way your cat prefers. That's a small detail, but it matters more than you'd think — some cats refuse to use an enclosure if the entry feels awkward.

The top surface is flat and sturdy enough to hold a small plant, a stack of books, or a cat bed. The unit ships flat-packed, and you'll need a Phillips screwdriver and about 30-45 minutes of assembly time.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the Dwanton earns its keep. The enclosure does what it's supposed to: hides the litter box, contains scattered litter, and gives you a clean surface to wipe down. The wood-grain finish resists moisture and cleans up easily with a damp cloth — a huge upgrade over raw wood that absorbs odors.

One thing buyers consistently mention is the **odor containment**. While the enclosure doesn't come with a carbon filter, the closed design traps smells far better than an open litter pan. Several reviewers noted that they added a small activated carbon pad to the top vent — a cheap DIY upgrade that makes a big difference.

The interior clearance is adequate for most domestic cats, but this is where I'd flag a caution. Several reviewers with larger breeds — think Maine Coons or hefty tomcats — mentioned their cats had to crouch lower than they'd like. If you have a big cat, measure your litter pan's height plus a few inches of clearance before committing.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the most common complaint. The panels fit together snugly, but the screw holes aren't always pre-drilled, which means you'll be applying more force than you'd like. A power drill with a Phillips bit makes this much easier — trust me on that.

The lack of a built-in odor filter is a missed opportunity at this price point. You can buy a carbon filter pad separately and stick it to the interior lid, but it's an extra cost and an extra step that some competitors include out of the box.

And while the finish looks good, it's still MDF. If you have a cat that likes to scratch vertical surfaces, you'll want to keep a scratching post nearby — the cabinet door will not survive a determined claw session.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** who need to hide a litter box in a shared or small space
- **Design-conscious owners** who don't want a plastic box as a permanent fixture
- **Owners of small-to-medium cats** who want a contained, cleanable litter station

**Skip it if** you have a very large cat, if you're not willing to do a bit of assembly, or if you expect built-in odor filtration without an extra purchase.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my standard litter box?**
Most standard 18-inch litter pans fit with room to spare. Measure your pan's dimensions before ordering — anything larger than 20x20 inches will be tight.

**Q: Does it really contain litter tracking?**
Yes, for the most part. The enclosed design keeps kicked litter inside, and the elevated entry step catches some before it hits the floor. You'll still see some litter near the entrance, but far less than with an open box.

**Q: How hard is assembly really?**
It's doable in about 30-45 minutes with a screwdriver. A drill with a Phillips bit speeds things up considerably. The instructions are clear, but the screw holes can be tight.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a litter box enclosure that looks like actual furniture, fits in tight spaces, and doesn't break the bank. The reversible entrance, sturdy build, and clean finish make it a solid choice for most cat households.

**Skip it if** you have a giant breed cat, want built-in odor filtration, or hate assembling flat-pack furniture.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good enclosure with a few assembly and filtration tradeoffs. For the price, it beats most of the competition in looks and build quality.
