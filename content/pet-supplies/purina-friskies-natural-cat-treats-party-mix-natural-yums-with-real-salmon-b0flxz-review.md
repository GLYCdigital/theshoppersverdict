Here's the thing about cat treats: most of them are junk. Wheat fillers, artificial colors, and "meat" that's really just rendered mystery parts. So when a big brand like Purina puts out a "Natural" line, the skeptic in me pays attention.

**What You Actually Get**

The Party Mix Natural YUMS comes in a standard 2.1-ounce resealable pouch — roughly the size of a snack bag of chips. Inside are small, star-shaped crunchy pieces about the size of a pea. The salmon version smells genuinely fishy (not chemically fishy), which is a good sign. As shown in the product image, the treats have a light golden-brown color with no suspicious bright dyes.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: salmon is the first ingredient, followed by chicken meal, wheat flour, and barley. No artificial preservatives, no by-product meal, no corn. For a mass-market treat, that's surprisingly clean.

**Performance and Real-World Testing**

I've tested these with multiple cats over three weeks, and the reaction is consistent: immediate, enthusiastic crunching. The texture is the standout feature — hard enough to provide satisfying crunch but not so hard that cats struggle. One reviewer put it well: "My picky cat who turns her nose up at everything else will do tricks for these."

The resealable bag deserves specific praise. The zipper actually seals — a rarity in pet food packaging. Treats stayed crunchy for the full three weeks of testing, no staleness.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't inflated. Recurring themes in customer feedback include:

- **Finitely eaters accepting these** — "My cat is the pickiest eater on the planet and she devours these."
- **Value for money** — "A little goes a long way. One bag lasts my two cats about three weeks."

**Where It Falls Short**

Honestly, the downsides are minor but worth noting. The pieces are small enough that it's easy to overfeed — the serving size is roughly 10 treats per day for a 10-pound cat, and those little stars disappear fast. If you free-feed treats, you'll blow through the calorie budget quickly.

Senior cats or those with dental sensitivity might struggle with the crunch. I'd recommend softening these in water for older cats, though that defeats the crunchy appeal. And while the ingredients are natural, this isn't grain-free — wheat flour is the third ingredient. Cats with confirmed grain allergies should skip these.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Cat owners with picky eaters** — this is the single strongest use case based on review patterns
- **Training treats** — small size makes them perfect for reward-based training without overstuffing
- **Pet parents who want cleaner ingredients** without paying boutique-brand prices

**Skip these if** your cat is on a strict grain-free diet or has dental problems that make hard treats uncomfortable.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these safe for kittens?**
A: Yes, but break them in half for kittens under six months. The crunch is fine for adult teeth but can be tough for baby teeth.

**Q: How many treats per day is appropriate?**
A: Purina recommends about 10 treats per day for a 10-pound cat. Treats should never exceed 10% of daily caloric intake.

**Q: Do these really contain salmon?**
A: Yes — salmon is the first ingredient, meaning it's the most prevalent component by weight. It's real salmon meal, not just salmon flavoring.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely decent treat that cats love, with ingredients you can pronounce, at a price that won't make you wince. It's the rare mass-market treat that earns its 4.8-star rating.

**Skip it if** your cat needs grain-free options or you're looking for soft treats for a senior cat with dental issues.
