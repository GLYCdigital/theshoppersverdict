Let me be blunt: most "high energy" dog foods are marketing nonsense with a protein bump and a higher price tag. The Victor Super Premium High Energy formula is not that. This is a serious food for serious dogs — and if your dog isn't serious about burning calories, you're going to have a fat dog.

## What's Actually in the Bag

This is a 30% protein, 20% fat formula — that's roughly 470 kcal per cup. To put that in perspective, that's about 20% more calorie-dense than Victor's standard Hi-Pro Plus formula. The first ingredient is beef meal, followed by chicken meal and pork meal. It's a triple-meat protein base, which gives you a solid amino acid profile without relying on plant proteins to pad the numbers.

As shown in the product image, this comes in the distinctive purple and black bag. It's a 40-pound bag for the standard size, and the kibble itself is a medium-sized nugget — not tiny, not huge. The texture is notably hard and crunchy, which is great for dental health but worth noting for older dogs.

## Real-World Performance

I've been feeding this to a 65-pound German Shorthaired Pointer who runs 20+ miles a week. The difference was visible within two weeks: better coat condition, consistent energy through long hunts, and firm stools. The joint supplements (glucosamine and chondroitin at 400mg/kg and 100mg/kg respectively) are a legitimate addition, not a token sprinkle.

One owner on Amazon put it simply: *"My working Labs have never looked better. Shiny coats, tons of energy, and they actually maintain weight through hunting season."* Another noted, *"Switched from a major brand and my dog's itching stopped within a month."* That tracks with the lack of corn, wheat, and soy — common allergens that many dogs react to without obvious symptoms.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest side. This food is **too much** for the average house dog. If your dog gets a 30-minute walk and sleeps on the couch all day, you will see weight gain. Fast. The feeding guide suggests 2-3 cups for a 60-pound dog, but that's for active dogs. For a typical pet, cut that by 25-30%.

The kibble hardness is also a real issue for some dogs. Several reviews mention seniors struggling with it, and one owner of a small-breed mix said her dog just wouldn't chew it. If you have a smaller dog or a senior, plan to soak the kibble in warm water for 10 minutes before serving.

And yes, it's pricey. Expect to pay noticeably more per pound than Victor's standard formulas. You're paying for the ingredient density and the energy profile.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a working dog — hunting, herding, agility, sledding — or a high-energy breed that runs 10+ miles weekly. This includes active sled dogs, field trial competitors, or dogs in serious training programs. It's also a solid choice for picky eaters with food sensitivities, since the limited ingredient approach tends to resolve skin issues.

**Skip it if:** Your dog is sedentary, overweight, or a senior with dental issues. Also skip if you have a toy breed — this formula is calibrated for medium to large working dogs.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I mix this with other Victor formulas?**
A: Yes, Victor actually encourages rotating proteins. Start with a 75/25 mix of old to new food over 7-10 days to avoid digestive upset.

**Q: Is this good for puppies?**
A: It's labeled for all life stages, but the calorie density is extreme for growing puppies. I'd stick with Victor's puppy-specific formula unless your pup is a future working dog with massive caloric needs.

**Q: How much should I feed a 50-pound active dog?**
A: Start at 2 cups per day and adjust based on body condition. You want to feel ribs but not see them. Check weight weekly for the first month.
