**Is this cage a smart buy or a flimsy compromise? Here's the honest breakdown.**

Most cat cages under $100 are either too small for a litter box or too wobbly to trust. The Oneluck Cat Cage Indoor with Litter Box tries to solve both problems with a modular, DIY design. At $99.99 with a 4.0-star average across 200 ratings, it's squarely in the "worth considering but read the fine print" zone. I've dug through the reviews and the specs so you don't have to gamble on a return.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains 12 metal wire panels, snap-together connectors, a plastic litter box tray, and a raised platform. The panels are roughly 14 x 14 inches each, giving you a base footprint around 42 x 28 inches when configured as a standard rectangle — enough room for one adult cat to stretch, but not two. The standout feature is the integrated litter box: a dedicated lower bay with a slide-out tray, so you're not cramming a separate box into the living area. Assembly is tool-free — panels click together with plastic clips. Expect 10–15 minutes to build, and it breaks down flat if you need to store it.

**How it holds up in real use**

The steel wire is thin — about 2mm — which is fine for a 10-pound cat but feels insubstantial if you have a Maine Coon or a rambunctious kitten that likes to body-check walls. The connectors are the weak point. They hold snugly at first, but several reviewers note they loosen over weeks of daily use. One buyer wrote, "My cat figured out how to push the bottom panel out within three days. I had to zip-tie the corners." That's not a dealbreaker — zip ties are a $3 fix — but it tells you the build tolerance is "budget" not "fortress."

The litter box tray itself gets consistent praise. It's deep enough to prevent scatter, and the slide-out design makes cleaning genuinely easy — no wrestling the whole cage apart. Ventilation is excellent, with gaps that keep ammonia fumes from pooling. However, there's no included bedding or shelf pad. The raised platform is bare wire, which cats tolerate but won't love. Budget $15–20 for a washable mat.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Three themes dominate the positive reviews: **versatility, cleanliness, and ease of setup.** The modular panels are the selling point — you can build a corner enclosure, a vertical tower, or a long run depending on your space. One reviewer summed it up: "I reconfigured it three times in the first week. It's like Legos for cats." Another noted the litter containment: "Finally a cage where the litter doesn't end up all over my apartment." The tool-free assembly is also a frequent win, especially for renters who need to set up and tear down quickly.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the connector loosening, the biggest complaint is **stability in tall configurations.** If you stack panels vertically for a multi-level tower, the structure gets top-heavy. Several buyers report it swaying when their cat jumps between levels. The fix is anchoring it to a wall, which isn't mentioned in the instructions. Also, the plastic connectors are not pet-proof — a determined chewer can destroy them. And there's no door latch option; the door is a simple swing panel that some cats learn to push open.

**Who should buy this**

- **Kitten owners** — perfect for a growing cat that needs a safe space while you're at work; the modular design grows with them.
- **Apartment dwellers with limited floor space** — the corner configuration fits tight spots.
- **Post-surgery or quarantine situations** — the open wire design lets you monitor a recovering pet easily.
- **Skip it if** you have a large-breed cat (15+ lbs), a heavy chewer, or need a permanent, rock-solid enclosure.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I connect two of these together?**
A: Yes, the connectors are universal, so a second unit can expand your layout. Just note that each additional unit runs about the same price — you're better off buying a larger cage upfront if you know you'll need the space.

**Q: Is the litter box easy to clean without disassembling the cage?**
A: The tray slides out from the side, so you can clean it in under a minute without touching the panels. This is genuinely one of the better designs at this price point.

**Q: Will this hold a cat that's aggressive about escaping?**
A: Honestly, no. The plastic connectors won't withstand a determined escape artist. If your cat is a known Houdini, invest in a welded-wire cage with metal fasteners.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, flexible enclosure for a small-to-medium cat and don't mind a little DIY reinforcement (zip ties, wall anchors). It's a solid 4-star value for kittens, recovery periods, or temporary housing, with a genuinely well-designed litter tray.

**Skip it if** you need a permanent, escape-proof structure for a large or determined cat — the lightweight build will frustrate you within a month.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Good bones, weak joints. Worth the price if you set realistic expectations.
