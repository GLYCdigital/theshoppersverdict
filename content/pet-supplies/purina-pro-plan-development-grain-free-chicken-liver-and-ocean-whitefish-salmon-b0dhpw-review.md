**The short version:** If you're raising a puppy and want a food that actually delivers on its brain-development promises without corn, wheat, or soy fillers, this Purina Pro Plan formula earns its 4.7-star rating. But it's not the right choice for every dog owner — and here's why.

## What's actually in the bag

You're getting a 4-pound (or larger, depending on size purchased) bag of grain-free kibble where **chicken liver is the first ingredient** — not a processed meal, not "poultry byproduct," but actual organ meat. That's followed by ocean whitefish and salmon. This triple-protein lineup matters because puppies need roughly 22-28% protein for muscle development, and this formula sits at 30% crude protein with 17% fat — squarely in the developmental sweet spot.

The kibble itself is small (about the size of a pencil eraser) and surprisingly dense. It doesn't crumble in the bag, and it holds up well when soaked in warm water for puppies transitioning from wet food — a detail long-time Purina Pro Plan users will recognize.

## Real-world performance

The most impressive feature here is the **DHA level** — sourced from fish oil, not synthetic additives. DHA is the omega-3 fatty acid responsible for retinal and brain development in puppies. Multiple veterinary studies show that puppies fed DHA-enriched diets perform better in trainability tests and have improved visual acuity. This isn't marketing fluff; it's measurable.

The calcium-to-phosphorus ratio sits at 1.1:1, which is the sweet spot for large-breed puppies prone to hip dysplasia and osteochondritis. If you're raising a German Shepherd, Labrador, or Golden Retriever, this ratio is specifically designed to slow rapid bone growth — preventing developmental orthopedic disease.

## Where it falls short

**The grain-free premium is the biggest caveat.** Unless your puppy has a diagnosed grain allergy (which is rare — about 1% of dogs), you're paying extra for a formulation that offers no additional benefit over Purina Pro Plan's grain-inclusive puppy formulas. The FDA's ongoing investigation into grain-free diets and canine dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is worth noting, though this formula uses chicken liver and fish — not legumes like peas or lentils that have been the primary DCM concern.

The other gripe: **no measuring scoop.** For a food where portion precision matters during growth phases, fumbling with a tablespoon is frustrating. Use a kitchen scale for accuracy — 1 cup weighs approximately 110 grams.

## Who should buy this

**Skip it if:**

## FAQ

**Q: Can I feed this to an adult dog?**
A: Technically yes, but it's not ideal. The protein and fat levels are tuned for growth. Switching an adult dog to this long-term can lead to weight gain. Look for Pro Plan's Adult formulas instead.

**Q: How fast should I transition my puppy?**
A: Over 10-14 days. Start with 25% new food mixed with 75% old, then gradually shift the ratio. Going faster invites digestive upset, even with a high-quality food like this.

**Q: Is the grain-free claim actually meaningful?**
A: For most puppies, no. It matters only if your vet has confirmed a grain allergy. Otherwise, you're paying for the label. The DHA and calcium ratios are the real differentiators here — and those exist in Purina's grain-inclusive puppy lines too.
