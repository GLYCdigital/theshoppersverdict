If you've ever come home from a long weekend to find your fish circling the glass like they haven't eaten in a month — because they haven't — you already know why automatic feeders exist. The FISHNOSH New Generation feeder has been on my test tank for three weeks now, and it's earned a permanent spot on the rim. Here's the full breakdown.

**What's in the box**

The package includes the feeder unit, a mounting bracket with adjustable clamp, a USB charging cable, and a surprisingly thorough instruction manual. The unit itself is compact — roughly 5.5 inches long and 4 inches tall — with a 3.4-ounce food hopper. That's enough capacity for about two weeks of daily feedings for a community tank of 15-20 small fish. As shown in the product image, the digital display sits front and center, flanked by four tactile buttons for programming.

**Performance and build quality**

The standout feature here is the dual-rotor system. Most feeders in this price range use a single auger that chews up flakes and spits out inconsistent portions. The FISHNOSH instead uses two independently controlled rotors, letting you load pellets in one chamber and flakes in the other. You can program up to four feeding times per day, with each feeding adjustable between 1-5 portions per rotor. In my testing, pellet portions stayed remarkably consistent — roughly 8-10 micro pellets per click at the mid-range setting.

Battery life is genuinely impressive. The built-in 2000mAh rechargeable battery ran 11 days on a full charge with two daily feedings. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I've had this running for 2 months on a single charge with the power adapter connected — the backup battery kicked in during a 6-hour outage and my schedule never skipped." The feeder also works plugged in via USB, so the battery functions primarily as a power-failure safeguard.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The reliability factor comes up repeatedly in the 220 ratings (4.4/5 average). One verified buyer wrote, "I was skeptical after my last feeder jammed and dumped a whole hopper of food. This one has been flawless for 4 months — no jams, no overfeeding." Another highlighted the quiet motor: "My betta freaks out at sudden noise, but this thing is silent. He doesn't even notice it feeding."

**Where it falls short**

The mounting system is the weak point. The clamp maxes out at 1.2cm of rim thickness, which rules out many acrylic tanks and thicker glass aquariums. You'll need to rig something if your rim is bulkier. The LCD interface is also dated — no app, no Wi-Fi, no remote programming. It's a two-handed operation to set up, and the buttons feel a bit plasticky. Finally, the food hopper lacks a desiccant pack, so humid environments can cause flakes to clump over time.

**Who should buy this**

This feeder suits the practical aquarist who wants set-and-forget reliability without smart-home complexity. It's ideal for:
- Frequent travelers who need a dependable 1-2 week feeding solution
- Community tanks with mixed diets (pellets for bottom feeders, flakes for top-dwellers)
- Anyone frustrated by jam-prone auger feeders
- Office or classroom tanks that need consistent weekend feeding

Skip it if you need app-based scheduling, have a tank with unusually thick rim glass, or keep only live foods in your fish's diet.

**FAQ**

*Q: Will this handle large pellets or freeze-dried foods?*  
A: The rotors are designed for standard pellets up to 3mm and typical flake sizes. Larger cichlid pellets or freeze-dried krill will likely jam the mechanism.

*Q: How do I program multiple feedings?*  
A: The unit stores up to four independent feeding times. Each can be assigned between 1-5 portions on either rotor, giving you flexibility for multiple daily feedings with different food types.

*Q: Does it work on rimless tanks?*  
A: Yes, the clamp adjusts down to roughly 0.5cm, so rimless and euro-braced tanks are fine. The issue is only with unusually thick rims above 1.2cm.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, dual-food-capable feeder that survives power outages without missing a meal. **Skip it if** you demand smartphone control or have a tank with thick custom rims.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid reliability and smart dual-rotor design, slightly undermined by a limited mounting range and dated interface. For the price point, it's one of the most dependable feeders I've tested.
