**A 71-inch cat tower for $66 — without the wobble. Here's the real story.**

Most cat trees in this price range are either short, flimsy, or both. The Gitelsnour 71" Multi-Level Cat Tree breaks that pattern, which is exactly why it's earned a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings. But taller isn't automatically better — let's break down what actually matters before you buy.

**What's in the box**

You get 19 components: three main platforms, two perches, a hideout cubby, four sisal-wrapped posts (two at 40mm diameter, two at 50mm), and a hanging toy with a replaceable bell. The plush covering is a short-pile faux fur that feels soft but isn't the scratchy felt you see on rock-bottom towers. The hardware kit includes the hex key, bolts, and washers — plus a spare of each, which is a thoughtful touch.

**Build quality and real-world stability**

The frame uses particleboard with a claimed 15mm thickness on the main platforms. In practice, that's adequate — no sagging under a 14-pound Maine Coon after three weeks of daily use. The base is 17.7 inches square, which is wider than most competitors at this height. That's the critical spec. A tall tree with a narrow base is a tipping hazard; this one holds firm.

The sisal posts deserve a specific mention. Instead of the thin jute rope that shreds in a month, these use a denser weave that's held up well against aggressive scratching. One buyer noted, *"My cat destroyed two cheaper trees in six months. This one doesn't even show wear after three months of daily clawing."*

**What buyers consistently praise**

Beyond stability, the two features that appear repeatedly in positive reviews are the hideout cubby and the height-to-price ratio. The cubby has a 12.5-inch diameter opening — large enough for most adult cats but not so big that timid cats feel exposed. The height is the real winner: *"Finally a tree my Bengal actually uses. She's on the top perch 80% of the day watching out the window."* For multi-cat households, the three distinct levels reduce resource guarding because cats can claim separate territory.

**Where it falls short**

Assembly is the biggest friction point. The picture-based instructions are functional but sparse — you'll likely disassemble one section when you realize a post orientation is wrong. Budget 45 minutes, not the 20 the listing suggests.

The top perch has no raised rim. Cats that prefer a backrest or headrest may avoid it. Position this tree against a wall to give your cat a sense of security.

The sisal sheds small fibers for the first week or two. It's cosmetic, not a defect, but you'll notice it on dark floors.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers with vertical space** — this gives your cat territory without taking up floor footprint
- **Owners of active, climbing breeds** like Bengals, Maine Coons, or Siberians who need height
- **Multi-cat households** where separate levels reduce conflict
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who want a tall tree without the $150+ premium brands charge

Skip it if you have a kitten under 6 months — they'll outgrow the smaller perches quickly, and you'd be better served buying a larger tree once they're full-grown.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this support a heavy cat?**  
The base platform is rated for 35 pounds, but the top perch is the weak point at roughly 22 pounds. A 15-18 pound cat is fine. Anything heavier, reinforce the top platform or keep it lower.

**Q: Does it require wall mounting?**  
No, and the wide base handles most cats without it. If you have a particularly rambunctious feline or hardwood floors, a single wall anchor strap adds peace of mind for under $10.

**Q: Is the plush material machine washable?**  
The platform covers zip off and are machine washable on cold. The sisal posts and base are not — spot clean those with a damp cloth and pet-safe cleaner.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely tall, stable cat tree under $70 and don't mind a 45-minute assembly session. The materials outperform the price point, and the height gives your cat real territory.

**Skip it if** you need a quick assembly or have a cat that prefers enclosed, rimmed perches. For those cases, a shorter tree with deeper baskets would serve better.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a rare value-to-height combination that delivers on its promise, with only minor assembly and design compromises holding it back from perfection.
