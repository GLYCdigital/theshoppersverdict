# Does This Wellness Wet Food Justify the Hype? A 204-Review Deep Dive

You've seen the amber cans stacked in pet stores. You've heard the "Complete Health" promise. But with a 4.1-star average across 204 Amazon ratings, the Wellness Complete Health Natural Grain Free wet food tells a more complicated story than the marketing suggests. I've broken down the ingredients, the texture complaints, and the actual feeding experience so you don't waste money on a case your cat refuses to touch.

## What's Actually in the Can

This is a chunked food, not a pâté. As shown in the product image, you get identifiable cubes of tuna suspended in a thin broth. The first ingredient is tuna, followed by fish broth — that's a solid sign you're getting real protein, not filler. The guaranteed analysis delivers 12% crude protein and 2% crude fat, which puts it in the right range for adult maintenance. Moisture sits around 78%, matching the hydration goals most vets recommend for cats who don't drink enough water.

The ingredient deck avoids the usual red flags: no carrageenan, no artificial preservatives, no meat by-products. If you're comparing this against Fancy Feast or Friskies, the gap in ingredient quality is obvious.

## What Buyers Actually Say

Positive reviews cluster around two themes: picky eaters and digestive health. One verified buyer wrote, "My 14-year-old has become increasingly picky and this is one of the few things he'll consistently finish." Another noted, "I rotate between three Wellness flavors and this is the only one my cat doesn't leave half-eaten."

The texture is the differentiator. Cats who hate pâté will often devour this because the chunks feel like real food. Multiple reviewers specifically mentioned their cats "eat the chunks and drink the broth" — a sign that both the protein and hydration aspects are working.

## Where It Falls Short

The criticism is consistent: the broth is thin. Several reviewers described it as "watery" compared to other grain-free options. One put it bluntly: "My cat lapped up the liquid and left half the chunks." That's a real problem if your cat prefers gravy-style foods with more body.

Then there's the price. At roughly $2.50–$3.00 per 5.5-ounce can depending on the seller, you're paying a premium. A 24-count case runs $60–$70. That's steep compared to similar grain-free wet foods that land closer to $1.50–$2.00 per can. For multi-cat households, this gets expensive fast.

One more caveat: the 4.1-star average isn't stellar. A meaningful chunk of the 204 ratings are 3-star or below, and the complaints beyond texture include "canned food smell is unusually strong" and "my cat vomited after eating" — though the latter is impossible to attribute to this food specifically without a vet's input.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you have a cat that's bored with pâté, a senior cat needing hydration support, or a cat with a sensitive stomach that tolerates fish-based diets well. The clean ingredient deck and high moisture content make it a defensible daily food.

**Skip it if** you're on a budget, your cat prefers thick gravy textures, or your cat has a known fish sensitivity. Also skip if you're feeding multiple cats — the cost per meal adds up quickly.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this food complete and balanced for all life stages?**
A: No — this is formulated for adult maintenance. Kittens and pregnant/nursing cats need different calcium-to-phosphorus ratios. Wellness makes separate kitten formulas; use those instead.

**Q: How many cans should I feed my cat daily?**
A: For a 10-pound adult cat, plan on roughly 1 can per 5–6 pounds of body weight per day, split into two meals. That's about 1.5–2 cans daily for an average cat. Adjust based on whether you're also feeding dry food.

**Q: Can I use this as a topper instead of a full meal?**
A: Yes — many reviewers do exactly that. The chunks and broth mix well with dry kibble, and the thinner texture means it coats food without turning it into a soggy mess.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

Buy it if your cat values texture and you prioritize clean ingredients over budget. Skip it if you need a value option or your cat demands thick gravy. This is a solid, above-average wet food that earns its place in a rotation — just don't expect it to be the only thing in the bowl. The 4.1-star average reflects genuine quality with real, texture-based limitations. For the right cat, it's worth every penny. For the wrong cat, it's a pricey lesson in feline stubbornness.
