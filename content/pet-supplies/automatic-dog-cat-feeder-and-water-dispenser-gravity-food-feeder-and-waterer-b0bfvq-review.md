If you're tired of refilling bowls twice a day, this gravity-fed combo unit deserves a hard look. With 220 Amazon ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's clearly doing something right. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through the specs and owner feedback.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains two separate reservoirs — a food dispenser and a water dispenser — each holding 1.5 liters. Both sit on stainless steel trays that lift out for cleaning. The whole assembly measures roughly 13 x 8 x 8 inches, so it fits under most kitchen cabinets or in a laundry room corner. The food side uses a gravity drop mechanism: kibble falls as your pet eats, keeping the bowl topped off. The water side works the same way with a simple vacuum seal.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The plastic reservoirs are BPA-free and feel sturdy enough to survive a knock-over attempt. The stainless steel bowls are the standout feature — they won't cause chin acne in cats or trap bacteria the way plastic bowls do. Owners consistently mention how easy cleanup is. One reviewer noted, "The bowls pop right out and go in the dishwasher. That alone sold me."

The anti-skid base matters more than you'd think. Multiple buyers with German Shepherds and Labs report the unit stays put during enthusiastic mealtimes. The weighted design handles a 70-pound dog shoving its face in without tipping.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Looking through the feedback, three themes dominate:

- **Convenience**: "I fill this once every three days for my two cats. Best purchase I've made for them."
- **Cleanliness**: The removable stainless bowls beat any plastic feeder hands-down.
- **Value**: Compared to $100+ automatic feeders with timers, this does the job at a fraction of the cost.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. This is a free-feeding system — there's no portion control. If your pet inhales food until it's gone, this will make problems worse. The reservoir empties faster than you expect with larger dogs.

The water reservoir has a design quirk: the translucent plastic looks clear from the outside, but the water level is genuinely hard to read without tilting the unit. You'll likely run your finger inside to check. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying.

A few buyers reported slow drips from the water side after assembly. The issue traces to the lid gasket — it needs to seat perfectly. If you're not careful during setup, you'll wake up to a small puddle. Make sure the seal is flush before you fill it.

**Who Should Buy This**

This feeder fits three specific situations:

1. **Cat owners with multiple pets** — the 1.5-liter capacity covers several days for most felines.
2. **Dog owners who travel on weekends** — it bridges the gap without buying an expensive programmable feeder.
3. **Anyone with plastic-bowl issues** — the stainless steel trays justify the purchase alone.

Skip it if you have a food-obsessed pet, a dog over 50 pounds that eats aggressively, or you need precise portion control for a weight-management plan.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I put wet food in this feeder?**
A: No. This works exclusively with dry kibble. Wet food will spoil in the reservoir and clog the gravity mechanism.

**Q: How often do I need to clean it?**
A: Weekly, minimum. The bowls need daily rinsing if you have multiple pets, but the reservoirs only need a thorough wash every 7-10 days to prevent bacterial buildup.

**Q: Will a large-breed dog knock this over?**
A: The anti-skid base helps, but a determined 80-pound dog can still tip it. It's better suited for cats and small-to-medium dogs under 50 pounds.
