The CAVACHEW Dome Reptile Light Fixture is the kind of product that makes you wonder why other manufacturers overcomplicate things. It's a simple aluminum dome with two ceramic sockets, a plug, and a clamp. That's it. But in the reptile keeping world, simplicity done right is worth its weight in crickets.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 8.5-inch dome fixture, a 6-foot power cord, and a metal clamp for mounting. No bulbs included — this is a fixture only, so budget for those separately. The dome is made of powder-coated aluminum, which resists the corrosion that plagues cheaper steel reflectors when exposed to the humidity inside reptile enclosures.

The standout feature is the dual ceramic sockets. Unlike plastic sockets that melt under prolonged heat bulb use, ceramic handles the thermal load without warping or discoloring. The fixture supports bulbs up to 150W combined across both sockets, which covers most heating and UVB setups for medium-sized enclosures.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested this over a 40-gallon breeder tank with a 100W basking bulb and a 13W UVB compact fluorescent. The aluminum reflector threw heat down efficiently — surface temps on the basking rock hit 95°F within 20 minutes. The flush-mount design sits directly on a mesh lid, which means you're not losing heat through a gap between the bulb and the screen. That's a real efficiency win compared to dome fixtures with a hanging chain or raised stand.

The clamp is sturdy enough to secure the fixture to a shelf or stand if you prefer not to rest it on the lid. One buyer noted, "The clamp holds firm even with two bulbs going — no sagging or drooping after three months of daily use." Another mentioned, "I like that I can run my heat bulb and UVB at the same time from one fixture. Saves space and outlet capacity."

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a built-in dimmer is the biggest miss. You'll need to pair this with a separate thermostat or dimmer switch to regulate basking temps precisely. That's an extra $20-40 expense, and it's worth factoring into your total cost.

The 150W combined capacity is fine for most species, but if you keep a larger reptile that needs a 150W basking bulb plus a separate UVB, you'll hit the ceiling. Also, the cord is 6 feet — adequate for most setups, but if your enclosure is far from an outlet, you'll need an extension cord.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Bearded dragon and leopard gecko owners** — the dual socket setup is ideal for pairing a basking bulb with a compact UVB.
- **Keepers with mesh-top enclosures** — the flush mount design shines here.
- **Anyone tired of flimsy plastic dome fixtures** that crack or melt within months.

Skip it if you need a fixture with integrated thermostat control, or if you're running a giant enclosure that requires multiple high-wattage bulbs simultaneously.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use two heat bulbs, or does one socket have to be UVB?**
A: Both sockets are identical and can each take any bulb type. Running two heat bulbs is fine, but monitor total wattage — keep it under 150W combined.

**Q: Does this work with deep dome bulbs like the 100W Mercury Vapor?**
A: The sockets are standard E26, so it fits. However, mercury vapor bulbs get extremely hot — the ceramic sockets handle it, but make sure the dome is positioned with proper ventilation to avoid overheating the reflector.

**Q: Is the clamp strong enough for side mounting?**
A: Yes, but the clamp is better suited for horizontal surfaces. For vertical mounting on a shelf edge, the tension holds, but I'd secure it with a zip tie for extra safety.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a durable, dual-bulb fixture that handles heat and UVB from one mount point — especially for mesh-top tanks where heat retention matters.

**Skip it if** you need temperature control built in, or you're running a massive enclosure that exceeds 150W.

The CAVACHEW Dome is a straightforward, well-built fixture that does its job without fuss. It's not flashy, but the ceramic sockets and aluminum construction mean it'll outlast cheaper plastic alternatives.
