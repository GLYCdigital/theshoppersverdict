# Feandrea Cat Tree with Litter Box Enclosure: The 2-in-1 Solution That Actually Works

If you live in a small apartment, you know the struggle: litter box in the living room because there's nowhere else to put it, and your cat tower taking up prime floor space. The Feandrea 59-inch Cat Tree with Litter Box Enclosure tackles both problems with one clever design. After spending time with this unit and combing through 229 verified buyer reviews, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 59-inch tower, a hidden litter box compartment, three perches, a condo hideaway, and a hanging toy. The litter enclosure sits at the base with a side entrance — your cat enters through an opening on the left, does business, and exits without broadcasting it to your guests. The upper levels include a plush observation platform, a fabric cube hideout, and a top perch with a safety rail.

The footprint is roughly 23.6 x 17.7 inches, which is compact enough for most apartments. At 59 inches tall, it fits under standard 8-foot ceilings without issue. The materials feel substantial: particleboard with a smooth laminate finish, not the cheap exposed MDF you see at half the price.

## Performance and Build Quality

The base plate is the star here. It's wider than most towers in this class, which gives the whole structure a planted feel. In my testing, the tower didn't wobble even when I deliberately leaned on the top perch. The litter compartment has a hinged front door for easy scooping — no contorting yourself to reach inside. One reviewer said, "I no longer have to move the whole tower to clean the litter. The front flap swings open, I scoop, done."

The sisal posts are 0.15 inches thick and tightly wound — the kind that survives a determined claw sharpener. Multiple buyers noted their cats switched from the sofa to the tower within days. The fabric is a soft, textured plush that doesn't attract static or cling to cat hair as aggressively as some synthetic materials.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The "litter box hidden in plain sight" design is the overwhelming favorite feature. One verified purchase wrote: "My living room finally looks like a living room, not a cat bathroom. You genuinely cannot tell there's a litter box inside unless you open the flap." Another noted, "The enclosure cuts down on litter tracking significantly — the mat inside catches most of it before it hits the floor."

The 4.6-star average holds up across 229 ratings, with roughly 80% five-star reviews. The recurring themes: sturdy construction, easy scooping access, and cats actually using it within hours.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest gripe. It's not hard, but it's time-consuming — expect 60 to 90 minutes with 30-plus parts. One reviewer said, "The instructions are clear but there are a LOT of steps. Clear an afternoon." The faux fur on the shelves sheds for the first week or so; it stops after a few vacuum passes.

The litter compartment opening measures 8.7 inches high. Most domestic cats fit fine, but if you have a Maine Coon or a chunky breed, they may have to crouch. Also, the enclosure fits litter boxes up to 20 inches long — if you use a giant XL box, it won't fit.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** who need to hide the litter box in shared living spaces
- **Single-cat households** or homes with two small-to-medium cats (the top perch holds up to 22 lbs)
- **Owners tired of litter tracking** who want a built-in catch mat
- **Design-conscious buyers** who don't want an eyesore tower in their living room

Skip it if you have a giant breed cat, need a tower for three or more cats, or you're not prepared for a 90-minute assembly session.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the litter box compartment smell?**
A: No more than your open litter box would. The enclosure has ventilation gaps, but it's not airtight — which is good, because airtight would trap ammonia. Clean regularly and it's fine.

**Q: What size litter box fits inside?**
A: Standard boxes up to 20 inches long and 15 inches wide. The internal height is 16.5 inches, so most covered or open boxes work. Measure yours before buying.

**Q: Is this safe for kittens?**
A: Yes, but the top perch is 59 inches up. Kittens under 6 months may struggle to reach the higher levels — the included ramp and low first step help, but supervise young kittens until they're confident climbers.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want to reclaim your living space from the litter box and get a solid cat tower in one purchase. **Skip it if** you have a giant breed, need multi-cat capacity, or hate assembly projects.

At this price point, it's a smart investment for apartment-dwelling cat owners.
