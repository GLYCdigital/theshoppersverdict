If you're feeding your cat wet food on a schedule, you already know the struggle: you're either rushing home at lunch or leaving food out to spoil. The PETLIBRO Automatic Cat Feeder with refrigeration promises to solve that — but with a 3.2-star average across 160 ratings, the question isn't whether the concept works. It's whether *this* unit delivers.

Here's what I found digging through the specs, the praise, and the complaints.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the feeder base, a 3-liter food container (holds roughly 8-10 cans of wet food depending on brand), a refrigerated compartment liner, an AC adapter, and a backup battery slot. The "New Upgr" in the title refers to the revised ice pack system and improved sealing gasket — both direct responses to earlier model complaints.

The unit measures about 13 inches wide and 9 inches tall, so it'll fit on most countertops but isn't exactly compact. The food drops into a stainless steel tray that you'll want to wash daily — the one part that *doesn't* go in the dishwasher, which is mildly annoying.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The refrigeration is the headline feature, and it's genuinely useful. The compartment keeps wet food at a safe temperature for up to 24 hours, which means you can set two meals in the morning and know the second one won't be rancid by dinner. That's a legit game-changer for multi-meal wet food schedules.

The app lets you schedule 1 to 4 meals per day with portions adjustable in 1/8-cup increments. Setting it up takes about five minutes, and the pairing process is straightforward — when it works. Multiple reviewers report Bluetooth dropouts and app sync failures, particularly on Android. One owner wrote, "The feeder works great when the app cooperates, but I've had to re-pair it three times in two months."

## What Owners Consistently Praise

The portion control gets steady thumbs-up. "I can finally feed my two cats different amounts without hovering over them," one reviewer noted. The battery backup is another bright spot — if the power blips, the feeder keeps its schedule, which matters if you travel.

The sealed lid also earns credit for keeping food fresh and odors contained. Several owners mentioned it's noticeably less smelly than leaving a can in the fridge.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the uncomfortable part. The 3.2-star rating isn't random noise — it's a pattern. The most common complaints cluster around three issues:

**Reliability.** Multiple buyers report units dying within 3-6 months. The auger mechanism (the part that pushes food into the tray) seems to be the weak point, with several owners describing it jamming or simply stopping entirely. One frustrated review: "Worked perfectly for four months, then stopped dispensing. Customer service sent a replacement that failed the same way."

**The ice pack problem.** Despite the "refrigerated" marketing, some versions rely on included ice packs rather than true compressor cooling. In warm rooms (above 75°F), owners report food reaching unsafe temperatures by hour 12. The upgraded version improves this, but it's still not a real refrigerator — it's an insulated box with cooling elements.

**App stability.** The mobile app is functional but finicky. Firmware updates sometimes reset schedules, and the Bluetooth range is short enough that you need to be in the same room to adjust settings.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you feed wet food on a fixed schedule, live in a climate-controlled home, and want portion control without hand-feeding. It's also reasonable for a single cat — multi-cat households will find the 3-liter capacity limiting.

**Skip it if** you need something bulletproof for daily use, your home runs warm, or you're not comfortable troubleshooting app connectivity. If reliability is non-negotiable, look at simpler gravity feeders or the more expensive PetSafe models with better track records.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the refrigeration actually keep food cold, or just cool?**
A: It maintains food below 40°F for about 24 hours using ice packs and insulation — not a compressor. In a 70°F room, it works. Above 75°F, food may approach unsafe temps after 12 hours.

**Q: Can I use this for dry food too?**
A: Technically yes, but it's overkill. The refrigerated compartment is designed for wet food; using it for kibble wastes the cooling feature.

**Q: How often do I need to replace the ice packs?**
A: Every 24 hours. You'll want a second set in your freezer to rotate, which adds cost to the setup.

## The Verdict

The PETLIBRO Automatic Cat Feeder is a great idea with inconsistent execution. The refrigeration genuinely solves a real problem, and the portion control is excellent — but the reliability complaints are too frequent to ignore. At this price point, you're gambling on whether you get a unit that lasts or one that dies at month four.

**Buy it if** you want automated wet food feeding and accept the maintenance (daily ice pack swaps, app troubleshooting). **Skip it if** reliability is your top priority — there are sturdier options, even if they lack the refrigeration feature.

**Rating: 3.3/5** — A promising concept held back by quality control.
