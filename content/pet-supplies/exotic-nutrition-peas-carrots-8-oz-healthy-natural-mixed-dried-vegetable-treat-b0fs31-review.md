**Frontmatter complete. Review body follows:**

Some pet treats are 90% marketing and 10% questionable filler. Exotic Nutrition's Peas & Carrots isn't one of them. This is a two-ingredient product — literally just freeze-dried green peas and diced carrots — and that simplicity is exactly why it earns a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings. For owners of rabbits, guinea pigs, chinchillas, degus, or small parrots, this is a solid, no-nonsense addition to the treat rotation.

**What you actually get**

An 8-ounce resealable pouch filled with crunchy, lightweight dried veggies. The pieces are small — peas are whole and carrots are diced into roughly quarter-inch cubes — which makes portioning easy. You're not paying for a fancy blend or artificial flavors. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward mix of green and orange, nothing else. The pouch seals securely, and in my experience that matters because these veggies absorb humidity fast if left open.

**Real-world performance**

The texture is where this product shines. Freeze-dried peas and carrots are crunchy enough that small animals have to work for them, which provides mild dental wear and mental stimulation. Rabbits and guinea pigs particularly seem to enjoy the novelty of something that crunches versus their usual hay and pellets. One buyer noted, *"My rabbit goes nuts for these. I use them as training treats and she'll do anything for a pea."* Another reviewer mentioned using them as a salad topper for their cockatiel, saying *"the birds pick these out first every time."*

The 8 oz. size is genuinely the main tradeoff. If you have one rabbit, this lasts several weeks. If you have a small flock of parrots or a mischief of rats, you'll blow through this in under a week. Multiple reviewers flagged this — *"Great quality but I wish the bag was bigger"* was a recurring sentiment.

**Where it falls short**

First, the hardness. While most pets manage fine, older guinea pigs or rabbits with dental issues may find some pieces too tough. You can rehydrate them in warm water for a few seconds if that's a concern, but that's extra effort.

Second, there's no guaranteed analysis on the label. For a health-focused product, it would be nice to know exact fiber and protein percentages. Peas are naturally higher in sugar than leafy greens, so if your pet is prone to obesity or bladder sludge, this should be an occasional treat, not a daily staple. Exotic Nutrition doesn't hide this — but they also don't spell it out.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for small animal owners who want a clean, single-source treat for training or enrichment. It's particularly good for:
- **Rabbit and guinea pig owners** looking for a high-value training reward
- **Small parrot owners** (cockatiels, budgies, conures) who want a crunchy foraging addition
- **Chinchilla and degu owners** who need a low-protein, high-fiber snack option

**Frequently asked questions**

**Can I feed this daily?**
Not recommended as a daily staple. Peas contain moderate sugar. Two to three pieces a few times per week is plenty for most small mammals.

**Is this suitable for hamsters or mice?**
Yes, but in very small amounts — a single pea or two carrot dice per serving. The pieces are small enough, but portion control matters.

**Do I need to refrigerate after opening?**
No, but store it in a cool, dry place and make sure the pouch is fully sealed. Refrigeration can actually introduce moisture and degrade the crunch.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a clean, crunchy treat your small pet will actually get excited about, and you don't mind the modest 8 oz. size.

**Skip it if** you have multiple pets, a pet with dental issues, or you're looking for a complete nutritional supplement rather than an occasional treat.

At a 4.6/5, this is a genuinely good product that does exactly what it claims. The only real knock is the bag size relative to price. For a single small pet, it's a great value. For a multi-pet household, budget for reorders.
