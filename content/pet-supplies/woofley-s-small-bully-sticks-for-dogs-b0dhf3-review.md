Let's be honest about bully sticks: they're the gold standard for aggressive chewers, but they come with baggage — smell, price, and the occasional splinter scare. Woofley's Small Bully Sticks claim to address the odor issue while delivering a safe chew for little jaws. After digging through 200 ratings and weighing the real-world feedback, here's the breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

Each bag contains 6-inch bully sticks sourced from grass-fed, free-range cattle — specifically the pizzle muscle, which is essentially 100% protein. There are no binders, no rawhide, no artificial flavors, and no chemical processing. The sticks are slow-roasted, which is what gives them that characteristic savory aroma (more on that in a minute). The packaging is a resealable pouch, which matters more than you'd think — bully sticks absorb moisture and turn stale fast, and this bag actually holds a seal.

## Real-World Performance

The 6-inch length is the sweet spot for small and medium breeds. It's long enough that a determined chewer can't swallow it whole, but compact enough that a 10-pound terrier can grip it comfortably. The density is where these shine: they're hard enough to provide meaningful chewing resistance but soften gradually as saliva breaks them down, so they don't turn into rock-hard shards mid-session.

Most dogs finish one stick in 20-40 minutes depending on chew intensity. That's reasonable for a natural chew — not a five-minute snack, not an all-day ordeal. The texture evolution is actually a safety feature: as the stick softens, it becomes pliable rather than brittle, reducing the risk of sharp fragments.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in positive reviews is simplicity. One verified purchaser noted, *"My picky Shih Tzu actually finishes these — no vomiting, no digestive upset, just a happy dog."* Another owner of a senior Chihuahua appreciated that the sticks were soft enough for aging teeth but still engaging enough to hold attention.

The single-ingredient aspect also earns repeated mentions. For dogs with sensitive stomachs or poultry allergies, a pure beef pizzle with zero fillers is a safe bet. Multiple reviewers confirmed their dogs with known food sensitivities tolerated these without issues.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. Woofley's markets these as "odor-free," and that's an exaggeration. They're less pungent than generic bully sticks — the slow-roasting process does reduce that barnyard funk — but they're not scentless. If your dog chews one in the living room, you'll notice. It's a warm, savory smell rather than an offensive one, but "odor-free" is a stretch.

The bigger concern is consistency. Several reviews flagged occasional brittle sticks that splinter at the ends, especially near the tapered tip. These aren't dangerous if you supervise, but they're not a "leave your dog alone with it" chew. One reviewer wrote, *"Two sticks in the bag were so dry they cracked rather than chewed. My dog still loved them, but I had to take the pieces away."* That's a quality-control issue worth noting.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a small or medium breed (under 30 pounds) that needs a digestible, single-protein chew. It's particularly good for dogs with poultry allergies, picky eaters who turn their nose at synthetic chews, and owners who want a natural alternative to rawhide without the chemical processing.

**Skip it if:** You have a power chewer — a 70-pound Lab will demolish these in five minutes and you'll go broke replacing them. Also skip if you're extremely sensitive to meat odors; despite the claims, these aren't scent-free.

## FAQ

**How many sticks come in a bag?**
The standard package contains 10 sticks, each 6 inches long. Check the listing for bulk options if you have multiple dogs or a heavy chewer.

**Are these safe for puppies?**
Yes, for puppies over 4 months with adult teeth. Supervise the first few sessions — the sticks soften as they're chewed, but puppies should never be left unattended with any chew.

**Can these cause digestive issues?**
They're 100% beef pizzle with no additives, so they're highly digestible. However, they're rich in protein — introduce slowly if your dog isn't used to bully sticks to avoid loose stools.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Woofley's Small Bully Sticks are a solid mid-tier option that gets the fundamentals right: clean ingredients, appropriate sizing, and better-than-average odor control. They're not the best bully sticks on the market — the inconsistent density between batches and the overstated odor claims keep them from a perfect score. But for small-dog owners who want a natural, digestible chew that won't wreck the budget, these deliver good value.

**Buy it if** you need a reliable, single-ingredient chew for a small dog with a sensitive stomach. **Skip it if** you have a heavy chewer or expect truly odor-free performance. At this price point, they're a reasonable everyday option — just keep an eye on the brittle ones.
