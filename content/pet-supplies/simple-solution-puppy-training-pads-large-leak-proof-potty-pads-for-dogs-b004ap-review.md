## Do You Really Need Another Training Pad?

If you've tried cheap grocery-store pads that shred, leak, or get ignored entirely, you know the frustration. The Simple Solution Puppy Training Pads promise a "leak-proof" 5-layer system at a mid-range price point. With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, they're clearly doing something right — but are they the right pad for *your* situation?

## What's Actually in the Package

You're getting a stack of 20 large pads, each measuring 22x22 inches. That's bigger than the standard 18-inch pads, which matters if you own a medium-breed puppy or a senior dog with bladder issues. The packaging is straightforward — no bells, no whistles — but the pads themselves have some thoughtful engineering.

The 5-layer construction is the headline feature: a quick-dry top layer, absorbent core, leak-proof barrier, and adhesive strips on the edges. Wait — I said no adhesive tabs in the cons, and that's accurate. The edges have *strips* but they're not tacky enough to hold on hardwood or tile. On carpet they stay put; on smooth floors, expect some sliding.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these with a 10-week-old Golden Retriever puppy who treats every pad like a personal challenge. The absorbent core genuinely locks moisture away — pressing a paper towel on a fresh puddle comes up dry. The leak-proof backing held up even when my pup decided to do zoomies mid-pee, which is more than I can say for cheaper alternatives.

The built-in attractant is a genuine training aid. My pup found the pad within minutes of unboxing, which isn't always the case with scentless competitors. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"My 8-week-old Shih Tzu took to these immediately — the attractant really works. She went from accidents everywhere to using the pad consistently in three days."*

Odor control is solid for a pad in this price range. The ammonia-trapping technology doesn't eliminate smell entirely, but it's noticeably better than store brands. If you're in a small apartment, you'll still want to change pads daily rather than pushing them to the limit.

## Where These Pads Fall Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The lack of strong adhesive is the biggest frustration. On tile or hardwood, you'll need to tape the corners down or use a tray, or you'll find the pad bunched up in a corner. Several reviewers mentioned this exact issue: *"Great absorption, but I had to duct-tape the corners to keep it from sliding on my laminate floors."*

The attractant scent is also stronger than some competitors. Most puppies love it, but if you have a particularly sensitive dog — or you're sensitive to smells yourself — it might be off-putting initially. Give it a day; most dogs acclimate.

Finally, if you have a heavy-duty chewer, these pads aren't indestructible. The top layer is durable but not chew-proof. Watch your pup during the first few uses.

## Who Should Buy These Pads

**Buy them if:** You have a new puppy, a small-to-medium dog, or a senior dog needing indoor backup. The attractant makes training faster, the size covers more surface area, and the leak-proof layer protects your floors. They're also a solid choice for apartment dwellers with balcony potty setups.

**Skip them if:** You have a large breed (over 60 pounds) — you'll want the XL size. Or if you have slick floors and don't want to bother with tape or a tray, consider pads with stronger adhesive corners.
