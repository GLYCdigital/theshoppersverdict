## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the booster seat itself, two adjustable seatbelt straps, a pair of metal anchor clips, and a removable fleece liner. The outer shell is a heavy-duty Oxford fabric with a PVC waterproof backing — this isn't the flimsy nylon you see on $20 boosters. The base is a solid high-density foam board wrapped in the same fabric, so it holds its shape instead of sagging into a hammock after a week.

Dimensions are roughly 20" L x 15" W x 10" H, and the interior is sized for dogs up to 20 pounds. For reference, that fits a Chihuahua, a Miniature Dachshund, a Shih Tzu, or a small Cocker Spaniel comfortably. The seatbelt straps thread through the back so the seat stays put during turns, and the anchor clips attach to your car's LATCH system if you prefer that route.

## How It Holds Up in Real Use

The waterproofing is the headline feature, and it's legit. Owners consistently report that muddy paws, drool, and the occasional accident don't soak through to the car seat underneath. One verified buyer wrote, "My golden retriever puppy rode in it after a beach trip, totally soaked, and the seat was bone dry when I pulled the booster out." The sealed zippers and the PVC lining do the job — no complaints about leaking in any of the critical reviews.

The rigid base is the other standout. A 4-star reviewer noted, "My 18-lb Jack Russell mix bounces around like a maniac, and this thing never tipped or collapsed. The base is solid." That matches the overall sentiment — the structure holds up where cheaper boosters fold like cardboard.

The fleece liner is removable and machine-washable, which matters when you're dealing with a dog that sheds. It attaches with simple straps, and owners report it survives repeated wash cycles without pilling or shrinking.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be blunt: this is a booster seat, not a crash-tested safety device. ZOMISIA doesn't claim crash-test certification, and neither should you. If you're in a serious accident, this won't protect your dog the way a crash-tested kennel or harness will. It's designed to keep your dog elevated, contained, and comfortable — not to survive a 40-mph impact. Buy it for the right reasons.

The tall side walls are a mixed blessing. They keep small dogs contained and give them something to lean against, but low-profile breeds like Pugs and French Bulldogs can't see over the top. Some owners solved this by adding a small cushion or blanket as a step-up. For a dog with vision anxiety, that blind spot could be a dealbreaker.

Also, the foam base is dense but only about an inch thick. On stiff car seats with firm suspension, older dogs with arthritis may feel every bump. A couple of reviewers added a memory foam pad on top, which works but adds clutter.

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right pick for **small-dog owners who drive daily** — to the park, the vet, or just errands — and want a waterproof, stable perch that's easy to clean. It's also ideal for **puppy owners** who are still dealing with house-training accidents; the waterproof shell means you're not scrubbing your back seat every week.

Skip it if you have a **dog over 20 pounds**, a **crash-safety obsession**, or a **brachycephalic breed** that needs to see out the window. For those cases, look at a crash-tested carrier or a bed-style booster with lower walls.

A: Not comfortably. The interior is roughly 15" deep, and 25 pounds of dog will be cramped and likely tip the seat. Stick to the 20-pound limit.

**Q: Does it attach securely with just a seatbelt?**

**Q: Can the liner go in the dryer?**
A: Air-dry it. The fleece is machine-washable on cold, but the dryer can shrink the fabric and warp the straps.
