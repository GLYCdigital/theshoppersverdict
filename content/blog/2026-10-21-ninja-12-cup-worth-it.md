## What It Actually Delivers

### Two Brew Styles That Aren't Just Marketing

This is the headline feature, and it's the one that holds up best. Classic produces a brighter, lighter-bodied cup — good for everyday drinking, especially if you like to taste the bean. Rich slows the brew and extracts more, giving you a deeper, more syrupy cup that stands up to milk and sugar. Reviewers consistently note the difference is real, not a placebo. That's rarer than you'd think in the drip-coffee world, where "bold" settings often just mean "we used more grounds."

If you and your household argue over strength, this single feature can justify the purchase.

### The Removable Water Reservoir Is a Quiet Hero

A 60-oz reservoir is generous, but the removable design is what makes it practical. You carry it to the sink, fill it, and click it back in. No awkward pouring, no splashing, no wrestling the whole machine under the faucet. It also makes descaling and cleaning noticeably less painful — a task most drip brewers turn into a chore. Reviewers call this out again and again as a daily quality-of-life win.

### Programmable Convenience and a Warming Plate That Adjusts

Delay brew lets you wake up to a full pot, and the adjustable warming plate lets you dial in heat retention rather than scorching the bottom of the carafe. It's not a thermal carafe, so coffee won't stay perfect for hours, but for a household that drinks a pot within the hour, it's more than adequate.

### Build and Footprint

It's a sizable machine with a classic drip look. It won't win a design award, but it feels substantial and the controls are straightforward. The 12-cup capacity suits families, small offices, and anyone who hosts regularly.

## What Reviewers Complain About

No product with 28,709 reviews has a flawless record, and the Ninja's gripes are worth knowing before you commit.

- **The warming plate can still over-hold.** Some users report that coffee left too long develops a cooked taste. The adjustable plate helps, but it's not a thermal carafe — if you sip one cup over three hours, manage expectations.
- **It's bulky.** The reservoir and 12-cup capacity mean counter space is a real consideration, especially in smaller kitchens.
- **Cleaning has nooks.** The removable reservoir is a win, but the brew basket and plate area still require regular attention to avoid buildup.
- **Price wobbles.** At around $185.71, it's a mid-premium drip brewer. Some reviewers feel it's a touch expensive for what is, fundamentally, a drip machine — though most concede the brew-style flexibility earns it back.
- **Not a grinder or a barista machine.** A handful of buyers expect espresso-adjacent results. It's a batch brewer; judge it on that.
