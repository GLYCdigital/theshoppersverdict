## Why Under $100 Is the Sweet Spot

There's a reason this bracket matters. Below $30, you're usually buying disposable stuff — and occasionally a gem. Between $30 and $100, you hit the point where materials, warranties, and build quality actually improve without the boutique markup. Above $100, you start paying for brand prestige and features most people never touch.

A few ground rules we applied:

- **Cost per use beats sticker price.** A $12 item used daily beats a $40 item used twice.
- **Review volume matters.** A 4.6 rating over 13,000+ reviews is signal; a 4.9 over 40 reviews is noise.
- **A flaw doesn't disqualify a product.** Every pick below has one. We tell you what it is.
- **Replacement cost counts.** Cheap things that break yearly are expensive.

## Buying Tips: How to Judge Value Per Dollar Yourself

Before you add anything to a cart, run it through these filters:

- **Divide price by expected uses.** A $79 storage set used five times a week for three years costs pennies per use. A $25 unitasker used monthly doesn't.
- **Read the three-star reviews first.** Five-star reviews confirm hopes; three-star reviews tell you where the product actually breaks down.
- **Check what's replaceable.** Lids, gaskets, and attachments wear out first — see if you can buy spares.
- **Prefer materials that don't degrade.** Glass over plastic, cotton over polyester, metal over glued seams.
- **Ignore feature lists.** Speed counts and preset buttons matter less than whether the thing is pleasant to use daily.
- **Watch for size traps.** Measure your cabinets and counter before buying; a great product that doesn't fit is a bad purchase.

If you're building out a kitchen from scratch, start with the items you touch every day — storage, cleanup, and one workhorse appliance — before you buy anything specialized.
