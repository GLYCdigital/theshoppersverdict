## Where the Mr Pen Combination Square Combo Wins

### 1. Price-to-performance is outstanding

At around $10, the Mr Pen is one of the cheapest ways to get a functional combination square with a level and scriber included. For most DIYers, that's all the square they'll ever need. You could buy two and still spend less than one THORVALD.

### 2. It covers the everyday jobs

A combination square is the Swiss Army knife of layout tools: 90° and 45° checks, depth marking, level checks, and scribing. The Mr Pen does all of that out of the box. If you're hanging shelves, building a workbench, or checking cabinet corners, it's plenty.

### 3. The included level and scriber save you a trip

Many budget squares make you buy a scriber separately. Mr Pen bundles it, along with a vial level in the head. That's genuinely useful for quick leveling tasks where hauling out a spirit level is overkill.

### 4. Huge review base

With over 2,000 Amazon ratings and a 4.6 average, the Mr Pen has a deep track record. That's not a guarantee, but it's a strong signal that most buyers get a square, accurate tool.

## Where the THORVALD 6-in-1 Carpenter Square Wins

### 1. Precision is the headline

The THORVALD is built around 1/16" precision markings on a 7" blade. For trim carpenters and finish work, that matters. If you're cutting miters for crown molding or laying out cabinet hardware, a square that's slightly off will show in the finished product. The THORVALD's etched markings are designed to stay legible and accurate.

### 2. Six functions in one tool

"6-in-1" isn't just marketing — it means the head doubles as a try square, miter square, protractor, and more. That versatility is why finish pros like it: fewer tools in the pouch, faster layout.

### 3. Slightly higher Amazon rating

A 4.7/5 average across nearly 2,000 reviews is excellent. Buyers consistently praise its accuracy and build quality, which is what you'd hope for at this price.

### 4. Better for repeatable, precise work

If your work demands consistent 1/16" accuracy, the THORVALD is the more confidence-inspiring tool. The Mr Pen is fine for rough framing and DIY, but the THORVALD is the one you reach for when the cut has to be right the first time.

## Where Each Falls Short

### Mr Pen Combination Square Combo — the trade-offs

- **Precision is good, not exceptional.** For finish carpentry, the markings aren't as refined as the THORVALD's. Most DIYers won't notice, but pros will.
- **Build feels budget.** At this price, the cast zinc head and blade are functional but not heirloom quality. Treat it well and it'll last; drop it off a ladder and you may knock it out of square.
- **Not a 6-in-1.** You get the core combination-square functions, but not the extra protractor/try-square versatility of the THORVALD.

### THORVALD 6-in-1 Carpenter Square — the trade-offs

- **Price.** At ~$23, it's more than double the Mr Pen. For occasional DIY use, that's hard to justify.
- **Overkill for simple jobs.** If you just need to check a corner is 90°, the extra functions and precision are wasted.
- **Smaller blade.** The 7" blade is shorter than the Mr Pen's 12" blade, which can matter for larger layout work.

Neither tool is perfect, but their weaknesses are aimed at different buyers. The Mr Pen's weakness (refinement) only matters to pros. The THORVALD's weakness (price) only matters to casual users.
