## Where Softsoap Wins

### Price per use

This is the headline. At roughly $7.75 versus roughly $31.30, Softsoap costs about a quarter of what Mrs Meyer's does. If you have multiple sinks, kids who pump way too much soap, or a household that washes hands 20+ times a day, that gap compounds fast. You can buy four bottles of Softsoap for the price of one Mrs Meyer's and still have change.

### Antibacterial protection

Softsoap's antibacterial formula is the point of the product. For kitchens where raw chicken and toddlers coexist, that's not a marketing line — it's a reason to buy. Mrs Meyer's is a cleaning soap, not marketed as antibacterial, so if germ-killing is your priority at the kitchen sink, Softsoap is the obvious call.

### Review volume

21,351 ratings versus 16,155 is a meaningful gap. More reviews means more chances for problems to surface — and Softsoap still holds a 4.6/5. That's a durable, battle-tested track record. Our [Softsoap review](/luxury-beauty/softsoap-antibacterial-liquid-hand-soap-kitchen-and-bathroom-hand-soap-white-tea-review/) covers the details of the white tea scent, which reads clean and mild rather than perfumed.

### Availability and refill convenience

Softsoap is everywhere — grocery stores, big-box retailers, Amazon. You can restock it without a second thought, which matters more than most people admit when they're on their last pump.

## Where Mrs Meyer Wins

### Scent quality and experience

Mrs Meyer's essential-oil blends are the reason people pay the premium. The Rain scent — the one in our [Mrs Meyer Hand Soap Made review](/luxury-beauty/mrs-meyer-s-hand-soap-made-with-essential-oils-biodegradable-formula-rain-review/) — is a fresh, clean, slightly green scent that doesn't smell like a hospital. If you've ever used a hand soap that made your bathroom smell like a chemistry lab, you understand the value here.

### Skin feel

Essential-oil-based formulas tend to leave hands feeling less stripped. For people who wash frequently and end up with dry, tight skin, Mrs Meyer's is usually the gentler daily option. The 4.8/5 Amazon rating from 16,155 reviewers suggests this is not a niche opinion.

### Biodegradable formula

Mrs Meyer's markets its formula as biodegradable and plant-derived. If you're trying to reduce the chemical load in your home — especially around kids or pets — that positioning has real pull. Softsoap doesn't make the same environmental claims.

### Aesthetics and gifting

There's no polite way to say this: Mrs Meyer's looks better on a countertop. The label design, the scent names, the whole presentation — it's a soap you don't mind guests seeing. It also makes a genuinely good housewarming or hostess gift. Softsoap is functional; Mrs Meyer's is decorative.

## Where Each Falls Short

### Softsoap's weaknesses

- **The scent is fine, not memorable.** White tea is pleasant and inoffensive, but nobody buys Softsoap for the scent experience.
- **Antibacterial ingredients are a tradeoff.** Some shoppers specifically avoid antibacterial formulas for everyday use, and Softsoap isn't the pick for them.
- **Packaging is utilitarian.** It looks like what it is: a value soap.

### Mrs Meyer's weaknesses

- **The price is the price.** At roughly $31.30, it's about four times the cost of Softsoap. There's no version of the math where it's the budget option.
- **Not antibacterial.** If you want germ-killing claims at the kitchen sink, you're looking at the wrong bottle.
- **Runs out faster in heavy-use homes.** Because it's the soap people actually enjoy using, it disappears quickly — and at this price, that stings.

## The Smart-Money Play

Here's the thing most comparison posts won't tell you: you don't have to pick one. The genuinely smart setup for a lot of households is both — Softsoap at the kitchen sink and in the kids' bathroom where volume and germ control matter, and Mrs Meyer's at the guest bath and primary bathroom where the experience matters. You get the antibacterial coverage where you need it and the nice-smelling soap where you'll actually notice it.

If you're only buying one, the decision comes down to a single question: **do you care more about cost and germ-killing, or scent and skin feel?**

- Cost + antibacterial → Softsoap
- Scent + skin feel + biodegradable → Mrs Meyer's

There's no wrong answer, but there is a wrong answer for your specific sink. Don't buy a $31 bottle of soap for a mudroom sink your kids destroy twice a day, and don't put value soap in the powder room you spent a month decorating.
