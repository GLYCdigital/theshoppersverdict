## Why the Under-$50 Bracket Wins

Here's the thing about home improvement: the gap between "I can't do that" and "I did that myself" is usually one cheap, reliable tool. A corded drill. A staple gun. A screwdriver that doesn't strip screws. None of these need to be fancy. They need to work every time you grab them.

The under-$50 bracket is also where the value math gets interesting. Above $100, you're paying for brushless motors, batteries, and brand ecosystems. Below $50, you're paying for steel, grip, and a mechanism that doesn't jam. The tools that survive in this bracket do so because they're simple and overbuilt — not because they have a marketing budget.

## Buying Tips: How to Get the Most Value Per Dollar

Before you add anything to a cart, run your next few projects through these filters. They're the difference between a tool that pays for itself and one that collects dust.

- **Buy for the job you have, not the job you imagine.** A corded drill covers 90% of homeowner drilling. Don't pay a cordless premium for a fence you may never build.
- **Check review volume before review score.** A 4.5 from 5,000 buyers is a safer bet than a 4.8 from 50. Volume filters out flukes.
- **Prioritize steel and simple mechanisms.** In this bracket, the tools that last are the ones with fewer parts to fail. All-steel bodies, mechanical chucks, and manual ratchets age well.
- **Price consumables before you commit.** A staple gun is only a value if T50 staples are cheap and available near you. They are — but check for your specific tool.
- **Ignore combo kits under $50.** They're almost always a bag of weak tools. Three good singles beat ten bad ones.
- **Read the one-star reviews first.** They tell you the failure mode: stripped chucks, jammed ratchets, hand fatigue. If you can live with the failure mode, buy the tool.
