If you’ve ever stared at a LEGO Harry Potter set and thought, *“This is great, but where are the creepy skeleton horses?”* — this is your set. The **LEGO Harry Potter Thestral Family (76434)** is the first standalone Thestral build in the theme’s history, and it’s been quietly racking up a 4.9-star average across 245 ratings. That’s not a fluke. This is a niche set done right, but it’s not without a few compromises.

## What’s actually in the box

You get 277 pieces, three minifigures, and two Thestrals — one adult and one foal. The adult Thestral is the showpiece here: it’s got dark grey molded segments, articulated legs at the hip and knee, and brick-built wings that attach via ball joints. The foal is simpler but still captures the skeletal, bat-winged look from *Fantastic Beasts*.

Minifigure selection is the real draw for collectors. You get **Luna Lovegood** in her classic blue coat, **Harry Potter** with a wand and his Patronus-casting face print, and a **Professor Grubbly-Plank** variant that hasn’t appeared in a set since 2010. That’s a genuinely rare figure. The build includes a small Forbidden Forest vignette with a brick-built tree, lantern, and a translucent “summoning” effect piece that connects to Harry’s Patronus spell.

## Build experience and play value

The build takes about 30–40 minutes for an experienced builder, maybe double that for the 8+ target age. The bags are numbered, the instructions are clear, and there’s a Starter Brick baseplate that locks the scene down — so it won’t slide apart mid-play. The wing assembly is clever: each wing uses layered plates to create that tattered, membrane-like texture, and they’re attached with friction pins so they can fold back or spread wide.

Play-wise, this is a “display and swoosh” set. The Thestrals stand well on their own, but the adult’s legs are finicky — they’ll hold a pose, but a hard drop will knock them out of alignment. The foal is sturdier. Kids who like reenacting the *Order of the Phoenix* carriage scene will have fun, but this isn’t a playset with action features. It’s a creature pack with a small scene.

## What buyers consistently praise

The reviews overwhelmingly focus on two things: the **minifigure selection** and the **creature design**. One buyer wrote, *“I bought this mainly for Grubbly-Plank, but the Thestral itself is stunning — the wing detail is better than any previous creature set.”* Another parent noted, *“My 9-year-old built it solo in under an hour and hasn’t stopped playing with it since. The foal is her favorite.”*

The rare minifigures are a big deal for collectors — Grubbly-Plank alone resells for more than the set’s retail price in some markets. If you’re a completionist, this is a must-buy.

## Where it falls short

Let’s be honest about the downsides. **The wings pop off.** It’s not a structural flaw — the ball joints are designed to detach under pressure to prevent breakage — but if your kid is rough with toys, you’ll be reattaching wings constantly. One reviewer said, *“My son loves it, but I’ve snapped those wings back on at least ten times in a week.”*

Also, **no Hagrid**. Given the Forbidden Forest setting and the Thestral lore, the absence of the Keeper of Keys feels like an oversight. And at 277 pieces, the price-per-piece ratio is steep compared to similarly sized LEGO City sets. You’re paying a premium for the IP and the exclusive figures — that’s fine, but know that going in.

## Who should buy this

- **Harry Potter collectors** — especially those missing Grubbly-Plank or wanting a rare creature display piece
- **Parents of 8–12 year olds** who want a manageable build that encourages imaginative play without a 1,000-piece commitment
- **Fans of Fantastic Beasts** looking for more obscure magical creatures

**Skip it if** you’re buying purely for play features — there are no traps, no moving parts beyond the wings, and the scene is static. For pure play value, the larger Hogwarts sets offer more bang for the buck.

## FAQ

**Is this set compatible with other LEGO Harry Potter sets?**
Yes. Standard LEGO system bricks, so it connects to any Harry Potter set with baseplates or studs. The Thestral uses standard Technic ball joints for the wings, so they can be swapped with other creature sets.

**How many minifigures are included?**
Three: Luna Lovegood, Harry Potter, and Professor Grubbly-Plank. Harry includes a Patronus effect piece, and Luna has her signature Spectrespecs.

**Is the adult Thestral stable for display?**
Mostly. It stands fine on flat surfaces, but the leg articulation means it won’t hold dramatic poses without a stand. The foal is rock-solid.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you’re a Harry Potter collector chasing rare minifigures or want a high-quality creature display piece. The Thestral design is genuinely impressive, and the minifigure selection justifies the premium price for fans.

**Skip it if** you need a set with active play features or you’re on a budget — the wing-popping issue and static scene make it a weaker choice for rough-and-tumble play.

This is a niche set with a clear audience, and it delivers exactly what that audience wants. Just be ready to reattach a wing or two.
