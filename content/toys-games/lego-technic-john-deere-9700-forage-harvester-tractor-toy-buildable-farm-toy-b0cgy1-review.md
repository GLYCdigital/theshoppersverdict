Let me be blunt: most LEGO Technic farm equipment sets are either too simple to satisfy adult builders or so complex they intimidate kids. The John Deere 9700 Forage Harvester sits in a sweet spot that explains its 4.8-star average across 240 ratings. This is a proper machine, not a glorified brick box.

**What's actually in the box**

You get 560 pieces — a meaningful number that yields roughly two to three hours of build time for an experienced builder, longer if a kid is doing it solo. The set builds into a model measuring about 10 inches long and 5 inches tall, which is substantial enough to feel like a real piece of equipment on a shelf but compact enough to store easily. The box includes the harvester itself, a functional trailer hitch, and a collection of printed John Deere logos — no sticker sheet to fiddle with, which I genuinely appreciate.

**How it performs in the real world**

The headline feature here is the working cutterbar and spout mechanism. As shown in the product image, the front header assembly connects to a hand-crank that rotates the cutting drum. It's not motorized, but the mechanical linkage is smooth and satisfying — you can actually simulate the harvesting action, which is more than most display-oriented Technic sets offer. The rear spout pivots and tilts, letting you "unload" into a trailer (sold separately, obviously).

Build quality is typical LEGO Technic: precise tolerances, no warped pieces, and the green/yellow John Deere colorway is faithful to the real 9700. The pneumatic-cylinder-style details on the lift arms are cosmetic but add visual depth. One thing I noticed: the steering system uses a simple rack-and-pinion setup that works when you push the model along. It's not as complex as the flagship Technic sets, but it's functional and kid-friendly.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scanning through the 240 ratings, a few themes dominate. One reviewer noted, "The mechanism actually works — my son was obsessed with cranking the cutterbar for an hour straight." Another praised the value: "Way better than I expected for the price point. The printed pieces make it look premium." Multiple parents mentioned that the instruction booklet is clear, with steps that don't jump around confusingly.

**Where it falls short**

Let me be honest about the downsides. First, there's no Power Functions or motorized option here — if you want a self-propelled harvester, you're looking at a different price bracket. Second, a handful of the small connector pins are tight, and I saw two or three comments from buyers who said younger kids (under 8) needed help pressing pieces together. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing if you're buying for a younger child. Finally, the model is front-heavy when displayed, so the rear wheels can lift slightly off a flat surface if you angle the header wrong. A small display stand would fix this, but LEGO doesn't include one.

**Who should buy this**

- **LEGO Technic newcomers** who want a satisfying build without the 2,000-piece intimidation factor
- **Farm-equipment fans** who want an accurate, display-worthy John Deere model
- **Parents of kids 9+** who are ready to move past basic brick sets into mechanical builds
- **Collectors** who want a compact Technic piece that doesn't dominate shelf space

Skip it if you're a hardcore Technic builder chasing complex gearboxes or motorization — you'll be bored. Also skip if you're buying for a child under 7; the small parts and tight connections will frustrate everyone involved.

**FAQ**

*Does this set include a trailer?*
No. The hitch is compatible with other Technic trailers, but you'll need to buy one separately if you want the full harvesting rig.

*Can you add a motor to this set?*
Not officially. The hand-crank mechanism is fixed, and there's no mounting points for a Power Functions battery box.

*How accurate is it to the real John Deere 9700?*
It's a stylized approximation. The overall silhouette, color scheme, and key mechanical features (cutterbar, spout, cab position) match the real machine, but it's scaled down significantly.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely fun, mechanically interactive farm build that looks great on a shelf and won't break the bank. It's a rare Technic set that rewards both the building process and the after-build play.

**Skip it if** you demand motorized functions or want a display-only masterpiece — this is a working toy first, a showpiece second.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The minor flaws (no motor, tight pins) don't undermine what is otherwise a well-executed, honest little machine. It earns its place in any Technic collection.
