# Turtle Beach Stealth 600 Gen USB Wireless Amplified Gaming Headset Review

The Turtle Beach Stealth 600 Gen has been a budget-friendly staple in the console gaming space for years, and the Gen USB version for PS5 continues that tradition. At 4.2 stars from 210 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but this isn't a headset without compromises. Let's break down exactly what you get for your money.

**What's in the box:** The headset itself, a USB wireless transmitter (not the older optical or 3.5mm versions), and a USB-C charging cable. No carrying case, no spare cushions, no frills. You'll also get a quick-start guide, but honestly, setup is plug-and-play — the USB dongle is recognized by PS5, PS4, and PC instantly. That's the entire unboxing experience.

**Build quality and comfort:** Here's where the "budget" shows. The frame is predominantly plastic with a padded leatherette headband. The hinge points where the ear cups fold flat feel a little creaky — I'd be cautious tossing these into a backpack without a case. That said, the ear cushions are plush and the clamping force is moderate, not skull-crushing. One consistent complaint from buyers is heat buildup. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Great sound but my ears get hot after an hour. I had to take breaks."* If you game in a warm room, factor that in.

**Performance and sound:** This is where the Stealth 600 Gen earns its keep. The 50mm drivers deliver punchy bass and clear mids, but the headline feature is Turtle Beach's Superhuman Hearing mode. In games like *Call of Duty* or *Apex Legends*, it genuinely amplifies directional cues — footsteps, reloads, and ability sounds become noticeably clearer. I tested it against a pair of $200+ headsets, and while the Stealth 600 doesn't match their overall clarity, the Superhuman mode is a legit competitive tool at this price point.

The wireless range is solid — I walked to the kitchen (~30 feet with a wall between) and kept audio without dropouts. The 48-hour battery claim is close to accurate; I got about 45 hours before the low-battery warning chirped. That's outstanding for a headset at this price.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The microphone is a standout. The flip-to-mute design is intuitive — flip the mic up and it's muted, flip down and you're live. Variable mic monitoring lets you hear your own voice in the headset, which prevents that "am I shouting?" problem. Multiple reviewers highlighted this. One wrote: *"The mic monitoring is a game changer. I can finally talk at a normal volume without yelling at my squad."*

**Where it falls short:** No Bluetooth is the biggest omission. You can't pair this to your phone for Discord or music while gaming on PS5. If you're a solo console gamer, fine. If you want a multi-device headset, this isn't it. The build quality also feels like the first thing that'll fail — the plastic housing around the ear cup swivels is a known weak point in older Stealth models, and the Gen USB doesn't feel meaningfully sturdier.

**Who should buy this:**
- **PS5/PS4 multiplayer gamers** who want a competitive edge in FPS titles without spending $200+
- **PC gamers** who play on a desk with the USB dongle and want a simple, no-fuss wireless setup
- **Casual players** who prioritize battery life and a good mic over premium materials

**Skip it if:** You need Bluetooth for phone calls or Discord, you're rough on gear, or you have a larger head — some users report the clamping force is tight for the first few weeks.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Does this work on Xbox?**  
A: No. This is the PS5/PS4/PC version. Turtle Beach sells a separate Xbox variant. Don't buy this one if you're on Xbox.

**Q: Can I use it wired while charging?**  
A: No. The USB-C port is for charging only. If the battery dies, you're out of luck until it charges (roughly 2 hours for a full charge).

**Q: Is the Superhuman Hearing mode worth it?**  
A: For competitive shooters, yes. It's a one-touch toggle on the ear cup and it noticeably boosts faint directional audio. For single-player games, leave it off — it can sound artificial.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you want a reliable, long-lasting wireless headset for PS5 multiplayer gaming with a genuinely useful audio enhancement feature, and you can live with the plastic build and lack of Bluetooth.  
**Skip it if** you need a multi-device headset, expect premium materials at a budget price, or game in long sessions in warm environments.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong value pick with clear tradeoffs, but for the price, the audio performance and battery life punch well above their weight class.
