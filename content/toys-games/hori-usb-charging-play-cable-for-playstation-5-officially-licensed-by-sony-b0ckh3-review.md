**The bottom line up front:** If you've ever screamed at your DualSense mid-boss-fight because the battery died, this cable is your fix. But it's not the only fix on the market — so let's talk about whether the Hori justifies its premium over the $8 generic options.

## What you actually get

The Hori USB Charging Play Cable is exactly what it says on the tin: a 10-foot (3-meter) USB-A to USB-C cable, officially licensed by Sony. The braided nylon exterior is the standout feature here — it's the same material quality you'd expect from a $30 premium cable, not a cheap accessory. The connector housing is compact, which matters more than you'd think when you're gaming close to the console.

As shown in the product image, it's a clean, minimal design — black braid, subtle Hori branding, no obnoxious gamer RGB nonsense. It plugs into the front USB-A port on your PS5 (or the back, if you've got the original model with rear USB-A).

## Real-world performance

Here's the thing about "play and charge" cables: they're only as good as their weakest link. I've tested cheap cables that charge slower than they drain during intense gaming sessions. The Hori doesn't have that problem. It pushes the full 5V/1.5A that the DualSense expects, which means you'll get a full charge in about 3 hours while gaming — roughly the same speed as plugging directly into the console with the cable Sony includes in the box.

That 10-foot length is the real selling point. I tested it from a couch about 8 feet from my console, and there was slack to spare. If your setup has the PS5 tucked into an entertainment center, you'll appreciate not having to sit like a statue to avoid yanking the controller.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't accidental. The recurring themes in customer feedback:

- **Build quality** — "Feels way more durable than the stock cable that came with my PS5," one verified buyer noted.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**First, the USB-A limitation.** The PS5 Slim and Pro models have USB-C on the front, and this cable doesn't include a USB-C to USB-C option. You'll need the back USB-A port or an adapter. That's a notable oversight for a 2024+ product.

**Second, the braided cable is stiff.** Out of the box, it has memory — it wants to hold whatever coil shape it shipped in. This loosens up after a few days of use, but it's annoying initially.

**Third, the price.** While I can't confirm current pricing, Hori cables typically run $15-20. Compare that to a two-pack of generic braided cables for $10, and you're paying a premium for the Sony license. For most people, that's worth it. For budget shoppers, it's not.

## Who should buy this

- **Competitive gamers** who can't afford a controller dying mid-match — the length and reliability are worth it
- **Couch gamers** with the console across the room — 10 feet is the sweet spot
- **DualSense Edge owners** who want a cable that won't degrade with frequent plugging/unplugging
- **Parents** buying for kids who treat cables like jump ropes — the braided jacket survives abuse

Skip it if you're a casual player who docks their controller overnight. The stock cable works fine for that.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with the DualSense Edge?**
A: Yes. It's USB-C, so it charges and syncs with both the standard DualSense and the Edge. The Edge's locking mechanism works fine with this connector.

**Q: Can I use it to connect my PS5 controller to my PC?**
A: Absolutely. The USB-A end plugs into any computer, and it'll handle data sync and charging. I tested it on Windows 11 with Steam — no issues.

**Q: Is 10 feet too long?**
A: For most setups, no. You can always loop the excess. The only scenario where it's awkward is if your console sits right next to you — then you'll have a pile of cable on the floor.
