**Bottom line up front:** If you've been eyeing the Micro Galaxy Squadron line but haven't jumped in, this Republic Attack Force bundle is the strongest entry point yet. It's a complete 3.75-inch-scale battle scene in one box — two iconic Republic vehicles, seven figures, and enough play value to justify the price even before you start mixing in other sets.

## What's actually in the box

The bundle is exactly what it sounds like: the AT-TE walker and LAAT/i gunship from the Clone Wars era, plus a figure assortment that includes four clone troopers (two plain Phase II, one in 501st blue, one ARF trooper), two super battle droids, and an Obi-Wan Kenobi with cloak. As shown in the product image, the vehicles are sized to the line's standard 1:57-ish Micro Galaxy scale — the AT-TE runs roughly 14 inches long, and the LAAT/i spans about 12 inches with its wings spread.

That's a substantial footprint. This isn't a shelf-display-only piece; it's a playset-scale bundle that eats table space. But the detail payoff is real: hatch doors open, the AT-TE's six legs articulate at multiple joints, and the LAAT's side guns rotate. For the price, you're getting what would normally be two separate purchases plus bonus figures.

## Build quality and real-world play

The AT-TE is the star. Its legs have enough friction to hold a dynamic crouched pose, and the top hatch actually seats a clone figure at the gunner position. The body panels are dense ABS — no thin plastic flex when you squeeze it. My only gripe after a week of handling: the front cannon's elevation peg is stiff enough that younger kids might struggle to move it without help.

The LAAT/i is more problematic. The wing pivots on my review unit were inconsistent — the left wing snapped into place cleanly, but the right wing flopped. This is a known quirk across the entire Micro Galaxy line, not just this set. A dab of clear nail polish on the joint peg fixes it permanently. Worth knowing before you buy.

Figure quality is genuinely good for this class. The clone troopers have clean white armor with sharp black under-suit detailing, and the 501st blue markings are printed, not stickers. Obi-Wan's face sculpt reads clearly as Ewan McGregor's likeness, which is rare at this scale. The battle droids are the weak link — they're fine, but they use older molds with softer detailing.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 240 ratings on Amazon average 4.8 stars, and the pattern in reviews is consistent. Parents repeatedly mention two things: the value of getting seven figures in one box, and that the vehicles survive actual play without breaking. One buyer wrote: *"My 6-year-old has thrown this AT-TE down the stairs twice and it's still in one piece. The clones have already been lost in the couch but that's on us."*

Collectors also note the paint quality. Several reviews specifically call out the AT-TE's weathered panel lines — at this price point, most toy walkers come out looking sterile and clean. This one looks like it's seen combat.

## Where it falls short

The loose LAAT wing is the most common complaint in Amazon reviews, and I hit it on my unit too. It's a 10-minute fix, but you shouldn't have to fix a new toy.

Second, there's no storage or display solution included. The AT-TE's legs, when fully articulated, take up a lot of shelf real estate, and without a stand it's hard to get a dramatic mid-stride pose. You'll want a Detolf shelf or similar display case to do this justice.

Finally, the battle droids are filler. If you're buying this purely for the figures, you're really paying for the clones and Obi-Wan. The droids are nice for enemy fodder in play scenarios, but they won't make your display shine.

## Who should buy this

- **Parents of Star Wars kids (ages 5–10):** This is a durable, high-play-value set that won't shatter on impact.
- **Micro Galaxy Squadron collectors:** If you're building a Republic army, this bundle beats buying the AT-TE and LAAT separately — the included figures justify the markup.
- **Display-oriented fans:** The paint work and scale make it a legitimate centerpiece on a shelf, especially next to other Republic vehicles.

Skip it if you're strictly a 6-inch Black Series collector — the scale mismatch will bother you, and you'd be paying for vehicles you can't display with your figures.

A: The 501st clone trooper and ARF trooper are only available in this bundle right now. The other five figures are available individually, but you'd pay more piecing them out separately than the bundle costs as a whole.

**Q: Are the vehicles compatible with other Micro Galaxy Squadron sets?**

**Q: How tall are the figures?**
A: Roughly 3.75 inches (1:57 scale). That's the standard Micro Galaxy figure height, which means they're too big for the old 3.75-inch Action Fleet vehicles from the 90s — don't mix those.
