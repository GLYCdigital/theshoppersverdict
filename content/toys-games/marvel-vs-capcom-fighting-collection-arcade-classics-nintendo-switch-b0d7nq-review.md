**Seven arcade classics. One cartridge. Zero filler.**

If you've been waiting two decades to legally play *Marvel vs. Capcom 2* without dropping $200 on a Dreamcast disc, this is your moment. Capcom packed seven of their most beloved 90s fighting games into a single Switch cartridge, and the result is a love letter to the arcade era that actually respects your time and money.

**What you actually get**

The collection includes *X-Men: Children of the Atom*, *Marvel Super Heroes*, *X-Men vs. Street Fighter*, *Marvel Super Heroes vs. Street Fighter*, *Marvel vs. Capcom: Clash of Super Heroes*, *Marvel vs. Capcom 2: New Age of Heroes*, and *The Punisher* (the beat-em-up, a nice bonus). Every game runs on its original arcade ROM — no remakes, no "enhanced" versions that mess with the feel.

Capcom also included the quality-of-life features that make retro collections worth buying: save states, rewind, a training mode with input display, and a museum mode packed with concept art and music players. The training mode deserves special mention — being able to see exactly which buttons register and watch hitbox overlays makes learning the notoriously strict 90s combo timing actually possible for newcomers.

**Performance and real-world use**

On a Switch OLED in docked mode, these games run at a rock-solid 60fps. The pixel art looks crisp on modern displays, and the CRT filter options help if you want that authentic scanline look. In handheld mode, things hold up well for most matches, though the original Switch model does show some resolution drops during the chaos of tag-team supers in *MvC2* — it's brief and doesn't affect gameplay, but it's there.

The online implementation is where this collection splits opinion. You get rollback netcode, which is essential for fighting games, and matchmaking works. But there's no crossplay, so your player pool is limited to other Switch owners. During peak hours you'll find matches quickly, but at off-hours you might wait a bit. *MvC2* also lacks the lobby system that Capcom's own Street Fighter collection gave players — you're stuck with quick match or ranked.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The Amazon reviews (4.8 stars across 240 ratings) repeatedly highlight two things: the completeness of the package and the training mode. One reviewer put it plainly: "This is the definitive way to play these games. Capcom actually cares about preserving arcade history." Another noted, "The input display in training mode is a game-changer — I finally understand how to do the infinite combos I could never pull off in the arcade."

**Where it falls short**

The lack of crossplay is the biggest miss. If you're buying this to play with a friend who owns a PS5 or PC, you're out of luck. The collection also doesn't include *Marvel vs. Capcom: Infinite* (that's a different game entirely), so don't come looking for that. And while the museum mode is nice, it's text-light compared to some other Capcom collections — don't expect deep documentary-style content.

**Who should buy this**

- **Nostalgic arcade veterans** who remember dropping quarters into *X-Men vs. Street Fighter* cabinets — this is your childhood, preserved perfectly.
- **Fighting game newcomers** who want to understand why these games are legendary — the training mode makes learning accessible.
- **Switch-only players** who missed the PS4/Xbox release and want the most complete retro fighting package available on the platform.

**Skip it if** you're a competitive online player who needs a large player pool and crossplay — look at the PC or PS5 versions instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this include Marvel vs. Capcom 2?**
A: Yes, the full arcade version with all 56 characters. This is the first time it's been re-released on a Nintendo platform.

**Q: Can I play online with friends on other consoles?**
A: No. Online play is Switch-only with no crossplay support. You can, however, play local multiplayer with up to four players.

**Q: Is the collection worth it if I already own some of these games digitally?**
A: If you own the individual releases on other platforms, it depends on how much you value the training mode and save states. If you own nothing, this is the most cost-effective way to get seven arcade classics for roughly the price of two modern games.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want the most complete, polished package of 90s Marvel fighting games ever released on a Nintendo console. **Skip it if** crossplay is a dealbreaker for your online play.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A near-perfect retro collection held back only by its online limitations. The single-player experience alone justifies the price.
