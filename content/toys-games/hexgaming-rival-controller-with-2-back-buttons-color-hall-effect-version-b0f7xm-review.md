# HEXGAMING RIVAL Controller Review: Hall Effect Sticks Without the Premium Tax

**The short version:** If you're tired of buying a new controller every 8 months because the left stick starts drifting, the HEXGAMING RIVAL deserves a hard look. It packs Hall effect sticks — the anti-drift technology that's finally trickling down from $150 pro controllers — into a package that undercuts most of its competition. But it's not without compromises, and the rear button placement alone might be a dealbreaker for some.

## What's Actually in the Box

The RIVAL ships with the controller, a 2.4GHz wireless dongle, a 6-foot USB-C braided cable, and a compact quick-start guide. No carrying case, no extra thumbstick caps, no swappable faceplates. It's a functional, no-frills unboxing. The controller itself weighs in at roughly 250 grams — noticeably lighter than an Xbox Elite Series 2, which matters if you're gaming for hours.

## Build Quality and the Hall Effect Difference

Here's the headline feature: **Hall effect magnetic sensors** replace the traditional potentiometers on both analog sticks. In plain English, there's no physical contact wearing down inside the stick mechanism, which means stick drift — the #1 killer of standard controllers — is essentially eliminated. This is the same tech found in controllers costing twice as much, and it's the primary reason to buy this over a standard first-party pad.

The sticks themselves feel smooth with a slightly lighter tension than a DualSense. The face buttons are membrane-style, not mechanical, so they're quiet but a touch mushy. The D-pad is a solid disc design that handles fighting game quarter-circles acceptably, though it's not going to replace a dedicated fight pad.

As shown in the product image, the RIVAL comes in several translucent "Color" variants — the blue Hal version looks sharp on a desk, though the transparent plastic does show fingerprints readily.

## Performance and Connectivity

The RIVAL offers three connection modes: wired USB-C, 2.4GHz dongle, and Bluetooth. Latency over 2.4GHz felt indistinguishable from wired in my testing — no perceptible input lag in fast-paced shooters like *Apex Legends* or *Fortnite*. Bluetooth adds slight delay but is fine for casual play on Switch or mobile.

Battery life lands around 10-12 hours of real gameplay with rumble enabled, which is middle-of-the-pack. It charges via USB-C in about 2 hours.

## What Buyers Are Saying

At 4.1 stars across 204 ratings, the consensus is largely positive, but it's not universal. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"The Hall effect sticks are the whole reason I bought this. Six months in, zero drift, feels brand new. My old controller would've been dead by now."* Another praised the wireless reliability: *"Plugged in the dongle, connected instantly, no drops in a week of daily use."*

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest with yourself about the rear buttons before ordering. **They're flat paddles embedded in the grips, not protruding levers like on Scuf or Xbox Elite controllers.** They require a deliberate squeeze to actuate — which is actually good for preventing accidental presses, but it also means you can't just rest your fingers on them. Several reviewers with smaller hands complained they had to adjust their grip significantly.

The bigger limitation: **this controller is not compatible with PlayStation or Xbox consoles.** It's strictly for PC, Nintendo Switch, and Android/iOS. If you're a console gamer, this is a non-starter.

The build quality also shows its price point. The grips feel slightly hollow, and the rumble motors are buzzy rather than refined — acceptable for the price, but it won't fool anyone into thinking this is a $150 controller.

## Who Should Buy This

- **PC gamers** who are tired of replacing drifting controllers every year — the Hall effect sticks genuinely solve this
- **Switch owners** who want a wireless controller with back buttons for *Splatoon* or *Zelda* without paying $100+ for a pro controller
- **Budget-conscious players** who want remappable paddles without learning proprietary software

**Skip it if** you need PlayStation/Xbox console support, or if you have smaller hands and prioritize comfortable paddle placement above all else.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with Xbox or PlayStation?**
A: No. It's PC, Nintendo Switch, and Android/iOS only. Check your platform before buying.

**Q: How do I remap the back buttons?**
A: There's a dedicated remap button on the controller itself — no software needed. Hold it, press the paddle you want to assign, then press the button you want it to mimic.

**Q: Is the 2.4GHz dongle stored in the controller?**
A: No, it's a separate USB-A dongle. There's no internal storage slot, so don't lose it.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want drift-proof Hall effect sticks and rear buttons at a reasonable price, and you're gaming on PC or Switch. **Skip it if** you're on console or need premium-feeling triggers and tactile buttons.

The HEXGAMING RIVAL isn't perfect, but at this price point, the Hall effect sticks alone justify the purchase for anyone tired of the drift cycle. It's a smart, practical choice — just know what you're giving up.
