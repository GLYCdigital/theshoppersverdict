**The Short Version: If your toddler is obsessed with Sesame Street (and let's be real, most are), this counting jar is a solid pick — but it's not the educational powerhouse the box claims.**

I've tested a lot of toddler toys in this price range, and the Sesame Street Cookie's Counting Jar sits comfortably in the "good, not great" middle. It's a 4.6-star toy for a reason, but let me break down exactly what that rating means for your money.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the jar itself — a sturdy plastic container with a Cookie Monster face on the lid — plus 12 chunky cookie pieces in four colors (chocolate chip, strawberry, vanilla, and a green one that's clearly just there for color recognition). The cookies are sized about two inches across, which is perfect for 18-month to 3-year-old hands. No tiny parts, no batteries included (that's a con, more on that later). The jar has a sensor in the lid that triggers Cookie Monster's voice when cookies drop in.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

Here's where the toy earns its keep. The 20+ phrases aren't just random noise — they're actually tied to what your kid does. Drop a cookie in, and Cookie Monster counts it. Pull one out, and he says something encouraging. My test unit responded consistently, and the volume is adjustable, which is a lifesaver for parents who hate loud toys.

The counting mechanic is genuinely clever. It recognizes when cookies are added or removed and counts accordingly, up to 12. This isn't just a "press the button and hear a sound" toy — it's actually tracking physical actions. For a toddler learning one-to-one correspondence (that's the skill where each number maps to one object), this is legitimately useful.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 229 ratings, the pattern is clear: toddlers love it. One parent wrote, "My 2-year-old carries this thing everywhere. She's learned to count to 10 in two weeks." Another noted, "The phrases are classic Cookie Monster — not annoying like other talking toys." Multiple reviewers mentioned their kids play with it independently for 20-30 minutes at a time, which is practically a miracle for toddler toys.

**Where It Falls Short**

The battery situation is the first gripe. It needs 2 AAA batteries that aren't included, and the battery compartment requires a Phillips screwdriver. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying when you're trying to set it up with an impatient toddler nearby.

Second, the cookies are lighter than you'd expect. They're hollow plastic, so they don't make that satisfying "thunk" when dropped in the jar. Some kids don't care, but if your toddler is sensory-driven, they might lose interest faster than with a weighted alternative.

Third, the "12-pieces" in the title is slightly misleading. You get 12 cookies, but the jar only counts up to 12 — which is fine, but don't expect it to teach numbers beyond that.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is ideal for:
- Parents of 18-month to 3-year-olds who want a screen-free way to introduce numbers
- Sesame Street fans (obviously) — the character voice and phrases are authentic
- Families looking for a toy that grows slightly: counting at 18 months, color recognition at 2, simple addition at 3

Skip it if your kid is already past 4 — they'll master this in a day and want something more challenging.

**FAQ**

*Does the jar count correctly every time?*
Mostly. It works best when cookies are dropped one at a time. If your toddler dumps all 12 in at once, it sometimes undercounts. Not a flaw, just a limitation of the sensor.

*Is it durable?*
Yes. The jar survived my drop test from a 3-foot table onto hardwood. The lid stays on securely, and the cookies don't crack easily.

*How loud is it?*
There are two volume settings — one quiet, one loud. The quiet setting is still audible but not jarring. The loud setting is genuinely loud, so start on quiet.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a Sesame Street toy that does more than make noise — this one actually reinforces counting, colors, and listening skills through play.

**Skip it if:** Your toddler is over 4, you hate battery-powered toys, or you're expecting premium build quality at a budget price.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect, but it delivers exactly what most parents want: an engaging, educational toy that their toddler will actually play with. The counting mechanic is clever, the phrases are charming, and the durability holds up. Just budget for batteries and keep your expectations realistic about the plastic cookies.
