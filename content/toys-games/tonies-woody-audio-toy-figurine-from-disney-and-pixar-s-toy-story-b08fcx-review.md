**What You Actually Get**

In the box: one Tonies character figurine of Woody, roughly 4 inches tall, with the signature Tonies magnetic base. Pop it on top of a Toniebox and it clicks into place, triggering the audio content automatically. There are no buttons to fiddle with—kids can operate this entirely on their own, which is the whole point of the Tonies ecosystem.

The audio runtime is around 40 minutes, which is substantial for a single figurine. It includes songs and a story based on the Toy Story universe. Just be aware: this isn’t a narration of the film. It’s an original story with Woody’s character, plus a few classic tunes. If your kid is expecting Buzz and the full gang, some characters are mentioned but the focus is squarely on Woody.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The figurine itself is solid. It’s made from a durable plastic composite, and the paint job is clean—Woody’s vest, hat, and boots look accurate to the character. The base is weighted with a magnet that’s strong enough to stay put on the Toniebox even when a toddler grabs it mid-play.

In terms of audio quality, it’s exactly what you’d expect from a Tonie: clear, kid-friendly narration with a warm tone. The volume is capped at a safe level, which parents will appreciate. The story is engaging enough to hold attention for a 3–6 year old, and the songs are catchy without being annoying on repeat—a real win for parents.

One thing that stood out in customer feedback: parents consistently mention how easy it is for kids to swap figurines independently. As one reviewer put it, *“My 3-year-old figured it out in seconds. She swaps between Woody and her other Tonies without any help.”* That ease of use is a big part of why the Tonies system works so well for young kids.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let’s be honest about the downsides.

First, **the content is short**. 40 minutes might sound like a lot, but it’s a single story plus a few songs. If your kid listens to it daily, they’ll have it memorized within a week. That’s fine if you’re rotating through a collection, but if this is a standalone gift, expect to hear “again!” a lot.

Second, **it’s not the full Toy Story experience**. Kids who love Buzz Lightyear specifically might be disappointed that this figurine is Woody-centric. The story does include Buzz as a character, but the focus is on Woody’s perspective. If your child’s favorite character is Buzz, you might want to look for a different Tonie.

Third, **the price point**. Tonies figurines are not cheap, and this one is no exception. You’re paying for the audio content plus the physical figurine, and the value is heavily dependent on how much use your kid gets out of it. If you already have a Toniebox, the incremental cost makes sense. If you’re starting from scratch, remember the box itself is a separate investment.
