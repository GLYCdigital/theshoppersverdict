**Poraxy 5 in 1 STEM Kits Review: Five Builds, One Box, Zero Boredom**

If you've browsed STEM toys on Amazon, you've seen the pattern: one flashy project, a $40 price tag, and a shelf full of dust after a single afternoon. The Poraxy 5 in 1 STEM Kit flips that script. For $16.99, you get five different motorized builds — a bubble machine, a doodling robot, a balance car, a robotic arm, and a racing car. That's roughly $3.40 per project, which is hard to argue with.

**What's Actually in the Box**

Open the box and you'll find five separate bags, each labeled for a different project. Every bag contains its own set of wooden boards, plastic gears, screws, axles, and the shared components: one DC motor, a battery holder, and connecting wires. The wood pieces are laser-cut and pop out cleanly — no splinters, no forcing pieces apart. There's no glue required, which means no permanent mess on your kitchen table.

The instruction booklet is where this kit separates itself from cheaper alternatives. Each build gets its own multi-step guide with clear diagrams. The steps are numbered sequentially, and the illustrations actually match the real parts. My one gripe: the booklet is printed small. If your kid needs glasses or you're helping in dim light, you'll squint.

**Real-World Performance**

The motor is the heart of this kit, and it's genuinely decent. It's a standard 3V DC motor that runs on two AA batteries (not included). The gearbox actually does something — you can see the gears meshing as the car moves, which is half the educational value. The bubble machine and doodling robot are the crowd-pleasers; they're novel enough to hold attention and simple enough to complete in one sitting.

Assembly time runs 20-40 minutes per project depending on your child's experience. The first build is the slowest while they learn the system. By the third or fourth project, most kids can work independently. Several Amazon reviewers echo this: "My 9-year-old built the car in about 25 minutes with zero help," and "The instructions are so clear my 8-year-old did the bubble machine by herself."

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the weak spots. The motor wires come bare-ended, and you have to thread them through the battery holder connection. Some reviewers report the wire strands fraying if you twist too hard — a steady adult hand helps here. The screws are tiny (M3 size) and there's no storage box, so losing one mid-build is a real risk. I'd recommend a small parts tray before you start.

Also, this is a "build it, use it, take it apart" toy. The models aren't meant to survive rough play. The wooden frames are sturdy enough for display but won't survive being dropped repeatedly. If your kid wants a durable toy robot, this isn't it. If they want to understand how things work, it's perfect.

**Who Should Buy This**

This kit is ideal for three types of buyers:

- **Parents of 8-10 year olds** who want a screen-free activity that teaches real mechanical concepts without a hefty price tag
- **Grandparents** looking for a gift that's more substantial than another Lego set — it's different, it moves, and it's affordable
- **Teachers or homeschoolers** who need a hands-on demonstration of gears, motors, and simple machines for a classroom or co-op

It's less ideal for kids under 8 (small parts, fine motor skills required) or kids who prefer freeform building over following instructions.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do I need any extra tools?**  
A: A small Phillips screwdriver is essential. The kit includes the screws and wooden pieces, but no screwdriver. A tiny one from a glasses repair kit works fine.

**Q: How long does each build take?**  
A: Expect 20-40 minutes per project for a typical 8-10 year old. The robotic arm is the most complex; the racing car is the quickest.

**Q: Can you buy replacement parts if something breaks or gets lost?**  
A: The kit comes with spare screws and a few extra pieces, but if you lose an axle or the motor fails, you'd need to contact Poraxy directly or buy a new kit. At $16.99, the whole set is cheaper than most single STEM projects.

**The Verdict**

The Poraxy 5 in 1 STEM Kit delivers exactly what it promises: five functional, educational builds at a price that won't make you wince. It's not a toy that will last years of rough play, and the tiny parts demand some patience, but the learning value per dollar is outstanding. For the 8-10 age range, this is one of the better STEM investments you can make.

**Buy it if** you want a hands-on introduction to motors and gears that gives your kid multiple projects to work through, with or without you.

**Skip it if** your child needs a durable toy they can bang around, or if you're not prepared to supervise the initial wire connections.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Remarkable value with minor assembly frustrations.
