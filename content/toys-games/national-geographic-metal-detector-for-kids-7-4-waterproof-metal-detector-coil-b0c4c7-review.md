## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find the detector assembled in two main pieces—the control housing and the stem with the 7.4-inch coil pre-attached. The stem telescopes from 27 to 34 inches, which is a thoughtful touch. As shown in the product image, the ergonomic D-shaped handle has a padded grip that small hands can actually wrap around. There's no carrying case, no batteries, and no instructional DVD—just a slim quick-start guide. National Geographic includes a small "treasure identification" card that shows what different tones mean, which is a nice touch for beginners.

## Real-World Performance

The coil is the star here. That 7.4-inch waterproof search coil means your kid can sweep it through puddles, shallow creek beds, or damp grass after rain without you hovering nervously. One parent noted, "My son found a quarter in a mud puddle the first day—he was hooked." The detector runs at a fixed frequency and uses a simple two-tone audio system: high pitch for coins and jewelry, low pitch for nails and bottle caps. For a toy in this price range, that's genuinely useful discrimination.

But here's the catch: there's no sensitivity adjustment or ground-balancing. On mineral-rich soil or wet sand, you'll get false signals—one reviewer called it "beeping constantly on nothing" in their rocky yard. The detection depth is honest for the price: coins at 4-5 inches, larger objects like soda cans at 6-7 inches. Don't expect it to find a dime buried at 8 inches; it won't.

Battery life is another reality check. Two AA batteries (not included) will give you roughly 8-10 hours of intermittent use. If your kid is genuinely obsessed, plan on buying batteries in bulk. The unit doesn't have an auto-off feature either, so it'll drain cells if left on in the garage.

## Where It Falls Short

The fixed sensitivity is the biggest limitation. On beaches with black sand or heavily fertilized lawns, expect frustration. The audio tones are also quiet—there's no headphone jack, so in windy conditions your kid will struggle to hear the difference between a penny and a pull tab.

The plastic build is functional but not rugged. The stem flexes when you push it into hard ground, and the battery compartment door feels flimsy. If you have a kid who's rough with toys, buy the extended warranty or expect a crack within a year.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a curious kid aged 5-10 who loves outdoor exploration. This is an excellent first detector that teaches the basics without overwhelming them. It's also great for families who camp or visit beaches—the waterproof coil is a genuine advantage over cheaper models.

**Skip it if:** Your child is over 12 or already shows serious interest in detecting. They'll outgrow this within a season and want something with adjustable discrimination and depth readouts. Also skip if you have highly mineralized soil; the false signals will kill the fun fast.

## 3 FAQs Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can it detect gold?**
A: It can detect gold jewelry at shallow depths (2-3 inches), but it won't find gold nuggets or tiny gold flakes. For that, you need a high-frequency detector that costs 5-10x more.

**Q: Is it really waterproof?**
A: The coil is waterproof, meaning you can submerge it in shallow water. The control box is not—keep it dry. Don't let your kid wade into water deeper than the coil.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
A: Expect 8-10 hours with standard AAs. Use lithium batteries for longer life and better performance in cold weather.
