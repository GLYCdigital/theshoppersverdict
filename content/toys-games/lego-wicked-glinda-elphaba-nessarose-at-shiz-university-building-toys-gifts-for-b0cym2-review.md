Let's be direct: if you're a Wicked fan, you've already made peace with the fact that LEGO was going to make you choose between your wallet and your love for Oz. The **LEGO Wicked Glinda, Elphaba & Nessarose at Shiz University** set (item 75679) is the first proper brick-built homage to the Broadway phenomenon, and it mostly delivers — but not without a few compromises.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting 740 pieces that assemble into a three-section dormitory scene. The main structure splits into two student rooms flanking the iconic Shiz library tower. That tower is the showpiece — it stands about 11 inches tall with a working bookcase door that swings open to reveal a hidden study nook. The rooms themselves include bunk beds for Glinda and Elphaba (complete with a pink canopy for Glinda's side and a plain cot for Elphaba — a nice storytelling touch), Nessarose's wheelchair, a phonograph, spellbooks, and a lantern that actually lights up via a flick-fire brick.

The three minifigures are the real draw. Glinda comes with her glittering crown and a shimmery pink dress piece. Elphaba has her black hat, pointed nails, and that signature green skin tone applied cleanly across her face and hands. Nessarose includes her wheelchair with printed roses on the spokes. These aren't repurposed parts — the torso prints and face details are exclusive to this set.

## Build Experience and Real-World Use

The build takes roughly 90 minutes for an adult, and it's a satisfying one. LEGO uses some clever angled brickwork to give the tower its curved silhouette, and the modular design means each room lifts off the baseplate independently — which makes it far easier to play with than a single fused structure.

The hidden bookcase door is genuinely fun. It swings on a hinge mechanism that's simple enough for an 8-year-old to operate but satisfying for adults who appreciate clean engineering. The lantern flick-fire brick is a nice touch, though it's a one-trick gimmick — you press it once, it lights, and that's it.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Looking at the 240 ratings (4.8 average), the consistent theme is that the minifigures exceed expectations. One verified purchaser notes: *"The likenesses are incredible — I honestly bought it for the figs but the dorm itself is super charming."* Another highlights the playability: *"My daughter and I built it together in an afternoon. The rooms actually feel lived-in, not just display pieces."* Several reviewers specifically call out Nessarose's wheelchair as a thoughtful inclusion that's rare in licensed LEGO sets.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. The price-per-part ratio is steep — you're paying a premium largely for the Wicked license. At 740 pieces, comparable LEGO sets in this range typically include 800–900 pieces.

The stickers are also a minor annoyance. Several curved window pieces require precision application that's finicky — one reviewer mentioned their sticker wrinkled and they had to order a replacement. If you're a stickler for perfect decals, budget extra time or have tweezers ready.

Finally, the modular design, while great for play, means the set doesn't display as a single cohesive scene. The rooms are separate models on their own bases, so if you want the full Shiz silhouette on a shelf, you'll need to place them side-by-side and accept visible gaps between sections.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a Wicked superfan who wants high-quality minifigures of the core trio. You appreciate clever build techniques and don't mind a shorter build time. You're looking for a gift for a tween/teen Wicked enthusiast — the play features will engage them, and the display quality will satisfy you.

**Skip it if:** You're a collector focused on piece-count value. You're buying for a younger child who might lose the small accessories (the spellbook is tiny and easy to misplace). Or you're not emotionally invested in Wicked — the licensing premium isn't justified by the build alone.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this set compatible with other LEGO sets?**
Yes, it uses standard LEGO bricks throughout. You can combine the dormitory rooms with other modular buildings, though the tower's scale is specific to this set.

**Q: How long does the build take?**
Most builders report 60–90 minutes. It's rated ages 12+, but a patient 8-year-old could handle it with adult help — the instructions are clear and the parts aren't excessively small.

**Q: Does the lantern actually light up?**
Yes, it uses a flick-fire brick that activates an LED light. It's not motorized or continuous — you press it to light, press again to turn off. It takes no batteries.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — This is a solid, character-driven LEGO set that nails the Wicked aesthetic. The minifigures alone justify the price for fans, and the build offers enough clever mechanics to keep it interesting. It's not the best value in LEGO's catalog, and the sticker work is fiddly, but the charm is undeniable. Buy it for the Wicked fan in your life — they'll be thrilled. Just don't expect to understand why they're crying while building it if you've never seen the musical.
