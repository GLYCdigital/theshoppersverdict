If you're on the fence about the PlayStation VR2, the Horizon Call of the Mountain bundle is the version to buy — not because the headset is perfect, but because it's the best-value entry point into Sony's current-gen VR ecosystem. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's clear most buyers feel the same way.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the PSVR2 headset, two Sense controllers, the Horizon Call of the Mountain game (either as a disc or download code, depending on region), and a bonus download code for Horizon Zero Dawn Remastered. That second game is a smart addition — it gives you a flat-screen title to play when VR fatigue sets in, and it's a legitimately great game in its own right. You'll also need a PS5, which the box doesn't include. That's the first thing to budget for if you don't already own one.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The headline feature here is the visual experience. The OLED panels with 4K HDR per eye and a 110-degree field of view make Horizon's jungles and mountains feel genuinely alive. Colors pop, blacks are deep, and the foveated rendering — which sharpens only where you're looking — keeps things running at 90-120Hz without noticeable frame drops. In a game as visually dense as Call of the Mountain, this matters.

The Sense controllers deserve their own praise. The adaptive triggers tension up as you draw a bow, and the haptics in both the controllers and the headset itself provide real feedback — you feel the rumble of a machine's footsteps through your skull. It's the kind of detail that separates this from cheaper headsets.

One thing that surprised me: the headset is comfortable for extended sessions. The scuba-style goggle design distributes weight well, and the ventilation keeps your face from fogging up. That said, the single cable tether is still there. It's lighter than the original PSVR's wiring, but you'll remain aware of it during fast movements, and the 4.5-meter cable means your play space is limited.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Across Amazon reviews, the visual quality and the game itself come up repeatedly. One buyer wrote, "The graphics are insane — it's like being inside the game. Horizon is the best looking VR title I've played." Another noted, "Setup was easier than I expected, and the eye tracking worked right out of the box."

The bundle's value also gets consistent love. "Getting two games for the price of the headset alone made this a no-brainer," one reviewer said. The included Horizon Zero Dawn Remastered gives you a full flat-screen game as a bonus, which softens the sting of the premium price.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest downsides start with the PS5 requirement. If you don't own one, this bundle becomes a $550+ investment, and that's a hard pill to swallow for many shoppers. Setup isn't as simple as plug-and-play either — you'll spend time with the eye-tracking calibration, and several reviewers noted it's tricky for glasses wearers or people with unusual pupil spacing.

The cable tether remains the biggest physical limitation. For a premium 2026 headset, it's frustrating that Sony hasn't gone wireless. You'll also find the library is still relatively small compared to the Meta Quest ecosystem, though Horizon alone justifies the purchase for many.

## Who Should Buy This

- **PS5 owners who want a premium VR experience** — this is the best option available for that system, period.
- **Horizon fans** — Call of the Mountain is a showcase title that delivers on the franchise's visual promise in VR.
- **First-time VR buyers with a PS5** — the bundle's two-game value makes it a smarter entry point than a bare headset.

**Skip it if** you don't own a PS5, if your play space is cramped, or if you're expecting a wireless headset. For that, you'd want the Meta Quest 3.

## FAQ

**Is the PSVR2 worth it over the Meta Quest 3?**
For PS5 owners, yes — the OLED displays and haptic feedback are superior. But the Quest 3 is wireless and has a larger game library. It depends on whether you value visual fidelity or freedom of movement more.

**Does the bundle include everything needed to play?**
Yes, except the PS5 console. You get the headset, controllers, and both games. You'll need to provide your own PS5 and enough floor space for the recommended 6.5 x 6.5-foot play area.

**Can I play non-VR games with this headset?**
Yes — the PSVR2 includes a cinematic mode that displays standard PS5 games on a virtual screen. It's not the same as a flat-screen TV experience, but it works well for games that don't require precise text reading.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you own a PS5 and want the definitive VR experience on that console. The Horizon bundle gives you the best-looking VR game available and a bonus title at no extra cost.

**Skip it if** you're on a budget, don't have a PS5, or need wireless freedom.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a premium product with real tradeoffs, but for PS5 owners, it's the VR headset to beat. The cable and PS5 requirement hold it back from a perfect score, but the visual quality and included games make it a strong value for the right buyer.
