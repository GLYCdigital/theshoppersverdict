Some bath toys are just floating plastic junk. This one actually creates a game. The SwimWays Disney Finding Dory Mr. Ray's Dive and Catch Game turns tub time into a target practice session, and at 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, it's clearly clicking with families. Here's the real story after looking at how it works, what breaks, and who actually gets use out of it.

**What's in the box**

You get Mr. Ray — the manta ray launcher with Dory-inspired coloring — plus six small rings. The concept is simple: you squeeze Mr. Ray's body, and he spits a ring across the water. The rings sink slowly, fluttering down like they're alive. That's the whole kit. No batteries, no parts to lose beyond the rings themselves, no assembly. As shown in the product image, Mr. Ray is sized for small hands — roughly palm-length for an adult, which means a 3-year-old can grip and squeeze it independently.

**How it actually performs**

The launcher mechanism is the make-or-break feature here, and it works. The squeeze chamber is firm enough to shoot rings a solid 3-4 feet across a standard tub, but soft enough that a preschooler can compress it without frustration. The rings sink at a pace that gives kids time to track them and dive — not so fast they hit the floor instantly, not so slow they float uselessly. That sinking flutter is what separates this from cheaper ring-toss sets.

Parents report the rings are durable, not brittle. One reviewer noted, "My son has thrown these across the room multiple times and they don't crack or warp." The Dory paint job is molded into the plastic rather than printed on, so it won't peel after a month of wet play.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The number one compliment in reviews is engagement. Kids who typically fight bath time will ask to stay in longer. One parent said, "This was the first bath toy my 2-year-old actually understood how to use — he figured out the squeezing in about a minute." Another praised the educational angle: "Great for hand-eye coordination and teaching turn-taking with siblings."

The size is also a recurring plus — it's compact enough for travel or small tubs, unlike bulky bath playsets that take up half the tub.

**Where it falls short**

The honest downsides: six rings is a thin supply. When you're playing with two kids, that's three each, and you'll spend half the bath retrieving them from under the faucet or the drain cover. The rings are small enough to slip past a standard tub drain guard, so keep an eye on that.

The bigger issue is maintenance. The launcher has an internal chamber that traps water. If you don't squeeze it dry and let it air out completely between baths, you'll get mildew inside. Several reviewers mentioned discovering black spots inside after a few weeks. It's manageable — just make it part of the post-bath routine — but it's a real consideration.

Also, there's no storage solution. You'll be gathering Mr. Ray and six rings off the bathroom floor unless you have a mesh bag handy.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for:
- Parents of kids aged 2-5 who are transitioning from passive bath toys to interactive play
- Families with multiple young children — the turn-taking dynamic works well
- Anyone looking for a screen-free activity that actually occupies a toddler for 15-20 minutes

Skip it if your kid is over 6 — they'll outgrow the challenge in a week. Also skip it if you're prone to leaving bath toys in the tub. The mildew issue is real.

**FAQ**

*Is this safe for a 1-year-old?*
The rings are small enough to be a choking hazard for under-3s. The manufacturer recommends 3+. If you have a baby in the tub, keep the rings out of reach or skip this until they're older.

*Can it shoot rings out of the water?*
No — it's designed for underwater launches. It works best when Mr. Ray is submerged and squeezing him sends the ring shooting forward. Out of water, the rings just flop.

*How do you clean it?*
After each bath, squeeze out all the water from Mr. Ray, then leave him and the rings on a towel to dry completely. Once a week, run a diluted vinegar solution through the launcher chamber to prevent mold.

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 4.6/5 (⭐ 4.6 out of 5)**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, engaging bath toy that builds coordination and actually keeps toddlers in the tub longer. The launcher holds up, the rings sink properly, and the Dory theme is applied well.

**Skip it if** you're not willing to dry it thoroughly after every use, or if your kid is old enough that "sinking rings" won't hold their attention more than once.

It's not a perfect product — the ring count is skimpy and the mold risk is real — but for the price of a fast-food run, you're getting months of engaged bath time. That's a solid trade.
