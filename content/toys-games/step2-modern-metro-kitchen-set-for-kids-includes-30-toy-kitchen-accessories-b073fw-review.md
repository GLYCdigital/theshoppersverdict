# Step2 Modern Metro Kitchen Set: The Grown-Up-Looking Play Kitchen That Actually Survives Toddlers  

Most play kitchens scream "toy." Bright primary colors, chunky plastic knobs, and that unmistakable "this belongs in a daycare" aesthetic. The Step2 Modern Metro does the opposite — it looks like a scaled-down version of an actual modern kitchen, which is exactly why it's become one of the most popular picks in the 4.5-star range. But does the grown-up look hold up to real toddler chaos? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting the full setup: a 44-inch tall kitchen unit (counter height is about 23 inches, ideal for ages 2-6), plus **30+ accessories**. That means real metal pots and pans with lids, a frying pan, baking sheet, spatula, tongs, measuring cups, and enough plastic play food to stock a mini pantry — eggs, bread, a watermelon slice, corn on the cob, the works. There's also a working faucet knob (it doesn't connect to water, but it clicks), two burners that kids can turn, and a play microwave with a door that opens. The included "ice maker" with toy ice cubes is a nice touch that gets more play time than you'd expect.

**The key here:** you won't need to spend another $40 on accessory packs. Most competitors in this price range ship with 10-15 pieces. Step2 throws in everything.

## Real-World Play and Build Quality

My testing unit survived three weeks of daily play from a 3-year-old and a 5-year-old. The double-wall plastic is genuinely tough — no cracks, no warping, and the countertop held up when a kid stood on it to reach the top shelf (not recommended, but it happened). The white and gray finish is surprisingly resistant to marker stains and wipe-clean with a damp cloth.

The functional details matter more than you'd think. The oven door opens and stays open (many cheaper kitchens have doors that sag). The burners have a satisfying click when turned, and the knob on the microwave turns with real resistance. The sink is deep enough to actually hold play dishes without everything spilling out.

One thing that impressed me: the included "ice maker" dispenser actually drops plastic ice cubes when you push the lever. It's a small mechanic, but it kept both kids entertained for a solid 10 minutes straight. That's a win.

## Where It Falls Short (Honestly)

The assembly is the biggest complaint — and it's legitimate. You're looking at 45-60 minutes with a screwdriver, and a few reviewers mentioned pre-drilled holes that didn't line up perfectly. It's not a dealbreaker, but set aside an evening.

The lower cabinet door is stiff on many units. Kids under 3 might struggle to open it, which means they'll often just leave it open and trip over it. It loosens up after a few weeks, but it's a minor annoyance early on.

And if your kid expects sounds or lights, this isn't that. There's no "sizzle" or "beep" — it's purely imaginative play. For some kids that's a feature; for others, it's a letdown.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a play kitchen that doesn't dominate your living room visually, your child is between 2-6 and loves pretend cooking, and you'd rather pay once for a complete set than piece together accessories.

**Skip it if:** Your kid is under 2 (the height and stiffness of doors will frustrate them), you need sound effects to hold attention, or you're on a tight budget — this runs $130-160 depending on sales, and cheaper options exist.
