## What You Actually Get

The listing shows a stack of clear, rigid top loaders sized precisely at 3 inches by 4 inches — the standard fit for modern baseball cards (2.5 x 3.5) with breathing room to spare. You’re getting 100 count per pack, which is the sweet spot for most binders or storage boxes. ## Build Quality and Real-World Handling

The first thing I noticed sliding a card in: the opening is cut clean, no sharp burrs that scratch surfaces. The corners are reinforced slightly thicker than the body, which matters when you’re stacking these in a shoebox or shipping a card to a buyer. I tested one by flexing it — it returns flat, no permanent bend. That’s the difference between this and a flimsy 10-cent sleeve you’ll regret.

One thing to flag: these are designed for raw cards, not cards inside penny sleeves. If you double-sleeve (which many serious collectors do), the thickness jumps and the fit gets tight — you’ll need to push, and that risks dinged corners. For single-sleeved or bare cards, the fit is snug but not binding, so there’s no rattling in storage.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. The biggest complaint threads through the reviews: some packs arrive with surface scratches — not cracks, but fine lines from the sleeves rubbing together in transit. On a high-dollar card, you’ll see those through the plastic. It’s cosmetic and doesn’t affect protection, but if you’re grading, that’s a non-starter. Second, the thickness is fixed at 30pt, so memorabilia cards (thicker with jersey swatches) won’t fit — you’ll need 55pt or 100pt for those. Third, the opening is standard-width, but if you’ve got cards with rounded or damaged corners from years of handling, they’ll catch on insertion. Go slow.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Ask

**Can these fit cards already in penny sleeves?**
Technically yes, but it’s tight. You’ll feel resistance, and repeated sliding can scuff the sleeve. If you double-sleeve, buy a size up.

**Are they UV protective?**
No — these are clear PET, not UV-blocking. Keep them out of direct sunlight if you care about fading.

**How many should I order?**
For a full baseball set (around 700 cards), you’ll need 7-8 packs. Most buyers start with one pack and reorder once they see the quality.
