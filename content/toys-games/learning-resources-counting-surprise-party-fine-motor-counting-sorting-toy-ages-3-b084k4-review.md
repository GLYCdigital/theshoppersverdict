**The hook: A counting toy that actually teaches two skills at once**  

Most counting toys stop at rote memorization—point at a number, say it, move on. The Learning Resources Counting Surprise Party does something smarter: it pairs number recognition with fine motor practice, then rewards both with a hidden surprise. That's a combination that keeps 3- and 4-year-olds engaged without you hovering over their shoulder.  

**What you actually get**  

Open the box and you'll find 10 plastic pops—each shaped like an ice pop with a numbered top (1 through 10) and a colored base. Twist or pull the two halves apart, and inside sits a small plastic character: a cat, a dog, a frog, a duck, and six others. Each pop's number matches the number of objects or the character's theme, so there's a built-in self-checking element. The pieces are roughly 4 inches long when assembled—sized for small hands but not so small that they vanish under the couch.  

**Real-world performance**  

The genius here is the dual-action play. Your child has to grip the pop with two hands, twist, and pull—that's bilateral coordination and hand strength in action. Then they count the surprise character inside, match it to the number on the pop, and repeat. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"My 3-year-old didn't realize she was learning. She thought she was just opening presents."* That's the sweet spot.  

The plastic is thick and forgiving. I've seen these pops launched across a room, stepped on, and chewed on the edges—they survive. The numbers are printed, not stickers, so they don't peel off after a month of play.  

**What buyers consistently praise**  

Parents and grandparents dominate the 240 ratings, and the 4.8-star average holds up. The most repeated praise: kids actually return to this toy. One reviewer noted, *"My son has had this for 6 months and still asks for it by name."* Another highlighted the surprise element: *"The hidden characters keep my daughter guessing, so she opens every pop every single time."*  

**Where it falls short**  

Let's be honest about three things. First, the surprise characters are small—about an inch tall—and they're a genuine choking hazard for kids under 3. This is a toy for 3 and up, and you should respect that age label strictly. Second, the pops require a firm twist-and-pull motion. Some 3-year-olds get frustrated on the first few tries and need adult help until their grip strengthens. Third, there's no storage container. The cardboard box is flimsy, and if you don't have a bin ready, you'll be chasing pops around the playroom within a week.  

**Who should buy this**  

- **Parents of 3- to 5-year-olds** who want a toy that sneaks in math and motor skills without feeling like homework.  
- **Preschool teachers** looking for a small-group activity—the self-checking design means kids can play independently while you work with others.  
- **Gift-givers** who want something durable and educational that isn't another plastic dinosaur.  

Skip it if your child is under 3, or if you're looking for a quiet toy—the surprise characters encourage excited squealing.  

**FAQ**  

*Q: Can a 2-year-old use this?*  
No. The small characters are a choking hazard, and the snap-apart motion is too complex for most 2-year-olds. Wait until age 3.  

*Q: Do the numbers and characters align?*  
Mostly. Pop #5 contains a character with 5 visible features (like a bug with 5 spots), and pop #7 has a character with 7 legs or stripes. It's not perfect on every pop, but the color matching (number top and base color) is always consistent.  

*Q: Is this durable enough for daycare or classroom use?*  
Yes. The plastic is thick, and the pops withstand aggressive opening and closing. Just be prepared to replace lost characters—they're small and easy to misplace.  

**The verdict**  

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely educational toy that holds up to rough play and holds a toddler's attention longer than a week. **Skip it if** you have a child under 3 or you're tired of toys with tiny parts that end up in the vacuum.  

At 4.6/5, the Counting Surprise Party earns its rating. It's not flashy, it won't sing songs, and it doesn't light up—but it teaches real skills in a way kids actually enjoy. That's worth the shelf space.
