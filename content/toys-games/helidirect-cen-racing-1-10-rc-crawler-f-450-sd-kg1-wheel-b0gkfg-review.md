Let's be direct: the CEN Racing F-450 SD KG1 Wheel E is a scale crawler that wants to be taken seriously, and for the most part, it earns that respect. At 1/10 scale, this isn't a toy-grade basher — it's a hobby-grade rig with real metal components and a surprisingly detailed Ford F-450 body. But at 3.8 stars from 190 ratings, there's clearly a split between buyers who got a gem and those who got a project.

## What's in the Box

You get the fully assembled truck — body mounted, electronics wired, wheels on. The KG1 wheel and tire combo is the headline here: these are licensed-style beadlock wheels wrapped in soft compound tires that bite well on rock and loose dirt. The F-450 SD body comes pre-painted with realistic trim, chrome bumpers, and light buckets (though lights aren't wired in stock). A 2.4GHz radio, receiver, ESC, and motor are all installed. What you don't get: a battery or charger. That's the single biggest point of contention in reviews, and it's worth knowing before you buy.

## Real-World Performance

On the trail, this rig shines in slow technical crawling. The steel C-channel frame and metal axles give it a planted feel that plastic-frame trucks lack at this price point. The motor has decent low-end torque, and the transmission's gear reduction is well-suited for rock sections. Ground clearance is respectable at 0.9 inches, and the wheelbase stretches to 12.6 inches, which helps it bridge gaps that shorter rigs struggle with.

But there's a catch. The stock servo is genuinely underpowered — multiple buyers note that steering gets lazy when the front wheels are loaded. One reviewer put it plainly: "Great truck, but swap the servo on day one." Also, the shocks use plastic bodies that weep oil after a few hard runs. Neither issue is a dealbreaker, but both are predictable upgrades you should budget for.

## What Buyers Love

The scale appearance is the runaway winner. Owners consistently praise the body detail and the wheel/tire combo. As one five-star review put it: "Looks like a $500 rig, crawls like a $300 rig." The assembly quality is also solid — no sloppy glue joints or misaligned panels out of the box. For someone who wants a realistic-looking truck without spending hours painting and assembling, this delivers.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the servo and shocks, there's the battery situation. The manual lists a 7.2V NiMH pack, but it's not included. Several reviews call this out as misleading, especially for first-time RC buyers who expect "ready-to-run" to mean everything's in the box. Also, the F-450 body is top-heavy — it looks great, but it raises the center of gravity. On side hills, you'll tip over sooner than you would with a lower-profile crawler body.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the scale enthusiast who wants a good-looking rig for trail runs and light crawling — not for someone planning hardcore comp courses. It's also a solid choice if you're comfortable with basic upgrades (servo, shocks) and want a platform with metal bones. Beginners should know upfront that they'll need to buy a battery and charger separately, and likely a better servo within the first month.

## FAQ

**Is a battery really not included?**
Correct. The truck is RTR, but you must supply your own 7.2V NiMH or 2S LiPo pack and a compatible charger. Factor that into your total cost.

**Can I use this for serious rock crawling?**
It'll handle moderate trails and ledges, but the top-heavy body and weak servo limit it. For competition-level crawling, look at lower-profile rigs with better stock servos.

**What upgrades matter most?**
A metal-gear servo (around $25-40) and aluminum shock bodies (about $30-50) transform the handling. After that, consider brass diff covers for lower center of gravity.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a scale-accurate F-450 that looks fantastic and performs decently on trails, and you're okay doing two or three sensible upgrades. It's a great value for the visual quality and metal drivetrain.

**Skip it if:** You're a first-time RC buyer expecting a true out-of-box experience, or you need hardcore crawling capability without modification.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A good-looking, well-built crawler held back by a weak servo, leaky shocks, and the missing battery. With $60 worth of upgrades, it becomes a genuinely great rig. Without them, it's a capable but slightly frustrating truck that looks better than it performs.
