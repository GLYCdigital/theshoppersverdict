**What You Actually Get**

The box contains a single 16-foot (5-meter) fiber-optic USB 3.2 Gen 1 cable. It’s braided, feels premium, and has a right-angle connector on the headset end—a small detail that prevents strain on your Quest’s port when you’re moving around. The other end is a standard USB-C, so it works with most modern gaming laptops and desktops. No wall adapter, no extra accessories. Just the cable.

**Performance and Build Quality**

This is where the Link Cable earns its keep. It delivers up to 5Gbps data transfer, which is enough for a clean, uncompressed PC VR signal. In practice, that means you get sharper textures and less smearing in fast-moving scenes compared to wireless streaming over Wi-Fi. Latency is effectively a non-issue—your movements feel immediate, which matters in rhythm games or competitive shooters.

The fiber-optic construction keeps the cable light (about 2.5 ounces), so it doesn’t drag on your headset during play. The braided nylon sheath resists kinking, and the 16-foot length gives you enough slack to move around a typical room-scale setup without yanking the cable out of your PC. One thing to note: it also charges your Quest while you play, so long sessions won’t drain the battery. That’s a real convenience if you’ve ever had a headset die mid-boss-fight.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let’s be honest: $79 is a lot for a cable. You can find USB 3.2 cables that meet the same spec for under $30, though buyer reviews suggest hit-or-miss reliability with those. The other issue is compatibility. If your PC only has USB-A ports, you’ll need a USB-C to USB-A adapter (or a PCIe card with USB-C), and that’s an extra purchase. Some users also report that the cable doesn’t play nicely with older USB 3.0 ports on certain motherboards—a known quirk that’s less about the cable and more about your hardware’s power delivery. Finally, it’s not wireless. If you absolutely hate being tethered, this cable isn’t for you—but that’s the tradeoff for maximum visual fidelity.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with the Quest 3?**  
Yes. It’s fully compatible with Quest 3, Quest 2, and Quest Pro. It uses the same USB-C port and PC Link software.

**Q: Can I use it to charge my headset without a PC?**  
Technically yes, but it’s not ideal. The cable will charge your Quest if plugged into a USB-C power adapter, but the fiber-optic design is optimized for data, not fast charging. You’re better off using the included charger for that.

**Q: Will it work with a Mac?**  
No. Meta’s PC Link software is Windows-only. Mac users will need to use virtual desktop streaming or other workarounds, which defeats the purpose of a wired connection.
