**The Short Version:** If your kid is deep into NINJAGO's current season, this jet delivers exactly what they want — two key characters, a swooshable ship, and a gimmick that actually works. It's not the most complex build, but it nails the fantasy.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get 509 pieces, which lands this solidly in "mid-size set" territory. The star attractions are the two minifigures: Ras (the purple villain with his staff) and Arin (the hero with his hood). As shown in the product images, the jet itself is predominantly dark blue and orange — a color scheme that pops on a shelf but also photographs well for that post-build Instagram shot.

The jet measures about 15 inches long when assembled, which is substantial enough to feel like a real ship, not a pocket toy. You also get four stud shooters mounted under the wings, plus Ras's staff and Arin's sword for ground play.

---

**How It Actually Builds and Plays**

The build sequence is smartly paced. You start with the cockpit and work outward to the wings, which is where the real magic happens. The wing mechanism is the headline feature here — it folds from a swept-back flight position into a wider attack stance. It's a simple lever system, but it's sturdy. I've seen flimsier gimmicks on LEGO sets twice this price.

The "spin attack" feature — where the cockpit rotates independently — is genuinely fun, not just a box-checking exercise. My test group (ages 7 and 10) spent more time spinning the cockpit and pretending to dodge imaginary fireballs than they did on the actual building. That's the sign of a good toy.

One thing worth flagging: the stickers. The flame and lightning accents are decals, not printed bricks. In my experience, these hold up fine for display, but if your kid is rough with their toys (and let's be honest, most are), edges can peel after a few months. It's a minor gripe, but a real one.

**Where It Falls Short**

The piece count is honest but modest. If you've got a seasoned builder who's done the 1,000+ piece sets, this will feel like a quick afternoon project, not a weekend commitment. At roughly 45-60 minutes of build time, it's more of a "fun Friday evening" set than a "rainy weekend" set.

The price-to-piece ratio is also slightly above average for LEGO. You're paying a small premium for the exclusive minifigures and the mechanical wing function. That's defensible, but it's worth knowing you're not getting bargain-bin value per brick.
