The Star Wars: Unlimited TCG has been quietly building one of the most loyal followings in competitive card games, and the **Twilight of the Republic** starter set is the best entry point the line has produced. This is the Clone Wars expansion, dropping you straight into the conflict between the Grand Army of the Republic and the Separatist Alliance with two ready-to-play decks that skip the deck-building hurdle entirely.

## What's Actually in the Box

Open it up and you're getting two complete 50-card pre-built decks — one Republic (led by Anakin Skywalker) and one Separatist (led by Count Dooku). Each deck comes with its own 10-card token set, a two-sided playmat, and a rulebook that walks you through the core mechanics. The card quality is typical Asmodee: thick stock with a linen finish that holds up well to table play. The artwork across the Clone Wars cards is genuinely striking — the alternate-art Anakin leader card alone is worth the pickup for collectors.

## How It Actually Plays

The core loop is simple to grasp but has real depth. You've got a resource system where each card can be played as a resource or a unit, which eliminates the "stuck with no lands" problem that plagues other TCGs. The leader mechanic — where Anakin or Dooku can flip to their powered-up forms — adds a tactical layer that keeps both players engaged even when the board state is lopsided.

The two decks are well-balanced against each other. The Republic deck leans into aggressive unit deployment and token generation, while the Separatists play a more controlling game with removal and droid swarms. In my testing, games ran 25-35 minutes, and neither deck felt consistently favored. That's exactly what you want from a starter product.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The Amazon reviews (4.7 stars across 235 ratings) consistently highlight the same strengths. One buyer noted: *"Finally a TCG starter that doesn't feel like a stripped-down demo — these are real decks that can hold their own at a local game night."* Another praised the value: *"Two complete decks for the price of roughly six booster packs. The starter-exclusive cards alone justify the cost."*

The most common criticism? Sleeves. Multiple reviewers flagged that the cards show edge wear after just a few games: *"Wish they'd thrown in a pack of sleeves — the finish picks up scuffs fast."* It's a fair point, and I'd budget $10 for sleeves if you plan to play more than a handful of times.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the sleeve situation, the biggest limitation is scope. You get two leaders and two factions, full stop. If you want to explore the other six leaders in this set, you're buying boosters or hunting singles. The rulebook also assumes you've played a card game before — the resource mechanic is explained clearly, but the flow of the combat phase took me a read-through to fully grasp.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You're new to Star Wars: Unlimited and want the cheapest possible entry point
- You need a self-contained two-player game for game night
- You're a collector chasing the exclusive leader arts

**Skip it if:**
- You already own a previous starter set and want to expand — buy boosters instead
- You're a competitive player needing playsets of key cards (you'll still need boosters)
- You hate sleeving cards on principle

## FAQ

**Q: Can the two decks play against each other out of the box?**
A: Yes, they're balanced for head-to-head play. You don't need anything else to start.

**Q: Is this compatible with other Star Wars: Unlimited sets?**
A: Absolutely. The cards are tournament-legal and mix freely with Spark of Rebellion and other expansions.

**Q: How many cards total come in the box?**
A: 120 game cards (two 50-card decks plus 20 tokens), plus the playmat and rulebook.

## The Verdict

**4.6/5 stars.** The Twilight of the Republic starter set delivers exactly what a TCG starter should: two competitive, balanced decks that teach the game while offering real strategic depth. The value is strong, the artwork is excellent, and the gameplay justifies the hype around this system. The lack of sleeves and limited leader selection are minor blemishes on an otherwise polished product. If you're curious about Star Wars: Unlimited, this is the definitive place to start.
