**Review Body:**

The LEGO NINJAGO Legacy Overlord Dragon 71742 is a set that fans of the series have been waiting for since the original 2013 release. It's a reimagined version of the iconic battle between the ninja and the Overlord, and it delivers on nostalgia while improving the build in meaningful ways. But is it worth your money in 2026? Let's break it down.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains 810 pieces, which breaks down to six minifigures — Zane, Kai, Cole, Nya, Lloyd, and the Overlord himself — plus the centerpiece dragon. The minifigures are the highlight here. The Legacy armor variants are exclusive to this set, and collectors will appreciate the printed detailing on the torsos and the gold accents on the Overlord's armor. As shown in the product image, the dragon itself is a substantial build that measures about 15 inches long when assembled, which gives it real presence on a shelf.

**Build Experience and Performance**

The build takes roughly 2 to 3 hours depending on your pace. The dragon's construction is clever — the neck and tail use ball joints that allow for a wide range of motion. You can pose the head to snap at enemies or curve the tail for a dynamic attack stance. The legs are similarly articulated, which is a step up from many older LEGO dragon sets that had static limbs.

One thing worth noting: the wings are not adjustable. They're locked into a spread position, which looks great in flight poses but limits your options if you want the dragon to sit with folded wings. It's a minor frustration, but one that parents should know about if their kids like to reconfigure sets after building.

The build quality is typical LEGO — everything clicks together with the satisfying precision you expect. The gold and black color scheme on the dragon pops nicely, and the translucent orange fire elements add visual flair.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. One verified buyer noted, "My son has been asking for this set since it was announced. The dragon is bigger than I expected, and the minifigures are the best part." Another reviewer highlighted the playability: "The ball joints make it easy for my 7-year-old to pose the dragon without pieces falling off constantly."

Multiple reviewers mention that the set bridges generations well — it appeals to adults who remember the original Overlord Dragon from 2013, while remaining engaging for younger builders.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The printed gold armor on the Overlord minifigure can chip if handled roughly. It's a print over standard LEGO plastic, not a molded gold piece, so kids who play aggressively may see wear within a few months.

There's also no display stand included. For a set that's clearly designed with collectors in mind, this feels like an oversight. You can pose the dragon on its own, but the legs have limited splay, so dynamic aerial poses require a third-party stand.

Finally, the $79.99 MSRP might feel steep for a set with 810 pieces. It's not a bad price per piece, but you're paying a bit of a nostalgia tax. If you're purely interested in piece count, you could get more elsewhere.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **NINJAGO fans** who want a definitive version of the Overlord Dragon — this is the best iteration of this set LEGO has produced.
- **Parents of kids 8+** looking for a build that offers a real challenge without being frustrating.
- **LEGO collectors** who focus on the Legacy line and want the exclusive minifigures.

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget, or if your child prefers sets with lots of small creatures and play features — the dragon is the main event here, and there's not much else in the box.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this set compatible with the original 2013 Overlord Dragon?**
A: No. This is a completely redesigned build with different piece geometry. You can't combine the two into a single model.

**Q: How many minifigures are included?**
A: Six total — Zane, Kai, Cole, Nya, Lloyd, and the Overlord. All are exclusive to this set.

**Q: Is the dragon easy for younger kids to pose?**
A: Yes. The ball joints hold their position firmly, and the design is sturdy enough to survive typical play. Just avoid the printed gold armor on the Overlord figure.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a NINJAGO fan or want a high-quality dragon set with excellent minifigures. **Skip it if** you're price-sensitive or need a set with more variation in play features.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-executed remake that honors the original while improving the build. The fixed wings and lack of a stand keep it from a perfect score, but for most buyers, this is a satisfying addition to any LEGO collection.
