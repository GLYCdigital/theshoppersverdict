**The Lego Porsche 911 (10295) Review: Two Iconic Cars in One Box**

Most Lego sets give you one build and a pile of spare bricks. The Creator Expert Porsche 911 (10295) gives you two complete cars — a Turbo cabriolet and a Targa hardtop — from the same 1,458-piece inventory. That's not a gimmick; it's the single best reason to spend your money here instead of on another supercar set.

Let's be clear about what you're getting. This is a 1:8 scale model that measures roughly 14 inches long, 5 inches wide, and 4 inches tall when assembled. It's a substantial display piece, not a pocket toy. The set uses the classic Creator Expert "2-in-1" approach, meaning you build one full car, then disassemble and rebuild the other. You can't have both on your shelf simultaneously — but you can choose which one suits your space and mood.

**What's in the box**

You get 10 numbered bags, a 352-page instruction manual, and zero stickers. That last point matters more than you'd think. The Porsche badging, dashboard details, and rear grille are all printed directly on the bricks. After a six-hour build, you won't be fighting with misaligned decals or bubbles under clear stickers. The included instruction booklet is clear and well-laid-out, though it's a hefty tome — make sure you have a decent table surface to work on.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The engineering here is genuinely impressive. The boxer-6 engine has moving pistons that fire as you roll the car forward. The steering wheel connects to the front wheels through a gear mechanism — turn the wheel, and the tires respond. Doors, front trunk, and rear engine cover all open. It's not a motorized set, but the mechanical functions make it feel alive in a way static display models don't.

Assembly takes most builders 5-7 hours. The techniques are varied enough to keep you engaged through the first car, though the second build will feel familiar. That's not necessarily a negative — it's a chance to appreciate the engineering you just completed. One Amazon reviewer put it well: "The attention to detail is remarkable. The way the body panels curve and the proportions look right from every angle. It's a work of art when completed."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dual-build feature dominates positive feedback. Owners love having two distinct looks — the open-top Turbo feels aggressive and sporty, while the Targa with its chrome roll bar and removable roof panel feels more classic and elegant. The orange color scheme is bold and photographs beautifully. Several reviewers noted the set's display value, with one saying, "I've had this on my shelf for six months and I still catch myself staring at it."

**Where it falls short**

Let's talk tradeoffs, because they're real. First, the price. At $170 retail, you're paying a premium for the dual-build gimmick. If you only build one car, you're essentially paying double for half the set. Second, the Targa's roof panel is held on by friction only. It fits snugly but will pop off if you pick the car up by the roof — a minor annoyance if you plan to handle it regularly. Third, the build is heavily symmetrical. The second side of the chassis mirrors the first, and some builders find that repetitive.

**Who should buy this**

This set is ideal for three types of people: Porsche enthusiasts who want a detailed replica without paying for the massive Technic 911 GT3 RS (which runs $450+); adult builders who want a challenging weekend project that produces a display-worthy piece; and gift-givers looking for a premium set for a LEGO fan who's "graduated" from play sets. It's also a strong choice if you want a set that offers rebuild value — you can switch between the two cars whenever you want a fresh project.

Skip it if you're on a tight budget, if you want a motorized or app-controlled model, or if you prefer minifigure-scale sets. This is unapologetically an adult-oriented display piece.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can you build both the Turbo and Targa at the same time?**
A: No. The set contains enough pieces for one complete car at a time. You build one, then disassemble and rebuild the other.

**Q: Is this set suitable for a child?**
A: The box says 18+, and that's accurate. The build is complex, the pieces are small, and the finished model is a display piece, not a toy. A skilled 12-year-old could handle it with supervision, but it's really aimed at adults.

**Q: Does it come with a minifigure?**
A: No. This is a scale model set — no minifigures included, and none are compatible with the scale.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a technically impressive, mechanically functional Porsche replica that offers two distinct display options. The printed details, working steering, and moving pistons deliver real value for the price.

**Skip it if:** You want a single, less expensive build, or you're looking for a set with play features for kids.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The dual-build concept adds genuine versatility, and the engineering quality is top-tier. It's expensive, but for Porsche fans and serious LEGO collectors, it's worth the premium.
