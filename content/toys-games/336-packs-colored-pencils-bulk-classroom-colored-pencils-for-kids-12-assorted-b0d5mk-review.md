## The Math That Actually Matters

Let's start with the number that makes or breaks this product: **336 pencils**. That's 28 separate boxes of 12 assorted colors. For a teacher buying for a class of 25–30 students, that's roughly a full year's supply (plus replacements for the inevitable lost-and-never-found pencils). For a parent with multiple kids or a youth group leader, it's the "buy once, stop thinking about it" option.

I've tested bulk pencil packs where "336" meant 28 tiny pencils that snapped on first sharpening. That's not the case here. The pencils measure a standard 7 inches, and the cores are thick enough to handle normal elementary-school pressure. As one verified buyer put it: *"I was honestly skeptical at this price point, but these have held up through a full semester of kindergarten art class. Only two broken points so far."*

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains 28 individual boxes, each with 12 pre-sharpened pencils. That's a thoughtful detail — you can hand out full boxes to students or keep them in a central bin without re-sorting. The color range covers the basics: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink, brown, black, and a few variations in between.

Here's the honest catch: **color distribution is not equal.** Some hues (typically red and blue) show up in abundance — you might get 30 of one color and 15 of another across the full set. This is common in budget bulk packs, but it's worth knowing if your classroom project requires specific color counts.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Falters

On standard copy paper, the color payoff is genuinely impressive for the price. The cores are soft enough to lay down saturated color with a few strokes, but firm enough not to crumble. Layering works — you can build up shading on construction paper without the waxy buildup you get from dollar-store brands.

Sharpening is where this product earns its 4.6 rating. The wood is soft enough that a standard handheld sharpener works fine, but the cores don't shatter mid-sharpen — a common failure in budget pencils. One teacher noted: *"I've sharpened about 200 of these with my classroom sharpener. Not a single snapped core. That's a record."*

**The downsides are real, though.**

The attached erasers are decorative at best. They smear pigment rather than lifting it cleanly — expect the gray smudge look if kids try to fix mistakes. And the barrels, while standard-sized, are thin. A kid who presses hard will eventually crack one. It's not rampant, but it happens.
