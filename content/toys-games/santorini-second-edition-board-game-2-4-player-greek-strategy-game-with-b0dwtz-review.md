Let me be direct: **Santorini: Second Edition** is one of the most visually arresting board games you'll ever put on a table, and it backs up that beauty with some of the tightest 2-player strategy in the hobby. But it's not without compromises — and the 4-player mode is where those compromises bite hardest.

## What's in the box

You get the iconic 3D island board — a hex-shaped two-tiered platform that looks like a miniature of the actual Greek island. The white-and-blue buildings stack in three levels, topped with dome roofs. Two sets of colored workers (the "builders"), a rulebook, and a stack of god power cards.

As shown in the product image, the production quality is exceptional. The buildings are chunky, the board is solid, and the color palette pops. This is a game that draws a crowd at game night before you've even explained the rules.

## How it plays

The core rules are deceptively simple: on your turn, move one worker one space, then build one level on an adjacent space. Win by getting a worker to the third level. That's it. The elegance is that every god card you draft at the start breaks one of those rules in a different way — some let you build twice, others move opponents' workers, a few win by losing.

The Second Edition's big improvement over the original is the addition of a 4-player team mode, where two players share control of one color, and the god powers get combined. That's where things get interesting — and occasionally frustrating.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews is well-earned. The common thread in customer feedback is the sheer replayability. One reviewer put it well: *"Played 15 games in the first week. Every god combo feels like a different game."* Another noted: *"My 8-year-old learned it in 10 minutes, and my hardcore gamer friends still lose to me."*

The accessibility-to-depth ratio is genuinely remarkable. A non-gamer can grasp the rules in under five minutes, but the god card drafting adds a layer of strategic depth that keeps experienced players engaged for dozens of sessions.

## Where it falls short

I'm going to be honest about the 4-player mode: it's the weakest part of this package. With four workers per side on the standard board, the space gets congested, and shared turns mean the more assertive player tends to dominate decisions. If you're buying this primarily for 4-player game nights, you'll be disappointed. It's a 2-player game with a 4-player afterthought.

The god cards also feel stingy. The base game includes just enough for 2 players to draft from a small pool. The full god deck is locked behind the Golden Fleece expansion — a $20+ add-on that arguably should have been in the box for this price point.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:**
- You're a couple looking for a head-to-head strategy game that plays in 20 minutes
- You want a game that non-gamers can learn instantly but that rewards repeated play
- You care about table presence — this is a centerpiece game

**Skip it if:**
- Your group is consistently 4+ players — get something like *Azul* or *Kingdomino* instead
- You hate games where the luck of the draft can swing the outcome
- You're on a tight budget — the expansions are almost mandatory for long-term variety

## FAQ

**Q: Is it suitable for kids?**
A: Absolutely, but with a caveat. Children 8+ can grasp the base rules quickly. However, the god cards add reading comprehension and abstract thinking, so younger kids may struggle with the advanced powers.

**Q: How long does a typical game last?**
A: 15-25 minutes for 2 players, 25-35 for 4-player teams. The game is designed for quick sessions, which is perfect for "one more round" syndrome.

**Q: Does the Second Edition fix the issues with the first edition?**
A: The artwork and components are improved, and the 4-player mode is new. But the core mechanics are identical — if you own the first edition, there's no compelling reason to upgrade unless you want the team mode.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

Santorini: Second Edition is a must-have for anyone who wants a beautiful, fast, deeply strategic 2-player game that bridges the gap between casual and serious gamers. The 4-player mode is a genuine letdown, and the stingy god card selection pushes you toward the expansion. But for its core purpose — a gorgeous, endlessly replayable head-to-head duel — it's near-perfect.

**Buy it if** you want a 2-player game that will stay on your rotation for years. **Skip it if** you're shopping primarily for group play — this isn't that game. At its heart, Santorini is a game for two, and within that lane, it's exceptional.
