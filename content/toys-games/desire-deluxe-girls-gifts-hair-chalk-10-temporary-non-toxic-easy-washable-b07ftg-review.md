If you've got a kid who's been begging for rainbow hair but you're not ready for the commitment (or the damage) of actual dye, temporary hair chalk is the classic compromise. The Desire Deluxe 10-piece set is one of the most popular options on Amazon with a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings. But is it actually worth your money, or just another toy that ends up in a drawer after one use? Here's the real breakdown.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get 10 full-size chalk sticks in a fairly broad spectrum: pink, purple, blue, green, orange, yellow, red, teal, and a couple of blend shades. Each stick is roughly the size of a thick crayon, so there's enough product for multiple applications — not just one party and done. The set comes in a simple plastic case that keeps the sticks from breaking during storage, which matters more than you'd think since chalk is fragile. There's no applicator brush or gloves included, so plan to use your fingers or grab a pair from the bathroom.

**How It Performs in Real Life**

Application is straightforward: dampen a section of hair, rub the chalk up and down the strand, and let it dry. The color deposits on the outside of the hair shaft rather than penetrating it, which is exactly why it's non-damaging. On light blonde or light brown hair, the payoff is genuinely impressive — one or two passes gives you a vivid, salon-adjacent streak. On darker hair, it's a different story. Brunettes and black-haired kids will need three or four passes to get anything close to the packaging photos, and even then, it reads more as a tint than a bold color.

The formula is non-toxic and paraben-free, which is the main selling point for parents. It doesn't stain skin badly — a little soap takes care of any residue on the scalp or forehead. The washout claim holds up: one shampoo with regular kid's shampoo removes nearly all of it, though very saturated sections might need a second wash. That's honestly a relief because nobody wants to send their kid to school on Monday with a green streak they can't explain.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Reading through the ratings, the dominant theme is that this set delivers exactly what it promises. One verified buyer wrote, "My daughter used this for her school's spirit week and it was so easy to apply. We did purple and pink streaks and it washed out completely after one shower." Another parent noted, "Great value for the price — we've used it for three birthday parties and still have plenty left." The variety of colors gets repeated shout-outs because most competing sets only include four or five shades.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest complaint is the dry, chalky texture. Some kids find it uncomfortable after a couple of hours, and if your child has sensitive skin, the friction of application can feel scratchy. The chalk also transfers onto clothing and pillowcases if you don't let it fully dry before the kid runs off. And as mentioned, dark hair requires serious effort for visible results — one reviewer with a black-haired daughter said, "We used the whole purple stick on one section and it still looked brown from a distance."

**Who Should Buy This**

This is a great buy for parents of kids aged 6 to 12 with light-to-medium hair who want a low-commitment way to experiment with color. It's also a solid option for party favors, sleepover activities, or school spirit days. Skip it if your child has very dark or thick hair and expects bold, dramatic results, or if you're looking for something that lasts beyond a single day — this is temporary in every sense of the word.

**FAQ**

*Does it work on black or very dark hair?* Technically yes, but the color is subtle — think shimmer rather than saturation. You'll need multiple applications and should choose darker shades like purple or blue over pastels.

*How do you apply it without making a mess?* Dampen the hair, hold a paper towel underneath the section you're coloring, and rub the chalk downward only. Let it dry completely before brushing or styling.

*Is it safe for toddlers?* The formula is non-toxic, but the choking hazard from the sticks and the need for adult supervision makes it better suited for kids 5 and up.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want an affordable, mess-tolerant way to let your kid experiment with hair color for special occasions, and your child has lighter hair that will show the colors well.

**Skip it if:** Your kid has dark, thick hair and expects dramatic results, or you're hoping for something that lasts more than one day.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It does exactly what it claims, with minor drawbacks around dark hair performance and texture. For the price and the 10-color variety, it's a solid value that most families will get real use out of.
