If your living room has become a makeshift obstacle course anyway, the TODIBY 6-Piece Climbing Set might save your furniture from becoming a toddler's personal Everest. At $89.97, this foam playset sits in the middle of the market — cheaper than the big-name wooden climbing triangles but pricier than basic foam blocks. The question is whether the build justifies the cost. After digging through 225 ratings and the construction details, here's what actually matters.

**What's in the Box**

You get six pieces: a sloped ramp, a wedge, two rectangular blocks, and two smaller square blocks. The set includes a slide configuration and a step configuration, so it adapts as your child grows from crawling to confident climbing. The foam is dense — around 1.8 inches thick on the blocks — and wrapped in soft PU leather that feels more premium than the standard vinyl covers on cheaper sets. As shown in the product image, the neutral gray and beige tones blend into most playrooms without screaming "kiddie gym."

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The high-density foam is the standout feature. Multiple reviewers note it holds up to repeated jumping and throwing without developing permanent dents. One parent mentioned their 2-year-old uses the ramp as a slide and a drum simultaneously, and the foam hasn't lost its shape after three months. The PU leather is equally durable — it resists tears better than fabric covers, and a quick wipe handles most messes.

However, there's a catch worth knowing before you buy. The base has no non-slip grip. On hardwood or tile, the pieces slide when kids climb aggressively. Several reviewers flagged this, with one writing, "My son pushed the ramp across the room while trying to climb it. We had to put a rug underneath." That's a fixable issue, but it's an extra step you shouldn't need at this price point.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The configuration versatility gets the most love. Parents appreciate that the six pieces can create a slide, a tunnel, stepping stones, or a small fort. One reviewer said, "My daughter rearranges these every day — it's like a puzzle and a gym in one." The weight is also a plus: each piece is light enough for a toddler to move independently, which builds gross motor skills during setup, not just play.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the sliding issue, the non-removable cover is the biggest annoyance. You can't toss it in the washing machine. Spot-cleaning works for surface messes, but if your toddler has a diaper blowout or a major food incident, you'll be scrubbing crevices with a cloth for a while. The cover is also not replaceable, so if it gets damaged, the whole piece is compromised.

**Who Should Buy This**

This set is ideal for parents of 6-month to 3-year-olds who want a safe, indoor climbing option that doesn't require assembly or wall mounting. It's especially good for apartment dwellers — no tools, no permanent installation, and it packs flat for storage. If you have a climber who's outgrown baby gyms but isn't ready for a full wooden climbing frame, this is a solid middle ground.

Skip it if you have a super-rough 4-year-old who needs serious structural challenge, or if you have only hard floors and don't want to add a rug. Also skip if you expect machine-washable covers — this isn't that product.

**FAQ**

*Is the foam safe for babies who still put things in their mouths?*
The PU leather is non-toxic and phthalate-free, but the pieces are large and not intended as teething toys. Supervise younger babies, and keep the small square blocks away from kids under 12 months.

*How much floor space does the full set need?*
In the longest slide configuration, it takes about 5 feet by 3 feet. You can also break it into smaller clusters for tighter spaces.

*Does it hold up to outdoor use?*
The PU leather is water-resistant but not waterproof. Keep it indoors; prolonged sun exposure can fade the color and degrade the foam.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, versatile climbing set that grows with your toddler and won't fall apart after three months of hard play. The foam quality alone justifies the price.

**Skip it if** you have slick floors without rugs, or if you need washable covers for a messy kid.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-built set with one notable design flaw (the non-slip base issue) that's easily solved with a rug or grippy mat underneath.
