## The $250 Question: Is the MGEX Strike Freedom Worth It?

When Bandai announced the MGEX (Master Grade Extreme) line, they promised something beyond a standard Master Grade. The Strike Freedom Gundam M is that promise realized — a 1/100 scale kit that carries a premium price tag and demands your attention. With 240 Amazon ratings averaging 4.8 stars, buyers are overwhelmingly satisfied. But at this price point, you deserve specifics before committing.

## What's Actually in the Box

This isn't your typical runner-loaded Master Grade. The MGEX Strike Freedom ships with over 400 pieces, but the crown jewel is the **pre-assembled inner frame** — a fully articulated skeleton with gold electroplating that catches light beautifully. You also get:

- 8 beam sabers and effect parts
- Two beam rifles that combine into one massive weapon
- The iconic Dragoon system with 8 detachable pods
- A display base with articulated arm for flight poses
- The massive "wings of light" effect parts — translucent pink panels that stretch nearly 14 inches wide

As shown in the product image, the kit's presence on a shelf is genuinely imposing. The gold frame isn't just painted — it's electroplated, giving it a mirror-like finish that standard kits can't match.

## Build Experience: Patience Required, Beauty Rewarded

The MGEX frame is the standout engineering achievement here. It's pre-built with a full articulation range — the waist rotates, the wings spread, the Dragoons detach individually. You're essentially assembling armor plates around a premium skeleton, which means the build is more about precision fitting than complex mechanical assembly.

That said, this is not a weekend-warrior kit. Expect **4-6 hours** of focused work. The nubs are the real challenge: the gold plating chips if you cut too close with standard nippers. Use a single-blade nipper and sand carefully. Several Amazon reviewers noted this exact issue — one wrote, "I ruined two armor pieces before I switched to glass files. Buy those first."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The articulation is the headline. The MGEX frame allows poses that would stress cheaper kits to breaking point. The wings fan out with satisfying clicks, and the Dragoons stay locked in place during dramatic action poses.

Display value is the other major win. One reviewer captured it well: "It's the centerpiece of my collection. Every guest asks about it." The gold frame, combined with the translucent effect parts, creates a visual depth that photos don't fully capture.

## Where It Falls Short

**The effect parts are genuinely fragile.** The wings of light are thin, brittle plastic that can snap if you force a pose. Several buyers reported breakage during assembly or repositioning. Handle them like glass.

**The price is steep.** At roughly $250, this is 3-4x the cost of a standard Master Grade. You're paying for the plated frame and engineering, but if you're a casual builder, that money might be better spent on multiple kits.

**Nub management is unforgiving.** The plating means mistakes are visible. This isn't a kit for beginners — it demands precision tools and patience.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a seasoned Gunpla builder who wants a showpiece with premium engineering. You appreciate articulated frames and don't mind spending hours on careful nub cleanup. You have a display space that does this justice.

**Skip it if:** You're new to the hobby, on a budget, or prefer kits you can complete in one sitting. The standard Master Grade Strike Freedom offers 80% of the visual impact at a third of the cost.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Ask

**Q: Does it require painting or glue?**
A: No. The frame is pre-colored and pre-assembled. Out of the box, it looks exceptional. Panel lining with a fine-tip marker is optional but adds depth.

**Q: How tall is the finished model?**
A: Approximately 15 inches with the wings fully extended. Without wings, about 7 inches tall — true to 1/100 scale.

**Q: Is the gold frame actually metal?**
A: No, it's ABS plastic with electroplated gold finish. It looks metallic but is still plastic — don't expect die-cast weight.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The MGEX Strike Freedom is a masterpiece of engineering with a genuinely jaw-dropping final display. It's the best-looking kit in its price range, and the pre-built frame eliminates the most tedious part of Gunpla assembly. But the fragile effect parts and unforgiving finish mean it's not for everyone. If you're ready to invest the time and money, this is a centerpiece-grade kit that will dominate your shelf for years.

If you're skilled, patient, and want the best Strike Freedom ever made — this is it.
