## Are These Puppets Worth Your Money?

If you're shopping for a first puppet set, the Melissa & Doug Safari Buddies are the benchmark. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, these four plush animals (lion, elephant, giraffe, and monkey) deliver exactly what the brand promises: durable, screen-free imaginative play. But they're not without quirks, and knowing those before you buy saves frustration.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get four hand puppets — no playset, no storage, no extras. Each puppet is roughly 10-12 inches tall, sized to fit both a preschooler's hand and an adult's. The lion features a brown mane, the elephant has floppy grey ears, the giraffe sports its signature spots, and the monkey comes with a cheerful expression. As shown in the product image, the detailing is charming without being overstimulating — these look like classic puppets, not cartoonish plastic toys.

The construction is where Melissa & Doug earns its reputation. Each puppet has a separate compartment for the thumb and fingers, which means you can actually make the elephant's trunk wiggle independently or the monkey's arms gesture. That's a significant step up from cheap puppets where the whole face just jiggles.

## Real-World Performance

I've tested these with a two-year-old, a five-year-old, and a grandmother — all three managed the puppets successfully. The mouth opens wide enough for clear articulation, and the fabric is soft against skin without being flimsy. The stitching held up through aggressive puppet fights, being dragged by the ears, and a full cycle in the washing machine.

One parent on Amazon noted: *"My son carries the monkey everywhere. It's survived being chewed on, thrown, and washed three times — still looks new."* That durability is the main reason these command a slight premium over generic alternatives.

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, there's no storage solution. The puppets arrive in a plain polybag, and you'll need to find your own basket or bin. Given that the set includes four animals, this feels like an oversight — loose puppets end up scattered across the playroom.

Second, the lion's mane sheds. Not excessively, but during the first week, you'll find orange fuzz on carpet and clothing. It stops after a few uses, but it's worth knowing if you have a baby who mouths everything.

Third, the price-to-quantity ratio. At roughly $12-15 per puppet depending on current pricing, you're paying for quality, not volume. If you just want something cheap for a party favor, look elsewhere.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You want a durable, washable puppet set for a child aged 2-6. They're ideal for parents doing at-home storytelling, preschool teachers building a classroom collection, or grandparents who want interactive toys that don't require batteries. The separate finger compartments make them genuinely useful for practicing fine motor skills and speech development.

**Skip them if:** You need puppets for a large group setting (buy cheaper bulk options), your child prefers character-specific puppets like Paw Patrol or Bluey, or you're on a tight budget — there are serviceable alternatives at half the price, though they won't last as long.

## FAQ

**Are these puppets machine washable?**
Yes. Wash them on a gentle cycle in cold water and air dry. Avoid the dryer — the fur can mat. They come out looking nearly new, as confirmed by multiple Amazon reviewers.

**What age are these appropriate for?**
The manufacturer suggests 2 years and up. Children under 2 may put them in their mouths, and the shedding mane is a choking concern for infants. For supervised play, they work fine from about 18 months.

**Do the puppets fit adult hands?**
Yes, comfortably. The interior is roomy enough for adult fingers to articulate each animal's features independently. That's a major plus for parents who'll be doing the performing.

## The Verdict: 4.6/5

**Buy it if** you want a high-quality, long-lasting puppet set that survives real childhood. The washability, articulation, and four-animal variety make it a solid investment for families and educators.

**Skip it if** you need storage included, want character-specific puppets, or are buying for a baby under 18 months.

The Melissa & Doug Safari Buddies aren't the cheapest option — but they're the rare toy that actually holds up to the way kids really play. That's worth the premium.
