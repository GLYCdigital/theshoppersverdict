# Bobor Trampoline Water Sprinkler Review: The Cheapest Way to Turn Bouncing Into a Water Park

If you've got a trampoline in your backyard, you already know the summer drill: kids bounce for an hour, then collapse from heat. The Bobor Trampoline Water Sprinkler exists to solve exactly that problem — and for around $20, it does the job better than you'd expect. But it's not without a few frustrations worth knowing before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the sprinkler ring itself (a flexible PVC tube with 12 spray nozzles), a garden hose adapter, zip ties, and Velcro straps. No tools required. The ring is designed to wrap around the outside of the trampoline frame and secure with the included fasteners. It fits round frames from 8 to 14 feet and rectangular ones too — the tube flexes and stretches to accommodate.

As shown in the product image, the ring sits on top of the frame pads, spraying inward across the jumping mat. The whole setup takes about five minutes. If you've ever assembled a trampoline, this feels refreshingly simple.

## Real-World Performance

The concept is straightforward: connect a garden hose, turn on the water, and the 12 nozzles create a dome of spray that covers the entire jumping surface. On a standard 12-foot trampoline, the coverage is genuinely impressive — kids get wet from every angle, not just one weak stream.

The build quality is decent for the price. The PVC tubing is flexible enough to survive being stepped on and thick enough to resist punctures from normal use. The brass hose adapter is a nice touch — it won't crack like the cheap plastic ones on competing products.

One thing that surprised me: the water pressure requirement. This isn't a "light mist" product. It needs a solid 40-50 PSI to get the full spray effect. If you have low water pressure, the nozzles produce a weak dribble rather than a satisfying spray. Several buyers echoed this, with one noting, "Works great with full pressure, but if your hose has any kinks, the spray gets spotty."

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The 4.1-star rating across 204 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction from most buyers. The consistent praise centers on three things:

**Installation ease.** Multiple reviewers mention it took under 10 minutes with zero frustration. One parent wrote, "I'm not handy at all and had this on in five minutes. The zip ties hold it perfectly."

**The "wow" factor for kids.** This is the product's core value. Parents describe kids who normally abandon the trampoline after 20 minutes staying out for hours. One review captured it well: "My three kids haven't stopped bouncing since this arrived. It's the best $20 I've spent on backyard fun."

**Durability across a season.** Several reviewers report using it for full summers without leaks or cracks, even with daily use.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint across reviews is the **hose management problem**. The sprinkler requires a garden hose connected at all times, which means a heavy hose dragging across the yard and constantly pulling on the frame. If your spigot is more than 20 feet away, you'll notice the tension. Some buyers solved this with a lightweight hose or by using a quick-connect system, but it's an annoyance you should plan for.

The **lack of an inline shut-off valve** is a close second. To stop the water, you must walk back to the spigot. If you're supervising from a lawn chair, that's a minor inconvenience. If you're trying to control water usage while kids come and go, it gets tedious.

Finally, **the spray can make the jumping mat slippery**. This isn't a defect — it's physics. But on a hot day, wet socks on a vinyl mat can lead to slips. Most kids adjust quickly, but it's worth noting for younger jumpers.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a trampoline, kids who love water, and a garden hose within 25 feet of the frame. It's a fantastic value for turning an existing investment into a summer attraction.

**Skip it if:** You have low water pressure, a trampoline larger than 14 feet (coverage gets thin), or you're looking for something that works without a continuous hose connection.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my trampoline?**
A: It fits round frames from 8 to 14 feet and most rectangular models. The tube stretches, and the zip ties accommodate varying frame thicknesses. Measure your frame diameter before ordering to be safe.

**Q: Does it need a special hose connection?**
A: No — it uses a standard garden hose thread. The included brass adapter connects directly. If you have a quick-connect system, you'll need an additional adapter.

**Q: Can I leave it attached during winter?**
A: You can, but you shouldn't. PVC tubing will become brittle in freezing temperatures. Drain it fully and store it indoors during the off-season to extend its lifespan.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

The Bobor Trampoline Water Sprinkler does exactly what it promises, and it does it well. For the price, it transforms an ordinary trampoline into the most popular spot in the neighborhood. The hose management and pressure requirements are real limitations, but they're predictable and manageable.

**Buy it if** you want maximum summer fun for minimal investment. **Skip it if** you have weak water pressure or aren't willing to deal with the hose connection. For everyone else, this is a no-brainer add-on that pays for itself in the first afternoon of use.
