Let’s be blunt: most Funko Pop! releases are the same mold with a new paint job. The Last Ronin Michelangelo isn’t that. This one actually earns its shelf space.

Based on the 2020 IDW comic series where Michelangelo is the last surviving turtle, this Pop! captures the character’s weary, battle-worn look. The sculpt is notably more detailed than your average Pop — the cracked shell, the tattered bandana, and the worn nunchucks all show real attention to the source material.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the standard single figure — roughly 4 inches tall, vinyl construction, weighing about 4 ounces. Nothing extra in the box, no sticker or limited-edition numbering. But the box itself is worth noting: sturdy cardboard, a clear front window that shows off the figure well, and the artwork on the side references the comic’s iconic cover. In-box collectors will appreciate that it ships in a protective sleeve.

**Build Quality and Paint**

This is where the Last Ronin Michelangelo separates itself from the pack. The paint application on the shell has a weathered, almost battle-scarred look that reads well even at arm’s length. The bandana is a muted orange-red — not the bright cartoon orange of classic Mikey — which fits the darker tone of the comic. The eyes have that tired, narrowed expression from the source material.

The vinyl is solid with no warping or off-gassing smell. The figure stands stable on its base without leaning — something that can’t be said for every Pop. The nunchucks are molded separately and attached securely; no flimsy parts that feel like they’ll snap off in shipping.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The Amazon reviews at 4.7 stars out of 235 ratings tell a consistent story. One buyer noted, "Finally a Funko that actually looks like the character, not just a generic turtle with different colored bandana." Another said, "The detail on the shell alone is worth the price. It's clear they actually read the comic."

Collectors also frequently mention the box quality — minimal crushing or damage in transit, which is a real concern with Funko shipments. The packaging department clearly got the memo.

**Where It Falls Short**

The head is noticeably larger than the body, even by Funko standards. It’s not a dealbreaker, but from certain angles it reads as more caricature than the comic’s serious tone. If you’re a stickler for proportions, you’ll want to display it at eye level or slightly above.

There’s also no chase variant or glow-in-the-dark version. Completionists who want every Last Ronin iteration will have to wait — this is the only one currently available.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **TMNT comic readers** — If you’ve read The Last Ronin, this is a faithful representation you’ll appreciate.
- **Funko collectors** — The detail level is above average for the line, making it a worthwhile addition.
- **Gift buyers** — It’s $12–$15, a safe bet for any TMNT fan, and the box is giftable as-is.

**Skip it if** you’re a strictly vintage cartoon TMNT fan — this is the gritty, post-apocalyptic version, not the pizza-loving jokester.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this fit with the original TMNT Funko Pops?**
Yes, it’s the same scale (about 4 inches) and will sit fine alongside the classic releases. The style is grittier, but the size matches.

**Q: Is the box collector-grade?**
Most buyers report mint condition arrival. The box has a clear window and sturdy cardstock. No damage reported in the first 235 ratings.

**Q: Will there be other Last Ronin turtles?**
Funko hasn’t announced them yet, but given the comic’s popularity, it’s likely. This Michelangelo is a solid start to a potential set.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a detailed, faithful representation of one of the best TMNT storylines. **Skip it if** you only collect cartoon-accurate figures or need a chase variant to feel complete.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely well-executed Pop that respects its source material. The oversized head is the only real flaw, and it’s minor. For the price, this is one of the better Funko releases this year.
