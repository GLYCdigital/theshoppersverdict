Let me cut through the hype: these are not the "all-day smart glasses" Meta's marketing suggests. They're a fantastic first-gen product with real compromises — and you need to know what those are before dropping serious money.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the Headliner frames in Shiny Caramel, a charging case that looks like a leather glasses pouch, a USB-C cable, and cleaning cloth. The case is smart: it holds about 8 extra charges and gives you roughly 32 total hours before needing a wall outlet. The glasses themselves weigh 48 grams — about the same as a standard pair of Ray-Ban Wayfarers. That's the magic trick here. As shown in the product image, the camera module sits on the right temple, subtle enough that most people won't clock you're wearing a computer.

**Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Real**

The open-ear speakers are the standout feature. They use directional audio that beams sound toward your ears without blocking ambient noise. Music sounds genuinely good — punchy bass, clear mids — and phone calls are crisp on both ends. I wore these on a noisy city street and could hear my podcast clearly; the person on the other end of a call couldn't tell I was outside.

The 12MP camera is a legitimate point-and-shoot replacement for quick moments. The 1080p video is stabilized well enough for casual POV clips. One-tap capture from the temple button is responsive — about a half-second from press to photo. The wide-angle lens captures a slightly wider view than your natural vision, which takes getting used to but proves useful for capturing what you actually see.

Here's where it gets sticky. Battery life is 4 hours of continuous use — but that's with active camera and AI features. If you're just listening to audio, you'll get closer to 6. Real-world usage means charging every night, and forgetting the case when you leave home means dead glasses by 3 PM.

**What Buyers Actually Say**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews reflects genuine enthusiasm tempered by real frustrations. One reviewer puts it well: "I was skeptical about the AI features, but the audio quality alone makes these worth it. The camera is a bonus." Another notes the practical reality: "Battery is the weak point. I charge them every night without fail — it's become part of my routine."

The biggest complaints cluster around Meta AI's regional availability. Buyers in non-supported countries report the AI features either don't activate or require workarounds. That's not a hardware flaw — it's a licensing limitation. Check Meta's support page for your region before buying.

**Where It Falls Short**

The Shiny Caramel finish is beautiful in person but a fingerprint magnet. You'll be wiping these down constantly. The touch controls on the temple can be finicky — accidental triggers when adjusting the glasses happen more often than they should. And while the camera is good, it's not great in low light; indoor shots get grainy quickly.

**Who Should Buy These**

This is for the early adopter who wants the camera and audio without looking like a cyborg. Content creators who need quick POV shots, commuters who want open-ear audio for situational awareness, and Ray-Ban loyalists who want smart features in a familiar frame. Skip them if you want an all-day wearable with robust battery, or if AI features are your primary reason — those are still in early days.

**FAQ**

**Can I use these with non-Meta phones?**
Yes, they work with any modern smartphone via the Meta View app — Android and iOS both supported.

**Do they have prescription lens options?**
Yes, you can order with prescription lenses from Ray-Ban directly, though it adds cost and shipping time.

**Are they water-resistant?**
They have an IPX4 rating — sweat and light rain are fine, but don't swim or shower with them.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want the best-looking smart glasses available, prioritize audio and camera over AI gimmicks, and can handle daily charging.

**Skip it if:** You need all-day battery, live in a non-supported region for AI features, or want a product that's fully mature.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Gen 1 limitations are real, but the core experience is genuinely good. These are the first smart glasses that feel like normal glasses first and tech second.
