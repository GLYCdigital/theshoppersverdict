**Is the LEGO Wicked Dormitory worth adding to your cart, or is it just a cash-grab tie-in?**

Let's be blunt: LEGO sets based on movies are hit-or-miss. Some are lazy repaints of existing themes. Others — like this Glinda & Elphaba's Dormitory set — actually earn their shelf space. With a 4.9-star average across 245 ratings, this one's clearly resonating with buyers. But let's dig into whether it's right for *you*.

**What's in the box**

You get 739 pieces, two minifigures (Glinda and Elphaba), and a two-story dormitory that splits open for play access. The set measures roughly 10 inches tall, 11 inches wide, and 3 inches deep when closed — compact enough for a shelf but substantial enough to feel like a real build. The box includes a 150-page instruction booklet with no stickers — everything is printed, which is a nice touch for a set this size.

**Build quality and the clever stuff**

The build takes about 1.5 to 2 hours at a relaxed pace. The dorm features two distinctly styled beds — Glinda's is pink and frilly, Elphaba's is darker with a green accent. Each has a desk, and there's a shared vanity with a mirror. The printed tiles on the desks (books, potion bottles, a letter) add depth without sticker alignment frustration.

The standout feature is the "Defying Gravity" lever on the back of the set. Flick it, and Elphaba's bed rises several inches — a mechanical nod to the show's most iconic moment. It's a clever engineering touch that elevates this from a static display piece to something with actual play value. However, the lever requires a firm push. One reviewer noted their 7-year-old "needed help triggering it every time" — so factor that in for younger builders.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The minifigures are the real stars. Glinda's crown and Elphaba's braided hairpiece are brand-new molds, and their printed faces capture the actors' expressions remarkably well. As one five-star review put it: "The minifigs alone are worth it — Elphaba's face print is the best I've seen in years." Another buyer highlighted the "genuinely thoughtful interior details," specifically the vanity mirror and the bookshelf with tiny printed spines.

**Where it falls short**

The price-per-piece ratio is a legitimate concern. At typical retail, you're paying roughly 10 cents per brick — above average for LEGO. You're absorbing the Wicked license cost, and that's a real tradeoff. Additionally, there's no Elphaba hat or Grimmerie book accessory in this set; those are exclusive to the larger Shiz University set (which costs significantly more). If you're a completionist, you'll end up buying multiple sets.

**Who should buy this**

- **Wicked fans** who want a display piece that nods to the story without committing to the $100+ castle set
- **LEGO collectors** chasing exclusive minifigures — these two are likely to appreciate in value
- **Parents of kids 8+** who want a build that's challenging but not overwhelming, with meaningful play features
- **Gift-givers** looking for something more distinctive than a generic licensed set

**Skip it if** you're purely a brick-builder who cares about piece count, or if the recipient is under 7 — the small accessories (potions, books) are choking hazards.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this set connect to other LEGO Wicked sets?**
A: Yes, it pairs with the Shiz University set (75681) to form a larger dorm scene, but it also works fine standalone.

**Q: Are the minifigures exclusive to this set?**
A: Yes — these specific Glinda and Elphaba versions with their unique hairpieces appear only in this set.

**Q: How long does assembly take?**
A: Expect 1.5–2 hours. The bags are numbered 1–4, and the build is logical without repetitive sections.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a high-quality, movie-accurate Wicked set with excellent minifigs and a genuinely fun play feature. **Skip it if** you're price-sensitive or need more brick value per dollar.

This is a 4.6-star product: the minifigs are top-tier, the design is thoughtful, and the lever mechanism adds real charm. The price-per-piece and the missing hat accessory knock it down slightly, but for the target audience — Wicked fans and LEGO collectors — it's one of the best licensed sets of the year.
