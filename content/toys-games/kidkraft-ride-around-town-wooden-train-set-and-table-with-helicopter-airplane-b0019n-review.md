If your living room floor has become a graveyard of flimsy plastic train tracks that snap the moment a toddler applies weight, the KidKraft Ride Around Town set is the upgrade you've been circling. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this wooden train table is one of the most consistently praised toys in its category — but it's not without quirks. Here's what you need to know before you commit the floor space.

**What You Actually Get**

The box delivers a 17-inch-high wooden activity table with a printed cityscape surface, plus a 100+ piece set: wooden train tracks (including bridge pieces and a figure-eight layout), two trains with magnets, a helicopter, an airplane, trees, traffic signs, buildings, and wooden people figures. The table has a built-in storage drawer underneath — a genuinely useful feature that keeps 100 tiny pieces from becoming a vacuum cleaner's snack.

The construction is MDF wood with a smooth, painted finish. As shown in the product image, the city-themed track surface has roads, a helipad, and a train station printed directly onto the tabletop, so kids can run vehicles beyond just the rails.

**Real-World Performance**

The tracks are compatible with standard wooden railway systems (think Brio or Melissa & Doug), which matters if you already own pieces. The wooden trains connect with magnets and roll smoothly on the grooved track. The bridge adds vertical play — kids love sending trains underneath while cars roll on top.

The table height is designed for toddlers to preschoolers — roughly ages 3 to 6. At 17 inches, it's comfortable for a child sitting or kneeling on the floor. That's the sweet spot for the intended age range, but a 6-year-old will likely be too tall to use it comfortably standing.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The recurring themes in positive reviews are durability and play value. One parent noted, *"My son has had this for two years and it still looks brand new. The pieces are thick and don't bend or break like the plastic sets we had before."* Multiple reviewers mention that their kids play with it daily for extended periods — the open-ended nature of the city layout encourages creative scenarios rather than just pushing trains in circles.

The storage drawer gets frequent shoutouts. *"I love that I can just toss everything in the drawer at the end of the day. It keeps the pieces contained and the room clean,"* one reviewer wrote.

**Where It Falls Short**

Assembly is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. The track pieces must be fitted into the pre-drilled holes on the tabletop in the exact right configuration, or the trains will derail at the joints. Plan for 45–60 minutes with a screwdriver. The instructions are adequate but not great — some reviewers reported needing to redo sections.

The trains are manual push-only. There's no battery-powered engine, so if your child expects Thomas the Tank Engine to move on his own, this won't deliver that. You can buy a compatible motorized engine separately, but it's an added cost.

Finally, the fixed 17-inch height means this has a finite lifespan. Once your child hits 5 or 6, they'll likely prefer floor-level play or a taller table.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Parents of toddlers (2–4)** who want a durable, contained train setup that survives daily use
- **Families with existing wooden train pieces** looking for a table to consolidate them
- **Gift-givers** who want a single, impressive box that delivers real play value for years
- **Skip it if** your child is over 6, hates manual trains, or you're not prepared for an hour of assembly

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this compatible with Brio or Thomas wooden tracks?**
A: Yes. The grooves match standard wooden railway dimensions, so most major brands' tracks and vehicles work. The KidKraft trains themselves are slightly lighter than Brio, but they roll fine on both systems.

**Q: How many trains come with it?**
A: Two wooden trains with magnetic couplers, plus a wooden helicopter and airplane with rolling wheels. The trains connect to each other and to compatible wooden cars from other brands.

**Q: Does the table require tools to assemble?**
A: Yes. You'll need a Phillips-head screwdriver. The track pieces press into pre-drilled holes, but the table legs and drawer require screws. Most reviewers report 45–60 minutes total.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, long-lasting train table that keeps pieces contained and delivers real open-ended play for toddlers and preschoolers. The 4.6-star rating is earned — this is a well-built toy that holds up.

**Skip it if** you need battery-powered trains, your child is already 6+, or you have zero patience for assembly. For everyone else, this is one of the best wooden train tables at this price point.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — minus half a star for the fiddly assembly and the manual-only trains. Everything else is solid.
