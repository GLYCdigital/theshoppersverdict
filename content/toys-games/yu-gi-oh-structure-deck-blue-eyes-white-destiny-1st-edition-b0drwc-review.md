**Bottom line:** This is the best Structure Deck Konami has released in years — but it's not a complete competitive deck out of the box. Here's what you actually need to know.

---

## What's in the box

You get a 50-card main deck, a glossy rulebook, a double-sided playmat, and a collector's card. The highlight is the foil Ultra Rare Blue-Eyes White Dragon — it's the new artwork, and it's genuinely good. The deck includes three copies each of the key new cards: **Maiden of White**, **True Light**, and **Ultimate Fusion**. That's the core engine, and having playsets of these immediately is the real value here.

## How it plays

The deck's game plan is straightforward: get Blue-Eyes White Dragon onto the field, use **Maiden of White** to generate advantage, and push for game with **Ultimate Fusion** into Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon or Neo Ultimate Dragon. The **True Light** continuous trap gives you recursion and a board presence that's surprisingly sticky.

What impressed me most is the **Primite** engine inclusion. The Level 4 **Primite Dragon** monsters let you search your normal monsters directly, which means you're not relying on top-decking Blue-Eyes. That's a meaningful consistency boost that older Blue-Eyes builds lacked.

## Where it falls short

Here's the honest part. **The deck ships with zero Extra Deck monsters.** That's a significant omission. You'll need to pick up **Blue-Eyes Spirit Dragon**, **Blue-Eyes Jet Dragon**, and the new **Blue-Eyes Ultimate Fusion** targets separately — that's another $20-$40 depending on market prices.

The deck also loses hard to **Nibiru** if you don't open interaction. There's no built-in negate for the standard combo line, and you'll feel it against meta decks like Tenpai or Snake-Eye. You're buying a foundation, not a finished product.

## Who should buy this

**Competitive players** — If you want to pilot Blue-Eyes at a regional or YCS level, this is the starting point. Buy three boxes and you'll have a solid core to build from.

**Returning players** — If you haven't played since 2015 and want a deck that's recognizable but actually functional, this is your entry point. The mechanics are straightforward, and the upgrade path is clear.

**Collectors** — The 1st Edition stamp and the new foil artwork will hold value. This is a good sealed-box investment candidate.

**Casual players** — You can play this out of the box against other Structure Decks, but you'll lose to anything more optimized. Budget for singles if you want to be competitive.

## FAQ

**Q: How many boxes should I buy?**
Three if you want maximum consistency — you'll get full playsets of every key card. One box works for casual play, but you'll feel the missing copies in competitive settings.

**Q: Is this deck viable at tournaments?**
Yes, with upgrades. The core is strong, but you'll need extra deck staples, hand traps, and potentially the Bystial package to compete at a high level.

**Q: Does it include the new Blue-Eyes fusion monsters?**
No. The Ultimate Fusion spell is included, but the fusion monsters themselves are not in the deck. You'll need to buy those separately.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want the best Blue-Eyes support ever printed at this price point, or if you're returning to the game and want a strong foundation to build toward competitive play.

**Skip it if** you're expecting a tournament-ready deck straight from the box — the missing Extra Deck and hand trap slots are real costs you'll need to absorb.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely excellent Structure Deck that's held back only by Konami's decision to leave out the Extra Deck. The core is exceptional, the value is solid, and this will be the definitive Blue-Eyes build for years.
