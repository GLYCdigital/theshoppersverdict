Let's be clear about one thing right away: the Step2 All Around Wagon is not a Radio Flyer. That's not a criticism — it's a distinction. This is a molded plastic wagon designed for toddlers and preschoolers, not a vintage steel hauler. And for that specific job, it does things a metal wagon simply can't.

**What's in the box**

You get the wagon body, two wheels with axles, the push handle, and the hardware bag. Assembly takes about 20 minutes with a Phillips head screwdriver. No tools beyond that. The seats come pre-installed, which saves the most fiddly part of the build. The instructions are picture-based and clear enough that you won't need to call tech support.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The body is double-walled injection-molded plastic — the same material Step2 uses in their playhouses and sandboxes. It's rigid, not flimsy. The 10-inch wheels are the standout feature here. They're larger than what you'll find on most plastic wagons, and that translates directly to how well it rolls over grass, packed dirt, and the cracks in your driveway. One parent put it this way: "This thing goes over terrain that would stop my old metal wagon cold."

The handle pushes rather than pulls, which is actually a design choice worth understanding. You push the wagon in front of you, keeping kids in sight. It locks into two positions and folds flat for storage. The push configuration means you're less likely to strain your back, though it does mean you can't easily pull it behind you on a hike.

Weight capacity is 100 pounds total. Two toddlers or one larger preschooler fit comfortably. The seats flip down to create a flat cargo bed when you're hauling mulch or groceries instead of kids.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The stability gets mentioned in nearly every positive review. The wide wheelbase and low center of gravity mean this thing doesn't tip on uneven ground. One reviewer noted: "My son tries to climb out and the wagon doesn't even wobble."

The cup holders are a small feature that parents genuinely appreciate. They're molded into the front edge, deep enough that a sippy cup stays put even on bumpy paths. The rear storage bin has a flip-down lid, protecting snacks from dirt. It's not weatherproof, but it keeps the day's essentials contained.

**Where it falls short**

The plastic scuffs. It's that simple. The red finish shows white hairline marks after a few months of regular use, especially if you're rolling over concrete or gravel. It's cosmetic, doesn't affect function, but it means the wagon won't stay showroom-clean.

There's no canopy. For the price, that's a legitimate gap. You'll need to add a stroller shade or plan walks around the sun's position. Several reviewers mentioned this as their main gripe.

The handle latch is stiff on a new unit. It loosens up within a week, but the first few folds require two hands and some patience.

**Who should buy this**

This wagon is for parents of kids aged 18 months to 5 years who want a durable, stable wagon for neighborhood walks, zoo trips, and park visits. It's also solid for grandparents who need something easy to push without the learning curve of a jogging stroller.

Skip it if you need a wagon primarily for cargo hauling, if you plan to pull it behind a bike, or if you live somewhere with extreme direct sun exposure and don't want to rig a shade.

**FAQ**

*Can it fit through standard doorways?* Yes — 24 inches wide at the wheels, which clears a standard 30-inch door frame.

*Is it safe for a 2-year-old?* The seatbelts are simple lap belts, not five-point harnesses. Fine for a calm toddler, but a determined escape artist might work the buckle loose.

*How does it handle a 1-mile walk?* The wheels roll smoothly, and the push handle keeps your posture upright. It's easier than pushing a stroller with a heavy load.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a stable, low-maintenance wagon that transitions from kid-hauler to garden cart without tools. **Skip it if** you need a canopy, expect showroom cosmetics after a year, or want a pull-behind wagon for bike use.

The Step2 All Around Wagon earns its 4.8-star rating. It's not perfect, but it's the right tool for the job most parents are actually doing: moving small children safely over imperfect terrain. At a typical price around $80–100, it's a fair value for three to four years of heavy use.
