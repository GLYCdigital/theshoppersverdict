Most ride-on toys for "big kids" are a joke — a 12V motor barely strong enough to move a 5-year-old on flat pavement. The Hikole 24V 2-Seater Electric Monster Truck is not that. This thing has actual torque, actual suspension, and a battery that doesn't quit after ten minutes. If your kids are aging out of plastic Power Wheels but aren't ready for a gas-powered go-kart, this is the sweet spot.

## What You Actually Get

The box arrives heavy — about 55 pounds of assembled vehicle plus parts. You're getting a two-seater monster truck with a 24V 10Ah battery, dual motors (one per rear wheel), LED headlights, MP3/Bluetooth audio, and a 2.4G parent remote. The tires are thick rubber with real tread, not the hollow plastic wheels you see on cheaper units. As shown in the product image, the body has legit monster truck styling — high ground clearance, chunky fenders, and a roll bar that looks like it belongs on something with a combustion engine.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where the Hikole separates itself. The 24V system delivers roughly double the power of standard 12V ride-ons. On flat pavement, it cruises at a claimed 7.5 mph top speed — realistic for a kid's vehicle, and genuinely fast enough to feel thrilling without being dangerous. More importantly, the dual motors pull uphill and through grass without bogging down. One parent described it as "the first ride-on my son didn't get stuck on the lawn."

The suspension is functional, not decorative. Coil springs on all four corners absorb sidewalk cracks and uneven terrain. The frame is steel, not flimsy plastic, and it holds up to the abuse two kids will throw at it. The 130-pound combined weight capacity means two average 7-10 year olds can ride together without strain.

The parent remote is the standout safety feature. It works from about 50 feet away, lets you kill the power instantly, and can steer the truck if your kid bails. For younger siblings or cautious parents, that's worth the price alone. The 2.4G connection is responsive — no lag between pressing the button and the vehicle responding.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews holds up. Parents repeatedly mention three things: the power, the two-seat capacity, and the remote. One review noted, "My 9-year-old and 7-year-old ride together every day. It handles our sloped backyard without any issues." Another parent appreciated that "the Bluetooth speakers are actually decent quality — my kids connect their tablets and it sounds fine."

Battery life with two kids on flat ground runs 60-70 minutes per charge, which is solid for the class. The 24V system charges fully in about 4-5 hours, so it's ready for an afternoon of use.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest complaint, and it's legitimate. The instructions are mediocre, the hardware isn't labeled clearly, and the included wrench strips bolts easily. Budget 45-60 minutes and bring your own tools. Some reviewers reported misaligned wheel hubs that required patience to seat properly.

The battery life drops to around 40-45 minutes when hauling two kids up hills or through thick grass. That's physics — the 24V motor draws more current under load — but it's worth knowing if you have a large property.

The MP3 player interface is clunky. Connecting Bluetooth is fine, but the built-in controls on the dashboard are small and awkward for kids to navigate. Most parents just pair their phone and let the kids use the remote.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for parents of kids aged 5-10 who have outgrown small ride-ons but aren't ready for anything with gas. It's especially good if you have two kids close in age who want to ride together — the two-seat configuration is rare at this price. It's also a solid pick if you have hilly terrain or grass, where cheaper 12V options fail.

Skip it if your kid is over 10 or over 65 pounds — they'll feel cramped even with the higher weight capacity. Also skip if you have zero patience for assembly; this isn't a 15-minute setup.

## FAQ

**How fast does it actually go?**
Around 7 mph on flat pavement with one rider. With two kids, expect closer to 5-6 mph. The high-speed setting is plenty for older kids; the low setting (about 3 mph) is good for younger siblings.

**Can the parent remote completely override the kid's controls?**
Yes. The remote takes priority over the steering wheel and foot pedal. You can steer, brake, and change speed from the remote, even while the kid is driving.

**Is this safe for a 5-year-old?**
With the remote and low-speed mode, yes. The seat belts are functional, and the remote gives you instant kill-switch power. For a 5-year-old, set it to low speed and supervise.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Buy it if your kids are 5-10 and you need a two-seater that handles real terrain. It's powerful, well-built, and the parent remote is a genuine safety feature, not a gimmick. The assembly headache and battery drain under load are the tradeoffs, but they don't outweigh what this vehicle delivers.

**Skip it if** you need a quick-setup gift, have one small child (you'll overpay for the second seat), or your terrain is nothing but steep hills — you'd want a 36V system for that.

For the price, this is the best two-seater ride-on for big kids on Amazon right now. Your kids will outgrow it eventually — but they'll have a blast doing it.
