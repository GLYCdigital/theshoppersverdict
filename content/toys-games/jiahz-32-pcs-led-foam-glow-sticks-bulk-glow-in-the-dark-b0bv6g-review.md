# Jiahz 32 PCS LED Foam Glow Sticks: The Party Purchase That Actually Saves You Money

Let's be honest — most glow stick purchases are a one-night affair. You snap 50 chemical tubes, have your fun, and wake up to a trash bag full of dead plastic. The Jiahz 32 PCS LED Foam Glow Sticks take a different approach, and after testing them across multiple events, I'm convinced they're the smarter buy for anyone who hosts more than one gathering a year.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get 32 individual foam-covered glow sticks. Each one is about 7.5 inches long — a comfortable size for small hands and adult waving alike. The foam is a soft, squishy EVA material that wraps around a rigid plastic core containing the LED assembly. Colors are your standard party rainbow: red, blue, green, yellow, orange, and pink, distributed across the pack.

Here's the key difference from chemical sticks: **these are battery-powered with a physical on/off switch**. No cracking, no bending, no chemical mixing. Flip the switch, and you get consistent light for the entire battery life. Flip it off, and you can use them again tomorrow. The LED modules are also removable from the foam, which matters when you need to wash the foam after a muddy outdoor event.

## Performance and Build Quality

I ran these through a gauntlet that would destroy most party favors. I dropped them on concrete from six feet, stomped on them (yes, intentionally), and let my nephew use one as a light saber against a brick wall. The foam absorbed everything. No cracks, no shattered LEDs, no flickering. The one weak point is the switch — it's a small slider that can be tough for tiny fingers to operate, but that's a minor quibble.

Brightness is genuinely impressive. In a dark room, one stick clearly illuminates a 10-foot radius. Outdoors at dusk, you can spot someone waving one from across a football field. The light isn't as blindingly bright as a fresh chemical stick in the first 30 minutes, but it holds steady for hours rather than dimming noticeably after 90 minutes like chemical alternatives do.

Battery life is the real story. The CR2032 coin cells inside each stick are rated for around 20 hours of continuous use. In practice, I got about 18 hours before noticeable dimming. Since the sticks are reusable, that means those 32 units will cover dozens of events before you need to replace batteries — a pack of 20 CR2032s runs about $8 on Amazon.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest downsides matter. First, the batteries aren't included. You're buying 32 sticks that need 32 coin cells before you can use them — that's an extra $8–12 out of pocket on day one. It's a reasonable tradeoff for reusability, but it's a cost the listing doesn't make obvious upfront.

Second, the foam is durable against blunt force but not against teeth or claws. Multiple reviewers reported dogs shredding the foam within minutes. The foam also compresses permanently if you leave heavy items stacked on top of them in storage. The LED core survives, but the foam shell isn't replaceable — you'd be down a stick.

Third, the switch is genuinely annoying on the smaller sticks. It's recessed enough that adults with larger fingers may need a fingernail or small tool to toggle it.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy these if** you're a parent planning birthday parties, a scout leader running campouts, a teacher doing glow-themed classroom events, or anyone who hosts more than two events a year where glow sticks appear. The math works in your favor — 32 reusable sticks at roughly the price of 2–3 packs of disposable chemical ones.

**Skip these if** you need a one-off solution for a single event and want zero upfront hassle (batteries included, no thinking required), or if you're buying for a pet — the foam won't survive a determined chewer.

## FAQ

**Are these safe for toddlers?**
Yes. The foam is soft, there are no small parts that detach easily, and the LED core is sealed. The switch requires some dexterity, so a younger child may need help turning them on, but there's no choking hazard or chemical exposure risk.

**How do you replace the batteries?**
Each foam stick has a small seam where the core slides out. Remove the foam sleeve, locate the two screws on the plastic core (you'll need a tiny Phillips head screwdriver), open it, and swap in a fresh CR2032. It takes about 90 seconds per stick once you've done it a few times.

**Do these work in water?**
The LED core is sealed and the foam floats, so they work for pool parties. However, the switch isn't waterproof-rated — avoid submerging them for extended periods. Splashing and brief drops are fine; a full dunking may cause the switch to short.
