**The Short Version**

You're looking at 24 plastic eggs, each holding a different mini plush animal. It's not a complex concept, but JOYIN executes it well. The toy quality is better than the dollar-store equivalent, and the variety keeps kids hunting until the last egg is cracked. That said, the small size of the toys means you need to be careful with younger siblings. Here's the full breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

As shown in the product image, the set includes 24 plastic eggs in bright pastel colors. Each egg splits cleanly in half — no screwing, no prying. Inside each one is a plush toy. The assortment I've seen in customer photos includes bunnies, chicks, ducks, bears, and a few random animals like foxes and pandas. The plush toys measure roughly 2 to 3 inches tall, which is smaller than a standard stress ball but bigger than a keychain charm.

The eggs come in a clear plastic bag, not a basket. You'll need to supply your own hiding container or basket for the hunt. The egg halves are thin but hold together fine — they're not meant for repeated opening and closing, so treat them as single-use.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The plush toys are the real story here. The stitching is tight — I didn't see loose threads or gaping seams in the customer photos. The fabric is soft, not that scratchy felt you find in cheap party favors. The faces are embroidered, not printed, which means they won't rub off after a few washes. Several reviewers mentioned their kids have had these for months and the toys still look new.

The eggs themselves are standard plastic, about 2.5 inches long. They crack open with a satisfying pop, which is half the fun for toddlers. The downside? The plastic is thin enough that a determined 2-year-old can crush an egg by sitting on it. That's not a dealbreaker, but it means these are strictly one-hunt items if you have a rambunctious kid.

**What Buyers Praise**

The most consistent praise is the variety. One reviewer said, *"My daughter opened all 24 and every single toy was different. No repeats, which never happens with these grab-bag style sets."* That's backed by multiple reviews mentioning the same thing.

Parents also like the hunt factor. *"The eggs are easy to find but hard to resist opening. My kids spent a full hour just cracking them open and comparing toys,"* wrote one customer. The softness of the toys is another recurring theme — several reviewers noted they're cuddle-worthy, not just cheap trinkets.

**Where It Falls Short**

The size is the biggest issue. At 2–3 inches, these plush toys are small enough to be a choking hazard for kids under 3. The box says "3+", but if you have a younger sibling who puts everything in their mouth, you'll need to police the aftermath of the hunt carefully.

The egg shells are also flimsier than I'd like. They work fine for a single hunt, but if you're hoping to reuse them for next year's egg hunt, they'll likely crack or warp in storage. Also, there's no basket or storage container included — you're paying for the toys and eggs only.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is best for families with kids aged 3 to 8 who want a ready-made Easter activity without assembling a dozen separate items. It's also a solid choice for classroom parties or daycare egg hunts where you need a bulk supply of small, non-candy prizes. If you're hosting a hunt with multiple kids, the 24-count is enough for 4–6 kids to each get 4–6 eggs.

Skip it if you're looking for decorative eggs to display, or if you need toys large enough for a 1-year-old. This is a functional toy set, not a keepsake.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are the plush toys machine washable?**
A: Yes, but air-dry them. The embroidered faces hold up fine in a gentle cycle, but the dryer can warp the stuffing and cause the fabric to pill.

**Q: Can I reuse the eggs for next year?**
A: Technically, but they're thin plastic. If you store them carefully in a box without crushing, they'll survive one more hunt. Don't expect them to last beyond that.

**Q: Are all 24 toys different?**
A: Most sets appear to have 24 unique animals. A few reviewers reported one duplicate, but that seems to be the exception rather than the rule.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a no-fuss Easter activity with quality plush toys that won't end up in the trash by dinner. The variety and softness make it worth the price.

**Skip it if** you have kids under 3, or if you're expecting reusable eggs or a storage basket.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A solid, well-executed Easter staple with only minor caveats around toy size and egg durability.
