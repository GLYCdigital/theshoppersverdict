You know that moment when your kid asks for a swing and you picture the flimsy plastic disc that flips over the first time they lean back? The RedSwing 43-inch saucer tree swing is not that. This is the swing that makes your backyard the hangout spot — and at 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, I'm not the only one impressed.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the 43-inch saucer seat, a three-point chain suspension system (not the cheap two-point you see on budget swings), heavy-duty carabiners, and a spreader bar that keeps the chains from tangling. The seat itself is 900D Oxford cloth over a padded interior — that's the same material used in heavy-duty luggage, not the tissue-paper fabric on $30 swings. The frame is a powder-coated steel ring that won't rust after one rainy season. Assembly takes about 20 minutes with two people; the chains need to be equal length or the swing hangs crooked, so have a second set of hands for the initial hang.

**Real-World Performance**

The standout feature is the three-point suspension. Most saucer swings use two chains, which means they wobble sideways and flip if a kid shifts weight suddenly. The RedSwing's third anchor point keeps the seat level and stable — even when my 9-year-old and her friend both pile on, it doesn't tilt or spin unpredictably. The 43-inch diameter genuinely fits two kids up to around age 12, or one adult who wants to relive childhood. Weight capacity is listed at 330 pounds; I'd trust it up to that but wouldn't push beyond.

The padding is the other surprise. It's not a thin layer over hard plastic — it's a proper cushion that doesn't bottom out. Kids will sit on this for hours without complaining about a sore backside, which is basically a parenting win.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The reviews echo what I found. One parent wrote, "My kids fight over this swing. It's the only thing they actually use every single day." **Where It Falls Short**

Honest downsides: no tree strap is included. If your branch is thicker than 6 inches, you'll need to buy a separate hanging kit — that's an extra $15–20 that should've been in the box. The chains also make a metal-on-metal clink sound when kids swing hard; it's not loud enough to be annoying indoors but worth knowing if you're hanging it in a playroom. And the assembly really does need two people — the spreader bar alignment is finicky, and doing it solo risks an uneven hang that makes the swing tilt.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for families who want a real swing, not a toy that breaks in a month. It's ideal for:

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this hang indoors?**
A: Yes, but you need a ceiling mount rated for dynamic weight — a kid swinging adds force beyond their body weight. A standard ceiling hook won't cut it; use a proper swing hanger rated for 300+ pounds.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
A: Hose it down and let it air dry. The Oxford cloth is mold-resistant, but don't store it wet — hang it in the sun for a few hours after washing.

**Q: Does it work on a playset with a swing beam?**
A: Yes, the carabiners fit standard 1-inch swing hangers. Just make sure your beam is rated for the swing's weight capacity.
