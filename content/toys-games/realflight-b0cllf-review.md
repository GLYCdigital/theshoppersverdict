Let me be blunt about what RealFlight is: it's the closest thing to a flight school for RC pilots that you can buy for $200. It's also the same price as a decent beginner drone. So the real question isn't whether it's good — it's whether you're the kind of pilot who actually needs it.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the software (download code, not a disc) and the InterLink DX controller — a full-size, two-stick transmitter that plugs into your PC via USB. This matters more than you'd think. A $30 USB dongle and free simulator won't teach you muscle memory because you're not holding anything resembling a real radio. The InterLink DX has proper gimbal tension, spring-centered sticks, and switch positions that mirror real transmitters. It also doubles as a gamepad for other flight sims.

The aircraft library is enormous — 500+ models ranging from foam park flyers to turbine jets, plus helicopters, multirotors, and even gliders. Each model has adjustable weight, power, and control throws. The physics engine is the real draw: stall characteristics, adverse yaw, and torque effects are modeled with enough fidelity that maneuvers you learn here genuinely translate to the field.

## Where It Shines

The training value is the entire point. New RC pilots crash — a lot. A single crash on a $300 foam plane costs more than this simulator. The "RealFlight" name isn't marketing fluff; the flight dynamics are noticeably better than free alternatives. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I flew my first real RC plane after 20 hours in RealFlight and didn't crash once. The muscle memory carried over exactly."*

The InterLink DX controller is also a genuinely good piece of hardware. It's not a cheap toy stick — it has the weight and resistance of a mid-range Spektrum transmitter, and it's built to survive years of desk abuse.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's talk about the $200 elephant in the room. Phoenix RC and AccuRC cost a third of this and cover 80% of the same ground. RealFlight's edge is the physics depth and aircraft variety — but if you're a casual flyer who just wants to practice loops and landings, you're overpaying.

The software itself feels dated. The UI is a maze of nested menus that looks like it survived from 2015. Graphics are functional but not pretty. And critically, the base version has no multiplayer and no VR support — both locked behind the $30 "Evolution" upgrade that should have been included at this price.

The learning curve is also steeper than it needs to be. Out of the box, the default settings are twitchy. You'll need to dig into the manual (or YouTube) to set up dual rates and exponential properly. A beginner who expects to launch and fly will be frustrated in the first ten minutes.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're serious about RC flying — you've already bought or plan to buy a real plane/drone and want to practice without risking gear. This is also ideal for parents of kids who want to fly; the sim will save you hundreds in replacement parts.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual gamer curious about RC flight, or you just want a fun flying game. You'll be happier with a $30 sim or even a free browser option. Also skip if you're on a Mac — RealFlight is Windows-only, and the workarounds are clunky.

## FAQ

**Q: Does RealFlight work with my own RC transmitter?**
A: Yes, via the included InterLink DX's trainer port or a USB dongle, but the InterLink DX is honestly good enough that most people just use it.

**Q: Is this worth it if I already fly FPV drones?**
A: Partially. The multirotor physics are decent, but dedicated FPV sims like Liftoff or Velocidrone are better for freestyle practice and cost far less.

**Q: Does the software require a powerful PC?**
A: No — it's lightweight. A laptop with integrated graphics from the last five years will run it fine at 60fps.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — RealFlight earns its reputation as the gold standard for RC flight training. The physics and controller are excellent, and the crash-prevention value justifies the price for anyone who owns real RC aircraft. But the dated interface, missing features, and premium cost mean it's not the right choice for everyone.

**Buy it if** you're investing in RC flying and want to avoid expensive mistakes. **Skip it if** you're casually curious — spend $30 on a cheaper sim and see if the hobby sticks first.
