## The Losi LMT Isn't a Toy — It's a Crawler Disguised as a Monster Truck

Most monster trucks in this price range are glorified skateboards with wheels — fast in a straight line, useless over anything more challenging than a curb. The Losi LMT is different. It's a legitimate rock crawler with a monster truck shell bolted on top, and that's exactly what makes it worth your money.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the fully assembled LMT, a 2.4GHz transmitter, and the manual. That's it. No battery, no charger. That's a real annoyance at this price point — you'll need to budget an extra $40–60 for a 2S LiPo with an EC3 connector and a compatible charger before your first run. The body is pre-painted and detailed well enough that you won't feel compelled to repaint it, though the decals are applied crooked on some units.

## Solid Axle Magic: How It Actually Performs

The headline feature is the solid front axle — the same design used on full-scale rock crawlers. Unlike independent front suspension (which is what nearly every monster truck uses), the solid axle keeps both front wheels planted when one side climbs over an obstacle. In practice, this means the LMT walks over rocks, roots, and ledges that would high-center or flip lesser trucks. The axle articulation is genuinely impressive; as shown in the product images, the front end twists dramatically while maintaining traction.

The brushed motor is fine for crawling and light bashing. It's got enough torque to climb steep grades and enough speed for backyard fun, but it runs out of breath quickly on grass or loose dirt. The ESC is brushless-ready, which tells you everything about Losi's intent — they know you'll upgrade eventually. Owners who've made the swap report the LMT transforms into a genuinely fast, aggressive truck.

## What 225 Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.5-star average holds up. The most common praise centers on durability. One reviewer put it simply: "I've bashed this thing into trees, flipped it down hills, and it just keeps going." Another noted, "The metal gear steering servo and sealed diffs mean I haven't replaced anything in six months of weekly use." That's consistent with what I've seen — the drivetrain is genuinely overbuilt for the price.

## Where It Falls Short

The brushed motor is the weakest link. It's not bad, but it's clearly a placeholder. Most serious owners upgrade within a few months, which adds cost to an already pricey truck. The battery situation is also annoying — the included tray fits 2S packs up to 5000mAh, but tight body clips make every battery swap a minor ordeal. Plan on picking up aftermarket thumb screws early.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Crawler enthusiasts** who want monster truck looks with real rock-crawling capability — this is your truck.
- **Parents of teens** who've outgrown toy-grade RC and want something that can take a beating.
- **Upgraders** who enjoy tinkering — the LMT is a fantastic platform for brushless conversions.

**Skip it if** you just want a fast basher for open fields. The LMT's gearing favors crawling, and you'll be disappointed by its top speed. Also skip if you want a ready-to-run experience — the missing battery and charger push the real cost well past the sticker price.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the LMT need a LiPo battery, or will NiMH work?**
A: NiMH works, but you'll notice reduced power and shorter runtime. The ESC supports both, but a 2S LiPo (7.4V) is the sweet spot for performance and run time.

**Q: Can I run this in water or mud?**
A: It's water-resistant, not waterproof. Light splashes are fine, but submerging the electronics will kill it. Dry off the chassis after wet runs.

**Q: How hard is the brushless upgrade?**
A: Straightforward. The ESC is already brushless-ready, so you just swap the motor and connect the sensor wire. Most owners complete it in under an hour with basic tools.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely capable crawler with monster truck styling and a drivetrain that survives hard use. **Skip it if** you need speed out of the box or refuse to budget for a battery and charger.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a serious crawler with a few cost-of-entry frustrations, but the solid axle performance and durability make it worth every penny.
