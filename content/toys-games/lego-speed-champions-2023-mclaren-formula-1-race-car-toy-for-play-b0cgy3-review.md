Let’s be blunt: LEGO Speed Champions sets are the best value in the entire LEGO catalog, and the 2023 McLaren Formula 1 car is a standout example. At 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, buyers are clearly happy. But is this set worth your money if you already own last year’s McLaren? That’s the real question.

## What’s in the Box

You get 242 pieces, one driver minifigure, and a build that takes roughly 20–30 minutes. The set retails around $20–25 depending on where you buy it. For that price, you’re getting the 8-wide chassis that LEGO introduced in 2020 — a massive improvement over the older 6-wide cars that looked stubby next to real F1 vehicles.

The star here is the papaya orange color scheme. LEGO nailed the McLaren livery, and crucially, most of the bodywork uses printed bricks rather than stickers. The main nose cone, side pods, and engine cover all have the orange finish molded in. You’ll only need stickers for the race number, some small sponsor details, and the rear wing endplates.

## Build Experience and Design Accuracy

The build is straightforward but clever. LEGO uses angled slopes and curved panels to replicate the MCL60’s distinctive nose and the stepped side pods that were a hallmark of the 2023 car. As shown in the product image, the proportions are surprisingly accurate for a brick-built toy — the car sits low, the wheels are properly exposed, and the front wing has the right multi-element look.

One detail that impressed me: the driver minifigure fits snugly into the cockpit with a removable steering wheel. It’s a small touch, but it makes the set feel more premium than the price suggests. The helmet and race suit both feature printed McLaren branding.

The rear wing is fixed in place — you can’t adjust the angle like you can on some Technic sets. If you’re a display purist who wants to pose the car in different aero configurations, that’s a limitation. But for a playset, it means the wing stays firmly attached during aggressive play.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews are overwhelmingly positive, and the common threads are value and accuracy. One buyer wrote, “The best Speed Champions set I’ve built. The printed pieces make it look like a real F1 car, not a toy.” Another said, “My 8-year-old built it in an afternoon and hasn’t stopped racing it around the house.”

Parents repeatedly mention that the 8-wide design holds up better than older LEGO cars during play. The wider stance means it doesn’t tip over as easily when kids push it across the floor. That’s a genuine improvement, not marketing talk.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint among reviewers is the piece count. At 242 pieces, this is a small set. If you’re an adult builder looking for a satisfying afternoon project, you’ll finish this before your coffee gets cold. It’s a quick build — satisfying, but quick.

The stickers are also a minor frustration. The race number decals are small and require precise placement. If you have shaky hands, expect to peel and re-stick a few times. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s worth knowing.

Finally, there’s the elephant in the room: if you already own the 2023 McLaren from the earlier Speed Champions wave, this set is nearly identical visually. The changes are minimal — a different race number and a few sponsor decals. It’s not worth buying both unless you’re a completionist.

## Who Should Buy This

- **F1 fans** who want an affordable, accurate replica of the MCL60 for their desk or shelf.
- **Parents** looking for a birthday or holiday gift for kids aged 7–12 who are into racing. It’s the right difficulty level for that range.
- **LEGO collectors** who build every Speed Champions release — though you probably already own this.

Skip it if you’re an adult builder who wants a long, complex build, or if you already have the previous McLaren F1 set.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this set compatible with other LEGO Speed Champions cars?**
Yes. It uses the standard 8-wide chassis, so it lines up perfectly with other Speed Champions sets for display or racing.

**Q: Does it come with a driver minifigure?**
Yes, one driver with a McLaren race suit, helmet, and a removable steering wheel.

**Q: How long does the build take?**
For an adult, about 20–30 minutes. For a child, expect 45 minutes to an hour.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want an accurate, durable, and affordable F1 car that looks great on a shelf and survives playtime. **Skip it if** you already own the previous McLaren Speed Champions set or you’re seeking a lengthy building challenge.

This is a 4.6/5 set — excellent value, great design, minor flaws. At the $20 price point, it’s hard to argue with the quality per dollar. LEGO continues to prove that Speed Champions is the best gateway into the hobby, and the 2023 McLaren is a prime example.
