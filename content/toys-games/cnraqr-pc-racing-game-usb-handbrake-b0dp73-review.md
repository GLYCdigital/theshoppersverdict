If you’ve priced dedicated sim racing handbrakes lately, you know the good ones start around $150 and climb fast. The CNRAQR PC Racing Game USB Handbrake undercuts that by half — and surprisingly, it doesn’t feel like a cheap compromise. At $69.99 with a 4.2-star average across 210 ratings, this is one of those budget peripherals that earns its place on a rig without making you feel like you’re racing with a toy.

**What’s actually in the box**

The unit ships with the handbrake itself, a USB-A cable, and a small hex wrench for the resistance adjustment. No mounting hardware — that’s the first thing you’ll notice. The body is all metal: a solid steel base plate and lever arm with a red powder-coated grip that looks sharp against a black rig. It’s compact, roughly the footprint of a large smartphone laid flat, so it won’t eat up precious cockpit space.

The cable is a generous 1.8 meters, which reaches most wheel mounts without a USB extender. Build quality at this price point is genuinely better than expected — no creaking plastic, no flimsy pivot. The lever moves on a bearing assembly that feels smooth out of the box.

**Performance and real-world use**

Here’s where the CNRAQR surprises. It uses a Hall effect sensor rather than a potentiometer, which means no contact wear and consistent input over time. That’s a feature you typically see on $200+ units. The resistance knob on the side adjusts pull weight from nearly effortless to noticeably stiff — useful if you switch between rally stages and drift sessions.

Plugging it in, Windows recognizes it immediately as a USB joystick device. No drivers needed. It works on PC, PS3, PS4, and Switch, though the primary use case is obviously PC sims. In Assetto Corsa, Dirt Rally 2.0, and BeamNG, it maps cleanly as an analog axis. One caveat: some titles detect it as a button rather than an axis, and you’ll spend a minute in settings reassigning it. Not a dealbreaker, but know it going in.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme in reviews is value. One buyer wrote, *“I was skeptical at this price, but it feels solid and the Hall sensor is a smart choice. Works perfectly in Dirt Rally after a quick calibration.”* Another noted, *“Better than the $120 handbrake I returned. This one actually has adjustable tension, which I didn't expect.”* The plug-and-play simplicity gets repeated shoutouts — no one wants to wrestle with drivers mid-setup.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of mounting hardware is the biggest friction point. The base has no pre-drilled holes that match standard 8020 aluminum extrusion rigs. You’ll either need a desk clamp, heavy-duty double-sided tape, or a trip to the hardware store for M6 bolts and T-nuts. It’s workable, but it’s an extra step that cheapens the out-of-box experience.

The lever throw is also shorter than pricier options like the Heusinkveld or Simagic units. For quick handbrake flicks in rally, it’s fine. For long, sweeping drift pulls where you want maximum travel to modulate slip angle, you’ll hit the end stop sooner than you’d like. Finally, the base can slide on a smooth desk without mounting — you’ll want it secured.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the sim racer on a budget who wants a dedicated handbrake without dropping $150+. It’s ideal for rally and drift players who are tired of binding a wheel button and want actual analog control. It’s also a smart pick for someone building a first rig who wants to test whether a handbrake adds enough to their driving before committing to a premium unit.

Skip it if you have a high-end 8020 rig and want a bolt-on solution with zero fabrication, or if you need a long-throw lever for serious drift competition. Also skip if you race exclusively on Xbox — this unit doesn’t support it.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work with my Logitech or Thrustmaster wheel setup?**
A: Yes. It connects via separate USB, so it functions independently of your wheel. You can pair it with any wheel brand.

**Q: Does it work with Forza Horizon 5 on PC?**
A: Yes, but you’ll need to map it in the control settings. Forza sometimes recognizes it as a button rather than an axis, so check the binding screen.

**Q: Can I mount it to a Playseat Challenge or similar folding chair?**
A: It’s possible but tricky. The base is flat, so you’ll need a clamp or zip ties. Some buyers report success with heavy-duty Velcro straps.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You race rally or drift on PC, want real analog handbrake control, and don’t want to spend over $100.

**Skip it if:** You need a true bolt-on solution for a professional rig, require a longer lever throw, or race on Xbox.

The CNRAQR USB Handbrake delivers 80% of the functionality of premium units at 40% of the price. The Hall sensor, adjustable tension, and solid metal build make it the smart budget pick in this category. Just budget for mounting hardware and a few minutes of mapping — after that, it’s pure fun.
