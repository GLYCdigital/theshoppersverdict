Most people buy a joystick for flying. You're here because you want to load a bale of hay without doing the digital equivalent of wrestling a greased pig. The Thrustmaster SimTask Farmstick is a niche peripheral built for one thing: making Farming Simulator feel less like keyboard gymnastics and more like actually running equipment. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's clearly working for a lot of virtual farmers. But it's not without quirks.

## What's in the box

You get the Farmstick itself, a detachable 6-foot USB-C cable, and the obligatory quick-start guide. No software disc — everything runs natively through Windows' built-in game controller support. The stick measures roughly 10 x 8 x 9 inches and weighs 3.3 pounds. That heft matters because this thing needs to stay put while you're working a front loader.

The layout is the headline feature: a main 3-axis grip (X, Y, and twist for rudder-style input) flanked by two independent rod-style throttle levers on the base. Those rods are the reason you're buying this. They mimic the twin-lever hydraulic controls on real tractors, letting you run a loader arm and bucket simultaneously with separate hands. Below the grip, you've got a throttle wheel, an 8-way hat switch, and a row of programmable buttons.

## Performance and real-world use

In Farming Simulator 22 and 25, the Farmstick shines exactly where you'd hope. Running a front loader becomes a two-handed ballet instead of a finger-tangling nightmare. One reviewer put it plainly: "It makes loading bales and pallets actually enjoyable. I can't go back to keyboard." Another noted, "The dual throttle levers are genius for combines — header height on one side, reel speed on the other."

The main grip uses contactless magnetic sensors for the X/Y axes, which means no potentiometer wear over time. Twist action on the Z-axis feels a bit stiff out of the box, but it loosens up after a few sessions. The base's six suction cups hold firm on glass or laminate desks — I tested it on a glossy IKEA top and couldn't budge it mid-operation.

## Where it falls short

The biggest dealbreaker: this is Windows PC only. No PlayStation, no Xbox. If you play on console, stop reading and move on.

The button quality is serviceable but not premium. The face buttons have a shallow, slightly mushy feel compared to Thrustmaster's flight sticks. You're paying for the mechanical layout, not the switch quality. Also, the suction cups demand a smooth surface. On a textured desk or mousepad, the base slides around at the worst moments. Several Amazon reviewers mention needing to clamp it down with velcro strips on wood-grain desks.

One more annoyance: there's no included software for remapping on non-Farming Simulator games. It works with other titles, but you're relying on in-game bindings, which is clunky.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You play Farming Simulator on PC for more than 10 hours a week, particularly if you spend meaningful time on loader work, combines, or forestry equipment. It transforms those tasks from tedious to tactile. It's also a solid pick for sim-enthusiasts who want a dedicated peripheral without spending $500 on a full wheel-and-pedal rig.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual player, a console user, or someone who wants a general-purpose joystick. The Farmstick is a specialist tool, and its layout is wasted on games that don't use twin levers. Also skip if your desk isn't smooth — you'll fight the base more than the game.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with Farming Simulator 25?**
A: Yes. It's plug-and-play with FS19, FS22, and FS25 on Windows. The game auto-detects it and pre-binds most controls.

**Q: Can I use it for other games?**
A: Technically yes — Windows sees it as a standard joystick. But the twin-lever design is purpose-built for agricultural sims. For flight sims or space games, you'd be better served by a traditional flight stick.

**Q: Do I need to install Thrustmaster software?**
A: No. It runs natively through Windows DirectInput. The lack of proprietary software is actually a plus for reliability.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Thrustmaster SimTask Farmstick earns its keep as a purpose-built tool for a specific audience. It nails the tractor-control fantasy with its dual-rod levers, solid build, and magnetic sensors. The PC-only restriction and finicky suction-cup base hold it back from a perfect score, but for dedicated virtual farmers, this is the difference between playing a game and operating a machine.

**Buy it if** you live in Farming Simulator and want genuine tactile control. **Skip it if** you're on console, on a textured desk, or only casually curious.
