**Opening Hook**

Most "science kits" for kids are junk — flimsy plastic, three experiments, and a pamphlet written by a marketing intern. The STMT Bath Bombs kit is different. It's a legit chemistry lesson disguised as a spa day, and with 215 ratings holding a 4.3-star average, it's clear families are getting real value. Here's what you actually need to know before buying.

**What's In the Box**

The kit arrives with everything needed to produce roughly six full-size bath bombs. You get the dry ingredients (baking soda, citric acid, Epsom salts), colorants, light fragrance oils, and two stainless steel sphere molds. There's also a mixing bowl, gloves, and a detailed instruction booklet that walks through the science of the fizzing reaction — not just "mix and press." For a toy in this price range, the component quality is genuinely solid. The molds are metal, not the cheap plastic you'd expect, and they hold up to repeated use.

**Real-World Performance**

Here's where the STMT kit earns its keep. The activity takes about 30–45 minutes from start to finish, which is the perfect attention span window for most kids aged 6–12. The instructions are clear enough that most children can work through it with minimal adult help — though I'd recommend parental supervision for the mixing step, because citric acid powder gets airborne easily.

The finished bath bombs actually work. They fizz properly in warm water, release decent color, and don't leave a gross residue ring around the tub. That's more than I can say for several higher-priced kits I've tested.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scanning through the 215 reviews, a few themes come up repeatedly. Parents love that the activity holds attention and teaches something. One reviewer put it well: *"My 8-year-old thought she was just making bath bombs, but she kept asking why it fizzed — best accidental science lesson we've had."* Another noted, *"The molds are surprisingly good quality. We've made three batches already and they're still holding up."* The reusable nature of the kit gets consistent shoutouts — this isn't a one-and-done craft.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honesty time. The bath bombs are finicky to get perfectly solid. If your kid packs the mold loosely (and they will), you'll get crumbly discs that break apart in the tub. The instructions warn about this, but it's a genuine frustration point. The fragrance oils are also underwhelming — light scents that vanish once the bomb dissolves. Kids expecting a strongly scented spa experience will be let down. And yes, the powder gets everywhere. Plan to do this on a covered table or outside.

**Who Should Buy This**

This kit is ideal for parents of curious 6–10 year olds who want a screen-free activity that produces something usable. It's also a strong pick for homeschool families covering basic chemistry concepts — the included booklet explains the acid-base reaction clearly enough that kids retain it. Skip it if your child lacks patience for multi-step projects, or if you're looking for a quick 10-minute activity. This requires focus.

**FAQ**

**How many bath bombs does the kit make?**
Six full-size bombs with the included ingredients. You can stretch it to eight if you make smaller ones. Refill packs are available separately.

**Is this safe for young kids?**
The ingredients are non-toxic and skin-safe, but the powders can irritate eyes. Recommended for ages 6+ with supervision. The gloves included are a smart addition.

**Can you reuse the molds?**
Absolutely. The stainless steel molds clean up easily and hold up for many batches. This is one of the kit's best values.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely educational activity that produces bath bombs kids will actually use. The quality-to-price ratio is strong, and the reusable molds give it lasting value.

**Skip it if** you're looking for a quick, mess-free craft, or your child has little patience for steps that require care.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid STEM toy that delivers on its promise, with minor gripes about mess and fragrance strength that don't undermine the overall value.
