If you’ve got a Bluey-obsessed preschooler, you already know the drill: you’ve seen every episode twice, you can quote Bandit’s best lines, and your house is slowly being colonized by Heeler-family merchandise. The TOMY Pop Up Bluey Kids Game is another entry in that universe, but it earns its shelf space. It’s a simple push-and-pop game that understands its audience — kids who want excitement, not rules.

## What’s in the Box

The game includes a plastic Bluey-themed base with four colored slots, a Bluey character piece that pops from the center, and four colored tokens (one for each player). Assembly is minimal — you snap the pieces together and it’s ready. The whole thing is compact, roughly the size of a large dinner plate, so it travels well for restaurant outings or grandparents’ houses. No batteries required, which is a win for parents tired of replacing AAAs.

## How It Plays

The rules are simple enough for a three-year-old: players take turns pressing down on colored buttons, hoping to make Bluey pop up. Each colored token corresponds to a button, and when Bluey jumps, the player who pressed the matching color collects the token. First to collect all their tokens wins.

The genius here is the suspense. The mechanism doesn’t pop every time — it’s randomized, so kids hold their breath with each press. When Bluey finally springs, there’s genuine delight. As one Amazon reviewer put it, “My 3-year-old daughter screams with joy every time Bluey pops up. We play it at least five times a day.” Another parent noted, “My son is 4 and can play this without help. He loves that it’s Bluey and not some character he doesn’t know.”

## Build Quality and Durability

The plastic construction is solid for a children’s game. The buttons are large enough for small hands, and the Bluey piece is sturdy — it won’t snap off with aggressive play. That said, the pop-up mechanism has a quirk: occasionally Bluey doesn’t spring fully, and you need to give the piece a nudge. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it breaks the flow. A few reviewers mentioned this: “The only issue is sometimes Bluey gets stuck halfway up. My son just pushes it the rest of the way, so it doesn’t bother him, but it might bother some.”

## Where It Falls Short

This is a luck-based game, pure and simple. There’s no strategy, no decision-making. For adults, it gets old fast — you’re essentially pressing buttons and watching a toy pop. If you’re looking for a game that teaches turn-taking or matching skills, this does that minimally, but it’s far more about the thrill than the learning. Also, the recommended age is 3 and up, but the small tokens are a choking hazard for younger siblings — keep them away from babies.

## Who Should Buy This

This game is tailor-made for Bluey superfans aged 3-5. If your child can’t get enough of the Heelers, this will be a hit. It’s also a smart pick for grandparents who want a no-fuss activity that doesn’t require reading or counting. It works well for two players, but it’s best with three or four — more kids means more suspense. If your child is over six, they’ll likely find it too simple and prefer something with more challenge.

## FAQ

**Is the TOMY Pop Up Bluey Game suitable for 2-year-olds?**
The box says 3+, and I’d stick to that. The tokens are small enough to be a choking hazard, and younger kids may not understand the matching mechanic. That said, a 2-year-old could still enjoy just pressing the buttons with supervision.

**How long does a game take to play?**
About 10-15 minutes per round. It’s short enough to keep kids engaged but long enough to feel like a real game. You’ll likely play multiple rounds in a sitting.

**Does it require batteries?**
No. The pop-up mechanism is spring-loaded, so it works right out of the box. That’s one less thing to replace.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have a Bluey-obsessed preschooler who wants a game they can actually play. It’s durable, exciting for young kids, and delivers exactly what it promises — simple, joyful fun.

**Skip it if:** Your child is over six, you’re looking for an educational game, or you need something with strategic depth. This is pure luck-based entertainment for the toddler set.

The TOMY Pop Up Bluey Kids Game earns its 4.5-star rating. It’s not a game you’ll want to play for hours, but for the target audience — kids aged 3 to 5 who love Bluey — it’s near-perfect. At its price point, it’s a solid gift that will get plenty of playtime. For the joy it brings, that’s a solid trade.
