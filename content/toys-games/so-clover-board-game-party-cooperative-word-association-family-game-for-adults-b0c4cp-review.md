**So Clover!** has quietly become one of the most talked-about party games since Codenames — and for good reason. With a 4.9-star average from 245 ratings, this cooperative word association game from Repos Production (the folks behind 7 Wonders) does something surprisingly rare: it makes everyone at the table feel clever at the same time.

Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 330 keyword cards (each with four words), 6 dry-erase clover-shaped boards, 6 markers, and a sand timer. The clover boards are the visual centerpiece — each has four quadrants where you write your clue words around a central keyword. Build quality is solid: the boards wipe clean easily, and the cards are thick enough to survive repeated shuffling.

## How It Plays (And Why It Works)

Unlike Codenames where teams take turns and one player does the heavy lifting, So Clover! has everyone simultaneously creating clues. Each player draws a random card, sees four words, and picks one to be their secret keyword. They then write three clue words in the other quadrants — clues that connect to the keyword AND ideally overlap with a neighbor's clues.

Here's the clever part: you pass your board face-down to the player on your left. That player sees only your three clue words and must guess your keyword. But they're also seeing their own board from a different player. The team scores points based on how many keywords get guessed correctly — but if even one is missed, the round's points are lost. That shared risk creates genuine tension.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The overwhelming consensus in reviews centers on engagement. One verified buyer wrote: *"Played with 8 people ages 14-60 and everyone was fully invested the entire time. No one checked their phone."* Another noted: *"The simultaneous play means you're never sitting around watching someone else think — you're always solving."*

That's the real magic. In Codenames, the spymaster's turn can leave others idle. Here, everyone's brain is engaged every single round. The cooperative element also eliminates the "alpha player" problem — you win together or lose together.

## Where It Falls Short

The scoring system is the biggest hurdle. The abstract way points accumulate (based on how many keywords are found across multiple boards) confused several reviewers on first play. One commenter admitted: *"We played the first game wrong and still had fun, but had to YouTube a rules explanation."*

The word selection also leans abstract — words like "justice," "balance," and "memory" appear frequently. Players who prefer concrete, visual clues might find themselves reaching. And there's no two-player mode, so date-night couples are out of luck unless they have friends over.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You host game nights with 4-6 people who enjoy wordplay but hate the betrayal of social deduction games. It's perfect for families with older teens, office parties, or any group where you want everyone laughing together rather than competing.

**Skip it if:** You're strictly a two-player household, you prefer competitive games with clear winners and losers, or your group checks out during games requiring creative thinking.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does a game take?**
A: About 30-45 minutes for 3 rounds. The sand timer is only for clue-writing; guessing happens in real-time conversation.

**Q: Is it good for non-native English speakers?**
A: It works if they're comfortable with conversational English, but abstract vocabulary can be challenging. Consider Codenames Pictures as an alternative if language is a concern.

**Q: Is it replayable?**
A: Very. With 330 cards and each card containing four words, the combination possibilities are enormous. Multiple buyers reported playing 10+ times without repetition feeling stale.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

So Clover! earns its near-perfect rating by solving the party game genre's biggest flaw — downtime. The simultaneous play, cooperative stakes, and clever clue-linking mechanics create a game that's simultaneously relaxing and intellectually engaging. The abstract scoring and lack of a two-player mode keep it from perfection, but for its intended audience — groups of 3-6 who want a genuinely fun, brain-tickling party game — this is an easy recommendation.

The 245 ratings at 4.9 stars aren't an accident. This is one of the rare party games that delivers exactly what it promises, and then some. If your shelf has room for one new game this year, make it this one.
