Let me be blunt: if you're a Dragon Ball collector, you already know the struggle. High-end figures from SH Figuarts and Banpresto run $60-$100, and the budget stuff looks like melted plastic. The Bandai Namco Dragon Ball Evolve line sits in that awkward middle ground — and this Super Saiyan Broly figure is the best argument yet for why that middle ground deserves your attention.

**What's in the box**

It's refreshingly simple: one figure, no assembly, no accessories, no effect parts. You get Broly in his Super Saiyan form from *Dragon Ball Super: Broly*, standing about 5.5 inches tall. The sculpt is where Bandai Namco earns its keep — the muscle definition on the chest, arms, and legs is genuinely impressive for this price tier. The face captures that feral, unhinged expression perfectly, and the hair has that spiky, gravity-defying silhouette Broly fans expect.

**Build quality and articulation**

The PVC construction feels dense, not hollow. I put this through a stress test — dropped it from desk height twice, twisted the joints past their natural range, and tried to force a crouching pose it wasn't designed for. No cracks, no stress marks, no loose joints after the abuse. The articulation points at the shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, and knees move smoothly and hold position without ratcheting or popping out.

Where it falls short: the neck and torso have minimal movement. You can't get that dramatic, leaning-forward "I'm about to destroy everything" pose that Broly is known for. The head swivels side to side fine, but up-and-down tilt is nearly nonexistent. For a character whose entire aesthetic is about aggressive forward momentum, this feels like a missed opportunity.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings tells the real story. One reviewer put it well: *"Finally a Broly figure that doesn't look like a cheap knockoff. The detail on the muscles and face is way better than I expected at this price."* Another noted, *"My son is rough on toys and this has held up for months. The paint hasn't chipped and nothing has broken."* The consistent themes are sculpt quality and durability — two things parents and collectors both care about.

**Where it disappoints**

Beyond the neck articulation issue, there's the accessories problem. For $20-ish retail, you're getting a single figure with zero extras. No interchangeable hands, no aura effect, no energy blast. The Dragon Ball Evolve line is clearly designed as a budget alternative, but the complete lack of accessories makes it feel more like a $12 figure than a $20 one. Also, the paint apps are good but not great — there's minor overspray on the hair tips if you look closely, and the green aura wash on the arms is subtle to the point of being easy to miss on some production runs.

**Who should buy this**

- **Parents of Dragon Ball fans**: This is the Broly figure you buy when you're not ready to drop $70 on a collectible. It survives play.
- **Budget-conscious collectors**: If you're building a display shelf and want a solid Broly without breaking the bank, this works.
- **Gift-givers**: It comes in recognizable packaging and makes an impressive impression for the price point.

**Skip it if** you're a serious collector who needs maximum articulation or display accessories — look at the SH Figuarts version instead, and be ready to pay triple.

**FAQ**

**Is this figure compatible with other Dragon Ball Evolve figures?** Yes, it scales consistently with the rest of the line at roughly 5.5 inches. They display well together.

**Does the figure have any die-cast metal parts?** No, it's all PVC plastic. That's actually a plus for durability — die-cast joints can loosen over time.

**Can this be posed with the Dragon Ball Super manga or anime?** It's a static figure with limited articulation, so it's best for display or light play — not for recreating complex action scenes.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a well-sculpted, durable Broly that won't wreck your budget. **Skip it if** you need a fully-articulated, accessory-packed collectible — this isn't that, and it doesn't pretend to be.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely good budget figure that earns its stars through sculpt quality and durability, docked slightly for the articulation limitations and lack of accessories.
