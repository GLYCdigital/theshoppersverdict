**The 24-day countdown that delivers actual Squishmallows — not just chocolate or cheap trinkets.** If you've got a Squishmallow superfan in your house, this advent calendar is the rare holiday countdown that feels genuinely worth the shelf space.

I've seen dozens of advent calendars come through my testing — most are overpriced cardboard filled with disappointment. This one isn't. Here's the real breakdown.

## What's actually inside

You get 24 doors, each hiding a 3.5-inch Micromallow. That's the key spec to note: these aren't the tiny 2-inch keychain versions, and they're not the full 8-inch plush. At 3.5 inches, they're big enough to feel substantial in hand but small enough that 24 of them fit in a reasonably sized box. Each one comes individually wrapped in plastic inside its door, which means no cross-contamination of scents or fabrics.

The character lineup includes a solid mix of classics and holiday exclusives. Think penguins, reindeer, snowmen, and some of the core squad members like Cam the cat and Fifi the fox. As shown in the product image, the box itself is festive without being tacky — the art is clean and the door numbering is clearly legible.

## Build quality and real-world use

Here's where Squishmallows earns their reputation. The Micromallows use the same proprietary marshmallow-like fill as the full-size versions. They're squishy, they rebound after compression, and the printed faces are sharp and consistent. I squeezed several side-by-side with a full-size Squishmallow — same feel, just smaller.

The door mechanics matter more than you'd think. Cheaper advent calendars rip or tear by day three. These doors are perforated with a clean punch-out design that opens without pulling the surrounding cardboard. That's a small detail, but when you're opening one every morning for 24 days, it matters.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, **there are duplicates.** I counted at least three repeated characters across the 24 doors. If you're a completionist collector, that's a real frustration — you're paying for 24 unique items and getting closer to 21.

Second, the **price-to-size ratio** is steep. At typical retail of $30–35, you're paying around $1.25–$1.50 per 3.5-inch plush. That's comparable to buying Micromallows in multi-packs, but those packs don't come with the daily countdown experience. You're paying a premium for the advent format itself.

Third, the **packaging is bulky.** The box is roughly the size of a shoebox, but the actual plushies take up maybe 60% of the interior volume. There's a lot of empty cardboard and plastic tray underneath. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if packaging waste matters to you.
