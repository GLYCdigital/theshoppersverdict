# Giant Bean 36-Piece Wooden Dollhouse Furniture Set: A Complete Home in One Box

Most dollhouse furniture sets force you into a slow, expensive drip-feed of purchases. One pack for the bedroom. Another for the kitchen. A third for the living room. By the time you’re done, you’ve spent triple the price of the dollhouse itself. The Giant Bean 36-piece set sidesteps that entire problem — it arrives with enough furniture to fill five rooms *and* four family dolls, all in a single box. That alone makes it worth a hard look.

## What’s Actually in the Box

Open the package and you get a genuine variety, not padding. The set breaks down into five room groupings: living room, bedroom, kitchen, dining room, and bathroom. You’re getting a sofa, chairs, tables, a bed with headboard, a kitchen stove, a bathtub, and the smaller accessories that make a dollhouse feel lived-in — lamps, a clock, a mirror. The four dolls include two adults and two children, which lets kids create actual family scenarios rather than just arranging furniture.

As shown in the product image, the pieces are cut from real wood, not the flimsy MDF you often find at this price. The furniture ranges from roughly 2 to 4 inches tall, sized for standard 1:12 scale dollhouses. That’s the sweet spot for most wooden dollhouses on the market.

## Build Quality and Real-World Play

The wood is the headline here. It’s solid pine or similar hardwood, and the edges are sanded smooth — no splinters, which matters when small hands are grabbing pieces constantly. The paint is applied in muted, realistic tones (soft blues, creams, wood browns) rather than garish primary colors. It looks like miniature real furniture, not a toy.

The dolls are the pleasant surprise. They’re articulated at the arms and legs, and their heads turn. For $20–$30, that’s unexpected. Kids can sit them at the table, put them to bed, or have them hold the included accessories. The dolls are roughly 4.5 inches tall, which matches the furniture scale well.

One note on durability: the paint finish is matte and not sealed with a heavy topcoat. If your child drops a piece on tile or hardwood, you may see small chips. It doesn’t affect playability, but it’s worth knowing if you’re buying for a rough-and-tumble toddler rather than a careful 5+ year-old.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires naming the tradeoffs. First, the scale. If you own a larger dollhouse like a KidKraft or Melissa & Doug, these pieces will look slightly undersized. They’re built for standard 1:12 scale, but some of the bigger-name houses run closer to 1:10. The furniture will work, but it won’t be perfectly proportioned.

Second, the assembly. Most pieces come pre-assembled, but a few of the smaller items — chair legs, lamp bases — require you to push wooden pegs into holes. It’s easy for an adult, but a younger child might struggle and get frustrated. Plan on helping with the initial setup.

Third, the paint chips I mentioned. It’s a minor cosmetic issue, but if your child is hard on toys, expect some wear within a few months.

## Who Should Buy This

This set is ideal for three specific buyers: parents buying their child’s first dollhouse furniture (the variety lets you figure out what they actually play with before investing in premium sets), grandparents looking for a complete gift without multiple purchases, and anyone who owns a wooden dollhouse and wants to fill it without spending $100+ on individual room sets.

Skip it if you have a high-end collector dollhouse where scale accuracy matters, or if you’re buying for a child under 3 — the small accessories are a choking hazard.
