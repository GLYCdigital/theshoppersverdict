## The Balance-Type That Actually Balances

Most starter packs promise "competitive potential." Most deliver a toy that spins for 10 seconds and flops over. The Beyblade X Claw Leon 5-60P Starter Pack is the exception — it's a genuine balance-type build that holds its own in the X-verse meta, especially for the price point. With 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, it's clearly doing something right. Here's what you're actually getting.

## What's in the Box

The starter pack includes the fully assembled Claw Leon blade (the X-shaped energy layer with its signature lion-claw contact points), a 5-60 ratchet, a P (Point) performance bit, the standard X-clusive launcher, and a ripcord. No stadium — you'll need to provide your own or snap up a battle set separately. As shown in the product image, the packaging is compact but secure, with the blade visible through the front panel so you know exactly what you're buying.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The Claw Leon layer uses the X-clusive line's trademark dual-blade contact design. In practice, those claw protrusions catch the opposing bey's layer at a slightly upward angle — which means you get consistent, mid-power hits rather than wild, unpredictable smashes. It's a deliberate design choice that rewards positioning over brute force.

The 5-60 ratchet adds height and a moderate burst resistance. The "60" height puts the weight up high, which helps with spin retention. The P bit — a flat, wide tip — is where the balance magic happens. It creates a stable, wide spin pattern that holds the center of the stadium with minimal drift. Against attack types that charge hard, Claw Leon absorbs the hit and stays planted.

**The catch:** the P bit is the weakest link. It's made of a softer plastic that wears down after roughly 30–50 launches. Once it rounds out, the bey starts wobbling and loses its balance-type advantage. Replacement bits are cheap (around $5–8 on Amazon), but it's something to budget for if you're battling regularly.

## Where It Falls Short

- **Plastic weight:** The blade layer is predominantly plastic. Attack types with metal weight rings (like the Dran Sword 3-60F) can physically shove Claw Leon around the stadium. If you're up against a heavy hitter, your balance advantage evaporates.
- **P bit durability:** As mentioned, the tip wears quickly. For casual play, it's fine. For competitive sessions, buy a spare.
- **No carrying case:** The starter pack doesn't include a storage case — just a cardboard box. If you travel with your beys, factor in another purchase.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Parents buying for kids 6–10:** This is the perfect entry point. The balance type is forgiving, the launcher is reliable, and the difficulty curve is gentle.
- **Competitive tweakers:** You'll want this for the 5-60 ratchet and P bit — both are usable in meta builds. The Claw Leon layer itself is decent, but not top-tier.
- **Collectors:** The lion-claw design is visually striking and distinct from the other X-verse releases.

**Skip it if:** you're a hardcore competitive player looking for a tournament-winning blade out of the box. You'll need to upgrade the bit immediately.

**Q: Is the launcher reusable for other Beyblade X beys?**
A: Yes. The X-clusive launcher works with all Beyblade X blades — it's a standard system, not a Claw Leon exclusive.

**Q: How long does the ripcord last?**
