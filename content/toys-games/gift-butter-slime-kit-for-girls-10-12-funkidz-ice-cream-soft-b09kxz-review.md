**Is this the slime kit that survives the tween test — or a sticky mess waiting to happen?**

Every parent of a 10–12-year-old knows the drill. You buy a slime kit, the kid plays with it for 20 minutes, and suddenly you're scraping dried rubber off the kitchen table. The FunKidz Ice Cream Butter Slime Kit tries to break that cycle with a premium approach. After digging through 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, here's what actually matters.

**What's in the box**

You get six 3.5-ounce containers of butter slime — that's 21 ounces total, which is substantial at this price point. The "ice cream" theme means pastel colors: strawberry pink, mint green, vanilla white, and similar dessert shades. Each container arrives sealed with a secure lid, which sounds basic but is the single biggest quality differentiator between this kit and the $8 grocery-store options. There are also foam bead mix-ins and modeling clay in separate packets, letting your kid customize the texture.

**Real-world performance**

Butter slime is a different beast than standard clear or glue-based slime. It's spreadable, almost dough-like, and doesn't snap or stretch the way other formulas do. The FunKidz version has a soft, pliable consistency that holds its shape briefly before melting back down — satisfying in a way that's hard to explain until you've squished it. The foam beads add a satisfying crunch when pressed, and the clay lets kids build actual mini "ice cream scoops."

The texture holds up for multiple play sessions. One buyer noted, "My 11-year-old has had this for three weeks and it's still soft and stretchy. Other brands dried out in days." The sealed containers genuinely make a difference here. If your kid re-lids them properly, this kit has legs.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The most common thread across positive reviews is the "giftability" factor. The packaging is presentable — this looks like a proper present, not a plastic bag of goo. One parent wrote, "Bought for a birthday party and every girl wanted one." Another mentioned, "The amount of slime is generous. My daughter and her friend could each use their own container without fighting over it."

The texture feedback is also overwhelmingly positive. Butter slime fans specifically call out the softness and the way it doesn't stick to hands as aggressively as traditional slime. That's a real advantage — less residue means less "MOM I NEED WIPES" screaming from the living room.

**Where it falls short**

The chemical smell is the most consistent complaint. It's not overpowering, but it's definitely there — a plasticky, sweet scent that doesn't match the ice cream theme. Letting the containers sit open for 10-15 minutes before play helps significantly. Also, the "ice cream" branding is purely aesthetic. There's no strawberry scent in the pink slime or vanilla in the white. If your kid expects scratch-and-sniff, they'll be disappointed.

The oily residue is another tradeoff. Butter slime contains more oil-based ingredients than standard formulas, which is why it stays soft — but it leaves a slight slick on hands. It's not greasy enough to ruin furniture, but plan for hand-washing after play. One review mentioned, "It leaves a bit of oil on the table, but nothing a wipe can't handle."

**Who should buy this**

This kit is ideal for tween girls who are already into slime and want something that feels more premium than the dollar-store versions. It's also a genuinely good birthday-party or sleepover activity — the six containers mean multiple kids can play simultaneously without territorial disputes. If you're looking for a screen-free activity that occupies a 10–12-year-old for an afternoon, this fits the bill.

Skip it if your kid is over slime entirely (it happens faster than you'd think) or if you're looking for a scent-based sensory experience. Also, if you have a younger child who still puts things in their mouth, the small mix-in beads make this a no-go.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does this slime dry out?* Not if you seal the containers after use. The airtight lids are genuinely effective. Left open overnight, any slime will dry — this one is no exception, but it recovers better than most if you knead it back together.

*Is it safe for sensitive skin?* The slime is non-toxic, but the oil content can bother kids with eczema or very sensitive skin. Test a small patch first if your child has known reactions to lotions or cosmetics.

*How many kids can play with this at once?* Six containers means six kids, easily. The mix-ins are shared, but each kid gets their own slime base.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a substantial, multi-kid slime experience that won't dry out in a week. It's a solid value at $23.39 for the volume you're getting.

**Skip it if:** Your kid is lukewarm on slime, or you need something scent-specific. The chemical smell and oily residue are real, if manageable, drawbacks.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely good butter slime kit that respects your money. Just air it out before the first play session.
