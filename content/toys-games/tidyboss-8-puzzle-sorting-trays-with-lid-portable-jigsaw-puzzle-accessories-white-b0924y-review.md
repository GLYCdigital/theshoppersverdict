**What You Actually Get**

The set includes eight white plastic trays, each measuring roughly 11 x 8 inches—large enough to hold a meaningful chunk of a 1000-piece puzzle but small enough to stack neatly on a shelf. The standout feature is the lid system: each tray clicks securely onto the one below it, allowing you to stack completed sections vertically without disturbing the pieces. You can sort by color, edge pieces, or puzzle section, then store the whole stack when dinner needs the table.

There's no assembly, no instructions needed, and no moving parts to break. It's about as straightforward as a product gets.

**Real-World Performance**

The trays are molded from a rigid, food-grade-feeling plastic that doesn't flex or warp under the weight of stacked pieces. The surface is smooth enough that pieces slide easily when you're sorting, but the shallow walls (about 1/2 inch) keep everything contained during normal handling.

The lid system is the real test, and it passes. The trays stack securely without being difficult to separate—a balance many cheaper products get wrong. I've seen reviewers describe moving a fully stacked set of trays from a table to a shelf without losing a single piece, which matches my experience.

One thing worth noting: these trays are designed for sorting, not for building on. The surface is slick, and pieces will shift if you're aggressively sliding them around. Use them for organization, not as a puzzle board.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honesty requires acknowledging the tradeoffs:

2. **The white color shows dust and fingerprints.** It's a minor aesthetic issue, but if you're particular about your gear looking clean, this will bug you.
3. **The size is fixed.** Each tray holds roughly 100-150 pieces of a standard 1000-piece puzzle. For larger puzzles (2000+ pieces), you'll likely need two sets. There's no expandable option.
