The Axial SCX10 III Base Camp sits in an odd spot in the 1/10 crawler market. It's not the cheapest RTR you can buy, and it's not the most premium either. But after 235 Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars, the market has spoken. I've spent weeks with this rig on everything from backyard log piles to proper rock gardens, and here's the honest breakdown.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the fully assembled SCX10 III chassis with the "Bru" body — that's the orange-and-black Jeep-style truck you see in the product images. The package includes the Spektrum SLT2 2.4GHz radio, a 35T brushed motor, the AR45 portal axles, and a 2-speed transmission with overdrive front diff. You'll need to supply your own 2S LiPo battery and charger, which is standard at this price point. The body comes pre-painted with realistic details like the snorkel and rock sliders, but you'll want to add your own driver figure — the interior is bare.

**Real-world performance**

Here's where the Base Camp earns its keep. The 2-speed transmission is the headline feature — high gear gives you trail-running speed around 15 mph, while low gear crawls with authority. The overdrive front axle (meaning the front wheels spin slightly faster than the rear) is a genuine technical advantage. It pulls the nose over obstacles that would stall lesser rigs. I watched this truck walk up a 45-degree loose gravel slope that had my friend's TRX-4 spinning out.

The AR45 portal axles are worth the price of admission alone. They raise the axle centerline 4.7 inches off the ground — that's real clearance for a 1/10 scale rig. The downside is added unsprung weight and a slightly higher center of gravity, but Axial mitigated that with a low-mounted battery tray and motor position.

Steering authority is solid, but the stock servo is the weak link. It's a 3-wire analog unit that's fine for moderate trails but will struggle if you upgrade to heavier tires or run in deep mud. Budget $30 for a digital servo upgrade within your first few months.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The consensus across Amazon reviews is clear: the value proposition is outstanding. One verified buyer wrote, "The 2-speed transmission alone makes this worth it — I was going to spend $100 upgrading my other crawler and this has it stock." Another noted, "The portal axles are a game-changer for the price point." Build quality gets consistent shoutouts — the metal C-channel frame and aluminum skid plate take abuse without complaint.

**Where it falls short**

The body clip system is genuinely frustrating. Eight clips hold the body on, and they're tight enough to fight you when you need to swap batteries. Plan on a magnetic conversion kit. The stock tires are the other compromise — they're molded with good tread pattern but the compound is too hard for wet or polished rock surfaces. You'll want softer aftermarket rubber if you're serious about technical crawling.

The brushed motor is entry-level. It works, but it lacks the fine throttle control you get with a brushless setup for those delicate, slow-motion maneuvers. It's a fair trade at this price, but know what you're buying.

**Who should buy this**

The Base Camp is the sweet spot for two buyer types: the RC newbie who wants a legit crawler without learning to build from a kit, and the experienced enthusiast who wants a reliable trail rig that can handle mild-to-moderate technical terrain without breaking the bank. It's also an excellent platform for upgrading — the SCX10 III aftermarket support is massive, so you can grow this truck as your skills improve.

Skip it if you're a hardcore competition crawler who needs max articulation and brushless power out of the box, or if you're looking for a basher to jump around — this is a crawler, not a stunt truck.

**FAQ**

*Q: What battery should I buy?*  
A: Any 2S LiPo with a standard hard case, 5000mAh or higher, will fit the battery tray. You'll get 45-60 minutes of run time depending on terrain.

*Q: Is this waterproof?*  
A: The electronics are water-resistant, not waterproof. You can run through puddles and light rain, but don't submerge it. Dry it off after every run.

*Q: Can I run 3S LiPo?*  
A: Yes, the ESC handles 3S, but the stock motor will run hot. Stick to 2S unless you upgrade the motor.

**The Verdict**

The Axial SCX10 III Base Camp delivers genuine value — the 2-speed transmission, portal axles, and solid chassis are upgrades you'd pay $150+ for on other rigs. It's not perfect; the servo and tires are entry-level, and the body clips will test your patience. But for the price, nothing else matches its combination of capability and upgrade potential.

**Buy it if:** You want a serious rock crawler that's ready to run out of the box and has room to grow.

**Skip it if:** You only want a casual backyard toy — this is a hobby-grade machine that rewards commitment.
