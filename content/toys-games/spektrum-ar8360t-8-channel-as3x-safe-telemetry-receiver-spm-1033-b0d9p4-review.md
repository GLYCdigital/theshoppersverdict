If you're flying anything larger than a UMX park flyer, the receiver is the nervous system of your aircraft. The Spektrum AR8360T+ packs AS3X+ stabilization, SAFE flight envelopes, and full telemetry into a 17-gram package that's become the default upgrade for sport and scale models. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but it's not plug-and-play for everyone.

## What's Actually in the Box

The AR8360T+ ships as a bare receiver with two satellite antennas, a bind button, and a connector for optional remote receivers. That's it. No case, no mounting tape, no Y-harness. If you're replacing a stock receiver in an ARF or bind-n-fly model, you'll need to supply your own double-sided tape and decide how to route those antennas. The unit measures roughly 1.5 x 1 x 0.5 inches and weighs about 17 grams — light enough for 1.2m to 2m sport planes without affecting CG.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature here is AS3X+, which is Spektrum's next-gen stabilization algorithm. It's noticeably smoother than the original AS3X — less "twitchy" on fast servos and better at filtering out airframe vibration. In flight, you'll feel it as a planted, locked-in feel, particularly in gusty conditions. The SAFE mode gives you self-leveling and bank-angle limits, which is a genuine safety net if you're transitioning from a trainer to something faster.

The telemetry suite is comprehensive: receiver voltage, flight battery voltage (via a separate wire), current draw, RPM, and altitude all stream back to compatible transmitters. Setup for basic telemetry is straightforward — bind it, assign the channels, fly. The forward programming interface lets you tweak gains and SAFE parameters from the transmitter, which is far more convenient than the old PC-based software.

One thing buyers consistently mention: the build quality feels solid. The case is robust, the antenna connectors are properly secured, and the binding process is reliable — a common complaint with cheaper receivers. As one pilot put it, "It bound on the first try and has been flawless for 30+ flights."

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest gotcha is transmitter compatibility. To unlock AS3X+ and forward programming, you need a Spektrum Gen 2 or newer transmitter (iX series, NX series, or DX series with the latest firmware). If you're flying an older DX6 or DX8, you'll get basic stabilization but lose the ability to tune it — which defeats half the purpose of buying this over a cheaper fixed-gain receiver.

The second issue is the learning curve. Forward programming is powerful, but the menu tree is dense. First-time users consistently report needing the manual open during initial setup. It's not intuitive, and if you're not comfortable with terms like "gain priority" and "heading hold," budget an evening to get it dialed in.

Also worth noting: the AR8360T+ doesn't include a case or mounting hardware, which feels stingy at this price point. And while the telemetry is excellent, the current sensor requires an optional adapter for most setups — factor that into your total cost.

## Who Should Buy This

This receiver is ideal for the intermediate pilot upgrading a sport or scale model who wants reliable stabilization plus telemetry in one unit. It's also a smart pick for someone stepping up from a trainer who wants SAFE as a backup while learning faster flight. Beginners flying their first 1.2m model will find it overkill — a basic AR631 would suffice. And if you're on an older non-Gen 2 transmitter, look elsewhere.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the AR8360T+ work with my DX6 G2 transmitter?**
A: Yes, for basic AS3X and telemetry. But to access AS3X+ tuning and forward programming, you need a Gen 2 transmitter with updated firmware (NX, iX, or DX Gen 2 series). Check your transmitter's firmware version before buying.

**Q: Can I use this receiver in a helicopter or multirotor?**
A: It's designed for fixed-wing aircraft. The AS3X+ algorithm is tuned for airplanes, and there's no heli-specific governor mode. For helis, look at Spektrum's dedicated line.

**Q: How many satellites can I connect?**
A: You can add up to two remote receivers (sold separately) for expanded channel count. With two satellites, you get 12 total channels, which covers most complex scale setups.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have a Gen 2 Spektrum transmitter, you want AS3X+ stabilization, and you value telemetry without running a separate sensor unit. It's a genuine upgrade over older receivers.

**Skip it if:** You're on legacy transmitter hardware, you're a strict beginner, or you're budget-conscious — the AR631 covers basic stabilization at half the cost.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The AR8360T+ is the best all-in-one receiver Spektrum makes for sport flyers, but only if you're already invested in the modern transmitter ecosystem. It's a smart upgrade, not a necessary one.
