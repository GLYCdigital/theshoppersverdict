Let's be honest about what this is: a gamble wrapped in plastic. The Pokemon TCG Mega Evolution: Ascended Heroes 2-Pack Blister gives you two booster packs from the Prismatic Evolutions-era Mega Evolution set, plus one random foil promo card from a pool of six. That's the entire product. No coins, no dice, no oversized card. Just three shots at something good.

**What's actually in the box**

The blister contains two standard 10-card booster packs from the Mega Evolution: Ascended Heroes expansion, and one promo card in a protective sleeve. The six possible promos are: Mewtwo EX, Gardevoir EX, Gengar EX, Tyranitar EX, Scizor EX, and Aerodactyl EX. All are foil, all are playable in some format, and all carry real secondary-market value — ranging from roughly $3 for the lower-tier options up to $15+ for Mewtwo EX on a good day.

The pack itself uses the standard 5-common, 3-uncommon structure with one rare-or-better slot and one basic energy. You're not getting any guaranteed reverse holos beyond the standard slot. That's worth knowing going in.

**Performance and pull rates**

Here's the hard truth about this set: the Mega Evolution cards are legitimately strong, but the hit rate isn't generous. Based on opening data from collectors, you're looking at roughly a 1-in-7 chance per pack of pulling an EX or better. That means across your two packs, you've got about a 26% chance of hitting something substantial. The other 74% of the time? You're getting two packs of bulk and a promo that might be worth less than the price of a coffee.

That said, the promo is the real value anchor here. One buyer put it plainly: "Bought it for the promo, got a Mewtwo EX worth almost as much as I paid. The packs were trash but the promo made it worth it." That's the experience you're buying into. The two packs are essentially a bonus gamble; the promo is the known quantity.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The blister packaging itself gets consistent credit. Cards arrive flat and undamaged — a real issue with loose packs that get crushed in mail sorting. Multiple reviewers noted this specifically: "Came in perfect condition. No bent corners, no creased packs." For collectors who care about pack integrity, that matters.

The promo selection is also well-regarded. All six options are competitively relevant, and none of them are outright duds. Even the "worst" promo, Aerodactyl EX, sees play in certain decks and holds a few dollars of value.

**Where it falls short**

The pricing on Amazon is the biggest issue. This blister has an MSRP around $12, but third-party sellers routinely list it at $16-$20. One frustrated reviewer wrote: "Great product, terrible price. Paid $18 and got $4 worth of cards." That's the risk of buying collectibles on Amazon — you're at the mercy of the current seller's markup.

The other honest complaint: no guarantee of anything exciting. If you're buying this hoping for a Mega Charizard pull, you'll likely be disappointed. The set doesn't include Charizard at all, and the overall hit rate means most buyers will walk away with nothing flashy from the packs themselves.

**Who should buy this**

Grab this if you're a player looking for specific Mega Evolution staples, a collector who wants the promo cards without chasing loose packs, or a parent buying a low-stakes treat for a kid who just wants to open something. Skip it if you're chasing specific hits, expecting retail pricing on Amazon, or hoping for a guaranteed chase card.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are the packs weighed or tampered with?**
A: No. The blister packaging is sealed and tamper-evident. The foil promo and both packs are visible through the plastic, so you can verify everything is intact before opening.

**Q: Can I get the same cards in regular Prismatic Evolutions packs?**
A: Mostly yes. The Mega Evolution: Ascended Heroes set shares its card pool with the broader Prismatic Evolutions line. The exclusives here are the six promo cards — those only come in this blister format.

**Q: Is this a good value for a kid's first pack?**
A: Reasonable. You get three cards with guaranteed foil treatment (two in the packs, one promo), which feels exciting for younger players. Just manage expectations that they might not pull an EX.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want the promo cards, need Mega Evolution staples for deck-building, or want a guaranteed-foil product without the lottery pressure of single packs. **Skip it if** you're chasing specific hits, refuse to pay above MSRP, or expect a 50% hit rate from your two packs.

This is a solid 4-star product that gets dinged primarily on Amazon's pricing volatility. At MSRP, it's a great value. At $18+ from a third-party seller, you're overpaying for the privilege of gambling. The promos are the star here, and as long as you treat the booster packs as a bonus rather than the main event, you'll be satisfied.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Worth the gamble at fair pricing, the promo cards carry the value, and the packaging quality is genuinely better than loose packs. Just watch what you pay.
