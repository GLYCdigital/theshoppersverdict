Let’s be blunt: the Cerastus Knight Lancer isn’t a starter kit. It’s a statement piece. If you’re here, you already know you want a towering war machine that makes your opponent audibly groan when it hits the table. The question is whether this specific plastic kit justifies its premium price and assembly effort. After handling the sprue, building it, and fielding it across a dozen games, here’s the honest breakdown.

## What’s Actually in the Box

Games Workshop packs this as a multi-part plastic kit. You get the full Cerastus Knight chassis, the iconic shock lance, a choice of head variants, and a couple of cosmetic options for the carapace. The kit includes a 170mm oval base — which you’ll need, because this thing has serious presence. As shown in the product image, the finished model towers over standard Space Marines. In 40k scale, that’s roughly 9 inches of angry metal.

The sprue quality is typical modern Games Workshop — crisp detail, minimal flash, and plastic that cuts cleanly. There’s no resin anywhere, which is a blessing for anyone who’s dealt with warped Forge World kits in the past.

## Build Quality and Assembly

This is where the kit shines and frustrates in equal measure. The engineering is genuinely impressive. Panels interlock with satisfying precision, and the leg joints articulate naturally. Most reviewers agree — one Amazon buyer noted, “The fit is so good I barely needed any gap filler, which is rare for a kit this size.”

But here’s the honest tradeoff: the pose is largely fixed. The torso, legs, and lance arm have limited swivel, and the left arm is basically glued in one position. You’re not building a dynamic, mid-lunge monster unless you’re comfortable with cutting and pinning. The instructions are clear, but this is a multi-hour project. Budget a weekend if you want it painted to a tabletop standard.

## Performance on the Tabletop

In both Horus Heresy and 40k, the Lancer is a melee terror. It moves fast for its size, and the shock lance hits like a freight train. In my games, it consistently deleted enemy armor and heavy infantry in a single charge. It’s not subtle — it’s a wrecking ball with a target painted on it. Expect your opponent to focus-fire it turn one. That’s fine; it’s doing its job by drawing fire.

## What Buyers Love

The overwhelming praise centers on the model’s visual impact and build quality. A recurring theme in the 229 Amazon ratings: “It’s the centerpiece of my army and absolutely worth the money.” Another buyer mentioned, “The detail is incredible — every panel, every rivet feels intentional.” The dual-system compatibility is also a frequent highlight, letting you run it in two game systems with zero conversion work.

## Where It Falls Short

The price is the obvious hurdle. This is not a budget impulse buy — it’s a deliberate investment. If you’re new to Warhammer, this is not your first kit. The fixed pose also limits hobbyists who love converting. And honestly, the instructions could be better at highlighting the optional build paths; I had to check online forums to figure out one of the head swaps.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The competitive player**: If you want a reliable, hard-hitting melee unit for your Knights list, this delivers.
- **The hobbyist**: If you love assembling and painting a showpiece that draws eyes at your local store, this is a top-tier canvas.
- **The collector**: If you’re building a display shelf, the Lancer’s presence is unmatched at this scale.

Skip it if you’re on a tight budget, new to the hobby, or prefer fully poseable kits for custom work.

## FAQ

**Is the Cerastus Knight Lancer good in 40k?**
Yes, especially in Chaos Knights or Imperial Knights lists that want a fast, aggressive melee threat. It’s less versatile than a Questoris class but far more deadly in close combat.

**Does it require painting?**
Yes, it comes unpainted and unassembled. You’ll need plastic glue, clippers, and paint supplies. This is not a play-out-of-the-box product.

**How tall is the finished model?**
Roughly 9 inches on its included 170mm oval base — it’s one of the largest standard plastic kits Games Workshop makes.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you want a tabletop centerpiece that performs as brutally as it looks. It’s a fantastic kit with modern engineering and genuine in-game muscle.

**Skip it if** you’re budget-conscious, a beginner, or demand full articulation from your builds. The fixed pose and premium price will frustrate you more than satisfy.

The Cerastus Knight Lancer is a premium kit for dedicated hobbyists. It earns its 4.6-star rating through sheer presence and quality — just know what you’re signing up for before you swipe that card.
