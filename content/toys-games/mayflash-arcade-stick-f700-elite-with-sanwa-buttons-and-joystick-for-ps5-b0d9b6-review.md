The MAYFLASH F700 Elite enters a crowded fight stick market with a simple promise: genuine Sanwa parts, multi-platform support, and a price that undercuts the big-name competitors. After digging through 220 Amazon ratings averaging 4.4 stars, here's the real story for anyone about to drop serious cash on their next arcade stick.

**What you actually get**

Out of the box, the F700 Elite includes the fight stick itself, a 4-meter braided USB-C cable, a wireless USB dongle, and a basic manual. The unit measures roughly 17 x 12 x 5 inches and weighs about 5.5 pounds — substantial enough to stay put during intense matches but light enough to rest on your lap without leg fatigue. The top panel is a glossy acrylic surface with the MAYFLASH logo and your standard 8-button layout plus a dedicated Options button and touchpad support for PS5.

The headline feature is the genuine Sanwa hardware inside. You're getting the OBSF-30 push buttons and the JLF-TP-8YT joystick — the exact components found in Japanese arcade cabinets. No knockoffs, no "Sanwa-style" substitutes. That matters because Sanwa parts have a distinct, satisfying feel: the buttons activate with a light, crisp press, and the joystick returns to neutral with a precise, springy snap. For players coming from budget sticks with mushy buttons, the difference is immediately noticeable.

**Performance and real-world use**

I tested the F700 Elite across PC, Switch, and PS5. Wired mode is plug-and-play with zero input lag — the Sanwa parts register inputs exactly when you press them. Wireless via the 2.4GHz dongle felt equally responsive in my testing; Bluetooth 5.0 worked fine but is best reserved for casual play where a few milliseconds won't decide the match.

The 1000mAh battery delivers roughly 20 hours of wireless play, and charging via USB-C takes about 3 hours. There's a battery indicator LED, which is a thoughtful touch that many competitors omit.

Here's the critical caveat: PS5 compatibility is limited. The F700 Elite works on PS5 *only* for fighting games and similar titles that support legacy arcade controllers. You cannot use it to navigate the PS5 dashboard, and it won't work with non-fighting PS5 games. This is a firmware limitation, not a hardware one — if you're primarily a Street Fighter or Tekken player on PS5, you're fine. If you're hoping to use this across your entire PS5 library, look elsewhere.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The Amazon reviews are overwhelmingly positive on one specific point: the Sanwa parts. One reviewer wrote, "The buttons feel exactly like the arcade cabinets I grew up with — crisp, responsive, and they don't stick after months of use." Another noted, "I've owned sticks twice this price and these Sanwa parts feel identical. MAYFLASH didn't cut corners here."

The multi-platform support also earns frequent praise. As one buyer put it, "Switching between my PC and Switch is just a toggle flip. No re-pairing, no headaches. This is how wireless should work."

**Where it falls short**

The glossy top panel is the most consistent complaint. It's a smudge magnet — after one session, the surface looks like you handled it with greasy fingers. It's cosmetic, not functional, but it's worth knowing if you care about aesthetics.

The stock spring in the Sanwa JLF is the light 2lb option. That's authentic to Japanese arcades, but American players often prefer a 4lb spring for a stiffer, more deliberate feel. Swapping it takes about 10 minutes with a screwdriver, but it's an extra step and an extra purchase.

There's also no carrying case included. At this price point, that's an omission. You'll need to buy a separate bag for transport.

**Who should buy this**

- **Competitive fighting game players** on PS5 who play SF6, Tekken 8, or Guilty Gear — the Sanwa parts give you tournament-grade performance at a mid-tier price
- **Multi-platform players** who switch between PC and Switch and want one stick that covers both without adapter headaches
- **Arcade purists** who want genuine Sanwa hardware without paying the $250+ premium for brands like Hori or Razer

**Skip it if** you need full PS5 compatibility across your entire game library, or if you're a casual player who won't notice the difference between Sanwa and cheaper parts — in that case, a $60-80 budget stick will serve you fine.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does this work on Xbox?* No. The F700 Elite supports PS5, PC, and Switch only. For Xbox, you'd need MAYFLASH's separate F700X model.

*Can I mod the buttons and joystick?* Yes. The Sanwa parts use standard 30mm button sizing and the JLF joystick is the industry standard for modding. Swapping parts is straightforward — just remove the bottom plate and unplug/replug the connectors.

*Is wireless input lag noticeable?* In my testing, the 2.4GHz wireless felt indistinguishable from wired. Bluetooth had a slightly more relaxed feel but remained playable. For tournament play, use wired or the 2.4GHz dongle.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want genuine Sanwa performance across PS5, PC, and Switch without paying flagship prices. **Skip it if** you need full PS5 library compatibility or want a ready-to-go travel setup with a case included.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The F700 Elite delivers exactly what it promises: authentic arcade feel, solid wireless, and honest multi-platform support. The PS5 limitations and missing case keep it from perfection, but for fighting game enthusiasts, this is a genuinely smart buy.
