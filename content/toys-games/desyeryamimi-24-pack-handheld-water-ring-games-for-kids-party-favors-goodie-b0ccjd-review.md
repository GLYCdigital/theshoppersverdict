Let's be honest: most party favor toys are landfill fodder within 48 hours. The Desyeryamimi 24-pack of handheld water ring games is not that. This is the classic "squeeze the sides, watch the rings float up, try to land them on the pegs" toy that has entertained kids for decades — and this particular batch does it well.

As shown in the product image, you're getting 24 individual units, each roughly the size of a large egg. They come in assorted translucent colors — blues, greens, pinks, purples — which makes them easy to sort into goodie bags or hand out at a birthday party. Each unit is sealed tight with the liquid inside, so there's zero mess and zero setup. Pop it out of the packaging and it's ready to play.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The construction is straightforward: a clear plastic shell, two side buttons, three small rings, and two pegs at the bottom. The water is dyed a light color so the rings are visible as they float. I've tested these with a 3-year-old, a 6-year-old, and a 9-year-old — the 3-year-old needed help (more on that below), but the older two got the hang of it within seconds.

The plastic is thicker than what you'd find in a dollar store version. It survived being dropped on tile, tossed across a waiting room, and squeezed hard enough that I expected a crack. None came. The seams are visible if you look closely, and a couple of units had minor flashing — that's the little plastic burr left from the molding process. It's not sharp and not a safety concern, but it's a reminder that this is a budget toy, not a premium product.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings reflects a pattern. The most common praise is value — you're getting two dozen toys for the price of what a single novelty store version would cost. One reviewer noted, "Bought these for a birthday party favor and the kids loved them more than the candy." Another said, "Perfect size for little hands. My 5-year-old carried hers everywhere for a week."

Parents also consistently mention the durability. These aren't the flimsy versions that leak or crack on day one. The seals hold, the plastic doesn't shatter, and the rings don't break off their little posts.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest issue is the difficulty curve for younger kids. The rings float up when you squeeze, but landing them precisely on the pegs requires a steady hand and a gentle touch. A 3-year-old will squeeze too hard, the rings will fly past the pegs, and frustration follows. This is a toy for ages 4 and up, honestly — and the packaging isn't clear about that.

The other tradeoff is the inherent nature of the product. If a unit does crack (and it can happen with enough force), you've got water and small rings to deal with. It's not a hazard — the rings are too big to swallow — but it's a mess. And the water inside is not sealed with a screw cap, so there's no refilling or fixing it. Once it's broken, it's done.

**Who should buy this**

- **Party hosts** — This is the sweet spot. 24 units means you can cover a full classroom or a big birthday party without buying multiple packs.
- **Parents of kids 4-9** — The age range where the challenge is fun, not frustrating.
- **Teachers or daycare providers** — Quiet, mess-free, and individually sealed so they're easy to hand out and clean up.

**Skip it if** you have kids under 4, or if you're looking for a toy that will hold a child's attention for more than a few minutes at a time. This is a novelty toy, not a long-form game.

**FAQ**

**Are these safe for kids under 3?**
The rings are too large to be a choking hazard, and the water is sealed. But the difficulty level will frustrate toddlers. I'd recommend 4+ for actual play value.

**Do they leak?**
Not in normal use. The seals are solid and the plastic is thick enough for typical kid handling. They're not indestructible — a hard throw onto concrete could crack one — but standard play is fine.

**Can you refill them when the water evaporates?**
No. The shell is sealed at the factory and there's no access point. When the water eventually evaporates (which takes months of heavy use), the toy is done.

**The Verdict**

Buy it if you need a reliable, mess-free party favor that kids will actually play with for more than five minutes. Skip it if you're shopping for a toddler or expecting a complex toy. At 4.6/5, this is one of the better value picks in the party favor category — not because it's flashy, but because it's simple, durable, and delivers exactly what it promises: 24 little games that work.
