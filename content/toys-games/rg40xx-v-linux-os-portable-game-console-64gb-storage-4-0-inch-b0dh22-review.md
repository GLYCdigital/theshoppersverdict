The RG40XX V is the handheld that keeps showing up in "best budget emulator" conversations, and after spending time with it, I get why. It's a vertical handheld with a 4-inch IPS screen, a Linux-based OS, and 64GB of onboard storage out of the box. But here's the thing — this isn't a device for first-timers who want zero setup friction. It's for people who like to tinker. Let me break down exactly what you're getting.

## What's in the Box

The package includes the console itself, a USB-C charging cable, a 64GB microSD card (pre-loaded), and a basic but functional manual. No case, no screen protector, no wall adapter — you'll need your own USB-C charger. The 64GB card is a genuine plus at this price point; many competitors ship 32GB or nothing.

The build is solid. The plastic shell feels dense, not creaky, and the vertical Game Boy-style layout is comfortable for extended sessions. At roughly 165g, it's light enough to pocket but substantial enough to feel like a real device.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The RK3326 chipset inside is a known quantity in the retro handheld world. It handles everything up to PlayStation 1 flawlessly, including enhanced resolution and some texture filtering. PSP and N64 are playable but inconsistent — lighter titles work well, heavier ones need frame-skip tweaks. Dreamcast is a stretch. If you're buying this for anything beyond PS1, you're asking too much.

What surprised me most is the screen. The 4-inch 640x480 IPS panel is genuinely excellent for retro content. Colors pop, viewing angles are wide, and the 4:3 aspect ratio means no awkward black bars on classic systems. As shown in the product image, the display dominates the front face — bezels are minimal.

The hall-effect joysticks are a standout feature. They're smooth, accurate, and drift-resistant by design. For $60-ish handhelds, most use cheap potentiometer sticks that wear out in months. These should last years.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 215 Amazon ratings, a few themes emerge. The screen gets near-universal love — one buyer wrote, "The display is shockingly good for the price. PS1 games look like I remember them, not like muddy emulation." Another praised the out-of-box experience: "Had 5,000+ games on the card already. I charged it, turned it on, and was playing Mario Kart in two minutes."

The Linux OS gets mixed reviews, but those who've flashed custom firmware are emphatic. A verified purchase noted, "Stock OS is fine, but I installed MuOS in 20 minutes and now it boots in 8 seconds with better battery. Night and day."

## Where It Falls Short

The stock firmware is the weakest link. It works, but it's slow to boot, the menu navigation is clunky, and some emulator cores feel under-tuned. If you're not comfortable flashing a microSD card with custom firmware, you're leaving performance on the table.

Battery life is serviceable, not spectacular. Realistic expectations are 5-6 hours on 2D systems and closer to 4 hours on PS1 with enhanced resolution. That's enough for most commutes and flights, but the Anbernic competition edges it out here.

The shoulder buttons are the one clear hardware miss. They're positioned fine but have a shallow, mushy actuation that feels out of place next to the crisp face buttons.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Retro enthusiasts** who want a dedicated PS1-and-below machine with a top-tier screen
- **Tinkerers** who enjoy customizing firmware and optimizing emulator settings
- **Commuters** who want a pocketable, long-lasting option for 2D classics

Skip it if you want modern game streaming, N64/PSP as a priority, or a zero-setup experience out of the box.

## FAQ

**Can it run PSP and N64 games?**
Yes, but with caveats. 2D PSP titles and lighter N64 games run well. Demanding 3D titles like God of War or Conker's Bad Fur Day will need frame-skip and reduced resolution. Manage your expectations.

**Does the 64GB card come with games?**
Yes, it's pre-loaded with thousands of titles across 20+ systems. The legality of ROM distribution is a gray area, and the card's included games are a mix of public domain, homebrew, and commercial titles. Many buyers replace the card anyway for better quality.

**Is this good for kids?**
The Linux OS is not kid-friendly. There's a learning curve, and the pre-loaded library includes games for mature audiences. If it's for a child, you'll want to curate the card yourself.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want the best sub-$70 retro handheld for PS1 and below, and you're willing to spend 30 minutes flashing better firmware. The screen, build, and hall-effect sticks make it a genuinely great value.

**Skip it if** you need N64/PSP performance or want a "just works" experience with zero setup. The RG40XX V rewards effort — it's a tinkerer's machine.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A nearly excellent budget handheld held back by mediocre stock firmware and a few hardware niggles. With a little customization, it punches well above its price class.
