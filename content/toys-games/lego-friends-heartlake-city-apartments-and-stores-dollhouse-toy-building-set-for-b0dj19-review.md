**The Short Version:** This is the LEGO Friends set that actually delivers on the "city" promise. Four stories of apartments above three storefronts, connected by a working elevator and a slide that drops minidolls from the top floor to the street. It's big, it's busy, and it's exactly what a LEGO Friends fan who's outgrown the small sets wants.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get a substantial build here — roughly 1,000 pieces that assemble into a two-building structure. The main tower houses four floors: a top-level apartment with a bedroom, a middle floor with a living space, and below that, two more floors of living quarters. The ground level splits into three storefronts: a bakery, a fashion boutique, and a smoothie shop. Each has its own entrance, signage, and stocked interior.

The minifigure count is generous: eight minidolls across three friend groups (the Heartlake City regulars plus new characters), along with four animal figures — a cat, a dog, and two birds. Every apartment floor has its own furniture: beds, kitchen counters, a bathtub, desks, and the little detail pieces LEGO does well, like a laptop, a coffee mug, and a plant.

**Build and Play Experience**

Assembling this takes real time. Expect three to four hours spread across a couple of sessions. The instruction booklet is clear, and the modular design means you can build each floor separately before stacking — which helps if you're working on a coffee table rather than a dedicated play space.

The standout feature is the elevator. It's a simple pulley mechanism, but kids actually use it — they're not just moving minidolls by hand. The slide from the top floor is also a hit. My test group of 7- and 9-year-olds spent more time sending dolls down that slide than doing anything else.

The modularity is a genuine strength. Each floor lifts off independently, and the buildings split into two halves. You can create a smaller apartment-only setup or a standalone storefront if space is tight. For parents worried about a giant assembled set taking over the living room, this flexibility matters.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The 4.7-star rating holds up. A common thread in reviews: "My 8-year-old daughter spent hours on this and then continued playing with it for weeks." Another parent noted, "The elevator actually works smoothly, which surprised me — most LEGO mechanisms feel flimsy." The variety of play spaces gets consistent mention — the combination of living areas, shops, and the slide keeps kids rotating between activities rather than losing interest after one setup.

**Where It Falls Short**

The interior lighting is the biggest miss. The floors are enclosed, and once you stack them, the back rooms get dark. LEGO sells light kits separately, but at this price point, a simple light brick would have been nice. Expect to play in a well-lit room or pull floors apart to see details.

The sticker sheet is also extensive. Around 30 stickers for storefront signs, food items, and wall decorations. They're applied during the build, and kids who misalign them will notice. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

Finally, the price. This sits in the upper tier of LEGO Friends sets. If you're buying for a child under 7, the piece count and small accessories (the smoothie cups and bakery items are tiny) could be frustrating. This is a set for the 7-12 range, ideally for kids who've already handled smaller LEGO builds confidently.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The LEGO Friends devotee** who's built the smaller sets and wants something with real scope
- **Parents of siblings** — the eight minidolls and multiple rooms mean two or three kids can play simultaneously without fighting over pieces
- **Anyone looking for a multi-session project** — this is a weekend build, not a one-hour activity

**Skip it if** your child prefers quick builds, or if you need something that fits on a small shelf when finished. This set is roughly 12 inches tall and 10 inches wide when fully assembled — it earns its footprint.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does it take to build?**
A: Plan for 3-4 hours total. The four floors are similar in construction, so the second half goes faster once you get the rhythm of the build. Older kids (10+) can manage it solo; younger ones will need intermittent help.

**Q: Does it connect with other LEGO Friends sets?**
A: Yes. The base uses standard LEGO stud dimensions, so it pairs with other Friends buildings. The modular floor design means you can swap floors between compatible sets, though the color scheme is specific to this model.

**Q: How durable is the elevator mechanism?**
A: It's a simple string-and-pulley system. It works well and held up through a week of testing, but it's not motorized. If a kid yanks the string hard, it can jam — a gentle pull works reliably.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely expansible playset that rewards multiple play sessions and can be reconfigured. **Skip it if** you're on a tight budget or need something compact.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The elevator and slide mechanics are the differentiators here; most competing dollhouses at this price are static. It's not perfect — the lighting issue and sticker count are real — but for the target age group, this is one of the most playable LEGO Friends sets currently available.
