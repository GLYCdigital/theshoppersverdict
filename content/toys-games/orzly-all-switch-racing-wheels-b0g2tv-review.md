# Orzly ALL SWITCH RACING WHEELS: The $20 Accessory That Makes Mario Kart Feel New Again

Let’s be honest: Nintendo’s official Joy-Con wheel is fine, but it’s also flimsy, cramped, and only works with one console generation. The Orzly All Switch Racing Wheel fixes two of those three problems—and for half the price.

This is a passive shell. You snap your Joy-Cons into the central hub, grip the rubberized ring, and tilt to steer. But don’t let that simplicity fool you. After 20 hours of Mario Kart 8 Deluxe, F-Zero 99, and even some Hot Wheels Unleashed, I can tell you exactly where this wheel earns its 4.5-star rating—and where it doesn’t.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two identical wheel housings (left and right, color-matched to your Joy-Cons), each measuring 12.5 inches in diameter. The plastic is a hard ABS with a soft-touch rubber coating on the grip ring—not the slippery glossy stuff Nintendo uses. Each wheel has a center hub that clicks open like a clamshell, and you slide your Joy-Con in with the analog stick facing inward.

Critically, this works with *all* Switch models. Original, OLED, and Lite—the hub is sized to accept the standard Joy-Con dimensions, so there’s no fiddling with adapters. The Lite works because the Joy-Cons are built in; you’ll need to use the system’s tabletop mode or prop it up, but the wheel itself attaches cleanly.

## Build Quality and Real-World Feel

Here’s the thing that surprised me: the weight. Each wheel weighs about 8 ounces, so there’s actual mass behind your movements. That translates to less wrist fatigue during long sessions and more precise steering input—you’re not fighting a hollow toy.

The grip texture is the standout feature. It’s the same material used on quality bike grips, with a subtle diamond pattern that stays grippy even when your palms get sweaty during a frantic last-lap battle. The button access is honestly better than Nintendo’s official wheel: the L/R buttons sit directly under your index fingers, and the ZL/ZR triggers are reachable without shifting your grip. You can even hit the home button with your thumb without removing your hand.

One thing to note: the wheel is *only* for motion controls. Tilt left, tilt right, that’s it. If you prefer button steering, this won’t add anything.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews (225 ratings, 4.5 stars) cluster around three themes. First, compatibility—one reviewer wrote, *“Finally, a wheel that fits my OLED and my kid’s Lite. No more buying separate accessories.”* Second, the grip: *“The rubber actually stays put when my 7-year-old gets excited. The official Nintendo wheel slips right out of his hands.”* Third, value—multiple buyers note this is “the same quality as the $30 official wheel, but for half the price.”

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be real about the downsides. There’s no force feedback, no rumble, no programmable buttons. It’s a plastic shell that translates tilt into steering—nothing more. If you’re expecting a Logitech G923 experience, you’ll be disappointed.

The size is also a factor. Kids under 8 with smaller hands will struggle to wrap their fingers around the 12.5-inch ring comfortably. And the center hub adds about an inch of thickness, so you can’t store the wheel attached to your Switch in most carrying cases. You’ll need to pop the Joy-Cons out each time.

Finally, the wheel is loud. The clamshell click when you insert Joy-Cons is satisfying, but the plastic-on-plastic contact during aggressive tilts produces a hollow *clack* that might annoy people in the same room.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Mario Kart parents**: Buy this. It makes the game accessible for kids who struggle with button steering, and the grip means fewer dropped controllers.
- **Switch Lite owners**: This is the best motion-control option available—the official wheel doesn’t work with the Lite at all.
- **Party game hosts**: Two wheels in the box means you’re ready for head-to-head races immediately.

**Skip it if** you’re a competitive player who prefers button steering, or if you already own Nintendo’s official wheel and don’t need a second set.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with the Nintendo Switch Lite?**  
Yes. The Joy-Cons are built into the Lite, and the wheel’s clamshell design accommodates the slightly different dimensions. You’ll need to use tabletop mode, but the controls work perfectly.

**Q: Do the wheels work with all racing games?**  
Any game that supports motion-controlled steering. Mario Kart 8 Deluxe, F-Zero 99, Hot Wheels Unleashed, and Crash Team Racing all work. Games that don’t support motion controls (like most F1 titles) won’t respond.

**Q: Can I use this with one Joy-Con (vertical grip)?**  
No. The wheel requires both Joy-Cons docked into the hub. Single-Joy-Con play isn’t supported.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a budget-friendly, universally compatible motion-steering upgrade that actually feels good in your hands. The rubberized grip, solid weight, and cross-model compatibility make this the best sub-$25 racing wheel for the Switch.

**Skip it if** you need force feedback, have very small children, or prefer the precision of button steering.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It’s not a real racing wheel, but it’s the best toy-grade accessory of its kind, and at this price, the value is undeniable.
