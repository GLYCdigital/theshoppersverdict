Dominion didn't just popularize deck-building — it *invented* the genre. Over 15 years later, the 2nd Edition proves the original is still the benchmark. If you've never played a deck-builder, this is the game that started it all. If you have, this revised edition fixes most of the original's flaws while keeping everything that made it a legend.

**What's actually in the box**

Open it up and you're looking at 500 cards — that's not a typo. You get 26 different Kingdom card types (down from 25 in the original, but with better choices), plus the basic Treasure and Victory cards every player needs. Each game uses just 10 of those Kingdom types, which means the box offers roughly 10 million possible game combinations. That's not marketing math — that's real replayability.

The 2nd Edition also trims the fat. Gone are the clunky, situational cards like Woodcutter and Spy. In their place: cleaner designs like Sentry and Artisan that actually do what they're supposed to without requiring a PhD in card interactions. The card art got a subtle upgrade too, with more vibrant colors that make the table less of an eyesore during long sessions.

**How it actually plays**

The core loop is deceptively simple: each turn you play treasure cards to buy better cards, which go into your discard pile, which eventually cycles back into your hand. But the strategy runs deep. Do you blitz for cheap cards to churn your deck faster, or save for expensive power cards that transform your engine? Every game forces different answers because the 10 Kingdom cards change what's possible.

A typical game runs 30-45 minutes with 2-4 players. The rules are straightforward enough that most players grasp the basics by turn three, but the strategic depth keeps veterans coming back. The 2nd Edition also cleaned up the rulebook — it's actually readable now, with clearer examples and better card terminology.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.9-star rating across 245 reviews isn't accidental. Players consistently highlight the same strengths. One reviewer wrote, "This is the game that got my entire family into tabletop gaming. My 12-year-old beats me regularly, and my 70-year-old father holds his own." Another noted, "I've played 50+ games and still discover new strategies every time."

The replay value gets the most love. As one long-time player put it: "I played the original for a decade, and the 2nd Edition breathes new life into it. The card swaps make sense, and the new inclusions are genuinely better." The fast setup and tear-down also earn frequent mentions — you're playing within five minutes of opening the box.

**Where it falls short**

This isn't a game for players who want direct confrontation. There's no attacking, stealing, or blocking — you're building your engine in parallel with opponents, only interacting through contested card purchases. Competitive players might find it too solitaire-like.

The box insert is genuinely bad. The card wells are undersized, and if you sleeve your cards (which I recommend for a game this heavily shuffled), they won't fit back in the organizer. Plan on a separate card storage solution or be prepared to stack cards loosely.

The learning curve is real. The rulebook is improved, but there are still edge cases and card interactions that require checking the included reference cards. First-time players should expect a slow first game — budget an extra 20 minutes for teaching.

**Who should buy this**

**Buy it if** you're new to deck-builders and want the definitive starting point, or if you're a veteran who played the original and wants the improved version. It's ideal for families with kids 12+, casual game nights with 3-4 players, and anyone who values replay value over component bling.

**Skip it if** you hate games with minimal player interaction, need games with high-quality inserts, or already own the original plus expansions — the 2nd Edition is an upgrade, not a revolution.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is Dominion 2nd Edition compatible with the original expansions?**
A: Yes. All original Dominion expansions work with the 2nd Edition base set. However, some cards changed between editions, so you'll want to use the 2nd Edition versions when both exist.

**Q: How many players does it support?**
A: 2-4 players out of the box. You can push to 5-6 with expansions, but the game plays best with 3-4. Two-player games are surprisingly strategic.

**Q: Is this good for kids?**
A: The recommended age is 14+, but mature 10-12 year olds can handle it with adult guidance. The reading requirement is significant — every card has text — so younger kids may struggle.

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the weak insert and the passive player interaction, but this remains the gold standard for deck-building games. At $35-45, you're getting hundreds of hours of gameplay. If you own zero deck-builders, this is the one to buy. If you own the original, the 2nd Edition's card improvements justify the upgrade. Just budget for sleeves and a card box.
