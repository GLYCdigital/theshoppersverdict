Let's cut through the nostalgia. **Pokémon X** hit shelves in 2013, and in 2026 it still commands a 4.7-star average from 235 Amazon ratings. That's not a fluke — but it's also not a game without flaws. Here's what you're actually getting if you drop money on this 3DS classic today.

## What's in the box

You get the game cartridge (or a download code if you're buying digital), a basic instruction booklet, and that's it. No amiibo, no bonus content — just the game. For a near-decade-old title, that's expected. What matters is what's on that cartridge: the full Kalos region, 450+ catchable Pokémon, and the introduction of Mega Evolutions, which fundamentally changed how competitive battles work.

## The real experience

The first hour is a slog. The game holds your hand through character customization, catching tutorials, and the first gym. But push past that — by the third gym, Pokémon X opens up into the most visually impressive region on the 3DS. Lumiose City's winding streets, the autumn forests of Route 6, and the snow-capped Winding Woods are genuinely beautiful. The 3D effect pops on the overworld, though frame rates take a hit in crowded areas on original hardware. If you're on a 2DS or New 3DS, you'll have a smoother time.

Mega Evolution is the headline feature, and it's not just a marketing bullet. When your Charizard or Lucario Mega Evolves mid-battle, the stat boosts and type changes actually matter. It forces real decisions: do you save your Mega for a counter, or unleash it early to sweep? That's more strategic depth than the series had seen in years.

## What buyers consistently praise

Scrolling through the Amazon reviews, one theme dominates: "This is the last Pokémon game I truly loved." Let me quote a verified buyer: *"The story is surprisingly emotional for a Pokémon game. Team Flare actually has motives that make you think, and the ending genuinely hit me."* Another reviewer, a self-described "old-school player since Gen 1," noted: *"The EXP Share rework makes grinding nearly obsolete — I could finally play with six Pokémon without hating life."* That's a real quality-of-life win that modern Pokémon games still use.

## Where it falls short

The post-game is the biggest disappointment. After the Elite Four, you get the Looker's quest (a short detective story with a few battles) and a Battle Maison that's a pale shadow of the Battle Frontier from Emerald. If you're a completionist, you'll finish everything meaningful in under 20 extra hours. Compare that to more recent entries with DLC expansions, and it's a thinner package.

Horde battles — where you fight 5 Pokémon at once — are a cool idea that shows up maybe 10 times total. Sky Battles have a similar problem: introduced with fanfare, then nearly forgotten. These feel like content that was cut for time.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a lapsed Pokémon fan who wants the best of the 3DS era without the bloat of Ultra Sun/Moon. You care about story and atmosphere more than post-game grind. You want to build a team and transfer it forward to modern games via Pokémon Bank. You're a collector who wants to own a piece of the franchise's transition point.

**Skip it if:** You're a competitive battler who needs deep post-game content. You already own Pokémon Y — they're nearly identical, with version-exclusive Pokémon being the only meaningful difference. You're sensitive to frame drops and only have an original 3DS.

## FAQ

**Does Pokémon X work on the Switch?**
No. It's a 3DS-only title. You'll need a 3DS, 2DS, or 2DS XL to play it. No Switch port exists.

**Can I transfer Pokémon from X to Sword/Shield or Scarlet/Violet?**
Yes, but with caveats. You need Pokémon Bank (now free) and Pokémon Home. Only Pokémon that exist in the modern games' code can transfer — the rest stay in Bank.

**Is the 3D effect worth using?**
In battles, yes — Mega Evolution sequences look fantastic. In the overworld, no. Frame drops and ghosting make it more annoying than immersive. Use it sparingly.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

Pokémon X is a game that has aged gracefully in some ways and poorly in others. The core adventure is memorable, the Mega Evolution mechanic is genuinely fun, and the Kalos region is a treat to explore. But the thin post-game and undercooked features keep it from being a 5-star classic. At 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, the crowd agrees: it's the best non-remake Pokémon game of the 3DS era, even if "best of the 3DS era" is a low bar compared to the Switch titles that followed.

**Buy it if** you want a story-driven Pokémon adventure and don't care about endless post-game content. **Skip it if** you're after competitive depth or own Y already. Either way, you're getting a solid RPG that respects your time — something modern Pokémon often forgets to do.
