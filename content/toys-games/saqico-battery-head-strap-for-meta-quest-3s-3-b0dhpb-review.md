## The Real Problem With Quest 3S Comfort

Let's be blunt: the stock fabric strap on the Meta Quest 3S is a torture device. After 45 minutes, the front-heavy weight digs into your cheekbones, and you start shifting the headset every few seconds. The Saqico Battery Head Strap fixes this the same way every good halo-style strap does — by moving the counterweight to the back of your skull.

But Saqico goes a step further: they built a 6500mAh battery into that rear pad. This isn't just a comfort upgrade; it's a playtime upgrade. For anyone who's had a multiplayer session die mid-match, that extra juice matters.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the halo strap assembly, a USB-C magnetic charging connector, and a soft carrying pouch. The strap itself uses a rigid ABS frame wrapped in a ventilated PU leather pad. The battery pack sits flush against the occipital bone area, and the whole unit weighs in at roughly 320 grams — about the same as a large smartphone.

Installation takes under a minute. You remove the stock strap, snap the Saqico arms into the same mounting points, and tighten the rear dial. The magnetic USB-C connector clicks onto the Quest's charging port, and you're done. No tools, no fiddly cable routing.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Here's where the Saqico earns its 4.6-star rating. The battery delivers a genuine 2–3 additional hours of playtime on a Quest 3S — enough to push a full session past the 4-hour mark. The counterweight effect is dramatic: pressure shifts from your brow and cheeks to the top of your head, which is how the headset should have shipped from the factory.

One reviewer put it well: *"I can finally play for more than an hour without feeling like I've been in a boxing match. The battery is a bonus; the comfort is the real win."* Another noted, *"The dial adjustment is smooth, and the strap stays put even when I'm swinging my arms in Supernatural."*

The magnetic connector is a thoughtful touch. If you need to use the headset with the stock strap for travel, the adapter stays on the Quest and charges normally. No proprietary cable required.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The extra weight is real — 320 grams on top of the Quest 3S's already substantial heft. During fast, head-snapping games like Beat Saber on Expert+, you'll feel the momentum. It's not a deal-breaker, but it's noticeable.

The charging passthrough is also slower than the stock wall charger. It maxes out at 18W, so if you're playing while plugged in, the battery drains faster than it charges. The manual explicitly says the passthrough is for maintaining charge, not rapid charging.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a Quest 3S owner who plays for over an hour at a stretch, especially for room-scale games or fitness apps. The comfort upgrade alone justifies the price, and the battery is a welcome bonus.

**Skip it if** you primarily play seated, short sessions, or if you're sensitive to head weight. The stock strap works fine for 20-minute bursts, and this adds significant mass. Also skip if you already own a Quest 3 Elite Strap with Battery — the Saqico is a better value, but the difference isn't worth double-dipping.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Saqico strap work with the Quest 3 (not just the 3S)?**
Yes. It fits both the Quest 3 and Quest 3S. The mounting points are identical, and the battery connects via the same USB-C port.

**Q: Can I wear glasses or headphones with this strap?**
The halo design leaves your ears completely unobstructed, so over-ear headphones fit fine. Glasses work as long as they fit under the stock facial interface — the strap doesn't change the face pad clearance.

**Q: How long does the battery take to charge fully?**
About 2.5 hours with the included USB-C cable and an 18W adapter. You can also charge the strap and headset simultaneously, but expect it to take longer overall.

## The Verdict

The Saqico Battery Head Strap is a smart, practical upgrade that solves the Quest 3S's biggest flaw. The 6500mAh battery roughly doubles your playtime, and the counterweight design makes long sessions genuinely comfortable. It's not perfect — the added weight and slow passthrough charging are real concerns — but for the price, it delivers more value than Meta's first-party Elite Strap at nearly half the cost.

**Buy it if** you want longer playtime and better comfort without paying Meta's premium. **Skip it if** you're a lightweight gamer who never exceeds 30-minute sessions or if every gram matters for your play style.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A near-perfect balance of comfort, battery life, and value.
