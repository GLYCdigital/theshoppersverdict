The LEGO Jurassic World Dinosaur Missions: Allosaurus Transport doesn't waste time with a slow build-up. Within the first few minutes you're snapping together the truck chassis and the gray cargo trailer, and the whole thing comes together in about 20 minutes if you're an adult, or closer to 45 if your kid is driving the build.

What you're actually getting here is a 332-piece set built around a single core fantasy: loading a captured Allosaurus into a transport truck and getting it out of there before the T. rex shows up. That's it. And it works.

**What's in the box**

The set breaks down into three distinct builds: the flatbed truck itself, the Allosaurus in its cage-like trailer, and a T. rex that's ready to ruin your day. The truck has a fold-down ramp at the back, a detachable trailer hitch, and a cab that opens to seat a single minifigure — though notably, none are included. The Allosaurus has posable legs, head, and tail, and it fits snugly into the trailer's cage. The T. rex is the smaller-scaled version, but it still towers over the truck and makes for a genuinely menacing centerpiece.

**Build quality and real-world play**

This is a sturdy set. The truck frame uses Technic-style pins in a few key places, which keeps it from feeling flimsy when your kid inevitably pushes it across the floor at full speed. The Allosaurus itself is the star — the dark green and orange color scheme pops, and the head sculpt is surprisingly detailed for this price point. The jaw opens and closes with a satisfying snap.

The ramp mechanism is clever but has a flaw. It folds down smoothly for loading the dino, but the hinge uses a single connection point that can pop loose if the trailer gets knocked around. A few Amazon reviewers noted this too — one wrote, "My son loves it but the ramp falls off when he plays rough. Easy to fix but annoying." That's fair. It's a minor design tradeoff for keeping the set affordable.

**What buyers consistently love**

The 4.9-star average with 245 ratings isn't an accident. Parents consistently mention two things: the play value and the dual dinosaurs. One review summed it up: "Finally a set where the dino actually fits in the truck. My kid plays with this every day." Another noted, "The T. rex alone is worth the price." The two-dinosaur approach gives kids options — they can stage a rescue, a breakout, or just have the T. rex chase the truck across the living room.

**Where it falls short**

The missing minifigure is the biggest miss. You're buying a transport truck with an empty cab. If you don't already own a Jurassic World minifigure, you'll need to grab one separately, and that feels like a deliberate upsell. The T. rex is also noticeably smaller than the standalone 76956 version — the legs are less articulated, and the tail is a single piece. It's fine for play, but collectors will notice the difference.

**Who should buy this**

This is squarely aimed at kids 6 to 9 who are past the "choking hazard" stage and ready for a build they can complete in one sitting. It's also a solid pick for parents who want a Jurassic World set without the $100+ price tag of the bigger kits. If you're a collector looking for display-grade dinosaurs, skip it — you'll want the larger sets. If you're buying for a kid who will actually play with it, this is the right call.

**FAQ**

**Does the truck seat a minifigure?**
Yes, the cab opens and has a steering wheel and seat, but no minifigure is included. You'll need to supply your own — any standard LEGO minifigure fits.

**Is the Allosaurus removable from the trailer?**
Yes. The cage roof lifts off and the rear ramp folds down, so the dino can be loaded and unloaded easily. The trailer also detaches from the truck.

**How does this compare to the bigger T. rex sets?**
The T. rex here is a scaled-down version with simpler articulation. It's about 60% of the height of the 76956 T. rex and has fewer points of movement. It's great for play, less impressive on a shelf.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** you want a functional, durable Jurassic World set with real play value and two dinosaurs for a reasonable price.

**Skip it if:** you're a collector chasing display-quality figures or you want a driver minifigure included.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely fun set that's only held back by the missing minifigure and a slightly fragile ramp hinge. For the play it delivers, it's an easy recommendation.
