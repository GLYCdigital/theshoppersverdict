Let's be real about kids' coloring books: most are flimsy, last a weekend, and end up in the recycling bin. The Crayola Giant Pokemon Coloring Book is not that. With 288 pages, it's the rare coloring book that actually earns the word "giant" — not in dimensions, but in sheer volume of content.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 288-page coloring book and two sticker sheets. As shown in the product image, the cover features Pikachu front and center with other popular Pokemon surrounding him — the kind of art that immediately gets a reaction from kids who recognize their favorites.

The pages are standard coloring book size (roughly 9x11 inches), which is worth noting because "giant" might make you expect oversized poster-style pages. You're not getting that. What you are getting is a thick book that lays reasonably flat when open, which matters when a five-year-old is trying to color in the corners.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

Crayola's paper is genuinely better than what you'll find in most licensed coloring books. It's not cardstock, but it handles crayons beautifully and holds up to moderate marker use. Regular Crayola markers will bleed through if a kid presses hard, so put a piece of scrap paper underneath — but that's true of virtually every coloring book on the market.

The line art is clean and the Pokemon are recognizable. This isn't the slapdash licensed art you sometimes see where characters look vaguely off. The pages feature a good mix of simple designs for younger kids and more detailed scenes that will keep older Pokemon fans occupied.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

At 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, the feedback is overwhelmingly positive. The phrase that shows up repeatedly is "worth the price" — buyers consistently note that the page count makes this a better value than the typical 50-page licensed coloring book that costs nearly as much.

One reviewer noted their "6-year-old has been working through this for weeks and still isn't halfway done." Another mentioned it's "the perfect gift for a Pokemon obsessed kid who already has all the toys and figures." Parents of multiple kids also appreciate that siblings can tear out pages and color simultaneously without fighting over the book.

## Where It Falls Short

The sticker sheets are underwhelming. You get maybe 30-40 stickers total across both sheets, and they're standard paper stickers, not vinyl or puffy. For a product that advertises stickers as a feature, it feels like an afterthought — a small add-on rather than a real selling point.

The paper also isn't suitable for water-based markers, gel pens, or anything wet. If your kid is into paint or watercolor, this isn't the book for them. Stick to crayons and colored pencils for best results.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for:
- **Pokemon-obsessed kids ages 4-10** who will actually work through the pages
- **Parents looking for screen-free entertainment** that lasts — this will survive road trips, restaurant waits, and sick days
- **Gift-givers** buying for a child who has everything — pair it with a fresh pack of crayons and you're done

Skip it if your kid is over Pokemon, under 3 (pages are too detailed), or if you're hoping for a genuinely oversized coloring experience.

## FAQ

**Is this book appropriate for toddlers?**
No. The line art is detailed and the pages are small-ish. Ages 4 and up is realistic. A 3-year-old will color on the same page for two minutes and move on.

**Do markers bleed through the pages?**
Regular Crayola markers can bleed through if pressed hard. Use crayons or colored pencils for best results, or place a protective sheet underneath. Avoid gel pens and anything water-based.

**How many stickers are included?**
Two sheets with roughly 30-40 total stickers. They're paper stickers, not vinyl. Realistic expectations: this is a small bonus, not a main feature.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely substantial coloring book that will occupy a Pokemon fan for weeks — the page count alone justifies the price. **Skip it if** stickers are the main draw or you're expecting oversized poster-style pages.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The page volume and Crayola paper quality make this a standout in its category. The weak sticker sheets and standard dimensions keep it from a perfect score, but for the core purpose — hours of Pokemon coloring — it delivers exactly what it promises.
