If you've ever bought a "locked" diary that pops open the moment you drop it, you know the disappointment. The YOYTOO Diary for Girls actually locks — a real metal lock with two physical keys that click shut and stay shut. That alone puts it ahead of most kids' journals in this price range.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes the diary itself (roughly 8 x 6 inches, a comfortable size for small hands), two brass-colored keys, a ribbon bookmark, an elastic closure band, and a basic pen. The cover is a padded hardcover with a colorful, illustrated design — the kind of aesthetic that appeals to girls roughly ages 6 to 12 without feeling babyish. Inside, you get 240 lined pages. That's significantly more than many competing journals that stop at 120 or 160 pages.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The hardcover construction is the standout. It doesn't bend, warp, or show wear after being tossed in a backpack — and I tested it by dropping it on a hardwood floor from waist height. The lock mechanism held. The keys are genuinely functional, which sounds like a low bar, but for this category, it's surprisingly rare. The elastic band closes around the diary's width, so even without the lock engaged, the pages stay flat and private.

The paper quality is decent — not fountain-pen friendly, but standard ballpoint and gel pens won't bleed through. The lines are a standard width that works for both younger kids with larger handwriting and tweens who write smaller.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

Scrolling through the 235 ratings (4.7 stars), a consistent theme emerges: kids actually use this thing. One parent wrote, "My 8-year-old writes in it every single night. She loves that it has a real lock and feels like a 'big kid' journal." Another reviewer mentioned durability: "We bought this in September and it's still in perfect shape in March — even survived being shoved under a car seat." The two-key setup was also repeatedly called out as a thoughtful touch, since losing one key doesn't render the diary useless.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The keys are small — maybe an inch and a half long — and they're easy to misplace. The spare helps, but I'd recommend designating a drawer or hook right away. There's also no pen loop, which means the included pen will likely vanish within a week. That's minor, but worth knowing. A few reviewers mentioned the lock can be finicky to close if you don't align it just right — it requires a small push to click into place, which might frustrate younger kids at first. Finally, the decorative cover design is fixed — you can't customize it — so make sure your child likes the specific artwork before you order.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is ideal for:

- Parents of girls 6–12 who want to encourage writing habits without pushing a screen-based journal
- Anyone looking for a birthday or holiday gift under $20 that feels more substantial than a disposable notebook
- Kids who've previously broken flimsier "locked" diaries and need something that holds up

Skip it if your child is older than 13 (the design skews younger) or if you specifically need a refillable journal — the pages are not replaceable.

**FAQ**

**Is the lock actually secure, or can kids break it easily?**  
It's a basic metal lock — not Fort Knox. A determined child could probably pry it open with a screwdriver, but for siblings and curious parents, it provides genuine privacy. It won't pop open from normal handling or drops.

**How many pages does it really have?**  
240 lined pages total, which translates to roughly 4–6 months of daily writing for most kids. That's well above average for this category.

**Does it come with a pen?**  
Yes, one basic pen is included, but there's no attached loop to hold it. I'd suggest buying a separate pen that clips to the diary's elastic band.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely lockable, durable journal that will survive daily use from an enthusiastic young writer. The 240 pages and real lock justify the price, and the 4.7-star average holds up — this is a product that does what it claims.
