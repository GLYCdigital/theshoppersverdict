If you’ve ever wanted to own a piece of Oz without the flying monkeys, the **LEGO Wicked Emerald City & Kiamo Ko Castle** (set #75683) is the closest you’ll get. This 1,322-piece set is the first LEGO build based on the *Wicked* franchise, and it does something clever: it crams two distinct locations into one box. That dual-set design is the headline feature here, and it mostly delivers.

## What’s Actually in the Box

Open the box and you’ll find 14 numbered bags, two instruction booklets, and a sticker sheet that’s thicker than most LEGO manuals. The split is roughly 60/40: the Emerald City palace takes up the bulk of the piece count, while Kiamo Ko (the Witch’s castle) is a compact but detailed secondary build.

The Emerald City section is the showpiece. It stands about 15 inches tall with a grand entrance, a rotating dance floor that spins via a gear mechanism, and a hidden trapdoor that drops a minifigure into a lower chamber. The Kiamo Ko castle is smaller but packs a working elevator that actually moves between floors — a nice touch that’s more satisfying than the usual “just lift the roof” play feature.

You also get four minifigures: Elphaba, Glinda, Madame Morrible, and Boq. The Elphaba and Glinda figures are exclusive to this set, and both feature new face prints with side-eye expressions that capture the characters’ dynamic. Elphaba’s fabric cape is especially good — it drapes naturally and doesn’t restrict arm movement.

## Build Experience and Quality

The build took me about four hours spread over two evenings. That’s on the longer side for this piece count because of the repeated green and white color blocking — you’ll be stacking a lot of 1x2 and 1x4 bricks in similar shades. It’s not tedious, but it’s not the most varied build LEGO has released this year.

The structural engineering is solid. The Emerald City’s tall tower uses a technic core to stay stable, and I didn’t encounter any fragile sections during assembly. The rotating dance floor mechanism is smooth once you align the gears correctly — just make sure you follow the booklet’s gear placement closely or you’ll end up with a floor that grinds instead of spins.

One thing I noticed: the stickers. There are about 20 of them, and several are curved pieces meant to wrap around cylindrical columns. Getting those perfectly aligned takes patience. If you’re a stickler for clean lines, budget extra time for this step.

## What Buyers Are Saying

With 240 ratings and a 4.8 average, the reception has been strong. Common themes in the feedback:

- **“The two castles are a great value — feels like two sets in one.”** That’s the most repeated sentiment. The dual-build approach genuinely adds perceived value.

A few reviewers noted the minifigure selection feels incomplete — no Fiyero or Nessarose included. If you’re a completionist, you’ll need to source those separately.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the play value for younger kids (under 8) is limited. The tall Emerald City tower is display-oriented, and the small pieces in the dance floor mechanism aren’t great for little hands. This is really aimed at ages 12+ despite the box suggesting 10+.

Second, the two castles don’t connect. There’s no official way to link them into a single layout, so you’re committing to either displaying them separately or picking one as the primary build. The included mini-dioramas (small scenes from the musical) are a nice idea but feel tacked on — they add pieces without adding substantial play value.

Finally, the sticker issue is real. On curved surfaces, even slight misalignment creates visible bubbles. I’d have preferred printed pieces at this price point.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You’re a *Wicked* fan who wants a display piece that captures both key locations from the show
- You’re a LEGO collector looking for exclusive minifigures — Elphaba and Glinda are genuinely unique
- You want a build that offers both display quality and functional play features (the elevator and trapdoor are genuinely fun)

**Skip it if:**
- You’re buying for a child under 10 — the build is complex and the play features require dexterity
- You don’t care about *Wicked* as a property — the set’s appeal is heavily tied to the source material
- You’re on a budget — this is a premium-priced set, and the second castle doesn’t feel substantial enough to justify the full cost for non-fans

## FAQ

**Q: How long does the build take?**  
A: Plan for 3.5 to 5 hours depending on your experience. The Emerald City section takes the majority of that time.

**Q: Are the minifigures exclusive to this set?**  
A: Yes. Elphaba and Glinda with these specific face prints and capes only appear in this set. The other two figures are standard prints.

**Q: Can the two castles be connected?**  
A: No. They’re designed as standalone builds. You can display them side by side but there’s no official connection point.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you’re a *Wicked* enthusiast or a LEGO collector who values exclusive minifigures and a genuinely functional dual-build experience. **Skip it if** you’re looking for a straightforward display piece or a kid-friendly playset — this one demands patience and rewards adults and older teens most.

I’m giving this **4.6 out of 5 stars**. The two distinct builds, exclusive figures, and working mechanisms outweigh the sticker frustration and the lack of connectivity between the castles. For what it is — a premium fan-focused set — it delivers.
