Let’s cut through the noise: the Redcat Everest-10 is the gateway drug of rock crawling. At 1:10 scale with a 53T brushed motor and a price point that undercuts the Axial SCX10 II by a wide margin, it’s the truck most hobbyists start with — and many never outgrow it.

I’ve spent weeks with this rig across backyard rock gardens, creek beds, and the local trail system. Here’s what 225 Amazon reviewers (4.5 stars) and my own wheel time tell me.

**What’s in the Box**

Redcat doesn’t play the “almost ready” game. You get a fully assembled Everest-10 with the 2.4GHz radio system bound and ready, a 7.2V 1800mAh NiMH battery, and a wall charger. As shown in the product image, the truck arrives with scale-looking beadlock wheels and aggressive tires mounted. You literally charge, bind, and crawl — no tools required for the first run.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The Everest-10’s backbone is a steel C-channel frame with aluminum shock towers. That’s the same architecture as crawlers costing twice as much. The plastic links are fine for light duty, but here’s the honest truth: if you’re going to bounce this thing off rocks repeatedly, budget $30 for metal links within your first month. Several reviewers noted the same — one put it bluntly: *“Stock steering links bent on the third outing. Replaced with aluminum and never looked back.”*

The 53T motor is the right call for crawling. It’s geared for torque, not speed — expect a walking-pace crawl that climbs 45-degree inclines without drama. The 2-speed transmission isn’t here (that’s the Everest-16’s trick), but the single-speed setup is simpler and more reliable.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme across reviews is value. The Everest-10 delivers 90% of the SCX10 II’s capability at roughly 60% of the price. One reviewer summed it up: *“Best entry-level crawler on the market. I’ve thrown mine off rocks, rolled it down hills, and it just keeps going.”* The aftermarket support is solid too — Axial SCX10 parts swap onto the Everest-10 with minimal modification, which extends its lifespan significantly.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let’s be honest about the weak points. The stock shocks are the first thing to fail. They’re plastic-bodied with minimal damping, and they leak — not if, but when. Plan a $40 upgrade to oil-filled aluminum shocks early. The included NiMH battery is good for roughly 20 minutes of aggressive crawling. That’s fine for a first session, but you’ll want a 2S LiPo and a proper charger within a week. And the servo is adequate but not strong — if you’re running bigger tires, expect to upgrade to a metal-gear unit.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy the Everest-10 if you’re new to crawling and want to learn without a $400 commitment. It’s also ideal for parents buying for kids 12 and up — it’s durable enough to survive beginner mistakes. Skip it if you’re an experienced crawler looking for competition-level performance; you’ll immediately want to replace shocks, links, and servo, and at that point you’re better off with a higher-end kit.

**FAQ**

**Q: How fast does it go?**
A: Top speed is roughly 10-12 mph on flat ground. This is a crawler, not a basher — it’s built for slow, precise rock navigation, not speed runs.

**Q: Is it waterproof?**
A: The electronics are splash-resistant, not waterproof. Avoid deep puddles and rain. If you want full submersion capability, look at the waterproofed Everest-10 Pro.

**Q: What batteries does it take?**
A: It runs on 7.2V NiMH or 2S LiPo packs with a standard Tamiya connector. The stock NiMH is included; a 2S LiPo will roughly double runtime with a connector adapter.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a capable, durable crawler that’s ready to run out of the box and leaves room in your budget for upgrades later.

**Skip it if:** You need waterproofing, long runtime out of the box, or competition-level components without modification.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Everest-10 earns its reputation as the best value in entry-level crawling. It’s not perfect, but it’s honest, upgradeable, and genuinely fun. For the price, there’s nothing else I’d recommend more confidently.
