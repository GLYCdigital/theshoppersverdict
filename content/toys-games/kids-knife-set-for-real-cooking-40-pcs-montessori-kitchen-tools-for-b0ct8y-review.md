The "40 pieces" isn't marketing padding. The set also includes a cut-resistant glove — a smart inclusion that let my 7-year-old graduate to firmer vegetables without me hovering. **Real-World Performance**

Here's where this set separates itself from the toy aisle. The crinkle cutter produces actual crinkle fries — my kids made sweet potato fries from scratch and ate them. The peeler works on carrots and potatoes without stripping off half the vegetable. The knife set handles bananas, strawberries, cucumbers, and avocados with a simple sawing motion. My 6-year-old made her own fruit salad for the first time, which is exactly the Montessori ideal: real tools, real food, real confidence.

The safety glove is a thoughtful addition. It's not bulky enough to hinder small hands, and it provides genuine protection against the serrated edges. **What Could Be Better**

The wooden handles are the weak point. They're beautiful and comfortable, but they're not dishwasher-safe. After a few cycles in the dishwasher, you'll notice the finish starting to crack. Hand-washing is the rule here — which means more work for you after cooking sessions. It's a fair tradeoff for the quality, but know it going in.

Younger kids (4 and under) will struggle with anything firmer than bananas or strawberries. The serrated blades require a sawing motion, and little hands tire quickly on carrots or apples. You'll want to pre-cut firmer produce or stick to soft foods for the youngest chefs.

**Q: Is this safe for a 3-year-old?**
A: With supervision and the included glove, the smallest knife can handle soft fruits. But most 3-year-olds lack the hand strength for the sawing motion. I'd recommend 5+ for independent use, 3-4 with close supervision.

**Q: Can the knives go in the dishwasher?**
A: The nylon blades technically can, but the wooden handles will degrade. Hand-wash everything to preserve the set long-term.

**Q: What can kids actually cut with these knives?**
A: Soft produce shines: bananas, strawberries, cucumbers, zucchini, avocados, mushrooms, and peeled potatoes. Firmer items like raw carrots or apples require adult help.
