If you've got a playset with a spare beam, the Gorilla Playsets 04-0037-G Dual Ride Glider is one of those rare accessories that actually earns its spot. With 235 Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars, this isn't a flash-in-the-pan novelty—it's a backyard workhorse that kids gravitate toward. But before you click "add to cart," here's what you need to know.

**What's in the box**

You get the fully assembled glider frame—two molded plastic seats mounted back-to-back on a powder-coated steel frame—plus all mounting hardware (bolts, washers, brackets) and an instruction sheet. The packaging is straightforward: no surprises, no missing parts reported in any of the customer reviews I analyzed. The frame weighs about 15 pounds, and the seats are 14 inches wide with a 12-inch deep seat pan. What you don't get: chains or ropes. The product is designed to hang from your existing playset beam, so you'll need to supply your own suspension hardware.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The frame is the standout feature here. Gorilla uses a thick-gauge steel tube with a powder-coated finish, and the pivot point—where the frame attaches to your beam—uses a zinc-plated bushing that rotates smoothly. Several reviewers specifically noted the difference between this and cheaper gliders. One parent wrote, "We had a plastic glider from Walmart that cracked in four months. This one still swings like new after two summers of daily use." That durability comes from the engineering: the seats are injection-molded with ribbed undersides for strength, and the frame's geometry puts the pivot point directly above the seats' center of gravity, which eliminates the jerky rocking motion you get on poorly designed gliders.

The back-to-back configuration is genuinely clever. Kids sit facing opposite directions, which means they can pump their legs independently without colliding. Face-to-face gliders often cause arguments when one kid stops and the other doesn't; this design sidesteps that entirely. The ride itself is smooth and gliding—not a swing's arc, but a gentle back-and-forth that's easier on younger kids. Weight capacity is 100 pounds per seat, which covers most kids up to age 10 or 11.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star rating isn't accidental. Across the reviews, three themes dominate:

- **Ease of installation** — "Took me 20 minutes with a socket wrench. The instructions are actually clear, which is rare."
- **Durability** — Multiple reviewers mention surviving full sun, rain, and snow without fading or rusting.
- **Kid appeal** — "My twins fight over who gets which seat. It's the most-used thing on our playset."

**Where it falls short**

The missing chains are the biggest friction point. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's an unexpected $15-25 expense and a trip to the hardware store. Also, the 100-pound per-seat limit is firm—I wouldn't recommend testing it with two adults, even though the frame could physically handle it. The tan-colored seats are practical for heat (dark plastic gets scorching in summer sun), but they show scuff marks from sneakers and dirt quickly. A quick scrub with soapy water handles it, but if you're particular about appearances, keep that in mind. Finally, this accessory requires an existing playset—it's not a standalone purchase.

**Who should buy this**

This is for families with an existing wooden or metal playset who want to add variety without replacing the whole structure. It's especially good for homes with two kids close in age who want to ride together. If you have a single child, a standard glider or swing might be more practical. And if your playset beam is less than 4 inches thick, double-check compatibility—the mounting bracket is designed for standard 4x4 or 4x6 beams.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this fit any playset, or just Gorilla brand?**  
A: It fits any playset with a standard 4x4 or 4x6 beam. The bracket wraps around the beam and bolts securely. I've seen reviews from owners of Gorilla, KidKraft, and Backyard Discovery sets—all confirmed fit.

**Q: How do I hang it? I've never installed a glider before.**  
A: You'll need two chains or ropes (about 3-4 feet each, depending on your beam height). Attach one end to the glider's eye bolts, the other to your beam using heavy-duty screw-in eye hooks or a chain connector. The mounting bracket included with the glider bolts to the beam first, providing the pivot point.

**Q: Can adults use this?**  
A: Technically, the frame is sturdy, but the 100-pound per-seat rating is conservative for a reason. Two average adults would exceed the combined rating, and the plastic seats aren't designed for adult weight distribution. This is a kids' accessory.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have a playset with a spare beam, two kids who'll use it together, and you want something that'll survive multiple seasons without rusting or cracking.

**Skip it if:** You don't have an existing playset, you have a single child, or you're unwilling to purchase chains separately.

At 4.6/5, this is a solid, well-engineered accessory that does exactly what it promises. The missing chains and the weight limit are honest tradeoffs, but the durability and kid appeal make it one of the better playset upgrades you can buy.
