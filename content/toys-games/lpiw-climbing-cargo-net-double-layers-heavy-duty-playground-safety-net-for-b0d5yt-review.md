## Is the LPIW Climbing Cargo Net Worth $40?

Most climbing nets under $30 are flimsy afterthoughts—thin rope that sags, knots that slip, and edges that fray within a season. The LPIW Double Layers Heavy Duty net is different, but it's not perfect. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 215 buyer experiences.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get one net measuring 36" x 36" (the product image shows the exact dimensions), with pre-tied knots at every crossing point. The double-layer construction means two separate rope grids are stitched together, creating a sturdier web than single-layer competitors. Mounting straps are included—four heavy-duty nylon loops with buckles that wrap around posts up to 4 inches in diameter. No tools needed beyond what's in your garage, but you'll want a ladder for taller playsets.

## Real-World Performance

The double-layer design is the headline feature, and it earns its keep. Each intersection has two ropes crossing plus a reinforcing stitch, which distributes a child's weight more evenly. One buyer noted: *"My 60-pound grandson hangs on this daily, and the net hasn't stretched a millimeter in two months."* That's the durability story most reviewers echo.

The polyester rope has a slight give that absorbs impact—good for landing after a climb. It's also UV-treated, which matters if your playset sits in direct sun. Several owners mentioned leaving it up through a full Midwest winter with zero cracking or fading.

## Where It Falls Short

The rope texture is the biggest complaint. It's rough—think nautical-grade, not playground-soft. One reviewer wrote: *"My 6-year-old got rope burn on his palms the first day. We bought cheap garden gloves and it's fine now, but that's an extra purchase."* If your kids have sensitive skin, factor that in.

At $39.99, you're paying a premium for the double layer. Single-layer nets from brands like Fun Little Toys run $20-25 and work fine for lighter use. The LPIW only justifies its price if you have multiple kids or a heavy-duty playset.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Backyard playset owners** with A-frame swingsets or tower kits—the straps fit standard 4x4 posts  
- **Parents of climbers aged 4-10** who need something that won't collapse under enthusiastic use

Skip it if you have a single child under 40 pounds and a budget mindset—the cheaper single-layer option will do.

## 3 Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can this attach to a tree branch?**  
Yes, but only branches 3-4 inches thick. The straps wrap around, not through, so thinner branches will sag.

**Q: Is the double layer actually safer?**  
Marginally—the second layer catches a foot or hand that slips through the first grid. It doesn't replace a safety mat underneath.

**Q: How long does installation take?**  
Most reviewers report 15-20 minutes solo. The straps are tool-free but require tightening both buckles evenly to avoid lopsided tension.
