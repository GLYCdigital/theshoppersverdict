Here's the thing about Forza Horizon 4 in 2026: it's a phenomenal racing game that's about to lose its life support. Microsoft announced online server shutdown for December 2025, which dramatically changes whether you should buy this today. Let me break down what you're actually getting.

## What's in the Box

The Standard Edition includes the base game disc and a download code for the "Formula Drift" car pack. That's it. You're not getting the Fortune Island expansion, not getting Lego Speed Champions, and not getting the VIP membership. If you want those, you'll need the Ultimate Edition or separate purchases.

## The Core Experience

Forza Horizon 4 drops you into a condensed, fictionalized version of Britain. The headline feature is the dynamic four-season system — the map literally transforms weekly. Summer brings dry, grippy asphalt. Winter turns lakes into ice racing surfaces. Autumn carpets the roads in leaves that affect traction. Spring floods low-lying areas. This isn't cosmetic; it changes racing lines, car choices, and strategy every single week.

The car list sits at over 450 vehicles spanning everything from a 1960s Volkswagen Beetle to a 2019 McLaren Senna. Each handles differently, and the tuning system is deep enough to satisfy simulator fans while the default "assisted" driving model keeps casual players competitive.

## What Buyers Praise

The Amazon reviews consistently highlight the same strengths. One verified buyer wrote, "The seasons mechanic is genius. I've put 80 hours in and still find new roads and events each week." Another praised the accessibility: "My 8-year-old plays with all assists on and wins races. I play with everything off and still have a challenge. It scales beautifully."

The Horizon Festival structure deserves credit too. You're never more than a few minutes from a race, stunt, or collectible. The world density is exceptional — there's always something to do, and the game respects your time by letting you fast-travel between discovered locations.

## Where It Falls Short

The elephant in the room: the online service ends in December 2025. After that, you lose seasonal events, the Eliminator battle royale mode, and any content tied to online connectivity. The single-player campaign remains playable, but you're buying a half-experience if you purchase after shutdown.

The Standard Edition also locks away the two best post-launch expansions. Fortune Island adds a drift-focused playground with extreme weather, and Lego Speed Champions turns the game into a whimsical bricks-and-bricks playground. Both are excellent and worth considering if you're going all-in.

Install size is another practical concern. With updates and the base game, you're looking at over 100 GB. On an original Xbox One, load times are sluggish — expect 45-60 seconds to boot into the world.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Xbox Game Pass subscribers**: Wait. The game is included in Game Pass, and buying it separately makes little sense while it's still available there.
- **Single-player racing fans**: If you want a gorgeous, content-rich driving experience and don't care about online modes, the post-shutdown single-player still delivers 40+ hours of gameplay.
- **Completionists**: Skip this and grab the Ultimate Edition on sale — the extra content justifies the price difference.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work on Xbox Series X|S?**
A: Yes, via backward compatibility. It runs at 60fps with improved resolution on Series consoles, but this is the Xbox One version — you won't get the native Series X|S enhancements that come with the separately sold version.

**Q: Can I play offline after the server shutdown?**
A: The single-player campaign, all base races, and the weekly season rotation (now automated) remain playable offline. Anything requiring a connection — the Eliminator, online multiplayer, and seasonal online events — disappears.

**Q: Is the disk required every time I play?**
A: Yes, this is a physical disc. You'll need to insert it to verify ownership each session.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a top-tier open-world racer with unmatched seasonal variety and don't care about online multiplayer. The single-player content alone justifies a budget price.

**Skip it if** you're buying primarily for online play, or if you already have Game Pass, where the same game is free.

The 4.2-star rating holds up for what the game is — a brilliant, sprawling racing adventure. But the clock is ticking on its full experience. If you're buying, do it now. After shutdown, you're getting a beautiful, slightly hollow shell of what this once was.
