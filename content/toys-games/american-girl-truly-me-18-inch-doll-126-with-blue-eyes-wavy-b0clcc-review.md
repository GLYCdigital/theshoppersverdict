Let's cut through the noise: the American Girl Truly Me #126 is a $115+ doll (before you buy her any clothes), and she's competing against dolls that cost a third as much. Here's why she's still worth it for the right family — and why she might be overkill for yours.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the doll herself, a simple underwear set, and a meet outfit (usually a casual tee and shorts or skirt — check the listing for the current version). No accessories, no brush, no stand. That's it. The 18-inch vinyl doll has rooted wavy hair in a warm brown tone, blue eyes with real lashes, and the classic Truly Me face mold that's been refined over the past few years.

## Build Quality and Real-World Play

American Girl's manufacturing has improved noticeably since the 2010s. The hair is the standout feature — it's soft, has a natural wave pattern, and doesn't feel like straw out of the box. The vinyl limbs are firmly attached (no wobbly arms), and the body is stuffed densely enough to hold a seated position. Her eyes are set properly — no cross-eyed or misaligned issues that plagued some earlier runs.

Here's what buyers consistently say across 235 reviews:

> "My daughter has had this doll for two years and it still looks new. The hair has held up through countless brushings."

> "The quality is unmatched. You can feel the difference between this and a Target brand doll immediately."

That durability matters if your child is rough on toys. Several reviewers mention hand-me-down status — the doll surviving to be passed to younger siblings. The vinyl is thick enough to resist scuffs, and the hair doesn't shed excessively.

## Where She Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The wavy hair is high-maintenance.** It's beautiful when styled, but it tangles at the nape of the neck if your child brushes it vigorously. You'll need a wide-tooth comb and patience. Some buyers report having to cut out knots. If your kid is under 6, a straight-hair doll is probably the better choice.

**The price is the elephant in the room.** The doll alone is expensive, and American Girl's clothing and accessories are a separate money pit. A basic outfit runs $28–$35. You're not just buying a doll — you're buying into an ecosystem.

**No stand or storage included.** For the price, a simple stand would be nice. You're paying extra for basic accessories that cheaper dolls include.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy her if:**
- Your child is 6–12 and wants a "real" American Girl experience
- You value durability over cost — this doll genuinely lasts years
- Your kid is gentle with hair and will use a proper brush

**Skip her if:**
- Your child is under 5 (hair will become a battle)
- You're on a tight budget — the Our Generation line at Target is a solid alternative
- You don't want to be locked into the American Girl accessory ecosystem

## FAQ

**Q: Is the hair really wavy or just crimped?**
A: It's naturally wavy, not crimped. The wave pattern is loose and falls nicely. It can be straightened with a warm (not hot) damp cloth, but it will revert to wavy after washing.

**Q: Can you buy replacement parts?**
A: Yes — American Girl sells replacement bodies, heads, and wigs separately. That's a huge advantage over cheaper dolls. If a limb breaks, you can repair rather than replace.

**Q: What size clothes fit?**
A: Standard 18-inch doll clothes. American Girl's own line fits perfectly, and most third-party brands (like My Life As) also work. Just avoid anything labeled "mini" — that's for smaller dolls.
