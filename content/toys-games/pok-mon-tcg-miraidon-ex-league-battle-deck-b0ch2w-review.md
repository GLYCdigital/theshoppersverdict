## What's Actually in the Box

You get a complete 60-card tournament-legal deck, which is the headline. The centerpiece is two copies of Miraidon ex, a Basic Pokémon with 220 HP that can search your deck for two Basic Lightning Pokémon on your first turn. That's not just good — it's format-defining speed. The deck also includes Raikou V, which hits for 180 damage for just two Lightning energies, and a full complement of support Pokémon like Flaaffy to accelerate energy from discard.

The Trainer lineup is where this deck punches above its price point. You get full playsets of Professor's Research, Ultra Ball, and Electric Generator — all tournament staples. There's also a code card for Pokémon TCG Live, so you can practice online before taking the physical deck to a league.

## Real-World Performance

Out of the box, this deck is genuinely competitive. The strategy is straightforward: use Miraidon ex's Tandem Unit ability to load your bench, then attack with either Miraidon ex or Raikou V while Flaaffy keeps energy flowing from the discard pile. It's fast, consistent, and forgiving for newer players.

The build quality is standard for official Pokémon products — cards are well-printed with that fresh-from-pack feel. The deck box is functional if unspectacular, and the included rulebook and card list are helpful for tracking what you have.

## Honest Shortcomings

This deck isn't perfect. First, it doesn't include damage counters or any condition markers — for a product marketed as competitive-ready, that's an annoying omission. You'll need to buy those separately or use dice.

Second, the deck list is about 85% optimized. It's missing a few expensive singles that top-tier Miraidon lists run, most notably Forest Seal Stone and sometimes a second Raikou V. You can win at locals without these, but at larger events you'll want to upgrade. That said, the upgrades are cheap compared to building a deck from scratch.

Finally, some players complain about the price if you're only interested in collecting. This is a deck for playing, not for the binder.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're new to competitive Pokémon, returning after a break and need a solid deck quickly, or want a second deck for testing without building from scratch. It's also a legitimate gift for a kid (or adult) who wants to play at actual events.

**Skip it if** you're a collector only, already own a competitive Lightning deck, or expect a fully optimized tournament list straight from the box.
