I've reviewed a lot of "educational" globes that are really just inflatable beach balls with country names printed on them. This one is different. The 13-inch illuminated constellation globe from Educational Insights actually tries to be two products in one — a functional political globe and a rudimentary star projector — and for the most part, it pulls it off.

**What's in the box**

You get the assembled globe (no DIY construction, thank goodness), a plastic base, and a power cord. There's a small on/off switch on the cord itself, which is a minor annoyance — you'll want a power strip with individual switches or you'll be reaching behind furniture to kill the light. The globe sits on a metal meridian ring that feels reassuringly solid, not the flimsy plastic you often find at this price point.

**The dual-mode trick is the selling point**

By day, it's a standard political map. Country borders are clear, continents are accurate, and the colors are muted enough that it doesn't scream "toy store." But flip the switch and the interior bulb illuminates the sphere from within. The political map fades into the background as a faint starfield emerges, with constellation lines and labels glowing through.

Here's what impressed me: the constellations aren't random. Orion's belt actually aligns with Betelgeuse and Rigel in the correct positions. Cassiopeia's W shape is right. The Northern Cross is properly oriented. For a kids' toy, that's a level of astronomical accuracy I didn't expect. One Amazon reviewer noted, "My 7-year-old can now find Ursa Major in the real night sky because of this globe." That's the exact use case this product nails.

**Where it falls short**

The most common complaint in the 225 ratings is size deception. The listing says "13-inch globe," but that's the total height including the base. The sphere itself is closer to 9 inches in diameter. Several reviewers mentioned this: "I expected a 13-inch sphere based on the photos. It's smaller than I thought." If you're buying this for a classroom or to be a statement piece, measure your space first.

The bulb also runs warm. After two hours on, the globe surface is noticeably hot to the touch — not dangerous, but worth knowing if you have toddlers who might grab it. There's no auto-shutoff, so it's easy to leave on overnight and shorten the bulb's lifespan. Replacement bulbs are inexpensive and easy to find, but it's a hassle you shouldn't need.

**Who should buy this**

- **Parents of curious kids (ages 6–12):** This is the sweet spot. It's educational without being homework, and the night-light mode makes it a functional bedroom addition.
- **Teachers:** The constellation mode is genuinely useful for basic astronomy lessons. It's sturdy enough for classroom handling.
- **Anyone who wants a "pretty" globe:** The illuminated mode looks genuinely nice in a dim room — like a low-budget star projector.

**Skip it if** you need a serious reference globe (the political map is too simplified for that) or if you're expecting the 13-inch sphere the photos imply.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this actually accurate for astronomy?**
A: The constellation positions and star connections are correct for the major constellations. It's not a precision instrument — the "stars" are just tiny light points, and fainter constellations can be hard to distinguish. But the major ones are accurate enough to teach real sky navigation.

**Q: Can it run on batteries?**
A: No, it's cord-only. The box says AC adapter required, and it's included. That means placement is limited by outlet proximity.

**Q: How bright is the illumination?**
A: It's dim enough to work as a night light but bright enough to clearly see constellation labels. It's not a reading light by any stretch, which is actually a plus for bedroom use.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a dual-purpose globe that teaches geography by day and basic astronomy by night, without breaking the bank. It's well-built, accurate enough for its purpose, and kids genuinely engage with the constellation mode.

**Skip it if** the smaller-than-expected sphere size will bother you, or if you need a true reference-grade globe for serious study.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Points off for size deception and the missing auto-off timer, but this is the best illuminated constellation globe I've tested in this price range.
