**LEGO Friends Restaurant and Cooking School: Is This the Best Friends Set Yet?**

When LEGO released the Friends Restaurant and Cooking School (set 42647), the 4.9-star rating with 245 reviews caught my attention immediately. That's a near-perfect score that's rare even for LEGO. After digging through owner experiences, builder feedback, and the set's actual specs, here's the real story.

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting a 755-piece set that assembles into a two-story restaurant with an attached cooking school section. The obvious centerpiece is the dumbwaiter — a working pulley system that physically lifts food trays from the kitchen to the dining floor above. That's not a sticker or a printed detail; it's a functional mechanism kids operate with their hands.

The set includes four mini-dolls: two student chefs, a restaurant owner, and a waiter. There's also a cat figure, which the Friends line has leaned into heavily. The accessory list is genuinely impressive: an oven with a transparent door, a blender that spins, three distinct food prep stations (pizza, pasta, and dessert), plus enough plates, utensils, and ingredients to stage a full service.

**How it performs in real play**

The two-story footprint gives you roughly 14 inches of width and 10 inches of height when assembled. That's substantial for a Friends set — it commands table space. The dumbwaiter is the standout feature. Multiple Amazon reviewers specifically mention it, with one parent writing, *"My daughter has played with the pulley system more than anything else. She's constantly sending food up and down."*

The cooking school section separates from the restaurant, so two kids can play independently. That's a smart design choice. The kitchen equipment is detailed enough to prompt actual imaginative scenarios — the oven door opens, the blender has spinning blades, and there's a chalkboard for writing daily specials.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Beyond the dumbwaiter, owners frequently highlight the color scheme. The coral, cream, and teal palette photographs beautifully and looks more modern than earlier Friends sets. Parents also note the instruction quality — LEGO's numbered bags and clear steps mean most 9-10 year olds can build this unassisted.

The four mini-dolls are another consistent point of praise. Unlike some sets that pad the count with duplicates, these have distinct outfits, hairstyles, and accessories. One reviewer noted, *"My kids immediately assigned personalities to each character. That doesn't happen with every set."*

**Where it falls short**

Honesty time. The dumbwaiter is clever, but it's finicky. The string tension has to be exactly right, and if a child pulls too hard, the tray tips and food scatters. A few reviewers report having to rethread the mechanism after aggressive play.

The accessory pieces are small — tiny forks, individual grapes, two-stud-wide plates. These vanish under couches with alarming speed. If you have a toddler in the house, designate a storage container immediately.

Finally, the price-to-piece ratio is on the higher side. You're paying for the mechanism and the licensed characters, not just raw brick count. If you're purely seeking the most pieces per dollar, other sets in the Friends line offer better value.

**Who should buy this**

This set is ideal for the 8-12 year old who loves culinary play, restaurant pretend scenarios, or building-then-playing sequences. It's also a strong choice for LEGO fans who collect Friends sets and appreciate mechanical features beyond standard brick building. Parents looking for a gift that combines construction with ongoing play value will get a lot of mileage here.

Skip it if your child is under 7 and gets frustrated with complex builds, or if you're on a strict budget and prioritize raw piece count over play features.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does assembly take?**
A: Expect 2.5 to 3 hours for an experienced builder. A 9-year-old working independently will likely stretch that to 4 hours across multiple sessions.

**Q: Does the dumbwaiter actually work?**
A: Yes, it's a functional pulley system. It lifts the small food trays between floors, though it won't support heavy items or rough handling.

**Q: Is this compatible with standard LEGO sets?**
A: Yes, it's standard LEGO brick connections. It integrates with other Friends sets and classic LEGO City buildings without issue.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a buildable playset with genuine interactive features, your child enjoys restaurant or cooking themes, or you're looking for a gift that combines construction time with sustained imaginative play.

**Skip it if:** Your child is under 7, you're buying purely for piece count, or you need something that survives extremely rough play without supervision.

The LEGO Friends Restaurant and Cooking School earns its 4.9-star reputation. The dumbwaiter alone justifies the purchase for most families — it's the kind of feature that turns a static display piece into an active toy. The minor frustrations around small pieces and mechanism finickiness are manageable with reasonable expectations. At 4.6 out of 5, this is a confident recommendation for the right household.
