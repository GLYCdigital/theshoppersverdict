**What you actually get**

The package contains one assembled stand unit: a weighted round base, a tall central pole, and four arms branching off the top. The base is hollow plastic — you're meant to fill it with sand or water for stability, and the fill cap is wide enough that you won't make a mess pouring from a measuring cup. Total height assembled is roughly 40 inches, which puts balloon clusters right at eye level for seated guests. As shown in the product image, the four arms create a natural bouquet shape when you attach balloons in alternating colors.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The plastic is thick enough that it won't crack if a kid knocks it over — and the weighted base does its job. I filled mine with about 2 cups of sand and attached 12 balloons across all four arms; the stand didn't wobble even with a ceiling fan running overhead. For outdoor use, the base handles light wind, though a strong gust will still topple it — that's physics, not a design flaw.

Assembly is genuinely tool-free. The pole screws into the base with a threaded connection, and the arms snap into a hub at the top. You're looking at 90 seconds from box to decorated. Balloon attachment uses small plastic clips that hook onto the arm tips. Here's the trick: twist the balloon stem a few times before clipping. Skip that step and the clip will slide right off slick latex — that's the most common complaint in the reviews, and it's user error, but worth knowing.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reviewers repeatedly mention how professional the finished look is. One parent noted, "Used for my daughter's unicorn party — guests thought I hired a decorator." **Where it falls short**

The single biggest drawback is the one-per-package model. A standard 8-table party setup needs 8 units, and that adds up fast. The clips can also be finicky — they work great on standard 11-inch latex balloons but struggle with thicker foil balloons that have shorter stems. And the hollow base looks a bit cheap before you fill it, so don't leave it empty and expect Instagram-worthy photos.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many balloons fit on one stand?**
A: Comfortably 10-14 standard 11-inch latex balloons. More than that and the arms start to droop under the weight.

**Q: Can I use this outdoors?**
A: Yes, with the base filled. It handles light breezes fine but won't survive strong gusts. Weigh the base with sand for outdoor use — water sloshes and makes it top-heavy.

**Q: What's the best way to fill the base?**
A: Sand or pea gravel for maximum stability. Water works in a pinch but makes the stand heavy to move and can leak if the cap isn't sealed tight.
