**Is this the bathtub toy that finally gets your kid to stop splashing you in the face? Maybe. Here's the honest breakdown.**

You've seen a hundred fishing toys. This one's different because it actually works in water — not just on a table. The floating fish bob around, the magnetic poles catch them with a satisfying *click*, and for 15-20 minutes your toddler is genuinely occupied. That's the pitch. Here's whether it delivers.

**What's in the box**

You get two fishing poles, twelve fish in bright colors (six per pole set), and a mesh storage bag. The poles are roughly 12 inches — right for small hands but not so short that parents have to hunch over. The fish are about 3 inches long, chunky enough that a 3-year-old can grip them but not so large that they crowd a standard bathtub.

One thing to note: the fish are **plastic, not wood**. Several listing photos show what looks like wooden pieces. They're not. They're glossy plastic with a hollow interior. Not a dealbreaker, but if you're specifically avoiding plastic toys, this isn't it.

**How it performs in real water play**

The magnetic catch is the star here. The magnets are strong enough to hold a fish once hooked, but not so strong that a 3-year-old can't pull them apart. In testing, the fish float upright — no tipping, no rolling over — which matters because a fish that floats belly-up is frustrating for little ones.

The poles are hollow plastic with a small magnet on the end. They float, which is convenient when your kid inevitably drops them in the tub. The grip has a subtle texture that helps when hands are soapy.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews holds up. Parents repeatedly mention two things: the **click moment** and **independent play**. One reviewer wrote, *"My 4-year-old played for 30 minutes without asking for a screen. That's a record."* Another noted, *"The fish actually stay hooked once caught — my son gets so excited when he lands one."* The two-pole design also gets consistent love from families with siblings — no fighting over the "good" pole.

**Where it falls short**

The magnet strength is the biggest issue. After a few weeks of regular bathtub use, some fish start taking on water through the seam where the two plastic halves meet. Once that happens, the internal magnet shifts and the catch becomes unreliable. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Worked great for two weeks, then the fish started sinking and the magnets got weak."* It's not universal, but it's common enough to mention.

The storage bag is flimsy mesh. It'll hold the pieces, but don't expect it to survive a trip in a beach bag or a toss in the toy bin. Plan to replace it with a zip-top bag eventually.

**Who should buy this**

This is a **bathtub toy first, water table toy second**. If your kid is 3-6 and loves water play, this delivers solid entertainment per dollar. It's also a smart choice for parents who want something that works in the tub without batteries, screens, or parental assembly. If you have a 7-8 year old, the novelty wears off faster — they'll master the magnet in one session and move on.

**Skip it if** you want a toy that survives years of abuse, or if you're looking for something educational beyond basic hand-eye coordination. This is pure, simple fun — and that's okay.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do the fish work in a saltwater pool or just fresh water?**
A: Fresh water only. Saltwater corrodes the internal magnets and will ruin the fish quickly.

**Q: Are the pieces safe for a 2-year-old?**
A: The fish are too small — 3 inches — and present a choking hazard. The listing says 3+, and that's accurate.

**Q: Can the poles be replaced if lost?**
A: Not individually through Amazon. The set is sold as a unit, so if you lose a pole, you're rebuying the whole thing.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a low-cost, no-battery bathtub toy that actually holds a 3-5 year old's attention and works well for sibling play. **Skip it if** you need something that'll survive years of use or if plastic construction bothers you.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely fun bathtub toy with a few durability quirks. At this price point, it's worth the gamble for the smiles alone.
