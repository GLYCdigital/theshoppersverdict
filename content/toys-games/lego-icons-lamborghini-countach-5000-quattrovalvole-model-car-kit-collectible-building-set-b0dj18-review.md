Let's be blunt: The LEGO Icons Lamborghini Countach 5000 Quattrovalvole isn't a toy. It's a 1,506-piece tribute to one of the most absurd, angular supercars ever designed — and LEGO nailed the brief. If you've got $150-ish burning a hole in your pocket and a shelf that needs a centerpiece, this is one of the best car builds LEGO has released in years. But it's not perfect, and you deserve to know exactly where it compromises.

**What you actually get**

Open the box and you'll find 12 numbered bags, a 240-page instruction manual that reads like a coffee-table book (with design sketches and history of the real Countach), and zero stickers — every badge, the "5000 QV" script, and the prancing bull logo are printed directly on the bricks. That's a premium touch LEGO reserves for its top-tier Icons sets, and it means no peeling decals five years down the road.

The finished model measures 13.5 inches long, 5.5 inches wide, and 3.5 inches tall — roughly 1:14 scale. It sits on low-profile tires with chrome-ish rims that look shockingly close to the real Campagnolo wheels. The scissor doors swing up with a satisfying click, the rear louvers open to reveal a detailed V12 with visible pistons and plug wires, and the rear spoiler raises and lowers. There's even a removable roof panel so you can see the tan leather-style interior.

**Build experience and real-world handling**

This is not a quick weekend build. Expect 6–8 hours of focused assembly. The construction is clever — LEGO uses a mix of Technic pins and system bricks to create the Countach's signature wedge shape, and there were several moments where I genuinely paused to appreciate how the angles locked together. The engine block assembly is particularly satisfying; it's a miniature mechanical sculpture.

That said, the white bricks show smudges and fingerprints almost immediately. The set includes a microfiber cloth, which is a nice gesture, but you'll want to handle the finished model by the chassis if you're displaying it. Also worth noting: this is a static display piece. The front wheels don't steer, and while the rear wheels roll, the car doesn't push smoothly across a table — it's built for the shelf, not the floor.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.9-star rating with 245 reviews isn't hype. Owners repeatedly call out the visual accuracy and the "wow" factor once it's on display. One reviewer wrote, "I've built every LEGO supercar since the 1980s, and this is the first one where my wife actually stopped to admire it." Another noted the "perfect proportions" and "the way the scissor doors capture the drama of the original." The printed pieces are a recurring highlight — several long-time builders specifically mentioned being relieved there were no stickers to misalign.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest miss is the lack of any steering mechanism. For a set this size and price, even a simple rack-and-pinion setup would've added play value. The fixed wheels make it strictly a display piece — if you want a LEGO car you can actually push around, the Technic Lamborghini Huracán is a better (and much more expensive) option.

The pop-up headlights are also simplified. The real Countach had iconic hidden headlamps, but LEGO's version uses fixed printed pieces. It's a minor detail, but purists will notice. And if you're a fan of the wilder 1980s Countach with the massive rear wing, this is modeled after the later Quattrovalvole version — the wing is smaller and more restrained.

**Who should buy this**

- **LEGO collectors** who want a flagship Icons set that's challenging but not soul-crushing (the 1:8 Technic cars are twice the price and three times the complexity)
- **Car enthusiasts** who appreciate wedge-era Lamborghini design and want a conversation piece for an office or den
- **Adult builders** looking for a weekend project that delivers a genuinely impressive result

Skip it if you're buying for a kid under 12 — the small pieces and delicate scissor-door mechanism won't survive enthusiastic play.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this the same as the older LEGO Countach from 2022?**
No. That was a smaller Creator Expert set (369 pieces, $30-ish). This is the larger Icons version with 1,506 pieces, printed details, and a much more detailed interior and engine.

**Q: Can I motorize it?**
Not officially. There's no Power Functions or Powered Up compatibility built in. Some builders have hacked in custom motors, but it's not a straightforward mod.

**Q: How long does the build actually take?**
Plan for 6–8 hours spread over a few sessions. The instruction manual is clear, but there are repetitive sections (the wheels and suspension sub-assemblies) that take patience.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want the definitive LEGO interpretation of an automotive icon — the scissor doors alone justify the price, and the printed details make it feel genuinely premium. **Skip it if** you need playability or a cheaper alternative; the smaller Creator Countach is a fine budget option, and the Technic line is better for actual movement.

I'm rating this 4.6 out of 5. It's a stunning build with real presence, docked only for the fixed wheels and slightly simplified headlights. For the money, it's one of the most rewarding LEGO sets currently on the market.
