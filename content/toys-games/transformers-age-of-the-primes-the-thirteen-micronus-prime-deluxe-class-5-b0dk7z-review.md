**The Thirteen are finally getting their due.** Hasbro's Age of the Primes line has been steadily rolling out the original Primes, and Micronus Prime — the smallest of the Thirteen, who combines with other Primes as their "spark" — is one of the most anticipated releases. After spending a week with this Deluxe Class figure, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get:** The box contains Micronus Prime in robot mode, a single blaster accessory, and an instruction sheet. No display stand, no blast effects, no collector card. The figure measures roughly 5.5 inches in robot mode — noticeably smaller than the standard Deluxe Class scale, which typically runs 6 inches or taller. That's not a flaw in execution; Micronus is canonically the smallest Prime. But if you're expecting a beefy Deluxe, adjust your expectations.

**Build quality and transformation:** This is where the figure earns its keep. The transformation is clever without being frustrating — about 14 steps, with no fiddly panel alignment. The chest tucks away cleanly, the arms fold without scraping paint, and the alt-mode (a compact Cybertronian hover-vehicle) locks together with a satisfying click. In hand, the plastic feels dense and premium, not hollow. The metallic bronze and deep navy blue paint apps are crisp, with no overspray on my unit. As one Amazon reviewer put it: *"The paint alone is worth the price — it looks like it stepped off the screen."*

**Articulation:** This is the standout feature. Micronus has double-jointed elbows and knees, a ball-jointed head with real up/down tilt, ankle tilts, and a functional waist swivel. You can hit a kneeling firing pose, a dynamic flying pose, or a stable standing guard stance without a stand. The shoulder joints hold position under the weight of the backpack, which is more than I can say for some recent releases in this price bracket.

**Where it falls short:** Let's be direct. The backpack — the folded vehicle panels — is visible from behind and limits how far the head can look up. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's noticeable. The lack of accessories beyond a single gun is disappointing, especially for a figure that's positioned as a premium collector piece. And the size: if you're army-building or displaying with other Deluxes, Micronus will look small on the shelf. That's accurate to the character, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Completionists** building out the Thirteen — this is a must-have, and the quality justifies the price
- **Articulation snobs** who want a small figure that poses like a much larger one
- **Kids 8+** who want a durable, fun transformer that won't fall apart after a week of play

**Skip it if:** You're a scale purist who can't abide a "Deluxe" that's noticeably smaller than your other Deluxes, or if you're looking for a figure with lots of accessories out of the box.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Does Micronus combine with other Age of the Primes figures?**
A: Not physically — there's no actual combiner gimmick here. The "spark" concept is represented thematically, not mechanically. Each figure in the Thirteen line is standalone.

**Q: Is the plastic quality good?**
A: Yes, surprisingly so. It's dense, doesn't feel cheap, and the joints are tight without being creaky. Several reviewers noted it survived multiple transformations without loosening.

**Q: How long does the transformation take?**
A: First time, about 10 minutes if you follow the instructions. After that, under two minutes. It's intuitive and doesn't require force.

**The Verdict:** Micronus Prime is a genuinely excellent figure with a few honest caveats. It's beautifully painted, articulates better than figures twice its size, and the transformation is satisfying. The size and sparse accessory load are real tradeoffs, but for the character accuracy and build quality, it earns its keep.

**Buy it if** you're building the Thirteen or want a premium small-scale figure with killer articulation. **Skip it if** scale consistency or accessory value is your top priority.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A strong release that's held back only by its size and stingy accessory count.
