Let's be honest: you've already self-diagnosed yourself with at least three conditions just reading the product name. That's exactly the audience DiagnosThis is built for. This isn't a serious medical game — it's a party game that weaponizes your WebMD addiction for maximum laughter.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 300 diagnosis cards and 100 symptom cards — that's 400 total cards, which is substantial for the price point. The cards are standard poker-size with a durable linen finish that holds up to repeated shuffling. The game comes in a compact box roughly the size of a hardcover novel, so it travels well for game nights at friends' houses.

Setup takes under a minute: shuffle the diagnosis deck, deal five cards to each player, then flip a symptom card face-up. That's it. No board, no tokens, no app, no scoring pad to lose. The instructions fit on a single card, which tells you everything about the intended audience.

## How It Plays

Here's the core loop: one player reads a symptom card aloud — think "You wake up with an unexplained rash that itches only when you think about it" — and everyone else races to play the funniest diagnosis card from their hand. A rotating judge picks the winner, who scores a point. First to seven points wins.

The genius is in the card combinations. The diagnosis cards range from absurd ("Spontaneous Combustion of the Soul") to oddly plausible ("Chronic Avoidance of Adult Responsibilities"), and the symptom cards are written to leave maximum room for creative interpretation. The result is that even the same symptom card produces completely different jokes depending on your hand.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews reflects genuine enthusiasm. One recurring theme in feedback is how the game works for non-gamers. As one reviewer put it, *"My parents, who hate Cards Against Humanity, actually requested this by name the next week."* Another common thread is the laugh-per-card ratio — *"We played for three hours and I cried twice. My abs hurt the next day."*

The flexibility on player count gets frequent shoutouts too. The game officially supports 3–20 players, and the judge rotation system scales surprisingly well. Large groups can play with the "house rules" variant where the judge can award points to multiple players for different jokes.

## Where It Falls Short

The medical comedy theme has a specific flavor that won't hit for everyone. Several cards lean into gross-out territory — body functions, questionable hygiene, and mildly graphic descriptions. If your group prefers witty wordplay over toilet humor, some cards will fall flat. I'd estimate 10-15% of the deck skews more "ew" than "ha."

The 3-player experience is noticeably weaker. With fewer hands in play, the diagnosis options are limited, and the judge dynamic becomes too predictable. This is genuinely a 4+ player game. Also, the "first to seven points" rule makes rounds last 30-40 minutes; if you want quick bursts, you'll need to house-rule it to first-to-five.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You host game nights with 4-8 people who enjoy Cards Against Humanity but want something fresher. You have a friend group that loves sharing the weird symptoms they've Googled. You want a game that works as an icebreaker for mixed groups of gamers and non-gamers.

**Skip it if:** Your group is easily grossed out, you only play with 3 people, or you prefer strategy games over pure party games.

## Common Questions

**How long does a full game take?**
About 30-40 minutes for a full 7-point game with 4-6 players. A 5-point house rule cuts it to roughly 20 minutes.

**Is this appropriate for teenagers?**
The packaging says adult, but most content is PG-13 level. The gross-out cards are more juvenile than offensive — no explicit language or mature themes. High schoolers will get most of the jokes.

**Do the cards repeat often?**
With 400 cards, you'll see maybe 10% of the deck in a single session. Even after 5+ playthroughs, repeat combinations are rare.

## The Verdict

DiagnosThis earns its 4.6/5 score through sheer replayability and a premise that nails its target audience. It's not deep, it's not clever in a sophisticated way — but it's consistently, reliably funny. At this price point, it's hard to find a party game that delivers this much laughter per dollar.

**Buy it if:** You want a party game that gets people laughing within five minutes and works for both casual and experienced game groups.

**Skip it if:** Your group prefers strategy games, you're playing with 3 or fewer, or you have a low tolerance for gross-out humor.
