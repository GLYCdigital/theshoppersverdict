**Is this the expansion that finally makes HeroQuest feel like a real campaign?**

When Joe Manganiello — a man who reportedly built a $40,000 custom HeroQuest table — partners with Avalon Hill, expectations run high. The Crypt of Perpetual Darkness isn't just another quest pack thrown together to milk the nostalgia market. It's a genuine attempt to evolve the 1989 classic while keeping the dungeon-crawling DNA intact. But does it justify its premium price tag? Let's dig in.

**What's actually in the box**

This expansion delivers substantial physical content. You get a 14-quest campaign book authored by Manganiello himself, over 30 new miniatures (including the centerpiece Crypt Beast), a set of new monster cards, treasure cards, and the innovative light/darkness token system. The miniatures follow the same chunky, pre-assembled style as the base game — they're not display-quality like Games Workshop, but they're a clear step up from the original 90s plastics. The quest book is full-color with atmospheric art, and the card stock is thick enough to survive repeated shuffling.

**How it plays**

The Crypt of Perpetual Darkness shines brightest in its campaign structure. Unlike the base game's somewhat episodic quests, this expansion weaves a continuous narrative with meaningful choices. Defeat a boss early or take the secret passage? Your decision locks off certain paths and opens others in later quests. It's a clever mechanic that rewards replayability — you'll genuinely want to run through twice to see what you missed.

The new darkness mechanic is the standout addition. Certain rooms are shrouded in darkness tokens, limiting visibility and forcing heroes to make tactical decisions about light sources. It's simple enough that new players grasp it in minutes, yet adds genuine tension to exploration. The cursed items are more of a mixed bag — some are brilliantly thematic, others feel punishingly random, especially for lower-level heroes.

**What buyers are saying**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 ratings reflects genuine enthusiasm. One reviewer notes, *"Manganiello clearly understands what makes HeroQuest fun — this feels like a love letter to the game, not a cash grab."* Another praises the difficulty curve: *"Finally a campaign that challenges my group without being frustrating. The Crypt Beast boss fight took us three attempts."*

**Where it stumbles**

The biggest hurdle is accessibility. This is not a standalone product — you need the HeroQuest base game (and ideally the core rulebook memorized). There's no tutorial quest, no rules refresher, no gentle reintroduction. If you're returning to HeroQuest after a decade away, expect to re-read the base rulebook first.

The price point also raises eyebrows. At roughly $70-80 depending on retailer, you're paying near base-game prices for an expansion. The miniatures and card quality justify much of that cost, but budget-conscious buyers should weigh how often they'll actually run a 14-quest campaign versus the base game's pick-up-and-play appeal.

**Who should buy this**

- **The returning HeroQuest fan** who's already cleared the base campaign and craves something meatier
- **The narrative-driven group** that prefers ongoing stories over one-shot dungeon crawls
- **Manganiello completionists** — if you're a fan of his other HeroQuest work, this is essential

**Skip it if** you're new to HeroQuest, play irregularly, or prefer quick sessions. The campaign demands commitment — each quest runs 90 minutes minimum, and the narrative payoff requires playing consistently.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I play this without owning the HeroQuest base game?**
A: No. You need the base game's board, dice, heroes, and core rules. This expansion only adds quests, monsters, and new mechanics.

**Q: How long is the campaign?**
A: 14 quests, roughly 2-4 hours each depending on group size and experience. Expect 30-50 hours total.

**Q: Is this suitable for kids?**
A: The complexity is manageable for ages 12+, but the cursed items and darkness mechanics add strategic depth that younger players may find frustrating. The miniatures also feature some genuinely creepy designs.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a committed HeroQuest group ready for a campaign that respects your intelligence and rewards your time. This is the definitive way to play HeroQuest in 2026.

**Skip it if** you're a casual player, new to the system, or on a budget. The base game plus this expansion is a significant investment, and the campaign format won't suit every group.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A fantastic, fan-made-with-professional-resources expansion that respects the source material. Deducting half a star for the accessibility barrier and the price point, but for the right group, this is essential.
