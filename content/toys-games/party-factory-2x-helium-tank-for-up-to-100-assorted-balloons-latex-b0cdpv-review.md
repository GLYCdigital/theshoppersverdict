**Party Factory 2x Helium Tank Review: Two Tanks, 100 Balloons, One Big Question**

You're planning a party, you need balloons that actually float, and you're staring at a $30 disposable helium kit. The Party Factory 2x Helium Tank promises up to 100 latex balloons across two tanks. At 3.7 stars from 185 ratings, the reality is more complicated than the marketing suggests. Here's what you need to know before checkout.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains two separate 8.9-inch tall aluminum tanks, each pre-filled with helium, plus a nozzle attachment for standard latex balloons. The two-tank design is smart—you can split the work between two people or use one tank, save the second for later. Each tank weighs under 3 pounds, which beats hauling a 20-pound rental cylinder from a party store.

The inflation process is dead simple: slide the balloon neck over the nozzle, press down, and it fills in 3-5 seconds. No twisting, no tying while holding a tank—just pull it off and tie. For a 50-balloon arch or a ceiling full of floating balloons, this speed matters.

**Real-World Performance: The Good and the Grinding**

The valve mechanism works reliably—no stuck nozzles, no leaking tanks in transit. The helium flow is consistent, and you get a predictable fill every time. For the first 20 balloons per tank, you'll feel like a pro.

But here's where the ratings dip. The advertised "float time" of 8-10 hours assumes ideal conditions. In practice, several buyers report balloons starting to droop within 4-6 hours, especially in warmer rooms or near ceiling lights. One reviewer noted, "Balloons looked great during the party, but by morning they were all on the floor." Another said, "Used all 100 balloons with 6 hours to spare before guests arrived—by hour 8, half were sagging."

That's not necessarily a dealbreaker—most parties run 3-4 hours—but if you're setting up the night before, you'll be disappointed.

**The Hidden Costs**

This is a single-use product. Once the helium is gone, the tanks are trash. At roughly $30, you're paying about 30 cents per balloon, which is competitive with party store helium but on par with buying pre-filled balloons. The bigger issue: if you're hosting more than one event, you're buying this kit again.

Also, this only works with latex balloons. If you've got foil numbers or mylar character balloons, you need a different valve type—this kit won't touch them.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The last-minute planner**: You need balloons in the next 2 hours and don't want to drive across town. This delivers fast.
- **The one-and-done host**: One birthday party, one baby shower, one graduation. You'll use it once and never again—fine.
- **The small-space decorator**: 100 balloons is plenty for a room corner or photo backdrop, not a full venue.

**Skip it if**: You're doing a multi-day event, you need foil balloons, or you want helium for a monthly balloon business. Rent a tank instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many balloons does one tank actually fill?**
A: Realistically 40-50 standard 11-inch latex balloons per tank. You'll get the full 100 across both if you don't over-inflate (which shortens float time). Over-inflating also risks popping.

**Q: Can I refill these tanks?**
A: No. They're disposable aluminum cylinders. Once empty, recycle them and buy a new kit.

**Q: How long do the balloons really float?**
A: 4-8 hours depending on room temperature, balloon size, and whether you use hi-float treatment. Set up the day-of, not the night before.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if**: You need a quick, convenient helium source for one event, you're using latex balloons, and you're setting up the same day.

**Skip it if**: You need long float times, you're using foil balloons, or you're planning multiple events.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — It does exactly what it claims, but the float time inconsistency and single-use nature keep it from being a clear win. For a one-time party, it's a reasonable convenience purchase. For anything more, rent a tank.
