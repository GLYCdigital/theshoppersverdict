**Gran Turismo 7 isn't just a racing game — it's a love letter to the automobile, wrapped in a PlayStation 5 showcase.** After 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this is the rare title that satisfies both the casual Sunday driver and the sim-racing obsessive. But it's not without its compromises. Let's break down what you're actually getting.

**What's in the box (or download):** The physical PS5 edition includes the game disc, a plastic case, and a code for a 1.5-million-credit in-game bonus. No steelbook, no art book, no soundtrack — Polyphony Digital keeps it lean. The real content is the game itself: 34 track locations with 90+ layout variations, over 400 cars from 60 manufacturers, and the full Gran Turismo Café menu system that walks you through automotive history one collection at a time.

**Performance and build quality:** This is where Gran Turismo 7 separates itself from every other racer on the market. The ray-traced reflections in replays are gorgeous, but the real magic is in the driving model. As shown in the product image, the car models are photorealistic — but the physics underneath are what keep players coming back. Tires heat up over laps, weight transfer shifts mid-corner, and the weather system can change your entire race strategy in real time. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "The rain physics alone are worth the price of admission. Watching puddles form on the racing line and then hitting them at 150mph is terrifying and incredible."

The DualSense controller integration deserves special mention. You feel the ABS pulsing through the trigger resistance, the rumble of curbs through the grip, and the subtle vibration of engine idle through the palm rests. It's the closest thing to driving a real car without leaving your couch.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The car list depth and the "Café" system that guides newcomers through car culture. One verified purchase noted: "I'm not a car guy, but the menu books taught me about the Porsche 917 and the Shelby Cobra in a way that made me care. It's the most educational racing game ever made." The PSVR2 support (if you own the headset) is another recurring highlight — the cockpit view becomes genuinely immersive.

**Where it falls short:** The always-online requirement is the biggest complaint across reviews. Even in single-player, the game checks in with servers. If PSN is down or your internet blips, you can't save progress or access your garage. During launch week, this was a genuine problem — servers were unstable for days. It's better now, but the design choice remains frustrating. The microtransaction system also draws criticism. While you can earn cars through normal play, the most desirable legendary cars cost 20 million credits — roughly 15-20 hours of grinding. Paying $40 to skip that grind feels exploitative, even for a game this good.

**Who should buy this:** 
- **Sim-racing enthusiasts** who own a racing wheel — the force feedback implementation is superb
- **Casual players** who want a beautiful, accessible driving game with genuine depth
- **Car culture fans** who appreciate the 1,000+ page in-game encyclopedia covering automotive history
- **PSVR2 owners** — this is the headset's killer app

**Skip it if:** You're offline frequently, you hate any form of microtransaction pressure, or you prefer arcade-style racing like *Need for Speed* — this is a simulation, not a power-slide fest.

**FAQ:**

*Q: Does it work on PS4?*  
A: Yes, there's a PS4 version, but you lose ray tracing and get longer load times. The PS5 version is the definitive experience.

*Q: Is a racing wheel necessary?*  
A: No. The DualSense controller is excellent. A wheel enhances precision but isn't required to be competitive.

*Q: How long is the campaign?*  
A: The main Café menu takes 20-30 hours. Full completion — all licenses, missions, and car collections — easily exceeds 100 hours.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you own a PS5 and have any interest in cars or racing. **Skip it if** you require offline play or can't tolerate microtransaction structures in a full-priced game.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A near-masterpiece held back by online requirements and monetization choices. The driving itself is the best on console.
