### What you actually get

Pull the box open and you'll find the headset, a USB-C charging cable, and a 3.5mm audio cable that works with controllers or phones. That's it — no dongle, no dock, no carrying case. The Carbon Black finish is matte, fingerprint-resistant, and looks more premium than the $99 price suggests.

The headset weighs 312 grams. That's on the lighter side for over-ears, and it shows in extended sessions. The ear cups rotate 90 degrees to lie flat, which is nice for storage but reveals the plastic hinge mechanism — the weakest physical point here.

---

### Performance: Where it earns the 4.3 rating

**Spatial audio is the headline feature.** The headset supports Windows Sonic and Dolby Atmos natively. Playing *Halo Infinite*, I could pinpoint enemy footsteps above and behind me with unnerving accuracy. The 20Hz-20kHz frequency response is standard, but the tuning leans toward clarity over bass — explosions have weight, but they don't rumble your skull like a dedicated bass-head set.

**Mic quality surprised me.** The flip-to-mute design is intuitive, and the voice pickup is clean. Party chat test: my squadmates said I sounded "crisper than my HyperX" — one even asked if I'd upgraded. There's a subtle voice-monitoring feature (you hear yourself speak) that some love and some find distracting. It's toggleable in the Xbox Accessories app.

**Battery life is rated at 20 hours.** In my testing, I hit 18.5 hours with volume at 70% and Atmos enabled. USB-C charging gets you 4 hours of play from a 15-minute top-up — a genuinely useful feature for forgetful gamers.

### Where it falls short

**The build is the compromise.** The plastic feels hollow at the ear cup swivels. Dropped mine from desk height onto carpet — no damage, but the creak when flexing it wide enough to fit over a head with thick hair is unsettling. This won't survive being stuffed in a backpack daily.

**No Bluetooth is a real limitation.** You can't pair this to your phone for Discord or music while gaming. The included 3.5mm cable works with phones, but that's wired-only and kills the mic function on most devices. If you're switching between PC, Switch, and Xbox, you'll need the Xbox Wireless Adapter for Windows ($25 extra, not included).

**Comfort runs warm.** The leatherette ear cushions trap heat. After a 2-hour *Elden Ring* session, my ears were noticeably toasty. The clamp force is moderate — not headache-inducing, but you'll feel it on the jaw after extended play.

---

### Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're primarily on Xbox Series X|S or One, want console-quality spatial audio without breaking $150, and value a no-fuss wireless setup. The flip-to-mute mic alone justifies the price for squad-based games.

**Skip it if:** You need Bluetooth for Discord on PC, you're a competitive player who needs a boom mic with noise-cancellation (this is a flip mic, not a boom arm), or you have a larger head — the ear cups are on the snug side.

### FAQ

**Q: Does this work with PC?**  
A: Yes, but only wired via the included 3.5mm cable, or wirelessly with the Xbox Wireless Adapter for Windows (sold separately). No native Bluetooth.

**Q: Is the mic removable?**  
A: No. It flips up to mute, but it's permanently attached. If you want a removable boom mic, look at the SteelSeries Arctis Nova 7x.

**Q: Does it come with Dolby Atmos license?**  
A: No. The headset supports Atmos, but you need the $15 app license from the Microsoft Store. Windows Sonic is included free and sounds nearly identical for most games.
