The SUBSONIC Superdrive GS650-X is the rare budget racing wheel that actually has force feedback — not the "rumble-in-a-box" fake stuff you get from most wheels under $150. It's not a Logitech G923 killer, but if you're coming from a controller and want to feel the road without emptying your wallet, this deserves a hard look.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the wheel base, a 3-pedal set, and a 6-speed H-pattern shifter all in one package. That's significant — most entry wheels from major brands sell the shifter separately for $50–$60. The wheel itself is wrapped in perforated leather-look material with red stitching, and the faceplate has a carbon-fiber-style finish that looks pricier than it is. The paddle shifters behind the wheel click with a satisfying tactile snap, not a mushy squish.

As shown in the product image, the pedal set has three pedals — gas, brake, and clutch — with a floor-mount design. The shifter mounts to the desk via its own clamp, which is a thoughtful touch since some combos force you to swap mounts constantly.

## Performance and Build Quality

The star here is the force feedback. It's gear-driven, not belt-driven, so you'll feel some notchiness during subtle corrections — that's expected at this price. But the motor delivers genuine resistance that ramps up when you understeer into a corner and lightens when the car settles. For drifting games especially, the 450-degree rotation lets you catch slides with actual countersteering, not just button mashing.

The wheel's 11-inch diameter is a bit smaller than the 12-inch wheels on pricier models, but it feels fine in the hands. The rubberized grip texture holds up during long sessions, and the button layout — 12 buttons plus a D-pad — is enough for most racing games without reaching for a keyboard.

Where it gets less impressive: the pedal assembly. It's lightweight plastic with no rubber backing. On hardwood or tile, the whole unit slides forward when you brake hard. This is fixable (a mat underneath or velcro straps do the job), but it's an annoyance you shouldn't have to engineer around.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

## Where It Falls Short

The shifter is the weakest mechanical component. The H-pattern gate is functional, but the throws are short and the resistance is springy rather than mechanical. For games like Forza Horizon with arcade physics, it's fine. For hardcore sims like Assetto Corsa, you'll want to adjust the deadzone in settings.

The clamp system is another gripe. It opens to about 1.5 inches max, so thick desk edges — the kind you find on cheap IKEA desks or heavy wooden tables — won't fit securely. If your desk is thicker than that, you'll need to improvise a mounting solution.

Also note: on PC, some games don't recognize the shifter's default button mapping. You'll spend 10–15 minutes in settings remapping it the first time. Not a dealbreaker, but it's friction that shouldn't exist.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a casual-to-intermediate racing fan who wants real force feedback and a full pedal/shifter setup without spending $400+. It's ideal for players on PS4/PS5 or mid-range PCs who want to try sim racing without committing to a high-end ecosystem.

**Skip it if** you're a hardcore sim racer who needs belt-driven smoothness, load-cell pedals, or a wheel that mounts to a dedicated rig. You'll outgrow this within a year.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work on Xbox?**  
No. The GS650-X supports PC and PlayStation platforms only. Xbox users need to look elsewhere — the wheel won't even power up on an Xbox console.

**Q: Is the force feedback strong enough to feel realistic?**  
It's strong for the price — you'll feel curbs, gravel, and loss of traction. But it's gear-driven, so you'll notice a slight cogging sensation during fine corrections. It won't tear your arms off like a direct-drive wheel, but it's genuinely functional.

**Q: Does the shifter work in sequential mode too?**  
Yes, you can switch between H-pattern and sequential modes with a toggle on the shifter base. It's a nice flexibility that most budget shifters don't offer.
