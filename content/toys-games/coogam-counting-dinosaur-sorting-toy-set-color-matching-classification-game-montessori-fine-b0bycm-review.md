**Spoiler: Your toddler will love these tiny dinosaurs more than you expect.**

The Coogam Counting Dinosaur Sorting Toy Set looks simple in the product photos — a pile of colorful plastic dinos, some matching cups, a pair of tongs. But this thing has quietly racked up 240 ratings at 4.8 stars, which tells you something. Parents don't usually get that enthusiastic about a $20-ish toy unless it actually keeps kids busy and delivers on its promises.

I've tested this set with my own kids and a few neighborhood guinea pigs aged 2 to 5. Here's the real story.

---

**What's in the box**

You get 72 dinosaurs in 6 colors (12 of each), 6 matching sorting cups, and 2 tongs — one sized for righties, one for lefties. The pieces are small, around 1.5 to 2 inches, which makes them perfect for little hands but also means they're a choking hazard for kids under 3. That's not a flaw — it's a feature of this kind of toy — but keep it away from mouthy toddlers.

The plastic feels sturdy. No sharp edges, no paint that chips off after a week of aggressive sorting. One mom in the reviews said her son "throws them across the room and they still look brand new." I believe it — these survived my kids, which is the highest compliment I can give a toy.

---

**How it actually performs**

The core activity is straightforward: pick up a dinosaur with the tongs and drop it into the matching colored cup. That's it. But that simple loop is doing a lot of heavy lifting.

For a 2-year-old, it's a color recognition and fine motor challenge. The tongs require real grip strength and control — I watched my 3-year-old struggle for a full minute to pick up one dinosaur without dropping it, then beam with pride when he finally got it in the cup. That's the Montessori-style skill-building working exactly as intended.

For a 4 or 5-year-old, you can extend it into counting practice, simple addition ("how many green dinos do you have?"), or pattern matching. The set is flexible enough to grow with your kid.

One thing that surprised me: the cups are shallow and lightweight. A determined toddler will absolutely knock one over mid-sort, spilling the dinos everywhere. That's not a dealbreaker — it's actually part of the fun for some kids — but don't expect a neat, contained activity. This is a "sit at the table with a tray" toy, not a "set it on the carpet and walk away" toy.

---

**What buyers consistently praise**

The reviews are overwhelmingly positive, and the common threads are specific. One parent wrote: "My 2-year-old has been obsessed with this for weeks. The tongs are the perfect size for her hands." Another noted: "Great for homeschool. We use it for counting, sorting, and color matching — it's gotten way more use than I expected."

The most common praise is about the tongs. Multiple reviewers specifically mention that their kids struggled at first but improved noticeably within days — which is exactly what you want from a fine motor toy. The 72-piece count also gets called out repeatedly; it's enough for siblings or classroom use without anyone fighting over dinosaurs.

---

**Where it falls short**

I'll be honest about the downsides.

First, the dinosaur species are hard to tell apart. The package suggests you can sort by species as well as color, but two of the six types look nearly identical at this size. My 5-year-old couldn't reliably distinguish them, and honestly, I had to squint. If you want species-based sorting, this isn't the set for you.

Second, there's no storage container. 72 loose dinosaurs and 6 cups arrive in a plastic bag. You'll need a bin or zippered pouch to keep them organized, otherwise you'll be finding stray dinos under the couch for months.

Third, the cups are flimsier than I'd like. They're functional, but a kid who's heavy-handed will bend or crack one eventually. It's a $20 toy, so I'm not outraged, but it's worth knowing.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this safe for a 2-year-old?**
A: Yes, with supervision. The pieces are small enough to be a choking risk, so don't leave a 2-year-old alone with them. That said, the tongs and cups are designed for toddler-sized hands, and most 2-year-olds handle the dinos fine once they're past the mouthing stage.

**Q: Can it be used for math practice?**
A: Absolutely. The 72 pieces in 6 colors make it easy to practice counting, addition, subtraction, and even simple fractions (what fraction of the dinos are red?). It's a common homeschool pick for this reason.

**Q: How durable are the pieces?**
A: The dinosaurs are very durable — I've seen them dropped, thrown, and stepped on without damage. The cups are the weakest link; they're fine for normal use but will crack if a kid sits on them or bends them aggressively.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely useful fine motor and color sorting toy that'll get months of use, not days. The 72-piece count, dual tongs, and Montessori-style design make it a solid value for the price.

**Skip it if** your kid is too young for small pieces, or if you need a self-contained activity that doesn't require supervision. The lack of storage and shallow cups are real annoyances.
