Let me be blunt: you're not buying this LEGO set for the 290-something pieces. You're buying it for Ahsoka Tano in her Season 7 Clone Wars gear, and you'll be relieved to hear she's every bit as good as the hype suggests.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the Delta-7B Jedi Interceptor, Ahsoka Tano with her dual white-bladed lightsabers, and a small buildable tactical droid — the latter being a nice bonus for diorama builders. The ship itself measures about 11 inches long and 9 inches wide with the wings in attack position, which makes it a substantial display piece despite the modest piece count. The orange-and-white color scheme pops in person; photos don't quite capture how clean the printed canopy piece looks against the white hull panels.

**Build experience and real-world handling**

The build takes roughly 45 minutes to an hour depending on your pace. It's a solid middle-ground complexity: not so simple that adults will breeze through it in 15 minutes, but straightforward enough that a confident 8-year-old can handle most of it solo. The s-foils — those iconic split wings — use friction pin connections that hold their position well during play. I've swooshed this thing around more than I'd like to admit, and the wings stayed locked in both attack and cruise modes.

One genuinely clever design choice: the spring-loaded missile launchers are integrated into the wing structure rather than bolted on as obvious stud-shooters. They fire with satisfying pop and don't ruin the ship's sleek profile. As one Amazon reviewer put it, *"The missiles actually add to the look rather than taking away from it, which is rare for LEGO Star Wars ships."*

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 240 ratings, three themes dominate. First, the Ahsoka minifigure herself — printed arm accents, detailed face, and proper dual-wielding hands. Collectors are calling it the definitive Ahsoka. Second, the accuracy of the ship's proportions compared to the on-screen Delta-7B. Third, the playability-to-display balance. One top review notes, *"My son hasn't put it down in three days, but it also looks incredible on the shelf when he finally does."*

**Where it falls short**

The price-per-piece ratio stings. At typical retail, you're looking at roughly 11 cents per brick, which is steep for a set with this piece count. You're paying the Ahsoka premium, plain and simple. There's also no display stand included — a head-scratcher for a set this collector-focused. You'll spend another $10-15 on a compatible stand or make your own.

The stickers are another minor frustration. There are about a dozen of them, including two curved pieces that require patience to align perfectly. If you're a stickler for clean application, budget extra time and a brick separator. The tactical droid is also simpler than the version from other Clone Wars sets — it's fine, but it's clearly an afterthought.

**Who should buy this**

- **Clone Wars completists** who've been waiting for a proper Ahsoka Interceptor since the 2012 original. This is a significant upgrade, particularly the minifigure.
- **Parents of kids aged 8-12** who are into Star Wars — it's a playable ship that also looks great on a shelf.
- **LEGO Star Wars collectors** filling out their Jedi starfighter lineup. This slots nicely alongside Obi-Wan's and Anakin's versions.

Skip it if you're a pure playset builder who wants maximum value for your money — you'll find better piece-per-dollar ratios elsewhere in the LEGO Star Wars lineup.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it come with a display stand?**
A: No. The ship sits flat on its landing gear, which works fine, but if you want it angled in flight, you'll need a third-party stand.

**Q: Is this the same scale as other LEGO Jedi starfighters?**
A: Yes, it's consistent with recent Delta-7B releases. It pairs well with the 2023 Obi-Wan Kenobi's Jedi Interceptor set if you want to build a display squadron.

**Q: How durable is it for actual play?**
A: Sturdy. The wing mechanisms are the most fragile point, but they held up in my testing. The canopy is a single printed piece, so no scratching or peeling issues there.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want the best Ahsoka Tano minifigure LEGO has ever produced, or you're building out a Clone Wars starfighter collection and want a set that plays as well as it displays.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget and prioritize piece count over the Ahsoka license tax — though honestly, most Star Wars fans will find the premium justified.
