## What's Actually in the Box

You get 1,176 pieces, a remote control (2.4GHz, so no line-of-sight issues), the rechargeable battery pack for Johnny 5 himself, and a fairly clear instruction manual. The box is hefty — this isn't a "build it in an afternoon" set. Most builders report spending 6 to 10 hours across multiple sessions, which is worth knowing if you're buying this for a kid expecting instant gratification.

The finished model stands roughly 12 inches tall and captures Johnny 5's signature look well: the blocky head, the tread base, the expressive "eyebrow" panels. As shown in the product image, the proportions are right — it reads as Johnny 5 from across the room, not just "some robot."

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The pieces are compatible with standard building brick systems, which is a plus for parts integration. Clutch is firm — joints hold their position, and the treads actually grip and move when you drive it. The remote controls forward, reverse, turning, head movement, and arm gestures. It's not RC-car fast, but that's appropriate for a tracked robot; it moves at a deliberate, satisfying pace.

The rechargeable battery is a standout feature. One charge gave me roughly 45 minutes of continuous play, and it charges via USB-C (cable included). Battery life will vary with how much you're moving versus just displaying, but it's solid.

The instructions are decent but occasionally dense. Some steps have multiple pieces added at once, and the color contrast in the manual isn't always clear. Take your time, sort pieces by color first — it'll save you frustration.

## Where It Falls Short

The remote needs 2 AAA batteries that aren't included. Petty? A little. But when the main unit is rechargeable, it feels like an oversight.

More significantly: this is not a kid's toy in the traditional sense. The 1,176 pieces include lots of small connectors, and the build requires patience. Under-10s will struggle. This is a building set for enthusiasts, collectors, and patient teens — not a "buy it for your 8-year-old and walk away" situation.

Also, some reviewers noted that the treads can slip on smooth surfaces like hardwood. Carpet or tile with texture works better. Not a dealbreaker, but manage expectations.
