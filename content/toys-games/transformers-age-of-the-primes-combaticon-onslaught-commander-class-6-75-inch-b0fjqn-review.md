The Combaticons are finally getting the Commander-class treatment they deserve, and Onslaught leads the charge. If you've been waiting for a definitive Bruticus torso that doesn't compromise on presence, this Age of the Primes release is the one you've been circling. But at this price point, you need to know exactly what you're getting before you commit.

**What's in the box**

You get Onslaught in robot mode, his signature twin cannon assembly, and a turret attachment. No gimmicks, no gimmick weapons that vanish in alt mode — just the pieces that matter. The 6.75-inch Commander-class scale means he towers over Voyager figures, giving him the command presence a Combaticon leader deserves. The plastic density is noticeably better than recent mainline releases; joints click with purpose, and the paint applications on the chest and face are clean with minimal bleed.

**Performance and build quality**

In robot mode, Onslaught's articulation is excellent for a figure this size. The double-jointed elbows and ball-jointed hips allow for dynamic two-handed cannon poses without any sagging. The ratchets in the shoulders and knees are satisfyingly tight — no unintended collapses mid-pose. The transformation to vehicle mode is where things get interesting. It's a genuine multi-step puzzle that respects your intelligence, but the first run-through will test your patience. Several panels require precise alignment, and the instructions are dense. Budget at least 15 minutes for your first conversion, and watch for the chest panel that needs a firm push to seat properly.

As the combined mode torso, Onslaught performs his primary duty admirably. The connection ports are robust, and once you lock in the limbs from the other Combaticons, the combined form holds together with zero wobble. The included turret mounts cleanly on top, completing the Bruticus silhouette. This is where the figure earns its keep — as the centerpiece of a combiner collection.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The Amazon reviews, sitting at 4.0 stars across 200 ratings, highlight the same wins I found. One buyer notes, "Finally a Combaticon with proper heft — this thing feels like it costs what it does." Another praises the combined mode stability: "My Bruticus actually stands without leaning for the first time." The cannon articulation gets consistent love, with multiple reviewers noting how poseable the weapon system is compared to prior releases.

**Where it falls short**

The backpack is the elephant in the room. In robot mode, the folded vehicle components sit prominently behind the shoulders, and no amount of careful positioning fully hides them. Purists will find it distracting. The transformation's tight tolerances also drew complaints — one reviewer got stuck on the leg panel for twenty minutes, and I don't blame them. It's a "read the instructions carefully" figure, not a "wing it" figure. Finally, at the Commander-class price point, the lack of any additional effect parts or blast accessories feels like a missed opportunity.

**Who should buy this**

- **Combiner collectors** building Bruticus — this is the definitive Onslaught torso
- **Commander-class enthusiasts** who appreciate complex engineering and don't mind a transformation challenge
- **Display-oriented collectors** who pose in vehicle or combined mode more than robot mode

**Skip it if** you're a Transformers novice, prefer quick conversions, or can't tolerate visible backpack kibble on your shelf.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this combine with other Age of the Primes Combaticons?**
A: Yes, the ports are standardized for the line. You can connect the limbs from the other Combaticon releases to form Bruticus. The connection points are sturdy and hold weight well.

**Q: Is the plastic quality comparable to older Generations figures?**
A: It's better than recent mainline releases but not quite Masterpiece-tier. The plastic feels dense and durable, with good paint coverage. The joints are tighter than most figures from the last five years.

**Q: How tall is the combined Bruticus form?**
A: With Onslaught as the torso and the other Combaticons attached, Bruticus stands roughly 12-13 inches tall — an imposing centerpiece for any collection.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're building a Bruticus and want a torso that doesn't compromise on stability or presence. The engineering is solid, the combined mode is rock-solid, and the Commander-class scale delivers the heft this character deserves.

**Skip it if** you're on the fence about complex transformations or the price point gives you pause — this is a figure for dedicated collectors, not casual buyers.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A worthy centerpiece with some visual compromises, but the combined mode functionality makes it a must-have for Combaticon completists.
