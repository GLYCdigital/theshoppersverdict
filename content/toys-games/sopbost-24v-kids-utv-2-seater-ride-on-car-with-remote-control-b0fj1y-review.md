**The 24V difference is real — this isn't your neighbor's 12V Costco special.**

Most ride-on cars in this price bracket run 12V and die on the first grass incline. The sopbost 24V Kids UTV skips that problem entirely. With a dual-motor 24V system, this 2-seater actually moves two kids with intent. If you've got siblings close in age or a kid who's outgrown the single-seat plastic buggies, this is the upgrade that makes sense.

**What shows up at your door**

The box is heavy — roughly 60 pounds. You get the UTV body, front and rear axles, four rubber EVA tires, the 24V battery, charger, remote control, and a hardware bag with everything labeled. The seats are padded vinyl with a 3-point harness each, and the roll cage is metal, not painted plastic. That's a meaningful durability point — kids will lean on this thing.

Set aside a solid hour. The instructions are mostly diagrams, and the steering column alignment is the one step that'll test your patience. If the front wheels don't sit perfectly straight before you tighten the tie rod, you'll chase a drift issue. Take your time on that step and you'll be fine.

**How it actually performs**

On flat pavement, this hits its advertised 8 mph top speed (low speed mode caps at 3.7 mph for beginners). The suspension is independent coil-over at all four corners — it absorbs sidewalk cracks and uneven lawn without the jarring bounce you get from rigid-axle rides. The rubber tires matter more than you'd think; they grip wet grass where plastic wheels just spin.

The 24V motor pulls two kids up a 15-degree grass slope without bogging down. One parent reviewer put it plainly: *"My 6-year-old and his friend ride this every day on our hilly yard — it climbs everything they try. My neighbor's 12V Jeep can't make it up our driveway."*

The remote control is the safety feature that sells this for younger kids. It gives you proportional throttle and steering override — meaning you can drive the UTV yourself while your child sits in the seat, then hand over control as they get comfortable. The remote has a 30-foot range and doesn't drop signal through walls.

**Where it falls short**

The battery is the real compromise. A 24V system draws more current, and the included 12Ah battery gives you roughly 45–60 minutes under a full two-kid load on grass. On flat pavement with one kid, you'll squeeze closer to 90 minutes. Plan for a recharge cycle between play sessions — there's no swappable battery option.

The assembly is also more involved than the "30 minutes" the box claims. Plan for 60–90 minutes with basic tools. And while the build quality is solid for the price, the front bumper is decorative — don't expect it to survive a full-speed collision with a tree.

**Who should buy this**

- **Parents of two kids ages 3–7** who want a ride both can enjoy simultaneously without fighting over a single seat
- **Families with sloped or grassy yards** where 12V rides constantly stall — this is the fix
- **Anyone who wants parent override control** for a younger child not yet ready for independent driving

**Skip it if** you need all-day battery life, want true off-road capability, or have a single child under 3 who'd be better served by a lighter, cheaper 12V model.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**  
Realistically 45–60 minutes under two-kid, grassy-terrain use. Single kid on pavement gets closer to 90 minutes. The charger takes about 4-5 hours for a full recharge.

**Q: Is the remote control easy for parents to use?**  
Yes — it's a straightforward proportional stick control. It overrides the pedal completely, so if your kid hits the gas while you're driving, the UTV ignores them. One button switches between parent and child drive modes.

**Q: Can this handle two kids of different sizes?**  
Each seat has a 66-pound weight limit, so two average-sized kids under 7 are fine. The 24V motor handles the combined weight without struggling on flat ground.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a 2-seater that won't embarrass itself on grass, has genuine parent override, and can survive daily sibling use. **Skip it if** the 45-minute battery life is a dealbreaker or you're not prepared for an hour-plus assembly session.

At a 4.2/5 rating, this is one of the better-value 24V 2-seaters on the market — just budget for a second battery if your kids ride hard.
