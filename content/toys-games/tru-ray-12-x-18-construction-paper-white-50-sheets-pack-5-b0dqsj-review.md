Tru-Ray has been the gold standard in construction paper since 1924, and this 12" x 18" white pack continues that legacy — but it's not without its quirks. With a 4.1-star average from 204 ratings, let's break down whether this oversized paper deserves a spot in your craft room or classroom.

## What You Actually Get

Open the pack and you'll find 50 sheets of bright white construction paper measuring a true 12 x 18 inches. That's a full 2 inches longer than the standard 12 x 16 sheets most schools stock, which makes a real difference for poster projects and large-format artwork. Each sheet weighs in at 76 lb — noticeably thicker than the flimsy 50 lb paper you'll find at dollar stores.

The texture is that classic construction paper tooth — rough enough to grab crayon and chalk pigment well, but smooth enough for clean marker lines. As shown in the product image, the sheets are cut cleanly with no ragged edges or off-kilter corners.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

Here's where Tru-Ray earns its reputation. The heavyweight stock handles wet media far better than budget alternatives. Glue sticks don't warp it, watercolor paints don't soak through immediately, and tempera paint won't turn the paper into a soggy mess. One buyer noted, "Best quality construction paper I've found. Doesn't tear like the cheap stuff from big box stores."

The sulfur-free and acid-free construction matters if you're making keepsake projects. Unlike cheaper papers that yellow within months, Tru-Ray maintains its bright white color for years. Teachers have confirmed this holds up in classroom settings where paper gets handled roughly.

Cutting is clean too — sharp scissors glide through without fraying, and paper trimmers don't crush the edges. This matters for precise craft work where jagged cuts ruin the final product.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the limitations. First, you're getting only white. If your project needs multiple colors, you'll need to buy the assorted packs separately, which doubles your upfront cost. Second, 50 sheets disappear fast in a classroom of 25+ students — a single project can burn through an entire pack.

Some buyers have reported inconsistent sheet counts, with one reviewer noting their pack came with 48 sheets instead of 50. It's not widespread, but worth checking your pack upon arrival.

The 12x18 size is also a double-edged sword. It won't fit standard 12x12 scrapbook sleeves or most storage bins designed for letter-size paper. You'll need dedicated large-format storage to keep sheets flat and undamaged.

## Who Should Buy This

**Art teachers and classroom educators** — the durability and large format make this ideal for group projects, murals, and displays that need to survive hallway traffic.

**Homeschool parents** — the heavyweight stock handles everything from finger painting to science fair boards without frustration.

**Serious crafters** — if you're making custom greeting cards, large-format collages, or paper flowers, the acid-free quality ensures your work stays vibrant for years.

Skip this if you need a quick, cheap paper for everyday scribbles — standard 50 lb construction paper at half the price will suffice. Also skip if you need a variety of colors from a single purchase.

## FAQ

**Is this paper good for acrylic paint?** Yes, but thin your paint slightly. The 76 lb weight handles acrylics better than most construction papers, though heavy impasto techniques may cause slight warping.

**Can I use this in a standard printer?** No — 12x18 is a large-format size. You'll need a printer that supports tabloid/ledger paper at minimum, and most home printers won't accommodate the thickness.

**How does this compare to Pacon or Crayola brand?** Tru-Ray is thicker and more durable than Crayola's standard construction paper. Pacon is comparable in quality but often costs more per sheet.
