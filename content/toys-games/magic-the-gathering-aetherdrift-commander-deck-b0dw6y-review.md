**The Short Version:** The Aetherdrift Commander Deck, specifically the **Eternal Might** precon, is the standout of the set — a surprisingly well-tuned Zombie tribal deck that works right out of the box. It's not perfect, but for $45–55, it's one of the best value precons Wizards has shipped in recent memory.

---

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you're looking at:
- **100-card Commander deck** (including the commander, Temmet, Master of the Dead)
- **1 foil commander** (alternate art, looks sharp)
- **A deck box** (the standard flimsy cardboard one — not a premium upgrade, but it does the job)
- **Strategy insert, token cards, and a life tracker**

The deck runs **black-blue** — a slightly unusual pairing for Zombies, which most players expect in mono-black or Golgari. That's the first thing that sets this apart.

## Performance and Build Quality

I've played this deck in roughly a dozen games across three weeks. Here's the honest breakdown:

**The good:** Temmet is a legitimate commander. He gives your zombies flying and lifelink, which turns your board from "annoying chump blockers" into a real clock. The deck's mana base is functional — 38 lands, with a few utility lands like [[Hall of the Bandit Lord]] that actually earn their slot. You'll be casting your commander on turn 4-5 consistently.

The new card [[Eternal Might]] is the real star. It's an enchantment that returns a zombie from your graveyard to the battlefield whenever you attack. In practice, this means your board is nearly impossible to clear — every combat step rebuilds your army.

**The not-so-good:** The deck has one glaring weakness: it's all-in on combat. If you're facing a control player with [[Cyclonic Rift]] or any decent board wipe package, you'll struggle to rebuild without Temmet in play. There's no real recursion engine in the 99 — the deck relies heavily on the commander staying alive.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the weak points:

1. **Board wipe vulnerability** — This is the biggest issue. If Temmet gets removed twice, you're in topdeck mode with no way to rebuild. You'll want to add [[Living Death]] or [[Patriarch's Bidding]] for protection.

2. **Filler cards** — About 10 cards in the 99 are obvious filler. [[Zombie Apocalypse]] is a trap — it costs 6 mana and destroys all humans, which most tables won't care about. [[Endless Ranks of the Dead]] is too slow for modern Commander. Budget for ~$30 in upgrades if you want it to compete at higher-power tables.

3. **No combo lines** — If you're a combo player, this deck won't satisfy you. It's a fair, combat-focused deck. That's fine for most tables, but worth knowing before you buy.

## Who Should Buy This

- **New-to-Commander players** — This is a forgiving, straightforward deck that teaches good fundamentals without being boring
- **Zombie tribal enthusiasts** — You'll appreciate Temmet as a fresh take on the tribe
- **Budget-conscious players** — The value is genuinely there, with several cards holding their price well

**Skip it if:** You're a cEDH player, you hate combat-focused strategies, or you already own a tuned Zombie deck with [[Wilhelt, the Rotcleaver]] — this won't replace that.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this deck playable out of the box?**  
Yes, genuinely. It's not a top-tier deck, but it'll hold its own at most casual tables. I'd rate it a solid 7/10 in terms of precon power level.

**Q: What's the best upgrade path?**  
Replace the filler with cheaper recursion and protection. [[Undead Augur]] and [[Gravecrawler]] are must-adds. [[Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth]] fixes the mana base significantly.

**Q: Does it include the guaranteed value reprints?**  
Yes — [[Rooftop Storm]] is the big one, plus [[Bitterblossom]] and [[Diregraf Colossus]]. You're getting solid value regardless of what happens to the new cards.
