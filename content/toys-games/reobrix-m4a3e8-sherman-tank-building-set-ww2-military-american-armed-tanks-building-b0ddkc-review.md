**A Brick-Built Sherman That Actually Rolls — But Check the Fine Print**

If you're shopping for a WW2 military building set, you've probably seen the big-name brick brands and balked at the price. The Reobrix M4A3E8 Sherman Tank sits in that sweet spot between budget sets and premium builds — but it's not without quirks. Here's what you need to know before you click "add to cart."

**What's in the Box**

The set arrives in a sturdy, color-printed box that shows the finished Sherman in an action scene. Inside you get numbered bags (a welcome touch), a full-color instruction booklet, and a small sticker sheet — though you'll barely need it. The build itself uses roughly 600 pieces, putting it in the "afternoon project" category for most adult builders. There's no minifigure in the box, which surprised me given the box art. More on that below.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The standout feature here is the track system. Unlike many budget sets that use a single rubber band or static links, the Reobrix Sherman uses individual tread links that articulate. Builders consistently praise this. One reviewer wrote, "The treads actually move like a real tank track — my son rolls it across the carpet and the suspension compresses over bumps." That's not marketing fluff; the suspension uses small coil springs that give it genuine give.

The turret rotates a full 360 degrees, and the main gun elevates up and down. The hatches open, so you can seat a minifigure if you have one — though again, none is included. The printed pieces are excellent: the white star, unit markings, and "M4A3E8" text are all pad-printed, meaning they won't fade or peel like stickers. For a set at this price point, that's a genuinely premium touch.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the 220 ratings, a few themes repeat:

- **The value proposition.** At its typical street price, you're getting a detailed, moving model for less than half what a comparable licensed set would cost.
- **The build experience.** "Instructions are clear, pieces fit well, and the finished model looks fantastic on my shelf," noted one reviewer.
- **The display factor.** The finished Sherman is roughly 10 inches long — substantial enough to be a desk centerpiece, not a pocket toy.

**Where It Falls Short**

Be honest with yourself about these tradeoffs:

- **No minifigure.** The box art suggests one is included. It isn't. For kids who want to play out battles, that's a real disappointment. You'll need to buy a compatible figure separately.
- **Tight tolerances on small pins.** The track links and some suspension components require firm pressure to seat. A 9-year-old may need adult help. Not a dealbreaker, but know it going in.
- **Brick separation tool not included.** You'll want one on hand when you make the inevitable misalignment.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **History buffs and AFOLs (adult fans of LEGO)** who want a detailed, affordable WW2 display piece without paying collector prices.
- **Parents of kids 10+** who are patient and enjoy a challenge — just budget for a minifigure.
- **Teachers or hobbyists** building a WW2 diorama who need a rolling tank that looks the part.

Skip it if you're buying for a child under 8, or if you demand licensed brand compatibility — this uses its own brick system, so it won't mix seamlessly with other major brands.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many pieces are in the Reobrix M4A3E8 Sherman Tank?**
A: The set contains approximately 600 pieces, including the articulated track links and suspension springs. The build takes most adults 2–3 hours.

**Q: Does it come with a minifigure?**
A: No. Despite the box art suggesting otherwise, no figure is included. You'll need to source a compatible one separately if you want a crew member.

**Q: Are the markings stickers or printed?**
A: Printed directly on the bricks. The white star, unit numbers, and text are pad-printed and won't peel or fade.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a detailed, rolling WW2 tank model that looks great on a shelf and won't break the bank. The printed details, functional suspension, and smooth-rolling tracks punch well above the price point.

**Skip it if** you need minifigures out of the box, or if the builder is too young to handle tight-fitting pins without frustration.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely impressive budget build with a few honest compromises. For the price, it's hard to beat.
