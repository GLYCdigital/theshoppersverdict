## Build Experience and Real-World Play

Assembly takes roughly 15–20 minutes for an experienced builder, maybe 30 for younger kids. The build is straightforward—no complex technic pins or fragile connections that'll snap on the first drop. Both mechs are sturdy enough for aggressive play, though the Spider-Man mech's thin legs can feel slightly wobbly if you're posing it mid-air.

The articulation is genuinely good. Shoulders rotate, elbows bend, hips swivel—you can recreate that classic "Spider-Man pointing" meme or have Anti-Venom looming over him menacingly. The web elements snap onto the hands cleanly and don't fall off mid-play, which is more than I can say for some older LEGO Marvel sets.

LEGO recommends this for ages 7+, and that feels right. Kids younger than that will struggle with the smaller pieces and the cockpit mechanism.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest talk: this set has two real weaknesses.

**Stickers.** You'll be applying roughly a dozen stickers for the cockpit consoles and mech detailing. Some buyers report them peeling after a few weeks of heavy play. If stickers are a dealbreaker, this isn't the set for you—though at this price point, printed pieces would've pushed the cost up significantly.

**Short build time.** At 217 pieces, this is a quick build. If you're buying this as a "big afternoon project" for a kid, it'll be done before lunch. It's a snack, not a meal—perfect for a birthday gift or impulse buy, but don't expect hours of construction.

The Anti-Venom mech's legs also have a slightly limited range of motion compared to the Spider-Man mech—you won't get the same dynamic crouching poses from the bad guy.
