If you’ve ever tried to explain “what makes ten” to a wiggly five-year-old using only your fingers and a dry-erase marker, you already know why this Coogam set exists. The magnetic ten-frame is a staple of early math instruction, and this EVA foam version has earned a 4.7-star average from 235 ratings for one simple reason: it works without feeling like a chore.

## What’s Actually in the Box

The set ships with two double-sided magnetic boards — one side shows a classic 10-frame grid, the other expands to a 20-frame layout for bigger numbers. You get 20 round counters in two colors (red and blue, as shown in the product image), each made from 2mm-thick EVA foam with a full magnetic backing. There’s also a dry-erase marker and a small eraser, so you can write equations directly on the boards. No sorting tray, no storage case — the counters come loose in a plastic bag. That’s my first honest gripe, because if you’re buying this for a classroom, you’ll want your own container from day one.

## How It Performs in Real Life

The magnets are the star here. They’re strong enough to hold the boards vertically on a whiteboard or fridge, and the foam counters stick firmly without sliding off mid-lesson. I tested one on a standard office whiteboard and it stayed put through a 10-minute explanation of addition facts. The EVA foam is thick enough that little fingers can grab and place counters easily — a critical detail for kids aged 4-7 who don’t have fine motor control for flimsy plastic chips.

The dual-sided board design is genuinely useful. The 10-frame side is perfect for introducing number bonds and “friends of ten.” Flip it over, and the 20-frame layout lets you demonstrate teen numbers, place value, and simple regrouping without buying a second set. That versatility justifies the price point for most homeschool families.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The reviews consistently highlight two things: durability and engagement. One parent wrote, “My son hated math drills until we started using these. Now he asks to ‘play’ with the counters.” Another teacher noted, “I’ve had mine for a full school year, and the magnets haven’t weakened.” The color contrast between red and blue counters makes patterns visually obvious — kids can literally see that 6 and 4 make 10 before they memorize it.

## Where It Falls Short

The foam is the weak point. After several months of aggressive use — think tossing counters into a bin, dropping them on tile floors, or letting a toddler chew-test one — the edges start to show scuffs and slight peeling. It doesn’t affect function, but it won’t win any beauty contests. Also, the marker included is basic; you’ll want a low-odor dry-erase marker for classroom use, because the stock one smells faintly of solvent.

The loose packaging is my second complaint. For a product aimed at teachers and parents, a small plastic container or zip bag would have been a five-minute fix. As-is, you’re responsible for corralling 20 magnets on day one.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeschool parents** teaching K-1 math: This is your core audience. The set covers counting, addition, subtraction, and early place value across a full year of lessons.
- **Classroom teachers** who want a small-group manipulative: Buy two sets and you have enough for a center activity with four kids. The magnets hold up on a whiteboard during whole-group instruction.
- **Parents of 4-6 year olds** who want a screen-free math activity: It’s more engaging than flash cards and less messy than playdough mats.

**Skip it if** your kid is already past two-digit addition — the 20-frame maxes out quickly. Or if you demand premium materials; you can get wooden or plastic versions for double the price, but the EVA foam is the budget-friendly sweet spot.

## FAQ

**Q: Are the magnets strong enough for a fridge?**  
Yes. The boards and counters stick to most metal surfaces, including refrigerators. They won’t hold a stack of papers, but they’ll stay put during normal play.

**Q: Can I use this on a magnetic whiteboard?**  
Absolutely. That’s the primary use case. The boards lay flat and the counters attach securely, even vertically.

**Q: How many counters come in the set?**  
Twenty — ten red and ten blue. That’s enough for a full 20-frame or two separate 10-frame activities.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, durable math manipulative that makes number sense visual and tactile for young learners. **Skip it if** you need a heavy-duty classroom set for constant daily rotation or you’re shopping for a child past second-grade math.

The Coogam Magnetic Ten-Frame Set earns a strong **4.6 out of 5**. It’s not flashy, but it’s effective — and for the price, that’s the best kind of math tool. Just buy a small container to store the counters before you open the bag.
