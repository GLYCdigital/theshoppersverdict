**The Fingerboard Skate Park Kit Review: 3 Finger Skateboards + Obstacles**

Look, I've tested my share of cheap fingerboard kits that promise "pro-level" setups and deliver wobbly plastic junk that snaps in a week. This one from the Fingerboard Skate Park Kit with three fingerboards and a modular park is different — but it's not perfect either. Here's the unvarnished truth after putting it through real sessions.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get three complete fingerboards (roughly 96mm long — true-to-scale mini decks), a set of ramps, rails, kickers, and a quarter pipe that assemble into a skate park. The boards have actual grip tape, working trucks, and wheels that spin freely out of the box. As shown in the product image, the park pieces connect into a continuous line — but here's the smart part: they're modular, so you can break them apart and reconfigure into different layouts. That's more than most kits in this price range offer.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The plastic park pieces are thicker than I expected — maybe 3-4mm on the ramp surfaces — and they withstood aggressive ollie attempts and repeated board slams without cracking. The fingerboards themselves have decent weight; they're not featherlight junk. Wheels roll smoothly on flat surfaces, though they're not precision bearings — expect some rolling resistance, not a free-spin luxury feel.

One thing that surprised me: the grip tape is real, textured material that actually holds your finger. That matters when you're trying to nail a kickflip over the rail. The boards' trucks are tight enough to stay stable but loose enough to carve. For a toy under most budgets, that's solid engineering.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 235 ratings (4.7 stars), the recurring theme is value and variety. One buyer noted, "My son and his friend each got their own board and they spent hours building different ramp setups." Another said, "The pieces fit together snugly and nothing broke on day one like the last set we bought." The three-board inclusion is a genuine differentiator — most kits give you one board and a ramp; this covers multiplayer sessions or backup boards when one inevitably gets lost under the couch.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest gripes: the ramps' painted surfaces are slick. The grip tape grips your finger, but the board's wheels don't grip the painted plastic like they would on wood or concrete. Slides feel more like glides — not a dealbreaker, but purists will notice. The connectors between park pieces are small tabs that require precise alignment; kids under 7 will likely need adult help snapping them together. And there's no carry case — the pieces are loose in the box, so you'll want a zip bag or shoebox to keep them organized.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Parents of kids 6-12** who want a durable, multi-player fingerboard setup that won't shatter in a week
- **Fingerboard beginners** who want to test the hobby without dropping $50+ on a pro wooden deck and single obstacle
- **Classroom or party favor buyers** — three boards mean three kids can play simultaneously without fighting over one

**Skip it if** you're a serious fingerboarder chasing competition-grade precision. This is a toy-grade product, not a pro setup. You'll outgrow it fast.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are the fingerboards real size?**
A: Yes, roughly 96-100mm long — standard mini fingerboard scale. They'll work with other fingerboard obstacles you might already own.

**Q: Does it need assembly?**
A: Minimal. The ramps and rails have tabs that snap together; no tools needed. Takes about 5 minutes to set up the full park.

**Q: Are the wheels replaceable?**
A: No — they're press-fit plastic. If a wheel wears out, you replace the whole board. At this price, that's acceptable, but worth knowing before buying.

**The Verdict: Buy It**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, multi-player fingerboard park that's genuinely fun and won't fall apart. The three-board setup is smart, the modular ramps add replay value, and the build quality beats the price point.

**Skip it if** you're chasing pro-level precision — this is a gateway toy, not an endgame setup.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a half-star deduction for the slick ramp surfaces and missing storage solution, but this delivers real value for the money.
