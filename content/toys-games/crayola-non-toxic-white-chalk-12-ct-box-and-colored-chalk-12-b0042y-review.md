Let me be direct: chalk is chalk until it isn't. Cheap sidewalk chalk crumbles in your hand, squeals on the pavement, and leaves pasty smears instead of crisp lines. Crayola's offering—two 12-count boxes, one white, one colored—sits at a familiar 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, and after breaking down what buyers actually say, here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

You get two separate 12-count boxes: one all-white, one assorted colors (typically red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and violet—two sticks each). That's 24 sticks total. Each stick is the standard 3.25-inch length, roughly 0.38 inches thick. The colored box uses a classic Crayola palette, saturated and bright on paper but—as you'll see—slightly different in practice on pavement.

**Performance and build quality**

The white chalk is the star. Buyers consistently describe it as smooth, dense, and reliable. It lays down opaque, even lines on slate boards and holds up reasonably well on rough concrete. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "These are the only chalk sticks that don't snap in half the moment my kids start drawing." That durability comes from a tighter compression than dollar-store brands—you can feel the difference in hand weight.

The colored chalk is where things get nuanced. It's softer than the white, which means brighter color payoff but also faster wear. On smooth surfaces like a chalkboard or a paved driveway, it's excellent. On rough brick or heavily textured concrete, expect pieces to break off. Several reviewers noted this tradeoff: "Colors are vivid, but my 6-year-old goes through a colored stick in about two sidewalk drawings." That's not a defect—it's physics. Pigment-heavy chalk is inherently more fragile.

Both boxes are ACMI AP-certified non-toxic, which matters if you've got toddlers who treat chalk like a snack. No dust clouds either—the formula is low-dust compared to generic brands, though you'll still want to rinse little hands after play.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the loudest theme. At typical pricing, this bundle costs barely more than a single 12-count box of premium chalk. Parents specifically call out the convenience of having white for homework and colored for play in one purchase. One verified buyer summarized the consensus: "Best chalk for the price. We've repurchased three times."

**Where it falls short**

No storage solution. The cardboard boxes are fine for shelf display but flimsy for backpacks or sidewalk totes. Loose sticks will rattle around and occasionally snap in transit. The colored chalk's softness is the other honest drawback—if your kids draw exclusively on rough concrete, you'll burn through the colored box twice as fast as the white.

**Who should buy this**

- **Parents of kids 3–10**: The non-toxic certification and smooth application make this the default choice for sidewalk art and classroom homework.
- **Teachers**: The white chalk especially is a reliable, cost-effective workhorse for daily board use.
- **Artists doing outdoor sketching**: The color payoff on smooth surfaces is genuinely good for the price.

**Skip it if** you need chalk for rough brick or textured stone exclusively—look for a harder, more expensive street-chalk brand. Also skip if you want a single, organized storage container.

**FAQ**

*Does the colored chalk stain clothing or driveways?* Colored chalk can leave pigment on porous surfaces, especially if wet. On dry concrete, it washes away with rain or a hose. On clothing, treat it like dust—shake it out before washing; no special stain treatment needed.

*Is this chalk safe for chalkboards?* Yes, for both slate and melamine boards. The white chalk erases cleanly with a standard eraser. The colored chalk is also fine but may leave a faint tint on porous boards—use a damp cloth if needed.

*How does this compare to sidewalk chalk sold in bulk buckets?* Crayola's sticks are denser and less crumbly than most bulk brands. You'll use fewer sticks per drawing because they don't shatter on impact. The tradeoff is a higher per-stick cost, but lower waste.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want dependable, low-dust chalk for school, home, or sidewalk play at a fair price. The white chalk alone justifies the purchase; the colored box is a solid bonus despite its softer build.

**Skip it if** your primary surface is rough masonry, or if you need a single, kid-proof storage container.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting a quarter point for the fragile colored chalk and missing storage. Everything else earns its keep.
