## What’s Actually in the Box

The kit arrives in a sturdy, zip-up case roughly the size of a hardcover book. Inside you get:  
- 12 eyeshadow pans (pastel-heavy: pinks, lavenders, mint, peach)  
- 2 lip glosses (one clear, one tinted strawberry)  
- 1 blush compact with a small mirror  
- 3 brushes (flat shadow brush, smudge brush, lip applicator)  
- A mini mirror embedded in the case lid  

No glitter bombs or neon clown colors — this is a “natural glam” palette designed for ages 4–9. The case itself feels like a real makeup bag, not a flimsy toy. That matters when your kid wants to “do makeup like mommy.”

## Performance and Build Quality: The Real Test

I tested this with my own 6-year-old niece over two weeks. The first thing I noticed: the formulas are **washable without scrubbing**. A full face of eyeshadow, blush, and gloss came off with warm water and a baby wipe — no staining on her skin, and her pajamas stayed clean after a post-play nap. That’s the #1 feature parents actually care about, and this kit nails it.

The brushes are the surprise standout. Most kids’ sets include scratchy nylon brushes that skate across skin. These have dense, soft synthetic bristles that pick up pigment and blend like a cheap adult brush. My niece managed to create a gradient eye look (on her own, unprompted) — which tells you the tools aren’t fighting the user.

One honest flaw: **the pigment intensity is low**. The eyeshadows are more like tinted powder than color. If your kid wants dramatic purple lids, they’ll be disappointed. But for a first makeup experience, that’s actually a safety feature — it’s nearly impossible to make a mess that looks bad.

The case latch is the weak point. It’s a simple snap closure that feels thin. One Amazon reviewer wrote: *“The latch broke after three weeks of daily use — the case still closes, but it won’t stay shut on its own.”* That’s a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if your kid is rough on toys.
