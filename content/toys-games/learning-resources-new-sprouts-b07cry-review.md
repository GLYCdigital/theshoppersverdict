## What You Actually Get

The New Sprouts set includes a mix of fruits and vegetables — think bananas, tomatoes, corn, carrots, and similar staples. The pieces are intentionally sized for toddlers: roughly 2 to 4 inches each, chunky enough to grip but not so large they're awkward. As one parent put it, "My 18-month-old can hold the corn in one hand and the banana in the other. Perfect size for little fingers."

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and New Sprouts has a couple of honest flaws.

**The basket is the weak link.** Some sets include a woven-look basket that's noticeably flimsier than the food pieces. It bends under the weight of the contents and won't survive aggressive play. One reviewer warned, "The basket collapsed after two weeks. The food is great, but don't expect the container to last."

**Color consistency varies.** A few buyers noted that colors can differ slightly between batches. One person's "orange" came out more yellow-orange than the product photos suggested. It's a minor cosmetic issue, but if your kid is particular about colors, it's worth knowing.

**No price point to anchor value.** Since pricing fluctuates, focus on whether the set's quality justifies the cost. At typical retail, you're paying a slight premium over generic brands — but you're also getting pieces that won't need replacing in three months.
