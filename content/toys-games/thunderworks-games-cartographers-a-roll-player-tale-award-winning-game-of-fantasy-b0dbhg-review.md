**The $30 map-drawing game that quietly became a modern classic — here's the honest breakdown.**

You've probably seen Cartographers on a shelf at your local game store, that box with the hand-drawn map aesthetic and the dragon curling around the title. It's one of those games that looks deceptively simple — you draw shapes on a grid, how hard can it be? — and then you lose an entire evening to it. With 240 ratings and a 4.8-star average on Amazon, it's clearly doing something right. The question is whether the Awa expansion version is worth your $30, or if you should hunt down the base game instead.

**What's actually in the box**

This particular listing bundles the base game with the Awa expansion. You get two dry-erase map boards (double-sided, so four different map layouts), four eras of scoring cards, the deck of terrain cards that drives the game, and the expansion's four new map packs. The components are exactly what you'd expect from Thunderworks at this price point: thick, durable cards that survive regular shuffling, boards that wipe clean without ghosting, and a rulebook that's actually readable. The box has a nice insert that keeps everything organized — a small thing, but you'll appreciate it after your third game.

**How it actually plays**

Each round, someone flips a terrain card that shows you a shape to draw on your map — a forest, a mountain range, a village. You draw it wherever it fits, but you're also trying to satisfy scoring objectives that change each game: place water near mountains, surround ruins with forests, keep your settlements connected. The clever bit is the "ambush" cards that force everyone to draw a monster shape on their map, which scores points at the end but blocks off real estate in the meantime.

The Awa expansion doesn't add new mechanics — it adds new scoring variants. Four new map packs that change what you're trying to accomplish, which is exactly what this game needed. After a dozen games with the base set, you'll find yourself gravitating to the same strategies. The expansion forces you to think differently, which is the whole point of buying it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The solo mode gets a lot of love. One reviewer put it well: "I bought this specifically for solo play and it has not disappointed. The automa system is simple but creates real tension — you're always competing against a score that's creeping up." Another noted that "the learning curve is perfect — you can teach it in five minutes but the strategy depth keeps pulling you back."

The game's pace is another recurring theme. It plays in about 45 minutes, which is the sweet spot for a filler game that has actual substance. Several buyers mentioned that it's become their go-to for mixed groups — experienced gamers and casual players both enjoy it, which is harder to pull off than it sounds.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. Player interaction is nearly nonexistent. You're all drawing on your own boards, and the only way you affect other players is indirectly through the shared terrain deck. If you like the cutthroat energy of games like Catan or Ticket to Ride, you'll find this too solitary.

The Awa expansion also has a learning curve issue. The new scoring cards double the number of things you're tracking, and for a game that's supposed to be light and accessible, that can feel like homework. Play the base game a few times before you crack open the expansion packs.

The dry-erase markers that come in the box are serviceable but nothing special. They work fine, but you'll probably want to swap in your own fine-tip markers for more precise drawing — a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the player who likes spatial puzzles and optimization. If you enjoy Sudoku, Tetris, or the map-drawing segments of tabletop RPGs, this will scratch that itch. It's also an excellent family game — kids as young as 10 can pick it up quickly, and the fantasy theme isn't so heavy that it puts off non-gamers.

Skip it if you need direct player conflict, or if you're a heavy gamer who prefers deep strategic complexity over elegant, accessible design. This is a light-to-medium game, and it knows it.

**FAQs**

*Does the Awa expansion require the base game?* Yes, this bundle includes both, but if you already own the base game, you can buy the Awa expansion separately for around $10-12.

*Is it actually good solo?* Yes, genuinely. The solo mode uses a simple scoring system that creates real pressure without adding bookkeeping. It's one of the few games where solo play feels as satisfying as multiplayer.

*How many players does it support?* It plays 1-5 players, and it scales well. At higher player counts, everyone draws simultaneously, so adding more players doesn't add downtime.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a game that's easy to teach, hard to master, and works at every player count from 1 to 5. The Awa expansion makes an already great game better, and at $30, you're getting solid value.

**Skip it if:** You need player interaction or direct conflict in your games, or you're looking for something with heavy strategic depth.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Awa expansion elevates an already strong design, but the minimal interaction and expansion learning curve keep it from perfection.
