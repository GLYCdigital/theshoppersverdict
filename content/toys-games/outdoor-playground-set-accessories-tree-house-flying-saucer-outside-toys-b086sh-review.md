Let's be honest: most swing set accessories are junk. They're plastic knockoffs that crack by August and leave you wondering why you bothered. The Tree House Flying Saucer is not that. This thing has a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, and after digging through what buyers actually say, the praise is specific and consistent — not the usual "my kids love it!" fluff.

**What you're actually getting**

The saucer itself is a 30-inch diameter nylon-webbed disc with a reinforced steel rim. The suspension system uses three independent straps that converge at a single swivel point, which means the spin is smooth and the wobble is controlled. It's rated for 250 pounds — that's two or three elementary-age kids, or one adult who wants to test their stomach.

As shown in the product image, the mounting bracket is designed to wrap around a horizontal beam up to 4 inches thick. The hardware is galvanized steel, not the painted zinc you see on cheaper units that rust within a season. The webbing is UV-stabilized, which matters more than most people think — sun-rotted nylon is how kids get hurt.

**Real-world performance**

The physics here are the selling point. Because the straps are evenly spaced, the saucer stays level when kids climb on from different sides. No tipping, no sudden dumping. One buyer noted, *"My 6 and 8 year old both get on and it doesn't tilt at all. They spin each other until they're dizzy and then just sit there laughing."* That's the experience you're paying for.

Installation is straightforward if you've got a basic socket set. You wrap the straps over the beam, thread the bolts through, and tighten. Most reviewers report doing it solo in about 15 minutes. The swivel mechanism is sealed, so you won't hear the "grind-grind" squeak that plagues cheaper saucers after a few weeks.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The durability is the recurring theme. Multiple reviewers mention leaving it up through winter and finding it intact in spring. One parent wrote: *"We've had this for a full year. Rain, snow, 100-degree heat. Still spins smooth, still no rust."* That's the kind of longevity that justifies the price over the $25 store-brand alternatives.

The other praise point is the weight capacity. Parents with older kids (9-12) report that it doesn't sag under the weight of two pre-teens, which is rare for this category.

**Where it falls short**

The mounting hardware is the weak point. The straps come with bolts, but no washers or lock nuts — you'll want to add those from your local hardware store, or the bolts can loosen over time. That's a $3 fix, but it's annoying.

More importantly, this needs a solid anchor. If your swing set is one of those lightweight tubular frames from a big-box store, the beam will flex under the saucer's weight when kids spin aggressively. Several reviewers mentioned reinforcing their frame with additional brackets before mounting. This isn't a flaw in the product — it's a reality of physics — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

**Who should buy this**

- **Families with kids aged 4-10** who have an existing wooden playset or sturdy A-frame — this is the upgrade that makes the set fun again.

**Skip it if** your swing set is a lightweight portable frame or if you're not comfortable adding your own hardware and potential frame reinforcement.

**FAQ**

**Will this fit my swing set?**
Measure your horizontal beam — it needs to be between 3 and 4 inches thick. Wooden playset beams are usually fine. Tubular metal frames often aren't, because the bracket is designed for flat surfaces.

**Can adults use it?**
Yes, up to 250 pounds. But be honest with yourself about your inner ear — spinning at speed as an adult is a different experience than it was at age 8.

**Does it come with a safety harness or enclosure?**
No, and it shouldn't — it's an open saucer. Kids hold on or sit down. If you have toddlers under 3, this isn't the right accessory yet.
