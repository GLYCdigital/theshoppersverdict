## What's Actually in the Box

You get the main workbench unit (which requires assembly), a pegboard back panel, storage shelf, and a tray of tools that includes a hammer, screwdriver, wrench, saw, drill, and a handful of wooden screws, bolts, and connector pieces. The set is designed for kids roughly ages 3 to 7, with the bench standing at about 30 inches tall — a solid height for preschoolers and early elementary kids to use comfortably standing up.

The wood is noticeably thicker than what you'd find in bargain-bin sets. It's not heirloom furniture, but it's sturdy enough that a determined 5-year-old won't snap a leg off during a "construction emergency."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be real about the downsides, because there are a few.

**The small parts problem.** The screws, bolts, and washers are exactly the size that gets lost under couches or swallowed by pets. Order a small parts organizer or zipper bag when you order this. You'll thank me in three weeks.

**Assembly is fiddly.** The workbench takes 20–30 minutes to put together, and the instructions are minimal — mostly diagrams with limited text. If you're not handy, budget extra time or have a patient partner help. It's not hard, but it's not intuitive either.

**Some pieces are tight.** A few reviewers noted that the wooden screws can be tough for younger kids (3-year-olds) to thread all the way in. It's a fine-motor challenge that requires supervision and patience at first. Older kids (5+) handle it fine.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a kid aged 3–7 who loves building, fixing, or imitating grown-ups doing chores. It's also great for parents who want toys that last through multiple kids or get handed down.

**Skip it if:** Your kid is under 3 (choking hazard on small parts) or over 8 (they'll outgrow it fast). Also skip if you're looking for a zero-assembly, open-and-play gift — this needs setup time before it's ready.
