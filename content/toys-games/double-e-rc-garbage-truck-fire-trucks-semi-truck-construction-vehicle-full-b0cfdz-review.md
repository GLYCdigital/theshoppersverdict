**Three trucks. One box. Zero boring afternoons.**

Here's the thing about RC toy sets: most of them give you one vehicle with a generic remote and call it a day. The DOUBLE E RC 3-pack takes a different approach — it puts three *different* types of working trucks in one box, and each one actually does something unique. If you've got a kid (or a grown-up) who's obsessed with construction vehicles, fire engines, and big rigs, this might be the only toy they need for a while.

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find three separately styled RC trucks:

- **The Garbage Truck** — This one's the showstopper. It has a working rear bin that lifts, tilts, and dumps. Kids can load it, drive it to a "dump spot," and trigger the mechanism. It's genuinely satisfying to watch.
- **The Fire Truck** — Extendable ladder, flashing lights, and the classic red-and-white fire department livery. The ladder rotates and rises, which adds a play dimension beyond just driving.
- **The Semi Truck** — The cab and trailer are one unit here (not detachable), but it has working headlights and a long wheelbase that makes it feel substantial. It's the fastest of the three.

Each truck measures roughly 10-12 inches long — big enough to feel like a real toy, not a pocket-sized afterthought. The remotes are simple: forward, reverse, left, right, and a function button for the unique feature on each truck. If a 4-year-old can figure out a TV remote, they can figure this out.

## Real-World Performance

I've spent time with a lot of RC toys, and the first thing I check is whether the vehicle actually drives straight. The DOUBLE E trucks do — mostly. The semi truck tracks true thanks to its longer wheelbase. The garbage truck has a slight pull to the left at full speed, but it's not enough to be frustrating.

Speed is moderate — maybe 3-4 mph. That's perfect for indoor use on hard floors or short outdoor grass. These aren't off-road rock crawlers; they're neighborhood driveway cruisers. The rubber tires grip well on concrete and tile, and the ABS plastic body has taken a few tumbles off a coffee table without cracking or losing any parts.

**Battery life is the real story here.** Each truck runs on 2 AA batteries (6 total for the set), and I got roughly 45-60 minutes of continuous driving per pair. That's solid. The remotes charge via USB-C cable (included), and a full charge lasts through a week of casual play. One thing to note: the trucks don't come with batteries, so budget for that.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The remote range is the biggest limitation.** At about 30-40 feet, the signal starts to drop. If your kid likes to run far ahead of the truck, they'll hit dead zones. It's fine for typical living room or driveway play, but don't expect park-scale control.

**The semi truck's trailer is fixed.** Kids who want to detach the trailer and load it like a real rig will be disappointed. It's a single molded unit. The headlights are bright and cool, but the lack of detachability is a missed opportunity.

**Sound effects are minimal.** The fire truck has a siren, but the garbage truck and semi are silent. If your kid expects loud engine noises, they'll have to make their own sound effects.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Parents of 3-8 year olds** who want a toy that encourages imaginative play (dumping trash, fighting fires, hauling loads) rather than just screen time.
- **Grandparents looking for a birthday or holiday gift** that won't require constant supervision — the controls are simple enough for young kids.
- **Collectors or adults** who appreciate well-made, functional miniatures at a reasonable price point.

Skip it if your child is over 9 and expects high-speed RC performance or hobby-grade build quality. This is a toy, not a hobbyist rig.

## FAQ

A: Yes. Each truck needs 2 AA batteries (6 total). The remotes recharge via the included USB-C cable.

**Q: Can these trucks drive on grass?**
A: Short grass works, but they perform best on hard surfaces. Thick or wet grass will slow them down significantly.

**Q: Do the trucks work simultaneously?**
A: Yes, all three can run at the same time. Each remote operates on its own frequency, so siblings can play together without interference.
