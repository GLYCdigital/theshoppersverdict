Let's be direct: a 3.1-star average across 155 ratings tells you something before you even open the box. The Q Gen 2 Adjustable Golf Club has a genuinely clever concept — a graphite shaft that splits into two pieces for travel — but the execution has left a trail of frustrated buyers. Here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

The package includes the club head, the two-piece graphite shaft, and the locking mechanism that joins them. As shown in the product image, the two-part design is the headline feature: twist the collar, pull the shaft apart, and the club collapses to roughly half its assembled length. That's genuinely useful for anyone stuffing a bag into a car trunk or a carry-on. The adjustable loft sleeve at the hosel lets you change face angle, and there's a length adjustment mechanism as well.

**Performance and build quality**

When it's assembled correctly and the collar is torqued down properly, the club performs respectably for what it is. The graphite shaft flexes predictably, and the adjustable loft settings do produce noticeable changes in ball flight — that part works as advertised. The weight distribution feels balanced, and the head has a decent sweet spot for a non-branded club.

But here's where the Q Gen 2 stumbles. The locking collar is the weak link. Multiple buyers report that the joint loosens during the swing, especially after a few dozen drives. One reviewer wrote, "The shaft separated on my third swing at the range. The collar just gave way — no warning." That's not an isolated incident; it's a recurring theme across the negative reviews. You can mitigate this by checking and re-tightening the collar before every shot, but that's not something you should have to do with a club at this price point.

The grip is the second complaint that keeps surfacing. Multiple buyers describe it as "thin" and "slippery," with one noting it "felt like a toy grip, not something you'd find on a real club." In humid conditions or with sweaty hands, that's a genuine issue.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The travel convenience is the clear winner. A buyer summarized it well: "I bought this specifically for a golf trip where I was flying with just a carry-on. The two-piece shaft made it fit perfectly. Honestly, that's the only reason I'd buy this again." Another echoed that sentiment: "The disassembly and reassembly is quick and tool-free — takes about 15 seconds once you get the hang of it."

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the collar and grip issues, there's the fundamental question of what this product actually is. It's marketed as a golf club, but at 3.1 stars with this build quality, it functions more like a premium toy or a practice aid. If you're a serious golfer expecting to replace your gamer driver or irons with this, you'll be disappointed. The head isn't from a recognized OEM, and the overall feel lacks the feedback and responsiveness you'd get from a proper club from a major brand.

**Who should buy this**

- **Travel golfers on a budget**: If you need a club that fits in a suitcase and you're okay with checking the collar before every shot, this works.
- **Casual players or beginners**: If you're just hitting balls at the range or playing a few rounds a year, this is serviceable — as long as you understand the limitations.
- **Golfers with adjustable-height needs**: The length adjustment genuinely helps if you're between standard sizes or sharing a club.

**Skip it if** you're a serious player who wants consistent performance without fiddling with a locking mechanism, or if you expect this to hold up to heavy practice sessions.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the two-piece shaft stay secure during a full swing?**
A: It can, but you must tighten the collar firmly before each use. Multiple buyers report it loosening mid-round, so check it regularly.

**Q: Is this suitable for a child or a beginner?**
A: The adjustable length makes it adaptable, but the grip and overall feel lean toward adult usage. For kids, look for a dedicated junior set instead.

**Q: Can I use this on a real golf course?**
A: You can, but it's more of a practice aid or travel club. The performance won't match a standard adult club from a reputable brand.

**The Verdict: 3.3/5**

The Q Gen 2 Adjustable Golf Club earns a middle-of-the-road rating because it nails the travel convenience but fumbles on durability and feel. **Buy it if** you prioritize portability and don't mind babying the locking collar. **Skip it if** you want a reliable club that performs without constant attention. For most golfers, this is a niche product with a specific use case — and you should know exactly what you're getting into before you buy.
