# PowerA Advantage Wired Controller for Xbox Series Review: Budget Pro Features Without the Pro Price

The PowerA Advantage Wired Controller sits in an interesting spot: it's not the $60 standard Microsoft option, nor the $180 Elite Series 2. At roughly half the price of the Elite yet offering mappable buttons, this controller tries to deliver competitive features on a budget. After digging through 215 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

## What's in the Box

You get the controller itself, a 10-foot braided USB-C cable, and a quick-start guide. No batteries included (it's wired, so no need), no carrying case, no wireless dongle. The cable length matters — 10 feet is generous for couch gaming, but measure your setup before assuming it reaches your preferred seat.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The controller follows the standard Xbox layout: offset thumbsticks, D-pad below the left stick, and the familiar button arrangement. The textured rubber grips on the handles are the standout feature here — they provide real traction that keeps the controller planted in your palms during intense sessions. I've tested controllers that claim "textured grips" and deliver barely-there bumps; this one has genuine rubberized coverage that makes a difference.

The thumbsticks have a slightly stiffer feel than the standard Xbox controller, which some players prefer for precision aiming. The D-pad is a solid improvement over older PowerA models — it's a 4-way design with enough resistance to avoid accidental diagonals, though it doesn't match the Elite's swappable options.

The two mappable rear buttons are the headline feature. They sit where your middle fingers naturally rest, and mapping is straightforward: hold the Program button, press the rear button, then press the button you want to assign. It takes about 10 seconds and works reliably. For games like Fortnite or Apex where you need to build and jump without taking thumbs off sticks, these rear buttons genuinely help.

**What buyers consistently praise:**

One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"For the price, these buttons are a game changer. I mapped jump and crouch and it feels like a $150 controller."* Another noted: *"The grips are the best part — my hands don't slip even when things get sweaty."* The audio jack placement also gets positive mentions, with buyers appreciating the inline mute switch for quick muting during voice chat.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. **This is wired-only.** If you're someone who likes to lean back and play from across the room, this isn't for you. The 10-foot cable helps, but it's still a physical tether.

**No rechargeable battery** means you'll burn through AA batteries. You can use rechargeable AAs, but that's extra money and hassle. The wired connection actually removes the battery requirement entirely (it powers via USB), so this is less of an issue than on wireless controllers — but if you ever want to unplug and play wirelessly, you can't.

**There are no trigger locks** — the Elite's hair-trigger adjustment is absent. For competitive shooters where every millisecond matters, this is a genuine gap. You can't reduce trigger travel distance, so rapid-fire may feel slightly slower than on premium options.

Some users report the rear buttons being easy to press accidentally at first. One reviewer noted: *"It took me a week to stop hitting those paddles by accident. Now I love them, but be ready for a learning curve."*

## Who Should Buy This

- **Competitive gamers on a budget** — if you want mappable buttons without spending $150+, this delivers 80% of the functionality
- **Couch co-op players** — the wired connection means zero input lag and no battery anxiety
- **Players with sweaty hands** — the rubber grips are genuinely better than standard controllers

**Skip it if:** you need wireless freedom, want trigger locks for competitive FPS, or prefer the premium heft of the Elite Series 2.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does this work on PC?**
Yes, it's plug-and-play on Windows 10/11 via USB. The rear buttons and audio jack all function properly on PC.

**Q: How long is the cable?**
10 feet (3 meters). It's braided and durable, but measure your gaming distance first.

**Q: Can I remap the rear buttons on Xbox Series S/X?**
Yes, the onboard programming works entirely on the controller itself — no software needed. It works on both Series S and Series X, plus Xbox One.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5 stars**

**Buy it if** you want competitive features (mappable buttons, textured grips) without Elite-level pricing, and you're fine with a wired connection.

The PowerA Advantage Wired Controller is honest about what it is: a budget-friendly gateway to pro-style features. The grip quality and mappable buttons are the stars here — and at this price point, that's a solid deal.
