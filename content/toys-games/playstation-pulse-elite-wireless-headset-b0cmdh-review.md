## Sony's Mid-Tier Bet: Planar Audio Without the Price Tag

The Pulse Elite is Sony's answer to the question nobody asked in 2023: *can you get planar magnetic audio for under $150?* The answer, surprisingly, is yes — with tradeoffs. This headset swaps the dynamic drivers of the Pulse 3D for planar magnetics, which usually live in audiophile gear costing twice as much. But at 4.0 stars across 200 ratings, the crowd is split. Some call it a revelation; others call it a vice grip on their skull. Both are right.

## What's in the Box

- Pulse Elite headset (fold-flat design)
- PlayStation Link USB dongle
- 3.5mm audio cable for wired use
- USB-C charging cable (no wall adapter)
- Quick-start guide

No case. No Bluetooth dongle for older consoles. The included USB-A to USB-C cable is short — about 3 feet — so plan your charging spot accordingly.

## Performance: Where It Wins and Loses

**Sound quality is the headline.** The planar drivers produce a wider soundstage than the Pulse 3D, with crisp highs and bass that's tight rather than boomy. In *Returnal*, enemy fire has spatial clarity that makes you turn your head. In *God of War Ragnarök*, the orchestral score breathes — strings don't smear into percussion. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"The audio clarity is unreal for the price. I hear footsteps in Warzone that I never caught with my old headset."* That's the consensus: if you play competitive shooters, this is a genuine upgrade.

**Battery life delivers.** Sony claims 30 hours, and real-world testing lands around 26–28 hours with the LED on. That's a full week of daily play for most people. The Pulse 3D's 12-hour battery feels ancient by comparison.

**PlayStation Link is the sleeper feature.** The included dongle gives you lossless, near-zero-latency audio on PS5 and PC, while Bluetooth simultaneously handles your phone. You can take a call mid-raid without removing the headset. It works seamlessly — a feature Sony buried in the marketing.

## Where It Falls Short

**Comfort is the elephant in the room.** Multiple buyers describe the same experience: *"Sound is great but the headband crushes my head after an hour."* The clamp force is aggressive — Sony designed this for staying put during motion, but it punishes extended sessions. If you have a large head, this will be a problem. If you're average, you'll likely need to break it in over a week.

**Microphone quality is mediocre.** The retractable boom mic is fine for party chat, but compared to the Pulse 3D's mic, it's a step back. Voice sounds slightly hollow, and there's no noise-cancellation on the mic itself. Your teammates will hear your keyboard clicks.

**Build feels budget-conscious.** The plastic headband flexes a bit more than you'd like at this price. No aluminum reinforcement. The ear cups are comfortable (fabric-wrapped memory foam), but the whole thing squeaks slightly when you turn your head quickly.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Competitive PS5 gamers** who want positional audio without dropping $300 on the Audeze Maxwell
- **PC + PS5 dual users** who want one headset that works on both without dongle-swapping
- **Long-haul players** who are tired of charging every other day

**Skip it if** you play casually in short bursts, have a larger-than-average head, or prioritize chat quality over game audio.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with PS4 or Xbox?**
A: PS4 works via the 3.5mm cable only (no wireless). Xbox doesn't support it at all — no Bluetooth, no dongle compatibility. This is a PS5/PC headset.

**Q: Is the sound worth upgrading from the Pulse 3D?**
A: For games, yes — the planar drivers are a genuine step up in clarity. For music, it's closer. If you already own the Pulse 3D and play casually, keep your money. If you're competitive, upgrade.

**Q: How's the mic for streaming?**
A: Serviceable but not great. It's fine for Discord chat, but you'll want a standalone mic for streaming or recording. The audio quality of your stream will outpace the mic noticeably.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want near-audiophile game audio under $150 and can tolerate (or fix) the clamp pressure. **Skip it if** comfort is your top priority or you need a headset that works across every console.

The Pulse Elite is a value proposition with a catch: incredible audio, questionable ergonomics. For the price, it's the best-sounding wireless headset for PS5 right now — just budget a week to break in the headband, or plan to swap the ear pads.
