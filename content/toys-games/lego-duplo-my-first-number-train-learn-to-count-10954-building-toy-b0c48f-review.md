**The Lego DUPLO Number Train is the rare toddler toy that grows with your kid — but it's not perfect for every family.**

Here's the thing about toddler toys: most are either pure entertainment (light, sound, chaos) or purely educational (flashcards, puzzles, bore-fest). The DUPLO Number Train 10954 splits the difference. It's a buildable train with numbered bricks 0 through 9, each emblazoned with a matching number of dots on the reverse side. That means you can use it as a stacking toy, a counting toy, a train toy, or all three simultaneously.

**What's in the box**

You get 23 pieces total. That breaks down to: three train carts (an engine with a chimney, a middle car, and a caboose), 10 numbered bricks (0-9), a DUPLO child figure, and a cat figure. The set also includes a few extra decorative bricks for the engine's smokestack and the carts' bases.

The numbered bricks are chunky — roughly 2x2 DUPLO size — and fit snugly into the open tops of the carts. Each number brick has the numeral on one side and the corresponding number of printed dots on the other. This dual-sided design is the smartest part of the set. Your child can stack the bricks in numerical order on the train, or flip them over and match dots to numbers.

**Performance and real-world use**

My first observation: the train carts connect with a simple peg-and-hole system that a 2-year-old can manage. My second observation: the wheels roll beautifully on hardwood and tile, but they catch on carpet. If your play space is rug-dominated, expect your toddler to pick the train up and carry it instead of pushing it. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

The bricks are true DUPLO quality — thick, durable, and easy for small hands to grip. The printing on the numbers is sharp and won't rub off with normal play. The included child figure has a friendly face and the cat is a nice addition for storytelling.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Parents in the Amazon reviews repeatedly highlight the same benefits. One reviewer wrote, "My 2-year-old loves this train. He's already recognizing numbers 1-5 and loves stacking them on the train." Another noted, "Great quality as expected from Lego. The numbers are easy to read and the train is the perfect size for little hands."

The versatility is the most common praise point. It's rare to find a toy that works as both a construction toy and a learning tool without feeling like either one is an afterthought.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest issue is the choking hazard concern. DUPLO is generally safe for 18 months and up, but the numbered bricks and figure are small enough that if you have a child under 2 who still explores with their mouth, you'll need to supervise closely. The box says 1.5+ years, but I'd recommend it for children closer to 2.

Also, the train only holds a few bricks at a time. If you want to build a full 10-digit train, you'll have to stack bricks on top of each other vertically — the carts are too shallow to hold all ten side-by-side. That's a minor design limitation, not a flaw.

**Who should buy this**

- **Parents of 2-3 year olds** who want a first "building" toy that also teaches numbers. This is the ideal entry point into DUPLO.
- **Grandparents looking for a gift** that's educational without being boring. The train theme has universal kid appeal.
- **Parents who already own DUPLO sets** — the numbered bricks integrate with any existing DUPLO collection for even more play options.

Skip it if your child is under 18 months, or if you're looking for a toy that works on carpeted floors.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this compatible with regular Lego bricks?**
A: Yes. DUPLO bricks are twice the size of standard Lego, but they connect to standard Lego bricks on the bottom. Your older kids can integrate these numbered pieces into their builds.

**Q: Can the train actually pull the carts?**
A: Yes, the carts connect with a simple peg system. A toddler can push the whole train along a hard floor, though it may detach if pushed too hard into a wall.

**Q: How many numbers are included?**
A: Ten — the digits 0 through 9. Each has the numeral on one side and dots on the other, so you can teach counting and number recognition simultaneously.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, educational first building toy that will hold up through years of play and actually teaches number recognition.

**Skip it if** your child is under 2, or if your primary play surface is carpet — you'll both be frustrated by the wheel performance.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not the flashiest toy on the shelf, but it's one of the smartest. The dual-sided number bricks are genuinely clever, the quality is impeccable, and it earns its place in any toddler's toy rotation.
