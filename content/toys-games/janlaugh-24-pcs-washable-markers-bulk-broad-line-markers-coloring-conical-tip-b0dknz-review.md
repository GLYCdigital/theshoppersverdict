If you’re a parent or teacher staring down a “washable markers” search with 47 tabs open, here’s the short version: the Janlaugh 24-piece set delivers exactly what the label promises, at a price that undercuts the big names. But “washable” doesn’t mean “magic,” and there are a couple of quirks worth knowing before you add to cart.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains 24 broad-line markers with conical tips, packed in a clear plastic carry case with a snap-lock latch. Each marker is a standard-length barrel (about 5.5 inches), chunky enough for preschool grips but not so fat they feel like toddler crayons. The color range covers the essentials — reds, blues, greens, yellows — plus useful secondaries like teal, magenta, and a decent light brown that’s surprisingly hard to find in budget sets. The tips are the standout feature: the conical shape lets you lay down a thick stripe for filling large areas, then flip to the point for writing names or adding details. That dual-purpose design makes this set more versatile than flat chisel-tip markers.

**Real-World Performance**

On standard printer paper, the ink flows smoothly without dragging or skipping. You’ll get moderate bleed-through on thin paper, but that’s typical for broad-line markers — put a scrap sheet underneath. On cardstock and construction paper, the colors stay vibrant and don’t feather. The washability claim holds up in practice. One Amazon reviewer noted, “My 3-year-old colored her entire forearm and it came off with a baby wipe before bath time.” Another parent mentioned a green streak on a white cotton shirt that disappeared after a cold-water rinse and a dab of dish soap. That tracks with my testing — dried ink lifts off skin with soap and water, and most fabrics respond to standard laundry detergent. Just don’t expect miracles on upholstery or carpet; blot, don’t rub, and treat stains quickly.

**The Consistent Praise**

Across the 225 ratings, buyers repeatedly highlight three things. First, the value — “24 markers for less than a 10-pack of the name brand” is a recurring theme. Second, the tip versatility, which parents say makes this one set work for both toddlers scribbling and older kids doing school projects. Third, the storage case. Multiple reviewers mention that it keeps markers organized and actually survives being dropped, which is high praise for a plastic case in a kids’ product.

**Where It Falls Short**

The caps are the biggest annoyance. They need a firm, deliberate push to click shut — a preschooler will often press, think it’s closed, and leave it barely seated. That leads to dried-out tips, and I saw a few complaints about markers arriving with tips that were already dry (likely from loose caps in transit). Second, the color saturation is good but not great. On cheap copy paper, the reds and blues look slightly washed out compared to Crayola. It’s fine for everyday coloring, but if your kid is doing something they want to frame, spend the extra couple dollars. Finally, no color names on the barrels. Minor, but teachers organizing by color will find it mildly irritating.

**Who Should Buy This**

Get this set if you need a bulk supply for a classroom, daycare, or birthday party goodie bag, or if your kid goes through markers like they’re free refills. It’s also a smart choice for parents who want a starter set for a toddler prone to testing the limits of “washable” — the cleanup forgiveness outweighs the minor color intensity tradeoff. Skip it if you’re a serious artist needing archival-quality inks, or if your child is old enough to demand specific color names and you don’t want to play guessing games.

**FAQ**

*Are these markers safe for 2-year-olds?*
Yes, they’re non-toxic and AP-certified. But the caps are a choking hazard like any marker, so supervise toddlers. The conical tips are durable and won’t mush under aggressive pressure.

*Do they dry out quickly?*
Only if caps are left loose. With proper resealing, most users report weeks of daily use without issues. Store them horizontally in the case to extend life.

*Can I use them on whiteboards?*
No, these are not dry-erase markers. They’ll stain whiteboard surfaces. Stick to paper and cardstock.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a budget-friendly, genuinely washable marker set that handles both broad coloring and fine details, and you don’t mind teaching kids to snap caps firmly. **Skip it if** you need maximum color punch for display-worthy art or want color-labeled barrels for classroom organization.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — an honest, capable set that overdelivers for the price, with minor cap-related frustrations keeping it from perfection. For the cost of a fast-food lunch, you’re getting a month of quiet coloring time. That’s a deal worth taking.
