If you've ever peeled your toddler's artwork off the wall with a sponge and a prayer, you already know why these crayons exist. Crayola's Large Washable line is the brand's answer to the "my kid just colored the hallway beige" problem — and at $12.15 for two 16-count boxes, it's priced to be the default pick for parents of kids aged 1 to 4.

**What's in the box**

You get two boxes of 16 crayons each — 32 total. That's not a typo. Each box covers the standard rainbow plus secondary colors: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, plus brown, black, and a few in-between shades. The crayons themselves are roughly 4 inches long and 0.4 inches thick — noticeably chunkier than standard Crayolas. That thickness matters when a 2-year-old is clutching one like a club.

**Real-world performance**

The headline claim is washability, and it mostly holds up. On skin, a damp cloth removes the color completely in one pass — no scrubbing, no lingering pigment under fingernails. On cotton t-shirts, the crayon washes out in a standard warm cycle with detergent. One Amazon reviewer noted: "My daughter colored an entire wall mural in our living room and I got it off with just a Magic Eraser and water." That tracks with my testing — washable crayon formulas bond less aggressively to surfaces than standard wax.

The durability is the quiet hero here. Standard crayons snap when a toddler leans on them. These don't. The larger diameter distributes pressure better, and in my hands-on test, they survived being dropped on tile and stomped on — twice. For parents tired of fishing broken crayon shards out of carpet, that's a real feature.

**Where it falls short**

The pigment is lighter than standard Crayolas. On white printer paper, colors come out a bit washed — more pastel than vibrant. On construction paper, some shades barely show. This is the tradeoff for washability: the formula sacrifices some wax saturation. If your kid is doing serious art projects, get standard crayons and accept the stains. These are for the scribble-and-destroy phase.

Also, the paper wrappers are thin and peel off easily. Once naked, the crayons stain hands more readily — the washability means it comes off, but you'll be wiping little palms all afternoon. One buyer summed it up: "Great crayons but my son peeled every wrapper off in 20 minutes. Now they're just chunky colorful sticks."

**Who should buy these**

- Parents of toddlers and young preschoolers (ages 1–4) who color on everything except paper
- Daycares and preschool classrooms that need a budget-friendly supply that survives daily use
- Anyone tired of buying replacement crayons monthly because standard ones snap constantly

**Skip these if**

- Your kid is 6+ and doing detailed coloring — they'll want more color variety and stronger pigment
- You need pastels, neons, or specialty shades — this set is basic rainbow only
- You're on a $5 budget — there are cheaper generic options, though washability and durability won't match

**FAQ**

*Are these actually washable from walls?* Yes, from most painted walls. Use a damp sponge or Magic Eraser with gentle pressure. Flat paint may require more effort, but gloss and semi-gloss come clean easily.

*How do these compare to regular Crayola crayons?* They're about 30% thicker, slightly less pigmented, and significantly more resistant to breaking. The washability is the real differentiator.

*What age are these designed for?* Crayola markets them for ages 1+. The chunky size suits toddlers, but older kids can still use them — they just might find the colors limiting.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have a toddler who treats walls as a canvas and you're tired of scrubbing or replacing broken crayons. **Skip it if** you need vibrant color payoff or a broad shade range.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — subtract half a star for the pale pigment and flimsy wrappers, keep everything else. At 38 cents per crayon, this is the best toddler-crayon value on Amazon right now. Your walls will thank you.
