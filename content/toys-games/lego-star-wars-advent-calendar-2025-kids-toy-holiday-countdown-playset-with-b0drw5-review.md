**The LEGO Star Wars Advent Calendar 2025 is the rare holiday countdown that actually delivers 24 days of genuine building fun — not just tiny filler bricks. Here's who should buy it, who should skip it, and what 229 Amazon reviewers are saying.**

Let's be honest: most advent calendars are overpriced cardboard with disappointing trinkets behind each door. The LEGO Star Wars version has always been the exception, and the 2025 edition continues that tradition with a festive twist on the galaxy far, far away.

**What you actually get**

Behind those 24 numbered doors, you'll find six minifigures — the standout being a holiday sweater-wearing Mandalorian and a festive Grogu (yes, Grogu gets a tiny Santa hat). The other four include a snowtrooper, a Rebel pilot in winter gear, and two more characters I won't spoil. Beyond the figs, LEGO packs in brick-built versions of the snowspeeder, a TIE fighter, and a handful of seasonal builds like a decorated Wookiee tree and a holiday Gonk droid.

The build quality is classic LEGO — no surprises there. Pieces click together with the satisfying precision you expect. The mini builds are genuinely small but not flimsy; they're sturdy enough for a 6-year-old to play with roughly and survive the holiday season.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews isn't inflated. The most common praise centers on the minifigure lineup. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"The Mandalorian with the ugly Christmas sweater is worth the price alone. My son screamed when he opened it."* Another mentioned, *"This is the first advent calendar where my kids actually fight over who opens the door each day — the builds are that good."*

Parents also appreciate that the builds aren't just dust collectors. Everything combines into a cohesive holiday scene, so you're left with a display piece rather than a pile of loose bricks come January.

**Where it falls short**

The price has crept up to $43.99. That's roughly $1.83 per day, which is fair for the quality — but if you're on a tight budget, know that past years' calendars have been cheaper. The other notable gripe: the 2025 edition doesn't use numbered bags inside the doors. Pieces for different builds are mixed together, so if your kid (or you) wants to build everything on Christmas morning, you'll need to open all 24 doors and sort the pieces yourself. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

**Who should buy this**

- **Star Wars families** with kids aged 6–12 who already love LEGO — this is a no-brainer gift
- **Adult collectors** who want the exclusive minifigures without paying resale prices later
- **Holiday tradition families** looking for a screen-free daily activity that builds toward something bigger

**Skip it if** you're buying for a child under 5 (small pieces are a choking hazard) or if your kid isn't already into Star Wars — the appeal is heavily tied to the IP.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does each build take?**
A: Most doors take 5–10 minutes for a kid to assemble. The larger vehicles (snowspeeder, TIE fighter) might stretch to 15 minutes. Perfect for a pre-breakfast activity.

**Q: Are the minifigures exclusive?**

**Q: Can you rebuild the sets into other things?**
A: No. This isn't a 3-in-1 set. The builds are what they are, though you can combine them into the larger holiday scene.
