# UNO Finally Fixed Its Biggest Problem — And It's Brilliant

You know UNO. Everyone knows UNO. It's the game that's ended friendships over a well-timed Draw Four and started family feuds that last through Thanksgiving. But here's what you might not know: the classic deck has a hidden flaw that millions of players have struggled with silently — the color coding. Red, green, blue, yellow all look nearly identical to roughly 300 million color blind people worldwide.

This Amazon Exclusive version fixes that. And then some.

## What's Actually in the Box

Open it up and you get the standard 108-card UNO deck — but every single card has a **distinct symbol printed in the corner** alongside the color. Red cards have a circle, green cards have a triangle, blue cards have a square, and yellow cards have a star. It's simple, it's intuitive, and it completely eliminates the "wait, is that green or blue?" moment that slows down every game.

You also get **50 blank customizable cards** — that's not a typo, fifty. These are double-sided blanks you can write on with any permanent marker to create house rules, extra wild cards, or personalized challenges. It's the most flexible UNO deck on the market.

## Build Quality and Real-World Play

The cards are printed on standard UNO cardstock — which is to say, they're functional but not luxurious. They shuffle fine, deal fine, and hold up to casual play. After a few dozen games you'll notice edge wear and slight bending at the corners. If your family plays aggressively (we all know that one person who slams cards down), consider sleeving them.

The box is the classic UNO slide-out style that's been around for decades. It's serviceable but not durable — the cardboard flap will eventually tear. That's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, especially at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The **blank cards are thinner** than the numbered cards — you can feel the difference when shuffling, and it gives observant players a slight clue about what's coming. It's not game-breaking, but it's noticeable.

The **color symbols take getting used to** if you've played standard UNO for years. Your brain initially reads the shape as a new rule rather than a color indicator. Give it one game and you'll adapt.

And if you already own a standard UNO deck, this is a genuine upgrade — but it's not a necessity. You're paying a small premium for the accessibility feature and blanks.

**Q: Can I really write on the blank cards?**
A: Yes, with any permanent marker. Dry-erase markers smear and wipe off. Use a fine-tip Sharpie for best results. The cards have a matte finish that takes ink well.
