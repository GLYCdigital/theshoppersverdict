## The Batman figure that made collectors argue — and why it's still worth your money

When Medicom's Mafex line announced a Batman based on Jim Lee's *Hush* storyline, the collecting community split into two camps. One side called it the definitive Dark Knight. The other side pointed at QC photos and said "hard pass." After 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, the market has spoken. Here's the real story.

## What's in the box

You get the figure itself, four head sculpts (neutral, angry, a masked Bruce Wayne variant, and an unmasked Bruce Wayne), six pairs of interchangeable hands, a batarang, a grappling hook, and a wired cloth cape already attached. No display stand. No flight pole. No diorama piece. For the premium price, that bare-bones accessory count stings a bit.

The figure stands roughly 6 inches tall to the top of the cowl — though the sculpt runs slightly small, closer to 5.8 inches when measured against other imports. That matters if you plan to display him next to your Figuarts or Legends figures. He'll look slightly undersized.

## Build quality and articulation

The sculpt is the headline. Medicom nailed Jim Lee's design language: the angular jawline, the pronounced brow over the white lenses, the segmented abdominal plating. The gray and black paint apps are clean, with the blue-black cape providing strong contrast. As shown in the product image, the cape is the showpiece — wired, fabric, and capable of dramatic bat-wing spreads.

Articulation is genuinely excellent. Double-jointed knees and elbows hit full bends. The butterfly joint at the shoulders lets you pull off that iconic "landing on a gargoyle" pose. The torso crunch is deep enough for crouching or flying poses. The wired cape adds a layer of posing flexibility you rarely get at this scale.

## What buyers consistently praise

The sculpt-to-source-material accuracy is the most cited strength. One reviewer put it plainly: "This is the closest thing to a comic panel come to life. The head sculpts alone justify the price." Another noted, "The articulation is better than the Figuarts version — you can actually get him into crouching poses without fighting the cape."

The cape itself gets repeated shout-outs. Buyers describe it as "game-changing" for display photography, with the wire holding whatever shape you bend it into.

## Where it falls short

Here's the honest part. Quality control issues appear in roughly one in six reviews. Loose hip joints are the most common complaint — one buyer reported, "Left hip was so loose he couldn't hold a standing pose. Had to apply floor polish to tighten it." Paint scuffs on the cowl edge and a slightly crooked bat-symbol paint application also show up in photos.

The smaller-than-standard scale is the second issue. If you're pairing him with Mafex's own Hush Superman or Catwoman, it's fine. Next to a Marvel Legends figure, he'll look like a younger, slimmer Batman. That's actually comic-accurate for Lee's artwork, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a comic-accurate collector who values sculpt fidelity over everything. You display figures in dramatic poses and want a Batman that can actually hold them. You're building a Mafex Hush lineup.

**Skip it if:** You're a play-oriented collector — this is a display piece, not a toy. You need strict scale parity with your existing 6-inch collection. You're on a tight budget, because the price-to-accessory ratio is thin.

## FAQ

**Is this Batman durable enough for kids?**
No. This is a collector's item with delicate joints and small accessories. It's designed for display and careful posing, not rough play. Keep it on a shelf.

**Does the wired cape limit articulation?**
Somewhat. The cape attaches at the shoulders and can impede extreme arm raises. You can unclip it entirely for full shoulder freedom, but it's a bit fiddly to reattach.

**How does it compare to the SH Figuarts Batman Hush?**
The Mafex has better proportions and a superior cape system. The Figuarts has slightly better paint application consistency and a more solid hip joint out of the box. Both are excellent; the Mafex wins on screen-accurate presence.

## The Verdict

This is a 4.6-star figure, and that rating is honest. When you get a clean copy, it's arguably the best comic-accurate Batman at this scale — the sculpt, articulation, and cape design combine into something genuinely special. The QC lottery is real, but Amazon's return policy covers you, and most buyers report receiving acceptable copies.

**Buy it if** you want the definitive Jim Lee Batman and can tolerate a small risk of QC issues. **Skip it if** scale consistency or out-of-box reliability is your top priority. At the current price point, you're paying for sculpt excellence — and that's exactly what you get.
