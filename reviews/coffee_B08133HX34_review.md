# Breville Barista Pro Espresso Machine BES878BSS Review: Is This $850 All-in-One Worth It?

**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐½ (4.4/5)
**Price:** $849.95
**Category:** Espresso Machines
**Best for:** Espresso lovers who want a serious upgrade without jumping to prosumer pricing

---

## The Short Version

The Breville Barista Pro BES878BSS is the Goldilocks of home espresso. It's faster to heat up than the Barista Express, has a better grinder, and produces shots that rival what you'd get at a decent cafe. At ~$850 it's not cheap, but it's genuinely one of the best values in the mid-range espresso space. The built-in grinder, thermocoil heating, and automatic steam wand make it a serious option for anyone who wants cafe-quality espresso at home without turning their kitchen counter into a mad scientist lab.

---

## Who This Is For (And Isn't)

**Buy this if:**
- You drink milk-based drinks daily (lattes, cappuccinos, flat whites)
- You want one machine that grinds, doses, brews, and steams — all in one footprint
- You're willing to spend a few weeks dialing in your perfect shot
- You've outgrown a pod machine but don't want a full commercial setup

**Skip this if:**
- You mostly drink black coffee (get a pour-over setup for less)
- You're strictly a French press / Aeropress person who never wants steam
- You want something you can ignore — espresso is a hobby, not a set-and-forget
- You're on a tight budget — this is a $850 investment

## The Good Stuff

### ☕ Shots That Punch Above Their Weight

The overwhelming consensus across thousands of reviews is clear: this machine pulls genuinely excellent espresso. The 15-bar Italian pump combined with Breville's digital temperature control (PID) means consistent extraction at the right temperature. The thermocoil heating system gets to temp in about 3 seconds — no waiting around.

What users consistently report: better espresso at home than most cafes they've visited. The 54mm portafilter (not the standard 58mm) is a minor compromise, but in practice almost no one noticed a difference in shot quality.

### ⚡ Fast Heat-Up, Fast Workflow

This is the biggest differentiator between the Barista Pro and Breville's cheaper Barista Express. The Pro heats up nearly instantly thanks to the thermocoil system. You're pulling your first shot within seconds of switching it on. For busy mornings, that matters.

The integrated burr grinder offers 30 grind settings — plenty of fine control. Many reviewers who'd previously used separate grinder setups admitted the built-in grinder was good enough that they never bothered upgrading.

### 🥛 Decent Steam Power

The steam wand is automatic (not manual), which trade purists might scoff at, but it's genuinely well-executed. It produces microfoam that's good enough for latte art. The wand sits at the right angle for your milk pitcher, and automatic steam means one less variable to manage while you're learning.

---

## The Not-So-Good Stuff

### 📉 Learning Curve (Real, But Passable)

Multiple reviews mention a "one-month hump." For the first few weeks, you'll pull bad shots. Grind too fine, and nothing comes out. Too coarse, and you get sour, watery espresso. This is not a machine problem — it's espresso in general. But if you're coming from a pod machine, be prepared: there's genuine skill involved here.

The included instruction manual isn't great. Most users ended up on YouTube guides to dial in their first truly good shot.

### 🔧 Quality Control and Support Are a Gamble

This is the most common 1-3 star complaint. About 2-3 weeks to a month in, some units develop issues: pressure irregularities, grinder jams, or steam wand quirks. When that happens, Breville's customer support can be slow — some users reported weeks to get a response.

This doesn't seem to be a widespread problem (most units work fine for years), but it's the #1 complaint pattern. If you get a lemon, prepare for a frustrating support experience.

The flip side: users who got good support from Breville rave about it. So it's inconsistent.

### 🔇 Steaming While Brewing (One Task at a Time)

The thermocoil design means you can't brew and steam simultaneously. You brew your shot, then steam your milk. For home use this is rarely an issue, but if you're trying to make two lattes back-to-back for guests, expect a slight rhythm mismatch.

## How It Compares

| Feature | Barista Pro (BES878) | Barista Express (BES870) | Bambino Plus |
|---------|----------------------|------------------------|--------------|
| **Price** | ~$850 | ~$750 | ~$500 |
| **Heat-up** | ~3 seconds (thermocoil) | ~30 seconds (thermocoil) | ~3 seconds |
| **Grinder** | 30 settings, stainless steel | 30 settings, stainless | None (requires separate) |
| **Steam** | Automatic with microfoam | Manual wand | Automatic |
| **Best for** | Daily espresso + milk drinks | Budget all-in-one | Espresso-only + small kitchens |

vs. the **Barista Express** ($100 less): The Pro heats up faster and has a slightly better grinder. Worth the premium if you make coffee daily. Skip if you're budget-conscious.

vs. the **Bambino Plus** ($350 less + no grinder): The Bambino is great black coffee (paired with a separate grinder). The Pro is better if you drink milk drinks and want one tidy machine.

## Accessories You'll Actually Need

The machine comes with everything to get started: portafilter, single and dual-wall baskets, tamper, cleaning tools, and milk jug. But you'll want:

- **A good tamper** — the included plastic one works, but a 54mm precision tamper improves consistency
- **A scale** (0.1g precision) — consistency requires weighing your dose
- **A knock box** — you're going to need somewhere to eject pucks

---

## Bottom Line

**The Breville Barista Pro is the best all-in-one espresso machine in its price range.** The shot quality is genuinely impressive, the workflow is fast enough for daily use, and the form factor is compact enough for most countertops.

The caveats are real: you need patience to learn espresso fundamentals, and customer support is a roll of the dice for the unlucky few. But for the 90%+ of buyers who get a properly functioning unit, this machine delivers cafe-quality results every day.

If you're ready to invest time (a few weeks of experimentation) and money (~$850) into better coffee at home, this is an easy recommendation. If you want instant, brainless coffee, grab an Aeropress and save $800.
