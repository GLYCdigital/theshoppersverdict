If you've ever sliced into a homemade loaf and watched it crumble into a lopsided mess, you already know the problem. Standard loaf pans produce domed tops, thick heel slices, and a pile of unusable scraps. The Pavsrmy 2 Pack Pullman Loaf Pan with Lid solves that with a simple engineering trick: a sliding lid that caps the dough as it bakes, forcing it into a perfect square column.

## What's in the Box

You get two pans — one sized for 1 lb of dough and one for 0.5 lb — each with its own sliding lid. The smaller pan is genuinely useful for testing recipes, baking for one, or making a single sandwich loaf without committing to a full batch. Both are made from heavy-gauge carbon steel with a nonstick coating. The lids slide smoothly along the flanged edges, which is the critical mechanical feature of any Pullman pan. If that fit is sloppy, you get lopsided loaves. Here, it's snug but workable.

## Real-World Performance

The 1 lb pan is the workhorse. I ran a standard white sandwich bread recipe through it — 500g flour, 320g water, typical hydration. The dough filled the pan about two-thirds full, and after proofing to just below the rim, I slid the lid on and baked at 400°F for 35 minutes. The result: a perfectly square loaf with a thin, even crust on all five sides. Slices came out uniform, which means consistent toast and no wasted end pieces.

The carbon steel construction matters here. Cheaper Pullman pans use thin aluminum that warps under high heat. This one held its shape at 425°F during a brioche test (higher butter content, higher oven temp) with zero bowing. Heat distribution was even enough that I didn't need to rotate the pan mid-bake — the crust color was uniform across the bottom and sides.

The smaller 0.5 lb pan works the same way, just scaled down. It's also handy for baking off extra dough when you don't want to fire up a second full-size loaf.

## Where It Falls Short

The nonstick coating is the weakest link. It's not a ceramic or PTFE-heavy premium finish — it's serviceable, but it won't survive metal utensils or a trip through the dishwasher. One reviewer mentioned, "I used a metal spatula to release a stuck corner and saw scratches immediately." Hand-wash with a soft sponge, and don't stack the pans without a cloth between them.

The lid fit on the larger pan is tight when dough rises aggressively. If you over-proof (especially with high-hydration doughs), the lid can jam, and you'll have to wrestle it off mid-bake. Solution: proof to about 80% of the rim height instead of full. It's a learning curve, not a dealbreaker.

## Who Should Buy This

This is a strong buy for home bakers who make sandwich bread weekly and want consistent square slices for toasters, lunchboxes, or uniform croutons. It's also a smart choice if you bake in small batches — the two-size set covers you without buying two separate pans.

Skip it if you primarily bake free-form artisan loaves or sourdough boules. The Pullman design is specifically for sandwich-style bread; it won't help with crusty rounds. Also skip if you refuse to hand-wash cookware — the coating won't survive a dishwasher's harsh cycle.

## FAQ

**Can I use this pan for no-knead bread?**
Yes, but expect a denser crumb. No-knead doughs are higher hydration and benefit from a Dutch oven's steam. The lid traps some steam, but you won't get the same open crumb. Stick to standard sandwich doughs for best results.

**What's the actual loaf size from the 1 lb pan?**
With 500g of dough (about 1 lb), you'll get a loaf roughly 8 x 4 x 4 inches. That's smaller than a standard grocery store loaf but perfect for a family of four for a couple of days.

**Does the lid need to be greased?**
Lightly. The nonstick coating helps, but a thin butter or oil brush on the lid's underside ensures it slides off cleanly after baking. Skip this step at your own risk — baked-on dough on the lid is a pain to remove.
