# Cook N Home Stainless Steel Stock Pot with Lid: The $40 Workhorse That Punches Above Its Weight

You don't need a $200 Le Creuset to make a decent stock. But you do need a pot that heats evenly, holds a lid properly, and won't warp after a year of abuse. The Cook N Home Stainless Steel Stock Pot — sold in various sizes with a matching lid — delivers exactly that for a fraction of the premium brands. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The pot features a tri-ply construction: a thick aluminum core sandwiched between two layers of magnetic stainless steel. This matters because aluminum conducts heat far better than steel alone — it's the same principle that makes All-Clad pots cost five times more. The rim is rolled and polished, and the glass lid fits snugly with a steam vent.

Depending on the size you order (6-quart and 8-quart are most common), you're looking at a pot that's roughly 10 inches in diameter and 7 inches tall. The handles are stainless steel with a brushed finish, riveted through the body — not welded, which is actually a plus for durability.

## Real-World Performance

I've simmered chicken stock for six hours in this pot. The liquid stayed at a gentle bubble with no scorching on the bottom. That's the tri-ply core doing its job — heat spreads across the entire base rather than concentrating in the center. For a pot at this price, that's genuinely impressive.

The lid seals better than I expected. When I tested it with a pot of boiling water, barely any steam escaped around the rim. That means less water loss during long braises and faster boil times. The glass is thick enough to withstand oven use up to 500°F, which makes this a surprisingly versatile piece — you can start a stew on the stovetop, then transfer it to the oven for slow cooking.

One notable quirk: the pot is induction-compatible thanks to the magnetic stainless steel base. If you've got an induction cooktop, this will work without needing an adapter disc.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The exterior is a fingerprint magnet.** Brushed stainless steel looks great in product photos, but in real life, it shows every smudge, water spot, and grease mark. You'll be wiping it down after every use if you care about appearances.

**The handles get hot in the oven.** On the stovetop, they stay cool enough to grab without a mitt. But if you're transferring this to a 400°F oven, you'll need oven mitts — the riveted handles conduct heat efficiently. This isn't a safety flaw, just something to know before you plan a braise-to-oven recipe.

**No pour spouts.** The rolled rim is smooth and drip-resistant, but there are no designated spouts on either side. If you're straining stock directly from the pot, you'll need a ladle or a separate strainer — pouring can get messy.

## Who Should Buy This

This pot is ideal for three types of shoppers:

- **Home cooks making weekly batches of stock or soup** — the even heating and tight lid make it perfect for low-and-slow simmering
- **Budget-conscious buyers who want tri-ply performance without the premium price tag** — this performs 90% as well as pots costing $150+

## FAQ

**Is this dishwasher safe?**
Technically yes, but hand-washing is strongly recommended. Dishwasher detergent can cause pitting or discoloration on stainless steel over time. A quick hand wash with warm soapy water keeps it looking new.

**Will this work on a glass-top electric stove?**
Yes, the flat bottom sits flush on glass cooktops without scratching. Just avoid sliding it — lift it when moving to prevent scratches.

**Can I use metal utensils with this pot?**
Yes, the stainless surface won't scratch from metal spoons or whisks. That's one advantage over nonstick — you don't have to baby the surface.
