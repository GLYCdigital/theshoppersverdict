Let's be honest: most peelers are $3 throwaways that dull after a bag of potatoes. The OXO Good Grips Large Y Peeler costs more and looks almost identical to the cheap ones — but it's not. After 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, this is the peeler that home cooks actually repurchase when they lose it. Here's why.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains one peeler, about 8 inches long, with a matte black soft-grip handle and a stainless steel blade set in a Y-shaped fork. The cutting head swivels slightly, which is the key engineering detail — it lets the blade follow the contour of lumpy vegetables without digging in. The handle is noticeably thicker than standard peelers, and that's deliberate. It's built for people who peel a lot, not for occasional garnish work.

**How It Performs in Real Use**

The blade arrives genuinely sharp. It glides through carrot skin without the "grab and snap" you get from duller blades. Potato eyes are the real test — this blade cuts them out cleanly without removing half the potato. The swivel head tracks curves on butternut squash and bell peppers without wasting flesh.

The large handle earns its name. If you have bigger hands or arthritis, the thick grip reduces the death-grip you need with skinny peelers. The soft-touch material stays grippy when wet, which matters more than you'd think mid-peel of a sink full of apples.

I ran this through the dishwasher for two months straight. No rust spots, no blade discoloration, no handle warping. That's rare — most peelers with soft grips degrade or harbor water inside the handle.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The theme across reviews is longevity. One user noted, "I've had mine for three years and it's still the sharpest peeler I own." Another said, "The handle makes a huge difference when I'm prepping for canning — no more hand cramps after 20 tomatoes." Multiple reviewers mention buying extras as gifts because they're tired of replacing cheap peelers.

**Where It Falls Short**

The Y-grip takes adjustment. If you've spent years using a straight peeler, the first few sessions will feel awkward — you hold it differently, and the angle changes how you approach the vegetable. Give it a week.

The blade is technically replaceable, but OXO doesn't sell replacements at most retailers. When the blade eventually dulls after years of use, you'll likely buy a new peeler rather than source a replacement part. At this price point, that's acceptable, but it's worth knowing.

It's also not ideal for delicate work like peeling asparagus or shaving chocolate. The blade is aggressive — it removes skin efficiently, but it will also shave off flesh if you're not careful.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy this if you: cook regularly, prep vegetables in bulk, have larger hands or grip issues, or are tired of replacing cheap peelers every six months. It's also a genuinely good gift for college students or new apartment-dwellers — one solid tool beats a drawer of junk.

Skip it if you: only peel occasionally, prefer the control of a straight peeler, or need a peeler for delicate precision work.

Yes, significantly longer than budget peelers. Most users report a year or more of regular use before noticing dulling. The stainless steel blade is properly heat-treated, not stamped junk.

**Can it handle thick-skinned vegetables like butternut squash?**

**Is it really dishwasher safe?**
