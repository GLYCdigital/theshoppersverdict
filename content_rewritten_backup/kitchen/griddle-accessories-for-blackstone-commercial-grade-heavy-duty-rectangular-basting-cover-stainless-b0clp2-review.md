Let’s be honest: most griddle accessories are flimsy afterthoughts designed to separate you from another $30. This basting cover is not that. It’s a thick, heavy slab of stainless steel that feels like it belongs in a commercial kitchen — because it’s built like one.

**What You Actually Get**

The package is simple: one rectangular stainless steel basting cover. No handles, no hinges, no gimmicks. It’s a single formed piece of metal measuring roughly 17 inches long and 12 inches wide, with a raised dome profile. The steel gauge is noticeably thicker than the cheap chrome versions you’ll find at big-box stores. It sits flat on the griddle surface with enough weight (about 3 pounds) that it won’t slide around when you’re scraping or flipping.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this on a 22-inch Blackstone over a weekend of smash burgers and breakfast service. The dome traps heat exactly as intended — cheese melted on burgers in under 60 seconds, and bacon cooked faster with less splatter on the cooktop. The steam circulation inside the dome keeps food moist without steaming it into mush, which is the balancing act most covers get wrong.

The fit on standard Blackstone models is solid. It covers roughly two-thirds of the cooking surface, leaving room to toast buns or keep eggs warm on the exposed side. If you’ve got a 17-inch model, it covers nearly the entire griddle.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews isn’t accidental. Recurring themes from verified purchases:

*“This thing is a tank. I’ve warped two cheaper covers and this one hasn’t budged after six months of heavy use.”*

*“Finally a dome that doesn’t condensate and drip water all over my food. The angle on the lid directs moisture back to the edges.”*

That last point is worth emphasizing. The interior angle is designed to channel condensation away from the center. Anyone who’s used a cheap dome knows the frustration of water pooling on your burger buns. This design largely eliminates that.

**Where It Falls Short**

The lack of a handle is the biggest annoyance. You will burn your fingers at least once if you grab it bare-handed after preheating. Plan to keep a mitt nearby at all times.

Also, this is not a set-and-forget accessory. Hand washing is required — putting it in the dishwasher will leave water spots and eventually dull the finish. It’s not hard to clean (a quick wipe with soapy water does it), but if you’re someone who throws everything in the dishwasher, this will annoy you.

Finally, the price runs higher than budget alternatives. You’re paying for the thicker gauge steel and the build quality. If you only griddle twice a year, skip it. If you cook outdoors regularly, the durability justifies the cost.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Weekend warriors** who use their Blackstone at least twice a month and want consistent melting and steaming performance.
- **Smash burger enthusiasts** who need fast, even cheese melt without lifting the lid repeatedly.
- **Campers and tailgaters** who want one piece of gear that won’t bend in a cooler or rattle in a trailer.

Skip it if you’re a casual user with a cheap griddle you’re already thinking about replacing, or if you despise hand-washing anything.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my 36-inch Blackstone?**
No. This cover is sized for 17-inch and 22-inch models. For a 36-inch griddle, you’d need a wider cover or two smaller ones positioned side by side.

**Q: Can I use this on a gas stove indoors?**
Technically yes, but it’s designed for flat-top griddles. On a standard range, it’ll work as a splatter guard but won’t perform as well since there’s no surrounding heat source.

**Q: Does it come with a handle or lid lifter?**
No. You’ll need your own mitt or tongs. Several reviewers mention using a spatula to lift one edge — that works, but it’s awkward with heavier cuts of meat.
