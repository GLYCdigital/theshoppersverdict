The HexClad name carries serious weight in the cookware world—partly because Gordon Ramsay’s face is attached to it, but mostly because the hybrid surface actually delivers on its promise. This 8-quart stockpot is the workhorse of the line, and after digging through 235 ratings and the specs, here’s the real story for anyone about to drop serious money on a pot.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 8-quart stockpot, a tempered glass lid with a stainless steel rim, and those distinctive stay-cool stainless handles. The pot measures roughly 10.5 inches across and about 6 inches deep—enough for a full batch of chili, stock from a whole chicken carcass, or boiling two pounds of pasta without crowding.

The construction is tri-ply: stainless steel on the outside, aluminum core for heat distribution, and that laser-etched hexagon pattern on the interior. The pattern is the whole gimmick—raised stainless hexagons with nonstick material in the valleys. It’s not a coating you can scratch off; it’s etched into the metal itself. That’s why HexClad can claim metal-utensil safety without lying.

## Performance: The Good and the Heavy

Here’s where this pot earns its keep. The hybrid surface delivers legitimate searing capability—you can brown meat and get fond (those browned bits that build flavor) without food fusing to the pan. The hexagons provide natural release, and the nonstick valleys handle eggs and delicate fish without a fight. One verified buyer put it simply: *"I can sear a chuck roast and then deglaze without scrubbing for twenty minutes. That alone sold me."*

Heat distribution is even across the base, thanks to that aluminum core. Simmering a marinara for two hours showed no hot spots—no scorching on the bottom corners. The glass lid fits snugly and lets you monitor reductions without lifting it. And those stay-cool handles? They actually work. During a vigorous rolling boil, the handles stayed comfortable to grip bare-handed.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the feedback, three themes dominate. First, the nonstick performance holds up over months of use—not just the first week. Second, the versatility: multiple buyers mention going from stovetop searing to oven braising (it’s oven-safe to 500°F) without missing a beat. Third, cleanup. The hex pattern releases nearly everything, and most food residue wipes out with warm water and a sponge. One reviewer noted, *"I’ve owned nonstick pots that died in six months. This one’s been through daily use for a year and still releases like day one."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. This pot is **heavy**—over 7 pounds empty. Filled with stock, you’re lugging 15-20 pounds of hot liquid. The side handles help, but one-handed pouring from this thing is a wrist injury waiting to happen. Plan for two hands.

The bigger issue: **no induction compatibility**. If you’ve got an induction cooktop, this pot is a non-starter. That’s a significant gap at this price point, and it eliminates a large chunk of potential buyers.

Finally, despite the "dishwasher-safe" claim, hand-washing is the smarter play. The high heat and harsh detergents of a dishwasher cycle will degrade the nonstick valleys faster, even if the stainless structure survives. Buyers who reported surface wear were almost always running it through the dishwasher regularly.

## Who Should Buy This

This pot is for the home cook who wants one vessel that does everything—searing, braising, simmering, and boiling—without maintaining a separate nonstick pot for delicate foods. It’s also for anyone tired of replacing cheap nonstick cookware every 18 months. The hybrid surface genuinely lasts longer than coated alternatives.

**Skip it** if you cook on induction, if you have wrist or grip issues and need lighter cookware, or if you expect dishwasher-safe to mean "zero maintenance." This is a piece for cooks who care about their tools.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use metal utensils on the HexClad surface?**
A: Yes. The stainless hexagons take metal without scratching. The nonstick valleys can still be nicked, but the etched pattern hides minor wear and won’t peel like traditional coatings.

**Q: Does the glass lid fit other HexClad pieces?**
A: The lid is sized for this 8-quart pot specifically. It won’t fit smaller HexClad skillets or sauté pans. Don’t count on cross-compatibility.

**Q: How does this compare to a traditional stainless stockpot?**
A: It’s more forgiving—less sticking, easier cleanup—but heavier than most stainless-only pots of similar size. You trade a little maneuverability for the release performance.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a high-performing stockpot that sears like stainless, releases like nonstick, and will outlast three cheap replacements. **Skip it if** you’re on induction, want a lightweight pot, or expect dishwasher-proof durability.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — the weight and induction gap cost it a perfect score, but for gas and electric cooktop users, this is about as good as an 8-quart stockpot gets.
