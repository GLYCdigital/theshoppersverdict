# Martha Stewart 16 Piece Lockton High Carbon Stainless Steel: Worth Your Counter Space?

Most knife sets under $100 are a trap. You get 14 blades that can't slice a tomato, a block that falls apart, and a "sharpener" that does more harm than good. The Martha Stewart 16 Piece Lockton set isn't that. It's not a professional-grade Wüsthof killer either — but it sits in a sweet spot that most budget sets completely miss.

## What You Actually Get

Let's clear up the "16 piece" marketing right now. The set includes: 8", 6", and 3.5" knives (chef's, santoku, and paring), a carving knife, utility knife, bread knife, kitchen shears, six serrated steak knives, a honing steel, and a wooden block. That's 13 blades plus the block, steel, and shears — hence 16. The steak knives are a genuine inclusion, not filler.

The blades are high-carbon stainless steel, which matters. Standard cheap sets use 3Cr13 or 420 steel that dulls within weeks. This Lockton line uses a higher carbon content that responds better to honing and holds an edge for months of regular home use. As shown in the product image, the full tang extends through the triple-riveted handle — that's structural integrity you can feel.

## Performance and Build Quality

Out of the box, the chef's knife will glide through onions, bell peppers, and raw chicken breast with minimal resistance. The santoku is the standout performer here — its granton edge (those oval divots) prevents food from sticking, which makes it the workhorse for vegetable prep. The bread knife features a scalloped serration that handles crusty sourdough without crushing the interior.

The full tang construction means these knives have real heft — roughly 8-10 ounces for the chef's knife — which helps with controlled chopping. Balance is slightly forward-weighted, favoring the blade, which most home cooks prefer for rocking cuts.

One thing buyers consistently mention in their reviews: the edge retention genuinely surprises them. One verified purchaser noted, *"I've had $200 sets that dulled faster than these. Six months of daily use and the chef's knife still slices paper."* Another praised the ergonomics: *"The handles are comfortable for smaller hands — my wife actually prefers these over my Wüsthofs."*

## Where It Falls Short

The block is the biggest physical drawback. It's solid acacia wood, but it's roughly 14 inches wide and takes up serious counter real estate. If you're working with limited space, this will be a constant annoyance.

The handles — while comfortable — are slick when wet. The smooth synthetic material offers zero traction if your hands are soapy or greasy. A textured grip would have made this set nearly perfect.

Also worth noting: the included honing steel is functional but basic. It works fine for weekly touch-ups, but don't expect it to restore a badly dulled edge — that requires a proper whetstone.

## Who Should Buy This

- **First-time apartment dwellers** setting up a kitchen — this covers all the bases without breaking the bank
- **Home cooks who prep daily** and are tired of replacing cheap knives every six months
- **Gift buyers** looking for a complete, presentable set that won't embarrass the recipient

**Skip it if** you're a serious home chef who needs a nakiri, boning knife, or other specialized blades — this set covers fundamentals only. Also skip if counter space is genuinely tight.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these knives dishwasher safe?**
A: Technically yes, but don't. The high-carbon steel will develop spots and the handles can degrade over time. Hand wash and dry immediately — it takes 30 seconds and preserves the edge.

**Q: How often do I need to sharpen them?**
A: Weekly honing with the included steel maintains the edge. A proper sharpening with a whetstone or professional service is needed every 6-12 months depending on use.

**Q: Can I use these on cutting boards other than wood?**
A: Yes, but avoid glass, stone, or ceramic boards — they'll dull the edge rapidly. Stick to wood, bamboo, or composite boards.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a complete, genuinely functional knife set that outperforms its price point and holds up for years of daily cooking. The edge retention, full tang construction, and included extras make this a legitimately smart buy.

**Skip it if** you need specialized blades, have minimal counter space, or expect professional-grade steel at a budget price. This is a solid home cook's set, not a culinary school requirement.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A rare budget set that actually delivers on its promises, held back only by the bulky block and slick handles.
