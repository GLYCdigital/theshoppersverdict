You've been there: you make a perfect bowl of guac, and two hours later it's a brown, sad mess. The PREPWORKS Progressive International Fresh Guacamole ProKeepe (yes, that's the actual name) is designed to kill that problem at the source. But does a $15 plastic bowl with a masher plate actually deliver? After digging through 215 ratings and the mechanics of how this thing works, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 3-in-1 system: a clear plastic mixing bowl, a masher plate that clicks onto the top, and an airtight lid that doubles as a storage cover. You mash your avocados right in the bowl, scrape down the sides, then swap the masher for the lid. The bowl holds roughly 3 cups—enough for 3–4 avocados worth of guac, which covers a decent game-day crowd.

The masher is the standout piece. It's a flat, perforated disc with a handle that fits inside the bowl's rim. It crushes avocado chunks efficiently without turning them into baby food unless you want that. As one buyer put it, "I've made guac three times this week just because it's fun to use." That's the kind of enthusiasm this gadget generates.

## Does It Actually Keep Guac Green?

Here's the honest answer: yes, but with effort. The lid has a rubber gasket and a center vent. To get the seal right, you press the lid down and push the vent closed, which forces excess air out. If you do that, guac stays green for about 48–72 hours in the fridge. One reviewer noted, "Day three and still no browning—I'm genuinely impressed."

The catch: you have to press firmly and make sure the gasket sits flush. Several buyers mention the lid needing a "hard push" to seal, and a few report it popping loose if the fridge is crowded and something shifts against it. If the seal breaks, you're back to brown guac within hours.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The biggest theme in reviews is convenience. People love that they can go from whole avocados to sealed storage in one dish. "I used to dirty a bowl, a masher, and a storage container. Now it's one thing to wash," one reviewer wrote. Another praised the masher's texture control: "You can leave it chunky or go totally smooth—it's all about how long you press."

The size also gets props. It's compact enough for small fridges, and the clear bowl makes it easy to spot when leftovers are getting old.

## Where It Falls Short

The plastic build is the main compromise. It feels lighter and less premium than glass or ceramic alternatives. If you're making guac with tomatoes or onions that sit for a few days, you'll likely see some staining—a few reviewers noted the bowl picking up a faint orange tint. It's also not microwave-safe, so if you want to reheat anything in it, you're out of luck (though honestly, you shouldn't be microwaving guac anyway).

The masher plate can also be a bit awkward to grip if you have smaller hands. It's functional, but not ergonomically perfect.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Weekend entertainers** who make guac, salsa, or even egg salad and want a single-vessel solution
- **Meal preppers** who want to make guac on Sunday and have it last through Tuesday
- **Small households** where 3 cups is the right portion size

Skip it if you're a heavy-duty cook who needs oven-safe, dishwasher-everything gear, or if you make guac in massive batches for parties of 10+. For that, you'll want a bigger bowl and a separate storage setup.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the masher work for other foods?**
A: Yes. It's great for mashing beans for refried beans, potatoes for small batches of mash, or even eggs for egg salad. It won't replace a potato masher for a full pot, but it handles small portions well.

**Q: Is it dishwasher safe?**
A: The bowl and lid are top-rack dishwasher safe, but hand-washing is recommended to keep the gasket in good shape over time. The masher plate is fine in the dishwasher.

**Q: How long does guac really stay green?**
A: With a proper seal and a fresh batch, 2–3 days is realistic. After that, even with a good seal, you'll start to see oxidation at the edges. It's not magic—it's just a better seal than plastic wrap.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you make guac regularly and hate the brown-soup problem. It's a functional, space-saving gadget that earns its place in the drawer for the price.

**Skip it if** you prefer glass storage, need to reheat in the same container, or you're already happy with your current bowl-and-wrap method.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The ProKeepe does its one job well, and the masher is a genuinely useful addition. It's not a premium product, but it's a smart one. If you're tired of wasting avocados, this is a solid, affordable fix.
