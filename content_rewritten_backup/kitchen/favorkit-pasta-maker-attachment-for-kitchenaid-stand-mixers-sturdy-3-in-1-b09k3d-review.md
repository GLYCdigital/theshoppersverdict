If you've priced the official KitchenAid pasta roller and cutter set lately, you know the sting — it's a $100+ investment on top of a mixer that already set you back. The FavorKit 3-in-1 attachment undercuts that price dramatically while promising the same functionality. The question is whether the savings cost you in performance. After digging through the customer feedback and examining the design specs, here's the real picture.

## What's Actually in the Box

The set includes three main components: a smooth roller with adjustable thickness settings (0.8mm to 3mm), a fettuccine cutter, and a spaghetti cutter. All three attach to your KitchenAid stand mixer's front hub using the same mechanism as the OEM version. It's all-metal — no plastic gears or housings, which is the first thing that separates this from the sub-$30 knockoffs flooding Amazon. The attachment fits all standard tilt-head and bowl-lift KitchenAid models, so compatibility isn't a concern for most buyers.

## How It Performs in Real Kitchens

The roller's adjustability is the heart of this system, and it works as intended. You can dial in everything from thick pappardelle-style sheets to paper-thin pasta for ravioli. Multiple reviewers noted the gears mesh smoothly and the crank turns without binding — a common failure point on cheaper attachments. One verified buyer put it plainly: "Works perfectly with my KitchenAid. Metal build, no plastic parts. Rolled pasta to a nice thin sheet without tearing."

The fettuccine and spaghetti cutters produce clean, consistent strands with minimal flour dusting required. As long as your dough is at the right hydration (around 40-45% for most recipes), the pasta comes through uniformly. The attachment clamps on and off the mixer quickly, and cleanup is straightforward — mostly a dry brush-down after letting the pieces air-dry.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The overwhelming theme in the feedback is value. Shoppers repeatedly compare this to the KitchenAid-branded set and find the FavorKit delivers 90% of the performance at roughly half the cost. One review captured the sentiment well: "Excellent quality for the price. Very solid, similar to the KitchenAid brand attachment but at a much better price." The all-metal construction and smooth operation get frequent shout-outs, and several buyers confirmed they've used it for months without any wear issues.

## Where It Falls Short

The most significant gap is the lack of a lasagna sheet roller. The KitchenAid set includes a third attachment specifically for wide sheets, and this kit doesn't offer it. If you make lasagna or filled pastas that need large flat sheets, you'll be hand-rolling those or buying a separate accessory.

A smaller but notable issue: some users report the wiper bar (the piece that scrapes dough off the roller) needs a slight adjustment out of the box. If yours arrives with the gap too tight, dough can stick and wrap around the roller. It's fixable with a screwdriver, but it's a manufacturing inconsistency that shouldn't exist. One customer noted, "Had to tweak the wiper bar a bit, but after that it worked flawlessly."

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a home cook who makes fresh pasta occasionally and wants a reliable, budget-friendly setup without dropping $130+ on the OEM version. It's also ideal if you already own a KitchenAid and want to test whether you'll actually use a pasta attachment before committing to the premium set.

**Skip it if:** You make lasagna or stuffed pasta regularly and need the wide sheet roller, or if you're a perfectionist who wants zero setup quirks out of the box. In that case, save for the official KitchenAid set — it includes the third attachment and has more consistent quality control.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my KitchenAid mixer?**
A: It fits all standard KitchenAid stand mixers with the front hub attachment point — that includes the Artisan, Classic, Pro, and Commercial series. If your mixer accepts other hub attachments, this will work.

**Q: Can I use this to make gluten-free pasta?**
A: Yes, but expect more fragility. Gluten-free doughs lack the elasticity of wheat-based pasta, so they tear more easily at thin settings. Start at 2.5mm and work down gradually, using extra dusting flour.

**Q: How do I clean it without ruining it?**
A: Never submerge it in water. Use the included brush or a dry pastry brush to remove flour and dough residue after each use. If needed, wipe with a slightly damp cloth and dry immediately. Oil the gears occasionally with food-grade mineral oil.
