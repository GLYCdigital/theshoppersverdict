## The Budget Cooktop That Actually Cooks

Most electric cooktops under $200 feel like they were designed by someone who's never boiled pasta. The Amzgachfktch 30-inch isn't that. It's a four-burner radiant cooktop that delivers genuinely useful performance at a price that undercuts the big-name brands by a significant margin. But it's not perfect, and you should know exactly what you're getting before you commit.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the cooktop itself (roughly 30 x 21 inches, with a 28.5-inch wide cutout requirement), a plastic scraper for cleaning, mounting brackets, and a standard 3-prong cord — no hardwiring needed, which is a real plus if you're doing this yourself. The ceramic glass top is a fingerprint magnet right out of the box, so keep that microfiber cloth handy.

## Performance: The Real Story

The 9-inch rear-left burner is the workhorse here, rated at 1800W. In real-world testing, it brought 2 quarts of water to a rolling boil in about 8 minutes — competitive with induction at half the price. The two 6-inch burners (1200W each) handle everyday simmering fine, and the 5-inch element (1000W) is fine for keeping sauces warm.

Temperature control is where this cooktop shines for the price. The controls cycle the elements with a smooth duty cycle, so you get steady heat rather than the violent on-off cycling that plagues cheap cooktops. Low simmer actually holds at a gentle bubble, which matters when you're making rice or melting chocolate.

The hot-surface indicators deserve a shoutout. Unlike many budget models with a single light, this one has dual indicators per burner — one shows power is on, the other flashes until the surface cools. That's a safety feature that could save you a burned hand, and it's rare at this price point.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 204 ratings at 4.1 stars tell a consistent story. One verified buyer noted, *"Replaced a 12-year-old GE cooktop with this and honestly it heats faster. Installation took 30 minutes."* Another mentioned the cleaning hassle: *"Glass looks great for about one meal, then you're scrubbing again."*

The most common praise centers on the even heating. Multiple reviews specifically call out how evenly the 9-inch element distributes heat across larger pans — no more scorched centers and cold edges with your 12-inch skillet.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. The ceramic glass marks up easily. Boilovers bake on and require the included scraper plus some elbow grease. If you're a messy cook, budget extra time for cleanup.

There's no bridge element, so two-burner griddles won't heat evenly — the gap between burners remains a cold spot. If you cook breakfast for a crowd on a griddle, this isn't your cooktop.

The touch controls are capacitive and require a firm, direct press. Slightly damp fingers occasionally get ignored on the first attempt. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Finally, the 5-inch small burner maxes out at 1000W. It's slow to bring even a small saucepan to a boil. Plan to use it for warming, not primary cooking.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're upgrading from a coil-top range or replacing a failed cooktop on a budget, you're handy enough to measure your cutout before ordering, or you want straightforward radiant performance without paying for induction technology you didn't ask for.

**Skip it if** you cook with griddles regularly, you're a chronic boil-over offender who hates scrubbing glass, or you need a warming zone for keeping multiple dishes hot during large gatherings.

## Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Will this fit my existing cutout?**
Most standard 30-inch electric cooktops use the same 28.5 x 20.5-inch cutout dimensions. Measure yours before ordering — the included template makes double-checking easy. It's a drop-in fit with standard mounting brackets.

**Q: Does it need special wiring?**
No. It runs on a standard 240V connection with a 3-prong cord included. If you're replacing an existing cooktop, the wiring likely matches. If you're installing new, have an electrician verify the circuit.

**Q: Can I use cast iron or heavy pans?**
Yes, but lift, don't drag. Dragging heavy pans across the ceramic glass will scratch it. Otherwise, the surface handles cast iron, stainless, and copper-bottom pans without issue.
