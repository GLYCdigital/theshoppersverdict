# BREETEE Portable Range Hood Kitchen Review: Does This Compact Exhaust Fan Actually Work?

**The short answer:** If you cook in a small apartment, dorm, or RV where a full range hood isn't an option, the BREETEE portable range hood is a genuinely useful tool — with real limitations you need to know before you swipe your card.

---

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the compact hood unit, a 5-layer aluminum mesh filter, a replaceable activated carbon filter, and a mounting kit if you want to secure it under a cabinet. The unit measures right around 12 inches wide and 8 inches deep — small enough to sit on a counter behind your induction burner or clamp to a shelf edge.

As shown in the product image, the design is straightforward: a rectangular intake face, a slide-out filter tray, and a simple control panel (typically 2–3 fan speeds plus a light). No smart features, no app, no touchscreen. It's a mechanical device that moves air. That's the appeal — and the limitation.

---

## Real-World Performance

Here's where expectations matter. This is *not* a replacement for a 400+ CFM over-the-range hood. The BREETEE moves roughly 100–120 CFM at max speed. That's enough to capture steam, light smoke, and cooking odors from a single burner or a small electric griddle. If you're searing a steak at high heat in a cast iron pan, expect some smoke to escape around the edges — the intake area is simply too small to catch everything.

What it does well: positioned 6–8 inches above a pan, it pulls grease-laden air through the 5-layer aluminum mesh before it reaches your kitchen ceiling. Buyers consistently report less residue on cabinets and less lingering smell after frying fish or garlic. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical, but after a month of daily use, my microwave and cabinets above my stove aren't getting the greasy film they used to."*

The carbon filter makes recirculating mode viable — air passes through the mesh to trap grease, then through carbon to neutralize odors before being blown back into the room. It's not as effective as venting outside, but it's dramatically better than no ventilation at all.

---

## Build Quality and Maintenance

The housing is powder-coated steel, not flimsy plastic. It feels solid and won't flex when you adjust the speed. The fan motor is quiet at low speed (about the volume of a bathroom exhaust fan), but at max speed you'll notice it — roughly 55–60 dB based on owner reports. Not loud enough to drown out a conversation, but you won't forget it's running.

**The honest con:** That 5-layer filter is effective, but it's also more work. You'll need to degrease it every 2–4 weeks depending on how often you cook. The mesh layers trap grease effectively — which means they *hold* grease. A soak in hot water with degreaser handles it, but it's a chore. Some owners recommend buying a second filter to swap while one cleans.

---

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You live in a rental apartment and can't install a permanent hood
- You have a dorm room, studio, or tiny kitchen with no ventilation
- You use portable induction burners or a hot plate and cook oil-heavy foods
- You're in an RV or van build and need compact ventilation

**Skip it if:**
- You regularly sear, deep-fry, or cook with high heat — you need real CFM
- You expect it to handle an entire kitchen's worth of cooking
- You hate cleaning filters more than once a month

## FAQ

**Q: Can this be vented outside?**
A: Yes, it supports a ducting kit (sold separately) for true external venting. The recirculating mode with carbon filter works, but ducting is always more effective for smoke removal.

**Q: How loud is it on the highest setting?**
A: Measured around 55–60 dB — comparable to a running dishwasher. Low speed is significantly quieter.

**Q: Does it come with the carbon filter included?**
A: Yes, one carbon filter is included. Replacement carbon filters need to be purchased separately and typically last 3–6 months with regular use.
