**What You Get**

The box contains a single filter, pre-wrapped in plastic with a small rubber gasket already seated on the twist-lock cap. **Real-World Performance**

The filter uses activated carbon block technology, which is exactly what GE uses in their own cartridges. It's certified to NSF/ANSI 42 for chlorine taste and odor reduction, plus NSF 372 for lead reduction. In plain English: your water will taste clean and your ice won't smell like a swimming pool.

Buyers consistently report the same results as the OEM filter. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Tastes identical to the GE brand filter. I honestly can't tell the difference."* Another noted the flow rate matches: *"Water pressure through the dispenser is exactly the same as the original."* That's the two things that matter most — taste and flow — and Pureline nails both.

The install is genuinely tool-free. Twist the old filter counterclockwise (it'll release with a small spurt of water — have a towel ready), line up the tabs on the new one, twist clockwise until it locks. Total time: about three minutes, including the mandatory two-gallon flush through the dispenser afterward.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Three themes dominate the positive reviews. First, value — at roughly half the OEM price, buyers feel they're getting the same result for less. Second, shipping speed — Amazon Prime delivery means you're never stuck with a blinking red "Replace Filter" light for days. Third, the leak-proof seal. Multiple reviewers specifically mention the gasket seating properly with zero drips, which is the classic failure point on cheap third-party filters.

**Where It Falls Short**

No filter is perfect, and this one has two honest drawbacks. First, there's no date sticker on the housing. GE prints a "replace by" date on their filters; Pureline doesn't. Set a calendar reminder for six months out, or you'll forget and end up drinking through a clogged carbon block.

Second, a small but persistent minority (maybe 3-4% of reviews) report the filter needs a firm push to fully seat in older GE refrigerators, particularly models from 2012-2015. It's not a dealbreaker — a solid twist-and-push resolves it — but if your fridge is a decade old, don't panic if the first twist doesn't lock smoothly. Just reseat it and try again.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this filter remove lead?**
Yes. It's NSF/ANSI 372 certified for lead reduction, along with chlorine taste and odor. It won't remove all contaminants — it's not a reverse osmosis system — but it handles the big municipal water complaints.

**Q: How often should I replace it?**
Every six months or 300 gallons, whichever comes first. If you have hard water, lean toward the five-month mark. Ignore the filter status light if your fridge doesn't have one — just set a reminder.

**Q: Will it fit my Kenmore refrigerator?**
If your Kenmore uses the standard GE MWF-style filter (models 46-9081 or 46-9906), yes. Double-check your current filter's model number before ordering. If it says MWF or MWFP on the side, you're good.
