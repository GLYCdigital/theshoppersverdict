## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides, because there are a few.

**Water spots are real.** 18/0 stainless is more prone to spotting than 18/10. If you run this through the dishwasher and let it air dry, you'll see mineral deposits. It's cosmetic, not functional, but if you're particular about how your kitchen looks, hand drying is the fix.

**Heat conduction.** The handles will get warm if you leave a spoon resting in a hot pot. Not scorching — just uncomfortable. Use the hanging hole on the handle end to rest it on the pot rim instead.

**Noise.** Metal on metal clangs. If you cook in a small apartment with thin walls, consider how much banging you'll tolerate. This is a nitpick, not a dealbreaker, but it's honest.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these scratch my nonstick pans?**
A: Used normally — stirring, flipping, serving — no. The edges are smooth. Aggressive scraping with the turner's edge could eventually mark a soft nonstick coating. If you're nervous, use the slotted spoon for stirring and reserve the turner for stainless or cast iron.

**Q: Are these really dishwasher safe?**
A: Mechanically, yes. Cosmetically, you'll get water spots with 18/0 steel. If you don't care about that, throw them in. If you want them looking pristine, hand wash and dry.

**Q: What's the difference between 18/0 and 18/10 stainless?**
A: The numbers refer to chromium and nickel percentages. 18/10 has more nickel, which gives it a shinier finish and better corrosion resistance — but it's more expensive and can trigger nickel allergies. 18/0 is food-safe, harder, and perfectly adequate for home use.
