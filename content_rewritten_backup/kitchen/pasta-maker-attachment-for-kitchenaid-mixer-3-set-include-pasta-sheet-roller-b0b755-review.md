If you've ever hand-cranked pasta dough for forty minutes only to produce uneven, torn sheets, you already know why the KitchenAid attachment exists. The question is whether this third-party 3-piece set — roller, fettuccine cutter, and spaghetti cutter — delivers the same experience as the $150 OEM version at a fraction of the price. ## What's Actually in the Box

The set includes three stainless steel attachments: a 5.5-inch-wide sheet roller with adjustable thickness (settings 1 through 7, from thick lasagna to paper-thin), plus two cutting heads — one for fettuccine (roughly 6mm wide) and one for spaghetti (2mm). The housing is a zinc-aluminum alloy with a matte finish that matches the KitchenAid mixer's aesthetic. It mounts to the front power hub in seconds — no tools, no adapter needed.

As shown in the product image, the roller assembly is more compact than the KitchenAid-brand version, which is actually a plus for storage. The whole set weighs just over 3 pounds and fits in a standard drawer.

## Performance: The Real-World Test

Here's where this attachment separates itself from cheap knockoffs. The rollers are machined stainless steel, not plated — meaning they won't flake or corrode after repeated use. Buyers consistently report smooth operation at the #3 thickness setting (the sweet spot for most fresh pasta). One reviewer noted, *"The dough went through like butter — no sticking, no tearing, even on the thinnest setting."*

The cutting heads are where budget sets usually fail. The teeth on this one are sharp enough to produce clean, uniform strands without dragging or crushing the dough. Multiple reviewers mentioned the fettuccine cutter specifically, calling it "restaurant-quality" and "perfectly even."

But there's a catch you need to know before buying: this attachment demands drier dough than you might be used to. If your dough has even slight stickiness, it will gum up the rollers and require disassembly to clean. This isn't a flaw unique to this set — it's true of all KitchenAid pasta attachments — but it's worth flagging because first-time pasta makers often skip this step and get frustrated.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 210 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **Build quality** — The metal housing doesn't flex or bend under pressure, unlike some cheaper sets that use plastic gears. One verified buyer wrote, *"I've used this weekly for six months and it still rolls like day one."*

## Where It Falls Short

The most common complaint in negative reviews is the cleaning process. Dried dough residue lodges in the cutting teeth and is genuinely difficult to remove. The included brush doesn't cut it — you'll need a wooden toothpick or pipe cleaner to fully clear the grooves. Budget 10 minutes of cleanup after each use.

Second, there's no ravioli or tagliatelle attachment in this set. If you want those shapes, you'll need to buy additional heads separately, which somewhat erodes the value proposition.

Third, a handful of reviewers reported the attachment doesn't fit *older* KitchenAid models (pre-1990s or the smaller Artisan Mini). Check your mixer's hub compatibility before ordering — the product listing says it fits "most" models, but that's doing a lot of work.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Does it fit the KitchenAid Artisan 5-quart?**
Yes, the Artisan 5-quart and 7-quart models are compatible. The attachment uses the standard front power hub. It won't fit the Mini or older models with a different hub design.

**Q: Can I use it to make lasagna sheets?**
Absolutely. Set the dial to #6 or #7 for thick lasagna sheets. You'll need to roll the dough through multiple times, gradually decreasing the thickness setting — same as any pasta machine.

**Q: How do I clean it without damaging the rollers?**
Let the dough dry completely, then brush off the loose flour and use a toothpick for stubborn bits. Never submerge the attachment in water — the metal will rust. A dry brush after each use is all you need for the rollers themselves.
