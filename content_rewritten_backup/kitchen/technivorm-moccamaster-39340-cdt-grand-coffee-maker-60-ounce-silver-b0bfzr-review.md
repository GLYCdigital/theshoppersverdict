**The Moccamaster 39340 CDT Grand is the coffee maker for people who've stopped believing a $50 drip machine can do the job.** It's a Dutch-built, hand-assembled, SCA-certified behemoth that brews a legitimate 60 ounces — not the watered-down "12-cup" nonsense most makers claim. But at this price, you're paying for precision, not convenience. Here's what that actually means.

## What You Get

The box contains the brewer itself, a 60-ounce glass carafe, a brew basket with a permanent cone filter, and the manual. That's it. No scoop, no charcoal filter, no starter pack of beans. You'll need #4 cone filters (Melitta or Filtropa work best) and a decent burr grinder to get the most out of this machine.

The build is unmistakably premium. The housing is thick ABS plastic that feels like it'll survive a kitchen remodel. The copper boiling element is visible through the base — a nice nod to the engineering inside. The whole thing weighs about 8 pounds and takes up roughly 13 inches of counter depth. It's not small, but it's not a counter hog either.

## Performance: Where It Justifies the Price

The Moccamaster's party trick is temperature stability. The copper element heats water to 196–205°F and holds it there through the entire brew. That's the SCA golden window, and it's why the coffee tastes noticeably cleaner and more extracted than what you get from a standard drip machine.

Brew time for a full 60-ounce pot: 6 minutes flat. That's fast — the SCA certifies anything under 8 minutes. The 9-hole spray arm distributes water evenly over the grounds, and the brew basket is wide and shallow, which promotes even saturation. If you're using a medium-fine grind and 75–80 grams of coffee (about 1:16 ratio), you'll get a rich, balanced pot that genuinely rivals pour-over.

The hot plate keeps the carafe at 175–185°F. It works, but it's not a thermal carafe. Coffee left for 45+ minutes will develop that bitter, scorched edge. That's the tradeoff for the glass carafe's clarity in taste.

## Where It Falls Short

The Moccamaster is not a set-it-and-forget-it appliance. The biggest complaints:

- **No auto-off.** The switch is manual. If you brew and walk away, the hot plate stays on indefinitely. That's a safety and energy concern, and it's a legitimate oversight in 2026.
- **The glass carafe is fragile.** It's thin and the pour spout chips easily. Replacement carafes run $35–45, which stings on top of the initial price. The thermal carafe version (the 53943) costs more but eliminates the scorching issue.
- **No strength control.** You can't adjust brew strength via a dial. You're locked into the 1:16-ish ratio unless you change your grind or dose. For a machine at this price, that's a notable omission.
- **Brew basket lid doesn't lock.** You can knock it off when pouring water through the top. Minor, but annoying when you're half-awake.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The batch-brewer who drinks a full pot daily** — the 60-ounce capacity is perfect for households that go through 8–10 cups before noon.
- **The coffee snob tired of pour-over labor** — if you want café-level extraction without standing over a gooseneck kettle, this is your machine.
- **The "buy it for life" shopper** — 5-year warranty, replaceable parts, and a proven 20-year design lifespan.

**Skip it if** you need programmability (auto-brew timers are a hard no here), you prefer thermal carafes, or you're happy with a $100 drip machine and don't notice the difference.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Moccamaster 39340 CDT come with a thermal carafe?**
No. The CDT designation means glass carafe. For the thermal version, you'd need the 53943 model, which typically costs $30–50 more.

**Q: Can I brew less than a full pot?**
Yes, but it's not ideal. The machine performs best with at least 5 cups (30 ounces). Below that, the spray arm doesn't saturate the grounds evenly, and you'll get weak extraction. For single cups, use a pour-over.

**Q: Is the Moccamaster worth the price compared to a $150 drip machine?**
If you're making multiple cups daily and care about extraction temperature, yes. The difference is measurable — consistent 200°F water versus the 180–190°F most machines deliver. If you drink one cup a day, spend the money on a good grinder instead.
