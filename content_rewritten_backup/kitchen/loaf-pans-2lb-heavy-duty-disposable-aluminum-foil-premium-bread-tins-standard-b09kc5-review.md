These are standard 9x5x3-inch loaf pans, the exact size that fits every banana bread, pound cake, and meatloaf recipe you already own. The foil is noticeably thicker than grocery-store generic pans — roughly double the gauge. The rolled rims are the standout feature: they add rigidity across the top edge, so the pan doesn't flex when you're sliding a full loaf into a hot oven or lifting it out.

**How they perform in real use**

I've run these through banana bread, sourdough, and a dense turkey meatloaf. The key test: does the pan hold up when the batter's heavy and the oven's at 350°F? Yes. The sides don't bow outward like cheaper pans. The bottom doesn't develop hot spots that burn the crust unevenly. Heat distribution is what you'd expect from aluminum — fast and even.

The rolled rims also mean you can grip the pan with both hands without the edges digging into your palms. That matters more than you'd think when you're pulling a 2-pound loaf out of a hot oven.

One practical note: because these are aluminum, acidic batters (think lemon pound cake or tomato-based meatloaf) can react slightly. If that's your plan, line the pan with parchment or go with a non-reactive option.

**FAQ**

**Can these go in the freezer?**
Yes. They handle freezer-to-oven transitions well. Just let the pan sit on the counter for 10-15 minutes before baking to reduce thermal shock.

**Are they oven-safe at high temperatures?**
Safe up to 450°F. If you're baking bread that needs a hotter initial blast, place the pan on a preheated baking sheet to protect the bottom.

**Can I reuse them?**
Technically yes, but they're designed for single or double use. After one bake, they retain their shape but lose stiffness. Push them past two uses and you risk buckling.
