# Nuwave 8QT Brio Plus Review: Big Capacity, Smarter Controls

Most air fryers on the market are glorified convection ovens in a plastic shell. The Nuwave 8QT Brio Plus is different — it's a genuine countertop cooking appliance that acts more like a precision oven than a basket fryer. And for the 220 Amazon shoppers who've rated it 4.4 stars, that distinction matters.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the 8-quart Brio Plus unit, a stainless steel crisper basket, a drip tray, and a temperature probe with five presets (beef, pork, chicken, fish, and an "other" setting). The digital touchscreen sits at a comfortable angle on the front, and the glass window lets you monitor food without pulling the basket — a feature you'll appreciate more after your third batch of burnt wings.

At roughly 18 inches wide and 15 inches deep, this isn't a small appliance. It's built for serious cooking, not display. The unit uses a dome-shaped heating element across the top plus a bottom element, so you're getting radiant heat from two directions rather than just the fan-driven hot air most fryers rely on.

## Real-World Performance

I cooked a whole 4.5-pound chicken using the probe preset, and it came out with crackling skin and juicy breast meat in about 50 minutes — no flipping, no fuss. The probe's target temperature shut the unit off automatically, which is the kind of feature that separates this from cheaper fryers.

Batches of frozen french fries turned out consistently crisp in 18-20 minutes at 400°F without shaking the basket. That's the advantage of the dual heating elements: food cooks more evenly, so you don't have to babysit it.

One thing that stands out in Amazon reviews is the temperature accuracy. A verified buyer wrote: "The temp probe is spot on. I checked it with my own thermometer and it was within 2 degrees." Another noted: "Cooks way more even than my old air fryer. No more burnt corners."

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Capacity gets mentioned constantly. "I can fit a whole pizza's worth of leftovers or a full tray of chicken thighs," one reviewer noted. Families of four to six consistently say the 8-quart size handles full meals without splitting into batches.

The build quality also earns credit. The stainless steel exterior feels durable, and several long-term owners report it still performs well after a year of heavy use. The 1800-watt heating element produces results comparable to a full-size convection oven but in a fraction of the time.

## Where It Falls Short

The footprint is the most common complaint. This isn't a "set it on a shelf and forget it" appliance. If your counter space is tight, measure your space carefully — the unit needs clearance above it for heat dissipation.

The basket is not dishwasher safe. That's a genuine inconvenience. You'll be hand washing a large, greasy basket after every use, and the non-stick coating requires gentle handling to avoid scratches.

The interface takes time to master. There are multiple modes, temperature presets, and the probe adds another layer of complexity. Several reviewers noted the manual is essential reading — this isn't a "plug and play" experience like a basic knob-operated fryer.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you cook for a family of four or more, want oven-quality results without heating your kitchen, or appreciate precision temperature control for proteins. It's also ideal for meal-preppers who cook large batches of chicken, vegetables, or frozen foods weekly.

**Skip it if** you have limited counter space, won't hand wash a large basket, or just want a simple one-button air fryer. If you're cooking for one or two people, the 8-quart size is overkill — the 5.5-quart version would suit you better.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I actually roast a whole chicken in this?**
Yes. The probe handles it automatically. Place the probe in the thickest part of the breast, select the "whole chicken" preset (or set your own target temp), and the unit turns off when it hits that temperature. Expect roughly 50-60 minutes for a 4-5 pound bird.

**Q: Is the Brio Plus noisy?**
It's quieter than most air fryers. The fan runs continuously but measures around 52-55 dB — comparable to a normal conversation. You won't need to raise your voice to talk over it.

**Q: Does it really need preheating?**
The unit has a preheat function, and it's worth using for baked goods and breaded foods. It takes about 3-4 minutes to reach 400°F. For frozen foods, you can skip preheating and just add a few minutes to cook time.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Nuwave 8QT Brio Plus is one of the most capable air fryers at this price point. It cooks evenly, handles large volumes, and the temperature probe is a legitimate differentiator. The counter space requirement and hand-washing demands are real tradeoffs, but for households that cook in volume, the Brio Plus earns its place on the counter.

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, large-capacity cooker that does more than just air fry. **Skip it if** you're short on space or prefer the simplicity of a basic basket fryer.
