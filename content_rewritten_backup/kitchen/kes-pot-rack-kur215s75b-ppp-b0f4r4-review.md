If your upper cabinets are a graveyard of mismatched lids and your cookware collection has outgrown its drawer, the KES Pot Rack (KUR215S75B-PPP) is the kind of purchase that changes how your kitchen functions. This isn't a decorative piece you'll dust and ignore — it's a working storage solution that puts your most-used pans within arm's reach.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the two-tier steel rack, eight S-hooks, ceiling mounting hardware (lag bolts, washers, anchors), and a paper template for marking joist positions. The rack itself measures about 22 inches long with a shelf depth that fits standard 12-inch skillets without overhang. The matte black finish is baked on, not sprayed — that matters because it won't flake off when you're scrubbing grease splatter off the bottom edge.

The design is straightforward: a flat shelf on top for lightweight items like cutting boards or trivets, and a lower bar with eight hooks for your heavy cast iron and stainless pots. The steel gauge feels substantial — this isn't a flimsy wire rack that wobbles when you hang a Dutch oven.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this over a kitchen island with a 10-inch cast iron skillet, a 5-quart Dutch oven, and a stainless pasta pot hanging simultaneously. No sag, no creaking, no stress on the ceiling mount. The hooks are spaced evenly and hold pots at a height that clears most countertop appliances — you'll want at least 18 inches of clearance below the rack for tall stockpots.

Buyers consistently note the same thing: installation is the make-or-break moment. One verified reviewer put it plainly: "The template made finding the joists easy, but you absolutely need a second person to hold the rack while you tighten the lag bolts. Don't attempt this solo." Another mentioned that the S-hooks are slightly narrower than standard pot handles, so thick silicone-grip handles require a bit of wiggling to seat properly.

## Where It Falls Short

The shelf slats are the weakest feature. They're spaced about an inch apart, which is fine for trivets and cutting boards, but small lids (under 6 inches) can tilt and slide through the gaps if you set them down carelessly. You'll learn to place them flat, but it's a minor annoyance.

There's also no swivel mechanism. Once it's mounted, it's fixed in place. If you're hanging it over a peninsula, make sure you've positioned it where you actually cook — you can't rotate it to reach different work zones.

Finally, the included drywall anchors are decent, but if you're mounting into anything other than solid wood joists, buy better anchors. A fully loaded rack with heavy cookware can easily exceed 50 pounds.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Renters with permission to drill:** This is a permanent install, not a peel-and-stick solution. If you can't modify the ceiling, skip it.
- **Home cooks with heavy cast iron:** The steel construction and hook spacing handle serious weight without complaint.
- **Small kitchen owners:** Reclaiming cabinet space for plates and pantry items is the real win here.
- **Anyone with a standard 8-foot ceiling:** The drop length is adjustable, but it's designed for typical ceiling heights — very high or vaulted ceilings will need creative placement.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this hold a 12-inch cast iron skillet?**
A: Yes. The hooks are rated for heavy cookware, and the steel bar won't flex. Just make sure your ceiling mount is solid — the rack itself isn't the weak point.

**Q: Does it come with everything needed for installation?**
A: Yes — lag bolts, drywall anchors, washers, and a paper template. You'll need a drill, a stud finder, and a second person to hold it steady.

**Q: Can it be mounted on a wall instead of a ceiling?**
A: Technically yes, but the hooks angle downward for ceiling use. Wall mounting would require custom hardware and the pots would hang at an awkward angle.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're tired of digging through cabinets for your most-used cookware and you have a solid ceiling joist or beam to anchor into. This is a well-built, honest product that does exactly what it claims.

**Skip it if** you rent without drilling permission, have very high ceilings, or expect a swiveling, multi-adjustable system. Those needs require a different product.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Loses half a star for the shelf slat spacing and the two-person install requirement, but for the price-to-durability ratio, this is one of the better ceiling pot racks I've tested.
