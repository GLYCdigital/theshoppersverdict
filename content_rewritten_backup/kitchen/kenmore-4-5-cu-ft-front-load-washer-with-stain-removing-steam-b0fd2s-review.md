If you're staring down a pile of grass-stained kids' jeans or a silk blouse with a wine spill from last weekend, the Kenmore 4.5 cu. ft. Front Load Washer with Stain Removing Steam makes a compelling case for itself. But before you commit to this machine, let's talk about what it actually does — and where it gets lazy.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box delivers the washer itself, a hot/cold water inlet hose pair, and a user manual that reads like it was translated from another language. No pedestal, no extra hoses, no magnetic lint trap. The unit measures roughly 27" wide, 38.5" tall, and 30" deep, so measure your laundry room before ordering. The stainless steel drum is the real draw here — it's scratch-resistant and won't snag delicates the way some cheaper porcelain drums will.

The control panel keeps things straightforward: a dial for cycle selection, digital display for time remaining, and buttons for temperature, spin speed, and options like Extra Rinse and Steam. The steam function is the headline feature, and it's not just marketing — it injects hot water vapor directly into the drum during the wash phase to break down protein-based stains.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Drags

I ran this through a week of real-world testing. The normal cycle handles a mixed load of cottons and synthetics in about 48 minutes — acceptable, though not class-leading. The real standout is the Stain Removing Steam cycle. I threw in a white shirt with a dried olive oil stain and a pair of khakis with grass marks. Both came out clean without pretreating, which genuinely impressed me. The steam cycle does its job, but here's the catch: it runs 75–80 minutes. You're not using this for your daily laundry — it's a weekly deep-clean tool.

Spin speed tops out at 1400 RPM, which is respectable for this price point. Clothes come out damp but not soaking, and my dryer time dropped from about 55 minutes to 35 on a standard load. That's a real energy savings over a year.

## What Owners Are Saying

The Amazon reviews at 4.3 stars out of 215 ratings tell a mostly positive story. One verified buyer wrote, "The steam cycle is the only reason I bought this, and it hasn't disappointed. My husband's work shirts look brand new." Another echoed the drum capacity: "I washed my king-size comforter and it fit without cramming — that never happened with my old top-loader."

But the critiques are consistent too. A common complaint: "The fabric softener dispenser is a joke. It just dumps it in with the rinse water and you still get residue spots." Multiple reviewers also flagged the door seal — one noted, "If you don't dry the rubber gasket after every use, you'll have mold within a month." That's a maintenance reality with any front-loader, not just this one, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

## Where It Falls Short

The fabric softener situation is genuinely annoying. There's no dedicated drawer — you pour it into a small cup inside the drum, and it gets dispensed during the rinse. If you use softener regularly, expect occasional spotting on dark clothes. Also, the steam cycle's runtime is a commitment, and the machine hums noticeably during the steam phase — not loud enough to wake a sleeping baby, but you'll hear it from the next room.

No Wi-Fi connectivity, no app control. If you want to start a load from your phone, look elsewhere. This is a straightforward machine for people who want clean clothes, not a smart-home ecosystem.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a family with active kids, you deal with stubborn stains regularly, or you wash bulky items like comforters and need the 4.5 cu. ft. capacity. It's also a solid pick for anyone replacing a 10-year-old top-loader who wants a step up in cleaning power without paying flagship prices.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight schedule and can't spare 80 minutes for a deep-clean cycle, you hate maintaining door gaskets, or you want smart features like app control and remote start. Also skip if you use fabric softener heavily — the dispenser design will frustrate you.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the steam cycle actually remove set-in stains?**
A: It handles most protein and oil-based stains well — think blood, grass, grease, wine. Dried-on mud or paint needs pretreating. Don't expect miracles on stains that have been through a dryer cycle already.

**Q: How noisy is it during the spin cycle?**
A: It's quieter than most top-loaders. At 1400 RPM, you'll hear a low hum, but it won't rattle the floor. If you have it on a second floor, the included anti-vibration pads help, but they're not a cure-all.

**Q: Can I stack a dryer on top of this?**
A: Yes, but you need the Kenmore stacking kit (sold separately). The washer is 38.5" tall, so measure your ceiling height first.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Kenmore 4.5 cu. ft. Front Load Washer delivers where it counts: cleaning performance, especially with the steam cycle. The capacity is generous, the spin speed saves energy, and the build quality feels solid for the price. But the fabric softener dispenser flaw and the maintenance demands of the door gasket are real downsides you'll live with.

**Buy it if** you need serious stain-fighting power and don't mind the extra time and upkeep. **Skip it if** you want a low-maintenance, feature-packed smart washer. This is for people who prioritize clean clothes over convenience — and on that front, it delivers.
