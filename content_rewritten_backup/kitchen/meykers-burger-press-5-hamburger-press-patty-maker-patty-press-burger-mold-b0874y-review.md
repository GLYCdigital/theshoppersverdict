If you've ever tried to hand-shape burger patties, you know the drill: uneven thickness, cracked edges, and one patty that's twice the size of the others. The Meykers 5-inch Burger Press solves that specific frustration with a simple spring-loaded design that cranks out uniform 1/3-pound patties in about five seconds each. At 4.7 stars across 235 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in home kitchens — but it's not without tradeoffs. Here's the full picture.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the press itself (base, plunger, and spring-loaded ejector mechanism), plus 100 precut parchment paper rounds. The press stands about 5 inches tall with a 5-inch diameter forming ring. It's made from food-grade plastic with a non-stick surface on both the base and the pressing plate. You won't find any metal components touching your food — this is a plastic-only tool, which matters if you're particular about materials.

The parchment rounds are a genuinely useful inclusion. They're cut to match the press diameter exactly, so you can stack finished patties with paper between them and freeze them without them fusing into a single block of meat.

## Performance in Real Use

The process is straightforward: place a ball of ground meat (about 1/3 pound) in the base, press down, and the ejector pops the formed patty out. The spring-loaded mechanism does the work — you're not fighting to pry the patty loose. One buyer put it simply: "It's so easy my 10-year-old makes the burgers now." Another noted the consistency payoff: "Every patty is exactly the same thickness, so they all finish cooking at the same time."

The non-stick surface works as advertised, even with lean ground turkey or chicken, which typically sticks to lesser presses. The 5-inch diameter means the patties fit standard hamburger buns perfectly with a slight overhang — exactly what you want for a proper burger.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, the uniform thickness — home cooks repeatedly mention that even cooking is the biggest upgrade over hand-formed patties. Second, the cleanup: the non-stick surface rinses clean in seconds, and a dishwasher cycle handles deeper cleaning. Third, the paper rounds: "I made 12 patties, froze them all, and they didn't stick together at all," reported one verified buyer.

## Where It Falls Short

The plastic construction is the main compromise. It feels lighter and less substantial than cast-iron presses like the Weston or Norpro models. For a home cook making 4–8 patties at a time, it's fine. If you're meal-prepping 20 patties weekly or running a food truck, the plastic will wear faster and the handle may eventually feel flimsy. A few reviewers mentioned that aggressive pressing can cause the base to shift on the counter — a rubber non-slip ring on the bottom would have helped.

The fixed 5-inch size is also limiting. You can't make sliders or thick pub-style half-pounders with this unit. It does one size, and it does it well, but it's a one-trick pony.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Weekend grillers** who want consistent patties without the mess of hand-forming
- **Meal preppers** making burgers in batches for the freezer — the included paper rounds are a real convenience
- **Families with kids** who want a safe, simple tool that's easy for little hands to operate
- **Anyone upgrading from flimsy single-piece molds** that require greasing and wrestling to release the patty

Skip it if you need commercial-grade durability, want multiple patty sizes from one tool, or prefer metal construction that can go directly on a grill or griddle.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with frozen meat?**  
No. Thaw the meat first. The press works best with cold (not frozen) ground meat that's been portioned into loose balls. Frozen meat will damage the plastic mechanism.

**Q: Does it really make 1/3-pound patties?**  
Yes, with a packed 1/3 pound of meat. If you use less, you'll get thinner patties. If you use more, the excess spills over the edges. A kitchen scale helps for precision.

**Q: Is it dishwasher safe?**  
The manufacturer says yes, but hand washing is gentler on the plastic and the spring mechanism. Most reviewers hand-wash to extend the life of the ejector.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want quick, consistent, restaurant-style patties at home and don't mind a plastic build. The included paper rounds and easy cleanup make this a genuinely useful addition to burger night.

**Skip it if:** You need a heavy-duty metal press, want multiple patty sizes, or plan to use it daily for years on end.
