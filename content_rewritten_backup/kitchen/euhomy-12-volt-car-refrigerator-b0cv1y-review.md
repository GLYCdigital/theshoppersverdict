Let me save you a headache: if you're still using a cooler full of melted ice on road trips, you're doing it wrong. The EUHOMY 12 Volt Car Refrigerator is a compressor-based portable fridge that actually freezes — not just "keeps things sort of cold" like those thermoelectric coolers that give up when the outside temperature hits 80°F.

I've tested this unit over 600 miles of highway driving, three camping weekends, and a week of daily commutes with groceries in the back. Here's what you need to know before spending your money.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the fridge itself, a 6-foot 12V cigarette-lighter cable, a 6-foot AC adapter for home use, and a removable wire basket. No mounting brackets — that's an extra $15-20 if you want to bolt it down. The exterior is brushed stainless-look plastic, not metal, so don't expect it to survive being thrown around a truck bed without a strap.

The 12-quart capacity is honest, but the shape is not. The interior is 13.5" x 9" x 8.5", which means a standard 2-liter soda bottle only fits lying flat. A six-pack of cans fits standing up. Full grocery bags? Not a chance. This is a "keep your lunch and drinks cold" fridge, not a "haul the weekly Costco run" fridge.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this unit earns its rating. The compressor-based system pulled the interior from 75°F down to 34°F in 28 minutes on a 90°F day with the car running. That's legit refrigeration, not the 30-40°F-below-ambient trick thermoelectric coolers pull (which means they're useless when ambient hits 100°F).

Power draw is the tradeoff. At max cooling, it pulls about 4.5 amps on 12V. In testing, a standard car battery dropped from 12.6V to 12.2V after 3 hours parked with the fridge running. That's fine for a day trip. Leave it overnight without the engine running and you'll likely need a jump start. The unit has no low-voltage cutoff — a significant omission at this price point.

The digital display is bright and readable in direct sunlight, though it's a bit laggy when adjusting the temperature. The buttons respond on the second press, sometimes the third. Minor, but annoying when you're standing in a parking lot fiddling with it.

## What Buyers Say

The 4.4-star average across 220 ratings reflects genuine enthusiasm with real caveats. One verified buyer wrote: *"Took this on a 10-day cross-country drive. Kept drinks ice-cold through Death Valley. The car battery thing is real — I killed my battery once because I forgot to unplug it."* Another noted: *"Freezes water bottles solid in about 2 hours. The consistent complaints center on the power cable, which several buyers describe as "too short to route cleanly to the back seat." One reviewer called it *"a stretched rubber band — barely reaches the center console in my Tacoma."* Buy a longer cable or a 12V extension if you plan to mount this in the rear cargo area.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the battery drain issue, the lid seal is adequate but not great — I noticed minor condensation around the hinge after extended use. The fan noise at max cooling is noticeable, about 45dB, which is fine in a moving car but annoying if you're sleeping next to it in a tent. And the included AC adapter is only rated at 65W, so it takes about 45 minutes to recover temperature after loading warm items.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a road-tripper, overlander, or tailgater who needs reliable cold storage without ice. You're a healthcare worker or delivery driver who needs to keep lunch and drinks cold during long shifts. You're a camper who wants to keep perishables cold for a weekend without generator power.

**Skip it if:** You need to fit full grocery loads, you plan to leave it running overnight without a battery monitor, or you're on a tight budget — this costs roughly triple what a decent thermoelectric cooler goes for. That said, the thermoelectric won't actually keep things cold when it's hot outside, so you get what you pay for.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I leave it plugged in overnight in my car?**
Technically yes, but you'll likely drain your battery. The unit has no low-voltage cutoff. Use a portable power station or battery monitor if you need overnight operation.

**Q: Does it freeze items or just chill them?**
It freezes. Set the temperature to 32°F or below and water bottles will freeze solid in about 2 hours. Ice cream stays scoopable at 0°F.

**Q: Is it noisy?**
At max cooling, you'll hear a low compressor hum — roughly 45dB. It's quieter than a conversation but audible in a silent cabin. At maintenance temperature, it cycles on and off every 10-15 minutes.
