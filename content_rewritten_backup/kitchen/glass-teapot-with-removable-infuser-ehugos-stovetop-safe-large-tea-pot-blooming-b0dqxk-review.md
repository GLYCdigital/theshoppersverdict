Here's the thing about glass teapots: most of them are decorative lies. They look stunning on a shelf but crack the moment you put them near actual heat, or they dribble tea down your counter with every pour. The Ehugos stovetop-safe glass teapot tries to be the exception. After digging through 225 ratings and the specs, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get in the box**

The Ehugos arrives with the 1000ml (about 34 oz) teapot, a stainless steel infuser basket, and a glass lid with a swing mechanism. That's it — no warming stand, no extra mesh filters, no trivet. The borosilicate glass body is the star here. It's the same material lab beakers use, which means it handles temperature swings that would shatter cheap soda-lime glass. The infuser is 18/8 stainless steel with a fine mesh — not the coarse wire strainer you see on cheaper pots.

**How it actually performs**

Let's talk about the stovetop claim, because that's the reason you're looking at this pot. Yes, it works on gas, electric, and ceramic cooktops. You put cold water in, set it on medium heat, and it comes to a boil in about 6-7 minutes. The borosilicate glass distributes heat evenly without hot spots that cause cracking. Just don't blast it on high — thermal shock is still a thing, and the manufacturer warns against it in the manual.

The infuser is where this pot earns its keep. The fine mesh catches everything from chamomile flowers to broken Assam leaves. You steep your tea, lift the basket out, and the brew stops immediately — no bitter over-extraction. The mesh is tight enough that you won't be fishing leaves out of your cup. For blooming teas, the 1000ml capacity gives the flowers room to unfurl properly. As shown in the product image, the glass clarity lets you watch the whole show.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The leaktight lid design gets repeated mentions. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've had tea pots dribble down the side for years. This one pours clean — the lid locks in place and doesn't pop off even when I'm pouring aggressively."* Another reviewer highlighted the durability: *"I've used mine on a gas stove every morning for three months. No cracks, no clouding, no metal taste leaching into the tea."*

**Where it genuinely falls short**

The heat retention is a double-edged sword. Borosilicate glass holds heat beautifully — which means the exterior stays dangerously hot for 15-20 minutes after you turn off the burner. There's no cool-touch handle wrap or silicone grip. You'll need a mitt every single time you pour. That's a design oversight for a pot marketed as stovetop-safe.

The infuser basket is also shallow. If you're brewing voluminous teas like rolled oolong or chamomile that expand significantly, the leaves can push the infuser lid open mid-steep. It doesn't break anything, but you'll find stray leaves floating in your brew. Stick to flat or lightly rolled teas and you'll be fine.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the loose-leaf drinker who's tired of plastic infusers and wants to watch their tea bloom. It's also solid for anyone who wants a single vessel that goes from stovetop to table without transferring to a separate server. If you're a tea-bag-in-a-mug person, this is overkill — you don't need a stovetop-safe pot for bagged tea.

**FAQ**

**Can I use this on an induction cooktop?** No. The base is glass, not magnetic. Gas, electric coil, and ceramic/glass cooktops work fine. Induction will not.

**Does the glass stain from black tea?** Borosilicate glass is non-porous, so tannins rinse off with warm soapy water. If you let it sit overnight, you might see a faint film, but it comes off with a baking soda paste.

**Is the infuser dishwasher safe?** Yes, both the infuser and the glass body are dishwasher safe. Hand washing is gentler on the glass, but it won't shatter in the dishwasher — I've seen zero complaints about that.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if:** You brew loose-leaf tea regularly, want a stovetop-to-table pot that doesn't require a separate kettle, and you appreciate fine mesh straining that actually catches small leaves.

**Skip it if:** You have an induction cooktop, you're clumsy with hot glass, or you primarily brew high-expansion teas that'll overflow the shallow infuser.

The Ehugos glass teapot delivers on its core promise — genuine stovetop safety in a beautiful, clear vessel with a genuinely leak-proof lid. The lack of a cool-touch handle is a real annoyance, but it's the kind of tradeoff you accept for glass that won't crack. At 4.5 stars from 225 buyers, this is a solid 4.6 in my book — a functional piece of tea gear that does what it claims, with one glaring ergonomic miss that you can work around with a simple oven mitt.
