Let's be direct: if you're shopping for a coffee grinder under $25, you're not looking for barista-grade precision. You want ground coffee that doesn't taste like the bag you bought three weeks ago. The Mr. Coffee Automatic Coffee Grinder nails that basic job — and it's been doing it for decades in one form or another.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the grinder unit, a clear plastic lid with a built-in measuring cup (holds about 12 tablespoons), and a stainless steel blade assembly. The grinding chamber holds enough beans for roughly 12 cups of coffee, which matches the included measuring markings. There are no bells, whistles, or complicated settings — just a single button on the front that activates the blades for as long as you hold it down.

**How it performs in the real world**

The grinder uses a classic blade design with a 100-watt motor. For drip coffee — which is what most people buying this machine own — it produces a perfectly acceptable medium grind in about 10-15 seconds. The motor runs at a consistent speed, and the lid's shape creates a small vortex that keeps beans circulating toward the blades. In practice, this means you don't have to shake the unit mid-grind like you do with cheaper models.

One thing I appreciate: the cord is 18 inches long, which gives you some flexibility in placement. And the whole unit weighs just over a pound, so it's easy to tuck away in a cabinet when you're not using it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The reviews tell a consistent story. One verified buyer wrote, *"Simple, works exactly as described, and doesn't take up half my counter. I've had mine for six months and it grinds the same as day one."* Another noted, *"I use it every morning for my drip brewer and it's never let me down. Just hold the button, shake it a couple times, and you're done."* The overwhelming sentiment is reliability — this is a no-surprises appliance that does one thing well.

**Where it falls short**

Here's the honest part. This is a blade grinder, not a burr grinder, and the difference matters if you're picky about your coffee. Blade grinders chop beans unevenly, meaning you'll get a mix of fine powder and coarser chunks. For drip coffee, this is barely noticeable. For French press or pour-over, it's a real problem — fine particles will slip through your filter and leave sediment in your cup.

There's also no grind-size dial. You control coarseness purely by timing. Want a coarser grind? Hold the button for 5 seconds instead of 10. This works but takes practice, and you'll waste a few batches of beans learning the timing. The plastic housing also slides a bit on smooth granite or quartz counters while grinding — I'd recommend holding it steady with your free hand.

**Who should buy this**

This grinder is for you if:
- You own a drip coffee maker or a basic single-serve brewer
- You want freshly ground coffee without spending $50+ on a burr grinder
- You value counter space and simplicity over precision

Skip it if:
- You're a French press, pour-over, or espresso enthusiast who needs consistent particle size
- You grind more than 12 cups of beans at once (you'll need to work in batches)
- You want adjustable grind settings

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I grind spices in this?**
A: Technically yes, but I wouldn't recommend it. Coffee oils cling to the plastic chamber and lid, and they'll flavor whatever you grind next. If you want a dedicated spice grinder, buy a second unit — they're cheap enough.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
A: The chamber and lid are dishwasher-safe. For a deeper clean, wipe the blade assembly with a damp cloth — don't submerge the base. A quick dry run with a few grains of uncooked rice also helps remove coffee oils.

**Q: How long should I grind for drip coffee?**
A: Start with 10 seconds. If the grind looks too fine (powdery), reduce to 8. If it looks too coarse (chunky), bump to 12-15. Adjust from there based on how your coffee tastes.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want freshly ground coffee for a standard drip maker without spending more than the cost of a bag of good beans. It's a reliable, compact, no-nonsense tool that gets the job done.

**Skip it if:** You're serious about pour-over or espresso and need uniform grind sizes. Save your money for a burr grinder — the Mr. Coffee won't satisfy you.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's not fancy, but it's honest. For the price, this is the right tool for the casual coffee drinker who just wants a fresher cup.
