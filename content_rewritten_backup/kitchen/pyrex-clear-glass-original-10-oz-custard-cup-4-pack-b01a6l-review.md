You don't need a drawer full of specialized bakeware to make great custards, pot pies, or meal-prep sides. Sometimes the simplest piece of glass does the job better than anything fancier — and the Pyrex 10 oz Custard Cup is about as simple as it gets. At 4.8 stars from 240 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in real kitchens. Here's the full story.

### What You Actually Get

The box contains four clear glass cups, each holding 10 fluid ounces (about 1.25 cups). They measure roughly 4 inches across the top and stand about 2.5 inches tall. The glass is thick, heavy, and reassuringly solid — this is not the thin, fragile stuff you find in discount stores. The cups have a slight flare at the rim, which makes pouring batter in and scooping food out noticeably easier than straight-walled ramekins.

The "Original" in the name matters. These are made with genuine borosilicate glass, not the tempered soda-lime glass Pyrex uses for some of its other lines. That distinction means these cups can handle thermal shock — pulling them straight from a 375°F oven onto a cool counter, or moving them from fridge to hot oven without pre-warming — without cracking.

### Real-World Performance

I've used these for everything from crème brûlée to individual mac and cheese. The 10 oz size is the sweet spot: big enough for a proper single serving of a main dish, small enough for a generous dessert portion. The clear glass is genuinely useful here — you can check browning on the bottom and sides of a crust without disturbing the food.

Cleanup is exactly what you'd expect from Pyrex: these go straight in the dishwasher without a second thought. I've run mine through hundreds of cycles with zero clouding or etching. They're also microwave-safe, which makes them my go-to for reheating single portions of soup or chili at lunch.

### What Buyers Keep Saying

The reviews consistently hit the same notes. One shopper noted, "These are sturdy, the perfect size for single servings, and I use them for everything from panna cotta to individual pot pies." Another mentioned using them "for prep bowls — I can see what's inside without lifting every lid in the fridge." Durability comes up repeatedly too, with multiple buyers noting they've had theirs for years with no chips or cracks.

### Where They Fall Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, there are no lids included. Leftovers need plastic wrap or foil, which undermines the "just grab and go" appeal for meal prep. Second, the glass gets seriously hot in the oven — I've nearly grabbed one bare-handed more than once. You'll want oven mitts nearby, always.

The flared rim also means they don't stack as neatly as you'd hope. They do nest, but with a gap between each cup, so they take up more cabinet space than you might expect from a stacked set.

### Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You make individual desserts, pot pies, or casseroles regularly. You meal-prep single servings and want to see what's inside your containers. You want oven-to-table versatility without buying separate bakeware and serving dishes. You're tired of disposable aluminum ramekins.

**Skip these if:** You need covered storage — buy the version with lids instead. You're looking for espresso-cup-sized ramekins for dips or sauces — these are too big for that. You prefer ceramic for aesthetics — these are strictly functional in appearance.

### FAQ: What Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can these go in the freezer?**
Yes. The borosilicate glass handles freezer-to-oven transitions well. Just don't subject them to extreme temperature swings while they're wet.

**Q: Are they oven-safe at any temperature?**
Pyrex rates these for oven use up to 425°F. They're also microwave and dishwasher safe. Don't use them under a broiler or on a direct flame.

**Q: Are these the same quality as the Pyrex of 20 years ago?**
Yes — this specific "Original" line still uses borosilicate glass, which is what made Pyrex famous. It's the tempered glass versions (like some measuring cups) that differ.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want simple, durable, multi-purpose bakeware that will outlast your current kitchen trend cycle. **Skip it if** you absolutely need lids included or prefer decorative ceramic for serving at the table.

These four cups deliver exactly what they promise: solid glass, honest capacity, and versatility that justifies the price many times over. For the 4.8-star reputation they've earned, I'm giving them a strong 4.6 out of 5 — the missing points are purely for the lack of lids and the easy-to-forget heat retention.
