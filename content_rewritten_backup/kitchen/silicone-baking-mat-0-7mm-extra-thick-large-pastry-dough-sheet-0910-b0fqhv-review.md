Let me be direct: most silicone baking mats are flimsy, slide around your counter, and curl at the edges the moment you put any real pressure on them. This one isn't. The 0.7mm thickness is the entire story here, and it changes how the mat actually performs in a kitchen.

**What you're getting**

The mat arrives rolled in a sturdy cardboard tube — a small detail that tells you the manufacturer understands shipping. Unrolled, it's a generous sheet that covers a standard countertop work area. The surface has a matte, slightly textured finish that grips both the counter beneath it and the dough on top. Measurement markings run along the edges in inches and centimeters, printed in a contrasting color that's actually legible (a rarity in this category).

**Real-world performance**

I put this mat through the paces with a stiff brioche dough — the kind that fights back. That's where the thickness earns its keep. Thinner mats (typically 0.3mm–0.5mm) wrinkle and shift under heavy rolling pressure, but this one stayed flat and locked in place. The non-stick surface released the dough cleanly after chilling, requiring zero flour dusting for medium-hydration recipes.

The mat also handles heat. It's rated to 480°F, which puts it safely in range for roasting vegetables, baking cookies, or sliding under a pizza stone. One buyer noted, *"I've used it for everything from macarons to roasted salmon. Nothing sticks, and cleanup is a 10-second wipe."* That tracks with my experience — a damp cloth handles 95% of messes, and soapy water takes care of the rest.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 240 ratings (4.8 stars), three themes dominate. First, stability: *"Finally a mat that doesn't slide around on my granite countertops."* Second, the measurement guides: *"The markings are actually accurate and easy to read — I use them daily for laminating dough."* Third, durability: multiple reviewers report months of daily use without peeling, cracking, or losing the non-stick surface.

**Where it falls short**

The thickness is a double-edged sword. This mat is stiff. It doesn't roll into a tight cylinder for drawer storage — you'll need a wider tube or a flat spot to keep it. If you fold it, you'll get permanent crease lines that can telegraph onto delicate pastries like croissants. That's a real consideration if you're short on storage space.

Another minor gripe: the mat's weight makes it slightly awkward to transfer from counter to baking sheet with one hand. It's not heavy — just less floppy than what most people are used to.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home bakers who roll dough regularly** — the stability and accurate markings justify the price alone.
- **Anyone who's ruined a thin mat within six months** — the extra thickness directly addresses the most common failure mode.
- **Bread and pastry enthusiasts** who need a large, reliable work surface.

**Skip it if** you need a mat for small half-sheet pans only, or if your storage situation demands a mat that rolls up tightly.

**FAQ**

**Is it truly non-stick without flour?**
For most recipes, yes. High-hydration doughs (75%+ hydration) may still benefit from a light dusting, but standard cookie and pastry dough releases cleanly.

**Can I cut on it with a knife?**
The manufacturer says no, and I'd agree. Use it as a rolling and baking surface, not a cutting board. Knife marks will eventually compromise the surface.

**Does it fit standard half-sheet pans?**
Yes, it's sized for full-sheet and half-sheet pans. It'll overhang a quarter-sheet, so trim if that's your primary pan.

**The verdict**
