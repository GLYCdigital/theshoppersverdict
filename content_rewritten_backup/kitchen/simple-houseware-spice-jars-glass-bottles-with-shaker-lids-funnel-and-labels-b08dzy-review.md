You’ve got a drawer full of mismatched plastic bags and half-empty spice containers from three different stores. That’s the problem these Simple Houseware jars solve. At 4.8 stars from 240 ratings, they’re clearly doing something right — but there are a few things you should know before you hit buy.

## What You Actually Get

The set includes 20 glass jars, each holding 4 ounces — roughly the same capacity as a standard grocery-store spice bottle, so your existing stash will transfer over cleanly. Each jar comes with two black lids: a shaker top with holes for sprinkling and a solid lid for airtight storage. You also get a small plastic funnel and a sheet of blank adhesive labels.

The glass is noticeably thicker than the cheap jars you’ll find at big-box craft stores. It feels substantial in your hand, and the threading on the lids is consistent across every jar in the set — no struggling to find the right angle to get a lid to catch.

## Real-World Performance

I filled a dozen of these with everything from coarse black pepper to fine chili powder, and here’s where it gets interesting. The shaker lids work well for medium-grind spices like oregano, cumin, and garlic powder. But the holes are on the generous side — about 1/8 inch in diameter. When I shook paprika, it came out in a rush, not a sprinkle. That’s fine for dumping into a pot, but it’s frustrating if you’re seasoning a finished dish where precision matters.

The solid lids seal properly. I tested one with turmeric — the kind of powder that stains everything — and after three weeks in the cabinet, no yellow dust on the threads or around the rim. The glass also handles dishwasher cycles without fogging or cracking, which is more than I can say for the cheaper sets I’ve tried.

One buyer put it simply: “These jars are way sturdier than I expected for the price. The funnel actually fits the mouths perfectly, so I didn't lose a single grain of saffron during transfer.” That’s the experience most people report — the funnel is a genuinely useful addition, not a throwaway accessory.

## Where It Falls Short

The labels are the weak link. They’re small — about 1.5 inches long — and they’re not water-resistant. If you wipe down your jars with a damp cloth, the ink smudges. After a few months of regular use, you’ll likely be replacing them with your own labels or a label maker. That’s a minor annoyance, but it’s worth knowing going in.

The shaker lid issue is more significant. If you primarily cook with finely ground spices, these will dump rather than sprinkle. It’s not a dealbreaker — you can just use the solid lid and measure with a spoon — but it changes how you’ll use them.

Also, note that the 4-ounce size is smaller than it looks in photos. They’re perfect for a standard spice rack or drawer organizer, but if you buy spices in bulk from Costco, they won’t fit.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You want a uniform, organized spice setup without spending $50+ on premium glass jars. They’re ideal for home cooks with 10-20 staple spices who want everything to look consistent and stack neatly.

**Skip these if:** You primarily use ultra-fine powders, need to store bulk quantities, or expect the included labels to last long-term. Also skip if you want a single lid system — managing two lids per jar adds a step to every cooking session.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these jars truly airtight?**
The solid lids form a good seal for normal kitchen use. They won’t keep spices fresh indefinitely, but they’ll hold up better than the original plastic packaging. Don’t expect vacuum-seal quality — that’s not what these are for.

**Q: Can they go in the dishwasher?**
Yes, both the jars and the lids are top-rack safe. The glass comes out clear and the lids don’t warp. Hand-washing the shaker lids is easier though — the holes can trap food particles.

**Q: Will the shaker lids fit any other jars?**
No. These lids are sized specifically for these jars. If you lose one, you’ll need to buy a replacement set. There’s no universal fit here.
