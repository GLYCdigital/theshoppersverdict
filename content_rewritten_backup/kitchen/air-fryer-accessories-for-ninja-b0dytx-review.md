Let’s be blunt: most air fryer accessory kits are Amazon junk — thin metal, loose fits, and liners that melt into your dinner. This set for Ninja models isn’t that. After 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it’s earned a spot in my kitchen. Here’s the real breakdown.

**What’s Actually in the Box**

You get a 7-piece kit: two round silicone liners, one square liner, a stainless steel expansion rack, four silicone skewers, and a grill rack. The liners are food-grade platinum silicone — the kind that bends without creasing. The expansion rack is the standout: it stacks two levels of food in the basket while the holes promote airflow. As shown in the product image, the rack’s raised legs lift food off the base, preventing the soggy-bottom problem that plagues single-layer air frying.

**Performance: Where It Earns Its Keep**

I ran this through a month of daily use — chicken wings, frozen fries, roasted vegetables, even a full sheet of bacon. The silicone liners are the MVP. They catch grease before it hits the Ninja basket, which means cleanup drops from scrubbing to a quick rinse. One buyer noted, “The liners make my DualZone basket look brand new after 20+ uses. No staining, no baked-on oil.” I’ll second that — my basket’s ceramic coating is still flawless.

The expansion rack genuinely works. For a family-size batch of fries, I loaded the bottom rack with a single layer and the top with another. Both crisped evenly — no raw centers, no burnt edges. The grill rack handles chicken breasts and fish fillets well, lifting them off the base so the bottom gets that sear instead of steaming.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the 225 ratings, three themes dominate: fit, non-stick performance, and durability. A verified purchaser wrote, “Fits my Ninja Foodi 6.5 qt perfectly. No rattling, no sliding around during the cook cycle.” Another said, “The liners are thick, not flimsy like other brands. I’ve used them in the basket and the baking pan — both work.” The silicone skewers also get love for kabobs — they’re flexible enough to bend into the basket without snapping.

**Where It Falls Short**

The cons are real but manageable. First, the silicone liners have a learning curve — they absorb soap residue. If you don’t rinse them thoroughly, your food tastes faintly of dish soap. Fix: hand-wash with white vinegar for the first few uses. Second, the skewers are 3mm thick — fine for shrimp and peppers, but chicken thighs will make them bow. Third, there’s no storage bag, and the metal rack can scratch the Ninja’s nonstick basket if you stack them carelessly. Buy a cheap mesh pouch.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Ninja Foodi and DualZone owners (5.5-8 qt)** — this kit is sized for your basket, not the smaller 3.7 qt models.
- **High-volume cooks** — if you air fry daily, the liners alone are worth it for cleanup time saved.
- **Meal preppers** — the expansion rack lets you cook a full pound of protein plus veggies in one cycle.

Skip it if you own a compact Ninja or prefer single-purpose tools over a kit. Also, if you’re strictly cooking delicate items like fish fillets, the grill rack’s wide gaps might let smaller pieces slip through.

**FAQ**

*Q: Are the liners safe for the Ninja’s broil function?*  
A: Yes, they’re rated to 450°F. But avoid the Max Crisp setting above that — the silicone will discolor and warp.

*Q: Will the expansion rack fit my Ninja Foodi 6.5 qt?*  
A: Yes. It’s designed for 5.5-8 qt models. For the 6.5 qt, it sits flush with about half an inch of clearance above the heating element — no contact issues.

*Q: Can I use the liners in the Ninja’s bake function?*  
A: Absolutely. They work in the basket and the baking pan. Just don’t use them with sharp utensils — they’ll puncture.
