Let’s get one thing straight: most “self-feeding” juicers are marketing fiction. You still chop carrots into inch-long batons and feed them one by one. The Hurom H310A is different — it actually lives up to the claim. Drop a whole cored apple in the wide chute, and the auger pulls it down without you hovering over the machine. That’s the headline feature, and it genuinely changes the morning routine.

**What’s in the box**  
You get the motor base, the auger, a strainer basket, the hopper with a built-in pusher, a 350ml juice cup with a lid, and a cleaning brush. No pulp container — that’s the first clue this is a personal juicer, not a family workhorse. The footprint is compact at roughly 8 x 7 x 16 inches, so it tucks under upper cabinets without dominating your counter.

**Performance and build quality**  
The 150-watt motor spins at a glacial 43 RPM. That slow speed is the secret to the H310A’s two big wins: quiet operation and high yield. At 60 decibels, it’s quieter than a normal conversation — you can juice while the kids sleep. More importantly, the masticating action crushes rather than shreds, which means leafy greens like kale and spinach yield noticeably more liquid than a centrifugal juicer. One buyer put it plainly: *“I got a full glass of celery juice from a bunch that my old Breville turned into dry pulp.”*

The self-feeding hopper is the standout. It fits whole small apples, halved oranges, and celery stalks — no pre-chopping. That’s a real time-saver, and it’s the reason most people pay the Hurom premium. The build quality matches: the body is BPA-free Tritan, the auger is solid, and the base doesn’t walk across the counter even when you’re pushing dense produce.

**Where it falls short**  
The 350ml cup is the obvious compromise. That’s about 12 ounces — one generous glass. If you’re juicing for two people, you’ll be pouring, rinsing, and repeating. Several reviewers flagged this exact issue: *“Great for a single-serving green juice, but I had to run it three times for my wife and me.”*

Cleaning is the second friction point. There are five components to disassemble, and the fine mesh strainer holds onto pulp aggressively. The included brush helps, but you’ll still spend five minutes at the sink. It’s not the worst in the category — Hurom’s parts are smoother than Omega’s — but it’s not the easy rinse-and-go you get with a centrifugal machine.

The third quirk: there’s no pulp chute. The chamber fills up, and the auger stalls out. You have to stop, open the lid, and scoop out the dry pulp every two or three batches. For a single glass, it’s fine. For continuous juicing, it’s a real interruption.

**Who should buy this**  
The H310A is for the solo juicer who prioritizes yield and quiet over speed and volume. It’s ideal if you make one daily green juice, hate pre-chopping, and live in a small kitchen where a 20-pound twin-gear machine would be overkill. It’s also a smart pick if you’re sensitive to noise — this thing purrs compared to the jet-engine whine of a centrifugal model.

Skip it if you’re juicing for a family of four, or if you want to batch-juice a week’s worth of produce at once. Look at the Hurom H400 or an Omega vertical masticator with a pulp container instead.

**FAQ**  
*Does it really juice whole apples without cutting them?*  
Yes, if the apple fits in the 43mm chute. Small-to-medium apples go in whole. Large ones need a halve. Celery, kale, and spinach go in as-is.

*Can you make nut milk or sorbet?*  
Yes. The same auger handles soaked almonds and frozen bananas, though you’ll need to alternate with some liquid to keep things flowing.

*Is the motor strong enough for hard produce like beets and carrots?*  
Yes, but cut them into halves or quarters. The 150-watt motor handles it, but pushing oversized chunks slows the auger and increases cleanup time.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a genuinely hands-free single-serving juicer with top-tier yield and whisper-quiet operation, and you don’t mind the small cup or the manual pulp removal. **Skip it if** you need to juice for more than one person at a time or want the convenience of a pulp bin.

The H310A earns **4.2 out of 5 stars**. It’s not perfect — the small capacity and tedious cleaning are real tradeoffs — but for its intended use, it’s the best personal masticating juicer I’ve tested at this price. The self-feeding feature alone justifies the upgrade over cheaper Hurom models.
