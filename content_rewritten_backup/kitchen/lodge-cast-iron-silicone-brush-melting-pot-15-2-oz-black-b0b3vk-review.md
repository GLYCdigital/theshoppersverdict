Let's be direct: most melting pots are plastic junk that scorch chocolate on setting two. The Lodge Cast Iron Silicone Brush Melting Pot is not that. This 15.2-ounce cast iron mini-pot earns its 4.8-star rating (240 ratings) by solving the two biggest problems with melting: uneven heat and messy application. But it's not for everyone.

**What you actually get**

The pot itself is pre-seasoned cast iron with a small integrated well — roughly 4.5 inches across and 2 inches deep. The attached silicone brush sits in a cradle on the side, angled so excess drips back into the pot rather than onto your counter. The handle stays cool enough to grip briefly, though I'd still recommend a mitt for extended use. Total weight is around 1.5 pounds — substantial for its size, which is exactly what you want for heat retention.

**Performance in the real world**

Where this pot shines is temperature stability. Cast iron distributes heat evenly and slowly, which matters when you're melting chocolate that seizes at anything above 115°F. On a low stovetop setting, I melted a full 12-ounce bag of dark chocolate chips in about eight minutes with zero scorching — something I've never managed in a double boiler without constant vigilance. The same goes for cream cheese and butter for cinnamon rolls; it holds a gentle melt without separating.

The silicone brush is the real differentiator. It's heat-rated to 450°F, so it won't warp or melt near the pot's surface. For glazing donuts, brushing garlic butter over bread, or painting a thin layer of chocolate onto parchment for decorations, this brush applies evenly without shedding bristles. One reviewer noted, *"The brush is firm enough to spread glaze but soft enough not to tear delicate pastries."* Another said, *"I've owned three other melting pots and this is the first one where the brush actually stays put and doesn't drip everywhere."*

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about limitations. The silicone brush is permanently attached to the pot's rim — you can't remove it for dishwasher cleaning. You'll need to hand wash carefully, and the brush head can trap chocolate in its crevices if you're not thorough. Also, the uncoated cast iron interior means you must season it like any other Lodge product, and you absolutely cannot use acidic ingredients like vinegar-based fruit glazes — they'll strip the seasoning and leave a metallic taste. Finally, 15.2 ounces is enough for one dipping session, but if you're doing multi-color chocolate work or coating a full sheet of cookies, you'll want two.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home bakers** who dip strawberries, pretzels, or make cake pops — this is your dedicated tool
- **Brunch cooks** who want melted butter or cream cheese ready to brush on pancakes, waffles, or biscuits
- **Small-batch candy makers** who need precise heat control without dragging out a double boiler

Skip it if you only melt chocolate once a year (a microwave and a piping bag will do), or if you refuse to hand-wash any kitchen tool.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this on an induction cooktop?* Yes. Cast iron is induction-compatible, and the flat bottom sits squarely on the burner.

*How do I clean the silicone brush?* Rinse immediately with warm water, then use a soft sponge with mild soap. For stubborn chocolate, soak for 10 minutes in hot water. Never use abrasive scrubbers on the cast iron.

*Will the handle get hot?* The short handle is cast iron and will conduct heat. Use a mitt or potholder for anything beyond a quick stir.

**The Verdict**

The Lodge Cast Iron Silicone Brush Melting Pot earns a 4.6/5 because it does one job exceptionally well: melt, brush, and serve without mess or scorching. It's not versatile enough to be an everyday pan, but as a specialized tool, it outperforms every plastic melting pot I've tested. 

**Buy it if** you make dipped treats or glazed baked goods more than once a month. **Skip it if** you want a multi-purpose pan or refuse to hand wash. At this price point, it's a solid investment for serious home cooks — just treat the seasoning right and it'll outlast you.
