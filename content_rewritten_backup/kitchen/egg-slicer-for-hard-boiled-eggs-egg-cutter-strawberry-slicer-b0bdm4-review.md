I've tested a lot of egg slicers that promised clean cuts and delivered mushy yolks. This one actually does what it says.

The **Egg Slicer for Hard Boiled Eggs** is a deceptively simple tool: a hinged ABS plastic base with a grid of stainless steel wires that press down to create uniform slices. What separates it from the $5 bargain-bin versions is the execution. The wires are tensioned properly — they bite through a fully cooked egg cleanly rather than squishing it flat. The frame doesn't flex or creak when you apply pressure, which is the #1 failure point on cheaper slicers.

As shown in the product image, there's also a secondary cutting grid on the base designed for strawberries and button mushrooms. I was skeptical of this combo feature, but it genuinely works. You place a hulled strawberry in the circular depression, press down, and get uniform fan slices for topping yogurt or desserts. It's not a gimmick — it's a functional second use that takes up zero extra drawer space.

**Build & Materials**

The frame is food-grade ABS that feels dense, not hollow. The wires are 18/8 stainless steel, and after two months of weekly use (roughly 40 eggs and a dozen strawberries), they show no rust, bending, or dulling. The whole thing is dishwasher-safe on the top rack, but honestly, a 5-second rinse under hot water does the job — egg residue doesn't cling to the wires or crevices. The base has four small rubber feet that keep it from sliding on a wet counter, which is a thoughtful detail most competitors skip.

Scrolling through the 225 ratings (4.5 stars), the dominant theme is "it just works." One verified buyer wrote: *"Finally an egg slicer that doesn't crush the yolk into a paste. **Where It Falls Short**

The wires are not replaceable. If one snaps — and it can happen if you accidentally press down on a bone fragment or twist mid-press — you're buying a new unit. There's no tension adjustment mechanism, so you can't tighten a wire that's started to sag slightly. Also, the strawberry cutter leaves a small core piece about ¼ inch in diameter. It's minor, but if you're prepping 20 strawberries for a party, you'll notice the waste. Finally, the base is a bit top-heavy. Pressing down on a large (jumbo) egg requires a firm, straight-down motion — if you press at an angle, the whole unit can tip. It's not a dealbreaker, but it requires a touch more care than a flat, wide-base slicer.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can you use it on warm eggs, or do they need to be fully cooled?**
A: Cool, fully hard-boiled eggs work best. Warm eggs — especially soft-boiled — will squish and tear. Chill for at least 15 minutes in ice water before slicing for perfect results.

**Q: Does the strawberry part actually work, or is it a gimmick?**
A: It works. Hull the strawberry first, place it flat-side down in the circular well, and press. You get 6-8 even slices. Small strawberries work better than jumbo ones, which tend to distort.

**Q: Is it really dishwasher-safe?**
A: Top rack, yes. But hand-washing takes 5 seconds and preserves the wire tension longer. Hot water and a quick shake is all it needs.
