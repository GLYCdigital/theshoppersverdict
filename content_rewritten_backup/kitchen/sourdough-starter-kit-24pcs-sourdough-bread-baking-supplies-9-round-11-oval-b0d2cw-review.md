If you're staring at a cart full of separate sourdough supplies wondering whether to pull the trigger on this 24-piece kit, the short answer is: it's the rare bundle that gets the essentials right without padding the box with junk. I've tested enough starter kits to know most are 60% filler. This one isn't.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The kit includes the full proofing-to-baking pipeline: two round bannetons (9-inch) and two oval ones (11-inch), all with cotton liners, a stainless steel scoring lame with 10 replacement blades, a bench scraper, a dough whisk, a digital thermometer, silicone bread slings, a proofing cover, and measuring spoons. That's not a bad haul for the price point.

The bannetons are the headline here. Rattan construction, properly coiled, and they hold their shape well. The 9-inch rounds are the workhorses — they fit a standard 1kg boule with room to spare. The ovals are a nice bonus if you prefer batards, which most home bakers graduate to once they realize round loaves don't sandwich well. As shown in the product image, the set is organized in a storage box that keeps everything in one place — a small detail, but one that matters when your counter is already covered in flour.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The scoring lame deserves specific praise. It's got a weighted stainless handle that gives you real control — not the flimsy plastic wands that flex mid-score and ruin your ear. The 10 extra blades mean you won't be buying refills for months. The bench scraper has a comfortable grip and a stiff enough blade to handle high-hydration dough without bending.

The thermometer is functional but basic. It reads in one-degree increments, which matters when you're trying to nail that 78°F dough temperature. It'll get you close, but if you're a precision baker, you'll eventually want a ThermoPop or similar. The dough whisk is decent for mixing starters but won't replace a stand mixer for larger batches.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The feedback pattern is remarkably consistent. One verified buyer noted, "I was surprised by the quality of the bannetons — they're not flimsy like other kits I've tried. The rattan is properly cured and doesn't shed." Another praised the storage box specifically: "Everything has a home. My wife actually likes that I keep my sourdough stuff out now instead of it living in three different drawers."

Multiple reviewers mentioned the lame as the standout piece, with one saying, "I've spent $25 on a single lame before that wasn't this good. The weight and grip make clean scores every time."

**Where It Falls Short**

The banneton liners are the weak point. They're functional, but thin — several reviewers noted they need gentle hand-washing to avoid fraying. Machine washing will shorten their life significantly. The thermometer's coarse resolution is the other notable gap. For a kit aimed at beginners who are still learning to read dough temperature, one-degree increments are adequate, but it's the first thing you'll upgrade if you get serious.

**Who Should Buy This**

This kit is ideal for three groups: beginners assembling their first sourdough setup, home bakers upgrading from a single banneton who want backup proofing baskets, and gift-givers who want a complete, practical set that won't embarrass them. It's less ideal for professionals or serious hobbyists who already own quality tools — you'd be paying for duplicates you don't need.

**FAQ**

*Does the kit include a jar for the starter?*
No. You'll need your own glass jar or container for the starter itself. The kit covers proofing and baking, not fermentation storage.

*Can I bake directly in the bannetons?*
No — the bannetons are for proofing only. The included silicone slings are for transferring dough to your Dutch oven or baking surface.

*Are the blades compatible with other lames?*
No, these are proprietary to this lame design. Stick with the included blades or buy replacements specifically for this model.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're starting sourdough from scratch and want one purchase that covers proofing, scoring, and mixing — the quality-to-price ratio is excellent, and the lame alone justifies the cost.

**Skip it if** you already own bannetons or a lame, or if you demand laboratory-grade temperature accuracy.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely complete starter kit that respects your money. The thin liners and basic thermometer keep it from perfection, but for most home bakers, this is the last sourdough purchase you'll make for a while.
