## What You Actually Get

The box contains the oven itself, a 14-inch cordierite baking stone, a pizza peel, and a crumb tray. No assembly required — it's ready to plug in. The exterior is brushed stainless steel (fingerprints will show, fair warning), and the interior is insulated to keep the outer shell cool enough to touch, though the door glass and top vent stay hot.

The 14-inch stone is the headline feature. Most indoor pizza ovens (Ooni Volt, Breville) max out at 12–13 inches. That extra inch and a half matters if you've ever wrestled with a wobbly 14-inch pie on a smaller stone.

## Performance: The Real Story

I ran five pizzas through this over two weeks — two Neapolitan-style, one Detroit-style in a pan, one frozen grocery store pizza, and one thin-crust experiment.

**The good:** At 800°F, a properly stretched Neapolitan dough cooks in 90 seconds. The top element gets you the leopard spotting; the bottom element delivers the charred, crisp underside. The dual controls are genuinely useful — I found the sweet spot at 100% top, 80% bottom for Neapolitan, and reversed it for the frozen pizza (which needs more bottom heat to crisp a floppy crust).

The stone retains heat well. Back-to-back pizzas were consistent — the third pie came out as good as the first, which is rare in cheaper ovens where the stone loses 100°F between bakes.

**The not-so-good:** The preheat claim is optimistic. The manual says 10 minutes to 800°F. In practice, mine took 18–22 minutes depending on ambient kitchen temp. Not a dealbreaker, but factor it into dinner planning.

There's no internal light or viewing window. You'll open the door to check, lose heat, and the next pizza will take 20% longer. It's a workflow annoyance, not a fatal flaw.

## Where It Falls Short

**It's heavy.** Roughly 40 pounds, with no side handles. The top gets hot, so you can't grab it there. Plan to leave it on the counter permanently — this is not a "put it away in the cabinet" appliance.

**The crumb tray is small.** The included tray catches drips, but it fills fast if you're making multiple pizzas with oily toppings. You'll be pulling it out mid-bake to clean it.

**No temperature display.** You get dials with markings, not a digital readout. The dials are accurate once you learn your oven's quirks, but a digital temp would've been nice at this price.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it smoke a lot?**
A: At 800°F, yes — especially with olive oil or flour on the stone. Keep your kitchen vent running or crack a window. Lower the top heat to 70% if smoke becomes an issue.

**Q: Can I use it for anything besides pizza?**
A: Technically yes — it'll roast vegetables, cook flatbreads, and even finish a steak. But the stone and heat profile are tuned for pizza. Don't buy it expecting a versatile oven.

**Q: How long does the stone last?**
A: Cordierite is durable. With normal use (avoid thermal shock — don't put a cold stone in a hot oven), expect years of service. Replacement stones are available if needed.
