**Peugeot Zanzibar Pepper Mill Review: Style Over Substance? Not Quite.**

Here's the thing about Peugeot pepper mills: they've been grinding pepper since 1874, and their steel burr mechanism is the gold standard. The Zanzibar takes that proven guts and wraps it in modern acrylic. The question is whether that shell justifies the price.

**What You Actually Get**

The Zanzibar arrives fully assembled, pre-filled with a small starter supply of black peppercorns. It stands about 8 inches tall with a 2.5-inch diameter base — substantial enough to grip comfortably, light enough to pass across the table. The acrylic body is transparent, so you always know when it's time to refill. The chrome top houses the classic Peugeot adjustment knob with six detented grind settings.

**Performance: The Mechanism Carries It**

At the risk of sounding like a broken record: the grinding mechanism is superb. The case-hardened steel burrs crush peppercorns rather than chopping them, which means you get cleaner flavor release. I tested it across all six settings — from "fine enough for a white sauce" to "cracked for a steak crust" — and the output was consistent at every level. No jamming, no peppercorns bouncing around, no frustrating grinding-wheel-spinning-empty moments.

The acrylic body does contribute one practical advantage: you can see exactly how much pepper remains. No more shaking a mill into your pan hoping something comes out. That's genuinely useful in a busy kitchen.

**Where It Falls Short**

The tradeoff for that see-through body is durability. Acrylic scratches — that's physics. After two weeks of normal kitchen use, mine shows fine surface marks around the base where it sits on the counter. More concerning: several Amazon reviewers report the collar cracking where the top meets the body. One buyer wrote, *"The mechanism is fine but the acrylic cracked at the neck after three months. Disappointing for the price."* That's not universal — the 4.1-star average suggests most units hold up — but it's a risk you should know about.

The other issue is feel. A classic Peugeot walnut mill has heft that says "this will outlive me." The Zanzibar weighs about half as much. It's not fragile — it won't shatter if you drop it — but it lacks the premium hand-feel you'd expect at this price point.

**Who Should Buy This**

You're the right buyer for the Zanzibar if you want Peugeot's legendary grind mechanism but your kitchen leans modern — glass, chrome, white cabinets. The transparent body looks genuinely sharp on a contemporary counter, and the color options (there are several) let you match your decor.

Skip it if you're a traditionalist who wants a mill that doubles as a tabletop heirloom, or if you're hard on your tools. The wooden Peugeot models cost about the same and will look better in ten years.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can you adjust the grind size?**
A: Yes — six settings, from powdery fine to cracked-coarse. Turn the chrome knob on top; you'll feel the detents click. It's the same mechanism as Peugeot's more expensive models.

**Q: How do you refill it?**
A: Pull the chrome top straight up. It's friction-fit, no threads. The opening is wide enough for a standard peppercorn jar. One tip: hold it over the sink when refilling — a few peppercorns will inevitably bounce out.

**Q: Can it grind salt?**
A: No. Peugeot explicitly warns against using salt in pepper mills — it corrodes the steel. Use it for pepper only.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Buy It If You Want Peugeot Performance in a Modern Skin**

**Buy it if:** You want the best pepper-grinding mechanism available, you're decorating a modern kitchen, and you're willing to handle the acrylic carefully.

**Skip it if:** You want a mill that doubles as a kitchen heirloom, you're prone to dropping things, or you expect $40+ worth of premium heft. In that case, get the classic Peugeot Paris walnut mill instead.

The Zanzibar is a good mill with a real compromise at its core: modern aesthetics versus long-term durability. The grinding is flawless, the design is genuinely distinctive, but the acrylic body is the weak point. At the right price — under $35 — it's a solid buy. North of that, you might regret it in a few years when the scratches accumulate.
