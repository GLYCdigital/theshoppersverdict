**The Hamilton Beach Machine with Auto Fruit and Nut Dispenser (model 29882) solves the one problem that's plagued bread machine owners for decades: you can't just dump raisins, walnuts, or dried cranberries in at the start.** They turn to mush, or worse, get crushed into the dough's gluten structure. This machine's automatic dispenser drops them in mid-knead, which genuinely changes the final texture. But that feature comes with its own quirks — and at 4.1 stars from 204 Amazon ratings, buyers have strong opinions.

**What you actually get:** The unit is a full-size 2-pound machine with a vertical bucket, not a horizontal loaf pan. That means taller, rounder loaves — think bakery boule, not sandwich-style slices. The control panel has 12 presets including basic, whole wheat, French, sweet, quick, and dough-only cycles. There's a 13-hour delay timer, a 2-hour keep-warm function, and a viewing window. The fruit and nut dispenser sits on top as a separate hopper with a small lid. It's not a huge footprint — roughly 12 x 10 x 12 inches — so it fits under most upper cabinets.

**Build quality and real-world performance:** The outer casing is brushed stainless steel with plastic accents. It feels solid enough, though the lid hinges are the weakest point — a handful of reviewers mention them loosening after months of regular use. The nonstick bucket genuinely works; loaves drop out with a gentle shake, and the kneading paddle leaves a minimal hole in the bottom crust (that's normal for every machine, by the way).

The dispenser is the headline feature, and it mostly delivers. As one verified buyer put it: *"I've made 15 loaves with this and the nuts come out perfectly distributed, not clumped in one section. My old machine turned walnuts into powder."* The mechanism uses a small rotating gate that releases ingredients at a set point in the knead cycle. It works well with standard-size raisins, chopped walnuts, sunflower seeds, and dried cranberries.

**Where it falls short:** Sticky ingredients are the problem. Dates, figs, or moist cherries clump inside the chute and dump all at once or not at all. You'll need to coat them lightly in flour before loading. Also, there's no gluten-free setting. For a machine in this price tier with modern features, that omission is noticeable — gluten-free bakers will need to use the basic or quick cycle and adjust manually. The kneading cycle is loud, too. It's not deafening, but it's a persistent whirring and thumping that'll wake light sleepers if you're running it overnight with the delay timer.

**Buyer consensus from the 204 ratings:** The praise clusters around the dispenser working as advertised, consistent rise on basic white and whole wheat loaves, and the easy-clean bucket. The complaints are mostly about the dispenser jamming with wet ingredients, the lack of gluten-free, and one recurring issue: the machine walks slightly on countertops during heavy kneading. It's not a dealbreaker, but put it on a non-slip mat. One reviewer noted: *"The bread is excellent, but I wish they'd made the dispenser hopper removable for cleaning. You have to wipe it out with a cloth."*

**Who should buy this:**
- **Home bakers who want visual variety** — the dispenser makes seeded, nutty, and dried-fruit loaves genuinely better.
- **Families** — 2-pound capacity yields a loaf that lasts a few days.
- **Busy people** — the delay timer means fresh bread at 7 AM without being awake at 7 AM.

**Who should skip it:**
- **Gluten-free households** — no dedicated cycle means fiddling.
- **Small kitchens** — it's not huge, but the vertical footprint plus the top-mounted dispenser adds height.
- **Anyone on a tight budget** — you're paying a premium for the auto-dispenser feature; basic machines cost significantly less.

**FAQ:**

*Can I use the dispenser for chocolate chips?* Yes, but freeze them first. Unfrozen chips melt and stick to the chute walls.

*Does it make a 1.5-pound loaf?* It makes 1-pound, 1.5-pound, and 2-pound loaves. The dispenser amount is the same regardless, so adjust your add-ins for smaller loaves.

*Is the bucket dishwasher safe?* The bucket is, but the paddle and dispenser are hand-wash only. The manual is explicit about this — ignore it and you'll degrade the nonstick coating.

**The Verdict:** The Hamilton Beach 29882 earns a 4.2/5. It's a genuinely better bread machine for fruit-and-nut loaves than anything without a dispenser, and the basic bread output is solid. The tradeoffs — sticky-ingredient jams, no gluten-free, and noise — are real but manageable.

**Buy it if** you make seeded or dried-fruit breads regularly and want consistent distribution without babysitting the machine.

**Skip it if** you're gluten-free, or if the premium over a standard machine isn't justified by how often you'll use the dispenser.
