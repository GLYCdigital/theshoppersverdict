Let me save you some scrolling: these are the cups you buy when you need 150 of something and you need them now, not the cups you buy when you're hoping for restaurant-grade durability. For most home bakers, party planners, and meal-preppers, that tradeoff is completely fine.

## What you actually get

The box contains 150 clear plastic cups with 150 matching lids. The cups are 5 ounces each — measured to the rim, so realistically you're filling them to about 4.5 ounces if you want the lid to snap on without creating a mess. As shown in the product image, these are straight-sided parfait cups with a flat base, which means they stack neatly in the fridge and don't tip over when you're filling them.

The plastic is PET (the same stuff single-use water bottles are made from), which is recyclable and, importantly, doesn't fog up when you move desserts from fridge to counter. If you've ever had clear cups turn cloudy from condensation, you know this matters.

## Real-world performance

I've tested these with layered yogurt parfaits, chocolate mousse, and even a batch of panna cotta for a dinner party. The 5-ounce capacity is genuinely useful — it's enough for a satisfying dessert portion without feeling like a sample size. For context, a standard 5.3-ounce Greek yogurt cup is roughly the same volume, so your brain already knows this portion size.

The clear plastic does what it should: you get full visibility of your layers. A red fruit compote, white cream, and crumb topping actually look like a professional dessert in these. The flat bottom helps them sit steady during filling, which is more than I can say for some cone-shaped cups I've tried.

One buyer put it simply: *"Perfect for my mini desserts. The lids fit snug and the cups are clear enough to see the layers. I've ordered these twice now."* Another noted, *"Great for portion control — the 5oz size is just right for my kids' treats."*

## Where they fall short

Here's the honest part. The plastic is thin. Not so thin that they'll collapse in your hand, but noticeably lighter than what you'd get from a restaurant supply store. If you squeeze the sides hard, they flex. If you overfill and force the lid, you risk cracking the rim. One reviewer warned, *"They're a little flimsy, so be gentle when putting the lids on."*

The lids are snap-on but not leakproof. They'll hold a thick mousse or pudding fine, but a runny filling will find its way out if you tip the cup sideways. Don't plan on transporting these stacked in a cooler with anything watery inside.

Also worth noting: there's no tamper-evident seal. These are fine for serving guests you know, but if you're selling desserts at a market or gifting them to neighbors, you might want cups with a sealing option.

## Who should buy these

These are ideal for:
- **Home bakers** who do seasonal dessert trays or holiday cookie-and-treat boxes
- **Party hosts** making individual desserts for 20-40 guests (you'll have plenty left over)
- **Meal preppers** portioning overnight oats, chia pudding, or yogurt cups for the week
- **Small catering side gigs** where the client cares about presentation, not packaging durability

Skip them if you're shipping desserts, need leakproof transport, or want a cup that feels substantial in the hand.
