# Frymate Commercial Deep Fryer Filter Review: Does It Actually Extend Oil Life?

If you run a food truck, concession stand, or commercial kitchen, you already know the math: fryer oil costs $40–$60 per jug, and changing it weekly burns through your margins. The Frymate Commercial Deep Fryer Filter promises to stretch that oil further. But at 4.1 stars across 204 ratings, is it the real deal or just another consumable you'll reorder once and ditch?

Here's what I found after digging through the specs, the customer feedback, and the physics of how this thing works.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes a stack of heavy-duty filter paper cones and a reusable metal filter screen. The cone design is the standout feature here — it's shaped to sit directly inside most standard fryer vats without needing a separate filter cone holder or stand. This matters more than you'd think. Competing systems like the Filter Magic require a $150+ pump assembly. The Frymate just sits in the fryer, you pour hot oil through it, and the filtered oil drains into a catch pot below.

The metal screen serves as a pre-filter for the big chunks — batter flakes, breading debris — while the paper cone catches the fine carbon particles that turn oil dark and give food a stale taste. For a commercial unit, the build feels appropriately industrial. The paper is thick, not the tissue-thin stuff you get with budget home fryer filters.

## Real-World Performance

The filtration process works, but it requires patience. You need to let the oil cool to under 300°F before pouring — the manufacturer warns that hotter oil will weaken the paper and cause tearing. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"Great filter, but you absolutely cannot rush this. I tried filtering at 325°F and blew a hole right through the cone."*

When used correctly, though, the results are solid. Multiple buyers report getting 30–50% more fry life out of their oil. One concession stand operator wrote: *"I used to change oil every 5 days. Now I'm pushing 8–9 days and the fries still come out golden. This paid for itself in the first month."*

The cone's interlocking edges are another thoughtful touch. They seat snugly against the fryer walls, so you don't get unfiltered oil sneaking around the sides. That's a common complaint with cheaper cone filters, and Frymate clearly engineered around it.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest practical downside: no catch container included. The product image shows oil draining into a neat steel pot, but that's not in the box. You'll need to supply your own heat-safe container that fits under your fryer's drain valve. For a commercial kitchen that already has stock pots around, this is minor. For a home user buying this on a whim, it's an unexpected extra purchase.

The paper cones are also single-use, and you'll go through them quickly if you filter daily. At roughly $1–$2 per cone depending on pack size, that's a recurring cost to factor in. It's still cheaper than changing oil, but it's not a one-time investment.

Finally, the 350°F temperature limit is non-negotiable. If you're filtering a fryer that's been running all day, you're waiting 30–45 minutes for the oil to cool. That's downtime you don't get with pump-based systems.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You run a food truck, concession stand, or small restaurant that goes through 30+ pounds of oil weekly. The oil savings alone justify the purchase, and the cone design works with most standard commercial fryers.

**Skip it if:** You have a high-volume kitchen filtering multiple fryers daily — you'll want a pump system to cut downtime. Also skip if you're a home cook with a countertop fryer; this is overkill for 2-quart oil capacity.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with a home deep fryer?**
Technically yes, but the cone is sized for commercial vats (roughly 14–20 inches wide). It'll likely be too large for most home units. Stick with home-grade filters for countertop fryers.

**Q: How often should I replace the paper cones?**
Every 2–3 filtering sessions, or immediately if you notice the oil draining slowly. Slow drainage means the paper is clogged with trapped particles — that's actually a sign it's working.

**Q: Does this work with peanut oil and canola oil?**
Yes. The filter doesn't react with any standard frying oil. Just keep the temperature under 350°F regardless of oil type.

## The Verdict

The Frymate Commercial Deep Fryer Filter is a legit money-saver for small-to-medium food operations. It's not the fastest system, and the missing catch container is an annoying oversight, but the oil life extension is real and the build quality holds up to daily commercial use.

**Buy it if** you're tired of dumping half-used oil down the drain. **Skip it if** you need speed over savings.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, practical purchase that pays for itself, provided you respect the temperature limits and bring your own catch pot.
