If you own a Ninja Nutri Bullet and use it daily, you already know the pain: the cups eventually crack, the blades dull, and Ninja charges nearly half the price of a new blender for replacement parts. At $20.99 for a 2-pack with blades and lids, this third-party set lands at about a third of the OEM cost. The question is whether the savings come with a catch.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get two complete 18-ounce cups — each with a blade assembly, a drinking lid, and a storage cap. That's the full kit, not just empty plastic cylinders. The cups are marked with the same ounce measurements as the originals (up to 18 oz), and they thread onto Ninja bases from the BN401, SS101, BN400, BN800, and BN801 models without any adapter.

The plastic is Tritan, which is the same material Ninja uses in the OEM cups. That matters — cheaper replacements often use polycarbonate that picks up odors and clouds up after a few uses. The blades are stainless steel with four prongs, and they seat into the base the same way the originals do.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these with frozen fruit, ice, peanut butter, and protein powder over two weeks. The blade assembly handled frozen strawberries and ice cubes without stalling or leaving chunks — performance is essentially identical to what you'd get from the Ninja-branded cups. The threading is tight enough that I didn't get any leaks even when shaking aggressively to mix a thick smoothie.

The blade base twists on and off cleanly, which sounds minor but is actually the most common failure point on these budget replacements. Some third-party cups have poorly machined threads that cross-thread after a few uses. That's not the case here.

One thing to note: the blade gaskets (the rubber ring inside the base) are visibly thinner than OEM. They seal fine right now, but they'll compress faster over time. If you're the type who disassembles and deep-cleans the blade assembly after every use, you'll need to replace these gaskets sooner than you would with the originals.

## Where It Falls Short

The two real complaints are legitimate. First, the measurement lines will fade if you put these in the dishwasher. The listing doesn't clearly state this, and several buyers learned the hard way. Hand wash only — that's the price of the savings.

Second, the drinking lids are slightly looser than OEM. They seal fine for shaking, but if you toss the cup in a bag with the lid on, you might get a small leak from the spout. Not a dealbreaker, but not identical to the originals.
