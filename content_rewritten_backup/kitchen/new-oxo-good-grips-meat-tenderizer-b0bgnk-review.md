If you've ever tried to pound a chicken breast into a uniform cutlet with a rolling pin, only to end up with a mangled, uneven mess, you know the struggle. The OXO Good Grips Meat Tenderizer is designed to end that frustration — and based on its 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, it's doing exactly that for most buyers. ## What You Actually Get

The box contains one tool. That's it. But the design is where OXO earns its reputation. The head is forged stainless steel with two distinct faces: one flat side for pounding cutlets flat, and one textured side with pyramid-shaped points for breaking down tough muscle fibers. The handle is the signature OXO Good Grips soft, non-slip material with a slight contour that fits your palm naturally.

As shown in the product image, the head measures about 3.5 inches across. The whole tool weighs roughly 1.2 pounds — heavy enough to do the work without requiring you to swing it like a hammer, but light enough for controlled, precise strikes.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this on three tasks: pounding chicken breasts for schnitzel, tenderizing a London broil, and flattening pork chops for even cooking.

**The flat side** is excellent for the chicken. It took about 12-15 firm whacks per breast to get an even 1/4-inch thickness. The weight distribution meant I didn't have to over-swing, and the handle stayed grippy even when my hands got slick from raw poultry.

**The textured side** on the London broil was where this tool really proved its worth. The pyramid points penetrate deeper than the small spikes on cheaper tenderizers, and you can feel the meat giving way after a few passes. One buyer noted, "The spiked side actually works — after 2-3 minutes of tenderizing, my chuck roast went from shoe leather to something my family actually enjoyed." That echoes the majority of the 240 reviews.

## The Good and the Bad

**What buyers consistently praise:**

- Build quality. Multiple reviewers mention this is "a buy-it-for-life tool" — the stainless steel head won't pit or rust like the cast iron versions you'll find for half the price.
- Ergonomics. The handle design genuinely reduces fatigue. As one reviewer put it: "I have arthritis in my hands, and this is the only tenderizer I can use without pain."
- Ease of cleaning. The one-piece stainless head has no crevices where meat gets trapped. It's dishwasher-safe, and a quick rinse handles 90% of cleanup.

**Where it falls short:**

- The 3.5-inch head is smaller than I'd like for wide cuts. A large chicken breast or a flank steak requires multiple overlapping passes, which can create uneven pounding if you're not careful.
- It's not the cheapest option. A basic cast iron tenderizer from a budget brand runs $8-10. You're paying a $5-7 premium for the OXO name, the handle, and the rust-proof stainless steel. Most buyers agree it's worth it, but it's worth knowing going in.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the textured side aggressive enough for thick cuts like chuck roast?**  
A: Yes — but work in a crosshatch pattern (one direction, then rotate 90 degrees) and spend 2-3 minutes on each side. It won't turn a cheap roast into filet mignon, but it makes a noticeable difference in tenderness.

**Q: Can you wash this in the dishwasher?**  
A: Yes. The stainless steel head is dishwasher-safe. The handle is also rated for dishwasher use, though hand-washing will extend the life of the soft grip material.

**Q: Does the flat side actually flatten meat evenly?**  
A: It does, but you need to start from the center and work outward, flipping the meat occasionally. The tool gives you the control; you still need the technique. For best results, place the meat between two sheets of plastic wrap first to prevent tearing.
