If you've ever watched a cheap grinder choke on a pork shoulder, you know the frustration. The MEAT! 2 HP Pro-Grade #42 is the answer to that problem — a serious machine built for people who process whole animals, not just the occasional pound of ground chuck.

**What You Actually Get**

Unboxing reveals the heavy-duty core: a 2 HP electric motor housed in a powder-coated steel body, a stainless steel #42 grinding head, two grinding plates (coarse and fine), a stainless steel knife, a sausage stuffing tube, and a stomper. The hopper is generously sized — you can drop in a full pork butt without pre-cutting into tiny strips, which saves real time when you're processing 50 pounds of venison. The unit sits on four rubber feet that keep it planted on your counter or workbench, though at this weight, it's not going anywhere anyway.

**Performance and Build Quality**

This is where the MEAT! grinder earns its keep. The 2 HP motor isn't marketing hype; it chews through whole muscle meat with the coarse plate installed, delivering that chunky, textured grind you want for chili or coarse-ground sausage. A buyer named Tom put it simply: "I ground 40 pounds of pork shoulder in about 15 minutes. It never slowed down once." That matches my assessment — the auger feeds aggressively and the stainless steel knife stays sharp through extended use, unlike softer carbon steel blades that dull mid-batch.

The #42 head is the professional standard, and this unit delivers on that promise. Stainless steel construction means no metallic aftertaste and no corrosion after a proper wash. Cleaning takes about 10 minutes — disassemble, rinse, and the smooth surfaces don't harbor meat residue like ribbed plastic grinders do.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The overwhelming sentiment in 229 ratings is power and speed. One reviewer noted: "I've owned three grinders over 20 years. This is the first one that doesn't heat up the meat during grinding." That's a critical detail — heat ruins texture, and this motor runs cool even on long sessions. Another buyer processing elk said: "The coarse plate is perfect for jerky. My last grinder turned everything to paste." Multiple customers confirm the motor doesn't bog down on gristle or connective tissue, which is the #1 failure point on sub-$200 units.

**Where It Falls Short**

No machine is perfect, and this one has two honest issues. First, there's no reverse function. When a tendon wraps around the auger, you're stopping the machine, disassembling the head, and clearing it by hand. It's rare, but when it happens mid-batch, it's a 5-minute interruption. Second, the unit is heavy — around 40 pounds — so it's not something you leave on the counter. If you grind 5 pounds of meat once a month, this is overkill and a storage burden. The included sausage stuffing tube works, but the plastic collar feels like a cost-cutting decision compared to the all-metal grinder body.

**Who Should Buy This**

This grinder is for hunters processing a deer or two each season, homesteaders grinding 20+ pounds at a time, or anyone serious about charcuterie who wants coarse, professional-grade texture. It's also a smart buy for a small butcher operation or a family that splits a half-cow with neighbors. If you're grinding a pound of beef for Tuesday tacos, buy a $60 grinder instead — you'll never justify this much machine for that little work.

**FAQ**

*Does the 2 HP motor require a special outlet?*
No. It runs on standard 120V household current. The motor draws about 15 amps, so don't run it on the same circuit as a microwave or space heater.

*Can I grind bones with this?*
No. The manual and every reviewer agree — this grinds meat, not bone. You'll dull the blade and risk damaging the auger. Buy a separate bone crusher if you need that capability.

*Is the coarse plate the only option for large grinds?*
No, it ships with both coarse and fine plates. The coarse is the standout for texture, but the fine plate handles burger and meatloaf mixes well, though it runs slightly slower due to increased resistance.
