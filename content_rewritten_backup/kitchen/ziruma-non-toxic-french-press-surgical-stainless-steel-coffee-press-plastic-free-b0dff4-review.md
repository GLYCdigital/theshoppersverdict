## The French Press That Finally Drops the Plastic

Here's the thing about most French presses: they look great on the shelf, but the plunger mechanism, the filter housing, even the lid — all plastic. When you're brewing at 200°F, that plastic touches your coffee for four full minutes. The **Ziruma Non-Toxic French Press** eliminates that problem entirely. As shown in the product image, the entire brewing assembly — mesh filter, center rod, plunger cap — is surgical-grade 18/8 stainless steel. Nothing else touches your water or grounds.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the press itself (available in 34-ounce and 17-ounce sizes), a stainless steel mesh filter assembly, and a scoop. No plastic scoop, no plastic measuring spoon — just steel and glass-free construction. The body is double-wall insulated stainless steel, which is the key design choice here. It's not just about durability; it's about heat retention.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested the 34-ounce model with a medium roast, using a coarse grind as recommended. The plunger moves smoothly — no sticking, no grinding metal-on-metal feel. The filter is fine enough to catch most fines while letting the oils through, which is what you want for that full-bodied French press character.

The double-wall insulation genuinely works. Coffee brewed at 8 a.m. was still pleasantly warm at 9:15 — not piping hot, but drinkable. That's a real advantage over glass presses, which lose heat fast and force you to either chug or reheat.

One thing to note: because the walls are insulated, you can't see the coffee level from outside. You'll need to know your water measurements or lift the lid to check. It's a minor inconvenience, not a dealbreaker.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. The fine mesh filter is a double-edged sword. If you use a fine or espresso-level grind, the filter clogs and the plunger becomes hard to push. This press wants a coarse, consistent grind — if you're buying pre-ground coffee, get the "French press" grind setting, not the drip or fine one.

The weight is also worth mentioning. At roughly 2.2 pounds empty, this is not a travel-friendly press. It's a countertop appliance. And the insulated walls mean no microwave reheating — you'll need to either drink it within the hour or reheat in a separate vessel.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The health-conscious brewer** who's read about BPA, BPS, and microplastics and wants zero plastic contact — this is your press.
- **The clumsy or busy cook** who's broken a glass press and is done with that cycle.
- **The slow sipper** who wants coffee hot for an hour without a warming plate.

Skip it if you need a lightweight press for camping, if you only have fine-ground coffee, or if you prefer to see your brew through glass.

## FAQ

**Does the stainless steel affect the taste?**
No. The 18/8 steel is passivated, meaning it doesn't react with coffee acids. Multiple reviewers confirmed no metallic aftertaste — the coffee tastes like the beans, not the vessel.

**Can I put this in the dishwasher?**
The body is hand-wash only — the insulation can be compromised by dishwasher heat. The filter and plunger can be rinsed or hand-washed easily. Plan on a 30-second rinse after each use.

**What's the actual capacity?**
The 34-ounce model brews about 4 standard 8-ounce cups. The 17-ounce version brews 2 cups. Both are measured to the fill line, not the rim.
