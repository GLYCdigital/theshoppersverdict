## Two pans, one footprint — and zero PFAS to worry about

Most combo cookers are a compromise. The Lodge Cast Iron Combo Cooker isn't. It's a 3.2-quart Dutch oven with a deep skillet lid that flips into a second cooking surface — giving you two genuinely useful pieces of cookware that nest into one storage slot. And after the recent PFAS concerns that hit the nonstick industry, Lodge's decision to keep this 100% uncoated cast iron looks smarter every month.

## What you actually get

Unboxing reveals two pre-seasoned cast iron pieces: a 3.2-quart deep pot (about 6.75 inches deep) and a lid that doubles as a 10.25-inch skillet with a 4.5-inch wall height. Both arrive seasoned with vegetable oil — no chemical nonstick coatings, no PFAS, no PFOA. The pot has two loop handles; the skillet lid has a single helper handle. Total assembled weight: 8.2 pounds.

## Real-world performance

The combo cooker shines in the oven. Because there's zero plastic or silicone, you can take it from a 500°F oven to a broiler without a second thought. Sourdough bakers love this thing — the deep pot traps steam for a crackly crust while the skillet lid catches overflow. One Amazon reviewer called it "the best bread baker I've owned," noting the pre-seasoning held up through dozens of loaves.

On the stovetop, the 10.25-inch skillet handles eggs, seared chicken thighs, and cornbread without sticking — provided you keep it seasoned. The deep pot simmers chili, braises short ribs, and shallow-fries with even heat retention that thin stainless steel can't match. As another buyer put it: "This replaced my $200 enameled Dutch oven for 90% of what I cook."

## What buyers consistently praise

- **Versatility**: The 2-in-1 design earns near-universal praise. Owners report using the skillet lid for breakfast while the pot simmers dinner simultaneously.
- **PFAS-free peace of mind**: Multiple reviewers specifically mention choosing this over coated alternatives due to health concerns.
- **Heat retention**: Cast iron's legendary even heating means no hot spots — a consistent theme across reviews.
- **Durability**: "Bought this in 2019 and it still looks new after weekly use," said one long-term owner.

## Where it falls short

Honest downsides exist. At 8.2 pounds, this isn't a lightweight — if you have arthritis or wrist issues, daily lifting gets old fast. The uncoated surface also demands care: hand-wash only, dry immediately, and oil after each use. Skip that and you'll see rust spots within weeks.

The shallow skillet walls (4.5 inches) are another tradeoff. Tossing vegetables or flipping a heavy frittata requires a confident wrist — ingredients can escape over the rim if you're not careful. And unlike enameled cast iron, this will take on flavors over time. Cook a heavy garlic dish, and your next batch of bread might carry a hint of it.

## Who should buy this

- **Sourdough bakers** who want a dedicated bread oven without spending $100+ on a cloche
- **Campers and off-grid cooks** who need one piece of cookware that does everything over a fire
- **Health-conscious cooks** avoiding PFAS and nonstick coatings entirely
- **Small-kitchen dwellers** who need maximum function from minimal storage space

**Skip it** if you want dishwasher-safe convenience, prefer lightweight cookware, or don't want to manage seasoning maintenance.
