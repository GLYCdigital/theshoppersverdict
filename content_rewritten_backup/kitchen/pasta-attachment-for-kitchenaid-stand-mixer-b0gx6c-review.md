## Fresh pasta without the arm workout — mostly

If you've ever rolled pasta by hand with a wooden pin, you know the pain. This KitchenAid attachment eliminates that grind, turning your stand mixer's hub into a mini pasta factory. But here's the catch nobody mentions in the marketing photos: you still crank the handle yourself. The motor drives the rollers, but your hand feeds the dough and controls the pace. That's actually a feature, not a bug — more on that below.

## What's actually in the box

You get three pieces: a roller with six thickness settings, a spaghetti cutter, and a tagliatelle cutter. Each clicks onto the mixer's power hub with a simple twist — no tools, no adapter plates. The instruction booklet is thin but honest about dough hydration ratios, which matters more than most people realize.

The construction is reassuringly heavy. Rollers are polished stainless steel, not chrome-plated plastic. The cutters use sharp, interlocking gears that slice cleanly through sheeted dough. If you've handled cheaper attachments that wobble and flex, this one feels like a proper tool.

## Performance: where it shines and where it fights back

The six thickness settings run from paper-thin (1) to thick lasagna sheets (6). For most home cooks, the sweet spot is 4–5 for fettuccine and 2–3 for ravioli. The rollers grip dough evenly without sticking, provided you've dusted with semolina or flour.

Real-world feedback from the 225 Amazon ratings highlights consistency. One verified buyer wrote: "The dough comes out silky smooth — no tearing, no wavy edges like my old hand-crank model." Another noted: "I've made pasta every Sunday for three months and the cutters still slice like day one."

The hand-crank requirement is the biggest adjustment. As the rollers pull dough through, you feed with one hand and crank with the other. It's not difficult, but it's not hands-free. Some reviewers wished for a motor-driven feed — but honestly, that would just shred dough that isn't perfectly hydrated. The manual crank gives you tactile control that prevents jams.

## Where it falls short

The missing fettuccine cutter is the most common complaint in the reviews. You get spaghetti (round, thin) and tagliatelle (flat, wide ribbon). If you want fettuccine's medium width, you're out of luck — you'd need to buy the standalone cutter attachment separately.

Also, this attachment demands respect for dough prep. If you skip the 30-minute rest or use a wet dough, you'll get sticky, torn sheets. That's not the attachment's fault, but it's a learning curve that catches people off guard. One review put it bluntly: "This doesn't make pasta for you. It makes pasta with you."

## Who should buy this

- **The Sunday cook** who wants fresh pasta without dedicating an hour to hand-rolling — yes
- **The gift-giver** shopping for a KitchenAid owner who already makes bread or pizza — absolutely, it's the natural next step
- **The occasional experimenter** who'll use it twice a year — maybe skip it; the cleanup and dough prep won't feel worth it

Cleanup is straightforward: the pieces are metal and plastic, so a dry brush and a damp cloth handle most residue. No dishwasher — the cutters will dull and the rollers will corrode.

## FAQ: What shoppers actually ask

**Does this fit my KitchenAid model?**
All tilt-head and bowl-lift models with the standard power hub on the side — that's every Artisan, Pro, and Commercial series from the last 20 years. It won't fit mini or ultra-power models that lack the hub.

**Can I make gluten-free pasta with this?**
Yes, but with caveats. Rice and chickpea flours need more binding (xanthan gum or eggs) and a wetter dough. Roll at setting 4–5, not thinner, or it'll tear.

**Is the stainless steel roller dishwasher safe?**
No. Hand-wash only. The cutters will rust and the rollers will lose their polish in a dishwasher cycle.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You make pasta at least twice a month, own a KitchenAid with a hub, and want restaurant-quality sheets without the arm fatigue. The stainless build will outlast your mixer.

**Skip it if:** You're buying this to "get into" pasta making, expect fully automatic operation, or specifically need fettuccine width. The learning curve and manual cranking will frustrate you.

**Rating:** 4.6/5 — a legitimate workhorse with one notable omission (fettuccine cutter) and a hands-on process that some will love and others will find tedious. For the price point and build quality, it's the best pasta attachment value for KitchenAid owners.
