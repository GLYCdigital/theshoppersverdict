Replacement filters are the recurring tax on owning a reverse osmosis system. Some brands charge $80–$120 for a single annual kit, hoping you'll forget that generic filters exist. Pure Blue H2O's 5-pack undercuts that pricing significantly, but does it actually filter as well as the name-brand cartridges? After digging into specs, customer feedback, and real-world performance reports, here's what you need to know.

## What's in the Box

This kit is designed for standard four-stage RO systems (the most common home configuration). You get:

- One 10-inch sediment filter (typically 5-micron)
- One 10-inch carbon block filter (typically 5-micron)
- One inline granular activated carbon (GAC) filter
- One inline carbon block (CTO) filter
- One extra inline filter — this is the 5th piece that makes the "5-pack" designation

The 10-inch filters fit standard housings from iSpring, Express Water, Home Master, and most generic systems. The inline filters connect via 1/4-inch push-fit fittings, which is the industry standard for RO tubing.

## Build Quality and Performance

The sediment filter uses melt-blown polypropylene — the same material used by premium brands. The carbon block is coconut-shell based, which generally provides better taste and odor reduction than cheaper bituminous coal carbon. I've tested both, and the difference is noticeable in the final glass of water.

What buyers consistently report matches my assessment: filtration quality is indistinguishable from OEM cartridges. One verified purchaser noted, *"Replaced the filters on my iSpring system that I've had for 3 years. Water tastes identical to when it was new. Zero issues with fitment."*

Another long-term reviewer mentioned, *"Second year using these. TDS meter still reads 8-10 ppm, same as with the expensive branded filters."* That's the metric that matters — if the carbon is exhausted, your TDS readings climb. These hold up for the full 6-12 month lifespan, depending on your feed water quality.

## Where It Falls Short

The most common complaint is fitment on specific brands. Some users with APEC systems reported that the inline filters' tubing lengths were slightly short, requiring a small extension. This isn't a defect — it's a universal-fit compromise. If you have a proprietary system, measure your existing inline filter lengths before ordering.

Also worth noting: this kit does **not** include the RO membrane. That's a separate $30–$50 purchase. If your system is 2+ years old and you haven't replaced the membrane, you'll need to factor that in. The filters will still work, but your water quality won't improve until the membrane is swapped.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners with standard 4-stage RO systems** (iSpring, Express Water, Waterdrop, or generic) — this is your best value option
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who don't want to pay 2-3x more for brand-name cartridges that perform identically
- **Anyone replacing filters on a 6-month cycle** — buying the 5-pack covers you for nearly a year and a half

**Skip it if** you own a proprietary system (some Home Master models, certain APEC units) without standard 10-inch housings, or if you're due for a full membrane replacement and want a single all-in-one kit.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my iSpring RCC7 system?**
A: Yes. The RCC7 uses standard 10-inch housings and 1/4-inch inline connections. This is one of the most commonly cited compatible systems in reviews.

**Q: How often should I replace these?**
A: Every 6-12 months, depending on your feed water quality. If you have well water or high sediment, lean toward 6 months. City water with decent pressure can stretch to 12 months.

**Q: Do I need to buy anything else for a full change?**
A: The RO membrane is not included. If your system has been running 2+ years on the original membrane, budget an additional $30-$50 for a replacement. You'll also want Teflon tape for the housing O-rings if you don't have it.
