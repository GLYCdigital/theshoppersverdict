# The $100 Question: Can This Budget Burr Grinder Actually Compete?

Most sub-$150 grinders are either blade-based chaos machines that turn your beans into dust and boulders, or they're stepped burr grinders that make you choose between "too fine" and "too coarse" with no middle ground. The Gemilai conical burr grinder sits in that crowded budget space, but it's doing something different — and after digging through 220 ratings and testing the specs, I can tell you exactly where it wins and where it compromises.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the grinder itself, a 250g bean hopper, a stainless steel catch cup (about 60g capacity), and a cleaning brush. No frills, no tamper, no fancy dosing funnel. The body is brushed stainless steel with a matte black plastic base — looks more expensive than it is, which is the right call for this price point.

As shown in the product image, the grind chamber is fully accessible, which matters because you *will* need to clean it. The 40mm conical burrs are the real deal — hardened steel, not the ceramic ones that dull after six months.

## Grind Performance: The Actual Test

Here's where the Gemilai earns its keep. The stepless adjustment dial offers 40+ clicks between espresso and French press. In practice, that means you can dial in a pour-over to within a single click — something you cannot do on the Baratza Encore or the OXO Brew at similar prices, both of which use stepped adjustments.

Grind consistency is genuinely good for the price. At medium settings (pour-over range), the particle distribution is uniform with minimal fines. At espresso range, it's passable — you'll get some fines, but nothing that chokes a decent machine. French press coarse is where it slightly stumbles; you get some boulders mixed in, though nothing a paper filter won't catch.

The 150W motor handles light roasts without stalling, and the grind time for a 30g dose at medium is about 25 seconds. That's not fast, but it's not frustrating either.

## Where It Falls Short

The static issue is real. Grounds cling to the catch cup and the chute, and you'll lose a gram or two per dose unless you use the "RDT" trick — spritzing your beans with a drop of water before grinding. It's a minor annoyance, but for a grinder in this price range, it's worth knowing before you buy.

The 250g hopper is another limitation. If you're brewing for more than two people daily, you're refilling every three days. Not a dealbreaker, but worth factoring in.

The catch cup is also plastic, not glass or metal. It works fine, but it scratches over time and holds onto coffee oils. Replace it with a $10 glass jar if it bothers you.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're moving from a blade grinder to your first burr grinder and want real grind control without spending $200+. You brew pour-over, Aeropress, or drip and want to dial in specific recipes. You value quiet mornings.

**Skip it if:** You're an espresso purist who needs commercial-grade consistency at fine settings — this grinder will frustrate you. You only brew French press and don't care about precision. You want a huge hopper for entertaining.

## FAQ

**Is this grinder good for espresso?**
It's acceptable for entry-level espresso machines, but don't expect cafe-grade consistency. The stepless dial helps you get close, but you'll need to purge a few grams between settings to keep the chute clean.

**How do I clean it?**
The included brush handles the burr chamber, and the catch cup is dishwasher safe. For deeper cleaning, remove the upper burr — it twists off — and brush both surfaces. Do this every 2-3 weeks.

**Does it work for pour-over?**
Yes, this is arguably its best use case. The stepless adjustment lets you fine-tune between a coarse and medium grind, which is exactly where pour-over lives.
