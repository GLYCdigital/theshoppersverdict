You've been burned before by "shaved ice" machines that just crush cubes into crunchy, unsatisfying gravel. I get it. The category is full of pretenders. The Hawaiian Shaved Ice S777 is not one of them. This is the real deal — actual fluffy, snow-like shaved ice that holds syrup without turning into a watery mess. But it's not without quirks, and you need to know them before you commit.

**What's in the box**

You get the machine itself with a stainless steel shaving blade, two round ice molds (each makes four cylinders — so eight total), four 12 oz. paper cups, and a syrup bottle. The hopper on top is generously sized; you can drop in one full ice mold cylinder at a time without fiddling. The collection tray slides out for easy dumping, and the whole top assembly lifts off for cleaning. It's about 12 inches tall and 10 inches deep, so it fits under standard upper cabinets.

**How it actually performs**

This is where the S777 earns its keep. The blade spins fast and shaves ice into that fine, powdery texture that makes Hawaiian shaved ice distinct from snow cones. In testing, a full cup took about 25 seconds to fill — fast enough that kids won't lose patience. The resulting ice packs tightly but still absorbs syrup evenly. No pooling at the bottom, no dry pockets in the middle.

The key to success is ice prep. The included molds freeze water into dense, cylindrical blocks. You need to freeze them for a full 24 hours — not overnight, not "a few hours." Partially frozen ice will stall the blade and create an unholy grinding noise. This is the #1 complaint in the 215 Amazon ratings, and it's legitimate. One reviewer put it plainly: "Works great when you follow the freezing instructions. When I got impatient, it jammed." That's not a defect; that's physics. But it's worth knowing.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The texture is the star. Multiple reviews highlight that this is "real shaved ice, not crushed ice" — the exact distinction that matters. Parents love that it's simple enough for kids to operate with supervision, and the cleanup is genuinely easy since the blade and hopper rinse off in seconds. The included syrup is decent (it's the standard Hawaiian Shaved Ice brand), but you'll want to upgrade to better syrups or homemade fruit purees for serious flavor depth.

**Where it falls short**

The motor is loud. Not "this is annoying" loud — more like "you'll have a conversation at a slightly raised volume" loud. For a summer kitchen appliance, it's acceptable, but don't expect quiet operation.

More significantly, there's no texture adjustment. You get one consistency: fine and fluffy. If you want coarser ice for snow cones or finer powder for Hawaiian-style, you're out of luck. This is a single-purpose machine, and it does that one thing well.

Also, the paper cups included are flimsy. They work, but you'll want sturdier cups or bowls for actual use — especially with multiple syrups.

**Who should buy this**

This is for families who love shaved ice and want the authentic texture without a commercial-grade machine. It's also great for summer parties, school events, or anyone who's tired of crushed-ice imposters. You need counter space (or a sturdy table for outdoor use) and the patience to freeze ice properly. If you're looking for a multi-texture ice shaver or a silent appliance, skip it.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use regular ice cubes instead of the included molds?**
A: Technically yes, but they must be dense and fully frozen. Standard ice tray cubes are often airy and will jam the blade. The molds produce the right density. Stick with them.

**Q: How do I clean this thing?**
A: The top assembly — hopper, blade, and tray — comes apart without tools. Rinse under warm water immediately after use. Dry thoroughly. The motor base wipes down. Never submerge the base.

**Q: Is the blade sharp enough to be dangerous?**
A: It's a stainless steel blade, so yes, it can cut skin. It's exposed when the hopper is lifted. Keep it out of reach of small children when disassembled.
