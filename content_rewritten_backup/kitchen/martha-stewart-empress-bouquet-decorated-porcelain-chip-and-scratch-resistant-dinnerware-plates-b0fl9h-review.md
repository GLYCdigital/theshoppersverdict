The Martha Stewart Empress Bouquet line is one of those rare dinnerware sets that actually looks better in person than in the product photos. The floral pattern — a soft, romantic bouquet in muted blues and blush tones against a cream porcelain base — has an heirloom quality that reads more expensive than the price point suggests. But pretty plates are easy to find. What's harder to find is dinnerware that survives a busy household. After digging through 220 Amazon ratings (averaging 4.4 stars) and examining the construction details, here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The Empress Bouquet line includes dinner plates, salad plates, bowls, and mugs, all available individually or in sets. The porcelain is fully vitrified — fired at high temperatures — which is what gives it that distinctive "clink" when you tap it and its density. As shown in the product image, the decoration sits beneath a clear glaze layer, which is the key difference between this and cheaper printed stoneware that scratches off within months.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The standout feature here is the chip resistance. The porcelain body is thicker and denser than standard commercial-grade dinnerware. One reviewer noted, "I've dropped these on tile twice and the plate survived — the tile didn't." That's consistent with the overall feedback pattern. The plates also resist thermal shock reasonably well, though you shouldn't push it with extreme temperature swings.

The weight is worth noting: dinner plates come in around 2 pounds each. That's substantial enough to feel solid but not so heavy that washing them becomes a chore. The glaze is smooth and non-porous, so food residue rinses off easily. Stains from tomato sauce or turmeric haven't been a reported issue, which speaks to the quality of the porcelain body.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

- **Durability**: "We've run these through the dishwasher daily for six months. No chips, no crazing, no fading. The pattern still looks brand new." This is the most common theme across reviews.
- **Aesthetics**: Multiple buyers describe the pattern as "elegant" and "more beautiful in person." The floral design is detailed without being busy.
- **Versatility**: The pieces transition well from everyday meals to holiday entertaining. Several reviewers mention using them for Thanksgiving and Easter without needing a separate "good" set.

**Where It Falls Short**

The microwave issue is the biggest friction point. The listing is ambiguous, and multiple reviewers report the gold trim sparking in microwaves. Even if the porcelain body itself is microwave-safe, that metallic accent is a liability. If you rely on reheating leftovers, this is a genuine consideration.

The gold trim also complicates dishwasher use. While the base porcelain handles machine washing fine, the metallic accents can dull over time. Buyers who've had the set for over a year recommend hand-washing to preserve the trim — which somewhat defeats the convenience factor.

Finally, the pattern options are limited. If you're not enamored with the Empress Bouquet floral specifically, there's no alternative design in this line. You're buying this exact look or nothing.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home cooks who entertain regularly**: The durability-to-elegance ratio is excellent for hosting.
- **Gift-givers**: This makes a strong wedding or housewarming gift, especially paired with the matching serving pieces.
- **Anyone replacing chipped, mismatched sets**: The chip resistance alone justifies the purchase.

**Skip It If**

- **You're a heavy microwave user**: The gold trim issue is a dealbreaker for daily reheating.
- **You prefer minimalist, solid-color dinnerware**: The floral pattern is the entire identity of this line.
- **You want dishwasher-forever convenience**: Hand-washing the trim pieces is the realistic path to keeping them pristine.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this set actually dishwasher-safe?**
A: The porcelain body is, but the gold trim will eventually dull. For best results, hand-wash pieces with the metallic accents. The base porcelain handles dishwasher cycles without issues.

**Q: How many pieces come in the set?**
A: It varies by listing — you can buy individual pieces or sets of 4 or 12. The dinner plates, salad plates, and bowls are all sold separately, so you can build exactly what you need.

**Q: Can these go in the oven?**
A: The porcelain is oven-safe up to about 350°F, but the gold trim may discolor at high temperatures. Stick to serving, not baking, with these.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want durable, genuinely beautiful porcelain that can handle daily use and still look special for holiday dinners.

**Skip it if** the gold trim's microwave limitations or the single floral pattern don't fit your lifestyle or aesthetic.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-made, attractive dinnerware line held back by the metallic trim complications. The porcelain quality itself is excellent, but the maintenance requirements aren't for everyone.
