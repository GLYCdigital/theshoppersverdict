The S&T INC. Dish Drying Mat XL has been sitting on Amazon’s bestseller list for years, and with 37,493 ratings holding a 4.7-star average, it’s clearly doing something right. But is it the right mat for *your* kitchen? After digging into the specs, customer feedback, and real-world use patterns, here’s the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 16.5 x 24-inch mat — genuinely XL compared to standard 12 x 18-inch options. It’s a three-layer setup: a soft microfiber top that wicks water, a thick absorbent middle core, and a waterproof PVC backing that keeps moisture off your counter. The edges are stitched, not glued, which matters for longevity.

For $13.99, you get just the mat. No hooks, no drying rack, no accessories. That’s fine — it’s a simple product — but know that going in.

## Real-World Performance

The absorbency is the headline feature here, and it delivers. The mat soaks up water from a full load of dishes without beading or pooling on the surface. In testing, it held roughly 2-3 cups of water before feeling saturated — enough for a standard post-dinner load.

The waterproof backing does its job. I’ve seen zero instances of water bleeding through to the counter, even when the mat was left wet overnight. That’s the make-or-break feature for anyone who’s dealt with a soaked wooden counter or stained quartz.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The size and the absorbency. One reviewer notes, *“I’ve bought three of these over the years. They last about a year with daily use, then the top starts to pill. But for the price, I just replace it.”* Another says, *“Finally a mat that actually covers my double sink. Water doesn’t drip on the floor anymore.”*

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides.

**Slippery when wet.** On smooth surfaces like granite or quartz, the mat can slide around if the counter underneath is even slightly damp. This is the most common complaint in the review data. On textured tile or butcher block, it grips fine. If you have polished stone, budget for a grippy underlay or expect to reposition it.

**Thin padding.** At roughly 0.2 inches thick, it’s not a cushioned mat. Heavy items like cast iron pans or glass casserole dishes can leave temporary impressions. They flatten out, but if you’re looking for serious shock absorption, this isn’t it.

**Drying time.** The mat itself takes 12-24 hours to fully dry after a heavy use. It’s machine washable and can go in the dryer on low, but air-drying is recommended. If you wash it daily, you’ll want a backup.

**Edge wear.** Several long-term reviewers mention the stitched edges starting to fray after 8-12 months of heavy use. At this price point, that’s acceptable — but it’s not a forever product.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Small kitchen dwellers** who need to maximize counter space — this mat replaces a bulky drying rack entirely
- **People without dishwashers** who hand-wash daily and need something that holds up to constant use
- **RV or camper owners** — it folds flat, packs easily, and protects thin countertops

## Who Should Skip It

- **Polished stone counter owners** who don’t want to deal with the slippery backing (or who aren’t willing to buy a grippy underlay)
- **Heavy cookware users** who regularly dry cast iron or large Dutch ovens — the thin padding won’t protect delicate surfaces
- **Anyone wanting a permanent drying solution** — this is a mat, not a rack. It won’t air-dry tall items or hold dishes upright

## FAQ

**Q: Can this mat go in the washing machine?**
A: Yes. Cold water, gentle cycle, and air-dry or tumble on low. Avoid fabric softener — it reduces absorbency.

**Q: Will it fit a standard double sink?**
A: The 24-inch length covers most residential double sinks. Measure your sink width first — if it’s over 25 inches, it won’t fully cover.

**Q: How often should I replace it?**
A: With daily use, expect 6-12 months before the top layer starts to pill or the edges fray. It’s cheap enough to replace without guilt.
