Let's be direct: grilling fish is the fastest way to ruin a perfectly good piece of salmon. It sticks, flakes apart, and ends up in the coals more often than on the plate. That's exactly the problem the UNCO Grill Basket is designed to solve — and for the most part, it does.

## What You're Actually Getting

This is a two-piece hinged basket, roughly 16 inches long and 5 inches wide, built from what feels like 304-grade stainless steel. The handles extend well past the grate edge, and the hinge mechanism runs the full length of one side. No plastic parts anywhere — a good sign, because cheap baskets with plastic handles melt or crack by the second cookout.

The design is simple: open the basket, lay in your fish or vegetables, close it, and flip the whole thing like a giant spatula. The wire mesh is tight enough to hold most fillets but open enough to let smoke and heat circulate. As shown in the product image, the long rectangular shape fits standard kettle grills and most gas grates without hanging over the edges.

## How It Performs on a Real Grill

I ran this through a weekend of cooking — salmon fillets, a whole trout, and a batch of asparagus. The headline feature works: flipping is genuinely easy. You grab both handles, rotate the basket, and the fish stays put. No spatula gymnastics, no broken fillets, no fish-on-the-ground moments.

The hinge is the weak point though. It swings freely, and there's no latch to hold the two halves together. If you flip one-handed or don't grip both handles evenly, the basket can open slightly and the contents shift. It won't dump everything into the fire, but it's an annoyance you'll notice by the third use.

Heat distribution is solid. The open wire design lets flames lick the food directly, and the stainless steel doesn't warp under high heat — a common failure point on cheaper baskets with riveted handles. Cleanup is straightforward: a soak in hot soapy water and a scrub with a stiff brush handles most residue. Don't run it through the dishwasher; the hinge collects gunk and the wire can discolor.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.3-star average across 215 ratings holds up. The most common praise centers on the flip-and-cook convenience — one verified buyer noted, "This is the first time I've grilled salmon without it sticking or falling apart. Flip is effortless." Another mentioned using it for "vegetables and shrimp skewers" beyond just fish, which matches my experience.

The complaints cluster around two issues. First, sticking: skinless fish still needs a solid oiling or cooking spray before it goes in. Second, the size — a few reviewers with smaller grills found the 16-inch length awkward over side burners or portable grills. Measure your grate before buying.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a locking mechanism is the biggest miss. At this price point, a simple latch would make the basket feel far more secure. The wire spacing also limits versatility — anything smaller than a quarter-inch (peppers, onions, garlic) needs a grill mat or foil underneath. And while the handles don't transfer heat aggressively, you'll still want a mitt for extended cooking sessions.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You grill fish more than twice a month, you're tired of losing fillets to the fire, or you want one tool that handles both delicate proteins and sturdy vegetables. It's also a smart buy for campers and tailgaters who need a rugged, no-plastic tool that survives rough packing.

**Skip it if:** You only grill once or twice a summer, you need a basket for fine diced vegetables, or you expect a latch-locked design for aggressive flipping.

## FAQ

**Does it fit a Weber Kettle?**
Yes. At roughly 16 inches long, it fits standard 22-inch kettles and most full-size gas grills. It will NOT fit small portable grills or side burners.

**Can you put it in the dishwasher?**
Technically yes, but don't. The hinge traps food particles, and dishwasher detergent can dull the stainless finish. Hand wash with soap and a brush.

**Is it actually non-stick?**
No. The stainless steel needs oil or cooking spray before use, especially for skinless fish. With proper prep, sticking is minimal.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The UNCO Grill Basket does its core job — making fish grilling foolproof — with solid materials and a design that genuinely works. The missing latch and limited versatility for small vegetables keep it from perfection, but for anyone who's lost a salmon fillet to the coals, this is a worthwhile upgrade.

**Buy it if** you grill fish regularly. **Skip it if** you need a multi-purpose basket for fine-diced veggies or want a positive-lock hinge. For the price, it's a dependable tool with honest limitations.
