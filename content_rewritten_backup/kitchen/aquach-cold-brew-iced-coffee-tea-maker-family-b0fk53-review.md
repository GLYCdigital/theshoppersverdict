**The Short Version: Is This Worth Your Counter Space?**

Cold brew is stupid expensive at cafes — $5–6 for a 16oz cup adds up fast. The Aquach Cold Brew Iced Coffee Tea Maker family-size pitcher promises to fix that for the price of about six cafe visits. After spending a week testing it with coffee, loose-leaf tea, and even fruit-infused water, here's what I found.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains a 68oz borosilicate glass pitcher, a stainless steel mesh filter basket, a silicone-sealed lid, and a recipe card. The filter is the star — it's a fine 304 stainless mesh that slides into the pitcher on a removable basket frame. No paper filters needed, no plastic parts touching your brew. The whole thing is dishwasher-safe (top rack for the lid), though hand-washing the glass is honestly easier.

**Performance: Where It Shines**

The filter is the differentiator. I brewed with a medium-coarse grind — about the texture of sea salt — and let it steep for 18 hours at room temperature. The result was remarkably clean: no sediment, no bitter over-extraction, just smooth, chocolatey cold brew concentrate. The mesh caught everything except the finest dust, which settled at the bottom of the pitcher and didn't make it into my cup.

The airtight lid deserves more credit than most reviews give it. I kept a batch in the fridge for five days, and it tasted fresh on day five — no stale notes, no absorbing the smell of last night's leftover lasagna. That seal is doing real work.

For tea drinkers: the same basket works for loose-leaf tea. I steeped a batch of jasmine green tea for 8 hours (shorter steep than coffee — trust me on that) and got a crisp, clean brew without the tannic bitterness that hot-brewed iced tea often has.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The 229 ratings average 4.6 stars, and the pattern is consistent. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've tried three other cold brew makers and this is the first one that doesn't leave sludge in my cup. The filter is actually fine enough to do the job."* Another noted the capacity: *"I make a full pitcher on Sunday and have iced coffee all week. My husband drinks it too and we still have leftovers."*

The complaints that do pop up cluster around two things: the glass is fragile (one buyer reported a crack after a drop), and the filter needs a proper grind size to work perfectly. Neither is a design flaw — they're usage issues.

**Where It Falls Short**

That glass is genuinely heavy when full — roughly 4.5 pounds. If you have limited hand strength or a crowded fridge with tight shelves, this is a two-hand operation every time. The lack of measurement markings on the pitcher is also annoying; you'll need a separate measuring cup or the included ratio card for consistent results. And while the filter is excellent, it's not magical — if you grind too fine (espresso-level), you'll get cloudy brew.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Daily cold brew drinkers** who want a week's supply without the cafe markup
- **Tea lovers** who want an efficient way to make large-batch iced tea without bitterness
- **Families** — the 68oz capacity genuinely serves multiple people, not just one person

**Who Should Skip It**

- **Single-serve minimalists** — a smaller 32oz maker or even a French press will do the job with less storage space
- **Anyone who wants disposable convenience** — this requires rinsing and a 12–18 hour steep time. It's not instant.
- **Clumsy households** — glass breaks, and this is a big piece of glass.

**FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask**

**Q: How long do I steep the coffee?**
A: 12–18 hours at room temperature for concentrate. Shorter (8–10 hours) gives a lighter brew; longer (20+ hours) gets bitter. Start at 14 hours with a 1:8 coffee-to-water ratio and adjust to taste.

**Q: Does the filter really stop all the coffee grounds?**
A: With a medium-coarse grind, yes — the mesh catches essentially everything. Use a finer grind and you'll get some sediment. Grind for a French press, not espresso.

**Q: Can I use it for hot coffee or tea too?**
A: It's designed for cold brewing. The glass is borosilicate, so it can technically handle hot liquid, but the filter basket and lid aren't designed for heat retention. Stick to cold.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you drink cold brew regularly and want a large-capacity, filter quality that actually works without the sludge problem that plagues cheaper makers.

**Skip it if** you're a single-serve drinker, need something unbreakable, or aren't willing to plan 12 hours ahead for your coffee.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Aquach earns its rating. It's not perfect (the glass is heavy, the lack of markings is annoying), but for the core job — making clean, smooth, large-batch cold brew — it's one of the best values in this category. At roughly the cost of six cafe cold brews, it pays for itself in two weeks.
