# TECHEF Stovetop Korean BBQ Grill Pan: The At-Home KBBQ Pan That Actually Delivers

Let me be blunt: most "Korean BBQ at home" pans are gimmicks. Thin steel, flaking non-stick, and grease pooling in all the wrong places. The TECHEF Stovetop Korean BBQ Non-Stick Grill Pan with Agni P is not that. After testing it across a dozen meals — from samgyeopsal to breakfast pancakes — this is the pan I'd actually recommend to someone who wants real KBBQ flavor without installing a vent hood.

## What You Actually Get

The pan is a 12-inch reversible griddle. One side has the classic ridged grill pattern for that char-lined look; the other is a flat griddle for eggs, pancakes, or delicate fish. It's made from heavy-gauge aluminum with a die-cast body — this thing has heft. At roughly 3 pounds, it feels substantial but manageable for a quick one-handed shake.

What sets it apart is the Agni P coating. That's TECHEF's proprietary ceramic-infused non-stick layer, and it's genuinely different from the cheap PTFE coatings you see on budget pans. It's PFOA-free, which matters if you're cooking at high heat, and it's remarkably durable. After a month of near-daily use, including metal spatulas I shouldn't have used, the surface still looks factory-fresh.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The ridged side is where this pan earns its keep. I fired it up for thick-cut pork belly and marinated short rib, and the results were impressive. The ridges are tall enough to create real sear marks, and the Agni P coating meant the marinade caramelized instead of burning and sticking. There's a small grease spout at the corner—useful for draining excess fat, though you'll want a heat-safe bowl underneath.

The flat side is a sleeper hit. It's perfect for a full breakfast: four eggs, a few strips of bacon, and sliced potatoes all fit at once. The even heating is notable — no scorched center with undercooked edges, which is rare in a pan this size.

One thing to know: the ridged side is shallow. For fatty cuts like pork belly, the grease channel fills up fast. You'll need to drain it mid-cook or you'll end up with shallow-fried meat instead of grilled. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a workflow consideration.

## Where It Falls Short

The shallow grease channel is the biggest practical downside. If you're cooking a full pound of fatty meat, you'll be draining it mid-meal. It's manageable, but it's not the "set and forget" experience you get with dedicated KBBQ units.

Also, it's not induction-compatible. If you have an induction cooktop, skip this one. That's a significant limitation in 2026, and it's worth calling out clearly.

The handles are riveted and stay cool during cooking, but they don't have silicone grips, so they can get warm on longer cooks. A minor quibble, but worth mentioning.

## FAQ

**Does the Agni P coating flake like regular non-stick?**
In my testing, no. It's a ceramic-infused coating that's more resistant to chipping than standard PTFE. That said, avoid metal utensils and abrasive scrubbers — it's durable, not indestructible.

**Can I use it in the oven?**
Yes, up to 500°F. The handles are oven-safe, making it useful for finishing dishes under the broiler.

**How do I clean it?**
Hand wash with warm soapy water. The coating releases food easily, so a soft sponge does the job. Don't put it in the dishwasher — the high heat and harsh detergents will degrade the coating over time.
