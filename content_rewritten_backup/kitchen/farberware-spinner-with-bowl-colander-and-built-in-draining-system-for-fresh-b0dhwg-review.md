**The Farberware Spinner with Bowl, Colander, and Built-in Draining Is Smarter Than It Looks — Here's Who Should Buy It**

Most salad spinners are a variation on the same 50-year-old design: a basket, a lid with a crank, a bowl that collects water. The Farberware Spinner with Bowl, Colander, and Built-in Draining system tries something slightly different — and honestly, it mostly works. After a week of heavy use (baby spinach, chopped romaine, herbs, and a pound of strawberries), here's the real story.

**What's in the box**

You get three pieces: the clear plastic outer bowl, the perforated colander basket, and the lid with the spinning mechanism. The built-in draining feature is the differentiator — the outer bowl has a pour spout and a strainer lip built into the rim, so after you spin, you tip the whole assembly over the sink and the water pours out through the spout while the colander basket holds your greens in place. No separate strainer step. No fishing wet lettuce out of a bowl of water. That's genuinely convenient.

**How it performs in real use**

The spinning action is smooth. The push-button brake on top stops the basket almost instantly, which sounds minor but saves real time when you're making dinner and don't want to watch a plastic basket wind down for 15 seconds. The basket locks into the bowl with a firm click — I tried to shake it loose and couldn't. That's a meaningful build-quality point, because cheaper spinners wobble or pop out mid-spin.

The built-in draining spout works as advertised. Spin your greens, tilt the whole unit over the sink, and the water exits through the spout while the colander catches everything. It's a genuinely better workflow than the traditional "lift basket, dump water, put basket back" dance. I timed myself: from wet greens to dressed salad, the Farberware saved about 30 seconds per batch compared to my old OXO. That adds up.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews tracks with what I found. Multiple reviewers highlight the same things:

*"I love that I don't have to dirty a separate colander. The built-in draining is genius."*

*"The brake button is the best feature — no more waiting for the basket to stop spinning."*

Another reviewer noted: *"Fits perfectly in my cabinet and the lid actually stays on when stored."* That storage point is real — the unit stacks compactly, and the lid seals well enough that you can store washed greens in the bowl for a day without them drying out.

**Where it falls short**

The lid doesn't have a locking latch. It sits on by friction, which is fine for normal spinning, but if you overload the basket with dense, heavy greens (think wet kale), the lid can pop loose mid-spin. It happened to me once; no mess, but it startled me and stopped the spin. Solution: don't overpack. Stick to about two-thirds full.

The basket capacity is modest — roughly 5 quarts. For a family of four making a side salad, it's perfect. For meal-prepping a week's worth of greens at once, plan on two batches. Also, the brake button requires a firm press; smaller hands might find it slightly stiff.

**Who should buy this**

- **Busy home cooks** who make a salad or washed produce part of every meal — the built-in draining genuinely saves steps
- **Small households** (1–3 people) where the 5-quart capacity is right-sized
- **Anyone replacing a worn-out spinner** who wants a straightforward, no-gimmick upgrade with better draining

**Who should skip it**

- **Meal preppers** washing multiple pounds of greens at once — you'll want a larger-capacity spinner (OXO's 8-quart is the benchmark)
- **People with limited hand strength** — the brake and crank both need a firm grip

**FAQ**

**Q: Can you wash berries in this without crushing them?**
A: Yes, if you're gentle. The basket's perforations are small enough to hold blueberries and strawberries, but don't spin them hard — a few gentle cranks to remove water is all they need. The brake helps here, stopping the basket before centrifugal force turns your berries to mush.

**Q: Is the bowl dishwasher-safe?**
A: All pieces are top-rack dishwasher-safe, but hand-washing is faster. The basket rinses clean in seconds, and the bowl's smooth interior doesn't trap water or debris.

**Q: Does the built-in drain really eliminate the need for a separate colander?**
A: For washing and spinning greens, yes. You can also use the colander basket alone over the sink for rinsing produce — it has stable little feet, so it stands on its own.

**The Verdict**

The Farberware Spinner with Bowl, Colander, and Built-in Draining is a solid 4.6/5. It won't replace a high-end OXO for heavy-duty use, but for everyday salad-making, the integrated drain is a genuinely thoughtful design improvement that saves time and dishes. It's not perfect — the friction-fit lid and modest capacity are real limitations — but for the price, it's a smart buy for most home kitchens.

**Buy it if** you want a spinner that streamlines the wash-to-salad workflow without taking up a ton of cabinet space.
