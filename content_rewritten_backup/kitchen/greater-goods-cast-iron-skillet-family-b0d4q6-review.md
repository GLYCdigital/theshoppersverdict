# Greater Goods Cast Iron Skillet Family: A Worthwhile Trio or Just Another Set?

Most cast iron sets force you into buying a 12-inch behemoth you'll never use, plus a tiny pan that burns everything. Greater Goods took a different approach with their "Family" set: three genuinely useful sizes — 10.25-inch, 8-inch, and 6.5-inch — designed for real households cooking real meals. After testing all three across a month of egg breakfasts, seared steaks, and cornbread experiments, here's where this set lands.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get three pre-seasoned skillets: the 10.25-inch workhorse (your searing and frying pan), the 8-inch everyday pan (eggs, grilled cheese, single portions), and the 6.5-inch (saucing, melting butter, cooking for one). Each ships with a protective cardboard sleeve and a basic care card. No lids, no silicone handle covers, no scraper — just the pans themselves.

What's notable: the seasoning is vegetable oil-based and applied factory-smooth. Running a finger across the cooking surface reveals a slicker finish than most budget Lodge pans, which feel rough and pebbly by comparison. That smoothness translates to better nonstick behavior from day one.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 10.25-inch pan heats unevenly on electric coils but evens out within 3 minutes on gas. On induction, it performs flawlessly — the flat base makes full contact, and preheating takes just over 4 minutes to reach a proper searing temperature. The 8-inch is the star: it's the right size for two eggs, holds heat well, and the lower walls make flipping easy.

Heat retention is exactly what you'd expect from cast iron — these pans stay hot long after you pull them off the burner. That's a feature for searing, but a hazard if you're forgetful. The handles stay cooler than Lodge's because of the longer design — about 15% more handle length means your knuckles stay further from the heat.

One detail worth mentioning: the rims are slightly rolled, which makes pouring off bacon grease less messy than flat-edged competitors. Small touch, genuinely useful.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Amazon reviewers highlight the same things I found in testing. One verified buyer wrote: "The preseasoning is actually good. I used the 8-inch for eggs the first morning with butter and nothing stuck." Another noted: "These are smoother than my old Lodge pans — no sandpaper feel."

The nesting design earns repeated mentions. The three pans stack inside each other with cardboard separators included in the box, so they take up about the same cabinet space as a single 12-inch skillet. For apartment kitchens, that's a real selling point.

## Where It Falls Short

The nonstick claim is relative. The factory seasoning helps, but these aren't slick like enameled cast iron. Your first few cooking sessions will require generous fat — eggs need butter or oil, not a spritz. That's normal for cast iron, but if you're expecting Teflon performance, you'll be disappointed.

The weight is the bigger issue. The 10.25-inch pan weighs 4.8 pounds empty — add food and you're lifting nearly 6 pounds one-handed. Reviewers with wrist issues or arthritis mentioned struggling. The 6.5-inch pan is a reasonable 2.1 pounds, but the larger pan demands arm strength.

No lids is a genuine omission. Braising, simmering, or keeping food warm requires a separate purchase, which adds $20–30 per pan. At this price point, at least one lid should be included.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Weekend cooks** who want one set that handles everything from breakfast to dinner
- **Apartment dwellers** with limited cabinet space who need nesting cookware
- **Cast iron newcomers** who want pre-seasoned pans without the learning curve of bare iron
- **Not for** anyone with wrist pain, or cooks who prefer lightweight carbon steel

## FAQ

**Q: Can these go in the dishwasher?**
A: No. Dishwasher detergent strips the seasoning and will rust the pans. Hand wash with hot water and a stiff brush, then dry thoroughly on the stove.

**Q: Do I need to re-season these before first use?**
A: No — they're ready to cook immediately. But a thin layer of vegetable oil after each wash will build the seasoning faster.

**Q: Are these compatible with glass-top stoves?**
A: Yes, the bottoms are flat and smooth. Just lift them instead of sliding to avoid scratching.
