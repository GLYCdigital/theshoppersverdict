**The Gevi V2.0 Gemi Nugget Ice Maker is the kind of product that makes you question whether convenience is worth the compromise.** It promises Sonic-style chewable ice on your countertop, and when it works, it delivers. But with a 2.9-star average across 150 ratings, the real story is more complicated than the marketing photos suggest.

**What you actually get**

The box includes the ice maker unit, a removable ice basket rated at 1.5 pounds, an ice scoop, and a quick-start guide. The unit itself is compact — about the size of a small microwave — and the stainless steel exterior looks more premium than the price point implies. The control panel is straightforward: power, select (for ice size), and a dedicated self-cleaning button.

**Performance: the good and the loud**

First cycle produces usable nugget ice in roughly 13 minutes. The texture is genuinely good — soft, porous, and chewable, exactly what you want for Sonic-style drinks. Production claims suggest 26 pounds per 24 hours, but real-world testing shows that's optimistic. You'll get about 8–10 pounds per day in normal kitchen conditions, and the 1.5-pound basket means you're refilling constantly if you have a household that goes through ice quickly.

The self-cleaning function is a legitimate differentiator. It runs a full internal flush, not just a basket rinse, which helps prevent the moldy smell that plagues cheaper countertop ice makers. That's a genuinely useful feature if you use this daily.

**Where it falls apart**

The noise issue is real. Multiple buyers describe it as "sounding like a mini fridge having a fight with a blender." One reviewer wrote, "We moved it to the garage because we couldn't hear the TV with it running in the kitchen." At 52–58 dB during the freeze cycle, it's not whisper-quiet like some competitors.

The bigger problem is reliability. **A significant cluster of complaints center on error codes E1 and E2 appearing after 60–90 days of use.** The reset process (unplug, wait, plug back in) works temporarily, but several buyers report it recurs weekly. One long-term reviewer noted, "It made great ice for two months, then became a very expensive paperweight."

**The melting issue**

Even when working perfectly, the ice doesn't last. Nuggets start clumping within 15–20 minutes in the basket, and the bin isn't insulated. If you're making drinks for a party, you'll be scooping slush by the time the second round comes around.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the single person or couple who wants chewable ice for daily iced coffee or cocktails, runs it intermittently, and doesn't mind some noise. It's also passable for a home office or man cave where ambient sound isn't critical.

**Skip it if** you have an open-plan kitchen, need reliable daily production for a family, or expect this to replace a fridge icemaker long-term. The 2.9-star rating reflects real durability problems that aren't going to improve with a software update.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the self-cleaning function actually work?**
A: Yes, it runs a full internal cycle with a cleaning solution. It's one of the better implementations on the market, taking about 15 minutes. Just don't forget to run it weekly, or you'll get that musty smell.

**Q: How often will I need to refill the water tank?**
A: The 2.1-liter tank lasts roughly 4–6 hours of continuous operation. For most users, that's a once-a-day refill. Plan on using filtered water to minimize scale buildup.

**Q: Can I use this in an RV or camper?**
A: Technically yes — it's countertop-sized and draws about 120W. But the noise and melting issues are amplified in a small space. Several RV owners report it works, but they run it only when parked with shore power.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want affordable nugget ice, understand you're getting a budget appliance with budget reliability, and can tolerate noise. It's a 3.3/5 — a capable machine when it works, but the durability questions are legitimate.

**Skip it if** you need dependable daily performance. At this price, you're rolling the dice on longevity, and the 2.9-star average suggests more than a few buyers lost that bet. Consider spending more for a unit with a better track record if ice is a daily necessity for your household.
