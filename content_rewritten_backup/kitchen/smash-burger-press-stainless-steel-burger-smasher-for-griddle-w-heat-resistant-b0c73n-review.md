## What You Actually Get

The package is refreshingly simple: the press itself, no unnecessary accessories or filler pieces. The entire unit is stainless steel — a critical detail I'll get into shortly. The pressing surface is flat and smooth, measuring wide enough to handle a standard 4-6 ounce burger ball without crowding. The handle rises at a comfortable angle, giving you the leverage needed to apply real, even pressure without your knuckles hovering over a 400-degree surface.

## Performance and Real-World Testing

The core job here is simple: flatten hot meat quickly and evenly. This press does that well. The flat bottom surface means no ridges or patterns — just pure, even compression. That's exactly what you want for maximum Maillard reaction, and the thin patties cook in about 90 seconds per side, developing those coveted crispy, caramelized edges.

The stainless steel construction matters more than you might think. Cheaper presses often use aluminum with a non-stick coating that scratches and flakes. This one is solid steel — it won't react with acidic ingredients, won't shed coating into your food, and cleans up with a quick wipe. One thing I noticed during testing: the handle does warm up after prolonged use on a large griddle. It's not burn-your-hand hot, but if you're cooking six patties in a row, you'll feel the heat transfer.

## What Buyers Are Saying

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the biggest limitation is the lack of adjustable weight. True smash burger enthusiasts know that a heavier press (think 2+ pounds) creates thinner patties with more crust with less effort. This one requires you to actively press down — which is fine for most people, but it's not a set-it-and-forget-it tool. The warm handle during marathon cooking sessions is a minor annoyance, not a safety hazard.

## FAQ

**Is this press dishwasher safe?**
Technically yes, since it's stainless steel, but hand-washing with warm soapy water is better. The heat during cooking will burn off most residue anyway — a quick wipe while warm is usually all it needs.

**Can I use this on a regular skillet or only a griddle?**
It works on any flat cooking surface — cast iron skillet, carbon steel pan, or electric griddle. Just make sure your pan is screaming hot (400-450°F) before you drop the meat.

**Does it come with parchment paper or patty papers?**
No, it's just the press. But you don't need them — the press doesn't stick to the meat if you use a small piece of parchment between the press and the patty, or just press directly into the meat as intended.
