**The 48-ounce twist on single-serve brewing: Is bigger actually better?**

Most single-serve coffee makers force you into a proprietary pod ecosystem or limit you to a tiny 4-cup tank. The Cuisinart DGB2 takes a different swing: it brews ground coffee directly into your favorite mug, with a 48-ounce reservoir that outlasts nearly everything in its class. But at 3.8 stars from 190 ratings, the real-world experience isn't flawless. Here's what you need to know before adding it to your cart.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the brewer base, a 48-ounce removable water reservoir, a permanent gold-tone filter basket, a drip tray, and a scoop. No pods, no capsules, no proprietary anything — you use your own ground coffee. That's a meaningful cost saver over the long run, since you're not locked into $0.70-per-pod pricing. The unit itself measures roughly 11 inches deep and 9 inches wide, with a footprint that fits under most standard upper cabinets.

**How it performs in real use**

Operation is refreshingly straightforward: fill the tank, scoop in ground coffee, choose your brew size (6, 8, or 10 ounces), and hit the button. The heater kicks in quickly, and a full 10-ounce brew completes in about 90 seconds. The gold-tone filter catches fines well, so you won't see sludge in the bottom of your cup. As shown in the product image, the swing-out brew head makes cleaning the filter basket a 10-second job.

The 48-ounce tank is the headline feature, and it delivers — you can brew four to five cups before refilling, which beats the 32-ounce tanks on most comparable units. For a household with two coffee drinkers, that's a morning's worth of coffee from a single fill.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Owners repeatedly highlight the convenience of using their own grounds. One reviewer noted, *"I was tired of buying K-cups every week. This thing pays for itself in a month."* Another praised the build quality: *"Solid weight, feels like a real appliance, not a plastic toy."* The compact footprint also earns consistent mentions for fitting neatly on cluttered countertops.

**Where it falls short**

The temperature inconsistency is the most common complaint. Several reviewers report that the first brew of the day comes out hot enough, but subsequent brews run cooler. One owner put it bluntly: *"My coffee was lukewarm by the third cup. I had to microwave it."* That's a real issue if you brew multiple cups back-to-back.

The drip tray is also shallower than it looks. Tall travel mugs can splash over the edges, leaving a mess on your counter. And there's no auto-shutoff timer beyond the standard 30-minute idle cutoff — if you want coffee waiting for you at 7 AM, this isn't the machine for you.

**Who should buy this**

This brewer suits the budget-conscious daily drinker who buys quality beans and wants to control strength and quantity without pod markup. It's also a solid fit for small kitchens where counter space is tight, or for households that don't need a full 12-cup carafe. Skip it if you need programmable brewing, if you prefer the convenience of pods, or if you drink more than five cups in a row without refilling the tank.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use paper filters instead of the gold-tone filter?**
A: No — the brew basket is designed specifically for the permanent filter. Paper filters won't sit correctly and can collapse during brewing.

**Q: How often do I need to descale?**
A: Every 2-3 months with white vinegar or a commercial descaler, depending on your water hardness. The reservoir is removable, which makes the descaling rinse cycle easier than on fixed-tank models.

**Q: Does it work with ground espresso?**
A: Yes, but use a fine grind sparingly — the filter can clog if you overfill. Start with a level scoop and adjust from there.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a no-nonsense, pod-free single-serve brewer with a large tank and simple controls. The cost-per-cup savings are real, and the build quality justifies the price for most users.

**Skip it if** consistent brewing temperature across multiple consecutive cups matters to you, or if you need programmable start times.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A capable, practical brewer with a few thermal quirks that keep it from greatness.
