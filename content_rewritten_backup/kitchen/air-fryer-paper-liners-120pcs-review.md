The air fryer is arguably the most-used appliance in my kitchen. The cleaning after? The least favorite part of my day. Baker's Signature's 120-pack of paper liners promises to fix that for $11.99. After two weeks of daily use across two different air fryer models, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains 120 square liners, each measuring 8 inches across. That's the first thing to verify before you buy — measure your basket. The liners are made of food-grade parchment with a perforated pattern across the bottom. The perforations aren't just for show; they allow hot air to circulate up through the liner while the parchment catches the grease and crumbs that would otherwise bake onto your basket's surface. As shown in the product image, they're designed to sit flat in the basket, not wrap up the sides like some cup-style liners.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these in a 5.8-quart basket-style air fryer and a smaller 3-quart model. In the larger unit, the liner fit flat with about a quarter-inch gap on each side — enough for airflow, not so loose that food slides off the edge. The perforations held up their end of the bargain: chicken wings came out with crispy skin on the bottom, not soggy from pooled grease. The liner caught virtually all the rendered fat, and cleanup was a matter of lifting the paper, folding it, and tossing it. No scrubbing.

The parchment weight is noticeably thicker than the generic liners I've used before. I loaded it with a full pound of frozen french fries and six breaded chicken thighs without a single tear. That matters — flimsy liners rip when you're lifting them out, and then you're scraping burnt paper off your food.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.6-star rating across 24,510 reviews isn't accidental. A verified buyer noted, "I was skeptical about the perforations but they really do help with air flow — my fries come out crispy on all sides, not just the top." Another long-term user said, "I've repurchased these three times. They fit my Instant Pot Vortex perfectly and I haven't scrubbed the basket in months." The recurring theme is fit and durability — people who measured their baskets first are overwhelmingly satisfied.

**Where it falls short**

This is where you need to check your specific model. These are flat square liners. If your air fryer has a raised center prong — some Ninja and Philips models do — the liner won't sit flat, and you'll have to cut a hole in it or skip it entirely. That's a dealbreaker for some owners. Also, at roughly 10 cents per liner, it's not the cheapest option on Amazon. The bargain packs at half the price exist, but they're thinner and tear more often. You're paying for the durability, and for most users that tradeoff is worth it.

One more thing: don't use these above 425°F. Parchment has a smoke point, and pushing past it will give you burnt edges and a smoky kitchen. Most air fryer recipes stay below that, but worth noting.

**Who should buy these**

- **The daily air fryer user** who's tired of soaking baskets in soapy water every night — this is your shortcut.
- **The meal prepper** who batches chicken, fish, or roasted veggies multiple times a week and wants consistent cleanup.
- **The family cook** making frozen foods like nuggets, fries, and mozzarella sticks — those are the messiest items and these liners handle them well.

**Skip them if** you have a center-prong basket, you're on a tight budget and don't mind scrubbing, or you cook most things above 425°F.

**FAQ**

*Can I reuse these liners?* Technically yes, if they're clean and intact after cooking. But they're designed for single use, and the perforations weaken after one cycle. Don't push it.

*Do they fit a 6-quart basket?* The 8-inch square fits most 4-6 quart models, but measure first. The gap between liner edge and basket wall should be at least a quarter inch for proper airflow.

*Are they safe for food contact?* Yes, they're food-grade parchment and BPA-free. No wax coating, no chemicals leaching into your food.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want to cut air fryer cleanup time from five minutes of scrubbing to five seconds of lifting and tossing. **Skip it if** your model has a center prong or you're okay with cheaper, thinner alternatives.

**Rating: 4.0/5** — Solid value, real durability, but the fit limitations on certain models keep it from a perfect score.
