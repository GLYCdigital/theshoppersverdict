Let me be direct: most silicone candy molds on Amazon are cheap, flimsy, and produce blobs that look nothing like the product photos. The Palksky Mini Dinosaur set is better than that — but with caveats you need to know before you buy.

**What's actually in the box**

You get three separate molds, each about 6.5 inches long and 4 inches wide. The set includes a T-rex, a triceratops, and a dragon (which honestly looks more like a winged dinosaur than a traditional Chinese dragon). Each mold has 4 cavities, so you'll produce 12 candies per batch. That's it — no recipe book, no dropper, no storage container. Just the silicone.

The detail work is the standout feature. As shown in the product image, the molds have sharp, deep impressions — scales, claws, and even individual teeth are clearly defined. This matters because shallow molds produce washed-out shapes. These don't.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these with both chocolate and a standard gelatin gummy recipe. The non-stick claim holds up: finished pieces pop out with a gentle push from the back, no greasing required. The silicone is genuinely food-grade — no chemical odor even when new, which is rare at this price point.

The mini size is the bigger story. Each dino comes out roughly 1.5 inches from nose to tail. That's perfect for topping cupcakes or adding to a dessert charcuterie board, but it's noticeably smaller than a standard gummy bear. If you're expecting full-size candies, adjust your expectations now.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.2-star rating across 210 reviews reflects strong satisfaction with the detail. One reviewer noted, *"The scales and eyes actually show up in the finished gummies — my kids were thrilled."* Another confirmed the durability: *"I've used these at least 20 times for resin and chocolate. Still no warping or melting."* The versatility is real — buyers use them for candy, bath bombs, and epoxy crafts.

**Where they fall short**

The thin silicone is the main complaint. The walls are flexible to the point of being floppy, so filling without spilling requires a piping bag or steady hands. One frustrated reviewer wrote, *"The mold bent while I was pouring and chocolate ended up everywhere."* That's not a defect — it's the tradeoff for the easy release. But it's worth knowing.

Cleaning is also fiddly. The deep cavities trap residue, and while they're dishwasher-safe, hand-washing with a soft brush is honestly easier. And if you're planning to make large batches for a party, the 12-cavity total means multiple rounds.

**Who should buy this**

- **Parents making themed party treats**: The detail will impress kids, and the size is ideal for lunchbox surprises.
- **Crafters doing resin or soap**: The deep cavities handle epoxy well, and the flexibility means easy demolding.
- **Bakers experimenting with chocolate**: The crisp definition holds up in tempered chocolate.

Skip it if you need full-size gummy bears or you're looking for a single large mold for efficiency.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these safe for hot liquids?**
A: Yes. The silicone is rated from -40°F to 446°F, so melted chocolate (around 115°F) and boiling gelatin are fine. Don't use them in the oven above 400°F.

**Q: How many candies come out per batch?**
A: Exactly 12 — four cavities per mold, three molds. The pieces are small, roughly bite-sized.

**Q: Can I use these for resin crafts?**
A: Absolutely. Many buyers do. Just use a release agent for epoxy resin, and clean thoroughly between uses.
