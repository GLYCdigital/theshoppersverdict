Let's be honest: most bakeware under $25 is a gamble. You either get pans that warp after three uses, or ones that release cakes so poorly you're eating crumbs with a fork. The Fig & Leaf 9-Inch Round Pans sit in that budget tier, yet they've earned a 4.6-star average from 229 ratings. That's not accidental.

I tested these pans across multiple bakes — layer cakes, a dense pound cake, even a cornbread. Here's what actually matters.

## What You Get

The set includes two 9-inch round pans with 2.25-inch sidewalls. That depth is significant — many budget pans are shallower at 1.5 to 2 inches, which limits how much batter you can use and makes leveling cakes trickier. These handle a standard boxed mix plus a bit extra without overflow risk.

The construction is carbon steel with a dark nonstick coating. They're lightweight but not flimsy — no noticeable flex when handling with a full batter. The rolled rims are smooth, which means no sharp edges digging into your hands and nothing catching on oven racks.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The standout trait here is even baking. I ran a two-layer vanilla cake side by side with a pricier aluminum pan, and the Fig & Leaf pans produced identical browning — no dark ring around the edge, no pale center. The carbon steel conducts heat consistently, and the dark coating absorbs it well.

Release is genuinely nonstick. Cakes dropped out cleanly after a 10-minute rest, and I only needed a light butter-and-flour prep, not parchment circles. One buyer noted, *"I've used these weekly for six months and my cakes still release perfectly — no sticking, no residue."* That mirrors my experience.

The pans are also oven-safe to 450°F, which gives headroom for recipes that need higher initial temps. They're not just cake pans — I used one for a frittata and another for roasting a small batch of vegetables. They performed fine.

## Where They Fall Short

The dark coating is a double-edged sword. It promotes browning, which is great for cakes, but you'll need to adjust your oven temperature down by 5-10°F or check doneness a few minutes early. I burned the edges of a chocolate cake on the first attempt because I followed the recipe's standard temp. That's a learning curve, not a dealbreaker.

Second, these are hand-wash only. The product description says dishwasher-safe, but multiple buyers report the coating degrading in the dishwasher. One review warned, *"Put them in the dishwasher once and the nonstick starts peeling."* If you're a dishwasher-everything household, these aren't for you.

Finally, the pans are lightweight enough that they can slide on a sheet tray if you're moving them in and out of the oven quickly. Not a functional issue, but worth noting.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You bake layer cakes regularly and want reliable, even results without spending $40+ per pan. You're willing to hand-wash and adjust oven temps slightly. You want a versatile 9-inch pan that can handle cakes, quick breads, and savory bakes.

**Skip them if:** You rely on your dishwasher for cleanup, or you prefer lightweight aluminum pans with lighter browning characteristics. If you bake professionally and need commercial-grade thickness, step up in price.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these pans truly 9 inches?**  
Yes. The top diameter measures 9 inches across. The bottom is slightly narrower (about 8.25 inches) due to the tapered walls, which is standard for round cake pans. Recipes calling for 9-inch pans will work correctly.

**Q: Do I need to grease them if they're nonstick?**  
Yes, lightly. The nonstick coating handles clean release, but a thin layer of butter or spray ensures even browning and makes release foolproof, especially with sticky batters like carrot cake.

**Q: Can I use metal utensils on these?**  
Technically yes, but avoid it. The coating is durable but not scratch-proof. Use silicone or wood tools to extend the pan's lifespan.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Fig & Leaf 9-Inch Round Pans deliver what matters most: even baking, reliable release, and enough durability to survive regular use. The dark coating and hand-wash requirement are real tradeoffs, but for the price, these outperform most comparable sets. If you're a home baker who wants consistent layer cakes, this is a solid buy. Just remember to adjust your oven temp and keep them out of the dishwasher.
