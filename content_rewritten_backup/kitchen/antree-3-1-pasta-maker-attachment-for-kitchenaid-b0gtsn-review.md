If you’ve priced the official KitchenAid pasta roller set, you already know it costs nearly as much as a small car payment. The ANTREE 3-in-1 attachment undercuts that price significantly, and with a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it’s clearly gaining traction among home pasta makers. But does it actually hold up to the OEM version, or is this a case of "you get what you pay for"? I dug into the specs, the build, and what buyers are saying after months of use.

## What’s in the Box

The ANTREE arrives as a three-piece kit: a 150mm (about 6-inch) roller for lasagna and flat sheets, a fettuccine cutter, and a spaghetti cutter. All three attach to the front hub of your KitchenAid stand mixer (tilt-head and bowl-lift models, excluding the Artisan Mini). Construction is stainless steel with a clear ABS plastic guard on the cutters—a design choice that helps you see the pasta feeding through but does raise a long-term durability question.

## Performance and Build Quality

The roller does exactly what it should: it produces smooth, even sheets from dough that’s been properly rested. The 6-inch width matches the KitchenAid brand attachment, so you're not losing capacity. The adjustment knob clicks through nine thickness settings, from thick lasagna sheets down to paper-thin for ravioli. One buyer noted, *"It handled a stiff egg dough without flexing or making noise—the gears feel solid, not cheap."* That’s a good sign, because cheap gears are the usual failure point on budget pasta rollers.

The cutters are where this kit separates itself. The fettuccine and spaghetti blades are sharp and produce clean, uniform strands—no tearing or ragged edges. The clear plastic hopper guard feeds the dough evenly, but it feels noticeably lighter than the metal guards on the pricier models. It’s functional, not premium.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Beyond the price, the most common thread in positive reviews is that the ANTREE simply works out of the box. Multiple reviewers mention they were skeptical of a third-party attachment, then found it performed identically to the KitchenAid brand set they'd previously owned. One verified purchase wrote, *"I've made pasta four times in the last two weeks. The fettuccine is perfectly even, and cleanup takes two minutes."* Another praises the roller’s consistency: *"The sheets come out the same thickness every pass—no more hand-cranking."*

## Where It Falls Short

Here’s the honest part. The plastic guard on the cutters is the weak point. A handful of reviewers report the guard cracking after a few months, especially if they were aggressive feeding dough. It doesn’t affect cutting performance, but it’s a cheap-feeling piece in an otherwise solid kit.

The clamps that attach to the mixer hub are also slightly tighter than the OEM version. Some users report needing to wiggle the attachment to get it fully seated, and one reviewer noted, *"It fits fine, but you have to push harder than I'd like to lock it in place."* Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you wrestle with it mid-recipe.

Finally, there’s no cleaning shortcut. The cutters are not dishwasher safe—you’ll need to brush them dry with a stiff-bristled brush (a small one is included). Anyone expecting to toss these in the dishwasher will be disappointed, but that’s standard for this category.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The Weekend Pasta Enthusiast:** If you make fresh pasta monthly, not weekly, this is the perfect middle ground between a hand-crank and a $150+ brand attachment.
- **The Budget-Conscious KitchenAid Owner:** You want the convenience of a mixer-driven roller without the premium price tag.
- **The Gift Giver:** This makes a practical, high-value gift for anyone who already owns a KitchenAid and mentions fresh pasta more than twice a month.

Skip it if you run a commercial kitchen or make pasta daily—in that case, the all-metal construction of the brand version is worth the upgrade. Also skip if you have an Artisan Mini; it won’t fit that model.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my KitchenAid model?**
A: It fits all standard tilt-head and bowl-lift models (Artisan, Pro, Professional, etc.) with the standard hub. It does *not* fit the Artisan Mini. Check the product listing’s compatibility list if you’re unsure.

**Q: Can I use it to make lasagna sheets?**
A: Yes, the roller produces sheets up to 6 inches wide. You can run the dough through multiple times at decreasing thickness settings for perfectly even lasagna noodles.

**Q: Is it dishwasher safe?**
A: No. The cutters have a plastic guard and must be hand-cleaned. Brush dry flour off immediately after use; do not soak. The roller can be wiped with a dry cloth.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if:** You want KitchenAid-level pasta performance for a fraction of the cost and you’re willing to hand-clean the cutters.

**Skip it if:** You require all-metal construction or need to run a high-volume pasta operation.

The ANTREE 3-in-1 is the rare third-party accessory that genuinely rivals the OEM product. It has one plastic component that feels like a cost-cutting measure, but the core metal roller and sharp cutters deliver flawless pasta every time. For the price, it’s the smartest pasta attachment you can clip onto a KitchenAid right now.
