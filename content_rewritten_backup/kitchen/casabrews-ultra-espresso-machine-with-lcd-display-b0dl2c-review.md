## Real-World Performance: The 15-Bar Pump vs. The 9-Bar Reality

The big selling point here is the 15-bar Italian pump, but here’s the truth every espresso enthusiast knows: **9 bars is what actually extracts espresso**. CASABREWS includes a 15-bar pump because it's a marketing number that sounds impressive. In practice, the machine throttles to a proper 9-bar extraction pressure, which is exactly what you want. The result is a shot with actual honey-colored crema, not the soapy foam you get from cheap pressurized baskets.

The LCD display is genuinely useful. The shot timer runs automatically, so you stop obsessing over a stopwatch and just watch the flow. **The milk frothing is where it gets real.** The steam wand is a commercial-style articulating wand, and it produces microfoam that's actually latte-art-worthy. ## Where It Falls Short (Honest Tradeoffs)

**The tamper is garbage.** It's a lightweight aluminum disc that doesn't fit snugly in the basket. You'll want a proper 58mm calibrated tamper ($20-30) immediately. Factor that into the total cost.

**The drip tray is shallow.** With the portafilter in place, you can't fit a tall espresso cup underneath. You'll need to remove the drip tray or use shorter cups. It's a design oversight that gets annoying.

**No automatic shut-off.** The machine stays on until you manually power it down. It's a minor energy waste, but for a machine in this price range, I'd expect an auto-off timer.

**The steam wand takes practice.** It's powerful—almost too powerful. If you're new to milk frothing, you'll spray hot milk everywhere before you learn to angle it properly. Not a flaw, but a learning curve.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I use ground coffee, or do I need a grinder?**
A: You can use pre-ground coffee, but you'll get dramatically better results with freshly ground beans. The 58mm portafilter is forgiving, but a basic burr grinder will elevate your shots significantly.

**Q: Does the LCD display show pressure?**
A: Yes, it displays pressure in real-time, but it's a reference gauge, not a precision instrument. It's useful for diagnosing grind issues (too fine = high pressure, too coarse = low pressure).

**Q: Is this loud?**
A: The pump is audible—similar to a mid-range blender—but it's not obnoxious. The steaming is quieter than most budget machines.
