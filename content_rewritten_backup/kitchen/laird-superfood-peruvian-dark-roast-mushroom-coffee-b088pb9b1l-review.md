# Laird Superfood Peruvian Dark Roast Coffee with Functional Mushrooms: The Honest Breakdown

Most mushroom coffees taste like you're drinking a forest floor that got roasted by mistake. This one doesn't. That's the first thing you need to know about Laird Superfood's Peruvian Dark Roast — it actually tastes like coffee. Real, smooth, slightly chocolatey coffee with zero of that earthy, funky mushroom aftertaste that plagues nearly every competitor in this category.

I've tested a dozen functional mushroom coffees over the years, and most end up as expensive pantry ornaments after one cup. This one earned a permanent spot in my morning rotation. Here's the full picture.

## What You Actually Get

A 12-ounce bag of certified organic, fair-trade Peruvian dark roast ground coffee. The roast is blended with Chaga and Lion's Mane mushrooms — 500mg of the proprietary adaptogen mix per serving. That's a modest dose compared to standalone mushroom supplements, but it's on par with other functional coffees in this price range.

The grind is consistent, medium-fine, and works well in drip brewers, pour-over, and French press. The bag itself is resealable, which matters more than you'd think for keeping the beans fresh over a few weeks of daily use.

## Real-World Performance

**The taste is the headline here.** Peruvian beans are naturally low-acid, and Laird's dark roast amplifies that smoothness. You get a deep, slightly nutty cup with hints of cacao and almost no bitterness. I brewed it side-by-side with a $20 bag of specialty dark roast I keep around, and honestly? Laird held its own. The mushroom blend is essentially invisible on the palate.

**The energy profile is where this gets interesting.** Each serving has about 75mg of caffeine — roughly 60% of what you'd get from a standard Starbucks drip coffee. But the combination of Chaga and Lion's Mane changes the delivery. No spike, no crash. Just steady, clear-headed focus that ramps up over 30 minutes and holds for hours. Multiple reviewers echo this: "I don't get the jitters at all, but I'm not tired either. It's like my brain is just... sharper."

**The brewing experience** is straightforward. It's ground coffee — nothing fancy. It doesn't foam, doesn't require special preparation, and doesn't leave weird residue in your mug like some mushroom blends I've tried. Cleanup is normal.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**The price stings.** At $35 for 30 servings, you're paying about $1.17 per cup. That's not outrageous for specialty coffee, but it's a real commitment if you're a 2-cup-a-day person. That's roughly $70 a month just for coffee.

**The mushroom dose is modest.** 500mg of the Chaga/Lion's Mane blend per serving is below what most clinical studies use for measurable adaptogenic effects. The coffee itself is the main event; the mushrooms are a nice bonus, not a therapeutic dose. If you're buying this specifically for cognitive benefits, you may need to pair it with a standalone supplement.

**The roast is dark — genuinely dark.** If you prefer light or medium roasts with bright, fruity notes, this will taste heavy and one-dimensional to you. It's smooth, but it's not nuanced.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The skeptical wellness-curious coffee drinker** who wants the potential benefits of functional mushrooms without sacrificing coffee quality.
- **The sensitive-stomach caffeine consumer** who loves coffee but can't handle the acid and jitters of regular roasts.
- **The low-caffeine focus-seeker** who wants a single clean cup for morning work sessions, not a triple-espresso spike.

**Skip it if** you're a light-roast enthusiast, you need clinically significant adaptogen doses, or you're on a tight coffee budget.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this taste like mushrooms?**
A: No. The dark roast and Peruvian beans completely mask the mushroom flavor. If I didn't tell you, you wouldn't know it's there. The only detectable difference is a slightly smoother mouthfeel.

**Q: How much caffeine is in a serving?**
A: About 75mg — roughly 60% of a standard cup of drip coffee. It's enough for alertness but won't give you the jitters or the 3pm crash.

**Q: Can I use this in an espresso machine or French press?**
A: Yes, both work well. The grind is medium-fine, which suits French press and moka pots. For espresso machines, you might want a finer grind, but it'll still pull a decent shot.
