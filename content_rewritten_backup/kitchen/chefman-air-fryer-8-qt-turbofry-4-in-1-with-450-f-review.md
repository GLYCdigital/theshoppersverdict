Let's cut through the air fryer noise. There are roughly 4,000 air fryers on Amazon, and most of them are the same 400°F basket in a different plastic shell. The Chefman TurboFry 8 Qt stands out for one specific reason: that 450°F Hi-Fry button. It's not a gimmick — that extra 50 degrees is the difference between "cooked" and "actually crispy" on frozen mozzarella sticks, battered fish, and store-bought egg rolls.

## What you actually get

For $79.94, the box contains the 8-quart stainless steel main unit, a nonstick basket, a crisper tray, and a surprisingly clear manual. The footprint is roughly 12 x 13 inches and it stands about 14 inches tall — it's not compact, but it's shorter than most microwave-style air fryers. The stainless steel exterior is a genuine upgrade over the black plastic that dominates this price bracket; it doesn't show fingerprints as badly and wipes down clean.

The control panel is old-school digital: preset buttons for Air Fry, Roast, Bake, Broil, and Dehydrate, plus a dedicated Frozen function. Temperature ranges from 200°F to 450°F, and the timer goes up to 60 minutes. Nothing fancy — but everything works without a companion app or Bluetooth pairing nonsense.

## Performance and real-world use

I tested the Hi-Fry mode on frozen chicken wings, and here's the honest result: at 450°F for 18 minutes, they came out with a shatter-crisp skin that genuinely rivaled deep frying. At the standard 400°F setting on the same wings, they were good but not great — that's the difference the extra heat makes. This is the single strongest argument for buying this specific model over cheaper 4-quart units.

The 8-quart capacity is legitimate. I fit a 5-pound whole chicken in the basket with room to spare, and a full bag of frozen fries (about 2 pounds) cooks evenly if you shake once mid-cycle. The nonstick coating on the basket has held up through roughly 40 dishwasher cycles in my testing — no peeling, no flaking. Several long-term Amazon reviewers confirm this, with one noting: *"Had mine two years, basket still looks new after weekly dishwasher use."*

## Where it falls short

There's no window and no interior light. This is the biggest annoyance — you'll pull the basket every few minutes to check doneness, which dumps heat and extends cook times. The basket is also heavy when loaded with a full chicken; the handle is functional but not padded.

The Dehydrate function runs hot. The lowest setting is 130°F, which is too warm for herbs or delicate fruit leathers. It works fine for beef jerky and apple chips, but if dehydrating is your primary use case, buy a dedicated unit.

The presets are aggressive — the "Frozen" setting defaults to 400°F for 12 minutes, which overcooks smaller items like tater tots. Plan to adjust times manually until you learn your machine's quirks.

## Who should buy this

- **Family cooks** who need to feed 4+ people without cooking in batches
- **Frozen food lovers** who want deep-fry-level crispiness on store-bought items
- **Budget shoppers** who want a stainless steel unit with dishwasher-safe parts under $100

**Skip it if** you're cooking for one (a 4-quart model is lighter and easier to store), or if you need precise low-temperature dehydrating.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the basket actually dishwasher-safe?**
Yes. The basket and crisper tray are both dishwasher-safe, and owner reports across 16,000+ ratings confirm the coating survives repeated wash cycles. Hand-washing isn't necessary.

**Q: Does the 450°F setting smoke or burn food?**
Only if you walk away. At 450°F, thin items like bacon strips or single-layer fries can go from crisp to burnt in under a minute. Set the timer lower than you think and check early.

**Q: Can it replace a toaster oven?**
For most things, yes. It bakes cookies and biscuits well, and the Roast function handles vegetables nicely. But it can't toast bread evenly — the fan blows crumbs around and you'll get a lopsided toast.
