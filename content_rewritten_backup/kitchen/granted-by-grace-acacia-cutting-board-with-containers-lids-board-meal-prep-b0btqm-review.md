## This isn't a gimmick — but it's not a full meal-prep system either

The Granted By Grace acacia cutting board with containers and lids sits in a crowded category of "board + storage" hybrids. Most are flimsy plastic with hollow compartments that crack within a month. This one is different: it's real acacia wood, it's substantial, and the integrated containers actually work. But it has limits you need to know before you buy.

## What's actually in the box

You get one acacia cutting board (roughly 15 x 10 inches) with three stainless steel containers nested into the base, plus matching lids. The containers sit flush with the board's surface when covered, so you can chop directly over them without catching a lip. The board includes a juice groove around the perimeter and a built-in handle for hanging.

The acacia is finished with what appears to be a food-safe oil coating. It's smooth out of the box — no splinters, no rough edges around the container wells. The stainless containers are decent gauge, not the flimsy pop-can metal you see on cheaper sets.

## Real-world performance

I chopped onions, bell peppers, and raw chicken on this board over two weeks. The surface holds up well — no deep knife scars, and the juice groove caught everything without dripping onto the counter. The containers work best for pre-chopped ingredients you'll cook within a day or two. Dice your onions, scrape them in, snap the lid on, and they're ready when you are.

The sealing isn't airtight. One buyer noted: "The lids fit but they're not leakproof. I wouldn't put anything liquidy in them and expect it to survive a bag toss." That matches my experience. Fine for dry or semi-dry prep, not for dressings or marinating liquids.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across 215 ratings, the most common compliments center on build quality and design integration. One verified purchaser wrote: "Finally a cutting board that doesn't take up extra counter space with separate bowls. The containers are perfect for mise en place." Another noted: "The acacia is beautiful. It's become my daily driver for veggie prep."

The wood's resistance to staining also comes up repeatedly. Beets, carrots, and turmeric — the usual board-ruiners — wiped clean with mild soap and water. That's a legitimate advantage over bamboo or cheaper hardwoods.

## Where it falls short

The containers are smaller than most shoppers expect. At roughly 1-cup capacity each, they're ideal for diced garlic, herbs, or a single onion — not for batch-prepping meals. If you're picturing three full meal-prep portions, you'll be disappointed.

The wood also demands maintenance. Acacia is a hardwood, but it's not self-oiling. Skip mineral oil treatments for a few weeks and you'll see the surface dry out, with hairline cracks appearing along the grain. One reviewer warned: "Beautiful board, but you have to baby it. I learned the hard way."

And at this price point, there's no dedicated knife included — some competing sets bundle one. Not a dealbreaker, but worth noting.

## Who should buy this

- **Home cooks who do mise en place** — if you prep ingredients before cooking, this keeps your station organized without extra bowls.

**Skip it if** you need large-capacity meal prep storage, or if you're not willing to maintain the wood with regular oiling.

## FAQ

**Can I put the containers in the dishwasher?**
The stainless steel containers are dishwasher-safe, but the wood board is not. Hand-wash the board and the lids. The lids may warp in a dishwasher's heat cycle.

**Is this board safe for raw meat?**
Yes, with caveats. Acacia is dense and naturally resistant to absorbing liquids, but you should hand-wash with hot soapy water immediately after use and dry thoroughly. For heavy raw meat prep, consider a dedicated plastic board.

**How often do I need to oil the board?**
Every 2-4 weeks with regular use, or whenever the surface looks dry or dull. Food-grade mineral oil is the standard choice — avoid vegetable oils that can go rancid.
