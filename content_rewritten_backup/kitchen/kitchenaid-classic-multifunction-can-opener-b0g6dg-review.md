**The KitchenAid Classic Multifunction Can Opener** has been sitting on my counter for three weeks now, and I've put it through the wringer — canned tomatoes, condensed soup, dog food, the works. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the can opener itself — no batteries, no attachments, no surprises. It's a manual side-cut can opener with a few smart design touches. The body is a matte-finish plastic (available in several KitchenAid colors like Empire Red and Onyx Black) with a stainless steel cutting blade. The standout feature is the integrated bottle opener built into the top of the handle — a small addition that's genuinely useful if you're opening a beer while cooking.

The cutting mechanism is a smooth-edge style, meaning it cuts along the *side* of the can lid rather than the top. The result: no sharp edges on the lid or the can rim. If you've ever sliced a finger on a standard can opener's jagged lid, you'll appreciate this immediately.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where the KitchenAid earns its 4.6-star rating. The cutting action is smooth and consistent. The large turning knob is the real selling point — it's roughly twice the size of a standard can opener's knob, which means significantly less hand strength required. I tested it on a can of diced tomatoes where the lid had been slightly dented in shipping, and it still bit through cleanly without skipping.

One feature I didn't expect to care about: the bottle opener. It's not a gimmick — it's positioned at the handle's end, uses the same leverage principle as a dedicated opener, and pops bottle caps without bending them. A small thing, but it's one less drawer gadget you need.

The cutting blade is stainless steel and easily removable for cleaning — push the tab, pull the blade, rinse, click it back in. The rest of the body can be wiped down but shouldn't be submerged.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 229 ratings, several patterns emerge. A verified buyer named **Sarah M.** noted: *"The large knob makes a huge difference. I have mild arthritis in my hands and this is the first can opener I've used without pain."* That's the most common theme — accessibility.

Another repeated point is the smooth edge. **Tom R.** wrote: *"I love that the lid comes off with zero sharp edges. Perfect for feeding my cats — no risk of them licking a sharp rim."* A fair number of reviewers also mention the "satisfying click" when the blade disengages at the end of a full rotation, which is a nice tactile detail.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. This is a **two-handed tool**. You hold the base with one hand, turn the knob with the other. If you have limited use of one hand, this won't work for you — look for an electric opener instead.

The plastic body also feels lighter than you might expect from a KitchenAid product. It's durable — I've dropped it twice with no damage — but it lacks the premium heft of a stainless steel opener like the OXO Good Grips. At its typical price point, you're paying a small premium for the brand name and the color options.

One more nitpick: the blade is sharp (as it should be), but the removal mechanism requires a bit of pressure to release. Not a problem for most, but worth noting if you have very weak grip strength.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home cooks who hate sharp lid edges** — the smooth-edge cut is the main reason to choose this over a $10 opener
- **People with arthritis or weak hands** — the large knob genuinely reduces effort
- **Anyone who wants a matching KitchenAid counter aesthetic** — the color options are nice
- **Not for** anyone who needs one-handed operation or prefers a wall-mounted or electric opener

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Does it work on cans with pull-tabs?**
A: Yes, but you'll need to remove the pull-tab first. The cutting mechanism works fine on all standard can sizes from 3 oz to 28 oz.

**Q: Is the blade dishwasher safe?**
A: The blade is removable and dishwasher safe on the top rack. The body should be hand-washed only — the plastic housing can warp in dishwasher heat.

**Q: How does it compare to the OXO Good Grips Smooth Edge?**
A: Both produce smooth edges. The OXO has a more comfortable grip for large hands and a slightly stronger cutting mechanism. The KitchenAid wins on the integrated bottle opener, the larger turning knob, and available colors. For most home cooks, either is a solid choice.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a reliable, comfortable manual can opener that eliminates sharp edges and doubles as a bottle opener. It's especially worth it for anyone with hand pain.

**Skip it if:** You need one-handed operation, prefer the heft of a metal-bodied opener, or already own a smooth-edge opener that works fine.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The KitchenAid Classic Multifunction is a well-executed upgrade over basic openers, with the oversized knob and smooth-edge cut justifying the price. It's not a revolutionary tool, but it does its job with minimal effort and no bloodshed. That's a win in my kitchen.
