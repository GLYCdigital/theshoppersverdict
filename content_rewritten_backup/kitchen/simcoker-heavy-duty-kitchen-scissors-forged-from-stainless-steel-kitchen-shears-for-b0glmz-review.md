Most kitchen scissors are flimsy afterthoughts — the kind you grab for opening mail and regret using on a chicken thigh. The SimCoker Heavy-Duty Kitchen Scissors are not that. These are forged, not stamped, which makes all the difference when you're staring down a whole bird or a pile of thick-cut bacon.

Here's what you actually get: a pair of full-tang, one-piece stainless steel shears that run about 11 inches tip to handle. Because they're forged from a single block of steel, there's no hinge pin to work loose over time. The blades are ground thin enough to slice cleanly, but the spine is thick enough that they won't flex when you're putting real pressure on them. The handles have a rubberized overmold that gives you traction even when your hands are slick with poultry juices.

**How they perform in real kitchens**

I've put these through the standard gauntlet: breaking down whole chickens, snipping through raw bacon strips, trimming green beans, and even cracking a few lobster claws. The built-in notch near the pivot — that little half-circle cutout — is the star feature. Slide a chicken bone into it, squeeze, and you get a clean snap without the violent crunching you'd expect from lesser shears. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I've owned three pairs of kitchen shears in the last two years. These are the first ones that haven't loosened up after a month of daily use."

The blades hold their edge remarkably well. After several weeks of regular use, I haven't noticed any dulling on the main cutting surface. They'll handle herbs and vegetables fine, but these are really built for meat, cartilage, and shell — don't expect them to replace your chef's knife for precision work.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews isn't an accident. The dominant theme in customer feedback is durability. Multiple reviewers mention using them for years without any loosening or rusting. Another common thread: how easy they are to clean. Since there's no spring mechanism or complex joint, they rinse off easily under hot water. One satisfied buyer noted, "I was skeptical about the price, but after breaking down six chickens in one afternoon with zero hand fatigue, I'm a believer."

**Where they fall short**

Nothing's perfect, and these have two honest drawbacks. First, there's no locking mechanism. If you toss them in a drawer with other utensils, the open blades are a minor hazard. A blade guard or lock would be a meaningful improvement. Second, the size — at 11 inches with substantial handles, they're not ideal for people with smaller hands or anyone who wants a compact kitchen tool. If you're looking for something to keep in a drawer for occasional snipping, these are overkill.

**Who should buy these**

This is the right purchase for three specific people: the home cook who breaks down whole chickens or roasts regularly, the hunter or fisher who needs to process game and clean fish, and anyone who's gone through three pairs of cheap scissors in the past year. If you're a vegetarian who only needs to open packaging, buy a $5 pair instead — you won't use what these can do.

**FAQ**

*Can these go in the dishwasher?* Technically yes, but hand-washing is better. Hot water and detergent can degrade the rubberized grips over time, and the blades will stay sharper longer if you dry them promptly.

*Will they cut through chicken bones?* Yes, that's what the notch is designed for. It handles raw poultry bones, ribs, and small game bones without much effort. Just don't try them on beef shanks or thick pork bones — that's what a cleaver is for.

*Do they come apart for cleaning?* No, they don't disassemble. But because the design is one solid piece, you can rinse the whole thing under running water and wipe it dry. Food doesn't get trapped in crevices the way it does with hinged scissors.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you cook meat regularly and want one tool that will last a decade. **Skip it if** you have small hands, want a locking mechanism, or only need scissors for light kitchen tasks.

These are the rare kitchen purchase that actually earns its place on the counter. At this price point, you're paying for forged construction and genuine longevity instead of marketing hype. That's a trade I'll make every time.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a strong, durable workhorse with two minor design oversights that keep it from perfection.
