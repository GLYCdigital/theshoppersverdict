**The Collapsible Microwave Steamer That Actually Earns Its Drawer Space**

Most collapsible kitchen gadgets are gimmicks — flimsy plastic things that collapse when you don't want them to and hold less than they promise. This silicone steamer from the "Microwave Steamer Cooker Collapsible Bowl" line is different. It's one of those rare products where the space-saving design doesn't compromise the actual cooking job.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The package includes the silicone bowl, a vented lid with a steam-release hole, and integrated side handles that fold flat when not in use. In its expanded state, the bowl holds roughly 1.5 quarts — enough for a full bag of frozen broccoli, four ears of corn, or a pound of baby potatoes. Collapsed, it stacks down to about 2 inches tall, which is the headline feature here. If you've got a cramped apartment kitchen or an overstuffed cabinet, that flat profile makes a real difference.

The material is food-grade silicone — no BPA, no phthalates, no weird chemical smell when you first open the box. It's flexible enough to collapse but sturdy enough to hold its shape when filled with water and vegetables.

**How It Performs in Real Use**

Here's the honest breakdown. Steaming vegetables in the microwave works — but it's not identical to a stovetop steamer. You'll need about 2 minutes per cup of vegetables, plus a resting period of 1-2 minutes after the microwave stops. The lid's steam vent does its job: it releases pressure gradually rather than letting the lid pop off mid-cycle.

One thing that surprised me: cleanup is genuinely easy. Silicone doesn't hold onto food residue the way plastic does. A quick rinse with warm soapy water handles most messes, and stubborn bits wipe off with a sponge. No scrubbing required.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.4/5 rating across 220 reviews reflects a pattern of satisfied users. One reviewer noted, "I was skeptical about the collapsible design, but this thing actually holds a full head of cauliflower when expanded. It's become my go-to for veggies." Another wrote, "The steam vents are the difference maker — my old steamer would whistle and spray water everywhere. This one just works quietly."

The space-saving claim gets consistent shoutouts. Multiple reviewers mention that they now keep it in a drawer instead of taking up a cabinet shelf, and that the silicone doesn't absorb odors from garlic or onion-heavy dishes.

**Where It Falls Short**

The floppiness issue is real. When the bowl is full of hot vegetables and water, the silicone walls don't provide much rigidity. You'll need both hands to carry it from the microwave to the sink or counter — one hand on each handle, moving slowly. It's not dangerous, but it's not a one-handed operation like a rigid plastic steamer.

There's also no measurement markings inside. For a product where water quantity matters (too little = scorched veggies, too much = soggy), that's an annoying omission. You'll learn the right amount through trial and error — roughly 2-3 tablespoons for leafy greens, 1/4 cup for root vegetables.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Small kitchen dwellers**: If cabinet space is precious, the collapsible design is worth the tradeoff.
- **Meal-preppers who eat lots of vegetables**: This makes 5-minute steamed broccoli or green beans effortless on weeknights.
- **RVs, dorm rooms, or office kitchens**: It packs flat and only needs a microwave.

**Skip it if** you regularly steam for more than two people (the 1.5-quart capacity means batch cooking), or if you prefer precise water measurements without guessing.

**Three Questions Shoppers Ask**

**Q: Can I use this in the dishwasher?**
A: Yes, but put it on the top rack only. The lid is more prone to warping than the bowl, so hand-washing the lid extends its life.

**Q: Will it fit a standard microwave?**
A: Expanded, it's about 8 inches in diameter and 4 inches tall. It fits in any standard countertop microwave — even the compact 0.7-cubic-foot models.

**Q: Does it actually steam, or just heat up food?**
A: It steams — the water at the bottom boils, and the rising steam cooks the food above it. You'll get properly cooked, not microwaved-soggy, vegetables if you follow the timing guidelines.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a space-saving steamer that handles everyday vegetable cooking without fuss, and you're okay with a slight learning curve on water amounts.

**Skip it if** you need large-capacity steaming, prefer rigid cookware, or want dishwasher-everything convenience without exceptions.

At 4.2/5, this is a solid purchase for the right buyer. It's not perfect — the floppy-when-full design and missing markings are genuine flaws — but for the price and the space it saves, it earns its place in most kitchens.
