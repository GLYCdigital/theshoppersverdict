## What You're Actually Getting

The MMmat comes as a single mat (roughly 16.5" x 11.7" — standard half-sheet pan size), though some listings offer two-packs. What matters isn't the size, it's the construction. This is a **fiberglass mesh core sandwiched between two layers of German-sourced silicone** — not the cheap Chinese silicone that smells like a tire fire at 350°F. The mat is 0.7mm thick, which is the sweet spot: thick enough to hold its shape, thin enough to transfer heat efficiently for crispy cookie bottoms.

The surface has a subtle texture — not slippery-smooth like some mats. That's intentional. It creates micro-air pockets that help baked goods release without needing a full grease layer.

## Performance: Where It Shines

I ran this mat through the gauntlet: sugar cookies at 375°F, roasted vegetables at 425°F, and a sticky caramel sauce spill that would destroy lesser mats. Results were consistent:

- **Roasted veg gets actual browning** — the mat doesn't insulate the pan the way thicker (1mm+) mats do.

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**Heavier than budget mats.** At roughly 0.7mm, it's sturdier, but that means it doesn't fold flat for storage. You'll need to roll it or lay it flat in a drawer. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**Price is higher than generic mats.** You're paying for the German silicone and the fiberglass reinforcement. If you bake once a month, the cheaper alternative will do. If you bake weekly, this pays for itself in parchment paper savings within months.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I cut the mat to fit my pan?**
A: Yes, but don't. Cutting the fiberglass core will cause the edges to fray and eventually separate. Buy the right size instead.

**Q: Does it work for freezing?**
A: Yes. The silicone stays flexible down to -40°F. Use it to freeze cookie dough balls, then pop them off easily.

**Q: Will it stain from tomato sauce or turmeric?**
A: Silicone can stain with highly pigmented foods, but stains don't affect performance. A paste of baking soda and water usually lifts them.
