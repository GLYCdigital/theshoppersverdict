If your spice drawer looks like a chaotic pile of mismatched bags and half-empty glass jars, the Mifoci 28-pack is a serious upgrade. But before you hit buy, know exactly what you're getting — because at 16 ounces and square, these aren't your typical 4-ounce shaker bottles. They're bulk storage workhorses.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get 28 identical jars with black screw-on caps. Each jar holds 16 fluid ounces (473 ml) — roughly the size of a standard pasta sauce jar. The plastic is clear, BPA-free PET, and the square design is the standout feature. As shown in the product image, these stack flush against each other with no wasted gaps, which matters if you're organizing a deep pantry shelf or a narrow cabinet.

The caps are simple one-piece screw tops with a thin foam liner inside. No shaker holes, no pour spouts — this is storage, not dispensing. You'll need your own measuring spoons.

## Performance and Build Quality

I filled a full set: turmeric, cumin, paprika, oregano, bay leaves, and even a few with rice and lentils. The 16 oz size is genuinely useful for buying spices in bulk (which is cheaper per ounce by 40-60% at most grocers). The square footprint let me fit 14 jars on one 24-inch shelf — try that with round jars.

The caps sealed well during a 10-minute shake test with water and olive oil — zero leaks. But here's the honest caveat: these are not vacuum-sealed. The foam liner compresses, but it's not a gasket. Over three months, my paprika darkened slightly from humidity exposure. Fine powders need a desiccant packet or a tighter jar.

The plastic is thick enough to survive a drop onto tile (I tested one — no crack), but it's not scratch-proof. After five dishwasher cycles, the jars clouded noticeably. Hand washing keeps them crystal clear.

## Where It Falls Short

Three real downsides, no sugarcoating:

1. **Not truly airtight** — for long-term storage of moisture-sensitive spices (paprika, garlic powder, onion flakes), you'll see clumping after a few months. Buy silica packets.
2. **Dishwasher damage** — the plastic clouds and the cap liners can warp. These are hand-wash-only if you want them to look good.
3. **Caps are basic** — no shaker holes, no hinged lids. If you want to sprinkle directly from the jar, you'll need a separate shaker top or transfer to smaller bottles.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Bulk spice buyers**: If you shop at Costco, restaurant supply stores, or ethnic grocers, these pay for themselves by letting you store 2-3x more per shelf.
- **Meal preppers**: 16 oz is perfect for storing grains, beans, dried fruit, or trail mix in uniform, stackable containers.
- **Small restaurants/cafés**: The durability and stackability make them practical for back-of-house ingredient storage.

**Skip them if** you need airtight, long-term spice preservation (go glass with gasket lids), or if you want dispensing caps.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these jars food-safe?**  
A: Yes — BPA-free PET plastic, same material as commercial food packaging. They're fine for dry goods, but don't microwave or freeze them.

**Q: Can I write on the caps?**  
A: The black caps take a Sharpie fine, but it rubs off with handling. Use adhesive labels on the jar body for a permanent solution.

**Q: Do the 16 oz jars fit standard spice racks?**  
A: No. These are 3.4" tall and 3.1" wide — too large for most countertop racks designed for 4-6 oz bottles. They're built for pantry shelves or drawers.
