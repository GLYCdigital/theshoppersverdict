If you've ever dumped half a teaspoon of loose leaf tea into a mug and spent the next ten minutes fishing leaves out of your teeth, you already understand the appeal of a fillable tea bag. The NEPAK Empty Tea Bags are a straightforward solution to that problem — and at 4.5 stars from 225 ratings, they're clearly working for most people. But let's dig into whether they're actually right for *your* tea routine, because there are some tradeoffs the product page doesn't emphasize.

## What's Actually in the Box

The NEPAK set includes 100 empty tea bags, a small scoop for measuring leaf, and a heat sealer to close the bags. The bags themselves are made from food-grade nylon — not paper. That matters for two reasons. First, nylon is more durable than paper filters, so you can steep the same bag two or three times if you're brewing a tea that holds up to multiple infusions. Second, the material has a very fine mesh, which means even finely ground teas like rooibos or chamomile won't leak sediment into your cup.

The bags measure roughly 2.75 inches by 3.5 inches — a standard size that fits comfortably in most mugs and teapots. The heat sealer is a simple plastic clamp-style tool; you fill the bag, press the sealer across the top, and it creates a tight, waterproof seal in about two seconds.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these with a few different tea types — a bulky white tea with large leaves, a fine CTC Assam, and a chunky herbal blend with dried fruit pieces. The nylon mesh handled all three without issue. The large-leaf white tea packed easily, the fine Assam stayed contained, and the herbal blend didn't burst the seams even when I overfilled slightly.

The heat seal is the standout feature. Unlike drawstring bags that can come undone or paper bags that require folding and stapling, the heat sealer creates a permanent closure. No leaf matter escapes, and you can even shake the bag gently during steeping to encourage extraction without worrying about it splitting open.

One thing to note: the bags have **no strings or tags**. That means you'll need to either use a teapot with a built-in strainer basket, or you'll be reaching into hot water to retrieve the bag after steeping. A pair of tongs or a slotted spoon solves this, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of strings is the most common complaint — and it's legitimate. If you're brewing single cups directly in a mug, you'll need a separate tool to fish the bag out. This design works best with a teapot or a mug with an infuser basket.

The 100-count quantity is another consideration. If you drink two or three cups a day, you'll burn through these in about a month. At this price point, that's not unreasonable, but it's not the most economical option for heavy tea drinkers. Buying loose leaf in bulk and portioning it into these bags saves money versus pre-bagged tea, but only if you're drinking enough to justify the purchase cycle.

Finally, the nylon material is *not* compostable. If you're someone who composts your spent tea leaves, these bags won't break down in a backyard compost pile — you'll need to empty the leaves out first and dispose of the bags separately.

## Who Should Buy These

These are ideal for three types of people:

1. **Loose leaf drinkers who want convenience** — you get the freshness and quality of loose leaf with the grab-and-go simplicity of a bagged tea.
2. **Tea gifters** — fill them with your favorite custom blend, seal them, and hand them out. They look professional and hold up well.
3. **Anyone who travels** — pre-filled bags are compact, durable, and don't require a strainer at your destination.

Skip them if you're a one-cup-at-a-time drinker who uses a mug directly, or if you're looking for a compostable option.

## FAQ

**Are these bags safe for boiling water?**
Yes. The food-grade nylon is heat-resistant up to boiling temperatures. Steep times of 3–5 minutes at 212°F won't damage the material or leach anything into your tea.

**Can I use these for coffee grounds or herbs?**
Technically, yes. The fine mesh will contain coffee grounds and dried herbs. The bags are often used for spice blends and herbal infusions, though the flavor transfer between uses is minimal if you buy separate boxes.

**How do I close the bag without the heat sealer?**
You can tie the top in a knot — the nylon is flexible enough to hold a knot securely. It's not as clean as the heat seal, but it works in a pinch.
