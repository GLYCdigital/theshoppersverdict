Let's be direct: most cold brew makers are over-engineered plastic contraptions that cost $40 and break by August. This one is different. It's a thick mason jar with a stainless steel filter basket dropped inside. That's it. And that simplicity is exactly why it works.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the 1-quart borosilicate glass jar, a stainless steel mesh filter cylinder, and a pour lid that fits snugly enough to shake without leaking. As shown in the product image, the filter basket sits suspended in the jar — coffee grounds stay contained while water circulates around them. No paper filters, no plastic parts touching your brew, no complicated valve system to fail.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Here's the honest math on cold brew ratios: for concentrate, you want roughly 1:4 coffee-to-water. That means about 1 cup of coarse grounds per quart. The filter basket handles that volume without overflowing, and the 200-micron stainless mesh does a respectable job keeping sediment out of your cup. Expect a clean brew — not crystal-clear like paper-filtered, but no gritty mouthfeel either.

Steep time runs 12 to 18 hours at room temperature. The glass lid seals well enough that you won't get fridge odors in your brew, which matters more than people think. One thing I genuinely appreciate: the jar's wide mouth makes pouring grounds in and rinsing the filter out dramatically easier than the narrow-neck bottles most competitors use.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. The 1-quart capacity is the big one. If you drink cold brew daily, you're brewing every other day, minimum. That's fine for singles or couples, but families will find it tedious. The mesh filter also demands immediate attention — if you let coffee oils dry on it, you'll be scrubbing. Warm water rinse right after brewing keeps it effortless; skip that and you've created a problem.

The pour lid, while functional, isn't airtight enough for aggressive shaking. You can swirl it, but if you're trying to agitate grounds mid-steep, do it gently or use the solid lid.

## FAQ

**How fine should I grind my coffee?**
Coarse, like breadcrumbs. Too fine and the mesh will clog, slowing the drip and making cleanup miserable. Most reviewers report best results with a burr grinder set to French press consistency.

**Can I make tea with this?**
Yes, and it works surprisingly well. Loose-leaf tea steeps cleanly in the same basket. Just remember the filter is porous — use larger-leaf teas or expect some sediment.

**Does the glass hold up to dishwasher cleaning?**
The jar is dishwasher safe, but the filter isn't — hand wash it immediately after use. The glass itself is borosilicate and handles heat cycling well, though you shouldn't be putting boiling water in it anyway.
