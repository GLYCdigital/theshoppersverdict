**Review Body:**

Most reusable lunch boxes fall into one of two camps: flimsy plastic that cracks within a month, or heavy stainless steel that dents your carry bag and can't go in the microwave. The Femuar Reusable Lunch Box sits in a third, more useful category — a durable, leak-resistant container that actually holds up to daily abuse.

**What You're Actually Getting**

This is a three-compartment container made from food-grade polypropylene (PP). As shown in the product image, the black base has a clear lid with four locking tabs on each side — a design choice that matters more than you'd think. The main compartment measures roughly 7.4 x 5.9 x 2.4 inches, which fits a standard sandwich, a piece of fruit, and a side without everything turning into a pile of mixed leftovers.

The lid isn't just a snap-on cover. It has a silicone gasket running around the perimeter, which is the difference between a "lunch box" and a "soup carrier." This is the feature that separates it from cheaper alternatives in the same price range.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this with the two things that kill most lunch containers: chili and salad dressing. The four-tab locking system requires pressing each corner down in sequence — get all four seated and the seal is genuinely tight. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"I've carried soup in this and had zero leaks in my bag."* That tracks with my experience. The seal holds.

Microwaving works, but pay attention — the lid must come off. The base handles reheating fine, and the black exterior means you won't see the tomato-sauce staining that makes white containers look permanently dirty after a month. It's also dishwasher-safe, though hand-washing the gasket is smarter if you want the seal to last.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings isn't accidental. The recurring themes in verified reviews are the leak resistance and the practical compartment sizing. The main section is genuinely large enough for a real meal, not a snack. Multiple reviewers noted they'd bought a second one after the first survived months of daily commutes.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest downsides exist. The lid's four-corner seal requires deliberate closing — if you're in a rush and just slap it shut, you'll get leaks. One reviewer captured the frustration: *"Works great when you take the time to seal all four sides, but it's not something you can close with one hand."* That's a fair criticism.

At 28 ounces, it's also not compact. It won't fit in slim lunch bags designed for bento boxes, and it takes up real estate in a crowded office fridge. If you're looking for something to slip into a briefcase side pocket, this isn't it.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Office workers** who pack real meals (leftovers, salads, grain bowls) and need something that won't leak in a commute bag
- **Meal preppers** who portion out 3-4 containers at once and want dishwasher-safe options
- **Anyone tired of replacing cracked, cheap containers** every few months — this is built to last

**Skip it if** you need something ultra-compact, or if you know you won't bother aligning four lid tabs every morning.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this go in the freezer?**
A: Yes, the PP plastic is freezer-safe, but leave headroom — food expands as it freezes. Don't overfill.

**Q: Does it actually prevent odor transfer between compartments?**
A: The compartments are separated by fixed walls, so no cross-contamination of flavors or smells. The gasket keeps everything contained.

**Q: Is the lid microwave-safe too?**
A: No. Only the base is microwave-safe. The lid has a silicone gasket that won't tolerate high heat. Always remove it before reheating.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a leak-proof, microwave-safe lunch container that survives daily use without looking gross after three weeks. **Skip it if** you need something smaller or refuse to take the extra second to seal all four tabs properly.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The best budget-friendly option I've tested in this category, with the only real flaw being a lid that demands a deliberate close.
