The Mr. Coffee 8-12 Cup Filters aim to be the boring, reliable workhorse of your morning routine. ## What's in the Box

You get a straightforward box of 100 natural brown basket-style filters. Each filter measures about 8 inches across the rim and is designed to sit in the flat-bottomed basket of standard drip coffee makers. The crimped seams along the sides are worth noting — that's where cheap filters fail, and these hold up noticeably better than dollar-store generics.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature here is the oxygen-bleaching process. Unlike the bright white filters that use chlorine bleach, these are processed with oxygen, which means no chemical residue that can leach into your brew. Coffee purists will appreciate that your beans' flavor profile comes through clean — one reviewer noted, "I can taste the difference between these and the bleached white ones. My coffee tastes cleaner, less papery."

In real-world use, these filters handle a full 12-cup brew without bowing or splitting. The paper is thick enough to trap fine grounds but porous enough that you won't get a slow, backed-up brew cycle. A consistent theme across reviews: "They fit my Mr. Coffee perfectly and I've never had a break-through, even with finely ground beans."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating isn't a fluke. Across the reviews, three things keep coming up:

- **Consistent fit** — "These are the only filters that don't crumple weirdly in my Hamilton Beach. They lay flat and brew evenly."
- **No paper taste** — Multiple reviewers specifically mention the lack of that cardboard flavor you get with cheaper filters.
- **Sturdy construction** — The crimped seams hold up. No mid-brew blowouts, even with aggressive blooming.

## Where It Falls Short

The 100-count box is the obvious drawback. If you brew a pot daily, that's barely three months of filters. The price per filter is fair, but you'll likely be reordering more often than you'd like. Also worth flagging: these are strictly basket-style. If you own a cone-shaped brewer, these won't fit — make sure you check your machine's filter shape before buying.
