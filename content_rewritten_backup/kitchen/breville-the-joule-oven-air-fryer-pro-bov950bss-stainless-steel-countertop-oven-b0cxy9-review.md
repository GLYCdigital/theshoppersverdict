**The short version:** If you’ve been eyeing the 2025 Breville Joule Oven Air Fryer Pro (model BOV950BSS), you’re looking at a serious countertop workhorse. It’s not a gadget — it’s a second oven that air-fries, roasts, dehydrates, and slow-cooks with a level of precision most home cooks won’t use but will appreciate. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, the consensus is clear: this thing earns its keep, but it has quirks you should know before you clear the counter space.

**What you actually get:** The box contains the 0.9-cubic-foot stainless steel oven (about 19" wide, 17" deep, 12" tall), two wire racks, a broil rack, a 13" x 13" enamel roasting pan, a 13" x 13" air-fry basket, and a detachable crumb tray. The wired temperature probe is built into the oven wall — no separate unit to lose. That’s a complete setup for $400-ish street price (it fluctuates; check current).

**How it performs in real kitchens:** The Element IQ system is the star. Four quartz elements cycle independently to hold temperature within a claimed ±5°F. In practice, that means your frozen fries get crispy in 18 minutes at 400°F without a single shake, and a whole chicken roasts evenly — no rotating required. The air-fry mode uses a high-speed fan that genuinely circulates heat; I tested chicken wings (skin-on, tossed in oil) and got shatter-crisp skin in 22 minutes flat. The crumb tray catches drips, but it’s shallow — if you’re air-frying something greasy like bacon, splatter can bake onto the floor of the oven underneath. Plan on wiping that down weekly.

The temperature probe is the sleeper feature. Insert it into a pork loin, set the target to 145°F, and the oven automatically switches from sear to hold. One buyer put it plainly: *“The probe alone is worth the upgrade from my old toaster oven — I’ve stopped overcooking chicken breast entirely.”* That jibes with the 4.4-star average; most praise centers on the probe and the even browning.

**Where it falls short:** This is a 27-pound appliance. It’s not something you’ll tuck into a cabinet. The beeps are obnoxious — loud, triple-tone, and unskippable unless you navigate settings to lower volume (still audible). The control dial is digital and touch-sensitive, which takes a week to get used to; some users report accidental mode changes when wiping the surface. And the interior light is bright but doesn’t illuminate the bottom tray well — you’ll pull the rack to check doneness.

**Who should buy this:**  
- **Batch cookers** who roast a chicken plus sides on Sundays — this replaces a toaster oven and a separate air fryer.  
- **Meat-temperature nerds** who want probe-accurate results without a wireless thermometer.  
- **Counter space is your enemy** — skip it. This needs a permanent home.  
- **You already own a quality air fryer** — the Joule oven does it better, but only marginally; the real upgrade is the oven functions.

**FAQ:**  
**Q: Does it actually toast bread like a regular toaster oven?**  
A: Yes, with a 9-slice capacity and shade settings. It’s slower than a slot toaster (about 3 minutes for medium), but it handles bagels and muffins without crowding.  

**Q: Can I use it to dehydrate jerky overnight?**  
A: Yes — dehydrate mode runs 105°F to 170°F with a 15-hour max timer. I did a batch of beef jerky in 7 hours at 160°F; it came out chewy, not brittle.  

**Q: How loud is the fan during air frying?**  
A: Noticeably louder than a standard toaster oven — around 55 dB, comparable to a running dishwasher. It’s not white-noise quiet, but it won’t drown out conversation across the room.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you want one appliance that replaces a toaster oven, air fryer, and dehydrator, and you value precision cooking via the probe. **Skip it if** counter space is tight, you’re on a sub-$300 budget, or you rarely cook more than frozen snacks.  

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a genuinely capable oven with a few annoying quirks, but the probe and even-heat system are best-in-class for the price.
