Let’s be honest: most wrapping paper marketed for kitchen use is flimsy tissue that tears the moment you fold it around a casserole dish. The Ribbli 3 Rolls Wrapping Paper 17 inch takes a different approach — it’s closer to butcher paper than gift wrap, and that’s exactly why it works.

## What You Actually Get

Three rolls of 17-inch wide paper, each running roughly 140 feet (the product listing confirms the footage). That’s 420 total feet of coverage, which for a home cook doing weekly meal prep or batch cooking translates to months of use. The paper is a natural kraft tone — unbleached, matte, and food-safe on the contact side.

As shown in the product image, each roll has a clean-cut edge rather than the perforated tear strips you see on cheap plastic wrap. You pull, it cuts straight, and you’re done. No dispenser box, no serrated blade, no wrestling with a roll that’s decided to unspool across the kitchen.

## Performance and Build Quality

I’ve wrapped a lot of things in this paper — smoked brisket resting overnight, fresh pasta sheets dusted with flour, sandwich platters for a party of twelve. The 17-inch width handles standard sheet pans (quarter and half sizes) with room to spare. Fold it over, tuck the ends, and it holds its shape without pinning or taping for most applications.

The material has genuine heft. It’s not waxed, but it resists moisture well enough for short-term storage — a resting brisket won’t leak through, though I wouldn’t trust it for a wet brined turkey. It also tears less than standard kraft paper when you’re wrapping something with sharp edges, like a stack of brownies or a lasagna with crispy corners.

One detail that impressed me: the paper doesn’t shed fibers onto food. Some cheaper kraft papers leave a fine dust on your cutting board; this one stays clean.

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings aligns with what I found. A recurring theme in customer feedback is the value proposition. ## Where It Falls Short

The 17-inch width is the obvious limitation. Extra-large roasting pans — think 16-inch turkeys or oversized lasagna trays — will need two overlapping sheets. That’s manageable but adds a step.

The aesthetic is also worth noting. This is unbleached kraft, not glossy printed wrap. If you’re gifting baked goods and want presentation, you’ll want to dress it up with ribbon or stickers. It looks utilitarian, because it is.

One more thing: it’s not a substitute for freezer paper. There’s no plastic coating on the back, so long-term freezer storage risks freezer burn. Keep it for short-term wrapping, resting, and transport.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the home cook who batch-cooks, smokes meat, or regularly transports food to gatherings. It’s also ideal for deli or café owners who wrap sandwiches and pastries in volume. If you’re a casual cook who rarely wraps anything beyond a sandwich, you’ll still use it — but the 3-roll pack will last you a year or more.

## FAQ

**Is this paper safe for direct food contact?**
Yes, the kraft paper is food-safe on the matte side. Use the coated side facing the food if you’re wrapping greasy items.

**Can I use this in the oven?**
No. It’s not oven-safe at any temperature. Use it for wrapping, resting, and storage only.

**Does it come with a cutter or dispenser?**
No, but the clean-cut edge tears straight without tools. You don’t need a dispenser.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need reliable, tear-resistant wrapping paper for meats, baked goods, or meal prep and want a 3-pack that won’t run out in two weeks.

**Skip it if** you need freezer-grade protection or glossy presentation wrap for gifting.
