**The NESCO CU Professional Coffee Urn: A Workhorse for Big Crowds, With a Few Rough Edges**

If you've ever watched a church potluck or office meeting descend into chaos because the single 12-cup drip machine couldn't keep up, you know the pain. The NESCO CU (that's the 42-cup model, roughly 2.5 gallons) is the answer to that specific problem. It's not a stylish pour-over. It's a volume machine built for one job: delivering hot coffee to a crowd without babysitting.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the urn itself, a detachable power cord, and a glass decanter-style lid. The body is brushed stainless steel, and critically, so is the interior. That matters. Cheaper urns use aluminum or plastic liners that absorb oils and impart a metallic taste over time. The NESCO's stainless interior (with a visible water level window on the side) means you can brew back-to-back batches without flavor crossover.

The brewing mechanism is straightforward: a heating element in the base brings water to boil, which cycles up through a center column and over the grounds in the basket. The basket is fine-mesh and reusable — no paper filters needed, though you'll want to rinse it immediately after use to avoid stubborn grounds sticking.

**Real-World Performance**

This is where the 4.2-star rating starts to make sense. The urn does exactly what it promises: 42 cups of hot coffee, fast. Multiple buyers note it brews a full batch in roughly 20-25 minutes, which is respectable for the volume. The dual thermostat maintains serving temperature (around 185°F) without scorching the coffee, a common failure point on cheaper urns.

One owner put it plainly: *"We used this for a 60-person family reunion and it kept up all morning. I was skeptical about the basket filter but the coffee came out clean, no grounds in the cups."* That sentiment — reliable volume plus decent brew quality — shows up repeatedly in the feedback.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides, because there are a few.

First, there's no automatic shutoff. If you forget to unplug it, it will keep heating until the water runs dry. That's a burn-out risk and a safety concern, especially in a busy event setting. Set a reminder.

Second, the lid doesn't lock. The glass lid sits in a rubber gasket, but if you need to carry the urn while it's full of hot liquid, you're risking a spill. This is a countertop appliance, not a transport vessel. Buyers have reported cracked lids from accidental knocks.

Third, the spigot drips. It's not a constant leak, but after you pour a cup, expect a few drops to follow. Keep a small tray or towel underneath to avoid a puddle on your tablecloth.

Finally, the mesh filter is fine but not perfect. Some reviewers noted ultra-fine sediment in the bottom of the last few cups. If you're serving a discerning crowd, use a paper filter liner (cut to fit) on top of the mesh.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Event hosts** who regularly serve 30+ people (church groups, family reunions, office meetings)
- **Camp or retreat kitchens** that need reliable volume without commercial pricing
- **Caterers** who need a backup urn or a dedicated hot-water station for tea and cocoa

Skip it if you're brewing for a household of four. You'll be cleaning a giant urn for three cups of coffee. Get a standard drip machine instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can you brew different strengths?**
A: Yes, by adjusting the amount of grounds in the basket. The urn doesn't have a strength dial, so you control it manually. Start with 1 cup of coarse-ground coffee per 8 cups of water and adjust from there.

**Q: Does it make tea?**
A: Absolutely. The basket works for loose-leaf tea or tea bags. Many buyers use it as a hot water urn for cocoa, cider, or tea service at events.

**Q: How hard is cleaning?**
A: The interior wipes clean with warm soapy water. The heating element and center column need periodic descaling with vinegar if you have hard water. The basket is the most tedious part — soak it immediately after use.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need dependable, high-volume coffee for events and don't mind a few quirks. It's a solid value for the capacity, and the stainless construction means it'll outlast cheaper plastic-lined rivals.

**Skip it if** you need portability (the non-locking lid is a dealbreaker for travel) or if you want set-and-forget safety features.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A capable workhorse with minor annoyances that are easy to work around. For the price per cup served, it pays for itself within a few events.
