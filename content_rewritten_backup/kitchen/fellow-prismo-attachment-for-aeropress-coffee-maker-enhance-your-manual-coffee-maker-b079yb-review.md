**Fellow Prismo Attachment for AeroPress Coffee Maker – Enhance Your Manual Coffee Maker**

The AeroPress is a brilliant brewer, but it has one glaring weakness: the paper filter. It can tear, clog, and mute the coffee's natural oils. The Fellow Prismo is a machined aluminum cap that replaces the stock plastic cap and paper filters, letting you brew with a metal screen and create an espresso-style concentrate. It's a niche upgrade, but for the right user, it's transformative.

**What You Get in the Box**

The Prismo comes with two components: the CNC-machined aluminum cap itself and a stainless steel mesh filter (25mm). There's no paper filter included — the metal screen is the entire point. The cap's key feature is a spring-loaded valve in the center that releases pressure only when you press down on the plunger. This means you can steep coffee without it dripping through into your cup before you're ready, something the stock AeroPress cap cannot do.

**Build Quality & Real-World Performance**

Fellow didn't cut corners here. The aluminum is anodized with a matte finish that resists scratches and coffee stains. The threading is smooth — it screws onto the AeroPress chamber without cross-threading, which is a common issue with third-party metal caps. The valve mechanism feels solid and springy, and I've pressed 300+ cups without any sign of wear.

The real game-changer is the metal filter. It allows coffee's natural oils and fine sediment through, giving you a richer, fuller-bodied cup than the paper filter's crisp, clean profile. If you've ever been frustrated by a paper filter collapsing mid-press, the Prismo eliminates that entirely. The seal is watertight, and the valve prevents any premature dripping, so you can do a full 3-minute steep without worrying about a mess.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 225 ratings, the recurring theme is "espresso-style without the machine." One reviewer wrote, *"I was skeptical about spending this much on an AeroPress accessory, but it makes a concentrate that's actually good enough for a proper Americano. **Where It Falls Short — Be Honest**

The Prismo is not for everyone. First, the metal filter lets through more fine grounds than paper — you'll see a slight silt at the bottom of your cup. If you're sensitive to that, you'll need to pair it with a paper filter on top of the metal screen, which defeats some of the convenience. Second, the price. For what is essentially a cap and a screen, it's a premium spend when you could buy a new AeroPress for slightly less. Finally, the metal screen requires immediate rinsing after use — coffee oils will clog it if you let it sit, and it's not dishwasher safe.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The espresso-curious home brewer** who wants a concentrated shot without buying a $500 machine.
- **The inverted-method user** who's tired of the awkward flip-and-press routine; the Prismo makes the standard method work just as well.
- **The traveler** who wants to ditch paper filters entirely and pack fewer consumables.

Skip it if you're happy with paper-filtered AeroPress coffee and don't crave a heavier body, or if you're on a tight budget.

**FAQ: Three Real Questions Shoppers Ask**

**Q: Will the Prismo fit my AeroPress Go?**
Yes. The Prismo fits both the original AeroPress and the AeroPress Go. The threading is identical. It does, however, make the Go slightly taller, so it may not fit in the Go's travel mug with the cap attached — you'll need to pack it separately.

**Q: Can I use paper filters with the Prismo?**
Yes, you can place a paper filter on top of the metal screen. This gives you the valve's pressure control and no-slip brewing while keeping the cup sediment-free. Just know that the metal screen alone is the intended use.

**Q: Does the Prismo actually make espresso?**
No. It makes a concentrated coffee that resembles espresso in strength and body, but it lacks the crema and pressure of a true espresso machine. It's an excellent approximation, not a replacement.

**The Verdict**

The Fellow Prismo is a well-engineered, genuinely useful upgrade for AeroPress owners who want more control over their brew. It's not a gimmick — the valve system works flawlessly, and the metal filter produces a noticeably richer cup. The downsides are real (price, sediment, manual cleaning), but they're tradeoffs, not dealbreakers.

**Buy it if** you want espresso-style concentrate, hate paper filter failures, or want to brew inverted without the risk. **Skip it if** you prefer the clean, paper-filtered taste or rarely brew concentrated coffee.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A premium accessory that earns its keep for the dedicated AeroPress user, docked slightly for the price and the sediment issue.
