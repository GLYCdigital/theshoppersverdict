**FAQ**

**Q: How do I know if my water inlet valve is actually broken?**
A: Unplug the dishwasher, remove the lower front panel, and locate the valve where the water supply line connects. Check for visible cracks or mineral buildup. Use a multimeter to test the solenoid for continuity — if it reads infinite resistance (open circuit), the coil is dead. Also, check the inlet screen for clogs; that's a free fix.

**Q: Will this fit my KitchenAid model?**
A: It fits models that use the W10327249, W10327250, or W11175771 part numbers. Search your model number online or check the existing valve's part number stamped on the side. KitchenAid, Maytag, and Whirlpool share many components, but not all.

**Q: How long does installation take?**
A: Plan for 20–45 minutes. You'll need to disconnect the water supply, remove the old valve (usually held by one screw and a bracket), swap the electrical connectors, and reattach the supply line. For most people, the hardest part is lying under the dishwasher — the actual work is straightforward.
