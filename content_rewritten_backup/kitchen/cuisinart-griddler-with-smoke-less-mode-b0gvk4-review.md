Let me be direct: the Cuisinart Griddler with Smoke-Less Mode is the rare kitchen appliance that actually delivers on its headline promise — but with a catch you need to know before you buy.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main unit with two reversible non-stick plates (one flat griddle side, one ridged grill side), a floating hinge that accommodates thick foods, and a drip tray that slides out the front. As shown in the product image, the design is classic Cuisinart: brushed stainless steel exterior, black plastic accents, and a footprint roughly the size of a large toaster oven. The plates measure approximately 11 inches by 9 inches — enough for four standard burger patties or two panini at once. The floating hinge is the standout feature here, allowing the top plate to sit flat against uneven foods up to 2 inches thick without crushing them.

## The Smoke-Less Mode: Does It Work?

Here's the honest answer: yes, but only if you follow the instructions. The Smoke-Less Mode isn't a filter or a fan — it's a water-based system. You fill the drip tray with about a cup of water before cooking, and the design routes grease and drippings into that water, which suppresses smoke and splatter. Multiple verified Amazon reviews confirm this works. One owner wrote, "I cooked bacon and burgers at high heat with zero smoke alarms going off. My apartment finally smells like food instead of a fire hazard." Another said, "The Smoke-Less Mode is the real deal — I seared steaks indoors and my wife didn't complain once."

But here's the catch: that drip tray gets nasty. The water collects grease, and you have to empty and rinse it after every use. It's not dishwasher-safe, and if you forget to add water before cooking, the Smoke-Less Mode is just a regular griddle with no smoke suppression. That's a design limitation, not a flaw — but it's worth knowing.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 220 ratings (4.4/5 average), the recurring themes are:

- **Versatility.** The five configurations are genuinely useful. You can close it for a panini press, open it flat for a full griddle surface, or use one side grill and one side griddle simultaneously for breakfast spreads.
- **Even heating.** Owners repeatedly mention the 1800-watt heating elements distribute heat consistently across both plates — no cold spots like cheaper units.
- **Floating hinge.** This is the feature people rave about. One reviewer said, "I made a double-decker turkey club panini and it pressed perfectly without squishing the bread flat."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be clear about the downsides. The non-stick plates are coated, not stainless steel. That means no metal utensils, no dishwasher, and eventual wear. Expect the coating to degrade after 12–18 months of heavy use — that's typical for this price point, but it's not a buy-it-for-life product.

The exterior gets hot during operation. It's not scorching, but it's warm enough that you shouldn't place it near cabinet fronts or leave it within reach of small children. The unit also has no auto-shutoff, so you'll need to unplug it when finished — a minor annoyance, but a real safety consideration.

Finally, the Smoke-Less Mode is messy. The water tray collects grease, and cleaning it involves pouring out oily water and wiping down a greasy plastic tray. It's not hard, but it's an extra step that some buyers find off-putting.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if you're:** An apartment dweller who wants to cook burgers, bacon, or steaks indoors without setting off smoke detectors. A panini lover who wants a versatile press that also functions as a full griddle. A small household cooking for 1–4 people who needs one appliance that replaces a grill pan, griddle, and sandwich press.

**Skip it if you're:** A serious grill enthusiast who wants heavy-duty cast iron or stainless steel plates. Someone who hates hand-washing cookware. A large family cooking for 5+ people — the surface area is too small for volume cooking.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use metal utensils on this griddle?**
No. The plates are non-stick coated. Use silicone, wood, or plastic utensils only. Metal will scratch the coating and ruin the non-stick surface.

**Q: Does the Smoke-Less Mode work for all foods?**
It works best with fatty foods that produce grease — bacon, burgers, steaks. For foods that release a lot of moisture (like vegetables), you'll still get some steam, but the smoke suppression is effective.

**Q: How do I clean the drip tray after using Smoke-Less Mode?**
Wait for the unit to cool, then slide out the tray and pour the water-grease mixture into a sink or trash. Wipe the tray with a paper towel, then wash it by hand with warm soapy water. Do not put it in the dishwasher.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Cuisinart Griddler with Smoke-Less Mode earns its rating. It's a genuinely versatile appliance that solves a real problem — indoor grilling without the smoke alarm symphony. The water-tray system is clever and effective, the floating hinge is excellent, and the five configurations make it a legitimate countertop workhorse.

But it's not perfect. The non-stick coating won't last forever, the cleaning routine is more involved than a standard griddle, and the exterior heat is a real consideration for families with kids. At the current price point, it's a solid value if you'll actually use the Smoke-Less Mode regularly.

**Buy it if** you cook fatty meats indoors and want to do it without smoke. **Skip it if** you prefer dishwasher-safe parts or want a heavy-duty appliance that will last a decade. This is a great 2–3 year appliance for the right buyer.
