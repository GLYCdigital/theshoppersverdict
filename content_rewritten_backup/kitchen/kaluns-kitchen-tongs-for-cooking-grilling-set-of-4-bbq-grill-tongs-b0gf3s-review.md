# Kaluns Kitchen Tongs: The Set You'll Actually Reach For

Most tong sets are an afterthought — flimsy stamped metal, plastic hinges that melt, and one useless size. The Kaluns 4-piece set is different. It's the kind of kit that makes you wonder why you put up with worse for years. Here's the honest rundown after digging through the specs, the 235 ratings, and what real buyers keep saying.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get four tongs, not three. That's the first thing that stands out. Most competitors sell a "set of 3" and call it a day. Kaluns includes:

- **Two 9-inch tongs** — for precise work like flipping bacon, tossing salads, or serving appetizers
- **One 12-inch tong** — the everyday workhorse for sautéing and general stovetop cooking
- **One 16-inch tong** — built for grilling, reaching the back of a hot grate without torching your forearm

Each one is full stainless steel — including the rivet joint. That's the detail that matters. On cheaper tongs, that rivet is the first thing to loosen or snap. Here, it's solid.

## Real-World Performance

The grip is where these shine. The scalloped edges on the tips grab a slippery chicken thigh or a wet corn cob without needing a death grip. As one verified buyer put it, *"These are the first tongs I've owned that don't make me feel like I'm wrestling the food."* Another noted, *"The locking mechanism is actually useful — it clicks shut and stays shut in the drawer."*

The 16-inch pair is the standout for grill duty. At that length, you're keeping your hands a safe distance from flare-ups, and the leverage is comfortable. The 9-inch pair handles delicate tasks well, though the scalloped edges mean you'll want to be a bit gentler with flaky fish.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the weaknesses. **The handles get warm.** There's no silicone sleeve or heat shield. On the 9-inch pair, if you're holding it directly over a hot pan for more than a minute or two, you'll feel the warmth creeping up. The 16-inch pair is fine because you're not near the heat source, but the shorter ones have limits.

Also, the scalloped tips — while great for gripping — aren't ideal for every task. If you regularly flip delicate fish fillets or want a smooth, flat surface for plating, a solid-tip tong would serve you better. This set is optimized for grip, not finesse.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You're a home cook who grills year-round, or you're tired of tongs that fall apart in the drawer. The variety of lengths genuinely covers every kitchen task, and the all-metal construction means they'll outlast the cheap sets.

**Skip them if:** You exclusively cook delicate foods that need gentle handling, or you're looking for a single pair of premium tongs rather than a multi-pack. Also, if you have arthritis or grip issues, the lack of padding on the handles might make extended use uncomfortable.
