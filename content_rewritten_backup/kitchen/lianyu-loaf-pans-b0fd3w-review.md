# LIANYU Loaf Pans Review: The $20 Pan That Thinks It's Premium

Most budget loaf pans are flimsy, warp-prone pieces of tin that give you burnt edges and raw centers. The LIANYU loaf pans aren't that. After three weeks of nearly daily baking — sandwich bread, banana bread, pound cake, and a dense sourdough experiment — these pans performed like options costing triple the price.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get two 9x5-inch loaf pans. That's it. No lids, no parchment paper, no silicone handles. Each pan is heavy-gauge carbon steel with a dark nonstick coating and rolled rims. They're not decorative — these are workhorse pans meant for daily use.

## Real-World Performance

The standout feature is heat distribution. I baked two identical white sandwich loaves side by side — one in the LIANYU, one in a cheap Wilton pan I've had for years. The LIANYU loaf came out with an even golden crust across all four sides. The Wilton had a dark spot on one corner and a pale streak on the other. That's the heavy-gauge steel doing its job.

The nonstick coating is genuinely nonstick. My banana bread recipe is notoriously sticky — it has 3 mashed bananas and a cup of oil. It slid right out with zero residue. I've also run these through the dishwasher about eight times, and the coating hasn't flaked or peeled. That's rare at this price point.

One thing to know: the dark coating absorbs heat aggressively. If your recipe says 350°F, drop it to 335-340°F. I learned this the hard way when my first loaf came out with a crust that was a shade too dark. It wasn't burnt — just darker than I'd like. Once I adjusted the temp, results were perfect.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews isn't inflated. One top reviewer wrote, *"I've bought expensive stoneware loaf pans that warped after three uses. These are my daily drivers now."* Another noted, *"Baked four loaves in one afternoon — they cooled, washed, and were ready to go again immediately."*

The most common praise centers on the nonstick surface and durability. Several reviewers mention using metal knives to slice bread directly in the pan — which I wouldn't recommend, but the coating survives it.

## Where They Fall Short

The size limitation is real. You only get 9x5. If you want mini loaves for gift-giving or a 8.5x4.5 for denser quick breads, you're out of luck. This is a one-trick pony in terms of dimensions.

The handles are small — functional but not comfortable. With thick quilted oven mitts, my grip felt clumsy pulling a hot pan out of the oven. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's an annoyance.

And the dark coating requires recipe adjustments. If you're a set-it-and-forget-it baker who follows recipes verbatim, expect a learning curve.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Budget-conscious home bakers** who want professional results without spending $40+ per pan
- **Frequent bread bakers** who need pans that won't warp after months of use
- **Anyone tired of parchment paper** — the nonstick coating genuinely eliminates the need

**Skip them if** you're a mini loaf enthusiast, you only bake once a month, or you refuse to adjust oven temps for dark cookware.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these pans oven-safe at high temperatures?**
A: Yes, up to 450°F. I tested at 425°F with a dense sourdough and saw zero warping.

**Q: Can they go in the dishwasher?**
A: They claim dishwasher-safe, and my eight cycles haven't damaged the coating. But hand-washing will extend the nonstick lifespan significantly.

**Q: Do I still need to grease the pan?**
A: For most recipes, no. But for high-sugar batters like caramel or honey-heavy breads, a light butter swipe prevents sticking.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you bake bread or quick loaves at least twice a month and want pans that perform like premium brands without the premium price.

**Skip it if** you need multiple sizes, bake rarely, or refuse to adjust your oven temperature for dark cookware.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — These are the rare budget pans that genuinely punch above their weight class. The size limitation and temperature adjustment are minor tradeoffs for the consistent, nonstick performance you get.
