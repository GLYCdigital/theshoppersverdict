**Martha Stewart Lockton Premium Nonstick Enameled Heavy Gauge Review**

I'll cut straight to it: if you're eyeing this set because the Martha Stewart name promises boutique quality at a big-box price, you're on the right track — but there's nuance. With a solid 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, this cookware has earned its place in many kitchens. But "heavy gauge" doesn't mean what you might think, and that's worth unpacking before you spend.

**What's Actually in the Box**

This is a full cookware set, which means you're getting the essentials rather than filler pieces. The lineup typically includes a 1.5-quart and 3-quart saucepan with lids, a 6-quart stockpot with lid, and an 8-inch and 10-inch fry pan. The construction is aluminum core with an enameled exterior and a nonstick interior coating. The handles are riveted — a detail that matters for longevity, as riveted handles won't loosen the way welded ones can.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The "heavy gauge" claim refers to the aluminum thickness, and it's genuinely respectable for the price point. The base is thick enough to distribute heat evenly across the cooking surface — you'll notice scrambled eggs cook uniformly without hot spots, and sauces won't scorch in the corners of the pan. The enameled exterior is more than cosmetic; it makes the outside of the pots easy to wipe clean and protects the aluminum from oxidation.

The nonstick interior is where this set performs best. Eggs slide off with minimal butter, and cleanup is genuinely a thirty-second rinse-and-wipe job. The coating held up well across the board in customer testing — no flaking or peeling reported in the early months of use. One buyer noted, "I've used these daily for two months and they still look brand new. Nothing sticks, even burned cheese comes off with a sponge."

The lids are glass with a stainless steel rim, which lets you monitor cooking without lifting the lid. They fit snugly — a small thing, but it keeps steam in and reduces cooking time.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

- **Even heating** — multiple reviewers mention the absence of hot spots, which is the defining quality test for any pan.
- **Easy cleanup** — the nonstick surface combined with the smooth enamel exterior cuts dishwashing time dramatically.
- **Weight handling** — at this gauge, the pans are substantial but not arm-breaking. The stockpot feels solid when full, but you can still lift it one-handed.

**Where It Falls Short — Be Honest About This**

The nonstick coating is the elephant in the room. It's PTFE-based, which means it will degrade over time — typically 2-3 years with regular use. At this price point, that's an acceptable tradeoff, but don't expect heirloom durability. You'll also need to hand-wash these; the dishwasher will eventually dull the nonstick performance and cloud the enamel exterior.

The handles are comfortable but get warm on the stovetop. Not dangerously hot, but you'll want a potholder for anything simmering longer than 15 minutes. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

One more honest caveat: the 8-inch fry pan is on the smaller side. If you're cooking for more than two people, you'll reach for the 10-inch most of the time. Plan your meals accordingly.

**Who Should Buy This**

This set is ideal for:

- **Home cooks who want nonstick convenience without paying Le Creuset prices** — you're getting 80% of the performance for 40% of the cost.
- **First-time apartment dwellers** — the full set covers all your bases without cluttering a small kitchen with redundant pieces.
- **Busy parents** — the easy cleanup is a genuine time-saver on weeknights.

**Skip it if** you're a serious home chef who needs oven-safe cookware above 350°F (the handles aren't rated for high-heat roasting) or if you're looking for a truly lifetime purchase.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this set oven-safe?**
A: Yes, up to 350°F. The nonstick coating and handles can handle moderate oven temperatures, but don't use it for high-heat roasting or broiling.

**Q: Can I use metal utensils on this cookware?**
A: Technically yes, but don't. Metal utensils will scratch the nonstick surface and accelerate its breakdown. Stick to silicone, wood, or nylon utensils to extend the coating's life.

**Q: How does this compare to the cheaper Martha Stewart sets?**
A: The difference is the gauge of the aluminum. The cheaper sets use thinner metal that warps and heats unevenly. This Lockton line is noticeably heavier and distributes heat better — it's worth the upgrade.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want reliable, attractive nonstick cookware that performs well for daily cooking and won't break the bank. The even heating and easy cleanup justify the price, and the set covers all your bases.

**Skip it if** you need oven-safe above 350°F, prefer stainless steel for high-heat searing, or expect a set that will last a decade without degradation.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong value with honest limitations. The nonstick coating won't last forever, but for everyday cooking, this set delivers where it counts.
