If you've ever wrestled a gallon freezer bag of soup into the freezer only to find a leaky, frostbitten mess three weeks later, this tray is the answer. The **Silicone Freezer Tray for Soup Ice Cubes** isn't revolutionary — it's just the first one I've tested that actually gets the fundamentals right. And with a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, I'm clearly not alone in that assessment.

**What You Actually Get**

The tray arrives as a single unit with two 1-cup compartments and a matching silicone lid. Total capacity is 2 cups — enough for a dinner-for-two portion of soup or four to six standard ice cube portions for quick pan sauces. The silicone is noticeably thicker than the flimsy muffin-tin-style trays I've used before; it holds its shape when you pour hot stock straight in, which matters more than you'd think.

**Performance in the Real World**

I tested this with chicken stock, a thick butternut squash soup, and leftover chili. The release mechanism is the star here: flex the tray from the bottom and each block pops out cleanly with zero sticking. No running warm water over the bottom, no butter-knife prying, no shattered cubes scattering across the kitchen floor.

The lids deserve special mention. Most soup trays on the market are open-top, which means your stock absorbs whatever's lurking in your freezer — last month's fish sticks, for example. This lid seals snugly enough to keep odors out and moisture in. One reviewer noted, *"I froze homemade bone broth and it didn't pick up any of the freezer smells. The lid is the reason I bought this and it doesn't disappoint."*

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The most common thread in positive reviews is the portion size. Two cups is the sweet spot — small enough that frozen blocks thaw quickly in a saucepan, large enough that you're not assembling dinner from sixteen tiny cubes. Multiple reviewers mentioned using it for baby food purees, and the 1-cup portions are ideal for that. One parent wrote, *"Perfect for freezing homemade baby food in exactly the right serving size. I bought a second one within a week."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Honesty requires noting the limitations. The 2-cup total capacity means serious batch-cookers will need two or three of these to handle a single stockpot's worth of liquid. At roughly $15–20 depending on current pricing, that adds up fast.

The soft silicone walls also bulge outward when you fill compartments to the brim. It's not a functional failure, but it makes the lid harder to seat properly. Leave about a quarter-inch of headroom in each compartment and you'll avoid the struggle entirely.

Some users reported that deeply colored soups — tomato-based ones especially — left a slight stain after repeated use. It doesn't affect performance or flavor, but if you're particular about your kitchen gear looking pristine, that's worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy it if you're a **meal-prepper who freezes stock or soup in single servings**, a **parent making homemade baby food**, or anyone who's tired of frozen soup tasting like last month's leftovers. Skip it if you only freeze soup occasionally or if you routinely make massive batches — in that case, a larger capacity tray with four 2-cup wells will serve you better.

**FAQ**

**Can you pour hot liquid directly into this tray?**
Yes. The food-grade silicone handles temperatures up to roughly 400°F safely. I poured simmering stock straight in without any warping or off-gassing.

**Does it fit in a standard freezer drawer?**
The tray measures about 9 x 6 inches and stacks flat. It fits comfortably in most side-by-side and bottom-freezer drawers. The lids allow for stacking if you buy multiple.

**How do you clean it?**
Top-rack dishwasher safe, though I found hand-washing with warm soapy water takes about 30 seconds. The silicone doesn't hold onto residue the way plastic trays do.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you freeze soup, stock, or purees in any regular rhythm and want clean portions without the plastic-bag gamble. **Skip it if** you need bulk capacity or rarely freeze liquids at all.

At 4.6 stars, this is a well-executed tool for a specific job. It does what it claims, does it well, and the lid is a thoughtful touch that most competitors overlook. For the price of a couple of takeout lunches, it'll earn its place in your freezer drawer for years.
