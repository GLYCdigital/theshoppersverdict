**What You Actually Get**

The board measures roughly 17.4 x 11.8 inches with a 1.5-inch thickness—substantial for a mid-size board. It's crafted from acacia, a dense hardwood that sits between maple and teak on the durability scale. You're getting a deep juice groove that runs the perimeter, catching everything from steak drippings to bread crumbs. Two carved handles on the sides make it easy to grip, though at 5-6 pounds, you won't be flipping it like a pancake. The surface arrives smooth with a food-safe finish, and the grain pattern varies board to board—some are honey-toned, others lean darker with striking striations.

**Real-World Performance**

Acacia's density is the headline here. It's harder than bamboo or rubberwood, which means knife blades stay sharper longer than they would on glass or plastic. The surface develops a patina of small cuts over time, which is normal and actually helps grip food while chopping. The juice groove is genuinely functional—not just decorative. It corrals liquids effectively, and the raised edge keeps them off your counter. The board handles bread without sliding, cheese without sticking, and raw meat without absorbing odors into the grain, provided you wash it promptly.

**Where It Falls Short**

Here's the honest part. Acacia is thirsty wood—it requires regular mineral oil treatments. Skip this for a few weeks, and you'll notice the surface drying, and in dry climates, small cracks can appear. Several reviews mention this exact issue. One buyer wrote, "Wish I'd known about the oiling before I bought it. It's cracked at the ends after three months." That's not a defect; it's the nature of hardwood. But if you want zero-maintenance, this isn't it.

The weight is also a factor. At over 5 pounds, it's not ideal for small hands or quick washing. And the board is hand-wash only—running it through the dishwasher will warp it beyond repair.

**Who Should Buy This**

The Samhita is perfect for home cooks who prep meat and bread regularly and want a board that doubles as servingware. It's also great for anyone upgrading from flimsy plastic boards and willing to spend 10 minutes a month on oiling. Skip it if you're looking for a dishwasher-safe board, need something lightweight for RV or small kitchens, or prefer a larger surface for heavy batch cooking.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is acacia wood safe for raw meat?**
A: Yes, with proper care. Wash with hot soapy water after each use and dry immediately. The wood's density resists bacterial penetration, but don't leave raw meat juices sitting on the surface for extended periods.

**Q: How often do I need to oil this board?**
A: Monthly, at minimum. Apply food-grade mineral oil or a beeswax-mineral oil blend, let it absorb overnight, and wipe off the excess. In dry climates, oil every two weeks.

**Q: Can I put this in the dishwasher?**
A: Absolutely not. The heat and moisture will cause the wood to split and warp. Hand wash and dry standing upright.
