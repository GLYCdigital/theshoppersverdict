## What You Actually Get

The package is refreshingly simple: one peeler, no gimmicks. The tool itself features a die-cast zinc body that gives it real heft — this isn't hollow plastic. The blade is stainless steel, mounted on a swivel head that adjusts to the contour of whatever you're peeling. The handle is the signature OXO Good Grips design: a thick, cushioned, non-slip grip that fits comfortably in both small and large hands.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The blade is genuinely sharp out of the box and stays that way. I've peeled 10 pounds of potatoes for a holiday dinner and the peeler glided through without snagging or leaving thick, wasteful strips. The swivel head does most of the work — it rotates to follow curves, which makes peeling round vegetables like butternut squash or oddly-shaped apples far easier than with a rigid blade.

The handle is the real differentiator. After extended use, my hand didn't cramp. The cushioned grip absorbs pressure that would normally transfer to your palm and fingers. For anyone who peels in bulk — think meal prep or large family meals — this matters more than you'd expect.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this peeler has tradeoffs. First, it's not dishwasher safe. The packaging and OXO's guidance recommend hand washing to maintain blade alignment and handle integrity. If you're someone who throws everything in the dishwasher, this will be a point of friction.

Second, the swivel head can feel loose to some users. If you're accustomed to rigid, fixed-blade peelers, the slight give in the swivel mechanism might feel imprecise at first. Most adapt quickly, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Finally, the potato eye remover, while functional, isn't as effective as a dedicated tool. It works for small eyes, but larger blemishes need a paring knife.
