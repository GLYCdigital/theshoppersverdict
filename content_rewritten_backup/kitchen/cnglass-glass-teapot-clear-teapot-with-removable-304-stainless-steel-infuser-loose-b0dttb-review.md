If you've ever watched loose-leaf tea swirl in a glass pot and thought, *"I want that at home,"* the CNGLASS teapot is the one that actually delivers. It's not flashy, not smart, not insulated — it's a straightforward borosilicate glass brewer that does one job exceptionally well. And at 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, owners mostly agree.

Here's the real story after handling the specs and digging through buyer feedback.

## What you actually get

The box contains the glass teapot (roughly 35 oz / 1000 ml capacity), a removable 304 stainless steel infuser basket, and a glass lid. That's it — no warming candle, no extra mesh filters, no coasters. The infuser is the star: it's deep enough to hold a solid two tablespoons of loose leaf without cramming, and the stainless mesh is fine enough to keep most teas contained. The pot itself is made from borosilicate glass, which is the same material used in lab glassware — it handles rapid temperature changes far better than standard soda-lime glass.

## Performance in the real world

I've seen enough glass teapots crack on first use to be skeptical, but the CNGLASS holds up. The borosilicate construction means you can pour boiling water straight in without pre-warming the pot. The wide mouth (about 4 inches across) makes filling and cleaning genuinely easy — you can reach in with a sponge and hit every surface. That's a big deal because most teapots in this price range have narrow necks that trap tannin stains.

The infuser sits snugly in the rim and lifts out cleanly, so you can stop the steep exactly when you want. It doesn't wobble or drop into the water, which is a common failure point on cheaper designs. One buyer noted, *"The strainer is actually big enough for a real amount of tea — not those tiny thimble baskets other pots come with."* That matches the specs: the basket is roughly 3 inches in diameter and 2 inches deep.

## What buyers consistently praise

- **Durability:** Multiple reviewers mention dropping the lid or tapping the pot against the sink with no damage. Borosilicate is legitimately tougher than the cheap glass used in $15 teapots.
- **Visual clarity:** The glass stays crystal clear even after dozens of brews, according to several long-term owners. No clouding or etching.
- **Infuser quality:** The 304 stainless is magnetic (so it won't rust) and the mesh doesn't clog with fine rooibos or chamomile.

## Where it falls short

No product is perfect, and this one has three honest flaws.

**First:** There's no strainer built into the spout. If you overfill the infuser or use very fine tea (matcha, powdered blends), a few leaves will sneak past the basket and end up in your cup. It's not a dealbreaker — just don't cram the basket to the brim.

**Second:** The lid sits loosely. It's designed that way for venting, but if you pour aggressively at a steep angle, you'll get a dribble down the side. Pour firmly but not frantically.

**Third:** The handle transfers heat. It's comfortable and well-shaped, but after the water's been boiling for a few minutes, the handle runs noticeably warm. Use a mitt or let the pot sit for a minute before pouring. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"Great pot, but I burned my fingers once. Not a design flaw — just physics."*

## Who should buy this

- **Loose-leaf tea enthusiasts** who brew daily and want to see the leaves unfurl — this pot is a visual treat.
- **People who hate cleaning teapots** — the wide mouth and removable infuser make this the easiest glass pot to maintain in its price class.
- **Gift-givers** looking for a practical, non-gimmicky present for a tea lover. It looks premium on a shelf.

**Skip it if** you need a stovetop-safe pot (it's not — stovetop use will crack it), if you brew only tea bags (waste of the infuser), or if you want an insulated pot that keeps tea hot for hours (this is a brewing vessel, not a carafe).

## FAQ

**Q: Can I put this on a stovetop or in a microwave?**  
A: No stovetop — the direct flame will shatter the glass. Microwave is possible but not recommended because the metal infuser will spark if left inside. Just use boiled water from a kettle.

**Q: How do I clean the tea stains?**  
A: Rinse with warm water immediately after use. For stubborn tannin stains, fill with warm water and a teaspoon of baking soda, let sit 15 minutes, then wipe. Don't use abrasive scrubbers — they'll scratch the glass.

**Q: Will the glass crack with boiling water?**  
A: The borosilicate construction is specifically designed to handle thermal shock. As long as you don't put it on a cold surface directly from boiling, you're fine. Let it cool naturally before washing.
