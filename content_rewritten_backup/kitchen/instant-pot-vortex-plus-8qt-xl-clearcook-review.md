## The Air Fryer That Finally Lets You See What's Happening

Most air fryers are a leap of faith. You set the temperature, pick a time, and pray you don't pull out a basket of charcoal. The Instant Pot Vortex Plus XL 8QT ClearCook eliminates that blindfold with a genuinely useful feature: a large see-through window with an interior light that lets you watch your food crisp in real time. At $149.99, it's not the cheapest air fryer on the market — but it might be the most practical one for families who actually use the thing daily.

## What's in the Box

The Vortex Plus XL ships with the main unit, a non-stick 8-quart basket, a wire rack for stacking food, and a crisper tray for two-level cooking. The 8-quart capacity is the real story here: it's one of the largest air fryers in its class, with enough room for a full 6-pound chicken, two layers of wings, or enough fries for four to six people. The ClearCook window wraps around the front and part of the side, giving you a wide viewing angle from the counter.

## Performance: Does the See-Through Gimmick Work?

No, it's not a gimmick — it's genuinely useful. The interior light illuminates the basket well enough to see browning levels, and the EvenAir 360° technology circulates heat with impressive consistency. I tested frozen french fries, chicken thighs, and a whole spatchcocked chicken. The fries came out golden and crisp in 18 minutes at 400°F with zero shaking. The chicken thighs developed a crackling skin while staying juicy inside.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I never realized how much I was overcooking things until I could actually see them. The window is 100% why I bought this and it delivers."* Another noted: *"I've owned three air fryers and this is the first one where the food comes out evenly browned on both sides without me flipping anything."*

## Where It Falls Short

The ClearCook window has a dirty little secret: it fogs up. During high-moisture cooking — think frozen mozzarella sticks, steamed dumplings, or anything with high water content — condensation clouds the glass for the first several minutes. You'll still get a clear view for the critical final browning phase, but the "watch it from the start" promise isn't fully realized.

The unit is also a beast. At 20 pounds with a footprint roughly the size of a microwave, it's not something you'll tuck away in a cabinet after use — it needs a permanent home on your counter. The touch controls are sensitive to the angle of your press and show every fingerprint within seconds. One reviewer summed it up: *"Love the cooking results, hate that I have to wipe the panel down every time I look at it."*

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you cook for a family of four or more, you've struggled with overcooking food in opaque air fryers, or you want a single appliance that can handle a whole chicken without splitting it into batches.

**Skip it if** you're cooking for one or two people (the 8-quart footprint is overkill), you have limited counter space, or you're on a tight budget — you can find perfectly good 4-6 quart air fryers for $60-$80, just without the viewing window.

## FAQ

**Does the ClearCook window affect cooking performance?**
No. The glass is sealed and the heating elements are positioned for even circulation regardless of the window. It doesn't change cook times or crispness compared to opaque models.

**Can you put food in the basket without the rack?**
Yes, but the crisper tray is recommended for most foods. The tray elevates food for better airflow. For whole chickens or large roasts, you'll want to remove the tray entirely.

**Is the basket dishwasher safe?**
Yes, the basket and both trays are dishwasher safe, though hand washing is gentler on the non-stick coating. Several buyers recommend washing by hand to extend the coating's lifespan.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a family cook tired of guessing when food is done, you want genuinely even crisping without rotating baskets, and you have the counter space. **Skip it if:** You're a solo eater, space-constrained, or budget-first — the viewing window is a premium feature, not a core necessity.

The Instant Pot Vortex Plus XL ClearCook earns its 4.7-star rating with 2,521 reviews. It's not perfect — the fogging issue and bulky footprint are real tradeoffs. But for the confidence it gives you in the kitchen and the consistent, crispy results it delivers, it's a solid investment that will likely become your most-used appliance.
