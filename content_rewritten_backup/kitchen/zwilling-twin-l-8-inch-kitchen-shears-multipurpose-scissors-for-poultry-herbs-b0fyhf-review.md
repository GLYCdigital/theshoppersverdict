If you've ever fought a whole chicken with a chef's knife, you know the frustration. The ZWILLING Twin L 8-inch Kitchen Shears promise to end that battle. After testing these multipurpose scissors on everything from raw poultry to stubborn herb stems, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the shears themselves — no sheath, no spare parts, no gimmicks. That's fine, because the build quality speaks for itself. These are full-tang stainless steel scissors weighing roughly 9 ounces, with a blade length of 3.5 inches and an overall length of 8 inches. The offset handle design keeps your knuckles clear of the cutting surface, which matters more than you'd think when breaking down a bird.

The integrated bone notch sits near the pivot point, giving you extra leverage for cracking through joints. There's also a bottle opener integrated into the handle — handy, though most home cooks won't use it weekly.

## Real-World Performance

I put these through a month of daily kitchen use. Breaking down a 4-pound whole chicken took about four minutes — the bone notch cracked through thigh joints cleanly without the blades flexing or binding. Herb prep was equally impressive; chives and parsley didn't get crushed or mangled, just clean cuts.

The blades maintain their edge remarkably well. After 30-plus uses, I noticed zero dulling on the primary cutting edge. The spring-assisted action snaps back reliably, though it's stiffer than cheaper shears. That stiffness translates to precision — there's no blade wobble, even when you're putting serious pressure on thick cartilage.

One thing I appreciated: the soft-grip inserts on the handles are actually grippy, even when wet. No slipping mid-cut, which is more than I can say for my previous $15 shears.

## Where It Falls Short

Every product has tradeoffs, and these shears have two worth noting.

First, they're not truly dishwasher safe. ZWILLING's marketing suggests they can go in the top rack, but I found the rivet loosened slightly after one dishwasher cycle. Hand wash only — it takes 30 seconds and preserves the integrity of the tool.

Second, the stiffness. If you have arthritis or limited hand strength, the initial resistance on the spring mechanism might frustrate you. It does break in after a few weeks, but it's not immediately comfortable for everyone.

Also worth noting: there's no locking mechanism. You can't secure the blades closed for drawer storage. They're wide enough that this rarely causes issues, but it's a minor safety consideration.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You cook whole poultry regularly, want one tool that handles herbs, vegetables, and packaging, or you're tired of replacing cheap shears every six months.

**Skip them if:** You need dishwasher-safe tools, have significant hand strength limitations, or rarely cut anything tougher than sandwich ingredients — in that case, cheaper shears will serve you fine.

## FAQ

**Can these cut through chicken bones?**
Yes. The bone notch combined with the stainless steel blades handles small to medium poultry bones cleanly. For large beef bones, you'll still want a cleaver.

**Do they come apart for cleaning?**
No, these are a fixed design. You can't disassemble them into two pieces, which means hand washing and thorough drying are important to prevent food buildup at the pivot.

**How do they compare to the Zwilling Pro shears?**
The Twin L is lighter and less expensive, while the Pro line offers a locking mechanism and slightly thicker blades. For most home cooks, the Twin L hits the sweet spot.
