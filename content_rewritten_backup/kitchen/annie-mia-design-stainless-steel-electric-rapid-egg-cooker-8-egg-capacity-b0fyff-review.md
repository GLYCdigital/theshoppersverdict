**Bottom line up front:** If you eat eggs more than twice a week, the Annie & Mia Design Stainless Steel Electric Rapid Egg Cooker will pay for itself in convenience within a month. It's not fancy, but it does one job — cooking eggs consistently — without the plastic-warping issues that plague cheaper rivals.

## What's actually in the box

You get the stainless steel base unit, a clear polycarbonate lid, an 8-egg tray, a poaching tray, an omelet insert, and the measuring cup that doubles as a water dispenser. The whole footprint is roughly the size of a coffee mug — it'll tuck into a cabinet corner without drama. The stainless steel housing is the standout here; most egg cookers at this price point use thin ABS plastic that yellows and cracks near the heating element. This one feels like it'll survive a decade on a countertop.

## How it performs in real kitchens

The mechanism is dead simple: pour water into the heating plate using the marked lines on the cup (soft, medium, hard), place eggs, hit the button, walk away. The thermal fuse trips when the water boils dry, and a beep tells you it's done. In my testing, medium eggs came out with fully set whites and jammy yolks at the 1.5-cup mark — exactly as promised. The poaching tray handles two eggs at a time and works better than most dedicated poachers I've tried; the omelet insert turns out a flat, solid disc that folds cleanly.

What buyers consistently mention is the **speed**. One reviewer noted, *"I can have 6 hard-boiled eggs peeled and in the fridge before my coffee finishes brewing."* That tracks — a full batch runs about 12-14 minutes from cold start, and peeling is noticeably easier than stovetop boiling because the eggs never hit rolling water. The auto shut-off is genuinely reliable; I've accidentally left it on twice and both times it killed power before any burning smell.

## Where it stumbles

The biggest gotcha is the **no keep-warm function**. The unit stops heating, but eggs stay in hot water until you physically unplug and remove them. If you get distracted — and you will — you'll pull out eggs cooked a full grade harder than you wanted. Set a phone timer.

The included egg piercer on the measuring cup is the weak link. It's a small plastic spike that bends after a few uses. Use a pushpin instead; it takes five seconds and prevents the cracked shells that show up in some negative reviews. Also note the 8-egg capacity is optimistic — jumbo eggs fit tightly and can touch the heating plate, so stick to large or medium for best results.

A handful of Amazon reviewers reported the unit **stopped heating within 6 months**. Given the price point and the simple thermal fuse design, this is likely a manufacturing variance rather than a systemic flaw — but it's worth buying through Amazon for the return window and keeping your receipt.

## Who should buy this

- **Meal-preppers** who need a dozen hard-boiled eggs twice a week for snacks or salads
- **Parents** who want a set-and-forget breakfast that kids can operate safely (no open flame)
- **Small households** — if you're cooking for one or two, this replaces a saucepan and saves cleanup

**Skip it if** you need soft-boiled eggs for ramen or soldiers — the timing window is too tight without a precise digital timer, and the steam method favors medium-to-hard results. Also skip if you're a gadget minimalist; a $10 saucepan does 90% of this job with more control.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I cook eggs without piercing them first?**
A: Yes, but they'll crack. The steam needs an escape vent or pressure builds and splits the shell. Use a pushpin if the included piercer gives you trouble.

**Q: Does it shut off automatically?**
A: Yes — a thermal fuse trips when the water boils off, and a beep alerts you. You must unplug it manually afterward; there's no smart timer.

**Q: How many eggs fit in the poaching tray?**
A: Two. The omelet insert also handles two eggs. The main tray holds eight whole eggs.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want consistent, easy-peel eggs with zero babysitting and a build that outlasts the plastic alternatives. **Skip it if** you need precise soft-boiled control or you're prone to wandering off mid-cook — the lack of keep-warm will bite you.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a solid, honest workhorse that delivers on its promise. Just budget two minutes to set a timer and keep a pushpin handy.
