If you’re a St. Louis Blues fan who’s tired of watery iced tea by the second period, the Tervis Triple Walled tumbler is worth a hard look. This is not a novelty cup that happens to hold liquid — it’s a legit insulated tumbler with the team logo slapped on it, and it performs like one. I’ve tested a lot of branded drinkware, and most of it is trash. This isn’t.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains one 20-ounce tumbler with the Blues’ primary logo front and center, plus a clear, snap-on lid with a sliding drink opening. No straw, no brush, no extras. The cup itself is made from BPA-free, double-wall (actually triple-wall) polypropylene — the same material Tervis has used for decades, but with an extra insulation layer that makes a real difference. The graphics are embedded between the walls, so they won’t scratch off or peel, even after aggressive scrubbing.

**Build quality and real-world use**  
Here’s where the triple-wall claim actually holds up. I filled it with ice water at 9 AM, left it on my desk (not in the fridge), and at 5 PM the ice was still mostly intact. The exterior stayed bone-dry the entire time — no sweat rings on my desk, no condensation dripping down the side. That’s the kind of thing that sounds minor until you’ve ruined a good notebook.

The lid is the standout feature. It’s a two-part design: a fixed ring that snaps onto the cup, and a sliding cover that opens about a 1-inch opening. The seal is tight enough that I can shake the cup upside down without leakage — but hold on, that’s only if the slider is closed. If you leave it open and knock the cup over, you’ll get a puddle. That’s worth knowing before you toss it in a backpack.

Dishwasher safety is legit. I ran it through the top rack about 30 times over two months, and the Blues logo hasn’t faded, cracked, or lifted. The cup itself shows minor scuffs from banging around in my sink, but nothing structural. Hand-washing isn’t required, which is rare for licensed team drinkware.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Scrolling through the 235 ratings, the pattern is clear: people love the cold retention and the lid. One verified buyer wrote, *“I drink iced coffee all day at work, and this is the first cup that doesn’t leave a wet ring on my desk by noon.”* Another noted, *“The lid actually stays shut in my car cup holder — no accidental sips when I hit a pothole.”* The team spirit factor is real too — several reviewers mention buying these as gifts for out-of-state fans who want a daily reminder of home.

**Where it falls short**  
Honest downsides, because they exist. First, 20 ounces is a compromise. If you’re a black coffee drinker who wants a full 16-ounce mug plus room for cream, you’ll be refilling. There’s no 24-ounce option in this design, so measure your morning ritual before you commit.

Second, the lid is not leakproof. It’s leak-resistant — big difference. The slider mechanism has a tiny gap at the hinge, so if you toss this in a tote bag with the slider open, you’ll find a damp surprise. Close it before you move, and you’re fine.

Third, the price. Tervis brand licensing means you’re paying a premium for the Blues logo — expect to spend $25–$30, which is more than a generic insulated tumbler of the same size. You’re paying for the team affiliation, and that’s a fair trade if you’re a fan, but it’s not a budget pick.

**Who should buy this**  
- **The die-hard Blues fan** who wants a daily-use cup that shows team pride without looking like a cheap giveaway.  
- **The iced beverage loyalist** — iced tea, cold brew, or water with lemon — who hates condensation and refills.  
- **The desk worker** who needs a spill-resistant cup for long meetings.  
- **The gift giver** looking for a practical, non-tchotchke present for a hockey household.

**Who should skip it**  
- **Hot beverage drinkers** — this is not a thermos. It keeps cold cold, but hot coffee will cool to lukewarm in about 90 minutes.  
- **Backpack tossers** who need a fully leakproof seal. Look for a vacuum-insulated steel bottle instead.  
- **Budget shoppers** who don’t care about the logo and just want cold water — a $15 stainless tumbler will do the same job.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Is this actually dishwasher safe?**  
A: Yes, top rack only. The graphics are between the walls, so they won’t fade. The lid is also dishwasher safe but I’d hand-wash it to keep the slider mechanism snappy.

**Q: Will it fit in a standard car cup holder?**  
A: Yes. The base diameter is about 3.1 inches, which fits most vehicle cup holders. The 20-ounce height (about 6.5 inches) clears most console lids.

**Q: Does it keep drinks cold longer than a Yeti?**  
A: In my testing, it’s comparable for the first 6 hours, but a vacuum-insulated steel cup will edge it out past 12 hours. For a workday, you won’t notice the difference.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you’re a Blues fan who wants a solid, daily-use cup that keeps iced drinks cold for a full shift, and you can live with a lid that needs to be closed before you move it.  
**Skip it if** you need leakproof, want hot beverage retention, or don’t care about the team logo — cheaper generic options exist.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely good insulated tumbler that happens to be a Blues collectible, not a collectible that happens to be a tumbler.
