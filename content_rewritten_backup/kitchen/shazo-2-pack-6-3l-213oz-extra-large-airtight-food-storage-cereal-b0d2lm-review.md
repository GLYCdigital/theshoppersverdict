These aren't your grandma's cereal keepers — each one swallows a full 5-pound bag of flour with room to spare, making them the biggest players in the standard pantry-container game.

## What You Actually Get

Two identical clear plastic containers, each measuring roughly 8.7 x 6.3 x 9.8 inches with a 6.3-liter (213-ounce) capacity. The bodies are BPA-free, food-grade PET plastic — clear enough to see exactly what's inside without opening. Each lid has four flip-lock latches and a silicone gasket running the perimeter. There are also measurement markings printed on the side, which sound gimmicky but genuinely help when you're eyeballing how much pasta you have left.

## Real-World Performance

The headline feature is the seal, and it's legitimately good. The four-latch system with the silicone gasket creates a lock that holds up to a water-submersion test — I filled one with dry beans and dunked it for two minutes; zero moisture inside. That's the level of protection you want for flour, sugar, and grains that attract pantry moths faster than a picnic attracts ants.

The clear plastic is thick enough to survive regular use without cracking — around 2mm on the walls, which feels substantial. One buyer noted, "I've had cheap containers crack within weeks. These have survived my toddler throwing them off the counter." That's the durability you're paying for.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about physics: a full 6.3L container of flour weighs around 9 pounds. Stack two of these on a shelf and you're handling some serious weight. The containers are stackable, but you'll want them at waist height — pulling a full one from an upper cabinet is a two-hand job that risks spills.

The lid requires deliberate effort. All four latches need to snap shut in sequence to get the full airtight seal. Close only two and you've got a good-enough seal that will keep bugs out but won't pass the dunk test. Several reviewers mentioned this learning curve.
