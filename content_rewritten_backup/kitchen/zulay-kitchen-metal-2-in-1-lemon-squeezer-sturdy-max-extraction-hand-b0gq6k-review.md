Most citrus presses are flimsy plastic things that flex and groan the moment you lean into a lemon. The Zulay Kitchen Metal 2-in-1 takes a different approach: it's built like a small mechanical press, and that weight changes everything about how it performs.

**What you actually get**

Out of the box, you're looking at a one-piece zinc alloy press with two cup sizes — a smaller reamer for lemons and limes, and a larger one that accommodates oranges and grapefruit halves. There are no interchangeable parts to lose or fumble with. The handles have a soft-touch rubberized grip, and the whole unit weighs roughly 1.5 pounds. It's noticeably denser than the $10 plastic squeezers you'll find at any big-box store.

**Performance and real-world use**

The geometry here matters. The pivot point sits high, which gives you better leverage than most presses in this price range. I squeezed a dozen lemons for a batch of lemonade, and the yield was consistently impressive — about 3 tablespoons per medium lemon, which is on par with electric juicers. The reamer is deeply ridged, so it tears through the flesh efficiently rather than just crushing it.

What's genuinely surprising is how clean the action feels. The handles glide smoothly without that grinding, metal-on-metal squeak you get from cheaper steel presses. The base sits flat and stable on the counter, so you're not chasing it around while you squeeze. One buyer put it well: "I've owned three different lemon squeezers and this is the first one that feels like it was engineered rather than stamped out."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme in the 235 reviews is durability. Multiple reviewers mention dropping it, knocking it off counters, and running it through the dishwasher (against instructions) without any bending or breaking. One long-term owner noted: "Had mine for eight months of daily use making lime juice for cocktails. Still looks brand new and the hinge hasn't loosened a millimeter."

The 2-in-1 design also earns consistent praise. People who cook with both lemons and oranges appreciate not having to switch tools mid-recipe. The larger cup handles a standard orange half cleanly, which is rare — most combo presses have a token big cup that's barely functional.

**Where it falls short**

The strainer holes are the main compromise. They're large enough that fine pulp and occasional seeds make it through into your juice. If you're making a delicate vinaigrette or a cocktail where clarity matters, you'll want to strain afterward. It's a minor annoyance, but it's worth knowing.

The weight is also a double-edged sword. While it makes the press feel substantial, it's genuinely tiring for anyone with arthritis or grip strength issues. This is not the tool for someone who wants a lightweight, one-handed squeeze. And despite the "dishwasher safe" claims you might see in some listings, the manufacturer recommends hand-washing. Acidic juice left to dry on the metal can leave water spots that are stubborn to remove.

**Who should buy this**

Get this if you cook or bartend regularly and want a tool that will outlast your current kitchen setup. It's ideal for home cooks who squeeze more than one lemon at a time, cocktail enthusiasts who juice limes by the dozen, and anyone tired of replacing flimsy plastic presses every six months.

Skip it if you have arthritis or limited hand strength, if you need pulp-free juice straight from the press, or if you refuse to hand-wash kitchen tools.

**FAQ**

*Can it handle pomegranates or other hard fruits?*
Technically yes, but the reamer is designed for citrus. Harder fruits will strain the mechanism and won't yield much juice. Stick to lemons, limes, oranges, and grapefruit.

*Does the metal affect the taste of the juice?*
No. The zinc alloy is food-grade and doesn't impart any metallic flavor. The juice tastes identical to what you'd get from a plastic press.

*How do you clean it properly?*
Rinse immediately after use with warm water and mild soap. Dry thoroughly with a towel. Avoid letting citrus juice sit on the metal, and don't run it through the dishwasher — the heat can damage the grip coating.
