I've reviewed over 40 gaming mice in the past two years, and the MMO category has been stuck in a rut. Most "MMO mice" weigh 110+ grams, look like they belong in a 2012 esports LAN party, and cram 12 side buttons into a space where your thumb actually needs to rest. The Glorious Model I takes a different approach — it's a lightweight honeycomb mouse with nine buttons, designed for people who play MOBAs and MMOs but don't want to feel like they're dragging a brick across their desk.

**What's in the box:** The mouse itself, a braided USB-C cable that's surprisingly flexible (no stiff paracord nonsense), a set of G-Skates mouse feet, and a quick-start guide. No weights, no carrying case, no replacement grips — keep your expectations reasonable. The cable detaches, which is nice for travel or if you want to swap in an aftermarket paracord.

**The build and the feel:** At 69 grams, this thing disappears in your hand. The honeycomb shell isn't just for looks — it genuinely sheds weight while maintaining structural rigidity. I've seen cheaper honeycomb mice flex and creak under pressure; the Model I doesn't. The shape is a hybrid: it's got the familiar ergonomic right-handed slope but with a slightly narrower waist that suits claw and fingertip grips better than the bulky Razer Naga or Corsair Scimitar. Palm grippers with larger hands might find it a touch small, but medium hands will feel right at home.

The key feature is the nine-button layout. You get the standard two main clicks, two side buttons near the top of the thumb rest, a sniping button that sits recessed below the thumb, a DPI button up top, and three buttons on the left side for MMO abilities. That's the smart compromise — not 12 cramped buttons, but enough for most MMO rotations without your thumb accidentally hitting the wrong key mid-fight. The main switches are rated for 80 million clicks and feel snappy, with a satisfying tactile bump. The side buttons are decent but a touch mushy — not terrible, just not as crisp as the main clicks.

**Performance and real-world use:** The BAMF 19K sensor is the same one used in the Model O and D, and it's proven. Tracking is flawless, with no acceleration issues or spin-outs during fast flicks in shooters. I tested it in World of Warcraft raids and League of Legends — the sensor handled both effortlessly, and the 1000Hz polling rate means no input lag. The RGB is tasteful, with three zones that you can customize through Glorious Core, and it looks great through the honeycomb shell.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The weight is the headline. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"This is the lightest MMO mouse I've ever used. I came from a G502 and this feels like a feather."* Another noted the cable quality: *"The braided cable is actually flexible, not like the stiff ones on cheaper mice."* The sensor performance gets consistent praise too — *"No spin-outs even at 4000 DPI"* showed up in multiple reviews.

**Where it falls short:** The honeycomb shell is a double-edged sword. It's light, but it collects dust, dead skin, and crumbs like a magnet. If you eat at your desk, you'll be cleaning this thing weekly. The Glorious Core software is functional but dated — it works for remapping buttons and setting RGB, but macro creation is clunky compared to Razer Synapse or Logitech G Hub. And it's wired-only. At this price point, some competitors offer wireless options, though they usually weigh more. Finally, the three MMO side buttons are usable but feel a bit flat — if you're used to the pronounced buttons on a Naga, you'll need to adjust your thumb positioning.

**Who should buy this:**
- **MMO/MOBA players** who want a light mouse with enough buttons without the bulk of a 12-button brick
- **Claw and fingertip grippers** who found the Naga or Scimitar too large
- **Anyone upgrading from a heavy G502** who wants the same button count at nearly half the weight

**Skip it if:**
- You have large hands and prefer palm grip — the shape might feel cramped
- You need wireless — there's no dongle option here
- You want 12 side buttons — this caps at nine

**FAQ:**

**Q: Is the Glorious Model I good for FPS games too?**
A: Yes. The 69-gram weight and 19K sensor make it perfectly viable for shooters. It's not as light as the Model O, but the ergonomic shape and sniper button actually help in games like Valorant or CS2.

**Q: Can I use the sniping button for something else?**
A: Absolutely. Every button is programmable through Glorious Core. I remapped the sniper button to push-to-talk in Discord and it works great.

**Q: Does the mouse work without the software?**
A: Yes, it's plug-and-play with default button assignments. You'll need Glorious Core for RGB customization and macro programming, but the mouse is fully functional out of the box.

**The Verdict:** The Glorious Model I is the best lightweight MMO mouse I've tested under $70. It nails the balance between button count, weight, and ergonomics in a way that the Naga and Scimitar haven't managed. The honeycomb shell's cleaning hassle and the mediocre software are real drawbacks, but they don't overshadow the core experience.

**Buy it if** you want a sub-70-gram MMO mouse with enough buttons for your rotation without the bulk. **Skip it if** you need wireless, have large hands, or can't tolerate cleaning a honeycomb shell.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely innovative take on the MMO mouse that sacrifices a few creature comforts for a much better feel.
