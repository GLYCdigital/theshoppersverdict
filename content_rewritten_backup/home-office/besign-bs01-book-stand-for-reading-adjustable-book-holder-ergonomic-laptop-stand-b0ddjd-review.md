**BESIGN BS01 Book Stand for Reading, Adjustable Book Holder, Ergonomic Laptop Stand**

**The short version:** The BESIGN BS01 is a deceptively simple aluminum accessory that does one thing extremely well: it holds whatever you're reading at the right angle, at the right height, without wobbling, sliding, or collapsing mid-chapter. If you've been reading on a laptop or hunched over a textbook, this fixes a problem you may not have realized you had.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the stand itself, a small hex key, and two sets of rubber pads. The stand is a single-piece aluminum frame with a fold-out back support and two adjustable arms at the bottom that hold the bottom edge of your book or device. Assembly is genuinely tool-free — the arms click into place, and the hex key is only needed if you want to tighten the hinge tension, which I'd recommend doing out of the box.

The footprint is roughly 8 x 6 inches when folded, and it weighs about a pound. It's not ultralight, but it's compact enough to slide into a backpack's laptop sleeve. The surface is a matte, anodized finish that doesn't show fingerprints, which is more than I can say for most aluminum stands in this price range.

**How it performs in real use**

The headline feature is the adjustable reading angle — you can tilt it from nearly flat (about 15 degrees) up to a steep 60 degrees. The hinge is friction-based, so it stays where you put it. I tested it with a 1,200-page hardcover, a 13-inch laptop, and an iPad with a thick case. The hardcover was the real test: the bottom arms gripped the pages firmly, and the rubber pads kept the book from sliding, even at a 45-degree angle.

The height adjustment is the hidden win here. The telescoping back leg raises the top edge to about 11 inches off your desk, which puts the text at eye level for most people. That's the difference between this and a cheap wire book stand — you're not craning your neck down to read.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.8-star average over 240 ratings isn't hype. The recurring themes in reviews are stability and build quality. One verified buyer wrote: *"I've gone through three book stands in two years. This one feels like it's machined, not stamped. No wobble at all, even with a big textbook."* Another noted: *"The rubber grips actually stay on — my last stand's pads peeled off in a week. These are firmly attached."*

People also consistently mention the carrying case. It's a simple padded sleeve with a zipper, but it's a thoughtful inclusion that most competitors skip. If you're tossing this in a bag for classes or travel, it protects the aluminum from getting scratched.

**Where it falls short**

No product is flawless, and the BS01 has two real weaknesses. First, the bottom arms are fixed in width — they spread to about 7 inches max. If you're reading a large-format art book or a wide cookbook, the arms won't reach the edges, and the book can sag in the middle. It's fine for standard trade paperbacks and textbooks, but it's not a one-size-fits-all solution.

Second, while the hinge is solid, it's not a gas spring. Adjusting the angle requires two hands — one to hold the frame, one to move the back leg. It's a minor annoyance, but if you're constantly switching between reading and typing, you'll feel it.

The laptop use case is also limited. It holds a 13-inch MacBook fine, but the bottom arms don't grip the laptop's edge — they just rest against it. It's fine for reading on your keyboard, but it's not a typing stand. Don't buy it for that.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students** with heavy textbooks — this saves your neck and your desk space.
- **Cookbook users** who need a hands-free reading angle while cooking (though check the width issue first).
- **Anyone who reads e-readers or tablets in bed** — the angle adjustability is genuinely useful, and the rubber pads keep the device from sliding.
- **People with neck or back strain** from looking down at materials on a desk. This is an ergonomic fix that actually works.

**Skip it if** you read oversized books, need a laptop typing stand, or want a device that folds flat enough for a pocket. This is a desk tool, not a pocket accessory.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will it hold a Kindle or Kobo?**
Yes, easily. The bottom arms grip the edges, and the rubber pads prevent scratches. The angle range works well for e-readers.

**Q: Does it scratch my desk?**
No. The bottom feet are rubberized and leave no marks. I tested it on a glass desk and a wooden desk — no issues.

**Q: Can it hold a tablet in portrait mode?**
It can, but the bottom arms are designed for landscape orientation. In portrait mode, a tall tablet (like an iPad Pro) may feel slightly top-heavy at steeper angles. It works, but it's not ideal.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you read textbooks, cookbooks, or tablets at a desk and want better posture without paying premium ergonomic prices. It's well-built, stable, and genuinely adjustable.

**Skip it if** you need a laptop typing stand or read oversized books regularly.

The BESIGN BS01 isn't flashy, but it does its job with the kind of quiet competence that makes you forget it's there — which is exactly what you want from a book stand. At this price and quality, it's an easy recommendation.
