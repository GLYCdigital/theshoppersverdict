If you own a Dell Optiplex Micro 9020, 7050, 7010, 7020, or 7040, you already know the pain: the original power brick dies, and Dell wants $60+ for a replacement. This 90W third-party adapter undercuts that price significantly while claiming to match OEM specs exactly. I've tested it across multiple Optiplex Micro models to see if the savings come with compromises.

**What's in the Box**

You get the adapter brick itself, a standard AC power cord (about 3 feet), and the DC barrel cable that connects to the PC (about 5 feet). No adapters for other laptop brands, no USB ports, no gimmicks — just a straight replacement for the OEM part. The brick is compact at roughly 4.5 x 2 x 1.2 inches, which is slightly larger than the Dell original but still easily hidden behind a monitor or under a desk.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The output is rated at 19.5V/4.62A — exactly matching Dell's OEM spec for these Micro models. In real-world testing, I ran a 7050 Micro with an i5-6500T under sustained CPU load (video encoding) for two hours. The adapter held steady voltage with zero dips, and the system never throttled or warned about power issues. The barrel connector fits snugly into the port — no wiggling, no intermittent power loss, which is the most common failure point on cheap third-party adapters.

The build quality is solid for the price point. The casing is hard plastic with a matte finish that resists scratches, and the strain relief where cables meet the brick is reinforced. It's not as premium-feeling as the OEM Dell brick, but it's not flimsy either. The LED indicator on the brick lights up green when powered — a small touch that helps with troubleshooting.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The compatibility claim holds up. One buyer noted: *"Works perfectly with my Optiplex 7040 Micro. I was worried about the connector size, but it clicks in securely and the PC boots without any 'adapter not recognized' warnings."* Another mentioned: *"Replaced a dying OEM brick that cost twice as much. **Where It Falls Short**

The 5-foot DC cable is the biggest practical complaint. If your Optiplex Micro is mounted behind a monitor arm or tucked into a corner, you may need a extension cord or a different mounting position. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's shorter than the OEM's 6.5-foot cable.

Heat is the second issue. Under sustained load, the brick runs warm — around 110°F by my infrared thermometer reading. That's within safe operating range, but it's noticeably warmer than the Dell original. If your adapter sits in an enclosed space with poor airflow, consider giving it room to breathe. A few reviewers reported the brick getting "hot to the touch" during heavy use, though none reported failures.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Optiplex Micro owners with a dead or dying OEM brick** — this is a direct, cost-effective replacement that matches factory specs.
- **Anyone setting up multiple Optiplex Micro units** (office managers, IT folks) — the price point makes it viable for bulk replacement.

**Skip it if** your Optiplex is positioned far from a power outlet, or if you're the type who wants OEM components only — this is a third-party part, and the slightly warmer operating temp reflects that.
