Graph paper notebooks are a dime a dozen on Amazon, but most are flimsy, bleed-prone, or both. The SUNEE 1-Subject Graph Ruled Spiral Notebook sits at 4.7 stars with 235 ratings for a reason — it gets the fundamentals right without overcomplicating anything. Here's the breakdown after digging through the specs and real buyer feedback.

## What you actually get

This is a single-subject notebook — one spiral, one set of pages, no dividers. The covers are made of a flexible poly material that resists water spills and creasing far better than the cardboard covers on cheaper competitors. The spiral binding runs the full length of the left side, and the pages are 8.5" x 11" standard size, so they'll fit in any binder or folder if you punch them out.

The grid itself is 5mm spacing, which is the sweet spot for most technical work — tight enough for precise graphing but loose enough for legible handwriting. The ruling is printed in a light blue-gray that scans well and doesn't fight with your ink.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The paper weight is the standout feature here. It's not specified exactly, but based on buyer feedback and handling, it sits around 70-80gsm — noticeably thicker than the tissue-paper stock on budget notebooks. One reviewer noted, "I use Pilot G2 pens and was worried about bleed-through, but the ink stays put. There's minor ghosting on the back side, but nothing that makes the page unusable."

That's consistent with what most buyers report. Gel pens, rollerballs, and even most fountain pens (with finer nibs) perform acceptably. Heavy markers and brush pens are still a no-go — this isn't watercolor paper — but for everyday note-taking, it's solid.

The spiral binding is reinforced at the top and bottom with a plastic sleeve that prevents the wire from pulling out of the cover. This is a common failure point on cheaper notebooks, and SUNEE addressed it directly. Multiple reviewers specifically called out durability, with one saying, "I've thrown this in my backpack for three months of engineering classes and it hasn't fallen apart. Covers still look new."

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 235 ratings, three themes keep coming up:

- **Crisp grid lines**: "The grid is actually straight and evenly spaced. Sounds basic but you'd be surprised how many notebooks get this wrong."
- **No bleed-through**: The most common praise. Students using gel pens and highlighters report minimal show-through.
- **Durability**: The poly covers and reinforced spine survive semesters, not just weeks.

## Where it falls short

Honest tradeoffs, because they exist:

- **Page count is thin**: 70 sheets sounds like a lot until you're taking notes in two classes. Heavy users will go through this in a month. If you want a full semester from one notebook, buy a 3-pack or look at the 5-subject version.
- **Perforated edge is rough**: The pages are perforated for clean tearing, but the perforation leaves a slightly jagged edge. It doesn't affect usability, but if you're a perfectionist about page edges, it'll bug you.
- **No page numbers or table of contents**: This is a bare-bones notebook. If you need structured layouts, look elsewhere.

## Who should buy this

- **STEM students**: Math, engineering, physics — the 5mm grid is ideal for equations, graphs, and circuit diagrams.
- **Architects and designers**: The clean grid works for technical sketches and scaled drawings.
- **Bullet journalers**: The grid is a perfect foundation for layouts, and the durable cover survives daily carry.
- **Anyone who hates flimsy notebooks**: If you've been burned by cheap spirals that fall apart, this fixes that.

**Skip it if** you need heavy paper for markers or fountain pens with broad nibs, or if you want a notebook with organizational features.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the paper bleed through with fountain pens?**
A: With fine and medium nibs, you'll get minor ghosting but no bleeding. Broad nibs and wet inks will show through more noticeably. If you're a fountain pen user, test one page first.

**Q: Can you tear pages out cleanly?**
A: Yes, the perforation works, but the torn edge is slightly rough. It's fine for submission or filing, just not a clean deckle edge.

**Q: Is this notebook good for math classes?**
A: Yes — the 5mm grid is specifically well-suited for equations, graphing, and problem-solving. The pages are large enough to lay out long problem sets without crowding.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a durable, bleed-resistant graph notebook for technical work, and you don't mind a thinner page count.

**Skip it if** you're a heavy note-taker who needs a full semester from one notebook, or you require heavyweight paper for markers.
