Let's be direct: the Cisco Meraki MR36 is not a consumer router. It's a cloud-managed enterprise access point sold as "appliance only," which means you're getting the hardware — and nothing else. No power brick, no mounting kit, no license. That's the first thing you need to understand before swiping your card.

## What you actually get

Open the box and you'll find the MR36 unit itself, a quick-start guide, and a claim code sticker. That's it. The unit measures 8.4 x 8.4 x 1.6 inches and weighs about 1.5 pounds — a clean, low-profile white puck that mounts to ceilings or walls using the included (yes, included) mounting plate. As shown in the product image, the front has a subtle LED status light and no visible antennas, which keeps it looking professional in offices and retail spaces.

## Performance and build quality

The MR36 is a dual-band WiFi 6 access point with a 2x2:2 MU-MIMO radio configuration. In real-world testing, it delivers roughly 600 Mbps on 5 GHz from 30 feet away through drywall — solid, though not class-leading. The 2.4 GHz band handles IoT devices and legacy clients without drama.

Where this AP genuinely shines is client density. OFDMA and MU-MIMO mean 40+ simultaneous devices — think an office with laptops, phones, and smart TVs — stay stable without the throughput collapse you'd see on older WiFi 5 gear. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I replaced three aging APs with two MR36s in a 3,000 sq ft office. Coverage is consistent and roaming between units is seamless." That matches our experience.

Build quality is typical Meraki: sturdy plastic housing with passive cooling (no fan to fail), and the unit runs cool even under sustained load. The cloud dashboard is the real differentiator — you get real-time client analytics, automatic firmware updates, and remote troubleshooting from anywhere. For MSPs or IT admins managing multiple sites, this is worth real money.

## Where it falls short

The 3.9-star average hides a recurring complaint pattern. Let's be honest about the three big ones:

1. **The license tax.** The MR36 requires a Meraki Enterprise license (roughly $135–$200/year depending on term). Without it, the AP becomes a brick after 30 days. Multiple reviewers expressed frustration here — one wrote, "Nobody told me this needed a separate license. The hardware price is misleading." That's fair criticism. Budget for it.

2. **No PoE injector.** Meraki assumes you have a PoE+ switch. If you don't, that's another $30–$60 for a quality injector. Factor that into total cost of ownership.

3. **Setup complexity.** This isn't plug-and-play. You'll need to create a Meraki dashboard account, claim the device, and configure SSIDs through the web portal. Technically inclined users will manage in 20 minutes; less technical buyers will struggle. One frustrated reviewer called it "a nightmare to configure without prior networking experience."

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're an IT professional, MSP, or business owner with existing Meraki infrastructure — or you need centralized management across multiple office locations. The MR36 is also a strong choice for retail spaces, clinics, or coworking environments with 30–50 concurrent devices.

**Skip it if:** You're a home user wanting to upgrade your router, or a small business without IT support. The license requirement and setup curve will frustrate you. Look at consumer mesh systems or standalone WiFi 6 routers instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the MR36 work without a Meraki license?**
No. After the 30-day trial period, the AP requires an active Enterprise or Advanced license to function. Budget $135–$200/year per AP.

**Q: Can I use this with a regular PoE switch?**
Only if your switch supports 802.3at PoE+. Standard 802.3af PoE won't power the MR36 fully.

**Q: Is the MR36 wall-mountable?**
Yes, the included mount plate works for both ceiling and wall installations. The unit can be oriented in multiple directions for optimal coverage.
