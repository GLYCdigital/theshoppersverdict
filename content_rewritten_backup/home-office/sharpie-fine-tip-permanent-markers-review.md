## What's in the box

You get 24 markers in assorted colors — the standard rainbow plus some deeper tones like burgundy and olive. The fine point tip measures 1.0mm, which puts it between the ultra-fine (0.5mm) and the chisel-tip (2.0mm+). That's the sweet spot for most everyday tasks: bold enough to read from across a room, precise enough to write a shipping label or address an envelope without looking like a third-grader did it.

The barrel is the classic Sharpie shape — hexagonal, so it won't roll off your desk as badly as round pens. Each marker is individually wrapped in plastic, which keeps colors fresh on store shelves but is mildly annoying when you crack open the box.

## How they actually perform

Here's where Sharpie earns its reputation. The ink is a permanent oil-based formula that dries in about 10–15 seconds on most surfaces. That quick-dry matters more than you'd think — no smeared addresses on packages, no ruined sticky notes, no ink transfer to your fingers when you grab a marker mid-project.

The line quality is consistent. I've written dozens of labels, marked plastic storage bins, and signed packages with these — the tip holds its shape without mushiness or spreading. That's not true of cheaper generic markers, which tend to get fuzzy after a couple of weeks. The fine point also handles glossy surfaces — photo paper, laminated cards, CD labels — where water-based markers bead up and smear.

Smell is present but manageable. Sharpie's characteristic chemical odor is there, so don't use these in a windowless closet. It dissipates quickly, but if you're sensitive to solvent smells, work near a window.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.8-star rating isn't random. Scanning through buyer feedback, three themes repeat:

- **Versatility**: People use these for everything from warehouse inventory to scrapbooking. One buyer noted, "I use these for labeling everything in my workshop — metal, plastic, wood. They haven't failed me yet."
- **Color variety**: The 24-color assortment covers more than basic needs. Multiple reviewers mention the range saving them from buying separate packs for different projects.
- **Longevity**: "I bought this pack 8 months ago and still have markers that work," one reviewer reported. The ink doesn't dry out quickly even with caps off for short bursts — a common failure point with cheaper permanent markers.

## Where it falls short

Two honest complaints.

First, **bleed-through**. On standard 20-lb printer paper, the ink seeps through visibly. If you're writing on both sides of a page, don't — use a marker pad or put something underneath. This is typical of permanent markers, but it's worth knowing before you ruin a notebook page.

Second, **no pocket clips**. Unlike Sharpie's chisel-tip versions, these caps are smooth. They'll roll off angled surfaces and disappear into the bottom of a work bag. If you carry markers in a pocket or on a lanyard, look elsewhere or buy a holder.

## Who should buy this

- **Office workers** who label files, folders, and equipment — the fine tip is precise enough for small labels
- **Teachers and classroom organizers** who need durable markers in multiple colors for whiteboards (test on your board first — some surfaces stain), grading, and posters
- **Warehouse or retail staff** marking inventory on boxes, pallets, and shelving
- **Crafters** working with non-porous surfaces like glass, plastic, or metal

Skip this if you only need black, or if you primarily write on thin paper — you'll get better results from a ballpoint or gel pen.

## FAQ

**Do these work on whiteboards?**  
They're permanent markers, not dry-erase. They'll leave a stain that's difficult to remove. Use them only if you don't care about the board.

**How long do they last before drying out?**  
With caps on properly, months to years. The ink reservoir is generous for a fine point, and most users report 6+ months of regular use per marker.

**Are the colors actually different, or just variations on the same hues?**  
The 24 colors are genuinely distinct — you get a full spectrum from red through purple, plus earth tones like brown and olive. No filler near-duplicates like you see in some cheap sets.
