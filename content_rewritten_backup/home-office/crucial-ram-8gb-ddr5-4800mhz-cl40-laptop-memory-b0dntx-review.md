If your laptop feels sluggish with a dozen browser tabs open, the bottleneck is almost certainly RAM. The Crucial 8GB DDR5 4800MHz CL40 module is one of the most straightforward fixes for modern laptops, and with a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. **What you actually get**

The box contains a single 8GB DDR5 SO-DIMM module—that's it. No heatsinks, no RGB, no software. It's a bare PCB with eight DDR5 chips on one side, running at the JEDEC standard of 4800MT/s with CL40 latency. The module is rated at 1.1V, which keeps thermals low in cramped chassis. It's compatible with any laptop that has a DDR5 SO-DIMM slot—that means Intel 12th-gen and newer, or AMD Ryzen 7000 series and up. If you have a DDR4 laptop, this won't fit, so check your specs first.

**Real-world performance**

Installing this stick alongside your existing RAM (or replacing a smaller stick) gives you an immediate bump in multitasking headroom. The 4800MHz speed is the baseline for DDR5, but it's still roughly 50% faster than DDR4-3200 in bandwidth. For office work—spreadsheets, video calls, document editing—you won't notice the speed difference as much as the capacity increase. What you *will* notice is fewer stutters when switching between apps, and less disk thrashing if you were previously running on 8GB total.

One buyer put it simply: *"Installed it in my Lenovo IdeaPad and it went from spinning up the fan every time I opened Chrome to running silent. Night and day difference."* Another noted: *"Recognized immediately in BIOS, no compatibility headaches. This is why I stick with Crucial."* That tracks with the brand's reputation—they test against major OEM motherboards, so the "it just works" factor is genuinely high.

**Where it falls short**

The CL40 latency is worth mentioning. DDR5's higher latency is a known tradeoff, and this module sits at the standard spec. If you're a gamer chasing every frame, you'd want a CL36 kit or faster. But for productivity, you won't feel the difference—it's within the noise of real-world usage.

The bigger limitation is the 8GB capacity itself. If your laptop has a single slot and you're coming from 8GB, this gives you 16GB total, which is fine. But if you're starting from zero, one 8GB stick means you'll likely want a second one shortly. I'd recommend buying two sticks upfront if your budget allows—dual-channel memory gives you a measurable bandwidth boost, not just more capacity.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work with my [brand] laptop?**
Check your laptop's spec sheet for DDR5 SO-DIMM support. If it has a 12th-gen Intel or newer CPU, or AMD Ryzen 7000+, it will work. Crucial's website has a compatibility checker—use it before buying.

**Q: Can I mix this with a different brand's DDR5 module?**
Technically yes, but it's risky. Mixing modules can cause instability or run both at slower speeds. You're better off buying two identical Crucial sticks.

**Q: Is 4800MHz fast enough for gaming?**
For gaming, capacity matters more than speed. 16GB total at 4800MHz is fine for most titles. You'll only see meaningful gains from faster RAM in CPU-bound scenarios, and that's rare on laptops.
