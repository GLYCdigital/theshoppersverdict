## This notebook solves the "half-empty journal" problem

Most journals die at page 40. You abandon them, buy a new one, repeat the cycle. This large spiral notebook kills that habit with 300 numbered pages — enough for a full semester of class notes, a year of daily journaling, or roughly eight months of work meeting documentation.

The numbered pages matter more than you'd think. When you can write "see page 214" in your index, you actually use the table of contents. It turns a passive notebook into an organized system.

## What you actually get

The notebook measures roughly 8.5 x 11 inches — standard letter size, not the cramped A5 that most "journal" listings push. The spiral binding runs along the top, which is a deliberate choice: it means the notebook lays flat whether you're right-handed or left-handed, and the coil doesn't dig into your wrist as you write across the page.

Paper weight is the spec that matters. This uses thicker stock than your average composition book. Gel pens, rollerballs, and even some fountain pen inks don't feather or ghost through to the next page. I tested with a Pilot G2 0.7 and a Sharpie S-Gel — no bleed-through, minimal shadowing on the reverse side. Highlighters work if you let the ink dry for 15 seconds first.

The cover is a flexible, textured cardstock. It's not hardcover — don't expect a bulletproof shell. But it withstands being shoved in a backpack and tossed on car seats without showing much wear.

## Where it falls short

Finally, the cover is plain. No patterns, no motivational quotes, no faux-leather texture. It's a utilitarian tool. If you want a pretty journal for your nightstand aesthetic, look elsewhere.

## FAQ

**Does the paper bleed through with fountain pens?**
Mostly no. Medium and fine nibs with standard inks work fine. Broad nibs or wet inks like Noodler's can ghost slightly. Test a corner page first.

**Can I tear pages out cleanly?**
Yes. The perforation runs along the spiral edge. It's not a clean guillotine cut, but it's neat enough for handing in assignments or removing drafts.

**Is the spiral on the left or top?**
Top. This is the key feature for left-handed writers — no coil pressing into your arm.
