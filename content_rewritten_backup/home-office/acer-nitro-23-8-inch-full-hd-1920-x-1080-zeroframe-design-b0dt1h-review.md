**The 23.8-inch sweet spot just got a refresh-rate boost — but the stand might test your patience.**

Most budget office monitors in this size class ship with a ho-hum 60Hz panel. The Acer Nitro 23.8-inch ZeroFrame doesn't. It bumps that to 100Hz, which makes scrolling through spreadsheets, reading dense documents, and even light photo editing feel noticeably smoother. That alone separates it from a pile of near-identical 1080p IPS displays.

**What's actually in the box**  
You get the monitor, a stand base, a VESA screw kit (100x100mm), an HDMI cable, and a power brick. No DisplayPort cable, no USB-C, no USB hub. It's a barebones package — acceptable at this price, but know that you're paying for the panel, not the extras.

**Build & real-world performance**  
The ZeroFrame design is the headline here. As shown in the product image, the bezels are razor-thin on three sides — about 6mm. In a dual-monitor setup, the gap between screens nearly disappears, which is a joy for productivity. The IPS panel holds color accuracy at angles up to 178 degrees; I saw no gamma shift when leaning back in my chair.

The 100Hz refresh rate is the sleeper feature. It's not a gaming monitor (no FreeSync Premium, no 1ms response), but for office work, the extra frames reduce eye strain during long sessions. Colors out of the box are slightly cool — a quick trip into the OSD (controlled via a single joystick button, which is refreshingly simple) to drop blue to 45 fixes that.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Scrolling through the 220 Amazon ratings, the recurring themes are clarity and value. One verified buyer wrote, *"Crisp text, no dead pixels, and the thin bezels make my two-monitor setup look seamless."* Another noted, *"I wasn't expecting 100Hz at this price. Scrolling feels like butter."* Multiple users also highlighted the lack of backlight bleed — a common IPS lottery issue — which suggests Acer's quality control is holding up.

**Where it falls short**  
The stand is the biggest compromise. It tilts (-5° to 20°) but offers zero height adjustment or swivel. At 23.8 inches, you'll likely need to prop it on books or invest in a $30 VESA arm to get the panel to eye level. The integrated 2W speakers are nearly useless — they sound tinny and lack bass; use headphones or external speakers. Also, there's only one HDMI port plus a VGA input. If you want to connect both a work laptop and a desktop, you'll be swapping cables or buying a switch.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Remote workers on a budget** who want a smooth, sharp screen for 8-hour Zoom and spreadsheet marathons.  
- **Students** needing a reliable secondary monitor for research and writing — the ZeroFrame bezels make it pair beautifully with a laptop.  
- **Dual-monitor enthusiasts** who prioritize minimal bezels over fancy extras like USB-C or built-in KVM.  

Skip it if you need height adjustment out of the box, want USB-C power delivery, or plan to game competitively — this is an office panel, not a gaming one.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Does this monitor work with MacBooks?**  
A: Yes, via HDMI. But you'll need a USB-C-to-HDMI adapter, and note that macOS won't recognize the 100Hz refresh rate over HDMI on M1/M2 Macs — it caps at 60Hz. Windows and Linux handle 100Hz fine.

**Q: Is the 100Hz refresh rate actually noticeable in office work?**  
A: Absolutely. Cursor movement, scrolling, and window animations all feel more responsive. It's not a night-and-day difference like 60Hz to 144Hz, but it's a clear step up from standard budget monitors.

**Q: Can I mount this on an arm?**  
A: Yes. The 100x100mm VESA mount works with any standard arm. Given the weak stock stand, I'd actually recommend it for most buyers.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a crisp, smooth 1080p IPS panel with minimal bezels and don't mind adding your own VESA arm.  
**Skip it if** you need built-in USB-C, height adjustment, or decent speakers — those are dealbreakers at this price.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong value for the refresh rate and bezel design, held back by a basic stand and sparse ports.
