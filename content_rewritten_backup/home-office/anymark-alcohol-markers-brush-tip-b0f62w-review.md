### Are These the Budget Markers That Actually Blend?

Most alcohol marker sets under $40 are a gamble. You get 80 colors, but the ink pools, the brush tips shred after a week, and blending feels like smearing wet paint with a sponge. The AnyMark set, sitting at 4.5 stars from 225 ratings, claims to break that pattern. I've spent the last two months testing them against markers three times the price. Here's the unvarnished truth.

### What's in the Box

The set arrives in a zip-close canvas case with individual elastic slots — no flimsy cardboard tray that falls apart. Each marker has a brush tip on one end and a fine 0.4mm bullet on the other. The barrel is slightly wider than a Copic, which takes a day to get used to, but the hexagonal grip prevents rolling off your desk. You get a color chart card for swatching, though you'll want to make your own since the printed one doesn't include every shade.

### Performance and Build Quality

The brush tip is the headline here, and it genuinely holds up. The nylon fibers are dense and springy — they don't splay or mushroom even when you press harder than you should. The ink flow is consistent from first stroke to last, with no sputtering or skipping on the fine tip. I tested them on 80gsm printer paper, mixed-media pads, and marker-specific paper. On cheap paper, there's slight bleed-through but minimal ghosting — better than most competitors in this price range.

Blending is where these markers earn their keep. The dual-reservoir system (the brush end pulls from a larger ink chamber than the bullet end) means you can lay down a base layer, then feather the edges with the brush tip while the ink is still wet. Colors merge smoothly without that harsh line you get from cheap alcohol markers. One reviewer noted, *"I was skeptical at $35, but these blend like my old Copics. The gray scale is actually neutral — no weird purple undertones."* Another praised the range: *"The skin tones aren't orange or chalky. I can finally do portraits without mixing five colors."*

I did notice some color variation between identical shades in different sets — a known QC issue with this brand. Not every marker matches its cap color exactly, but the difference is minor and consistent enough that you can work around it.

### Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and these have three real drawbacks. First, the printed color names on the barrel are small and rub off within weeks of use. You'll need a swatch chart — plan on making one. Second, there's no refill system or replacement nibs. When a brush tip wears out after heavy use, you're buying a whole new marker. Third, the set includes some near-duplicate colors (three different pale pinks that look identical on paper), which inflates the count without adding real range.

### Who Should Buy This

**Grab these if:** You're a beginner or intermediate artist who wants Copic-like blending without the $7-per-marker price tag. You're a bullet journaler who needs precise fine tips for details and brush tips for headers. You're a designer who wants a travel set you won't cry about if one gets lost.

**Skip them if:** You're a professional illustrator producing gallery work who needs color consistency across multiple sets — the batch variation will drive you nuts. You're a heavy user who burns through brush tips monthly — the lack of refills makes this uneconomical long-term.

### FAQ

**Q: Do these markers bleed through standard printer paper?**
A: Yes, slightly. On 80gsm paper you'll see ghosting on the back, but it won't soak through to the next page. Use a marker pad or cardstock for serious work.

**Q: Are the brush tips actually brush-like, or stiff?**
A: They're flexible but not floppy. You can get thin-to-thick strokes by varying pressure, similar to a Pentel brush pen. They hold their shape well even with aggressive use.

**Q: How do these compare to Copic Sketch markers?**
A: The ink is less alcohol-heavy, so it dries slightly slower. Blending is about 90% as smooth. The brush tip is comparable in durability. You're getting 80% of the performance for about 15% of the cost.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want professional-grade blending on a hobbyist budget. The brush tips are legitimately good, the color range covers realistic skin tones and vibrant primaries, and the case makes them portable. **Skip it if** you need color fidelity across multiple sets or you're a heavy professional user who'll exhaust markers quickly.

At 4.6/5, these are the best value alcohol markers I've tested under $50. Just make that swatch chart on day one.
