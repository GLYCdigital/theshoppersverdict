Carpal tunnel doesn't care about your deadline. That's the real reason you're looking at the POJTK Ergonomic Wireless Rechargeable Mouse — and honestly, it's a legitimate one. With 200 ratings averaging 4.0 stars, this vertical-style mouse has carved out a following among desk workers who've had enough of palm rests that don't rest anything.

Here's what I found after digging through buyer feedback and the spec sheet.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The package includes the mouse itself, a 2.4GHz USB-A receiver that stores inside the battery compartment (smart — you won't lose it), and a USB-A to Type-C charging cable. No dongle-hunting, no proprietary charger. The mouse weighs in around 95 grams — light enough for all-day movement but with enough heft to feel planted.

**The Three-Mode Setup That Works**

The headline feature is the 3-in-1 connection: Bluetooth, 2.4GHz via the USB-A receiver, and wired through the Type-C port. The toggle switch on the bottom cycles between modes, and buyers consistently report the device-switching button on top works without the usual Bluetooth pairing dance. One reviewer noted, "Switched between my work laptop (2.4G) and personal MacBook (Bluetooth) in under three seconds. No re-pairing headaches."

That's the experience most multi-device mice promise but rarely deliver.

**Real-World Performance and Comfort**

The vertical grip angle is roughly 57 degrees, which positions your hand in a handshake posture rather than flat on the desk. The textured thumb rest isn't just decorative — it gives your thumb a defined landing spot, which reduces the death-grip tension that causes forearm fatigue.

Battery life is the other standout. The 800mAh built-in cell lasts most buyers 3–4 weeks with 8-hour workdays. Charging via USB-C means you can top it off during lunch and get another full day. No disposable AAs, no battery drawer to wrestle.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The multi-device switching and battery life dominate positive reviews. One buyer wrote: "The only mouse I've used where the Bluetooth actually reconnects instantly when I wake my laptop." Another confirmed the ergonomic benefit: "My wrist pain disappeared after week one. The angle is aggressive enough to matter but not weird."

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the downsides because they're real.

First, **sizing**. This is built for small-to-medium hands. If you wear L/XL gloves, the vertical height (about 3.1 inches) forces your palm into an arched position that causes cramping by hour three. Multiple reviewers with larger hands returned it for this reason.

Second, **the silent switches**. They're quiet — genuinely office-appropriate — but the actuation is soft and mushy. There's no satisfying click feedback. For spreadsheet work and browsing, fine. For gaming or precision design work, you'll feel disconnected from your clicks.

Third, **DPI tops out at 1600**. On a standard 1080p display, that's plenty. On a 4K or ultrawide monitor, you'll be doing multiple swipes to cross the screen — and there's no software to adjust acceleration curves.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Office workers** spending 6+ hours daily on spreadsheets, email, and web apps who want wrist relief without a steep learning curve
- **Multi-device users** juggling a work laptop and personal computer who are tired of Bluetooth pairing rituals
- **Anyone tired of buying AA batteries** — the built-in rechargeable battery pays for itself in months

**Skip it if** you have large hands, need precise click feedback, or run a high-resolution monitor setup.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it work with Mac and Windows?**
A: Yes. Bluetooth works with macOS, Windows, iPadOS, and Android. The USB-A receiver works with Windows, macOS, and Linux. The wired mode is plug-and-play on any device with a Type-C port.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Expect 3–4 weeks with 8-hour workdays. Heavy Bluetooth use drains faster than the 2.4G receiver. Charging takes about 2 hours for a full top-up.

**Q: Is this truly silent?**
A: The click buttons are quiet — roughly 20dB quieter than standard mice. But the scroll wheel has a subtle tactile bump that makes a soft ticking sound. It's library-appropriate, not dead silent.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you want genuine wrist relief, reliable multi-device switching, and a rechargeable battery at a budget-friendly price point.

**Skip it if** you have larger hands, need high-DPI precision, or want satisfying mechanical-feeling clicks.

The POJTK isn't the fanciest ergonomic mouse on the market — it lacks the premium materials of Logitech's MX series and the customization software of gaming brands. But for the price, it delivers the two things that matter most: comfort and reliability. That's a trade most desk workers will happily make.
