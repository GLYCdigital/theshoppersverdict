Most notebook reviews bury the lead. Let me save you the scroll: if you're a fountain pen user, this is one of the few sub-$40 journals that won't turn your writing into a feathered mess. The CAGIE Leather Notebook pairs a 100 GSM paper weight with 256 lined pages, and at a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But "right" depends entirely on how you intend to use it.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the notebook itself — roughly A5 size (5.7 x 8.5 inches) — finished in genuine leather with a stitched spine, an elastic closure band, a pen loop, and a ribbon bookmark. There's also a small inner pocket on the back cover for loose cards or receipts. The 256 pages are acid-free, which matters if you're archiving anything long-term. No slipcase, no box, no extras. It's lean, which is fine at this price point.

## The Paper: Where This Notebook Wins or Loses

The 100 GSM thickness is the headline feature, and it earns the hype. I tested it with a Lamy Safari (medium nib) and a Pilot G2 gel pen. Zero bleed-through on both. The paper has a slight tooth to it — not glossy, not rough — which gives fountain pen ink a nice grip without dragging. Drying time is reasonable, though left-handed writers should still be careful with wet inks.

Here's the honest catch: this paper is *not* bleed-proof. Heavy markers and paint pens will ghost through. If you're a bullet journalist using Tombow brush pens, you'll see shadowing on the reverse side. It's a tradeoff — the paper is thick enough for serious writing but not coated enough for heavy art use.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The leather cover is the real deal, not bonded. It's stiff initially — the kind of stiffness that makes you wonder if you made a mistake for the first week. Then it softens. The stitching is clean and double-stitched at stress points. The elastic closure holds firm, and the pen loop fits a standard rollerball without stretching out.

What buyers consistently mention is durability. As one reviewer put it: *"I've carried this in my work bag for three months. The corners are scuffed, the leather has darkened, and it looks better now than the day I bought it."* That's the genuine-leather experience — it wears in, not out.

## Where It Falls Short

Three things keep this from a perfect score. First, no numbered pages. If you're a serious journaler who references past entries, you'll be counting pages manually. Second, the notebook doesn't lie flat out of the box. You'll need to break in the spine over a few weeks — a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker. Third, the leather smell is potent initially. It's not unpleasant, but it's strong enough to notice in a closed drawer for the first few days.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a fountain pen user who's tired of feathering and bleed-through. You want a journal that lasts six months of daily writing. You appreciate leather that develops patina rather than peeling like faux leather.

**Skip it if:** You're a heavy mixed-media artist who needs watercolor-capable paper. You need numbered pages and index features for structured journaling. You prefer lightweight notebooks you can carry without noticing.

## FAQ

**Does the 100 GSM paper work with fountain pens?**
Yes, this is one of its strongest points. Standard nibs (fine to broad) perform well with minimal show-through. Extra-wet inks may ghost slightly but won't bleed.

**Is the leather real or synthetic?**
Genuine leather. It's not top-grain luxury, but it's real hide that will age and darken with use.

**How long does one notebook last?**
At one page per day, roughly 8 months. At two pages daily, about 4 months. The 256-page count is generous compared to the standard 192-page journals.
