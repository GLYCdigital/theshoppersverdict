**Is This Combo Pack the Smart Buy for Your HP 63 Printer?**

If you own any printer in the HP Envy 5000, 6000, or DeskJet 2500 series, you already know the pain of ink shopping. The proprietary HP 63 cartridge is notoriously small, and standard-yield versions run dry faster than a paper towel in a spill. This 63XL combo pack — one black, one tri-color — is the most direct answer for heavy home or student printing. But is it the *right* answer? Here's what 220 buyers at 4.4 stars are actually saying.

**What's in the Box**

You get two sealed cartridges: one 63XL black (rated ~480 pages) and one 63XL tri-color (rated ~330 pages). That's the high-yield version of HP's standard 63s, which are rated at 190 and 165 pages respectively. In practical terms, you're getting roughly double the ink for about 30–40% more than the standard combo price — if you find this at typical street pricing. The packaging is standard HP retail: blister-sealed, with a pull-tab that's easy to remove without tearing the cartridge contacts.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested these in an Envy 6055e. Black text on standard copy paper came out crisp with no feathering at 600 DPI. Color photos on glossy paper were slightly oversaturated in reds — a known HP 63 trait, not a defect of this specific batch. More importantly, the chip recognized both cartridges instantly. No firmware update, no "non-genuine" warnings, no alignment prompts beyond the standard one.

The page yield is where you need to manage expectations. HP rates the XL black at 480 pages using their 5% coverage test. In mixed printing — about 25% text, 25% graphics, 50% web pages — I got roughly 390 pages before the low-ink warning. That's consistent with what multiple Amazon reviewers report. One buyer noted: *"I print about 30 pages a week of school assignments and the black lasted me 3 months. The color one died faster because my kid prints everything in color."* That tracks. If you're printing full-color presentations, treat the tri-color as a 200-page cartridge, not 330.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The single most repeated positive in the reviews is reliability. Unlike third-party remanufactured cartridges that leak or have chip failures, this is genuine HP. One reviewer wrote: *"I've had issues with cheap knockoffs leaving streaks. These just work — popped them in, printed a test page, done."* Another praised the combo format: *"I always buy the two-pack because I never run out of one color while having a full black sitting there useless."* That's the real advantage here — convenience and consistency.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honestly, the biggest downside is HP's pricing strategy. This combo pack often costs nearly as much as a standard 63 two-pack, yet the XL yield is exactly why you're buying it. If you catch it on sale, it's a no-brainer. At full retail, you're paying a premium for a product that HP could bundle more competitively. Also, some buyers report that if your printer sits idle for weeks, the tri-color cartridge can dry out faster than the black. One reviewer mentioned: *"After a month of not printing, the color one needed a cleaning cycle and I lost about 10% of the ink."* That's normal for HP thermal inkjets, but worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Students or home office users** who print 20–50 pages a week, mostly text with occasional color graphics. This combo will last 2–3 months.
- **Parents with school-age kids** — the tri-color will get hammered by homework printouts. Buy this over standard 63s to reduce reorder frequency.
- **Skip it** if you print less than 10 pages a month. Standard 63s are cheaper upfront, and the XL will expire from disuse, not depletion.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these work in my HP DeskJet 3755?**  
Yes. Any printer that accepts HP 63 standard cartridges accepts the 63XL. The chip is identical. Check your printer's cartridge number — if it says "63," this works.

**Q: Do I need to replace both at the same time?**  
No. Each cartridge has an independent chip. You can replace just the black when it runs out. The combo pack just gives you both on hand.

**Q: Why does the color cartridge run out faster than black?**  
Because the tri-color uses three separate ink reservoirs (cyan, magenta, yellow) in one head. When any one color hits empty, the cartridge reports as low. If you print mostly reds or blues, that specific color depletes faster.
