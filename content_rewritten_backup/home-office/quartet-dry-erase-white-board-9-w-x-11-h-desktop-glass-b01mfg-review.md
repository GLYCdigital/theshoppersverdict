Let’s be honest about desktop whiteboards: most are flimsy plastic slabs that warp, stain, and look like they belong in a 1990s cubicle. The Quartet 9x11 Desktop Glass Pad is not that. This is a tempered glass board that feels like a premium piece of desk hardware, not a promotional giveaway. At just 9 inches wide and 11 inches tall, it’s compact enough to tuck next to your keyboard, but the real story is the surface.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the glass pad itself, one black dry erase marker, and a small magnetic clip. That’s it. No easel, no stand, no mounting hardware. The board is meant to lie flat on your desk. The glass is roughly a quarter-inch thick with rounded edges — it feels substantial, not fragile. The magnetic clip on the top edge is a thoughtful touch; it holds the marker securely and doubles as a way to clip notes or a photo to the board.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The glass surface is where this board earns its keep. Unlike porcelain or plastic boards that develop ghosting after a few uses, tempered glass wipes clean with a dry cloth or the included eraser (technically the marker cap has a felt eraser). I’ve seen buyers report using the same board for months with zero staining. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: "I've had this for two years and it still looks brand new. The surface is completely smooth and nothing sticks to it." That’s the tempered glass advantage — it’s non-porous, so marker ink sits on top rather than seeping in.

The size is genuinely useful. At 9x11 inches, you can jot a to-do list, sketch a quick diagram, or work through a math problem without it eating your entire desk. It’s also light enough to toss in a bag for a meeting or a study session. The flat design works well for lefties and righties alike since you can rotate it freely.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme in the 229 reviews is the erasing quality. Multiple reviewers use the word "effortless." One wrote: "I was skeptical about glass, but this erases cleaner than any whiteboard I've owned." Another praised the durability: "Dropped it twice on hardwood — no cracks, no chips." The magnetic clip also gets consistent love for keeping the marker from wandering off.

**Where It Falls Short**

Here’s the honest tradeoff: no markers are included beyond the single black one. If you want colors or a thinner tip for detailed writing, you’re buying those separately. Also, because the board lies flat, there’s no tilt option. For some tasks, like reading notes while typing, a slight angle helps. You can prop it against a book or laptop stand, but that’s a workaround, not a feature.

Another minor gripe: the 9x11 size is great for quick notes, but it’s too small for serious brainstorming sessions. If you’re mapping out a project timeline or doing group planning, you’ll want a larger board. This is a personal notepad, not a collaboration tool.

**Who Should Buy This**

Three groups get real value here. First, students — this is ideal for working through practice problems, memorizing formulas, or sketching diagrams without wasting paper. Second, programmers and engineers who need a scratch pad for pseudocode or system designs. Third, anyone with a cluttered desk who wants a small, permanent jotting surface that doesn’t add visual noise. It also makes a genuinely good gift for a coworker who’s always scribbling on sticky notes.

**FAQ**

*Does the marker that comes with it dry out quickly?*
The included Quartet marker is decent, but like all dry erase markers, it can dry out if you leave the cap off. Most users report it lasting several weeks with regular use. Replacements are cheap and widely available.

*Can you use any dry erase marker on this?*
Yes, but lower-quality markers may leave a slight residue. Stick with reputable brands like Expo or Quartet’s own markers for the cleanest erase. Avoid permanent markers — they won’t come off glass easily.

*Is the glass actually shatterproof?*
It’s tempered, not shatterproof. It’s much stronger than standard glass and can handle drops onto hard floors, but hitting it with a heavy object at the right angle could crack it. For normal desk use, it’s plenty durable.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a compact, zero-maintenance writing surface that erases perfectly every time and looks sharp on any desk. **Skip it if** you need a tilted board, require multiple markers out of the box, or need more writing space than a notepad provides.

At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, the Quartet Glass Pad earns its reputation. It’s simple, well-built, and does exactly what it promises. For the price of a couple of coffees, you get a desk accessory you’ll use daily for years.
