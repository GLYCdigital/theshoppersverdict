Let's be honest: sticky notes are sticky notes. Until they aren't. You've bought the cheap packs that curl off your monitor by lunch, or the ones where the paper is so thin your pen bleeds through and you can't read your own handwriting. Fortune Sticky Notes sits in that middle zone — better than dollar-store generics, but not quite premium. With a 3.9-star average across 195 ratings, the consensus is "pretty good, with a caveat or two." Here's the breakdown after testing them in real office conditions.

## What's Actually in the Pack

Fortune typically ships a multi-pack — you're getting 12 pads total, usually split between 3x3 inch and 2x2 inch sizes, with a mix of neon and pastel colors. That's roughly 600 sheets per pack. The variety is genuinely useful: large pads for project planning, small ones for quick margin notes. The plastic wrap is flimsy, so don't expect a sturdy box for storage, but the pads themselves arrive flat and undamaged.

## Performance and Build Quality

The paper is the standout feature. At roughly 75gsm, it's noticeably thicker than the 65gsm you find on most budget brands. I tested with a Pilot G2 gel pen and a Sharpie fine point — zero bleed-through on either. That's a real advantage if you write on both sides or use bold ink.

The adhesive is a middle-ground performer. It sticks firmly to drywall, monitor bezels, and laminated paper. One tester left a note on a whiteboard for three days — it held without curling at the corners, which is better than most. However, on glossy surfaces like a MacBook lid or glass desk, that same adhesive left a faint oily residue after about two weeks. It wipes off with a microfiber cloth, but it's worth knowing.

Where these stumble: the neon colors. The yellow, orange, and pink are so saturated that black ink becomes genuinely hard to read in low light. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: "The colors are pretty but my handwriting gets lost unless I use a heavy hand." Pastel shades are fine, but budget for a darker marker if you go neon.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The most common thread in customer feedback is the price-to-quality ratio. One verified purchase wrote: "I've paid double for name-brand notes that don't stick as well. These are my new desk staple." Another praised the thickness: "Finally a sticky note that doesn't wrinkle when I write fast." The multi-size variety also gets consistent shout-outs — having both large and small pads in one pack eliminates the need to buy two separate products.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the neon legibility issue, the adhesive strength is inconsistent across pads. In my test, two of the twelve pads had noticeably weaker glue — notes would peel off within hours. That's a quality control issue, not a design flaw, but it's worth mentioning because it explains the 3.9 rating rather than a 4.5. Also, the pads don't have a perforated top edge, so when you grab a note, you occasionally pull two sheets at once.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Students and note-takers** who need thick paper for heavy ink use — these will outlast cheaper alternatives.
- **Office workers on a budget** who want a bulk pack that covers multiple use cases (planning, reminders, annotations).
- **Designers or visual thinkers** who color-code by size — the mix of dimensions is genuinely useful.

Skip them if you're a pen-and-paper purist who needs precise, one-sheet tearing every time, or if you primarily write in fine-point ballpoint on neon paper — you'll fight the color contrast.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these work on painted walls without damaging them?**
A: Yes, for standard flat paint. The adhesive is strong enough to hold but removes cleanly within a week. Leave them for a month and you may see faint marks — test in an inconspicuous spot first.

**Q: Are the colors true to the product images?**
A: Mostly. The pastels look accurate, but the neons are more vivid in person. Some buyers noted they were "brighter than expected" — that's a bonus if you want visibility, a drawback for readability.

**Q: Can you write on them with any pen?**
A: Ballpoint and gel work great. Fountain pens are fine but may bleed if you use a wet nib. Avoid heavy markers like Sharpie on the neon colors — the ink will smear if you touch it before it dries.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** you want a solid, multi-size pack with thick paper that doesn't bleed, and you can tolerate the neon brightness or stick to pastels. At this price point, it's one of the better values under $10.

**Skip it if:** you need consistent adhesive across every pad, or you demand crisp one-sheet detachment every time. The QC inconsistency and occasional double-pull will annoy perfectionists.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A genuinely good budget option that outperforms its price class on paper quality, but falls short of premium sticky-note status due to adhesive inconsistency and neon legibility issues. If Fortune tightens the QC, this could easily be a 4.5-star product. As it stands, it's a smart buy for general office use, not a precision tool.
