Let's be direct: most planners under $20 are flimsy throwaways with paper so thin you can see next week's entries bleeding through. The AT-A-GLANCE DayMinder Weekly with Telephone/Address is not that. At 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, it's earned its reputation the old-fashioned way — by being genuinely useful.

**What's actually in the box**

You get one planner, roughly 8.5" x 11" — full letter size, not the compact pocket versions that force tiny handwriting. The cover is a stitched leatherette in black, and the twin-wire binding means it opens flat on your desk without you wrestling it open. Inside: 12 months of weekly spreads, each with an hourly schedule running 7 AM to 8 PM. There's also a telephone/address section with A–Z tabs, plus reference pages (holidays, time zones, weights/measures). The paper is a heavy 60-pound stock that handles pen without ghosting — tested with ballpoint, gel, and even a Sharpie pen.

**Real-world performance**

The genius here is the layout. Each weekday gets a full column with hourly increments down the left margin. If your life runs on appointments — client calls, therapy sessions, contractor visits — you can see your entire day at a glance without flipping pages. Saturday and Sunday share a single block, which is fine for most professionals who treat weekends as recovery time anyway.

The hourly increments are roomy enough for short notes. One buyer put it plainly: *"I've tried digital calendars and every app out there, but physically writing my schedule keeps me accountable. This planner has the space I need without feeling like I'm writing in a coloring book."* Another noted the binding holds up: *"Mine survived 11 months in a work bag getting thrown around. No torn pages, no broken spiral."*

**Where it stumbles**

No product is perfect, and this one has three honest flaws. First, the week starts on Monday. If you're a Sunday-start person, this will annoy you 52 times a year. Second, the contact section is genuinely limited — about 30 entries with name, phone, address, and email fields. For a salesperson or consultant managing hundreds of contacts, this is a starter book, not a solution. Third, there's no monthly overview. You live week-to-week. If you need to see the whole quarter at a glance, you'll need a second tool.

**Who should buy this**

- **The appointment-driven professional**: Real estate agents, therapists, tutors, field service techs — anyone whose day is a series of timed slots. The hourly layout is your natural habitat.
- **The paper planner loyalist**: If you've tried digital and bounced back, this is a solid, no-nonsense weekly companion.
- **The student with a packed schedule**: Full-size pages give you room for classes, work shifts, and study blocks without cramping.

**Skip it if**: You need monthly or yearly overviews, you require a Sunday-start week, or you maintain a contact list beyond 30 people. Also skip if you want something pocketable — this is desk-sized.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the hourly schedule include weekends?**  
A: No. Weekdays (Monday–Friday) get the full 7 AM–8 PM hourly breakdown. Saturday and Sunday share a single, smaller block without hour markers. Fine for most, but if you work weekends, you'll need to improvise.

**Q: Can I use it for 12 months starting any time?**  
A: The planner is dated for a specific January–December calendar year. If you're buying mid-year, you'll either wait or accept the unused months. There's no undated version of this exact model.

**Q: Does it fit in a standard bag?**  
A: It's letter-sized (8.5" x 11"), so it fits in most laptop bags and larger totes, but it won't slide into a purse or slim briefcase. It's built for desk use, not daily commuting in a small bag.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a durable, desk-friendly weekly planner with serious hourly scheduling space and want the convenience of built-in contact pages. At 4.8 stars, it's earned its rating — this is a workhorse, not a gimmick.

**Skip it if** you require monthly overviews, Sunday-start weeks, or a pocketable format. And if you manage more than 30 contacts, budget for a separate address book.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the missing monthly view and the limited contact capacity. Everything else is exactly what a weekly planner should be.

This is the planner you'll actually use — and at the end of the year, you'll have a physical record of where your time went. That's worth more than any app notification.
