# LotFancy Name Tag Stickers: The 500-Pack That Actually Makes Sense

If you've ever run a conference, workshop, or church event, you know the drill: you need name tags, you need a lot of them, and you don't want to pay $20 for 100 stickers that barely stick. The LotFancy 500-count pack solves the quantity problem outright — but the real question is whether those 500 labels hold up when it matters.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 100 sheets, each with 5 labels sized at 3.5" x 2.25" — a comfortable badge dimension that fits standard lanyard holders or works standalone. That's 500 total stickers, which is enough for a mid-size event with leftovers for the office.

The labels are matte-finish white, designed for both inkjet and laser printers. The sheets are letter-size (8.5" x 11"), so they'll feed through any standard printer without wrestling with custom paper sizes.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these on a two-day training session with about 80 attendees. The adhesion is the standout feature — these labels stick firmly to cotton, polyester blends, and even light fleece. After eight hours on lanyards and shirts, none had curled at the edges or peeled off. That's better than several premium brands I've tested that start lifting by lunch.

The tear-away release is clean. You won't be picking paper fibers off your fingers or wrestling with half-peeled labels. The matte finish writes well with ballpoint, gel, and sharpie — though if you're printing, expect slightly muted colors. The matte coating absorbs ink rather than letting it sit on top, so your logo won't pop like it would on glossy stock.

## Where It Falls Short

The packaging is the weak link. The labels arrive in a thin poly bag with no resealable zipper. Once opened, sheets can slide out if you store the bag upright. Transfer them to a file folder or plastic container when they arrive — it's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

The matte finish, while great for writing, means printed colors look washed out compared to glossy alternatives. If you need vibrant, photo-quality badges for a high-end event, look elsewhere. For standard name tags, it's perfectly acceptable.
