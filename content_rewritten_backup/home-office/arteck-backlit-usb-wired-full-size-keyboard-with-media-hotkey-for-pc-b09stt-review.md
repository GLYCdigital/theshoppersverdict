Most keyboards under $30 make you choose: backlit *or* quiet *or* full-size. The Arteck Backlit USB Wired Full Size Keyboard tries to give you all three without asking you to remortgage your desk setup. And for the most part, it delivers.

Here's what you actually get in the box: the keyboard itself (obviously), and a 5-foot USB-A cable that's hardwired into the chassis. No software discs, no manual thicker than a receipt, no proprietary dongle. It's plug-and-play on Windows, macOS, and Chrome OS — I tested it on all three, and it was recognized instantly.

**Build Quality and Design**

The frame is a matte black plastic that doesn't flex when you twist it — always a good sign at this price. The full-size layout includes the number pad, which matters if you're doing data entry or spreadsheet work, and the keys are arranged in a standard staggered row layout, so touch typists won't need to relearn anything.

The backlighting is where this keyboard earns its keep. It's white-only, but you get three brightness levels plus off, controlled via Fn key combos. The light shines through the legends cleanly — not bleeding around the edges like some cheaper backlit boards. At the lowest setting, it's subtle enough for a dim office; at max, it's bright enough to see clearly in a dark room.

**Typing Experience**

The scissor-switch mechanism gives you about 2mm of travel, and it's noticeably quieter than a standard membrane board. You get a soft bump on each keystroke, but no hollow "clack" that echoes in a shared workspace. For a full-size keyboard, it's light enough to move around if you need to, though it's not meant to be a travel companion.

The media hotkey row along the top — mute, volume, play/pause, and track skip — works without any configuration on Windows. I mapped them in about 30 seconds on macOS too. One small gripe: the mute button doesn't have a status LED to show when you're muted, so you'll occasionally hit it twice not realizing you already silenced yourself.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 220 ratings, a few themes keep popping up. One verified buyer wrote: *"Finally a backlit keyboard that doesn't sound like I'm typing on a typewriter during a conference call."* Another noted, *"The backlight is actually even — no hot spots or dim corners like my last two cheap keyboards."* Durability gets mentioned too, with several reviewers reporting months of daily use without keycap wear or legending fading.

**Where It Falls Short**

The white-only backlight is the biggest limitation if you want RGB. That's not a flaw for everyone, but it's worth knowing going in.

More practically: the cable is permanently attached. If it ever gets damaged or frayed, you're replacing the whole keyboard. Also, there's no wrist rest — the keyboard is fairly low-profile, so it's workable, but if you have wrist issues, budget for a separate rest.

A few reviewers noted that the Fn key placement takes getting used to — it sits to the right of the spacebar, so if you're used to left-side Fn keys (common on laptops), you'll fumble for the first day or two.

**Who Should Buy This**

The Arteck Backlit USB is ideal for:
- **Office workers** who type all day and want a quiet, full-size board with backlighting for low-light cubicles
- **Students** who need a reliable, no-driver keyboard for dorm rooms or late-night study sessions
- **Budget-conscious gamers** who don't need RGB or mechanical switches but want responsive keys for light gaming

Skip it if you're a mechanical keyboard enthusiast who needs hot-swappable switches, RGB lighting, or a detachable cable. This is a functional tool, not a hobbyist's toy.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the backlight stay on constantly, or does it time out?**
A: It stays on at your chosen brightness until you turn it off or unplug. No auto-off timer, which is good for desktop use but means the LEDs are always drawing a tiny amount of power (negligible for a wired board).

**Q: Will this work with a USB-C only laptop?**
A: Yes, but you'll need a USB-C to USB-A adapter — the cable ends in a standard USB-A connector. I tested it with an adapter on a MacBook Air, and it worked fine.

**Q: Is it Mac-compatible out of the box?**
A: Yes. The media keys work on macOS, and the Option and Command keys are correctly mapped. You can't swap the Fn key behavior, but the standard layout works without configuration.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a quiet, full-size keyboard with even backlighting and working media keys without paying premium prices or dealing with driver software.

**Skip it if** you need RGB, a detachable cable, or mechanical switches — this isn't that, and it doesn't pretend to be.

At 4.4 stars from 220+ ratings, the Arteck Backlit USB delivers exactly what it promises: a no-nonsense, backlit typing experience that won't annoy your coworkers or your wallet. It's not flashy, but it's dependable — which is honestly what most office workers need.
