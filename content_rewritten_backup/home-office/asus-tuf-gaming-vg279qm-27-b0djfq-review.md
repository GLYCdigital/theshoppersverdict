The 280Hz refresh rate is the headline, but here’s what nobody tells you about the ASUS TUF Gaming VG279QM: it’s not just a gaming monitor. The Fast IPS panel pushes 95% DCI-P3 coverage with factory calibration that actually holds up in photo editing. But the stand? It’s the weakest link in an otherwise solid package. Let me break down what you’re really getting for your money.

## What's in the Box

You get the monitor, a DisplayPort 1.2 cable (good — you need it for 280Hz), a power adapter, and the infamous stand assembly. There’s no HDMI 2.1, which is fine at this resolution — the two HDMI 2.0 ports cap at 240Hz, so DisplayPort is your only path to the full 280Hz. The 27-inch panel has a 1920x1080 resolution, which is a deliberate choice: higher frame rates over pixel density. That's the right call for competitive gaming, but if you’re used to 1440p, text will look slightly softer.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The VG279QM's party trick is ELMB Sync — ASUS's blur-reduction tech that works in tandem with G-Sync Compatible VRR. Most monitors force you to choose between smoothness and clarity; this one gives you both. In fast-paced shooters like Valorant or Counter-Strike, motion stays crisp with zero visible ghosting on the 280Hz mode. Input lag measured in third-party tests sits around 3ms, which is imperceptible in real play.

The overdrive settings deserve attention. The "Level 4" overdrive is the sweet spot — Level 5 introduces inverse ghosting (white trails behind moving objects) that's noticeable on the desktop. Default settings are fine, but spend five minutes tweaking this and the monitor transforms.

## Build Quality: The Elephant in the Room

Here's where the 4.2 verdict gets honest. The panel itself is solid — no backlight bleed on my unit, uniform brightness across the corners. But the stand is genuinely bad. At 280Hz, screen shake from mouse vibrations is visible, especially if your desk isn't rock-solid. The height adjustment is stiff, and the base is a thin plastic frame that flexes under pressure. Multiple Amazon reviewers echo this: one writes, "The stand is the only thing holding this monitor back from being perfect." Another notes, "Bought a $30 VESA arm and it completely changed the experience."

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Customers consistently highlight three things. First, the 280Hz refresh rate genuinely feels different from 144Hz — one buyer says, "I didn't think I'd notice the jump from 144Hz, but the difference in tracking moving targets is night and day." Second, the color reproduction surprises people: "I use this for photo editing and gaming, and the color accuracy is better than my previous dedicated editing monitor." Third, the GamePlus crosshair overlay and timer features are actually useful, not gimmicky.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the stand, there are two real issues. HDR 400 is marketing math — the peak brightness hits around 400 nits but the backlight is edge-lit with no local dimming. HDR content looks washed out compared to SDR. Just leave HDR off and enjoy the excellent SDR image.

Second, the 1080p resolution at 27 inches results in a pixel density of about 81 PPI. For office work, you'll see individual pixels on text if you sit close. This is a gaming-first monitor; if you're splitting time between spreadsheets and shooters, consider the 1440p VG27AQ instead.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Competitive gamers on a budget** — this is the cheapest way to get true 280Hz with minimal motion blur
- **The frame-rate purist** who prioritizes responsiveness over resolution
- **Anyone with a VESA arm ready** — you'll bypass the stand issue entirely

## Skip It If

- **You work with text-heavy documents** — 1080p at 27" will frustrate you daily
- **You want real HDR** — this isn't that monitor, and the price doesn't justify the claim
- **You're sensitive to stand wobble** — stock setup is unstable at high refresh rates

## FAQ

**Q: Can I run 280Hz on any GPU?**
A: You need DisplayPort and a GPU that supports it. GTX 10-series and newer work, but you'll want a modern card to actually hit those frame rates in games.

**Q: Is this good for console gaming?**
A: The HDMI 2.0 ports cap at 240Hz, and the PS5/Xbox Series X won't use the full refresh anyway. It works fine, but you're paying for PC-focused features you won't use.

**Q: Does the ELMB Sync cause flicker?**
A: Some users report slight brightness fluctuation in menus. It's not visible in gameplay, but if you're sensitive to PWM flicker, test it before committing.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a competitive FPS player who values response time above all else and doesn't mind a mediocre stand. **Skip it if** you need a hybrid work/gaming monitor or expect HDR to matter.

The VG279QM is a specialist's tool. It delivers the fastest smoothness in its price class with accurate colors that exceed expectations. But the stand and HDR limitations are real compromises. At 4.2/5 stars, it earns its rating — a top-tier gaming monitor that's not quite a top-tier overall display. Pair it with a decent VESA arm and you've got a genuinely excellent setup for under $300. Just know what you're buying before you click that button.
