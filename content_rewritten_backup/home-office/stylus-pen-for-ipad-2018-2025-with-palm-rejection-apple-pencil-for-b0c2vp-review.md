Let me save you the research time. This stylus is the Apple Pencil alternative that actually works — with caveats you need to know before you buy.

I tested this pen across an iPad Air (M2), a 9th-gen iPad, and a Mini 6. I used it for note-taking, sketching, photo editing, and document markup. Here's what 220 Amazon reviewers (4.4 stars) and my own hands agree on.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the pen, a USB-C charging cable, and two replacement tips. The pen itself is 6.3 inches long and weighs 13 grams — nearly identical to the Apple Pencil 2's dimensions. It's matte aluminum, not glossy plastic, which gives a better grip during long writing sessions. The flat edge prevents rolling off your desk, a small detail that matters more than you'd think.

**Performance: where it shines**

The headline feature — palm rejection — genuinely works. I rested my entire hand on the screen while writing in GoodNotes and Notability. Zero stray marks. The pen uses the same active capacitive technology as Apple's stylus, so it's recognized by the iPad's touch system natively. No app setup, no Bluetooth handshake required.

Latency is the other surprise. At 60Hz refresh, I couldn't measure a meaningful difference between this and a first-gen Apple Pencil. For note-taking and markup, it's effectively identical. One reviewer put it simply: *"I use this for 6+ hours daily taking patient notes. My hand rests on the screen the whole time. It just works."* Another noted, *"Bought this as a backup to my Apple Pencil. Honestly, I reach for this one more often now because the matte finish feels better."*

Battery life is a practical win. A full charge lasts roughly 10 hours of continuous use. The USB-C port sits under a small cap on the end — not the most elegant solution, but functional. A 10-minute charge gives you about 2 hours of use, which is enough to rescue you before a meeting.

**Where it falls short**

The big one: **no pressure sensitivity**. If you draw or paint digitally, this pen will feel flat. Varying line weight by pressing harder doesn't work — you're locked to one stroke width unless you manually adjust brush settings. For serious artists, this is a dealbreaker. For everyone else, it's a non-issue.

Magnetic charging is also inconsistent. The pen snaps onto the side of my iPad Air and charges there — great. But on the standard 9th-gen iPad, there's no magnetic strip. You're stuck with the USB-C cable, which means remembering to charge it ahead of time.

Bluetooth pairing is a minor annoyance. The pen works instantly without pairing, but the battery widget won't display unless you pair it manually in Settings > Bluetooth. It's a 30-second setup, but it's not "it just works" out of the box.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students and professionals** who take handwritten notes daily and want palm rejection without paying Apple Pencil prices
- **Office workers** who annotate PDFs, sign documents, and edit spreadsheets on an iPad
- **Casual sketchers** who doodle but don't need professional-grade pressure curves

**Skip it if** you're a digital artist, illustrator, or designer whose work depends on pressure-sensitive line work. Spring for the genuine Apple Pencil instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work with my iPad?**
A: It's compatible with iPad 2018 and newer, including iPad Air 3rd-gen+, iPad Pro 2018+, iPad Mini 5+, and all standard iPad models from 2018 onward. Check that your iPad supports Apple Pencil 1 or 2 — if it does, this pen works.

**Q: Does it work with Procreate?**
A: Yes, for drawing and basic brushes. Palm rejection functions perfectly. But without pressure sensitivity, you lose dynamic brush behavior. You can manually set brush opacity and size, but it's not the same experience.

**Q: How does battery life compare to Apple Pencil?**
A: It's comparable — about 10 hours of active use. The Apple Pencil 2 lasts roughly 12 hours. The USB-C charging is faster than the original Apple Pencil's Lightning connector.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you need reliable palm rejection for note-taking, document markup, and everyday iPad work at roughly half the cost of an Apple Pencil.

**Skip it if** pressure sensitivity is non-negotiable for your creative work, or if you own a standard iPad without magnetic charging and hate managing cables.

For the price, this pen delivers 90% of the Apple Pencil experience for the tasks that matter most to office workers and students. The missing pressure sensitivity is a real limitation, but it's one that most users won't hit in daily work. Honest recommendation: this is the smart buy for anyone who uses an iPad as a productivity tool, not a canvas.
