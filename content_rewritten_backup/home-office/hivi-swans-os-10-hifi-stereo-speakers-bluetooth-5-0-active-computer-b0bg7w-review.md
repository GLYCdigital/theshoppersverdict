# HiVi-Swans OS-10 Review: The $171 Desk Speakers That Punch Way Above Their Size

Most "computer speakers" are an afterthought — plastic boxes that turn your music into mush. The HiVi-Swans OS-10 active monitors are not that. These are genuine HiFi speakers from a company with serious audio pedigree (Swans has been building reference monitors since 1991), shrink-wrapped into a desktop-friendly package.

At $170.99, they sit in an awkward spot: too expensive for casual buyers, too cheap for audiophiles. But for anyone who spends 8 hours a day at a desk and actually cares what their music sounds like, the OS-10 is the sweet spot.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the pair: one active speaker (the right channel with all the controls and amp) and one passive mate. The active unit houses a 20-watt RMS per channel Class D amplifier. Also included: a 3.5mm-to-RCA cable, a USB cable for direct computer connection, an optical cable, and a remote control.

The build quality is immediately reassuring. The cabinet is real MDF with a vinyl wrap that looks like wood grain, not cheap plastic. Each speaker measures 8.6 x 5.5 x 7.3 inches and weighs about 7.5 pounds — substantial enough to stay planted but compact enough for a standard desk.

## Sound Quality: Where It Earns the HiFi Label

Here's the spec sheet that matters: a 1-inch silk-dome tweeter paired with a 4-inch Kevlar-cone woofer. That's real audio engineering. The silk dome delivers airy, detailed highs without the harsh "ssss" sibilance you get from cheap metal tweeters. The Kevlar cone stays rigid at higher volumes, meaning less distortion when you push them.

In practical terms: acoustic guitar has that woody attack, vocals sit forward and clear, and jazz cymbals shimmer rather than splash. Bass is present and punchy down to about 60Hz — you'll feel kick drums, but you won't shake your desk. That's expected at this size; these are near-field monitors, not party speakers.

Bluetooth 5.0 pairing is instant and stable. I streamed from both iPhone and Android without dropouts at typical desk distances. The optical input is a nice touch for plugging into a TV or console.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The 210 Amazon ratings average 4.2 stars, and the sentiment is consistent. One verified buyer wrote: *"I was skeptical about the price for such small speakers, but they replaced my $300 Audioengine setup and sound better in near-field."* Another notes: *"The imaging is incredible — you can pinpoint each instrument in the soundstage."*

The front-facing controls are a genuinely thoughtful design choice. Bass and treble knobs plus a volume dial are all on the front panel — no fumbling behind the desk to make adjustments.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. There's no subwoofer output, so if you want serious low-end, you'll need a separate DAC and powered sub — an extra $200+ investment.

The rear power switch is annoying. It's a small toggle tucked next to the RCA inputs, and you'll likely leave the speakers on constantly because reaching behind is a hassle. There's no auto-standby, so they draw power 24/7.

The included cables are about 3 feet long. If your computer sits under your desk, plan on buying longer RCA or USB cables. The remote is basic and feels cheap — it works, but it's clearly where they saved money.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Remote workers and programmers** who want music that sounds like music, not tin-can noise, during long work sessions
- **Gamers** who want positional audio for immersion without a bulky 5.1 system
- **Small-office users** who need a speaker that can fill a room at moderate volume
- **Anyone who has suffered through $30 "multimedia speakers"** and knows it's time to stop

Skip them if you need chest-thumping bass for EDM or movies — you'll want a system with a subwoofer.

## FAQs

**Q: Do these need a separate amplifier?**
A: No. The right speaker contains the amp — just plug in power and your audio source. The left speaker connects via the included speaker wire.

**Q: Can I use these with a TV?**
A: Yes, via the optical input. That's actually one of the best features at this price point. You'll need a TV with optical output.

**Q: How do they compare to Audioengine A2+?**
A: The OS-10 has similar clarity but slightly less bass extension. However, they cost about $100 less and include Bluetooth 5.0, which the A2+ lacks at this price.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want genuine HiFi sound on your desk without spending $300+. The HiVi-Swans OS-10 delivers detail, imaging, and build quality that punches well above its $171 price tag.

**Skip it if:** You need sub-bass for electronic music, want wireless multi-room features, or just need something cheap to hear notifications.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Real HiFi engineering at a reasonable price, with a few irritating compromises that keep it from perfection.
