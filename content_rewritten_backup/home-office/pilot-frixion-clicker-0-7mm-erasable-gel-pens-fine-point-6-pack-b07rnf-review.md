**The Erasable Pen That Actually Erases — With a Catch**

Most "erasable" pens are a compromise: either they write like a crayon or they erase like a smear campaign. The Pilot FriXion Clicker 0.7mm sidesteps both problems with thermo-sensitive gel ink that disappears when friction heats it above 60°C. It's the same tech Pilot has refined for years, and in this retractable clicker format, it finally feels like a legitimate everyday writing tool rather than a school supply gimmick.

**What You Actually Get**

This listing is a 6-pack of FriXion Clicker pens in assorted ink colors—typically black, blue, red, and a few brighter shades depending on the pack variant. Each pen is a full-length retractable with a metal clip, rubberized grip section, and a visible ink window so you're never caught empty-handed mid-thought. The 0.7mm tip is a true fine point, not the scratchy 0.5mm that some prefer or the mushy 1.0mm that bleeds on cheap paper.

**Real-World Performance**

The writing experience is the star here. The gel ink flows wet and smooth—comparable to a Pilot G2 but with slightly less drag. It dries fast enough that left-handed writers won't smear their work, and the color saturation is genuinely impressive for an erasable ink. When you need to correct something, the FriXion's built-in friction eraser (on the pen's end cap) removes the ink completely. No pink eraser dust, no gray ghosts, no torn paper. It's clean, instant, and doesn't degrade the paper surface even after repeated erasures.

I've used these for daily journaling, meeting notes, and sketching out project timelines. The eraser works best within the first few hours; after a day or two, the ink sets more permanently. That's fine for most uses—you're erasing mistakes, not rewriting history.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews reflects consistent satisfaction. **Where It Falls Short**

Here's the honest truth: the thermo-sensitive ink has a real vulnerability. If you leave a notebook in a parked car on a summer day, or if you place a document on a warm printer, the ink will vanish. Permanently. There's no way to restore it. This isn't a theoretical concern—it's a documented limitation that catches people off guard.

The second issue is cost. Each pen is roughly $2–3, and the refills (which you'll need to buy separately) cost nearly as much as the complete pen. The plastic body isn't designed for a lifetime of use; it's a disposable tool. That's fine, but don't expect to save money over standard pens.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the eraser leave residue or smudge the paper?**
No. The friction eraser removes the ink cleanly. On standard 20lb paper, there's no visible ghosting or paper damage. On glossy or coated paper, you may see a faint mark.

**Q: Can I write over erased areas?**
Yes, but the ink may appear slightly lighter on the first pass after erasing. A second pass over the same line usually restores full color.

**Q: How long does the ink last before it becomes permanent?**
The ink remains erasable for roughly 24–48 hours. After that, the chemical bond stabilizes and erasure becomes difficult. Heat exposure can make it permanent instantly.
