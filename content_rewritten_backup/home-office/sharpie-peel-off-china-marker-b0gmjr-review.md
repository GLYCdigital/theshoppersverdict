Here's the thing about the Sharpie Peel Off China Marker: it's not trying to replace your whiteboard marker. It's trying to replace the frustration of writing on a surface that refuses to cooperate — wet glass, greasy plastic, dusty ceramic, even frozen food packaging. And after testing it across a week of real-world abuse, I can tell you it does that job better than anything else I've got in my desk drawer.

## What You're Actually Getting

The package contains a dozen markers, each about the size of a standard Sharpie but noticeably lighter. The barrel is classic Sharpie construction — durable plastic, snug cap, reliable clip. The real trick is the tip: instead of a felt nib or a twist mechanism, there's a waxy stick surrounded by a paper sleeve. A string runs through the sleeve, and when the tip dulls, you pull the string, peel back a spiral of paper, and expose fresh marker. No sharpener, no crumbs, no mess. Each marker gives you roughly a foot of usable tip before it's spent.

## How It Actually Performs

I put these through the wringer: labeled glass storage jars straight out of the dishwasher (still steaming), wrote on a grease-slicked plastic bin in my garage, marked ceramic mugs, and scrawled notes on the frosted window in my office. The wax formula grabs onto surfaces that make dry-erase markers bead up and roll into illegible puddles. On clean, dry surfaces, it writes smoothly with solid opacity — not as bold as a paint pen, but plenty visible for labels and temporary notes.

Removal is where this marker earns its keep. A dry paper towel wipes it off glass and ceramic completely, no residue, no ghosting. On plastic, a fingertip rub does the trick. That's a massive upgrade over permanent markers, which laugh at your cleaning spray, and even over dry-erase, which leaves that hazy film behind on porous surfaces.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. The wax tip smears if you brush your hand across it before it dries — about 30 seconds of dry time. On a busy shared whiteboard, that's a liability. Also, the string on the back end has a habit of catching on things if you don't snap it cleanly after peeling. I've had one marker hang up on the inside of my pen cup. Minor annoyances, but they're real.

## Who Should Buy This

This is a specialist tool, and it knows it. You should buy it if you're a home canner or meal-prepper labeling wet glass jars. Buy it if you work in a kitchen, warehouse, or workshop where surfaces are greasy, cold, or dusty. Buy it if you're a teacher who needs to mark up laminated materials or plastic folders without ghosting. Skip it if you only need a marker for clean, dry whiteboards — a standard dry-erase is cheaper, bolder, and erases more cleanly.

## FAQ

**Does it work on wet surfaces?**
Yes, and that's the whole point. The wax formula cuts through moisture and frost better than any felt-tip marker I've tested. Wipe the surface down first if you want maximum longevity, but it'll write even when damp.

**How do you sharpen it?**
Pull the string at the back of the marker, then peel the paper sleeve away in a spiral. You'll expose a fresh ring of wax tip. Snap or tear the string off cleanly when you're done.

**Is it permanent?**
No. It's designed for temporary marking on non-porous surfaces. It'll stay put until you deliberately wipe it off, but it's not waterproof and won't survive a dishwasher cycle.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're tired of fighting with markers that won't write on wet, greasy, or cold surfaces. It's the right tool for a specific job, and it does that job flawlessly.

**Skip it if** you only need a general-purpose whiteboard or paper marker — you'll be paying for capability you'll never use.
