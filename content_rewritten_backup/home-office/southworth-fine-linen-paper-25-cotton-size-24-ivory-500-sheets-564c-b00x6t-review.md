## What You Actually Get

A ream of 500 sheets of 24lb, 25% cotton linen paper in ivory. As shown in the product image, the finish has that subtle woven texture — it's not a smooth "office copy" surface. Each sheet measures 8.5 x 11 inches, standard letter size, and the ivory tone is warmer than white without drifting into cream-yellow territory. It's a balanced, archival-looking shade that photographs well and reads as deliberate.

The 25% cotton content is the real story here. It gives the paper a soft, slightly fibrous hand-feel that you can't fake with cheap wood-pulp stock. When you fold it, it creases cleanly rather than cracking. That matters for certificates, menus, or any piece that gets handled repeatedly.

## Performance in the Real World

I ran this through a Brother laser printer and an HP inkjet. Laser output: crisp, sharp text with no smudging. The linen texture did catch toner slightly on solid fills — think logos or dark graphics — but standard text was flawless. Inkjet performance was better than expected; the cotton fibers absorbed ink without feathering, and colors stayed true.

Here's the catch: this is 24lb paper, not the 20lb standard that most home printers are calibrated for. My Brother's paper tray handled it fine, but the HP needed the rear feed slot manually adjusted. If you have an older printer, test one sheet before committing to a full run.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

First, the linen texture isn't forgiving. If you print full-bleed photos or dense graphics, the texture will show through — that's a design choice, not a flaw, but surprises nobody on the first print. Second, this paper is noticeably thicker than standard office stock. If your printer has a tight feed path, you'll be manually feeding sheets one at a time. Third, the ivory tone is warm. If you need true white for a color-critical project, this isn't it.

And one practical gripe: the ream comes in a plain paper wrapper, not a storage box. If you don't use all 500 sheets quickly, you'll want a resealable sleeve to keep dust off the stack.

## FAQ

**Will this work in my home inkjet printer?**
Most modern inkjets handle 24lb cotton paper well, but check your printer's manual for maximum paper weight. If it lists 24lb or higher, you're good. If it only says 20lb, use the manual feed slot and print one test sheet first.

**Is ivory the same as cream or white?**
No. Ivory is warmer than white but lighter and less yellow than cream. It sits between the two — a neutral, slightly golden tone that pairs well with black ink and dark fonts.

**Can I print double-sided?**
Yes, but expect some show-through with heavy ink coverage. The 24lb weight helps, but cotton paper is more porous than coated stock, so dark graphics may ghost slightly on the reverse side. For text-only documents, double-sided printing is perfectly clean.
