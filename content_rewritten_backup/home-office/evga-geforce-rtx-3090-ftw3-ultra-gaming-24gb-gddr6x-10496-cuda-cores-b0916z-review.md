**Review Body:**

The EVGA GeForce RTX 3090 FTW3 Ultra is a monster — literally and figuratively. This is a 3-slot, 335mm-long, 1.4kg brick of copper, aluminum, and silicon that demands you rearrange your workspace to accommodate it. But if your work involves 4K video editing, 3D rendering, or AI model training, the 24GB of GDDR6X VRAM makes this card a legitimate productivity tool, not just a gaming toy.

**What you actually get**

The box includes the card itself, a hefty support bracket (use it — this thing sags), and a dual 8-pin to 12-pin adapter. There's no RGB software CD, no useless extras. EVGA keeps it clean. The card's build quality is excellent: a full metal backplate, three 90mm fans that don't spin below 50°C, and a massive heatsink array that makes the whole assembly feel premium.

**Real-world performance**

In productivity tasks, the 24GB VRAM is the headline feature. Rendering 8K video timelines in DaVinci Resolve, loading massive Blender scenes, or running LLM inference — the 3090 FTW3 doesn't choke. The 10496 CUDA cores chew through compute workloads that would make 12GB cards cry for mercy.

For gaming, it's still a top-tier 4K card in 2026. Cyberpunk 2077 with ray tracing ultra runs 60-70fps at native 4K without DLSS. With DLSS Quality, you're looking at 90-100fps. But here's the honest truth: if gaming is your only use case, the RTX 4080 Super delivers comparable or better ray tracing performance while drawing 100W less power. The 3090's value proposition is squarely in the creator/AI space.

**Thermals and noise**

EVGA's iCX3 cooling is genuinely impressive. Under sustained full load, the card holds at 68°C in a standard ATX case with decent airflow. Fan noise tops out around 45dB — audible but not obnoxious. The idle fan-stop mode means silent operation at desktop. One buyer noted, "The cooling is overbuilt. I've run this card for 6-hour rendering sessions and it never throttles."

**Where it falls short**

The power draw is no joke. 400W under load means your power bill will notice, and your room will heat up. You need a quality 850W PSU — don't cheap out here. The physical size is also a real constraint. Check your case specs: this card needs 335mm of clearance and three full slots. Many mid-tower cases can't accommodate it with front-mounted radiators.

**Who should buy this**

- **Video editors** working with 8K RAW footage who need VRAM headroom for timelines and effects
- **3D artists and architects** running Blender, Maya, or Lumion who need to render complex scenes without out-of-memory crashes
- **AI researchers** doing local LLM fine-tuning or Stable Diffusion work — the 24GB VRAM is the whole point
- **Gamers at 4K** who also do content creation and want one card for both

**Skip it if** you're a pure gamer (the 4080 Super does more with less power), you have a small-form-factor build, or you're on a budget where the price-to-performance ratio matters more than raw VRAM.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit in my case?**
Measure first. You need 335mm of GPU length clearance and three expansion slots. Check your case's max GPU length spec before ordering. The support bracket is mandatory for horizontal mounting — this card is heavy.

**Q: Is 850W PSU enough?**
Yes, if it's a quality unit. EVGA recommends 750W minimum, but I'd go 850W for headroom, especially if you have a power-hungry CPU like an i9 or Ryzen 9.

**Q: How does it compare to the RTX 4080 Super?**
The 4080 Super wins on ray tracing performance and efficiency (320W vs 400W TDP). The 3090 wins on VRAM (24GB vs 16GB) and price if you find a good deal. For AI work, the 3090's VRAM is non-negotiable.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need 24GB of VRAM for professional work — rendering, video editing, AI — and want a card that's proven, reliable, and has excellent cooling. The EVGA FTW3 is the best-built 3090 variant you can still find.

**Skip it if:** You're primarily gaming, building a small-form-factor PC, or want the latest architecture. This is a 2020 card that's now a niche professional tool, and the power draw and physical footprint reflect that.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A powerhouse for creators, but the size and power requirements make it a specialist's tool, not a universal recommendation.
