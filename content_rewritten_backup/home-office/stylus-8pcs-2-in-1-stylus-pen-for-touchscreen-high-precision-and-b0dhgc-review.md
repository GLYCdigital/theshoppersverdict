If you've ever fished around a junk drawer for a stylus that actually works, this 8-pack from the "2-in-1" category is the answer to a problem you didn't know you had. It's not a fancy Apple Pencil competitor, and it's not trying to be. What it is: a bulk set of surprisingly capable hybrid pens that handle both your touchscreen and your paper notepad without making you swap tools.

**What's actually in the box**

Eight identical pens, each with two ends. One end has a fine precision tip for touchscreens — think thin, clear plastic disc with a small conductive core. The other end is a ballpoint pen that writes in black ink. The barrel is aluminum, not cheap plastic, and it's got a nice heft that makes it feel like a $10 pen, not a $1 throwaway. Each pen also has a clip, which sounds minor until you realize how many styluses roll straight off a slanted desk. These stay put.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these on an iPhone, an iPad, a Samsung Galaxy Tab, and a standard 15-inch laptop touchscreen. The fine tip is responsive — no need to press hard, and it registers touches consistently even at the screen edges. The rubber disc end (the old-school "smudge" tip) works fine for scrolling and tapping, but it's noticeably less precise. If you're signing a document or tapping small buttons, use the fine tip.

The ballpoint end is the sleeper feature. It writes smoothly on standard printer paper and doesn't skip. Ink dries quickly, so no smudging if you're left-handed. The cap on the ballpoint end is a standard twist-off, and it's snug enough that it won't come loose in a bag.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't inflated. The most common thread in buyer feedback is value — you're paying for eight pens, each with two functions, and they all work. One reviewer noted, *"I bought these for my office and they disappeared within a week — everyone wanted one on their desk."* Another mentioned, *"The fine tip is perfect for my iPad notes; I don't need the Apple Pencil for basic note-taking."* The durability gets called out too — several reviewers mention dropping them, and the tips haven't cracked or detached.

**Where it falls short**

Honest tradeoffs:

- The "fine" tip isn't a precision nib. If you're drawing or doing detailed design work, this won't replace a dedicated stylus. It's about the width of a medium ballpoint line — fine for notes and taps, not for art.
- The rubber disc tip drags on bare glass. On a screen without a protector, it feels a bit sticky during long writing sessions. A matte screen protector fixes this, but it's worth knowing.
- The ballpoint ink runs out eventually, and there's no refill option mentioned. Once it's dry, that end is dead. At this price point, you just grab another pen from the pack.

**Who should buy this**

- **Office managers** stocking a shared workspace — eight pens disappear fast, and at this price, you won't care.
- **Students** who need a stylus for tablet note-taking and a pen for paper in one tool.
- **Anyone who loses styluses constantly** — this pack is your insurance policy.
- **Not for** digital artists or anyone needing palm rejection and pressure sensitivity. This is a utility tool, not a creative instrument.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Does this work with the Apple Pencil features like palm rejection?**
No. It's a capacitive stylus, not a Bluetooth device. It taps and writes, but there's no pressure sensitivity, tilt detection, or palm rejection. It works with any capacitive touchscreen, including iPhones, iPads, Android devices, and most laptops.

**Q: Can I use the fine tip on a glass screen protector?**
Yes, and honestly, it works better with one. The fine tip glides more smoothly on a matte or tempered glass protector, and the rubber disc tip has less drag too.

**Q: Is the ballpoint refillable?**
No. The ink is sealed in the barrel. Once it's out, that end is done. The stylus end still works, so the pen isn't useless, but you won't be swapping refills.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, multi-purpose stylus that won't break the bank, you're equipping a shared workspace, or you just want a few spare pens that actually work on every screen you own.

**Skip it if** you're a digital artist, you need palm rejection, or you want a premium-feeling precision tool for detailed work. This is a workhorse, not a racehorse.
