If your home office or small business runs IP cameras, VoIP phones, or wireless access points, you’ve hit the wall where a standard switch just doesn’t cut it. You need power delivered over the same Ethernet cable — that’s where the MokerLink PoE Switch 100M steps in. With a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, it’s clearly doing something right. But is it the right switch for *your* setup? Let’s get into the specifics.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the switch itself, an external power adapter, and a basic mounting kit. No manual worth reading — you won’t need one. As shown in the product image, it’s a compact metal unit that feels more substantial than its price point suggests. The ports are clearly labeled, and the LED indicators on the front panel give you immediate status at a glance.

## Build and Real-World Performance

The metal chassis isn’t just for looks. It acts as a heat sink, which matters when you’re pushing Power over Ethernet continuously. I’ve seen plastic switches throttle or drop connections under sustained load — the MokerLink holds steady. The fanless design means zero noise, so it’s fine sitting on a desk or shelf in a shared workspace.

The 100Mbps speed cap is the elephant in the room. For IP cameras streaming 1080p or 4K video, 100Mbps is honestly fine — those streams rarely exceed 20Mbps per camera. Same for VoIP phones and access points. Where it *isn’t* fine is bulk file transfers between computers or NAS devices. If you’re moving large video files or running a backup server, the gigabit version of this switch is the one you want. Know your use case before you buy.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Across the 225 reviews, a few themes come up repeatedly. The phrase "just works" appears often — one buyer noted, "Plugged in four cameras and a phone, no configuration, everything came up instantly." Another reviewer mentioned using it for two years without a single dropout, which speaks to reliability.

The power budget is another consistent highlight. At this price point, some budget PoE switches struggle to deliver consistent wattage across all ports. MokerLink manages it without the voltage sag that causes devices to reboot randomly. That’s the kind of thing you don’t notice until it stops happening.

## Honest Downsides

The 100Mbps limitation is the biggest one, and it’s not for everyone. If you’re a video editor or run a small server rack, this will bottleneck you. Skip it.

The external power brick is clunkier than integrated power supplies on pricier switches. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it does eat up outlet space and adds cable clutter behind your desk.

And if you’re a network tinkerer, note that this is strictly unmanaged. No VLANs, no QoS, no port mirroring. It’s a dumb switch in the best sense — it moves packets and powers devices, and that’s it. If you need management, you need a different product entirely.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You’re setting up a small camera system, adding VoIP phones to an office, or extending Wi-Fi coverage with access points. You want something that works out of the box and won’t fail under daily load.

**Skip it if:** You need gigabit speeds for file transfers, or you require managed features for a complex network. Also skip if you’re running PoE devices that draw near the maximum power budget — you’ll want headroom.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can this power a 4K IP camera?**
Yes. 4K camera streams sit well under the 100Mbps port speed, and the power budget handles typical camera draws around 10-12W per port.

**Q: Does it work with non-PoE devices?**
Yes. Standard Ethernet devices work fine on any port — the switch simply doesn't send power to devices that don't request it.

**Q: How many cameras can it realistically run?**
With typical cameras drawing 10-12W, you can comfortably run all available PoE ports. If you're using high-power PTZ cameras or devices with heaters, you'll want to calculate your total draw against the switch's power budget.
