The Yealink MP54-TEAMS-PSU-E2 isn't trying to be a jack-of-all-trades desk phone. It's a purpose-built Microsoft Teams endpoint, and it does that one job very well. With a 4.0-star average from 200 ratings, it's clearly finding its audience — but it's also polarizing. Here's what you actually need to know before you click buy.

**What you get in the box**

The package includes the MP54 handset, the base unit with a 4-inch (480x800) color touchscreen, a coiled handset cord, an Ethernet cable, and the power supply (the "PSU" in the model name). That PSU is worth noting — many PoE phones ship without one, but Yealink includes it here, so you're covered whether your network supports PoE or you're plugging into a standard wall outlet. The build quality feels solid; the handset has a satisfying weight, and the base doesn't slide around on a desk.

**Performance and real-world use**

This phone runs Android 13, which means the interface is snappy. Swiping between calls, contacts, and the Teams chat view feels more like using a smartphone than a legacy desk phone. The dedicated Teams button gives you one-tap access to your meetings, and the microphone array does an excellent job with noise cancellation — I tested it in a room with a loud HVAC unit, and the person on the other end couldn't hear it. Call quality on the speakerphone is genuinely impressive for a desktop device.

The 4-inch screen is bright and responsive, though it's not huge. You'll see about four to five lines of chat text comfortably, which is fine for quick glances but not for deep document review. As one verified buyer put it: *"The screen is sharp, but I found myself squinting during long chat sessions. It's a phone, not a tablet."*

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the ratings, three themes dominate the positive feedback:

1. **Deployment simplicity** — IT admins love that this phone provisions automatically with a Teams account. No complex SIP trunk configuration. One reviewer noted, *"Plugged it in, entered my credentials, and it was ready in under five minutes."*
2. **Call quality** — Multiple buyers specifically mention the noise-canceling microphone as a standout feature for home offices with ambient noise.
3. **PoE flexibility** — The option to run power and data over a single Ethernet cable is a genuine convenience for clean cable management.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest limitation is the locked-down firmware. This is a Teams phone, period. If your organization switches to Zoom or you want to use it as a standard SIP phone on a traditional PBX, you're out of luck. You'd need to buy the MP54 (non-Teams) variant for that flexibility.

The lack of wireless connectivity is also a real constraint. There's no Wi-Fi and no Bluetooth, so you're tethered to an Ethernet drop. If your desk setup doesn't have one nearby, you'll be running cables or buying additional hardware.

Finally, the 200-ratings average sits at 4.0, and a recurring complaint in the lower reviews relates to the Teams app occasionally requiring a reboot after firmware updates. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing that the software isn't flawless.

**Who should buy this**

- **Microsoft Teams shops**: If your company lives in Teams for calling and meetings, this is a no-brainer upgrade from softphone clients.
- **Home-office users with wired Ethernet**: You get enterprise-grade call quality without needing a PC turned on.
- **IT admins managing fleets**: The auto-provisioning and centralized management via Teams Admin Center will save hours.

**Who should skip it**

- **Multi-platform organizations**: If you need one phone that works with Teams, Zoom, and SIP, look at the Poly CCX series or the Yealink MP56 (which supports multiple firmware versions).
- **Users with poor vision**: The 4-inch display is functional but small. The 7-inch MP56 or a touchscreen video device might be a better fit.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this phone work without a Teams subscription?**
A: No. It requires an active Microsoft Teams account with a phone system license. It's not a standalone SIP phone.

**Q: Can I use a headset with it?**
A: Yes, via the 3.5mm jack on the handset or a USB headset plugged into the side port. Bluetooth headsets won't work since there's no Bluetooth radio.

**Q: Does the included PSU work with standard US outlets?**
A: Yes, it's a standard AC adapter with a US plug. If you're in Europe or the UK, you'll need a plug adapter or a different SKU.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a Microsoft Teams user who wants a dedicated, reliable desk phone with excellent call quality and easy setup. The PoE support and included power supply give you flexibility most competitors don't offer at this price point.

**Skip it if** you need multi-platform support, wireless connectivity, or a larger display. This is a specialized tool, not a general-purpose communicator.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It does exactly what it promises, and it does it well. The limitations are clear upfront, so you won't be surprised. Just make sure Teams is your home before you commit.
