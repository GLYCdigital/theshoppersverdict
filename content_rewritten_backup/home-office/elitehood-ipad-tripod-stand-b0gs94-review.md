If you've ever propped an iPad against a stack of books for a video call, only to watch it slide off mid-sentence, the elitehood iPad Tripod Stand is the upgrade you didn't know you needed. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this compact tripod is earning its keep in home offices, kitchens, and content creator setups. But is it the right stand for *your* workflow? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's in the Box

The package includes the tripod itself, a universal device clamp, and a phone holder adapter. The clamp opens wide enough to accommodate devices from 4 to 13 inches — so your iPad Pro 12.9" fits, and so does your iPhone. The legs extend from a collapsed 15.7 inches up to a full 51 inches, which means it works both on a desk and as a floor-standing option.

The aluminum construction feels more premium than the price suggests. Each leg has a twist-lock collar, and the center column uses a similar mechanism. Everything cinches down tight with no wobble — a common failure point on cheaper tripods in this category.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this with an iPad Air (10.9") and a phone mounted in the clamp. The spring-loaded jaws hold devices firmly without blocking the screen edges or pressing volume buttons — a detail that shows the designers actually used tablets before shipping this thing. The 360-degree ball head lets you tilt from portrait to landscape and lock in any angle in between, which is essential for recording cooking videos or angling the screen toward a webcam.

The stability is genuinely surprising for a 1.4-pound tripod. I used it on a hardwood floor and a carpeted surface; both were solid. The rubber feet grip well, and even at full extension with an iPad mounted, there's minimal sway. A few Amazon reviewers noted the same. One wrote, *"Surprisingly sturdy for the price — I was worried it would tip over with my 12.9" iPad Pro, but it held firm during an hour-long Zoom call."* Another mentioned, *"The height range is perfect. I use it on my desk at the lowest setting and on the floor when I need to record full-body shots."*

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and the elitehood has two notable tradeoffs. First, the twist-lock collars take several rotations to fully loosen or tighten. If you're frequently adjusting height mid-task, it gets tedious. A flip-lever system would be faster, but it would also add weight and bulk.

Second, there's no included remote shutter. For solo content creators who want to record themselves without touching the device, you'll need to buy a separate Bluetooth remote — a minor cost, but an extra step nonetheless. A couple of reviewers also mentioned that the clamp can feel tight on thinner phone cases, so you may need to remove bulky cases before mounting.

## Who Should Buy This

This tripod is ideal for:
- **Remote workers** who want an eye-level camera angle for video calls without stacking books
- **Home cooks** who follow recipes on a tablet while keeping hands free
- **Budget content creators** who need a portable, lightweight rig for filming with a tablet
- **Students** who want to record lectures or use their iPad as a secondary display

Skip it if you need a heavy-duty studio tripod for professional camera gear — this is built for tablets and phones, not DSLRs.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this hold a 12.9-inch iPad Pro?**
Yes. The clamp expands to 13 inches and the load capacity is 2.2 pounds. The iPad Pro 12.9" weighs about 1.5 pounds, so you're safely within spec.

**Q: Can I use this with a phone and a tablet at the same time?**
No, the clamp holds one device at a time. But you can switch between them in seconds.

**Q: Is it portable enough for travel?**
It collapses to 15.7 inches and weighs 1.4 pounds, so it fits in a backpack or carry-on. The included carrying bag (some versions ship with one) keeps it organized.
