If you've priced a full set of Copic markers recently, you've probably winced. The Ohuhu 120-color set undercuts that cost by a massive margin while promising refillability and professional-grade blending. The question isn't whether it's cheaper — it's whether it's actually good enough for serious work. After digging through 240 Amazon ratings and the technical specs, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 120 double-tipped markers, each with a chisel tip on one end and a fine point on the other. Every color is unique — no filler duplicates or "close enough" shades hiding in the case. You also get a clear carry case that keeps the markers organized by color family, which matters more than you'd think when you're mid-project and need a specific warm gray.

The refillable design is the headline feature. Each marker unscrews to accept replacement ink, which extends the lifespan dramatically compared to disposable alternatives. The AP certification means the ink is non-toxic and low-odor — you can use these in a shared workspace or studio without fumigating the room.

## Performance and Build Quality

The chisel tip handles broad strokes and fills with minimal streaking, and the fine tip holds a sharp point for detail work without fraying after extended use. The ink lays down smoothly with good saturation on the first pass, which is the hallmark of a quality alcohol marker.

Blending is where this set earns its keep. The 120-color range includes a solid gradient of grays, skin tones, and primaries that let you transition shades without harsh lines. One Amazon reviewer summed it up: "These blend like markers twice the price. I've used Copics for years and switched to these for client sketches." Another noted, "The color selection is genuinely useful — I haven't hit a color gap yet in my illustration work."

The refill system works as advertised. Unscrewing the barrel and adding ink takes about 30 seconds, and the markers hold a consistent color before and after refilling. That's a reliability point that cheaper refillable brands often miss.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: these markers drink ink faster than premium brands. Under heavy use — think large fills or layering multiple passes — you'll reach for the refill bottle sooner than you would with a Copic. That's the tradeoff for the price, and it's worth knowing before you buy.

The fine tip is firmer than a brush tip. If your style relies on variable-width strokes from a flexible brush nib, this set won't give you that. You'll need to supplement with a few brush-tip markers for that specific technique. Also, while the ink doesn't ghost through most quality paper, cheap printer paper will bleed — use marker paper or cardstock for best results.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a hobbyist, student, or working illustrator who needs a full color range without dropping $500+ on Copic. You value refillability and don't mind a firmer fine tip. You want a marker set that travels well in the included case.

**Skip it if:** You're a professional who relies exclusively on brush-tip flexibility, or you need maximum ink longevity per dollar spent. If you're just testing whether alcohol markers are for you, a smaller starter set makes more sense than jumping to 120 colors.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these markers bleed through standard printer paper?**
A: Yes, with heavy layering. Use marker paper, cardstock, or mixed-media paper for best results. Standard 20-lb paper will show ghosting.

**Q: How long does a refill bottle last?**
A: Each refill bottle typically restores a marker 3-5 times, depending on how heavily you use it. Heavy users should stock up on the colors they use most.

**Q: Are the colors consistent between batches?**
A: Generally yes, but alcohol markers can vary slightly between production runs. Buy refills for your existing set from the same batch when possible.
