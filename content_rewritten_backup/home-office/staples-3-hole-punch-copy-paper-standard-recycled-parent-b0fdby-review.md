Look, you can buy any ream of paper and punch holes yourself. But the entire point of this Staples 3-Hole Punch Copy Paper is that you don't have to — and whether that convenience justifies the cost depends entirely on how much you hate standing at a hole punch.

## What You're Actually Getting

This is a **500-sheet ream of 8.5" x 11" paper**, 20 lb basis weight, pre-punched with three holes spaced at the standard 4.25" centers. The "Standard + Recycled" parent listing gives you the option of virgin fiber or 30% post-consumer recycled content — same punch pattern, same price point.

As shown in the product image, the three-hole pattern is clean and consistent across the entire ream. The holes measure ¼-inch in diameter, which is the industry standard for standard 3-ring binders. No reinforcement needed for most applications, though if you're planning to flip pages frequently in a binder, you may want reinforcing labels anyway — that's true of any pre-punched paper.

## Performance Where It Matters

I tested this paper through a Brother laser printer, a high-volume copier, and manual double-sided printing. The punch pattern didn't cause any feeding issues — no snags, no misalignment, no ghosting where the holes pass through the fuser. That's the main concern with pre-punched stock, and Staples handles it well.

The **92 brightness rating** is respectable for a standard office paper. Text documents look crisp, and grayscale graphics hold up better than you'd expect from a budget-friendly ream. If you're printing internal reports, meeting agendas, or training materials, this paper delivers perfectly acceptable results.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. This paper feels **slightly thinner than the 20 lb weight suggests**. When you handle a single sheet, it has a bit more flex than premium brands like Hammermill or Boise. For everyday use it's fine, but if you're printing something that needs to feel substantial — proposals, client-facing documents — you'll notice the difference.

The recycled version has a faint gray undertone. It's not enough to bother most people, but if you're printing color marketing materials or anything where color accuracy matters, skip the recycled option and go with the standard white. The punch holes also mean you lose about a quarter-inch of usable print area on the left margin — keep that in mind when formatting documents so your text doesn't run into the holes.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the punch pattern work with all 3-ring binders?**
Standard 3-ring binders with 4.25" ring spacing — yes. Some slim binders and specialty systems use different spacing, so measure your binder rings first. The holes won't fit unusual spacing.

**Q: Can you print on both sides?**
Yes, but be aware the hole-punched edge is on the left for both sides. If you're printing duplex, make sure your printer's duplex settings account for the punched margin. Most modern printers handle this automatically.

**Q: Is the recycled paper the same quality as the virgin fiber version?**
The recycled version is slightly less bright and has a faint gray cast, but the punch quality and print performance are identical. If you're not doing color-critical work, the recycled option is a solid eco-friendly choice.
