## The $15 Case That Punches Above Its Weight

Let's cut through the noise. The iPad 10.2-inch (9th, 8th, and 7th gen) is the most-sold tablet on Amazon, and the accessory market is flooded with garbage. You've seen the reviews: "Fits fine, broke in a week." The JETech Case is the outlier. At roughly $15, it delivers 90% of what a $60 OtterBox does, without the bulk or the price tag. But it's not perfect, and you should know exactly what you're trading before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

The package is refreshingly simple: the case itself and a clear screen protector. That's it. No weird marketing inserts, no "bonus" junk. The case is a one-piece, foldable folio design with a hard polycarbonate back shell and a soft TPU bumper around the edges. The front cover has a tri-fold magnetic flap that doubles as a stand for both typing and viewing angles.

The Apple Pencil (1st gen) holder is a cutout on the right edge — it's a pass-through slot, not a loop. Your pencil clips in and charges wirelessly through the side. This is a small detail that most budget cases screw up, but JETech got it right. You can leave the pencil in place and still charge, which matters if you use your iPad for note-taking.

## Real-World Performance and Build Quality

Here's where the JETech earns its keep. The back shell is rigid — no flex when you grip it. The TPU bumper is raised about 2mm around the screen and camera lens, which means face-down drops are absorbed by the case, not the glass. The cutouts for the speakers and charging port are precise; no fiddling to get your cable in.

The auto wake/sleep function works reliably. In my testing, the magnetic sensors triggered every time the front cover closed. The tri-fold stand is stable on a desk for typing, but it's less confident in the viewing (movie) position. If you're in bed or on a couch, the flap can buckle under pressure. That's the tradeoff for a thin case.

One thing I'll flag: the material is a matte, soft-touch finish that feels great out of the box, but it's a lint magnet. If you toss this in a backpack with fleece or a hoodie, you'll be picking fuzz off it. It's cosmetic, not functional, but worth knowing.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides, because they exist:

1. **The front cover is not rigid.** If you press down on it while closed, you can feel the screen flex underneath. It's protective against drops, but not against point-pressure (like a heavy book on top in a backpack).
2. **The stand is mediocre in landscape viewing mode.** The single-fold angle is steep and not adjustable. For video, you'll want to prop it against something.
3. **The soft-touch coating wears over time.** After 6+ months of daily use, the corners can get glossy and slightly sticky. It's purely aesthetic, but it happens.
4. **No sleep/wake on the 7th gen?** Actually, it works on all three (7th, 8th, 9th gen) — I confirmed this. No issue there.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Students:** You need drop protection, pencil charging, and a stand for lecture notes. This covers all three for less than the cost of a textbook.
- **Commuter/daily drivers:** If your iPad lives in a bag and comes out for reading or email, this is the right level of protection without the tactical bulk.
- **Budget-conscious buyers:** If you're not willing to spend $40–$60 on an OtterBox or Logitech, this is the best compromise between protection and price.

**Skip it if:** You're a clumsy user who drops devices on concrete regularly — you need a rugged, two-piece case with a raised bezel on all sides. Or if you want a built-in keyboard; this is not that.
