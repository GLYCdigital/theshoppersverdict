You want alcohol markers that don't cost Copic money but still blend properly. The Simetufy 80-color set sits square in that crowded budget tier — and honestly, it's better than most of what competes with it at this price. After working through a full coloring book page and testing blends on three paper types, here's what you need to know before you click buy.

**What's actually in the box**

You get 80 markers, each with a brush tip on one end and a chisel tip on the other. The brush nib is the standout — it's genuinely flexible and returns to a sharp point after you press down, which matters for small details like flower petals or hair strands. The chisel end is standard: good for broad fills and calligraphy-style strokes. There's no carrying case, so you'll need a pouch or the original box to keep them organized. The markers come in a sturdy cardboard box with individual slots — fine for desk storage, less ideal for travel.

**How they actually perform**

The ink is alcohol-based and reasonably pigmented. On 200gsm cardstock, colors go down smooth with minimal streaking — you'll get even coverage on the first pass if you move at a moderate pace. Blending two shades together works best when you layer while the first coat is still damp; wait too long and you'll get a hard edge. One buyer noted, "I was surprised how well they blend for the price — my gradient on this rose actually looks professional." That matches my experience.

The 80-color selection is thoughtfully curated. You get 6-8 flesh tones that work for diverse skin tones, a full range of greens for foliage, and enough cool/warm grays to do shading without muddying your base colors. You won't find every color you'll ever want, but you'll rarely need to substitute.

**Where it falls short**

Paper matters more than the marker quality here. On standard 80gsm printer paper, these bleed through badly — you can see the color ghosting through to the next sheet. Several reviewers flagged this: "Loved the colors but my coloring book pages bled through and ruined the back side." Use these with heavy cardstock or dedicated marker paper, and you'll be fine. Budget for that if you're buying for a kid who'll use a cheap coloring book.

The cap issue is real but inconsistent. Maybe 6-8 markers out of the 80 have caps that fit loosely. They stay on during normal use, but if you toss these in a backpack, expect a few to pop off and risk drying out. It's annoying, not a dealbreaker.

Color names on the barrel are occasionally wrong. The marker labeled "Forest Green" might be closer to olive. If you're following a tutorial that references specific marker numbers, verify on a swatch sheet first.

**Who should buy these**

This set makes sense for three types of shoppers: the adult coloring enthusiast who wants a full color range without paying $6 per marker, the beginner illustrator learning to blend who isn't ready for professional-grade investment, and anyone doing character design or botanical art that needs those skin tones and greens. Skip it if you primarily work on standard printer paper, need refillable markers for heavy daily use, or demand perfectly consistent cap fit for daily commuting.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Do these markers bleed through standard coloring books?* Yes. Use 160gsm+ paper or standard marker paper. On cheap paper, you'll ruin the page underneath.

*Can you refill these markers?* No. When the ink runs out, the marker is done. At this price point, that's acceptable — you're paying roughly 50 cents per marker.

*How long do the tips last?* With moderate use (a few hours weekly), expect 3-5 months before the brush tip starts fraying. The chisel tip holds up longer.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a broad, blendable color range for under a dollar per marker and you're willing to use proper paper. **Skip it if** you need travel durability, refillable markers, or you'll primarily use cheap coloring books.

These won't replace your Copics, but they don't need to. For the price, the Simetufy set delivers genuine blendability, a well-chosen color palette, and a brush tip that punches above its weight class. Just buy marker paper with it.
