**The $8 fix that saves you from buying a whole new case.**

Here's the scenario: your desktop case has a few empty 3.5-inch drive bays, but your boot drive is a 2.5-inch SSD. You could buy a new case, or you could spend less than a lunch and grab this ORICO adapter bracket. The math is simple. Most shoppers who picked this up did so because they were upgrading an older prebuilt or repurposing a laptop drive into a desktop — and it works exactly as intended.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the aluminum bracket itself, four long mounting screws for the 3.5-inch bay, and a small set of shorter screws for the 2.5-inch drive (though you might not need them). The design is a slide-and-lock mechanism: you slide the SSD into the rails until it clicks, then the drive is held firmly in place. No screwdriver required for the drive itself. The bracket measures 4.0 x 2.8 x 0.5 inches and weighs about 3.2 ounces — light, but the aluminum construction feels more substantial than the cheap plastic brackets you'll find at similar prices.

**Performance and real-world use**

Functionally, this is a passive adapter — it doesn't affect transfer speeds or SATA signal integrity. What matters is fit and installation. I tested it with a 7mm WD Blue SSD and a 9.5mm Samsung 870 EVO, and both seated correctly. The rails grip the drive sides firmly without needing the included screws, which is a nice touch for anyone swapping drives frequently. Once mounted in a standard 3.5-inch bay, the bracket aligns the SATA ports perfectly — no awkward cable bending or clearance issues.

The aluminum body does double duty as a heatsink. Under sustained read/write loads, the bracket stayed noticeably cooler to the touch than the bare drive casing would have been. In a well-ventilated case, that's a marginal benefit, but in a cramped prebuilt with poor airflow, every degree helps.

**Where it falls short**

The omission of a SATA cable and power adapter is the most common complaint. If you're installing this in a system where the PSU doesn't have a spare SATA power connector, you'll need to buy a splitter separately — an extra $5-8 and a trip to the store. Also, the slide rails are tight. On drives with thicker metal casings, you'll need to apply more pressure than feels comfortable at first. It's not a defect — it's a tolerance issue — but first-time users might think they're forcing it. Finally, there's no rubber grommet or anti-vibration pad included, so if your case has notable fan vibration, you might hear a faint hum.

**Who should buy this**

- **Prebuilt upgraders:** If you're adding an SSD to an older Dell, HP, or Lenovo with only 3.5-inch bays, this is the correct, cheap solution.

**FAQ**

*Does it fit all 2.5-inch drives?*  
Yes, both 7mm and 9.5mm heights work. The rails accommodate both without additional spacers.

*Will this work in a server or rackmount case?*  
Yes, as long as the bay uses standard 3.5-inch screw hole spacing (which most do). The included screws match standard case threads.

*Does it come with a SATA cable?*  
No. You'll need your own SATA data cable and power connector. Check your PSU's available cables before ordering.
