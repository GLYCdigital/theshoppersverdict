Let's be honest: a manila folder is a manila folder. Until it rips at the tab, spills your client's paperwork across the floor, or the fastener tears through the paper. That's where the Blue Summit Supplies 100-count box tries to earn its keep — and for the most part, it delivers.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 100 letter-size folders made from 11-point manila stock. That's heavier than the flimsy 9-point folders you'll find at dollar stores, and it shows in hand. Each folder has a 2-inch brass prong fastener positioned at the center top, plus a reinforced end tab — the kind that tells you the manufacturer actually watched how people use these things.

The end tabs are reinforced with an extra layer of material where the tab meets the folder body. That's the exact spot where cheap folders split after a few months in a packed drawer. As shown in the product image, the tabs are clearly printed with "END TAB" positioning, making them easy to spot in a lateral file cabinet.

## Real-World Performance

I've handled enough filing systems to know the fastener is the make-or-break feature. The 2-inch prongs here are genuinely useful — they'll hold a thick client file (think 100-150 sheets) without the metal bending or the paper tearing at the punch holes. The metal is sturdy brass, not the tinny silver stuff that rusts or bends on contact.

The folders sit flat in a drawer, which sounds trivial until you've dealt with curled edges from cheap stock. The 11-point weight also means these survive being pulled in and out of an overstuffed cabinet without the bottom edge shredding.

## Where It Falls Short

The fastener prongs are the double-edged sword here. They're sturdy enough to hold documents securely, but they'll snag on papers if you're sliding them in quickly rather than aligning the punch holes first. That's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing if you're processing high volumes of paperwork.

Second, these are pure fastener folders — no interior pockets for loose receipts or unfiled documents. If you're the type who stuffs stray papers into folders before filing them properly, you'll need a separate solution.

## FAQ

**Do these fit standard file cabinets?**
Yes, they're standard letter-size (9.5 x 11.75 inches) and fit any standard drawer or hanging-file frame.

**Can the fasteners be removed and reattached?**
The brass prongs are fixed to the folder. Once you bend them down, you can unbend them a few times before the metal fatigues, but they're designed for permanent fastening.

**Are these acid-free/archival quality?**
No. They're standard manila stock, not acid-free archival quality. Fine for active files, not for long-term preservation of irreplaceable documents.
