If you own an HP Envy 6000 or DeskJet 2700 series, you already know the sting of OEM ink prices. The Skyink 67XL combo pack steps in as the budget-friendly alternative that claims to match HP's output at roughly half the cost. After digging through 210 ratings and the specs sheet, here's what actually matters before you click buy.

**What's in the box**

You get two cartridges: one black 67XL and one tri-color 67XL, both wrapped in sealed foil pouches. The orange protective caps on the print heads are a small touch that prevents dried ink or leaks during transit — something budget brands often skip. Installation is genuinely tool-free: pop out the old cartridges, peel the tape, snap these in. No firmware gymnastics required on most HP models.

**Performance and print quality**

The first thing you'll notice is the yield. Rated at approximately 600 pages for black and 415 for color, these XL cartridges genuinely last longer than standard HP 67s — most users report getting through a full semester of coursework or several months of home office printing before swapping. Text documents come out crisp with deep, dark blacks that hold up well on plain copy paper. For a third-party cartridge, the consistency is impressive.

The tri-color cartridge handles standard office graphics and charts well. Where it shows its limits is photo printing. Color output runs slightly warm — skin tones lean a bit orange, and skies come out more cyan-heavy than neutral. If you're printing family photos, you'll notice the difference against OEM cartridges. For everyday documents, invoices, and school assignments, it's perfectly serviceable.

One buyer noted, "I was skeptical about third-party ink, but these printed flawlessly in my Envy 6055. No smudging, no streaks, and the printer recognized them immediately." That sentiment echoes through the reviews — compatibility issues are rare, which is the biggest risk with off-brand cartridges.

**Where it falls short**

The warm color cast is the most consistent complaint. Another issue: the yield estimates are optimistic. The 600-page black claim assumes 5% page coverage — essentially plain text documents. Print a 20-page presentation with charts and headers, and you'll see the ink gauge drop faster than expected. That's not a flaw unique to Skyink, but it's worth calibrating your expectations.

Some users also reported the printer's ink level indicator showing inaccurate readings after installation. The cartridges work fine, but the low-ink warnings may appear prematurely or not at all. It's a minor annoyance rather than a dealbreaker, but know that the chip communication isn't always perfect.

**Who should buy this**

This combo pack is ideal for students printing essays and lecture notes, home offices handling standard documents, and anyone who burns through ink quickly and wants to cut costs without sacrificing basic quality. Skip it if you're a photographer or someone who prints high-volume photos — the color accuracy gap will frustrate you.

**FAQ**

**Will these work in my HP Envy 6000 series?**
Yes. They're compatible with the full HP 67 family, including Envy 6000/6400/7800, DeskJet 2700/2800/4100 series, and HP Tango printers. The chip is recognized without firmware updates on current models.

**How do these compare to the HP 67XL originals?**
They cost significantly less upfront and deliver comparable page yields. Print quality is close for documents, but OEM cartridges still win on photo color accuracy. Expect roughly 90% of the OEM experience for 50-60% of the price.

**Can I use just the black cartridge if my color runs out?**
Yes. The printer will prompt you to replace the empty cartridge, but you can continue printing black-only documents by selecting grayscale mode in the printer settings.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need reliable, high-yield ink for everyday printing and want to cut your ink costs in half without dealing with technical headaches.

**Skip it if:** Photo printing is a primary use case, or you need precise color matching for client-facing materials.
