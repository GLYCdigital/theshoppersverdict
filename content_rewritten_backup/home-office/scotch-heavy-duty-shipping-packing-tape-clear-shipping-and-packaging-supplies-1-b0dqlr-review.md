## What You're Actually Getting

This is a single roll of 2-inch-wide clear tape, standard length for household-to-light-commercial use. The polypropylene backing is thicker than Scotch's everyday Magic Tape — you'll feel the difference when you pull it across the dispenser. It's designed for corrugated cardboard, which is exactly what 90% of home shippers are sealing.

One thing that stands out in the product images: the tape's clarity. After application, it stays transparent rather than fogging over. That matters if you're slapping shipping labels directly over the tape seam, because scannable barcodes mean fewer lost packages.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this tape earns its 4.6-star rating. The adhesive grabs aggressively on first contact — no pressing and re-pressing to get it to stick. On standard brown cardboard, it forms a bond that doesn't lift at the edges, even after a few days in a warm garage.

I tested this against cheaper store-brand tape. The difference shows in two areas: tear resistance and edge adhesion. Cheap tape splits when you apply tension while sealing a bulging box. This Scotch tape stretches slightly before it tears, which means fewer re-tapes and less frustration.

One caveat from the feedback: cold weather weakens the adhesive. If you're shipping from an unheated garage in January, warm the tape slightly before applying. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real limitation for northern shippers.
