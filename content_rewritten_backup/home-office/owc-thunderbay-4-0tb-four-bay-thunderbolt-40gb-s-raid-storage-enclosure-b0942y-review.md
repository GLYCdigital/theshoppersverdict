Let's cut through the marketing. The OWC ThunderBay 4 is a four-bay Thunderbolt RAID enclosure that's been a staple in pro video and audio workflows for years. At 4.2 stars across 210 Amazon ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but it's also not for everyone. Here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find the ThunderBay 4 enclosure itself, a Thunderbolt cable, a power brick (yes, it's external — which keeps the chassis slimmer), and a quick-start guide. No drives. No caddies. This is a bare enclosure, and that's a critical point: you're paying for the chassis and the RAID controller, not storage. Factor in the cost of four drives and you're looking at a serious investment.

The build is exactly what you'd expect from OWC's pro line. The all-aluminum chassis is rigid, with a brushed finish that resists fingerprints. The front bays are tool-free — you pop the latch, slide in a 3.5\" or 2.5\" drive, and lock it down. The mechanism feels solid, not flimsy like some cheaper enclosures I've tested.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The headline feature is Thunderbolt 40Gb/s (that's Thunderbolt 3/4). In real-world testing, you'll hit around 1,500–2,500 MB/s read speeds depending on your RAID configuration and drive types. With four SSDs in RAID 0, you'll saturate the Thunderbolt connection. With spinning HDDs, you'll be bottlenecked by the drives, not the interface — which is expected.

The built-in hardware RAID controller is the standout feature. You can configure RAID 0, 1, 4, 5, or 10 via dip switches on the back, no software required. That's a big deal for reliability — if your OS crashes, your array is still intact. OWC's SoftRAID software is optional but recommended for monitoring and notifications.

Setup takes about five minutes. Set the dip switches, insert drives, power on, and format. The manual is clear, though a total beginner might find the RAID terminology daunting.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in user feedback is reliability. One reviewer noted, *"I've been running this 24/7 for 18 months with four 8TB drives in RAID 5. Zero issues. It just works."* Another echoed that sentiment for video editing: *"Editing 4K ProRes directly off this thing is flawless. No dropped frames, no stutters."*

The daisy-chaining capability also gets frequent mentions. You can connect up to five additional Thunderbolt devices through the second Thunderbolt port — a lifesaver for laptop users with limited ports.

## Where It Falls Short

The fan noise is the most common complaint. It's not loud enough to be a dealbreaker in a studio, but in a quiet home office, you'll hear it. One buyer put it plainly: *"It's not silent. If you're recording audio in the same room, you'll pick it up on a sensitive mic."* 

The size and weight are also worth noting. At roughly 8.5 pounds empty and dimensions of 8.7\" x 5.7\" x 7.5\", this is a desktop fixture, not a portable drive. It's meant to sit on your desk and stay there.

The lack of included drive trays is another consideration. While the tool-free design is convenient, you can't just pop in any drive — you need to secure it properly, and the included mechanism works best with standard 3.5\" drives.

## Who Should Buy This

**Video editors and photographers** working with large 4K/8K files will get the most value. The hardware RAID 5 configuration gives you speed with redundancy, and the Thunderbolt throughput won't bottleneck your timeline.

**Audio engineers** should look elsewhere unless you can place this in a separate room or closet. The fan noise is real, and it will interfere with sensitive recordings.

**Casual home users** are better served by a single-drive Thunderbolt SSD. This is overkill for backing up a laptop — you'd be paying for RAID flexibility you'll never use.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I mix different drive sizes and brands in the same array?**
A: Yes, but it's not recommended for RAID 5 or 10. The array will operate at the speed of the slowest drive and use the smallest drive's capacity as the baseline. For RAID 0, mixing is fine but you'll lose the capacity of the larger drives.

**Q: Does this work with Windows or just Mac?**
A: It's Thunderbolt, so it works with both, but OWC's SoftRAID software is Mac-only. On Windows, you'll use the hardware RAID via dip switches, which works fine — you just lose the software monitoring features.

**Q: Is the Thunderbolt 40Gb/s actually faster than USB-C 10Gb/s?**
A: Yes, significantly. In RAID 0 with SSDs, you'll see 2,000+ MB/s versus roughly 1,000 MB/s on USB 10Gb/s. But with spinning HDDs, you'll likely cap out around 250 MB/s regardless of the interface — the drives are the bottleneck.

## The Verdict: 4.2/5

**Buy it if:** You're a working professional with a Thunderbolt-equipped Mac or PC, you need RAID 5 reliability for irreplaceable footage, and you understand you're buying a chassis, not a storage solution.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget, you need quiet operation for audio work, or you just want plug-and-play external storage without RAID configuration.

The OWC ThunderBay 4 is a workhorse. It's not cheap, it's not silent, and it's not portable — but for professionals who need dependable, fast, redundant storage, it earns its keep. The 4.2-star rating reflects a product that does its job exceptionally well, with a few compromises you should know about before you commit.
