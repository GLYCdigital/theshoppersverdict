**XPG CORE Reactor 650W Review: A Quiet, Reliable Power Supply for Everyday Builds**

When you're assembling a workstation or upgrading an office PC, the power supply is the last component you want to cut corners on. The XPG CORE Reactor 650W sits in a crowded field of 80 Plus Gold units, but it brings some genuinely compelling specs to the table—most notably, a 10-year warranty and fully modular cabling at a price that undercuts many big-name rivals. After digging into the specs and what 210 Amazon buyers are saying (4.2 stars), here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the PSU itself, a full set of modular cables (ATX 24-pin, CPU 8-pin, PCIe, SATA, and peripheral), a power cord, mounting screws, and a velcro cable tie kit. The unit measures 160mm long, which is standard ATX, but check your case clearance—some compact mid-towers will be tight with this depth.

The star feature is the 135mm fluid dynamic bearing fan. Unlike cheaper sleeve-bearing fans, FDB designs don't degrade over time and are significantly quieter. The fan doesn't spin at all under low loads (below roughly 30% capacity), so in a typical office setup pulling 150-250W, this thing is dead silent. That's a meaningful quality-of-life improvement if you work in a quiet room.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The CORE Reactor uses a modern LLC resonant converter design with DC-to-DC conversion for the 5V and 3.3V rails. Practically, this means rock-solid voltage regulation—your components see clean, stable power even during sudden load spikes. Ripple suppression is excellent, measuring well within ATX spec in independent tests.

The build quality is a step above what you'd expect at this price point. The casing is sturdy, the cables are flat and flexible (easy to route behind motherboard trays), and the connectors click in with satisfying firmness. The Japanese capacitors are rated for 105°C, which directly supports that 10-year warranty—this is a PSU designed to outlive your current PC.

**What Buyers Actually Praise**

The consistent theme in customer feedback is reliability and silence. One verified buyer noted, "Installed this in a new build and it's been rock solid. The fan never spins up during normal use, and the modular cables made cable management a breeze." Another commented, "Replaced a noisy 750W unit with this and my PC is now whisper quiet. **Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the downsides. The 650W capacity is the entry point for this series, and it's not enough for high-end gaming rigs with power-hungry GPUs like an RTX 4080 or RX 7900 XTX. If you're building anything beyond a mid-range gaming PC, step up to the 750W or 850W version.

The cables, while flexible, are on the shorter side—the CPU 8-pin is about 60cm. In full-tower cases with bottom-mounted PSU bays, you may struggle to route cleanly behind the motherboard tray. Plan your routing carefully.

Also, while the fan is quiet in normal operation, it's not completely inaudible under sustained heavy load. At 80-100% output, you'll hear it. That's expected, but worth knowing if you're sensitive to fan noise during long renders or gaming sessions.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Office workers and professionals** building a quiet, reliable workstation that won't crash during important work
- **Casual gamers** with mid-range GPUs (up to around a 4070 or 6700 XT) who want a clean, efficient power supply
- **Budget-conscious builders** who prioritize long-term reliability and a 10-year warranty over brand-name prestige

**Skip it if** you're building a high-end gaming rig with flagship components, or if your case has a very shallow PSU compartment (under 160mm depth).

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this power supply compatible with my motherboard?**
A: Yes, it includes the standard 24-pin ATX connector and 8-pin CPU cable that works with virtually all modern motherboards, including AM4, AM5, LGA1700, and LGA1851 platforms.

**Q: Will this handle a 3D printer or other workshop equipment?**
A: Yes, it's a standard ATX PSU. You'll need a 24-pin jumper or an adapter to turn it on without a motherboard, but it's commonly used for this purpose due to its stable output and quiet operation.

**Q: Is the 10-year warranty actually honored?**
A: XPG is a subsidiary of ADATA, a major memory and storage manufacturer. Warranty claims are handled through ADATA's support system, and while some users report slower response times than premium brands, the warranty is legitimate and backed by a real company.
