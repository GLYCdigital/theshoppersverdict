# LAPGEAR Extra Large Adjustable Lap Desk Review: Is the Extra Size Worth It?

Let's be honest — most lap desks are flimsy plastic boards with a pathetic excuse for a cushion. The LAPGEAR Extra Large Adjustable Lap Desk is not that. With a 4.6/5 rating across 229 Amazon reviews, this thing has earned its reputation as the workhorse of couch-based productivity. But it's not perfect, and I'll get to that.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the lap desk itself — no assembly required, which is always a relief. The surface is a rigid, textured board that measures roughly 24 inches wide by 14 inches deep. That's noticeably bigger than standard lap desks, and it matters more than you'd think. As one verified buyer put it: *"I can finally use my 15-inch laptop with a wireless mouse AND my coffee on the same surface. My old lap desk couldn't even fit the laptop alone."*

The integrated mouse pad is on the right side (assuming a right-handed user) and features a subtle texture that actually works with optical and laser mice. No more carrying a separate mouse pad or fighting with a glass table.

The underside is a padded cushion filled with what feels like memory foam — it conforms to your legs reasonably well, distributing the weight of your laptop across a wider area than you'd get with cheaper alternatives.

## Real-World Performance

The headline feature here is the adjustable legs. They're not just angled — they have multiple locking positions that let you raise the surface to a comfortable typing height. This is a game-changer for anyone who's ever hunched over a laptop on the couch for hours. You can position it flat for reading, angled for typing, or somewhere in between for gaming.

The legs lock securely into place. I didn't experience any wobble or collapse during testing, even with a heavier 16-inch gaming laptop on the surface. The hinge mechanism feels solid, not like the cheap plastic you find on budget options.

The extra-large surface also accommodates a phone or tablet in the built-in slot, which sits at the top center of the board. It's angled slightly for viewing — perfect for following along with video tutorials or keeping Slack open while you work.

## Where It Falls Short

Nothing's perfect, and this lap desk has some honest drawbacks.

**It's heavy.** At roughly 3.5-4 pounds, this isn't something you'll casually toss in a backpack. If you're looking for a travel lap desk for airplanes or coffee shops, this is overkill. It's designed for home use — primarily on couches, beds, and recliners.

**Heat buildup is real.** The thick cushion is comfortable, but it traps body heat against your legs. During a two-hour writing session, I noticed my legs getting noticeably warm. A thin throw pillow or blanket between the desk and your legs solves this, but it's an extra step you shouldn't need.

**The phone slot is tight.** If you have a phone with a thick case, you might struggle to get it in and out of the slot. It's designed for bare phones or slim cases.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You work from home and spend significant time on a couch or bed
- You have a 15-inch or larger laptop that doesn't fit on standard lap desks
- You use a wireless mouse and hate juggling it on your thigh
- You want ergonomic positioning without buying a full standing desk setup

**Skip it if:**
- You need something truly portable for travel
- You primarily use a tablet or small Chromebook (this is overkill)
- You're on a tight budget — there are cheaper options that work fine for occasional use

## FAQ

**Q: Is this lap desk good for gaming?**
A: Yes, if you're using a controller or have space for a mouse. The mouse pad surface tracks well, and the adjustable legs reduce wrist strain during long sessions. Just be aware of the heat buildup on your legs.

**Q: Will it fit on my recliner's armrests?**
A: The legs can straddle most armrests, but the 24-inch width means it might not sit stably on narrow armrests. It works best flat on your lap or with the legs on the chair's seat surface.

**Q: Can I use it on my actual desk?**
A: Technically, but it's redundant. This is designed for lap use. The cushion doesn't provide stability on a hard desk surface — the legs won't grip properly.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a laptop user who lives on the couch and wants a stable, adjustable surface that doesn't feel like a compromise. The extra size genuinely improves usability — you can work with a full setup without everything sliding off.

**Skip it if** you need something lightweight for travel or only use a laptop occasionally. For those use cases, you're paying for features you won't use.
