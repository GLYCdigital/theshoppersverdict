Let's be direct: the Arris G18 is a solid modem wrapped in a frustratingly average router. If you're shopping for an office or home workspace combo unit, here's what 190 Amazon reviewers have figured out the hard way.

**The hardware story**

Out of the box, you get the G18 unit, a power adapter, and an Ethernet cable. That's it. No mounting bracket, no setup guide worth reading — you'll find better instructions on Arris's support site. The vertical tower design is genuinely space-efficient, roughly the footprint of a coffee mug, which matters if your desk is already crowded with monitors and peripherals.

The headline feature is the 2.5 Gbps Ethernet WAN port. That's not marketing fluff — it's real hardware that future-proofs your setup for gigabit-plus cable plans. The DOCSIS 3.1 modem inside is certified for major providers: Xfinity, Spectrum, Cox, and Charter all work. If you're currently paying rental fees to your ISP, this pays for itself in about 10 months.

**Real-world performance**

Connected to a 1 Gbps Xfinity plan, the G18 consistently delivers 850–940 Mbps down. That's excellent — right at the practical ceiling for cable. Buyers on multi-gig plans report seeing 1.2–1.4 Gbps through the 2.5 Gbps port, which is genuinely impressive for a combo unit.

But here's where praise turns to caveats. The Wi-Fi 6 implementation is underwhelming. In open spaces, you'll get solid throughput. Put a wall between you and the router, and speeds drop noticeably. One reviewer put it plainly: "Great modem, terrible router. I had to buy a separate access point within a week." That sentiment appears repeatedly in the feedback.

Another recurring complaint: the 2.4GHz and 5GHz bands are locked into a single SSID with no option to split them. Smart home devices that only support 2.4GHz often fail to connect on the first try. If you have IoT gear, budget time for troubleshooting.

**Setup gotchas**

Activation isn't plug-and-play. With Xfinity, you'll need to call or use their app to provision the modem. Some reviewers report the activation failing the first time, requiring a second call. Spectrum users had smoother experiences — one noted, "Activated in 10 minutes on the phone, no issues." Your mileage will vary by provider.

**Who should buy this**

This is a solid pick for a solo office or small workspace on a gigabit-plus cable plan who wants one device instead of a modem-router stack. The 2.5 Gbps port is ahead of the curve, and the modem itself is rock-solid. It's also a good value if you're tired of paying $15/month in rental fees.

Skip it if you have a larger space, multiple walls between rooms, or a house full of smart home devices. You'll end up buying a separate router or mesh system anyway, which defeats the cost savings.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this with my own router?* Yes — the G18 has a bridge mode that turns off Wi-Fi and lets it function purely as a modem. This is the workaround several reviewers used to fix the Wi-Fi range issue.

*Does it work with fiber internet?* No. This is a cable modem. It requires a coax connection from a cable provider.

*Is the 2.5 Gbps port worth it?* Only if your plan exceeds 1 Gbps. If you're on a standard 300–600 Mbps plan, you'll never notice the difference.

**The verdict**

The Arris G18 is a quality DOCSIS 3.1 modem with a mediocre router bolted on. At 3.7/5, it earns a conditional recommendation.

**Buy it if:** you're on a gigabit-plus cable plan, need the 2.5 Gbps port, and have a small space where Wi-Fi range isn't critical.

**Skip it if:** you need whole-home coverage, have many smart devices, or want a truly plug-and-play setup. In those cases, a separate modem and router combo will serve you better — and probably cost less in the long run.
