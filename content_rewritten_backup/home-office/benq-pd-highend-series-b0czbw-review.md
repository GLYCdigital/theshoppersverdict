# BenQ PD Highend Series Review: A Color-Accurate Workhorse That Skips the Frills

Walk into any design studio and you'll see a row of BenQ PD monitors. There's a reason for that, and the Highend Series doesn't break the pattern. ## What You Actually Get

Unboxing reveals the essentials: the panel, a two-piece stand that tool-free snaps into place, DisplayPort and HDMI cables, and a quick-start guide. The stand is a genuine highlight though: 155mm of height adjustment, tilt, swivel, and a smooth 90° pivot for portrait work. It's the kind of stand you'd otherwise spend $100 on separately.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The chassis is dense and feels premium — no creaking plastic panels or flex at the corners. The anti-glare coating is aggressive enough to kill reflections without adding a distracting sparkle to white backgrounds, which is a common issue on cheaper matte panels.

Performance-wise, the factory calibration holds up. The Delta E ≤ 3 spec isn't just marketing; whites look neutral, skin tones render naturally, and there's no obvious color cast out of the box. The uniformity compensation feature is the quiet hero here — it actively adjusts brightness across zones so a white screen stays uniformly white, not slightly brighter in the center and dimmer at the edges like you'll see on most monitors even at this price point.

Across user feedback, three themes keep surfacing. The absence of USB-C is the biggest miss. The built-in speakers are forgettable. They'll handle a Zoom call, but music or video content sounds thin and hollow. This is a monitor that expects you to have external audio, and it doesn't pretend otherwise.

**Skip it if** you need USB-C connectivity for one-cable laptop setups, you're a gamer who needs high refresh rates, or you're a casual user who just wants a bright screen for browsing — you'll pay a premium for color accuracy you won't use.
