The Waterman Expert sits in an awkward spot. It's priced above entry-level pens but below the gold-nibbed prestige models. So what are you actually getting for your money? I've spent several weeks writing with it, and here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box**

You get the pen, one blue ink cartridge, and a small booklet. That's it. No converter included, which is mildly annoying at this price point. The pen itself measures 139mm capped — slightly shorter than a Pelikan M200 but longer than a Lamy 2000. Posted, it extends to a comfortable 168mm, and the cap posts deeply enough that it doesn't throw off the balance. The brass body gives it a reassuring heft that cheap plastic pens simply can't match.

**The writing experience**

The medium steel nib is the star here. It's smooth — genuinely smooth — with just enough feedback to tell you where you are on the paper. I tested it on Rhodia, cheap copier paper, and a Moleskine notebook. No skipping, no hard starts, no ink starvation on fast strokes. The flow is consistent and slightly wet, which gives your writing a rich, saturated line without turning into a blotter on absorbent paper.

One thing to note: the nib is firm. You won't get line variation from pressure like you would with a gold nib. It's a nail, but a very competent nail. If you want flex, look elsewhere. If you want a dependable daily writer that starts instantly after days of sitting unused, this does that job flawlessly.

**What owners consistently praise**

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings reflects a pattern. Buyers repeatedly mention the build quality and the writing smoothness. One reviewer wrote: "I've owned pens three times the price that don't write this consistently." Another noted: "The weight feels perfect — substantial but not fatiguing during long writing sessions." The lacquer finish gets frequent compliments too, with owners describing it as "deep and glossy" and "nicer in person than the photos suggest."

**Where it falls short**

The steel nib is the elephant in the room. At this price, many competitors (and even some Waterman models) offer gold nibs. You're paying a premium for the brand heritage and the French craftsmanship, not the nib material. If you're a collector or someone who values nib responsiveness, this will feel like a compromise.

The glossy lacquer also shows every fingerprint. If you're the type who sets your pen down between sentences, you'll be wiping it constantly. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

The included cartridge is fine, but the converter (sold separately) is overpriced for what it is. Budget an extra $15-20 if you plan to use bottled ink.

**Who should buy this**

- **The professional upgrading from a Lamy Safari or Pilot Metropolitan**: This is a genuine step up in materials and presence. The brass body and lacquer finish make it look the part in a boardroom.
- **The gift giver**: The box is elegant, the brand is recognized, and the writing experience won't disappoint a recipient who's new to fountain pens.
- **The daily writer who wants reliability**: It starts every time, writes smoothly on varied paper, and takes standard Waterman cartridges available anywhere.

Skip it if you're a nib enthusiast who values flex and line variation, or if you're looking for the best value in a steel-nibbed pen — in that case, a TWSBI or a Pilot Custom 74 offers more for less.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it come with a converter?**
A: No. It includes one blue cartridge. The Waterman converter fits perfectly and costs around $12-15 on Amazon.

**Q: How does it compare to the Waterman Hemisphere?**
A: The Expert is noticeably heavier and has a larger, smoother nib. The Hemisphere is slimmer and lighter — better for small hands, but the Expert feels more substantial and writes more confidently.

**Q: Can I use standard international cartridges?**
A: No. It uses Waterman-specific cartridges, not the standard international size. Stick with Waterman cartridges or the Waterman converter for bottled ink.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, good-looking everyday writer with a prestigious name on the clip. It writes beautifully, feels solid in the hand, and will last for years with basic care.

**Skip it if** you're seeking a gold nib experience or maximum value for money. At this price, you're paying for the brand and the finish — both are excellent, but they're not nib material.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-executed everyday pen that earns its reputation, with the steel nib and missing converter as the only meaningful strikes against it.
