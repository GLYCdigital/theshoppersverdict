Here's the thing about cheap mechanical pencils: most of them are either disposable junk or they snap the lead the moment you look at them wrong. The Four Candies set, though, is trying something different — and it mostly works. With 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, this isn't a niche product. It's a workhorse set that's earned its place in backpacks and desk drawers. But is it worth your money? Let's break it down.

## What You Actually Get

This isn't a "6 pencils and a pack of lead" situation. The set includes 12 mechanical pencils in pastel colors, each loaded with 12 pieces of 0.5mm lead. But here's the smart part that most people miss: each pencil barrel also contains a hidden 30-piece lead refill reservoir. Do the math — that's 12 pencils × 42 pieces of lead (12 pre-loaded + 30 in reserve) = 504 total lead pieces, plus 12 eraser caps. The "360PCS" in the title counts the lead refills separately, but the actual usable lead is higher.

The pencils are labeled 0.5mm HB, and the pastel colors aren't just for looks — they're color-coded to lead hardness. That's a thoughtful touch you don't see in most budget sets.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

I've handled dozens of mechanical pencils in this price range. The Four Candies pencils feel denser and more substantial than the typical plastic-body sets. The grip section is knurled metal, not smooth plastic, which means your fingers won't slide during long writing sessions. The pocket clip is also metal — a rarity at this price point — and it actually clips rather than just wobbling around.

Lead advancement is the real test. The click mechanism is firm with a satisfying snap — no mushy, half-advancing lead that breaks on the next stroke. The 0.5mm lead is standard HB hardness, which strikes the right balance for general note-taking. It's not too soft (smudging) or too hard (faint lines).

I'll be honest about one thing: the eraser caps are small. They work fine for quick corrections but won't survive heavy editing sessions. This is a minor flaw, not a dealbreaker — you'll likely replace them with a dedicated eraser anyway.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reading through the reviews, three themes keep coming up. First, the value proposition. One reviewer noted: *"I was skeptical about the lead claim, but I've been using the same pencil for three weeks of daily notes and haven't run out."* Second, the color-coding system — students especially appreciate grabbing a pink pencil and knowing it's the soft-lead one. Third, the durability. Multiple reviewers mention dropping these on hard floors without the lead snapping inside the mechanism.

## Where It Falls Short

The pastel finish is pretty but not rugged. If you're the type to throw pencils loose into a bag, the paint will chip within a few weeks. It's cosmetic only — the writing performance doesn't suffer — but if you bought these for the aesthetic, that's worth knowing.

The eraser caps are also genuinely small. They're fine for emergency fixes, but you'll want a quality standalone eraser for serious work. And while the lead reservoir is clever, refilling it requires unscrewing the barrel — doable, but not something you'll want to do mid-class.

## Who Should Buy This

Yes. It's standard 0.5mm HB lead, compatible with any other 0.5mm mechanical pencil you own. The color coding corresponds to lead hardness, not lead size.

**Q: How many pencils and lead refills are included?**

**Q: Are these good for drawing or just writing?**
They're fine for light sketching and drafting, but the HB lead is on the harder side. If you're doing detailed art, you'll want a dedicated drafting pencil with softer lead options.
