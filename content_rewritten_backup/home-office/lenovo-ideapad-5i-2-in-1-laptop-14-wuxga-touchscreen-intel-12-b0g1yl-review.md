**The 2-in-1 That Actually Gets Work Done**

Here's the thing about most budget-friendly 2-in-1 laptops: they make you pay for the flexibility with flimsy hinges, dim screens, or keyboards that feel like typing on a trampoline. The Lenovo IdeaPad 5i breaks that pattern. At a price point that undercuts most premium convertibles, it delivers the build quality and display clarity that make you forget you're not holding a ThinkPad.

**What You Actually Get**

Lenovo ships the IdeaPad 5i with the 65W USB-C charger and the usual documentation. The laptop itself weighs 3.3 pounds — not ultraportable, but perfectly reasonable for a bag carry. The 14-inch WUXGA touchscreen (1920x1200) uses a 16:10 ratio, which is the real selling point here. That extra vertical resolution means more spreadsheet rows, more of a webpage visible without scrolling, and less constant zooming in documents. It's one of those features you don't realize you need until you've used it for a week.

The build is where Lenovo earns its reputation. The aluminum lid and bottom cover feel solid — no creaking when you twist it, no flex in the keyboard deck. The hinge is firm enough to hold the screen at any angle but smooth to adjust one-handed. As shown in the product image, the design is clean and professional, with minimal branding. It won't win beauty contests, but it's a laptop you won't be embarrassed to pull out in a client meeting.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The 12th-gen Intel processor (depending on your config, you're looking at an i5-1235U or similar) handles everyday office tasks with ease. We're talking 15+ Chrome tabs, a Word document, Slack, and Spotify running simultaneously without stutter. The 16GB of RAM keeps things snappy. Integrated Iris Xe graphics won't game at high settings, but they'll handle 4K video playback and light photo editing fine.

The keyboard deserves specific mention — it has 1.5mm of travel, which is generous for a thin-and-light. Typing feels crisp with a satisfying bottom-out. The trackpad is glass and accurate, though it's on the smaller side. If you're coming from a MacBook, you'll notice the difference, but it's perfectly usable.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

Scrolling through the 235 Amazon ratings at 4.7 stars, a few themes emerge. One verified buyer wrote: "The screen is gorgeous for the price. I was expecting dim and washed out, but it gets plenty bright and colors pop." Another noted: "I've owned $1,500 laptops that felt cheaper than this. The aluminum body is a huge step up from the plastic competition."

Build quality and display clarity are the consistent winners here. Multiple reviewers mention using tablet mode for note-taking and presentations, with the touchscreen responding accurately to both finger and stylus input (note: the stylus is sold separately, and it's not the Lenovo Active Pen 2 — make sure you grab the right compatible one).

**Where It Falls Short**

Honesty requires me to flag the RAM situation. It's soldered — 16GB max, no upgrade path. For most users, 16GB is fine for the next 3-4 years. But if you're a heavy multitasker who runs virtual machines or does video editing, this laptop isn't for you. Buy for today's needs, because you can't future-proof this one.

Battery life is another tradeoff. Lenovo claims up to 12 hours, but real-world mixed use lands at 7-9 hours. That's a full workday if you're not hammering it, but don't expect to leave the charger at home on a travel day. The 65W USB-C charger is compact and fast, topping up to 80% in about an hour.

The speakers are serviceable but tinny at high volume. Headphones or external speakers are recommended for anything beyond casual YouTube viewing.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Office workers on a budget**: You get a professional-looking, solidly-built machine that handles spreadsheets, email, and video calls without breaking a sweat.
- **Students**: The 2-in-1 flexibility for note-taking and the 16:10 screen for research papers make this an excellent value.
- **Frequent travelers**: The light weight and USB-C charging (compatible with most phone chargers) are big wins.
- **Skip it if**: You need heavy processing power, plan to upgrade RAM later, or want all-day battery life beyond 9 hours.

**FAQ**

*Q: Does this laptop support stylus input?*
A: Yes, the touchscreen supports active pens, but the stylus is NOT included. Make sure to buy a compatible one — the Lenovo Active Pen 2 works well but verify compatibility with your specific config before purchasing.

*Q: Is the RAM upgradeable?*
A: No. The RAM is soldered to the motherboard. Buy the 16GB version if you buy this laptop at all — there's no adding more later.

*Q: How does the 2-in-1 hinge hold up over time?*
A: Lenovo's hinges are generally reliable, and the 360-degree mechanism here feels solid. No widespread complaints in the review data about hinge failure, which is a good sign.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if**: You want a well-built, versatile 2-in-1 with an excellent screen and don't need heavy processing power or future RAM upgrades.

**Skip it if**: You need more than 16GB of RAM, plan to game, or require 10+ hours of battery on the regular.

The Lenovo IdeaPad 5i 2-in-1 earns its 4.7-star rating honestly. It's not the flashiest laptop, but it nails the fundamentals — build, display, keyboard, and performance — at a price that makes sense. For the majority of office workers and students, this is the smart buy.
