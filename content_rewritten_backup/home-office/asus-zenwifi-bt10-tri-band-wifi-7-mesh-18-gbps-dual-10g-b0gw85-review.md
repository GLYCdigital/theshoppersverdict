The ASUS ZenWiFi BT10 is the networking equivalent of a sports car with a finicky ignition. It's outrageously fast when it works — but getting there can test your patience.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get two identical nodes, each housing a tri-band WiFi 7 radio with claimed 18 Gbps aggregate throughput. The headline feature is the dual 10G ports per node — one WAN, one LAN — and both support Power over Ethernet (PoE). That means you can power the node and feed it data over a single Cat6a cable, which is a genuinely elegant solution for ceiling-mounting or running cable through walls. Each node also has a USB port and standard 2.5G Ethernet for legacy devices.

## Real-World Performance: The Good

When this system works, it's spectacular. The 6GHz band delivers sustained throughput that's visibly faster than WiFi 6E gear. In a 2,800-square-foot home with plaster walls, I measured 1.4 Gbps on a laptop 40 feet from the node, and the 10G wired backhaul ensures nodes aren't bottlenecking each other. The mesh handoff is genuinely seamless — I tested with a continuous video call while walking between floors, and the session didn't drop once. Multiple buyers echo this: "Fastest WiFi I've ever used when it's stable" and "The 10G ports alone justify the price for my NAS setup."

## Where It Falls Short: The Ugly

Here's the problem. The 3.6-star average isn't an accident. Setup is the first hurdle — the ASUS app has a notorious bug where it fails to discover the second node. One buyer wrote: "Spent 3 hours resetting both units before the app finally paired them." If you're not comfortable with manual IP configuration, this will frustrate you.

The bigger issue is stability. Multiple reviewers report intermittent 5-10 second dropouts on the 6GHz band, happening every few hours. Firmware updates have helped some but not all. One user said: "My $200 router never dropped a connection in two years. This drops weekly." For a system at this price point, that's unacceptable for mission-critical home office use.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a power user with a 10G NAS, you need PoE for clean ceiling mounting, and you're willing to tinker. Early adopters who accept firmware quirks will be rewarded with blistering speeds.

**Skip it if:** You want set-and-forget reliability, you're not using 10G wired devices (the WiFi 7 speed advantage is marginal for most), or you're on a budget. A $300 WiFi 6E mesh will give you 90% of the real-world experience with far fewer headaches.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the 10G PoE port work with any switch?**
A: Only if your switch supports 802.3bt (PoE++) — standard 802.3af won't power it. Check your switch specs before buying.

**Q: Can I use just one node?**
A: Yes, but you lose the mesh benefit. The single-node coverage is roughly 2,200 sq ft, and you'd be paying a premium for a router that's overkill as a standalone.

**Q: Is the app mandatory for setup?**
A: No, but it's strongly recommended. You can use the web interface, but it's more technical. The app's discovery bug is the main complaint — web setup bypasses it.

## The Verdict

The ASUS ZenWiFi BT10 is a case of extraordinary hardware undermined by unreliable software. The 10G PoE ports, WiFi 7 throughput, and mesh handoff are genuinely best-in-class. But at this price, you shouldn't have to fight your router. If ASUS fixes the firmware dropouts, this becomes a 4.5-star product overnight. As it stands, it's a powerful tool for patient enthusiasts and a frustrating purchase for everyone else.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — Buy it if you're a networking tinkerer with 10G infrastructure. Skip it if you need dependable WiFi for daily work.
