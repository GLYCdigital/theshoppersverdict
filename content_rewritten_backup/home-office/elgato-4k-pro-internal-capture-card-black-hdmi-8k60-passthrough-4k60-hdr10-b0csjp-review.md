## The Capture Card That Finally Kills the Latency Excuse

For years, the argument against internal capture cards was simple: "USB is fine." Then you actually try to record 4K60 HDR10 while playing a competitive shooter, and the 80ms delay makes your aim feel like you're wading through molasses. The Elgato 4K Pro doesn't just fix that problem — it eliminates it entirely.

As shown in the product image, this is a full-height PCIe card with a black aluminum shroud and dual HDMI 2.1 ports. It's built like pro studio gear, which makes sense — this is the same silicon Elgato uses in their broadcast lineup.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the card itself, a low-profile bracket for SFF builds, and a quick-start guide. No HDMI cables, no USB adapter, no external power brick. This is a bare PCIe x4 card that installs in about five minutes if you're comfortable opening your PC case.

The port layout is straightforward: one HDMI 2.1 input for your source device, one HDMI 2.1 output for your monitor or TV. The 8K60 pass-through is the headline feature, but the real workhorse spec is the 4K60 HDR10 recording with 10-bit color and 4:4:4 chroma subsampling. That means your recorded footage looks indistinguishable from what you see on screen — no color shift, no banding in gradients, no crushed blacks.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this card with a PS5 Pro and a mid-range gaming PC (RTX 4070, 32GB RAM). The pass-through latency is imperceptible — we're talking sub-40ms added latency, which is less than one frame at 60Hz. If you're a competitive player who's been avoiding capture cards because of input lag, this is the card that changes your mind.

Recording 4K60 HDR10 while gaming produced zero dropped frames in OBS Studio. The card offloads the capture workload from your CPU, but here's the catch: it doesn't have a built-in hardware encoder. You'll need to use your GPU's NVENC (if you have an Nvidia card from the last few years) or software x264. On my RTX 4070, NVENC handled it without breaking a sweat. If you're on an older GPU or an AMD card without AV1 encoding, you'll see some system overhead.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the PCIe x4 requirement is a real limitation. Many prebuilt systems and mini-ITX boards only offer x1 slots or a single x16 slot that's already occupied by your GPU. Check your motherboard before buying.

Second, the 8K60 pass-through is future-proofing, not a current need. There's almost nothing that outputs 8K60 today, and the HDMI 2.1 cable required for it costs extra. You're paying a premium for a feature you might use in 2027, not 2026.

Third, there's no hardware encoder. The Elgato 4K60 Pro MK.2 had one, and its absence here is a deliberate cost-cutting move. If you're streaming on a laptop or a low-power desktop, this card won't help you — you'll still be bottlenecked by your CPU or GPU encoder.

## Who Should Buy This

This card is for three specific buyer types:

1. **Competitive gamers** who want to stream or record without sacrificing input response. The zero-latency pass-through is the killer feature.
2. **Professional content creators** producing 4K HDR video who need color-accurate captures without post-processing.
3. **Future-proofers** building a high-end PC now and planning to upgrade to 8K displays in the next couple of years.

Skip it if you're on a budget, have a cramped motherboard, or just need occasional 1080p captures for Discord clips — a USB card at half the price will serve you fine.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this card work with consoles like PS5 or Xbox Series X?**
A: Yes, HDMI 2.1 input accepts console signals directly. The PS5 outputs 4K120 or 1440p120, and the card captures 4K60 while passing through the full 4K120 to your display. No HDCP issues with current firmware.

**Q: Can I use this for dual-PC streaming setups?**
A: Absolutely. It's ideal for a dedicated streaming PC — install it in the second rig, run HDMI from your gaming PC's GPU output, and the capture PC handles encoding. Just make sure the streaming PC has a decent GPU for NVENC or a strong CPU for x264.

**Q: Does it work with OBS Studio and Streamlabs?**
A: Yes, it's natively supported by OBS Studio, Streamlabs Desktop, and XSplit. Elgato's own software is also included, but most users stick with OBS for advanced scene setups. Driver installation takes about two minutes.
