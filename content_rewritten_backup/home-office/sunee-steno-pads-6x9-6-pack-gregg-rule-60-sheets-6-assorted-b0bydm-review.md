Let me be blunt: most steno pads are an afterthought. You grab whatever's cheapest, scribble notes, and throw the thing away. The SUNEE 6x9 6-pack doesn't reinvent the wheel — but it fixes the small annoyances that make cheap pads infuriating. Here's what 235 Amazon reviewers (4.7 stars) and my own testing found.

**What you actually get**

Six spiral-top pads, each 6x9 inches with Gregg rule ruling — that's the narrow-spaced format with a vertical line down the center, standard for shorthand and structured note-taking. Each pad has 60 sheets of 16-pound paper. As shown in the product image, the covers come in six assorted colors: black, blue, green, purple, red, and yellow. No cardboard backing that bends on day one — these have a stiff chipboard base that actually supports writing on your knee or in a car.

**Where these pads earn their keep**

The paper is the star. At this price, I expected newsprint-level quality that bleeds through with a Pilot G2. Instead, the 16-lb stock handles ballpoint, gel, and even fountain pens with minimal ghosting. Bleed-through is rare unless you're pressing hard with a wet nib. One reviewer put it plainly: "I use these for court reporting notes and they hold up — the paper doesn't tear when I flip pages fast."

The binding is glued and taped at the top, not stapled. That matters more than you'd think. Stapled pads shed pages within a week. These stay intact even under left-handed writing, where your hand drags across the spiral. The stiff backboard also means you can write without a desk — a real plus for students in lecture halls or anyone taking notes on the go.

**Where it falls short**

The perforation is functional but not clean. If you tear pages out slowly and carefully, you're fine. If you're in a rush, you'll get jagged edges. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

At 60 sheets per pad, heavy users will run through one every two to three weeks. The 6-pack lasts a couple of months if you're taking daily notes. Buy two packs if you're a court reporter or journalist — you'll thank me later.

A few reviewers also noted the colors aren't as vivid as the product images suggest. They're muted pastels, not saturated tones. Minor, but if you were hoping for neon-level distinction, adjust expectations.

**Who should buy this**

- **Court reporters and transcriptionists** — the Gregg rule spacing is correct, and the paper takes rapid shorthand without snagging
- **Students** — the 6x9 size fits in most bags without folding, and color-coding by subject is genuinely useful
- **Journalists and interviewers** — the stiff backboard means you can write anywhere, and 60 sheets per pad covers a full day of interviews
- **Therapists, counselors, and HR pros** — the vertical line down the center naturally separates client notes from actions taken

**Skip it if** you need legal-size (8.5x14) pads, or if you prefer wide-ruled spacing — the Gregg rule is tighter than standard college rule.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these true Gregg rule or just narrow ruled?**  
A: Genuine Gregg rule — the line spacing is 3/8 inch with a vertical center line. Verified against other Gregg rule pads.

**Q: Will fountain pen ink bleed through?**  
A: With a fine or medium nib, no. With a broad nib or heavy pressure, you'll see slight ghosting but not bleed-through.

**Q: Do the pads lie flat when open?**  
A: Yes, the spiral top binding lets the pad fold back completely. The stiff backboard keeps it stable.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, no-nonsense steno pad that won't fall apart mid-note and handles multiple pen types without feathering. **Skip it if** you need perfect perforation or larger format.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The paper quality and build are genuinely impressive for the price. The perforation issue is the only real flaw, and it's minor. This is the steno pad I'd buy again — and I've already added a second 6-pack to my cart.
