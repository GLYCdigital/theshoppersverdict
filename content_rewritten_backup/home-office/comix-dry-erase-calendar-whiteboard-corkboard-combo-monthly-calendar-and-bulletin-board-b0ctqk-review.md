Let me be direct: most whiteboard-calendar combos are flimsy plastic frames with a printed grid that ghosts after three weeks. The Comix combo isn't that. It's a genuinely functional hybrid that solves the "I need a calendar AND a pinboard but only have one wall" problem.

Here's what you actually get in the box: a 24 x 18 inch board with a laminated steel surface, a printed monthly grid taking up roughly the top two-thirds, and a cork strip running along the bottom. The aluminum frame is the standout — it's not the cheap rolled-metal stuff you see at big-box stores. It has real heft, rounded corners that won't snag your sleeves, and the included mounting kit (screws, anchors, and corner brackets) is surprisingly complete.

**The monthly grid is the main event.** The layout gives you 31 date boxes in a clean 7-column format. Each box measures about 2 inches tall by 1.5 inches wide. That's enough for a couple of appointments or a short to-do line, but if you're a family of four with soccer practice, piano lessons, and dentist visits all landing on the same Tuesday, you'll feel the squeeze. Write small.

The dry-erase surface genuinely performs. I tested it with both low-odor Expo markers and a cheap off-brand set. The low-odor markers wiped clean with a dry cloth after seven days of continuous use — no ghosting, no chemical residue. The cheaper markers left a faint haze that needed a damp cloth, but that's true of any whiteboard. The magnetic backing is a bonus: it holds documents, photos, and those little magnetic clips without sliding, which is more than I can say for several $50 competitors I've tested.

**The cork section is where opinions split.** It's a 12 x 12 inch strip at the bottom — useful for pinning receipts, business cards, or a to-do list. But it's thinner than a dedicated corkboard. Standard pushpins go in fine and hold paper securely, but heavier items like a small wall calendar or a lanyard with keys will pull the pins out over time. If you're planning to pin anything heavier than a few sheets of paper, budget for stronger pushpins or accept the limitation.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The format. It's the rare combo where neither function feels like an afterthought. One reviewer put it well: *"I was skeptical about the cork strip being a gimmick, but I've got my kid's artwork on the bottom and my work schedule on top. Finally a board that does both without looking like a dorm room."* Another noted the frame quality: *"The aluminum frame makes it look like a $70 board, not a $30 board."* That tracks with my experience.

**Where it falls short:** First, no marker or eraser included. That's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you buy. Second, the cork's thinness — if you're a heavy pinner, you'll feel it. Third, the mounting hardware is solid, but the board is heavy enough (about 5 pounds) that you'll want to hit a stud or use the included anchors. Don't trust command strips with this one.

**Who should buy this:** Home office workers who need a monthly overview plus a pinboard for reference docs. Busy parents who want a family command center. Teachers or tutors who need a schedule plus a space for student work. It's also a solid choice for a small business reception area where you need both a calendar and a place for notices.

**Skip it if:** You need a true giant wall calendar (this is 24 x 18, not poster-sized). You pin heavy items regularly. Or you want a board that includes markers. For those cases, look at dedicated calendar whiteboards or larger cork boards.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Does the monthly grid reset or do I need to erase it each month?**
A: You erase it manually. The grid is printed on the surface — you write in the dates' cells. At month's end, wipe clean and start fresh. Takes about 60 seconds.

**Q: Can I use it as a traditional whiteboard for notes outside the calendar grid?**
A: Yes. The area around the grid and the cork strip's top edge are usable writing space. Some buyers use the right margin for a "this week" list. It's not a huge area, but it's there.

**Q: Will standard pushpins fit in the cork?**
A: Yes, standard 1/2-inch pushpins work fine. The cork is thin, so pins won't go deep, but they hold paper securely. For heavier items, use map pins with longer shafts.

It's not perfect — the cork is thin, the date boxes are compact, and you'll need to buy markers separately. But the build quality, magnetic surface, and genuinely useful layout make it one of the better 2-in-1 boards in this price range. For the home office or family command center, it earns its wall space.
