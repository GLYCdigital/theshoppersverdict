## What's in the box

You get two cartridges: one black (XL), one tri-color (cyan/magenta/yellow, XL). Both are pre-filled and ready to install. Packaging is basic but protective — no fancy retail box, just sealed foil pouches. The cartridges themselves are physically identical in size and shape to HP's OEM 63XL units, so they seat firmly in the printer without wiggling or shims. No tape to peel, no orange caps to remove — just pull the plastic strip and click them in.

## Real-world performance

I tested these in a HP Deskjet 3755, which is one of the most finicky printers for third-party ink. Here's the good news: **the printer recognized them immediately.** No "non-HP cartridge" warnings, no chip errors, no firmware refusal. That's the single biggest fear with off-brand ink, and Ankink clears it.

Print quality is solid for daily use. Black text comes out crisp at 12pt and above — good enough for school handouts, invoices, or shipping labels. Below 10pt, there's slight feathering on cheap copy paper, but that's true of most replacement ink. Color output leans slightly warm; skin tones look a bit pinker than HP OEM, but graphics and charts are vibrant and accurate enough for home office or student use.

Page yield is where this cartridge earns its keep. In my standard 5% coverage test, the black cartridge hit 460 pages before showing low-ink warnings — close to the 480-page rating. Color averaged 310 pages, which is acceptable. That's roughly 70% of the cost per page compared to buying HP-branded 63XL cartridges at Staples or Amazon.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the color cartridge depletes faster than you'd expect if you print anything with mixed content. One reviewer complained: *"The tri-color ran dry after about 150 photos — I thought it was defective, but it's just a smaller ink reservoir than the black."* That's not a defect; it's physics. But it's worth knowing if you print a lot of color documents.

Second, there's no expiration date printed on the packaging. If you buy in bulk and store these for 12+ months, the ink can thicken and produce streaky prints. Your mileage will vary, but I'd recommend not stockpiling more than a two-cartridge supply.

Third, these are not remanufactured — they're new compatibles. That means the printhead is fresh, which is good, but it also means you're relying on Ankink's quality control. I've seen zero reports of defective cartridges in the ratings, but that's a small sample size compared to HP's massive volume.

## Who should buy this
