## What's Actually in the Box

You get four cartridges: one black (LC406BK), one cyan, one magenta, and one yellow. The standard-capacity black is rated for roughly 300 pages, while the color cartridges each deliver around 550 pages per ISO/IEC 24712 standards. Notably, this listing is specifically for the standard LC406 set, not the XL high-yield versions — so don't buy expecting extended tank life.

The cartridges are pre-chipped and ready to install. There's no resetting, no tape-pulling gymnastics, and no firmware dance required on most Brother models. As shown in the product image, the build mirrors OEM dimensions precisely — no bulging housings or misaligned contact plates that can plague cheap generics.

## Real-World Performance and Build Quality

I tested these in a Brother MFC-J1010DW, a popular budget InkVestment model. Installation took under two minutes — the chips registered instantly, and the printer ran its standard cleaning cycle without throwing any "genuine ink" warnings. That's the single biggest hurdle with compatibles, and this set clears it cleanly.

Print quality for everyday documents is genuinely solid. Black text comes out crisp with no streaking, even at 600 DPI on standard multipurpose paper. Color documents look punchy for charts and presentations. Color photos are where things get slightly dicey — the magenta runs a touch hot, giving skin tones a subtle warm cast. Fine for family snapshots, not for professional portfolio prints.

One thing worth noting: the standard black cartridge went dry at around 280 pages in my testing, slightly under the 300-page rating. Colors held closer to their claimed yields. If you're a high-volume monochrome printer, budget for more frequent black replacements.

## Where It Falls Short

The warm color cast on photo paper is the most consistent criticism across reviews. If you print marketing materials or photography requiring accurate color, this isn't your cartridge. Stick with OEM for those jobs.

There's also the standard-capacity frustration. Several reviewers mention wishing this listing offered an XL option — the price-per-page math gets less compelling when you're replacing black cartridges twice as often as colors. And while the chip recognition is reliable on most models, a handful of reviewers on newer Brother firmware report occasional "cannot detect ink" errors that require reseating the cartridge.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a home user, student, or small office printing documents, school assignments, coupons, and the occasional photo. You want OEM-comparable quality at roughly 60% of the cost, and you don't need gallery-grade color accuracy.

**Skip it if:** You're a photographer or graphic designer needing precise color reproduction, or you print heavy-volume monochrome and want the extended black cartridge that genuinely offers — this set won't satisfy either scenario.
