The ASUS Vivobook S 15 OLED is a laptop that makes you do a double-take — first at the price, then at the screen. It packs a 15.6-inch 3K OLED panel, an Intel Core Ultra 7 155H, and 24GB of RAM into a chassis that often sells for under $1,000. That's an impressive spec sheet on paper. But the 3.2-star average across 160 Amazon ratings tells a more complicated story. Let's dig into what's actually good, what's not, and whether this is the right machine for your money.

**What's in the box**

You get the laptop itself, a 90W USB-C charger, and the usual paperwork. No dongle, no sleeve, no stylus. The port selection is genuinely useful: two Thunderbolt 4 ports, one USB-A 3.2, one HDMI 2.1, and a microSD card reader. That's enough for most desk setups without a hub. The 180-degree lay-flat hinge is a nice touch for presenting or sharing screens in meetings.

**Performance and real-world use**

The Core Ultra 7 155H with 24GB of LPDDR5X RAM is a solid mid-range workhorse. In my testing, it handled a typical office workload — 25+ browser tabs, Slack, Spotify, and a Google Meet call — without breaking a sweat. Light 1080p video editing in DaVinci Resolve was usable, though exports took noticeably longer than a dedicated GPU laptop would. The 1TB NVMe SSD is fast and spacious enough for most users.

**The screen: the star of the show**

This is where the Vivobook earns its keep. The 15.6-inch 3K (2880x1620) OLED panel is stunning. Colors pop, blacks are truly black, and the 120Hz refresh rate makes scrolling feel silky smooth. For photo editing, spreadsheets, or binge-watching Netflix, this display is a genuine pleasure. Brightness maxes out at around 600 nits, which is plenty for indoor use and even readable outdoors in shade.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Reading through the 160 Amazon reviews, the display gets near-universal love. One verified buyer wrote: *"The screen alone is worth it. Coming from a MacBook Air, the colors on this OLED are noticeably richer and deeper."* Another noted: *"Build quality surprised me — the all-metal chassis feels like a $1,500 machine, not a budget laptop."* The keyboard also gets consistent praise for its comfortable travel and quiet, tactile feedback.

**Where it falls short**

Here's the uncomfortable part. The battery life is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. ASUS claims up to 15 hours, but real-world usage with the OLED at 70% brightness and mixed browsing/streaming nets closer to 5–6 hours. That's a full workday if you're careful, but not if you're doing anything demanding. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"Battery is a joke. I got 4.5 hours on a full charge with normal use."*

Fan noise is the second recurring gripe. Under sustained load — video calls plus heavy multitasking — the fans spin up with a noticeable whine. It's not deafening, but it's audible in a quiet office. The deck also warms up near the function keys, which gets uncomfortable during long typing sessions.

The most concerning issue, though, is OLED uniformity. A handful of reviewers reported visible banding and a faint pink tint on gray backgrounds. This is a known OLED lottery — some panels are perfect, others aren't. ASUS's quality control seems inconsistent on this front. If you're sensitive to display imperfections, buy from a retailer with a good return policy.

**Who should buy this**

- **Creative professionals on a budget**: If you edit photos, design graphics, or work with color-critical content, this OLED is a steal at this price point.
- **Students and remote workers**: The lightweight 3.3-pound chassis and excellent keyboard make it great for long writing sessions.
- **Anyone who prioritizes display over battery**: If you're always near an outlet, the short battery life is less of a dealbreaker.

**Skip it if**

- You need all-day battery for travel or field work.
- You're sensitive to fan noise in quiet environments.
- You're doing heavy video editing or 3D rendering — the integrated Arc graphics won't cut it.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the ASUS Vivobook S 15 OLED good for gaming?**
A: No. The integrated Intel Arc graphics can handle light titles at low settings, but this is not a gaming laptop. Expect playable frame rates only on esports titles like CS2 or League at 720p.

**Q: Does it have a backlit keyboard?**
A: Yes, with adjustable brightness levels. The backlight is even and doesn't bleed through the keycaps.

**Q: Can I upgrade the RAM or storage?**
A: The RAM is soldered and non-upgradeable, but the M.2 SSD is user-replaceable. If you think you'll need more than 24GB, look elsewhere.

**The Verdict**

The ASUS Vivobook S 15 OLED is a tale of two products. The display and build quality are genuinely excellent for the price, and the performance handles everyday work with ease. But the disappointing battery life, inconsistent fan behavior, and occasional panel quality issues hold it back from greatness. At a 3.2-star average, the reviews reflect this split — many buyers love it, a significant minority got a lemon.

**Buy it if** you want a stunning OLED display for creative work and can live with mediocre battery life. **Skip it if** you need all-day portability or demand flawless quality control.

**Rating: 3.3/5** — A great value display wrapped in a laptop with compromises you should know about before you click buy.
