**Is the Brother TZe-241 6-pack worth $98.90?** That’s the first question any smart shopper asks when they see that price tag. The answer depends entirely on how many labels you actually print. If you run a warehouse, a clinic, or a home office that labels everything from surge protectors to spice jars, this six-roll bundle is the cheapest per-millimeter way to feed a P-Touch. If you need one label a month for a fuse box, you’re overpaying for convenience.

**What you actually get:** Six sealed cartridges of Brother’s laminated black-on-white tape. Each spool holds 26 feet (8 meters) of 0.47-inch (12mm) tape. The laminated layer is the star here — it’s a clear polyester film bonded over the printed text, which is why these labels shrug off spilled coffee, garage grease, and three years of outdoor sun without turning into illegible smudges. The cartridges are drop-in compatible with any TZe-series P-Touch (models like the PT-D210, PT-P710BT, and PT-H110 all take them). No adapters, no firmware tricks, no fiddling.

**Real-world performance:** I’ve run these through a PT-D410 and the print quality is sharp down to 8-point font — legible on a breaker panel from arm’s length. The adhesive is the same industrial-grade acrylic Brother uses across the TZe line: it sticks to textured plastic, powder-coated steel, and even lightly dusty surfaces, though I’d still wipe down any surface with isopropyl first for permanent jobs. One buyer noted, “I’ve had labels on chemical bottles in a lab for two years — no peeling, no fading.” Another mentioned, “The lamination is the difference; cheap tape curls at the edges in a month. These don’t.”

**Where it stumbles:** The 12mm width is the real limitation. It’s perfect for file folders, equipment panels, and drawer fronts, but it’s too narrow for wrapping around Ethernet cables (you’ll need the 24mm TZe-261 for that) and too skinny for labeling the front of a storage bin. Also, the price. At $16.48 per roll, you’re paying a premium for the Brother name. Third-party tape runs $6–8 per roll on Amazon, but buyer reviews on those show a 50/50 split between “works fine” and “ink faded in six months.” If you need labels to last, genuine tape is the insurance policy.

**Who should buy this:**  
- **Facilities managers** who label servers, patch panels, and safety equipment — the lamination saves re-labeling labor.  
- **Small businesses** (salons, auto shops, clinics) that burn through a roll every few weeks.  
- **P-Touch power users** who already own the printer and want bulk pricing without subscription games.  

**Skip it if:** You only label Christmas storage boxes once a year. Buy a single roll for $12–15 and call it done. Also skip if you need wide labels — this won’t serve that purpose.

**FAQ:**  
**“Does this fit my old P-Touch PT-1280?”**  
Yes. Any P-Touch that accepts TZe-series tape works with these. Check the printer’s manual or the tape door — if it says “TZe” on the cartridge slot, you’re good.

**“Is the lamination worth the extra cost over generic tape?”**  
For permanent labels, absolutely. The polyester layer prevents the ink from scratching off and blocks UV fading. For temporary labels on bins you’ll relabel next quarter, generic is fine.

**“Can I use this tape for wire labeling?”**  
Not well. The 12mm width wraps awkwardly around cables thinner than a USB cord. Get the 18mm or 24mm version for that job.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you need durable, long-term labels in volume and your printer is a TZe-compatible P-Touch. The cost-per-roll works out to roughly $0.63 per foot of laminated tape — competitive for genuine Brother quality.  
**Skip it if** your labeling needs are sporadic, you need wider tape, or you’re willing to gamble on cheaper third-party cartridges for non-critical labels.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — the product is genuinely excellent; the price and narrow width keep it from a perfect score.
