If you run an auto shop, fabrication shop, or any workspace where paper work orders get shuffled, dropped, and lost, the Epakh 30-pack of 9x12 shop ticket holders is the kind of simple fix that pays for itself in a week. These aren't fancy—they're clear vinyl pouches with magnets—but they solve a real problem: keeping job cards visible and attached to the thing they belong to.

## What You Actually Get

Thirty individual ticket holders, each sized at 9x12 inches. That's the full letter-size paper footprint, so standard work orders slide in without folding. The vinyl is 0.2mm thick—stiff enough to hold its shape but flexible enough to survive being bumped against toolboxes and equipment. Each pouch has a magnet strip across the back, not just a corner magnet. That full-width strip is what makes these hold tight on vertical surfaces.

The open-top design is the smartest part. There are no flaps, no zippers, no Velcro. You drop the paper in and it stays because the vinyl grips it. When the job's done, you pull it out and the next one goes in. That's it.

## Real-World Performance

The magnets are the headline feature, and they earn their keep. On steel toolboxes, machine housings, and shop carts, these stay put. One buyer noted, "I stuck these on the side of my Snap-On box and they haven't budged in three months of daily use." That's the experience most people report—set it and forget it.

The vinyl clarity matters more than you'd think. When a work order is in the holder, you need to read it from a couple feet away without pulling it out. These stay transparent enough for that, though they do show smudges. Another reviewer mentioned, "I can read the job notes without opening the pouch, which saves time when I'm grabbing parts."

The 30-count quantity is genuinely useful. Shops with multiple bays can put a holder on every tool cart, workbench, and machine. I counted—that's enough for a 6-bay shop with spares for the office.

## Where They Fall Short

The magnets are strong—almost too strong. On thin sheet metal, repositioning the holder can leave scuff marks on painted surfaces. If you're sticking these on a freshly painted cabinet, place them once and leave them. One customer reported, "The magnet is so strong it pulled the holder off the door and the vinyl tore at the corner." That's the exception, not the rule, but it's worth knowing.

The 9x12 size is exact. If your work orders are multi-page packets or you use those thick card-stock job jackets, these won't fit comfortably. Standard single sheets are fine; anything bulky will fight you.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Auto repair shops** — stick them on toolboxes, lift posts, and bay doors
- **Fabrication and machine shops** — job travelers stay with the workpiece
- **Warehouse managers** — pick lists and shipping docs stay visible on racks
- **HVAC or field service offices** — organize paper intake before dispatch

Skip them if you need fully sealed, weatherproof document protection—these are indoor tools, not outdoor enclosures.

## FAQ

**Will these hold on painted or coated steel?**
Yes, but the magnet is strong enough to mark softer finishes. Test on an inconspicuous spot first, or stick them once and don't move them.

**Can I fit a double-sided work order?**
Yes, but the vinyl is snug on 9x12. If you need to read both sides frequently, you'll be pulling the paper out more often than you'd like.

**Do the magnets lose strength over time?**
No. The strip magnets are ceramic-based and hold their pull indefinitely. The vinyl will wear before the magnets do.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a simple, durable way to keep paper visible and attached in a busy workspace. The 30-pack covers a whole shop, the magnets hold, and the price per unit is hard to beat.

**Skip it if** you need document protection from weather or chemicals, or if your work orders come as thick multi-page packets.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid, functional, and appropriately priced for what it delivers. The magnet strength is the only real caveat, and for most users, it's a feature, not a bug.
