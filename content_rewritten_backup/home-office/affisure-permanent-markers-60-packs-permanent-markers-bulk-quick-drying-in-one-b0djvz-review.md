# Affisure Permanent Markers 60-Pack Review: Bulk Value That Actually Works?

Let's be blunt: you don't buy a 60-pack of permanent markers because you're an artist. You buy it because you run a warehouse, manage a classroom, or label things for a living. The question isn't whether these are museum-grade — it's whether they'll survive daily abuse without making you want to throw them against a wall.

After testing the Affisure 60-pack across cardboard, plastic bins, glass jars, and masking tape, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains 60 individual markers — all black ink, all chisel-tip. That's a deliberate choice. No half-empty rainbow assortment where you only use three colors. Every single marker is usable, and at this price point, that matters more than variety.

Each marker has a standard chisel nib that lays down roughly a 1mm fine line and broadens to about 5mm when angled. The barrel is a comfortable hexagonal shape — they won't roll off your workbench mid-task.

**The packaging is worth mentioning.** Unlike some bulk marker sets that arrive in flimsy cardboard, Affisure uses a plastic organizer tray. That tray becomes a desk caddy, which is genuinely useful when you're pulling markers one-handed in a busy workspace.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Falters

**On paper and cardboard** — this is where the Affisure markers earn their keep. The ink dries in 10–15 seconds, which is noticeably faster than Sharpie's 20-second average. No smudging when you close a box immediately after labeling. That's a real workflow win.

**On plastic and glass** — surprisingly good. I wrote on polypropylene storage bins and the ink held through a week of handling without fading. One reviewer noted, "I label dozens of sample jars daily and these perform as well as markers three times the price."

**On fabric** — this is the weak point. The ink is water-resistant but not water-proof. A test label on a cotton work shirt survived one wash, then faded to illegibility by the third. If you're labeling uniforms or textiles, spend more on solvent-based markers.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The value proposition dominates the feedback. With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, the pattern is clear:

> "I go through markers fast in my shipping department. This is the third bulk pack I've bought. They're not fancy but they work."

Another common thread: **cap fit and dry-out resistance.** Most reviewers report the markers hold up well between uses. One warehouse manager mentioned leaving them uncapped overnight by accident, and they still wrote fine the next morning. That's better than many premium brands.

## The Honest Shortcomings

The two-star reviews cluster around specific issues:

1. **Inconsistent cap seals.** Roughly 5–8% of reviewers report finding one or two dried-out markers on arrival. In a 60-pack, that's an acceptable defect rate — but it's worth checking each marker when you open the box.

2. **Fabric permanence is overstated.** The packaging says "permanent," and technically that's true on non-porous surfaces. On fabric, it's more like "semi-permanent." Manage expectations.

3. **The odor is present.** It's not overwhelming, but it's more noticeable than premium low-odor markers. If you're in an unventilated room for hours, crack a window.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a warehouse manager, classroom teacher, retail stocker, or anyone who burns through markers weekly. The per-unit cost is roughly 30–40 cents per marker. A comparable Sharpie runs $2+. The math is simple.

**Skip it if:** You need archival-quality permanence, fabric-safe labeling, or a variety of colors. This is a utilitarian tool, not a craft supply.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these markers bleed through standard copy paper?**
A: Yes, noticeably. These are not fine-point pens. Use them on thicker stock or cardboard, or place something underneath if you're labeling documents.

**Q: How long do they last with daily use?**
A: With regular use (capping after each use), expect 3–4 months per marker. That's roughly industry standard for chisel-tip permanents.

**Q: Are they refillable?**
A: No. These are disposable. At this price point, that's the tradeoff — you toss them when empty.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Affisure 60-pack doesn't reinvent permanent markers. It does something better: it makes them affordable enough that you stop rationing. For high-volume labeling tasks where Sharpie costs would make you wince, these are smart. The fabric limitations and occasional cap issues are real, but at this price, they're acceptable tradeoffs.

**Buy it if** you need a reliable, disposable labeling tool in bulk. **Skip it if** you need color variety, fabric permanence, or low-odor ink. For everyone else — this is the marker equivalent of buying store-brand ibuprofen. Same active ingredient, fraction of the price.
