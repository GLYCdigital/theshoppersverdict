Let's be honest: most cardstock is either too flimsy to hold up or so thick it jams your printer. The Neenah Paper Exact Index Card Stock sits in that sweet spot — 110 lb index weight means it's roughly 199 GSM, noticeably stiffer than standard 65 lb cardstock but still flexible enough for most home and office printers. If you've ever printed a business card and watched it bend into a sad little taco, this paper is the fix.

### What You're Actually Getting

The package contains 250 sheets of 8.5" x 11" cardstock, cut to standard letter size. The 110 lb index classification translates to a caliper of about 0.0075 inches — that's thicker than a standard greeting card but not quite as rigid as 14-point cover stock. The surface is a bright white (around 94-96 brightness) with a smooth, uncoated finish. You can expect a crisp, matte look that takes inkjet ink well without smearing, and it feeds through laser printers without curling issues.

### Real-World Performance

I tested this with both a Brother laser printer and an Epson EcoTank inkjet. Laser output was sharp with zero ghosting — text looked like it came off a commercial press. Inkjet performance was slightly slower to dry (uncoated paper absorbs ink), but colors stayed vivid without bleeding into the fibers. The stiffness is the standout feature: a folded greeting card made from this stock holds its crease cleanly without cracking, and a business card printed on this won't fold in your wallet unless you're actively trying to destroy it.

### What Buyers Keep Praising

Scrolling through the 229 ratings, a pattern emerges: people are using this for things that require durability, not just printing. One reviewer noted, "I use this for my Etsy shop's hang tags and it holds up beautifully through shipping." Another mentioned, "Perfect for tabletop signs at events — doesn't flop over like the cheap stuff." The consistency of the cut and the absence of jamming in standard printers gets repeated mention. For crafters and small business owners, the value proposition is clear: you're paying for paper that won't embarrass you in front of a client.

### Where It Falls Short

Let's not pretend this is perfect. The uncoated surface means it's not ideal for high-end photo reproduction — glossy images will look flat. It's also not acid-free, so if you're making archival-quality scrapbooks or fine art prints, you'll want a cotton rag paper instead. The 250-sheet count is generous, but the ream is thicker than standard paper, so check your printer's paper path capacity — some front-loading tray printers will only fit about 100 sheets before the stack gets too tall. And while it's stiffer than typical cardstock, it's still not suitable for rigid packaging projects like boxes or stands.

### Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a small business owner printing business cards, product tags, or signage. You're a crafter making greeting cards, invitations, or scrapbook bases. You're a teacher printing flashcards or classroom displays that need to survive little hands. Anyone who needs reliable, everyday heavy paper that won't jam.

**Skip it if:** You need photo-grade glossy output, you're doing archival work requiring acid-free materials, or you need rigid board stock for packaging prototypes. Professionals needing exact color matching should also look at coated stocks.

### FAQ

**Q: Will this work in my home inkjet printer?**
A: Yes, most standard inkjets handle this weight fine. Just switch your paper type setting to "cardstock" or "heavy paper" so the printer adjusts its feed speed. Expect slightly longer drying time than copy paper.

**Q: Can I run this through a Cricut or Silhouette cutting machine?**
A: Absolutely. The 110 lb index weight cuts cleanly with the standard blade on medium pressure. It's thick enough to make sturdy tags but not so thick it dulls your blade prematurely.

**Q: Is this the same as Neenah's Astrobrights line?**
A: No. Exact is their standard business-grade line — bright white, smooth, no specialty textures. Astrobrights is the colored version. If you want the same weight in colors, look for Neenah Exact in their pastel or bright collections.

### The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Neenah Exact Index Card Stock delivers exactly what it promises: dependable, stiff, print-friendly paper at a fair price. It's not flashy, it won't wow you with texture or archival longevity, but it will make your projects look professional without destroying your printer. For everyday heavy paper needs — business cards, invitations, signs, tags — this is the one. The half-star deduction is for the uncoated surface limiting photo quality and the lack of acid-free formulation for archival use. If those don't matter to you, this is a near-perfect purchase.
