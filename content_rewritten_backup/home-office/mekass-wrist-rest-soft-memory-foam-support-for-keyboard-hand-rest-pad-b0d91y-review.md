Here's the thing nobody tells you about wrist rests: most of them are just squishy lies. They flatten in six weeks, slide across your desk, or sit so tall they actually crank your wrists into a worse angle than no support at all. The MEKASS Memory Foam Wrist Rest is not that. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, it's quietly become one of the most dependable budget ergonomic accessories on Amazon — and for once, the hype is earned.

## What You Actually Get

The package is refreshingly simple: one wrist rest, no gimmicks. As shown in the product image, it's a contoured memory foam pad with a smooth fabric top and a textured rubber base. The dimensions are compact — roughly 17.5 inches long, 3.5 inches deep, and under an inch thick — which keeps it low-profile enough to sit flush with most keyboards. The construction is solid: dense foam wrapped in a breathable polyester blend that doesn't pill or snag, even against rough desk edges.

## Real-World Performance

I've been hammering on this thing for weeks, and the difference from cheaper gel rests is night and day. The memory foam rebounds slowly, cradling your palms without that "sinking into quicksand" feeling that makes your hands feel unstable mid-keystroke. The height is the real winner here — at roughly 0.8 inches, it aligns your wrists neutrally with standard keyboards, which is exactly what ergonomists recommend. The non-slip base is genuinely non-slip; I've shoved it across my desk during frustrated deadline sprints, and it stays put.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The recurring theme in the 229 reviews is durability. One buyer noted, *"I've had this for four months now and it still feels brand new — my old gel rest was flat by now."* Another praised the firmness: *"It's soft but supportive, not just a squishy pillow. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. First, the fabric cover is permanently attached — no zipper, no removable sleeve. When coffee drips or dust accumulates, you're stuck with spot cleaning and hoping it dries without staining. Second, the width is tailored for standard keyboards. If you're using a full-size board with a media cluster or a 96% layout, your wrists may hang off the ends. It's a minor gripe, but worth noting if you've got a sprawling setup. Finally, the memory foam runs slightly firm out of the box — give it a few days to break in.
