Let me be upfront: the Lenovo V15 Gen 4 is not a flashy laptop. It won't turn heads at a coffee shop, and it's not going to render 4K video. But if your job is spreadsheets, email, video calls, and the occasional 20-tab browser session, this machine punches well above its price tag. With 210 Amazon ratings averaging 4.2 stars, the consensus is clear: this is a dependable workhorse that gets the fundamentals right.

## What's actually in the box

The V15 Gen 4 ships with the laptop, a 65W USB-C power adapter, and the standard documentation nobody reads. No stylus, no sleeve, no included mouse — just the machine. That's fine; you're paying for the hardware, not accessories you'll toss in a drawer.

## Build quality and real-world use

The chassis is all-plastic, and at 3.74 pounds with a 0.74-inch profile, it's portable without being ultrabook-thin. The lid flexes more than I'd like — open it one-handed and you'll see it bend — but the base deck and keyboard tray hold up well to daily typing abuse. The 180-degree hinge is a genuine business feature; you can lay it flat for quick screen sharing with a colleague.

The 15.6-inch FHD (1920x1080) anti-glare panel is the star here. At 250 nits it's not the brightest on the market, but the matte finish kills reflections in fluorescent office lighting. Text stays sharp, and the 16:9 aspect ratio gives you plenty of vertical space for documents. Colors are slightly washed out — this is a 45% NTSC panel, not a creator display — but for business use it's perfectly adequate.

Performance depends on which Intel Core processor you pick. The base models run 13th-gen Core i3 or i5 chips, and for typical office workloads — Microsoft Office, Slack, Zoom, web apps — the i5 variant handles it all without breaking a sweat. The 8GB base RAM is workable but tight; I'd strongly recommend the 16GB configuration since it's soldered on the base models. The 512GB NVMe SSD boots Windows 11 in under 15 seconds, and app launches feel snappy.

Battery life lands around 7-8 hours of mixed use with the 45Wh cell. That's not all-day territory, but it'll get you through a standard shift if you're not hammering video calls the whole time. The 65W USB-C charger tops it up from empty to 80% in about an hour.

## What buyers consistently praise

Scrolling through the ratings, a few themes dominate. First, the keyboard. One buyer noted, "The tactile feedback is excellent — I type 80 WPM and never bottom out." The full-size layout with dedicated Page Up/Down keys is a godsend for anyone who edits long documents. Second, the display gets repeated shout-outs: "The anti-glare screen is perfect for my sunlit home office." Third, upgradability. Multiple reviewers mention popping the bottom panel off to add a second drive or swap RAM — a dying art in modern laptops.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The speakers are weak — 2x1.5W drivers that get muffled at high volume. Fine for a quick Zoom call, frustrating for anything else. The webcam is 720p and mediocre in low light; you'll want external lighting for professional video meetings. And the lid flex I mentioned earlier is a real quality concern if you're rough on gear.

There's also a pre-installed McAfee trial that nags you constantly. Budget 10 minutes to uninstall it on day one.

## Who should buy this

This is for the office worker, the remote employee, the student who needs a reliable machine for research and writing. It's also a solid choice for small business owners who want a warranty-backed workhorse (Lenovo offers a standard 1-year depot warranty) without paying ThinkPad premiums. Skip it if you're a creative professional needing color accuracy, a gamer, or someone who prioritizes premium build materials.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I upgrade the RAM and storage later?**
A: Yes. The V15 Gen 4 has two SODIMM slots (8GB soldered on base models, one free slot) and a 2.5-inch SATA bay alongside the M.2 SSD slot. You can add up to 32GB total RAM and a second drive.

**Q: Does it have a USB-C charging port?**
A: Yes, the included 65W adapter uses USB-C. It also supports DisplayPort over USB-C for external monitors.

**Q: Is the display good for outdoor use?**
A: The 250-nit anti-glare panel is usable in bright daylight but not ideal. Indoors near windows, it's great. Direct sun will wash it out.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, upgradeable business laptop for daily office tasks and value the anti-glare display and excellent keyboard. **Skip it if** you demand premium build quality, strong speakers, or a brighter panel for creative work.

The Lenovo V15 Gen 4 earns its 4.2-star rating through sheer competence. It's not exciting, but it's honest — a tool that does its job without drama. For the price, that's exactly what most business users need.
