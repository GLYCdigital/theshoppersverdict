If you're running a whole-home network or wiring a small office, buying cable by the box is the only sane approach. The Syston Cable Technology Cat 6A+ Ethernet Network Cable comes in a 700-meter (2,297-foot) pull box — enough to wire a typical 3-4 bedroom house with runs to spare. After digging through 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, here's the honest breakdown for anyone about to spend a full afternoon pulling cable.

**What's in the Box**

You get one continuous 700-meter spool of Cat 6A+ cable. The key specs matter here: 23AWG solid bare copper conductors (not CCA), a 550MHz bandwidth rating, and a CMR riser-rated PVC jacket. The cable is also ETL verified and meets TIA-568-C.2 standards. The spool itself is solid but plain — no integrated payout system, so plan to build a simple PVC pipe dispenser or use a helper to feed the cable as you pull.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The 23AWG solid copper is the headline feature. Many budget cables use copper-clad aluminum to cut costs, but CCA fails PoE (Power over Ethernet) applications and degrades signal integrity over distance. Syston uses genuine bare copper, which matters for both 10GbE performance and long-term reliability with powered devices like cameras and access points.

As shown in the product image, the jacket is a standard blue PVC riser rated for in-wall installation. The 550MHz rating exceeds the 500MHz minimum for Cat 6A, and while you won't notice the difference in most home setups, it's insurance for future 25GbE or high-density environments. Buyers consistently confirm it terminates cleanly into keystone jacks and patch panels. One reviewer noted, "Terminated all 24 runs and every single one passed my Fluke tester at Cat 6A spec." That's the kind of real-world verification that matters.

**Where It Falls Short**

The stiffness of solid 23AWG is the tradeoff for performance. If you're used to handling stranded patch cables, this will feel like coax. Bending it around tight corners requires care — keep the bend radius generous or you'll kink the pairs. The weight is also a factor; at roughly 40+ pounds, the box is manageable but not fun to carry up stairs. Several buyers mentioned needing a second person or a proper cable dispenser to avoid frustrating tangles during payout.

**Buyer Sentiment**

The praise is consistent: "Heavy duty, quality cable at a fair price" appears across reviews. Another buyer highlighted the copper purity, saying "You can tell it's real copper when you strip it — no silver-colored aluminum underneath." The occasional negative reviews center on delivery damage (the box arriving crushed) or user error in termination, not manufacturing defects.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Homeowners doing a full network retrofit** — one box covers an entire house with runs to spare
- **Small business owners or IT pros** pulling cable for offices, warehouses, or retail spaces
- **DIY enthusiasts** who want future-proof 10GbE capability without paying commercial installer markup

Skip it if you only need a single 50-foot run — buy a pre-terminated patch cable instead. And if you're a first-timer who's never terminated solid-core cable, budget time to practice on a few scrap pieces before your real runs.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this cable suitable for outdoor use?**
A: No. The CMR riser rating is for indoor vertical and in-wall applications only. For outdoor runs, you need a CMX or burial-rated cable with UV protection.

**Q: Can I use this for PoE devices like security cameras?**
A: Yes, and it's actually ideal. The 23AWG solid copper conductors handle the current draw of PoE+ (up to 30W) without the voltage drop issues common with thinner or CCA cables.

**Q: Will this cable support 10GbE at full length?**
A: Yes, up to 100 meters (328 feet) per the Cat 6A spec. The 550MHz rating gives you margin beyond the minimum requirement.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're wiring a home or office and want professional-grade cable that will pass certification tests and last decades. The genuine copper construction and 550MHz rating make this a legitimate value for serious network builds.

**Skip it if** you're a casual user who only needs a few short runs — the upfront cost and installation effort aren't worth it for small projects.
