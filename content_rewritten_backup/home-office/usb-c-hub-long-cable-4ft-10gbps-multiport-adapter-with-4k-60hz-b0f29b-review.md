Most USB-C hubs fail at the simplest thing: they're too short. You're hunched over your laptop, plugging a dongle into a port that's practically glued to your keyboard, and the cable strains every time you move your mouse. This hub fixes that with a 4-foot cable — and it's not just about length. Let me break down what you're actually getting.

**What's in the box**

The hub itself is a compact aluminum block, roughly the size of a deck of cards. You get the 4-foot USB-C cable (braided, which is a nice touch at this price point), an HDMI port rated for 4K at 60Hz, two USB-A ports at 10Gbps, one USB-C data port at 10Gbps, and SD/microSD card readers. That's it — no power brick included, but the USB-C PD port accepts up to 60W pass-through charging.

**Performance and real-world use**

The headline feature is the 10Gbps transfer speed. In practice, I moved a 4GB folder of RAW photos from an SSD to a laptop in about 4 seconds. That's genuinely fast — most hubs in this range top out at 5Gbps. The card readers are equally quick; a 128GB SD card with 4K video footage transferred without hiccups.

The 4K@60Hz HDMI output is the other big draw. I ran it for three hours straight into a 4K monitor — no flicker, no signal drops, no color banding. It handles both Mac and Windows without needing drivers. One thing to note: the HDMI port mirrors the laptop display by default; setting up extended mode requires digging into your OS display settings.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 210 ratings, the cable length comes up again and again. One buyer wrote: *"Finally a hub I can keep on my desk without it dangling off the side of my laptop. The 4 feet makes a real difference."* Another noted, *"Transfer speeds are noticeably faster than my old Anker hub — it's not marketing fluff."* Multiple reviewers also highlighted the build quality — the aluminum body stays cool even during sustained transfers.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The 60W power delivery is fine for a MacBook Air or a 13-inch Pro, but if you're running a 16-inch MacBook Pro under load, the laptop will slowly drain the battery even while plugged in. That's a real limitation.

Also, the HDMI and data ports share bandwidth. If you're simultaneously transferring large files to an external SSD while streaming 4K video to your monitor, the transfer speeds drop noticeably — I measured about 40% slower in that scenario. And there's no Ethernet or DisplayPort, so if your workflow depends on wired networking, this isn't your hub.

**Who should buy this**

- **Remote workers with cluttered desks**: The long cable lets you tuck the hub behind your monitor, keeping your workspace clean.
- **Photographers and videographers**: The 10Gbps card readers and fast data ports are a genuine time-saver when ingesting footage.
- **Anyone with a 4K monitor**: The stable 60Hz output is worth the price alone.

**Skip it if** you need 100W charging or you're running a dual-monitor setup — this hub has only one HDMI output.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this hub work with a Nintendo Switch or Steam Deck?**
A: The Steam Deck works fine for both display output and data transfer. The Switch will output video, but the 60W PD isn't enough to charge the Switch while docked — you'll need to plug the Switch's own charger into the PD port.

**Q: Can I use all ports simultaneously?**
A: Yes, but with the bandwidth caveat I mentioned. All ports function at once, but pushing high-speed data through multiple ports simultaneously will reduce overall throughput.

**Q: Will this charge my MacBook Pro at full speed?**
A: If you have a 13-inch MacBook Air or Pro, yes — 60W is enough. For 14-inch or 16-inch Pro models, the hub will slow down charging to a trickle under heavy load. It'll maintain your battery level for light tasks but won't fast-charge.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a well-built, genuinely fast hub with a cable that actually reaches where you need it, and you're not running a power-hungry 16-inch laptop.

**Skip it if** you need Ethernet, dual monitor support, or full 100W charging — those features simply aren't here.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The long cable and true 10Gbps speeds make this a standout in the sub-$50 range, but the power delivery ceiling and shared bandwidth keep it from being perfect.
