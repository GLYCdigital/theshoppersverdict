Let’s be honest about what these bags are: they’re not a luxury purchase. They’re a security tool for anyone who moves cash, sensitive documents, or controlled materials between locations. And for that job, the ControlTek SafeLok 9" x 12" clear bags are quietly excellent.

**What you actually get**

A box of 100 clear polyethylene bags, each measuring 9 by 12 inches. That’s a standard letter-size footprint — think deposit slips, cash stacks, ID documents, or small evidence envelopes. The bags are printed with a sequential ID number on the front, and the clear material means you can verify contents at a glance without breaking the seal.

The closure is the star here. It’s a red zip-style adhesive strip that, once pressed shut, cannot be reopened without leaving a visible "VOID" pattern in the plastic. That’s the tamper-evidence working exactly as designed. No scissors, no tape, no heat sealer required.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these the way most buyers actually use them: cash deposits for a small retail operation. The 2-mil plastic is plenty for currency, checks, and paperwork. The seal takes about two seconds to close — you press firmly along the entire strip, and you’re done. A quick tug test confirmed the bond holds; you’re not getting back in without visible damage.

The clear material is a practical advantage most security bags don’t offer. Bank tellers can see what’s inside without opening, which speeds up deposits. For internal audits, the sequential numbering creates a simple chain-of-custody log. One reviewer noted, *"We use these for nightly cash drops and the numbering alone has saved us from two internal disputes."* That’s the kind of real-world validation that matters more than spec sheets.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 235 ratings, three themes dominate. First, the seal reliability — multiple reviewers mention testing the VOID feature and being impressed with how clearly tampering shows. Second, the clarity — being able to verify contents without opening is a genuine workflow improvement. Third, the value — at roughly $0.20–$0.30 per bag depending on current pricing, it’s cheaper than most branded security deposit bags and performs comparably.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be direct about limitations. The 2-mil plastic is thinner than the 4-mil material used in heavy-duty evidence bags. If you’re storing sharp items, tools, or anything with rough edges, these bags will puncture. They’re also not intended for long-term archival storage — this is a short-cycle transport bag, not a preservation envelope.

The adhesive also demands a deliberate close. If you rush it — pressing only the middle and missing the edges — you’ll get a weaker seal that might peel partially. It’s not difficult, but it requires attention. One customer put it plainly: *"Works great if you actually press the whole strip down. My first bag I was in a hurry and it didn't seal fully."*

**Who should buy this**

- **Retail managers** running nightly cash drops — the numbering alone justifies the cost
- **Medical or dental offices** sending lab specimens or controlled records
- **Property managers** collecting rent or security deposits
- **Event organizers** handling ticket sales or cash bars
- **Anyone who needs a visible, verifiable seal** on documents moving between departments

Skip these if you need puncture-proof packaging for physical evidence with long chain-of-custody requirements — that’s a different product category entirely.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these bags be resealed after opening?**
No. That’s the point. Once the seal is broken, the VOID pattern appears permanently. You cannot re-close a tampered bag convincingly.

**Q: Will a permanent marker write on the clear plastic?**
Yes. Sharpie and other permanent markers work well on the surface for adding dates, amounts, or initials.

**Q: Are these safe for shipping via mail or courier?**
They’re fine for internal transport or courier handoffs, but they’re not padded or structured for standard postal mailing. Use an outer envelope if you’re sending them through the mail system.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you handle cash, controlled documents, or sensitive materials between locations and need a simple, verifiable seal at a reasonable price. The sequential numbering, clear visibility, and reliable VOID tamper-evidence make this a genuinely useful security tool.

**Skip it if** you need heavy-duty puncture resistance for evidence-grade storage, or if you need bags that can survive rough handling without an outer protective layer.

This is a 4.6 out of 5 product — not perfect, but for its intended job, it’s hard to beat at this price point. The thin plastic and seal-precision requirement are real tradeoffs, but they don’t undermine the core function. For most small businesses and offices, the ControlTek SafeLok is the right call.
