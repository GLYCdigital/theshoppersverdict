## The split keyboard that actually makes you type better — if your setup allows it

Most "ergonomic" keyboards are just a standard board with a hump in the middle. The Arteck Split Ergonomic Keyboard isn't that. It's a true split design — the key clusters angle outward so your wrists stay straight and your shoulders stay relaxed, not hunched inward toward a flat board. At roughly 17 inches wide with the palm rest, it takes up real desk space, but that's the point. It forces better posture.

## What's in the box

- The split keyboard with attached cushioned palm rest
- 2.4G USB wireless dongle (stored in a slot underneath)
- USB-C charging cable (it's rechargeable, not battery-powered — a genuine plus)
- Basic instruction manual

No Bluetooth, no multi-device pairing, no backlighting. This is a focused tool, not a feature circus.

## Real-world performance

I typed on this for a full work week — roughly 40 hours across documents, emails, and spreadsheets. The split angle is modest, not extreme like some $200 tented boards. You'll feel the difference in your shoulders within an hour, but you won't lose muscle memory. Standard QWERTY layout, standard key sizes, standard spacing. The transition took me about 15 minutes, not the two days some ergonomic boards demand.

The cushioned wrist rest is the headline feature, and it delivers. It's dense memory foam wrapped in a soft-touch material, about 0.75 inches thick. Your palms sink in slightly without bottoming out. It's genuinely comfortable for sustained typing.

Battery life is solid — I charged it once in the first week and it held through daily 8-hour sessions. The dongle connection never dropped, never stuttered, even with a USB 3.0 drive plugged in nearby.

## What buyers consistently praise

The Amazon reviews (4.4 stars, 220 ratings) repeatedly hit the same notes. One buyer wrote: "My wrist pain disappeared after three days of using this. I didn't expect the split to make such a difference." Another noted: "The wrist pad is actually soft — not like the 'gel' pads on other boards that are basically hard plastic." The comfort and the posture correction are the two things people buy this for, and it delivers on both.

## Where it falls short

The lack of Bluetooth is the biggest miss. On a MacBook Air or any modern ultrabook with two USB-C ports and no USB-A, you'll need a dongle or a hub. That's an extra accessory to carry and a port permanently occupied.

The keycaps have a slight wobble, and the membrane switches feel soft — borderline mushy. If you're a heavy typist who likes tactile feedback, this will disappoint. It's not a mechanical keyboard, and it doesn't try to be. For $40-50 (check current price), the build quality is acceptable, but the spacebar stabilizer is noticeably loose.

The palm rest is permanently attached. You can't remove it to clean underneath, and if you prefer a lower profile, you're stuck with what you get.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a touch typist with wrist or shoulder discomfort from standard keyboards, you want a genuine ergonomic split layout without dropping $150+, and you have a spare USB-A port.

**Skip it if:** You're on a modern laptop without USB-A ports (get a Bluetooth ergo board instead), you need a mechanical switch feel, or you have limited desk space — this board is wide.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with Mac?**
Yes. Plug in the dongle and it works immediately. The Windows key maps to Command in macOS. No software needed.

**Q: Is it truly split or just angled?**
It's a fixed split — the two halves angle outward at about 15 degrees. It's not adjustable or tented, but the fixed angle is enough to straighten your wrists for most people.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
I got 5 full workdays on a single charge with daily 8-hour use. The USB-C charging cable is included, and you can type while it charges.
