**The $40 question: can you trust remanufactured ink with your HP printer?** Most shoppers assume you have two choices — pay full price for OEM 220XL cartridges or gamble on cheap knockoffs that leak. E-Z Ink's remanufactured option sits in the middle, and after digging through 225 ratings and real-world usage patterns, the answer is more nuanced than a simple yes or no.

**What's in the box:** You get a standard 220XL combo pack — one black and one tri-color cartridge, sealed in recyclable packaging. The cartridges are remanufactured from genuine HP shells, meaning the internal print heads and electronics are recycled and refilled rather than built from scratch. As shown in the product image, they're visually identical to OEM cartridges from the outside, which matters because some HP printers are picky about cartridge geometry.

**Performance and real-world use:** This is where remanufactured ink usually lives or dies. E-Z Ink holds up well. The black cartridge uses a pigment-based ink that dries fast and resists smudging on standard copy paper — a common failure point for budget inks. Color output leans slightly warmer than OEM, but for everyday documents, spreadsheets, and school projects, the difference is imperceptible. Photo printing on glossy paper shows good saturation, though skin tones run a touch magenta compared to HP originals.

Page yield is the headline number. E-Z Ink claims 600 pages for black and 415 for color, matching HP's official specs. In practical testing across mixed document types, users report hitting close to those numbers — one reviewer noted, "I've printed over 500 pages and the black cartridge still shows half full on my printer's dashboard." That said, yield varies dramatically with print density; draft-mode printing stretches it further, while heavy photo work drains the tri-color cartridge noticeably faster.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The value proposition dominates. Across ratings, the most common refrain is cost savings without quality sacrifice. A verified purchase put it bluntly: "Half the price of HP's cartridges and I can't tell the difference in my daily printing." Another long-term user mentioned they're on their third set and have never had a leak or clog — a genuine concern with remanufactured products.

**Where it falls short:** The recycled shells are the weakest link. Several buyers noted minor cosmetic imperfections — small scuffs or slightly loose plastic seams — that don't affect function but signal the refurbished nature. More significantly, a subset of users reported their HP printers displaying a "non-genuine cartridge" warning on startup. It's a one-tap dismiss, not a functional block, but if you're not comfortable overriding manufacturer warnings, this isn't for you. Additionally, remanufactured cartridges carry a shorter shelf life than new OEM units — if you don't print regularly, the ink can dry out faster in storage.

**Who should buy this:** If you're a home user, student, or small office printing 50-200 pages monthly, this is a smart buy. The savings add up quickly — you'll recoup the cost difference within two refill cycles. If you're a professional photographer or print color-critical marketing materials, stick with OEM. The slight color shift and non-HP warning aren't worth the risk in that workflow.

**FAQ:**

*Q: Will these cartridges void my HP printer warranty?* No. In the U.S., the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act prevents manufacturers from voiding warranties for using third-party consumables unless they can prove the product caused the damage. E-Z Ink also offers a 12-month replacement guarantee.

*Q: Do they work with HP Instant Ink?* No. Instant Ink requires HP's proprietary chipped cartridges. These are for standard 220XL-compatible printers only, not subscription-based programs.

*Q: How do they handle long periods of inactivity?* The pigment-based black ink handles idle periods reasonably well, but the dye-based color inks can clog if left unused for 3+ months. Run a print head cleaning cycle after extended breaks.
