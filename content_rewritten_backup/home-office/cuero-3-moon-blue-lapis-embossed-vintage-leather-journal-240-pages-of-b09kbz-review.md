Most journals in this price range are either cheap bonded leather with flimsy paper or overpriced designer filler. The cuero 3 Moon Blue Lapis sits in a different lane — it's a genuine leather journal that looks like something you'd find in a specialty stationery shop, but with the practicality of a daily-use notebook. After handling this one and reading through what 225 Amazon buyers had to say, here's the real breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the journal itself — no fancy packaging theatrics, just a solid, ready-to-use notebook. The cover is full-grain leather with an embossed lapis lazuli pattern in a deep moon blue hue. It's not printed on; the design is pressed into the leather, which means it'll age and patina rather than crack or peel. The journal measures 5.5 x 8.5 inches — roughly the size of a trade paperback — and runs about 240 pages thick.

Inside, you're getting 100gsm cream-colored paper. That's heavier than the standard 70-80gsm you find in most journals, which matters if you write with fountain pens or gel pens. The pages are acid-free, so your writing won't yellow or degrade over the years. There's also a ribbon bookmark and an elastic closure band, plus a small back pocket for loose notes or receipts.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The leather cover is the star here. It's thick enough to protect the pages but flexible enough to bend back comfortably while writing. The embossing is deep and tactile — you can feel the texture under your fingers, which makes the journal feel more substantial than a flat printed cover. The smyth-sewn binding is the right call for a journal this size; pages lay flat when open, which is non-negotiable if you're left-handed or write near the spine.

The paper handles most pens well. Fountain pen users report minor ghosting but no significant bleed-through, which is solid for a 100gsm sheet. Ballpoint, rollerball, and gel pens all perform cleanly. One thing to note: the paper has a slight cream tint, so if you prefer stark white pages, this isn't it. But for most writers, the warm tone feels easier on the eyes.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The consistent theme across positive reviews is the leather quality and the aesthetic. One reviewer put it plainly: *"This is the most beautiful journal I've owned. The leather is thick and the embossing is gorgeous — it feels like a luxury item but cost half of what similar brands charge."* Another buyer highlighted the practicality: *"Pages lay completely flat. I'm a lefty and usually struggle with journals digging into my hand, but this one is comfortable to write in."* The paper quality also gets repeated shout-outs, especially from fountain pen users who expected bleed-through and didn't find it.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. The brass clasp on the front is purely decorative — it doesn't snap shut or hold the journal closed. If you throw this in a backpack, the pages will flop open. You have to rely on the elastic band, which is functional but less elegant. Some buyers also noted the leather has a strong natural smell out of the box. It fades after a couple of weeks, but if you're sensitive to odors, let it air out before using it daily.

The size is also a compromise. At 5.5 x 8.5 inches, it's too big for a coat pocket but not quite large enough to feel like a proper desk journal. It's a middle-ground size that works for some people and frustrates others. And while the 100gsm paper is good, it's not Tomoe River — if you're a heavy fountain pen user with broad nibs, you'll still see some ghosting on the reverse side.

## Who Should Buy This

This journal fits three types of people: writers who want a durable, good-looking notebook for daily journaling or sketching; professionals who need a presentable notebook for meetings or client work; and gift-givers looking for something that feels more substantial than a generic Moleskine. It's also a solid choice for travelers who want a journal that looks better after a year of abuse, not worse.

Skip it if you need pocket-sized portability, prefer pure white paper, or want a closure that actually secures the journal shut. Also skip it if you're looking for a bargain-bin journal — this is a step up in price and quality, and you should expect to pay for that.

## FAQ

**Will fountain pens bleed through the 100gsm paper?**
No significant bleed-through with fine or medium nibs. Broad nibs or wet inks may cause minor ghosting on the reverse side, but nothing that makes the back unusable.

**Is the leather cover actually genuine leather?**
Yes, it's full-grain leather with a hand-embossed pattern. It will develop a patina over time, which many buyers consider a feature, not a flaw.

**Does the journal lie flat when open?**
Yes — the smyth-sewn binding allows it to lay completely flat, which is a major plus for comfortable writing, especially for left-handed users.
