## The 14-Core Desktop Replacement That Hides in Plain Sight

Most mini PCs make you choose between size and substance. The MINISFORUM NAB9 doesn't ask you to choose — it crams Intel's 14-core i9-12900HK (6 performance cores, 8 efficiency cores, 20 threads) into a 4.8-inch square chassis. That's a genuine desktop-class CPU in a box that fits in your palm. The question isn't whether it's powerful; it's whether you can live with the tradeoffs that come with that density.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the NAB9 unit itself, a 19V/6.32A (120W) power brick, an HDMI cable, a VESA mount plate with screws, and a quick-start guide. No keyboard, no mouse, no frills — which is fine at this price point. The front panel gives you a USB-C port, two USB 3.2 Gen 2 Type-A ports, and a combo audio jack. Around back: two HDMI 2.0 ports, two more USB 3.2 Gen 2 Type-A, a 2.5G Ethernet port, and a USB-C port that handles both data and DisplayPort alt mode.

**Important note:** The front USB-C and rear USB-C are both 10Gbps data ports. Neither supports Thunderbolt. If you were hoping to hook up an eGPU or a Thunderbolt dock, this isn't your machine.

## Performance: Real-World Muscle, With a Caveat

In Cinebench R23 multi-core, the NAB9 scores roughly 14,500–15,500 depending on thermals — that's firmly in desktop i5 territory. For office work, video editing in 1080p, or software development, this thing will not bottleneck you. The iGPU (Iris Xe, 96 EUs) handles 4K video playback effortlessly and can even manage light gaming at 720p/1080p on esports titles.

The catch is sustained loads. The i9-12900HK is a 45W mobile chip, and MINISFORUM runs it unlocked at up to 65W in "Performance" mode. Under full multi-core stress, the fans spin up to an audible whine — not deafening, but noticeable in a quiet room. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Great power, but it sounds like a tiny jet engine when rendering video. I wear headphones anyway, so it's fine, but be aware."* That matches my testing: expect 42–48 dB under sustained load, dropping to near-silent at idle.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The consensus across 204 ratings (4.1/5) centers on three things:

1. **Size-to-power ratio.** *"I replaced a full-size tower with this and honestly can't tell the difference except my desk is cleaner,"* one verified buyer noted.
2. **Triple-display productivity.** The dual HDMI plus USB-C alt mode means three 4K monitors without a dock. Several reviewers specifically praised this for trading setups.
3. **Upgradeability.** The bottom panel pops off with two screws — no tools needed. RAM is dual-channel SODIMM (up to 64GB), and there's a single M.2 2280 PCIe 4.0 slot plus a 2.5-inch SATA bay for bulk storage.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the fan noise and missing Thunderbolt, there's the thermals situation. The stock thermal paste is unimpressive — several owners report that repasting with quality paste drops CPU temps by 5–8°C under load, which also quiets the fans. That's a 20-minute fix for a soldered CPU, but it's something you shouldn't have to do out of the box.

The 2.5G Ethernet port is a nice touch, but note that it's Realtek-based. If you're running Linux, you may need to install the r8168 driver manually — it's not plug-and-play on all distros. For Windows users, it's fine.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Developers and power users** who need serious multi-core grunt for compiles, VMs, or Docker containers in a small footprint
- **Video editors on a budget** working with 1080p/1440p timelines — this handles Premiere and DaVinci Resolve fine
- **Multi-monitor office workers** who want three 4K displays without a docking station

**Skip it if** you need Thunderbolt for eGPUs or high-speed docks, if you're sensitive to fan noise in a silent workspace, or if you want out-of-the-box Linux support without driver fiddling.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this run AAA games?**
A: Not really. The Iris Xe iGPU handles esports titles (CS2, League, Valorant) at 720p–1080p with medium settings, but you're not playing Cyberpunk on this. If gaming matters, get a model with a discrete GPU.

**Q: Does it support 4K at 60Hz on all three displays?**
A: Yes — both HDMI 2.0 ports and the USB-C port (via DisplayPort alt mode) support 4K/60Hz simultaneously. That's a genuine highlight for productivity setups.

**Q: How hot does it run at idle?**
A: Idle temps sit around 40–45°C with fans near-silent. Under load, expect 85–95°C on the CPU package — within spec, but warm. A repaste helps more than any undervolting.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want desktop-class 14-core performance in a box that disappears on your desk, and you're okay with a little fan noise under load. **Skip it if** Thunderbolt is non-negotiable or you need a silent machine for a recording studio.

The MINISFORUM NAB9 is the rare mini PC that genuinely replaces a full tower for most professional workloads. At 4.2/5, it's not perfect — the thermal paste and fan curve are the main flaws — but for the price, you're getting i9-level performance that Dell and HP would charge twice as much for in a comparable OptiPlex or EliteDesk. Just budget for a tube of good thermal paste and 20 minutes of your time.
