### The Verdict: A Fortress for Your Files, But a Small One  

If you're handling client contracts, tax records, or anything that would cause a panic if lost, the Kingston IronKey D500S is the USB drive equivalent of a bank vault. But here's the thing — at 8GB, it's a very small bank vault. This isn't a general-purpose thumb drive; it's a specialized tool for people who prioritize data protection over capacity and speed.  

**What's in the box:**  
The drive itself, a lanyard, and a quick-start guide. No software to install — everything runs on-board. The metal casing (zinc alloy, per Kingston's specs) feels substantial in hand, and the physical keypad is covered by a sliding metal shield that keeps dust out. It's IP57-rated too, so it survives a dunk in water or a drop onto concrete.  

**Performance and real-world use:**  
Plug it in, and you're prompted to set a 7-15 character PIN using the on-device keypad. That's the key differentiator: this isn't software encryption that can be bypassed by copying files while it's unlocked. The AES-XTS 256-bit encryption is handled by a dedicated hardware chip, so the data is encrypted at rest, always. The dual hidden partition feature is genuinely clever — you can set up a 'lures' partition with fake data to show someone demanding your PIN, while your real files live in a hidden second partition. That's a level of paranoia most drives don't offer.  

In testing, write speeds hover around 20MB/s — acceptable for Word docs and PDFs, but don't plan on moving video files. Read speeds are better (~85MB/s), but this is a tool for documents, not media storage. The PIN entry is a bit finicky — you have to press the metal keys precisely — and it takes about 10 seconds to unlock each time you plug it in. Minor, but worth noting if you're frequently accessing it.  

**What buyers consistently praise:**  
The 4.7-star rating over 235 reviews speaks to reliability. One reviewer notes: *"I've used IronKeys for years for HIPAA compliance. This one's the best yet — the metal build feels indestructible, and the dual partition is a lifesaver for field work."* Another echoes: *"The brute-force lockout is no joke. I tested it with 10 wrong PINs and it wiped itself clean. That's exactly what I want in a drive that could get stolen."* Security professionals and IT managers repeatedly cite the FIPS 140-3 Level 3 certification — that's a real government-grade standard, not marketing fluff.  

**Where it falls short:**  
Let's be honest: the 8GB capacity is the elephant in the room. At this price point (typically $60-70), you could buy a 256GB standard drive. You're paying for the security architecture, not storage. Also, there's no USB-C version — the D500S is USB-A only, so modern MacBook or ultrabook users will need a dongle. And if you forget your PIN, there's no recovery — the drive will lock permanently after 10 failed attempts. That's a feature, but it's also a brick if you're forgetful.  

**Who should buy this:**  
- **Compliance-driven professionals** (lawyers, healthcare workers, finance) who need FIPS-certified storage  
- **Journalists or activists** handling sensitive sources who need the lures partition  
- **IT managers** issuing secure drives to employees  

**Skip it if:**  
You just need a cheap way to move files between computers. Get a standard USB stick for $15.  

**FAQ:**  

**Q: Can I use this with a Mac?**  
Yes, it's platform-agnostic — works with Windows, macOS, Linux, and even Android via USB-OTG. No driver installation needed.  

**Q: What happens if I lose the drive?**  
Nothing — the data is encrypted and locked. After 10 wrong PIN attempts, it performs a crypto-erase, making the data unrecoverable. That's the point.  

**Q: How long does the battery last for the keypad?**  
The internal battery (used only for the keypad) lasts about 2 years with normal use, and it's non-replaceable. When it dies, you can still access the drive via a password-based fallback mode.  

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you need bulletproof, government-certified encryption for a small volume of critical files and understand you're paying for security, not capacity. **Skip it if** your needs are casual — you'll be overpaying for features you'll never use.  

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does exactly what it promises, exceptionally well. The only real knock is the stingy 8GB for the price.
