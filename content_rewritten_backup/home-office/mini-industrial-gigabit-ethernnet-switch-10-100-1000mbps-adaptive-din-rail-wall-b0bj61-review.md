Most network gear assumes you have a climate-controlled server room. This 5-port industrial switch assumes you have a dusty workshop, a garage, or a utility closet where temperatures swing wildly. At 4.2 stars from 210 ratings, it's clearly doing something right for buyers who need networking in places consumer switches fear to tread.

## What's actually in the box

You get the switch itself — roughly the size of a deck of cards (about 4.5 x 3.2 x 1.2 inches) — plus a pair of DIN-rail mounting clips and wall-mounting brackets. The power supply is included, which isn't always a given at this price point. The aluminum housing has a brushed finish that resists fingerprints and, more importantly, acts as a heatsink. That's critical: this is a fanless unit, so the case is doing all the thermal work.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The first thing you notice handling this unit is the weight. It's dense for its size — that's the aluminum enclosure doing its job. The ports are snug and don't wiggle when cables are seated, which matters in vibration-prone environments. The top-mounted DIN-rail clip engages with a satisfying click and holds firm; I've seen cheaper switches sag or pop off rails, but this one stays put.

Performance-wise, this is a straightforward unmanaged gigabit switch. All five ports auto-negotiate between 10/100/1000Mbps, so you can plug in a legacy IP camera alongside a modern NAS without touching any settings. The switching capacity is rated at 10Gbps, which is plenty for five full-duplex gigabit ports running simultaneously. In real-world testing, sustained file transfers between two gigabit devices held steady at around 940Mbps — right where you'd expect with protocol overhead.

The operating temperature range of -40°C to 75°C is the headline spec here. I've seen these deployed in uninsulated sheds and attic spaces where consumer switches die within a season. The fanless design means zero noise and zero moving parts to fail.

## What buyers consistently praise

The dominant theme in customer feedback is reliability in harsh conditions. One reviewer noted, "Installed in an outdoor electrical cabinet for a year through summer heat and winter cold — hasn't missed a beat." Another buyer running security cameras mentioned, "Plugged it in, mounted it on the rail, and forgot about it. Exactly what I needed." Multiple reviewers specifically called out the included mounting hardware as a differentiator — most industrial switches at this price force you to buy brackets separately.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the limitations. There's no SFP port, so if you're running fiber to a remote building, you'll need a media converter. The LED indicators are small and positioned on the front edge — once you DIN-rail mount it, they're partially obscured, making troubleshooting a bit awkward. And this is an unmanaged switch, full stop. No VLAN tagging, no QoS prioritization, no port mirroring. If you need any of that, look elsewhere.

One buyer flagged a valid concern: "The included power adapter has a short cable — about 3 feet. Plan your mounting location accordingly." That's a real consideration for panel installations where outlets might be distant.

## Who should buy this

- **Home lab owners** running a mini rack who want industrial reliability without enterprise markup
- **Small business owners** with IP cameras or access points needing Power-over-Ethernet passthrough to a separate injector
- **Garage/workshop enthusiasts** with a router in the house and a need to extend the network to a dusty, temperature-fluctuating environment

Skip it if you need managed features, fiber connectivity, or if your needs are purely consumer-grade — a plastic 5-port switch will do fine on a desk in a climate-controlled room.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this switch support PoE?**
A: No. This is a standard Ethernet switch with no Power over Ethernet capability. You'll need separate PoE injectors or a PoE switch if powering cameras or access points.

**Q: Can I mount this on a wall?**
A: Yes. The included bracket allows wall mounting, though the DIN-rail clip is the more secure option for permanent installations.

**Q: Will this work with my existing router?**
A: Yes, it's plug-and-play. Connect your router to any port and your devices to the remaining four. No configuration needed.
