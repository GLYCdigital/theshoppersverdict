## What You Actually Get

The hub itself is a compact PCB board roughly the size of a business card. It's not powered by your motherboard headers — this thing demands SATA power (included in the box, along with the cable). That's actually a smart design choice: pulling power from SATA means the hub can handle up to 8 fans without overloading your motherboard's puny fan headers.

The layout is split into two banks: 8 PWM fan ports on one side, 8 ARGB ports on the other. This separation is the single biggest selling point. Most competing hubs force you to choose between fan control OR RGB sync. Thermalright gives you both on one board.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The PCB is solid, with no visible solder defects or flimsy connectors. The magnetic backing holds firm to steel cases — one reviewer noted, "Stuck it to the back of my Lian Li case and it hasn't budged in six months." The included double-sided tape covers you if your case is aluminum or glass.

PWM signal passthrough is clean. Fans ramp up and down with your CPU temperature exactly as they would plugged directly into the motherboard. One buyer reported running six Noctua NF-A14s and two Arctic P12s off a single hub with zero RPM detection issues.

The ARGB side uses standard 3-pin 5V headers, so it works with ASUS Aura Sync, MSI Mystic Light, Gigabyte RGB Fusion, and ASRock Polychrome. If your motherboard lacks an ARGB header, this hub won't help — you'd need a separate controller.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The dominant theme in reviews is cable management relief. "I was about to buy three separate splitters. This replaced all of them with one tidy board," said one verified purchaser. Another called it "the best $20 I've spent on my build."

Multiple reviewers specifically mention the SATA power as a feature, not a downside — it prevents the motherboard header overload that plagues cheaper splitters.

## Where It Falls Short

The connector spacing is genuinely tight. If you're using fans with thick, braided cables, you'll fight to seat adjacent plugs. Plan your wiring order before mounting.

The SATA power requirement is a double-edged sword. If your PSU is fully populated, you'll need a SATA splitter — which adds clutter, defeating part of the purpose.

There's no software. This is purely a hardware hub. All control happens through your motherboard's BIOS or vendor RGB apps. If you wanted a standalone RGB controller with its own software, this isn't it.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The multi-fan builder**: Case with 6-8 fans? This consolidates everything into one board.
- **The RGB tinkerer**: Wants motherboard sync without buying into a single-ecosystem controller.
- **The cable-management perfectionist**: Magnetic mount + dual banks = clean routing.

Skip it if you're running 3-4 fans (a simple splitter suffices) or if you need standalone control without motherboard dependency.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I mix 3-pin and 4-pin fans on this hub?**
A: Yes, but 3-pin fans will run at full speed or at whatever voltage the motherboard sets — PWM speed control only works with 4-pin fans.

**Q: Does the ARGB hub support addressable RGB strips, not just fans?**
A: Yes. Any 5V 3-pin ARGB device works — strips, AIO pumps, GPU brackets. Just keep total current under the 3A limit.

**Q: What happens if I plug in more than 8 devices?**
A: Don't. The board is rated for exactly 8 groups per side. Overloading can damage the hub or, worse, your motherboard's ARGB header.
