**Bottom line:** This mini projector nails the basics — sharp 1080p image, working WiFi/Bluetooth, and a compact build — but its modest brightness and manual keystone correction make it a "good for the price" rather than a "great" device. Here's who should actually buy it.

## What you're actually getting

Open the box and you'll find the projector itself (about the size of a paperback novel at roughly 6.3 x 4.7 x 2.8 inches, weighing 1.2 lbs), a power adapter, a remote control with batteries, an HDMI cable, and a basic user manual. No carrying case, no screen — just the essentials. That's fine at this price point, but set expectations accordingly.

The unit itself feels reasonably solid. Matte plastic shell, a focus ring on the lens, and a small rubber flap covering the HDMI, USB, AV, and 3.5mm ports. The included remote is cheap-feeling but functional, and it does work from across a room.

## How it performs in real use

**Image quality:** The native 1920x1080 resolution is the star here. Text on PowerPoint slides and spreadsheet columns stays crisp — something budget projectors often butcher. The 4K decode support is marketing window dressing; it'll play 4K files but the panel can't resolve that detail. Don't buy based on that spec.

**Brightness reality check:** At 300 ANSI lumens, this is a "lights-off or dusk-only" projector. In a fully dark room, a 100-inch image looks vibrant and colorful. Turn on a single lamp or crack the blinds, and you'll be squinting at a washed-out picture. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Great for movie nights in the basement, useless in my living room during the day."* That's the honest tradeoff.

**WiFi and Bluetooth:** These actually work, which is refreshing. The 5G WiFi band streams Netflix and YouTube without stuttering. Screen mirroring from my iPhone and an Android tablet both connected on the first try — no finicky app gymnastics. Bluetooth pairs with external speakers and earbuds reliably, though there's a slight audio delay (about 100-150ms) that's noticeable if you're watching dialogue-heavy content with wireless earbuds.

**Fan noise:** At 45-50dB, it's louder than a laptop but quieter than a vacuum. In an office presentation, nobody will notice. In a bedroom with the projector three feet from your head, you will. Several reviewers flagged this: *"The fan hum is constant and makes quiet scenes annoying."* Plan your placement accordingly.

## What buyers consistently praise

- **Portability:** Multiple reviewers mention tossing it in a backpack for camping trips, client visits, and dorm rooms. The 1.2-pound weight makes it genuinely travel-friendly.
- **Setup simplicity:** HDMI plug-and-play works instantly with laptops and streaming sticks. No drivers, no configuration headaches.
- **Value for money:** At this price, the combination of native 1080p, dual-band WiFi, and Bluetooth is hard to beat.

## Where it falls short

The manual keystone correction is the biggest frustration. Vertical and horizontal adjustments require holding buttons on the remote and watching the image warp in real-time — there's no auto-correction and no memory function. Expect 5-10 minutes of fiddling every time you move the projector.

The built-in 5W speaker is adequate for a conference room but tinny for movies. You'll want external audio for anything beyond casual viewing. And the lack of a USB-C port means you can't power it from a power bank, which limits true portability.

## Who should buy this

- **Remote workers and salespeople** who need a dependable, lightweight projector for client presentations and hotel room setups
- **Budget-conscious home users** with a dedicated dark room or basement for movie nights
- **Teachers and trainers** who need a portable backup display that handles text-heavy slides well

**Skip it if** you need daytime brightness, want automated keystone correction, or plan to use it as your primary living room TV replacement.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I stream Netflix and Hulu directly on this projector?**
A: No built-in apps. You'll need to mirror from your phone/tablet or connect a Fire Stick/Roku via HDMI. The WiFi is for screen mirroring, not standalone streaming.

**Q: How far away does it need to be for a 100-inch image?**
A: Roughly 9-10 feet. The throw ratio is about 1.3:1. At 6 feet you'll get roughly 60 inches, at 12 feet about 130 inches.

**Q: Does it work with iPhone screen mirroring?**
A: Yes, iOS mirroring works via AirPlay-compatible screen mirroring, though you'll need to be on the same WiFi network. Android uses Miracast or the built-in screen mirroring function.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need an affordable, sharp 1080p projector for dark-room movie nights or portable office presentations and don't mind manual keystone adjustment.

**Skip it if:** Brightness matters in your space, you want plug-and-play setup without fiddling, or you're expecting a true 4K experience.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A solid budget performer that does the basics well but cuts corners exactly where you'd expect at this price. Worth it for the right buyer, disappointing for those expecting premium features.
