**The Small Desktop Glass Whiteboard** sits in that awkward middle zone between a sticky note and a wall-mounted whiteboard. It's not big enough to replace your planning system, but it's far more substantial than a paper notepad. After digging through 220 ratings and the specs, here's the real story.

## What you actually get

The box contains the glass board itself (about the size of a small tablet), a foldable aluminum easel stand, a magnetic pen tray, three dry-erase markers, and a small eraser. Assembly takes under a minute — the stand snaps into place and the board rests in a groove. No tools, no screws, no frustration.

As shown in the product image, the design is clean and modern. The tempered glass surface has a matte black backing that looks genuinely premium on a desk. It's the kind of item that doesn't scream "office supply" — it looks like a deliberate workspace accessory.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The tempered glass surface is the star here. Unlike traditional melamine whiteboards that stain and ghost over time, glass wipes completely clean. Multiple buyers noted this specifically. One reviewer wrote, *"I've had this for three months and it still looks brand new. No ghosting at all, which my old whiteboard definitely had."* That tracks — glass is non-porous, so marker ink doesn't seep into the surface.

The aluminum stand is sturdier than I expected for a desktop accessory. It holds the board at roughly a 75-degree angle, which is comfortable for writing while seated. The rubber feet keep it from sliding around on a desk. It won't tip over during normal use.

Where it stumbles: the included markers. They're cheap, dry out quickly, and skip on the glass. Several reviewers mentioned this. Budget $8–10 for a pack of good dry-erase markers (Expo or similar) and this issue disappears. The eraser is also small — fine for a board this size, but don't expect to clean large sections quickly.

## What buyers consistently praise

- **Zero ghosting** — the glass surface genuinely erases clean, every time
- **Aesthetic appeal** — it looks far more expensive than it is and elevates a desk setup
- **No magnets needed** — the magnetic pen tray attaches to the board's steel backing, keeping markers and eraser within arm's reach

## Where it falls short

**Size.** This is a *small* board. At roughly 12x9 inches, you're looking at a daily to-do list, a few phone numbers, or a quick sketch. It's not for project planning, brainstorming sessions, or anything that requires sustained writing. If you need a real whiteboard, buy a wall-mounted one.

**No mounting option.** The easel stand is the only way to use this. You can't hang it on a wall or mount it to a monitor arm. That limits placement flexibility. If you wanted it vertical on a cubicle wall, this isn't the product for you.

**Stand angle isn't adjustable.** The board sits at one fixed angle. It works, but if you prefer a flatter or more upright writing surface, you're out of luck.

## Who should buy this

- **Remote workers** who need a visible, attractive surface for daily tasks and quick reminders
- **Students** who want a reusable scratch pad for formulas, vocabulary, or study notes
- **Anyone who hates sticky note clutter** and wants something that looks intentional on their desk

Skip it if you need serious whiteboard real estate, want wall-mounting capability, or expect the included markers to be usable long-term.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the glass surface scratch easily?**
A: No. Tempered glass is significantly harder than melamine. Using standard dry-erase markers won't scratch it. Just avoid metal-tipped tools or abrasive cleaning pads.

**Q: Can I use any dry-erase marker with this?**
A: Yes. Any standard dry-erase marker works. The surface handles all brands fine. Low-odor and fine-tip markers work particularly well for this size board.

**Q: How do I clean it if marker gets left on too long?**
A: Glass is forgiving. Standard whiteboard cleaner works, but isopropyl alcohol or even hand sanitizer removes stubborn residue instantly. Unlike porous boards, glass doesn't trap old ink.
