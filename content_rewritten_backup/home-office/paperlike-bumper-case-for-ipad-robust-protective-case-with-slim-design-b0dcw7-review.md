If you've ever watched an iPad slide off a couch cushion and brace for impact, you know the value of a good bumper case. The Paperlike Bumper Case for iPad is designed for exactly that moment — but it's not without compromise.

**What you actually get**

The package includes the case itself — one piece, no screen protector, no stand, no frills. It's a two-piece construction: a flexible TPU bumper wrapped around a harder polycarbonate backplate. The design is deliberately slim, adding roughly 12mm to the iPad's thickness. As shown in the product image, the raised bezel extends about 2mm above the screen surface, which is the key spec for face-down drops.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The TPU bumper has a matte finish that doesn't feel cheap or slippery. In hand, the case adds noticeable grip — one Amazon reviewer noted, "I've dropped my iPad twice since putting this on, and both times it landed face-down. Screen survived both." That's the core job, and it does it well.

The backplate is rigid enough that the iPad doesn't flex when you grip it from one corner, which matters if you carry it one-handed. The button cutouts are precise — volume and power buttons retain their click without requiring a hard press through the case material. The charging port opening is wide enough for third-party cables, which is more than many cases offer.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 200 ratings (4.0 average), the recurring themes are drop protection and grip. One verified buyer wrote: "The texture on the back is subtle but effective. I no longer feel like the iPad is going to slide off my lap during meetings." Another noted the case doesn't interfere with the Apple Pencil charging — the side is left partially exposed, which is a thoughtful design detail.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest complaint in reviews is lint accumulation. The TPU bumper's soft edges attract pocket fuzz and dust within days. It's cosmetic, not functional, but if you keep your iPad in a bag, expect to wipe it down regularly.

There's no integrated stand and no Apple Pencil holder. For a case at this price point, that's a notable omission — you'll need to buy a separate stand for video calls or media viewing. Some reviewers also mentioned the case is snug on first installation; it takes deliberate pressure to snap the corners into place, which can feel nerve-wracking with a brand-new iPad.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students and commuters** who toss their iPad into backpacks need the drop protection and don't mind the lint issue.
- **Artists using the Apple Pencil** will appreciate the exposed side for charging and the slim profile that doesn't add bulk to the drawing surface.
- **Skip it if** you want a case that doubles as a stand or if you prefer a folio-style cover to protect the screen when not in use.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Does this case work with the Magic Keyboard?** No. This is a standalone bumper case. You'll need to remove it to attach Apple's keyboard accessories.

**Will the raised bezel interfere with screen protectors?** No. Reviewers report standard glass screen protectors sit flush without lifting at the edges.

**How does the drop protection compare to rugged cases like OtterBox?** It's lighter and thinner, but the tradeoff is less corner cushioning. For desk-to-floor drops, it's fine. For throwing down stairs, get a rugged case.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a slim, protective case that prioritizes screen safety without turning your iPad into a brick. The grip texture and raised bezel justify the cost, and the precise cutouts show attention to detail.

**Skip it if** you need an all-in-one solution with a stand and pencil storage, or if you're rough on gear — the TPU edges will wear faster than a full rugged case.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, honest case that does one thing well: protecting your iPad from the drops that actually happen.
