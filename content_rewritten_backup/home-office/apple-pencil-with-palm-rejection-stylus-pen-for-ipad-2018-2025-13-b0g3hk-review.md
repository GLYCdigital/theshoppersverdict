Let me be blunt: if you've been eyeing this Apple Pencil with palm rejection for your iPad, you're likely weighing it against the first-party option that costs twice as much. After digging through the specs, user feedback, and real-world performance reports, here's what actually matters.

**What's in the box**

You get the stylus itself, a replacement tip, and a USB-C charging cable. The pen measures about 6.3 inches long and weighs roughly 15 grams — noticeably lighter than Apple's Pencil 2. It charges via USB-C on the bottom, and a full charge reportedly delivers around 10 hours of continuous use. The packaging is minimal, which is fine. No case, no extra accessories — just the essentials.

**Performance and build quality**

The headline feature here is palm rejection, and it genuinely works. Multiple buyers describe writing or sketching with their hand flat on the screen with zero accidental marks. One reviewer noted, "I can rest my palm naturally like I'm writing on paper — no ghost lines, no interruption." That's the experience you're paying for, and it delivers.

Latency is also solid. The tip tracks smoothly with minimal lag, and tilt sensitivity responds well when you angle the pen for shading or broader strokes. For note-taking, journaling, and light sketching, this performs admirably. Battery life checks out too — a few hours of daily use gets you through the week without hunting for a charger.

The build is where tradeoffs appear. The plastic barrel feels hollow compared to Apple's metal construction. It's not fragile — it survived drops in several user accounts — but it doesn't feel premium in hand. The tip is replaceable, which extends the lifespan considerably.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest limitation: **no pressure sensitivity**. If you're a digital artist who relies on varying line weight through pressure, this pen won't cut it. Tilt works, but pressure is absent. One frustrated artist wrote, "Great for notes, but I hit the ceiling fast trying to paint." That's the honest truth — this is a productivity tool, not a pro art instrument.

Also, the charging setup is slightly awkward. The USB-C port sits at the bottom, and you'll need to remove the cap to plug it in. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's less elegant than magnetic charging.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students and professionals** who take handwritten notes, annotate PDFs, or mark up documents — this is your sweet spot.
- **Casual doodlers** who sketch occasionally but don't need pressure-sensitive precision.
- **Budget-conscious iPad owners** with 2018-2025 models who want reliable palm rejection without paying Apple's premium.

**Skip it if** you're a working illustrator, graphic designer, or serious digital painter. The lack of pressure sensitivity will frustrate you within a week. Spend the extra money on the genuine Apple Pencil 2.
