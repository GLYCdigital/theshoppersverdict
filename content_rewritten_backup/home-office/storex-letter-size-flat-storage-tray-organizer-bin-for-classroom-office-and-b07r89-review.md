**The $10 Desk Organizer That Classrooms Swear By — Storex Flat Storage Tray Review**

Let me guess. You've bought those flimsy mesh trays that sag after three months. Or the wooden ones that cost $40 and still warp. The Storex Letter Size Flat Storage Tray is neither. At roughly $10–$15 per tray depending on pack size, this is the pragmatic middle ground that 229 Amazon reviewers have pushed to a 4.6-star average. Here's the honest breakdown before you add to cart.

**What You Actually Get**

Each tray measures 13" x 10.75" x 2.25" — sized for standard letter paper (8.5x11) to sit flat without edge curl. The polypropylene plastic is surprisingly rigid, especially at the corners where extra reinforcement ribs run along the base. You can stack them via interlocking tabs on the bottom that seat into the tray below; no separate frame or riser needed. The open front gives you finger access to grab documents, while the back and sides are enclosed to keep papers from sliding out.

**Real-World Performance**

I've seen these in actual classrooms, not just staged product shots. Teachers stack them five-high on countertops for daily worksheet distribution. The interlocking mechanism holds firm — there's no wobble when you pull a full tray forward, though the whole tower moves if you're not holding the base. The plastic resists the acetone in dry-erase markers and survives drops from desk height without cracking. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "Bought 12 for my classroom. They stack perfectly and hold up to constant use from 6-year-olds." That's the durability testimonial that matters.

**Where It Falls Short**

The open front is a double-edged sword. It's great for access, but there's no lid option. Dust, paperclips, and rogue eraser shavings settle on your papers. If you're storing items long-term, this is a dealbreaker. Also, the lightweight construction (about 8 ounces) means a fully loaded tray will slide across a smooth desk when you pull papers from the front. On carpet or rubber mats it's fine; on glass or laminate, anchor the bottom tray or accept the shuffle.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Teachers**: This is the target user. Stack them for daily handouts, homework collection, or center rotations. The colors (black, blue, red, green) help color-code subjects.
- **Home office organizers**: Perfect for "action files" — bills to pay, forms to sign, items you need visible daily.
- **Crafters**: Holds 12x12 scrapbook paper flat, though the 2.25" depth limits bulky embellishments.
- **Skip it if**: You need dust protection, archival storage, or you're organizing thick binders — these are flat trays, not file boxes.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Can these stack without a separate riser?**
A: Yes. The bottom of each tray has molded channels that lock onto the tray below. Five-high stacks are stable, but don't go past six without a wall or frame for support.

**Q: Will they fit hanging file folders?**
A: No. These are flat trays for loose paper, not hanging file systems. The 2.25" depth is too shallow for Pendaflex folders.

**Q: Are the colored trays the same plastic as the black ones?**
A: Yes, same polypropylene. Colors are molded through, not painted — they won't scratch off with use.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need sturdy, stackable, visible-access paper organization and don't mind the lack of a lid. **Skip it if** you need dust protection or archival storage.

This is a workhorse product that does exactly what it claims with no gimmicks. The 4.6 rating is earned — not inflated by marketing. For the price, it's hard to beat. I'd rate it 4.6/5, docking a half-point for the missing lid option and slide-prone base on slick surfaces.
