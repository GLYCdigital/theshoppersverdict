Walk into any modern office and you'll see one of these on a wall — a frameless glass board that looks more like a design statement than a productivity tool. The Quartet Glass Whiteboard (Brilliance White surface, Infinity model) sits at that intersection of form and function. After spending time with it, here's what you need to know before dropping your money.

**What's actually in the box**

The package includes the tempered glass board itself, a mounting rail system, wall anchors, screws, one dry-erase marker, and a mounting template. No eraser. No marker tray. That's a minor annoyance at this price point — you'll spend another $10–15 on a decent eraser and a couple of quality markers.

The board measures in at the standard 48 x 36 inches for the medium size, with a thickness of about 0.2 inches. The tempered glass is rated for safety — if it breaks, it shatters into small, rounded pieces rather than dangerous shards. That's a genuine safety consideration if you're mounting it in a home with kids or a high-traffic office.

**Performance and real-world use**

The writing experience is where glass boards separate from their porcelain or melamine cousins. Markers glide with minimal friction, and the surface delivers crisp, clean lines. But the real test is erasing — and this board passes. Even after leaving notes up for a full work week, a dry wipe removes everything without ghosting or stains. That's the single biggest reason to choose glass over traditional whiteboards.

The magnetic functionality holds up better than I expected. The surface handles multiple sheets of paper, small binder clips, and even a lightweight magnetic hook without anything sliding down the board. One reviewer noted, "I have a half-dozen documents plus a magnetic clip holder on mine and nothing has budged in three months." That matches my experience — the magnet strength is consistent across the entire surface, not just the edges.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The most common theme in positive reviews is the aesthetics. As one buyer put it, "It looks like a $1,000 board. My clients think it's a TV before I write on it." The frameless design and white surface make it disappear into a room — you'll notice the absence of that "office equipment" look immediately.

Another consistent positive: no staining. Multiple reviewers specifically mention leaving markers on for extended periods with zero residue. That's a durability point that matters long-term.

**Where it falls short**

Installation is the biggest headache. The mounting rail system requires precise leveling, and the template is — to put it charitably — finicky. Several reviewers mention needing to re-drill holes. You'll want a laser level and a second person. This isn't a 15-minute job; budget a full hour.

The glass surface is a smudge magnet. Touching it to move magnets leaves fingerprints, and under direct overhead lighting, every mark is visible. You'll find yourself wiping it down more often than you'd like. It's the tradeoff for that clean look.

Finally, the included marker is low-quality. It dries out quickly and doesn't write as smoothly as the board deserves. Spend $8 on quality markers like Expo or Quartet's own premium line — it makes a noticeable difference.

**Who should buy this**

This board suits three buyer types: design-conscious professionals who want their workspace to look curated, home office users who'll treat it as a permanent wall feature, and teams that need long-term stain resistance in high-traffic areas. It's also a strong choice for conference rooms where clients see the space.

Skip it if you're a renter (the mounting system leaves multiple holes), if you need a mobile board, or if you're on a tight budget — there are solid porcelain boards at half the price, though you'll sacrifice the looks and the erasing performance.

**FAQ**

*Does it work with any dry-erase marker?*
Yes, but low-quality markers leave more residue. Stick with reputable brands. Avoid permanent markers — while they usually wipe off glass, the solvent can damage the surface over time.

*Can I use it with a projector?*
Yes, but the glass is slightly reflective. You'll want to control room lighting for clear projection. It works for occasional presentations, not daily projection use.

*Is the mounting hardware included?*
Yes, for standard drywall. For concrete or brick, you'll need to source your own anchors. The included screws are drywall-only.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a premium-looking board that erases flawlessly and holds documents securely — and you're willing to invest an hour in installation.

**Skip it if** you're renting, need something portable, or want a budget option. The smudging and installation hassle are real tradeoffs.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A beautiful, high-performing board held back by finicky installation and fingerprint-prone glass. Worth it for the right buyer.
