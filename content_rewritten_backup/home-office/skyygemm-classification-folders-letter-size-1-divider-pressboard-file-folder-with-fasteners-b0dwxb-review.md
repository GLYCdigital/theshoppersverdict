# Skyygemm Classification Folders Letter Size, 1 Divider Pressboard File Folder with Fasteners Review

**The Short Version:** If your filing system has devolved into a chaotic pile of loose papers, the Skyygemm Classification Folder with its built-in pressboard divider and metal fasteners might be the organizational intervention you need. It's a straightforward, no-nonsense solution for keeping related documents together without the bulk of a full binder system.

---

## What You Actually Get

The Skyygemm folder is a letter-size (8.5" x 11") classification folder made from sturdy pressboard material. The key feature is the **1 divider press** — a built-in internal divider that splits the folder into two distinct sections. Each section comes equipped with **metal fasteners** (the prong-style brads) so you can punch and secure documents directly into the folder.

This is a "1 divider" model, meaning you get two compartments for filing. It's designed for situations where you need to separate two categories of documents within a single file — for example, "Invoices" on one side and "Receipts" on the other, or "Client Contracts" and "Correspondence."

## Performance and Build Quality

The pressboard material is the standout here. It's noticeably more rigid than your standard manila folder — think of it as the difference between cardboard and cardstock. This means it holds up to repeated handling, survives being stuffed into crowded drawers, and won't flop over when you're trying to pull it out of a filing cabinet.

The fasteners are metal, not plastic, which matters for longevity. Plastic prongs tend to crack after a few months of regular use; metal ones hold their shape and grip documents securely. The fastener heads sit flush enough that the folder doesn't feel bulky when stacked with others.

One thing worth noting: The divider is *inside* the folder, not a tab that sticks out. This keeps the profile slim but means you'll need to rely on the folder's main tab for labeling. If you were hoping for a visible internal separation marker, that's not what this is.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations:

- **The fasteners require a 2-hole punch** — If you don't have one, you'll need to buy one. The folder doesn't come with pre-punched holes, and the fastener posts are positioned for standard 2.75" spacing.
- **No expansion capacity** — Unlike accordion-style folders, this is flat. You won't be cramming 200 pages into it successfully.
- **The divider adds thickness** — At roughly 50% thicker than a standard manila folder, it takes up more drawer space. If you're filing 500 of these, you'll notice the difference.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Small business owners** juggling client files, invoices, and receipts
- **Students** organizing course materials or research papers by topic
- **Home office users** tackling medical records, insurance paperwork, or warranty documents
- **Anyone** who needs to keep two related document categories physically separate but in one place

Skip it if you need heavy-duty archival storage, if you're looking for a folder with pre-punched holes, or if you need more than two compartments.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this folder fit in a standard filing cabinet?**
Yes. It's letter-size and uses the standard top-tab configuration, so it slides right into any vertical or lateral filing system.

**Q: Can I use it without the fasteners if I don't want to punch holes?**
Technically yes — the fasteners sit at the top of each section, so you can just place loose papers behind them without punching. But they'll slide around more, and the folder's design clearly expects you to use the prongs.

**Q: How many sheets can each side hold comfortably?**
Realistically, 30-40 sheets per side before the folder starts to bulge and the fasteners strain. For heavier use, you'd want a binder or expanding file.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a durable, two-compartment folder that keeps related paperwork organized and protected. The pressboard build quality justifies the slightly higher price over standard folders, and the metal fasteners are a genuine upgrade over flimsy plastic alternatives.

**Skip it if** you're looking for a bulk-storage solution, need pre-punched paper compatibility, or prefer the flexibility of a simple pocket folder without fasteners.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid, functional, and built to last. It does exactly what it claims, with minor ergonomic quirks around the fasteners keeping it from a perfect score.
