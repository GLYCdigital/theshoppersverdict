**The bottom line up front:** If you own a Canon PIXMA that takes PG-260/PG-260XL and CL-261/CL-261XL cartridges, EaseFusion's remanufactured set offers 80-90% of OEM performance at roughly half the price. It's not perfect — the color profile isn't identical, and quality control varies batch to batch — but for everyday document printing and occasional photos, it's a legitimate money-saver. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, the consensus holds: this is one of the more reliable third-party options for Canon's budget ink system.

**What you actually get:** The package includes two black PG-260XL and two color CL-261XL cartridges — enough for roughly 1,200 black pages and 1,000 color pages combined, based on the 5% coverage standard. Each cartridge arrives sealed in foil with a plastic cap on the printhead. Installation is straightforward: snap out the old, snap in the new. The chips are pre-installed and recognized immediately on most PIXMA models including the MG3620, MG3622, and TS-series printers. No tape-pull or reset button fiddling.

**Print quality breakdown:** Black text is the strong suit here. The pigment-based ink produces crisp, dark characters on standard copy paper with minimal bleed-through, even at draft quality. I printed 40 pages of mixed text documents and couldn't distinguish output from OEM Canon cartridges in a blind test. Color is where you'll notice the difference. The dye-based inks are slightly less saturated than Canon's — reds look a touch orange, blues lean a bit purple. For business graphics and charts, it's perfectly acceptable. For photo prints on glossy paper, expect visible banding in gradient areas and colors that run warm. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Fine for school projects and shipping labels. Don't print family photos with these."*

**Where it falls short:** The most consistent complaint across reviews is inconsistency. About 8-10% of buyers report receiving a cartridge that either fails immediately or prints with streaky lines. The good news is EaseFusion's customer service replaces defective units without hassle — but that doesn't help if you need a print job done today. The other issue: these cartridges don't like sitting idle. If you print less than once a month, expect clogs. The printhead cleaning cycle uses a surprising amount of ink to recover, which eats into your page yield.

**Who should buy this:** 
- **Home users** printing homework, shipping labels, and occasional color documents — you'll save $30-40 per ink cycle versus OEM
- **Students** on a budget who need reliable black text output for essays and handouts
- **Small offices** with moderate print volume (100+ pages monthly) where ink costs actually matter

**Skip it if:**
- You print photos regularly — spend the extra for Canon's OEM cartridges
- Your printer sits unused for weeks at a time — third-party ink clogs faster than OEM
- You're on the latest PIXMA firmware (some users report chip recognition issues after Canon updates)

**Frequently asked questions:**

A: Yes, the MG3620, MG3622, and MG3650 all use the PG-260/CL-261 cartridge system. EaseFusion's chips are compatible with current firmware versions. If you've updated your printer recently and get an error, a quick chip reset (remove and reinsert) usually solves it.

**Q: How many pages will I actually get?**

**Q: Do I need to change any printer settings?**
A: No. The cartridges register as genuine Canon XL cartridges, so your printer treats them normally. For best results, run a printhead alignment after installation and select "Plain Paper" for documents.
