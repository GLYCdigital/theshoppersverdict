Let's be direct: the **Acer Nitro 60 N60** is a 27-inch 1440p IPS monitor that targets the sweet spot for gamers who want sharpness without paying 4K prices. At 3.8 stars from 190 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but the complaints are consistent enough that you should read this before clicking buy.

## What you actually get

The box contains the monitor, a DisplayPort 1.4 cable, a power cord, and a quick-start guide. No HDMI cable — that's a minor annoyance, but the DP cable is the one you'll want anyway for full 180Hz at 1440p. The stand attaches with a screw-free clip mechanism that's genuinely tool-free. Build quality is typical Acer: functional plastic, nothing premium, but nothing flimsy either.

## Real-world performance

The headline specs are legit: 2560x1440 resolution, 180Hz refresh rate, 1ms VRB response time, and a Fast IPS panel. Colors pop out of the box with decent coverage of the sRGB gamut, and viewing angles are solid — you won't see color shifting when you lean back. Motion clarity at 180Hz is noticeably smoother than a standard 144Hz panel, especially in fast-paced shooters.

Here's the catch, and it's a big one: **peak brightness tops out at 250 nits**. In a dim room, it's fine. Next to a sunny window, you'll be squinting. The HDR badge on the spec sheet is marketing window dressing — at this brightness, HDR content looks washed out. If HDR matters to you, this isn't your monitor.

## What buyers consistently praise

Scanning the 190 ratings, the recurring theme is value. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"For the price, you're getting a 1440p 180Hz panel that performs like monitors costing $100 more. The colors are vibrant and I haven't noticed any ghosting in fast games."* Another noted the zero-dead-pixel guarantee as a deciding factor — Acer will replace the panel if you find even one stuck pixel within the return window, which is rare at this tier.

## Where it falls short

The 250-nit brightness is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. The other recurring gripe: **the stand only tilts**. No height adjustment, no swivel. At 27 inches, that means the center of the screen sits lower than ergonomic guidelines recommend unless you raise it with books or buy a VESA arm. The 2W speakers are also universally panned — one reviewer called them *"barely audible at max volume"* — so budget for separate audio.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a budget-conscious gamer or hybrid worker who wants 1440p clarity and high refresh rates, you game in a controlled lighting environment, and you're willing to spend $30–50 on a VESA arm for proper ergonomics.

**Skip it if:** You work in a bright office, care about HDR content, or need a monitor that works well out of the box without accessory purchases.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Acer Nitro 60 N60 support G-Sync or FreeSync?**
A: It supports AMD FreeSync. G-Sync is not officially certified, though many users report it works in "compatible" mode over DisplayPort. Your mileage may vary with NVIDIA cards.

**Q: Can it run 180Hz over HDMI?**
A: No. The two HDMI 2.0 ports cap at 144Hz at 1440p. You need the DisplayPort 1.4 connection for the full 180Hz. The included DP cable handles this fine.

**Q: Is the 1ms response time real?**
A: It's 1ms VRB (Visual Response Boost), which is a backlight strobing mode that reduces motion blur but can introduce flicker and dim the already-limited brightness. In normal mode, expect around 4–5ms — still fine for most gamers.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5**

The Acer Nitro 60 N60 is a classic "good value, real compromises" product. The 1440p/180Hz combo at this price point is genuinely competitive, and the IPS color quality punches above its weight. But the dim panel, basic stand, and weak speakers mean you're accepting tradeoffs that a $50–80 premium would eliminate on higher-tier models.

**Buy it if** you're on a tight budget, prioritize refresh rate and resolution over brightness, and don't mind a VESA arm purchase. **Skip it if** you need all-day brightness in a lit room or want a monitor that's truly plug-and-play ergonomically. It's a solid 3.7-star monitor — not a steal, but a fair deal for what it delivers.
