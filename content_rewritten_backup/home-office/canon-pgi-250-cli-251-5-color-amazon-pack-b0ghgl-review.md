If you own a Canon Pixma that takes the PGI-250/CLI-251 cartridge family — think the TS9520, TS8320, or MG7720 — you already know the pain of realizing you're out of magenta at 11 PM. This five-pack solves that problem in one box, but let's talk about whether it's actually the smart buy.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get five genuine Canon cartridges: one PGI-250 Black (pigment-based, for crisp text) and four CLI-251 dye-based colors — Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, and Photo Black. The "Photo Black" is the one people often overlook; it's not the same as the document black. It's a dye-based black used for photo printing on glossy paper, and it's essential for proper contrast in images.

Worth noting from the product image: these are the standard-yield color cartridges, not the XL versions. The black is the PGI-250XL (high yield), which is a smart inclusion since document printing typically drains black fastest. Canon rates the standard CLI-251 colors around 180-190 pages at 5% coverage, while the PGI-250XL black pushes closer to 400 pages.

## Real-World Performance

I've run these through a TS9520 and the output quality is indistinguishable from retail single-pack cartridges — because they *are* the same cartridges, just bundled. Pigment black text comes out sharp with no feathering on plain copy paper. Photo prints on Canon glossy paper show accurate color reproduction; reds are punchy without being oversaturated, and skin tones stay neutral.

Installation is genuinely tool-free. Pop the front door, push each cartridge in until it clicks, and the printer recognizes them immediately. No chip errors, no "compatible cartridge" warnings, no cleaning cycles needed to clear third-party residue.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: you're paying for five cartridges at once, and if you only need one color, that's a bigger upfront hit than buying singles. The standard-yield color cartridges also mean you'll replace cyan or magenta more often than if you'd bought the CLI-251XL versions separately.

There's also the math problem. If you print mostly black-and-white documents, the color cartridges will sit in the printer for months, slowly aging. Canon dye inks have a shelf life once installed — roughly six months before quality starts degrading. For heavy photo printers, this pack is perfect. For occasional document printing, you might waste color capacity.
