## The Heavyweight That Does More Than You'd Expect

Most cardstock under $30 falls into one of two camps: too flimsy for real projects, or so textured it jams every printer you own. The Exact Index Cardstock sits in a sweet spot that's genuinely rare. At 90 lb / 163 gsm, it's thick enough to feel like a real product — think business cards, hang tags, or invitation suites — yet stiff enough to feed through a standard laser or inkjet without drama.

## What You Actually Get

Open the carton and you're looking at a full 1000 sheets of bright white (94 brightness) index stock. That's not a typo — a thousand sheets. The weight translates to about 0.0095 inches per sheet, which puts it right between standard copy paper and true cover stock. You get a smooth, uncoated surface that takes toner beautifully and handles most inkjet inks well, though it's not engineered for photo-grade output.

The sheets measure a true 8.5 x 11 inches with clean, square cuts. No ragged edges, no weird sizing variance. For a bulk product at this price point, the consistency is notable.

## Real-World Performance

I ran this through a Brother laser printer, an HP inkjet, and a Cricut cutting machine. Laser printing? Flawless. Text is sharp, graphics are dense, and there's zero ghosting or bleed-through even with heavy coverage. The inkjet did well too — colors looked vibrant on the bright white base — but I did notice slight feathering on saturated photo areas. That's the tradeoff for uncoated stock.

The Cricut cut through it cleanly at standard pressure settings. No tearing, no fuzzy edges. For crafters, that's the difference between a frustrating afternoon and a productive one.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the downsides. This is uncoated cardstock, so if you're printing glossy photos or need water-resistant output, look elsewhere. The brightness is good but not blinding — it's 94, not 98 — so if you're a graphic designer matching Pantone proofs, you'll want a premium coated stock instead.

Double-sided printing on budget inkjets can cause minor curl, especially with heavy ink coverage. Let the sheets flatten under a book for an hour and you're fine, but it's a real quirk.

The packaging is the other weak point. It ships in a flexible carton, and a few reviewers report dinged corners. The sheets themselves arrive pristine, but if you're the type who wants a pristine box for storage, set expectations accordingly.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a teacher making flashcards, a small business owner printing your own business cards or hang tags, a crafter running a Cricut or Silhouette, or anyone who burns through cardstock regularly and wants a reliable, budget-friendly workhorse.

**Skip it if:** You need photo-realistic inkjet output, want a coated or textured surface, or only print occasionally — in that case, a smaller pack of premium stock makes more sense than 1000 sheets.

## FAQ

**Will this jam my printer?**
It's 90 lb, which is within spec for most modern printers. If your printer struggles with heavy media, use the manual feed tray and set your paper type to "cardstock" or "heavy weight." I had zero jams across three different machines.

**Can I use it in a Cricut or Silhouette?**
Yes. It cuts cleanly at standard pressure. Use a fine-point blade for intricate designs and a deep-point blade for heavy scoring.

**Is it double-sided printable?**
Yes, but be mindful of ink saturation. On inkjets, heavy coverage on both sides can cause slight see-through. Laser printing handles double-sided without issue.
