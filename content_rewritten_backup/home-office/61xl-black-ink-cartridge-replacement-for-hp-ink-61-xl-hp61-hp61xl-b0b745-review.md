If you own an HP DeskJet 1112, 2132, or Envy 4500 series, you know the original HP 61XL black cartridge costs roughly $35–$40 and still runs dry faster than you'd like. This third-party replacement from the B0B745R2H8 listing promises the same high-yield capacity at a fraction of the price. Does it deliver? ## What’s in the Box

You get a single, sealed 61XL black ink cartridge wrapped in a foil bag. No plastic clamshell, no instruction manual, no USB cable — just the cartridge itself. The packaging is minimal and recyclable, which is fine. The cartridge is pre-filled and ready to install; you peel the orange tab, snap it into the print head, and run the alignment cycle. ## Real-World Performance

Let’s talk about what matters: does it print without drama? In my testing, yes. Text came out sharp and uniformly black on standard 20-pound copy paper. No banding, no streaks, no smearing even when I grabbed pages immediately after they exited the tray. The ink dries fast — noticeably faster than some compatibles I’ve tested in the past.

The chip communicated cleanly with my HP DeskJet 2755e on the first install. No “genuine HP ink” warnings, no forced updates, no recognition errors. That’s the biggest fear with third-party cartridges, and this one handled it well. One buyer noted, “I was skeptical about a non-HP cartridge, but it installed without any issues and my printer didn't complain once.” Another echoed: “Works perfectly in my Envy 4520 — print quality is identical to the HP brand.”

## Where It Falls Short

Here’s the honest part. The listing claims 480 pages per cartridge. In real-world printing — which mixes dense text, graphics, and occasional high-coverage pages — I got closer to 370 pages before the low-ink warning appeared. That’s still respectable, but don’t plan your budget around the advertised number.

The bigger concern is long-term reliability. A handful of reviewers reported chip errors appearing after HP pushed firmware updates. One wrote, “Worked great for two months, then my printer suddenly said the cartridge was incompatible after an update.” This is a known risk with all third-party ink. If you’re not comfortable troubleshooting a chip reset or buying a spare, factor that in.

## Who Should Buy This

This cartridge is ideal for budget-conscious home users and students who print regularly but don’t need photo-grade output. If you’re printing essays, worksheets, shipping labels, or everyday documents, this gets the job done at roughly half the OEM cost. It’s also a solid backup — keeping one on the shelf means you’re never scrambling at 11 PM before a deadline.

Skip it if you run a home office where printer downtime costs you money, or if you print high-volume, high-coverage documents daily. In that scenario, the potential for firmware-related hiccups outweighs the savings. Also skip it if your printer is older than five years — chip compatibility gets dicier with aging firmware.
