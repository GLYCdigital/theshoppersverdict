The Logitech G PRO X Superlight 2c isn't a mouse. It's a statement. At 51 grams, this thing is so light it feels like a hollow shell in the box — until you plug it in and realize that weight reduction is exactly what makes it disappear in your hand. I've reviewed dozens of gaming mice over the years, and this is the first one that genuinely made me forget I was holding anything at all.

## What's in the box

Logitech keeps it lean: the mouse, a LIGHTSPEED USB receiver, a 1.8m Micro-USB charging cable, and the standard G logo sticker. No carrying case, no extra grips, no weight-tuning kit. That's fine — the Superlight 2c isn't about options, it's about one thing done perfectly. The cable is braided and flexible, which matters more than most people think for charging while playing.

## Performance and real-world feel

The HERO 2 sensor is the star here. Rated at 32,000 DPI with 888 IPS tracking, it's overkill on paper — but the real win is how it handles surface variance. I tested it on a glass desk, a wooden table, and a cheap fabric pad. It tracked flawlessly on all three, with zero spinouts or jitter. That's rare. Most flagship mice get twitchy on glossy surfaces; the Superlight 2c just works.

The 8K polling rate is the other headline feature, and it's not marketing fluff. On a 240Hz monitor, the difference between 1000Hz and 8000Hz polling is noticeable in fast flicks — there's a smoothness to the cursor movement that feels almost analog. That said, you'll need a modern CPU to handle the overhead. If you're on a mid-range laptop from 2020, you might see frame dips in games while it's active.

The shell uses a matte finish that's grippy without being tacky. After four hours of CS2 and Destiny 2, my palm stayed dry and my grip stayed consistent. The main buttons use Logitech's Lightforce hybrid switches — optical actuation with a mechanical feel. They're crisp, with a satisfying click that's quieter than most mechanical switches but still gives clear feedback.

## What buyers consistently praise

The weight is the headline, and reviewers agree. One Amazon buyer put it simply: "Feels like nothing in your hand. I forgot I was holding a mouse." Another noted the battery life: "Charged it once, used it for two weeks of daily gaming, still at 60%." That's the LIGHTSPEED efficiency working as advertised — Logitech claims 95 hours on a full charge, and real-world usage backs that up.

## Where it falls short

The lack of Bluetooth is a real omission. If you're buying this for a hybrid work-gaming setup, you'll be stuck with the dongle — which means carrying it around or leaving it plugged in. For a $200 mouse, that feels like a deliberate cut to protect the pricier Superlight 2 model.

The Micro-USB port is the bigger annoyance. It's 2026. Every other flagship mouse at this price point has USB-C. The cable works, but if you lose it, you're hunting for a legacy cable instead of grabbing any USB-C line you own.

And left-handed users? This mouse doesn't care about you. The side buttons are only on the left side, and the ambidextrous shape is a lie — it's sculpted for righties. Lefties will need to look at the Razer Viper V3 Pro instead.

## Who should buy this

- **Competitive FPS players** who want the lightest possible mouse without sacrificing sensor quality
- **People with larger hands** who find smaller ultralights like the Model O- too cramped
- **Office workers who game** — the 51g weight reduces wrist fatigue during long spreadsheet sessions too

Skip it if you're a lefty, need Bluetooth for multi-device workflows, or you're on a tight budget — the standard Superlight 2 at 60g offers 90% of the performance for less.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Superlight 2c work with Logitech's G Hub software?**
A: Yes, but you'll need the latest version. G Hub handles DPI settings, polling rate, and button remapping. It's also where you enable the 8K polling mode — it's off by default.

**Q: How does it compare to the regular Superlight 2?**
A: The 2c is 9g lighter and has the newer HERO 2 sensor. The regular Superlight 2 uses the older HERO sensor. For FPS players, the 2c feels noticeably faster on flicks. For everyone else, the difference is marginal.

**Q: Is the 8K polling rate worth it?**
A: Only if you have a high-refresh monitor (240Hz+) and a modern CPU. If you're on a 60Hz or 144Hz display, you won't notice the difference. It also drains battery faster, so I recommend keeping it at 1000Hz for daily use.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want the lightest serious gaming mouse money can buy, and you're a right-handed player who values raw performance over extra features.

**Skip it if:** You need Bluetooth, USB-C, or left-handed support — or you're not willing to pay a premium for a 9g weight difference.

The Logitech G PRO X Superlight 2c is a masterclass in focused design. It's not for everyone, but for its target audience — competitive, right-handed FPS players — it's the best mouse on the market. The 4.5-star Amazon rating with 225 reviews reflects that: gamers who get it love it, and anyone expecting versatility will feel shortchanged. At 51g, it's a featherweight that hits like a heavyweight.
