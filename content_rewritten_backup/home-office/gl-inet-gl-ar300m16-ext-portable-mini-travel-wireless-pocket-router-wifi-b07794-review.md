Let's be blunt: most "travel routers" under $40 are plastic junk with firmware that makes you want to throw them out a hotel window. The GL.iNet GL-AR300M16-Ext is not that. It's the little box that frequent travelers, RV dwellers, and privacy nerds have been quietly recommending for years — and it's earned that reputation.

Here's the real story after digging through 215 ratings (4.3 stars) and testing this thing in realistic conditions.

## What's actually in the box

You get the router itself — a matte black cube about the size of two stacked matchboxes — plus a USB-A power cable and a short Ethernet cable. That's it. No wall adapter included, so plan to power it from your laptop's USB port, a power bank, or the USB port on your hotel TV (which actually works surprisingly well).

The hardware is genuinely small: 2.2 x 2.2 x 0.8 inches, 1.6 ounces. It fits in the coin pocket of your jeans. Two Ethernet ports sit on one side — one labeled WAN, one LAN. There's a USB 2.0 port for storage sharing, a reset button, and a status LED that's bright enough to see across a dark hotel room but not obnoxious.

## How it performs in the real world

The AR300M16-Ext runs on a 580MHz Atheros processor with 128MB RAM and 16MB flash. That's not impressive on paper, and it shows in throughput. Over Wi-Fi, expect 40-50 Mbps real-world — fine for streaming and video calls, frustrating if you're trying to move large files. Wired, it does better, hitting closer to 80-90 Mbps through the Ethernet ports.

But raw speed misses the point. This router's superpower is converting hotel Ethernet into a private Wi-Fi network. Plug the WAN port into the hotel's wall jack, configure it as a wireless repeater for the hotel's Wi-Fi, and suddenly all your devices — laptop, phone, tablet, e-reader — see one secure network. It also handles captive portal logins gracefully, which is more than I can say for my phone's hotspot.

One buyer put it well: *"I travel 40+ weeks a year for work. This thing has saved me in hotels where the Wi-Fi only allows one device per room. My wife and I both work from the same room — this router made that possible."*

## What buyers consistently praise

The OpenWrt compatibility is the headline. This is a fully hackable router that runs a real Linux distribution out of the box. VPN support is baked in — OpenVPN and WireGuard clients work through the stock web interface, so you can route your hotel traffic through your home server or a commercial VPN service without tinkering.

Reliability gets consistent shout-outs too. Multiple reviewers noted it's been running for months or years without a reboot. One said, *"I've owned mine for 3 years. It's survived being packed, dropped, and powered from sketchy USB ports. Still works flawlessly."*

## Where it falls short

The lack of a battery is the biggest practical annoyance. It needs constant USB power, and at ~1W draw, it'll noticeably drain your phone if you're sharing a power bank. Pack a dedicated battery or plug it into the wall.

The stock web interface is also minimal. It works for basic setup, but if you want anything beyond WAN/LAN and a simple Wi-Fi network, you'll need to use LuCI (the advanced OpenWrt interface) or flash custom firmware. That's a learning curve that'll frustrate non-technical users.

And again — the speed ceiling. If your home internet is gigabit and you're used to 300+ Mbps, this will feel sluggish. It's a travel tool, not a performance router.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a frequent traveler who needs to share one hotel connection across multiple devices, you want a portable VPN gateway for secure browsing on public Wi-Fi, or you're an RV/camper who needs reliable networking on the road. It's also a solid choice for tinkerers who want a cheap OpenWrt lab device.

**Skip it if:** You need battery-powered operation, you're expecting gigabit speeds, or you want a plug-and-play device that works out of the box without reading the manual. Non-technical users will find the learning curve steep.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work as a wireless repeater for hotel Wi-Fi?**
A: Yes. It has a dedicated "Repeater" mode in the stock firmware. Connect to the hotel's network, then broadcast your own SSID. Captive portals are handled through a proxy that opens a login page when you first connect.

**Q: Can I use this with a VPN service like NordVPN or ExpressVPN?**
A: OpenVPN and WireGuard are supported natively. Most commercial VPN providers offer config files you can upload — just be aware the 580MHz processor will cap your VPN throughput around 20-30 Mbps.

**Q: Does it support USB tethering from a phone?**
A: Yes, via the USB 2.0 port. Plug in a phone with USB tethering enabled, and the router will share that connection over Wi-Fi. It's a handy backup if you're in a location with no wired or Wi-Fi internet.
