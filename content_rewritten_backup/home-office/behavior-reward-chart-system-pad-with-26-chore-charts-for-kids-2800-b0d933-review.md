If you've tried chore charts before, you know the problem: they're either flimsy magnets that slide off the fridge or reusable boards that lose their novelty after week two. The Behavior Reward Chart System takes a different angle—a tear-off pad of 26 individual charts, each designed to run for a full week. That's roughly six months of structure before you need to restock. At 4.5 stars from 225 ratings, it's clearly resonating with families. But is it actually worth your money, or just another parenting gadget that gathers dust? Let's dig in.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes a pad of 26 perforated chart sheets, each measuring roughly 8.5 x 11 inches—standard paper size, so they fit in a binder or on a clipboard easily. The back of the pad is magnetic, so you can slap the whole thing on the fridge and tear sheets off as needed. Each chart has spaces for seven days, with columns for three daily tasks or behaviors. There's also a "reward" box at the bottom where you can write what the child is working toward.

The sheets themselves have a dry-erase coating, which is a clever touch. You can use the included marker to write tasks, then wipe and rewrite if goals change mid-week. One buyer noted, *"I love that I can change the chores every week without printing new charts—just wipe and rewrite."*

## Real-World Performance

The magnetic backing is genuinely strong. I've seen cheaper charts slide off fridge doors the moment a toddler bumps them; this one stays put. The paper is thick enough to survive being handled by small, sticky fingers without tearing at the perforation lines prematurely.

The dry-erase surface works as advertised, though you'll want to let the ink dry for a few seconds before touching it—smudging is possible if you're too eager. The included markers are the weak point here. They're the standard skinny dry-erase pens, and multiple reviewers report they dry out within a couple of weeks. Plan to buy a pack of fine-tip dry-erase markers alongside this.

One structural quirk: the tear-off design means you commit to one chart per week. If your child has a bad Tuesday and you want to restart, you're either burning a sheet or white-knuckling through a week with a mostly-empty reward column. Some parents handle this by using the dry-erase surface to just erase and start over on the same sheet.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 26-sheet count is the most common point of praise. Parents appreciate not having to reorder every few weeks. As one reviewer put it, *"Finally a chart that lasts longer than a month. We're on week 14 and still have plenty left."*

The simplicity also earns points. There's no app, no tokens, no complicated point system—just a visual tracker that kids can understand. Multiple reviewers mention their children actually look forward to filling in the boxes each evening.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the marker issue, there's no built-in reward system. You're writing "ice cream" or "extra screen time" on the bottom, but you're on your own for tracking and delivering. A sticker sheet or star stamps would have made this more complete.

The size is also worth noting. At 8.5 x 11, it's bigger than the typical fridge chart. That's fine for most kitchens, but if your fridge is already covered in artwork and schedules, you might need to clear space.

## Who Should Buy This

This is best for parents of kids aged 3-8 who want a low-tech, visual system for building habits like making the bed, brushing teeth, or picking up toys. It's also solid for teachers running a classroom reward system—the tear-off format works well for rotating weekly focuses.

Skip it if you want a reusable board you never have to replace, or if your child needs more granular tracking (like hourly behavior checks). This is a weekly, three-task system, not a behavioral analytics tool.

## FAQ

**Can I reuse the same chart if my child misses a day?**
Yes. The dry-erase coating lets you wipe the completed days clean and restart. Just don't press too hard or the laminate can scratch.

**Does it come with stickers or stars?**
No. You'll need to supply your own reward markers. A basic star stamp set works great.

**Is the magnetic backing strong enough for a metal door?**
Yes, but it's best on a smooth metal surface. Textured or painted metal can reduce grip slightly.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a straightforward, long-lasting chore system that doesn't require batteries, apps, or constant reordering. The 26-sheet supply and magnetic back make it a practical, no-nonsense tool.

**Skip it if** you're looking for a reusable board or want a self-contained reward system with stickers included.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid, functional, and genuinely useful. Just buy extra markers and a stamp set at the same time.
