If your desk setup is a shrine to clean aesthetics, you’ve likely stared at a standard boom arm and cringed at the industrial clamp and the tangled XLR cable snaking across your mousepad. The Elgato Wave Mic Arm LP is designed specifically for that pain point. It is a low-profile arm, meaning it sits behind your monitor, not above it. The question is whether this $99.99 (check current price) piece of aluminum justifies its premium price tag over a $40 Amazon Basics arm. After spending a week with it, the answer is nuanced: it’s the best-looking arm for a specific setup, but it isn’t for everyone.

### What You Actually Get

Unboxing the Wave Mic Arm LP feels premium. You get the arm itself (constructed of full aluminum), a desk clamp that supports surfaces up to 1.8 inches thick, a C-clamp, and a magnetic cable management system. Unlike the standard Wave Arm, the LP uses a horizontal, folding design with a 90-degree pivot at the base. The included magnetic clips are a nice touch—they snap onto the arm’s channels and hold your USB or XLR cable securely out of sight.

One thing to note: there is **no internal cable routing channel** like you see on the Rode PSA1+. The cable runs *along* the underside of the arm, held by those magnets. It looks clean, but it’s not invisible.

### Build Quality and Real-World Use

This thing is solid. The aluminum extrusion is thick, and the desk clamp uses a rubberized pad that won’t mar your desk. The tension adjustment is tool-free—you twist a knob to set the weight capacity (up to 2.2 pounds). That’s enough for a Shure SM7B or Elgato Wave:3, but you’ll struggle with a heavy condenser like an AT2020 with a shock mount.

In practice, the LP’s low-profile design is a game-changer for monitor risers. It slides under a raised monitor, positioning the mic just below your eyeline. This gets the mic out of your camera’s frame entirely, which is perfect for streamers who use a top-down or desk-level camera angle.

The movement is smooth but stiff. I found the horizontal reach to be slightly limited compared to a traditional boom. You get about 13 inches of reach, which is fine for a single-person setup, but you won’t be swinging this between two positions easily.

### Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. **The spring tension is not adjustable for vertical spring weight.** You turn a knob, but it’s a single screw that locks the arm in place—it doesn’t fine-tune the lift counterbalance like a $200+ arm. If your mic is on the lighter side (under 1.5 pounds), the arm will drift upward slightly. You’ll need to tighten the pivot bolt with a hex key (included) to compensate.

Also, the **fold-flat design eats desk depth**. If your desk is shallow (under 24 inches), the base clamp will stick out and hit your chest. You need at least 4 inches of clearance behind your monitor for the arm to fold flat. This is not a zero-clearance arm.

### Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You use a monitor riser or your monitor sits on a stand, and you want the mic out of your camera frame.
- You value cable management aesthetics over raw reach.
- You stream from a desk-level camera angle.

**Skip it if:**
- You have a shallow desk (under 24 inches deep).
- You need to swing the mic out of the way frequently—the LP is stiff and awkward to move laterally.
- You own a heavy condenser mic over 2.2 pounds.

### FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**1. Does it fit the Elgato Wave:3 without an adapter?**
Yes. The included 3/8" and 5/8" thread adapters fit the Wave:3, Wave DX, and most standard shock mounts. It’s a universal mount.

**2. Can I use it with a vertical mic position?**
Yes, but the low-profile design makes vertical positioning tricky. You’ll likely need to fold the arm up to a 45-degree angle, which defeats the low-profile purpose. It’s best used horizontally.

**3. Does the clamp scratch my desk?**
No. The bottom pad is a dense rubber, and the top plate has a clear silicone strip. I saw zero marks on a walnut desk after a week.

### The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Elgato Wave Mic Arm LP is a specialized tool. It’s not the most versatile arm on the market, but it nails its specific use case: a clean, low-profile setup for streamers and podcasters who value desk space and aesthetics. The build quality is exceptional, and the magnetic cable management is the best in its price class.

The only real barriers are desk depth and mic weight. If you have a standard 24-inch+ desk and a dynamic mic, this is the best low-profile arm under $150. If you need maximum flexibility or have a shallow desk, look at the standard Wave Arm or the Rode PSA1+ instead.
