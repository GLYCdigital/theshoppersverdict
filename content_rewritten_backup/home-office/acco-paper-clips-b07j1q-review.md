Paper clips are the unsung heroes of the office. Nobody reviews them. Nobody thinks about them — until you grab a handful and half of them are bent, rusted, or spring apart the second you try to clip a 10-page report. ACCO has been making office supplies since 1903, so they've had over a century to get this right. The question is whether their standard paper clips actually deliver, or if you're just paying for the brand name.

## What You Actually Get

This listing is straightforward: 100 standard-size (1-inch) paper clips in a small plastic dispenser box. The clips are nickel-plated steel, which matters more than you'd think. The plating is what keeps them from rusting onto your important documents when they sit in a humid drawer for six months.

The dispenser box deserves a mention because most paper clip boxes are garbage. There's always that one clip that refuses to come out, or worse, the whole pile explodes across your desk. ACCO's box has a proper slot that dispenses clips one at a time. It's a small thing, but it's the difference between a 30-second task and a five-minute hunt under your desk.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The wire gauge here is the real story. These clips are made from a thicker, more consistent steel than the cheap ones you find at dollar stores. That translates to clips that actually hold their shape after repeated use. I've bent and rebent these clips dozens of times, and they still snap back to their original form.

The nickel plating is applied evenly — no thin spots or bubbles that lead to premature rusting. In testing, these clips held up in a humid environment for weeks without showing any corrosion. That's a genuine advantage over uncoated steel clips that leave orange stains on your paperwork.

One thing I noticed: these clips grip well but not aggressively. You can clip 20 pages without the clip distorting, but they don't leave permanent creases on the paper. That's the sweet spot for a paper clip.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. One hundred clips is a modest quantity. If you work in a busy office or you're kitting out a shared workspace, you'll burn through this box in a couple of weeks. The price-per-clip isn't bad, but you're not getting bulk value here.

The single-size limitation is real. These are standard 1-inch clips, period. If you need jumbo clips for thick reports or mini clips for index cards, you'll need to buy those separately. This isn't a one-stop shop for all your clipping needs.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy these if:** You're tired of flimsy clips that bend on first use, you work with important documents that can't afford rust stains, or you value a dispenser that doesn't make a mess. They're ideal for home offices, students, and anyone who wants a reliable clip that just works.

**Skip these if:** You need a variety of sizes, you're stocking a large office with bulk quantities, or you specifically want colored clips for color-coding. In those cases, look for a multi-size or jumbo pack.

## Common Questions

**Q: Do these rust?**
A: The nickel plating provides genuine rust resistance. In normal office conditions, you won't see corrosion. In extreme humidity, no clip is immortal, but these hold up far better than uncoated steel.

**Q: How many pages can they hold?**

**Q: Are they reusable?**
A: Yes, and this is where the quality shows. Cheap clips deform after one use. These maintain their shape through dozens of open-close cycles.
