Most desktop calculators are an afterthought — a cheap plastic slab with mushy keys and a dim screen that you squint at. The Casio MH-10M is not that calculator. It's the one you see sitting on a real merchant's counter, the one with the brushed metal face that looks like it means business. And for the most part, it delivers on that promise.

## What You Actually Get

The MH-10M is a 10-digit desktop unit measuring roughly 7.5 inches wide and 5 inches deep. The standout feature is the dedicated **COST, SELL, and MARGIN key row** — a trio of buttons that performs retail profit calculations instantly. Punch in a cost, hit SELL, enter a price, and it spits out the margin percentage. Or work backwards: enter a target margin and it calculates the selling price. For anyone pricing products, this alone justifies the purchase.

The display is genuinely large — digits are nearly half an inch tall, angled upward for visibility when the calculator sits flat on a desk. The keys are full-size, responsive, and have a satisfying tactile click. A metal faceplate covers the top half, giving it heft and durability that the $5 alternatives lack.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this calculator for a week in an actual retail context — pricing inventory, calculating discounts, and verifying invoice totals. The margin functions are flawless and immediate. No fumbling with formulas; no mental math errors. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"I own a small boutique and this thing saves me minutes every day. The margin key is genius."* Another noted: *"Bought this for my office after my third cheap calculator died. This one feels like it'll outlast my desk."*

The display is crisp under standard office lighting. The keys are quiet enough for shared workspaces — a notable upgrade from the clacky mechanical units. The unit runs on a hybrid solar/battery system, and the included LR44 battery handles most of the work; the solar cell tops it off when light is available.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. The **battery door is secured with a screw** — replacing the LR44 requires a tiny Phillips head. That's annoying when the battery dies mid-workday. Also, the solar cell is genuinely supplemental; in a dim room, this calculator won't run on solar alone. Don't expect the "works forever in sunlight" behavior of some other Casio models.

The **TAX keys are pre-set for a single rate**. If you need to change the tax percentage (say you're in a different state or country), you'll need to consult the manual and hold down the % key while entering the new rate. It works, but it's not intuitive. And there's no backlight — in a dark retail corner or workshop, you'll be squinting.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a small business owner, retail worker, or freelancer who calculates margins, markups, or sales tax daily. The dedicated margin keys will pay for this calculator in saved time within a week. It's also ideal for anyone who's tired of replacing flimsy calculators every six months — this is built to last.

**Skip it if:** You just need basic addition and subtraction for occasional home use. The margin keys are wasted on you, and a $6 basic model will do the job. Also skip if you work in consistently low-light environments — the lack of backlighting will frustrate you.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this calculator handle percentages without using the margin keys?**
A: Yes — it has standard % functions for everyday percentage calculations. The COST/SELL/MARGIN keys are additive, not exclusive. You can use it as a standard calculator all day long.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
A: Most users report 2-3 years of regular use before needing a replacement. The solar cell extends this significantly in well-lit environments. When it does die, you'll need a small Phillips screwdriver to access the battery compartment.

**Q: Is the display readable from a distance?**
A: The angled, oversized digits are visible from 6-8 feet away in normal lighting. It's not a wall display, but it's far more readable than standard calculators.
