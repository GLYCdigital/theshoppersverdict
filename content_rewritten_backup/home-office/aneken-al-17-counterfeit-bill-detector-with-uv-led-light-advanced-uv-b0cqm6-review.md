**Bottom line:** If you handle cash even a few hours a week, the Aneken AL-17 pays for itself the first time it catches a fake. But it's not a set-and-forget tool — you need to know your currency.

## What you actually get

The box contains the detector unit, a USB power cable (no wall adapter — you'll use your phone charger or a USB port), and a basic instruction card. The unit itself is compact at roughly 5.5 x 3 x 2 inches and weighs under half a pound, so it's genuinely portable if you run a booth at markets or events. The build is plastic but feels solid — not flimsy. The UV LED array sits under a hinged flap, and the magnetic sensor strip is on the side.

## How it actually performs

This is a dual-detection device, which is the main selling point. The UV light is bright and covers the full bill width — you don't have to reposition the note to check the security thread. It warms up instantly, unlike older fluorescent tube detectors that take 30 seconds to reach full brightness.

The magnetic detection is the more interesting feature. You slide the bill through a slot on the side, and a small LED indicator lights up if the magnetic ink pattern is present. This catches what UV alone misses — some high-quality counterfeits have passable UV features but fail the magnetic test. That said, the magnetic sensor has a learning curve. Slide the bill too fast or at a slight angle, and you'll get a false negative. One reviewer noted, "Took me a week to figure out the right speed — I was ready to return it until I got the hang of it."

Real-world feedback across 225 ratings (4.5 stars) consistently praises the UV brightness. A verified buyer wrote: "The UV light is genuinely strong — I compared it side-by-side with a $200 detector at my bank and there's no practical difference in visibility." Another mentioned using it for foreign currency: "Works fine on euros and pounds, not just dollars."

## Where it falls short

The biggest limitation: **there's no audible or visual counterfeit alarm.** This isn't a "insert bill and get a verdict" machine. It's a verification tool that shows you the security features, and *you* make the call. If you're training new employees who don't know what a genuine bill's UV strip looks like, this won't help them — they'll need to learn the reference first.

The magnetic sensor also isn't universal. Older US bills (pre-1990s) don't have the magnetic strip, so it'll always flag them as suspicious. That's a false positive, not a false negative, but it wastes time if you handle older notes regularly.

One more thing: the unit is USB-powered only. If your register doesn't have a USB port, you'll need an adapter, and the cable is short (about 3 feet). Plan your placement accordingly.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it detect all counterfeit bills?**
No detector catches everything, but the dual UV + magnetic approach covers the two most common US counterfeiting methods. Supernotes (high-quality fakes) can still pass — no consumer device catches those reliably.

**Q: Can it check other currencies?**
Yes, UV security threads exist on most modern currencies. The magnetic detection works on any bill with magnetic ink, which includes euros, pounds, and Canadian dollars. Just know what the genuine features look like for each currency.

**Q: How long does the UV LED last?**
LEDs are rated for roughly 50,000 hours — that's years of daily use. Unlike fluorescent tubes, there's no replacement bulb to buy.
