## What's in the Box

You get the full mounting kit: the gas spring arm, a VESA plate (75x75mm and 100x100mm compatible), C-clamp and grommet mounting hardware, cable management clips, and the necessary Allen wrenches. No power drill required for the C-clamp route — just a screwdriver for the desk bracket. The arm itself is all steel with a matte black finish; it doesn't feel hollow or cheap when you handle it. As shown in the product image, the arm has a clean, low-profile design that looks professional on a desk, not like a robotic appendage from a sci-fi set.

## Real-World Performance

The headline feature is the gas spring mechanism. It's supposed to let you adjust your monitor with one finger, and it mostly delivers. The arm has a 19-inch reach, 15 inches of vertical travel, and a full 360-degree swivel at the base. Tilting, panning, and rotating from landscape to portrait all work smoothly. The key spec to check: maximum weight capacity is 17.6 pounds. A standard 24-inch monitor (around 8-10 lbs) sits right in the sweet spot — the gas spring holds position with zero sag, even after weeks of daily use. A 27-inch gaming monitor near 15 lbs also works, but you'll need to dial in the tension carefully.

One thing buyers consistently praise is the stability. One reviewer noted, "I was skeptical about a budget arm holding my 27-inch Dell, but it hasn't budged in three months. No drooping, no wobble when I type." That tracks with my experience — the arm feels rigid, and the tilt joint doesn't creep downward overnight like cheaper spring-less arms do.

## Where It Falls Short

The VESA plate attaches to the monitor with a single large screw. It's secure, but you'll need a second pair of hands — holding a 15-pound monitor with one hand while threading a screw with the other is an ergonomic nightmare. Plan for a 30-minute install, not the 10 minutes the manual suggests.

The spring tension adjustment is the other quibble. You'll need an Allen key to turn the tension screw, and finding the right setting is trial-and-error. Go too tight and the arm springs up; too loose and it sinks. For monitors under 10 lbs, you'll be fine. For anything pushing 17 lbs, budget extra time to dial it in.

Also, the 19-inch reach is average. If you have a deep standing desk or want to push the monitor far back against a wall, this arm will fall short. It's built for standard 24-30 inch deep desks, not corner setups.

## FAQ

**Will this hold my 32-inch monitor?**
Only if it weighs under 17.6 lbs. Many 32-inch monitors exceed that — check your monitor's specs before buying. If it's a slim 32-inch model around 14-15 lbs, you're fine, but expect extra tension adjustment time.

**Does it fit a desk with a thick edge?**
The C-clamp handles desks up to 3.15 inches thick. If your desk has a metal frame or a lip, use the grommet mount instead — it passes through a hole in the desk surface and is rock-solid.

**Can I rotate my screen to portrait mode?**
Yes, the VESA plate rotates 360 degrees and locks in place. The gas spring holds its position in portrait mode without drooping, though you'll need to rebalance the tension for the different weight distribution.
