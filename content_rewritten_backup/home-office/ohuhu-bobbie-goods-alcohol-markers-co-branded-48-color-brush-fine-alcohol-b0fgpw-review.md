**The 48-color co-brand that actually earns its hype — but read this before you buy**

Most co-branded marker sets are just a sticker swap with a price bump. This Ohuhu x Bobbie Goods collab isn't that. The exclusive pastel-heavy palette genuinely expands what you can do with alcohol markers, and the dual-tip setup (brush + fine) covers both base filling and detailing duties. At 4.9 stars from 245 ratings, it's clearly landing with buyers — but there are a couple of quirks worth knowing about before you commit.

**What's actually in the box**

You get 48 markers in a compact fold-out case, each with a brush nib on one end and a 0.4mm fine tip on the other. The case is the same rigid plastic style Ohuhu uses for its Oahu series — markers click in securely, and the whole thing folds flat for storage. No colorless blender in this set, which is worth noting since the standard Ohuhu 48-set includes one. You'll want to grab that separately if you plan on blending.

The color range skews noticeably softer than Ohuhu's core sets. There are 12 Bobbie Goods exclusive shades — think muted peaches, dusty lavenders, sage greens — that you cannot get in any other Ohuhu 48-pack. If you draw characters, plants, or cozy scenes, this palette is the selling point. If you need heavy-duty primaries and deep shadows, you'll be supplementing with a second set.

**How it performs in real use**

I tested these on 120gsm marker paper, standard copy paper, and a mixed-media sketchbook. The brush nib holds its shape well — it doesn't splay after moderate pressure, and it delivers a controlled stroke from hairline to full-width sweep. The fine tip is crisp with no bleed at the nib base, which matters when you're doing linework in the same pass as fills.

Ink laydown is smooth and consistent. No streaking on the marker paper, and layering two passes gives you a clean gradient without the first layer lifting. On regular copy paper, there's expected ghosting — that's true of any alcohol marker, not a flaw in this set. The chisel-free design means you won't get that wide-edge fill option, but for character art and illustration work, the brush tip is arguably more useful anyway.

**What buyers keep saying**

The 245 ratings at 4.9 stars aren't padded. Common threads in verified reviews: "The brush tip is actually good — not stiff like some cheaper sets," and "The Bobbie Goods colors are adorable, they fill in gaps in my other sets." Several reviewers note that the markers don't dry out quickly even with caps off for a few minutes, which is consistent with Ohuhu's general build quality. The main complaints, when they appear, center on the missing blender and the fact that the case doesn't have a clear window to see colors without opening it.

**Where it falls short**

The absence of a colorless blender is the biggest practical gap. For a set aimed at illustration, blending is a core technique, and you're paying a co-brand premium — include the blender, Ohuhu. Second, the barrel is slightly thicker than classic Ohuhu markers. It's a minor difference, but if you have small hands or grip close to the nib, you'll notice it over a long session. Finally, the pastel-heavy palette means this isn't a standalone set for realistic portraiture or moody landscapes — know what you're buying.

**Who should buy this**

- **Copic/Ohuhu users who want a pastel expansion set** — the 12 exclusive colors are the real draw
- **Character and sticker artists** who need brush-tip control with fine detail work
- **Bobbie Goods fans** who want the aesthetic without sacrificing marker quality
- **Beginners** who want a solid starter set with a forgiving palette

**Skip it if** you need deep, saturated colors for realism or if you already own the standard Ohuhu 48-set and only want a few new shades — the overlap is significant.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these refillable like regular Ohuhu markers?**
A: No, these are disposable. The Oahu series is refillable; this co-branded line is not. Factor that into cost per use if you burn through markers quickly.

**Q: Do the brush tips fray with heavy use?**
A: They hold up well under normal pressure. I've done dozens of illustrations without visible fraying. Hard stabbing or heavy scrubbing will damage any brush nib, so use a light touch.

**Q: Will these bleed through thin sketchbook paper?**
A: Yes, like any alcohol marker. Use 120gsm or thicker, or put a waste sheet behind your work. On standard 80gsm copy paper, expect ghosting on the reverse side.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a high-quality pastel/soft-color set with a genuinely useful brush tip and you appreciate the Bobbie Goods exclusive shades. **Skip it if** you need a versatile standalone set with deep darks and a blender included — get the standard Ohuhu 48-set instead and save the difference.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Excellent markers, slightly incomplete kit. The palette and nib quality justify the price; the missing blender and thicker barrel keep it from a perfect score.
