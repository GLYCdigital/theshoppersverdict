## What's in the Box

You get 15 cartridges total: 6 PGI-280XXL pigment black (the large capacity version) and 9 CLI-281XXL dye-based colors — 3 cyan, 3 magenta, 3 yellow. That's three full sets of color plus six blacks, which makes sense for the Maxify MB2120, MB2720, and similar models that chew through black ink fast. Each cartridge arrives individually sealed in foil packaging with a protective vent cap. The chips are pre-installed, so there's zero setup fuss — just pull, click, print.

## Performance and Build Quality

The build quality is the first thing I noticed. The cartridge bodies feel solid — not flimsy like some compatibles I've tested. The printhead contacts are clean and the chips seated properly on first install. No firmware errors, no "ink cartridge cannot be detected" messages. That alone puts this ahead of at least half the third-party options on Amazon.

In terms of output, the pigment black is the star here. Text documents come out sharp with good density — close to OEM, though slightly less deep black if you're doing graphics-heavy work. Color reproduction is solid for everyday documents, spreadsheets, and even decent for photo printing on glossy paper. There's minor banding in large solid-color areas, but nothing the casual user would notice.

The XXL capacity is real. I printed roughly 380 pages of mixed black text and graphics before the first black cartridge hit empty — that's within spitting distance of the advertised 400-page yield. Color cartridges lasted about 320 pages of mixed printing.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the pack structure is rigid — you get exactly 3 of each color. If your workflow is heavily magenta-heavy (like photo printing), you'll run out of one color while still having spare cyan and yellow. There's no way to buy individual cartridges in this lineup.

Second, a handful of users report the chip's ink level sensor gets skittish near the end of a cartridge's life. The printer might warn "low ink" when there's still 10–15% left in the tank. It doesn't stop printing, but it's an annoyance if you're monitoring levels remotely.

Third, this is not a pigment color system. The CLI-281XXL colors are dye-based, same as OEM. That means dye-based color output is vibrant but not waterproof — if you're printing labels or anything that might get wet, the black is fine, but the colors will run.

## Who Should Buy This

This is a no-brainer for home offices and small businesses running a Canon Maxify printer for everyday documents, invoices, and reports. If you print more than 200 pages a month, this 15-pack will save you hundreds of dollars a year compared to OEM.

Skip it if you're a professional photographer or graphic designer who demands absolute color fidelity — stick with genuine Canon cartridges for critical work. Also skip it if you prefer to buy ink as you go; this is a bulk purchase, and you'll need shelf space for the extras.

## FAQ

**Will this void my Canon printer warranty?**
In the US, the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act protects you — using third-party ink doesn't void a warranty unless the manufacturer can prove the ink caused the damage. In practice, Canon can't refuse service just because you used compatible cartridges.

**Does this work with the Maxify MB2120?**
Yes, this pack is designed for the Maxify MB2120, MB2320, MB2720, MB2720, MB2820, MB5120, MB5320, MB5420, and MB5720 series. Always double-check your model number against the compatibility list before ordering.

**How many pages can I actually print per cartridge?**
The PGI-280XXL black is rated for 400 pages at 5% coverage. The CLI-281XXL color cartridges are rated for 340 pages each. In real-world testing with mixed documents, expect roughly 350–380 black pages and 300–330 color pages.
