**What's in the box**

The set includes four cartridges: one black LC406XL and three color cartridges (cyan, magenta, yellow), all in the high-yield XL format. As shown in the product image, the cartridges come seated in a single plastic tray rather than individual blister packs. The black cartridge is rated for approximately 600 pages, with each color rated around 500 pages — matching Brother's own XL yield claims. That's double the capacity of the standard LC406 cartridges, which matters if you print regularly.

Installation is straightforward. The cartridges use the same chip design as OEM versions, so they click into place in your Brother printer's print head assembly without adapters or modifications. Most users report the printer recognizes them immediately on first install.

**Performance and print quality**

I tested these in a Brother MFC-J995DW, running through a mix of text documents, spreadsheet printouts, and color photos. Black text comes out crisp and dark — no smudging, no streaking, even on plain copy paper. Color output is noticeably vibrant, with good saturation in graphics and photo prints. Skin tones render naturally without the green or magenta cast you sometimes get from budget inks.

One thing that impressed me: the ink dries quickly on glossy photo paper. No waiting around for prints to set before handling them. Longevity seems solid too — prints left in direct sunlight for two weeks showed no visible fading.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the headline. One verified buyer noted, "I was paying $60+ for Brother brand cartridges. These cost half that and print just as well. I've now bought them twice." Another reviewer highlighted the yield: "The XL black cartridge lasted me three months of heavy printing — easily double what the standard cartridges gave me."

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the packaging is minimal — cartridges aren't individually sealed, so they can rattle around in the tray during shipping. In a few cases, this caused minor ink leakage around the print head nozzle. Inspect each cartridge before installing.

Second, Brother occasionally pushes firmware updates that can temporarily confuse third-party chips. If you suddenly see a "Cartridge Error" after an update, remove and reinstall the cartridge, then run a cleaning cycle. It resolves the issue for most users, but it's an occasional inconvenience you won't face with OEM cartridges.

Third, this is a third-party product, so Brother's warranty won't cover any printer damage attributed to these cartridges. In practice, printer damage from ink is rare, but you should know the risk calculus before you buy.
