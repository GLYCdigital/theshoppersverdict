# Amazon Basics 2.4 GHz Wireless Optical Computer Mouse with USB Nano Review

## The $10 Mouse That Does Everything Right (Almost)

Let's be honest: wireless mice are a commodity. You can spend $100 on a gaming mouse with RGB lighting and a charging dock, or you can grab this Amazon Basics wireless mouse for pocket change and get 95% of the functionality. The question isn't whether this mouse works—it's whether it's the right *kind* of basic for your setup.

## What's in the Box

- The mouse itself (ambidextrous design, matte black finish)
- USB nano receiver (stored inside the battery compartment—smart move)
- 1x AA battery (included, pre-installed)
- Quick start guide (you won't need it)

That's it. No software, no drivers, no dongle to lose. Plug the receiver into any USB-A port, flip the switch, and you're moving within seconds. The receiver is so small it sits flush with your laptop's USB port—you can leave it in without worrying about snapping it off in a bag.

## Performance and Build Quality

**The sensor:** Optical, 2.4 GHz wireless, with a 30-foot range. Real-world performance is solid for everyday tasks—scrolling through documents, switching between spreadsheets, browsing. The 1000 DPI optical sensor is nothing special, but it tracks reliably on most surfaces. You'll notice some skip on glossy tables, so keep a mousepad handy if your desk is shiny.

**The click feel:** Here's where Amazon Basics made a smart choice. The buttons have a satisfying tactile click—not mushy, not overly loud. For an office environment, it's quiet enough not to annoy coworkers but audible enough to confirm your input registered. The scroll wheel has distinct detents, which I prefer over free-spinning wheels for document work.

**Battery life:** Amazon claims 12 months on a single AA. In real-world testing, users report getting 6-9 months of heavy use (8+ hours daily). That's still excellent for a device this cheap. The auto-sleep feature kicks in after a few minutes of inactivity, and it wakes instantly when you move.

**Dimensions:** 4.4 x 2.5 x 1.4 inches, 2.9 ounces with battery. It's a compact mouse—not ideal for large hands, but perfect for travel or smaller workspaces.

## Where It Falls Short

**The scroll wheel is loud.** Several users mention it's clackier than premium mice. In a quiet room, you'll hear it.

**No back/forward side buttons.** If you're a power user who lives in a browser, you'll miss these. This is strictly a two-button mouse.

**Ambidextrous means no ergonomic curve.** For all-day use, your hand may fatigue faster than with a sculpted mouse. It's fine for casual use, but if you're logging 8-hour workdays, consider something with better palm support.

**Surface limitations.** The optical sensor struggles on glass and dark glossy surfaces. You'll need a mousepad for those.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Students and budget-conscious shoppers** who need a reliable mouse for classes or dorm-room setups
- **Travelers** who want a disposable-quality mouse they won't cry about losing
- **Office IT managers** buying peripherals in bulk
- **Anyone replacing a dead mouse** who needs something now, without the research rabbit hole

**Skip it if:** You need programmable buttons, ergonomic support for heavy daily use, or you're a gamer. This isn't that mouse.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with Mac?**
Yes. The USB receiver is standard HID—works with macOS, Windows, Chrome OS, and Linux. No drivers needed.

**Q: How do I store the receiver when not in use?**
The battery compartment has a small slot for the nano receiver. You won't lose it—that's a thoughtful design choice.

**Q: Is it a Bluetooth mouse?**
No. It uses a 2.4 GHz wireless connection via the USB receiver, not Bluetooth. If you need Bluetooth for a tablet or device without USB-A, look elsewhere.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a no-nonsense, reliable wireless mouse that doesn't cost more than a lunch. It delivers exactly what it promises, and the battery life makes it a genuinely low-maintenance device.

**Skip it if:** You need side buttons, ergonomic support, or you're sensitive to scroll wheel noise.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Amazon Basics Wireless Mouse is the definition of "good enough" done right. It's not exciting, but it's dependable, and at this price point, that's the only metric that matters.
