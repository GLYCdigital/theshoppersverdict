Let's be straight about this: if you're fishing Ethernet through a wall, you don't want the cheap stuff from a big-box store that claims "in-wall rated" but doesn't have the paperwork to back it up. The Cable Matters CM-rated Cat6 bulk cable is the kind of product you buy because you want to run cable once, pass inspection, and not worry about it again.

**What you're actually getting**

This is bare bulk cable — no connectors, no boots, no tools. Just the spool. As shown in the product image, it's a clean, uniformly wound spool of solid 23AWG bare copper (not copper-clad aluminum, which is a common cost-cutting trick that kills performance at longer runs). The CM rating means it's UL-listed for in-wall installation in residential settings, which is a legal requirement in most jurisdictions when you're running cable inside wall cavities. If you're doing this without the rating, you're rolling the dice on inspection and insurance.

**Performance and real-world use**

I've terminated a fair amount of this cable, and here's what matters: the solid conductors punch down cleanly into keystone jacks and hold a solid connection. The jacket strips back cleanly without nicking the inner pairs. At Cat6 spec, you're good for 10GbE up to 55 meters — more than enough for any home run.

The stiffness is the tradeoff. This is not a patch cable; it's built to stay in a wall, not flex behind a desk. That rigidity is a feature when you're pushing it through conduit or around corners, but it's a pain when you're terminating. If you're using pass-through RJ45 connectors, you'll be fine. If you're old-school with standard plugs, budget extra time per end.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of a spooler is worth noting. If you're pulling 250 feet through an attic, you'll spend ten minutes just managing the coil. Grab a $15 cable spooler and save yourself the headache. Also, this is bare cable — if you're not comfortable terminating your own ends, you'll need to factor in the cost of connectors and a crimping tool, or hire someone who has them.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this actually legal to run inside walls?**
A: Yes. The UL CM listing means it meets the National Electrical Code fire-safety requirements for residential in-wall installation. That's the standard inspectors look for.

**Q: Can I use it outdoors?**
A: No. CM rating is for indoor use only. For exterior runs, you need CMX or burial-rated cable.

**Q: Will this do 10GbE?**
A: Up to 55 meters at 10GbE, and full 1GbE at the full 100-meter spec. Solid copper conductors handle signal integrity properly at distance.
