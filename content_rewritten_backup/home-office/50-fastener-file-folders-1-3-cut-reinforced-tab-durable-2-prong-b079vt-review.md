## What You Actually Get

The package arrives as a stack of 50 letter-size folders, each measuring a standard 9.5 x 11.75 inches. Every folder features a 2-prong metal fastener at the center, designed to secure documents with a metal compressor bar that slides down to hold papers in place. The 1/3-cut tabs are positioned for standard file-cabinet visibility, and critically, they’re *reinforced* — meaning there’s an extra layer of material fused where the tab meets the body, the exact spot where cheap folders tear.

The stock is a 11-point manila-style paper, which translates to roughly 0.009 inches thick. That’s noticeably sturdier than the 9-point folders you’ll find at big-box stores. The surface takes pen and pencil well, and the fastener heads are flush enough that they won’t catch on adjacent files.

## Real-World Performance

I loaded these up with 100-page contracts — the kind of documents that weigh down lesser folders — and the prongs held firm. The compressor bar slides smoothly and locks in place, keeping papers from sliding out even when the folder is pulled from a packed drawer by the tab alone. The reinforced tabs are the standout feature: after 30+ in-and-out cycles from a metal filing cabinet, no fraying, no peeling, no bent corners.

One thing I tested specifically: the glue seam where the prongs attach. On cheap folders, this is where the whole thing disintegrates. After a week of daily use and aggressive page-flipping, the fastener assembly remained rock solid. These are built for active files, not just archival storage.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides exist. First, these are **not** acid-free or archival-grade. If you’re storing documents for decades, the paper will yellow over time — that’s expected for this price tier, but worth knowing. Second, the 2-prong design means you must punch holes in documents to file them. That’s a dealbreaker if you need to store unpunched papers or prefer open-top pockets. Finally, the metal fasteners add bulk; a fully loaded folder takes up noticeably more drawer space than a plain pocket folder. If you’re cramming a 4-drawer cabinet to capacity, this matters.
