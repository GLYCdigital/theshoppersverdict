If you've been hovering your keyboard on your lap or craning your neck at a too-high desk, this under-desk tray from the "Extra Sturdy C Clamp" line is the ergonomic fix you've been putting off. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's not perfect — but for the price point, it solves the biggest home-office problem without requiring a drill or a handyman.

**What's in the Box**

You get the main tray (24" wide, 10" deep), a slide-out mouse platform that extends alongside the keyboard surface, the C-clamp mounting hardware, and rubber padding strips. The metal frame is powder-coated steel, not cheap plastic. The clamp opens to 2 inches, which covers virtually all standard desks — including those thick IKEA butcher-block tops. Assembly takes about 10 minutes with the included hex wrench. No instructions are great, but the diagram is clear enough that you won't need YouTube.

**Real-World Performance**

The claim of "extra sturdy" holds up. The C-clamp grips hard — one reviewer noted, "I have a heavy mechanical keyboard and rest my palms on the tray all day; it doesn't budge." That's the experience most buyers report. The height adjustment range is generous (about 4 inches of travel), and the tilt locks at negative angles up to 15 degrees, which is where your wrists want to be. The slide mechanism isn't ball-bearing, so it's not buttery — but it's smooth enough for daily use. At full extension with a heavier keyboard, there's slight wobble, but nothing that feels unsafe.

**What Buyers Applaud**

The dominant praise is the clamp installation. "No drilling, no damaging my rental desk, and it took five minutes to put on," wrote one verified buyer. Another highlighted the mouse platform: "The extended surface means my mouse isn't dangling off the edge — that alone was worth it." Multiple reviewers with standing desks confirmed it mounts cleanly to both fixed and adjustable surfaces.

**Where It Falls Short**

The clamp is strong, but that strength has a downside. On softer woods, the clamp jaws will leave visible pressure marks. The included rubber pads help, but they're thin — expect indentations if your desk is pine or MDF. The cable management clip is almost an afterthought; it holds one thin cable and won't manage a keyboard plus mouse cord. And if you're left-handed, the mouse platform only extends to the right — there's no mirrored option.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Renters:** You need ergonomic height without drilling holes. This is your tray.
- **Office workers with shoulder pain:** If you're reaching up to a keyboard, this drops it to the correct elbow height.
- **Mechanical keyboard users:** The steel frame handles the weight without sagging.

**Skip it if** you have a glass desk (clamp pressure risks cracking) or a keyboard with a thick wrist rest that pushes the tray's depth limit.

**FAQ**

**Will it fit my standing desk?**
Yes, as long as the desktop is between 0.4 and 2 inches thick. The clamp grips the underside lip cleanly. Just ensure your standing desk's crossbar doesn't block the clamp position.

**Does the mouse pad surface attach to the tray or slide independently?**
It's a fixed extension, not a separate sliding platform. The mouse surface is part of the main tray, so it moves with it.

**Can I install it alone?**
Yes. The clamp holds the tray in place while you tighten the bolts. A second person helps with alignment, but it's not required.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a no-drill ergonomic upgrade with a rock-solid grip and a usable mouse surface. **Skip it if** you're on a glass desk or need left-handed configuration.

The tray earns a 4.2/5. It's not luxury furniture, but it does exactly what it promises — and for most home offices, that's all you need.
