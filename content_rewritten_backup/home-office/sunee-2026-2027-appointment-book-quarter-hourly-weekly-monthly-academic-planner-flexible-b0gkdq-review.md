If you're a therapist, medical professional, or freelancer who lives by 15-minute increments, the SUNEE 2026-2027 Appointment Book deserves a hard look. It's a no-nonsense academic-year planner (July 2026 through June 2027) built for people who need to see their day in granular detail — not just a vague "morning/afternoon" block.

**What you actually get**

The package includes the 8.5" x 11" planner, 12 tabbed monthly dividers, and a storage pocket inside the back cover. The weekly spreads are where this book earns its keep: each day runs from 7:00 AM to 8:00 PM in true quarter-hour increments. That's 52 time slots per day, each with enough vertical space to write a client name and a note without cramping. The layout splits days across two pages, so you get generous real estate per day rather than a cramped week-at-a-glance.

The monthly view sits at the front of each tabbed section with a full-page grid for each month. Reference calendars for the previous and next month appear in the corner — small touch, but genuinely handy when scheduling ahead.

**Build quality and daily use**

The twin-wire binding is the standout feature here. It lays completely flat on a desk, which matters when you're writing with one hand while holding a phone in the other. The 320gsm cover has a subtle linen texture and resists scuffs from being tossed into a bag daily. The monthly tabs are printed on reinforced card stock — one Amazon reviewer noted, "I've had this for three months and the tabs still look brand new, unlike my previous planner where they frayed within weeks."

The paper is 100gsm, which is decent for the price point. Ballpoint and fine-tip gel pens write cleanly with minimal bleed-through. Heavier ink users should note: there's visible ghosting on the reverse side with broad gel nibs, so stick to finer tips if that bothers you.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across 235 ratings at 4.7 stars, the recurring theme is the quarter-hour format. One five-star review captured it well: "Finally a planner that respects my schedule. I'm a massage therapist and book back-to-back sessions — the 15-minute blocks mean I can see my whole day at a glance without doing math." Another buyer highlighted durability: "Survived a full semester in my work bag. Binding intact, cover still clean."

**Where it falls short**

The monthly pages lack time references entirely — they're date grids only. If you use the monthly view to track appointments rather than just deadlines, you'll need to flip to the weekly spread for times. For a book marketed as an appointment planner, that's a miss.

The paper weight also won't satisfy fountain pen enthusiasts. It handles standard ink fine, but if you're a heavy-handed writer or prefer wet inks, expect show-through. Neither issue is dealbreaking, but both are worth knowing before you buy.

**Who should buy this**

This planner fits three specific profiles: healthcare providers booking 15–30 minute patient slots, tutors or instructors running back-to-back sessions, and anyone who tracks billable hours in fractions. It's also a solid choice for students managing a block-scheduled semester. Skip it if you only need a simple daily to-do list — the granular layout will feel like overkill.

**FAQ**

*Does it cover 18 months?*  
No. It runs July 2026 through June 2027 — a standard academic year. If you need calendar-year coverage, this isn't it.

*Is there a ribbon bookmark?*  
Yes, a single ribbon marker is included. However, with 12 tabbed sections, most users rely on the tabs and don't need it.

*Can I remove pages?*  
No. The twin-wire binding is fixed, so pages aren't perforated or removable. If you make errors frequently, consider a disc-bound system instead.

**The Verdict**

Buy it if you need precise quarter-hour scheduling with durable construction at a fair price. The monthly-view limitation and paper weight are real but minor tradeoffs given the core strengths. Skip it if you want removable pages or a simpler daily layout.
