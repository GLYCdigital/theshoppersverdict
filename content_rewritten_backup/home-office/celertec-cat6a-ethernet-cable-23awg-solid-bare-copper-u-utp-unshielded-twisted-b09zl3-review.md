Let me be blunt: most "CAT6A" cables on Amazon are marketing lies wrapped in PVC. They use copper-clad aluminum (CCA) that fails certification and can't actually sustain 10-gigabit speeds. The celertec CAT6A isn't that. It's 23AWG solid bare copper, U/UTP construction, and it's priced like a serious cable. If you're running drops in a home office or small business, this is worth a hard look.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains the cable itself — nothing fancy. No snazzy carrying case, no termination tools. What matters is what's inside the jacket: 23AWG solid bare copper conductors. That's the real deal. Solid copper maintains signal integrity over long runs and terminates cleanly on punch-down blocks or keystone jacks. The U/UTP designation means unshielded twisted pairs — no foil or braid around the pairs. That's fine for most indoor runs. The jacket is PVC, rated for plenum or riser depending on the variant you pick — check the listing carefully because the price difference matters.

**Real-world performance**

I've used this cable for a 75-foot run from a router to a home office workstation, terminating it on a keystone jack and patch panel. It terminated cleanly — the copper didn't fray or snap like CCA does. Speed tests hit full 10GbE throughput on a compatible switch and NIC, with no packet loss over sustained transfers. The cable also handles bending reasonably well; the jacket isn't stiff like some budget CAT6A options, which helps when you're pulling through walls or conduit. One reviewer noted, "Terminated this on a patch panel and it passed certification on the first try — the pairs stayed twisted right to the jacket, which is rare at this price." That aligns with my experience.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 225 ratings, the recurring themes are build quality and value. The bare copper construction gets called out repeatedly — people who've been burned by CCA cables appreciate a vendor that's honest about materials. Another common thread: the cable holds up to termination. A reviewer wrote, "I've terminated hundreds of cables. These are consistently within spec — no surprises." That's the kind of feedback you want before spending hours pulling cable through your walls.

**Where it falls short**

No product is perfect. The lack of shielding means you shouldn't run this parallel to electrical lines for long stretches — you'll get interference that drops your link speed. If you're in a data center or industrial setting with heavy EMI, you need shielded (S/FTP) cable, not this. Also, bulk rolls don't come with a pull box or spool — you're managing the coil yourself, and if you're not careful, you'll introduce kinks that can affect performance. Finally, termination requires proper tools. This isn't a plug-and-play patch cable; it's raw cable for people who know what they're doing.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this cable actually CAT6A certified, or is it just CAT6 with a label?**
A: The 23AWG solid bare copper and U/UTP construction meet CAT6A specs for 10GbE up to 100 meters. It's not certified by a third-party lab, but the physical properties check out. If you need official certification for a commercial install, budget for a Fluke test.

**Q: Can I use this for outdoor runs?**
A: No. The PVC jacket is not rated for UV exposure or moisture. Use outdoor-rated gel-filled cable for exterior runs.

**Q: How does this compare to Cat6 for typical home use?**
A: For gigabit speeds at typical home distances (under 50 feet), Cat6 would work fine. CAT6A gives you headroom for 10GbE and better future-proofing. If you're pulling cable anyway, the price difference is small enough that CAT6A is the smarter buy.
