## The Unsung Hero of Your Filing Cabinet

Nobody thinks about brass fasteners until the moment they need one. Then suddenly the cheap ones bend, the heads catch on everything, and you're holding a mangled prong with a deadline breathing down your neck. The Charles Leonard Round Head Fasteners are the answer to that specific frustration — the kind of office supply you buy once and forget about because they just work.

## What You Actually Get

A box of brass-plated round head fasteners with split prongs. They come in the standard size range you'd expect for filing duty — roughly 2 to 3 inches of prong length depending on which pack you grab. The brass plating is the standout feature here: it's not just a thin wash like some budget options. It's a proper coating that survives repeated open-close cycles without flaking or leaving rust-colored smudges on your documents.

The heads are smooth and domed, which matters more than you'd think. They sit flush against the top sheet, so when you stack folders or slide papers across your desk, nothing catches. The prongs themselves are the split type — they fan open cleanly and fold flat against the back of the file. No sharp edges, no snags.

## Real-World Performance

I've hammered these through manila folders, cardstock, and even the occasional laminated sheet. The split prongs don't bend at the tip when you're pushing them through thick stacks — a common failure point with cheaper fasteners. The brass plating also means they don't seize up or resist folding after sitting in a file for months. One reviewer noted, "These are the only fasteners I've found that don't leave rust marks on my documents after a year in storage." That tracks with what I've seen.

The round head profile is the right call for everyday filing. Flat-head fasteners have a tendency to push through thin paper over time; the dome here distributes pressure better. The tradeoff? They're slightly bulkier in a packed drawer, but you'd have to be extremely space-constrained for that to matter.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest gripes, because nothing's perfect. The packaging is criminally basic — a plain box with a flap that doesn't stay shut. If you're the type who keeps fasteners loose in a drawer divider, you'll want a small container, because these will escape. Also, there's no size printed prominently on the box front. You'll need to read the fine print or measure the prongs yourself. For a product where size matters, that's a small but real annoyance.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for anyone who maintains physical files — administrative assistants, legal offices, medical records staff, teachers managing student paperwork. If you're a home user who fastens a few documents a month, the price difference between these and a cheap generic pack is small enough that you should still go with Charles Leonard. The reliability is worth the extra dollar or two.

Skip these if you need heavy-duty binding for thick reports or if you're looking for a colored finish — this is strictly brass-plated, and it's designed for standard filing depth, not industrial use.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit standard two-hole punched paper?**
A: Yes, the prong spacing matches standard file folder hole patterns. They work with any two-hole punch set to standard 2.75-inch spacing.

**Q: How do I fold the prongs down properly?**
A: Separate the split prongs, push them through the holes, then fold them flat in opposite directions against the back of the folder. They lock in place once folded — no tape needed.

**Q: Are these reusable?**
A: Yes, but repeated bending weakens the metal over time. You'll get several uses per fastener before the prongs lose their grip. Most people treat them as single-use for filing, which is fine at this price.
