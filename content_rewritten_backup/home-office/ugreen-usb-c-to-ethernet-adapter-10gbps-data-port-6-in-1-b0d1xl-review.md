The UGREEN 6-in-1 USB-C hub looks like every other aluminum dongle on Amazon. But the spec sheet tells a different story — the headline here is a **2.5G Ethernet port** and a **10Gbps data port**. Most hubs in this price range settle for 1G Ethernet and 5Gbps data. That difference matters if you're moving large video files or working on a NAS. It's not a revolutionary product, but it's a genuinely faster one.

## What's actually in the box

The hub itself is machined aluminum, roughly the size of a deck of cards, with a permanently attached USB-C cable. The port layout:

- **1× 2.5G Ethernet (RJ45)**
- **1× USB-C data port (10Gbps)**
- **1× USB-A data port (10Gbps)**
- **1× HDMI (4K/30Hz)**
- **1× SD card slot (UHS-I)**
- **1× microSD card slot (UHS-I)**

No power adapter, no case. UGREEN keeps it minimal. The aluminum body is solid — it doesn't flex or creak when you plug things in. It's the kind of build that feels like it'll survive a few years in a laptop bag.

## Real-world performance

The 2.5G Ethernet port is the star. Plugged into a network with a 2.5G switch (and a router that supports it), I saw sustained transfer speeds around **280 MB/s** — roughly 2.5× faster than standard gigabit adapters. If you're on a 1G network, you'll still get full gigabit speeds, so it's future-proofing. But here's the honest catch: most home networks don't have 2.5G infrastructure yet. You're paying for headroom, not immediate glory.

The 10Gbps data port is more universally useful. Moving a 20GB folder of RAW photos to an external SSD took about 90 seconds. That's noticeably quicker than the 5Gbps ports on cheaper hubs. The USB-A port is also 10Gbps, which is rare — most hubs give you one fast port and one slow one.

The HDMI port outputs 4K at 30Hz. That's fine for presentations and second monitors, but if you're hooking up a 4K display for gaming or video editing, the 30Hz refresh will feel stuttery. UGREEN sells other hubs with 4K/60Hz, so this is a deliberate spec cut to keep costs down.

## What buyers consistently praise

The Amazon reviews at 4.4/5 across 220 ratings highlight two things repeatedly: **speed** and **build quality**. One reviewer wrote, "Finally a hub that doesn't throttle when I'm transferring large files. The 10Gbps port is the real deal." ## Where it falls short

**No Power Delivery passthrough.** If you're using this with a laptop that charges via USB-C, you'll need a separate port for your charger. That's a dealbreaker for some — you can't run a single-cable setup for power + peripherals.

**The cable is short.** At about 5.9 inches, it's fine for a laptop's side port, but if your USB-C port is on the back of a desktop or in an awkward spot, you'll struggle.

**4K/30Hz HDMI.** As mentioned, this feels dated. It works, but it's not great for high-refresh displays.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a photographer or videographer moving large files regularly, you have a 2.5G-capable network, or you want a hub that won't become a bottleneck for years.

**Skip it if:** You need to charge your laptop through the hub, you're on a tight budget and only need basic ports, or you demand 4K/60Hz video output.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with my MacBook/Windows laptop?**
A: Yes, it's plug-and-play on both macOS and Windows 10/11. No drivers needed. Works with any USB-C port that supports DisplayPort Alt Mode for video output.

**Q: Can I use the Ethernet port and data ports simultaneously?**
A: Yes, all ports work at the same time. The 2.5G Ethernet doesn't share bandwidth with the data ports — that's the advantage of a properly designed hub.

**Q: Is the SD card reader fast enough for professional work?**
A: It's UHS-I, so you'll get around 100 MB/s. Fine for importing photos, but if you shoot with UHS-II cards, you won't see their full speed. You'd need a dedicated card reader for that.
