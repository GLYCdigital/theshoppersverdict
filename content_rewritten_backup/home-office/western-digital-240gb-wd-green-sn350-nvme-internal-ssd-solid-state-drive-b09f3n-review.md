Let's be direct: at $74 for 240GB, this drive isn't winning any capacity-per-dollar awards. But if you're stuck with a mechanical hard drive or an aging SATA SSD, the WD Green SN350 is the cheapest way to jump into NVMe speeds — and for office work, that's exactly the upgrade that makes an old machine feel new.

## What's in the box

WD keeps it minimal: the M.2 2280 drive itself, a quick-start guide, and not much else. No mounting screw (check your motherboard — most include one), no cloning software license, no thermal pad. As shown in the product image, it's the standard single-sided stick design, which means it'll fit in slim laptops and compact desktops without clearance issues.

## Real-world performance

Specs say 2,400 MB/s sequential read. That's honest — CrystalDiskMark shows around 2,300 MB/s on a typical NVMe slot. But here's what actually matters in an office context: Windows boots in about 15 seconds from cold, Excel and Chrome launch instantly, and large PDFs open before you finish clicking.

The catch is sustained writes. This drive has no DRAM cache (something WD doesn't advertise prominently), so once the pseudo-SLC cache fills — roughly 20-40GB of continuous writing — speeds drop to around 300-400 MB/s. For daily office work, you'll never notice. For video editing or moving 50GB project folders, you will.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings is unusually strong for a budget component. Recurring themes in customer feedback:

- **Instant boot times**: "My old laptop went from 3-minute boot to 20 seconds. Best $70 I've spent on it."
- **Easy installation**: Multiple reviewers note it took under 10 minutes to install and clone their old drive.
- **Reliability**: Several long-term owners report zero issues after 6+ months of daily use.

## Where it falls short

The 240GB capacity is the elephant in the room. After Windows, Office, and a few essential apps, you're looking at roughly 80-100GB of free space. That's manageable if you're disciplined about cloud storage or external drives — genuinely frustrating if you hoard files locally.

Also worth noting: this is a QLC drive (quad-level cell NAND). That's fine for office workloads, but if you're planning heavy gaming installs or creative work, step up to the WD Blue SN580 or a DRAM-equipped drive. The extra $20-30 buys meaningful headroom.

## Who should buy this

- **Budget laptop owners** with a free M.2 slot — this is the cheapest meaningful speed upgrade available
- **Office workers** running standard productivity apps who want faster boots and app launches
- **Home users** building a basic PC where every dollar counts

**Skip it if** you're storing large media files, doing video editing, or want room for multiple AAA games. The 1TB version of this drive exists, but at that price point, a DRAM-equipped competitor makes more sense.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work in my older laptop?**
A: If your laptop has an M.2 slot and supports NVMe (check your manual — most 2018+ models do), yes. It will not work in SATA-only M.2 slots.

**Q: Do I need a heatsink?**
A: No. Office workloads rarely push this drive past 60°C, and it's rated for those temps without active cooling.

**Q: Can I clone my existing drive?**
A: Yes, but WD doesn't include cloning software. Use free options like Macrium Reflect or Samsung Data Migration (works with non-Samsung drives, despite the name).

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want the cheapest possible NVMe upgrade for an office machine and don't need massive storage. **Skip it if** you need capacity above 240GB or do sustained large-file transfers.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses points only on capacity and the missing DRAM cache, both of which are fair tradeoffs at this price point.
