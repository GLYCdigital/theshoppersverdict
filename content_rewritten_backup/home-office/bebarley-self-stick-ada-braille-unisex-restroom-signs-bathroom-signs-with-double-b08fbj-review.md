Most ADA-compliant restroom signs require a drill, anchors, and a level of patience most of us don't have on a Tuesday afternoon. The Bebarley Self-Stick version eliminates all of that. But the real question isn't whether the adhesive works — it's whether a stick-on sign can hold up to daily abuse in a public or semi-public space. After digging through 240 ratings (4.8 stars), the answer is mostly yes, with a few honest caveats.

The package includes one 6" x 6" unisex restroom sign with raised tactile lettering and Grade 2 Braille. ## Real-World Performance

The adhesive is the star here. It's a 3M-style industrial foam tape, and multiple long-term reviewers confirm it holds firmly to painted drywall, metal doors, and even textured surfaces. One reviewer noted: *"Stuck it on a hollow core door three months ago. Still hasn't budged, even with kids swinging it open hard."* That matches the general consensus — the bond is strong and doesn't degrade quickly.

The tactile lettering is genuinely raised, not just printed. Running a finger across the sign, you can feel the characters clearly. Braille dots are properly spaced and sized per ADA standards. This isn't a decorative sign pretending to be compliant — it checks the functional boxes.

Visually, the blue-on-white color scheme is clean and legible from across a hallway. The 6x6 size is slightly smaller than the traditional 8x8 ADA sign, but it's within code for most applications.

## Where It Falls Short

**The adhesive is permanent.** Once it's on, it's on. If you misalign the sign during installation, you're not repositioning it without damaging the surface or the sign itself. Measure twice, stick once.

**Not ideal for extreme environments.** A handful of reviewers used these in outdoor or semi-outdoor settings (uncovered breezeways, garage restrooms) and reported peeling within a few months. Temperature swings and moisture compromise the adhesive. Indoors, it's fine. Outdoors, buy a screw-mounted sign.

**The 6x6 footprint is slightly non-standard.** If you're replacing an existing sign with a larger footprint, the old screw holes or paint marks will show around the edges. Check your current sign's dimensions before ordering.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this actually ADA compliant?**
Yes, for tactile lettering, Braille placement, and contrast. The 6x6 size meets minimum requirements, though some inspectors prefer larger signs. Check your local code if you're in a strict jurisdiction.

**Q: Will it stick to a painted metal door?**
Most users report success on metal doors, including hollow-core units. Clean the surface with isopropyl alcohol first and let it dry completely. That's the difference between a sign that stays and one that slides.

**Q: Can I remove it without damaging the wall?**
No. This is a permanent adhesive. Removal will likely pull paint or drywall paper. Plan your placement carefully.
