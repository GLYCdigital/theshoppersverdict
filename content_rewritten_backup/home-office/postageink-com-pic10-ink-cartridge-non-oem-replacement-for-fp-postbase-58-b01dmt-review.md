## The $50 Question: Can a Third-Party Postage Cartridge Actually Deliver?

If you own an FP PostBase postage meter, you already know the pain: the OEM ink cartridges cost a small fortune, and running out means your mail operation grinds to a halt. The PostageInk.com PIC10 cartridge promises the same prints at a fraction of the price. After digging through 190 ratings and user experiences, here's the real story.

## What's in the Box

The package includes either one or two PIC10 cartridges (depending on which listing you choose), plus a plastic reset tool specifically designed for the FP PostBase meter. The cartridge itself is a straightforward drop-in replacement — same form factor, same connector, no adapter needed. The included reset tool is the smart part: it lets you clear the meter's "replace cartridge" warning without calling FP's support line, which saves you a 15-minute phone hold.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where it gets complicated. The cartridge installs cleanly and most users report it prints acceptably at first. The ink is pigment-based and handles standard envelope stock fine. But the consistency issues start to show within the first few dozen prints.

The most common complaints cluster around two things: yield and print quality over time. Several verified buyers noted the cartridge ran dry noticeably faster than the FP original — one user put it bluntly: "Got maybe 60% of the prints compared to the genuine FP cartridge. The math still works out cheaper, but not by as much as I hoped."

Print quality is another mixed bag. Some users report crisp, dark impressions that rival OEM output. Others describe light or streaky prints after the first 30-50 stamps, especially on glossy or coated envelopes. If you're printing hundreds of mail pieces a day, this inconsistency is a real operational risk.

## What Buyers Actually Say

The positive reviews focus on the obvious value proposition. One five-star review noted: "Half the price of the FP cartridge and works fine for my small business. The reset tool is a lifesaver." Another mentioned: "Two-pack means I don't have to think about reordering for months."

The negative reviews are equally specific. A one-star review cited a cartridge that leaked inside the meter, while others mentioned receiving cartridges that were dead on arrival — no ink flow at all. The pattern suggests quality control isn't as tight as FP's own manufacturing, which is the risk you accept with any third-party consumable.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the yield and QC issues, there's the warranty gap. FP backs their cartridges with their own support; PostageInk relies on Amazon's return window. If a cartridge fails at week five, you're eating that cost. Also, the reset tool works on the PostBase, but if you have a different FP model, double-check compatibility before ordering.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Small business owners** with low-to-moderate mail volume (under 100 pieces per day) who want to cut costs without major risk
- **Home office users** who rarely run the meter dry and can tolerate occasional quality hiccups
- **Cost-conscious buyers** who already understand third-party consumables are a trade-off

**Skip it if** you run high mail volume where a mid-batch cartridge failure costs you time and money, or if you need consistent print quality for client-facing mail.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this void my FP PostBase warranty?**
A: No. Using third-party consumables doesn't void the meter warranty itself, though FP won't cover damage caused by a defective third-party cartridge.

**Q: How many stamps can I expect per cartridge?**
A: FP rates the OEM at roughly 1,000 prints. Real-world reports here suggest 700-800 prints is more realistic for the PostageInk version.

**Q: Is the reset tool reusable?**
A: Yes. It's a simple plastic tool that works every time you swap cartridges, so you only buy it once.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** your mail volume is modest, you're comfortable with the QC gamble, and the 40-60% cost savings outweigh the risk of an occasional dud cartridge.

**Skip it if** you need guaranteed performance for high-volume mailing or can't afford unexpected downtime.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A legitimate budget option with real savings, undermined by inconsistent quality control and lower yield. The two-pack price makes it worth trying once; just keep a spare on hand.
