## The budget business laptop that actually gets work done

Most sub-$700 business laptops force you to choose between a decent screen, a fast processor, or a keyboard you can type on for eight hours. The HP Pavilion 15.6" FHD Touchscreen with the AMD Ryzen 7 7730U tries to give you all three — and mostly succeeds. That's why it holds a 4.2-star average across 210 Amazon ratings. But "mostly" is doing heavy lifting here, and you need to know where the corners got cut before you buy.

## What's actually in the box

You get the laptop, a 65W barrel charger (not USB-C, which annoys some buyers), and the standard paperwork. No sleeve, no stylus — even though the touchscreen begs for one. The 15.6-inch display is the headline feature: 1920x1080 IPS with 250 nits brightness and a 16:9 aspect ratio. It's not a colorist's panel, but for spreadsheets, documents, and video calls, it's perfectly serviceable. The touch layer responds accurately and supports 10-point multitouch.

Port selection is genuinely useful: two USB-A 3.1 ports, one USB-C (data only — no DisplayPort or charging), HDMI 1.4, a full-size SD card reader, and a 3.5mm headphone jack. The 720p webcam has a physical privacy shutter, which matters more than most shoppers realize.

## Performance and build quality

The Ryzen 7 7730U is the star here. Eight Zen 3+ cores and 16 threads running up to 4.5GHz. In real-world terms, that means 20+ Chrome tabs, a massive Excel workbook, and a Zoom call running simultaneously without the fan spinning into jet-engine mode. The integrated Radeon graphics handle light photo editing and 1080p video playback fine — just don't expect to game on it.

The keyboard is the other highlight. Full-size with a numpad, 1.5mm key travel, and decent tactile feedback. The backlight has two brightness levels. Typing this review on it, I hit a comfortable rhythm immediately. The trackpad is less impressive — plastic, slightly wobbly on the left edge, but accurate enough for precision work.

The chassis is where the Pavilion shows its budget DNA. The lid and base are aluminum-look plastic, and the palm rest flexes under firm pressure. It won't crack, but it doesn't inspire confidence when you're carrying it one-handed by the corner. At 3.85 pounds and 0.74 inches thick, it's portable enough for a commuter bag, though not what I'd call svelte.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 210 Amazon ratings, the overwhelming theme is performance-per-dollar. One verified buyer wrote: "I upgraded from a 5-year-old i5 laptop and this thing flies. The touchscreen is crisp, the boot time is under 10 seconds, and I can actually have multiple applications open without it choking." Several reviewers specifically called out the keyboard as comfortable for extended typing sessions, and multiple users confirmed the display is bright enough for use near windows — a common weak point at this price.

## Where it falls short

The RAM situation is the biggest trap. The 8GB base configuration has the RAM soldered to the motherboard. There's no SODIMM slot to add more later. If you buy the 8GB version, you're locked in. The 16GB config is worth the extra money — period. One reviewer put it bluntly: "Get the 16GB version. I bought the 8GB and I'm already hitting memory limits with normal office work."

Battery life is the second disappointment. HP claims up to 10 hours, but real-world testing lands closer to 6-7 hours with the screen at 70% brightness and typical office workloads. It'll get you through a workday if you're not pushing it, but don't plan on a full day of intensive use away from a charger.

The speakers are also underwhelming — they get loud enough but sound thin and tinny. Fine for notifications, not great for music or video calls.

## Who should buy this

- **Office workers and students** who need a fast, reliable daily driver with a touchscreen for less than $700
- **Spreadsheet warriors** who live in Excel and need a numpad — this is one of the few touchscreen laptops in this price range that has one
- **Google Docs / Microsoft 365 users** who want a responsive screen for scrolling long documents

- **Gamers** — the integrated graphics aren't cutting it
- **Heavy multitaskers on a tight budget** — the 8GB version will frustrate you; budget for the 16GB upgrade
- **Anyone needing all-day battery** — 6-7 hours is fine, not fantastic

## FAQ

**Q: Can I upgrade the RAM or storage later?**
The RAM is soldered — no upgrade path. The storage is a single M.2 NVMe slot, so you can swap the SSD for a larger one, but you'll need to clone your drive first.

**Q: Does the USB-C port support display output or charging?**
No. It's data-only. You'll need to use the HDMI port for external displays and the included barrel charger for power.

**Q: Is the touchscreen useful for note-taking?**
It's responsive, but without stylus support, it's better for tapping and scrolling than handwriting. If you need to write on the screen regularly, look at a 2-in-1 with active pen support.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5 — Buy it if** you want a genuinely fast business laptop with a touchscreen and numpad under $700. Get the 16GB RAM version and you'll be happy with this for years. **Skip it if** you need all-day battery life, plan to game, or want a premium-feeling chassis.

The HP Pavilion 15.6" FHD Touchscreen is a smart buy for the right buyer — just go in knowing its limits. The performance is real, the screen is solid, and the keyboard is a joy. The soldered RAM and so-so battery are the compromises you're making to hit that price point. For most office work, those tradeoffs are acceptable. For power users, they're dealbreakers.
