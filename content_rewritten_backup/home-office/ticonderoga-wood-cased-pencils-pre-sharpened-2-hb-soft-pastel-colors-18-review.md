## Build Quality and Performance

This is where Ticonderoga separates itself from the bargain bin brands.

- **The Core:** The graphite is smooth. I’m talking glides-across-the-paper smooth. It lays down a consistent dark line without heavy pressure, and it doesn't scratch or dig into standard printer paper. This is the "soft" in "2 HB Soft"—it writes darker than a standard HB but doesn't smudge as easily as a B lead.
- **The Wood:** The cedar casing sharpens beautifully. You won't get the splintering or chipping you see with cheaper pine pencils. It peels away in clean curls, exposing a long, sturdy point that resists breakage during normal writing.
- **The Eraser:** This is a critical component. Ticonderoga erasers are pink, rubbery, and actually work. They remove graphite cleanly without tearing the paper or leaving that sticky gray smear behind. It’s a small thing, but it saves you from having to buy a separate eraser for daily note-taking.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs.

1.  **The Price:** At roughly $0.95 per pencil, this is a premium price. You are paying a significant markup over the standard yellow Ticonderogas (which usually run about $0.30–$0.50 per pencil). You are paying for the color, plain and simple.
2.  **The Pastel Coating:** While it looks great, the colored lacquer can show wear quickly if you have a heavy grip or store them loose in a bag. The paint can chip at the edges where the wood is exposed after sharpening, revealing the raw cedar underneath. It doesn't affect function, but it does compromise the pristine aesthetic over time.
3.  **Softness:** If you are a lefty or have a heavy hand, the "soft" lead means you’ll burn through the tip faster than a standard HB. You'll be sharpening more often than a writer with a light touch.
