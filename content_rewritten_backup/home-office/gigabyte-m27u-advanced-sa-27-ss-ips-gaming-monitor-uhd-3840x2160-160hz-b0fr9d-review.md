# GIGABYTE M27U Advanced (SA) Review: 4K 160Hz Done Right — Mostly

The GIGABYTE M27U Advanced sits in an awkward but interesting spot. It's not the cheapest 4K monitor, not the fastest, and certainly not the most feature-packed. But it's one of the few 27-inch panels that tries to be everything at once: sharp enough for spreadsheet marathons, fast enough for FPS sessions, and color-accurate enough for light photo work. The question is whether it pulls off that balancing act or falls flat trying.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box, the M27U Advanced includes the monitor, a displayPort 1.4 cable, an HDMI 2.1 cable, a USB-C cable (with DP alt mode and 18W power delivery), and the power brick. The stand attaches with a quarter-turn screw — tool-free, which is nice. You're also getting a surprisingly clean OSD controlled by a five-way joystick on the back. No speakers, which is fine for most buyers, but worth noting if you expected them.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's the headline: this is a 27-inch, 4K (3840x2160) SS IPS panel running at 160Hz. That pixel density — roughly 163 PPI — makes text look printed rather than rendered. If you're coming from a 1080p or even 1440p display, the difference is immediate. Excel grids, code editors, and dense PDFs all look dramatically crisper.

The 160Hz refresh rate is the real reason to consider this over a standard office monitor. Scrolling through long documents feels fluid, and if you game at all, the motion clarity is a genuine upgrade. G-Sync Compatible and FreeSync Premium both work, and the 1ms GTG response is believable in practice — no obvious ghosting in fast-paced scenes.

Color performance is solid for the price. GIGABYTE claims 95% DCI-P3 coverage, and while I'd take that with a grain of salt, out-of-the-box calibration is decent. Whites look neutral, and skin tones don't have that sickly green cast some budget IPS panels show. Brightness peaks around 400 nits in SDR, which is fine for indoor use but not a sun-drenched office.

## What Buyers Praise

The sharpness and speed get consistent love. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"The jump from 1440p to 4K on a 27-inch is more noticeable than I expected — everything just looks cleaner."* Another noted, *"160Hz with 4K is a killer combo for both work and Warzone."* The build quality also gets nods — the panel is rigid, and the thin bezels make it look more expensive than it is.

## Where It Falls Short

Now the honest part. The KVM switch — a big selling point for productivity users — is temperamental. Several buyers report the USB-C connection drops randomly, and the 18W power delivery is too weak to charge a laptop properly. If you planned to run a single USB-C cable to your MacBook, expect to keep the charger plugged in anyway.

The stand is another frustration. It offers tilt and swivel, but height adjustment is limited on many units, and the base is unnecessarily wide. Budget for a VESA arm if you care about ergonomics.

And the HDR claim? Ignore it. Local dimming is so weak it's effectively a marketing checkbox. HDR content looks washed out compared to even mid-range OLEDs or Mini-LED panels. Run it in SDR and you'll be happier.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The hybrid worker/gamer**: You spend 8 hours in spreadsheets and 2 hours in shooters. This handles both without compromise.
- **The pixel-density snob**: You want 4K sharpness without jumping to a 32-inch monitor that feels like a TV.
- **The dual-PC user**: If you can get the KVM working, switching between work laptop and gaming rig is convenient.

Skip it if you're a serious HDR enthusiast, need reliable USB-C laptop charging, or want a fully adjustable stand out of the box.

## FAQ

**Is the GIGABYTE M27U good for Mac?**
Yes, but with caveats. USB-C works for video and data, but 18W charging won't keep a MacBook Pro topped up under load. You'll need the MagSafe charger anyway. Text rendering is excellent thanks to the high PPI.

**Can it run 4K at 160Hz on a PS5 or Xbox?**
Yes — HDMI 2.1 supports it, though console games rarely hit 160fps. You'll get the full bandwidth, which is more than many monitors in this price range offer.

**Does it have built-in speakers?**
No. Plan for external speakers or a headset.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely sharp 4K panel with a fast refresh rate for a hybrid work/gaming setup, and you're okay using a VESA arm to fix ergonomics.

**Skip it if** you need reliable USB-C docking, care about HDR, or want everything to work perfectly out of the box.

The GIGABYTE M27U Advanced is a strong monitor that's let down by half-baked extras. The core — panel, speed, color — delivers. The KVM and HDR are afterthoughts. At 3.7/5, it's a smart buy for the right person, not a universal recommendation.
