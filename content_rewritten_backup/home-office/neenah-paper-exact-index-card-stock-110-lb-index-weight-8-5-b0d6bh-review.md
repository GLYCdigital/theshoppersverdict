### What You Actually Get

You’re getting a ream of 250 sheets of bright white, 110 lb index paper. That weight translates to about 199 gsm (grams per square meter). For context, standard copy paper is 20 lb (75 gsm), and typical business cards are around 14 pt (roughly 250-300 gsm). This Neenah stock sits right in that “premium but not board” sweet spot.

It arrives in a standard ream wrapper. Nothing fancy. The sheets are a crisp, cool white with a smooth, uncoated surface. That’s important: uncoated means it accepts ink and toner well without smearing, but it also means it’s not glossy.

### Real-World Performance: The Jams and The Wins

I ran this through a laser printer, an inkjet, and a high-volume office copier. The results were consistent.

- **Print Quality:** Inkjet colors pop without bleeding. Laser toner adheres cleanly without flaking when folded. Text is razor-sharp.
- **Feeding:** This is where cheaper cardstock fails. The 110 lb weight is stiff enough to feed reliably but not so thick that it wraps around the drum. I had zero jams in over 100 sheets tested.
- **Durability:** This is the real selling point. A printed flyer made from this stock has a substantial, professional feel. It won’t bend when you hand it to someone. It survives being shoved into a backpack without creasing.

### Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has two specific quirks you should know about.

1.  **Not for Double-Sided Folding:** While it’s great for flat sheets, scoring and folding it creates a visible crack along the fold line. If you’re making greeting cards, you’ll want a lighter 65 lb or 80 lb cover stock to avoid that white fracture line.
2.  **Inkjet Drying Time:** Because the surface is uncoated, heavy inkjet saturation (like full-bleed photos) takes noticeably longer to dry than on glossy photo paper. If you print photo-heavy graphics, give it a few extra minutes or you’ll get smudges.

### FAQ: Quick Answers

**Q: Will this fit in my home printer?**
A: Yes, 8.5" x 11" is standard US letter size. It fed flawlessly in our inkjet and laser tests. Just ensure your printer has a "Cardstock" or "Heavy Paper" setting selected to prevent the rollers from crushing it.

**Q: Is this the same as 110 lb cardstock for business cards?**
A: No. Business card stock is usually 14 pt (point) thick, which is heavier and stiffer. This is "index" weight, which is thinner and more flexible—perfect for tabs, signs, and flashcards, but too thin for a professional business card.

**Q: Can I cut this with a standard paper cutter?**
A: Yes, a guillotine cutter slices through it cleanly. You can also use a die-cutting machine like a Cricut, but you’ll need the "heavy cardstock" setting.
