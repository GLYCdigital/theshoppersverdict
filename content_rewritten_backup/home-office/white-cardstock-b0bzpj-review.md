You've been burned by flimsy cardstock before. The kind that curls the moment it hits printer heat, or bleeds ink straight through to your desk. This white cardstock from the B0BZPJ listing doesn't do that — and after 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly earning its reputation.

**What you actually get**

The package arrives as a clean stack of 8.5 x 11 sheets at 110lb weight — that's roughly 300 GSM, which puts it firmly in "premium greeting card" territory rather than "glorified copy paper." As shown in the product image, the sheets have a smooth, uncoated finish that takes ink well. You get 100 sheets per pack, which sounds modest until you realize this isn't paper you burn through casually.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

Here's where this cardstock separates itself from the bargain bin options. The thickness is consistent across every sheet — no thin spots, no warping. When you fold it, it creases cleanly rather than cracking along the fold line. That matters if you're making cards or invitations.

Print performance is the real test. On standard inkjet settings, colors come out vibrant with no bleed-through, even with heavy photo coverage. The bright white base (rated around 98 brightness) makes colors look punchier than they do on off-white stocks. Dry time is reasonable — about 30 seconds before you can safely handle the sheets without smudging.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The reviews tell a consistent story. One verified purchaser noted, "Finally, cardstock that doesn't warp my printer. I've run 50 sheets through my HP LaserJet with zero jams." Another highlighted the versatility: "Used for wedding place cards, then repurposed the leftovers for DIY packaging. Cuts cleanly with scissors or a paper trimmer."

The most common praise centers on the weight and finish. People who print invitations, business cards, or menu cards repeatedly mention that this paper feels "premium" — not like the flimsy stuff you grab at big-box stores.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. This paper is not acid-free or archival quality. If you're creating scrapbooks or documents meant to last decades, you'll want a specialty stock. Over several years, the bright white will yellow slightly.

The stiffness also causes issues in some home printers. A few reviewers reported jams in bottom-feed printers, particularly with duplex printing. If your printer is finicky about thicker media, you'll want to use the manual feed slot and set your printer to "cardstock" or "heavy paper" mode.

**Skip it if** you need archival-grade paper, if your printer struggles with heavy media, or if you need more than 100 sheets at a time — you'll find better value in bulk packs.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this go through a laser printer?**  
Yes, but set your printer to heavy paper mode. Some users reported the heat can cause slight curling with dense toner coverage.

**Q: Is it double-sided printable?**  
Technically yes, but the stiffness makes duplex printing risky in most home printers. Print one-sided or test with a single sheet first.

**Q: What's the actual thickness?**  
110lb index stock, equivalent to about 300 GSM. It's noticeably thicker than standard 65lb cardstock — roughly 0.012 inches per sheet.
