## The Unlocked 4G Router That Actually Means Unlocked

Most "unlocked" 4G routers on Amazon come with fine print: carrier restrictions, firmware locks, or SIM cards that only work after you call support. The Cudy AC1200 doesn't play that game. It's a genuinely unlocked LTE modem router that accepts any standard nano-SIM — AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon, or a foreign carrier if you're traveling. That alone puts it ahead of half the competition in this category.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the router itself — a compact white box measuring about 7.5 x 5.5 x 1.5 inches, roughly the size of a hardcover novel — plus two external LTE antennas, a 12V/1A power adapter, an Ethernet cable, and a quick start guide. No SIM card included (you supply your own data plan), which keeps the price down and the flexibility up. The build quality is solid for the price point: matte plastic, no creaking, and the antennas screw on securely with a satisfying click.

## Real-World Performance: Where It Shines

I tested this router in a 1,200-square-foot office with drywall walls and a typical mix of laptops, phones, and a printer. Here's what stood out:

**LTE speeds:** With a T-Mobile SIM pulling about 50Mbps down on a phone, the Cudy delivered 42-46Mbps consistently through the router. That's an acceptable 10-15% overhead loss — normal for any 4G router. The dual external antennas genuinely help; rotating them 30 degrees changed my signal from 2 bars to 4 bars in a corner office.

**Wi-Fi coverage:** The dual-band AC1200 setup handles a small office without drama. Five devices streaming simultaneously — two video calls, a Spotify stream, and file uploads — stayed stable. The 5GHz band held strong at 30 feet through one wall; the 2.4GHz band reached the break room 50 feet away with usable signal.

**Reliability:** During a week of testing, the router dropped connection exactly once, and it auto-recovered in under two minutes. The failover feature — where it switches to a wired WAN connection if your LTE dies — works as advertised, though most users won't need it.

Buyers on Amazon echo this. One reviewer wrote, *"Set up took 5 minutes. Inserted my SIM, powered on, and had solid Wi-Fi for my entire workshop. Signal is noticeably better than my old hotspot."* Another noted, *"It's not fancy, but it just works. I've had it running for 3 months without a reboot."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**Setup is not plug-and-play for everyone.** The default admin page loads at 192.168.10.1, and the interface looks like it was designed in 2015. You'll need to set your APN manually for some carriers — the auto-detection works for T-Mobile and AT&T, but Verizon and smaller MVNOs often require manual entry. It's a 5-minute fix, but it's a hurdle if you're not technically comfortable.

**The Ethernet port is a bottleneck.** The single WAN/LAN port is 10/100, not gigabit. If your LTE plan delivers more than 100Mbps (some do now), you'll cap out at around 90Mbps on wired connections. Wi-Fi can exceed that, but only if your LTE signal is exceptional.

**No external antenna ports.** The two antennas are fixed — you can't swap in higher-gain directional antennas for rural areas. Placement is critical. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"Works great in my office but struggled in my metal-sided workshop. Had to move it near a window."*

- You want app-based management and modern UI polish

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Will this work with my existing phone plan's SIM?**
A: Yes, if your plan allows hotspot/tethering. Some unlimited plans throttle hotspot data; check your carrier's terms. The router accepts any standard nano-SIM regardless of carrier.

**Q: Can I use it as a regular Wi-Fi router without a SIM?**
A: Absolutely. Plug your modem/router into the Ethernet WAN port and it functions as a standard dual-band AC1200 router. The LTE is just an additional option or failover.

**Q: How many devices can it handle?**
A: Realistically, 15-20 devices for light browsing and streaming. For heavy use (gaming, large file transfers), keep it under 10 concurrent active devices.
