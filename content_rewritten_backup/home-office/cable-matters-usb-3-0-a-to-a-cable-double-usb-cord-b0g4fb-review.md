## The A-to-A Cable You Didn't Know You Needed

Most USB cables are A-to-B or A-to-C. But if you're running a USB DAC, an older external hard drive enclosure, or a KVM switch with A-to-A ports, you're stuck with a cable category most brands ignore. Cable Matters fills that gap with a $7.99 double-ended USB 3.0 cord that punches well above its price tag.

As shown in the product image, this is a straightforward A-to-A cable with the signature blue USB 3.0 connectors. Nothing flashy — just a braided nylon jacket and reinforced boots. But the engineering underneath matters more than the aesthetics.

## What You Actually Get

The Cable Matters USB 3.0 A-to-A cable ships in a simple plastic bag — no retail box, no frills. You get:

- **One 3-foot (0.9m) A-to-A cable** in black braided nylon
- **USB 3.0 spec** with 5Gbps theoretical bandwidth
- **28/24 AWG wire gauge** — thicker than cheap cables, which matters for power delivery to bus-powered devices
- **Gold-plated connectors** with strain-relief boots on both ends

The 3-foot length is the sweet spot for desktop use. It reaches from a tower PC to a DAC or external drive without leaving a tangled mess. Cable Matters also offers 6-foot and 10-foot variants if your setup demands more reach.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this cable transferring a 12GB folder of mixed files between an SSD and an external drive enclosure. Sustained transfer speeds held at 420-450 MB/s — right at the practical ceiling for USB 3.0. No drops, no reconnects, no "device not recognized" errors.

The braided jacket is genuinely durable. It's stiff enough to hold its shape but flexible enough to route around desk corners. The strain-relief boots at both ends are the real differentiator — they're thick and molded, not the flimsy rubber nubs you see on bargain cables. This is where most cheap cables fail after a few months of daily plugging and unplugging.

One thing I noticed: the connectors fit snugly. Not annoyingly tight, but secure enough that they won't wiggle loose in a KVM switch or a rear-panel port. That's a small detail that matters in real use.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this cable has a few honest drawbacks:

- **No cable tie included** — at $7.99, a velcro strap would be nice but isn't a dealbreaker
- **Stiff jacket**: The braided nylon is durable but less flexible than rubber cables. In tight spaces with sharp bends, you'll fight the cable's natural stiffness
- **No USB 3.1/3.2 support**: This is a USB 3.0 (5Gbps) cable, not 10Gbps. If you need faster transfers, look elsewhere — but most A-to-A devices don't support higher speeds anyway
