Most overbed tables are flimsy, wobbly things you'd hesitate to put a full mug of coffee on. The FLEXISPOT H7 PRO isn't that. It's a pneumatic standing desk on wheels that happens to slide over a bed, couch, or recliner — and it's built like it actually means it. With a 4.5-star average across 225 Amazon ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in bedrooms and living rooms alike. Here's the honest breakdown after digging into specs, user feedback, and real-world quirks.

**What You Actually Get**  
The box contains the assembled tabletop, the steel column, a five-star base with six 2-inch casters, and a gas-spring lift mechanism. Assembly takes about 10 minutes — no tools beyond the included Allen wrench. The desktop measures 23.6 x 15.7 inches, which is roughly the size of a large laptop tray, not a full desk. The base is 23.6 inches wide, so it clears standard bed frames and couch arms without drama.

**Performance and Build Quality**  
The headline feature is the pneumatic lift. A lever under the tabletop releases the gas spring, letting you adjust height from 25.6 to 43.3 inches. It's genuinely one-handed — you don't need to brace the table or wrestle with a crank. Users consistently mention this in reviews: *"The height adjustment is buttery smooth — my wife uses it in bed, and I use it standing at the couch."* That's the real strength here: it serves two completely different postures without any fiddling.

The six casters are another standout. Two of them lock, which matters more than you'd think. On carpet, the table rolls easily; on hardwood, it glides without scratching. One reviewer noted, *"I was skeptical about the wheels, but they lock tight — no sliding when I lean on it to type."* The steel frame feels rigid, with minimal wobble at full height. Rated for 22 pounds, it handles a laptop, mouse, and a drink without flexing.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**  
- **Versatility**: It's not just for beds. People use it as a couch-side laptop station, a breakfast tray, even a portable standing desk for small apartments. The height range covers seated and standing positions, which is rare in this category.  
- **Stability at height**: Multiple reviewers compared it favorably to cheaper overbed tables that shake. The wide base and locking casters keep it planted.  
- **Assembly ease**: Nearly every review mentions how simple setup is. No confusing diagrams, no stripped screws.

**Where It Falls Short**  
The desktop size is the biggest compromise. At 23.6 inches deep, a full-size keyboard plus mouse will feel cramped. You'll likely use a laptop directly on the surface or a compact keyboard. There's also no cable management — the column is hollow, but there's no grommet or clip. You'll need to route cords yourself with adhesive clips or zip ties. Finally, the 22-pound capacity is fine for laptops and tablets, but don't plan on mounting a monitor arm or loading it with books.

**Who Should Buy This**  
- **Chronic bed workers**: If you work, game, or read from bed regularly, this is a proper alternative to a cheap lap desk.  
- **Small-space dwellers**: Its mobility means one table serves the bedroom, living room, and kitchen.  
- **Recovery or mobility-limited users**: The pneumatic lift and locking wheels make it genuinely accessible — no bending or heavy lifting to adjust.  

Skip it if you need a full-size work surface with monitor support, or if you expect to leave it in one spot permanently — a fixed standing desk gives you more surface for the same money.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Can it hold a 15-inch laptop plus a mouse?**  
Yes, comfortably, as long as you don't need a full-size keyboard tray. The surface fits a 15-inch laptop and a compact mouse side by side.

**Q: Does the height adjustment require much effort?**  
No. The gas spring does the work — you pull the lever and guide it up or down. Even at the highest setting, lowering it takes minimal pressure.

**Q: Will it fit over a thick mattress or recliner arm?**  
The base is 23.6 inches wide, so it clears standard bed frames and most recliners. For oversized mattresses (18+ inches thick), measure the gap first — the lowest height is 25.6 inches, which may be too tall for very low beds.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a genuinely mobile, stable surface that works over a bed, couch, or as a compact standing desk — and you value one-hand height adjustment. **Skip it if** you need a full-size work surface or plan to mount heavy equipment.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect (the desktop is small, cables need managing), but for what it is — a well-built, versatile mobile table — it's the best in its class.
