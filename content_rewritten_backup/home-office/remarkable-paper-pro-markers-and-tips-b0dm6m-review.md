Let's be direct: the reMarkable Paper Pro is a niche device for people who take their digital handwriting seriously. The Markers and Tips are the unsung workhorses of that experience. With 204 ratings averaging 4.1 stars, there's a consensus forming — but it's not unanimous. Here's what you actually need to know before spending your money.

## What You're Getting

The package includes the Marker (or Marker Plus, depending on which listing you're viewing) and a set of replacement nibs. The standard Marker is a minimalist aluminum barrel with a triangular cross-section that locks into your grip naturally. The Plus adds a built-in eraser on the top — just flip it over and rub. Both use the same ceramic-tipped nibs, which reMarkable claims last longer than traditional felt tips.

## The Real-World Performance

The writing feel is where reMarkable has always excelled, and the Paper Pro's textured glass screen actually enhances it. The nib glides with a subtle drag that mimics a gel pen on premium paper — not the slippery, glassy feel you get with an Apple Pencil. If you're coming from an iPad, the tactile feedback will feel like a revelation.

The triangular grip is a genuine ergonomic win. During a two-hour note-taking session, my hand cramped noticeably less than with cylindrical styluses. The weight distribution is balanced — heavier at the tip, which gives you natural control without feeling top-heavy.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The eraser on the Marker Plus is the star feature in customer feedback. One verified buyer wrote: "I didn't think I'd care about the eraser, but now I can't live without it. It's the closest thing to a real pencil eraser I've found on any tablet." Another long-term user noted: "The nibs last about 4-6 weeks of daily use, which is better than my old Wacom."

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest tradeoff. The textured screen eats nibs faster than the smooth glass of the reMarkable 2. Multiple reviewers report swapping tips every 3-4 weeks with heavy daily use — that's roughly 12-16 tips per year. At the replacement pack price, that's a recurring cost you need to budget for.

The standard Marker's lack of magnetic attachment is also a real annoyance. The Plus snaps to the side of the Paper Pro; the base model doesn't. If you're prone to setting things down, you will lose this pen. That's not speculation — it's in the reviews.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The note-taking professional**: If you're replacing a paper notebook for meetings, lectures, or design brainstorming, the Marker Plus is worth the premium.
- **The reMarkable 2 owner upgrading**: The new nibs are compatible with the older device, so this is a reasonable accessory refresh.
- **The casual sketcher**: The Paper Pro's larger canvas and this marker's precision make a solid combo for quick concept sketches.

## Skip It If

- You're on a strict budget. The Marker Plus is genuinely pricey, and the standard Marker's missing eraser and magnet make it feel incomplete.
- You're expecting Apple Pencil-level pressure sensitivity with tilt shading in third-party apps. This works flawlessly within reMarkable's ecosystem, but it's not a universal stylus.

## FAQ

**How long do the tips actually last?**
With daily use (2-3 hours), expect 3-5 weeks per tip. Light users report 6-8 weeks. The textured Paper Pro screen accelerates wear compared to the smooth reMarkable 2.

**Are the tips compatible with the original reMarkable 2?**
Yes, the nibs work across reMarkable's current lineup. The Marker itself is interchangeable between devices.

**Does the eraser work in all apps?**
It works in reMarkable's native Notebook and PDF annotation tools. Third-party apps may not support the eraser gesture.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if**: You're committed to the reMarkable Paper Pro as your primary note-taking device and want the best writing experience available for it. The Marker Plus's eraser and ergonomics justify the premium.

**Skip it if**: You're a casual user who writes sparingly. The standard Marker's missing magnet and eraser make it a frustrating compromise, and you'd be better served by a third-party alternative.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The hardware is excellent, but the nib wear rate and the base model's feature gaps keep it from perfection. It's a great accessory for the right user, and an overpriced stick for everyone else.
