**The PILOT G2 is the office pen everyone claims to love — but is the Mini version worth your desk space?**  

I’ve tested both the standard G2 and the new Mini in fine point (0.7mm) over two weeks of notes, lists, and margin scribbles. Here’s the honest breakdown before you add to cart.  

**What’s in the box**  
You get a two-pack: one full-size G2 and one Mini, both in fine point with black ink. The standard measures 5.6 inches capped, 5.1 inches posted — a classic ergonomic shape. The Mini runs 4.1 inches capped, 4.6 inches posted, which is noticeably shorter. Both use the same refill system (PILOT G2 refills fit both), so you’re not locked into proprietary cartridges.  

**Performance and build quality**  
The fine point is where the G2 shines. The 0.7mm tip lays down a clean, consistent line with zero skips — even when I wrote fast on cheap printer paper. The gel ink is pigment-based, so it’s water-resistant and doesn’t feather. I tested it on a sticky note, a Moleskine, and a glossy receipt; it handled all three without bleeding through.  

The rubberized grip on both models is a genuine upgrade over the older G2s. It’s contoured and slightly squishy, which helped during a 3-hour note-taking session. The clip is metal and sturdy — it won’t snap off in a backpack like cheaper pens.  

The Mini, however, is a niche tool. It’s designed for planners, pockets, or as a backup pen. For average adult hands, it’s cramped when writing for more than a page. The cap posts but sits at an odd angle, making it feel top-heavy. If you buy this expecting a full-size G2 experience, you’ll be disappointed.  

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Scrolling through the 240 ratings at 4.8 stars, three themes dominate:  

- **Ink smoothness** — one reviewer wrote, *“Glides like butter on every surface I’ve tried. No more scratching or skipping mid-sentence.”*  
- **Durability** — another noted, *“I’ve dropped this pen on concrete three times and the tip hasn’t bent. My old gel pens would’ve been dead by now.”*  
- **Value** — several mention the two-pack is a smart way to test the Mini without committing.  

**Where it falls short**  
The drying time is the real issue. On standard paper, the ink sets in about a second. On glossy or coated paper, it takes 3–4 seconds — if you’re left-handed or write fast, you’ll smear. I tested this on a photo paper envelope and got a faint streak.  

The Mini’s short length is the second drawback. If you have larger hands, it’s uncomfortable for extended writing. It’s a “grab-and-go” pen, not a workhorse.  

**Who should buy this**  
- **Planner enthusiasts** — the Mini fits in A5 and half-size layouts without poking out of the binding.  
- **Left-handed writers** — but only if you stick to matte paper; glossy surfaces will test your patience.  
- **Students or office workers** — the fine point is ideal for margins, checklists, and dense notes.  

**Skip it if**  
You need a pen for marathon writing sessions (get the full-size G2 0.7mm instead). Or if you prefer bold, thick lines — the 0.7mm is precise, not dramatic.  

**FAQ**  

*Do the G2 and Mini use the same refills?*  
Yes, both take standard PILOT G2 refills. You can swap ink colors or buy multi-packs without worry.  

*Is the fine point scratchy on cheap paper?*  
No — the 0.7mm tip is smooth even on rough copy paper. It’s one of the best fine-point gels I’ve tested under $10.  

*Will the Mini fit in a standard pen loop?*  
Mostly yes, but it’s short enough to slide out. I’d recommend a loop with a snap or zip closure.  

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a reliable fine-point gel pen for everyday notes, and you’re curious about the Mini’s portability. **Skip it if** you need a full-size grip or write on glossy paper regularly.  

The standard G2 is a 5/5 — but the Mini’s ergonomic flaws knock the set to a solid 4.6. At this price, you’re getting two quality pens that will last months. Just know the Mini is a backup, not a primary.
