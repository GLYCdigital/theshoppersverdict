**The Short Version**

If you own a Brother HL-L2300D, DCP-L2520DW, or MFC-L2700DW, the TN730 is the cartridge your machine was engineered for. This 2-pack gives you two genuine Brother cartridges, each rated for approximately 1,200 pages. That's not a huge page count — more on that below — but what you sacrifice in yield you gain in reliability and print quality. At 4.7 stars across 235 ratings, owners overwhelmingly agree: this is the safe, smart choice for Brother printers.

---

**What You Get**

Two individually sealed TN730 cartridges. Nothing else in the box — no drum unit (that's separate, the DR730, and lasts ~12,000 pages), no cleaning kit, no gloves. Each cartridge is a straightforward drop-in replacement: open the printer's front cover, pull out the old cartridge, slide in the new one, close the cover. Installation takes about 90 seconds and doesn't require tools or a tech support call.

---

**Print Quality and Performance**

Text output is crisp and deeply black — solid for everyday documents, invoices, and school assignments. Brother's genuine toner formulation produces consistent density across the page, with no streaking or ghosting out of the box. I've seen knockoff cartridges produce grayish, uneven results; this isn't that. It's also a dry toner system, so no smudging on standard copy paper.

One thing worth noting: the TN730 is a **standard yield** cartridge. At 1,200 pages per cartridge, heavy users will be swapping these out every month or two. If you print 50+ pages daily, the high-yield TN760 (3,000 pages) is almost always a better cost-per-page play. But for light-to-moderate home or small-office use, the standard yield keeps your upfront cost lower and reduces the risk of toner drying out before you finish a cartridge.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs:

1. **Cost per page is higher than high-yield or third-party options.** At typical retail, you're paying roughly 6–8 cents per page with the TN730. The high-yield TN760 drops that closer to 4–5 cents. If you print a lot, the math favors the bigger cartridge.
2. **No page-count tracking on the cartridge itself.** The printer will warn you when toner is low, but you can't get a precise remaining-page estimate — annoying if you're planning a big print job.
3. **Environmental quibble:** Each cartridge is individually boxed in plastic. For a 2-pack, that's twice the packaging waste compared to a single high-yield unit.

**FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask**

**Q: Will the TN730 work in my Brother HL-L2350DW?**  
A: Yes. The TN730 is compatible with the HL-L2300D series, DCP-L2500D series, and MFC-L2700D series, including the L2350DW. Check Brother's compatibility list if you're unsure — but if your printer takes the DR730 drum, it takes this toner.

**Q: How many pages does the TN730 actually print?**  
A: Brother rates it at 1,200 pages based on 5% coverage — that's a typical text document. If you print dense graphics or photos, expect less. If you print mostly light text, you might squeeze out a bit more.

**Q: Is this cartridge compatible with the TN760?**  
A: No. The TN730 and TN760 are different cartridges. The TN760 is high-yield (3,000 pages) and physically larger. You can't swap one for the other without changing the drum unit.
