If you've ever wanted a fountain pen that behaves like a ballpoint — click, write, click, pocket — the Pilot Vanishing Point is the only mainstream option that does it right. And the Metallics Collection adds a brushed-metal finish that makes it feel like a precision tool rather than a fragile writing instrument.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the pen itself (28g, 5.5 inches capped), a single black ink cartridge, and a CON-40 converter for bottled ink. No fancy presentation case, no extra nibs. The body is brass under a metallic coating — the matte finishes (gunmetal, copper, matte black) are the ones most buyers recommend over the glossy versions, which show fingerprints badly.

The 18K gold nib is the star: it's springy without being flexy, lays down a consistent medium line, and starts instantly every time — no hard starts, no skipping, even after sitting capped for a week. That's the Vanishing Point's secret: the trapdoor mechanism seals the nib when retracted, so it doesn't dry out like other retractable pens.

## Real-World Performance

The click mechanism is the reason this pen costs what it does. One click and the nib deploys; another click and it's safely retracted. No cap to unscrew, no fear of dropping the cap into a coffee cup. In a meeting, that's a genuine advantage — you can jot a note, click it shut, and toss it in your pocket without ink stains.

Buyers consistently praise the nib smoothness. One reviewer noted: *"It writes buttery smooth out of the box — I've had $300 pens that needed tuning before they felt this good."* Another said: *"The retraction mechanism is flawless after 2 years of daily use — still clicks tight, still doesn't dry out."*

## Where It Falls Short

The clip. It's positioned directly over the grip section, and if you hold your pen with a traditional tripod grip, your thumb will rest right on it. Some people adapt within a day; others never get used to it. Try before you buy if possible, or buy from a retailer with a good return policy.

Also, this is a pen that requires commitment. It takes Pilot's proprietary CON-40 converter or cartridges — you can't use standard international ink. And at this price, it's not a gift for someone who's never used a fountain pen. They'll lose it, or worse, lend it to a coworker who'll click it shut while the nib is still wet (which, to be fair, is the one thing that can jam the mechanism).

## Who Should Buy This

- **The professional who lives in meetings** — you need a reliable pen that writes instantly and disappears into your pocket without a cap.
- **The fountain pen enthusiast who wants a practical daily driver** — something that survives a bag, a pocket, or a drop without a cracked barrel.
- **The gadget lover** — the mechanism is genuinely fun. It's a fidget toy that happens to write beautifully.

Skip it if you want a "normal" fountain pen experience (capping and uncapping is half the ritual for some), or if your budget can't absorb a $150+ pen that needs specific ink refills.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the retractable mechanism ever jam or dry out?**
It can jam if you retract the nib while it's still wet and then let ink dry inside the trapdoor. But normal use — write, click, store — is fine. Drying out is not an issue because the trapdoor seals airtight.

**Q: Can I use any fountain pen ink?**
Only Pilot's proprietary cartridges or the CON-40 converter. You can't use standard international cartridges. The CON-40 is small (0.6ml), so if you write a lot, carry a spare cartridge.

**Q: What nib sizes are available?**
The Vanishing Point ships in Extra Fine, Fine, Medium, and Broad. Medium is the most popular — it's smooth and forgiving. Extra Fine is great for small handwriting but can feel scratchy on cheap paper.
