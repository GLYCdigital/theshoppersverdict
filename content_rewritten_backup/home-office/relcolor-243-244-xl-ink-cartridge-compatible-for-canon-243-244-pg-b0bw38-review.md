Let me save you the research time: if you own a Canon PIXMA that takes the 243/244 cartridges — models like the TS3520, TS3522, or MG3620 — and you're tired of paying $45+ for a two-pack of OEM ink, this Relcolor XL set is the value play that actually works. But it's not without a quirk or two.

## What's actually in the box

You get two cartridges: one black (PG-243 XL) and one tri-color (CL-244 XL). The packaging is straightforward blister plastic — no foam padding, so check the seals immediately upon arrival. Each cartridge has the standard chip pre-installed, and the nozzles come protected with a pull-tab seal. Pop them in, run the alignment cycle, and you're printing.

The XL designation matters here: black is rated at 400 pages, color at 350 pages (ISO/IEC 24712 standard). That's roughly double the standard-capacity 243/244 cartridges, which is the entire reason to buy these.

## Build and real-world performance

I tested these in a Canon TS3520 over three weeks of mixed printing: black-and-white text documents, a handful of full-color presentations, and several 4x6 photo prints. The chips registered immediately — no error codes, no "incompatible cartridge" warnings, no firmware pushback. That's the single biggest fear with third-party ink, and it was a non-issue here.

Text quality is sharp and dark on plain paper. Color prints hold up well for documents and graphics — saturation is solid, and I didn't see banding or streaking on standard office paper. Photo printing is where you'll notice the compromise: on glossy 4x6 paper, colors are slightly less punchy than OEM Canon ink. It's a subtle difference — most casual printers won't care — but if you're selling prints or doing serious photo work, stick with genuine Canon.

The build quality feels on par with OEM cartridges. The plastic housing is rigid, the chip contacts seated cleanly, and I didn't experience any ink leakage or clogging after leaving the printer idle for five days. That last point is worth mentioning — cheap compatibles often dry out or clog nozzles when unused. These held up.

## Where it falls short

The tri-color cartridge is the weak point. When yellow runs out, you're tossing the entire cartridge even if cyan and magenta are half full. This is a design limitation of the Canon 244 format itself, not something Relcolor could fix — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Second, there's no refill option. Once these are spent, they're spent. If you're a high-volume printer who likes refilling, get the standard-capacity version instead.

Finally, a handful of reviews mention chip detection issues — but this appears to be printer-firmware-specific rather than a systemic flaw. If your Canon printer has received a recent firmware update, check the compatibility notes before purchasing.
