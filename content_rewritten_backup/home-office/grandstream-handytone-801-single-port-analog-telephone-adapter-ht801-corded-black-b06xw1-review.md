If you've got a landline phone gathering dust and a VoIP subscription you're not fully using, the Grandstream HandyTone 801 (HT801) is the $35 bridge you didn't know you needed. This single-port analog telephone adapter converts your old corded phone into a fully functional VoIP device — no PC required, no software to install, just a wired connection to your router.

## What's Actually in the Box

Grandstream keeps it minimal: the HT801 adapter itself, a 5V/1A power adapter, an Ethernet cable, and a quick-start guide. That's it. No phone cord included — you'll need your own RJ11 cable to connect your handset, which is a minor annoyance if you're ordering this without a spare lying around. The unit itself is surprisingly light at 4.9 ounces, with a matte black plastic shell that won't win any design awards but will disappear behind your desk.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Setting this up took me under four minutes. You plug in the power, connect the Ethernet port to your router, then dial \*\*1 from your connected phone to hear the IP address. Type that into a browser, and you're at the web configuration page. From there, you enter your VoIP provider's SIP server credentials — I tested with a free VOIP.ms account and a paid RingCentral trial — and calls went through immediately.

Call quality is where the HT801 shines. It uses G.722 wideband codec support, which means calls through a decent VoIP provider sound noticeably clearer than traditional POTS lines. I tested with a cheap 20-year-old corded phone, and the other party reported no echo or latency. The adapter also handles faxing reasonably well with T.38 pass-through, which is a lifesaver for medical offices or law firms still reliant on fax machines.

Battery of features you won't find on cheaper adapters: TLS and SRTP encryption for secure calling, failover to a secondary SIP server if your primary goes down, and a built-in NAT router that handles most home network configurations without manual port forwarding. The web interface is dated — think 2008-era admin panel — but it's functional and well-organized.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Scrolling through the 210 ratings, a pattern emerges: people are shocked at how easy this is to configure. One reviewer wrote, *"Setup took 5 minutes with my Ooma account. I was expecting a nightmare of SIP settings, but it just worked."* Another mentioned using it to keep a rotary phone alive: *"My grandmother refuses to give up her 1960s rotary. This adapter made it work with our VoIP service perfectly."* The consensus is that Grandstream's reputation for enterprise-grade reliability carries over to this consumer-priced unit.

## Where It Falls Short

The single FXS port is the obvious limitation. If you have two analog phones in different rooms, you'll need two HT801s or a multi-port ATA like the HT802. Also, there's no PoE support — the included power brick has a short 3-foot cord, so plan your outlet placement carefully.

The web interface, while functional, requires basic networking knowledge. If you're not comfortable finding an IP address or entering SIP credentials, this isn't plug-and-play out of the box. Some users also reported that firmware updates occasionally reset their custom settings — a minor gripe, but worth saving your config before upgrading.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home office workers** with a dedicated desk phone who want to ditch expensive landline service
- **Small business owners** with a fax machine that needs VoIP compatibility
- **Retro phone enthusiasts** who want to use vintage handsets with modern services
- **Budget-conscious VoIP users** who already have a softphone on their PC but want a physical handset

Skip it if you need multiple lines, want wireless DECT support, or expect a consumer-friendly app for configuration.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my existing home phone service?**  
No. The HT801 requires a VoIP provider — it won't convert your existing analog landline. You need a SIP-based service like Vonage, Ooma, or a BYOD provider.

**Q: Can I use it with a cordless phone?**  
Yes, as long as the cordless base station connects via a standard RJ11 jack. The adapter treats it like any analog phone.

**Q: Does it support emergency 911 calls?**  
Yes, but you must configure your address in the VoIP provider's dashboard. The adapter itself doesn't handle E911 registration.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, secure way to keep using your existing analog phone with a VoIP service and don't mind a quick web-based setup. **Skip it if** you need multiple phone ports or want a device that works without any technical configuration.

The Grandstream HT801 isn't flashy, but it's the kind of dependable office gear that quietly does its job for years. At 4.2 stars from 210 buyers, it earns its rating — just know what you're getting into before you buy.
