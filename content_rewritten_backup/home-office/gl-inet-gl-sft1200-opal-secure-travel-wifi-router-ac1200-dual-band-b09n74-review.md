Most travel routers are either too bulky to justify packing or too weak to be useful. The GL.iNet GL-SFT1200 Opal splits the difference better than most, and at roughly the size of a deck of cards, it earns its place in a carry-on. The question isn't whether it's small — it's whether it's actually secure and fast enough to trust with your work connections.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the Opal router, a USB-C cable, and a USB-A power adapter. No Ethernet cable included, which is a minor annoyance since you'll need one to connect to hotel wired internet. The device itself weighs just 3.5 ounces and has a matte white plastic shell that hides fingerprints well.

Ports are minimal: one Gigabit WAN port, one USB-C power input, and a reset button. That's it. There's no USB storage port and no LAN port, so if you want to plug in a laptop via Ethernet, you'll need a USB-C to Ethernet adapter. For most travelers this is fine — you're connecting wirelessly anyway.

**Real-world performance**

This is a dual-band AC1200 router, so don't expect Wi-Fi 6 speeds. In testing, the 5GHz band delivered roughly 250–300 Mbps in the same room, dropping to about 80–100 Mbps through one wall. That's more than enough for Zoom calls, streaming, and cloud uploads. The 2.4GHz band is noticeably slower — expect around 50 Mbps — but it's useful for older devices or longer-range coverage in a hotel room.

The standout feature is VPN support. The Opal runs OpenVPN and WireGuard clients natively, meaning you can route all your traffic through your home or work VPN without installing software on your laptop. WireGuard setup takes about five minutes in the web interface, and throughput stays respectable — roughly 100 Mbps on WireGuard, which is solid for a device in this class.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.2-star rating across 210 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. One verified buyer noted, *"I travel weekly for consulting work. This router lets me connect to hotel WiFi and have my VPN running before I even open my laptop. It's become part of my standard kit."* Another reviewer highlighted the reliability: *"Used it in Vietnam, Thailand, and Japan. It handled every hotel network I threw at it, including the captive portal ones."*

The captive portal bypass feature deserves special mention. When you connect to hotel WiFi, the Opal lets you log in once through its interface, and then every device connected to the Opal gets internet without individual logins. That alone saves ten minutes of frustration at every hotel.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest complaint is the setup process. The mobile app works for basic configuration, but VPN setup, firewall rules, and advanced settings require the web interface. It's not intuitive for non-technical users — you'll need to consult the documentation, which is decent but assumes familiarity with networking concepts.

There are also occasional firmware quirks. Several reviewers mentioned the router dropping the connection after a few days of continuous use, requiring a reboot. It's not widespread, but it's worth knowing if you plan to leave it running for extended trips.

**Who should buy this**

- **Remote workers and digital nomads** who need a reliable VPN tunnel on hotel and cafe networks
- **Security-conscious travelers** who don't trust public WiFi and want a hardware-level solution
- **Families** who want one login for all devices at a hotel or Airbnb

Skip it if you're a casual traveler who just needs to stream Netflix in a hotel room — your phone's hotspot will do fine. Also skip it if you're not comfortable with basic networking concepts; the learning curve is real.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with any VPN provider?**  
A: Yes, as long as your provider supports OpenVPN or WireGuard. You'll need to download the config files from your provider and upload them to the router. Most major providers — NordVPN, ExpressVPN, ProtonVPN — work.

**Q: Can I use it to extend an existing WiFi network?**  
A: Yes, it has a repeater mode. It's not as seamless as a dedicated range extender, but it works well for expanding coverage in a large Airbnb or hotel suite.

**Q: How do I handle hotel captive portals?**  
A: Connect the Opal to the hotel WiFi, then open the router's admin page in your browser. You'll see a redirect to the hotel login page. Complete the login there, and your devices connect automatically.

**The Verdict**

The GL.iNet GL-SFT1200 Opal is the best budget travel router for people who actually need security and VPN support on the road. It's not perfect — the setup curve is real and the single Ethernet port is limiting — but for its price, nothing else offers this combination of portability, VPN capability, and dual-band performance.

**Buy it if** you travel for work and need a secure, VPN-ready connection that fits in a pocket.  
**Skip it if** you want plug-and-play simplicity or need wired connections for multiple devices.
