The GOVO T4 is the office equivalent of buying the "nice" scissors — you didn't know you needed it, but once you use it, everything else feels like a downgrade. In a sea of flimsy $3 badge holders that yellow within a month, this no-clip polycarbonate version stands out for one simple reason: it's built like a tank and stays crystal clear.

## What You Actually Get

Straight out of the package, the difference is obvious. This isn't the soft, bendy PVC you're used to. The T4 is rigid polycarbonate — the same material used in safety glasses and phone cases. The holder measures roughly 3.2 x 2.2 inches, sized for standard CR80 ID cards, and features a vertical slot orientation. As shown in the product image, it's a clean, minimalist design with a pre-punched slot at the top for any standard lanyard or badge reel. No clip, no moving parts, no metal — just a solid, transparent case.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the T4 earns its keep. After three months of daily use — clipped to a lanyard, tossed in bags, swiped at turnstiles — the case still looks new. The polycarbonate resists the fine hairline scratches that turn cheap holders into frosted glass within weeks. One buyer put it simply: *"I've had this for six months and it still looks brand new. My old holder was yellowed and cracked by now."*

The no-clip design is genuinely better for certain use cases. If you wear dress shirts or thin polos, a metal clip leaves unsightly marks and pulls fabric. The T4 slides smoothly against clothing without snagging. The rigid structure also means your card stays flat and protected — no bending in your pocket, no flexing when you squeeze past a crowded doorway.

RFID and proximity cards work fine through the polycarbonate. I tested it with a HID iCLASS card and a standard 125kHz proximity badge — both read instantly at the reader. No interference, no dead zones.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs. The "no-clip" feature is a double-edged sword. If you're someone who attaches badges to a belt loop or waistband, this holder won't work for you — you're locked into a lanyard or retractable reel setup. Also, the rigid polycarbonate takes getting used to. Inserting a card requires a bit of finesse; you can't just squish the plastic apart like you can with PVC. One reviewer noted: *"Took me a minute to figure out how to get the card in without fighting it. Slide it in at an angle, then push straight down."*

There's also no lanyard included. At this price point, that's a minor quibble, but worth knowing before you order.
