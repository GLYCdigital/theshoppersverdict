## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting one sealed Epson T702XL120 black cartridge. That's it. No packaging theatrics, no USB cable, no instructions you'll throw away. The cartridge itself is the standard Epson clamshell design — snap it into your WorkForce printer, run the auto-alignment, and you're printing in under three minutes.

The "XL" designation matters here. This is the high-capacity variant, which Epson rates at roughly 2,600 pages for black ink. The standard T702 runs about 1,100 pages. That's more than double the output for maybe 30-40% more cost per cartridge.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

DURABrite Ultra is Epson's pigment-based ink line, and the difference between pigment and dye ink becomes obvious the moment you print a document you actually need to keep. Pigment ink sits on top of the paper rather than soaking in, which means:

- **Highlighting doesn't smear.** Drag a yellow highlighter across a printed page and the text stays crisp. Try that with a dye-based cartridge and you'll get a streaky mess.
- **Water resistance is real.** Spill coffee on a page and the text survives. The paper will wrinkle, but the ink won't run.
- **Double-sided printing works.** Because the ink dries fast and doesn't bleed through, automatic duplex printing doesn't produce ghosting on the reverse side.

Black text comes out dense and sharp — no grayish cast, no feathering on cheap multipurpose paper. If you're printing contracts, invoices, or anything with fine print, this is the cartridge you want.

One thing I verified from buyer feedback: multiple users confirmed the cartridge actually hits its rated page count. A reviewer named Mark noted, *"I print about 30 pages a day for my real estate business. This cartridge lasted me three months. The standard one lasted five weeks."* That tracks with the math.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The cost per cartridge stings.** At full retail, you're looking at roughly $60-70 for a single black cartridge. That's a hard pill to swallow if you're used to $15 compatibles. Yes, the yield is higher, but the upfront investment is real.

**The smart chip is unforgiving.** Epson's chip monitors ink levels and reports empty when it hits a threshold — not when the cartridge is bone dry. Some users report getting "empty" warnings with a few pages of life left. You can't reset these chips, so that residual ink is wasted.

**No color option in this listing.** This is black only. If you need the full CMYK set, you're buying four separate cartridges, which multiplies the upfront cost significantly.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my printer?**
A: Check your model against Epson's compatibility list. It fits most WorkForce series printers from the WF-3600 through WF-7800 range, plus some EcoTank-free models. Verify before ordering — this cartridge won't fit older Stylus models.

**Q: How long does a cartridge last once installed?**
A: Epson states 2,600 pages at 5% coverage. Real-world usage with full-page documents will be less. Most buyers report 2-3 months of regular office use. The cartridge has a shelf life of about two years sealed, so stockpiling one extra is reasonable.

**Q: Is this ink really waterproof?**
A: Yes, once dry. The pigment particles resist water penetration. Keep in mind — the paper itself will still absorb water and wrinkle, but the text won't run or bleed.
