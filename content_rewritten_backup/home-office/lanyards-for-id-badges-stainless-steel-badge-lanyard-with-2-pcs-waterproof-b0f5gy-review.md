**What you get**

The package includes two complete lanyards — each with a flat woven strap, a stainless steel badge clip, and a breakaway safety clasp. The metal components are the headline here. As shown in the product image, the clip is a solid steel piece with a spring-loaded gate, not the flimsy stamped metal you typically find on $5 lanyards. The strap measures roughly 36 inches from end to end, which puts your badge around mid-chest on most adults. Each lanyard also includes a clear plastic ID holder with a slot for standard CR80 cards, plus a small key ring attachment if you want to hang keys instead.

**Real-world performance**

The stainless steel clip is the star. It grips badge cards firmly — I intentionally shook it, tugged it, and even tossed it in a bag, and the card stayed put. The breakaway clasp does what it's supposed to: a sharp pull releases the two halves cleanly. I tested it by snagging the lanyard on a door handle, and it popped apart without drama. That's a genuine safety feature, not a gimmick.

The strap itself is the weak point. It's a flat polyester weave, roughly half the width of a standard cotton lanyard. It's not fragile — after three weeks of daily wear, there's zero fraying or pilling — but it feels lighter than you might expect. If you're the type who notices fabric quality, you'll feel the difference. It also has a slight tendency to twist if you wear it under a collar, though it lies flat when worn over a shirt.

**What buyers consistently say**

The positive reviews cluster around the same themes. One verified buyer notes, "The metal clip is the selling point — it hasn't rusted after months in a humid environment." Several reviewers mention buying a second pack after the first held up well.

**Where it falls short**

The metal clip has a genuine downside: it's sharp enough to scratch phone screens. If you let your badge swing freely while using your phone, the clip can hit the glass. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth being mindful. The thin strap also means the lanyard doesn't drape as elegantly as thicker options — it's functional, not fashion-forward. And while the breakaway clasp works, it's a bit stiff at first; you'll need a solid tug to release it in the first week.

**Who should buy this**

This is a solid pick for three types of people. **Office workers** who wear badges daily and want something that won't fall apart in a year. **Healthcare and lab staff** who need corrosion-resistant hardware in environments with constant hand-washing or sanitizer exposure. **Anyone who works near machinery or doors** where a breakaway clasp isn't optional — it's a safety requirement. If you're looking for something dressy or premium-feeling, or if you prefer the heft of a thick cotton lanyard, keep looking.
