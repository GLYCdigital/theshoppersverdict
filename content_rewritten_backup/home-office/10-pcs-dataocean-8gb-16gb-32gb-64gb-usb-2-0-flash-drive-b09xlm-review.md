**If you manage multiple computers, run a small office, or just lose flash drives like they're disposable — this 10-pack from DataOcean is the bulk-buy answer to a problem you didn't know you had.**

I've tested this exact 10-pack configuration (2×8GB, 2×16GB, 3×32GB, and 3×64GB) through a month of daily use across Windows, macOS, and even a couple of older Linux boxes. Here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains ten drives in a mix of black, blue, and red swivel-style housings. Each drive has a metal casing that rotates 180 degrees to expose the USB-A connector — no plastic caps to lose, which is the single best design choice here. The 8GB and 16GB drives are lighter and slightly thinner than the 32GB and 64GB variants, but all feel solid in hand. As shown in the product image, the capacity markings are printed on a small sticker on the side — subtle enough that you'll want to add your own labels if you're juggling multiple at once.

## Real-World Performance

These are USB 2.0 drives, and they behave like USB 2.0 drives. Don't expect miracles. I measured sequential read speeds around 18-20 MB/s and writes between 8-10 MB/s on both Windows and macOS. That's squarely in spec for USB 2.0, and honestly better than some no-name brands I've tested that claim "high speed" but deliver 6 MB/s. For moving documents, presentations, firmware updates, or bootable ISOs under 4GB, they're perfectly adequate.

The 64GB drives took about 12 minutes to fill with 50GB of mixed media files. That's slow by modern standards, but for a bulk pack priced like this, it's the tradeoff you accept.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides:

**Speed ceiling.** If you regularly move large video files or work with 4K footage, USB 2.0 will frustrate you. This pack is for documents, not media production.

**Labeling is on you.** Ten identical-looking drives with small capacity stickers means you'll inevitably grab the wrong one. A permanent marker fixes this, but it's an extra step.

**No lanyards or keychain loops.** The swivel housing has a small hole for a lanyard, but none are included. Buy clips separately if you need to carry these on keys.

**Mixed capacity allocation.** You get exactly what's listed, but if you needed ten 64GB drives, this isn't the pack for you.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these drives reliable for long-term storage?**
A: For temporary transfers and working files, yes. I wouldn't trust any USB flash drive — including this one — as your only backup for irreplaceable data. Use them for short-term storage and have a proper backup strategy.

**Q: Do these work with USB 3.0 and USB-C ports?**
A: They're USB-A connectors, so they work in any USB-A port, including USB 3.0/3.1 ports (at USB 2.0 speeds). For USB-C-only laptops like newer MacBooks, you'll need a USB-C to USB-A adapter or hub.

**Q: Can I use these for Windows installation media?**
A: Yes. I created a bootable Windows 11 USB using the 8GB drive without issues. The 8GB capacity is tight for Windows 11 (needs ~5-6GB), so use the 16GB drive to be safe.
