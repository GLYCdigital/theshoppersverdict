Losing your S23 Ultra's S-Pen is a special kind of panic. The replacement from Samsung runs $50–60, and you're stuck waiting for shipping. This third-party option with Bluetooth costs less and, based on 225 ratings sitting at 4.5 stars, it's clearly earning its keep. But "less expensive" doesn't automatically mean "as good." Here's the real breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the pen itself, five extra nibs (the OEM gives you zero spares), a tweezer tool for swapping tips, and a basic instruction card. The pen measures in at the same 105mm length and 5.8mm diameter as the original, so it slides into the S23 Ultra's silo with the same satisfying click. It's available in the standard black finish, which matches the Phantom Black phone.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature is Bluetooth — and it works. The pen pairs like a standard Bluetooth device, and the Air Actions function (waving the pen to control camera zoom, presentations, and media playback) responds reliably in my testing. The click-button shutter release on the camera app has roughly a one-second delay compared to the OEM's near-instant response. Not a dealbreaker, but noticeable if you're shooting fast-moving subjects.

The Wacom digitizer layer inside the pen is the same technology Samsung uses, so handwriting conversion and pressure sensitivity (4,096 levels) feel identical to the original. Palm rejection works as expected since that's handled by the phone's display, not the pen itself.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The consistent theme across positive reviews is value. One buyer put it plainly: *"Works exactly like the original for half the price. I've dropped my phone twice since getting this and the pen still works flawlessly."* Another mentioned the spare nibs as the deciding factor: *"The fact that I get five extra tips sealed it for me. My original pen's nib wore out in eight months."* Long-term durability reports are encouraging — multiple reviewers note six-plus months of daily use without battery degradation or Bluetooth pairing issues.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. Battery life is the biggest one. The OEM S-Pen sips power and can last over a week between charges. This replacement consistently dies around day three or four with moderate Air Action use. The pen charges fully in the silo in about 40 minutes, so it's manageable — just not forget-about-it reliable.

The Bluetooth reconnection can also be sluggish. Several reviewers note that after the phone sits idle overnight, the pen takes 3–5 seconds to re-sync when you pull it out. One reviewer said: *"I have to wiggle it back in the slot for a second to wake it up sometimes."* It's a minor annoyance, not a functional failure.

Finally, there's no firmware update path. Samsung occasionally tweaks S-Pen behavior through One UI updates. When that happens, this pen won't receive those refinements. For most users this won't matter, but it's worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **S23 Ultra owners who lost their S-Pen**: Obvious candidate. You get 90% of the functionality at roughly half the OEM cost.
- **Heavy note-takers**: The included spare nibs make this a better long-term value than the original.
- **Presentation users**: The Air Actions work well enough for slides and media controls.

Skip it if you're a professional photographer relying on the S-Pen's remote shutter multiple times daily — the lag and battery drain will frustrate you. Also skip if you're the type who never charges their devices and expects the pen to always be ready.

## Three Questions Buyers Ask

**Does it fit in the original S23 Ultra silo?**
Yes. The dimensions match the OEM pen exactly, and it charges wirelessly in the slot. No adapter or modification needed.

**Will it work with other Samsung devices?**
It works with any device that supports the S-Pen protocol — Tab S7/S8/S9 series and Note20/Note20 Ultra included. However, it won't fit in the silo of those devices since the form factor differs.

**Is the Bluetooth actually functional or just marketing?**
It's fully functional. Air Actions, remote shutter, and gesture controls all work as expected. The pairing process takes about 30 seconds through the phone's Bluetooth settings.
