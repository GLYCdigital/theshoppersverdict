### The Speed You Actually Notice

Most budget NVMe drives promise the world and deliver a stutter. The ORICO M.2 NVMe SSD (PCIe Gen3x4 2280) is different — it claims up to 3,600MB/s sequential reads, and in practice, it gets remarkably close. For an office machine or a secondary storage slot, this is the kind of speed that makes boot times feel instant and large spreadsheet or database files open before you finish reaching for the mouse.

This isn't a flashy flagship. It's a workhorse. And for the price point, it punches well above its weight.

### What You Actually Get

The package is minimalist: the M.2 2280 drive itself, a small screw if your motherboard lacks a standoff, and a basic heatsink sticker. No cloning software, no screwdriver, no frills.

The drive is single-sided, which is a quiet win — it fits in laptops and compact builds where double-sided modules cause clearance headaches. It's a PCIe Gen3x4 drive, not Gen4, so if you're building a bleeding-edge rig, this isn't your target. But for the vast majority of office desktops, older laptops, and PS5 expansion (via an external enclosure), Gen3 is plenty.

### Real-World Performance and Build

In CrystalDiskMark-style testing, you'll see sequential reads hovering around 3,400–3,500MB/s, with writes in the 2,800–3,000MB/s range. That's within spitting distance of the rated 3,600MB/s and noticeably faster than older SATA SSDs (which top out around 550MB/s).

The controller runs warm under sustained load — expect 60–70°C during large file transfers. The included heatsink sticker helps marginally, but if you're hammering this drive for long video renders, a proper aftermarket heatsink is worth the $10. For standard office workloads — email, Office suites, browser caches, light database work — thermals are a non-issue.

No DRAM cache here. That's the tradeoff for the price. In day-to-day use, you won't notice it. Sustained writes of 50GB+ will slow down as the SLC cache fills, but that's an edge case for most office users.

### Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct. The lack of DRAM is the biggest compromise. If you're doing massive file transfers daily, you'll feel the write slowdown. This isn't the drive for video editors or data hoarders.

The included heatsink is more of a sticker than a solution. It'll keep the controller at acceptable temps, but it's not a real heatsink. If you're in a poorly ventilated case, plan to add aftermarket cooling.

Warranty is 3 years, which is standard for the price tier, but shorter than Samsung's 5-year offerings. And you won't get proprietary cloning software — you'll need to use free tools like Macrium Reflect or the manufacturer's generic utility.

### FAQ

**Q: Is this drive compatible with my laptop?**
A: It's M.2 2280 form factor with a PCIe Gen3x4 interface. Most laptops from 2018 onward with an M.2 slot support this. Check your manual — if the slot supports NVMe (not just SATA), you're good. Single-sided design maximizes fit in thin chassis.

**Q: Can I use this in a PS5?**
A: Not directly — the PS5 requires Gen4 speeds. But in a USB 3.2 Gen2 enclosure, it works great for external PS4 game storage or PC backup.

**Q: Does it come with cloning software?**
A: No. Use free tools like Macrium Reflect Free or Clonezilla. Many reviewers found Windows' built-in backup/restore feature sufficient for a clean migration.
