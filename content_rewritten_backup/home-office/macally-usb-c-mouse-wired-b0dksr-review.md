Most wired mice are afterthoughts — cheap plastic throwaways with cables that kink and sensors that skip. The Macally USB-C mouse doesn't try to be anything it isn't. It's a straightforward, no-nonsense peripheral that does exactly what it promises: plug in, click, scroll, done. At $19.99, it sits in a weird spot between disposable $8 office mice and $40+ ergonomic options. Here's where it actually lands.

## What You Get

The box contains the mouse itself and a 5-foot braided USB-C cable that's permanently attached. There's no dongle, no software disc, no manual worth reading. As shown in the product image, this is a compact, ambidextrous design in matte black — about 4.3 inches long and 2.5 inches wide. The 5-foot cable is generous for desktop setups, and the braided sleeve prevents the kinking that plagues rubber cables on cheaper mice.

## Performance and Build

The optical sensor tracks accurately on wood, fabric, and even glossy surfaces without a mousepad — a common failure point for budget mice. The 1200 DPI isn't adjustable, but for standard office work, spreadsheet navigation, and web browsing, it's more than adequate. The scroll wheel has defined detents without feeling cheap or rattly.

Build quality is genuinely better than the price suggests. The matte finish resists fingerprints, and the chassis doesn't creak when you squeeze it. The cable exits at a reinforced angle that keeps it out of the way. Macally rates the silent switches for 3 million clicks, which translates to years of daily use.

What buyers consistently mention in their reviews is the true plug-and-play nature. One reviewer noted, "Plugged it into my MacBook Pro and it worked instantly — no setup, no drivers, no restart." Another praised the silent operation: "I work in a shared office and nobody hears me clicking anymore."

## Where It Falls Short

The most significant omission is the lack of side buttons. For a $20 mouse in 2026, this is a notable gap. If you're used to thumb-forward/back navigation in browsers, you'll feel the loss immediately.

The compact size is a double-edged sword. Users with larger hands — especially palm grippers — will find their fingers overhanging the edges. One Amazon reviewer summed it up: "Great for travel or small hands, but I had to adjust my grip after a week." It's workable, but not ideal for extended 8-hour sessions.

There's also no DPI switch, which matters if you toggle between a 1080p monitor and a 4K display. And while the cable is braided, it's permanently attached — if it fails, the whole mouse is done.

## Who Should Buy This

This mouse fits three specific scenarios. First, MacBook and Chromebook users who need a reliable, no-drama USB-C peripheral without dongle fuss. Second, anyone who's tired of Bluetooth pairing drops and battery anxiety — a wired mouse eliminates both variables. Third, shared office environments where you don't want someone walking off with a $60 wireless mouse.

Skip it if you need programmable buttons for creative work, if you have large hands, or if you require adjustable DPI for high-resolution displays.

## FAQ

**Does this work with an iPad Pro or Android tablet?** Yes. Any device with a USB-C port that supports mouse input will recognize it immediately. iPads running iPadOS 13.4+ and Android tablets work without configuration.

**Is the cable long enough for a desktop tower?** The 5-foot cable handles most setups, but if your tower sits under a desk with a cable tray, consider a USB-C extension. For laptops and all-in-ones, 5 feet is plenty.

**Does it work on glass surfaces?** No. The optical sensor handles wood, fabric, and glossy plastic, but transparent glass surfaces will defeat it. Use a mousepad on glass.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5 — Buy it if** you want a dependable, silent, plug-and-play mouse for a USB-C laptop and don't need extra buttons. The build quality exceeds its price point, and the 5-foot braided cable is a genuine upgrade over competitors. **Skip it if** you need side buttons, have large hands, or demand adjustable DPI. For its intended purpose — a reliable workhorse for everyday computing — the Macally delivers without fuss.
