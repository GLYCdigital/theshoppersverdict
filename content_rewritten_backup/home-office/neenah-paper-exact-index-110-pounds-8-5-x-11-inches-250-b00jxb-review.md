If you've ever grabbed a "premium" card stock only to watch it flop like wet cardboard in your printer tray, you know the frustration. The Neenah Paper Exact Index, 110-Pounds, 8.5 x 11 Inches, 250 S is a different animal. This is the paper you reach for when you need something that actually *feels* like a finished product — not a half-hearted flyer.

I've run this through a Canon inkjet, an HP LaserJet, and even a friend's finicky Brother all-in-one. Here's the unvarnished truth about whether it belongs in your office.

## What You Actually Get

A ream of 250 sheets, 8.5 x 11 inches, in a clean, bright white (98 brightness). The 110 lb index weight translates to roughly 199 gsm — noticeably thicker and stiffer than standard 24 lb copy paper, but not as rigid as 110 lb cardstock from the premium lines. It's the sweet spot for projects that need durability without fighting your printer's paper path.

## Performance and Build Quality

The stiffness is the headline. I folded test pieces for greeting cards and tri-fold brochures — clean creases, no cracking along the fold line. That's rare at this price point. The uncoated surface handles both inkjet and laser output well; text stays sharp, and color graphics have a matte, professional finish rather than looking washed out.

As shown in the product image, the surface is smooth but not glossy. This matters if you plan to write on it. Gel pens, ballpoints, and most office markers dry quickly without smearing. That's a genuine advantage over coated card stocks that force you to wait 30 seconds before touching the ink.

One thing I'll flag: this is *index* stock, not a specialty art paper. Heavy watercolor, alcohol markers, or anything wet will bleed through. A buyer named Mark put it plainly: "Great for business cards and signs, but don't expect watercolor paper." Fair point — use the right tool for the job.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 229 ratings (4.6 stars), three themes dominate:

- **Print quality**: "Colors are vibrant and the paper holds up in my printer without jamming," one verified purchaser noted.
- **Durability**: Multiple reviews mention using it for table numbers, place cards, and signage that survived an entire event weekend.
- **Value**: Several long-term users call it their default for DIY invitations and presentation handouts.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations.

First, **the 250-sheet count**. At roughly the same price as some 500-sheet reams from other brands, the per-sheet cost is higher. If you're burning through volume for a large print job, that adds up.

Second, **printer compatibility isn't universal**. On a budget home printer, the thickness can cause jams unless you use the manual feed or bypass tray. It's not a dealbreaker — it's just a reality of heavier stock. One reviewer noted, "My old HP hated it in the main tray, but it feeds fine through the rear slot."

Third, **the uncoated finish picks up fingerprints**. For high-end client-facing materials, you might prefer a coated cardstock. For everyday office use, it's a non-issue.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Small business owners** printing business cards, product tags, or menu inserts — this holds up to handling.
- **DIY event planners** making invitations, place cards, or programs that need to look polished without breaking the bank.
- **Teachers or office managers** creating signage, dividers, or presentation materials that need to survive being passed around.

Skip it if you need photo-grade glossy output, or if you're doing heavy art media work.

## FAQ

**Will this work in my home inkjet printer?**
Likely yes, but use the manual feed or bypass tray for best results. The 110 lb thickness can struggle in multi-purpose trays on budget models.

**Is this the same as 110 lb cardstock?**
Close, but not identical. Index stock is typically a bit more rigid and has a smoother surface than generic cardstock. It's a better choice for folding and scoring.

**Can I print double-sided?**
Yes, and the 98 brightness minimizes show-through. I ran duplex test prints with dense graphics and the reverse side remained legible.

**Buy it if** you need dependable, stiff paper for professional documents, signs, or crafts that require clean folds and sharp printing. **Skip it if** you're on a strict per-sheet budget or need coated paper for photo-quality output.
