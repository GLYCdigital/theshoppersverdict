If your team is still clustering around a wall-mounted board that you can't turn, you're losing time. The maxtek 48x32 rolling whiteboard solves that specific problem — and it does it with a double-sided twist that effectively doubles your writing surface without doubling your floor space.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the assembled board panel (already mounted in its aluminum frame), the steel base with four locking casters, the connecting column, a marker tray, and the hardware bag. There's no easel clip or paper pad — just the bare board. The surface measures a true 48 x 32 inches per side. As shown in the product image, the reversible design means you flip the entire frame to access the second side — there's no rotating hinge, so you physically turn the unit around.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

Here's where maxtek separates itself from the $90 rolling boards that wobble every time you write. The frame is extruded aluminum with reinforced corner joints, and the writing panel is a solid 0.7-inch thick composite. On a standard office vinyl floor, the board stays planted — the locking casters engage with a positive click and don't drift mid-session. The magnetic surface holds standard neodymium magnets firmly; even a stack of three sheets of paper stays put.

Dry-erase performance is genuinely good. Low-odor markers wipe clean with a standard felt eraser, and even after leaving a sketch up for 72 hours, a dry wipe removed 95% of the ghosting. A damp cloth handled the rest. The white surface has a slight matte finish that reduces glare under overhead fluorescents — a small detail you'll appreciate during long planning sessions.

## Where It Falls Short

The marker tray is the weakest link. It's a thin plastic channel that clips onto the frame. It holds four standard markers fine, but a tall dry-erase brush or a block eraser can tip it over the edge if you bump the board. Not a dealbreaker, but you'll learn to place items carefully.

Assembly is a two-person job. The panel is heavy — around 35 pounds — and attaching it to the base column requires aligning bolts while holding the board steady. Budget 30 minutes. One-handed assembly is not realistic.

Finally, there's no paper-pad clamp. If you regularly use flip charts, you'll need to buy a separate easel clip. The board is magnetic, so you could rig a solution, but it's not built-in.
