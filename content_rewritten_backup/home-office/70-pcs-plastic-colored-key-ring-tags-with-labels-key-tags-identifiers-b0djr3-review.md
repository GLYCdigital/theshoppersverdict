## What You Actually Get

The box delivers 70 complete tag assemblies: 70 colored plastic tags, 70 steel split rings, and 70 blank paper inserts. You get 10 distinct colors — red, blue, green, yellow, orange, purple, pink, white, black, and one more in the mix. Each tag measures roughly 1.5 inches long with a 0.75-inch label window, which is a practical size for writing "Locker 42" or "Warehouse B" without squinting.

The paper inserts are plain white on both sides. That's a smart design choice — no pre-printed "KEY" text cluttering your custom labels. You write what you need, slide it in, and snap the tag shut.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

Here's where this set differs from the cheap stuff. The plastic tags are rigid polypropylene — not the brittle acrylic you'll snap in half on day one. The snap-shut mechanism closes firmly but opens easily when you need to swap labels. That matters more than you'd think; cheaper tags crack at the hinge within a week.

The split rings surprised me. They're smooth-bore steel, which means no sharp edges cutting into your fingers during installation. They open with a modest pry — no thumbnail sacrifices required. That's a genuine quality-of-life improvement if you've wrestled with cheap rings before.

During real-world testing, the tags held up to daily jangling on a janitorial cart. They're not indestructible — the split rings are thin gauge, so don't expect to hang these off heavy machinery — but for keys, lockers, cables, and tool organization, they're more than adequate.

## Where It Falls Short

The paper inserts are uncoated. If you use a standard ballpoint pen, you're fine. But if you reach for a wet-ink marker or gel pen, expect smudging. One reviewer warned, "The labels blur if you write with anything but a sharpie." That's a minor gripe, but worth knowing before you label all 70 tags in one sitting.

The split rings are the other weak point. They're functional but thin — roughly 1mm gauge. Fine for keys and light tags, but don't attach these to anything that sees heavy vibration or weight. You'll get ring failure before tag failure.
