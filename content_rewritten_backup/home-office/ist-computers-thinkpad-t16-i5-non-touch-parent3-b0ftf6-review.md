Let's cut through the listing copy. This is a refurbished Lenovo ThinkPad T16, sold by a third-party seller called "ist computers." The 2.7-star average across 150 ratings tells you something important: the hardware is fine, but the seller experience is where things get dicey.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the laptop, a 65W USB-C charger, and — if you're lucky — a basic documentation sheet. That's it. No sleeve, no docking station, no external mouse. The listing photos show a clean T16, but several buyers note the actual unit arrives with scuffs, scratches, or a worn palm rest that wasn't visible in the product images.

The T16 itself is a solid business laptop. It's the current-gen ThinkPad chassis with MIL-SPEC durability claims, a spill-resistant keyboard, and that iconic red TrackPoint nub in the middle of the keyboard. The i5 processor (likely a 1240P or 1340P depending on the batch) is more than adequate for typical office tasks. The 16-inch display at 1920x1200 resolution gives you 11% more vertical screen real estate than a standard 1080p panel — genuinely useful for spreadsheets and document review.

## The Good Stuff

When the unit is in good condition, buyers consistently praise the keyboard. One reviewer put it simply: "Typing on this feels like my old T480 but with a bigger screen. The key travel is deep and tactile." That's the ThinkPad signature — no other laptop brand matches it for typing comfort.

Performance is predictable. The i5 with 16GB of RAM (most configurations ship with this) handles 20+ browser tabs, Slack, and a video call simultaneously without stutter. Storage is typically a 512GB NVMe SSD — boot times are under 10 seconds. For an office workhorse, this is plenty.

The port selection is genuinely useful: two USB-C (one with Thunderbolt 4), two USB-A, HDMI 2.0, and a full-size Ethernet jack. No dongle needed for most office setups.

## Where It Falls Apart

Here's the honest part. The laptop itself is a 4/5 product. The seller drags it down to a 2.7 average.

**Quality control is the biggest issue.** Multiple buyers report the same pattern: the listing shows "Excellent condition" but the unit arrives with a cracked corner, a loose hinge, or a battery that's clearly at end-of-life. One verified review states: "Battery lasted 2 hours 40 minutes on a full charge. Contacted seller, got a runaround for a week before they offered a partial refund."

**Battery variance is a real gamble.** The T16's 52.5Wh battery should give 6-8 hours of mixed use. But refurbished units don't always get fresh batteries. Some buyers report the full 8 hours; others get the 2-3 hour experience. There's no way to know before purchase.

**The display is mediocre.** At around 300 nits, it's fine indoors but struggles near windows or in bright conference rooms. The non-touch panel also has noticeable backlight bleed on some units — a QA issue, not a design flaw.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're on a strict budget, need a durable business laptop, and are comfortable dealing with a potential return or exchange. The ThinkPad T16 chassis is genuinely excellent — the typing experience and build quality are worth the risk if you can handle the seller roulette.

**Skip it if:** You need reliable battery life out of the box, or you're buying for a non-technical family member who can't troubleshoot. The 2.7-star rating is a real signal. If you can stretch your budget by $100-200, look for a certified refurbished ThinkPad from Lenovo directly or a reputable seller like Amazon Renewed.
