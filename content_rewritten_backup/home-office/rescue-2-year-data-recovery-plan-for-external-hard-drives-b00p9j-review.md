# Rescue - 2 Year Data Recovery Plan for External Hard Drives Review

## The Insurance Policy Your Hard Drive Desperately Needs

Here's the uncomfortable truth about external hard drives: they *will* fail. Not might—will. The average lifespan of a consumer external drive is 3-5 years, and when they die, the data they hold—family photos, business records, years of work—often dies with them. That's exactly the scenario the Rescue 2 Year Data Recovery Plan is designed to address, and at 4.3 stars from 215 ratings, it's a service that's proven its worth to real users.

## What You're Actually Getting

This isn't a physical product—it's a service contract. For two years from activation, you're covered for professional data recovery on one external hard drive. The plan covers drives up to 2TB, which means most consumer external drives qualify. When your drive fails, you ship it to the recovery lab, and their engineers attempt to pull your data using specialized equipment and cleanroom environments.

The activation process is straightforward: you purchase the plan, register your drive online, and you're covered. The service includes a success-or-no-fee guarantee—if they can't recover your data, you don't pay for the recovery attempt.

Here's where this product gets interesting. Professional data recovery services typically charge **$300 to $1,500+** depending on the severity of the failure. The Rescue plan costs a fraction of that upfront, and it covers you for 24 months. For anyone storing irreplaceable data on an external drive, the math makes sense immediately.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. First, this covers **one drive only**—if you have multiple external drives, you need multiple plans. Second, the 2TB limit excludes larger drives, which is increasingly common for video editors and photographers. Third, and this is important: the plan doesn't cover SSDs, only traditional HDDs. If you're using a solid-state external drive, this isn't for you.

There's also the shipping concern. You're responsible for packaging and sending the drive to their facility, which means trusting your dead drive to a shipping carrier. Most buyers report this goes fine, but it's a point of anxiety for some.

Finally, some users report the registration process was more cumbersome than expected, requiring model numbers and serial verification before the plan activates.

## FAQ

**Q: What happens if my drive fails in year two?**
A: You're covered for the full 24 months from activation. Ship the drive, and the recovery lab will attempt the recovery. If successful, you get your data back on a new drive or via digital transfer.

**Q: Does this work if my drive is physically damaged?**
A: Yes, provided the data platters are intact. The plan covers mechanical, electrical, and logical failures. Physical damage like a cracked case or bent connector is generally fine—the lab works on the internal components.

**Q: Can I transfer the plan to a new drive?**
A: No. The plan is tied to the specific drive you registered at activation. If you replace the drive, you need a new plan.
