If you're still weighing packages on a bathroom scale and guessing at the difference between 3.2 and 3.8 pounds, you're losing money on shipping. The KKZ 440 lb Heavy Duty Postal Scale is built for the seller who's past hobby stage — or the home office that gets hit with a brick of Amazon returns every month. With 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, this thing has real-world mileage. Here's whether it earns a spot on your packing table.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the scale itself, a removable stainless steel platform, and a user manual. That's it — no AC adapter included, which is worth noting before you order. The scale runs on 3 AA batteries (not included either), or you can pick up a 6V adapter separately if you want it plugged in permanently. The unit measures 16.5" x 16.5" overall with a 14.2" x 14.2" weighing surface — big enough for a medium-sized moving box, not just padded envelopes.

**Performance and build quality**

The headline spec is the 440 lb capacity with 0.1 lb resolution. That's the sweet spot for most small businesses — you can weigh a single poly mailer or a box of cast iron parts without switching equipment. In practice, the scale reads consistently and settles quickly; you're not standing there waiting for the numbers to stop bouncing. The tare function works cleanly for zeroing out packing materials or a tote you're weighing into.

The stainless steel platform is the right call. It wipes down easily, resists the scuffs and scratches that come with tape guns and box cutters, and doesn't flex under heavy loads. The backlit LCD is readable in dim garages or bright warehouses — no squinting. One design choice to flag: the display is fixed, not rotating. If you plan to use this on a cart or shelf, you'll need to orient the scale so you can actually read it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 229 ratings, the recurring themes are accuracy and capacity. One verified buyer notes, "I've checked it against the scales at the post office and it's always within an ounce or two — good enough that I stopped second-guessing my labels." Another seller mentions, "I ship heavy auto parts and this handled a 380 lb box without flinching. The platform is actually big enough for what I throw on it." The tare function gets repeated love from people who weigh items inside containers.

**Where it falls short**

The missing AC adapter is the most common gripe — it's a $10 add-on, but it's the kind of thing that should ship in the box. A few buyers also mention that the scale can be sensitive to being placed on uneven surfaces, so if your packing table wobbles, you'll see it reflected in the readings. And while 0.1 lb resolution is fine for USPS and UPS, if you ship small, expensive items where every ounce matters, you might want a finer-grained scale for those packages and use this for the heavy stuff.

**Who should buy this**

This is for three people: the eBay or Etsy seller shipping 10+ packages a week, the home business owner dealing with freight or heavy returns, and anyone tired of the "is this 70 lbs or 80 lbs?" guessing game. It's not for someone shipping only bubble mailers — you'd be better off with a smaller, cheaper unit. But if you've got a mix of light and heavy, this covers the whole range.

**FAQ**

*Does the KKZ scale come with a warranty?*
Yes — KKZ includes a 12-month warranty on the scale. Register your purchase through the included documentation to activate it.

*Can I use this to weigh myself?*
Technically yes, it supports up to 440 lbs. But the platform is small for human feet, and you'll probably be more comfortable with a dedicated bathroom scale.

*How accurate is it at low weights?*
At weights under 1 lb, resolution drops to 0.1 lb increments, which can feel coarse. For precise small-package weights, consider a separate gram-scale for items under a pound.

**Skip it if:** You only ship lightweight items, need ounce-level precision on small packages, or require a rotating display for your setup.
