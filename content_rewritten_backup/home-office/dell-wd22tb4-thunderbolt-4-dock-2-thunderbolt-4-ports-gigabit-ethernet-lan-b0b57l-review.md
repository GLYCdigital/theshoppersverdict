## This Dock Solves a Real Problem — If You Have the Right Laptop

The Dell WD22TB4 isn't trying to be the cheapest dock on Amazon. It's trying to be the *only* dock you need for a modern Thunderbolt 4 laptop — particularly one of Dell's own Precision or XPS machines. And for that specific use case, it genuinely delivers. But with a 3.8-star average across 190 ratings, there's clearly a gap between what Dell intended and what buyers experienced. Let's sort through that.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the dock itself — a compact, dark-gray aluminum slab roughly 4.5 inches wide and 3 inches deep. It's heavier than it looks, which is good: it won't slide around your desk. Included are a 180W AC adapter (the dock uses up to 50W for itself, leaving 130W for your laptop), a 0.8-meter Thunderbolt 4 cable, and a small stand that props the dock vertically if you're tight on desk space. Notably absent: any HDMI or DisplayPort cables, which matters — more on that below.

The port layout is sensible. On the back: two Thunderbolt 4 downstream ports, one USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 (10Gbps), one USB-A 3.2 Gen 1 (5Gbps), Gigabit Ethernet, and the DC-in. On the front: a second USB-A port, a 3.5mm audio jack, and a combo SD/microSD card reader. It's a practical arrangement — you can plug your mouse, keyboard, and card reader into the front without reaching around the back of your desk.

## Real-World Performance: The Good and the Hot

Connected to a Dell XPS 15 or Precision 5570, this dock shines. The 130W power delivery means your laptop charges at full speed even under heavy load — something many cheaper docks can't manage. I saw the XPS battery climb from 20% to 80% in about an hour while running a video render. Dual 4K@60Hz external displays ran flawlessly, with no flicker or dropout during a full day of spreadsheets, browser tabs, and video calls.

The Gigabit Ethernet port is rock solid — no packet loss, full 940Mbps throughput on a wired connection.

But here's where it gets uncomfortable: the dock runs hot. Under sustained load — say, two external displays plus laptop charging — the aluminum body gets noticeably warm to the touch. It's not a safety hazard, but it's uncomfortable to rest your hand on, and several Amazon reviewers flagged the same thing. One buyer wrote: *"After three hours of work, it's hot enough that I moved it off my desk to a shelf."* That matches my experience.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint in owner reviews is the firmware update process. Dell requires you to install Dell Command Monitor software on your host laptop to update the dock's firmware — and it's Windows-only. Mac users who buy this dock for a MacBook Pro (it works, mostly) can't update firmware at all. That's a real limitation.

Second, there's no HDMI or DisplayPort output on the dock itself. Every display connection runs through USB-C. If your monitors are older with only HDMI or DP inputs, you're buying USB-C to HDMI/DP cables or adapters — another $20-40 per display. For a dock in this price range, that's a puzzling omission.

Third, the card reader is slow. The SD slot reads at roughly 85MB/s — fine for photo offloading, but if you're editing video from an SD card, you'll feel the bottleneck.

## Who Should Buy This — And Who Shouldn't

**Buy it if:** You own a Dell XPS 13/15/17 or Precision laptop and want a single-cable docking solution with full 130W charging. The integration with Dell's power management is seamless, and the two Thunderbolt 4 downstream ports future-proof you for daisy-chaining fast external SSDs or second docks.

**Skip it if:** You're on a Mac. The firmware limitations and warm chassis make it a poor fit. Also skip if your monitors are HDMI-only and you want a dock that comes ready to go without extra cables.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this dock charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro at full speed?**
A: No. It delivers 130W over USB-C Power Delivery, which will charge a MacBook Pro, but not at the full 140W the M1/M2/M3 Max models support. Expect slower charging under heavy load.

**Q: Can I connect three external monitors?**
A: Only if your laptop supports Thunderbolt 4 daisy-chaining and you use the downstream TB4 port for the third display. Most laptops will top out at two 4K@60Hz displays through this dock.

**Q: Is the SD card reader fast enough for 4K video?**
A: Barely. It reads at about 85MB/s — sufficient for 4K photo offloading but too slow for sustained 4K video editing. Use a dedicated USB-C card reader for that.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a Dell laptop owner who needs reliable 130W charging, dual 4K displays, and stable wired networking in one clean package.

**Skip it if:** You're on a Mac, you have HDMI-only monitors, or you're hoping for a cool-running dock — this one runs warm.
