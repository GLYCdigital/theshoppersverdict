If you're the type who needs your week laid out in front of you — not buried in an app notification — the POPRUN Planner 2025-2026 deserves a hard look. This is a no-nonsense weekly notebook that skips the fluff and focuses on one thing: giving you a clear, dated spread for every week from July 2025 through June 2026. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right planner for *your* workflow? Let's dig into the details.

## What You Actually Get

The POPRUN is a 5.7" x 8.3" softcover notebook — roughly A5 size — that runs from July 2025 to June 2026. That mid-year start is a smart move for educators, academic-year planners, and anyone whose "new year" begins in September rather than January. Inside the 160 pages, you get:

- A yearly overview at the front (compact, not detailed)
- 52 dated weekly spreads
- Each spread features a left page with hourly breakdowns (7 AM to 8 PM) and a right page with a lined notes section
- A back pocket for loose receipts and business cards

The binding is the real standout. The lay-flat design means you can crack it open to any week and it stays put — no hands, no paperweights. That's rarer than you'd think in this price range.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 100gsm paper is genuinely good. As shown in the product image, the pages are bright white with a subtle grid — actually, the weekly spreads use a light gray layout that's visible but not distracting. I tested it with a Pilot G2 gel pen and a mildliner highlighter. No bleed-through. No ghosting. That's a level of paper quality you'd expect from a $30+ notebook, not a budget weekly planner.

The cover is a soft-touch matte finish that feels premium in hand. The downside? It picks up fingerprints and smudges like crazy. If you're tossing this in a bag with loose items, it's going to look scuffed within a couple weeks. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"The cover is beautiful but I wish it was more durable — it's already showing scratches after a month in my work bag."* That's a fair criticism.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The layout is the star here. A 4.6-star average across 229 ratings tells a consistent story. Shoppers repeatedly mention:

- **The hourly breakdown** — one buyer wrote, *"Finally a planner where I can actually time-block my workday without drawing my own lines."*
- **The notes page** — the right-side lined page for each week gives you room for meeting notes, grocery lists, or journaling without needing a second notebook.
- **The compact size** — it fits in a standard tote or briefcase without adding bulk.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**No monthly spread.** This is the big one. If you plan by the month — mapping out deadlines and appointments on a calendar grid before drilling into weeks — this planner will frustrate you. It's weekly-overview only, plus that thin yearly glance at the front. You'll need a separate monthly calendar or a digital companion.

**Hourly slots are compact.** Each hour gets roughly a half-inch of vertical space. That's fine for short notes like "10 AM — client call," but if you write large or need to capture detailed meeting agendas, you'll run out of room by Tuesday. The notes page on the right helps, but it's not a substitute for spacious time blocks.

**Cover durability.** As mentioned, the soft-touch finish looks great out of the box but doesn't age gracefully. If you're rough on your gear, consider a cover sleeve.

## Who Should Buy This

This planner is ideal for two distinct buyers:

1. **The working professional** who needs a daily time-blocking system. The hourly layout is perfect for consultants, project managers, or anyone juggling multiple meetings per day.
2. **The student or educator** on an academic calendar. The July-to-June span means you can use it straight through the entire school year.

Skip it if you're a monthly-first planner, or if you need a hardcover that can survive being tossed into a backpack without a care.
