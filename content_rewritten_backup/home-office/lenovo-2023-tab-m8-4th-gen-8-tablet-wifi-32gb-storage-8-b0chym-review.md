**The 8-inch tablet is a weird middle child — too big for a pocket, too small for serious work. But for a specific kind of buyer, the Lenovo Tab M8 (4th Gen) hits a sweet spot that bigger flagships miss entirely.**

I've spent a week with this 2023 refresh, and here's the honest picture: this is a $100-ish device that knows exactly what it is. It's not a laptop replacement. It's not a gaming rig. It's a dedicated media-consumption and light-browsing companion that gets the fundamentals right without pretending to be something more.

**What you actually get:** The tablet itself (with that surprisingly nice metal back — check the product images and you'll see it's not the usual glossy plastic), a 10W wall charger, and a USB cable. That's it. No case, no stylus, no screen protector. At 312 grams and 8.1mm thin, it's genuinely portable — toss it in a bag and forget it's there until you need it.

**Display and performance — the real story.** The 8-inch IPS panel runs at 1340x800 with 350 nits brightness, and honestly? For the price, it's a standout. Colors are punchy, viewing angles hold up, and it's perfectly legible outdoors at moderate brightness. Streaming 1080p on YouTube or Netflix looks sharp at this screen size.

The MediaTek Helio A22 is where expectations need adjustment. This is a quad-core chip from 2018, and it shows. Single-app use is fine — scrolling social feeds, reading, watching video all work smoothly. But push it: open Chrome with 4-5 tabs, switch to YouTube, hop to WhatsApp, and you'll see stutter. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"Works great for basic tasks, but don't expect to do real work on it. It's a consumption device."* They're right.

**Battery life genuinely impresses.** The 5000mAh cell gives 10-12 hours of mixed use. I streamed two full movies, browsed for three hours, and still had 35% left. That's real-world, not spec-sheet, endurance. The flip side: it charges via Micro USB at 10W, so a full recharge takes nearly three hours. That's an annoying throwback decision from Lenovo.

**Storage reality check.** The 32GB spec sounds workable until you realize Android 13 and preinstalled apps eat roughly 15GB. You're left with about 17GB — enough for a handful of offline Netflix episodes, or maybe 3,000 photos. There's a microSD slot (up to 1TB), so that's the workaround. Budget for a card if you buy this.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The display quality at this price, battery endurance, and the build quality. Multiple verified purchasers noted the metal back makes it feel like a more expensive device — one called it *"surprisingly premium for the money."* The 4.1-star average across 204 ratings reflects real satisfaction for the right use case.

**Where it falls short:** The processor, the Micro USB port, and the limited storage. Also, the front camera is 2MP — fine for video calls, useless for anything else. And the speaker is single, downward-firing, and gets muffled if your hand covers it. Use headphones for anything beyond casual listening.

**Who should buy this:**
- **The binge-watcher:** Someone who wants a dedicated Netflix/Prime Video device that lasts all day and fits in a purse or small bag
- **The kids' tablet buyer:** Parents who want something durable, cheap, and easy to lock down with Google Family Link
- **The budget-conscious reader:** Heavy Kindle/PDF consumers who want a bigger screen than a phone without spending iPad money
- **The secondary device seeker:** Someone who wants a kitchen counter or nightstand companion that won't hurt if it gets dropped

**Skip it if:** You need to multitask, do any photo/video editing, play demanding games, or want modern USB-C charging. The Tab M8 will frustrate you in those scenarios.

**FAQ — what shoppers actually ask:**

**Can this run Microsoft Office or Google Docs?** Yes, but only for light editing. Typing on the 8-inch screen is cramped, and the A22 chip slows down with larger documents. Fine for quick notes, not for serious work. Pair it with a Bluetooth keyboard if you must.

**Is the screen good for reading?** Excellent. The 350-nit brightness and IPS panel make for comfortable e-reading, and the compact size means you can hold it one-handed for hours. It's lighter than most paperbacks.

**Does it support Netflix/Prime Video in HD?** Yes, both stream at 1080p. No Widevine L1 issues here — a common budget-tablet complaint that Lenovo got right.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 stars**

**Buy it if:** You want an affordable, reliable media-consumption device with standout battery life and a surprisingly good screen. At this price, it's the best 8-inch option for streaming and casual browsing.

**Skip it if:** You need performance for work or gaming, or you're already invested in USB-C accessories. The Tab M8's limitations are real — but so is its value for the right buyer.
