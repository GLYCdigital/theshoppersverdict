**Is the HP 63 black cartridge worth your money, or just a printer-company cash grab? Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 235 verified buyer experiences.**

Ink is where printer companies make their real profit, and the HP 63 is one of the most widely used cartridges on the market—it fits dozens of Deskjet, Envy, and OfficeJet models. But "widely used" doesn't automatically mean "good value." Let's cut through the marketing and look at what you're actually getting.

## What's in the Box

You get a single HP 63 black ink cartridge in sealed retail packaging. It's the standard (non-XL) version rated for approximately 190 pages. The cartridge ships with a pull-tab protective seal—no separate printhead to install, no messy priming. As the product image shows, it's a compact unit with the integrated printhead built directly into the cartridge, which is why installation takes about 30 seconds.

## Performance and Build Quality

The HP 63 uses a thermal inkjet printhead with pigment-based black ink. That pigment formulation matters: it dries quickly and resists smearing when you highlight over printed text. In real-world testing, text comes out sharp at 600 dpi effective resolution—fine for school papers, contracts, and shipping labels.

One thing buyers consistently note is the cartridge's reliability. There's no "first-print streakiness" that plagues cheaper remanufactured options. One verified buyer put it this way: *"It works perfectly, no streaks, no clogs, and the ink lasts a good amount of time for normal printing."* Another mentioned it's *"genuine HP so no compatibility issues whatsoever."*

The integrated printhead is the key advantage here. Because the printhead is replaced with every cartridge, you're less likely to deal with clogged nozzles than with older printer models that have permanent printheads.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 235 ratings, three themes dominate:

- **Zero compatibility headaches** — It's genuine HP, so the printer recognizes it instantly. No "non-genuine cartridge" warnings, no firmware conflicts.
- **Dependable page yield** — Most users report hitting close to the advertised 190 pages with standard 5% coverage documents.
- **Crisp, professional-looking output** — Even on plain multipurpose paper, text comes out dark and uniform.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the economics. At standard capacity, the HP 63 costs roughly **$0.06–0.07 per page**. That's not terrible, but the XL version cuts that nearly in half. If you print more than 20 pages a week, you're throwing money away by not buying the high-yield cartridge.

Second, HP has locked these cartridges with a chip that makes refilling impractical. Some users report that third-party refill kits work initially, but the printer often stops recognizing the cartridge's ink level after a refill. You're essentially locked into buying new OEM cartridges—which is exactly how HP designed it.

Finally, a small but notable complaint: the cartridge can be hard to find in physical stores. It's frequently out of stock at big-box retailers, so online ordering (with a backup cartridge on hand) is the practical approach.

## Who Should Buy This

**The home office warrior** printing 10–30 pages a week of documents, schoolwork, and occasional labels will find this cartridge perfectly adequate. It's also ideal for **infrequent printers** who need reliability over months of idle time—the sealed design prevents drying out.

**Skip it if** you're a high-volume printer (50+ pages weekly). Go straight for the XL version and save yourself the per-page cost. And if you're on a tight budget and your printer is older, consider whether a tank-based printer would serve you better long-term.

## FAQ

**Q: How many pages does the HP 63 actually print?**
A: HP rates it at 190 pages with 5% page coverage. In practice, expect 150–200 pages for standard text documents. Heavy graphics or photos will reduce that significantly.

**Q: Will this work in my HP Deskjet 3755 or Envy 5055?**
A: Yes. The HP 63 is compatible with over 20 printer models, including the Deskjet 1110–3755 series, Envy 4500–6075 series, and OfficeJet 3830–4650 series. Check HP's compatibility list if you're unsure.

**Q: Is the XL version worth the extra cost?**
A: Absolutely—if you print regularly. The XL yields 480 pages and typically costs about 1.5x the standard cartridge, dropping your cost per page by roughly 40%. For regular printing, it's the smarter buy.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want hassle-free, OEM-quality printing without compatibility risks, and you print modest volumes. It's the safest, most dependable choice for your HP printer.

**Skip it if** you print frequently and want better economics—or if you're considering a switch to a refillable tank printer for long-term savings.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Reliable, sharp, and painless. The premium price for standard capacity is the only real friction point, but for most households, the convenience justifies the cost.
