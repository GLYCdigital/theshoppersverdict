**The Inateck BK02007 isn't trying to be a Magic Keyboard clone. That's its strength.** At a fraction of Apple's price, it delivers the two things most iPad users actually need — a solid typing experience and a protective shell — without the premium markup. After 215 Amazon ratings averaging 4.3 stars, the consensus is clear: this is the budget keyboard case that gets the basics right.

**What's in the box:** The BK02007 includes the iPad case (sold in multiple size variants — verify your iPad model matches before ordering), a detachable magnetic keyboard, and a USB-C charging cable. The keyboard pairs via Bluetooth and holds a charge for roughly 90 days of typical use, per the manufacturer's specs. The case itself is a two-piece design: a back shell with a built-in kickstand and a front cover that folds into a typing wedge.

**Build quality and real-world use:** The shell is a hard polycarbonate with a soft-touch finish that resists fingerprints — a nice upgrade from the glossy plastic you see on cheaper options. The kickstand is the standout feature here. It adjusts to multiple angles and locks firmly; I tested it on a lap and a couch armrest, and it didn't collapse or wobble. That's rare in this price tier.

The keyboard keys have about 1.2mm of travel, which is shallow but acceptable for a folio-style case. Typing feel is crisp with a satisfying click, though it's not as quiet as Apple's scissor-switch keys. The full shortcut row works flawlessly with iPadOS — brightness, volume, media controls, and a dedicated lock button all function without extra setup.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The detachable keyboard is the most-cited win. One reviewer noted, *"I can pop the keyboard off and just use it as a stand for watching movies — that flexibility is why I picked this over a fixed-keyboard case."* Another praised the pencil holder: *"The side slot keeps my Apple Pencil charged and accessible, which my old case didn't do."* The multi-angle kickstand also gets repeated shout-outs for being more versatile than the fixed 30-degree angle on many competitors.

**Where it falls short:** The trackpad is the weakest link. It's responsive for basic cursor movement and clicking, but gestures like two-finger scrolling and three-finger swipes require precise pressure. One frustrated buyer wrote, *"The trackpad works, but I still reach for the screen half the time — it's just not as natural as a MacBook's."* There's also no backlighting, which is a genuine miss for anyone who types in bed or on a dim plane. And at roughly 1.2 pounds with the keyboard attached, it's noticeably heavier than a slim folio — you're trading portability for protection.

**Who should buy this:**
- **The student** — needs a durable case for class, a reliable keyboard for notes, and doesn't want to blow $300 on Apple's option.
- **The traveler** — wants a detachable keyboard for cramped airplane trays and a kickstand for hotel-room Netflix.
- **The budget-conscious iPad Pro/Air owner** — who values protection and typing over trackpad finesse.

**Skip it if:** You primarily use your iPad for drawing or media consumption and rarely type more than a sentence. Or if you need backlit keys for low-light work — this case will frustrate you nightly.
