### The Budget Gen4 King? Not Quite, But Close

If you’re looking at the fanxiang NVMe SSD, you’re likely playing a familiar game: squeezing as much speed as possible out of every dollar. This drive promises PCIe Gen4 speeds—up to 5,200 MB/s—for a price that often undercuts the big names by 20–30%. As shown in the product image, it’s a standard 2280 form factor (22mm wide, 80mm long), which means it fits in virtually every modern motherboard, laptop, and the PlayStation 5.

But is it a reliable daily driver for your workspace, or just a cheap benchmark queen? I’ve dug through the specs and the 220+ ratings (4.4 stars) to give you the real story.

### What You Actually Get

In the box, you get the M.2 drive itself and a basic graphene heat-dissipation sticker. That’s it. No screw, no screwdriver, no cloning software license. For a PC build, you’ll likely already have a screw; for a PS5 upgrade, you’ll need to make sure you have the correct screwdriver handy. The drive is available in capacities ranging from 500GB up to 4TB, and it uses the NVMe 1.4 protocol.

### The Speed Reality Check

Let’s address the elephant in the room: the 5,200 MB/s claim. In synthetic benchmarks like CrystalDiskMark, multiple reviewers confirm hitting 5,100–5,200 MB/s read speeds. That’s genuinely fast for the price. However, this is where the "DRAM-less" design comes into play. The fanxiang uses HMB (Host Memory Buffer) technology, which borrows a small chunk of your system RAM to manage the mapping table.

**The practical impact:** For booting Windows, loading Office suites, or launching a game like *Cyberpunk 2077*, you will not notice a difference between this and a $200 Samsung 990 Pro. The drive feels snappy and responsive. The issue arises with sustained writes—moving a 50GB video file or installing a massive game. Once the SLC cache (roughly 45GB on the 1TB model) is exhausted, write speeds plummet to around 600–800 MB/s. That’s still faster than a SATA SSD, but it’s a significant drop-off.

### What Buyers Are Saying

The consensus on Amazon is overwhelmingly positive, with users praising the value proposition. One verified buyer noted, *"Upgraded my old laptop, and it’s like a new machine. Boot time went from 2 minutes to 15 seconds."* Another user highlighted the PS5 compatibility, stating, *"Fits perfectly in the PS5, read speeds are well above the minimum requirement for the console."*

However, there are consistent warnings about heat. One critical review mentioned, *"After 20 minutes of heavy file transfer, the drive got very hot to the touch."* This is the tradeoff of the graphene sticker. Most buyers report it works fine in a desktop with decent airflow, but if you have a cramped ITX build or a laptop without a dedicated thermal pad, you should invest in a cheap aluminum heatsink ($10–$15) to keep thermals in check and prevent throttling.

### Where It Falls Short

Beyond the lack of DRAM, the warranty is a point of contention. While it comes with a 5-year warranty, the endurance rating (TBW) is lower than premium competitors. The 1TB model is rated for 600 TBW, whereas a WD Black SN850X is rated for 1,200 TBW. For a standard office worker or gamer, you will never hit that limit in the drive's lifespan. But if you are doing heavy 4K video editing or constant data logging, this might not be the drive for your main workstation.

### Who Should Buy This?

- **The Budget PC Builder:** If you're building a gaming rig on a tight budget and want Gen4 speeds without the Gen4 price tag, this is a no-brainer.
- **The PS5 Gamer:** It meets the console's speed requirements easily and is one of the cheapest ways to expand storage. Just buy it without the heatsink version and add your own, or get the version that includes one.
- **The Laptop Upgrader:** The single-sided design makes it a safe bet for thin laptops where double-sided drives won't fit.

### FAQ: Real Shopper Questions

**Q: Is this compatible with my PS5?**
Yes. It meets the minimum read speed requirements and uses the standard 2280 form factor. You will need to install the included graphene sticker and ensure you have a heatsink (either the one sold with the SSD or a third-party one) to fit the console's slot.

**Q: Is it faster than a SATA SSD?**
Absolutely. You can expect roughly 5–6 times faster sequential read speeds. If you're upgrading from an older 2.5" SSD or a hard drive, this is a massive leap in system responsiveness.

**Q: Does it run hot?**
It can. The graphene sticker is not a heatsink. For desktop use with a case fan blowing over the motherboard, it's fine. For laptops or consoles, I strongly recommend adding a low-profile copper or aluminum heatsink to prevent thermal throttling during long transfers.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want the best performance-per-dollar on the market right now, and you're okay with a slight dip in sustained write speeds and adding your own cooling solution.

**Skip it if:** You are a content creator dealing with massive files daily, or you want a "set it and forget it" drive with the highest endurance rating and a bundled heatsink. In that case, spend the extra cash on a DRAM-equipped drive from a tier-one brand.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A fantastic value that delivers on its headline speed, as long as you understand its limitations. It’s the smart choice for gamers and general users who want a fast boot drive without breaking the bank.
