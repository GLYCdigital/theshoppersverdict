If you're still buying disposable plastic mechanical pencils from the office supply aisle, you're missing out. The Nicpro 6-piece set is the kind of upgrade that makes you wonder why you waited so long — and at a price point that undercuts single premium pencils from brands like Rotring or Pentel Graph Gear.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get six pencils: three in 0.5mm and three in 0.7mm. Each body is full aluminum alloy — not painted plastic pretending to be metal. The knurled grip section is machined, not molded, which gives you a secure hold even with sweaty hands. The clip is spring-loaded steel, and each pencil has a retractable lead sleeve, a feature usually reserved for $20+ drafting pencils.

Also included: 72 pieces of 2B lead (12 per pencil) and six eraser caps. The lead refills are genuinely useful — 2B is darker and smoother than standard HB, which is nice for writing, though softer lead means more frequent clicks.

## How They Perform in Real Use

I tested these for a week of daily notes, sketching, and drafting. The weight distribution is the standout feature. At roughly 15 grams, they feel substantial without tiring your hand during long writing sessions. The knurled grip actually works — no slipping, no death grip needed.

The 0.5mm pencils are precise enough for technical drawing and margin notes. The 0.7mm versions are better for general writing and for people who press hard — less lead breakage. The retractable sleeve is a practical touch: push the tip back in and you can toss one in a bag without worrying about bent leads or poked fingers.

One thing I noticed: the mechanism advances lead smoothly with a single click, no double-feed jams. That's more than I can say for several more expensive brands I've tested.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The Amazon reviews at 4.7 stars (235 ratings) consistently highlight the same strengths. One verified buyer wrote, "I bought these for my engineering student son and he immediately stole them from my desk. The metal body feels like a $30 pencil." Another noted, "I've dropped one three times on concrete floors and it still works perfectly — the lead sleeve retracted on impact and saved the mechanism."

The most common praise? The value proposition. Multiple reviewers compared them favorably to pencils costing three times as much, specifically calling out the knurled grip and the metal construction as features you'd expect at a higher price point.

## Where It Falls Short

The eraser caps are the weakest link. They're small, stiff, and barely functional — you'll want a separate eraser for anything beyond a quick fix. And the included 2B lead smudges more than HB if you're left-handed or tend to drag your hand across the page.

Also worth noting: the color-coded grips are the only way to distinguish lead sizes. If you mix them in a pencil case, you'll occasionally grab the wrong gauge. Minor, but annoying when you're in a flow.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Students** — especially engineering, architecture, or art students who need precision and durability without dropping $25 per pencil
- **Writers and journalers** — the comfortable grip and smooth lead delivery make long sessions easier
- **People who lose pencils** — at this price for a six-pack, you won't cry when one walks off your desk

**Skip it if** you're a professional draftsman who needs specific lead hardness options or a heavier weighted pencil for fine control. This set is excellent for general use, but it's not a Rotring replacement.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the lead sleeve actually retractable?**
A: Yes — press the tip against a surface and the sleeve slides back into the body. This protects both the lead and the mechanism when stored.

**Q: Does the 2B lead smear a lot?**
A: More than HB, yes. If you're left-handed or write fast, consider swapping in HB or H lead — the pencils accept standard refills from any brand.

**Q: Are these good for drawing?**
A: The 0.5mm versions work well for line work and technical illustration. The 2B lead gives good contrast. For heavy shading, you'd want a dedicated drafting pencil, but for mixed use, these hold up.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want premium-feeling mechanical pencils at a fraction of the premium price. The metal bodies, knurled grips, and retractable sleeves deliver genuinely better daily performance than anything in the plastic-pencil aisle.

**Skip it if** you need specific lead hardness options or expect a functional eraser built in — this set is about the writing experience, not the correction process.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Nicpro nailed the essentials: solid build, smart features, and honest value. Just bring your own eraser.
