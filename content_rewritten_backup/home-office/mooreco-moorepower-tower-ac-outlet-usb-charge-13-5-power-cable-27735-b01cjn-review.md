Let’s cut through the marketing: the MooreCo MoorePower Tower is a floor-standing power strip with a 13.5-foot cord, four AC outlets, and two USB ports. It’s not a surge protector. It’s not a smart hub. It’s a big, heavy-duty extension cord that stands up so you don’t have to crawl under your desk. That’s it. And for many people, that’s exactly what they need.

**What You Actually Get**

The unit ships fully assembled. You get the tower itself — roughly 14 inches tall with a weighted base — plus the attached 13.5-foot power cord. No mounting hardware, no wall bracket, no remote. The base is wide enough that it doesn’t tip when you yank a plug out. The four AC outlets are spaced generously, so bulky wall warts won’t block adjacent sockets. Two USB-A ports sit on the side, angled slightly downward for cable management.

**Real-World Performance**

The 13.5-foot cord is the headline feature. It’s genuinely long — enough to run from a wall outlet behind a couch to a desk across the room. The cord is thick and feels durable, not like the flimsy cords on cheap power strips. The tower itself stays put on carpet and hard floors thanks to the weighted base. I’ve seen reviewers use it in classrooms and home offices, and the stability holds up.

The AC outlets deliver as expected — no flickering, no heat issues under normal loads. The USB ports are the weak point. They’re rated at 2.1A total, which means if you plug in a phone and a tablet, both charge at reduced speed. A modern iPad will trickle-charge. If USB charging speed matters to you, budget for a separate wall charger.

**What Buyers Praise**

The cord length gets consistent love. One reviewer wrote, *“Finally a power strip that reaches across my entire office without an extension cord.”* Another noted the stability: *“The base is heavy enough that I can unplug things one-handed without holding the tower.”* The compact footprint also earns points — it tucks behind furniture without sticking out like a traditional strip.

**Where It Falls Short**

The 3.8-star average tells the story. Complaints cluster around the USB ports (slow, as noted), and the lack of surge protection surprises people. At this price point, most competitors include at least basic surge suppression. The black plastic base also shows scuffs and dust quickly — not a dealbreaker, but it looks worn fast in high-traffic areas. A few buyers reported the cord being too stiff to route neatly along baseboards, which is a fair criticism for a product designed to sit on floors.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Teachers and trainers:** The long cord and stable base work well in classrooms or meeting rooms where wall outlets are scarce.
- **Home office workers with awkward layouts:** If your desk is far from the wall, this beats daisy-chaining extension cords.
- **People with mobility issues:** No bending down to reach a low strip — the tower puts outlets at a comfortable height.

**Skip It If**

- You need surge protection for expensive electronics. This is a power distributor, not a protector.
- You rely on fast USB-C charging. The 2.1A USB-A ports are dated.
- You want something wall-mountable or discreet. This is a visible floor unit.

**FAQ**

**Is this a surge protector?**  
No. It’s a power tap with a long cord. Check the product listing carefully — MooreCo does not advertise surge protection, and the unit has none. If you’re plugging in a computer or TV, use a separate surge protector.

**Can I mount it under a desk?**  
Technically, but it’s not designed for that. The base is weighted for floor use, and there are no mounting holes. You’d have to rig something. Better to use it as a floor tower.

**Will it tip over with heavy cables?**  
The base is substantial and the center of gravity is low. With four plugs and two USB cables attached, it remains stable on flat surfaces. On thick carpet, it wobbles slightly but doesn’t fall.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a long-reach, stable floor tower for basic AC power and don’t care about fast USB charging or surge protection.

**Skip it if** you’re protecting expensive gear, need modern USB-C speeds, or expect surge suppression at this price.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The long cord and sturdy base are genuine wins, but the slow USB ports and missing surge protection keep it from being a standout. A solid, functional tool with clear limitations.
