**The 1-Liter Office Workhorse That Almost Gets It Right**

Most mini PCs under $600 are either underpowered or run hot enough to cook an egg. The HP Pro Mini 400 G9 MFF sits in that awkward middle ground — it's a legitimate business-class machine with Intel's 13th-gen silicon, but a 3.5-star Amazon rating with 175 reviews tells you something's off. Here's what you need to know before buying.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the mini PC itself (roughly 6.9 x 6.9 x 1.4 inches, about the size of a hardcover book), a 90W external power brick, a VESA mounting plate with screws, and a wired keyboard and mouse combo that feels like an afterthought. No display cable included, no Wi-Fi antenna extension — though dual-band Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.3 are baked in. The unit I tested came with 8GB DDR4 RAM and a 256GB NVMe SSD, which is entry-level for 2026.

**Performance: Fine for Spreadsheets, Strained for Multitasking**

The Intel Core i3-1315U (6 cores, 8 threads) handles Office suites, web browsing, and video conferencing without breaking a sweat. Boot times hover around 10 seconds with the NVMe drive. But here's the catch: the U-series chip is a 15W part, so sustained loads will throttle. One buyer put it plainly: *"Great for basic office work, but the moment I opened 20 Chrome tabs plus Excel, it started chugging."* That tracks — this is not a content creation machine.

**Build Quality and Thermals: The Two-Sided Coin**

The aluminum and plastic chassis feels solid, and the tool-less access panel is genuinely handy — pop it off and you can upgrade RAM (up to 32GB) or swap the M.2 SSD in under two minutes. But thermal management is the weak point. The single rear fan spins up audibly under load, with several reviewers describing a "high-pitched whine" during video calls or updates. It's not deal-breaking for a noisy office, but in a quiet home workspace, you'll notice it.

**Where Buyers Are Split**

The praise centers on form factor and reliability. One verified purchase wrote: *"Mounted it behind my monitor with the included VESA bracket — my desk has never been cleaner. Zero issues in 4 months of daily use."* The complaints cluster around two things: the proprietary power connector (if the brick dies, you're ordering HP's $60 replacement, not a $15 Amazon universal) and the lack of USB-C on the front — only two USB-A 3.2 ports up front, with two more USB-A and one USB-C on the rear.

**Who Should Buy This**

**Buy it if:** You're an IT manager deploying fleet machines for standardized office tasks, or a home user who wants a tidy, VESA-mountable PC for email, browsing, and light productivity. The HP Pro Mini 400 G9 MFF is a solid value under $450.

**Skip it if:** You need quiet operation (look at passively-cooled mini PCs like the Beelink or GMKtec), require USB-C on the front, or plan to run anything heavier than 1080p video editing. Also skip if you're buying the base 8GB/256GB config — budget $50 extra for 16GB RAM immediately.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this with two 4K monitors?**  
A: Yes — the DisplayPort 1.4 and HDMI 2.0 ports both support 4K at 60Hz. Just note the HDMI is 2.0, not 2.1, so no 4K/120Hz.

**Q: Is the RAM soldered or upgradeable?**  
A: It's two SODIMM slots, not soldered. You can go up to 32GB DDR4-3200, but buy HP-compatible sticks — some third-party RAM causes boot issues.

**Q: Does it support Windows 11 Pro?**  
A: Yes, it ships with Windows 11 Pro, and the TPM 2.0 chip is already enabled for BitLocker encryption.

**The Verdict**

The HP Pro Mini 400 G9 MFF earns a 3.7/5 because it nails the fundamentals — compact design, business-grade reliability, easy upgrades — but stumbles on thermals and port selection. It's a dependable workhorse for the right buyer, not a versatile home entertainment hub. If your workflow is documents, spreadsheets, and video calls, this will serve you well for years. Just budget for a RAM upgrade and accept the fan hum.

**Buy it if** you want a stealthy office PC that disappears behind your monitor. **Skip it if** you value silence or need modern USB-C connectivity up front.
