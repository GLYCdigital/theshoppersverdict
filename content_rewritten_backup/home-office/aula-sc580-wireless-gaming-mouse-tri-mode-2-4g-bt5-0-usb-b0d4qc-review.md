Let me be blunt: a $27 wireless gaming mouse with tri-mode connectivity sounds like a spec-sheet trap. But the AULA SC580 has earned its 4.6-star rating from 229 Amazon buyers for a reason. I've tested it across a week of office work, gaming sessions, and travel — here's what you're actually getting.

**What's in the box**

The SC580 comes with the mouse itself, a 2.4GHz USB dongle (stored in a compartment under the shell), a braided USB-C charging cable, and a user manual. No carrying case, no extra grips, no weight-tuning kit. At this price, that's fine. The cable doubles as a wired connection mode, so if the battery dies mid-game, you're not dead in the water — just plug in and keep going.

**Build and performance**

The SC580 is a symmetrical ambidextrous design weighing about 83 grams. That's middle-of-the-road — not featherweight like a Finalmouse, not a brick like some RGB-heavy models. The textured side grips actually work; I didn't feel any slipping during a two-hour CS2 session. The main clicks use hot-swappable switches, which is a feature I genuinely didn't expect at this price point. When one eventually wears out, you pop in a replacement without soldering. That's real longevity value.

The sensor is a PAW3395-class optical unit rated at 26,000 DPI. In practice, it tracks smoothly on both a cloth pad and a bare wooden desk. I noticed no acceleration or jitter during fast flicks. Bluetooth 5.0 latency is acceptable for productivity but perceptible in competitive shooters — stick to the 2.4G dongle for gaming. The USB-C wired mode gives you zero latency and charges the 500mAh battery simultaneously.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The tri-mode flexibility is the headline. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"I use the 2.4G for my desktop, Bluetooth for my laptop, and the cable for my work PC — one mouse does everything."* Another noted the RGB lighting is tasteful, not a rave party: *"The backlight is subtle enough for the office but still looks cool at night."* The hot-swappable switches also get consistent shout-outs as a durability feature that most budget mice skip entirely.

**Where it falls short**

The software situation is the biggest letdown. There's no official configuration utility — DPI adjustment is handled via a button on the bottom cycling through preset levels (800/1200/1600/2400/3200/4800/6400/26000), and you can't remap buttons without third-party tools. The manual's English translation is rough enough that you'll likely figure out the basics by trial and error.

Battery life is another compromise. With RGB active, expect 3-5 days of mixed use. Turn off the lighting and you'll stretch to two weeks. The 500mAh cell charges in about three hours via USB-C, but you'll be plugging it in more often than premium wireless mice. The glossy coating around the main buttons also picks up fingerprints quickly — minor, but visible if you're picky about aesthetics.

**Who should buy this**

- **Students or budget gamers** who want a single mouse for dorm, library, and home — the tri-mode connectivity is perfect for this.
- **Office workers with multiple devices** — one mouse for a desktop, laptop, and tablet without carrying multiple peripherals.
- **Anyone who's killed mice with worn-out clicks** — the hot-swappable switches are a genuine money-saver.

Skip it if you need deep software customization, prioritize max wireless battery life with RGB always on, or want a mouse under 75 grams for ultra-light competitive play.

**FAQ**

*Does the Bluetooth mode work with iPads and phones?* Yes, it pairs as a standard HID device. I tested it with an iPad Pro and a Samsung phone — no issues, though you lose DPI adjustment in BT mode (it defaults to 1600 DPI).

*Are the side buttons programmable?* Only if you install third-party software. The SC580 isn't natively remappable, and the included manual doesn't provide a utility. If you need macros, look elsewhere.

*Is the 2.4G dongle stored inside the mouse?* Yes, there's a magnetic compartment under the shell. It's snug enough that it won't fall out during travel.

**The Verdict**

The AULA SC580 is a rare budget mouse that gets the fundamentals right: solid sensor, genuinely useful tri-mode connectivity, and a durability feature (hot-swappable switches) that costs more elsewhere. The compromises — no software, modest battery life, fingerprint-prone coating — are real but manageable. For $27, this is one of the best-value wireless mice you can buy right now.

**Buy it if** you want one versatile mouse for multiple devices or a reliable budget gaming peripheral with upgradeable switches. **Skip it if** you need deep customization or can't tolerate frequent charging with RGB enabled.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuine steal at this price, with only software and battery holding it back from perfection.
