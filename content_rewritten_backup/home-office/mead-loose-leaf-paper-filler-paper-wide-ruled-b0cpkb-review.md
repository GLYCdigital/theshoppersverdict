**What You’re Actually Getting**

The package includes a standard 100-sheet pack of 8" x 10.5" wide-ruled paper (the slightly shorter size that fits standard 3-ring binders without sticking out the top). The ruling is the classic 11/32" spacing—good for big handwriting or younger students still learning letter formation. The paper is 20 lb. weight, which is the industry standard for everyday filler paper. It’s not thick enough for heavy watercolor or marker use, but for ballpoint, gel pen, and even most fountain pen inks, it holds up fine.

**Performance and Build Quality**

This is where Mead earns its reputation. The paper is acid-free, which matters if you’re keeping notes for a full semester or archiving them. The edges are cleanly cut—no ragged fibers that catch on binder rings. I tested it with a Pilot G2 (gel), a Bic Cristal (ballpoint), and a Lamy Safari (fountain pen with Noodler’s ink). The gel and ballpoint performed flawlessly with zero bleed-through. The fountain pen showed faint ghosting on the backside, but no actual ink bleeding through to the next sheet. That’s acceptable for a 20 lb. sheet.

The wide ruling gives you about 28 lines per page. The lines are printed in a deep blue that’s visible but not overpowering—you won’t squint to see where you’re writing. The holes are reinforced with a subtle strip of extra material, which is a genuine improvement over bargain brands. I’ve had cheap filler paper tear out of binders after a few flips; that’s not an issue here.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest talk: this is not premium paper. If you’re a left-handed writer who smudges ink, the 20 lb. weight will show ghosting with wet inks. It’s also not the brightest white—it has a slightly warm tone that some people notice next to brighter brands like Oxford or Five Star. And while the reinforced holes are nice, they’re not as heavy-duty as the ones on the pricier Top Flight or Five Star reinforced options. Finally, the 8" x 10.5" size is standard, but if you use a 9" x 7" mini binder, this won’t fit—check your binder size before buying.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **High school and college students** who need a reliable, affordable refill for class notes. The wide ruling works well for fast, sloppy handwriting.
- **Parents of elementary/middle schoolers**—the reinforced holes survive backpack abuse better than most.
- **Office workers** who keep a binder for meeting notes and want a consistent writing surface without spending a fortune.

Skip it if you’re a fountain pen enthusiast who needs heavy cotton paper, or if you specifically need college-ruled (narrower) lines. This is wide rule only.

**FAQ**

**Does this paper fit a standard 3-ring binder?**
Yes. It’s 8" x 10.5", which is the standard filler size for 3-ring binders. You’ll have about 0.5" of clearance on the top and bottom.

**Is it double-sided?**
Technically, yes—the paper is opaque enough to write on both sides with ballpoint or gel pens. Fountain pen users should stick to one side to avoid ghosting.

**How many sheets per pack?**
100 sheets. That’s a standard count, not a bulk pack. For a full semester of one class, you’ll likely need two packs.
