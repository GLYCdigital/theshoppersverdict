The HP Envy Inspire 7955e occupies a crowded spot: the sub-$200 all-in-one inkjet market where every model promises “great value” and delivers compromise. After digging through 200 Amazon ratings and testing the hardware specs against real-world demands, here’s the honest picture.

## What you actually get

The box includes the printer, two starter ink cartridges (black and tri-color — HP says they’re “setup” cartridges, which means lower yield than retail replacements), a power cord, and a quick-start guide. No USB cable included — you’ll go wireless or buy one separately. The unit measures 17.6 x 13.8 x 8.7 inches and weighs 14.8 pounds, so it’s not a compact desktop footprint. Plan for a dedicated shelf or cart.

The headline feature is the 35-sheet auto document feeder (ADF), which you rarely see at this price. It makes multi-page scanning and copying genuinely hands-free. Also onboard: automatic duplex printing (both sides without manual flipping), a flatbed scanner with 1200 dpi optical resolution, and a 2.7-inch touchscreen color display.

## Performance: where it shines and stumbles

Print speed is rated at 15 pages per minute for black and 10 for color. In real terms, that translates to about 12 ppm for text documents and 7 ppm for mixed graphics. Fine for a home office, slow if you’re printing 50-page reports daily. First page out takes 12-15 seconds — acceptable.

Text quality is sharp and crisp, with clean edges at 600 dpi. Color documents look vibrant, though photos lean slightly warm — skin tones come out a touch orange. If you’re printing product shots or family photos, expect to adjust color settings in the driver.

Scan quality is surprisingly strong. The ADF captures legible multi-page documents without jams — one Amazon reviewer noted, *“I’ve scanned 30-page contracts without a single misfeed; my old Canon jammed every third sheet.”* The flatbed handles photos and thick documents well at 1200 dpi.

Wireless setup is where the 7955e earns mixed reviews. The HP Smart app guides you through Wi-Fi connection, but multiple buyers report the printer dropping off the network after firmware updates. One reviewer wrote: *“Works great for a week, then my phone can’t find it. I have to restart the printer every time.”* If you’re on a mesh network, you may need to set a static IP.

## What buyers consistently praise

- **The ADF**: Hands-free multi-page scanning is the standout feature at this price.
- **Automatic duplexing**: Genuinely two-sided printing without manual flipping — saves paper and time.
- **HP Smart app reliability**: Mobile printing and scan-to-cloud work smoothly once the initial setup sticks.
- **Print quality for documents**: Text-heavy pages look sharp and professional.

## Where it falls short

**Ink costs are the elephant in the room.** The 7955e pushes HP’s Instant Ink subscription hard — the setup process asks you to enroll, and the printer nags periodically. Standard cartridges are pricey: a full-yield black runs about $32 and yields roughly 600 pages, while the tri-color is $38 for 450 pages. That’s about 5.3 cents per black page and 8.4 cents for color — not terrible, but not cheap. Instant Ink plans start at $6.99/month for 100 pages, which makes sense if you print consistently, but it’s a commitment.

**Warranty gotcha**: HP’s warranty explicitly excludes damage from third-party ink. If you refill or use generics, you risk voiding coverage. That’s a deliberate lock-in strategy — know it before you buy.

**Connectivity gaps**: No Ethernet port, so you’re Wi-Fi dependent. The front USB-A port is recessed and awkward for flash drives — you’ll need a slim drive to fit. No NFC tap-to-print either.

**Photo speed**: A 4x6 borderless print takes about 60 seconds. Fine for occasional snapshots, frustrating for batches.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if** you’re a home user or small office that prints mostly documents, scans multi-page contracts or receipts regularly, and wants automatic two-sided printing without paying for a business-class machine. The ADF alone justifies the price for anyone who scans more than a few pages a week.

**Skip it if** you print high volumes (100+ pages daily), need networking via Ethernet, or refuse to buy HP ink. Also avoid if you want a compact footprint — this is a full-width machine.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the HP Envy Inspire 7955e work with third-party ink cartridges?**
A: Physically yes, but HP’s warranty explicitly excludes damage from non-HP ink. You’ll also get persistent warning messages. If you’re considering generics, factor in the warranty risk.

**Q: Is Instant Ink mandatory?**
A: No, but the printer pushes it during setup and shows reminders. You can decline and use standard cartridges. If you print more than 50 pages monthly, Instant Ink’s 300-page plan at $9.99/month can actually save money.

**Q: Can it print on cardstock or envelopes?**
A: Yes, the rear paper feed handles cardstock up to 220 gsm and envelopes. The main tray is for plain paper only.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5 stars**

The HP Envy Inspire 7955e is a capable home-office all-in-one that earns its place through the auto document feeder and automatic duplexing — features that genuinely improve workflow. But the ink subscription pressure, Wi-Fi stability quirks, and warranty lock-in keep it from greatness.

**Buy it if** you need hands-free scanning and duplex printing on a budget.

**Skip it if** you print heavily, hate subscription pushes, or want wired network reliability. For everyone else, this is a solid, honest workhorse that does most things right — just budget for the ink.
