Let's be straight about something: RITZ Toasted Chips aren't potato chips, and they're not crackers either. They're this oddly satisfying hybrid — a crispy, baked chip with that familiar RITZ buttery undertone, but with the heft and snap of something you'd actually dip. The variety pack throws three flavors at you: Cheddar, Sour Cream & Onion, and Original. That's 18 single-serve bags total (six of each), which works out to a sensible rotation if you're stocking a pantry or packing lunches.

**What you get in the box**

The package is straightforward — 18 individually wrapped 1.25 oz bags. Each bag is about a solid handful, roughly 10-12 chips depending on how the factory gods were feeling that day. The Original flavor is the baseline: that classic RITZ buttery, slightly salty taste, but with a toasted crunch that feels closer to a pita chip than a cracker. The Cheddar version coats each chip with a sharp, tangy orange dust that clings generously. The Sour Cream & Onion is the lightest of the trio — more sour cream than onion, with the onion note playing backup rather than lead.

**How they actually perform**

The texture is the headline here. These chips have a serious crunch — a dense, satisfying snap that holds up to thick dips like French onion or spinach-artichoke without bending into a sad, soggy mess. That's a real advantage over standard potato chips, which tend to give up the ghost halfway through a scoop. The chips are also noticeably thicker than your average snack crisp, which gives them a hearty mouthfeel that makes a single bag feel more substantial than it is.

I tested these over a week — some straight out of the bag, some with hummus, some crushed over a salad for texture. The Original works best as a neutral vehicle for dips. The Cheddar stands alone fine but really shines when you're not competing with other strong flavors. The Sour Cream & Onion is the one I'd pair with something lighter — it's subtle enough that a bold dip will completely bury it.

**What buyers keep saying**

The 4.6-star average across 229 ratings tracks with what I'm seeing. One recurring theme in the feedback is the crunch — multiple reviewers call out that these don't go stale quickly and keep their snap even a few days after opening. Another common thread is portion control. As one shopper put it, "The single-serve bags are perfect for packing in lunches without the whole bag disappearing in one sitting." That's a fair point — the 1.25 oz size is enough to kill a craving without encouraging a full bag binge.

**Where it stumbles**

The Sour Cream & Onion is the weakest link. If you're a fan of strong, vinegar-forward onion flavor — think Lay's Sour Cream & Onion — you'll find this version disappointingly tame. It's more creamy than tangy, and the onion is barely there. Also, the Cheddar version leaves that unmistakable orange powder on your fingers. It's a minor annoyance, but if you're snacking at a desk, you'll be wiping your hands before touching anything important. And there's no resealable closure on the bags — once open, you're committed to finishing them, which is fine for a single serving but something to know going in.

**Who should buy this**

If you pack lunches for kids or yourself, this variety pack is a solid rotation option — three flavors keep it from getting boring. If you're hosting a game day or movie night, the individual bags mean guests grab their own portions instead of double-dipping into a communal bowl. If you're someone who likes a hearty, dippable chip that doesn't crumble under pressure, these deliver. Skip it if you want bold, aggressive flavor profiles — the seasoning is present but polite across the board.

**FAQ**

**Are these actually toasted or fried?**
They're baked/toasted, not fried. That's why they have that dry, crisp texture rather than an oily mouthfeel. They're not health food, but they're lighter than traditional fried chips.

**How many bags come in the variety pack?**
18 total — six bags each of Cheddar, Sour Cream & Onion, and Original. Each bag is 1.25 oz.

**Do these work well with dip?**
Yes — that's arguably their best use. The thickness and crunch hold up to heavy dips without breaking, which is a clear step up from standard potato chips.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, crunchy snack that works for lunches, parties, or solo dipping sessions, and you appreciate having flavor variety in one order.

**Skip it if** you demand intense, punchy seasoning or prefer the classic thin-crisp potato chip texture.

These aren't revolutionary, but they're dependable, well-made snacks that earn their 4.6 stars. The texture alone puts them a step above most shelf-stable options, and the variety keeps things interesting. Just manage your expectations on the Sour Cream & Onion.
