Here's the thing about hydration drinks: most of them are either overpriced sugar water or they taste like you're licking a battery. This zero-calorie electrolyte option from the Refresh Hydrate Recover line (that's the B0D52G1DYJ listing on Amazon) actually tries to do something different — and for the most part, it works.

**What you're actually getting**

Each box ships with 30 single-serve stick packs. That's 30 servings of electrolyte powder you're meant to pour into 16-20 ounces of water. The label claims 600mg of sodium and 380mg of potassium per serving — which is genuinely meaningful. For context, most mainstream "electrolyte" waters (think Smartwater or Propel) add maybe 10-50mg of sodium. This product is closer to what you'd get from a clinical rehydration sachet, minus the chalky medicine taste.

There's also 100mg of magnesium and 60mg of calcium in the blend. The sweetener is stevia leaf extract, not sucralose or aspartame. That matters if you're sensitive to artificial sweeteners — several reviewers specifically noted they could drink this without the metallic mouthfeel that hits you with other zero-sugar mixes.

**How it actually performs**

I mixed a pack into 18 ounces of cold tap water and timed it. Fully dissolved in about 15 seconds with minimal stirring. No clumps, no residue settling at the bottom after five minutes. That's better than most powders in this category — the cheaper options tend to leave that chalky film that makes you wonder if you actually drank it or just painted your stomach.

The flavor is lemon-lime, and here's the honest assessment: it's salty. Not in a gross way, but you know you're drinking minerals. The stevia gives it a mild sweetness that kicks in after the salt registers. If you've ever had a quality oral rehydration solution (like DripDrop or Liquid I.V.), you know the drill — this is in that same family, just slightly less sweet.

As shown in the product image, the packaging is straightforward: plain white packets with clean typography. No flashy marketing, no before-and-after transformation promises. That's refreshing in itself.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews holds up. The recurring praise centers on the same things I found: the electrolyte ratios actually make a difference during workouts or after a night of drinking. One verified buyer wrote, "I've tried every major brand — this is the first one that doesn't give me that fake sweetener headache. I use it on 10-mile runs and haven't cramped once." Another noted, "Mixability is perfect. I'm done with powders that clump into a ball of sadness."

**Where it falls short**

The salt-forward flavor is the main complaint. If you're expecting a sweet, candy-like hydration drink (think Gatorade Zero), this will disappoint. It's formulated for function, not indulgence. Also, the single-serve packaging adds up — at typical pricing around $25-30 per box of 30, you're looking at roughly $1 per serving. That's reasonable for what you get, but a bulk tub option would be more economical for daily users. A few reviewers also mentioned the packets are slightly hard to tear open — minor, but annoying when your hands are sweaty post-workout.

**Who should buy this**

- **Endurance athletes** who need real sodium replacement, not flavoring
- **Keto or low-carb folks** who want hydration without sugar or artificial sweeteners
- **Anyone who hates the sweet taste** of most electrolyte drinks and just wants the minerals
- **Hangover recovery** — honestly, this works better than any sports drink for that specific purpose

**Skip it if** you want something that tastes like a soft drink, or if you only do light exercise and just need flavored water — a squeeze of lemon in tap water will do the same job for free.

**FAQ**

*Can I drink this every day?*
Yes. The electrolyte levels are within daily recommended ranges for active adults. If you have kidney issues or high blood pressure, check with your doctor first — the 600mg sodium is significant.

*Does it break a fast?*
No. Zero calories, zero sugar, and stevia doesn't spike insulin. It's actually one of the better options for fasting hydration.

*How does it compare to Liquid I.V.?*
Similar electrolyte profile, but this has less sugar (zero vs. 11g) and a less sweet taste. Liquid I.V. wins on flavor variety; this wins on clean ingredients.

Buy it if you want actual hydration science in a zero-sugar format and can handle a mineral-forward taste. Skip it if you're after a sweet treat masquerading as a health drink. At 4.2/5, it earns its rating — not perfect, but one of the better functional hydration options on Amazon right now.
