Here's the thing about coffee gear maintenance: most people either never clean their brewer, or they blow $15 on a tiny bottle of descaling solution that lasts three uses. This 11 lb bag of sodium carbonate washing soda sits in a weird middle ground — it's marketed for laundry, but it's secretly one of the best budget coffee equipment cleaners available.

**What you're actually getting**

A heavy-duty plastic bag holding 11 pounds of sodium carbonate (also called washing soda or soda ash). The granules are fine, similar to coarse salt, and dissolve readily in hot water. There's no scoop included, no fancy packaging — just a utilitarian bag of white powder. You'll want to transfer it to an airtight container immediately because the bag doesn't reseal well, and moisture will turn it into a brick.

**How it performs on coffee gear**

Here's the practical use: mix 1 tablespoon per liter of hot water, run it through your drip machine, French press, or pour-over carafe, then follow with two plain water cycles. The alkaline nature of sodium carbonate breaks down coffee oils (that sticky brown residue that builds up in reservoirs) and neutralizes odors effectively. For espresso machines, it works well for backflushing with a blind basket — though you'll need to flush thoroughly afterward.

One thing to know: this is not a limescale destroyer. If you have hard water and heavy mineral buildup, citric acid is the better choice. Sodium carbonate is for *organic* gunk — coffee oils, residue, stains on ceramic and glass. For routine maintenance, it's excellent. For descaling a neglected machine, pair it with a dedicated descaler.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest practical issue is rinsing. Sodium carbonate residue, even in small amounts, leaves a distinct soapy flavor. If you don't run enough clean water through your machine after cleaning, your next cup will taste flat and slightly alkaline. Budget your time accordingly — a proper rinse cycle takes 5-10 minutes.

Also, the lack of a resealable bag is genuinely annoying. You'll need your own storage solution, and if you live in a humid climate, the powder will clump within weeks of opening. And to be clear: this isn't a specialized coffee product. If you want something purpose-built with clear dosing instructions for your specific machine, you'll find more convenience in a liquid descaler — you'll just pay a lot more per use.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home espresso enthusiasts** who backflush weekly and want a cheap, effective cleaning agent
- **Coffee shop owners** or heavy home brewers with multiple machines to maintain
- **Anyone who already uses washing soda for laundry** and wants a multi-purpose cleaning powder

Skip it if you only clean your coffee maker twice a year — you'll be better off with a small bottle of commercial descaler you'll actually finish.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Is sodium carbonate safe for coffee makers?**
Yes, when rinsed properly. It's food-safe and leaves no toxic residue after a thorough rinse. Just don't use it on aluminum components — it can cause discoloration.

**Q: How much should I use per cleaning cycle?**
Start with 1-2 tablespoons per liter of hot water. For heavily soiled machines, you can double it, but always follow with two full water-only cycles.

**Q: Can I use this for descaling my espresso machine?**
It removes coffee residue and oils effectively, but for hard water scale, use citric acid instead. Sodium carbonate alone won't break down mineral deposits.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you clean your coffee gear regularly and want a low-cost, high-volume solution that also handles laundry and household cleaning. **Skip it if** you need a dedicated descaler for hard water buildup or want a resealable, purpose-built product with machine-specific instructions.
