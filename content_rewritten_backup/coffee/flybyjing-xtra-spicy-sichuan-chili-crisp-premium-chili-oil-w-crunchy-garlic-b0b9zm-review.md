If you've ever stared down a jar of Lao Gan Ma and thought "I wish this hurt more," FLYBYJING's Xtra Spicy Sichuan Chili Crisp is your answer. This isn't a condiment — it's a controlled burn with a crunchy, fermented backbone that turns bland rice into an event.

**What's actually in the jar**

You're getting a 5.3-ounce glass jar of chili oil packed with visible ingredients: dried Sichuan chilies, fermented broad bean paste, garlic, shallots, and Sichuan peppercorn oil. The oil-to-solids ratio leans heavily toward solids — roughly 60/40 — which is exactly what you want. The texture as shown in the product image is dense, not soupy. No MSG, no preservatives, no sesame (a relief for those with allergies, though it does change the flavor profile from traditional chili crisp).

**The heat is real — and the numbing is the point**

Most hot sauces just burn. This one delivers the full málà experience: the chilies bring the burn, but the Sichuan peppercorn oil adds that distinctive tongue-numbing tingle that builds over 30 seconds and lingers pleasantly. It's not a gimmick — the numbing actually enhances the flavor of whatever you're eating, especially fatty foods like dumplings or fried eggs.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I've gone through three jars. It's the only chili crisp that actually makes my lips go numb. Everything else just tastes spicy."* Another noted, *"The crunch is insane — I pick out the crispy garlic bits with chopsticks."*

**Where it falls short**

The price stings more than the chilies. At roughly $1.80 per ounce, you're paying a serious premium over the $3 jar of Lao Gan Ma sitting on the same shelf. FLYBYJING's sourcing and clean-label production justify some of that cost, but budget shoppers will feel it.

The "Xtra Spicy" label isn't marketing fluff. If you're someone who orders "medium" at Thai restaurants, this will be uncomfortable. Several reviewers mentioned it's too hot for eggs or mild applications — you'll want the regular version for breakfast, not this one.

Also, the numbing effect is polarizing. Some buyers genuinely dislike the tingle, describing it as "weird" or "chemical." It's authentic Sichuan flavor, but if you've never experienced málà before, it's a shock.

**Who should buy this**

- **Dumpling enthusiasts** who want restaurant-quality dipping sauce without leaving home
- **Heat seekers** who've graduated from Sriracha and find standard chili crisp too tame
- **Clean-label shoppers** who won't compromise on ingredients, even for a condiment
- **Sichuan cuisine fans** who miss the numbing sensation of authentic málà dishes

Skip it if you're on a tight grocery budget, prefer straightforward heat over numbing complexity, or you're buying for someone who thinks black pepper is spicy.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Is this actually spicier than the regular FLYBYJING?*
Yes, noticeably. The regular version has a warm, approachable heat. The Xtra Spicy doubles the chili content and adds more Sichuan peppercorn oil. If you're new to the brand, start with the original.

*How long does it last after opening?*
The oil acts as a preservative. Stored in the fridge, it'll stay good for 3-4 months. At room temperature, expect 6-8 weeks. The crunchy bits soften faster in the fridge, so if texture is your priority, keep it in the pantry and use it quickly.

*Is this gluten-free?*
The fermented broad bean paste is traditionally made with wheat. FLYBYJING doesn't label this product gluten-free, and cross-contamination is possible. If you have celiac disease, this isn't safe.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a premium, authentic Sichuan chili crisp that delivers genuine málà heat and a satisfying crunch, and you're willing to pay for quality. **Skip it if** you're budget-conscious, heat-averse, or prefer your chili oil simple and one-dimensional.

This is the chili crisp for people who take their spice seriously. It's not cheap, and it's not for the faint of heart — but for the right buyer, it's worth every penny. **4.6/5 stars.**
