## What's Actually in the Jar

Toschi is a century-old Modena-based company (founded 1895) known for premium cocktail garnishes and dessert syrups. These aren't your grocery store cocktail cherries. You're getting whole amarena cherries — a specific Italian variety that's smaller and more tart than Bing or Rainier — packed in a dense, dark syrup made from cherry juice and sugar. The jar typically weighs around 400 grams (roughly 14 ounces) with a significant portion of that weight being syrup.

The cherries themselves are deep burgundy, almost black, with a firm texture that holds up well. They're not candied or bleached like traditional maraschinos — the flavor is genuinely fruity with a pleasant sour note that cuts through sweetness.

## The Real-World Performance

Here's where these shine: cocktails. Drop one into an Old Fashioned or a Manhattan, and you get a burst of tart cherry flavor that actually complements the whiskey instead of coating your tongue in corn syrup. Bartenders love these because they don't bleed artificial color into clear spirits. That dark syrup also works wonders — drizzle it over vanilla gelato, pancakes, or into sparkling water for a quick Italian soda.

The texture is the other standout. These cherries stay firm — no mushy collapse after sitting in syrup for months. That's a direct result of the traditional preservation method Toschi uses, which relies on the sugar syrup itself rather than chemical stabilizers.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Are these cherries sweet or sour?**
A: Predominantly sour with a sweet syrup coating. The fruit itself has a noticeable tart bite — that's the authentic amarena profile. If that sounds off-putting, start with a smaller jar.

**Q: Can I reuse the syrup after the cherries are gone?**
A: Absolutely. The syrup is excellent in cocktails (try it in a bourbon smash), over ice cream, or mixed into sparkling water. It keeps well refrigerated for several weeks.

**Q: How do these compare to Luxardo maraschino cherries?**
A: Both are premium Italian products, but they're different. Luxardo cherries are sweeter and larger with a thicker, candy-like syrup. Toschi amarena cherries are smaller, tarter, and have a thinner, more fruit-forward syrup. For Old Fashioneds, many prefer Luxardo; for lighter cocktails or dessert applications, Toschi often wins.
