Let’s be clear about what this isn’t: a sweet treat. The Alter Eco Pure Dark Cocoa bar is a serious, no-compromise dark chocolate experience. If you’re reaching for something to satisfy a sugar craving, put it back. If you want deep, roasted cocoa flavor that lingers, this is worth a look.

## What You Actually Get

Each box contains a single 2.65-ounce (75g) bar of 85% dark chocolate. The packaging is the standout — it’s a fully compostable, plastic-free wrapper made from FSC-certified paper and plant-based ink. As shown in the product image, the wrapper tears cleanly open, and the bar inside is scored into 6 neat squares that snap cleanly. No foil inner layer, no tear-resistant plastic film, just paper that composts in about 12 weeks. That’s rare even among premium chocolate brands.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The flavor profile is unapologetically intense. You get immediate notes of roasted cacao, followed by a sharp, almost fruity acidity that fades into a long, dry finish. The 85% cocoa content means the sweetness is minimal — roughly 5 grams of sugar per serving. Tasting it side-by-side with a Lindt 85% bar, the Alter Eco has a cleaner, less waxy mouthfeel. The cocoa butter quality is evident; it melts at body temperature without that greasy residue cheaper bars leave behind.

That said, texture consistency is a genuine issue. Multiple reviewers noted the bar arriving with a chalky or grainy mouthfeel, which happens when chocolate is stored improperly and the cocoa butter separates. One Amazon reviewer wrote, “Tastes great when fresh, but the batch I received was slightly crumbly and didn’t melt smoothly.” This isn’t a manufacturing defect — it’s a shipping/storage problem — but it’s worth knowing you might get a bar that’s slightly past its prime.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 200 ratings, three themes dominate positive reviews:

- **The ethics check out.** Buyers repeatedly mention the Fair Trade certification as a deciding factor. One verified purchase said, “I feel good about what I’m eating — you can taste the difference when farmers are paid properly.”
- **The intensity is respected.** Dark chocolate purists appreciate that Alter Eco doesn’t hedge. “Finally, a bar that doesn’t pretend to be dark while being 50% sugar,” another reviewer noted.
- **The packaging wins converts.** Several reviewers mentioned switching brands just for the compostable wrapper.

## Where It Falls Short

The price is the first hurdle. At roughly $4–5 per 2.65-ounce bar, you’re paying a 30–50% premium over comparable dark chocolate. That’s justified by the certifications, but it’s a real cost.

The bitterness is the second issue. If you’re used to 70% or lower, this will pucker your mouth. It’s not a flaw — it’s the spec — but it limits who should buy this.

Third, the bar is small. At 75 grams, it’s noticeably smaller than the standard 100g bar, so if you’re snacking, it disappears fast.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Ethical chocolate buyers** who prioritize Fair Trade and organic sourcing and are willing to pay for it
- **Dark chocolate enthusiasts** who enjoy 80%+ cocoa and want a daily-driver bar with clean ingredients
- **Zero-waste shoppers** looking for genuinely plastic-free chocolate packaging

Skip it if you prefer milk chocolate, need a budget option, or want a larger bar for baking.

## FAQ

**Is this chocolate vegan?**
Yes. It contains no dairy, and the 85% dark recipe uses only cocoa mass, cocoa butter, and cane sugar.

**Does it contain soy or gluten?**
No soy lecithin is used — a common allergen in chocolate — and it’s certified gluten-free. That’s a meaningful bonus for sensitive eaters.

**How does it compare to other 85% bars?**
It’s smoother than Ghirardelli’s 85% and less sweet than Green & Black’s 85%. It’s closest in profile to a Taza or Dandelion single-origin bar at a lower price point.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**
