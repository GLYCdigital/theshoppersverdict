## What's Actually in the Bag

You're getting a 5.5-pound bag of callets—those round, coin-shaped discs that look like oversized chocolate buttons. The callet shape isn't cosmetic; it's engineered for faster, more even melting than traditional chips or bars. Each piece is roughly the size of a nickel and about a quarter-inch thick.

The 33.6% cocoa content is the key spec here. That's lower than typical dark chocolate (which runs 55-70%) but higher than most American milk chocolate (which often sits around 30% or below). This matters because lower cocoa content means more milk fat and sugar, which translates to easier melting and a creamier final texture.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about your expectations. First, these are not eating chocolate. At 33.6% cocoa, they're sweeter than you'd want for a snack, and the texture is optimized for melting, not for a satisfying bite. If you're looking for a bar to eat out of hand, buy something else.

Second, the flavor profile is sweet and milky, not intensely chocolatey. If you're making brownies or chocolate cakes that call for dark chocolate, this will make them taste milder and sweeter than you might expect. One reviewer flagged: "These are great, but I thought they'd be richer. They're definitely milk chocolate, not a dark chocolate substitute."

Third—and this is niche but real—the bag is resealable but not airtight. If you don't use it within a few months, you'll want to transfer to an airtight container to prevent flavor absorption from your pantry.

## FAQ

**Can I use these in cookies instead of regular chocolate chips?**
Yes, but expect a different texture. These melt more completely than chips, giving you chocolate pockets rather than distinct chunks. If you want defined chips in your cookies, chop them and freeze before adding to the dough.

**Do these need to be tempered?**
For melting and dipping, no—just melt gently. For molded chocolates or anything requiring a glossy, snap-able finish, yes, tempering is necessary. The callets respond well to tempering, including microwave tempering methods.

**How long does a 5.5-pound bag last?**
For a weekly baker, expect 3-4 months. Store in a cool, dark place (60-70°F) and keep the bag sealed. Chocolate absorbs odors, so keep it away from spices and coffee.
