If you've ever stood in the condiment aisle wondering whether the $6 French mustard is genuinely better than the $2.49 store brand, here's your answer: yes, it is — but only if you actually taste the difference. MAILLE Natural Dijon isn't just a label upgrade. It's a completely different product from the yellow stuff in the squeeze bottle.

## What You're Actually Getting

The 7.5-ounce glass jar arrives with MAILLE's signature burgundy-and-gold label — the same design that's been on French tables since 1747. Inside, you'll find a pale golden paste with a surprisingly delicate aroma. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: mustard seeds, water, vinegar, salt. No sulfites, no artificial coloring, no gums or thickeners. That's it.

The texture is what sets this apart from mass-market Dijon. It's smooth but not gluey — think heavy cream rather than mayonnaise. When you stir it into a vinaigrette, it emulsifies almost immediately without that clumpy separation you get from cheaper mustards.

## How It Performs in Real Kitchens

I tested this three ways: straight off the spoon, whisked into a classic vinaigrette, and slathered on a pork tenderloin before roasting. The heat is the first thing you notice — that sharp, sinus-clearing burn that's characteristic of true Dijon. But it's balanced, not aggressive. The vinegar gives it a bright acidity that cuts through fatty meats without making your mouth pucker.

One thing that surprised me: it holds up to heat better than expected. After 25 minutes in a 400°F oven, the mustard on the pork maintained its flavor profile instead of turning bitter or flat. That's a real advantage over cheaper mustards that degrade when cooked.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The 7.5-ounce jar is modest, especially if you cook with mustard regularly. At the price point, heavy users will go through it quickly — expect to reorder monthly. The foil seal under the lid also has a tendency to tear unevenly when you peel it back, leaving small fragments that can end up in your first spoonful. It's a minor annoyance, but it's there.

And yes, you're paying a premium. If you're making mustard-based potato salad for a crowd or just need something to squirt on hot dogs, this is overkill. The flavor difference won't matter in those applications.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for home cooks who treat condiments as ingredients, not afterthoughts. If you make your own vinaigrettes, glaze salmon, or want a mustard that actually tastes like something on a charcuterie board, this is worth every penny. It's also a solid choice for anyone trying to avoid artificial additives — the clean label is a genuine differentiator here.

Skip it if you're feeding a family of five who goes through a bottle of French's every two weeks, or if you just need mustard for sandwiches. The value proposition doesn't hold up in those scenarios.

## FAQ

**Is MAILLE Natural Dijon gluten-free?**
Yes. The ingredient list contains only mustard seeds, water, vinegar, and salt — no wheat or gluten-containing additives. It's generally safe for celiacs, though always check the label if you have severe sensitivity.

**How long does it last after opening?**
Refrigerated, it stays fresh for 6-8 months. The vinegar acts as a natural preservative. You'll know it's past its prime when the color darkens or the aroma turns sharp and acrid.

**Is this the same as what you get in French restaurants?**
Close, but not identical. MAILLE produces several Dijon variants; this Natural version is their most straightforward, without added white wine or other flavorings. It's the closest thing to classic French Dijon available at retail in the US.
