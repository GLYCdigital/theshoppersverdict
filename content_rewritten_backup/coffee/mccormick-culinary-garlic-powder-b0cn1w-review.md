Let's cut through the noise: McCormick Culinary Garlic Powder is the same garlic you've been buying under the "Gourmet" label, just in a bigger, no-frills plastic jug. The 13.5-ounce container is the real story here. It's roughly double the size of a standard 6.5-ounce grocery-store jar, and the price per ounce works out to a genuine discount — not a marketing illusion.

## What you actually get

The container arrives with a resealable plastic lid and a shaker top. As shown in the product image, it's a utilitarian white jug — this is not decorative kitchen counterware. Inside, you get a fine, beige powder with a uniform grind. The texture matters more than you'd think: coarse garlic powders clump in wet rubs, but this one dissolves cleanly into olive oil, yogurt-based marinades, and simmering sauces.

## Real-world performance

I've been using this for three months of serious cooking — dry rubs, roasted vegetables, and the occasional emergency pasta sauce. The flavor is punchy and clean, with none of the stale, dusty taste you get from cheap store-brand powders. It's not as bright as fresh garlic, obviously, but it holds its own in anything cooked for longer than 15 minutes.

One thing that surprised me: the resealable lid actually works. I've owned McCormick's metal tins that dented and lost their seal after a few weeks. This plastic closure snaps shut with a satisfying click and stays shut.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings is legitimate. The recurring theme in positive reviews is value — buyers are consistently surprised by the quantity. One reviewer put it plainly: "Same quality as the smaller jar but you don't have to reorder every two weeks." Another noted, "I use this in bulk for meal prep and it lasts months longer than expected." The practical angle matters for anyone who cooks regularly.

## Where it falls short

The shaker top is the weak link. It's thin plastic, and if you drop the container — which you will, because the jug is tall and top-heavy when full — the lid can crack. It's not catastrophic; you can still scoop from the container. But it's annoying.

The other issue is shelf life after opening. The powder absorbs humidity and the aroma fades faster than you'd expect. Within six months, you'll need to double the amount to get the same punch. This isn't unique to McCormick — it's how dehydrated garlic works — but the big container makes it easy to over-buy if you're a casual cook.

## Who should buy this

- **Meal preppers and home cooks** who go through garlic powder weekly — this is your buy.
- **Anyone tired of tiny jars** that run out mid-recipe.
- **BBQ enthusiasts** making dry rubs in bulk — the fine grind mixes evenly with paprika and brown sugar.

Skip it if you're an occasional cook who uses garlic powder twice a month. You'll have a half-empty jug losing potency by the time you finish it.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as McCormick's regular garlic powder?**
Essentially yes. The Culinary line is sold in bulk packaging at a better per-ounce price. The grind is slightly finer than the standard retail jar, but the garlic is the same quality.

**How long does it actually last?**
The best-by date is roughly two years from production. After opening, you'll get full flavor for 4-6 months, then it gradually declines. Store it in a cool, dark cabinet — not above the stove.

**Does it contain additives?**
No. Ingredients list is literally "garlic." No anti-caking agents, no silicates, no flow agents. That's why it can clump in humid climates, but it's a fair tradeoff for a cleaner product.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you cook with garlic powder at least weekly and want to stop buying tiny jars at a premium. **Skip it if** you're a light user who won't finish this before the flavor fades.

The value is undeniable, the quality is solid, and the flaws — the flimsy shaker top and inevitable aroma degradation — are manageable. For the price per ounce, this is one of the better spice purchases on Amazon.
