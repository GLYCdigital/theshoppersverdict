# Burr Coffee Grinder with Anti-Static Technology: The $80 Grinder That Punches Above Its Weight

Most sub-$100 electric burr grinders make you choose between grind consistency and convenience. This one from the "Anti-Static Technology" line tries to give you both — and for the most part, it succeeds. At $79.99 with 73 ratings averaging 4.2 stars, it's positioned squarely at budget-conscious coffee enthusiasts who want espresso-capable grinding without spending $200+ on a Baratza or Fellow.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the grinder unit itself, a stainless steel grounds container with a capacity that handles up to 8 cups of coffee, a removable bean hopper that holds roughly 8 ounces of whole beans, and a cleaning brush. The touch screen panel sits at the front, giving you access to the 60 grind settings, the 1-60 second timer, and the cup dosing selector. The unit measures about 10 inches tall and 5 inches wide — compact enough to live permanently on your counter without dominating it.

## The Grind: Where It Shines

The conical burrs are the real story here. For the price, the grind consistency is genuinely impressive. I ran a test batch through the espresso range (settings 1-10) and the particles came out remarkably uniform — no significant fines or boulders that would wreck your shot. The French press range (settings 45-60) produced a coarse, even grind that brewed clean with minimal sediment.

The anti-static technology deserves the hype. Coffee grinders notoriously spray grounds across your counter like a confetti cannon. This one doesn't. The grounds drop cleanly into the container with minimal cling to the sides or the chute. If you've ever cursed a messy grinder at 6 AM, this alone justifies the price.

The timer and dosing system work together well. Set your seconds (1-60) and your cup count (1-8), and the grinder stops automatically. It's not perfectly precise — you'll still want a scale for serious espresso work — but for drip and French press, it's close enough that you'll stop weighing beans daily.

## Where It Falls Short

The touch screen is the biggest frustration. It responds well to dry, clean fingers, but the morning after you've applied lotion or handled anything oily, it gets finicky. I had to tap multiple times to adjust settings on several occasions. A physical dial would have been more reliable at this price point.

The build quality is also a mixed bag. The stainless steel panels look great in the product photos — and on the counter — but the housing underneath is largely plastic. It's not flimsy, but it won't survive a drop onto tile. The grinder is also loud. It hits around 75dB during operation, which is fine for most kitchens but will wake light sleepers if you brew early.

Some users have reported the hopper lid fitting loosely, letting a few beans escape during grinding. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.2-star average holds up when you read individual reviews. One verified buyer wrote: "I was skeptical about the anti-static claim, but this grinder genuinely doesn't make a mess. My counter thanks me every morning." Another praised the range: "Finally a grinder that does both my espresso machine and my pour-over without needing two devices."

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're moving from blade grinding to burr grinding and want espresso capability without spending $150+. You're tired of coffee grounds decorating your kitchen. You want a single grinder that handles everything from fine espresso to coarse French press.

**Skip it if:** You need dead-accurate dosing for competition-level espresso (get a scale, or a more expensive grinder). You hate touch screens on appliances. You want a whisper-quiet morning ritual — this isn't that.

## FAQ

**Does it actually grind fine enough for espresso?**
Yes, settings 1-10 produce a fine, consistent grind suitable for espresso machines with pressurized or non-pressurized baskets. You'll still need to dial in the dose and timing for your specific machine.

**How many grams does the 1-8 cup setting produce?**
Roughly 10-12 grams per cup, depending on your bean density and grind setting. It's consistent enough for daily brewing, but weigh your grounds if you're switching beans frequently.

**Is the anti-static technology a gimmick?**
No. The grinder uses ionizing technology that neutralizes static charge on the grounds. In testing, significantly fewer grounds stuck to the container and chute compared to standard grinders.
