If you've ever wondered why your homemade pizza crust doesn't taste like the one from that corner pizzeria, the flour is usually the answer. Antimo Caputo Chef's Flour is the same 00-grade flour used across Naples, and it's the single most impactful ingredient swap you can make without buying a wood-fired oven.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 2.2 lb (1 kg) bag of double-zero flour — the "00" refers to the milling fineness, not protein content. Caputo's Chef's Flour sits at 12.5% protein, which puts it squarely in bread-flour territory while maintaining that impossibly fine texture. It's the middle child of Caputo's lineup: stronger than their classic Pizzeria flour (11.5%) but more manageable than the high-gluten Americana blend.

The bag is straightforward. No fancy packaging, just a paper sack with a resealable zipper. Inside, the flour is impossibly soft — it feels like powdered silk compared to standard all-purpose.

## Real-World Performance

I've run this through multiple dough recipes: a 24-hour cold ferment Neapolitan-style, a same-day pan pizza, and a batch of ciabatta. The hydration handling is where this flour earns its keep. It absorbed 65% hydration without turning into a gluey mess — the dough stayed supple and elastic, not sticky.

The 12.5% protein means it can handle a proper fermentation window. A 48-hour cold ferment produced a crust with that characteristic leopard spotting and a chew that had actual structure, not just crispness. In a standard home oven preheated to 500°F on a steel, you'll get a solid 60-70% of the wood-fired experience. The crust puffs in the oven rather than flattening out — that's the 00 milling doing its job.

One note: there are no additives in this flour. Some commercial 00 flours include malted barley for enzyme activity. Caputo Chef's doesn't, so if you're using active dry yeast (not instant), give your dough an extra 30-45 minutes of proofing time. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real adjustment.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs.

First, the price. At a per-pound cost roughly 30-40% higher than the 11 lb bag, you're paying a convenience tax. If you're baking weekly, buy the big bag. This size is for testing or occasional bakes.

Second, the resealable zipper is flimsy. After a handful of opens, it stops sealing cleanly. The flour clumps if exposed to humidity, so keep a bag clip nearby.

Third, this is not a substitute for bread flour in sandwich loaves. The fine milling gives you a tender crumb, not the chewy structure you want for a sourdough boule. It's a specialist tool.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this the same flour used in real Neapolitan pizzerias?**
A: Caputo is the dominant brand in Naples. The Chef's Flour is a slightly stronger variant than their classic Pizzeria flour, designed to handle both long ferments and higher-protein needs. It's authentic — just not the exact same SKU used in every pizzeria.

**Q: Can I use this for pasta?**
A: Yes, but it's overkill. Pasta benefits from a finer, lower-protein flour like Caputo's 00 Pasta flour. This will work in a pinch, but the higher gluten makes the dough springier and harder to roll thin.

**Q: How does it compare to King Arthur Bread Flour?**
A: King Arthur's bread flour (12.7% protein) is a fine substitute for bread, but it mills coarser. You'll notice the difference in pizza dough texture — Caputo produces a more delicate, airy crust. For bread loaves, King Arthur wins.
