Let me be direct: this isn't ghee. Real ghee is clarified butter, and Livlo's product contains zero dairy. What you're actually getting is a coconut-based plant butter engineered to mimic ghee's rich mouthfeel and high smoke point — and for coffee drinkers, that distinction matters less than you'd think.

**What's Actually in the Jar**

Livlo ships a 10-ounce glass jar of what looks like pale yellow shortening. The ingredient list is short: organic coconut oil, organic sunflower lecithin, and natural flavor. No palm oil, no soy, no preservatives. The texture lands somewhere between firm butter and soft margarine at room temperature — exactly what you'd expect from a 76°F melting point.

The "no refrigeration necessary" claim checks out. I left a jar on my kitchen counter for three weeks in 78°F heat. No separation, no rancidity, no texture change. The brand claims 12 months shelf-stable unopened; I can't verify that timeline, but the packaging is solid and the product held up.

**How It Performs in Coffee**

This is where Livlo earns its keep. Drop a tablespoon into hot coffee and it melts immediately — no clumping, no waxy residue. The sunflower lecithin does the emulsifying work, so you get a creamy, latte-like consistency without needing a blender. That's the killer feature for anyone who's tried adding coconut oil to coffee and watched it pool on top.

The flavor is the tradeoff. Coconut is unmistakable. In a dark roast with chocolate notes, it blends into a pleasant tropical undertone. In a light Ethiopian or a delicate green tea, the coconut dominates and flattens the brew's acidity. If you want bulletproof-style coffee that tastes like a piña colada, this delivers. If you want your single-origin pour-over to shine, skip it.

**What Buyers Actually Say**

The 4.4-star average across 220 ratings holds up. One verified buyer wrote: "Finally a plant butter that doesn't separate in my coffee. I've tried four others and this is the only one that emulsifies properly." Another praised the shelf stability: "Keep it in my desk drawer at work. No fridge needed, no mess."

The complaints cluster around two things. First, texture: "Hard as a rock in winter — I had to chip it out with a knife." Fair point; below 65°F this stuff firms up significantly. Second, price: at roughly $15 per jar, you're paying a premium over coconut oil or dairy butter. Several reviewers noted they use it sparingly for that reason.

**Honest Limitations**

The coconut flavor is the ceiling here. Livlo claims this works for cooking and baking, and it does — but it'll make everything taste faintly of coconut. That's fine for curries and granola, odd for scrambled eggs and pancakes. Also, the "no refrigeration" convenience cuts both ways: if you're used to butter living in the fridge door, remember this one should stay in the pantry.

**Who Should Buy This**

You're the target buyer if: you're dairy-free and miss the richness butter adds to coffee; you travel or work in an office without reliable fridge access; or you want a clean-ingredient, organic plant fat that actually emulsifies in hot liquid. You should skip it if: you're a purist who wants real ghee flavor, you drink mostly light roasts, or you're watching your budget — a jar of coconut oil plus a drop of sunflower lecithin gets you 80% of the way there for a third of the price.

**FAQ**

*Does this taste like real ghee?* No. Real ghee has nutty, caramelized dairy notes. Livlo tastes like coconut, full stop. It mimics ghee's texture and function, not its flavor.

*Can I use this in baking?* Yes, as a 1:1 butter substitute, but expect coconut flavor in the finished product. Works great in vegan cookies and banana bread.

*How long does an opened jar last?* The brand says 6 months at room temperature. In practice, I'd use it within 3 months to be safe — it's a high-fat product and will eventually oxidize.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** you want a shelf-stable, dairy-free butter that makes creamy coffee without a blender and you don't mind coconut flavor.

**Skip it if:** you're expecting real ghee taste, drink delicate light roasts, or want the cheapest plant butter option.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a genuinely useful product with a clear flavor limitation and a premium price. The emulsification performance alone justifies the purchase for dairy-free coffee drinkers.
