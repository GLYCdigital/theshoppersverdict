### Performance and Real-World Use

This is not a "dump and microwave" side. It requires a stovetop, a saucepan, and about 10-12 minutes of active simmering. The instructions are straightforward: combine pasta, seasoning, water, and a tablespoon of butter (or margarine), bring to a boil, then reduce heat and stir occasionally until the liquid is absorbed.

The texture is where Knorr usually shines or fails, depending on your cook timing. When done right, the pasta is al dente and the sauce is a glossy, slightly thickened coating. When rushed, you get gummy starch. The chicken flavor is distinct, but it’s not pretending to be real chicken broth. It’s a savory, salty, umami-forward seasoning that tastes like the powdered bouillon it is. For pasta, it’s genuinely satisfying — think of it as a more flavorful, quicker alternative to plain buttered noodles.

One thing to note: this is a *side dish*, not a main. The 4.3-ounce box yields roughly 2 cups cooked, which serves 2-3 people as a side. If you’re feeding a family of four, you’ll need to double it or add protein.

### Where It Falls Short (Honest Tradeoffs)

Let’s not sugarcoat it. The sodium content is high — around 580 mg per serving — which is roughly 25% of your daily recommended intake before you add anything else. If you’re watching your salt, this is a problem. The flavor is also unmistakably processed. It’s salty, savory, and a bit one-dimensional. You won’t mistake it for homemade chicken broth.

Another minor issue: the pasta-to-sauce ratio can be inconsistent if you don’t stir frequently. Some reviewers mentioned the sauce clumping at the bottom if they walked away for too long. Stirring every couple of minutes is non-negotiable.

**Q: How long does the 12-pack last?**
