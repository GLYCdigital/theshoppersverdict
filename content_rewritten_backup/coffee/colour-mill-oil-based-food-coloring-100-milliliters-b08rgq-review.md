# Colour Mill Oil-Based Food Coloring: The Fat-Lover's Secret Weapon

If you've ever watched chocolate frosting turn into a gritty, seized mess the moment you added standard food coloring, you already know why Colour Mill exists. This 100ml oil-based colorant is built for one specific job: coloring anything with a fat base — chocolate, buttercream, ganache, macaron batter — without wrecking the texture. And at 4.6 stars from 229 Amazon ratings, it's clearly doing that job well.

But here's the catch: it's not a universal coloring. If you're expecting to use this in your morning coffee or a standard cake recipe, you'll be disappointed. Let's break down exactly who this is for.

## What's in the Bottle

You get a single 100ml squeeze bottle with a fine-tip nozzle. The product image shows a rich, saturated red — that's not marketing. The pigment is genuinely thick and concentrated. One or two drops is often enough for a full batch of buttercream. The oil base is typically a blend of vegetable oil and pigment, which means it disperses seamlessly into any fat-based medium.

The squeeze bottle design is practical: the precision tip lets you add drop-by-drop, and the 100ml size is generous for a home baker who colors frosting weekly. It's not a set — you buy individual colors — so factor that into your budget if you need multiple shades.

## Performance: Where It Shines (and Where It Doesn't)

Here's the real-world breakdown from buyers:

**The Good:**
- **Chocolate compatibility:** This is the standout feature. Water-based gels cause chocolate to seize — the cocoa butter separates and the texture turns grainy. Colour Mill blends in smoothly, giving you rich, stable colors for chocolate molds, cake pops, or drip effects.
- **Buttercream stability:** Because it's oil-based, it doesn't add liquid that can make your frosting soggy. It emulsifies cleanly. Multiple reviewers note that colors deepen over 24 hours, so mix it, then let it sit — the shade intensifies.
- **Concentration:** A single 100ml bottle lasts a long time. One buyer noted, *"I've made four batches of macarons and a full drip cake, and I've barely used a quarter of the bottle."*

**The Not-So-Good:**
- **Water-based recipes are a no-go.** This will not work in cake batter, royal icing, or anything with significant water content. The oil separates and floats. It's a hard limitation, not a minor quibble.
- **Staining is real.** The pigment is powerful, and it will permanently stain light-colored plastic bowls, silicone spatulas, and fabric. Wear gloves when handling, and use dedicated tools. One reviewer warned, *"It stained my white countertop — I thought I wiped it up in time. I didn't."*
- **Limited color range per purchase.** Since bottles are sold individually, building a full palette gets pricey. You're looking at roughly $10–12 per color depending on seller.

## Who Should Buy This (and Who Should Skip It)

**Buy it if:**
- You make chocolate candies, cake pops, or chocolate-based desserts regularly
- You're a macaron maker who needs consistent, non-drying color
- You work with American or Swiss meringue buttercream and want deep, true colors without adding liquid

**Skip it if:**
- You mostly bake standard cakes, cookies, or cupcakes — stick with water-based gels
- You're looking for a single all-purpose coloring for every recipe
- You're on a tight budget and need multiple colors upfront

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I use this in coffee or hot drinks?**
No. This is for food coloring, not beverages. Oil won't dissolve in coffee — it'll float on top. Don't add it to your latte.

**Q: Will it change the taste of my buttercream?**
Generally, no — not at the amounts you'll use. One or two drops per batch is typical. But if you use a heavy hand (more than 5–6 drops), you may notice a slight oily mouthfeel.

**Q: How do I get darker colors?**
Add more drops, but be patient. The color deepens over 12–24 hours as it fully emulsifies. Mix, refrigerate, then check the shade before adding more.
