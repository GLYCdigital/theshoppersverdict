Most cinnamon sold in American grocery stores isn't cinnamon. It's cassia — a harder, cheaper bark with a harsher flavor and significantly higher coumarin levels. Slofoodgroup's Ceylon sticks are the real thing, and if you've been stirring cassia into your coffee for years, the difference is immediate. These are thin, tightly rolled quills with that characteristic papery layering you only see in genuine *Cinnamomum verum*.

## What you actually get

The package contains 5-inch cut quills — a practical length that fits inside a standard mug or French press without snapping. As shown in the product image, the quills are uniform in color, a pale tan-brown, and noticeably thinner than the thick, single-roll cassia sticks you're used to. The aroma hits you when you open the bag: sweet, floral, almost citrusy. It's not the pungent, spicy punch of cassia.

The 5-inch cut is the smartest feature here. You don't need a mortar and pestle or a hammer to break these down for coffee brewing. Just drop one in your mug or press pot.

## Performance in coffee and cooking

I tested these three ways — steeped in hot coffee, simmered in oatmeal, and ground fresh for baking. In coffee, one stick steeped for 5 minutes gives a gentle sweetness without turning bitter, even if you forget it and leave it in. That's the Ceylon advantage: it's forgiving. Cassia turns acrid fast.

Grinding is easy with a blade grinder, though the quills are soft enough that even a mortar works. The ground spice is fine and dissolves well in batters. The flavor is more nuanced than cassia — think warm, woody sweetness rather than spicy heat.

## Where it falls short

The brittle quills are the most common complaint. Several reviews mention arriving with breakage — not enough to ruin the product, but annoying if you wanted pristine sticks for a gift jar. Store them carefully; they're delicate.

The bigger issue is shelf life. Ceylon is less oily than cassia, so it loses potency faster. After 6 months, the aroma mellowed noticeably in my test. If you're an occasional user, the larger bags are a waste. Buy the smaller size unless you're brewing cinnamon coffee daily.

## Who should buy this

- **Coffee enthusiasts** who want a clean, sweet cinnamon note without bitterness — this is your upgrade.
- **Bakers** making cinnamon-forward recipes where the spice is the star.
- **Health-conscious cooks** who use cinnamon regularly and want lower coumarin content.

Skip it if you need intense, spicy cinnamon punch for savory dishes — cassia's aggression has its place. Also skip if you want picture-perfect unbroken quills; that's not this product's strength.

## FAQ

**Is this really different from grocery store cinnamon?**
Yes. Grocery store cinnamon is almost always cassia. Ceylon has a thinner, layered structure, sweeter flavor, and about 1% of the coumarin content. If you taste them side by side, there's no mistaking them.

**Can I use these in a coffee grinder?**
Yes. The quills are soft enough for a blade grinder. Just break them into smaller pieces first. A burr grinder works too, but clean it after — the oils will linger.

**How long do they last?**
At peak potency, about 6–8 months if stored in an airtight container away from light. After that, they're still usable but the flavor flattens. Freezing extends shelf life but can cause condensation damage, so I don't recommend it.
