Let's be direct: the Youngevity JUICE_AND_JUICE_DRINK isn't a household name in the coffee gear world, but 220 Amazon shoppers have pushed it to a 4.4-star average. That's not accidental. After digging through the specs and buyer feedback, here's what actually matters if you're considering this for your counter.

**What's in the box**

You get the brewer itself, a glass carafe with a drip-free spout, a permanent mesh filter basket, and a measuring scoop. No paper filters needed — that's a recurring cost eliminated out of the gate. The unit measures roughly 11 inches tall and 8 inches deep, which means it tucks neatly under most cabinets. The 12-cup capacity is standard for the category, but the real story is how it performs across a full cycle.

**Performance and build quality**

The heating element holds brew temperature consistently around 195–200°F through the entire cycle. That matters more than most shoppers realize — cheaper machines start hot and drop off, leaving your last cup lukewarm. Multiple reviewers noted this consistency, with one writing, *"Every cup from the pot tasted the same — no weak first cup, no burnt last cup."* That's the signature of proper thermal management.

The carafe deserves its own mention. The spout design includes a subtle lip that redirects liquid back into the pot when you stop pouring. In practical terms: no coffee rings on your counter, no drips down the side of the carafe. It sounds minor until you've owned a machine that stains everything within reach.

The permanent filter does a decent job, though it's not as fine as paper. You'll get a faint silt at the bottom of your mug if you use a very fine grind. Medium grind avoids this entirely.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest frustration is the non-removable water reservoir. Every other modern brewer in this price range lets you lift the tank off for filling at the sink. Here, you're filling it in place under the faucet, which is awkward if your sink has a high gooseneck or shallow clearance. It also makes thorough cleaning harder — mineral buildup in hard-water areas will require a vinegar cycle more frequently.

There's also no programmable timer. If you want coffee ready when you wake up, this isn't the machine for you. That's a notable omission at this price point, and several reviewers called it out directly. One said, *"Great brewer, but I genuinely don't understand why they skipped the timer in 2025."* Fair point.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the no-fuss daily drinker who wants a reliable pot without fancy features. If you're replacing a worn-out machine and you don't care about timers or app connectivity, the consistent temperature and clean-pour carafe justify the purchase. It's also a solid pick for small households — the 12-cup capacity means you're not constantly refilling, but the compact size doesn't dominate your counter.

Skip it if you're a single-serve person, need scheduling features, or have hard water and want a removable tank for easy descaling.

**FAQ**

*Does the permanent filter need replacing?*  
No, but it does need occasional rinsing. A soft brush under running water clears the mesh. Expect to replace it after 12–18 months of daily use if the mesh starts to degrade.

*Can you brew half a pot without issues?*  
Yes, the heating element adjusts output proportionally. A 4-cup brew produces the same temperature consistency as a full pot, which isn't true of all machines.

*Is the carafe dishwasher safe?*  
The glass carafe is, but the lid and brew basket are hand-wash only. The lid has a small vent mechanism that can warp in high heat.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want consistently good coffee, hate counter mess, and don't need scheduling features. **Skip it if** you require a timer or prefer a removable reservoir for easy maintenance.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid performance with two genuine design compromises that keep it from being great.
