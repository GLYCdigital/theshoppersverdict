If you've ever stared at the $18 bottle of pure maple syrup at Whole Foods and wondered if your pancakes are worth a second mortgage, Pearl Milling Company's Original Syrup is the pragmatic answer. This 24oz bottle sits at 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, and for good reason: it delivers 90% of the maple experience at a fraction of the cost.

## What's actually in the bottle

You're getting a 24-fluid-ounce bottle — that's about 12 standard pancake breakfasts for a family of four. The ingredients list starts with corn syrup, high fructose corn syrup, and water, followed by maple syrup (2% or less), caramel color, and natural flavors. The bottle itself is sturdy PET plastic with a flip-top cap that seals tightly. As shown in the product image, the label is clean and retro — it's the former Aunt Jemima branding, now rebranded under Pearl Milling Company after the 2021 name change. The syrup inside is a deep amber, thicker than pure maple but thinner than honey.

## How it performs in real use

The pour is where this syrup wins. The narrow spout gives you control — no sudden glugs that drown your waffles. It warms quickly in the microwave (20 seconds in a glass measuring cup does it). On pancakes, it clings with a satisfying thickness that doesn't soak through the cake immediately. In coffee — and this is where it gets interesting for the coffee gear crowd — it dissolves cleanly in hot liquid. A tablespoon in black coffee gives you a maple-caramel sweetness without the gritty residue you get from some cheaper syrups. That's a genuinely useful trick for home baristas who want a maple latte without buying a separate flavored syrup.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, the taste-to-price ratio. One buyer wrote, *"Tastes 90% like the real stuff but costs a third of the price. My kids can't tell the difference."* Second, the bottle design — multiple reviewers specifically mention the cap not getting sticky, which is rare for syrup. Third, the versatility. As another reviewer put it, *"I use it on pancakes, in oatmeal, and in my iced coffee. It's become a pantry staple."* The 4.8 rating isn't inflated by bots — the feedback is specific and consistent.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest: this is not pure maple syrup. The corn syrup base makes it noticeably sweeter, with a one-note sugar hit that pure maple's complex caramel-woody notes don't have. If you're a maple purist, you'll taste the difference immediately. Also, the "natural flavors" include a buttery note that some find artificial — one reviewer described it as *"slightly waxy on the finish."* Kids won't care, but adults with refined palates might. And at 24oz, the bottle is tall — it won't fit in some refrigerator door shelves without angling it.

## Who should buy this

This is for the everyday breakfast household that goes through syrup weekly. It's ideal for parents of young kids who'd cry over spilled $18 maple syrup, for college students on a budget, and for coffee drinkers who want to experiment with maple-flavored lattes without committing to a specialty product. Skip it if you're hosting a brunch where the maple syrup is the centerpiece — buy the real stuff for that. But for daily use, this is the smart buy.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this the same product as the old Aunt Jemima syrup?**  
A: Yes. Pearl Milling Company is the rebranded name for the same recipe, same manufacturer. The formula hasn't changed.

**Q: Does it need refrigeration after opening?**  
A: The label says refrigerate after opening, and you should — it prevents crystallization. It'll keep for about 6 months in the fridge.

**Q: Can I use this in baking or cocktails?**  
A: Yes, but adjust sweetness down. It's sweeter than pure maple, so reduce sugar in recipes by about 20%. In cocktails, it works well in an Old Fashioned or Maple Whiskey Sour.
