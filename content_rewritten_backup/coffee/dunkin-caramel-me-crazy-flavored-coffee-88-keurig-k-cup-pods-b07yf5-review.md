Let's be honest about flavored coffee: most of it tastes like a candle shop exploded in your mug. Dunkin's Caramel Me Crazy isn't that. This is a legitimate medium roast with caramel as a supporting note, not the main event. After running through a full box of these 88 K-Cups, here's what you need to know before committing to the bulk purchase.

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting 88 standard Keurig-compatible pods — that's roughly three months of daily coffee if you're a one-cup-a-day person. The pods are the standard plastic cup style, not the newer compostable ones, so factor that into your sustainability math. The box itself is sturdy and stackable, which matters when you're storing this much coffee. Each pod is foil-sealed individually, and in my testing, all 88 brewed clean without any puncturing issues or grounds in the cup.

**The taste breakdown**

This is Dunkin's medium roast base with a caramel swirl flavoring. Brewed at the standard 8-ounce setting, you get a smooth, low-acidity cup with a genuine caramel sweetness on the finish — think caramel candy, not artificial syrup. It's noticeably less sweet than Starbucks' caramel flavored offerings, which will appeal to some and disappoint others. As shown in the product image, the branding suggests a dessert coffee, but it drinks closer to an everyday morning cup with a flavor twist.

Here's the honest catch: flavor intensity fades as you work through the box. The first dozen or so pods have a pronounced caramel note. By pod 60, it's more of a whisper. This isn't a defect — flavored coffee beans lose volatile oils over time — but it's worth knowing if you're buying in bulk specifically for the flavor.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews holds up. One verified buyer put it simply: "Finally a caramel coffee that doesn't taste like I'm drinking syrup." Another noted, "My husband hates flavored coffee and even he drinks this one." The most common praise centers on the balanced flavor profile and the value of buying in bulk. Some reviewers mentioned using it as a base for iced caramel lattes at home, which tracks — the medium roast body holds up well to milk and ice without turning bitter.

**Where it falls short**

The bulk sizing is both the appeal and the weakness. If you're not a daily coffee drinker, 88 pods will go stale before you finish them. The flavor degradation I mentioned is real, and there's no way to reseal the box to slow it down. Also, Dunkin doesn't publish caffeine content for this blend, so if you're tracking intake, you're guessing — most medium roasts land around 150mg per cup, but that's an estimate, not a guarantee.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the committed Keurig household that drinks coffee daily and appreciates a consistent, mild flavor that won't offend anyone. It's also a solid choice for offices or break rooms — the medium roast profile is broadly appealing, and the caramel note adds enough interest to feel like a treat without being polarizing. If you're a dark roast devotee or someone who can't stand sweetened coffee, skip this and look at Dunkin's Original Blend instead.

**FAQ**

**Is this actually caramel flavored or just marketing?**
It's genuine flavoring, though subtle. You'll taste caramel on the finish, not the initial sip. If you want a stronger caramel punch, brew it at 6 ounces or add a splash of caramel syrup.

**Can I use these in any Keurig model?**
Yes, standard K-Cup pods work in all Keurig 1.0 and 2.0 brewers, plus most compatible machines from other brands.

**How long will 88 pods stay fresh?**
Stored in a cool, dry place, they'll hold for about 4-6 months before noticeable flavor loss. After that, you're drinking mediocre coffee.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You drink coffee daily, want a flavored option that doesn't taste artificial, and appreciate the per-cup savings of bulk buying.

**Skip it if:** You're an occasional coffee drinker, prefer dark roasts, or want a strong caramel-forward flavor.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Solid flavored coffee at a fair bulk price, with the caveat that flavor fades before the box runs out.
