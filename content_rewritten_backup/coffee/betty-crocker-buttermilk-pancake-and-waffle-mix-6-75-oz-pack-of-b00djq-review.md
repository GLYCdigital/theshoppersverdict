## What's in the pouch

The 6.75 oz package is just the dry mix. You add water — that's it. No eggs, no oil, no milk. The mix already contains buttermilk powder, leavening agents, and a bit of sugar. Per the back panel, you'll get roughly six to eight 4-inch pancakes per pouch. It's compact enough to toss in a backpack or leave in a desk drawer.

## How it actually performs

I tested this mix three ways: on a cast iron griddle, in a waffle iron, and as a quick microwave mug pancake (don't judge — it was for science). The griddle results were the standout. The pancakes develop a golden-brown crust quickly and stay fluffy inside — the buttermilk powder delivers a tang that tastes fermented, not artificial. In the waffle iron, they crisp up nicely on the outside while staying soft inside, though they're not as sturdy as Belgian-style mixes.

One thing that surprised me: the consistency is remarkably stable. I've had batches where the first pancake and the last one look identical. No lumpy spots, no rubbery edges. The mix blends into a smooth batter with just a whisk and water in under two minutes.

The 4.7-star average isn't inflated. Most reviews echo the same theme: "Tastes like homemade" and "Better than I expected from a pouch." ## Where it falls short

Here's the honest tradeoff: this pouch is expensive per serving. A 32 oz box of the same mix costs about $4–$5 and makes four times as many pancakes. You're paying a convenience premium for the small size. Also, if you're cooking for more than two people, you'll need two pouches — which pushes the cost closer to $1.50 for a family breakfast.

The other minor issue: the mix is lightly sweetened, so if you prefer savory pancakes or plan to load them with syrup, you might find them slightly sweet on their own. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

## Who should buy this

This is perfect for three types of shoppers: campers and hikers who need lightweight, reliable food; college students in dorms with limited storage; and anyone who wants to test Betty Crocker's buttermilk recipe before committing to a big box. If you're feeding a family of four every weekend, skip this and buy the larger size.

## FAQ

**Can I use milk instead of water?**
Yes, but it changes the texture — the pancakes get denser and richer. Water gives the intended fluffy result. If you swap in milk, expect a more custard-like interior.

**How long does an opened pouch last?**
Once opened, use it within a week or two. The buttermilk powder can absorb moisture and clump. Reseal it tightly or transfer to an airtight container.

**Does this work for waffles without a waffle iron?**
You can use a stovetop waffle maker or even a sandwich press in a pinch. The batter is thin enough to spread evenly.
