# FLYBYJING Original Sichuan Chili Crisp: The Málà Middle Ground

Here's the honest truth about FLYBYJING's Original Sichuan Chili Crisp: it's not the hottest chili crisp you'll find, and it's not the cheapest. What it *is* is the most balanced, thoughtfully constructed version of the genre — a condiment that actually tastes like Sichuan, not just a jar of angry red oil.

I've tested this against Lao Gan Ma, Momofuku, and several boutique brands. FLYBYJING sits in a distinct lane: it prioritizes the numbing *málà* sensation over pure heat, and that changes how you use it.

## What You Actually Get

The 5.6-ounce jar arrives with a satisfying weight. As shown in the product image, you're looking at a dense matrix of fried garlic, onion, and chili flakes suspended in deep-red soybean oil. The lid seals tight, and the glass jar is sturdy enough to survive a drop (I tested this accidentally — it did).

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: soybean oil, chili, Sichuan peppercorn, garlic, onion, and salt. No MSG, no preservatives, no "natural flavors" hiding anything. That's a genuine differentiator in this category.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The texture is where FLYBYJING earns its premium price. Those garlic and onion bits stay crunchy — genuinely crunchy — even after three weeks in my fridge. Most chili crisps turn to soggy paste within days. This one doesn't.

The heat profile deserves explanation. The initial hit is mild, but then the Sichuan peppercorns kick in with that distinctive tongue-numbing buzz that builds over 30 seconds and lingers. It's not painful; it's *interesting*. This makes it ideal for eggs, dumplings, noodles, and even roasted vegetables where you want depth without dominating the dish.

One practical note: the oil separates quickly. You'll need to stir from the bottom every single time, and the first few spoonfuls from a fresh jar are oil-heavy. Not a flaw — physics — but worth knowing.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Amazon reviewers at 4.3 stars (215 ratings) repeatedly highlight two things. One buyer wrote: "Finally a chili crisp that doesn't taste like pure salt and oil — the numbing is real and the crunch lasts." Another noted: "I've bought four jars. It's ruined all other hot sauces for me."

The MSG-free formulation gets consistent shoutouts from people avoiding it for dietary reasons, and multiple reviewers mention the heat is "perfect for everyday" rather than extreme.

## Where It Falls Short

The jar is small. At roughly two tablespoons per serving, heavy users will burn through it in two weeks. That's $1+ per serving, which is steep for a condiment.

The heat ceiling is a real limitation. If your baseline is habanero or ghost pepper, this will feel mild. It's a flavor enhancer, not a spice bomb.

A few reviewers noted inconsistent jars — some arriving with more oil than solids. And the price fluctuates on Amazon; it's worth checking for subscribe-and-save discounts, which bring the per-ounce cost down meaningfully.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want authentic Sichuan málà flavor, you appreciate clean ingredients, or you're tired of chili crisps that are all heat and no nuance.

**Skip it if:** You need extreme spiciness, you go through condiments slowly (it will lose crunch after months), or you're on a tight budget — Lao Gan Ma delivers 80% of the experience at half the price.

## FAQ

**Is FLYBYJING actually spicy?**
Moderately. The heat is gentle initially, with a numbing tingle that builds. It's more about flavor complexity than pain. If you want brutal heat, look elsewhere.

**Does it need refrigeration?**
No, but I'd recommend it after opening. Cold storage helps preserve the crunch. The oil solidifies slightly when chilled — let it sit out 10 minutes before use.

**What's the best way to use it?**
Stir thoroughly, then spoon over eggs, dumplings, noodles, or mix into mayo for a quick dipping sauce. A little goes a long way — start with a teaspoon.
