## Performance and Real-World Use

Here's where whole-herb rosemary separates itself. Standard extracts are typically steam-distilled or solvent-processed, which strips away many of the volatile compounds. Whole-leaf powder retains the full spectrum of rosemary's natural chemistry — rosmarinic acid, carnosic acid, and the essential oils that give the herb its characteristic pine-and-lemon aroma.

Open the bottle and you'll smell it immediately. That's fresh rosemary, not the faint dust odor you get from cheap products. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've bought three other rosemary brands before this one. None of them smelled like actual rosemary. This one does. You can tell it's not processed to death."*

Users report the expected benefits — improved focus, better circulation, and some anti-inflammatory relief — but the consistency of feedback on digestion is notable. Multiple reviewers mention it's gentler on the stomach than extracts, likely because whole-herb fiber buffers the active compounds.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**Capsule size is the biggest complaint.** At roughly 22mm, these are large capsules — noticeably bigger than a standard vitamin. If you struggle with pills, this will be a problem. Several reviewers mention having to break capsules open and mix the powder into food or tea.

**No enteric coating.** The powder begins dissolving in the stomach, which means some users experience mild rosemary-flavored burps. It's not unpleasant — more like eating a strong herb — but it's worth knowing if you're sensitive to that.

**Price premium.** You're paying 15-20% more than generic rosemary capsules. The quality justifies it, but if you're purely price-driven, you can find cheaper options.
