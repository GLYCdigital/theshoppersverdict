Ito En has been bottling tea in Japan since the 1960s, and their Jasmine Green Tea is one of the few bottled teas that actually tastes like tea leaves were involved. This isn't a sweetened, lemon-flavored, or “green tea flavored beverage” — it's just brewed jasmine green tea, water, and a bit of vitamin C. That's the entire ingredient list.

As shown in the product image, this comes in a slim 10.6-ounce bottle with a clean, Japanese-minimalist label. It's sold as a single bottle here, which matters for pricing expectations — you're paying for convenience and quality, not bulk quantity.

**What you actually get**

One 10.6 oz bottle of unsweetened jasmine green tea. Ito En uses whole tea leaves (not powder, not concentrate) and brews it in small batches. The tea is then bottled immediately to lock in freshness. There's no sugar, no artificial sweeteners, no preservatives. The only additive is ascorbic acid (vitamin C), which protects the tea's color and flavor without altering taste.

**Performance and real-world use**

The flavor profile is where this bottle shines. You get a clean, grassy green tea base with a genuinely floral jasmine top note — not that perfume-y, potpourri taste you find in cheaper jasmine teas. It's refreshing and light, with no bitterness or astringency. The tea is cold-brewed, which explains why it lacks the harsh edge that hot-brewed-then-chilled teas often develop.

It's best served ice cold. At room temperature, the jasmine notes flatten out and the tea tastes flatter than it should. Keep it refrigerated and this is a genuinely pleasant, hydrating drink.

**Where it falls short**

The 10.6 oz bottle is the biggest practical complaint. Most bottled teas in the US come in 16.9 oz or 18.5 oz sizes. At roughly 60% of a standard bottle's volume, you'll go through this quickly — especially if you're drinking it with lunch or as a workout recovery drink. Some reviewers felt the jasmine flavor was too gentle. If you're used to strongly scented jasmine teas (like you'd get at a Chinese restaurant), this might underwhelm you. It's jasmine-forward but restrained.
