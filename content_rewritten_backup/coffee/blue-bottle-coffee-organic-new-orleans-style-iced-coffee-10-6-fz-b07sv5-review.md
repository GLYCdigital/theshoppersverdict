**The Shortcut to Chicory-Kissed Cold Brew That Actually Tastes Like New Orleans**

Most bottled iced coffees are either sugar-bomb milkshakes or bitter, watery afterthoughts. Blue Bottle’s Organic New Orleans Style Iced Coffee is neither. It’s a concentrated, shelf-stable cold brew that delivers the city’s signature chicory bitterness and dark chocolate depth without requiring a plane ticket or a 14-hour steep. If you’ve been burned by “premium” canned coffee before, this one earns its 4.6-star reputation through honest, robust flavor.

**What’s Actually in the Bottle**

You get a 10-ounce bottle of liquid concentrate. This is not a ready-to-drink single serving — it’s designed to be diluted. Blue Bottle recommends a 1:1 ratio with water or milk, which means each bottle yields roughly 20 ounces of finished iced coffee. The ingredients are refreshingly short: organic coffee, organic chicory, and water. No preservatives, no cane sugar, no “natural flavors” hiding artificiality. The coffee itself uses a dark roast blend, and the chicory root (a New Orleans staple) adds that earthy, almost smoky bitterness that cuts through milk beautifully.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The concentrate is thick and syrupy, almost like a coffee extract. When you pour it over ice and cut it with cold water, it holds its character remarkably well — you still taste the molasses-like sweetness and the chicory’s woody edge. It’s not acidic, which is the hallmark of proper cold brewing. For a 10-ounce bottle at this price point, you’re paying for convenience and quality, not volume.

Here’s the catch: it’s not a “grab and go” drink. You must mix it. If you’re expecting a ready-to-pour bottle, you’ll be disappointed. But if you treat it as a concentrate, it’s incredibly versatile. Add oat milk for a creamy, dessert-like treat, or keep it black over ice for a sharp, wake-you-up punch. It also works as a base for homemade coffee cocktails or a quick affogato.

**FAQ: Three Real Questions, Answered**

**Is this concentrate or ready-to-drink?**
It’s a concentrate. You must dilute it 1:1 with water or milk. Drinking it straight will be intensely strong and bitter.

**Does it taste like Café du Monde?**
Similar, but smoother. Café du Monde’s is chicory-forward and gritty. Blue Bottle’s version is cleaner and more refined, with the chicory as a supporting note rather than the main event.

**How should I store it after opening?**
Refrigerate it. The bottle recommends consuming within a week of opening, though most users finish it in a few days.
