There are dark roasts, and then there’s *the* dark roast. For over 50 years, Peet’s Major Dickason’s Blend has held a cult-like status among coffee drinkers who find Starbucks too bitter and supermarket Folgers too weak. It’s the blend that built Peet’s reputation, and with a 4.6-star average across 229 Amazon ratings, it’s clearly still connecting with buyers.

But is this 32-ounce bag of whole beans actually worth your counter space and your grinder’s effort? Let’s break down what you’re really getting.

## What’s in the Bag

You’re getting a full **32 ounces (2 pounds)** of whole bean coffee. That’s roughly double the size of a standard 12-ounce specialty bag, which matters for price-per-cup. This is a dark roast blend of Central and South American beans, with a few Indonesian beans thrown in for that earthy, spicy complexity.

The beans arrive oily — that’s a hallmark of a true dark roast, not a defect. You’ll notice the sheen immediately when you open the bag. The fragrance is intense: think baker’s chocolate, toasted nuts, and a whiff of smoke.

## Performance and Build

Since this is whole bean, your grind size dictates everything. For a dark roast this dense, I recommend a **medium-coarse grind** for drip or pour-over. Too fine, and you’ll extract bitter, ashy notes. Too coarse, and you’ll miss the depth entirely.

In a French press, this blend shines — it holds up to the long steep without turning acrid. In an espresso machine, it’s forgiving and produces a thick crema with a molasses-like sweetness. If you’re using a standard drip machine, aim for a water temperature around 200°F (just off boiling) to avoid scorching the oils.

One caveat: these beans are **oily enough to gunge up a burr grinder** over time. Plan to clean your grinder’s burrs every few weeks if this is your daily driver.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The reviews are remarkably consistent. The word "smooth" appears constantly, which is unusual for a dark roast. One verified buyer wrote: *"I’ve tried every dark roast on Amazon. This is the only one that doesn’t taste like burnt tires. Rich, full-bodied, and somehow never bitter."*

Another recurring theme is **freshness**. Multiple reviewers noted the roast date on the bag was recent, and the beans arrived with that just-roasted aroma intact. That’s a big deal when buying coffee online — stale beans are the #1 killer of home brew.

Strength is also a common compliment. Several espresso drinkers mentioned pulling great shots without needing to dial in extra fine, which saves time and frustration.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides.

**It’s a dark roast, and it tastes like one.** If you prefer light, fruity, tea-like coffees, this will be overwhelming. There’s no acidity, no floral notes — just deep, roasty, almost smoky intensity. That’s the point, but it’s a hard stop for some palates.

**The 2-pound bag is a commitment.** Coffee loses its peak flavor about 3-4 weeks after roasting. Unless you’re drinking 2-3 cups daily, you’ll be drinking past-prime beans by the end of the bag. Consider freezing half in an airtight container if you’re a light drinker.

**The oiliness is real.** As mentioned, it will dirty your grinder faster than medium roasts. Also, if you use a blade grinder, the oils can cause uneven grinding and clumping.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The dark roast loyalist** who finds Starbucks Pike Place too mild and French Roast too ashy.
- **The home espresso enthusiast** who wants a forgiving, chocolate-forward shot without spending $20 for a 12-ounce bag.
- **The cold brew maker** — this blend’s boldness holds up beautifully to 18-hour steeps, producing a concentrated, syrupy brew.

## Who Should Skip It

- **Light roast lovers** who want bright, citrusy, or floral notes. You’ll hate this.
- **Single-cup brewers** who don’t want to commit to 2 pounds of the same coffee.
- **Anyone with a blade grinder** — the oily beans will make a mess and inconsistent grounds.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Major Dickason’s Blend the same as Peet’s French Roast?**
No. French Roast is darker and more one-dimensional — basically charcoal. Major Dickason’s has more complexity, with a noticeable sweetness and spice underneath the roast. It’s a “dark roast for people who want flavor, not just bitterness.”

**Q: How should I store a 2-pound bag?**
Keep it in the original bag (it has a one-way valve) in a cool, dark cabinet. Don’t put it in the fridge — that introduces moisture and absorbs odors. If you won’t finish it in 3 weeks, portion it into freezer-safe bags and freeze only what you’re not actively using.

**Q: Does this work for a Keurig with a refillable pod?**
Yes, but grind it slightly finer than drip — closer to a medium-fine. And expect a stronger cup than typical K-cups, so you may want to brew a smaller serving size.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — This is a benchmark dark roast for a reason.

**Buy it if** you want a bold, smooth, complex cup that’s versatile across brew methods and won’t break the bank per serving.

**Skip it if** you’re sensitive to roast-heavy flavors, don’t have a decent burr grinder, or can’t get through 2 pounds before it stales.

The 4.6-star rating is earned. It’s not a subtle coffee, but it’s a *good* coffee — one that respects the bean and the drinker. Just clean your grinder and don’t let the bag linger too long.
