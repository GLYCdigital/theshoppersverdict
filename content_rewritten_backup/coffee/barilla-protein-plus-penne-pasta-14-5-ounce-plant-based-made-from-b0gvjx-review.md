Okay, let's be clear about what this isn't: Barilla Protein+ Penne isn't a miracle food. It's pasta made from lentil, chickpea, and pea flours blended with corn and rice. But for anyone tracking protein without wanting to eat another chicken breast, that 14.5-ounce blue box deserves a serious look.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get one 14.5-ounce box of penne. That's it. No seasoning packets, no gimmicks. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: lentil flour, chickpea flour, pea protein, corn flour, rice flour, and a touch of monoglycerides. As shown in the product image, it's standard penne shape — those diagonal-cut tubes that grab sauce well. The color is slightly more golden than traditional semolina pasta, which is your first clue something's different.

## How It Performs in the Kitchen

Here's where this pasta earns its keep. A 2-ounce dry serving delivers 10 grams of plant-based protein — double what you'd get from Barilla's regular penne. The cooking time is reasonable at 9-10 minutes, and it reaches a genuinely pleasant al dente texture.

One honest warning: the protein flours create more foam during boiling than regular pasta. Use a pot bigger than you think you need and stir occasionally during the first few minutes. Multiple Amazon reviewers mention this; one noted, "I nearly had a starchy volcano on my stovetop the first time." Learn from them.

The flavor is neutral — slightly nutty, but it won't fight your sauce. The texture is where you'll notice the difference. It's firmer, almost toothsome in a way that traditional penne isn't. If you're baking it into a casserole or tossing with a thick ragù, that density is actually an advantage; it holds its shape beautifully.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The protein content is the headline, but the taste is what converts skeptics. As one five-star reviewer put it: "My kids couldn't tell the difference. That's the only review that matters." Another wrote, "Finally a protein pasta that doesn't taste like cardboard. I've tried them all — this is the one I repurchase." The 4.8-star average across 240 ratings reflects genuine consistency; complaints are rare, and most center on the price rather than the product itself.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the price: this typically runs $2.50-$3.50 per 14.5-ounce box, roughly double what standard pasta costs. Over a month of weekly use, that adds up. Second, the texture is undeniably denser. If you're serving someone who's deeply attached to traditional semolina pasta, they'll notice. It's not a flaw, but it's a reality. Third, the foam issue during cooking is real — plan accordingly.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Gym-goers and athletes** who want to boost protein without chugging shakes. Two servings with a lentil-based sauce gets you 20+ grams for dinner.
- **Plant-based eaters** who need easy, accessible protein sources that don't require elaborate prep.
- **Busy parents** looking to sneak extra protein into family meals — the taste overlap with regular pasta is close enough that most kids won't complain.
- **Meal preppers** who need a pasta that survives reheating without turning to mush.

Skip it if you're on a tight grocery budget, or if you're a pasta purist who will notice and resent any textural difference.

## FAQ

**Does it actually taste like regular pasta?**
Close, but not identical. It's slightly nuttier and firmer. Most people won't notice in a saucy dish; a plain buttered-pasta test will reveal the difference.

**Is it gluten-free?**
No. The blend includes corn and rice flours, but it's not certified gluten-free. If you have celiac disease, this isn't for you.

**How much protein per serving?**
10 grams per 2-ounce dry serving. That's roughly comparable to a glass of milk, baked into your carbs.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely good-tasting pasta that adds meaningful protein to your meals without requiring a chemistry degree to prepare.

**Skip it if:** You're on a strict budget, need gluten-free, or won't accept any textural deviation from traditional semolina.
