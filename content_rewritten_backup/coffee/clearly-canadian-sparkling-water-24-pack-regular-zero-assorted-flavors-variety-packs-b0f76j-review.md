**The 90s called — and this time it brought legit flavor.**

Clearly Canadian isn't new. It's a blast from the past that's earned a cult following for a reason. But nostalgia alone doesn't justify a 24-pack taking up fridge space. After digging through 215 ratings and the product specs, here's what actually matters.

**What you're getting**

This 24-pack splits between the classic sweetened line and the Zero Sugar version. The regular bottles use real cane sugar — a detail that matters more than you'd think. Most flavored sparkling waters lean on sucralose or stevia, which leaves that weird metallic aftertaste. Clearly Canadian sidesteps that entirely. The Zero line uses erythritol and monk fruit, which is a smarter choice for anyone watching carbs.

Each bottle is 11.5 ounces of glass. That's not a gimmick — glass holds carbonation better and doesn't leach flavors the way aluminum can. The bottles are recyclable, and the packaging in the listing photo shows the assorted flavor lineup clearly.

**How it performs in the real world**

The carbonation here is assertive but not aggressive. It's a middle ground between LaCroix's whisper-light fizz and a hard seltzer's punch. The flavor profile is where this brand separates itself. Wild Cherry tastes like actual cherries, not perfume. Black Cherry has a depth that most competitors miss entirely.

One buyer put it plainly: *"These taste like the 90s but somehow better. The carbonation is perfect and the flavors aren't fake-tasting like other sparkling waters."* Another noted: *"I bought these for nostalgia and ended up buying three more packs because my kids stole them."*

That tracks with the 4.3-star average. People aren't just buying a drink — they're buying a flavor experience that's genuinely hard to replicate.

**Where it stumbles**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The price per bottle is noticeably higher than store-brand sparkling waters. You're paying for the brand, the glass, and the cane sugar — all legitimate, but your wallet will feel it.

The Zero Sugar versions get mixed reviews. Some buyers say the monk fruit sweetener leaves a slight cooling sensation on the tongue. That's a known quirk of the ingredient, and it bothers some people more than others. If you're sensitive to alternative sweeteners, grab a single bottle before committing to a 24-pack.

Shipping is the bigger wildcard. Glass bottles in a 24-pack is a lot of weight, and several reviewers reported cracked bottles or delayed deliveries. Amazon's packaging is usually solid, but this is a real risk with glass.

**Who should buy this**

This is for three specific buyers:

- **The nostalgic millennial** who remembers these from childhood and wants that taste back without artificial junk
- **The sparkling water snob** who finds LaCroix too weak and Spindrift too tart — this sits in the sweet spot
- **The party host** who wants something that looks nice in glass bottles and tastes better than generic seltzer

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget, you hate any sweetness in your sparkling water, or you're buying in bulk for daily chugging — cheaper options exist.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the Zero Sugar version actually zero calorie?**
A: Yes, both Zero flavors come in at 0 calories per bottle. The sweetened versions run about 90 calories each.

**Q: How long does the carbonation last after opening?**
A: The glass bottles hold fizz well — expect 2-3 hours of active carbonation if you reseal and refrigerate. It goes flat faster than canned options once opened.

**Q: Can I mix and match flavors?**
A: No. The 24-pack comes as a fixed assortment. If you want specific flavors, you'll need to buy single cases instead.
