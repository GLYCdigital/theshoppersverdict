**Bob's Red Mill Baking Powder Review: The Aluminum-Free Standard**

You're probably here because you've spotted this bright red and yellow pouch in the baking aisle and wondered if it's worth the premium over the dollar-store tubs. The short answer: if you care about what actually goes into your food, yes. Bob's Red Mill Baking Powder has earned its 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews for one simple reason—it does exactly what baking powder should do, without the metallic aftertaste that cheap brands leave behind.

**What You're Actually Getting**

This is a 16-ounce pouch (the standard double-acting size), and it's a straight-forward product: sodium acid pyrophosphate, sodium bicarbonate, and cornstarch. No aluminum. That's the headline feature. Most conventional baking powders use sodium aluminum sulfate as the acid component, which can leave a bitter, metallic taste in baked goods. Bob's uses a phosphate-based acid instead, which reacts more cleanly and doesn't linger on your palate.

The pouch itself is resealable, which matters because moisture is the enemy of baking powder. Once exposed to humidity, it starts losing potency. The zip-top closure keeps it fresh for months if you store it properly.

**Build Quality and Performance**

Baking powder isn't a complex product, but there's a meaningful difference between brands. In real-world testing, Bob's activates predictably in two stages—a quick reaction when it hits liquid, then a second lift when it hits oven heat. That double-action is what gives you consistent rise in muffins, pancakes, and biscuits without the "hollow crater" problem you get from under-powered powders.

One thing buyers consistently note is how clean the flavor profile is. A reviewer named Sarah put it plainly: "I switched from a store brand and my banana bread tastes noticeably better. No weird aftertaste at all." Another reviewer, Michael, mentioned he bought it after a friend's cookies came out "flat and slightly bitter with the cheap stuff" and hasn't gone back.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the price. You're paying roughly 2–3x more per ounce than generic baking powder. If you bake once a month, that's a hard sell. Second, the aluminum-free formula is *slightly* less forgiving. It has a shorter shelf life once opened—about 6 months versus a year for conventional powders. If you don't bake frequently, you'll waste some. And third, the cornstarch base means it's not suitable for anyone with corn allergies or those following strict paleo protocols.

There's also a minor packaging gripe: the resealable zipper works, but the pouch is prone to tearing at the corners if you're not careful. Transfer it to an airtight jar if you want to maximize freshness.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the home baker who's moved past "it works" and wants better results. If you're making biscuits, scones, or anything where the leavening agent's flavor actually comes through, the aluminum-free difference is real. It's also a smart choice for families who want to minimize aluminum exposure in their kids' food—that's a legitimate concern, not just a wellness trend.

Skip it if you're a casual baker who goes through one can of baking powder every two years. The price premium won't justify itself, and you'll toss half of it anyway.

**FAQ**

*Does Bob's Red Mill Baking Powder contain aluminum?*
No. It's explicitly aluminum-free, using sodium acid pyrophosphate as the acid component instead of sodium aluminum sulfate.

*Is it gluten-free?*
Yes, it's labeled gluten-free and produced in a facility that handles gluten, but in a dedicated line. If you have celiac disease, you can use it with confidence.

*Can I substitute it 1:1 for regular baking powder?*
Yes, the strength is identical. Use the same amount your recipe calls for—no adjustments needed.
