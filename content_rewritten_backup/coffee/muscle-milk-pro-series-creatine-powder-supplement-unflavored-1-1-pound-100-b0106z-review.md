## What you actually get  

The 1.1-pound tub (500g) delivers 100 servings of 5g creatine monohydrate. That’s the clinically effective dose — no proprietary blends, no “loading phase” nonsense required. The powder is micronized (finer particles), which is why it dissolves faster than standard creatine. As shown in the product image, the tub is compact — fits in a kitchen cabinet, not a garage shelf. No scoop in my batch, which was annoying. Check yours immediately; Amazon reviewers mention this inconsistency.  

## Performance and real-world use  

I mixed a scoop into cold water, black coffee, and a chocolate protein shake. Results:  

- **Water**: Dissolved in about 20 seconds with one stir. Slight grit at the bottom if you chug it, but nothing like the sand-texture cheap creatine leaves behind.  
- **Coffee**: Zero flavor impact. That’s the point of unflavored — you taste your drink, not the supplement.  
- **Shake**: Completely invisible. No clumps, no foam.  

The micronization is the headline feature. Standard creatine requires vigorous shaking or a blender bottle; this one mixes with a regular spoon. That matters when you’re rushing to the gym at 6 AM.

## Where it falls short  

Three honest caveats:  

1. **No scoop included** — This is a manufacturing inconsistency, not a design choice. Some tubs have it, some don’t. If you get one without, use a kitchen scale (5g = 1 teaspoon, slightly heaped).  
2. **Not third-party tested** — Muscle Milk doesn’t pay for NSF or Informed Sport certification. For most lifters, that’s fine. If you’re a competing athlete subject to drug testing, this is a dealbreaker.  
3. **Unflavored is a double-edged sword** — It won’t mask bitter liquids. Mix it with juice or water, not grapefruit juice or black coffee unless you enjoy sour notes.
