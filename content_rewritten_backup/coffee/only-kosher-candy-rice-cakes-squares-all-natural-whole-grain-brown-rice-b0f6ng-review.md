Let's get one thing out of the way: these are not candy. The brand name is "Only Kosher Candy," but what you're actually getting is a bag of unflavored, whole grain brown rice cakes. If that sounds like a letdown, hold on — because 240 Amazon reviewers averaging 4.8 stars suggest there's something worth talking about here.

## What's Actually in the Bag

Each 5.3-ounce bag contains roughly 12 square rice cakes, and the ingredient list reads like a minimalist's dream: whole grain brown rice, salt. That's it. No soybean oil sprayed on for "texture," no maltodextrin, no natural flavors that are anything but natural. Each square runs about 50 calories, 1 gram of protein, and 11 grams of carbs, making them a legitimate snack for anyone tracking intake.

As shown in the product image, these are classic rice cake squares — lightly browned edges, visible grain texture, about 4 inches across. They're thinner than Quaker's classic rounds, which is actually a point in their favor for crispness.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Here's where these distinguish themselves from the grocery store generic. The crunch is genuinely satisfying — a clean snap that doesn't disintegrate into a mouthful of styrofoam. I tested these over two weeks, and they held their texture in a partially open bag (my mistake) far better than most rice cakes, which tend to go chewy and sad after a few days. The whole grain brown rice gives them a subtle nuttiness that comes through on the finish, though we're talking subtle — these are not flavor bombs.

One buyer put it well: "I was skeptical because rice cakes usually taste like eating a packing peanut. These actually have a toasty, pleasant grain flavor. My kids eat them plain, which never happened with the name brand."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The kosher certification is a genuine selling point, not just a label. Multiple reviewers mention using these for Passover and year-round because the OU Pareve designation means they can be served with meat or dairy meals. One reviewer noted: "Finally a rice cake my rabbi approved for our family's dietary needs. The texture is better than the ones from the kosher market, and they're cheaper."

The clean ingredients matter to people too. Several reviews specifically call out that there's no palm oil coating — a common trick in mass-market rice cakes that adds saturated fat just to keep seasoning stuck to the surface.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the plain flavor is *plain*. If you're used to apple cinnamon or caramel corn rice cakes, these will taste like eating a paper plate. That's not a flaw — it's the point — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Second, the packaging is cheap. The bag isn't resealable, and the cakes arrive loosely packed. Expect some breakage in transit; roughly one in five squares arrives cracked. It doesn't affect the taste, but if you're buying these for uniform presentation, you'll be disappointed.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Kosher households** looking for a versatile, certified Pareve snack that works with any meal
- **Clean-label shoppers** who want a single-ingredient-plus-salt snack with no processing tricks
- **Rice cake purists** who actually like the taste of toasted grain and want maximum crunch
- **People on calorie-restricted diets** who need a crunchy, voluminous snack at 50 calories per square

Skip this if you want flavored rice cakes, need individually wrapped portions for lunches, or don't care about kosher certification — cheaper generic options exist.

## FAQ

**Are these actually kosher?**
Yes. They carry an OU Pareve certification, verified on the packaging. Pareve means they contain no meat or dairy, so they're suitable for either type of meal under kosher dietary laws.

**How do these compare to Quaker rice cakes?**
They're thinner and crunchier, with a cleaner ingredient list. Quaker's plain variety contains whole grain brown rice and salt too, but these have a more pronounced toasted flavor and better structural integrity. The trade-off is price — these cost more per ounce.

**Can I use these for snacking with toppings?**
Absolutely. Their sturdy texture handles hummus, peanut butter, avocado, or cream cheese without breaking. Just note the squares are about 4 inches, so they're a bit smaller than standard rounds — portion your toppings accordingly.
