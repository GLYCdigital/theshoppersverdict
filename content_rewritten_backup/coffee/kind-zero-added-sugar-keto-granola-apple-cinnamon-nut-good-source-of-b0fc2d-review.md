Let's be upfront about what this isn't: KIND's Zero Added Sugar Keto Granola in Apple Cinnamon Nut is not trying to fool you into thinking you're eating a bowl of sugar-glazed granola. It's not that product. What it *is* — and does well — is a genuinely satisfying keto-friendly crunch that doesn't taste like you're being punished for your dietary choices.

As shown in the product image, you're getting a standard 10.6-ounce bag of granola clusters. But the ingredient list is where this earns its keep: almonds, pecans, pumpkin seeds, coconut, apple pieces, and a cinnamon blend held together with chicory root fiber and egg whites. No sugar alcohols pretending to be sugar. No "natural flavors" hiding suspect chemistry. At 3g net carbs and 11g protein per ⅓-cup serving, the macros hold up against serious keto competition.

**The real-world test: Does it hold up in a bowl?**

I tested this three ways: dry snacking, with unsweetened almond milk, and on top of Greek yogurt. The dry test is where the apple cinnamon shines — the apple pieces are chewy and tart, the cinnamon is warm rather than cloying, and there's a buttery note from the pecans that keeps it interesting. With almond milk, the clusters held their structure for a full five minutes before softening. That's genuinely good for a keto granola — most competitors turn to paste within two minutes. On yogurt, it adds the texture contrast you're looking for.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.0-star average across 200 ratings undersells the enthusiasm in the written feedback. One recurring theme: keto veterans who've tried every granola on the market and found this one actually passes the "would I eat this if I weren't on keto?" test. Several reviewers specifically mention the lack of that artificial sweetener bite that plagues most sugar-free granolas. One long-term buyer noted: *"I've reordered this four times. It's the only keto granola my non-keto husband will steal from the pantry."* Another praised the serving size honesty: *"The ⅓ cup serving actually looks like enough food, not a sad handful."*

**Where it falls short — honestly**

First, the price. At roughly $6 per bag, you're paying a significant premium for the keto labeling. Second, quality control on bag-to-bag consistency appears to be an issue. Multiple reviews mention receiving bags with excessive "fines" — the powdery bits that settle at the bottom and make the last quarter of the bag less enjoyable. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Great product, but my last bag was 30% crumbs. Still tasted good, but the crunch factor was gone."*

Third, the sweetener situation. KIND uses a blend of monk fruit and erythritol. Most people won't notice, but if you're sensitive to erythritol's cooling aftertaste, you'll catch it — especially when eating the granola dry by the handful.

**Who should buy this**

- **Keto dieters who miss cereal texture**: This is your best option on the market right now
- **Low-sugar households with mixed dietary needs**: It's sweet enough for non-keto eaters to enjoy
- **People who eat granola on yogurt or as a snack**: The cluster integrity holds up

**Skip it if** you're on a tight grocery budget, you're looking for a traditional sugary granola experience, or you have a known sensitivity to erythritol.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this actually keto-friendly or is it marketing?**
A: It's legit. 3g net carbs per ⅓ cup, 12g fat, 11g protein. The first ingredients are nuts and seeds, not fillers. This is one of the rare products where the keto claim survives ingredient scrutiny.

**Q: How does it compare to regular KIND granola?**
A: The regular version has about 8-9g more sugar per serving and a different texture profile. The keto version is less sweet, more savory-nutty, and the clusters are slightly denser. If you're not watching carbs, the regular version tastes more indulgent.

**Q: Does the apple flavor taste artificial?**
A: No — and that's the surprise. The apple pieces are real, and they're tart rather than candy-sweet. It tastes like baked apples with cinnamon, not like apple-flavored cereal.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're keto or low-carb and genuinely miss granola's crunch. **Skip it if** you're price-sensitive or expecting a sugar-sweetened cereal experience. For what it claims to be — a zero-added-sugar, keto-friendly granola that actually tastes good — it delivers. The quality control inconsistency on fines keeps it from a perfect score, but the taste, macros, and cluster integrity make it the best keto granola I've tested this year.
