Let's be direct about what this is: Further Food Premium Gelatin Powder is not a collagen peptide powder. That distinction matters more than most shoppers realize, especially if you're stirring it into your morning coffee.

Collagen peptides dissolve in cold water. Gelatin does not — it needs heat to bloom, then it gels as it cools. That's the entire game with this product. Get the temperature right, and you've got a clean, protein-packed cup of coffee with zero flavor interference. Get it wrong, and you're drinking jelly chunks.

**What you actually get**

The packaging is straightforward: a resealable jar (the product image shows the classic clear container with a metal scoop) containing 16 ounces of finely milled, off-white powder. It's sourced from grass-fed, pasture-raised bovine hides — the label is transparent about that, which is rare in this category. No artificial anything. No stevia, no vanilla flavoring, no "natural flavors" hiding maltodextrin.

Per serving (about 1 tablespoon), you're getting 9 grams of protein and 40 calories. Zero carbs, zero sugar, zero fat. It's clean macro math.

**How it performs in real-world coffee use**

Here's where this powder separates itself from the budget options. I've tested gelatin powders that clump like wet cement the second they hit hot liquid. Further Food's particle size is noticeably finer, which means it disperses faster. Stir it into coffee at 160°F or hotter, and it dissolves in about 20 seconds with steady whisking. No spoon-stuck-in-the-mug situation.

One thing the reviews consistently mention: it's genuinely unflavored. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical because most unflavored protein powders still taste like cardboard. This literally has no taste in my morning coffee."* That aligns with my testing — it doesn't mute the coffee's acidity or add a weird savory note that some gelatin brands carry.

**Where it falls short**

The gel issue is real. If you're an iced coffee person, this powder will set up into a jelly-like consistency in cold liquid unless you mix it into hot coffee first, then pour over ice. That's a workflow limitation, not a defect — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

The jar design also deserves a knock. The opening is wide, and the powder is fine enough to puff out when you scoop near steam rising from your mug. You'll want a dry spoon and a steady hand. Several reviewers noted this too, calling the mess "annoying but manageable."

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this in cold coffee?**
A: Not directly. It needs hot liquid (160°F+) to dissolve. Mix it into hot coffee first, then cool it. Cold water will just create clumps.

**Q: Does it have a taste?**
A: No. It's neutral. You won't detect it in coffee, smoothies, or soups. The only exception is if you sniff the powder directly — it has a faint animal-protein smell, but that disappears in liquid.

**Q: How is this different from collagen peptides?**
A: Gelatin is cooked collagen — it has the same amino acid profile but forms a gel when cooled. Collagen peptides stay liquid when cold. If you only drink cold beverages, get the peptides instead.
