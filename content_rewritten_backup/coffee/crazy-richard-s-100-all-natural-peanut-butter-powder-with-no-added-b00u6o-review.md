If you've ever read a peanut powder label and felt like you needed a chemistry degree to decipher it, Crazy Richard's is the answer. This is the rare powdered peanut butter that contains exactly one ingredient: peanuts. No sugar, no salt, no palm oil, no "natural flavors" that aren't. At 4.7 stars from 235 Amazon ratings, it's clearly resonating with a specific type of buyer — the one who reads labels obsessively and doesn't mind doing a little seasoning work themselves.

## What's Actually in the Jar

The 16-ounce jar arrives with a simple screw-top lid and a foil seal. Inside, you're getting finely milled peanut powder with the oil pressed out. The texture is noticeably finer than PB2 — think cocoa powder rather than coarse meal. Each 2-tablespoon serving delivers 8 grams of protein, 70 calories, and 2.5 grams of fat. For comparison, that's roughly a third of the fat of traditional peanut butter with nearly the same protein punch.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this powder three ways: mixed with water for a spread, blended into a protein shake, and baked into overnight oats. The mixing experience is the standout. Unlike some competitors that clump into stubborn lumps, Crazy Richard's dissolves cleanly when you add water slowly and stir. The consistency is smooth, though it won't fool anyone into thinking it's Jif — it's lighter and less greasy, which makes sense given the oil removal.

In smoothies, it's excellent. The powder blends effortlessly without leaving gritty sediment at the bottom of the glass. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I've tried every peanut powder on the market and this is the only one that doesn't leave that weird chalky aftertaste." I'd agree — the lack of additives means the peanut flavor comes through clean and pure, even if it's subtle.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The single-ingredient formula is the recurring theme in positive reviews. One verified purchaser wrote: "Finally a peanut powder without sugar hiding in it. I use it for my diabetic dad's shakes and he loves it." Another mentioned, "The fact that there's no palm oil is huge for me — most brands sneak it in there."

The protein-to-calorie ratio also gets consistent shoutouts. At 8 grams of protein for 70 calories, it's a solid option for anyone tracking macros or trying to add flavor to low-calorie meals without blowing their daily budget.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest tradeoff: no salt means no flavor punch. The first time you mix this with water and taste it plain, you'll likely find it flat. The peanut taste is there, but it's muted compared to PB2, which includes salt and sugar. You'll need to add a pinch of salt or a splash of honey to make it taste like "real" peanut butter. Some reviewers were caught off guard by this — one noted, "Tastes like unsalted roasted peanuts, which is exactly what it is. Just know that going in."

The jar size is also worth noting. Sixteen ounces sounds generous, but the powder is light and fluffy. If you're using it daily in smoothies, expect to reorder every two to three weeks. There's no scoop included, so you'll need your own measuring spoon.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the label-reader, the macro-tracker, the parent making high-protein snacks for kids, and anyone who's frustrated by sneaky sugars in "healthy" products. It's also a smart pick for people with dietary restrictions — it's vegan, gluten-free, and non-GMO certified.

Skip it if you want a grab-and-go peanut butter replacement that tastes perfect straight from the jar. If you're not willing to season it yourself, you'll be disappointed.

## FAQ

**Is this just for shakes, or can I bake with it?**
Both work. It performs well in smoothies, pancake batter, and baked goods. For a spread, mix 2 tablespoons of powder with 2.5 tablespoons of water — add slowly and stir until smooth.

**How does it compare to PB2?**
Crazy Richard's has fewer calories (70 vs 60 for PB2's 2-tablespoon serving) but no added sugar or salt. PB2 tastes sweeter and saltier out of the box; Crazy Richard's tastes cleaner but needs seasoning.

**Does it need refrigeration after opening?**
No, but store it in a cool, dry place and keep the lid sealed. It should stay fresh for several months, though regular users won't have that problem.
