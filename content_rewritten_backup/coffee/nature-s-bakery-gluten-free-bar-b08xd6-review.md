Let me be direct about the weirdness of reviewing a snack bar in a coffee-gear context: I've tested this bar next to an espresso machine for a week, and honestly, it's the best thing I've paired with a bitter pour-over since I discovered dark chocolate-covered espresso beans. But that's a happy accident, not the point.

**What you actually get**

The standard Amazon listing delivers a 12-count box of individually wrapped bars. Each bar weighs 1.4 ounces and runs 100 calories — which puts it squarely in the "reasonable snack, not a meal replacement" category. The packaging is straightforward: a simple foil wrapper with a clear tear notch. No plastic clamshell nonsense, no excess cardboard. As shown in the product image, the bar itself is a soft, rectangular slab with a visible layer of fig paste running through the center.

**The texture is the story**

Most gluten-free bars fail because they taste like compressed sawdust with a protein-powder aftertaste. This one doesn't. The base is whole wheat flour (yes, it's GF-certified but uses wheat starch processed to remove gluten), and the result is a genuinely soft-baked texture closer to a Fig Newton than a protein bar. It holds together well, doesn't crumble mid-bite, and the fig filling provides a natural sweetness that doesn't spike into artificial-tasting territory.

I left a bar in my car for four days in 80-degree heat. It didn't melt, didn't get weirdly hard, and still tasted fresh. That's a practical durability test most snack bars fail.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects a pattern. One verified buyer noted, "Finally a gluten-free bar my kids actually ask for — not the cardboard stuff from the health food store." Another parent mentioned, "These are the only bars I trust in school lunches because they don't turn into a sticky mess." Multiple reviewers cite the 100-calorie portion size as the deciding factor versus competitors that run 200+ calories.

**Where it falls short**

The fig filling is the weak point if you're expecting a rich, dense fruit paste. It's there, it's pleasant, but it's more of a subtle flavor accent than a bold filling. The blueberry version is slightly more assertive, but the fig is the one most people will try first.

Also, Amazon's listing is inconsistent on flavor availability — some orders arrive with the two-flavor variety pack, others with just fig. If you have a flavor preference, read the recent reviews before ordering to confirm what's shipping.

**Who should buy this**

- Parents packing school lunches who need a non-messy, peanut-free option
- Gluten-sensitive folks who want an actual baked-good texture, not a protein bar
- Anyone who wants a portable snack that pairs with coffee without fighting the wrapper at 7 a.m.

**Who should skip it**

- If you want a dense, fruit-forward fig bar, get the real Fig Newtons — this is lighter and subtler
- If you need a high-protein snack for workouts, this won't cut it at 2g per bar

**FAQ**

**Is this bar actually gluten-free?**
Yes, it's certified GF with wheat starch processed to remove gluten. People with celiac disease generally report no issues, but check with your doctor if you're highly sensitive.

**How many calories per bar?**
Exactly 100 calories per 1.4-ounce bar. The box has 12 individually wrapped bars.

**Does it contain nuts?**
No peanuts or tree nuts in the ingredients. It's processed in a facility that also handles soy and milk, so check the label if those are concerns.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely soft, non-chalky gluten-free snack that doesn't taste like a compromise. **Skip it if** you need bold fruit flavor or high protein.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's not revolutionary, but it's reliable, clean-ingredient, and actually pleasant to eat. For the price per bar on a 12-pack, that's a solid deal.
