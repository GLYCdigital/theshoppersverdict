There are two kinds of French press owners: people who love the rich, full-bodied brew, and people who are tired of chewing their coffee.

If you've been in the second camp — staring into a cup that looks more like muddy river water than morning coffee — the ESPRO Light P3 is the solution you didn't know existed. It solves the one problem that has plagued French press design for over a century: the grit.

**The filter is the whole story here.** Most French presses use a single stainless steel mesh that traps the biggest grounds but lets fine silt through. ESPRO's patented double micro-filter stacks a BPA-free plastic mesh with an ultra-fine stainless steel screen that's 12x finer than standard designs. The result is coffee that tastes like French press — full-bodied, rich, oily — without the muddy bottom.

One reviewer puts it plainly: *"I opted to try a French Press for its simplicity...no electricity, no motherboard to fail, no grinder to stop grinding, & no high-cost outlay. I chose Espro based upon Wirecutter's recommendation."* Another confirmed: *"The bitter undertones and acidic undertones were gone. I could taste the difference right away!"*

**But here's what nobody mentions in the product video:** the double filter basket is massive. It takes up so much internal volume that you can only fill the carafe about 3/4 of the way. One frustrated (but honest) reviewer went from making 3-4 cups to barely 2: *"I took 2 stars away because of the amount of coffee this press makes vs others. The double basket is so large it takes up the space of a single cup of coffee."*

That's the trade-off. Grit-free brewing costs you capacity.

**Build quality is genuinely good.** The borosilicate glass is 40% thicker than standard French press glass, which means it handles temperature changes better — pour boiling water in without worrying about shattering. The plastic base and plunger mechanism feel solid, not cheap. And everything except the filter mesh is top-rack dishwasher safe.

**The learning curve is short but real.** You'll want a medium-coarse grind and about 5 minutes of steeping time before plunging. One reviewer figured out: *"Turns out 5 minutes works best for bold coffee."* Too fine a grind and the filters work harder than they need to; too coarse and you leave flavor on the table.

**Who is this for?** People who love the depth of French press coffee but hate the sediment. People who've burned through three expensive electric coffee makers in two years and want something that can't break. People who don't mind a slightly more involved cleaning routine for a genuinely better cup.

At $51.69, the ESPRO P3 is not cheap for a French press. But it's the last French press you'll buy — not because it's indestructible, but because it finally does what French press coffee was always supposed to do: taste rich, not gritty.
