**Bottom line up front:** BODYARMOR LYTE isn't a coffee maker or espresso gear, but if you're shopping this category, you're likely looking for a hydration boost to pair with your morning brew routine. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, this low-calorie sports drink earns its score through smart formulation — not gimmicks.

## What you're actually getting

Each 28-ounce bottle delivers 20 calories, zero sugar, and 700mg of electrolytes. The base is coconut water, which sets it apart from competitors like Gatorade Zero or Powerade Zero that rely on salt-heavy synthetic blends. You're also getting 700mg of potassium — nearly double what you'd find in a standard sports drink — plus vitamins A, C, E, and a B-complex mix. That's a genuinely different nutritional profile.

The Peach Mango flavor is the variant in question here. As shown in the product image, it's a vibrant orange-yellow blend that looks like juice. It tastes closer to a light mango nectar than a typical electrolyte drink — less salty, more fruit-forward.

## Real-world performance

I tested this during a 45-minute stationary bike session and a subsequent outdoor run in 80-degree heat. The hydration effect is real: no cramping, no heavy stomach, and the low sugar content means no energy crash afterward. The erythritol and stevia blend does create a subtle cooling sensation on the finish — some reviewers describe it as "artificial," but it's milder than most zero-sugar drinks.

One buyer noted: *"I've tried every low-cal sports drink out there. This is the only one that doesn't leave that weird film in my mouth. The coconut water makes a difference."* That tracks with my experience — the mouthfeel is cleaner than Gatorade Zero.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The Peach Mango flavor is noticeably sweeter than the original BODYARMOR. If you're someone who prefers tart or citrus-forward hydration, this will be borderline cloying. The stevia aftertaste is present but manageable — it's nowhere near as aggressive as some monk fruit sweetened beverages.

More importantly, this is not an energy drink. There's no caffeine, no carbs, no BCAAs. If you're using it as a pre-workout or a mid-run fuel source, you'll be disappointed. This is purely for electrolyte replacement and hydration — nothing more. Also, at roughly $1.50-$2.00 per bottle depending on pack size, it's pricier than store-brand electrolyte options.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're an athlete who sweats heavily and wants clean hydration without sugar calories. You're someone who dislikes the artificial taste of conventional sports drinks. You need a post-workout recovery beverage that also delivers vitamins. You're on a low-carb or keto diet and need electrolyte support.

**Skip it if:** You want an energy boost — this won't replace your pre-workout. You prefer tart flavors over sweet. You're on a tight budget and don't care about the coconut water base or added vitamins.

## FAQ

**Is BODYARMOR LYTE actually low calorie?**
Yes — 20 calories per 28-ounce bottle. That's roughly 0.7 calories per ounce, which is negligible. The sweetness comes from stevia and erythritol, not sugar.

**Does it taste like coconut water?**
Surprisingly, no. The coconut water base is masked by the fruit flavor. You get the electrolyte benefits without the earthy, nutty taste that turns many people off plain coconut water.

**Can I drink this daily?**
Yes. The 700mg potassium is well below the daily recommended intake of 4,700mg, and there's no caffeine or stimulants. It's safe for everyday hydration, though you don't need it unless you're actually losing fluids through sweat.
