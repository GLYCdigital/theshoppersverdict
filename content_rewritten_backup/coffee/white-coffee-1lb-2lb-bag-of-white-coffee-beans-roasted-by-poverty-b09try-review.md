Let's be blunt about what white coffee actually is: it's not a new trendy variety. It's a coffee bean roasted at roughly 325°F instead of the usual 450-480°F, stopped almost immediately after the first crack. The result looks like pale, dense pebbles — not the oily brown beans you're used to. Poverty Roasters has been doing this longer than most, and their 1-2lb bag option is the one I keep coming back to.

## What You Actually Get

The bag arrives whole-bean, not ground. You choose between 1lb and 2lb sizes. Inside, the beans are strikingly light — almost beige or khaki-colored. They're also noticeably heavier than normal roasted beans of the same volume. That's not a defect; it's the density of under-roasted coffee. The aroma out of the bag is more like roasted nuts or grains than coffee. Don't expect the chocolatey, caramel notes of a medium roast — this is a completely different animal.

## Performance and Brewing Reality

Here's where things get specific. **You need a serious grinder.** These beans are hard — some buyers describe them as "like small rocks." A blade grinder will produce uneven chunks and dust. Even entry-level burr grinders can struggle. If you're using a hand grinder, prepare for an arm workout. This is a genuine barrier for casual coffee drinkers.

Once ground, brewing requires precision. White coffee extracts much slower than dark roasts. Pour-over and French press work best with a slightly finer grind and cooler water — around 195-200°F rather than boiling. The flavor that comes through is nutty, almost like roasted almonds or macadamia, with a hint of sweetness. There's zero bitterness, which is a revelation if you've been choking down over-roasted supermarket beans.

The caffeine kick is the headline. Because the beans aren't roasted long enough to break down caffeine molecules, you're getting close to triple the caffeine of a dark roast. One cup in the morning genuinely replaces your usual two or three.

## Where It Falls Short

The grinding requirement is the biggest hurdle. Budget another $50-100 for a capable burr grinder if you don't already own one. The brewing curve is real — your first two or three attempts will likely be weak or chalky. Also, the flavor is genuinely unusual. Some people love it; some find it closer to tea or barley water than coffee. And if you're caffeine-sensitive, this will wreck your sleep. Respect the dosage.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I drink this straight without grinding?**
No. These beans are too dense to chew or use whole. You must grind them.

**Q: Does white coffee taste like regular coffee?**
Not really. It's nutty, sweet, and grain-like. Think roasted barley tea crossed with light coffee undertones.

**Q: Is the caffeine actually triple?**
Lab testing consistently shows 2.5-3x the caffeine of typical dark roasts. It's not marketing hype — but start with half a cup.
