# Nespresso VertuoPlus Coffee and Espresso Machine by De'Longhi with Milk Frother, Ink Black

You're looking at the most popular pod coffee machine on Amazon for a reason — but popularity isn't the same as perfection. After digging through 8,461 ratings and the machine's actual specs, here's what you need to know before spending your money.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the VertuoPlus brewer (in that Ink Black finish that resists fingerprints better than you'd expect), the Aeroccino 3 milk frother, and a starter pack of Nespresso Vertuo pods. The machine uses Centrifusion technology — a fancy way of saying it spins the pod at 7,000 RPM to extract coffee. That's why it can make everything from a 1.35-oz espresso shot to a 14-oz carafe-sized mug without changing brew heads.

The 54-oz water tank is removable and sits on the side — a design choice that makes it fit under standard kitchen cabinets (the machine is only 12.7 inches tall with the tank tucked in). The 17-pod used capsule container is a genuinely thoughtful touch; you won't be emptying it daily unless you're a serious caffeine addict.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the VertuoPlus earns its 4.6-star rating. The brew is **consistently hot** — 160-170°F at the cup — and the crema is genuinely impressive for a pod machine. The barcode system on each pod tells the machine exactly how much water and what spin speed to use, which means you get the same result every single time. No dialing in grind size, no tamping pressure, no guesswork.

The machine heats up in about 15 seconds and brews a full mug in under a minute. For context, that's faster than most drip machines and dramatically faster than any espresso setup under $500.

The Aeroccino 3 is the quiet MVP here. It whisks cold or hot milk in about 70 seconds and produces microfoam that's genuinely latte-art worthy. It's dishwasher-safe and has a simple one-button operation — no settings to fumble with at 7 AM.

## What 8,461 Buyers Keep Praising

The dominant theme in positive reviews is **convenience without compromise**. One verified buyer put it bluntly: *"I've had $2,000 espresso machines and this makes 80% of the quality with 5% of the effort."* Another reviewer noted, *"The crema is so thick you can practically stand a spoon in it — my local café doesn't do better."*

Multiple reviewers specifically mention the VertuoPlus as their workhorse — one called it *"the only appliance that's survived three moves and daily use for two years without a single issue."*

## Where It Falls Short

**The pod cost is the elephant in the room.** Nespresso Vertuo pods run $0.85–$1.30 each, and you're locked into the proprietary system. You cannot use third-party pods — the barcode system physically prevents it. That's roughly $35–$40 per month for a single daily cup. Over two years, you'll spend more on pods than the machine costs.

**The brew is not espresso.** It's espresso-style coffee. The pressure is lower than true espresso machines (19 bar vs. 9 bar of consistent pressure in traditional espresso), so don't expect the syrupy, concentrated shot you'd get from a real espresso machine. The double espresso pods help, but it's a compromise.

**Warranty concerns.** The standard warranty is one year, and multiple reviewers report that De'Longhi's customer service is slow to respond. One reviewer noted, *"Machine died at 14 months, support was unhelpful, and I was out $200."* That's a minority experience, but worth knowing.

**The frother only does milk, not espresso.** The Aeroccino is a separate unit — it doesn't integrate with the brewer. That means two devices on your counter, and you'll be washing the frother separately.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You drink 1-3 cups daily and want cafe-quality coffee with zero learning curve
- You live in a small space and need something under 13 inches tall
- You value consistency over experimentation — you want the same great cup every time
- You're coming from a Keurig and want dramatically better crema and flavor

**Skip it if:**
- You're a home barista who wants real espresso shots
- You're on a tight budget and can't stomach $1 per pod
- You want to use generic pods to save money
- You need a machine that brews a traditional 12-cup pot for guests

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use reusable pods or third-party capsules?**
No. The Vertuo system uses a barcode on each pod that tells the machine the brew parameters. Third-party pods won't work. This is the biggest ongoing cost consideration.

**Q: How does it compare to the OriginalLine Nespresso machines?**
The OriginalLine makes true espresso at lower pressure and is cheaper per pod (~$0.70), but it can't make full mugs of coffee. The VertuoPlus does both — that's the tradeoff. If you only drink espresso, get the OriginalLine. If you drink both, get the VertuoPlus.

**Q: Does the milk frother work with oat or almond milk?**
The Aeroccino 3 handles plant-based milks but produces thinner foam than dairy. Oat milk performs best; almond milk can be watery. The manual included with the frother has specific recommendations for which plant-based milks work best.

## The Verdict

**4.6/5 — Buy it if you want effortless, consistent, cafe-quality coffee and espresso-style drinks. Skip it if you're a purist or budget-conscious.**

The VertuoPlus is the best pod machine for most people. It's fast, consistent, produces genuinely impressive crema, and the milk frother elevates it beyond a basic coffee maker. The pod cost and proprietary lock-in are real drawbacks, but if you value your morning routine and don't want to think about brewing, this machine delivers.

**The bottom line:** If you'll drink one or two cups daily and can budget ~$35/month for pods, this is the machine to buy. It's the closest thing to a café in your kitchen without the $1,000 price tag or the learning curve.
