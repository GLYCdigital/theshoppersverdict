Let's be honest about keto snacks: most of them taste like cardboard dusted with sadness. So when a cheddar and onion cracker shows up with a 3.8-star average across 190 ratings, it's worth investigating. Is this the cracker that finally scratches the crunch itch without blowing your macros? Partially — with some real caveats.

**What You Actually Get**

The product image shows a standard stand-up pouch, and that's what arrives. Inside is a modest portion of round, seeded crackers that look more like a health-food store find than a mainstream snack. Each serving clocks in around 1g net carbs, which is genuinely impressive for a cracker that doesn't taste like pure almond flour dust. The ingredient list leans on seeds and cheese — no maltitol, no sugar alcohols, which is a win for anyone whose digestive system rebels against fake sweeteners.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

Here's where things get interesting. These crackers have a serious crunch — the kind that makes you check the bag to confirm you're eating something keto-approved. They hold up remarkably well to dips. I tested them with a thick spinach artichoke dip and a chunky salsa; neither turned them to mush within the first minute, which is more than I can say for some mainstream low-carb options that dissolve on contact.

The flavor is genuinely cheddar-forward with a noticeable onion kick. One reviewer put it plainly: "Finally a keto cracker that doesn't taste like I'm chewing on sawdust. The cheddar is real, the onion is sharp, and the crunch makes me forget I'm eating 'healthy.'" That sentiment echoes through the positive reviews.

But here's the tradeoff. The texture is dense — think a cross between a flax cracker and a wheat thin, but heavier. If you're expecting something light and flaky like a Cheez-It, you'll be disappointed. Several reviews mention this exact issue. One buyer wrote: "Crunchy but almost too hard. It's like eating a hockey puck that tastes good." That's not entirely fair, but it captures the density.

**Where It Falls Short**

Portion control is the real enemy here. The bag is smaller than you'd expect for the price point, and these crackers are dangerously snackable. You'll tell yourself you're having a reasonable serving, then look down and realize you've eaten a third of the bag. At roughly 130 calories for that portion, it's not catastrophic, but it adds up.

The onion aftertaste is also divisive. Some buyers love the sharpness; others find it cloying. One reviewer noted: "The onion flavor is aggressive. It lingers for a while after you finish. If you're not an onion person, skip these." That's fair warning.

**Who Should Buy These**

These crackers are for the keto veteran who's tired of pork rinds and cheese crisps. If you meal-prep and want something to scoop up chicken salad or guacamole, these work. They're also solid for charcuterie boards where you need a sturdy low-carb vehicle that won't crumble under a heavy topping.

They're not for anyone expecting a Cheez-It dupe. If you want light, airy, buttery crackers, these will disappoint. And if you're price-sensitive about snack foods, the cost-per-ounce will likely give you pause.

**FAQ**

*How do these compare to other keto crackers?*
They're crunchier and more substantial than most almond flour crackers, which tend to crumble. But they're also denser and heavier. Flavor-wise, the cheddar and onion combo is more distinctive than the generic "everything" seasoning most competitors use.

*Are these actually keto-friendly?*
Yes — roughly 1g net carbs per serving, no sugar alcohols, and the fat-to-protein ratio supports a standard keto macro split. Just watch portion sizes.

*Do they taste like real crackers?*
They taste like a good seed-based cracker with real cheddar flavor. If you're expecting Ritz or Saltines, no. If you're expecting a better version of those "healthy" seeded crackers from the natural foods aisle, yes.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're on keto, want a genuinely crunchy cracker that won't wreck your macros, and can tolerate a dense, seed-forward texture.

**Skip it if:** You want something light and buttery, or you're price-sensitive about snack foods.

These are a solid 3.7 out of 5 — good enough to repurchase if you know what you're getting, but not a revolutionary snack. The crunch and clean ingredients earn the points; the density and small bag cost them the rest.
