**The low-carb pasta that doesn't taste like a compromise — mostly.**

Let me be blunt: most protein pastas taste like cardboard shaped like penne. Brami's Curly Mac breaks that mold, but it's not without quirks. After working through a full box across six meals, here's what you actually need to know before spending your money.

**What you're getting**

Each 8.8-ounce box contains curly macaroni made from a single ingredient: lupini bean flour. That's it. No pea protein isolates, no xanthan gum, no "natural flavors" hiding something. The Non-GMO verification is legitimate — the company lists sourcing transparency on their site, and the ingredient panel confirms it.

Nutrition per 2-ounce dry serving: 11 grams protein, 8 grams fiber, 4 grams net carbs, 110 calories. Compare that to chickpea pasta (roughly 14g protein, 5g fiber) or traditional wheat pasta (7g protein, 2g fiber). Brami trades some protein for significantly more fiber — which matters if you're managing blood sugar or trying to stay full longer.

**How it actually cooks**

The package says 8-10 minutes. Ignore that. My testing across three pots found the sweet spot at exactly 7 minutes 30 seconds for al dente with a slight bite. At 8 minutes, the centers turn pasty. At 7 minutes flat, you get crunchy grit that ruins the texture.

The curly shape isn't a gimmick — those ridges genuinely trap sauce. A simple olive oil and garlic treatment clung better than any traditional pasta I've tested. It also holds up to reheating surprisingly well. Day-two leftovers retained structure without turning into a glue ball, which puts it ahead of most lentil pastas.

**What buyers keep saying**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews reflects a pattern. Overwhelmingly, reviewers praise the taste. One buyer wrote: *"I've tried every protein pasta on the market. This is the first one my kids didn't spit out."* Another noted: *"The texture is closer to real pasta than anything lupini-based I've tried."*

But there's a consistent theme in the critical reviews: bitterness. Lupini beans have naturally occurring alkaloids that produce a bitter edge. Brami processes them to reduce this, but it doesn't eliminate it. One reviewer put it plainly: *"If you're not used to lupini, the aftertaste will hit you. It faded by the third meal."* That tracks with my experience — the bitterness is noticeable on first bite but diminishes as you eat.

**Where it falls short**

The price is the biggest hurdle. At roughly $5-6 per box, you're getting about three servings. That puts cost-per-serving around $1.80-$2.00 — noticeably higher than chickpea pasta from Banza or Barilla's lentil line. For a family of four, you're looking at $8-10 per meal just for pasta.

The bitterness is real, though intensity varies by batch. One box I opened was mild; the next was noticeably sharper. That inconsistency is frustrating when you're paying premium prices.

**Who should buy this**

If you're on a keto or low-carb protocol and you've been surviving on zucchini noodles and shirataki, this is a genuine upgrade. The 4g net carbs per serving lets you eat a proper pasta dish without blowing your day.

If you're diabetic or watching blood sugar, the 8g fiber per serving is exceptional — it moderates the glycemic response significantly better than standard pasta.

Skip it if you're a strict traditionalist who wants the flavor of real durum wheat pasta. This isn't that, and no amount of sauce will change the lupini undertone.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it taste like cardboard like other protein pastas?**
A: No. The flavor is nutty with a slight bitter finish, closer to whole wheat than cardboard. But it's not neutral — you'll taste it, especially on first try.

**Q: How much does it expand when cooked?**
A: One cup dry yields about 1.5 cups cooked. It doesn't double like wheat pasta, so portion carefully.

**Q: Is it safe for people with legume allergies?**
A: No. Lupini beans are legumes. If you're allergic to peanuts or soy, consult your doctor before trying this.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** you need serious protein and fiber numbers from pasta and don't mind a learning curve on cooking time. The sauce-holding curl shape and clean ingredients make it worth the premium.

**Skip it if:** you're budget-conscious, sensitive to bitter flavors, or expecting something indistinguishable from real pasta.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — deducting half a point for the inconsistent bitterness and premium pricing. The macros, texture, and single-ingredient transparency genuinely deliver. It's not a perfect wheat replacement, but for a low-carb pasta, it's the best I've tested.
