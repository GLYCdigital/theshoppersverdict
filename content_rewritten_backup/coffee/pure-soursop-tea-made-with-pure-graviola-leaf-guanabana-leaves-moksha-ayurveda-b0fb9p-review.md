**The soursop tea you see on Instagram is usually a sugary fruit blend. This is not that.** Moksha Ayurveda's Pure Soursop Tea is exactly what the label claims: just graviola leaf (Annona muricata), nothing else. That's either a relief or a rude awakening, depending on what you're after.

If you're shopping for the documented wellness benefits of graviola — traditionally used for its antioxidant properties and as a calming nighttime beverage — you want the leaf, not the flavoring. This delivers that. If you expect a tropical, pineapple-adjacent taste, prepare to recalibrate your expectations.

### What You Actually Get

The pouch contains 100 tea bags, each filled with visibly whole and crushed graviola leaves. As shown in the product image, the contents are a uniform deep green with visible leaf structure — not a fine powder, which is a good sign. Powdered botanicals often lose potency and can indicate lower-quality sourcing. These are recognizable leaves.

Each bag weighs in at a standard 1.5–2 grams. One pouch at roughly 200 grams total gives you a solid 3-month supply if you drink one cup daily, which is the common usage pattern for graviola tea.

### Performance and Real-World Brewing

Here's where the 4.6-star rating makes sense. The tea steeps into a pale amber-green liquor with a distinctly earthy, slightly bitter profile. It's reminiscent of a strong green tea crossed with mate — but without the caffeine jolt. Most reviewers describe it as "calming" and "soothing," which aligns with traditional use.

A consistent praise point in the 229 ratings is the strength and re-steepability. One buyer noted, *"I steep the same bag twice and it's still flavorful the second time around."* That matches my experience: the second steep is milder but not watery. For the price per cup, this is genuinely economical.

**The catch:** there's no steeping guidance on the label. If you leave the bag in for 8+ minutes, you'll get a puckering bitterness that turns first-time drinkers off. The sweet spot is 4–5 minutes at just-below-boiling water (around 200°F). Strain promptly or remove the bag.

### What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Purity and sourcing:** Multiple reviewers specifically mention appreciating that it's "single-ingredient with no fillers." The brand's Ayurvedic positioning appears credible to buyers, not gimmicky.
- **Calming effect:** A recurring theme is using it in the evening. One reviewer wrote, *"I've replaced my nighttime tea with this — it helps me wind down without the caffeine crash."*
- **Value:** The 100-bag count is repeatedly called out as excellent value. At roughly 20 cents per cup, it's cheaper than most grocery-store herbal teas.

### Where It Falls Short

**The bitterness is real.** If you're not accustomed to unsweetened herbal teas, this will be a challenge. It's not "sweet" or "fruity" — descriptors you'll see on some competing soursop blends that actually contain added flavoring. Read the label carefully; this is leaf tea, not fruit tea.

**The grit issue.** Because the leaves are crushed rather than powdered, small particles escape the bag and settle at the bottom. The last quarter-inch of your cup will have sediment. It's harmless, but if that bothers you, use a fine-mesh strainer when pouring.

**No dosing guidance.** For a botanical with documented bioactive compounds, the lack of any usage instructions or serving-size recommendations is a minor oversight. If you're taking medication or have health conditions, consult a healthcare provider before regular use — this is a potent botanical, not a flavor tea.

### Who Should Buy This

- **Wellness-focused tea drinkers** who want a caffeine-free, single-ingredient botanical beverage for evening relaxation.
- **Soursop/guanabana enthusiasts** who know the difference between leaf tea and fruit-flavored blends.
- **Budget-conscious herbal tea users** who want a 3-month supply without repeated ordering.

**Skip it if** you want a sweet, fruity tea, if you're pregnant or nursing (graviola is contraindicated), or if you're sensitive to bitter flavors.

### FAQ

**Q: Does this taste like soursop fruit?**
No. The fruit is sweet and tropical; the leaf is earthy, slightly bitter, and grassy. They share a botanical source but taste nothing alike. If you want fruit-flavored soursop tea, look for a blend with added flavoring — this is not that.

**Q: Is it caffeinated?**
No. Graviola leaf contains no caffeine. It's often used as a calming evening beverage.

**Q: Can I drink this daily?**
Most users report drinking 1–2 cups daily without issues. However, graviola has documented bioactive properties — start with one cup daily, monitor how you feel, and consult your doctor if you take blood pressure medication or have Parkinson's-related conditions, as graviola has known interactions.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a pure, unadulterated graviola leaf tea at a fair price, and you're comfortable with a bold, earthy flavor. The quality-to-price ratio is excellent, and the 100-bag count makes it a workhorse pantry staple.

**Skip it if** you need a sweet, approachable herbal tea or want steeping instructions handed to you. This is a no-frills botanical product that assumes some familiarity.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the missing brewing guidance and the sediment issue. The purity, value, and potency earn the rest.
