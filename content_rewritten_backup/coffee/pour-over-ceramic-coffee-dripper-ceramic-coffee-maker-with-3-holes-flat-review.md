Look, you don't need a $200 espresso machine to drink better coffee. This $14 ceramic dripper proves it. The Pour Over Ceramic Coffee Dripper is a flat-bottom, three-hole cone that mimics the geometry of a $40 Kalita Wave at a fraction of the price. And for most home brewers, it'll be the last pour-over device you buy.

## What's actually in the box

You get the ceramic dripper itself — nothing else. No carafe, no filters, no scoop. That's fine at this price point, but know that you'll need #2 cone filters (Melitta or store-brand work) and a mug with a rim diameter under about 3.5 inches. The dripper sits flush on standard coffee mugs; the wide base shown in the product image also fits most travel mugs and camping cups.

## Build quality and real-world brewing

The porcelain is thick and glazed smoothly inside and out. It feels substantial — not flimsy like some cheap ceramic knockoffs. The flat bottom has three small holes arranged in a line, which slows the flow compared to a single-hole cone. That's the point: slower extraction, sweeter cup, less bitterness.

In practice, this dripper shines with medium roasts. You'll want to grind slightly coarser than drip — think sea salt texture — and pour in slow circles. The 3-hole design self-levels the coffee bed reasonably well, so you don't need to be a pour-over perfectionist. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I was skeptical at this price, but it brews a noticeably cleaner cup than my old plastic Melitta cone. No more channeling or sour hits."

The ceramic retains heat during brewing, which keeps the slurry at a consistent temperature. That's a real advantage over glass or plastic, and it's why this dripper punches above its price in terms of cup quality.

## Where it falls short

Honestly, there are three things to know before you buy.

First, there are no measurement markings inside the cone. If you're new to pour-over, you'll need a digital scale to nail your ratio — this dripper won't hold your hand.

Second, the drip rate is genuinely slow. A full 12-ounce mug takes 3–4 minutes of active pouring, not counting the bloom. If you're in a rush, this isn't your brewer.

Third, it's ceramic. That's a plus for heat retention but a minus for travel. It's heavy and breakable. One reviewer noted, "I bought this for camping and cracked it on the second trip. It's a home brewer, not a backpacking one."

## Who should buy this

- **Home brewers** who want better coffee without buying a $60 metal dripper — this is your upgrade path.
- **Office workers** who want a quiet, no-electricity brew at their desk. It fits on a standard mug and rinses clean in seconds.
- **Campers** who drive to their site and don't mind weight — just pack it in a padded bag.
