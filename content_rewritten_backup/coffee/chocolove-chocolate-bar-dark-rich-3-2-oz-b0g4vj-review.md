**The 3.2 oz bar that punches above its price point—but has a packaging flaw that might trip you up.**

Let’s be honest: most grocery store dark chocolate tastes like waxy cardboard dipped in sugar. The Chocolove Dark Rich bar is not that. This 3.2 oz slab sits in a sweet spot—literally and figuratively—between mass-market candy and boutique bean-to-bar offerings. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, the consensus is clear: this is the bar you grab when you want something genuinely satisfying without needing a loan.

Here’s the breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The package contains one 3.2 oz (90g) bar. That’s slightly larger than the standard 3 oz bars from Lindt or Ghirardelli. The bar is divided into thick, rectangular segments—not the thin, brittle squares you find on cheaper brands. As shown in the product image, the chocolate is a deep, glossy brown with a clean snap when you break it. There’s no fancy outer sleeve or gimmicky flavor inclusions here. Just chocolate.

**Performance and real-world use**

The first thing you notice is the texture. This bar melts at a slow, creamy pace—not instant like waxy chocolate, but not stubbornly hard either. The 60% cacao content is the smart choice for most palates. It’s dark enough to feel sophisticated, but the cocoa butter content keeps the bitterness in check. You get a distinct flavor profile: initial notes of roasted cocoa, followed by a subtle, almost fruity finish that lingers without being cloying.

I tested this in three scenarios: eating it straight, melting it for drizzling over espresso, and chopping it into cookies. For melting, it behaves beautifully—smooth and glossy without seizing. For baking, the flavor holds up well against sugar and flour, which is rare for a bar at this price point.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Digging through the Amazon reviews, a few themes dominate. First, the texture. One reviewer noted, *“This is the smoothest dark chocolate I’ve had under $5. It doesn’t have that chalky aftertaste.”* Another said, *“The snap is perfect—loud enough to know it’s fresh.”* The size also gets consistent shout-outs. Multiple buyers mentioned that the 3.2 oz bar is big enough to share (or to hoard) without feeling shortchanged.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest flaw is packaging clarity. The front label emphasizes “Dark Rich” but doesn’t prominently display the cacao percentage or origin. If you’re blind-buying, you might mistake this for a 70% or 85% bar—it’s neither. It’s 60%, which is a notable difference in bitterness. Also, because this bar has a higher cocoa butter ratio than some premium single-origin bars, it’s slightly softer at room temperature. If you store it in a warm pantry, it can develop a slight bloom (white streaks) faster than harder, lower-fat bars.

**Who should buy this**

- **The espresso drinker:** This is your ideal pairing. The 60% cacao cuts through the acidity of a dark roast without overpowering it.
- **The baker:** If you need a reliable melting chocolate that won’t break your budget, this is it.
- **The skeptic:** If you think all dark chocolate is bitter, this will convert you.

**Skip it if** you’re hunting for a 70%+ high-cacao bar, or if you strictly prefer single-origin chocolate with a distinct terroir. This is a blended bar, not a vintage wine.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this bar vegan?**
A: Yes. The ingredients are cocoa liquor, sugar, cocoa butter, and vanilla. No milk solids. Check the label for potential cross-contamination if you have severe allergies.

**Q: How does it compare to Lindt 70%?**
A: It’s smoother and less bitter. Lindt has a sharper, more aggressive flavor. Chocolove is more approachable, especially for snacking.

**Q: Does it contain soy lecithin?**
A: No. This bar uses sunflower lecithin as an emulsifier, which is a plus for those avoiding soy.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a high-quality, versatile dark chocolate bar that works for snacking, baking, and pairing with coffee. It delivers 90% of the experience of a $8 artisan bar for a fraction of the price.

**Skip it if** you need a specific cacao percentage printed on the front, or if you prefer intensely bitter, high-cacao chocolate.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A reliable, delicious everyday dark chocolate with a minor packaging clarity issue.
