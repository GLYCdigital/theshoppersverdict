## What you actually get

The box contains 400 individual Domino sugar packets, each holding 2.8 grams of pure cane sugar. That's the standard single-serving size you see at diners and coffee shops. The dispenser box itself is the headline feature — it has a flip-top opening that lets you pull one packet at a time without the whole stack sliding out. As shown in the product image, the box is designed to sit upright on a counter or shelf, and the opening mechanism is genuinely more functional than the typical tear-away cardboard flaps you get with most bulk sugar boxes.

## Performance and real-world use

The packets themselves are standard Domino quality — fine granulated sugar that dissolves quickly in hot coffee, no clumping issues reported. The 2.8g size is calibrated for an 8-12 ounce cup of coffee. For reference, that's roughly one teaspoon, which lands right in the "medium sweet" zone for most drinkers.

The dispenser box is where this product earns its keep. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"The box design is genius — I just flip the top and grab one packet without making a mess. Way better than the bags that spill everywhere."* That feedback tracks with my assessment: the box holds its shape well, and the opening is sized so packets come out one at a time, not in frustrating clumps of three.

## Where it falls short

Honest downsides exist. The box has no resealable closure beyond the flip-top, which is fine when it's upright but a disaster waiting to happen if the box gets knocked over. If you store it in a drawer or pantry where things shift around, you'll be picking up loose packets off the floor. Also, the 2.8g packet is slightly smaller than a standard sugar cube (about 3g). If you prefer your coffee on the sweeter side or use a 16-ounce travel mug, you'll likely need two packets per cup — which means the 400-count goes faster than you'd expect.

## FAQ

**Q: Is 2.8g enough sugar for a standard cup of coffee?**
For an 8-12oz cup, yes — that's about one teaspoon, which is the standard sweet spot. For larger mugs or heavy sweetener users, plan on two packets.

**Q: Can I use these for baking or cooking?**
Technically yes, but it's wildly inefficient. Each packet is roughly one teaspoon, so you'd need 48 packets for a cup of sugar. Buy loose sugar for baking.

**Q: How should I store the box after opening?**
Keep it upright on a counter or shelf. The flip-top holds fine, but the box isn't spill-proof if it tips over. Avoid storing it in a drawer where other items can knock it around.
