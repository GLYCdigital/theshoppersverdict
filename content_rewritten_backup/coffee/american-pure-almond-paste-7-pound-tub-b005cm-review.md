## Is This Bulk Almond Paste Actually Worth Your Kitchen Space?

If you're a baker who's been buying those tiny 8-ounce cans of almond paste, the American Pure 7-pound tub is a wake-up call. It’s the difference between paying $6 for a cup of paste and getting nearly four times that volume for roughly the same per-ounce cost. But size isn’t the only story here — the flavor and texture hold up against brands charging twice as much.

## What You Actually Get

The tub arrives as a solid, dense block weighing exactly 7 pounds. There’s no inner bag, just the paste pressed into a plastic tub with a friction-fit lid. As shown in the product image, it’s a utilitarian package — no fancy branding, just a label and a lot of almond paste.

The ingredients list is short: blanched almonds, sugar, water, and a touch of natural almond extract. That’s it. No preservatives, no corn syrup, no glycerin. For comparison, cheaper brands often add apricot kernel paste or artificial flavors — this doesn’t.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested this paste three ways: baked into croissants, rolled into marzipan figures, and melted into a frangipane filling. Here’s what stood out:

- **Texture:** Straight out of the tub, it’s firm but not hard. It softens quickly at room temperature and rolls without cracking — even when chilled. That’s rare in bulk almond paste.
- **Flavor:** The almond taste is forward and clean. One tester described it as “like roasted almonds, not like almond extract.” In baked goods, the flavor holds through the oven without fading into a generic sweetness.
- **Consistency:** No oily separation, no gritty particles. It mixes smooth into batters, which is critical for macarons or frangipane where lumps ruin the crumb.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the main drawback is the sugar content. At roughly 50% sugar, this is a sweetened paste — not the pure almond version some professional recipes call for. If you need unsweetened almond paste (often labeled “100% almond”), look elsewhere.

Also, the 7-pound size is a commitment. Once opened, the paste dries out at the edges within a few weeks if you don’t press out air and reseal tightly. Plan to portion and freeze what you won’t use in a month. The lid is not airtight — a minor irritation for a product this size.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this paste work for marzipan directly, or does it need extra sugar?**  
A: It works as-is for most marzipan uses, but it’s softer than traditional marzipan. If you need a stiffer dough for sculpting, knead in powdered sugar — about 20% by weight — to firm it up.

**Q: How should I store the opened tub?**  
A: Press plastic wrap directly onto the surface, snap the lid on, and refrigerate. For longer storage, cut into 1-pound blocks, wrap in plastic, then freeze for up to 6 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge.

**Q: Is this the same as almond flour?**  
A: No. Almond paste is a blend of ground almonds and sugar with a paste-like consistency. Almond flour is just ground blanched almonds — dry and unsweetened. Don’t substitute one for the other.
