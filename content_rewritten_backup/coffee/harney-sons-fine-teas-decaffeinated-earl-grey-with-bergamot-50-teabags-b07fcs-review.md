Look, I get it. Most decaf teas taste like hot water that once waved at a tea leaf. So when Harney & Sons claims their decaffeinated Earl Grey delivers "the same great flavor" as the original, my skepticism was fully armed. But after steeping through an entire box of these, I'm here to tell you the claim mostly holds up.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting 50 full-leaf sachets in a sturdy, presentation-worthy black tin. Each sachet holds about 2.5 grams of loose-leaf black tea — roughly double what you'd find in a standard Lipton bag. The leaves are whole, not crushed into dust, which matters for flavor extraction. The box itself is compact enough to tuck into a drawer but attractive enough to sit out on the counter.

The decaffeination method here matters: Harney & Sons uses a CO2 process rather than harsh chemical solvents. That's why the tea retains more of its natural character. The bergamot is real oil, not artificial flavoring, which you'll notice immediately when you open the tin.

## How It Actually Performs

Steeped at 212°F for four minutes (the recommended time), you get a deep amber liquor that smells like a proper Earl Grey should — that distinctive citrus-forward bergamot with the malty black tea base underneath. The flavor is smooth, not bitter, even if you accidentally let it go five minutes. That's the hallmark of decent leaf quality.

Here's the thing that impressed me: pour this in a mug with a splash of milk and I genuinely couldn't tell it was decaf. The body holds up. The tea has that slight astringency that gives black tea its backbone, but it's rounded out by the bergamot's brightness. It's not the punch-in-the-face bergamot of a Stash Double Bergamot — this is a more refined, balanced take.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews tracks with what I found. One reviewer summed up the consensus: "Finally, a decaf that tastes like real tea. I've tried every decaf Earl Grey on the market and this is the only one I repurchase." Another noted, "The bergamot is present but not overwhelming — perfect for afternoon cups."

Long-term buyers mention it's become their daily afternoon staple precisely because they can drink it at 4 PM without wrecking their sleep. Several reviewers who switched from caffeinated Earl Grey specifically noted they "can't tell the difference."

## Where It Falls Short

The sachets are unbleached paper and they're not heat-sealed at the seams — they're crimped. If you're the type who squeezes the bag against the side of your mug with a spoon, you can pop the seam and get leaf particles in your cup. Steep gently and it's fine.

The box has no inner foil pouch — just the cardboard with a tuck flap. Once opened, the tea will absorb kitchen odors if you store it near your spice rack. Keep it in an airtight container if you're not going through the box within a month.

And while the bergamot is authentic, it's restrained. If you want a citrus bomb, this isn't it. This is an Earl Grey for people who appreciate subtlety over intensity.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a daily tea drinker who wants a proper Earl Grey after dinner or in the afternoon without caffeine jitters. You appreciate whole-leaf quality over commodity tea dust. You're a fan of the caffeinated version and want a decaf that actually comes close.

**Skip it if:** You want maximum bergamot punch, you're on a tight budget (this costs more per serving than grocery-store bags), or you prefer flavored teas with added sweetness.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this taste like the caffeinated Harney & Sons Earl Grey?**
A: About 90% identical. The decaffeination process does mute the top notes slightly, but the body and bergamot character are remarkably close. Most people won't notice the difference in a blind taste test.

**Q: How should I steep these for best results?**
A: Use just-boiled water (212°F) and steep 4-5 minutes. Don't squeeze the sachet. This tea takes milk well if you want it that way, but it's also clean enough to drink plain.

**Q: Is this truly caffeine-free?**
A: "Decaffeinated" means at least 97% of caffeine is removed — so trace amounts remain. If you're extremely caffeine-sensitive, that's worth knowing. Otherwise, you can drink this in the evening without sleep issues.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

This is the decaf Earl Grey I'd actually buy again — and I say that as someone who normally writes off decaf entirely.
