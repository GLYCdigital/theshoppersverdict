# PARENT - Fortisimo: A Decent Machine That Demands Patience

The PARENT Fortisimo sits in a crowded market segment: budget-friendly espresso machines that promise café results without the café price tag. With a 3.9-star average across 195 ratings, it's neither a breakout star nor a disaster. The truth is more nuanced — this machine can pull genuinely good shots, but it won't hold your hand getting there.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the Fortisimo unit itself, a portafilter with both single and double baskets, a plastic tamper (you'll want to replace this immediately), a milk frothing pitcher, and a measuring scoop. The stainless steel body feels sturdier than the price point suggests — no cheap plastic wobble during operation. The 1.2-liter water tank is removable, which sounds basic but beats the fixed-tank competitors in this range.

## Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Fiddly

The 15-bar pump is the headline feature, and it's not marketing fluff. When you nail the grind size and tamp pressure, the Fortisimo produces espresso with legitimate crema — that golden-brown foam that separates real espresso from bitter coffee. One verified reviewer noted, "The first shot I pulled with freshly ground beans rivaled my local shop. The second one was undrinkable." That inconsistency is the Fortisimo's defining trait.

The steam wand is where patience wears thin. It produces adequate foam for lattes, but microfoam — that silky, glossy texture baristas achieve — is nearly impossible. Expect bubbles that separate quickly. Another buyer summed it up: "The wand works, but it's like learning to drive stick. Frustrating initially, manageable once you adapt."

The control dial is the design flaw that keeps coming up in reviews. The markings between espresso, steam, and off positions are subtle enough that users report accidentally entering steam mode and scalding milk. There's no audible click or tactile detent — just smooth rotation that requires visual confirmation every time.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Owners agree on a few things. The heating speed genuinely impresses — the thermoblock system reaches brewing temperature in under a minute, which beats many machines costing twice as much. The compact size fits tight kitchens, and the stainless steel exterior wipes clean easily. Multiple reviewers mention the "solid, heavy feel" that suggests durability beyond the price point.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the steam wand and dial issues, the drip tray is genuinely undersized. If you pull two shots and purge the wand afterward, you're emptying it. The included tamper is a useless plastic disc that will cause channeling — factor in $15-20 for a proper metal tamper. The machine also lacks a three-way solenoid valve, meaning the portafilter will be messy when you remove it post-shot, with residual water and coffee sputtering into the tray.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're an espresso beginner who wants to learn on a machine that won't punish your budget, you already own a decent burr grinder (essential — pre-ground coffee will disappoint), and you primarily drink straight espresso or Americanos rather than milk-heavy drinks.

**Skip it if:** You're a milk-drink enthusiast expecting latte-art-quality foam, you want set-and-forget consistency without dialing in, or you entertain often — the small tank and tray will have you constantly refilling.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the Fortisimo use ground coffee or only pods?**
A: It uses ground coffee exclusively. You'll need a burr grinder for best results — pre-ground supermarket coffee will produce weak, sour shots.

**Q: How long does the machine take to heat up?**
A: Roughly 45-60 seconds from cold start. The thermoblock system is genuinely fast.

**Q: Does the steam wand work for cappuccinos?**
A: It produces acceptable foam for cappuccinos, but you won't get the microfoam texture of professional machines. Plan for bubbly milk that separates quickly.

## The Verdict

The PARENT Fortisimo is a 3.7/5 machine — competent, affordable, and capable of excellent espresso, but requiring patience and a few accessory upgrades to reach its potential. If you're willing to learn espresso mechanics and accept its limitations, it's a solid entry point. If you want effortless consistency, spend more elsewhere.

**Buy it if** you're a hands-on learner who wants real espresso on a budget. **Skip it if** you value convenience and milk texture over everything else.
