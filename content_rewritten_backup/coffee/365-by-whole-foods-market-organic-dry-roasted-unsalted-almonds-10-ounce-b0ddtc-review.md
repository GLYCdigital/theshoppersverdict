You'd think a bag of almonds is a bag of almonds. Then you open a 10-ounce bag of 365 by Whole Foods Market Organic Dry Roasted & Unsalted Almonds and realize how much texture actually separates a good nut from a mediocre one.

These are dry roasted — meaning no oil, no salt, no sugar, nothing but heat and organic almonds. The result is a serious crunch that doesn't soften or turn stale a week after opening. If you're buying nuts for snacking, baking, or meal prep, that texture matters more than you'd expect.

## What's Actually in the Bag

The ingredient list is exactly one item: organic almonds. That's it. No sunflower oil, no "natural flavors," no preservatives. For anyone tracking sodium, these are a true zero-milligram option — which is harder to find than you'd think in the roasted nut aisle.

The 10-ounce bag is smaller than the bulk bins at Costco, but it's sized reasonably for a household that goes through nuts in a few weeks. The resealable zipper is a genuine plus; it's sturdy enough to survive repeated openings without tearing at the corners like some thinner bags do.

## How They Actually Perform

Roasting method matters. Oil-roasted nuts can taste greasy and leave residue on your fingers. These don't. The dry roast gives them a matte finish and a snap when you bite down that you'll notice immediately.

I tested these three ways: straight out of the bag, mixed into Greek yogurt, and chopped into banana bread. They held up in all three. The crunch survived baking, which isn't true of every roasted almond on the market — cheaper ones tend to go soft when exposed to moisture.

One thing to note: because they're unsalted, the flavor is purely nutty and slightly sweet. If you're used to seasoned almonds, these will taste flat at first. That's not a flaw — it's the point. But it's worth knowing before you buy.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 229 ratings average out to 4.6 stars, and the pattern in the feedback is consistent. Buyers repeatedly mention freshness — "These are always crunchy and fresh, never rancid," one reviewer noted. Another called them "perfect for anyone watching sodium intake."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

First, the broken pieces. Multiple reviewers have noted that the bag contains a fair amount of almond fragments and dust at the bottom. It's not a dealbreaker — the pieces are fine for cooking — but if you want uniform whole almonds for a charcuterie board, you'll be picking through the bag.

Second, the unsalted profile isn't for everyone. If you're accustomed to salted or flavored nuts, these will taste bland. You can fix that by tossing them with sea salt and a little olive oil, but that's extra work.

Third, the price point. Whole Foods' 365 brand is usually competitive, but at certain times this 10-ounce bag costs more per ounce than buying in bulk from warehouse clubs. If almonds are a daily staple for you, compare unit prices before committing.

## Who Should Buy These

**Get these if:** you're on a low-sodium or no-added-sugar diet, you bake regularly and want a clean almond option, or you prefer the texture of dry-roasted over oil-roasted nuts.

**Skip these if:** you want seasoned or salted almonds for snacking, you go through nuts fast enough to justify bulk buying, or you need perfectly uniform whole almonds for presentation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are these truly unsalted?**
Yes. Zero sodium per serving. The only ingredient is organic almonds. No salt spray, no seasoning blend.

**How should I store them after opening?**
The resealable bag works fine for a few weeks. For longer storage, transfer to an airtight container in a cool, dark place. You can freeze them for up to six months without losing much quality.

**Are these good for baking?**
Excellent. The dry roast means they won't add extra oil or salt to your recipes. Chopped or slivered, they hold up well in breads, cookies, and granola.
