## What You Actually Get

The pack contains four individually wrapped cookies. Each one is substantial — roughly 3.5 inches across and about a half-inch thick. As shown in the product image, these are thick, hearty cookies with visible chunks of chocolate (not chips) studded throughout the dough. The brown butter color is apparent even before baking — a deep golden tan that hints at the nutty flavor inside.

## The Real Test: How Do They Taste?

Brown butter is one of those buzzwords brands throw around without delivering. Here, it's legitimate. The first bite hits you with that distinctive toasted, almost caramelized nuttiness that separates a good cookie from a great one. The chocolate chunks are large and high-quality — they melt properly rather than turning waxy.

Texture is where these shine. The edges crisp up nicely while the center stays dense and chewy — think Levain Bakery style, not your grocery store soft-bake. One reviewer captured it well: *"Tastes like something from a high-end bakery, not a box. The brown butter really comes through and the chocolate chunks are huge."* Another noted, *"I've bought these three times now. They're dangerously good."*

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be honest about the drawbacks. First, you're getting four cookies. That's it. For the price, you're paying a premium per cookie — this is a treat, not a stock-up item.

Second, they arrive soft at room temperature. To get that perfect crispy-edge/chewy-center contrast, you'll need to warm them — I found 8-10 minutes in a 350°F oven or 3 minutes in an air fryer hits the sweet spot. Eating them straight from the package is fine, but you're missing the full experience.

Third, if you're team "cakey cookie," move along. These are dense and buttery — closer to a scone's heft than a pillowy chocolate chip cookie. That's a feature for some, a flaw for others.
