Let's be honest about sugar-free coffee syrups: most taste like chemically sweetened regret. Jordan's Skinny Syrups is the exception that's earned a 4.3-star average across 215 Amazon ratings — and for good reason. But it's not perfect, and here's what you need to know before you add it to your cart.

## What You're Actually Getting

Jordan's ships in a 25.4-ounce squeezable bottle — roughly 60 servings per bottle if you're using the standard 1-tablespoon pour. The bottle itself is practical: the squeeze top dispenses cleanly without the sticky mess you get from pour-top syrups. No pump included, but honestly, you don't need one with this consistency.

## How It Performs in Real Coffee

The magic here is the sucralose base. Unlike stevia-based syrups that carry that signature herbal bitterness, Jordan's sweetens cleanly. I tested the vanilla and caramel flavors in both hot drip coffee and iced lattes, and the syrup dissolves without clumping — no gritty residue at the bottom of the mug.

The flavor concentration is where Jordan's earns its keep. One tablespoon transforms a 12-ounce coffee noticeably. Two tablespoons gets you a genuinely sweet, flavored cup. Compare that to some competitors where you're pouring half the bottle for any flavor to register.

The zero-calorie, zero-sugar, zero-carb profile is legit. If you're tracking macros, fasting, or managing blood sugar, this fits cleanly. No hidden maltitol or sugar alcohols that spike insulin.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The praise consistently centers on the same things. One reviewer summed it up: "Finally a sugar-free syrup that doesn't taste like artificial sweetener. I've tried every brand on Amazon and this is the only one I repurchase." Another noted: "The salted caramel flavor is dangerously good — I have to stop myself from using too much."

The variety matters too. Jordan's offers 30+ flavors, and buyers frequently mention discovering new favorites. The seasonal releases (pumpkin spice, peppermint bark) get particular love.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight about the downsides.

**Sucralose sensitivity is real.** If you're prone to bloating or GI upset from artificial sweeteners, this syrup could trigger that. Several reviewers mentioned this exact issue. Start with half a tablespoon to test your tolerance.

**The consistency is thin.** Traditional coffee syrups have a thicker, almost viscous body. Jordan's is watery by comparison. In hot coffee, you won't notice. In iced drinks, the syrup doesn't create that layered, syrupy effect some people want — it just thins into the drink.

**Flavor consistency varies.** The vanilla and caramel are solid. But some flavors (looking at you, certain fruit varieties) taste more artificial and perfume-like. Read recent reviews for the specific flavor you're considering before committing.

**No caffeine, no creamer base.** This is flavoring only. You're still adding your own milk, cream, or sweetener if you want a full latte experience.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're keto, diabetic, or calorie-conscious and want flavored coffee without the sugar spike. Also great if you're tired of watery, weak-flavored syrups and want something that actually tastes like the flavor on the label.

**Skip it if** you're sensitive to sucralose, want a thick indulgent syrup for iced caramel macchiatos, or prefer naturally sweetened options like honey or maple-based syrups.

## FAQ

**Q: How much syrup should I use per cup?**
A: Start with 1 tablespoon per 12-ounce coffee. For iced drinks, use 1.5-2 tablespoons since the cold temperature dulls sweetness. Adjust to taste — the bottle says 1 tablespoon is a serving, but many users report needing 1.5-2x that for a noticeably sweet cup.

**Q: Does this syrup work in cold brew and iced coffee?**
A: Yes, it dissolves fine in cold liquids, though it won't create that thick syrup layer you get from coffee shop pumps. For iced drinks, mix it with a splash of warm water or milk first to ensure even distribution.

**Q: Is this actually zero calories?**
A: Yes — zero calories, zero sugar, zero carbs per serving. The sweetness comes from sucralose (Splenda), which passes through the body without being digested.
