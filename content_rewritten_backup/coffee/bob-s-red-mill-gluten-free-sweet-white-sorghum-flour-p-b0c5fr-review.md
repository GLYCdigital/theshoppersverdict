Sorghum flour is that "something else" — and Bob's Red Mill's Sweet White version is one of the most accessible, consistently milled options on the market. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, it's clearly earning its place in pantries. But is it right for your baking? Let's break it down.

## What You Actually Get

A 22-ounce resealable bag of finely milled sorghum flour, stone-ground from whole white sorghum grain. The texture lands somewhere between all-purpose wheat flour and cornstarch — fine, slightly powdery, with a pale cream color. The "sweet" in the name refers to the variety of sorghum, not added sugar; the flavor is mild and faintly nutty, closer to wheat than you'd expect from a gluten-free grain.

Bob's Red Mill certifies this as gluten-free and processes it in a dedicated facility, so celiac shoppers don't need to worry about cross-contamination. The bag's resealable zipper actually holds up — a small thing, but it matters when you're reaching for it weekly.

## Performance in Real Baking

Sorghum flour behaves differently depending on how you use it. In quick breads, muffins, and cookies, it shines as part of a blend — typically 30-40% of your total flour weight. It adds structure without the grittiness that brown rice flour can bring, and its mild sweetness lets chocolate, cinnamon, and fruit flavors come through clearly.

For yeast breads, you'll want to pair it with a starch like tapioca or potato starch plus a binder like xanthan gum. Used alone, sorghum produces a crumb that's dense and slightly dry — it lacks the elasticity of wheat gluten, and no amount of kneading will fix that. One reviewer noted, *"It's my go-to for pancakes and banana bread, but I always mix it with sweet rice flour for yeast rolls."* That's the honest reality: this is a component, not a complete solution.

The grind is consistently fine across batches, which matters more than most shoppers realize. Coarser gluten-free flours absorb liquid unevenly and create pockets of grittiness in finished baked goods. Bob's Red Mill keeps this one reliably smooth.

## Where It Falls Short

Sorghum flour is not a pantry shortcut. If you're new to gluten-free baking and expect to swap this 1:1 for all-purpose flour, you'll be disappointed. It needs blending, and it needs recipe specificity. The dryness issue is real — baked goods made with high sorghum ratios stale faster than wheat-based equivalents. Store your finished goods in an airtight container and plan to eat them within two days, or freeze them.

Also note: the 22-ounce bag is smaller than standard 5-pound flour bags, and at roughly $6-8 per bag, it's pricier per pound than conventional flour. That's the gluten-free tax — nobody likes it, but it's the reality.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're building a gluten-free flour blend and want a mild, versatile base flour. You bake quick breads, cookies, pancakes, or muffins regularly. You tolerate sorghum well and want an alternative to rice-based flours.

**Skip it if:** You want a single flour that replaces all-purpose in every recipe. You're on a tight budget and need the cheapest gluten-free option (cornstarch or white rice flour stretch further). Or you're baking artisan-style yeast breads and need higher protein structure.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I substitute sorghum flour for all-purpose flour 1:1?**
A: No. For cookies and quick breads, you can replace up to 25-30% of wheat flour with sorghum in conventional recipes, but full substitution requires adjusting liquids and adding a binder. For gluten-free recipes, follow the blend ratios the recipe specifies.

**Q: Does sorghum flour taste sweet?**
A: The "sweet" refers to the sorghum variety, not added sugar. The flavor is mild and slightly nutty — sweeter than brown rice flour but far less pronounced than coconut flour. It won't make your baked goods noticeably sweeter.

**Q: How should I store it?**
A: Keep it in the resealable bag in a cool, dry place for up to a few months. For longer storage, refrigerate or freeze in an airtight container — sorghum flour can go rancid if stored warm for extended periods.
