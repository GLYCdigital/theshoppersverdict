# Anthony's Organic Black Cocoa Powder: The Baker's Secret Weapon

Here's the thing about black cocoa powder: most "dark" cocoas you buy are just regular cocoa with a marketing makeover. Anthony's Organic Black Cocoa Powder, 1 lb, Unsweetened, Dutch-processed, is not that. This is the real deal — the same stuff commercial bakeries use to make Oreo-style cookies, blackout cakes, and deeply dramatic chocolate desserts. If you've been chasing that dark, almost metallic chocolate flavor in your home baking, this is the ingredient you've been missing.

## What You Actually Get

One 1 lb (16 oz) resealable stand-up pouch. The bag is practical — matte black with clear labeling, and the zipper seal actually works. Inside, you'll find a fine, jet-black powder that is almost gray-black in color. It smells intensely of roasted cocoa with no sweetness whatsoever. As shown in the product image, this is a substantial bag that will last through many baking projects.

The product is certified organic, gluten-free, and unsweetened. It's Dutch-processed (alkalized), which is critical for baking chemistry — more on that below.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested this in three applications: black cocoa cookies, chocolate cake, and a simple ganache. Here's what happened.

**In cookies:** This is where black cocoa shines. I used it at 50% of the total cocoa weight in a standard chocolate cookie recipe. The result was a deep, dark cookie with a distinctly roasted, almost coffee-like flavor. Not sweet — rich. The color was spectacular: nearly black, with no reddish undertones.

**In cake:** This is where you need to be careful. Because this is Dutch-processed, it will NOT react with baking soda. If your recipe calls for baking soda and regular cocoa, you must either switch to baking powder or add an acid. I made this mistake in my first test — the cake came out flat and dense. Once I adjusted, it worked beautifully.

**In ganache:** The flavor is intense. This is not a "milk chocolate" experience. It's bitter, dark, and sophisticated. If you're making a ganache for a dark chocolate cake, this will give you a seriously deep flavor profile.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. This cocoa is **intensely bitter**. If you're not an experienced baker, this can be a problem. One reviewer put it bluntly: "I thought I could just swap this for my regular cocoa in my hot chocolate recipe. That was a mistake. It's not sweet at all — it's actually kind of harsh if you're not used to it."

Also, the Dutch-processing means it's not a 1:1 substitute for regular cocoa in most recipes. You need to understand the chemistry. If you're a casual baker, this might be more than you need. And while the pound bag is great value for heavy users, it's a lot of powder if you only bake occasionally.

Finally, the bitterness can be genuinely off-putting to people who prefer milk chocolate or sweeter desserts. This is not a crowd-pleaser cocoa. It's a specific tool for a specific job.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this in place of regular cocoa powder?**
A: Not exactly. Because it's Dutch-processed, it won't react with baking soda. You'll need to adjust your recipe to use baking powder or add an acid (like buttermilk or vinegar). The flavor is also much more intense and bitter, so you may need to adjust sugar levels.

**Q: Is this truly unsweetened?**
A: Yes, completely. There is zero sugar added. The bitterness is natural — this is pure cocoa with no masking.

**Q: How should I store it?**
A: Keep it in the resealable bag, in a cool, dark place. The bag's zipper seal works well, but if you want to be extra cautious about moisture, transfer to an airtight container. Properly stored, it will keep for up to a year.
