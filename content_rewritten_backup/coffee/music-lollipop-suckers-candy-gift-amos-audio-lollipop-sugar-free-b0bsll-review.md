# Music Lollipop Suckers Candy Gift, AMOS Audio Lollipop Sugar Review: The Candy That Sings in Your Skull

You've seen the viral videos — someone pops a lollipop in their mouth, sticks their fingers in their ears, and suddenly they're hearing music like they've got a concert playing inside their head. The AMOS Audio Lollipop is that exact product, and here's the honest truth after testing: it's not a replacement for your headphones, but it's one of the most fun novelty gifts you can buy for under $20.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes 8 lollipops, each in a different flavor (strawberry, blueberry, grape, orange, lemon, apple, and a couple of blended options). Every sucker is 3D-printed from food-grade sugar with a unique shape — stars, hearts, rings, and other geometric forms. The candy is hollow, and inside sits a small transducer chip that converts audio vibrations through your teeth and jawbone directly to your inner ear.

The box also includes a tiny screwdriver and a replacement battery for each lollipop. That's a thoughtful touch — the battery lasts about 4-5 hours of total playback across multiple sessions, and you can swap it out when the candy's gone. Each lollipop comes pre-loaded with one track, and the songs differ between flavors. There's no way to change the music, so what you get is what you hear.

## How It Actually Performs

The bone conduction trick works — genuinely. When you put the lollipop in your mouth and cover your ears, you hear the track clearly enough to recognize the melody and rhythm. It's like listening to a transistor radio from 1985: tinny, compressed, and low-fi, but unmistakably music.

Here's the catch: the sound quality depends heavily on how you hold the lollipop. If it's not positioned just right against your teeth, the audio fades to a faint buzz. And the music only plays while the candy is thick enough to keep the sensor engaged. Once you suck the lollipop down to a thin sliver — which takes most people 5-7 minutes — the track cuts out. You're left with a tasty but silent piece of sugar.

Amazon reviewers at 4.3 stars largely echo the same sentiment: it's a crowd-pleaser. One verified buyer wrote, *"Bought these for my niece's birthday. The adults fought over them more than the kids. ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: the audio quality is a novelty, not a listening experience. Anyone expecting headphone-level fidelity will be disappointed. The tracks are also fixed — you can't load your own music, and the selection is generic royalty-free pop and electronic tunes. If you don't like the song on your flavor, you're stuck with it.

The battery replacement process is fiddly. The screwdriver is tiny, and the chip compartment requires careful handling to avoid breaking the thin plastic casing. Several reviewers mentioned losing the music entirely after a battery swap gone wrong.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Gift-givers for tweens and teens**: Kids love showing this to friends — it's the kind of thing that gets passed around a classroom.
- **Novelty collectors**: If you enjoy quirky gadgets and conversation pieces, this delivers.
- **Party hosts**: Drop a few in a candy bowl and watch the chaos unfold.

Skip it if you're looking for serious audio gear, if you have dental work that might be sensitive to vibration, or if you're buying for a child under 5 who might try to bite through the plastic casing.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I hear the music without covering my ears?**
A: No. The bone conduction needs your fingers or earplugs in your ears to block ambient sound. In a quiet room, you'll hear a faint whisper of the track; in a noisy environment, you'll hear nothing.

**Q: Are the lollipops safe for kids?**
A: The candy is food-grade sugar, and the chip is sealed inside a food-safe plastic casing. That said, the casing is a choking hazard if bitten through. For kids under 8, supervision is strongly recommended.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
A: Roughly 4-5 hours of total playback. Each lollipop comes with one replacement battery, and extra batteries are available from third-party sellers. The music stops when the candy melts below the sensor, regardless of battery life.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely fun, affordable novelty gift that will spark laughs and confusion in equal measure. **Skip it if** you expect real audio quality or any kind of customization.
