You get a 30.6-ounce plastic canister (about 30 servings per the label, though real-world scooping tends to yield 28–32 mugs depending on how generous you pour). No fancy single-origin cacao here—this is mass-market comfort, and it knows it. The scoop is a standard tablespoon-sized measure, clearly marked, so you won’t be guessing.

**Performance: How it actually behaves in the mug**  
Here’s where Swiss Miss separates itself from cheap store brands. Drop two scoops into 6–8 ounces of hot water (or milk, if you’re fancy), stir for ten seconds, and you get a *smooth* drink—no undissolved powder clumps at the bottom, no chalky residue on your tongue. The marshmallows bloom into soft, spongy bits within a minute, not the rock-hard pellets you get from off-brand mixes. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *“I’ve tried every generic cocoa out there, and this is the only one that doesn’t taste like dusty sadness.”* That tracks with my testing—the chocolate flavor is rounded, slightly sweet, with a clean finish that doesn’t leave a syrupy coating in your throat. It also plays well with a splash of peppermint extract or a shot of espresso if you’re building a mocha.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Second, *value*: the canister undercuts the 10-count packet boxes by roughly 30% per serving. **Where it falls short (and you should care)**  
Let’s be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the sugar: 20 grams per serving (that’s 5 teaspoons). This is *not* a health product, and if you’re watching carbs, this will blow your daily budget in one mug. Second, the canister lid is fiddly—it snaps on securely but can crack at the hinge if you torque it too hard. Store it on a middle shelf, not a high one where a fumble means a cocoa avalanche. Third, the flavor leans *milk-chocolate sweet*, not dark or intense. If you prefer a bitter, European-style cocoa, this will taste thin to you. And a minor gripe: the marshmallow-to-mix ratio is about 15%, which is fine, but don’t expect the “marshmallow overload” you see in the marketing photos.

**FAQs shoppers actually ask**  
*Q: Can I use it with cold milk for chocolate milk?*  
A: Yes, but you’ll need to whisk vigorously—the powder dissolves slower in cold liquid. Use a shaker bottle or blender for a smooth result.  

*Q: How long does an open canister stay fresh?*  
A: About 6 months if you keep the lid tightly sealed and away from humidity. The whey powder can clump if exposed to steam, so don’t store it above the kettle.  

*Q: Does it contain dairy?*  
A: Yes—whey and milk ingredients are present. For a dairy-free alternative, look for Swiss Miss’s “Simply Cocoa” line, which is lactose-free.
