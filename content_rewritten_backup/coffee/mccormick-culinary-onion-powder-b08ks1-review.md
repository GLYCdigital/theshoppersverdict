Let’s be honest about what this is: a massive jar of onion powder for people who cook a lot. If you're a once-a-week stir-fry kind of cook, McCormick's Culinary line is overkill. But if you're feeding a family, meal-prepping, or making your own spice blends, this 17-ounce container changes the economics of your pantry.

**What you actually get**

The product image shows a clear plastic jar that's roughly the size of a large pasta sauce container. Inside is 17 ounces of fine, pale-tan powder. That's the equivalent of roughly three standard 6-ounce grocery store jars. The package is straightforward: no fancy label, no shaker top—just a wide-mouth jar with a resealable lid. You'll want to transfer some to a smaller shaker for daily use; the big jar is awkward to sprinkle from.

**Performance and real-world use**

I tested this in three applications: a dry rub for pork shoulder, a vinaigrette, and a batch of French onion dip. The grind is noticeably finer than store-brand powders—it dissolves into liquids almost instantly. In the rub, it clung evenly to the meat without clumping, which happens with coarser powders that absorb moisture unevenly.

The flavor is clean and straightforward. It's potent for a dried spice, but it's not fresh-onion potent. Plan on using about two tablespoons of this powder to replace one medium fresh onion in cooked dishes. In raw applications like dips, it needs about 30 minutes to rehydrate and release its full aroma—don't judge it immediately after stirring.

One thing that stood out: no anti-caking agents. That means it can clump if you store it near steam or in a humid environment. Keep the lid tight and it's fine, but this isn't a jar you want living next to the stovetop.

**Where it falls short**

The bulk size is the main tradeoff. If you use onion powder sparingly, you'll have this jar for two years, and dried spices do lose potency over time. The flavor will fade noticeably after about 12 months. There's also the freshness issue with the packaging—once opened, you're committed to using it consistently.

Price-wise, there's no hiding that this is a restaurant-pack. It's not expensive per ounce, but the upfront cost is higher than a single grocery jar. That's a budget issue for some, not a value issue.

**Who should buy this**

Get this jar if you make your own spice rubs, cook for four or more people regularly, or do any kind of meal prep. It's also ideal for anyone who's tired of rebuying tiny jars every few weeks. Skip it if you're a light user, live in a humid climate without good pantry storage, or prefer fresh aromatics in most dishes.

**FAQ**

*Does it taste like fresh onion?*

No. It has the savory, slightly sweet character of onion but lacks the sharp bite and moisture of fresh. It's best in cooked dishes where it can meld with other flavors.

*Is this the same as the McCormick in grocery stores?*

Yes, same brand and quality. The Culinary line is just larger packaging for food service and heavy home use.

*How should I store it?*

Airtight container in a cool, dark place. A cabinet away from the stove works. Avoid keeping it near a humidifier or steamy area.

**The verdict**

This is a straightforward value play for serious home cooks. The quality is solid, the price per ounce beats grocery store equivalents, and the fine grind performs well in everything I threw at it. The size and mild flavor intensity are real considerations, but they're manageable if you cook with volume in mind.

**Buy it if** you cook often and want to stop overpaying for tiny spice jars. **Skip it if** you're a light user who values maximum freshness over bulk savings.
