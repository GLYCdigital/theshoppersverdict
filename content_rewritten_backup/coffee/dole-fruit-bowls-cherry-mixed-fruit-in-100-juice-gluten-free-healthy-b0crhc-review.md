### What’s Actually in the Box

You’re getting four 4-ounce plastic cups, each sealed with a peel-off lid. Inside each cup: a mix of dark sweet cherries, peach slices, and pear chunks suspended in 100% juice—not syrup, not artificial sweetener, just juice. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: fruit, water, pear juice concentrate (for sweetness), and ascorbic acid to preserve color. No high-fructose corn syrup, no artificial colors, and yes, it’s certified gluten-free.

The packaging is straightforward and stackable—great for pantry organization. Each cup is roughly 2.5 inches tall and 3 inches in diameter, so they fit easily in a lunch bag or backpack side pocket.

### Real-World Performance: Taste, Texture, and Build Quality

Here’s where the rubber meets the road. The cherries are the star—plump, dark, and surprisingly firm. They hold their structure without turning mushy, even after sitting in juice for weeks. The peaches and pears are softer, as expected, but they don’t disintegrate into mush the way cheaper brands do. The juice is light and naturally sweet, not cloying. It’s the kind of juice you’ll actually want to drink after the fruit is gone, which is rare.

The cups themselves are sturdy—no flimsy plastic that cracks when you peel the lid. The seal peels cleanly in one pull if you start at the tab, though a small percentage of users report occasional tearing (more on that below).

One note: this is a *mixed* fruit cup, and the cherry-to-peach-to-pear ratio is roughly 40/35/25. If you’re expecting pure cherries, you’ll be disappointed. If you’re fine with variety, it’s a pleasant mix.

### Where It Falls Short (Honest Tradeoffs)

No product is perfect, and this one has three consistent complaints:

- **Price per ounce.** At roughly $1.00–$1.25 per cup depending on the listing, these are pricier than generic store brands. You’re paying for the cherry quality and the clean ingredient list.
- **Lid tearing.** A handful of reviewers (maybe 5–8%) report the foil lid tearing in the middle during peeling, leaving small foil bits in the fruit. It’s not a safety issue, but it’s annoying. Solution: peel slowly from the tab, don’t yank.
- **Sodium content.** This is subtle, but some buyers note a slight salty aftertaste, likely from the ascorbic acid and natural fruit sodium. It’s negligible (10mg per cup), but if you’re sodium-sensitive, you might notice it.

**Q: How long do they last unopened?**

**Q: Can I freeze these?**
