Let me be upfront: I've tested dozens of "healthy" chocolate products that tasted like chalk dust wrapped in wax paper. SKINNYDIPPED's Dark Chocolate Peanut Butter Cups aren't that — but they're also not a Reese's dupe, and understanding that distinction is the difference between loving these and feeling ripped off.

**What You Actually Get**

The package contains four individually wrapped cups (two larger cups in one pack, two in the other). As shown in the product image, each cup is smaller than a standard Reese's — about 0.7 oz — which matters because you're paying for portion control as much as the chocolate itself. The wrapper lists 60 calories, 5g protein, and 1g sugar per cup, which is the entire reason anyone buys these.

**Taste and Texture: The Honest Breakdown**

The dark chocolate shell is genuinely good. It's 65% cacao with real cocoa butter, so it snaps cleanly when you bite into it rather than bending like compound chocolate. That's rare at this price point.

The filling is where things get interesting. It's not the melted, gooey peanut butter center you grew up with. It's drier — think peanut butter mixed with protein powder and a little salt. The texture is closer to a peanut butter fudge than a creamy cup. Some buyers love this; one Amazon reviewer wrote, "Finally a protein chocolate that doesn't taste like a protein bar." Others noted, "The center is a little crumbly, but the dark chocolate makes up for it."

The sweetness comes from stevia and monk fruit. If you're used to sugar-free products, you'll barely notice. If you're not, there's a slight cooling aftertaste on the finish. It's not offensive, but it's there.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The macros dominate the conversation. Across the 220 reviews, the most common theme is people using these to satisfy chocolate cravings without blowing their daily targets. A verified purchase review sums it up: "I keep these in my desk drawer for the 3pm sugar craving. One cup is enough, and I don't feel guilty." Multiple reviewers mention that the 2-cup pack prevents the "I ate the whole bag" problem that plagues regular peanut butter cups.

The dark chocolate quality also gets consistent shout-outs. People who normally hate "diet chocolate" note that the cocoa flavor is legitimately rich, not bitter or chalky.

**Where It Falls Short**

The price is the biggest hurdle. At roughly $1 per cup, you're paying 3-4x what standard peanut butter cups cost. If you're not counting calories or macros, this is a hard sell.

The texture is the second issue. If you're expecting the soft, whipped peanut butter center of a classic cup, you'll be disappointed. It's dryer, denser, and less indulgent. One reviewer put it bluntly: "Good, but not a Reese's replacement. It's its own thing."

Finally, the stevia aftertaste. It's mild, but if you're one of those people who detects sugar alcohols and natural sweeteners from a mile away, you'll notice it on the finish.

**Who Should Buy These**

Buy these if: you're tracking protein or sugar intake, you want a portion-controlled chocolate fix, or you actually prefer darker, less sweet chocolate.

Skip these if: you want a decadent, classic peanut butter cup experience, you're on a tight budget, or you hate stevia/monk fruit aftertaste.

**FAQ**

**Are these actually healthier than regular peanut butter cups?**
Yes, significantly. A standard Reese's cup has 88 calories, 5g sugar, and 1.5g protein. SKINNYDIPPED has 60 calories, 1g sugar, and 5g protein. The protein boost comes from added whey and milk protein in the filling.

**Do they melt in shipping?**
They can. The dark chocolate coating has a higher melting point than milk chocolate, but during summer months, Amazon reviewers report occasional melting. If you're ordering in hot weather, consider buying from a local retailer instead.

**How do these compare to other SKINNYDIPPED products?**
These cups are significantly better than their almond bark or pretzel offerings. The dark chocolate-to-filling ratio is better, and the protein addition actually works here rather than making things chalky.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a legitimately good dark chocolate treat that fits into a macro-conscious diet and you don't mind paying a premium for portion control.

**Skip it if:** You want a classic peanut butter cup experience, hate sugar substitutes, or aren't willing to spend $1 per small cup.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — These aren't a Reese's replacement, but they're not trying to be. As a standalone product — a protein-forward, low-sugar dark chocolate peanut butter cup — they deliver. The chocolate is quality, the macros are real, and the portion control is built-in. Just know what you're buying before you do.
