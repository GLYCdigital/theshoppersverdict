If you've ever opened a jar of tahini and spent five minutes fighting a brick of separated paste at the bottom, you already know the pain. Soom Foods claims to have solved that problem with their Premium Tahini — a single-origin Ethiopian sesame product that's become a cult favorite among home cooks. But at roughly double the price of supermarket brands, is it actually worth the upgrade? Here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The 11-ounce jar arrives with a clean, minimalist label and a foil safety seal. Inside is a pale, ivory-colored paste that's noticeably thinner than standard tahini — closer to a thick salad dressing than a nut butter. The ingredient list is exactly one item: roasted Ethiopian sesame seeds. No salt, no oil, no stabilizers. That's the whole story.

The texture is where Soom differentiates itself. Most tahini brands require vigorous stirring to reincorporate the oil that separates during storage. Soom's is ground so finely that it pours straight from the jar. This isn't just convenient — it changes how you cook. Dressings emulsify faster, hummus comes together in a food processor without the dreaded clumping, and you can drizzle it directly over roasted vegetables without warming it first.

**How It Actually Performs**

I tested this across three uses: classic hummus, a lemon-tahini dressing, and straight off a spoon. The flavor is the first thing you notice — it's nutty and slightly sweet with none of the bitter aftertaste that cheap tahini often carries. The Ethiopian sesame seeds are roasted more lightly than Middle Eastern varieties, which gives it a milder, almost buttery profile. If you're used to bold, aggressive tahini, this will taste subtle by comparison.

The hummus came out exceptionally creamy — the fine grind means no graininess, and you need less oil to achieve a silky texture. The dressing emulsified in about thirty seconds with just a whisk. For baking, it works as a straight substitute for peanut butter in cookies, though you'll want to add a touch more sweetener since it's less sweet.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Reading through the 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, a few themes dominate. Over and over, reviewers mention the consistency. One buyer wrote, "Finally a tahini that doesn't separate. I've thrown away so many jars of sludge — this one is smooth from first use to last." Another noted, "The flavor is so clean, you can taste the sesame itself rather than the bitterness of over-roasting."

Multiple reviewers also call out the packaging. The jar is sturdy glass with a wide mouth, which means you can actually get a spoon in there to scrape the last bits out — a minor detail that matters more than you'd think.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The price per ounce is steep — typically $15 to $18 for an 11-ounce jar, which is roughly double what you'd pay for a decent supermarket brand. If you go through tahini slowly or use it only occasionally, you'll be paying a premium for freshness you might not fully appreciate.

There's also a recurring complaint about shipping. Several reviewers report the foil seal arriving broken or the lid slightly loose, likely due to temperature changes during transit. The product itself is fine, but if you're ordering multiple jars, check the seals immediately upon arrival.

Finally, the mild flavor cuts both ways. If you're making a bold, garlicky hummus or a spicy sauce, the subtlety of Soom's tahini might get lost. You're paying for nuance — and nuance doesn't always survive a heavy hand with cumin.

**Who Should Buy This**

This jar is for the home cook who makes tahini-based dishes at least weekly. If you're a hummus enthusiast, a salad-dressing regular, or a baker who experiments with seed butters, the consistency alone justifies the price. It's also excellent for anyone who's been burned by gritty, bitter tahini and wants to reset their baseline.

Skip it if you're a casual user who buys tahini twice a year for a recipe, or if you prefer the aggressive, bitter edge of traditional Middle Eastern tahini. The mild flavor won't satisfy that craving.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this need refrigeration after opening?**
A: No, but storing it in the fridge will extend freshness. Keep it in a cool, dark cabinet and it'll last several months. If you refrigerate it, the texture thickens slightly — let it sit at room temperature for ten minutes before using.

**Q: Is this gluten-free and vegan?**
A: Yes on both counts. The only ingredient is roasted sesame seeds, so it's naturally free of gluten, dairy, and animal products.

**Q: How does this compare to Whole Foods or Trader Joe's tahini?**
A: It's smoother and less bitter than both. The TJ's version is a solid budget pick, but Soom's finer grind makes a noticeable difference in emulsified sauces and dips.

**The Verdict**

Soom Foods Premium Tahini delivers exactly what it promises: a smooth, clean-tasting tahini that works effortlessly in any application. The price stings, but for frequent users, the convenience and flavor justify the premium.

**Buy it if** you cook with tahini regularly and want the best texture you can get without DIY grinding.
**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget or prefer the bold, bitter style of traditional tahini.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the price and the occasional shipping seal issue, but earns every bit of its reputation for quality.
