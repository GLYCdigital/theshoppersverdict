You remember Razzles. The candy that *becomes* gum. It's been a novelty since 1966, and this bulk box of 24 individual 1.4-ounce bags delivers exactly what the packaging promises — a fistful of those pastel discs that start as a crunchy, slightly chalky candy and morph into a soft, sweet gum as you chew.

Here's the thing: Razzles isn't trying to be the most intense candy experience you've ever had. It's trying to be a *fun* candy experience, and it nails that. The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings reflects that this product knows exactly what it is — a nostalgic, shareable treat that doesn't overpromise.

---

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find 24 separate 1.4-ounce bags. Each bag holds roughly 15-18 pieces, depending on how the factory filled them. That's about 360-400 candies total, which makes this a solid value for bulk buyers — think classroom parties, office candy bowls, or stocking stuffers that need to go to multiple people.

The individual bags are a genuine advantage here. You're not committing to one giant pouch that goes stale after two weeks. Each bag is sealed and holds its freshness until you open it. As shown in the product image, the packaging is bright, retro, and immediately recognizable — the red, white, and blue design hasn't changed much in decades, and that's part of the charm.

---

## How It Actually Performs

The experience is the selling point. Pop a Razzle in your mouth, and for the first 5-10 seconds you're chewing through a hard, slightly chalky candy shell. Then it softens, and suddenly — *boom* — you're chewing gum. It's a cheap magic trick, but it works every single time.

The flavors are the standard lineup: cherry, grape, lemon, orange, and lime. They're sweet but not overpowering, with a fruitiness that leans more "classic candy" than "hyper-realistic juice." One thing to note: the flavor doesn't linger long once the gum forms. Expect maybe 3-4 minutes of decent flavor before the gum goes bland and starts to stiffen. That's not a dealbreaker — it's just a reminder that this is a novelty candy, not a long-lasting gum substitute.

The texture transition is the real star. The initial crunch gives way to a chew that's soft and pliable, and the gum itself has a pleasant, slightly dense mouthfeel. It's not juicy or wet like some modern gums — it's drier, closer to a classic bubble gum base.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. If you're expecting a flavor punch like a modern Warhead or a juicy, long-lasting gum like Extra, you'll be disappointed. The flavor is mild, and the gum phase is short-lived. It's also worth noting that the pieces can vary in hardness — occasionally you'll get one that's slightly softer out of the bag, which shortens the candy phase almost immediately.

The 1.4-ounce bags are convenient, but they're not resealable. You'll want to finish a bag in one sitting or transfer leftovers to a zip-top container. Given that each bag holds roughly 15 pieces, that's rarely a problem — but it's worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're planning a party, need classroom treats, or want a nostalgic candy experience to share with kids. This is also a smart pick for anyone who enjoys retro candy and wants a bulk supply that'll last months.

**Skip it if** you're looking for intense flavor or a gum that lasts through a long car ride. This is a novelty candy, not a serious gum — and it's best enjoyed as a fun, occasional treat rather than an everyday snack.
