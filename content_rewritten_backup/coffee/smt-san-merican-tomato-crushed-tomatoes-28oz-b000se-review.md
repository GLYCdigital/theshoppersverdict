## Performance and Real-World Use

The first thing you'll notice when you crack open the can is the color — a deep, brick-red that signals real ripeness. The aroma hits next: sweet, slightly tangy, and unmistakably tomato-forward. The texture is where SMT sets itself apart. This is *crushed* tomato, not puree. You get visible chunks and a thick, almost rustic body that holds up beautifully in long simmers.

I tested this in a weekend bolognese — three hours on low heat. The sauce reduced into something glossy and rich without turning watery or separating. That's the mark of a high-quality pack: it doesn't break down into mush. For pizza sauce, it's even better. Spread it thin, and you get a concentrated tomato flavor that doesn't turn the crust soggy.

The salt level is modest. You'll still need to season to taste, which is a plus for anyone watching sodium. There's no added sugar or citric acid — two common additives in cheaper brands that mask underripe fruit. San Merican doesn't need them.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be real about the tradeoffs. The 28-ounce can is generous — if you're cooking for one or two people, you'll need to freeze half or commit to a week of tomato-heavy meals. There's no resealable lid, so plan your storage ahead. The price per ounce also runs higher than generic store brands. You're paying for quality, and it shows, but budget shoppers will feel the difference at checkout.

One more note: if you prefer completely smooth tomato sauce, this isn't it. The crushed texture is intentional, and while some cooks love it, others may need to run it through a food mill for a silkier result.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Is this the same as San Marzano tomatoes?**
No. SMT San Merican is a California-grown product, not imported from Italy's San Marzano region. It's a different variety and growing region, but the quality is comparable to premium imported brands — often better, since it doesn't suffer from long shipping times.

**Can I use this in place of tomato paste?**
Not as a direct substitute. Crushed tomatoes are much thinner than paste. If a recipe calls for paste, you'll need to reduce this down significantly or adjust your liquid ratios.

**How should I store an opened can?**
Transfer leftovers to an airtight container and refrigerate. It'll keep for about 4-5 days. For longer storage, freeze in portion-sized containers or ice cube trays — it thaws well and retains most of its texture.
