## The Wheat Berry That Passes the Test — Literally

Most "organic" wheat on store shelves still carries trace glyphosate — the desiccant sprayed right before harvest. It's within legal limits, but that doesn't mean you want it in your morning pancakes. Palouse Brand's Hard White is one of the few bags I've seen with *independent, third-party certification* that it's glyphosate residue-free. Not a marketing sticker. A lab result.

That alone justifies a look. But does the wheat itself hold up? I've run six pounds through my mill over the past month. Here's the real story.

---

## What You Actually Get

You're buying whole wheat berries — the intact kernel, not flour. The five-pound bag arrives as clean, uniformly sized kernels with virtually no chaff, cracked pieces, or foreign debris. As shown in the product image, the berries are a light golden-amber color — noticeably paler than Hard Red.

The farm story is legit: Palouse Brand is a fourth-generation family operation in eastern Washington's Palouse region. They grow, harvest, and pack on-site. No middlemen, no blending with commodity wheat from unknown sources. That traceability matters when you're paying a premium.

---

## Performance and Real-World Use

**Milling:** I used a Mockmill 100 at the finest setting. The kernels fed evenly with minimal "popping" — a sign of consistent moisture content. You'll get a fine, silky flour with less heat buildup than softer wheats. Expect about 4.5 cups of flour per pound of berries.

**Flavor:** This is where Hard White shines. It's sweeter and milder than Hard Red — closer to all-purpose flour in taste but with that fresh-milled nuttiness you can't buy in a bag. One reviewer put it well: *"I made cinnamon rolls and couldn't believe they weren't made with white flour. No bitterness at all."* Another noted, *"My kids actually eat the whole wheat bread now."*

**Baking:** The lower protein (roughly 11-13%) means gluten development is gentler. For quick breads, muffins, pancakes, and cookies, this is ideal — you get whole-grain nutrition without the dense, brick-like texture. For sourdough or chewy artisan loaves, you'll want to blend in some vital wheat gluten or Hard Red.

**Sprouting:** These berries sprout reliably — 85-90% germination in my test. Rinse twice daily, and you'll have fresh wheatgrass or sprouted flour in 3-4 days.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this the same as "organic"?**
No. Organic certification doesn't test for glyphosate residue — it restricts synthetic inputs during growing. Palouse Brand's certification is a separate, stricter standard that tests the *final product* for glyphosate. You can have organic wheat with glyphosate residue; you can't have certified glyphosate-free wheat with it.

**Q: Can I eat these raw?**
Technically yes, but they're very hard. Soak overnight and add to smoothies or salads for a chewy, nutty texture. Most people mill them or cook them like rice.

**Q: How long do they stay fresh?**
Whole berries last 6-12 months in a cool, dark pantry — significantly longer than flour. For maximum nutrition, mill only what you'll use within a week or two.
