## How It Performs in Real Use

Here's where the True Citrus Shakers separate themselves from the gimmick pile.

**In coffee:** A single shake into a hot cup of black coffee dissolves in seconds. No stirring required, no gritty residue at the bottom. The lemon and orange varieties add a brightness that cuts through bitter roasts without needing sugar. It's not a syrup — it won't sweeten anything — but it transforms a boring cup into something that tastes intentional.

**In cooking:** This is the sleeper use case. A few shakes over roasted vegetables, into a vinaigrette, or rubbed onto chicken before searing gives you that acid pop without adding liquid. Since it's sodium-free, it's a legitimate substitute for lemon pepper seasoning or citrus salt blends when you're watching salt intake.

**In cold drinks:** I tested it in sparkling water and iced tea. It dissolves cleanly in cold liquid too, which is rare for powdered flavorings. Most need hot water to activate. This doesn't.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest downsides:

**Portion size.** At 4.2 ounces per bottle, heavy users will blow through the lemon in two to three weeks. That's fine for trying it out, but you'll want to find the bulk refill bags once you know your favorite.

**Tartness dial is unforgiving.** The lime and grapefruit varieties are genuinely tart — more than fresh juice, because the concentration is higher. A heavy hand will make your dish puckeringly sour. Start with a half-shake and adjust up. This isn't a flaw, but it's a learning curve.

**Not a sweetener.** If you're hoping to replace sugar in your coffee, this won't do it. It adds acidity and brightness, not sweetness. Pair it with a zero-calorie sweetener if that's your goal.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Is this the same as True Lemon (the single-flavor packets)?**
A: Same core product concept, but the shaker format is more practical for daily use, and you get four flavors instead of being locked into one. The powder composition is comparable.

**Q: Can I use these in baking to replace lemon zest?**
A: Yes, but adjust quantities. One teaspoon of powder roughly equals the zest of one lemon. Add it to dry ingredients so it distributes evenly — don't sprinkle it into wet batter.

**Q: Does it clump in humid environments?**
A: The flip cap seals well, but any powder will clump if stored above the stove or near a steamer. Keep the cap sealed and store in a cabinet, not on the counter next to the kettle.
