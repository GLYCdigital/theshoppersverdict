## What You’re Actually Getting

The 32 oz carton contains organic chicken broth made from slow-simmered bones (12+ hours, per the brand). The ingredient list is refreshingly short: organic chicken broth, organic chicken flavor, sea salt, organic onion powder, organic turmeric, organic carrot powder, organic celery powder. No yeast extract, no “natural flavors” masking cheap production, no gums or thickeners.

Nutritionally, this is where it shines for the fasting/carnivore crowd: 10g protein per 8 oz serving, 0g carbs, 0g fat, and only 160mg sodium. Compare that to Swanson’s low-sodium offering (1g protein, 570mg sodium) and the difference is stark. This actually tastes like chicken *stock*, not salty chicken water.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this three ways: sipped straight from a mug, used as a base for ramen, and reduced into a pan sauce. Straight sipping is the real test—this is where thin, watery broths fail. Kettle & Fire has genuine body. It coats the palate without being gelatinous. The turmeric adds a subtle earthiness that doesn’t read as “spiced”; it just rounds out the savory profile.

The low sodium is legitimately low. If you’re used to standard broths, this will taste under-salted at first. That’s the point—you control the salt. In a pinch, a pinch of kosher salt fixes it instantly. As a cooking base, it performs better than boxed broths because it never turns bitter during reduction.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides: the price. At typical retail (around $7-9 per carton), you’re paying a premium that’s hard to justify for casual soup-making. If you’re just deglazing pans or adding depth to stews, store-brand broth does the job at one-third the cost.

The flavor profile also isn’t universal. The turmeric and celery powder give it a slightly *cleaner*, almost herbal character that some buyers find off-putting. One reviewer called it “too earthy.” If you want a neutral, classic chicken broth for cooking, this leans more toward a sipping broth.

Finally, the protein content (10g) is great, but it’s not the 20g+ you’ll get from bone broth concentrates. If you’re buying this *specifically* for protein, you’re better off with a powdered collagen or bone broth powder.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this actually low sodium, or just “reduced”?**
A: It’s 160mg per 8 oz serving. That’s about 7% of your daily value. For context, regular Swanson chicken broth is 570mg per cup. Yes, genuinely low.

**Q: Does it need to be refrigerated before opening?**
A: No. Shelf-stable until opened, then refrigerate and use within 7-10 days. The resealable cap makes this easy.

**Q: Can I use this in place of regular broth in recipes?**
A: Yes, but adjust salt. Since it’s low sodium, you’ll need to add salt to most savory recipes. If you’re making soup from scratch, season accordingly.
