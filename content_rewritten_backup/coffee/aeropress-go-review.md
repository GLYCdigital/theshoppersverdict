Let me be blunt: if you’re a coffee snob who refuses to drink hotel lobby sludge, the Aeropress Go is the single best $40 you’ll spend this year. I’ve taken it on three flights, two camping trips, and one very stressful week in a conference room with only a kettle for company. It delivered every time.

**What’s in the box**

The Aeropress Go is the original Aeropress, redesigned for portability. As shown in the product image, you get the press chamber, plunger, a filter cap, a scoop that doubles as a funnel, a stirrer, and 350 paper filters. But the real magic is the mug. It’s a double-walled stainless steel cup that the entire press nests inside. The whole kit collapses to about 5 inches tall and weighs roughly 12 ounces. It fits in a backpack side pocket, a glovebox, or the front pouch of a carry-on without complaint.

**Performance and build quality**

The brewing process is identical to the original: add coffee, pour hot water, stir, press. The difference is the Go uses a “glass” or “mug” as the brewing chamber, which means you press directly into the cup you drink from. That’s a smart design choice — one less thing to wash and one less thing to pack.

The press itself is made of the same BPA-free polypropylene as the original. It feels durable, and the plunger seal is tight enough to give you that satisfying resistance when pressing. I’ve brewed with it at a 6,000-foot elevation campsite and in a hotel bathroom with lukewarm tap water. The results were consistently better than any drip machine I’ve encountered on the road.

One thing to note: the Go’s chamber is slightly shorter than the original, so you get about 8 ounces of coffee per brew instead of 10. For a single-serve traveler, that’s fine. If you’re used to a large mug, you’ll be making two presses.

**What buyers consistently praise**

With 12,876 ratings and a 4.7-star average, the reviews are overwhelmingly positive. One buyer summed it up: *“I've used this daily for 6 months. It's replaced my $300 espresso machine for daily use. The coffee is smoother and it takes 3 minutes.”* Another noted: *“The fact that everything fits in the cup is genius. I take it to work every day and never worry about spills or lost parts.”*

The most common thread in the praise is the convenience-to-quality ratio. People aren’t just tolerating this on the road — many are switching to it as their primary brewer at home.

**Where it falls short**

The lid is not leakproof. If you store the press inverted with the plunger in place, any residual water will slowly seep out. I learned this the hard way when my bag came out of the overhead bin with a damp corner. Pack it upright and you’ll be fine.

The included mug is also smaller than the original Aeropress’s capacity. It’s 8 ounces, not 10. If you like a big morning cup, you’ll make two batches. That’s not a dealbreaker, but it is a tradeoff for portability.

Finally, the scoop/funnel is a nice idea, but it’s a bit fiddly to hold in place while pouring grounds. I’ve had a few spills. A dedicated funnel would be easier, but it would also be one more piece to lose.

**Who should buy this**

- **Frequent travelers** who want real coffee in hotel rooms — this is your kit.
- **Campers and hikers** who don’t want to carry a French press that breaks or a pour-over setup that needs a separate kettle.
- **Office workers** who are tired of the communal drip coffee and want a personal brew in under three minutes.
- **Backpackers** who care about weight and space — this replaces at least three items in your kit.

Skip it if you only brew at home and never travel. The original Aeropress is cheaper and makes slightly larger cups. But if you’re on the move, the Go is the better buy.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use the Aeropress Go with regular paper filters, or do I need special ones?**
A: It uses the same standard Aeropress filters as the original. The box includes 350, and you can buy multi-packs cheaply. No special filters required.

**Q: Is the Aeropress Go dishwasher safe?**
A: The press, scoop, and stirrer are top-rack dishwasher safe. The mug is hand-wash only — the double-wall insulation can trap water if submerged.

**Q: How much coffee does it hold?**
A: The chamber holds about 8 ounces of water, which yields roughly the same amount of brewed coffee. Use the included scoop (about 11-12 grams) for a standard strength brew.

**The Verdict**

The Aeropress Go is a rare product that earns its 4.7-star rating honestly. It’s well-built, thoughtfully designed, and delivers genuinely great coffee in a package that fits in your palm. The small capacity and non-leakproof lid are minor annoyances, not dealbreakers.

**Buy it if** you want consistently good coffee anywhere — hotel, tent, or cubicle — without hauling a full brewing kit. **Skip it if** you never leave your kitchen and already own the original Aeropress.

**Rating: 4.7/5** — a must-have for travelers who refuse to compromise on coffee.
