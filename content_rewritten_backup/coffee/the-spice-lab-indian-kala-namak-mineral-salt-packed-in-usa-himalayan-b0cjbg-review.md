If you've ever tried to make a convincing tofu scramble and ended up with something that tasted like spiced cardboard, the missing ingredient is probably kala namak. This isn't a coffee maker or gear — it's the kind of pantry staple that changes how you cook, and The Spice Lab's version is one of the more reliable options on Amazon with 229 ratings holding a 4.6-star average.

**What you actually get**

A 3.5-ounce resealable jar of finely ground Indian black salt. Despite the name, the crystals are actually a pale pinkish-brown when ground — the "black" refers to the whole mineral before processing. The grind here is noticeably finer than some competitors, which matters because it dissolves quickly into hot dishes. No gritty crunch in your chana masala.

**How it performs in real cooking**

The defining characteristic of kala namak is its sulfuric profile — it smells like eggs, and that's precisely why vegans and vegetarians use it. The Spice Lab's batch has a robust, unapologetic sulfur aroma that carries through cooking. In a tofu scramble with a pinch of turmeric, it genuinely mimics the flavor of scrambled eggs. It also works in avocado toast, salad dressings, and even sprinkled over fresh fruit — a common Indian street-food trick with mango or papaya.

One buyer noted: "I was skeptical, but this is the real deal. The smell is strong out of the jar, but once cooked into a dish it mellows and gives that authentic eggy taste." Another mentioned: "Better than the rock-hard chunks I got from another brand — this is ready to use straight from the jar."

**Where it falls short**

The fine grind is mostly a strength, but it means the salt is potent. A heavy-handed pour can turn a dish sulfurous in a way that's hard to fix. Start small. Also worth flagging: kala namak is not iodized, so it shouldn't replace your regular table salt entirely if you depend on it for iodine. Finally, the 3.5-ounce size is modest — if you cook South Asian food weekly, you'll reorder in a couple of months.

**Who should buy this**

- **Vegans and vegetarians** who want egg flavor without eggs — this is the single best plant-based ingredient for that purpose.
- **Home cooks exploring Indian cuisine** who need authentic chaat masala or raita seasoning.
- **Anyone on a low-sodium diet** looking for a salt that delivers more flavor per pinch than standard table salt.

Skip it if you can't tolerate strong sulfur aromas — the smell out of the jar is genuinely intense, and it lingers on your hands after handling.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does this taste like regular salt?*  
Not really. It's salty, but the dominant note is sulfurous and egg-like. If you're looking for a neutral finishing salt, this isn't it.

*How much should I use?*  
Start with 1/4 teaspoon for a tofu scramble serving two. You can always add more, but you can't subtract once it's in the pan.

*Is it the same as Himalayan pink salt?*  
No. Himalayan pink salt is mostly sodium chloride with trace minerals. Kala namak contains sulfur compounds that give it that distinctive eggy aroma. They're not interchangeable in recipes.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're serious about plant-based cooking or want to add authentic depth to Indian dishes. **Skip it if** you're sensitive to sulfur smells or need an all-purpose iodized salt.
