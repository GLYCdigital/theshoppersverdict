**What you actually get**

One 8-ounce bag of whole green cardamom pods (Elettaria cardamomum). As shown in the product image, the pods are a pale green, slightly wrinkled, and look like what you'd find in a high-end spice market — not the crushed, faded stuff in supermarket jars. Open the bag and the aroma hits immediately: floral, sweet, with that unmistakable eucalyptus-like coolness. The pods are mostly intact, which matters because the essential oils live inside the seeds. Pre-ground cardamom loses its punch within weeks; these stay potent for months.

**Performance in the cup**

I crushed six pods per 12-ounce cup of pour-over and another batch into a stovetop chai. The difference is obvious. The pods crack open cleanly under a mortar and pestle, releasing dark, oily seeds that grind into a fragrant powder in seconds. In coffee, the cardamom reads as a bright, citrusy spice that cuts through bitterness without dominating. In chai, it does what cardamom should — it threads through the milk and tea, sweet and warming, not harsh or medicinal. One buyer put it simply: "Finally, cardamom that actually smells like cardamom. My chai went from good to incredible." Another noted, "These pods are fat and oily — you can tell they're fresh. I've reordered three times."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The freshness is the headline. Multiple reviewers mention the pods arrive whole with minimal crushing, and the oil content is visibly higher than grocery-store options. The value math works too: at roughly 400 pods per bag, you're looking at pennies per cup. **Where it falls short**

Honesty time. A few reviewers flagged inconsistency between batches — some bags have a higher ratio of lighter, thinner pods that pack less punch. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're buying in bulk expecting identical quality every time, know that natural products vary. Also, the bag isn't resealable. Unless you transfer the pods to an airtight jar, they'll lose potency faster than they should. That's a minor design flaw, not a product flaw.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use these in a coffee grinder?**
Yes, but clean the grinder afterward or your next batch of beans will taste like chai. A dedicated spice grinder or mortar and pestle is better for preserving the oils.

**Q: How should I store them?**
Transfer to an airtight glass jar in a cool, dark place. They'll stay potent for up to a year. Avoid the original bag unless you're going through them fast.

**Q: How many pods per cup of coffee?**
Start with 3-4 pods crushed per 12-ounce cup. Adjust to taste — cardamom is strong, and a little goes a long way.
