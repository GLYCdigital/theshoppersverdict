## What's Actually In The Box

As shown in the product image, you're getting a hefty box of stand-up pouches, each holding 1.5 oz (43g) of trail mix. The blend is classic: roasted peanuts, raisins, M&Ms, sunflower seeds, and cashews. Nothing exotic, nothing weird — just the five ingredients that make trail mix a universal crowd-pleaser. The resealable pouches are a thoughtful touch; you can rip one open, snack on half, and save the rest without it going stale.

## Real-World Performance

I've been through three boxes of this stuff, and here's what stands out. The mix ratio is surprisingly fair. Too many bulk trail mixes skimp on the M&Ms and cashews, leaving you with a sad raisin-and-peanut pile. Kirkland's blend has enough chocolate and cashews that every handful feels balanced — you're not digging for the good stuff.

The portion size is genuinely smart. At 1.5 oz, each packet lands around 210-220 calories (depending on the exact batch). That's enough to kill a hunger pang without blowing your daily calorie budget. For hikers, it's a solid single-serving fuel stop. For office workers, it's a desk-drawer lifesaver that doesn't invite mindless overeating.

The peanuts are consistently crunchy, the raisins are soft and plump, and the M&Ms hold up reasonably well — though see the caveat below. In three boxes, I've had one dud packet with a stale cashew, which feels like acceptable variance for a bulk product at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, shipping can be brutal on chocolate. If the box takes a hard drop, you'll open pouches to find M&M dust mixed into the mix. It still tastes fine, but the texture suffers. This is a known complaint in the reviews, so temper your expectations.

Second, this is a nut-heavy product. If you're buying for a nut-free classroom, office, or daycare, this is an absolute no-go. The label is clear, but it's worth stating plainly: peanuts and tree nuts are everywhere in this box.

Third, the 1.5 oz serving is genuinely small. For a 200-pound adult with a real appetite, one packet is a tease. You'll likely grab two, which doubles the cost-per-snack. It's portion control, yes — but for some, it's *too* much control.
