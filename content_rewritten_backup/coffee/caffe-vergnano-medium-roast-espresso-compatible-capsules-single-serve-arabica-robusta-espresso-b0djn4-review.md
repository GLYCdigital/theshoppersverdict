If you've been stuck in the Lavazza or Illy S-capsule rut, Caffe Vergnano's Medium Roast is the Italian heritage play that actually delivers on its 1882 pedigree. This is a Torino-based roaster that's been at it for nearly 150 years, and it shows in the cup — but you'll pay a premium for that history.

## What you're actually getting

The box contains 20 aluminum capsules in the standard "S" format — the same size used by Lavazza's A Modo Mio machines, Nescafé's Dolce Gusto (with adapter), and Bosch Tassimo models. Each capsule holds 7 grams of a 60% Arabica / 40% Robusta blend, which is the classic Italian espresso ratio. The aluminum construction is a real differentiator here: unlike plastic pods that go stale within weeks, these stay sealed and fresh for months on the shelf.

## The cup: where it shines

I pulled these through a Lavazza A Modo Mio machine at the standard 40ml setting. The crema is the headline act — thick, persistent, and tinted a deep hazelnut brown. You get that classic Italian biscotti-and-cocoa aroma on the nose, and the flavor follows through with a medium body that's balanced rather than bitter. The Robusta component gives it enough caffeine kick to wake you up without the harshness you get from cheaper blends.

One thing worth noting: this is a true medium roast, not the dark, almost-burnt profile many American drinkers associate with espresso. If you're used to Starbucks' over-roasted beans, this will taste almost delicate by comparison. That's a feature, not a bug — but know what you're buying.

## What buyers consistently say

Scrolling through the 204 ratings (4.1 stars average), the praise centers on the same things I found. One reviewer noted, "Finally an S-capsule that doesn't taste like it was roasted last decade — the crema is genuinely restaurant-quality." Another called it "the closest thing to a real Italian bar espresso I've found in a pod." The consistency complaints are predictable too: several mention that the capsules work perfectly in Lavazza machines but have occasional fit issues in third-party brewers.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the price — these run meaningfully higher per capsule than Lavazza's own S-pods, and the quality gap doesn't fully justify the premium. Second, the Robusta content means slightly higher acidity than pure Arabica blends; if you have a sensitive stomach, consider the dark roast version instead. Third, there's no strength marking on the packaging, which is an annoying omission for a product aimed at espresso purists.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You own an S-system machine, you value authentic Italian espresso character over cost-per-cup, and you want capsules that stay fresh longer than plastic alternatives.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight coffee budget, you prefer light roasts with fruity notes (this is not that), or you use a machine that isn't strictly S-compatible — the fit issues aren't worth the hassle.

## FAQ

**Will these fit my Nespresso machine?**
No. These are S-format capsules, not Nespresso OriginalLine or Vertuo. They fit Lavazza A Modo Mio, Nescafé Dolce Gusto (with the included adapter), and select Bosch models.

**Are these capsules recyclable?**
Yes, the aluminum shells are recyclable. Rinse them out and drop them in your standard aluminum recycling bin.

**How does this compare to Lavazza's S-pods?**
The Vergnano has a more pronounced crema and a slightly more complex flavor profile, but Lavazza is roughly 20% cheaper per capsule. If budget matters more than subtle taste differences, stick with Lavazza.

## The Verdict

This is a 4.2-star product: excellent flavor and crema, genuinely fresh packaging, and a legitimate Italian heritage — but with a price premium and a roast profile that won't suit everyone. If you're an espresso enthusiast who wants the closest thing to a Torino café experience from a single-serve machine, this is your capsule. If you're looking for the best value per cup, look elsewhere.

**Buy it if** you want premium Italian espresso character in an S-capsule. **Skip it if** price-per-cup is your primary metric.
