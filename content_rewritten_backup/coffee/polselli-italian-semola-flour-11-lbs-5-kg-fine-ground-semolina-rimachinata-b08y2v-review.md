## What you actually get

The bag is substantial — 11 pounds of fine ground semolina rimacinata, which is Italian for "re-milled." That double milling is the entire point. Standard semolina is gritty; rimacinata is milled twice to achieve a flour-like fineness. As shown in the product image, this isn't the coarse yellow meal you'd use for polenta — it's a smooth, pale golden powder that feels almost like cake flour between your fingers. The 5 kg bag arrives sealed and sturdy, though it's a big bag, so plan your storage before you rip it open.

## Performance in the kitchen

I've tested this flour in fresh pasta, bread, and even pizza dough. The protein content sits around 12.5%, which is the sweet spot for pasta — high enough to give structure, low enough to avoid the rubber-band toughness you get from high-gluten bread flour. ## Where it falls short

Honesty time. This is a bulk bag, and that's a double-edged sword. If you're a casual baker who makes pasta once a month, 11 pounds is a lot of flour to store. You'll want an airtight container in a cool, dark pantry. Also, it's not organic. If you specifically require certified organic grains, skip this and pay the premium elsewhere.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as regular semolina flour?**
No. This is semolina rimacinata, which is milled twice for a much finer texture. Regular semolina is coarse and gritty — better for couscous or some bread crusts, not for silky pasta dough.

**Can I use this in a bread machine?**
Yes, but not at 100%. Use it as 30-50% of your total flour blend for a tender crumb and crisp crust. Pure semolina dough is dense and doesn't rise as well on its own.

**How should I store 11 pounds of flour?**
Transfer it to a large airtight container with a tight lid. Keep it in a cool, dark pantry. If you live in a humid climate, consider freezing a portion — semolina freezes well and thaws without texture loss.
