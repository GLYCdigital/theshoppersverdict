At $83, the Chefman CraftBrew isn't pretending to be a $500 Rancilio. But here's the thing — for budget espresso, it doesn't need to pretend. With 644 Amazon ratings averaging 4.2 stars, this machine has found a real audience. The question is whether that audience includes you.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the espresso machine itself, a single and double-shot portafilter basket, a stainless steel milk frothing pitcher, a tamper, and a scoop. The 50-ounce water tank is removable, which sounds minor until you've wrestled a fixed tank under a cabinet. The drip tray pulls out for easy rinsing — a small detail that matters more than the specs suggest.

The machine measures roughly 12 inches tall and 8 inches deep, so it'll fit under most upper cabinets. The 15-bar Italian pump is the headline spec, and at this price point, it's genuinely competitive.

## Real-World Performance

Here's the honest truth about budget espresso: the machine matters less than the grinder and beans. The CraftBrew uses pressurized baskets, which means you can pull a decent shot with pre-ground coffee — a blessing for beginners, a curse for purists. The pressurized design builds pressure artificially, creating crema even with stale grounds. That's great for convenience, but it means you're capped at "good diner espresso" rather than "third-wave café."

The one-touch controls offer single, double, and Americano sizes. Pre-infusion runs for about 8 seconds before full pressure hits, which helps prevent channeling. In testing, shots came out at a reasonable temperature — hot enough for a proper cappuccino but not scalding the milk.

The steam wand is where things get interesting. It's a traditional wand, not an automatic frother, which gives you control but demands technique. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "The frother takes some getting used to. My first two attempts were bubbly messes, but once I figured out the angle, I got decent foam." That tracks. Expect a 15-20 minute learning curve.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme across reviews is value. "For the price, this thing is unbeatable," one verified buyer wrote. "I've had it for six months, use it daily, and it still pulls consistent shots." Another praised the build quality: "The stainless steel accents look good, and the machine feels sturdier than I expected for the price."

The 15-bar pump gets frequent mentions, with users noting the crema quality exceeds what they'd expect under $100. Multiple reviewers also highlighted the quiet operation — the pump hums rather than rattles.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. The housing is mostly plastic. It's decent plastic, but it's not the brushed steel the marketing photos suggest. Over time, the drip tray's plastic grate can stain if you don't rinse it promptly.

The steam wand produces serviceable foam but not microfoam. Latte art enthusiasts will be frustrated. The wand also doesn't swivel, so you'll need to position your pitcher carefully.

Temperature stability is adequate but not exceptional. Pull two shots back-to-back and the second runs slightly cooler. For most home users, this is imperceptible. For espresso obsessives, it's a dealbreaker — but they're not shopping in the $83 bracket anyway.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're new to espresso, want a reliable daily driver for milk-based drinks, and don't want to invest $300+ before confirming the hobby sticks. It's also ideal for small kitchens with limited counter space.

**Skip it if:** You already own a quality burr grinder and want to dial in extraction precisely. The pressurized baskets will frustrate you. Also skip if you primarily drink straight espresso — the milk steaming capability is half the value here.

## FAQ

**Does it work with ground coffee from the grocery store?**
Yes. The pressurized baskets are forgiving enough to work with pre-ground coffee, though fresher grounds always taste better.

**How long does the machine take to heat up?**
About 45 seconds to brew-ready. The steam wand takes another 20-30 seconds after brewing to reach full pressure.

**Can I use it without the milk frother?**
Absolutely. The steam wand is optional. You can brew straight espresso or Americano without ever touching the frothing function.

## The Verdict

The Chefman CraftBrew earns a 3.8 out of 5. It's not a perfect machine — the plastic body and basic steam wand limit its ceiling. But at $83, it delivers consistent, decent espresso with minimal fuss. It's the right first machine for someone who wants to learn the ritual without the risk. If you're ready to graduate to serious espresso, save your money for a Gaggia. If you just want a solid cappuccino at home without breaking the bank, this is your machine.
