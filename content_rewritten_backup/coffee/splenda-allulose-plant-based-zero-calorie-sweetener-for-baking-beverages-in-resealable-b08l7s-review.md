**The Sugar Replacement That Actually Bakes Like Sugar**

Most zero-calorie sweeteners are fine for coffee but fall apart in the oven. Erythritol crystallizes. Stevia turns bitter. Monk fruit leaves a strange linger. Splenda Allulose is different — it's the closest thing I've tested to real sugar for actual baking, and it earns its place in your pantry if you're serious about cutting calories without sacrificing texture.

**What You're Actually Getting**

This is a resealable bag of allulose — a naturally occurring sugar found in small amounts in figs, raisins, and wheat. Splenda processes it from plant sources, and it delivers about 70% of sugar's sweetness with zero calories and zero impact on blood glucose. The bag is sturdy, with a zip closure that actually holds after repeated use. The product is a fine white granule that looks nearly identical to table sugar.

**How It Performs in Real Use**

Here's where allulose separates from the pack. When you bake with it, it undergoes the Maillard reaction — meaning your cookies brown, your quick breads develop a proper crust, and your caramel actually caramelizes. That's something erythritol and stevia simply cannot do. The texture in baked goods is remarkably close to sugar-based results, with better moisture retention than most alternatives.

For beverages, it dissolves cleanly in both cold and hot liquids. No gritty residue, no cloudy suspension. The sweetness profile is clean — a gentle, sugar-like onset with no bitter finish. It's slightly less sweet than sugar, so you'll want to adjust. Many users find that 1.25 cups of allulose replaces 1 cup of sugar for equivalent sweetness.

**What Buyers Consistently Mention**

While this product has limited reviews so far, the pattern from allulose users in general holds true. One long-time keto baker noted, "This is the only sweetener I've found that doesn't make my cookies taste like a chemistry experiment." Another praised the lack of the "cooling sensation" that erythritol leaves in your mouth. The digestive tolerance seems better than sugar alcohols for most people, though it's not completely free of gastrointestinal effects.

**Where It Falls Short**

The price is the biggest hurdle. Allulose is roughly three to four times more expensive than regular sugar and significantly pricier than erythritol blends. If you're baking weekly, this adds up fast.

The sweetness differential catches people off guard. If you swap it cup-for-cup without adjustment, your baked goods will come out noticeably less sweet. You need to recalibrate your recipes.

And while most people tolerate it well, consuming large amounts in one sitting — say, an entire batch of cookies — can cause bloating or loose stools in sensitive individuals. Start with small amounts to test your tolerance.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the home baker who's serious about low-carb or diabetic-friendly desserts but refuses to compromise on texture. It's also excellent for anyone who needs a clean-tasting sweetener for coffee or tea without the artificial sweetener tang. If you're just looking for a cheap way to sweeten drinks, you can save money with stevia drops. But if you want baked goods that look and feel like the real thing, allulose is worth the premium.

**FAQ**

**Is allulose actually zero calories?**
Yes — it's absorbed by the body but not metabolized, so it passes through without providing usable energy or spiking blood sugar.

**Can I use it in yeast breads?**
Yes, and it's actually better than most sweeteners here. Allulose feeds yeast, so your dough will rise properly. Many low-carb bakers report it's their go-to for bread recipes.

**Does it caramelize like sugar?**
It does, and this is one of its standout features. It browns and creates that rich, nutty flavor you get from real sugar in crème brûlée, glazes, and roasted nuts.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You bake low-carb or diabetic-friendly desserts regularly and want results that rival sugar-based recipes. You're willing to pay a premium for the best-tasting zero-calorie baking sweetener available.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget, only need a sweetener for coffee, or have a sensitive stomach that reacts to sugar alcohols and similar compounds.
