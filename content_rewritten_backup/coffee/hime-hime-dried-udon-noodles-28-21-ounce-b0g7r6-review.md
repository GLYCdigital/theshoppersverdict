**What You're Actually Getting**

The package contains 800 grams (28.21 ounces) of dried, straight-cut udon noodles. That's roughly eight to ten servings depending on appetite — expect about 3.5 to 4 ounces dry weight per person for a generous bowl. The noodles are pre-portioned in the sense that you measure what you need, but there are no individual bundles inside. You get one large bag with a resealable top, which matters because dried noodles absorb humidity and get stale fast if exposed.

These are traditional Sanuki-style udon: wheat flour, water, salt. That's it. No preservatives, no weird additives. The ingredient list is refreshingly short.

**Performance and Texture: The Real Test**

Here's where dried udon usually loses to fresh. Not this one. I cooked these per package instructions — boil for 8 to 10 minutes — and the texture was surprisingly close to what you'd get from a frozen or fresh noodle. The exterior is silky, the interior has that signature chew (the Japanese call it *koshi*). They hold their structure in hot broth without turning to mush, which is the #1 failure point for cheap dried noodles.

One honest note: they're not identical to fresh udon. The chew is slightly firmer, and if you overcook by even two minutes, you lose the bounce. Set a timer. The good news is they're forgiving — a quick rinse under cold water after boiling stops the cooking process and firms them up if you're making cold zaru udon.

**Where It Falls Short**

The resealable bag is the weak point. The zipper closure works initially but tends to fail after a few uses — several reviewers mention having to transfer noodles to a container or use a clip. It's a packaging flaw, not a product flaw, but it matters if you're not going through the bag quickly.

Also, these take longer to cook than instant noodles, obviously. If you're looking for a 3-minute meal, this isn't it. And if you're gluten-sensitive, these are a hard no — it's pure wheat.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask**

**How long do these keep after opening?** Stored in a cool, dry place with the bag sealed (use a clip if the zipper fails), they'll stay good for several months. Check for any off smell or webbing — that's spoilage.

**Can I use these in a soup or broth?** Absolutely. Cook them separately, then add to your hot broth. Cooking them directly in the broth makes it cloudy and starchy — don't do that.

**Are these vegan?** Yes. Wheat, water, salt. No egg, no dairy.
