Let's get one thing straight upfront: if you've been sprinkling that red-lid supermarket cinnamon on your coffee, you've been using cassia — a different species entirely. Frontier Co-op's Organic Ceylon is *Cinnamomum verum*, the "true cinnamon" from Sri Lanka, and the difference is immediately obvious in both aroma and taste.

This 16-ounce refill pouch is the value play in Frontier's lineup. The same cinnamon in their shaker jar runs roughly $1.50–$2 more per ounce; buying the refill and reusing your own container saves real money over time. As shown in the product image, you're getting a simple foil pouch — no fancy packaging, no shaker included. That's the tradeoff for the price.

## What You Actually Get

Open the pouch and you'll notice the grind is finer and more uniform than most grocery-store cinnamon. The color is a lighter, warmer tan rather than the reddish-brown of cassia. Smell it: there's a floral, almost citrusy sweetness — think orange peel and vanilla — with none of the harsh, penetrating bite of cassia. This is cinnamon that whispers instead of shouts.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this three ways: in a pour-over coffee, blended into oatmeal, and baked into a simple coffee cake. In coffee, it's transformative — a quarter teaspoon stirred into the grounds before brewing gives a warm, aromatic cup without any bitterness or sediment. It also dissolves remarkably well into steamed milk for a homemade cinnamon latte; no clumping, no gritty texture left in the mug.

For baking, the difference is more subtle. If you're expecting that punchy, spicy kick that dominates American cinnamon rolls, you'll be disappointed. Ceylon is delicate — it enhances rather than dominates. One reviewer put it well: *"It tastes like real cinnamon, not the spicy stuff that burns your tongue. I use it in my morning oatmeal and it's perfect."* Another noted, *"The aroma is incredible — way fresher than anything I've bought at the grocery store."*

That freshness is worth emphasizing. Frontier sources directly and rotates stock, so the grind is noticeably more aromatic than supermarket jars that may have sat on shelves for months.

## Where It Falls Short

The honesty here: this is a niche product, and it's not for everyone. If you're used to the bold, aggressive flavor of cassia (which is what 95% of grocery-store cinnamon actually is), Ceylon can initially taste almost *weak*. It's a different profile, not a better or worse one — just different.

The packaging is the bigger practical issue. The refill pouch is not resealable beyond folding it over and clipping it. Once opened, the cinnamon will lose its volatile oils and stale within a few months. Transfer it to an airtight glass jar immediately — this is not optional if you want the flavor to last.

Finally, the price. Even with the refill economy, this runs $0.80–$1.20 per ounce depending on current pricing. That's 3–4x the cost of generic cassia. You're paying for organic certification, fair trade premiums, and a superior product — but it's still a premium price.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Coffee enthusiasts** who add cinnamon to their brewing routine and want a cleaner, more aromatic cup
- **Bakers** making delicate pastries, rice puddings, or custards where cassia's bite would overwhelm
- **Health-conscious shoppers** who care about coumarin levels (Ceylon has trace amounts; cassia can have concerning levels with daily heavy use)
- **Anyone who already owns a shaker jar** and wants to refill it at a better price

Skip it if you want bold, spicy cinnamon for cinnamon rolls or you're on a tight budget and just need something to sprinkle on toast.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this actually Ceylon cinnamon, or is it mislabeled cassia?**
A: It's genuine *Cinnamomum verum* from Sri Lanka. The lighter color, delicate aroma, and crumbly texture are unmistakable. Frontier has a strong reputation for accurate sourcing, and this product is Fairtrade and organic certified.

**Q: How should I store the refill pouch?**
A: Transfer it to an airtight glass jar immediately. The foil pouch isn't resealable, and exposure to air and light will degrade the essential oils. Stored properly in a cool, dark cabinet, it'll stay fresh for 6–12 months.

**Q: Can I use this in my coffee maker without clogging it?**
A: Yes, but use a fine grind setting and consider adding it to the grounds rather than the water reservoir. A quarter teaspoon per 12-ounce brew is a good starting point. It won't clog filters or leave residue at that amount.

**Buy it if** you appreciate nuanced, high-quality cinnamon and want the authentic Ceylon experience at the best per-ounce price Frontier offers. **Skip it if** you prefer the bold, spicy kick of cassia or need resealable packaging.
