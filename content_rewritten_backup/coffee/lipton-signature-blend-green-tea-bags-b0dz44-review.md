If you've ever bought a budget green tea that turned bitter the second you looked away from the kettle, you know the struggle. Lipton's Signature Blend sidesteps that trap entirely. This isn't a fancy single-origin artisanal tea — it's a dependable, smooth daily drinker that's earned a 4.7-star rating from 235 Amazon shoppers for good reason.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 100 individually wrapped tea bags. Each bag is a traditional flat paper tea bag with a string and tag — nothing fancy, but functional. The individual wrapping matters more than you'd think: it locks in freshness and makes these perfect for office drawers, travel, or camping kits where moisture and odor are constant threats.

## Flavor and Performance

Here's where Lipton Signature Blend separates itself from cheaper supermarket greens. The leaves are cut finer than standard Lipton yellow-label black tea, which allows for quicker infusion. Steeped for two to three minutes in water just below boiling (around 175–180°F is ideal), you get a clean, mildly vegetal cup with no astringency.

The flavor profile leans toward the mellow side — think Chinese-style gunpowder green rather than the sharp, ocean-forward Japanese sencha. There's a subtle sweetness and none of the fishy or cardboard notes you sometimes find in mass-market teas. One reviewer put it simply: "This is the only green tea that doesn't taste like I'm drinking grass clippings. Smooth every single time."

The bags hold up well. No tearing, no leaves escaping into your cup, and the string is long enough to dangle outside a standard mug without slipping in. Even if you accidentally steep for five minutes, the bitterness stays remarkably controlled — a forgiving quality that makes this a solid choice for beginners.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 235 ratings, a few themes dominate. First, value: at roughly 8–10 cents per bag, shoppers repeatedly note this outperforms teas costing three times as much. Second, consistency — buyers who've purchased multiple boxes report the same flavor batch after batch. Third, versatility: several reviewers mention using these for cold brew iced tea in the summer, and the smooth profile works well both ways.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. If you're coming from premium loose-leaf greens, this will taste one-dimensional. There's no complexity, no lingering finish — it's a straightforward, pleasant cup that won't blow your mind. The tea bags are also non-biodegradable (standard paper with polypropylene seals), which matters if you compost your tea waste.

One other note: the caffeine content is moderate — roughly 25–35 mg per cup versus 45–55 mg in black tea. If you're using this as a coffee replacement, you may need a second cup.

## FAQ

**Does this taste like the Lipton green tea you get at restaurants?**  
Close, but smoother. Restaurant green tea often sits in a dispenser and turns bitter. Fresh-brewed from these bags, it's noticeably cleaner and slightly sweeter.

**Can I use these for iced tea?**  
Yes. Steep two bags in one cup of hot water for three minutes, then pour over a glass of ice. Add honey or lemon if you like.

**How long do the bags stay fresh?**  
The individual wrappers keep them viable well past the printed date. I've used bags from a year-old box with no flavor loss, as long as they stayed sealed.
