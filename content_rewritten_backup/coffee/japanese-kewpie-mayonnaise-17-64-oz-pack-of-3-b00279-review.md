**What You Actually Get**

Three full-size 17.64 oz. squeeze bottles. That's roughly 1.1 pounds of mayo per bottle, which is more than the standard 15 oz. American jars. The squeeze nozzle matters more than you think—it's narrow, so you get fine lines for drizzling, not glops. The bottle is soft polyethylene, easy to squeeze even when cold. No knife required.

**Why It Tastes Different (And Better)**

This isn't marketing talk. The ingredient list tells the real story. Kewpie uses only egg yolks, not whole eggs. That's why it's thicker, richer, and more yellow. It also uses apple cider vinegar instead of distilled white vinegar, giving it that unmistakable tang—not sharp, but round and slightly sweet. There's MSG (monosodium glutamate) in the form of yeast extract. That's not a dirty word; it's what makes your taste buds light up. The savory depth is why Kewpie makes everything from fried rice to roasted vegetables taste more "complete."

**Where It Falls Short**

Be honest with yourself before buying. This isn't a pantry staple for everyone. The flavor is noticeably different—more acidic, more umami, less neutral than Hellmann's. If you prefer a bland, creamy base for heavily flavored recipes, this might overpower them.

Also, the price per ounce is higher than standard mayo. You're paying for imported quality. And once opened, it must be refrigerated. It keeps fine for months, but if you don't use mayo often, the 3-pack might expire before you finish it. The flip-top cap can also clog if you let the nozzle get crusty—wipe it clean after each use.

**FAQ: Three Real Questions**

**Q: Is this the same as the Kewpie sold in Japanese grocery stores?**
A: Yes, this is the Japanese version, made in Japan, not the sweeter American-made Kewpie. The label is in Japanese, and the ingredient list includes MSG. Some US stores sell a reformulated version—this is not that.

**Q: How long does it last after opening?**
A: Refrigerated, it stays good for 3-4 months. The high egg yolk content means it can spoil faster than commercial mayo if left out, so always return it to the fridge immediately.

**Q: Can I use it in place of regular mayo in recipes?**
A: Yes, but adjust. It's tangier and thinner, so it spreads more easily. In baking or salads, you might want slightly less. For dressings, it's a straight upgrade.
