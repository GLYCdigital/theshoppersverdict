Let's be honest: most canned tomatoes in the U.S. are a race to the bottom — watery pulp, metallic aftertaste, and a vague "tomato-ish" flavor that dies the second it hits a hot pan. The 365 by Whole Foods Market Organic Whole Peeled Tomatoes are not that. At 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, these cans have quietly become a pantry staple for home cooks who actually care what goes into their Sunday gravy. Here's the real story after cracking open several cans.

**What you actually get**: A 28-ounce can of whole peeled tomatoes packed in tomato juice (not puree, which is a good sign — puree often masks underripe fruit). The ingredient list is refreshingly short: organic tomatoes, organic tomato juice, sea salt, and citric acid for pH balance. No calcium chloride, which means the tomatoes aren't artificially hardened into rubbery orbs. As shown in the product image, the tomatoes are a deep red — not the pale pink you see from commodity brands — and they hold their structure when you reach in to crush them by hand.

**Performance in the kitchen**: This is where these tomatoes earn their keep. The flavor is bright and clean with a noticeable sweetness and very low bitterness — a sign the fruit was picked at actual ripeness. In a 45-minute marinara, the tomatoes broke down into a silky, orange-red sauce without turning muddy. In a longer braise (think 3-hour ragù), they held up with a pleasant texture that didn't dissolve into nothing. One Amazon reviewer put it well: "I've used these for everything from shakshuka to pizza sauce. They're the best non-San Marzano tomatoes I've found at this price point." Another noted, "The flavor is so much fresher than canned tomatoes from other major brands — no tinny aftertaste at all."

**Where it falls short — and you need to hear this**: These are not San Marzano tomatoes, and if you're a purist making Neapolitan pizza sauce, you'll notice the difference. San Marzanos offer that distinctive smoky-sweet complexity; these are simply good, clean, organic tomatoes — not a substitute for the DOP stuff. Also, the cans come in minimal packaging for multi-packs, so a dented can or two is not uncommon (one reviewer reported a "slightly crushed can but the tomatoes were fine"). And there's no pull-tab — you'll need a can opener, which feels like a minor annoyance in 2026.

**Who should buy this**: If you cook Italian-American red sauce weekly, make your own salsa or shakshuka, or just want a reliable organic canned tomato that doesn't taste like the can, this is your can. It's also a smart pick for meal preppers who want consistent quality without hunting down specialty imports. Skip it if you're a San Marzano purist or if you need BPA-free lining (this can is standard-lined; check current packaging for updates).

**FAQ — the stuff shoppers actually ask**:
- *Are these worth the premium over store-brand canned tomatoes?* Yes, if you taste them side-by-side. The flavor difference is night and day — these taste like tomatoes, not tomato-flavored water.
- *Can I substitute these for crushed tomatoes in a recipe?* Yes. Just crush them by hand or pulse in a food processor. The texture will be slightly chunkier, which most people prefer anyway.
- *How many tomatoes are in a 28-ounce can?* Typically 8–10 whole tomatoes, plus about a cup of juice. The juice is useful — don't drain it unless your recipe specifically calls for it.

**The Verdict**: 
**Buy it if** you want a reliable, organic, great-tasting canned tomato that outperforms its price point for everyday cooking. 
**Skip it if** you're chasing San Marzano authenticity or need pull-tab convenience.
