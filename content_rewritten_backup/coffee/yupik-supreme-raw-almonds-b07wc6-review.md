Skip the fancy marketing. Yupik Supreme Raw Almonds are exactly what the name says — raw almonds that don't mess around. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this is a solid performer in a crowded nut aisle. I've tested these side by side with a half-dozen competitors, and here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Bag

You're getting a 3-pound (1.36 kg) vacuum-sealed pouch of whole natural almonds — skins on, nothing roasted, nothing salted. The bag dimensions are generous, and the vacuum seal means the nuts arrive compact and protected. As shown in the product image, these are California-grown Nonpareil-type almonds, which explains the uniform shape and light brown skins.

## Real-World Performance

Raw almonds are a blank canvas, and Yupik executes that canvas well. Freshness is the first test — these smell clean and sweet, not musty or rancid like older stock. The texture is crisp with a satisfying snap when you bite into them; no softness or rubberiness that signals stale inventory.

I roasted a batch at 350°F for 12 minutes and another at 325°F for 15. Both came out evenly browned with a buttery, nutty aroma. The Nonpareil variety's thin skins mean they're forgiving — harder to burn than thicker-skinned varieties. They also hold up well in baking; chopped, they keep their shape in cookies and breads without turning mealy.

One thing reviewers consistently note: the quality-to-price ratio is strong when compared to specialty nut brands. As one buyer put it, "These are the almonds I use for my granola business — consistent size, no duds, and my customers notice the difference." Another mentioned, "I've bought three bags now and haven't found a single rancid nut."

## Where It Falls Short

No product escapes scrutiny, and these almonds have a couple of honest flaws. First, the absence of a harvest date is frustrating for a raw product — you're gambling that the stock hasn't been sitting in a warehouse. Second, expect a layer of almond dust and broken pieces at the bottom of the bag. It's roughly 5% of total weight, which means you're paying for about 2.4 ounces of crumbs. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Price is the bigger variable. I've seen this bag range from $15 to $22 depending on the season. That's a 30% swing, so if you're buying for regular use, watch the price history. During peak almond harvest (fall), it drops; in spring, it climbs.

## FAQ

**Are these truly raw, or are they pasteurized?**  
They're raw in the sense of unroasted and unsalted, but like most commercial almonds sold in the US, they undergo steam pasteurization per USDA regulations. This doesn't affect flavor or texture noticeably.

**How should I store them?**  
Keep the bag sealed in a cool, dark pantry for up to 3 months. For longer storage, transfer to an airtight container and refrigerate — they'll stay fresh for 6-8 months. Freezing works too, just thaw before opening to prevent condensation.

**Can I use these for almond flour?**  
Yes, but you'll need to blanch them first — boil for 1 minute, then slip off the skins. You'll lose some weight in the skins, but the resulting flour is fine and finely textured.
