Let me be direct about something: MGO 830 is not beginner's manuka honey. This is the heavy artillery of the honey world, and Steens is positioning it as a certified UMF 20 product. Before you spend this kind of money, you need to understand exactly what you're getting.

## What You're Actually Buying

Steens MGO 830 is raw, unpasteurized manuka honey sourced from New Zealand. The MGO rating refers to methylglyoxal content — the compound responsible for manuka's antibacterial properties. At 830 mg/kg, this is roughly 3-4 times stronger than entry-level therapeutic manuka (MGO 100-200). The UMF 20 certification is the gold standard in the industry, independently verified by the UMFHA (Unique Manuka Factor Honey Association).

What arrives is a jar of thick, dark honey with a distinctly earthy, slightly bitter, medicinal aroma. The texture is dense and creamy — not runny like regular honey. As shown in the product image, the packaging includes the UMF certification number and MGO rating clearly displayed, which matters for authenticity verification.

## The Real-World Performance

Here's where high-grade manuka separates itself. The texture is almost spreadable — it doesn't drip off a spoon like conventional honey. The taste is intense: earthy, slightly bitter, with a lingering herbal finish. If you're used to clover honey, this will be a shock to the palate.

For everyday use, you're looking at a teaspoon a day for wellness purposes, or more targeted use for sore throats and digestive support. The high MGO content means you need less — a small dose goes further than weaker honeys.

## FAQ

**Q: How is MGO 830 different from regular manuka honey?**
A: MGO 830 has over 8 times the antibacterial methylglyoxal content of entry-level manuka (MGO 100). This means stronger therapeutic potential but also a more intense, bitter flavor.

**Q: How should I consume this honey?**
A: Take 1 teaspoon daily, either straight or dissolved in warm (not boiling) water. Avoid mixing into hot tea — heat can degrade beneficial compounds. Don't give to infants under 12 months.

**Q: Is UMF certification worth the premium?**
A: Yes, for authenticity. UMF is independently tested and verified. Non-certified "manuka" honey can be adulterated or blended. The certification guarantees you're getting genuine, active manuka honey.
