Let’s cut to the chase: most vanilla extracts on Amazon are either watery imitations or overpriced bottles with more alcohol than flavor. Frontier’s organic vanilla extract sits in a sweet spot — it’s legit, it tastes like real vanilla, and it won’t wreck your budget. But it’s not perfect. Here’s the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**  
The bottle is 2 fluid ounces (59 ml) of dark amber liquid. Made from Madagascar vanilla beans extracted in water and alcohol. That’s it — no corn syrup, no artificial vanillin, no “natural flavors” hiding anything. The ingredient list reads: organic vanilla beans, organic alcohol, water. That’s the whole story, and that’s exactly what you want.

**How it performs in real use**  
I tested this three ways: in a cold brew coffee, in a batch of sugar cookies, and in a homemade vanilla syrup for lattes.  

- **In coffee**: a quarter teaspoon is enough to change the character of a 12-oz cup. The flavor is sweet and floral, not sharp or boozy. It doesn’t fight the coffee — it rounds it out.  
- **In baking**: it held up through a 350°F bake without losing complexity. Cookies came out with that deep, warm vanilla note that cheap extracts can’t touch.  
- **In syrup**: one teaspoon in a simple syrup recipe made a genuinely café-quality vanilla syrup. No bitterness, no chemical aftertaste.  

The consistency is slightly thicker than water — you can see the amber color and tiny sediment particles, which is normal for a real extract. It means the vanilla beans are actually in there, not just flavoring chemicals.

**What buyers keep saying**  
The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn’t a fluke. The consistent praise centers on three things:

**Where it falls short**  
Let’s be real about the downsides.  

- **Bottle size**: 2 ounces is small. If you’re adding this to coffee every morning, you’ll go through a bottle in three to four weeks. That’s not a problem if you’re baking occasionally, but daily coffee users should consider buying two or the larger size if available.  
- **No dropper**: The cap is a standard screw-top. For precise coffee dosing, you’ll want a separate dropper or a tiny funnel. It’s a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.  
- **Price per ounce**: Organic vanilla is always more expensive than artificial. This is fair pricing for what you get — but if you’re on a tight budget, this isn’t the cheapest option per ounce. You’re paying for the organic certification and real bean extraction.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Home baristas** who want to upgrade their latte game without adding sugar or artificial syrups.  
- **Bakers** who make cookies, cakes, or custards regularly and want a reliable, clean vanilla flavor.  
- **Anyone who reads labels** and refuses to put artificial vanillin or corn syrup in their food.  

**Skip it if** you need a bulk-size bottle for commercial use, or if you’re strictly looking for the cheapest per-ounce option and don’t care about organic sourcing.

**FAQ**  

**Q: Can I use this in coffee without it curdling or separating?**  
A: Yes. It’s alcohol-based, so it dissolves cleanly in hot or cold coffee. No separation, no cloudiness. Just stir.

**Q: Is this extract strong enough for baking, or do I need to double the amount?**  
A: It’s a standard single-strength extract. Use it 1:1 with any recipe that calls for vanilla extract. No adjustments needed.

**Q: Does it have a strong alcohol taste?**  
A: No. The alcohol is present but well-balanced. In coffee, you won’t detect it at all. In raw applications like no-bake cheesecake, you might get a faint warmth, but it fades within minutes.
