If you've ever ordered an amarena cherry in a real Italian cocktail bar, you know the difference between these and the neon-red maraschino things that taste like wax. Amarena Fabbri is the brand behind that difference — a Bologna-based company that's been making wild cherries in syrup since 1905. The jar in front of me holds whole dark cherries swimming in a deep ruby syrup that smells faintly of almond and vanilla. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The 400-gram jar (about 14 ounces) contains roughly 30–35 whole Amarena cherries, a smaller wild variety grown in northern Italy. The syrup is made from sugar, water, and natural flavorings — no artificial colors, no gluten, no GMOs. As shown in the product image, the cherries are dark purple-black, not bright red, which is your first clue these aren't your average cocktail garnish. Net weight is 400 grams, and the glass jar is sturdy with a resealable lid that keeps the remaining syrup fresh for weeks in the fridge.

**How they perform in real use**

The texture is where these shine. Each cherry has a firm bite with a slight resistance before giving way — nothing like the mushy, bleached-cherry texture you get from the grocery store. The flavor is tart first, then sweet, with a distinct almond note that comes from the Amarena variety itself. The syrup is thinner than you might expect — think fruit syrup, not honey — but that actually makes it more usable.

I tested these three ways: over vanilla gelato, in a bourbon old fashioned, and in a sparkling water with a spoonful of the syrup. All three worked. The cherries held their shape in the cocktail for over an hour, and the syrup dissolved cleanly into the soda without clumping. One reviewer put it simply: "These are the real deal. I've bought them three times now and they're always perfect for my Manhattan."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings is genuinely earned. The most consistent praise centers on authenticity and texture. People who know Italian desserts comment on how close this comes to what they had in Italy. Multiple reviewers mention buying them specifically for tiramisu and affogato, while others use them as a cocktail cherry substitute that doesn't carry that artificial maraschino flavor.

The almond-vanilla aroma gets called out repeatedly. It's subtle but present, and it elevates everything from plain yogurt to a dark chocolate dessert. One long-time buyer noted: "I've been buying these for years. The quality has never dipped — same tart-sweet balance every time."

**Where they fall short**

The price is the biggest hurdle. Per ounce, these run significantly more than standard cocktail cherries — often double or triple the cost. If you're just looking for something to drop in a Manhattan and don't care about the flavor profile, you're overpaying.

The other issue is shipping. The cherries are fragile, and a few reviewers report jars arriving with broken or crushed fruit. It's not widespread, but it's worth checking the jar when it arrives. If the cherries are damaged, the syrup still tastes fine, but you lose the visual appeal.

The syrup is also thinner than some expect. It won't coat a spoon like a compote or jam. If you want a thick cherry glaze for desserts, this isn't it — you'd want to reduce the syrup yourself.

**Who should buy these**

- **Cocktail enthusiasts** who want a premium garnish without artificial flavor
- **Home bakers** making tiramisu, cassata, or cherry-forward desserts
- **Anyone who remembers amarena cherries from a trip to Italy** and wants that authentic taste back
- **People who eat vanilla ice cream regularly** — one jar upgrades it for weeks

**Skip these if** you're on a tight budget, you only need cherries once a year, or you prefer the bright, candy-like flavor of standard maraschino cherries. There's no judgment — but these are a different product entirely.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long do these last after opening?**
A: Kept refrigerated in the original jar, they stay good for 4–6 weeks. The syrup helps preserve them, but the texture degrades after that.

**Q: Can I use the syrup in cocktails or cooking?**
A: Absolutely. The syrup is excellent in sodas, lemonades, or as a sweetener in an old fashioned. It also works brushed over cakes or stirred into yogurt.

**Q: Are these the same as the cherries served in Italian coffee shops?**
