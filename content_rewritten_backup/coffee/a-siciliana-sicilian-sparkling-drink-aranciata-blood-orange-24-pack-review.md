## The Blood Orange Soda That Actually Tastes Like Sicily

Most "Italian" sparkling drinks you find on Amazon are basically sugar water with orange extract and artificial color. A' Siciliana's Aranciata is not that. This is the real thing — made with actual Sicilian blood orange juice from the Moro variety, which gives it that deep ruby color and a tart, complex flavor you simply cannot fake. If you've been burned by watery spritzers before, this one sets the bar.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get a 24-pack of 11.15 fl oz (330ml) slim aluminum cans. The case is sturdy and the cans arrive well-packed — no crushed product in the reviews I've seen. Each can delivers 110 calories and 24g of sugar, which sounds like a lot until you compare it to a standard orange soda at 39-42g. It's sweetened with real sugar (no HFCS or artificial sweeteners), and the ingredient list is refreshingly short: carbonated water, blood orange juice, sugar, natural flavors. That's it.

## How It Performs in the Glass

Here's where this drink separates itself. That first sip hits with immediate tartness — think fresh-squeezed blood orange, not orange candy. The carbonation is fine and persistent, more Champagne-like than the aggressive fizz of a Coke. It finishes clean with a slight bitterness from the orange peel, which is exactly what you want from a craft soda.

The real test? Use it as a cocktail mixer. The blood orange flavor holds its own against gin or vodka in a way that San Pellegrino's Aranciata simply doesn't. It's also fantastic over ice with a splash of sparkling water if the tartness is too intense straight from the can.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews at 4.7 stars (226 ratings) repeatedly mention two things: authenticity and the taste-to-sugar ratio. One reviewer put it well: *"Finally a blood orange soda that tastes like the ones I had in Taormina — not the syrupy stuff they sell in American grocery stores."* Another noted, *"My kids drink these instead of juice. I feel better about that trade."* Multiple reviewers also mention using them for mimosas and Aperol spritzes with excellent results.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. At $52.99 for the case, you're paying premium craft-soda prices — roughly $2.21 per can. That's steep for a daily soda habit. The aluminum can is my bigger gripe: a blood orange soda this good deserves glass. There's a faint metallic note on the finish that's barely noticeable when chilled but present at room temperature.

And the tartness is real. If you're expecting the sweet, mellow orange flavor of San Pellegrino, you'll find this too sharp. It's an adult soda, not a kid's treat.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a cocktail enthusiast who wants a premium mixer, you miss authentic Italian sodas, or you're trying to cut sugar without switching to diet drinks. It's also a thoughtful gift for a foodie friend.

**Skip it if:** You prefer sweeter sodas, you're on a tight beverage budget, or you're looking for a daily drinker — the price per can adds up fast.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Ask

**Is this like San Pellegrino Aranciata?**
Not really. San Pellegrino is sweeter and milder. This has more pronounced blood orange tartness and a deeper, more complex flavor. If you love San Pellegrino, you'll probably like this — but it's a step up in intensity.

**Can I use these as a mixer?**
Absolutely. They shine in Aperol spritzes, gin and tonics, or with vodka and a splash of Campari. The tartness cuts through spirits better than sweeter sodas.

**How long does the carbonation last after opening?**
About 24 hours in the fridge if you cap it. The fine bubbles hold better than most sodas, but it will go flat eventually like any carbonated drink.
