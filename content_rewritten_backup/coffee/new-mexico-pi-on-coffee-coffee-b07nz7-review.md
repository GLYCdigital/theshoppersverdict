# New Mexico Piñon Coffee Review: The State's Signature Roast, Tested

There's a reason New Mexico made piñon coffee its unofficial state beverage. The pine nut — harvested from piñon trees across the high desert — adds a buttery, slightly sweet character that transforms ordinary coffee into something distinctive. New Mexico Piñon Coffee Company has been roasting this blend for over 25 years, and their Amazon presence makes it accessible anywhere in the country. But does it hold up outside the Land of Enchantment? I brewed several batches to find out.

## What's in the Bag

You're getting a 12-ounce bag of medium-roast whole beans with piñon nuts roasted directly alongside the coffee. The roast date is printed on the bag — a detail many specialty roasters skip, so it's appreciated here. As shown in the product image, the beans have a uniform medium-brown color with visible nut fragments mixed in. The aroma hits immediately: toasted pine, caramel, and a hint of earthiness that reads distinctly Southwestern.

## Flavor Profile and Performance

The piñon flavor isn't a punch-you-in-the-face nuttiness. It's more of a warm undercurrent — think toasted almond butter meets cedar, with a smooth, low-acidity body. Brewed as a pour-over, I got notes of brown sugar and a clean finish with zero bitterness. In a French press, the nuttiness came forward more, and the mouthfeel turned almost velvety.

The medium roast handles heat well. I've pushed it with boiling water and it never turned harsh. That's a sign of quality beans — likely a blend of Central and South American origins, though the company doesn't disclose exact sourcing.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews isn't accidental. Shoppers repeatedly mention three things:

**The authentic flavor.** As one reviewer put it: *"I've tried other piñon coffees and they taste like they use artificial flavoring. This one actually tastes like the real thing — you can see the nuts in the bag."*

**Freshness.** Multiple buyers noted the roast date and how much fresher the beans are compared to supermarket brands. One wrote: *"Ordered on Monday, roasted on Tuesday, arrived Thursday. You can't beat that."*

**The aroma.** It's a recurring theme — the smell while brewing fills the kitchen in a way that synthetic-flavored coffees can't replicate.

## Where It Falls Short

The subtlety cuts both ways. If you're expecting a dessert-coffee intensity like a hazelnut or caramel-flavored roast, this will underwhelm. The piñon is a supporting player, not the star. One reviewer captured it: *"Lovely coffee, but I had to really concentrate to taste the piñon. It's more of a background note."*

There's also a practical annoyance: loose piñon shell fragments settle at the bottom of the bag. They're harmless, but you'll want to pour the last few tablespoons carefully or use a filter that catches debris. And at roughly $1.30 per ounce, it's pricier than your standard grocery-store bag — though for small-batch roasting, that's fair.

## FAQ

**Is this coffee caffeinated?**
Yes, it's regular coffee with standard caffeine levels. The piñon nuts don't affect caffeine content.

**Can I use it in an espresso machine?**
Technically yes, but it's a medium roast designed for drip and pour-over. In espresso, the nutty notes can turn slightly smoky. You're better off with a dedicated espresso blend.

**Does it contain tree nuts?**
Yes — it's roasted with actual piñon pine nuts. If you have a tree nut allergy, avoid this product.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely unique, well-executed regional coffee that tastes like New Mexico in a cup. The freshness, aroma, and authentic piñon character justify the price.

**Skip it if:** You need heavy, in-your-face flavoring or prefer dark roasts. This is a nuanced, medium-bodied experience that rewards attention.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A standout small-batch roast that delivers on its signature flavor, with minor quibbles around subtlety and stray shells.
