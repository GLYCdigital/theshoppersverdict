Let’s be honest: most “ube” products on Amazon taste like sweetened purple Play-Doh. The Butterfly Ube Flavoring Extract is not that. This 2 oz bottle delivers the real deal — a concentrated essence that actually tastes like roasted purple yam, not a chemistry experiment. But is it worth your money for home coffee drinks? Here’s the full breakdown.

**What’s in the Bottle**

You get a single 2 oz (60 ml) glass bottle with a dropper top. The liquid is thin, dark violet, and intensely aromatic. This is a concentrated extract, meaning you use it in drops or teaspoons — not cups. The brand, Butterfly, is a well-known Filipino food manufacturer, and this extract is a staple in Asian grocery stores for making ube halaya, ice cream, and baked goods. The ingredient list is straightforward: water, propylene glycol, artificial and natural flavors, and FD&C colors. Nothing fancy, but it does the job.

**Performance in Real-World Use**

I tested this three ways: hot ube latte, iced ube milk tea, and ube cheesecake. In a 12 oz hot latte, ½ teaspoon gave a distinct ube flavor that cut through the espresso without overwhelming it. The iced version needed slightly more — about ¾ teaspoon — because cold temperatures dull aromatic compounds. The cheesecake was where it shined: a full tablespoon mixed into the batter produced a subtle, earthy sweetness that tasted closer to fresh ube than any powder I’ve tried.

The extract is potent. One 2 oz bottle made roughly 15-20 lattes at my usage rate. If you’re drinking one daily, you’ll restock every three weeks. That’s the biggest practical limitation — the bottle is tiny.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. One buyer wrote, *“Finally, a ube flavoring that doesn’t taste like chemicals. I’ve tried three others and this is the only one that tastes like my grandma’s halaya.”* Another noted, *“A little goes a long way. I put two drops in my pancake batter and the whole stack tasted like ube.”* Multiple reviewers also confirmed it performs well in both hot and cold applications, which is rare — many flavorings break down or turn bitter with heat.

**Where It Falls Short**

The color is the biggest letdown. As shown in the product image, the extract itself is a deep violet, but once mixed into milk or batter, it fades to a pale lavender. If you want that vibrant purple ube aesthetic, you’ll need to add ube powder or a natural purple food coloring. The extract alone won’t deliver the visual punch.

Also, the dropper cap is slightly loose. If you store the bottle upright, it’s fine, but lay it on its side in a pantry and you risk leaks. Keep it upright.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home baristas** who want an affordable ube latte without buying a $15 syrup that goes bad in a month.
- **Bakers** making Filipino desserts like ube rolls, cookies, or leche flan who want an authentic flavor base.
- **Meal preppers** who mix a few drops into overnight oats or smoothies for a quick ube kick.

**Skip it if** you need vibrant purple color in your final product, or if you bake ube desserts more than twice a week — the 2 oz size will frustrate you.

**FAQ**

**Q: How much should I use in a coffee drink?**  
A: Start with ¼ teaspoon per 12 oz cup. Adjust up to ½ teaspoon for stronger flavor. For iced drinks, add 25% more since cold dulls the taste.

**Q: Does it contain real ube?**  
A: The label lists “artificial and natural flavors.” It’s primarily flavor compounds, not actual ube puree. For 100% real ube, buy frozen grated ube or ube powder.

**Q: Does it need refrigeration?**  
A: No, but store it in a cool, dark place. Heat and direct sunlight degrade the flavor over time.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an authentic, versatile ube flavoring for coffee, baking, or Filipino recipes and don’t mind a muted color.

**Skip it if** visual presentation matters more than taste, or you need bulk quantity for frequent use.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The flavor is genuinely excellent and versatile, but the small bottle and lackluster color keep it from a perfect score. For $8-10, it’s a solid value for occasional use. For daily ube lattes, buy two bottles upfront and keep one in the pantry.
