**The Review**

Almond extract is one of those ingredients people buy once, use in a batch of cookies, and then forget about for six months. If that's you, this Amazon Fresh bottle is perfectly fine. But if you actually bake with almond extract regularly — Danish pastries, amaretti, frangipane, almond cakes — the difference between this and the $3 supermarket stuff is stark. This is the real deal, and here's why.

**What you're actually getting**

The 2 oz bottle (the standard size for extracts) contains alcohol, water, and **bitter almond oil** — the genuine source of that intense, cherry-adjacent almond flavor. That's the same ingredient listed on premium brands like Nielsen-Massey, which sells a comparable 2 oz bottle for roughly double the price. Amazon Fresh skips the middleman markup but doesn't cut corners on the core ingredient.

As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward amber glass bottle with a simple screw cap. Nothing fancy, no dropper, no decorative label. It's utilitarian in the best way — you're paying for what's inside, not the packaging.

**Performance and real-world use**

I tested this in three applications: classic sugar cookies, a homemade amaretto sour, and a frangipane tart. In all three, the flavor was unmistakably "real" — a deep, slightly floral almond note rather than the sharp, almost artificial taste you get from extracts made with synthetic benzaldehyde. That difference is most noticeable in no-bake applications where the alcohol doesn't cook off; in a sour, the extract added a complex nuttiness that cheap extracts turn cloying.

One thing to note: this is concentrated. In a standard cookie recipe calling for 1 teaspoon of almond extract, you can often reduce to ¾ teaspoon with this product and get the same intensity. That's a money-saver in the long run, even though the upfront cost per ounce is higher than the grocery-store basics.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this the same as vanilla extract? Can I substitute one for the other?**
No. Almond extract is made from bitter almond oil; vanilla extract comes from vanilla beans. They're not interchangeable. Almond extract is much more potent — use about half the amount if a recipe permits swapping.

**Q: Does this contain nuts?**
It's made from bitter almonds, which are technically a stone fruit kernel, not a true nut. But if you have a severe tree nut allergy, check with your allergist before using — it's still derived from almond sources.

**Q: Does the alcohol cook off?**
In baked goods, mostly yes, but the flavor compounds remain. In no-bake applications like frosting or drinks, the alcohol is present — though at typical usage amounts (a teaspoon or less), it's negligible.
