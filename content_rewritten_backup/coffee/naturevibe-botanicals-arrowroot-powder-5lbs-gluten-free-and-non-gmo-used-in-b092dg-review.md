## Performance in the Kitchen

I tested this powder three ways: as a gravy thickener, in a fruit pie filling, and as a coating for pan-fried tofu.

**Gravy:** Whisked 2 tablespoons into hot chicken broth. It thickened within seconds—no lumping, no chalky residue. The finish was glossy, not matte like flour-based gravies. Flavor was completely neutral.

**Pie filling:** Used it at a 1:1 cornstarch replacement ratio. The filling set beautifully after cooling, and here's the thing that surprised me: it didn't turn cloudy. Cornstarch leaves a milky haze in berry fillings; arrowroot stays crystal clear.

**Tofu coating:** This is where arrowroot shines. The powder creates a delicate, crispy crust that cornstarch can't match—lighter, almost tempura-like. It held up during pan-frying without turning gummy.

One thing to know: arrowroot doesn't freeze-thaw well. If you're meal-prepping sauces or soups for the freezer, this thickener will break down and turn watery. That's a real limitation, not a nitpick.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I substitute this 1:1 for cornstarch in any recipe?**
A: Yes, for hot applications like sauces, gravies, and pie fillings. But don't use it in recipes you plan to freeze—arrowroot breaks down after thawing and turns watery. Also note it thickens at a lower temperature than cornstarch, so add it later in the cooking process.

**Q: Does arrowroot powder have any taste?**
A: No. It's completely neutral, which is actually an advantage over flour (which can taste raw if undercooked) and cornstarch (which can leave a slightly metallic note in some dishes). That's why it's popular for fruit pies and clear sauces.

**Q: How should I store this 5-pound bag?**
A: Transfer it to an airtight glass jar or plastic container immediately. Arrowroot absorbs moisture from the air, which can cause clumping and reduce its thickening power. Stored properly in a cool, dark pantry, it'll last 2-3 years.
