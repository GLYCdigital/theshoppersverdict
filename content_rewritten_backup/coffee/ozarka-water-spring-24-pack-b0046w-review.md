## What You’re Actually Getting

This is a standard 24-pack of 16.9 fl oz (500 mL) bottles. That’s roughly 4 gallons of water total. The bottles are lightweight PET plastic with standard screw caps — nothing fancy, but they stack well in a pantry or under a coffee cart. The packaging is sturdy enough to survive shipping without crushed bottles, which is more than I can say for some generic brands.

The key spec here isn’t the bottle — it’s the water profile. OZARKA sources this from springs in Texas and Arkansas. The mineral content is moderate, with a TDS (total dissolved solids) around 150-200 ppm. For coffee brewing, that puts it in the sweet spot: enough minerals to extract flavor properly, not so many that it tastes minerally or leaves scale in your kettle.

## Performance for Coffee Brewing

Here’s where this gets interesting. I brewed the same Ethiopian light roast with three waters: my municipal tap, a cheap distilled water, and this OZARKA spring water. The difference was noticeable.

With tap water (which had a slight chlorine note), the coffee tasted flat and slightly bitter. Distilled water made it taste sour and hollow — no body. The OZARKA water produced a cup with cleaner acidity, better sweetness, and a rounder mouthfeel. The mineral content is just enough to help the coffee solids extract evenly.

For pour-over and drip coffee, this is genuinely better than tap water if yours is hard or chlorinated. It’s also consistent — every bottle tests the same, so your brew won’t vary day to day.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be direct about the downsides.

**Price per gallon:** You’re paying a premium for convenience. At typical retail, this comes to roughly $1.50–$2.00 per gallon. If you brew two cups daily, you’ll burn through a case in about two weeks. That adds up to $40–$50 per month just for water.

**Plastic waste:** 24 individual bottles is a lot of plastic. If you’re eco-conscious, this will bother you. A 5-gallon glass carboy with a spring water delivery service gives you the same water quality with zero bottle waste.

**Not distilled:** If you need zero-mineral water for a specific appliance (like a steam iron or a battery refill), this won’t work. It’s spring water, not purified or distilled.

**Taste variability:** Some reviewers reported a slight plastic aftertaste if the bottles sat in the sun during shipping. It’s not widespread, but it happens.

A: Yes, the mineral content is moderate enough to prevent rapid scale buildup while still providing the dissolved solids needed for proper extraction. It’s a solid choice for machines with boilers.

**Q: How does it compare to filtered tap water?**
A: If your tap water is already soft and neutral, you won’t see a huge difference. But if you have hard or chlorinated water, this will noticeably improve coffee flavor and eliminate scale issues.

**Q: Can I use this for cold brew?**
A: Absolutely. Cold brew extracts slower, so water quality matters even more. The clean mineral profile gives you a smooth, sweet concentrate without any off-flavors.
