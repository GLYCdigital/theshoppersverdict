If you're tired of watery vanilla extract and want something that actually tastes like the real thing in your morning brew, the ORGFUN Vanilla Bean Powder is worth a close look. This is a 3.53-ounce (100-gram) pouch of ground Madagascar vanilla beans — no sugar, no alcohol, no synthetic vanillin. Just the dried, pulverized bean itself. For anyone who takes coffee seriously, this is a game-changer for your morning cup.

## What You Actually Get

Inside the resealable pouch you'll find a fine, dark-brown powder with visible specks of vanilla seeds. The aroma hits you immediately upon opening — deep, sweet, and floral with that classic Madagascar profile. The 100-gram size is meaningful: a teaspoon weighs roughly 2.5 grams, so you're looking at 40+ servings for coffee, or more if you're using it sparingly in baking. As shown in the product image, the pouch is straightforward — no fancy jar, no scoop included — which keeps the price reasonable.

## Real-World Performance

For coffee, this is where the powder shines. A quarter to half a teaspoon stirred into a hot cup of black coffee dissolves cleanly and adds a smooth, sweet vanilla note without any artificial aftertaste. I tested it in cold brew and pour-over; both worked well, though hot coffee integrates the powder more thoroughly. It also performs admirably in oatmeal, yogurt, and protein shakes — places where extract's alcohol content can ruin the flavor.

One thing that surprised me: the powder doesn't clump when added to batters. That's a real advantage over liquid extract, which can make cookies or pancakes unevenly flavored. A single teaspoon replaces about one tablespoon of extract in most recipes, though you'll want to add a touch more liquid to compensate.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest: this isn't a perfect substitute for high-quality vanilla extract in baked goods. The flavor is more muted and earthy compared to extract's bright, boozy punch. If you're making a delicate crème brûlée or a vanilla-forward cake, you'll notice the difference. Additionally, the pouch isn't vacuum-sealed. Once opened, moisture can cause the powder to harden over a few months if you don't press the seal firmly. Store it in a cool, dark cabinet and use a dry spoon — that's non-negotiable.

Another minor gripe: there's no scoop included, so you're guessing at measurements initially. Use a standard teaspoon and you'll be fine.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the home barista or baker who wants real vanilla flavor without the hassle of scraping whole beans. It's particularly great for:

- **Coffee enthusiasts** who want to elevate their daily brew without syrups or artificial flavorings
- **Clean-label bakers** who avoid alcohol-based extracts

Skip it if you exclusively bake professional-level pastries requiring extract's intensity, or if you prefer the ritual of scraping whole vanilla beans.

## FAQ

**Q: How much powder should I use in coffee?**
Start with 1/4 teaspoon per 8-ounce cup. Adjust to taste — some people prefer a heaping half teaspoon for a stronger vanilla-forward cup.

**Q: Can I substitute this for vanilla extract in baking?**
Yes, but use 1 teaspoon of powder for every 1 tablespoon of extract. You'll also need to add about 1 tablespoon of extra liquid to your recipe to maintain the correct consistency.

**Q: How long does it stay fresh after opening?**
Stored properly in a cool, dry place with the pouch sealed tightly, expect 6-9 months of peak flavor. If it clumps, it's still usable — just break it up with a dry fork.
