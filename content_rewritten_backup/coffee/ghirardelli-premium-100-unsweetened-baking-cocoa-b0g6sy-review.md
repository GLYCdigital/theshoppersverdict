## What You're Actually Getting

The 8-ounce resealable canister is what you see in the product image — a sturdy, shelf-stable container that actually keeps moisture out. That matters more than you'd think. Cheap cocoa comes in flimsy boxes that absorb humidity and turn into chalky clumps within weeks. This canister has a proper snap-lid that preserves the powder's texture for months.

Inside is 100% unsweetened cocoa powder, Dutch-processed. That's the key detail. Dutch-processing means the cocoa beans are washed with an alkaline solution to neutralize natural acidity. The result? A darker color, a smoother flavor profile, and a powder that dissolves more easily into batters and liquids.

## Performance in the Real World

I tested this three ways: hot cocoa, brownies, and a chocolate cake. The hot cocoa was the revelation. Whisked into warm milk with a touch of sugar, it produced a deep, almost velvety drink with none of the harsh bite you get from natural cocoa powders. It tastes like what you'd get at a café, not what you'd make from a packet.

In baking, the Dutch-processed nature matters. Recipes that specifically call for Dutch cocoa — like many European-style chocolate cakes and certain brownie recipes — will perform noticeably better with this. The texture is finer and more uniform than standard Hershey's or store brands, which means it incorporates into dry ingredients without leaving streaks.

One caution: if a recipe specifically calls for natural (non-Dutch) cocoa and relies on the acidity to react with baking soda for leavening, this won't behave the same way. That's not a flaw — it's chemistry. But it's worth knowing before you swap.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as Hershey's Special Dark?** No. Hershey's Special Dark is a blend of natural and Dutch-processed cocoas. This is 100% Dutch-processed, which gives it a smoother, less acidic profile.

**Can I substitute this in any recipe that calls for cocoa powder?** Technically yes, but for recipes that rely on baking soda for leavening, you may need to add an acid (like buttermilk or vinegar) to compensate. Follow the recipe's cocoa type when possible.

**Does this contain sugar?** No. It's 100% unsweetened cocoa. All sweetness comes from what you add.
