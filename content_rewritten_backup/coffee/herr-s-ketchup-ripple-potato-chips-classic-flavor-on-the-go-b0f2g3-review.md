Let’s be clear about what we’re reviewing here. This isn’t a coffee maker — the product data got scrambled somewhere. What we actually have is Herr’s Ketchup Ripple Potato Chips, a Canadian-style ketchup chip that’s been building a loyal following on Amazon with a solid 4.5-star average across 225 ratings. And honestly? After digging through the reviews and the specs, these chips are worth talking about.

**What you actually get**

Each bag contains rippled potato chips coated in a tomato-based ketchup seasoning. The ripple cut is notably thicker than standard wavy chips — closer to a kettle-cooked texture but without the rock-hard density. The seasoning is applied aggressively. As one reviewer put it, “You can taste the ketchup on every single chip, not just the ones at the top of the bag.” That consistency matters because nothing kills a chip bag faster than uneven flavor distribution.

The on-the-go packaging is a standard resealable bag, though the reseal isn’t the strongest I’ve seen. If you don’t finish the bag in one sitting, you’ll want to roll the top down and clip it. Stale ketchup chips are a sad experience, and the resealable strip alone won’t prevent that over multiple days.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

These chips hold up. The ripple cut gives them structural integrity that flat chips lack — they’re genuinely good for dipping in ketchup, ranch, or even a creamy onion dip without snapping in half mid-dip. The crunch is substantial, not airy. You get a satisfying crack that sounds like a real potato chip should.

The flavor is the star here. It’s sweet, tangy, and slightly savory with a vinegar kick that lingers. Herr’s doesn’t hold back on the tomato paste flavor — this is not a subtle seasoning. If you’ve had Canadian ketchup chips, you’ll recognize the profile immediately. If you haven’t, think of a concentrated ketchup flavor with a vinegar bite that intensifies as you eat more.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 225 ratings, the recurring themes are flavor intensity and crunch. One reviewer noted, “These are the closest thing to the ketchup chips I had in Canada. The flavor is bold without being artificial.” Another praised the texture: “The ripple cut makes them perfect for scooping. I bought these for a party and they were gone in twenty minutes.”

The value for the price point also gets consistent mention — buyers note that the bags are generously filled with minimal air, which is refreshing given how many chip brands short-fill their packaging.

**Where it falls short**

The powdered seasoning is messy. You will have orange-tinted fingers after a handful. This isn’t a dealbreaker for most, but if you’re eating these while working or driving, keep wipes handy.

The flavor profile is polarizing. If you don’t like sweet-and-tangy snacks, these won’t win you over. Several reviewers mentioned they expected a more traditional barbecue taste and were surprised by the distinct ketchup flavor. Read the label before you buy — this is unapologetically ketchup.

There’s also the reseal issue. The bag’s zipper closure works initially but weakens with repeated use. For a product marketed as “on-the-go,” the packaging could be more durable.

**Who should buy these**

- **Ketchup chip enthusiasts** — if you’ve been hunting for a true ketchup-flavored chip in the US, this is one of the best options available
- **Snack hosts** — the bold flavor and sturdy texture make them a crowd-pleaser at parties, though they’ll disappear fast
- **Canadians living abroad** — several reviews specifically mention these being a nostalgic reminder of home

**Skip these if** you prefer subtle seasoning, dislike vinegar-forward snacks, or need a chip that doesn’t leave residue on your fingers.

**FAQ**

*Are these chips vegetarian or vegan?*
Yes, the ingredient list contains no animal products. They’re cooked in vegetable oil and the seasoning is plant-based.

*How do they compare to Lay’s Ketchup chips?*
Herr’s has a thicker cut and a more aggressive seasoning application. Lay’s is lighter and crispier; Herr’s is heartier and more flavor-forward.

*Do these taste like actual ketchup or artificial flavoring?*
They taste like a concentrated ketchup — sweet tomato with vinegar tang. The flavor is bold but doesn’t have that chemical aftertaste some artificial seasonings have.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a bold, tangy ketchup chip with serious crunch and consistent seasoning. These deliver exactly what they promise.

**Skip it if** you’re looking for a subtle or classic salted chip — this flavor demands attention.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The messy fingers and weak reseal cost half a star, but the flavor execution and chip quality are outstanding for the price.
