# Fabbri Amarena Cherries Review: The $20 Jar That Actually Earns Its Keep

Let's be clear: These are not your childhood maraschino cherries. The bright red, artificially-flavored orbs that float in neon syrup? Not these. Fabbri's Amarena cherries are dark, sour-sweet, and stem-on — preserved in a thick, almost jammy syrup that tastes like actual fruit, not candy. The 2-pack gives you two 21-ounce jars, which is a serious commitment to quality cherries. Here's what you need to know before you add to cart.

## What's Actually In the Jar

Each jar contains roughly 45-55 whole Amarena cherries — a small, dark variety grown in Bologna, Italy. They're packed in a sugar syrup with natural flavoring, and the cherries keep their firm texture rather than turning mushy. The 2-pack format is smart: one jar for the bar, one for the kitchen. At 21 ounces per jar, you're getting roughly 2.5 cups of cherries plus about a cup of syrup per jar.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The cherries themselves are consistently firm with a clean snap when bitten. The flavor profile is distinctly tart — more sour cherry than sweet — with a subtle almond-like undertone that comes through in the finish. In a Manhattan or an Old Fashioned, these hold their own against the whiskey instead of getting lost. The syrup is the real sleeper hit: it's dense enough to use as a sweetener in an Amarena soda (just add sparkling water) or drizzled over vanilla ice cream.

One thing buyers consistently note is the packaging. As one reviewer put it, "The jars arrived well-sealed with no leakage, but two cherries in the second jar were crushed — still tasted great, just not pretty." That's a recurring theme: the flavor never disappoints, but the cherries can arrive less than pristine.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The praise is remarkably consistent. A verified purchase notes: "These are the only cherries I use in my Negronis now. The syrup alone is worth the price." Another long-time customer writes: "I've bought these for years. The 2-pack is the best value — one jar lasts me about three months of daily espresso and cocktail use."

The most common complaint? Price. At roughly $1.00-$1.50 per ounce depending on current pricing, these are a luxury item. Some reviewers also mention that the syrup is sweeter than they expected — it's definitely a dessert syrup, not a light fruit juice.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the downsides are minor but real. First, the price-per-ounce stings compared to a $4 jar of maraschinos. Second, I'd estimate 10-15% of Amazon reviews mention at least one crushed cherry in their shipment — cosmetic damage, not a quality issue, but worth knowing. Third, once opened, the jar needs refrigeration and the syrup can crystallize if you leave it in the fridge door where temperatures fluctuate.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a home bartender who cares about cocktail garnishes, a dessert enthusiast who wants something beyond grocery-store cherries, or someone who appreciates authentic Italian ingredients. The 2-pack makes sense if you use cherries weekly.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight grocery budget, you're fine with standard maraschino cherries for baking, or you're buying these purely for a one-time party — the 2-pack is a commitment you'll need to use within a few months.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How long do these last once opened?**
A: Refrigerated, they'll keep for 3-4 months. The syrup acts as a preservative, but always use a clean spoon to avoid introducing bacteria.

**Q: Can I use the syrup for anything besides cocktails?**
A: Absolutely. It's excellent in sparkling water, over pancakes, in yogurt, or as a glaze for pork or duck. It's essentially an Italian amarena syrup.

**Q: Are these the same cherries used in Italian coffee shops?**
A: Yes — Fabbri is the brand you'll see behind most Italian bars, typically served with a small spoon alongside espresso or in a Spritz.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5 stars**

Fabbri Amarena Cherries in the 2-pack format are the gold standard for home use. The flavor is authentic, the syrup is versatile, and the value is reasonable for the quality. They're not cheap, and the occasional crushed cherry is annoying, but for anyone who takes their cocktails or desserts seriously, these are worth every penny.

**Buy it if** you want the real thing. **Skip it if** you're happy with the neon stuff. Your Old Fashioned will thank you.
