If you've only seen the 9-ounce grocery store shaker of McCormick Grill Mates Brown Sugar Bourbon, the 27-ounce version might look like a typo. It's not. This is the restaurant-sized bottle, and for anyone who grills more than twice a month, it's the smarter buy. At 4.8 stars from 240 ratings, the math on this one checks out — but let's talk about whether it belongs in your kitchen.

## What you're actually getting

The 27-ounce bottle is a serious container — bring your own shaker, because the cap that comes with it is a standard sprinkle top that's awkward on a bottle this size. You're getting a dry rub blend built around brown sugar, bourbon flavoring, garlic, onion, and a whisper of cayenne. The texture is fine and sandy, not chunky like some steak rubs. One 27-ounce bottle will cover roughly 25–30 pounds of meat, depending on how heavy-handed you are.

## How it performs in real use

The flavor is the headline here. The brown sugar comes through first, then the bourbon note — it's warm and slightly smoky, not like cheap vanilla extract pretending to be alcohol. The garlic and onion sit underneath, keeping it savory rather than dessert-like. The cayenne is barely there on the front end but leaves a gentle warmth on the finish. On pork chops, chicken thighs, or salmon, it creates a caramelized crust that's genuinely restaurant-quality.

One thing to know: this is a high-sugar rub, which means it will burn at high temperatures. If you're grilling over direct, screaming-hot coals, the sugar will scorch and turn bitter. Keep the heat medium (around 350–400°F) and flip often, or use it on the indirect side of the grill for larger cuts. I've also sprinkled it on roasted sweet potatoes and even over air-popped popcorn — both work surprisingly well.

## What buyers keep saying

The reviews consistently mention two things: the value and the flavor. One buyer put it plainly: *"This is the same seasoning I use at home but 3x the size for a fraction of the price per ounce."* Another noted, *"Perfect on ribs and brisket — my family thinks I spent hours on the rub when it took 10 minutes."* The few critical reviews mostly revolve around the sugar-burning issue or people who expected a wet marinade rather than a dry rub.

## Where it falls short

- **The cap is impractical.** A 27-ounce bottle with a sprinkle top means you'll either shake too much or grip awkwardly. Transfer to a Mason jar or old spice shaker.
- **It's sweet — really sweet.** If you prefer savory-dominant rubs, the brown sugar will be too forward for your taste. This is a glaze-style seasoning, not a blackened steak rub.
- **No resealable pouch inside.** The shaker top doesn't have a flip-cap, so if you store it upright in a pantry, moisture can get in over time. Keep it in a dry, cool spot.

## Who should buy this

- **Weekly grillers** who go through a standard shaker every few months — this will last you most of a season.
- **Meal preppers** who want one seasoning that works on chicken, pork, and vegetables without buying three different blends.
- **Brisket and rib smokers** who want a sweet, caramelized bark without mixing their own rub from ten spices.

Skip it if you only grill a handful of times a year — the smaller 9-ounce bottle makes more sense. Also skip it if you hate sweet barbecue flavor profiles entirely.

## FAQ

**Does this contain actual bourbon?** No alcohol. It uses natural bourbon flavoring, so it's safe for kids and won't trigger any alcohol sensitivity concerns.

**How does this compare to the 9 oz bottle from the grocery store?** Identical blend, same formula. The 27 oz bottle is just the food-service size at a better per-ounce price. No difference in flavor.

**Can I use this in a smoker?** Yes, and it works well. Apply it as a rub before smoking at 225–250°F. The sugar won't burn at those low temperatures, and it will help form a beautiful dark bark.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a regular griller who wants a versatile, crowd-pleasing sweet-and-savory rub at a genuinely better price per ounce, and you don't mind transferring to a smaller shaker.

**Skip it if:** You prefer dry, savory rubs without sugar, or you only fire up the grill on holidays.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The flavor and value are undeniable, but the impractical cap and sugar-scorch learning curve cost it half a star. For the price per pound of meat covered, this is one of the best value seasonings on Amazon right now.
