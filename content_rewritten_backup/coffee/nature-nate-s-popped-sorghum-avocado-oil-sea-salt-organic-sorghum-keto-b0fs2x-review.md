If you’re scanning the snack aisle for something that actually fits a keto or gluten-free lifestyle without tasting like cardboard, Nature Nate’s Popped Sorghum deserves a closer look. This isn’t popcorn, and it isn’t a puffed rice cake pretending to be food. Sorghum — an ancient whole grain — gets dry-popped here, tossed in avocado oil, and finished with sea salt. The result is a snack that’s lighter than air but delivers a legitimate crunch.

**What’s actually in the bag**

The 4-ounce resealable pouch contains popped sorghum, avocado oil, and sea salt. That’s the entire ingredient list. No maltodextrin, no yeast extract, no soybean oil lurking at the bottom. For anyone tracking macros, that’s a meaningful difference from most shelf-stable snacks. One serving (about 1.5 cups) runs 130 calories, 3 grams of net carbs, and 7 grams of fat — all from the avocado oil. It’s also certified organic, which checks the box for shoppers who care about sourcing.

**How it performs in the real world**

The texture is where this product wins or loses you. Sorghum pops smaller than popcorn — think mini puffed balls, roughly the size of a pea — and they hold their crunch remarkably well. There are no hulls to pick out of your teeth, no unpopped kernels at the bottom of the bag. That’s a genuine advantage over popcorn. The oil gives it a faint richness, and the sea salt provides a clean, sharp finish without the greasy residue you get from conventional chips.

One thing to note: the flavor is delicate. This is not a bold, buttery snack. It’s more like a lightly salted rice cake with better texture. That works if you’re using it as a vehicle for dips, or if you just want something crunchy to eat out of hand. But if you’re expecting popcorn-level seasoning, you’ll be reaching for the salt shaker.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews holds up when you read the specifics. One reviewer called it “the only keto snack that doesn’t taste like a compromise,” while another noted, “I bought these for my toddler and ended up eating the whole bag myself.” Recurring praise centers on the clean crunch and the fact that it’s one of the few snacks you can hand to a kid or a guest without reading a label first.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be direct about the downsides. The serving size is optimistic. A 4-ounce bag yields roughly eight servings, but in practice, most people will finish it in two or three sittings. That makes the per-ounce price steep compared to popcorn or veggie chips. Also, quality control isn’t perfect — a few reviewers reported bags with a dusty, powdery texture, likely from sorghum that sat too long before popping. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s worth knowing that the crunch can vary.

**Who should buy this**

This is for keto eaters who are tired of pork rinds and cheese crisps, parents who want a non-messy snack for kids (the pieces don’t shatter or leave crumbs everywhere), and anyone who prefers snacks with a short, pronounceable ingredient list. Skip it if you want bold flavor, need a snack that fills you up for hours, or you’re on a tight budget — this is a treat, not a staple.

**FAQ**

*Is this actually keto-friendly?* Yes. At 3g net carbs per serving with fat from avocado oil, it fits standard keto macros. Just weigh your portions — the bag makes it easy to overeat.

*How does it compare to popcorn?* It’s lighter and cleaner. No hulls, no kernels, and the crunch lasts longer. But it’s less buttery and doesn’t have that popcorn aroma.

*Does it need seasoning?* The sea salt version is lightly seasoned. If you like bold snacks, add a pinch of flaky salt or your favorite spice blend.

**The Verdict**

Buy it if you want a genuinely clean, crunchy snack that fits keto and doesn’t taste like a compromise. Skip it if you need bold flavor or value per ounce — this one costs more and tastes subtler than the average snack. At 4.2/5, it earns its rating: a well-executed niche product with minor consistency quirks.
