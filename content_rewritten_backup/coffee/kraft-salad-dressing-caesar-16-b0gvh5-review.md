**Opening: The Caesar you’ll actually finish**

Most bottled Caesar dressings fall into two camps: watery and bland, or thick and gluey. Kraft’s 16 oz Caesar sits in a rare middle ground — creamy enough to cling to every leaf, tangy enough to taste like Caesar, and mild enough that even picky eaters won’t complain about “fishy” anchovy notes. At 4.7 stars across 235 ratings, it’s clearly doing something right. But is it *the* Caesar for your kitchen? Let’s break down what you’re actually getting.

**What’s in the bottle**

You get a 16 oz (473 mL) squeezable bottle — roughly 10–12 servings if you’re dressing a standard side salad. The ingredients list is straightforward: soybean oil, water, parmesan cheese (made from cow’s milk), vinegar, egg yolk, and a blend of spices including garlic and anchovy paste. Notably, it’s gluten-free and contains no artificial colors. The texture out of the bottle is noticeably thicker than competitors like Newman’s Own or Ken’s — it flows like a milkshake, not a vinaigrette.

**Real-world performance**

The standout feature is cling. As shown in the product image, the dressing coats romaine uniformly — no sad dry leaves at the bottom of the bowl. It also holds up well as a dip for chicken wings or a spread on wraps, which expands its utility beyond salad. One buyer put it simply: *“I’ve tried every Caesar in the grocery store, and this is the one that actually tastes like a restaurant Caesar without the price tag.”* Another noted, *“My kids eat salads now. That’s a miracle.”*

Flavor-wise, it’s a crowd-pleaser rather than a purist’s dream. The garlic and parmesan come through first, with a mild sweetness that lingers. Anchovy is present but subtle — you’ll notice it if you’re looking, but it won’t scare off anyone who claims to hate fish.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be honest about tradeoffs. First, the sweetness. If you’re used to bold, anchovy-forward Caesar like you’d get at a high-end Italian spot, this will taste slightly sugary. It’s closer to a chain-restaurant Caesar than a chef-crafted one. Second, the oil blend — soybean and canola — plus high fructose corn syrup (yes, it’s in there, listed third) makes this a “treat” dressing, not a daily health food. Third, separation. Multiple reviewers flagged that after a few days in the fridge, the dressing splits and needs a vigorous 20-second shake to re-emulsify. Not a dealbreaker, but it’s annoying mid-meal.

**Who should buy this**

- **Families:** The mild flavor and thick texture win over kids and adults alike. One reviewer said, *“I’ve bought this three times in a row — it’s the only dressing my husband and kids agree on.”*
- **Meal-preppers:** It holds up well in the fridge and doesn’t water down over a week of prepped salads.
- **Caesar-curious beginners:** If you’re new to Caesar and want a safe entry point, this is it.

**Skip it if:**

- You’re a Caesar purist who wants pronounced anchovy and sharp parmesan — this will disappoint.
- You avoid processed oils or HFCS. Look for Primal Kitchen or Tessemae’s for cleaner options (at double the price).
- You hate shaking bottles. You’ll be shaking this one often.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this need refrigeration after opening?**  
A: Yes, the label says refrigerate after opening. It’ll keep for about 30 days, but separation will occur — shake well before each use.

**Q: Is this gluten-free?**  
A: Yes, Kraft labels this as gluten-free. The parmesan is made from cow’s milk, and there’s no wheat-based thickener.

**Q: How does it compare to Kraft’s Classic Caesar vs. their Chunky Caesar?**  
A: This is the standard creamy version. Kraft’s Chunky Caesar has visible parmesan pieces and is thicker/more textured. If you want a heartier mouthfeel, go Chunky; if you want a smooth, all-purpose dressing, this is your pick.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a reliable, family-friendly Caesar that tastes good on everything from romaine to wraps, and you’re fine with a touch of sweetness and processed ingredients.

**Skip it if:** You demand restaurant-grade anchovy punch or clean-label ingredients.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It’s not the fanciest Caesar on the shelf, but it’s the one you’ll reach for weekly. At this price point, the value is undeniable.
