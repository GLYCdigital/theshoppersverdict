**Is this the granola that actually tastes good AND doesn't spike your blood sugar? Mostly yes — with one caveat.**

Most "healthy" granolas taste like sweetened sawdust. One Degree's Quinoa Cacao Sprouted Oat Granola takes the opposite approach: it leans into the bitterness of real cacao and lets the sprouted grains carry the texture. It's a gamble that mostly pays off.

Here's the honest breakdown.

**What you're actually getting**

The 11-ounce bag contains a loose, mostly-cluster-free mix of sprouted oats, sprouted quinoa, coconut sugar, cacao, and coconut oil. As shown in the product image, the granola is dark — almost cocoa-colored — with visible cacao nibs scattered throughout. The ingredient list is refreshingly short and entirely recognizable: no artificial flavors, no gums, no refined sugar. Everything is organic and non-GMO verified, and the sprouting process is the brand's core differentiator — it breaks down phytic acid, making the minerals more bioavailable and the grains easier on your stomach.

**Performance and real-world use**

The texture is where this granola divides opinion. It's crunchy but delicate — think granola that shatters rather than snaps. The sprouted oats give it a lighter, airier crunch than conventional granola, which some buyers love and others find disappointing. One Amazon reviewer noted, "The texture is perfect — crispy without being hard on my teeth." Another was less impressed: "I expected bigger clusters; this is more like loose cereal."

Flavor-wise, the cacao is the star. It's dark, slightly bitter, and unmistakably real — not the milk-chocolate sweetness most granolas use. The quinoa adds a subtle nuttiness and a faint pop when you bite down. If you're used to sweet breakfast cereals, this will take adjustment. If you're coming from a paleo or low-sugar diet, it'll feel like a treat.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, three themes dominate. First, digestibility — multiple reviewers with sensitive stomachs report no bloating, which they attribute to the sprouting. Second, the honest cacao flavor — "Tastes like actual dark chocolate, not sugar," one reviewer wrote. Third, the staying power — the 5g protein and 5g fiber per serving keep people full until lunch, which is rare for granola.

**Where it falls short**

The price is the biggest hurdle. At roughly $0.50–$0.60 per ounce depending on the current listing price, you're paying a premium for the organic sprouted process. That's justified if you prioritize ingredient quality — but it's not a budget buy.

The lack of clusters is the second issue. If you're the type who picks out the big crunchy chunks from your granola, this bag will disappoint. The loose texture works well as a yogurt topping but less so as a standalone cereal.

**Who should buy this**

- **The clean-eating athlete** who wants a high-protein, low-sugar breakfast that digests easily
- **The ingredient-label reader** who refuses to eat granola with refined sugar or artificial additives
- **The dark chocolate lover** who wants dessert-ish flavor without the sugar crash

**Skip it if** you need big clusters, prefer sweet granola, or are shopping on a tight budget.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this keto-friendly?**
A: No. It has 18g net carbs per serving (23g total minus 5g fiber). It's low-sugar, not low-carb.

**Q: Does the cacao taste like chocolate?**
A: Yes, but more like 85% dark chocolate than a candy bar. It's bitter, earthy, and genuinely cacao-forward.

**Q: Can I eat this dry as a snack?**
A: Absolutely — it holds up well out of the bag. Just be aware the pieces are small, so it's more of a trail-mix texture than a handful-of-clusters experience.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy it if you prioritize clean ingredients and real cacao flavor; skip it if you need sweet, clustered granola.**

This is one of the few granolas that delivers on its health claims without sacrificing taste. The sprouting process is a genuine differentiator, the macros are exceptional, and the flavor is honest. It's not for everyone — but if it's for you, it'll become a staple.

*Buy it if: You want a low-sugar, high-protein granola that won't upset your stomach.*
*Skip it if: You're after traditional sweet, clustered granola at a budget price.*
