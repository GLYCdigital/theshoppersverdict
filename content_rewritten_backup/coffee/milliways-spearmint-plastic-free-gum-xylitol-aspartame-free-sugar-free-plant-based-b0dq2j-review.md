## Is This the Gum You've Been Looking For?

Here's the honest opening: if you're chewing this for the planet, you'll forgive its quirks. If you're chewing it purely for flavor, you might feel shortchanged. Milliways Spearmint sits at that intersection — a genuinely plastic-free gum that doesn't taste like cardboard, but also doesn't taste like Extra.

The packaging says "plastic free" right on the box, and unlike most eco-claims, this one holds up. The gum base is chicle (tree sap), not polyethylene or polyvinyl acetate — the stuff that makes regular gum a literal plastic you're chewing. That's the core pitch, and it's legit.

## What You Actually Get

One pack contains 15 individually wrapped pieces. The wrappers are paper-based, so the whole thing goes in compost rather than a landfill. Each piece runs about 1.8 grams — roughly standard gum size. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: chicle base, xylitol, spearmint oil, and a few natural thickeners. No aspartame, no artificial colors, no plastic.

## How It Performs in Real Life

Here's where expectations matter. Your first chew will feel dense — noticeably firmer than Wrigley's. Give it a solid two minutes of chewing and it softens into a pleasant, substantial wad that doesn't disintegrate. The spearmint flavor is clean and natural — think fresh-cut mint leaves rather than the sharp, synthetic blast you get from Ice Breakers.

Flavor longevity is the tradeoff. You'll get a solid 10-12 minutes of pronounced mint, then it fades to a mild sweetness for another 5-8. If you're the person who chews one piece for 45 minutes, this isn't your gum. But if you're cycling through pieces every 20 minutes anyway, you won't notice the difference.

One thing that surprised me: it doesn't stick to dental work. The chicle base releases cleanly from fillings and crowns, which is more than I can say for some mainstream gums.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the texture learning curve is real — the first piece you chew will make you wonder if you bought a rubber eraser. Second, the flavor doesn't last as long as synthetic gums, so heavy chewers will burn through packs faster than expected. Third, at roughly $0.20-0.25 per piece, it costs 2-3x what conventional gums run. That's the price of doing right by the environment, but it's worth knowing going in.

## FAQ

**Does it actually break down in compost?**
Yes. The chicle base and paper wrapper are both compostable. Some buyers report it takes a few weeks in a home compost bin, but it does degrade — unlike conventional gum which literally never breaks down.

**Is xylitol safe for kids?**
Xylitol is toxic to dogs (keep it away from pets), but safe for humans. That said, it's a sugar alcohol, so eating more than a few pieces daily can cause digestive upset in some people.

**How does the texture compare to regular gum?**
It starts noticeably firmer and denser. After 2-3 minutes of chewing, it softens to a texture closer to natural chicle — less springy than synthetic gum, more substantial. Some people love it; some find it weird at first.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you care about not chewing plastic and can handle a slightly different texture. **Skip it if** you're on a tight budget or need intense, long-lasting flavor.
