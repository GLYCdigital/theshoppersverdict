Let's be honest about what this is: a shelf-stable, sugar-free pudding cup. It's not a Michelin-star dessert, but it doesn't need to be. What the Snack Pack Sugar Free Chocolate and Vanilla 12-pack delivers is a genuinely useful tool for anyone watching carbs, calories, or blood sugar who still wants something that tastes like a treat.

I've tested these against the major competitors — Jell-O sugar-free, Kozy Shack, and the store-brand knockoffs — and here's the real story.

## What you actually get

The family pack contains twelve 3.5-ounce cups, split evenly between chocolate and vanilla. Each cup runs 60 calories, 5 grams of net carbs, and 3 grams of protein. That's a nutrition profile that actually fits into keto, diabetic, or general low-sugar eating plans without blowing your daily budget.

The cups are standard plastic with peel-back foil lids. Nothing fancy, but they're sturdy enough to survive a lunchbox without leaking.

## How it performs in the real world

The texture is where these cups surprised me. Most sugar-free puddings have that telltale gelatinous stiffness or a chalky mouthfeel from the thickeners. This one pours out with a proper pudding consistency — smooth, spoonable, and close to the full-sugar original. It's not as dense as a mousse, but it holds its shape on the spoon and doesn't turn watery as it sits.

Flavor-wise, the vanilla is the stronger of the two. It's clean, sweet, and actually tastes like vanilla rather than artificial extract. The chocolate is serviceable but leans sweeter and lighter than I'd like — think milk chocolate rather than dark. If you're a dark chocolate person, you'll want to doctor it with a pinch of cocoa powder or a spoonful of peanut butter.

One thing worth noting: sucralose is the sweetener here. Most people won't notice, but if you're among those who detect a cooling or slightly bitter aftertaste from artificial sweeteners, you'll pick it up on the finish, especially with the chocolate.

## Where it falls short

No product is perfect, and there are real tradeoffs here:

- **Sucralose sensitivity:** If you're one of the ~25% of people who taste artificial sweeteners strongly, this might not work for you. The aftertaste is subtle but present.
- **Chocolate intensity:** The chocolate cups are sweet but lack depth. They're fine for a quick fix, but they won't satisfy a serious chocolate craving.
- **Not a premium dessert:** These are convenience snacks, not dessert-showcase material. If you're plating something for guests, buy better pudding.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're on a low-carb or diabetic diet and need a grab-and-go sweet option. You pack lunches and want a shelf-stable dessert that doesn't require refrigeration until opened. You want portion-controlled treats that fit a 60-calorie budget.

**Skip it if:** You're sensitive to sucralose aftertaste. You're expecting a rich, indulgent chocolate experience. You prefer clean-label ingredients — these aren't free of thickeners and stabilizers.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these actually sugar-free?**
A: Yes — each cup has 0 grams of sugar. They're sweetened with sucralose (Splenda), which is a zero-calorie artificial sweetener. The 5g net carbs come from the cornstarch-based thickeners used to create the pudding texture.

**Q: Do they need to be refrigerated?**
A: No, the unopened cups are shelf-stable. That's a big convenience advantage over refrigerated puddings. Once opened, they should be refrigerated and eaten within a few days.

**Q: How do these compare to Jell-O sugar-free pudding cups?**
A: Snack Pack has a slightly smoother, creamier texture than Jell-O's cups, which tend to be a bit stiffer and more gelatinous. The flavor profile is comparable, though Snack Pack's vanilla is noticeably cleaner. Both use sucralose and have similar macros.
