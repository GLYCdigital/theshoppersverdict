These Chocolate Brownie Cookies Thins aren't just a "good for gluten-free" option—they're genuinely good cookies that happen to be gluten-free, grain-free, and made with a short, recognizable ingredient list.

If you've been burned by dry, crumbly GF cookies before, these are worth a second look.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains a 4.25-ounce pouch. That's roughly 14 cookies—thins, not the thick, soft-baked style. The packaging is simple, resealable, and travel-friendly. No filler, no plastic tray inside, just the cookies in a pouch.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short: almond flour, arrowroot, coconut sugar, cocoa, coconut oil, and a few other pantry staples. No gums, no weird stabilizers, no "natural flavors" hiding behind a number. That's the Simple Mills promise, and they deliver on it.

---

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

Let's talk texture first. These cookies are thin and crunchy—closer to a crisp chocolate wafer than a brownie. They snap cleanly, don't crumble into a mess in your bag, and hold up well for dipping in coffee or milk. The chocolate flavor is dark and slightly bitter, balanced by coconut sugar that doesn't spike sweetness. You taste real cocoa, not artificial chocolate.

One thing to note: the almond flour base gives them a slightly sandy, nutty mouthfeel. That's not a flaw—it's a characteristic. If you're used to wheat-based cookies, it takes a moment to adjust. But the flavor is rich enough that most reviewers don't mind.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Reviewers repeatedly mention two things: taste and ingredients.

**Where It Falls Short (Honestly)**

These aren't perfect. The biggest complaint is price-to-quantity. At roughly $4–5 per 4.25-ounce box, you're paying a premium for the clean ingredients. If you eat them quickly, that's an expensive habit.

Second, the texture won't win over everyone. If you expect a chewy, fudgy brownie, you'll be disappointed. These are crisp, not gooey. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Good, but not a brownie. It's a thin, crunchy cookie with brownie flavor."* That's accurate.

Third, the almond flour base means they're not nut-free. If you're allergic, this isn't your snack.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

Not strictly. Coconut sugar and arrowroot add carbs. They're lower sugar than most cookies, but not ketogenic. Each cookie has about 5–6g net carbs.

**Q: Do they taste like brownies?**
The flavor is brownie-like—dark, chocolatey, slightly sweet. But the texture is thin and crunchy, not fudgy. Manage expectations accordingly.

**Q: How do they hold up in shipping?**
Surprisingly well. The thin format and snug pouch design mean minimal breakage. A few reviewers noted some chips, but that's rare.
