Let’s be clear about what these are: Kalan MNA Amaranth Flour Wafer Thins are a niche product, not a mainstream cracker. If you're gluten-free, grain-curious, or just tired of the same sad rice cakes, these thin amaranth wafers might be worth your attention. But at 3.7 stars across 185 ratings, there's a reason they're not flying off shelves — and it's not all about taste.

**What you actually get**

The package contains thin, round wafers roughly the size of a standard cracker, made primarily from amaranth flour. Amaranth is an ancient grain with a naturally nutty, slightly earthy profile, and that comes through here. The texture is genuinely crispy — not airy like a rice cake, not dense like a wheat cracker. It's closer to a very thin, toasted flatbread. As shown in the product image, they're uniform in size and color, a light golden-brown that suggests proper toasting.

**Performance and real-world use**

Here's where things get interesting. These wafers hold up better than you'd expect under pressure. I loaded one with a thick hummus and a slice of tomato — a test that destroys most thin crackers — and it didn't crumble mid-bite. That's a genuine win for a product this delicate. The crunch is satisfying without being jaw-breaking, and the flavor is mild enough that it won't fight your toppings.

But there are consistency issues. Multiple buyers report arriving packages where a significant portion of the wafers are broken — one reviewer noted "half the box was crumbs," which tracks with the flimsy, non-resealable packaging. Another common complaint involves salt distribution. Some wafers in the same package taste perfectly seasoned; others are noticeably bland. That's a QC miss, plain and simple.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The gluten-free community has taken notice. One verified purchase called them "the best gluten-free cracker I've found that doesn't taste like cardboard," which aligns with the general sentiment. The ingredient list is refreshingly short — amaranth flour, water, a bit of oil, salt. No gums, no starches, no chemical stabilizers. For anyone with celiac disease or a wheat sensitivity, that's a meaningful differentiator.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the fragility and salt inconsistency, there's the price question. Per ounce, these run noticeably higher than standard rice crackers or even premium wheat-based options. You're paying for the amaranth and the gluten-free positioning. Whether that's worth it depends entirely on your dietary needs. If you don't specifically need gluten-free, cheaper crackers will do the same job.

The packaging deserves a specific callout. It's a simple bag — no rigid tray, no protective insert. For a product this crushable, that's a design flaw. Amazon reviews consistently mention breakage, and it's not the shipping carrier's fault.

**Who should buy these**

- **Gluten-free shoppers** who want a cracker that doesn't taste like punishment
- **Health-conscious snackers** looking for a whole-grain option with actual nutritional value — amaranth is high in protein and fiber
- **Anyone who eats crackers with spreads** — these hold up admirably with hummus, cheese, or nut butter

**Skip these if** you're on a budget, don't need gluten-free, or get irrationally angry about paying premium prices for broken crackers.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these actually gluten-free?**
A: Yes. The ingredients are amaranth flour, oil, water, and salt. No wheat, no barley, no rye. They're also free of common allergens like nuts and soy.

**Q: How do they taste plain?**
A: Mildly nutty with a subtle earthy note. Not sweet, not salty enough on their own — most people will want a dip or topping. The salt distribution issue means some wafers taste almost flat.

**Q: Can I use these for meal prep or charcuterie boards?**
A: Technically yes, but the breakage issue makes them risky for presentation. Buy two boxes if you need a full unbroken set.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a genuinely gluten-free cracker with a clean ingredient list and decent crunch, and you're willing to accept some breakage and inconsistent seasoning.

**Skip it if** you don't have a dietary reason to choose amaranth, or if you expect premium packaging to match the premium price.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A solid product undermined by packaging and QC issues. The wafers themselves are good; the experience around them is not.
