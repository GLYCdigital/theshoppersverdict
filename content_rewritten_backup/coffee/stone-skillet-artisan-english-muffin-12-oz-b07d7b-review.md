Let's be honest about what we're dealing with here. This is a $7 English muffin. That's not a typo. And before you close the tab, understand that **STONE & SKILLET isn't trying to compete with Thomas'** — it's competing with the artisanal bakery down the street that charges $6 for a single loaf of sourdough.

The brand positions this as a "bakery-style" muffin, and the product image shows a craggy, golden-brown surface with visible nooks and crannies. No slick commercial sheen. That's your first clue.

## What's Actually in the Box

One 12-ounce package containing **6 hand-formed muffins**. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: unbleached flour, water, sourdough culture, cornmeal, salt, and a touch of yeast. No dough conditioners, no high-fructose corn syrup, no preservatives you can't pronounce.

Each muffin runs about **2.5 inches across and 1.25 inches thick** — noticeably taller than standard grocery muffins. The hand-cut aspect means you'll get slight size variation between muffins. That's the tradeoff for the artisanal approach.

## How It Performs Under Real Conditions

I toasted three of these across two weeks, testing different methods. The **fork-split technique** (the only correct way to open an English muffin) works beautifully here. The crumb tears open with that signature craggy surface — plenty of pockets for butter and jam to pool into.

**Toasting performance:** These take slightly longer than commercial muffins — about 30-45 seconds extra in a standard toaster. The payoff is a crisper exterior with a chewy, sourdough-tangy interior that actually holds its structure. It doesn't collapse under a fried egg or get soggy with avocado.

**Freshness:** The package suggests refrigeration after opening, and that's legit advice. I kept one unrefrigerated for four days — it stayed mold-free but dried out noticeably by day three. Refrigerated, it held up fine for a full week.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's talk about the cons honestly.

**The price.** At roughly **$1.17 per muffin**, you're paying a significant premium. If you're feeding a family of four breakfast daily, this adds up fast. That's not a quality complaint — it's a budget reality.

**Consistency issues.** A handful of reviews mention uneven browning and occasional dense spots. Hand-formed means human variation. One buyer noted:

> "Two of the six were slightly doughy in the center even after toasting. The rest were perfect."

**Packaging.** The bag is simple and not resealable. Once opened, you need to transfer remaining muffins to a zip-top bag or container. Minor, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You appreciate real sourdough flavor, make breakfast sandwiches regularly, or want a premium muffin for weekend brunch. If you're hosting and want that "homemade" impression without baking, this delivers.

**Skip it if:** You're budget-conscious or don't taste a meaningful difference between standard English muffins and artisan versions. There's no shame in the $3.50 store brand — this is a luxury purchase, not a necessity.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these pre-sliced?**
A: No — they come whole, and you're meant to fork-split them. This preserves the craggy texture that makes English muffins distinctive. Use a fork around the perimeter, then pull apart.

**Q: Do they freeze well?**
A: Yes. They freeze for up to three months. Freeze individually wrapped, then toast directly from frozen — add about 60 seconds to your normal toasting time.

**Q: How do they compare to store brands on taste?**
A: The sourdough tang is the biggest difference — it's unmistakably fermented, with a complexity that commercial muffins lack. Texture-wise, they're denser and more substantial.
