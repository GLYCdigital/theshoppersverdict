**Is this celebrity coffee actually worth grinding, or is it just another athlete-endorsed cash grab?** I've tested enough branded coffees to be skeptical. But the Más+ By Messi multi-pack — two 12oz bags of Miami Punch and Berry Copa Crush — has enough substance to warrant a closer look. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

Two 12-ounce bags of whole-bean coffee. Miami Punch leans medium roast, described as tropical and citrus-forward. Berry Copa Crush is darker, promising berry sweetness with a heavier body. Both are designed for the Messi brand — the packaging is sleek, modern, and looks good on a counter. No single-serve pods, no ground coffee option; this is for people who own a grinder.

**Performance and real-world brewing**

I ran both through a standard drip machine and a pour-over. Miami Punch brews clean with a noticeable but not overwhelming citrus brightness — think orange zest rather than orange juice. Berry Copa Crush is the more interesting of the two: it has a jammy, almost wine-like depth that comes through even with milk. As shown in the product image, these are substantial bags, and 12oz each is enough for roughly 30-35 cups depending on your strength preference.

One thing that stands out: the finish. Both blends have a smoothness that suggests careful roasting — no harsh bitterness at the back of the throat, even when I accidentally let a cup sit for 20 minutes. That's rare in this price tier.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects real satisfaction. A recurring theme in the feedback is the variety factor. One reviewer noted, *"I bought this for the Miami Punch but ended up loving the Berry Copa Crush more — it's nice having two totally different moods in one order."* Another praised the smoothness: *"Finally a coffee that doesn't wreck my stomach. I drink it black and it's genuinely pleasant."* Multiple reviewers mention repurchasing, which is the strongest signal for consumables.

**Where it falls short**

Honesty requires naming the tradeoffs. First, the flavor names overpromise slightly. These are *suggestive* of tropical fruit and berries, not flavored coffees. If you're expecting vanilla-syrup-level sweetness, you'll be disappointed. The fruit notes are natural, which means they're subtle.

Second, there's no roast date on the bag. For coffee enthusiasts, that's a yellow flag — freshness is the single biggest factor in taste. Amazon's inventory rotation means you might get beans roasted a month ago or three months ago. The smoothness helps mask age, but it's a gamble.

Third, the multi-pack forces a commitment. You can't buy just one flavor. If Miami Punch isn't your thing, you're stuck with 12oz of it.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the home brewer who wants variety without committing to two separate orders. It's also a solid gift for a coffee-drinking Messi fan — the branding is tasteful, not loud. If you're a pour-over purist who obsesses over single-origin geishas, skip it. If you're a practical drip drinker who wants two solid, smooth blends to rotate through the week, this delivers.

**FAQ**

*Is this coffee flavored or naturally fruity?* Naturally fruity. The flavor notes come from the beans and roast profile, not added syrups. Don't expect artificial sweetness.

*Can I use this in an espresso machine?* Yes, but grind it fine yourself. Both blends have enough body to pull decent shots, though Berry Copa Crush performs better for espresso due to its darker roast.

*How does it compare to major brands like Starbucks or Dunkin?* Smoother and less bitter than Starbucks' darker roasts, with more character than Dunkin's standard blend. The fruity notes are the differentiator.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** you want two genuinely different, smooth coffees for daily brewing, or you're looking for a thoughtful gift for a coffee-loving soccer fan.

**Skip it if:** you demand roast dates, prefer bold flavored coffees, or you're too picky to commit to a two-bag set.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid value for the variety and smoothness, docked slightly for the freshness transparency gap and the subtle (not punchy) flavor delivery.
