You don't buy anchovy paste because you love anchovies. You buy it because you want that deep, savory backbone in your puttanesca, Caesar dressing, or bagna càuda — without the fuss of mashing fillets. Cento's 100% Italian version, in the familiar red-and-yellow tube, has been a pantry staple for home cooks for years. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the *right* anchovy paste for you? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The tube contains 2.1 ounces (60 grams) of paste made from anchovies, salt, and olive oil. That's the entire ingredient list — no MSG, no preservatives, no wheat fillers. As shown in the product image, it's a standard toothpaste-style tube with a flip cap, which means you control the squeeze and it reseals tightly.

For context: one tube equals roughly 20-25 teaspoons of paste. A typical recipe calling for anchovy fillets uses 2-4 fillets, which translates to about ½ to 1 teaspoon of paste. So you're getting 20-40 recipe uses per tube, depending on how heavy-handed you are.

## Real-World Performance

The paste itself is smooth and spreadable, not stiff or pasty. It dissolves readily into warm liquids — a key advantage over chopped fillets, which can leave unpleasant chunks if not fully broken down. In a Caesar dressing, it emulsifies cleanly with the oil and egg yolk. In a slow-simmered tomato sauce, it melts in within minutes, leaving no trace of its fishy origin — just a deeper, rounder savoriness.

One thing buyers consistently mention: a little goes a long way. Several reviewers note that a single tube lasted them months. One customer wrote, *"I use this in my Caesar dressing and it's perfect — no more chopping anchovies, and the flavor is just as good as the jarred fillets I used to buy."* Another praised its convenience: *"Squeeze, stir, done. The tube keeps it fresh way longer than an open jar."*

The flavor is genuinely Italian — briny, salty, and intensely umami, but not overly fishy once incorporated into a dish. It's noticeably cleaner-tasting than some budget pastes that can taste muddy or overly processed.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The aroma when you first open the tube is **strong**. If you're sensitive to fish smells, you'll want to squeeze carefully and reseal quickly. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's real.

The tube size is also a limitation for heavy users. If you're making Caesar dressing weekly or cooking Italian multiple times a week, 2.1 ounces will disappear faster than you expect. Several reviewers mention buying two or three tubes at a time.

And while the paste is excellent, it's *not* a substitute for high-quality whole anchovy fillets in dishes where they're meant to be visible — like a classic Sicilian-style puttanesca with fillets on top. The paste is for when you want the flavor *inside* the dish, not on display.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home cooks who make Caesar dressing from scratch** — this is the single best use case. One squeeze replaces the tedious mincing of fillets.
- **Anyone who cooks Italian sauces, stews, or braises** — add a teaspoon to your next ragù or slow-cooked tomato sauce for instant depth.
- **Meal preppers and weeknight cooks** — the tube format means no leftovers going bad in the fridge, unlike open jars of fillets.

## Who Should Skip It

- **If you want anchovies as a visible ingredient** — buy whole fillets instead.
- **If you're on a strict low-sodium diet** — this is intensely salty (anchovies are salt-cured by nature).
- **If you only cook Italian once a month** — the tube will technically last, but you might find the flavor fading after 6+ months in the fridge (it's still safe, just less vibrant).

## FAQ

**Q: How long does the tube last after opening?**
A: Stored in the fridge, it stays fresh for 3-4 months. The olive oil layer on top helps preserve it. If it darkens or smells overly sharp, it's past its prime.

**Q: Can I substitute this 1:1 for anchovy fillets in recipes?**
A: Roughly, yes. One fillet ≈ ¼ teaspoon of paste. Start with less and taste — the paste is more concentrated than you'd expect.

**Q: Is it gluten-free and keto-friendly?**
A: Yes on both counts. Just anchovies, salt, and olive oil — zero carbs, zero gluten.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want the easiest possible way to add authentic Italian umami to your cooking, value convenience, and cook saucy, braised, or dressed dishes regularly.

**Skip it if** you need visible anchovy fillets for presentation, or if you rarely cook dishes that call for that savory depth.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not glamorous, but it's reliable, clean-tasting, and genuinely useful. For a two-dollar pantry staple that upgrades everything from salad dressing to Sunday gravy, this tube earns its place in the fridge door.
