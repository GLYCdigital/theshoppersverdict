Let me be upfront: I don't usually review bread on an Amazon-focused site. But when a grocery store staple racks up 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, shoppers are clearly searching for honest answers about whether it's worth the premium over the bakery's standard loaf. Here's the real story after testing the Whole Foods Market Rosemary Sourdough Half Loaf.

## What you're actually getting

The half loaf weighs in around 12 ounces — roughly half of a full-size artisan sourdough. As shown in the product image, it arrives in a simple paper bag with a clear window, letting you inspect the crust before committing. The loaf itself is what you'd expect from Whole Foods' in-house bakery: a deep golden-brown crust with visible rosemary flecks throughout the crumb, not just sprinkled on top.

The ingredient list is refreshingly short — organic wheat flour, water, sourdough culture, sea salt, and rosemary. No preservatives, no dough conditioners, no palm oil. That's a meaningful difference from commercial sandwich bread, and it shows in both texture and shelf life.

## Performance and real-world use

The crust delivers that satisfying crackle when you squeeze it — a sign of proper fermentation and a hot oven. Slicing it (I used a serrated knife, which you'll want) reveals an open, irregular crumb with good air pockets. The interior is moist but not gummy, with that characteristic sourdough tang that's more pronounced than Whole Foods' plain sourdough.

The rosemary is the star here. It's not shy — you smell it the moment you open the bag — but it doesn't overpower the sourdough's acidity. Each bite delivers a herbal brightness that pairs exceptionally well with olive oil, roasted vegetables, or a sharp cheddar. Toasted, it transforms into something almost cracker-like: the crust crisps further while the interior stays tender.

For storage, the half-loaf format is genuinely smart. A full loaf of artisan bread goes stale in 3–4 days; this half loaf is realistically gone in 2–3 meals. It also freezes exceptionally well — slice first, freeze in a zip-top bag, and pull slices as needed. Toasted straight from frozen, you'd be hard-pressed to tell it wasn't fresh.

## What buyers consistently praise

Scrolling through the Amazon reviews, a few themes dominate. One verified buyer wrote, "This is the only bread my kids will eat without asking for something else — the rosemary makes it feel special." Another noted, "The crust stays crisp for days, which is rare for grocery store bread." Multiple reviewers specifically call out the portion size as a selling point, with one saying, "The half loaf is perfect for two people — we actually finish it before it goes bad."

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the price. At roughly $5–6 per half loaf (pricing varies by region), you're paying a 30–50% premium over Whole Foods' plain sourdough. The rosemary is flavorful, but it's not a transformative ingredient — you're paying for convenience and artisanal positioning.

Second, freshness at delivery is inconsistent. Several Amazon reviewers note receiving loaves with only 2–3 days of shelf life remaining, which is frustrating when you're paying a premium. If you're buying via Amazon Fresh or delivery, check the "best by" date before accepting the order. In-store, this is less of an issue since you can inspect the loaf yourself.

Third, the rosemary can be slightly gritty if you're eating the bread plain. It's not unpleasant, but if you're sensitive to texture, you'll notice it.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does this bread stay fresh?**
A: About 3 days at room temperature in the paper bag. Beyond that, it's best toasted or frozen. The crust holds up better than most grocery store loaves, but the interior will eventually dry out.

**Q: Can I freeze it?**
A: Yes, and it's the best way to extend its life. Slice it first, wrap tightly, and freeze for up to 3 months. Toast directly from frozen — no need to thaw first.

**Q: Is this gluten-free?**
A: No, it's made with organic wheat flour. If you need gluten-free, this isn't the product for you.
