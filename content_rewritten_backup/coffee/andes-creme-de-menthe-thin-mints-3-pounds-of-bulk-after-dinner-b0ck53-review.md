**The 3-Pound Andes Mint Bag: The Real Story for Bulk Buyers**

You’re not here for a single box of after-dinner mints. You’re looking at three pounds of Andes Creme De Menthe Thin Mints — that’s roughly 200 individually wrapped pieces. The question isn’t whether these taste good; it’s whether buying in bulk makes sense for your situation. Short answer: yes, but with a few caveats that matter.

**What’s Actually in the Bag**

The 3-pound bag is exactly that — a large, plain foil pouch filled with loose, individually wrapped Andes mints. You won’t get the iconic green boxes with the serving-size graphics. This is the food-service style packaging. Each mint is the classic rectangular piece with the green creme de menthe layer sandwiched between chocolate layers. The wrappers are the standard foil with the Andes logo, so they’re fine for a candy dish or party tray.

**Flavor and Texture: The Real Deal or Not?**

The core question — does the bulk version taste like the boxed original? Mostly yes. The mint flavor is unmistakably Andes: cool, sweet, with that distinctive creme de menthe kick that doesn’t veer into toothpaste territory. The chocolate is on the sweeter side, not dark or bitter.

Here’s where it gets nuanced. Several Amazon reviewers noted the texture is *slightly* different from the boxed version — a touch softer and a bit more chalky on the outer chocolate layer. One buyer wrote, “The taste is spot-on, but the texture feels a little mealy compared to the individual boxes.” Another countered, “I couldn’t tell a difference once they were chilled.” That’s the trick — these mints are noticeably better when cold. Straight from the fridge, the texture firms up and the flavor pops. At room temperature, they’re fine but not as crisp.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The value is the loudest theme in the 235 ratings. At this bulk size, you’re paying well under half the per-piece cost of buying individual boxes. One reviewer summed it up: “I used these for a wedding favor table and still had half the bag left for the office. Unreal value.” Another said, “Perfect for stocking stuffers — I portioned them into small bags and had holiday gifts done in an hour.”

Freshness is another consistent positive. The individually wrapped pieces keep well, and reviewers report the mints arriving with a solid shelf life — typically several months out. The foil wrappers do their job; no stale or melted pieces reported in recent reviews.

**Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself**

First, the packaging. There’s no resealable zipper on the bulk bag. Once you open it, you’re committed to either using a chip clip or transferring to an airtight container. Not a dealbreaker, but it’s a practical annoyance for a 3-pound purchase.

Second, the presentation. If you’re buying these to gift or display, the loose bag lacks the charm of the boxed version. You’ll need to do extra work — bowls, jars, or repackaging — to make them look intentional.

Third, the texture variation. As noted, a subset of buyers find the bulk version slightly less refined than the boxed mints. It’s subtle, but if you’re a strict texture person, you might notice.

**Who Should Buy This — And Who Shouldn’t**

**Buy it if:** You’re planning a wedding, office event, or holiday stocking-stuffer assembly line. You’re a household that goes through mints like water. You want to keep a candy dish stocked without constant refills.

**Skip it if:** You’re buying a single gift and want the classic packaging. You’re sensitive to texture changes in chocolate. You don’t have storage space for a 3-pound bag that needs a container after opening.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

*Q: How many mints are in the 3-pound bag?*  
Roughly 180–200 pieces, depending on the batch. Each mint weighs about 7.5 grams, so you’re looking at around 200 units. Plenty for a large event or several months of snacking.

*Q: Can I freeze these to extend shelf life?*  
Yes, and it’s actually recommended. The mints freeze well in their individual wrappers. Thaw at room temperature for 10–15 minutes before serving. Freezing also improves the texture — they come out with a satisfying snap.

*Q: Is this the same product as the Andes in the green box?*  
Same recipe, same manufacturer, different packaging. The bulk bag is food-service grade. The taste is essentially identical, though some reviewers report minor texture differences. Chilled, most people can’t tell the difference.

**The Verdict**

The Andes Creme De Menthe Thin Mints 3-pound bag is a smart buy for the right scenario. At this volume, you’re getting genuine value for gifting, events, or heavy household consumption. The flavor delivers the classic Andes experience, and the individually wrapped format is practical for portioning.

The tradeoffs are packaging and a subtle texture variance — manageable with a clip or a fridge. If you’re a casual buyer who wants a single box for the coffee table, skip this and grab the standard pack. But if you’re buying in bulk intentionally, this delivers on its promise.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Value and flavor win; packaging and minor texture differences keep it from a perfect score.
