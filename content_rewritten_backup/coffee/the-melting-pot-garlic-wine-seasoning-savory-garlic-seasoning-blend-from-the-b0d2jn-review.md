Let me be direct: a garlic and wine seasoning blend from a fondue restaurant chain sounds like a novelty gift, not a kitchen staple. But after testing this across chicken, roasted vegetables, and a quick pan sauce, I'm revising my assumptions. This is legitimately good seasoning — with a few caveats worth knowing before you buy.

**What's Actually in the Jar**

The Melting Pot Garlic & Wine Seasoning arrives in a 4.5-ounce shaker jar — roughly the size of a standard spice container. The blend includes garlic, sea salt, sugar, red wine powder, and a mix of herbs (thyme and rosemary are the most detectable). The texture is fine enough to distribute evenly but coarse enough that you can see the herb flecks.

One thing that surprised me: the wine powder isn't just for show. It's the second or third ingredient, and it shows up in the flavor profile. This isn't a garlic salt with a wine marketing label slapped on it.

**How It Performs in Real Cooking**

I tested this three ways: as a dry rub on chicken thighs, sprinkled over roasted Brussels sprouts, and stirred into a pan sauce with butter and stock.

The chicken thighs were the standout. The seasoning formed a nice crust without burning — the sugar content is moderate enough that you get browning, not charring. The wine note came through as a subtle acidity that brightened the dark meat, something most garlic-heavy blends miss entirely.

The Brussels sprouts worked well too. The salt level is calibrated smartly — I used a generous sprinkle and didn't need to add additional salt. That's rare for a commercial seasoning blend.

The pan sauce was where I noticed the limitations. The wine powder dissolved fine, but the flavor got lost. If you're looking to add wine depth to a sauce, you're better off using actual wine. This seasoning is best used as a finishing or pre-cook rub, not a sauce builder.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

Scrolling through the 235 ratings (4.7 average), a few patterns emerge. One reviewer wrote, "I bought this on a whim for a steak marinade and now it's my go-to for everything from eggs to roasted potatoes." Another noted, "The wine flavor is subtle but present — it's not overwhelming, it just rounds out the garlic."

The most common complaint? Price-to-quantity ratio. Multiple reviewers noted the jar feels small for what you pay. One wrote, "Love the flavor but I'm already halfway through the jar after two weeks." That tracks with my experience — if you cook daily, this will last about a month.

**Where It Falls Short**

The sugar content is the main issue. It's fine for oven roasting and pan searing at moderate heat, but if you're doing a screaming-hot grill or cast iron sear, you'll get burning. Also, the wine flavor is definitely subtle. If you're expecting an intense wine-forward punch, look elsewhere. This is a "wine-accented" seasoning, not a "wine-dominant" one.

The resealable cap works well, but the jar isn't moisture-proof long-term. Keep it away from your stove's steam.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home cooks who want one versatile seasoning** for chicken, pork, vegetables, and eggs. This replaces at least two or three jars in your cabinet.
- **Anyone who likes the Melting Pot's flavor profile** — this tastes like their house seasoning, not a generic imitation.
- **Gift-givers** looking for something beyond another barbecue rub or salt blend.

**Who Should Skip It**

- **Grill purists** who cook everything over high, direct heat — the sugar will burn.
- **Wine lovers** expecting a robust vino flavor. This is garlic-first with a wine accent.
- **Budget shoppers** — the per-ounce cost is higher than making your own blend.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this gluten-free?**  
A: Yes, the ingredient list contains no wheat or gluten-containing items. That said, if you have celiac, double-check the label on your specific jar since manufacturing processes can change.

**Q: Can I use this in a marinade?**  
A: Yes, and it works well. The seasoning dissolves easily in oil and acid. Use about one tablespoon per cup of marinade liquid. It's particularly good with olive oil and lemon juice.

**Q: How long does the jar last?**  
A: At moderate use (seasoning a few meals per week), expect 4-6 weeks. Heavy daily use will drain it in about three weeks. Store it in a cool, dry place and it'll stay fresh for about a year.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely versatile garlic-forward seasoning with a subtle wine kick that works on proteins and vegetables alike, and you don't mind the modest jar size.

**Skip it if:** You're a high-heat griller, you want intense wine flavor, or you're on a tight spice budget.

This is one of those rare restaurant-branded products that actually delivers on the promise. It's not perfect — the sugar content and jar size are real limitations — but for the flavor payoff, it earns its place in the rotation. At 4.6/5, this is a solid buy for anyone who cooks regularly and values convenience without sacrificing taste.
