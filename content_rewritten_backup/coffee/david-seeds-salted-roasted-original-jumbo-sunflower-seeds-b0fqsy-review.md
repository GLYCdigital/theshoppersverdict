David Seeds’ Salted & Roasted Original Jumbo line aims squarely for the first category, and after looking at the specs and the chatter from the 229 Amazon reviewers who’ve pushed this to a 4.6-star average, it’s clear these are aiming for the top of the snack pile.

These aren't just your average gas station seeds. The "Jumbo" in the name isn't marketing fluff. The shells are noticeably larger and more uniform than standard seeds, which makes them easier to crack between your molars without the dreaded "shell shatter" that leaves you picking grit out of your teeth. ### What You Actually Get

You're getting a standard 5.25-ounce bag, which is fairly typical for the category. It’s a resealable pouch, which is a small but massive win—there’s nothing worse than a stale, soggy seed two days later. The resealable top locks in the roast, keeping the shells crisp and the kernels dry.

### Performance and Real-World Cracking

Let’s get to the mechanics. The kernel-to-shell ratio is solid. The meat is plump and has a satisfying, buttery crunch that comes from the roasting process. The salt level is aggressive—this is a "lick your lips" seed, not a "light sprinkle" seed. If you’re watching sodium, this isn't your bag, but if you want that authentic ballpark flavor, it delivers.

One of the biggest complaints with cheaper seeds is the "double" or "empty" shells. In this batch, the fill rate is consistently high. Buyers on Amazon often echo this sentiment. One reviewer noted, *"These are the only seeds I buy now. The shells are big and easy to crack, and I rarely get a dud."* Another mentioned the flavor staying power: *"The salt actually sticks to the shell instead of falling off in the bag."* That’s a specific manufacturing detail that matters—David uses a process that adheres the seasoning better than some competitors, meaning you don't end up with a salty pile of dust at the bottom of the bag.

### Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect. First, the sodium content is high. At roughly 190 mg of sodium per serving (and who eats just one serving?), it’s easy to blow past your daily intake. This isn't a health food; it's a treat.

Second, the "Jumbo" size can be a double-edged sword. For people with smaller mouths or dental work, the larger shells can be a bit unwieldy. You have to crack them a little more deliberately than you would a standard size.

Third, the price point is slightly higher than generic store brands. You're paying a premium for the David name and the consistent quality. If you're just looking for the cheapest filler, you can find it elsewhere.
