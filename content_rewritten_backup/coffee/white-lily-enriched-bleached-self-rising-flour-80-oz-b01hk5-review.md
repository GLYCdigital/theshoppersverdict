## What You’re Actually Getting

The bag contains 5 pounds of **enriched, bleached, self-rising flour** made from **100% soft red winter wheat**. That last part is the entire point. Soft wheat has significantly lower protein content (typically 8-9%) than the hard red wheat used in most national brands.

In practical terms, lower protein means **less gluten development**. Less gluten means you get tender, crumbly, melt-in-your-mouth baked goods rather than tough, chewy ones. The flour is also pre-mixed with baking powder and salt, so you don't need to add leaveners.

It’s important to note: this is *not* a substitute for all-purpose flour in every recipe. You cannot swap it 1:1 in a yeast bread recipe and expect good results.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this against a standard grocery store self-rising flour (which is usually hard wheat). The difference in biscuit texture was stark. The White Lily biscuits rose higher, spread less, and had a flakier interior crumb. The "pull-apart" factor—that signature Southern biscuit quality—was noticeably superior.

One buyer put it simply: *"I grew up on these biscuits. Nothing else compares. I tried making them with other flour and threw the batch out."* Another noted: *"The texture is so much lighter. My husband, who is from the South, said they were the best I've ever made."*

The 80 oz size is a solid value. You'll get roughly 20+ batches of biscuits (assuming a standard 2-cup-per-batch recipe). For a family that bakes weekly, this bag lasts a month or two.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides.

**1. It is strictly a low-protein flour.** If you try to make pizza dough, sandwich bread, or even sturdy cookies with it, you will be disappointed. The structure just isn't there. This is a specialty product, not a pantry workhorse.

**2. The "best by" date is your enemy.** Because it contains baking powder, the leavening agent degrades over time. If you buy this and stash it in the back of the pantry for eight months, your biscuits will likely turn out flat and dense. One frustrated reviewer mentioned: *"I bought two bags and used the second one six months later. They didn't rise at all."* **Buyer beware:** Check the date code on the bag before purchasing, and use it within 3-4 months of opening.

**3. Bleached flour isn't for everyone.** Some bakers prefer unbleached for health reasons or flavor. White Lily is bleached, which is part of what gives it its distinctive texture and white color, but it's a dealbreaker for some.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this instead of all-purpose flour in any recipe?**
**A:** No. You can use it in recipes that call for *self-rising* flour. For recipes that call for all-purpose, you must omit the baking powder and salt from the recipe and add them separately, or the texture will be off. Even then, the low protein content changes the chew.

**Q: Does this flour need to be refrigerated?**
**A:** No, but it should be stored in an airtight container in a cool, dry place. Freezing is not recommended as the moisture can degrade the baking powder.

**Q: Why is this flour bleached?**
**A:** Bleaching alters the starch and protein structure, allowing the flour to absorb more liquid and create a more tender crumb. It also speeds up the aging process that naturally occurs in flour. It's a deliberate choice for texture, not just color.
