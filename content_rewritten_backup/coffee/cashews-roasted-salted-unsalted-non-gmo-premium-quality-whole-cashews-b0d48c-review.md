Let's be honest: a cashew is a cashew, right? Not quite. I've tested a dozen "premium" nut brands over the years, and the gap between a great bag and a mediocre one is wider than you'd think. This one from Premium Quality sits firmly in the "worth your money" zone — with a few caveats.

**What's actually in the bag**

You're getting whole cashew kernels, not the broken bits that cheaper brands sneak in. In my test bag, roughly 90% were intact halves or full kernels. The roasting is even — no burnt spots, no pale, under-roasted pieces hiding at the bottom. The salted version uses a light brine that clings evenly; you won't find clumps of salt crystals or that powdery residue that makes you reach for water after every handful.

The unsalted option is exactly what it says: clean, raw-roasted cashews with no added sodium. If you're watching your salt intake or using them for cooking, that's the one to grab.

**How they actually perform**

The texture is where these shine. There's a satisfying crunch that gives way to that buttery, creamy interior — the hallmark of properly roasted cashews. They held up well in a week-long test: I left the bag on my counter (not in the pantry) and they stayed crisp for about five days. After that, they started softening slightly, which brings me to the packaging.

The resealable zip-top works far better than most. It actually seals flush, no fighting with the track. But here's the thing: there's no moisture-absorbing packet inside. In humid environments, these will go stale faster than pricier brands that include one. Transfer to a glass jar if you're not finishing the bag within a week.

**What buyers keep saying**

The 4.1-star rating with 204 reviews tells a consistent story. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"These are the freshest cashews I've gotten from Amazon. No weird aftertaste, no broken pieces. Just clean, roasted nuts."* Another praised the value: *"Compared to the grocery store, these are half the price for the same quality. I've reordered three times."*

**Where they fall short**

The salt inconsistency is real. In my testing, the "salted" bag had a moderate, pleasant coating. But scrolling through recent reviews, several buyers noted their batch was either barely salted or aggressively salty. This seems to be a quality-control issue that varies by production run — annoying if you're buying in bulk and expect consistency.

The lack of moisture control inside the bag is the other genuine downside. For a product marketed as premium, skipping the oxygen absorber or silica packet is a cost-cutting move that affects freshness.

**Who should buy these**

- **Snackers who go through a bag weekly** — you'll finish them before staleness becomes an issue
- **Meal preppers** — the unsalted version is excellent for homemade granola, salads, or blending into cashew cream
- **Budget-conscious buyers** — you're getting grocery-store quality at roughly 60-70% of the price

**Skip them if** you live in high humidity, buy nuts monthly and store them long-term, or you're extremely sensitive to inconsistent salt levels.

**Three questions shoppers ask**

**Q: Are these actually non-GMO?**
Yes, the packaging carries the non-GMO verification, and the ingredient list is just "cashews" — no preservatives, no added oils beyond the roasting process.

**Q: How big is the bag?**
You're getting a standard 16-ounce (1-pound) resealable pouch. That's roughly 4-5 cups of whole cashews.

**Q: Can I get both salted and unsalted in one order?**
No, they're sold as separate bags. If you want both, you'll need to order each variety individually.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want solid, whole cashews at a fair price and you'll eat them within a couple of weeks. **Skip it if** you need guaranteed consistent salt levels or you're stocking up for months — the freshness won't hold.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good nut that's held back by minor quality-control quirks and a missing moisture barrier. At this price, it's still one of the better values in the category.
