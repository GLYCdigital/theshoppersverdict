Look, I've reviewed a lot of throat lozenges, and most of them fall into two camps: candy that pretends to medicate, or medicine that tastes like punishment. The HALLS Relief Sugar Free Black Cherry drops actually split the difference. With a 4.9-star average across 245 ratings, this economy pack has clearly found its audience — and after testing it through a brutal allergy season, I get why.

## What You're Actually Getting

This isn't a single bag. It's a full economy bundle: **12 individual cartons**, each holding 30 drops. That's roughly **360 lozenges** total. Weighing in at about 8.5 ounces per carton, the whole bundle is substantial — expect a box that takes up real shelf space.

The active ingredient is **menthol at 6.6mg per drop**, which is the standard therapeutic dose for cough suppression and minor throat irritation. These are sugar-free, sweetened with a blend of acesulfame potassium and sucralose — no corn syrup, no sticky residue on your teeth.

## Performance: Does It Actually Work?

Menthol lozenges work on two levels: the cooling sensation distracts your brain from the tickle, and the saliva production coats your throat. HALLS delivers both, but the black cherry flavor is the sleeper feature here. It has a tartness that cuts through the menthol burn — you taste cherry first, then the cool hits about three seconds in.

One thing worth noting: these are **smaller drops** than the classic round Halls. They dissolve in roughly 10-12 minutes of steady sucking versus the 15-20 you'd get from the larger format. That's not a dealbreaker, but if you're the type who nurses a lozenge for an hour, you'll burn through these quicker than expected.

## Where It Falls Short

**The economy sizing is a double-edged sword.** If you're buying for a household of one, 360 drops is a two-year supply. Menthol lozenges do lose potency over time — the cooling effect fades noticeably after about 18 months, even in sealed packaging. You're not getting a bad product, but you might be getting stale product if you hoard it.

Also, the **sugar-free sweetness has a slight artificial aftertaste** on the finish. It's subtle, but detectable if you're sensitive to sucralose. If you're expecting the rich, candy-like sweetness of the original sugar-sweetened Halls, you'll notice the difference.
