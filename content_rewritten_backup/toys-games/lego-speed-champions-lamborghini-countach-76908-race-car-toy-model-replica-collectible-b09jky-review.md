If you grew up staring at Lamborghini posters on your bedroom wall, this Lego Speed Champions set hits a specific nostalgia nerve. The Countach is the poster car of the 1980s — all sharp angles and absurd presence — and Lego's 8-stud-wide version captures that better than any previous attempt. It's not just a toy; it's a tiny piece of automotive history you can rebuild in under an hour.

**What's in the box**  
You get 262 pieces, one Lamborghini-branded minifigure (complete with race suit and printed torso), and a small but satisfying build. The Countach measures about 6 inches long, 2.5 inches wide, and 1.5 inches tall — small enough to park on a shelf without dominating, but with enough heft to feel substantial. The white body with gold accents matches the classic "Countach 25th Anniversary" vibe, and the gold wheel rims are printed, not stickers — a small detail that makes a big difference in finish quality.

**Build experience and real-world play**  
The build takes roughly 30–45 minutes, depending on your pace. Lego's engineers did something clever here: the 8-stud-wide chassis (introduced in 2020) allows for proper side-by-side seating, so two minifigures can actually ride together. That's a huge improvement over older 6-wide Speed Champions cars that looked cramped and cartoonish.

The scissor doors are functional — they pop up via a simple hinge mechanism, and they stay open without sagging. The rear engine cover lifts to reveal a detailed V12 block, and the front hood opens too. For play value, it's sturdy enough to survive aggressive pushes across a table, though the thin side mirrors will snap off if you're careless. That's a known weak point across the entire Speed Champions line.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
At 4.9 stars across 245 ratings, this set is a crowd-pleaser, and the reviews back that up. One buyer wrote, *"The proportions are spot-on. I've owned a real Countach and this captures the silhouette perfectly."* Another praised the build quality: *"No flimsy parts, everything clicks tight. The gold rims alone are worth it."* Multiple reviewers note that the white and gold color scheme photographs beautifully, making it a favorite for display.

**Where it falls short**  
The sticker sheet is the main annoyance. The iconic side stripes and the "Countach" script on the rear are stickers, not printed bricks. They're easy to apply if you're steady-handed, but misaligned stickers look sloppy up close — a common complaint in the reviews. Additionally, at roughly $20 (MSRP), you're paying a premium for the licensing and branding. If you're after pure piece count per dollar, larger Creator or City sets offer better value.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Car enthusiasts** who want a faithful replica of a legendary machine will appreciate the design accuracy.  
- **Lego collectors** completing the Speed Champions lineup will find this a must-have, especially given the 8-wide upgrade.  
- **Parents** looking for a gift for a kid 7+ who loves cars — it's a manageable build with high playability.  
- **Display builders** who want a shelf-ready model without spending hours on a 1,000-piece set.

**Skip it if**  
You prefer printed details over stickers, or you're on a strict budget and don't care about the Lamborghini branding. Also, if you're buying for a child under 6, the small parts (mirrors, exhaust pipes) are a choking hazard.

**FAQ**  
*Q: Does this set include the minifigure?*  
Yes — one Lamborghini racing driver with a printed torso and helmet.

*Q: Are the scissor doors actually functional?*  
Yes, they hinge open and stay in place. They're not spring-loaded, but they work smoothly.

*Q: How does this compare to the older 6-wide Lamborghini sets?*  
The 8-wide chassis is a massive improvement — better proportions, room for two figures, and more realistic scaling. If you own older versions, this is a clear upgrade.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a near-perfect miniature of an automotive icon with solid playability and display appeal. **Skip it if** stickers are a dealbreaker or you're hunting for maximum brick value.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not flawless, but for what it is — a $20, 262-piece tribute to a legendary supercar — it delivers exceptional charm and authenticity. Lego got the Countach right.
