# Nemesis Lockdown Review: The Underground Horror That Fixes the Original's Biggest Flaws

The original Nemesis was already a cult classic—a love letter to Alien with a semi-cooperative twist that turned friends into suspects. Nemesis Lockdown takes that formula, moves it to a claustrophobic research base on Mars, and tightens nearly every mechanical screw. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, the buzz is real. But is it worth the hefty price tag and the table space? Let's dig in.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting a lot of plastic and cardboard. The box contains 27 highly detailed miniatures (Intruders, characters, and the terrifying Night Stalker), double-layered player boards that feel premium, a massive game board depicting the underground base, and hundreds of cards and tokens. As shown in the product image, the board features multiple levels and rooms with distinct functions—from the medical bay to the reactor core. The miniatures are crisp with good detail, though they come unpainted. If you're a hobby painter, that's a blank canvas. If you're not, the gray plastic still reads well on the table.

## How the Game Actually Plays

Lockdown is a semi-cooperative game for 1–5 players. Everyone has a personal objective that might conflict with the group's survival goal—you could be trying to escape while others want to detonate the base. This creates genuine tension. You'll share information selectively, form temporary alliances, and occasionally sabotage someone's plan without them knowing.

The signature mechanic here is the **Containment System**. Rooms can be sealed and vented into space, which offers a clever way to deal with Intruders—but it also risks venting a crewmate. The new **Pathogen** system replaces the original's infection mechanic. You can develop symptoms that might be harmless... or might turn you into a host. It's a roulette wheel of dread that keeps everyone on edge.

The Intruder AI is genuinely smart. They spawn from vents, chase noise, and can lay eggs. The game escalates from quiet exploration to chaotic firefights quickly. Every decision matters, and the tension is palpable.

## What Players Keep Saying

The 4.6 rating isn't accidental. One buyer noted, "The semi-cooperative element is genius—you're always doing the math on who might betray you." Another praised the replayability: "Every game tells a different story. I've played 10 times and still haven't seen all the event cards." The consensus is that Lockdown improves on the original's pacing and introduces mechanics that feel fresh even for veterans.

## Where It Stumbles

Let's be honest about the pain points. **Setup is a slog**—budget 30–45 minutes to unbox, sort, and set up. The rulebook is the biggest offender. It's thick, poorly indexed, and assumes you've played the original. Multiple buyers described their first game as "a rules-checking marathon." You will need the official FAQ or a YouTube tutorial.

**Game length is another commitment.** A full session runs 2–3 hours, and that's if everyone knows the rules. This isn't a Tuesday-night filler. Also, the game requires frequent table space—the board plus player areas can eat a 6-foot table.

Finally, the price. This is a premium product, but it's not cheap. If you're new to the hobby, that's a significant investment.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Nemesis veterans** who want a more strategic, refined experience
- **Alien and sci-fi horror fans** who enjoy emergent storytelling
- **Semi-cooperative enthusiasts** who love betrayal mechanics (like Battlestar Galactica)

Skip it if you want a light, quick game or if your group prefers full cooperation—the hidden agendas can cause real friction.

## FAQ

**Q: How many players does it support?**
A: 1–5 players, with a solid solo mode using an automa enemy. The solo experience is decent but loses the paranoia that makes multiplayer shine.

**Q: Is it compatible with the original Nemesis?**
A: No. The components and mechanics are separate. Some fans have house-ruled crossovers, but out of the box, it's a standalone game.

**Q: How long is the average game?**
A: Expect 2.5–3 hours for a full 4–5 player session. Your first game will run longer due to rules checks.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a tense, replayable sci-fi horror experience with real strategic depth and don't mind the setup time or rulebook homework.

**Skip it if** you're a casual gamer, short on table space, or prefer games where everyone works toward the same goal without suspicion.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Nemesis Lockdown is a standout in the genre. The mechanical refinements and component quality justify the price for dedicated players. Just clear your evening and set aside the FAQ.
