**The crown jewel of preschool board games — but it's not without a few cracks.**

Let's be honest: most kids' board games are either painfully boring for adults or too complicated for the actual target audience. Pretty Pretty Princess sits in that rare sweet spot — a game that 4-year-olds genuinely grasp and 6-year-olds still request by name. With 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, this classic has staying power. Here's what you're actually getting.

**What's in the box**

You get the game board, four plastic pawns, a spinner, and the star of the show: the jewelry. There's a ring, bracelet, necklace, earrings, and a tiara — each in four color options (pink, purple, blue, and green). The pieces are better quality than I expected at this price point. The chains are actual metal links, not flimsy plastic, and the gems are embedded rather than glued on. That matters because kids will absolutely test the durability by yanking these things off each other's wrists mid-game.

**How it plays**

The rules are elegantly simple: spin, move, land on a space. Certain spaces let you claim a piece of jewelry; the black "cursed" space forces you to return everything you've collected. First player to collect all six pieces and reach the castle wins. That's it. No reading required, no strategy beyond basic counting, and rounds run 15-20 minutes — short enough to hold attention, long enough to feel like a real game.

**What parents consistently praise**

The engagement factor is off the charts. One reviewer noted, *"My 4-year-old daughter asks to play this every single day. She loves putting on the jewelry and prancing around the living room between turns."* Another parent highlighted the social dynamics: *"It teaches turn-taking and handling disappointment when the black ring steals your stuff. My kids actually play this together without fighting."* The dress-up element is the hook — kids aren't just moving pawns, they're becoming princesses mid-game.

**Where it falls short**

The jewelry sizing is the biggest complaint. The ring barely fits an adult pinky, and the bracelet is tight on most 6-year-olds. If your child is on the larger side or has older siblings who want to play, expect some frustration. One parent put it bluntly: *"My 7-year-old can't get the ring past her first knuckle. She still plays but she's annoyed she can't wear everything."*

The "lose all jewelry" mechanic can also create meltdowns. It's dramatic and fun in theory, but if one kid lands on it three times in a row (which happens), you'll hear about it. Some parents house-rule it to only lose one piece — and honestly, that's a smart adjustment for younger players.

**Who should buy this**

This is squarely aimed at girls aged 4-7 who are already into dress-up and princess culture. It's an excellent first "real" board game — the kind you pull out at family gatherings or rainy-day afternoons. It's also a solid gift for birthday parties in that age range. Skip it if your kids are 8+ — they'll find it repetitive and the jewelry won't fit anyway. And if you're looking for a game that boys will equally enjoy, the princess theme is pretty locked in, so manage expectations there.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the jewelry safe for toddlers under 3?**
A: No. The necklace chain is long enough to be a strangulation hazard, and the small gem pieces could detach with enough chewing. Stick to the 4+ age recommendation.

**Q: Can you play with just two players?**
A: Yes, but it's better with three or four. With two, the game swings feel more punishing since you're always landing on each other's cursed spaces. That said, my daughter and I played plenty of two-player rounds without issue.

**Q: Does it require batteries or assembly?**
A: No batteries, no assembly. The game board folds flat and the pieces store inside the box, though you'll want a rubber band to keep the jewelry organized.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have a princess-obsessed kid in the 4-7 range who needs an introduction to board games, or you want a reliable gift for a young niece or granddaughter.

**Skip it if:** Your kids are 8+, you're hoping for a game adults will genuinely enjoy, or you need something that works equally well for boys.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A classic for a reason. It's not perfect (the jewelry sizing and meltdown potential are real), but for its target audience, it's hard to beat the combination of simple rules, tactile fun, and genuine excitement. The 4.7-star average is earned.
