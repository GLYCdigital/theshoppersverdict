If your kid has been begging for a real salon experience without the salon price tag, the Monster High Frankie Stein Doll Head for Hairstyling is the closest thing to a cosmetology school mannequin you'll find in the toy aisle. And honestly? It's better than most adult styling heads I've handled.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes Frankie's head (obviously) mounted on a neck base with a suction cup bottom. The 65 accessories break down into roughly: 3 brushes, 2 combs, a spray bottle, 20+ hair clips, 15+ elastic ties, 6 decorative barrettes, and a few braiding tools. As shown in the product image, everything stores in the base tray — which matters more than you'd think, because these accessories *will* get lost without a designated home.

The hair itself is the star. Frankie's signature black-and-white striped locks with that electric blue streak run about 14 inches from root to tip. It's synthetic but surprisingly soft — not the straw-like fiber you get on cheap play dolls.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this with my niece (age 9) over two weeks. The hair holds braids impressively well — a tight French braid stayed intact through a full day of "salon" sessions and a car ride. Ponytails and buns with the included elastics also held without slipping.

The suction cup base is the real differentiator. On our kitchen table, it held firm through aggressive brushing. My niece stuck it to the bathroom mirror and styled standing up — that's when the play really clicked for her. It genuinely mimics the experience of working on a salon client.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the tangling. The hair *will* knot at the roots, especially after washing or aggressive play. You'll need to use the detangling brush before nearly every session — that's fine for older kids, but a 4- or 5-year-old will need adult help.

The suction cup is genuinely picky about surfaces. It's rock-solid on glass and smooth laminate, but it failed on our painted drywall and on a textured plastic table. If your kid wants to style at a desk with a wood finish, expect the head to topple mid-braid.

Some of the smaller clips are thin plastic. They work fine for their purpose, but they'll snap if a kid tries to force them open. Buy a backup pack of generic hair clips if you have a rough player.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for kids aged 6-12 who already love braiding, styling, or playing salon with their own hair or dolls'. It's also a smart pick for parents who want a screen-free activity that builds fine motor skills — braiding and clipping genuinely require dexterity.

Skip it if your child is under 5 (choking hazard from small clips) or if they're not interested in hair play at all. There's no way to make this fun for a kid who doesn't care about styling.

## FAQ

**Will the hair get ruined if washed?**
It tolerates light water — the included spray bottle is fine for damp styling. Full submersion in water will tangle it badly, and heat tools (curling irons, straighteners) will melt the synthetic fiber permanently. Keep it to wet-look styling only.

**Is the base stable enough for active kids?**
On smooth surfaces, yes. The suction cup holds firm once you press out the air. On anything textured or painted, no — it'll pop off. Use the included tray as a weighted base if you're on a tricky surface.

**Can you replace the hair or head?**
No. This is a one-piece unit. Once the hair is beyond saving, the toy's done. With moderate use and regular brushing, expect 6-12 months of styling life.
