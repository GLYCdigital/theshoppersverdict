**Super7 Ultimates SilverHawks Space Racer Review: A Heavyweight Throwback That Mostly Delivers**

If you grew up in the 1980s, the SilverHawks hold a specific, neon-drenched place in your memory. The Super7 Ultimates line has been giving cult-classic characters the premium treatment, and this 7-inch Space Racer figure is their latest shot at capturing that lightning. The question is whether it's a worthy addition to your shelf or a nostalgia cash-grab. After handling one, I can tell you: it's mostly the former, with a few caveats.

**What You Actually Get**

The box is hefty — Super7 doesn't skimp on packaging. Inside, you'll find the figure itself, two interchangeable heads (the standard masked version and an unmasked Quicksilver portrait), four extra hands (two gripping, two relaxed), a display base with a character-specific nameplate, and the Space Racer's signature weapon. For the roughly $55 price tag, that's a solid loadout. The presentation is collector-friendly, though the lack of a window means you're buying blind — so check those Amazon photos carefully.

**Build Quality and Real-World Handling**

This is where the Ultimates line earns its reputation. The figure features die-cast legs and a die-cast chest plate, which gives it a satisfying heft you don't find in most modern 7-inch figures. The metallic silver paint is clean and even, and the facial sculpt on the unmasked head is surprisingly sharp — a detail that often gets botched at this scale. As one buyer put it, "The paint apps are flawless; the metallic finish looks like actual chrome."

Articulation is a mixed bag. The arms, neck, and wrists move smoothly with good range. But the sculpted tunic restricts the hips significantly — you're not getting any deep crouches or flying kicks. In hand, the figure feels sturdy, but the die-cast lower half makes it top-heavy. Plan on using the included stand for anything beyond a static museum pose.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 229 ratings (4.6 stars), a few themes stand out. The die-cast construction gets near-universal love. "Finally a figure with some weight to it," one reviewer notes. "The legs are heavy and solid — feels like a premium product." The paint application is also frequently highlighted, with multiple buyers calling out the crisp detail on the wings and chest emblem. The unmasked head is a crowd-pleaser too, with one collector saying, "The Quicksilver head sculpt is the best I've seen from Super7."

**Where It Falls Short**

The hip articulation issue is the most common complaint — and it's legitimate. If you want dynamic flight poses, this figure will frustrate you. There were also a few reports of tight shoulder joints right out of the box. One reviewer warned, "The left arm was stuck; I had to heat it up before it would move. Be careful." It's a known QC quirk, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you force anything. The lack of a clear package window also means you can't inspect the paint before buying — a minor risk at this price point.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is squarely aimed at two groups: die-hard SilverHawks fans who've waited decades for a premium figure, and collectors who appreciate heavy die-cast construction in the 7-inch scale. If you're a completionist building out a Super7 Ultimates collection, this fits right in. But if you're looking for an action figure to pose aggressively or hand to a kid, this isn't it — it's a display piece first, toy second.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is the die-cast metal or just painted plastic?**
A: The legs and chest plate are actual die-cast metal — you can feel the cold weight immediately. It's not a gimmick.

**Q: Will this fit with other 7-inch figures like Marvel Legends?**
A: Yes, scale-wise it fits. But the aesthetic is clearly retro-80s, so it'll look more at home next to other Super7 Ultimates or vintage-style lines.

**Q: Are the shoulder joints stiff out of the box?**
A: Some units have tight shoulders. Run the joint under warm water or use a hair dryer for a few seconds before moving it — that solves the issue without risking breakage.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a SilverHawks collector, you value die-cast build quality, or you want a shelf piece with genuine presence.

**Skip it if:** You need full articulation for dynamic posing, or you're on a budget and can't justify $55 for a display-focused figure.

The Super7 Ultimates SilverHawks Space Racer earns its 4.6-star rating. It's a premium, nostalgic piece with real weight and excellent paint — just don't expect it to hit a flying kick.
