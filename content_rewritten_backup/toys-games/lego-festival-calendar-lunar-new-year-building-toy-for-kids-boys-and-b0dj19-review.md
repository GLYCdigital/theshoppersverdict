---

Let’s be clear about one thing upfront: LEGO’s Lunar New Year sets rarely miss. The Festival Calendar (set 80124) continues that tradition, but it’s not just another seasonal shelf-filler. It’s a 24-day countdown that doubles as a permanent, culturally-rich playset. Before you hit “add to cart,” here’s what you’re actually getting.

**What’s in the Box**

This is a 1,089-piece set that builds a three-dimensional, tiered calendar display. You get 24 mini-builds — one for each day leading up to the Lunar New Year — plus the main calendar structure itself. The set includes 8 minifigures, which is generous for a set in this price range. You’re not paying for a cardboard advent calendar here; every single piece is a real LEGO brick that can be rebuilt into other things after the holiday passes.

The calendar displays a festive scene with lanterns, a dragon head, firecrackers, and a traditional doorway. The build is spread across 12 numbered bags, which is standard for modern LEGO. Expect assembly time in the 4–6 hour range for a patient builder, though younger kids will need adult help with the trickier connection points.

**Build Quality and Real-World Play**

The finished model measures roughly 10 inches wide and 9 inches tall, making it a substantial display piece. The tiered design is clever — each of the 24 small builds slots into a numbered space, creating that “countdown” effect. Once the holiday is over, the mini-builds can be arranged around the base or incorporated into other LEGO scenes.

The minifigures deserve a mention. You get two adults in traditional attire, two kids, a lion dancer, and a few others that add genuine play value. The printing on the torsos is crisp, and the lion dancer costume piece is a standout — it’s a specialty mold you won’t find in other sets.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

With a 4.9-star average across 245 ratings, the consensus is strong. Parents repeatedly mention that this set teaches cultural traditions in a hands-on way. One buyer said, *“My son asked so many questions about why we celebrate this way — it turned into a learning moment he still talks about.”* Others note the build quality is typical LEGO: precise clutch, no warped pieces, and clear instructions.

The value proposition is also a recurring theme. Multiple reviewers point out that you’re getting 24 separate mini-builds plus the main structure, which makes the per-day cost feel reasonable. One reviewer summed it up: *“It’s not just a countdown — it’s a full playset that stays out long after the holiday ends.”*

**Where It Falls Short**

Here’s the honest part. The 24 mini-builds are small — most are 40-60 pieces each. That’s fine for a countdown, but if you’re expecting 24 substantial models, you’ll be disappointed. Several smaller builds are essentially decorative props (a lantern, a teapot, a bowl of oranges). They’re charming, but they’re not “wow” builds.

The price point is also worth scrutinizing. This set retails higher than LEGO’s standard advent calendars, and while the piece count justifies it, the play value is different. If you’re buying this purely for the countdown experience and don’t care about the final display piece, a generic advent calendar might serve you better.

Finally, the set’s cultural specificity means it won’t appeal to everyone. This is a celebratory set for Lunar New Year, and while it’s educational for other audiences, it’s clearly designed with that holiday in mind.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Families celebrating Lunar New Year**: This is a no-brainer. It turns a 24-day countdown into a daily bonding activity.
- **LEGO collectors**: The lion dancer mold and the tiered display design make this a distinctive shelf piece.
- **Parents of culturally-curious kids (ages 8+)**: The mini-builds spark conversations about traditions, food, and family gatherings. It’s learning disguised as fun.
- **Skip it if**: You’re on a tight budget and want the most bricks per dollar. Or if you’re looking for a fast one-afternoon build — this is designed to be spread out.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this set suitable for younger kids (under 7)?**
A: Not really. The mini-builds involve small pieces and some fiddly connections. A 6-year-old will need significant adult help. The recommended age is 8+, and I’d stick to that unless your child is a confident builder.

**Q: Can the mini-builds be rearranged after the holiday?**
A: Yes. Each mini-build is a standalone model. They can be placed around the base, combined into a larger scene, or broken down to add to your loose brick collection.

**Q: Does this set include any exclusive or rare pieces?**
A: The lion dancer costume is the standout exclusive. You’ll also get printed tiles with traditional characters (福, 春) that don’t appear in many other sets.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a meaningful, culturally-rich countdown activity that becomes a permanent display piece. The build quality is excellent, the minifigures are top-tier, and the learning value is a genuine bonus.

**Skip it if:** You need pure brick quantity for the price, or you want a single-sitting build. This is a slow-burn set meant to be enjoyed over weeks, not hours.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It’s a beautiful, thoughtful set with minor value concerns. The mini-builds could be more substantial for the price, but the overall experience outweighs that shortfall. For families celebrating Lunar New Year, this is the best countdown set LEGO has produced.
