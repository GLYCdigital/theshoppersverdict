## Pretty, Pretty Princess (1990's Edition): The Retro Reissue That Actually Delivers

If you grew up in the '90s, you remember the *Pretty, Pretty Princess* obsession. That plastic tiara. The gaudy rings. The smug satisfaction of being the first to "get dressed up" and win. Winning Move's reissue with the original artwork isn't just a cash grab — it's a faithful time capsule that plays exactly like you remember, for better and worse.

### What's in the Box

You get the game board (a pastel castle path), four pawns, the iconic cardboard spinner, and the jewelry: a crown, necklace, earrings, bracelet, and rings in four colors. The pieces are the same lightweight plastic from the original run — thin, shiny, and prone to scratching, but that's part of the charm. The 1990's cover art is untouched: the glittery princess, the bubble font, the slightly-off-model faces. It's exactly what you'd find in a 1993 Toys "R" Us catalog.

### How It Plays

The rules haven't changed: spin, move your pawn, and land on spaces that give you jewelry or *take it away* (the "lose a piece" spaces are pure childhood betrayal). First player to collect all six pieces and put on the crown wins. Games run 15-20 minutes, which is perfect for short attention spans.

The spinner is thick cardboard and held up after a dozen rounds in our test — no bent tabs or jams. The board is glossy and resists spills better than the matte original. Jewelry snaps together securely; the earrings clip without pinching, and the bracelet fits most 4-8 year old wrists.

### What Buyers Love

Parents consistently mention the nostalgia factor as the #1 reason to buy. One verified review put it plainly: *"My daughter asked to play this every night for a week. It's the same game I played in 1995, and watching her get excited over the same plastic crown I wore is surreal."* Another noted the educational angle: *"Great for teaching turn-taking and dealing with disappointment when someone steals your necklace."* The simplicity is a recurring theme — no batteries, no app, no assembly beyond snapping the jewelry together.

### Where It Falls Short

The crown is the weak link. It's sized for small heads, and on a 7-year-old it sits perched like a tiny bowl. You'll be fixing it mid-game constantly. The box is also flimsier than modern games — thin cardboard that creases if you stack anything on it. And while the retro artwork is the selling point, kids today might find it dated (though in our test, the kids didn't care — they were too busy hoarding rings).

One genuine gameplay flaw: the "lose a piece" spaces are brutal for younger players. A 4-year-old can burst into tears when forced to return their bracelet. That's authentic to the original, but know it going in.

### Who Should Buy This

- **Millennial parents** wanting to share a childhood favorite — this is the exact version you remember, not a "modernized" reinterpretation.
- **Gift-givers** for princess-obsessed kids ages 4-7 who want a screen-free activity.
- **Collectors** of retro board games — the artwork alone justifies the shelf space.

Skip it if your kid is over 8 (they'll find it too simple) or if you hate games with player elimination mechanics (there are none here, but the jewelry-stealing might sting).

### FAQ

**Q: Is this the same as the original 1990s version?**  
A: Yes, gameplay and artwork are identical. The only difference is slightly thinner box cardboard and new plastic (which is still the same cheap-but-durable type).

**Q: Can 3-year-olds play?**  
A: They can spin and wear the jewelry, but they won't grasp turn-taking or the "lose a piece" rule. Best for 4+.

**Q: How many players does it support?**  
A: 2-4 players. With more than 4, you'll need to improvise or take turns.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a faithful, playable slice of 1990s childhood that your kids will actually enjoy — not just a display piece. **Skip it if** you're looking for a modern, polished game with deep strategy; this is pure, shallow, glittery fun.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the crown fit and flimsy box, but earns full marks for delivering exactly what it promises: the same Pretty, Pretty Princess you loved, warts and all.
