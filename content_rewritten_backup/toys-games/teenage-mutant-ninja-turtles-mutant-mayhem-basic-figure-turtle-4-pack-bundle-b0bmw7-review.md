If you're shopping for a TMNT fan who loved the 2023 *Mutant Mayhem* film, this 4-pack is the obvious starting point. At $39.99, you're getting all four brothers — Leonardo, Donatello, Michelangelo, and Raphael — in their movie-accurate, teenage-scrawny designs. That's about $10 per figure, which undercuts most single-packed action figures on shelves today. But is the quality there, or are you just paying for the novelty? Let's break it down.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get four basic figures, each roughly 5 inches tall. The sculpts are the star here — the *Mutant Mayhem* art style translates shockingly well to plastic. The skin has that bumpy, textured look from the film, and the eyes are wide and expressive rather than the angry slits of older turtle iterations. Each figure comes with his signature weapon: Leo's katanas, Donnie's bo staff, Mikey's nunchucks, and Raph's sai. That's it. No alternate hands, no pizza slice, no effect parts. For this price, that's acceptable, but kids who like to swap accessories will notice.

**Build Quality and Articulation**

The figures use a straightforward articulation scheme: swivel-hinge shoulders, single-joint elbows, and cut hips with ball-jointed necks. It's not Marvel Legends-tier, but it doesn't need to be. The joints are tight out of the box — a common complaint with cheaper figures is floppy limbs, and I didn't find that here. They hold weapon poses without sagging. One thing to note: the paint apps are a little inconsistent. On my Raphael, the red mask paint had slight overspray onto the green skin. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're a mint-in-box collector, inspect before you commit.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The 4.6-star average across 229 ratings reflects genuine satisfaction. One reviewer put it plainly: "My 6-year-old loves these. They're sturdy enough for rough play but detailed enough that I don't mind displaying them." Another mentioned, "Great value for four figures. The sculpts are way better than the photos suggest." But it's not universal praise — a few buyers noted the lack of accessories as a letdown, and one parent said Mikey's nunchucks snapped at the chain within a week of aggressive play. That's worth knowing if your kid is a rough-and-tumble type.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest gap is accessories. For $40, competitors like the *Super7* or *NECA* lines include multiple hands, effect pieces, and display stands. Here, you get exactly what's shown: four turtles and four weapons. The lack of interchangeable hands limits posing options, especially for two-handed weapon grips. Also, the figures are on the smaller side — if you're expecting *Star Wars Black Series* scale, you'll be disappointed. These are compact, kid-oriented toys, not collector-grade display pieces.

**Who Should Buy This**

Three buyer profiles fit this set well:

- **Parents of TMNT fans (ages 4-9):** This is a play-ready set with no fragile parts that'll break in the first hour. The price per figure makes it forgiving when one gets lost in the couch.
- **Movie fans on a budget:** If you want *Mutant Mayhem* characters without paying $25+ per single figure, this is the smart play.
- **Gift-givers:** It's a complete set in one box — no awkward "which turtle do I buy" decisions.

Skip it if you're a serious collector needing display-grade paint apps, or if your kid is prone to destroying thin plastic weapons.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these the same scale as other TMNT figure lines?**
A: No. These are roughly 5 inches, smaller than the 6-inch *NECA* movie figures but larger than *Playmates*' older 4-inch line. They won't scale perfectly with most other TMNT collections.

**Q: Can the weapons be removed and swapped between turtles?**
A: Yes, the weapons fit in the hands snugly and can be swapped. However, the hands aren't shaped for specific grips — katanas fit best, while the bo staff is a bit loose.

**Q: Are they durable enough for heavy play?**
A: Generally, yes. The joints are sturdy, and the plastic is solid. The weakest point is Mikey's nunchuck chain, which can snap under stress. If your kid is hard on toys, consider reinforcing that one.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a complete, screen-accurate set of the four brothers without paying collector prices. This is a solid, honest value for the money.

**Skip it if** you need accessories, premium paint, or collector-grade articulation. This is a toy first, a collectible second.

**Rating:** 4.6/5 — a strong, practical buy for most families and fans.
