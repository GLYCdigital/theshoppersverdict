The WPL C24 has been a budget crawler legend for years, and the Apawband version packages that proven platform with a few sensible upgrades. If you've been eyeing a rock crawler but balked at $300+ Traxxas or Axial prices, this $70-ish truck deserves a hard look. Just know what you're getting into before you commit.

## What's Actually in the Box

The Apawband C24 arrives mostly assembled—you're looking at 10 minutes of work to mount the wheels and clip in the battery. The box includes the truck, a 2.4GHz radio with a surprisingly decent range of about 50 meters, a 7.4V 900mAh Li-ion battery, USB charger, and a spare set of shock springs. A small Phillips screwdriver is included, but you'll want your own tools for the inevitable upgrades.

## Real-World Crawling Performance

This is where the C24 earns its keep. The ladder-frame chassis with leaf springs at the rear and coil springs up front gives you genuine articulation, not just suspension travel. On loose gravel and packed dirt, the four-wheel drive with locked diffs claws forward with authority. The 180-size brushed motor provides enough low-end torque to climb a 40-degree slope on grippy surfaces.

The 1/16 scale is the sweet spot here. Small enough to crawl over the couch cushions for indoor demos, but with enough ground clearance to handle backyard rocks. As one verified buyer put it: *"Took it to a local hiking trail with rocky sections—it climbed stuff I figured would flip it. The gearbox whines a bit, but it just kept going."*

## Build Quality: The Good and the Ugly

The chassis is a molded ABS ladder frame that's stiffer than older WPL models. The metal gearbox and full ball bearings are genuine upgrades over the bushing-equipped versions. You can feel the quality when you rotate the drivetrain by hand—it's smooth with no binding.

But here's the honest truth: this is still a budget RC. The stock servo will struggle on steep technical climbs, often stalling when the wheels are turned full-lock against a rock. The plastic driveshafts are the weak link—sudden throttle inputs can twist them. And the shocks are friction-damped, not oil-filled, so they're bouncy on fast descents.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews is earned. Common themes in positive reviews include the ease of assembly, the genuine 4x4 capability, and the upgradeability. One reviewer noted: *"This thing is a tinkerer's dream. I've swapped the servo, added metal driveshafts, and now it's a mini beast. Stock it's fun, upgraded it's fantastic."*

The parts ecosystem is a major selling point. Because the WPL C24 platform is so popular, you can find metal gears, aluminum shock towers, and even brushless motor conversion kits for under $30. This truck grows with you.

## Where It Falls Short

The battery situation is the biggest annoyance. You get 15-20 minutes of runtime, then a 2-3 hour charge via the included USB cable. Buy a second battery—they're cheap—or you'll spend more time waiting than driving.

The stock remote feels toy-grade, with a stiff trigger and no endpoint adjustment. It works, but it's the first thing you'll want to upgrade if you get serious. Also, the included instruction manual is sparse—schematics are minimal, and part names are translated awkwardly. You'll rely on YouTube and RC forums more than the paperwork.

## Who Should Buy This

- **First-time crawler buyers** wanting to test the hobby without a big investment
- **Parents** looking for a rugged toy that can take backyard abuse and be repaired cheaply
- **RC hobbyists** who enjoy upgrading—this platform is a blank canvas

Skip it if you want a ready-to-run performance machine out of the box, or if you need something that can handle 20+ minute sessions without a battery swap.

## FAQ

**Is this waterproof?**
No. The electronics are exposed, and the manual explicitly warns against water. Light damp grass is fine, but avoid puddles and wet mud.

**What's the top speed?**
Around 10-12 mph on flat ground. This is a crawler, not a speed demon—the gearing prioritizes torque over velocity.

**Can my kid drive this?**
A 6-7 year old can handle it with supervision. The 2-speed gearbox (hi/lo) lets you keep it in low gear for slower, more controlled crawling.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, genuinely capable 4x4 crawler that you're willing to tinker with. The upgrade path is unbeatable at this price, and even stock, it outperforms its price point.

**Skip it if** you want plug-and-play reliability with no maintenance, or if you need longer runtime out of the box.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuine value with real crawling chops, held back only by its budget servo and short battery life. For the price, it's the best entry point into the crawler hobby you'll find.

**The bottom line:** This isn't a toy that'll end up in a closet by Christmas. It's a legitimate hobby-grade platform that rewards effort. If you're willing to learn, wrench, and upgrade, the Apawband C24 will surprise you with what it can do.
