The frame is a mix of ABS plastic and transparent acrylic panels. The clear front and side windows give kids a full view of the claw’s movement, which is half the fun. The joystick is responsive for small hands—it moves the claw in two axes (left-right and forward-back), and a dedicated button drops it. The claw grip strength is the critical spec here, and it’s adjustable via a small screw on the side of the unit. Out of the box, the grip is intentionally weak, which is actually a smart default: it makes the game challenging but not impossible. You can tighten it so a 3-year-old actually wins, or loosen it for older kids who appreciate the struggle.

Power comes from a USB cable (adapter not included, so budget for one if you don’t have a spare phone charger block). It also runs on 4 AA batteries if you want cordless placement.
