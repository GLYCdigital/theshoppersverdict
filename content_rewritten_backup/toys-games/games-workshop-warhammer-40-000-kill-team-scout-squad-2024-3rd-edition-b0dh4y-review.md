If you've been eyeing the new Scout Squad for Kill Team, you're probably wondering whether it's worth the premium over the old firstborn Scouts. Short answer: yes, but with a caveat. This isn't just a refresh — it's a complete redesign that brings Scouts into the Primaris era with MK X Phobos armor, and it shows in every sprue.

**What's in the box**

You get 10 multi-part plastic Scouts spread across two sprues, each with multiple head, arm, and weapon options. The kit builds a full Kill Team with all the specialist roles: Sergeant with choice of power weapon, sniper, missile launcher, heavy bolter, and the rest as standard warriors. Critically, it includes the token sheet and rules cards for the Scout Squad kill team — that's a genuine value-add since most kits make you buy the rulebook separately. There's also a transfer sheet with enough Chapter markings to do a full squad.

**Build quality and detail**

Games Workshop's plastic quality here is top-tier. The detail on the armor is sharp — the Mk X pauldrons have clean ridging, and the cloaks on the sniper and heavy gunner drape naturally rather than looking like stiff slabs. The poses are dynamic without being absurd; you get Scouts crouching, aiming, and advancing in ways that read well on the tabletop.

Assembly is straightforward for anyone who's built modern GW kits. The parts fit tightly, and you won't need much gap-filling. That said, the mold lines on the backpack vents require attention — they're fine enough that you'll miss them until after priming, and then they're annoying to fix. Not a dealbreaker, but budget time for cleanup.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.6-star rating over 229 reviews reflects real satisfaction. Multiple buyers specifically call out the weapon options. One reviewer noted, "The missile launcher and heavy bolter are beautifully sculpted — they actually look like they could fire, not just prop guns." Another praised the value: "Getting the full rules cards and tokens in the box made this a no-brainer for starting a new Kill Team." The consensus: this is one of the better plastic kits GW has produced recently.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest gap is that this is a Kill Team box without terrain or barricades. If you're brand new to Kill Team, you'll still need the Core Book and a terrain set to play a proper game — the cards alone won't get you there. Also, the cloaks are molded as part of the body, not separate pieces. That's great for beginners but frustrating if you want to convert or swap heads on the sniper. Finally, there's no option to build all three heavy weapons at once — you'll need to pick two, and the third goes to the bits box.

**Who should buy this**

- **New Kill Team players**: Buy this — the rules cards and tokens give you a complete team out of the box, and the build is forgiving for beginners.
- **40K collectors**: Buy this — the Primaris-scale Scouts are a significant visual upgrade over the old squatting models, and they'll fit right into a modern Space Marine army.
- **Conversion hobbyists**: Skip unless you're comfortable cutting cloaks — the molded-on fabric seriously limits kitbashing potential.
- **Budget-conscious buyers**: Wait for a sale. The price is reasonable for 10 models, but GW kits drop 15-20% regularly on Amazon.

**FAQ**

*Do I need the Kill Team Core Book to play?* Yes. The cards cover your team's rules, but you still need the core rules for movement, shooting, and terrain. The box doesn't include those.

*Can I build these as regular 40K Scouts?* Yes, but they're not the same datasheet. Kill Team Scouts use Mk X armor while 40K Scouts use the older sculpts. You can proxy them in casual games, but tournament players should check with organizers.

*Are these compatible with the old Scout arms/heads?* No. The new models are noticeably larger, and the weapon proportions don't match. Don't plan on mixing old and new bits.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a detailed, modern Scout team with real gameplay value out of the box, or if you're building a Primaris-era Space Marine army.

**Skip it if** you're a conversion-focused hobbyist, or you're expecting a complete Kill Team starter experience without additional purchases.

The Scout Squad is a strong 4.6/5 — excellent sculpts, smart rules integration, and a kit that rewards careful painting. It's just not the all-in-one package the box art might suggest.
