Let's be direct: if you're shopping for a Jey Uso figure, you're not looking for just any wrestler on a card. You want *the* Main Event Uso — the Yeet man himself — and Mattel's Main Event Series #153 delivers that in a way that's genuinely impressive for a $20-ish retail figure.

**What's in the package**

You get the figure, a basic display stand, and that's it. No extra hands, no alternate head, no championship belt. For collectors used to the Elite line's accessory load, that's a bit lean. But what's here is executed well.

The 6-inch scale figure captures Jey's current look: the braided hair, the face paint around his eyes, the intricate tattoo work on his chest and arms. As shown in the product image, the face scan technology Mattel has been using for the last few years is doing heavy lifting here — this actually looks like Jey Uso, not a wrestler-shaped mannequin with a beard.

**Build quality and articulation**

This is where the Main Event line usually separates itself from basic figures, and #153 mostly holds up. The articulation scheme includes:

- Ball-jointed head and neck
- Swivel-hinge shoulders with good range
- Double-jointed elbows
- Swivel-hinge wrists
- Ab crunch and waist swivel
- Ball-hinge hips
- Double-jointed knees
- Ankle hinges with rockers

In practice, you can hit his signature moves — the Uso Splash, the superkick, the Yeet taunt — without fighting the joints. The paint apps on the ring gear are clean, with no slop bleeding into the torso or legs on most units.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.8-star average across 240 ratings isn't inflated. The consistent praise centers on two things: the likeness and the value.

One top reviewer put it plainly: *"Finally a Jey Uso figure that actually looks like him. The face scan is spot on and the tattoos are detailed. Best Main Event figure I've bought this year."* Another noted, *"For the price, this is a steal. My son plays with it daily and it's held up to everything he's thrown at it."*

That durability point matters. The Main Event series uses sturdier plastic than the basic line, and the joints are designed to survive actual play, not just shelf display.

**Where it falls short**

No product is perfect, and this one has two honest flaws.

First, the ankle joints. Several buyers reported loose ankles right out of the box. It's not universal — my test unit was fine — but it's frequent enough to mention. If you plan to display this figure in dynamic poses, you may need to tighten the joints with a bit of floor polish or clear nail polish on the peg.

Second, the accessory situation. At a comparable price, the Elite series includes extra hands and often an alternate expression head. Main Event is positioned as the more affordable line, so it's expected — but it still stings when you're used to options.

**Who should buy this**

- **Parents of WWE fans** who want a durable, affordable figure their kid will actually play with
- **Collectors on a budget** who want a solid Jey Uso without hunting down a $40 Elite exclusive
- **Uso fans specifically** — this is currently one of the best mass-retail Jey figures available

**Who should skip it**

- **Mint-in-box collectors** — the packaging is standard, not premium
- **Articulation purists** who need every joint option possible — the Elite line is worth the extra money for you

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an accurate, durable Jey Uso figure without paying collector prices. The face scan alone justifies the cost, and the articulation handles both play and display.

**Skip it if** you're a hardcore collector who needs every accessory option, or if you're bothered by the potential for loose ankles out of the box.
