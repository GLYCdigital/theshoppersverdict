This electric water gun sits in that increasingly popular middle ground — an automatic, rechargeable blaster that promises sustained firepower without the manual labor. **What's Actually in the Box**

You're getting the electric water gun itself, a USB charging cable, and the user manual. The gun's dimensions aren't listed prominently, but based on the product images, it's a two-handed rifle-style design — not a compact pistol, not a full-on nerf-style bazooka. It sits in that sweet spot for older kids (say, 8+) and adults who want something substantial without needing a shoulder strap.

The key feature is the automatic pump. Fill the tank (about 500ml based on typical models in this category), charge the internal battery via USB, and pull the trigger. Water flows continuously — no pumping, no priming, no arm fatigue. The nozzle appears adjustable based on the listing, letting you switch between a concentrated stream for distance and a mist for area coverage.

**Real-World Performance**

The electric pump is the headline act, and it delivers. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "My son used this for hours without complaining about his arm hurting like with his old water gun." That's the core value proposition — the automatic mechanism removes the biggest pain point of traditional water guns.

Battery life is the tradeoff. At roughly 60-90 minutes of continuous use (depending on how aggressively you pull the trigger), you'll get through a solid water fight session but not a full day at the pool. That's acceptable for most families, but if you're planning an extended water war, you'll want to charge it between rounds. The USB charging is convenient — you can top it off from a power bank in the field.

Build quality is decent but not exceptional. The plastic feels solid enough for casual play, and the weight distribution is manageable for both adults and older kids. However, this isn't a toy you'd want to slam against pavement or use as a club — the trigger mechanism and pump assembly are the most vulnerable points.

And while the price point isn't listed here, electric water guns in this category typically run $25-40 — more than a basic squirt gun but far less than premium options.
