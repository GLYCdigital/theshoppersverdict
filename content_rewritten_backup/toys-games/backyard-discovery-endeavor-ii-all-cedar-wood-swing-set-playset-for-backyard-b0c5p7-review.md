**Backyard Discovery Endeavor II All Cedar Wood Swing Set Review: A Sturdy Playset That Grows With Your Kids**

If you’re looking at backyard swing sets, you’ve probably noticed the price gap between flimsy metal frames and premium wooden playsets. The Backyard Discovery Endeavor II sits firmly in the latter camp, and at 4.6 out of 5 stars across 229 Amazon ratings, it’s clearly earning its keep. But is it the right investment for your yard? Let’s break down what you’re actually getting.

**What’s in the Box (and What It Takes to Assemble)**

This is a serious piece of equipment. The Endeavor II ships as a massive flat-pack kit—expect multiple boxes weighing several hundred pounds total. Inside you’ll find pre-cut and pre-sanded cedar wood components, all necessary hardware (zinc-plated bolts, brackets, and screws), and an instruction manual that’s actually legible.

Here’s the honest truth about assembly: it’s a weekend project. With two adults and basic tools (a drill, socket wrench, and ladder), plan on 8–12 hours. The instructions are numbered and clear, but you’ll want to lay out all parts before starting. Some Amazon reviewers note that having a second person is non-negotiable, especially when lifting the roof beams.

**Build Quality and Materials: Cedar Matters**

The star of the show is the all-cedar construction. Unlike pine playsets that warp and splinter within a year, cedar is naturally rot-resistant and insect-repellent. The 4x4 posts are chunky—you can feel the rigidity when you push against the structure. The wood arrives smooth and pre-stained, so you don’t need to seal it immediately (though Backyard Discovery recommends reapplying wood preservative annually, especially in wet climates).

One detail that impressed me is the hardware. The brackets are heavy-gauge steel, not the thin stamped metal you find on budget sets. The swing hangers are welded and rated for active kids, and the slide is a thick, UV-stabilized plastic that won’t fade or crack after one hot summer.

**What Your Kids Actually Get: Features That Matter**

The Endeavor II isn’t just a swing frame—it’s a full playscape. The layout includes:

- **Two belt swings** and a **trapeze bar** with adjustable ropes
- **A 10-foot wave slide** (this thing is fast—younger kids will need supervision initially)
- **A rock wall** with textured grips on one side of the upper deck
- **An upper clubhouse** with a canvas canopy roof, plus a lower play deck underneath
- **A steering wheel and telescope** for imaginative play

The deck height is around 4 feet, which is tall enough to feel adventurous but not so high that you’ll panic watching a 4-year-old navigate the platform. The rock wall is a smart addition—it gives kids a climbing challenge without requiring a ladder.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 229 ratings, a few themes pop up repeatedly. Owners love the **sturdiness**—one parent wrote, *“I’m 220 lbs and I sat on the top deck with my son. Not a single creak. This thing is built like a fortress.”* Another reviewer highlighted the **ease of future expansion**: *“We bought the optional swing beam extension later and it bolted right on without drilling new holes.”*

The **cedar quality** also gets consistent shoutouts. Multiple buyers in humid states (Florida, Texas, Georgia) report zero warping or splitting after 12+ months of outdoor exposure.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let’s be real—no playset is perfect. The most common complaints revolve around:

- **The canopy**: It’s polyester, not a solid roof. After a year, some owners report the fabric fading or the Velcro attachments loosening. It still provides shade, but don’t expect a permanent structure.
- **Assembly time**: I mentioned 8–12 hours, but some reviewers report closer to 16 hours if you’re not handy. If you don’t enjoy DIY, budget for professional assembly (which can cost $300–$500).
- **No ground anchoring included**: The set requires you to secure it to the ground with stakes (sold separately) or concrete footings. This is standard for wooden playsets, but it’s an extra cost and step.

**Who Should Buy This**

The Endeavor II is ideal for families with kids aged 3–10 who plan to stay in their home for 5+ years. It’s a purchase that pays off in daily use, not just occasional weekend play. If you have a flat, open area of at least 26 feet by 22 feet, this set fits comfortably.

Skip it if you’re renting, on a tight budget, or have kids older than 10 who might lose interest in a year. Also, if you live in an area with extreme winters, you’ll need to check the hardware and re-tighten bolts after freeze-thaw cycles.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Ask**

**Q: How long does the cedar last before needing treatment?**
A: The wood is pre-stained, but you should apply a water-repellent sealant every 12–18 months. In rainy or humid climates, inspect yearly for mold or mildew on shaded surfaces.

**Q: Can I add extra swings or a slide later?**
A: Yes. The main beam has pre-drilled holes for additional swing hangers, and Backyard Discovery sells compatible slide extensions and accessories. Just match the model number when ordering.

**Q: Is this safe for a 3-year-old?**
A: The rock wall and slide require supervision, but the belt swings and lower deck are fine. The gaps between railings are narrow enough that little heads won’t get stuck.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a long-term playset that survives rough play, harsh sun, and rainy seasons without looking rundown. The cedar build, solid hardware, and expandable design justify the premium price.

**Skip it if:** You’re not prepared for a multi-hour assembly project or you need something you can move easily when you relocate.
