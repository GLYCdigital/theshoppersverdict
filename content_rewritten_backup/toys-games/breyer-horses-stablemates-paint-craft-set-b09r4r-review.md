If you've got a horse-obsessed kid at home, you already know Breyer. The Stablemates Paint Craft Set is the brand's entry point into the custom-model world — six unpainted horses, a palette of authentic coat colors, and a brush. At a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it's clearly connecting with families. But is it a craft kit that delivers, or a packaging exercise that disappoints once the paint dries? Here's the honest breakdown.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The set ships with six Stablemates-scale horses (about 3–4 inches tall at the shoulder). These are the same quality sculpts as Breyer's standard Stablemates line — good anatomical detail, clean mold lines, no obvious flash or defects. You get a decent range of poses: a couple of standing gaits, a grazing horse, and a canter mold. The paint set includes eight small pots — bay, chestnut, black, gray, palomino, white, plus a brown for hooves and a pink for muzzle/nose detailing. There's one fine-tipped brush included.

**How It Performs in Real Use**

The paints are the standout feature. They're pre-thinned acrylics matched to Breyer's official coat colors — the bay is a rich mahogany, the palomino has that golden honey tone. One coat gives decent coverage on the white plastic; two coats get you to a finish that looks surprisingly close to a factory-painted Breyer. The brush is better than the typical craft-kit throwaway: the tip holds a point, and it handles the small eye-and-hoof detailing work without shedding bristles.

The models themselves take paint well. The plastic has a slight sheen that acrylics adhere to without priming, though a light sanding on the legs and mane areas helps paint grip better. For kids 8 and up, this is a genuinely achievable project — one horse can be finished in an afternoon. Younger kids will need adult supervision, mainly because the paint pots are tiny and spill easily.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Reading through the reviews, the same themes keep coming up. Parents love that this is a screen-free activity that holds attention for hours. One reviewer noted: *"My 9-year-old painted all six horses in two days and hasn't stopped asking for more."* Others specifically call out the authentic colors — *"Finally a paint set where the palomino actually looks like a palomino, not orange."* The value proposition is also a recurring theme: you're getting six quality horse molds for roughly the price of two pre-painted Stablemates.

**Where It Falls Short**

The paint quantity is the biggest complaint. Each pot holds roughly 2ml — enough for one horse with careful use, but a heavy-handed painter will run dry before finishing all six. The white and black pots go fastest. Buyers should plan on picking up a basic acrylic paint set as a companion purchase.

There's also no sealant included. Once painted, the models will chip if they're played with roughly. A clear matte spray (Testors Dullcote works well) is a smart addition if the finished horses will be handled. And the brush — while decent — won't survive heavy use. Expect to replace it after the second or third horse.

**Who Should Buy This**

This set is ideal for the 8–12-year-old who loves horses and wants a hands-on creative project. It's also a strong pick for parents looking for a birthday or holiday gift that combines art and imaginative play. Adult hobbyists will find it a bit basic — you're better off buying individual Stablemates blanks and quality paints separately.

**FAQ**

**Is the paint washable?** No — these are acrylic paints. They'll come off skin with soap and water if caught quickly, but they will stain fabric. Cover your work surface.

**Can you buy replacement paints?** Yes, Breyer sells refill paint sets separately on Amazon. The colors match this kit's palette.

**How long does one horse take to paint?** Most kids finish a single horse in 30–60 minutes, including drying time between coats.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a quality introduction to model horse painting that produces display-worthy results. The authentic colors and solid sculpts justify the price, and it's an activity kids actually finish rather than abandon.

**Skip it if** you're looking for a one-and-done project with no additional purchases — budget for extra paint and a sealant. Also skip if your child is under 7, unless you're prepared to help with the fine detail work.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely good craft kit held back only by stingy paint quantities and the missing sealant. For the target age range, it's a winner.
