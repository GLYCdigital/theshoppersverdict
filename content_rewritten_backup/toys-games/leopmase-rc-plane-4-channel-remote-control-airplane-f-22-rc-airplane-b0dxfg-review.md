**A fighter jet that actually flies — with caveats**

Most RC planes in this price bracket fall into one of two categories: glorified paperweights that look cool on a shelf, or fragile toys that disintegrate on first impact. The Leopmase F-22 R tries to split the difference, and for the most part, it succeeds. But "succeeds" comes with a few asterisks worth knowing before you hit buy.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the F-22 Raptor-style airframe in grey military livery (the product image shows it accurately — it's a decent scale look, though not museum-grade), a 2.4GHz transmitter, one 3.7V 500mAh LiPo battery, a USB charging cable, and a set of spare propellers. The wingspan is roughly 20 inches with a length around 16 inches — compact enough to fly in a schoolyard but not so small it vanishes at distance.

**How it actually performs**

Here's the headline: the gyro stabilization system is the real deal. It auto-levels the wings when you release the sticks, which is the difference between a frustrating first flight and an actually enjoyable one. I've seen reviewers who've never flown RC before get this thing airborne on their second or third attempt. That's rare at this price point.

The 4-channel control (throttle, rudder, elevator, aileron) gives you genuine maneuverability. The one-key aerobatic functions — rolls and loops — are gimmicky in theory but genuinely fun in practice. One tap and the jet flips on its axis; it's a great way to build confidence before trying manual stunts.

The EPP foam construction deserves specific praise. This is the same material used in higher-end park flyers — it bends and compresses on impact rather than shattering. Multiple buyers report cartwheeling this thing into the ground and flying again after straightening the wing. As one verified purchase put it: *"Crashed it at full throttle into a tree. Bent the nose. Popped it back out and flew another battery. Pretty impressed."*

**Where it falls short**

Battery life is the biggest frustration. You're looking at 8-10 minutes of flight time, and realistically closer to 7 once you factor in throttle management. The included 500mAh pack is small, and replacement batteries run about $15-20 a pop — not outrageous, but it adds up if you want multiple flights in a session.

The assembly experience is also rougher than it should be. The instructions are a single sheet with minimal text and some awkward translations. The wing attachment in particular confused several buyers — one noted: *"The manual shows a diagram but it's unclear which tab goes where. Took me 20 minutes and a YouTube video to figure it out."* It's not hard once you get it, but it's an unnecessary hurdle.

The transmitter is functional but feels like a toy. No screen, no telemetry, no battery indicator. You'll be timing your flights with a phone or watch. For a plane with this short a flight time, that's a real oversight.

**Who should buy this**

This is squarely aimed at beginners and casual hobbyists who want a taste of RC flight without dropping $200+ on a trainer plane. If you're a complete novice, the gyro system genuinely helps. If you're an experienced pilot, you'll find it underpowered and floaty — this isn't a precision machine.

It's also a solid choice for parents with kids aged 12 and up. The durability factor is huge — this thing will survive the inevitable crashes of a learning curve.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're new to RC planes and want something that flies well out of the box, survives crashes, and doesn't cost a fortune. The gyro system is a legitimate learning aid, not a marketing gimmick.

**Skip it if:** You're an experienced pilot expecting performance, or if you can't live with the short flight times. Also skip if you don't have a reasonably open outdoor space — this is not a backyard toy.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A genuinely good beginner plane with real durability and flyability, held back by short battery life and mediocre documentation. At its price point, it's a fair deal. Just buy a spare battery or two.
