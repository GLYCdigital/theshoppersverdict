If you've got a LEGO fan who's also obsessed with video games and cute creatures, the DREAMZzz Izzie and Bunchurro set is probably already on their wishlist. The question is whether it delivers once it's out of the box. After digging through the specs and what 240 buyers are saying, here's the honest picture.

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting 328 pieces, one Izzie minifigure, a small frog companion, and the star of the show: Bunchurro, a buildable bunny that's roughly the size of a large soda can when assembled. The set comes with a full-color instruction booklet — no app required, which is refreshing for parents who don't want another screen involved.

The clever part is the dual-build mechanic. LEGO gives you two complete build options: a gaming-themed bunny mech with a cockpit, or an alternate gaming station setup. You pick one, build it, and the other set of instructions goes back in the box for a future rebuild. Buyers consistently mention this as the set's biggest selling point, and it's easy to see why — it's effectively two builds in one box.

**Build experience and real-world play**

The build itself takes most kids aged 8 and up about 45 minutes to an hour. The pieces are standard LEGO quality — they click together firmly and stay put. Bunchurro's head, ears, and legs are all posable, which matters more than you'd think. Kids actually want to move these things around, not just park them on a shelf.

One mom noted in her review: *"My son built the bunny mech in one sitting and hasn't stopped playing with it since. The ears are surprisingly sturdy — he's yanked on them multiple times and nothing's broken."* That tracks with what I've seen in similar DREAMZzz sets; the articulation points are reinforced well.

The Izzie minifigure is a highlight too — dual facial expressions (happy and determined) and her signature outfit from the show. The little frog companion is a nice touch that doesn't cost extra pieces but adds charm.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be direct: the alternate gaming station build isn't as solid as the main bunny. Several buyers reported pieces popping off when kids interacted with it aggressively. If your child is a gentle builder, it's fine. If they're a throw-it-around-the-room type, you might be reattaching bits regularly.

The stickers are another minor frustration. The controller and screen details require careful alignment, and if your kid is sticker-averse or has shaky hands, those details will look slightly off. It's cosmetic, but worth knowing.

And the price-per-piece ratio — at 328 pieces, you're paying a premium for the licensed theme and the dual-build feature. You're not getting ripped off, but you're also not getting the value-per-pound you'd find in a Creator set.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for three types of shoppers: kids who watch the DREAMZzz show and want the characters (that's a no-brainer), LEGO fans who enjoy the build-and-rebuild mechanic, and parents looking for a screen-adjacent toy that isn't actually a screen. The gaming theme makes it a natural bridge gift for kids who love Fortnite or Minecraft but need a hands-on alternative.

Skip it if your child is under 7 — the small pieces are a choking hazard, and the build complexity will frustrate younger kids. Also skip if you're strictly a display collector; the bunny is cute but it's a playset, not a showpiece.

**FAQ**

**Is this set compatible with other LEGO sets?** Yes, it uses standard LEGO bricks and connectors. You can incorporate Bunchurro into any existing LEGO collection.

**Can you build both versions at the same time?** No — the set includes enough pieces for one build at a time. You'd need to disassemble one to build the other. The instructions for both are in the booklet.

**Is Bunchurro fragile?** The main bunny build is solid. The alternate gaming station is less stable. For display, both are fine. For heavy play, stick with the bunny.

**The verdict**

This is a strong 4.6/5 set. The dual-build feature genuinely doubles the play value, the minifigure is excellent, and the build quality on the primary model is exactly what you'd expect from LEGO. The sticker application and the slightly flimsy alternate build keep it from a perfect score.

**Buy it if** your kid loves DREAMZzz or wants a creative, screen-free gaming-themed toy.
**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget or your child prefers building from their imagination over following instructions.

At 4.8 stars from 240 ratings, the consensus matches my assessment: this is a well-designed, fun set with minor flaws that won't matter to most buyers.
