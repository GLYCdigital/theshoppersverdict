Let’s cut through the hype: this is the best NINJAGO dragon LEGO has produced in years, but it’s not perfect. I’ve built it, posed it, and stress-tested the joints. Here’s what you need to know before dropping your cash.

## What’s Actually in the Box

You get 1,279 pieces and six minifigures: Egalt (with a gold armor piece and double-sided head), Arin, Sora, Rapton, and two Claw Warriors. The dragon itself stands roughly 16 inches tall with wings spanning 20 inches — it dominates a shelf in a way few LEGO sets do. The build splits into four logical stages: legs and hips, torso and neck, wings, then the head and tail assembly.

The instruction booklet is clear, but the mechanical joints require attention. These aren’t standard LEGO ball joints — they use clip-and-pin combinations that lock tighter but demand precise alignment. Expect a solid 2.5 to 3 hours for an adult builder. Not a marathon, but not a quick evening build either.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here’s where Egalt separates from the pack. The neck articulation alone is impressive — six independent joints let you curve it into dramatic S-shapes or snake it low for attack poses. The wings use a ratcheted gear mechanism at the shoulder, which means they hold position firmly without sagging. I’ve left this dragon in a diving pose for three weeks; it hasn’t drooped a millimeter.

The tail sections clip together with a satisfying click and stay rigid when posed. The legs have double-jointed knees and ball-joint hips, giving you a wide stance or a coiled spring pose. It genuinely functions as an action figure — you can swoop it around, re-pose it, and stick it back on the stand (included, and sturdier than most LEGO stands).

The color scheme is where design shines: deep emerald green with gold accents and translucent amber eyes. The printed details on the head — no stickers for the face, thankfully — give it a fierce, intelligent expression that photographs beautifully.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The 4.8-star average across 240 ratings reflects genuine enthusiasm. One reviewer put it simply: *“Best LEGO dragon I own, and I have the big red one from 2023. The poseability is unreal.”* Another noted, *“My 9-year-old built this mostly alone and the joints survived being re-posed constantly for a month. Zero breakage.”* The minifigure selection also gets repeated shoutouts — Egalt’s gold armor piece is exclusive and the Claw Warriors’ weapons are brand-new molds.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The wing shoulder gears, while excellent for holding position, do wear slightly with aggressive play — I noticed a bit of slop after maybe 200 re-poses. For display-only collectors this is a non-issue; for rough-and-tumble kids, expect to re-tighten the axle connections occasionally.

The price is also a sticking point. At roughly $110, you’re paying a premium. The piece count is solid, but comparable Creator 3-in-1 sets offer more bricks per dollar. You’re paying for the specialized joints and the exclusive minifigs — which is fair, but know that going in.

The stand, while functional, is basic. It clips to the dragon’s belly with a single connection point. Heavier poses with wings fully extended can wobble slightly. A second support arm would have been nice.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You’re a NINJAGO collector who wants a centerpiece display piece that also withstands play. You appreciate mechanical engineering in LEGO form. Your kid is 8 or older and can handle complex builds with minimal help.

**Skip it if:** You’re primarily a value-driven builder looking for maximum pieces per dollar. You need a set that survives being thrown into a toy bin daily. You already own multiple LEGO dragons and want something structurally different — though this one is arguably the most articulated yet.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this compatible with other LEGO sets?**
Yes, fully. The joints use standard LEGO pins and clips, so you can mod it or combine it with other NINJAGO sets. The dragon’s neck uses bar-and-clip connections that attach to most LEGO Technic-style parts.

**Q: How does it compare to the Legacy Red Dragon from 2023?**
The older dragon is bulkier and more “monster-like.” Egalt is sleeker, more articulated, and has a better minifigure lineup. If you want a display piece, Egalt wins. If you want a chunkier beast for play, the red one is sturdier.

**Q: What age is this designed for?**
The box says 9+, which is accurate. The mechanical joints require more precision than standard LEGO. A motivated 7-year-old could do it with adult help, but a 9-year-old should handle it independently.

## The Verdict

**4.6/5 — Buy it if you want the most posable LEGO dragon ever made. Skip it if you’re on a tight budget or need a set that survives rough-and-tumble play without maintenance.**

This is a premium product with a premium price. The engineering is genuinely innovative — no other LEGO creature offers this level of articulation without resorting to Technic panels. The minifigure selection is top-tier, the build is engaging, and the finished model looks stunning on a shelf.

Yes, it costs more per piece than other sets. Yes, the wing gears need occasional tightening. But for what it delivers — a truly interactive, poseable dragon that doubles as a display centerpiece — it earns its place as one of the best NINJAGO sets of the year. If that sounds like your kind of build, pull the trigger. You won’t regret it.
