Let's be blunt: the base game of Catan has a problem. Once the board fills up and everyone hits 8 points, it becomes a game of "roll the dice and pray." Cities & Knights doesn't just patch that flaw — it rips out the engine and rebuilds it.

**What you actually get**

The box contains 36 commodity cards, 36 progress cards, 54 city improvement tokens, 12 metropolis pieces, a barbarian ship and army, three knight levels, and the new die. As shown in the product image, the components match the quality of the base game — thick cardboard, clear iconography, and the familiar muted color palette. Nothing feels cheap. You'll also need a copy of the base Catan (5th edition or later) to play; this is an expansion, not a standalone game.

**How it changes the game**

Three new progress tracks — politics, trade, and science — replace the simple resource economy. Each time you build a city improvement, you unlock a different set of progress cards. The science track gives you free resource cards on certain rolls. Trade lets you swap commodities more efficiently. Politics hands you defensive advantages.

Here's the kicker: these tracks aren't separate. A trade card might let you steal a progress card from someone else's science track. The barbarians attack every few turns, and if your collective knight strength isn't high enough, the player with the fewest active knights loses a city. That's a real consequence — losing a city can drop you from 9 points to 7 in one turn.

The commodity system deserves special mention. Base Catan has a notorious "sheep problem" — you always roll too many or too few. Commodities (paper, cloth, coin) are tied to specific terrain types and city upgrades, so every hex type stays relevant for the entire game. A sheep hex that feels dead in the base game becomes valuable because you need cloth for trade progress.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The Amazon reviews at 4.8 stars reflect a pattern. One reviewer wrote, "This is the only way my group plays Catan anymore. The base game feels incomplete without it." Another said, "The barbarian mechanic finally gives us a reason to coordinate instead of just hoarding resources." The depth is the most common compliment — players report that the strategy ceiling is dramatically higher, and the game rewards planning over luck.

**Where it falls short**

The rulebook is genuinely bad. It's organized thematically rather than procedurally, which means you'll be flipping back and forth during your first game. Plan for a 30-minute teach even if your group knows base Catan cold. The game also runs long — 2-3 hours is realistic for four players, and that's with everyone paying attention. If your group likes quick games, this will overstay its welcome.

The 5-6 player extension issue is worth flagging. If you have a six-person group, you'll need to buy a separate expansion piece, which is an extra $15-20. That's a frustrating nickel-and-dime situation for an already pricey expansion.

**Who should buy this**

This is for groups that have played base Catan at least five times and are starting to find it repetitive. If you're a casual player who enjoys the light strategy of the base game, skip it — the added complexity won't feel like an improvement. But if you're a group that loves deep euro-style mechanics and doesn't mind longer sessions, this is the definitive Catan experience.

**FAQ**

*Is it worth the higher price compared to other Catan expansions?*  
Yes, if you want more depth. Seafarers adds exploration but keeps the same core loop. Cities & Knights fundamentally changes how you play. It's a bigger rules investment but a bigger payoff.

*Can you play it with two players?*  
Technically yes, but it's not great. The barbarian mechanic works better with more players because the threat level scales. Three or four is the sweet spot.

*Do I need the 5-6 player extension right away?*  
Only if your group has more than four people. If you typically play with three or four, you're fine.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** your group wants a deeper, longer, more strategic Catan that rewards planning over luck.  
**Skip it if** you play casually, have limited time, or prefer the simple elegance of the base game.

Rating: 4.6/5 — a must-have expansion for dedicated Catan players, held back only by its steep learning curve and long playtime.
