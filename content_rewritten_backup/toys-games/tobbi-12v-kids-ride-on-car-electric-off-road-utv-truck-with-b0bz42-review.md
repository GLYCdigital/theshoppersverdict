If you've ever watched a kid's eyes light up at a ride-on car only to watch the toy die on a patch of grass, you know the frustration. The TOBBI 12V UTV is built to answer that specific problem — and for the most part, it delivers.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the UTV body, four knobby tires, two 12V motors (one per rear wheel), a 12V 7Ah battery, the charger, a parental remote, and the assembly hardware. As shown in the product image, this is a two-seater with a roll cage, LED headlights, and a rear cargo bed — not a single-rider toy. The unit measures roughly 47 inches long and 30 inches wide, which means it needs a real chunk of garage or shed space between uses.

## Real-World Performance

The dual motors are the headline feature here, and they earn their keep. On flat pavement, this thing moves at a claimed 3.7 mph — about a brisk walking pace. The difference from single-motor ride-ons shows up the moment you leave the driveway. Grass, packed dirt, and even mild gravel don't bog it down. The independent suspension on all four wheels absorbs bumps that would rattle cheaper units to pieces. One verified buyer noted, "My son drives this over our uneven backyard daily and it hasn't missed a beat in three months." That tracks with the 4.3-star average across 215 ratings.

The parental remote is a genuine safety net, not a gimmick. It gives you forward, reverse, and — critically — an emergency stop function that overrides the kid's foot pedal. Range is about 60 feet, which is enough to stay in control from a lawn chair or porch.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Parents repeatedly mention three things: the build quality of the frame, the ease of the remote control, and the sheer joy kids get from the two-seat layout. One review put it simply: "My 3 and 5 year old both fit comfortably and fight over who gets to drive." The 3-point harnesses and locking doors (yes, the doors actually latch) get consistent shout-outs for safety.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. Assembly is the biggest hurdle — expect a solid 45 to 60 minutes of work, and the manual's axle installation diagram is genuinely confusing. Multiple reviewers mention this. Set aside extra time and have a socket set handy.

The music and sound effects panel has no volume control. The horn and engine sounds are loud — louder than most parents want. You can disconnect the speaker wire, but that's a hack, not a feature.

Battery life is the other real issue. TOBBI advertises up to 2 hours, but that's on smooth, flat ground at low speed. On grass or hills, you'll see 45 to 60 minutes max. That's typical for this class, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

## Who Should Buy This

This UTV is ideal for parents with kids aged 3 to 7 who want a ride-on that works beyond the driveway. If you have a yard with actual grass or uneven terrain, the dual motors and suspension justify the price premium over single-motor alternatives. It's also a smart pick for siblings or friends close in age — the two-seat layout doubles the fun and the utility.

Skip it if your kid is over 7 or already tall — the seats are snug for bigger kids. And if you only need pavement cruising, a cheaper single-motor model will do the job.

## FAQ

**How fast does it actually go?**
About 3.7 mph on flat ground. There are high/low speed settings, and the low setting is genuinely gentle enough for a 3-year-old.

**Can the battery be replaced?**
Yes, it's a standard 12V 7Ah sealed lead-acid battery. Replacement costs around $25–30 and takes about 10 minutes to swap.

**Does the remote work if the kid is driving?**
Yes, and that's the point. The remote overrides the pedal controls entirely — you can brake, steer, and drive it yourself if needed.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The TOBBI 12V UTV earns its keep with real off-road capability, a functional parental remote, and a two-seat layout that extends its lifespan across siblings. Assembly frustration and short real-world battery life are honest tradeoffs, but for families with grass to conquer, this is one of the better values in the 12V ride-on category.

**Buy it if:** You want a ride-on that survives grass, hills, and two kids at once.

**Skip it if:** You need quiet operation, easy assembly, or your child is already too big for a 47-inch frame.
