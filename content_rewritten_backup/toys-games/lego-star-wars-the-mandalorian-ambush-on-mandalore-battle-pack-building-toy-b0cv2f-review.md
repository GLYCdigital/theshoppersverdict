The 2024 wave of LEGO Star Wars battle packs has been hit-or-miss, but the Ambush on Mandalore set (set number 75373) is the one collectors have been circling since the first leaked images. At roughly $20 for 157 pieces, you're paying a premium per brick — but you're not buying this for the bricks. You're buying it for the four minifigures, and those are genuinely excellent.

## What's in the Box

Inside the small box you get four numbered bags, one instruction booklet, and a sticker sheet with just a few decals. The build itself is a compact Mandalorian forge scene: a dark grey platform with a trans-orange molten metal element, a small control console, and a flick-fire missile launcher mounted underneath. It takes about 15 minutes to assemble. Nothing here is revolutionary, but the dark grey and dark bluish-grey color blocking reads well on a shelf.

The real stars are the figures. You get two Mandalorian warriors in blue and orange armor, a Praetorian Guard, and Moff Gideon in his Dark Trooper exoskeleton. The Praetorian Guard is the headline — it's the first time LEGO has produced this character with a molded fabric skirt piece and the deep red armor printing carries over to the legs and arms. The helmet is a new mold, not a repaint.

## Performance and Build Quality

The figures hold up to handling. The Mandalorian helmets sit snugly, the Praetorian Guard's skirt doesn't impede leg articulation, and Moff Gideon's Dark Trooper armor — a new torso and leg combo with metallic silver printing — looks sharp from every angle. The printing quality is what you'd expect from LEGO at this price point: crisp, no misalignment, and durable enough for regular play.

The small forge build is sturdier than it looks. The missile launcher actually fires with satisfying force, and the platform connects to the base using standard studs so it won't collapse during play. That said, it's a display piece at heart. The set is designed to be a backdrop for the figures, not a standalone playset.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't an accident. The minifigure selection is what drives the score. One buyer put it plainly: "These are the best Mandalorian figures LEGO has released since the 2020 Mandalore set. The Praetorian Guard alone is worth the price." Another noted, "The Dark Trooper armor is far more detailed than I expected for a battle pack."

Multiple reviewers also mention the playability — the flick-fire missile and the fact that the figures fit into other Mandalorian-themed builds seamlessly. If you're a collector who army-builds Mandalorians, this set gives you two solid warriors with new torso prints that don't look like carbon copies of previous releases.

## Where It Falls Short

The elephant in the room is the price-to-piece ratio. 157 pieces for $20 is steep, even by LEGO's inflated 2024 standards. The build is a glorified stand, and if you're not interested in the figures, you're overpaying for a small platform. The sticker sheet also feels unnecessary — the console details could have been printed for a set this size.

The second issue is availability. This set sells out quickly at retail, and third-party prices on Amazon can creep toward $30. At that price, the value equation shifts. If you see it at MSRP, grab it. If it's marked up, wait for a restock.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Mandalorian completionists**: If you're collecting every version of Din Djarin's allies, the two Mandalorian warriors are must-haves.
- **Praetorian Guard collectors**: This is the best version LEGO has made, and it's unlikely to be reissued soon.
- **Kids who want a quick build**: The 15-minute build time and working missile launcher make it a solid impulse buy for young builders.

Skip it if you're a pure playset builder — you'll want a larger set for actual scene-building.

## FAQ

**Are the minifigures exclusive to this set?**
Yes. The Praetorian Guard and both Mandalorian warriors feature new torso and helmet prints that don't appear in any other 2024 set.

**Does the missile launcher actually work?**
It does — it's a flick-fire design that shoots the included missile about three feet. It's not powerful enough to be dangerous, but it's satisfying for kids.

**Will this set fit with other Mandalorian sets?**
The build uses standard stud connections, so it can attach to larger builds like the Mandalorian's N-1 Starfighter or the Imperial Armored Marauder.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want the best value-per-minifigure in the current battle pack lineup, or if you're a Praetorian Guard fan. **Skip it if** you're looking for a substantial build or can only find it marked up above $25.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The figures are exceptional, the build is minimal, and the price is fair at MSRP. This is a collector's set first and a playset second, and it nails the former.
