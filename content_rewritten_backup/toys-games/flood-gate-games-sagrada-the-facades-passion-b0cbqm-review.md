If you've played Sagrada more than a dozen times, you already know the problem: you've seen every window pattern, memorized the optimal dice placements, and the base game's scoring starts to feel automatic. The Facades-Passion expansion exists to fix exactly that, and for the most part, it succeeds.

**What you actually get**

The box contains 24 new window pattern cards, 12 new translucent dice (in Passion's signature color scheme), 4 private objective cards, and a set of divider cards to organize everything. As shown in the product image, the new dice have a distinct translucent quality that sets them apart from the opaque base game dice — they catch light differently on the table and make the game visually pop in a way the original doesn't.

The 24 window patterns are divided across four difficulty levels, from Level 1 (accessible for newer players) up to Level 4 (genuinely challenging even for veterans). This isn't just a reskin — the new patterns introduce placement restrictions that force different drafting strategies than the base game.

**Performance in real play**

I've played this expansion roughly 15 times across groups ranging from casual family gamers to a weekly board game night that takes things seriously. The private objective cards are the standout addition. They give each player a hidden scoring goal that can shift your entire approach to dice placement. One game, I was chasing a diagonal color scheme while another player focused on maximizing adjacent number values — the tension at the draft phase was noticeably higher than in the base game.

The new window patterns at Level 2 and 3 hit the sweet spot. They're different enough to feel fresh but don't require the spreadsheet-level planning that Level 4 demands. The Level 4 patterns are genuinely tough — one of them requires specific color placement in corners that conflicts with how you'd naturally draft for value — and I'd only recommend them for groups that enjoy the puzzle aspect more than the social drafting.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.8-star rating across 240 ratings reflects real satisfaction. One reviewer noted, "The new windows alone make this worth it — I was getting bored with the base patterns after 30 plays and this doubled the game's lifespan." Another called the translucent dice "gorgeous on the table" and said they "photograph beautifully," which matters more than you'd think for a game that's already visually strong.

The difficulty level system gets consistent praise too. Players appreciate being able to tailor the challenge to their group — one commenter said, "We mix Level 1 and Level 2 for family game night and pull out Level 4 when the serious gamers come over."

**Where it falls short**

The expansion requires the base Sagrada game. That's not hidden anywhere, but it's worth stating plainly — if you don't own Sagrada, this box won't do you any good on its own.

The Passion dice are a minor frustration. They're translucent, yes, but the color is close enough to the base game's opaque red dice that during a fast draft, I've seen players grab the wrong ones. It's a small issue that disappears once you sort them into separate bags, but it's worth knowing.

The Level 4 difficulty jump is steeper than the progression from Level 1 to Level 3 suggests. If you're playing with mixed skill levels, you'll want to keep everyone on the same difficulty or the newer players will get crushed.

**Who should buy this**

- **Sagrada owners with 20+ plays** — this is your purchase. It breathes new life into a game you've exhausted.
- **Solo players** — the private objectives add meaningful variety to the solo mode.
- **Groups with mixed experience levels** — the difficulty tiers let you level the playing field.

Skip it if you're a new Sagrada player still working through the base game's patterns, or if you only play occasionally and haven't exhausted the base content.

**FAQ**

*Does this work with Sagrada: Life?* Yes, the Facades-Passion expansion is compatible with other Sagrada expansions, though adding multiple expansions at once can make the draft phase feel cluttered. Start with just this one.

*How long does a game take with this expansion?* About the same as the base game — 30-45 minutes. The private objectives don't add meaningful time, they just add strategic depth.

*Are the dice compatible with the base game?* The 12 translucent dice are fully compatible for drafting, but they're not a replacement for the base game dice — you'll still need the original dice for the color pool.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you love Sagrada and want to double its lifespan. The private objectives alone justify the price, and the 24 new windows give you months of fresh plays.

**Skip it if** you're still exploring the base game or play casually a few times a year — you haven't exhausted what you already own.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — the best Sagrada expansion currently available, with minor visual confusion between dice colors being the only real complaint.
