**Is the LEGO City Car Wash 60362 worth your money?** With a 4.9-star average across 245 ratings, this set has clearly impressed most buyers. But I've dug through the reviews and built enough LEGO sets to know that high ratings don't always tell the full story. Here's what you're actually getting.

## What's in the box

The 244-piece set builds a two-story car wash structure with a hand-cranked conveyor belt, spinning roller brushes on top, and a side-mounted sprayer arch. You also get a tow truck with a tilting flatbed, two minifigures (a driver and an attendant), and a few accessory pieces like a bucket and squeegee.

As shown in the product image, the car wash itself is colorful and detailed — blue and yellow accents, a "CAR WASH" sign, and a small office area on the second level. The tow truck is a nice bonus, not just filler. It has working wheels and the flatbed actually tilts, which adds play value.

## Build quality and real-world play

The headline feature is the working conveyor. You crank a dial on the side, and the belt moves the car through the wash tunnel. The roller brushes on top spin as the car passes underneath. It's mechanically simple — gears and axles, no motors — but it works reliably. Multiple reviewers mention their kids repeatedly running cars through it, which is the real test.

The tow truck is sturdier than I expected for a set this size. It's not a Technic-level build, but the tilting bed holds position when loaded, and the wheels roll smoothly on carpet and hard floors.

One thing that surprised me: the set is more compact than the box photos suggest. The finished car wash measures roughly 10 inches wide and 7 inches tall. That's a plus if you're short on shelf space, but kids expecting a sprawling playset might find it small.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.9 rating isn't an accident. Across the 245 ratings, a few themes come up repeatedly:

- **The conveyor mechanism** — "My son loves cranking the handle and watching the car go through. It's his favorite part." This is the set's standout feature, and it holds up to repeated use.
- **Build quality** — Parents note the pieces fit tightly and the set survives rough play. One reviewer said, "My 5-year-old has knocked it off the table twice and it hasn't broken."
- **Age appropriateness** — The 6+ rating is accurate. Kids at that age can build it mostly independently, though a few steps with the gear mechanism might need adult help.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The build is fast.** At 244 pieces, most kids will finish in 20-30 minutes. If you're buying this as a "big project" gift, it's not that. It's a playset first, a build second.

**The water features are fake.** The sprayer arch is a single molded piece. It doesn't spray, mist, or even move. Younger kids who expect actual water action will be disappointed. The "car wash" is really just the conveyor and brushes.

**No motor.** The crank mechanism is fine, but some kids expect the wash to run automatically. It doesn't. If your child is used to motorized sets like LEGO Technic, this will feel manual by comparison.

## Who should buy this

- **Parents of kids 6-9** who want a set that's more about play than display. The conveyor adds interactive value most LEGO City sets lack.
- **LEGO City collectors** completing their vehicle-themed lineup. It pairs well with other City sets and doesn't take up much shelf space.
- **Gift givers** looking for a reliable, age-appropriate option under $30. The 4.9 rating and strong play value make it a safe bet.

**Skip it if** your kid is a builder first, player second — they'll finish this in one sitting and want more. Also skip if you're expecting motorized or water-based features.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the conveyor belt motorized?**
A: No. It's hand-cranked via a dial on the side. It works smoothly and is easy for small hands to turn, but there's no battery or motor.

**Q: How long does the build take?**
A: For a 6-7 year old, roughly 30-45 minutes with some help. An adult can finish it in about 20 minutes. It's a quick build.

**Q: Does it actually spray water?**
A: No. The sprayer is a non-functional molded piece. The set is purely mechanical — no water, no batteries.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a LEGO set with genuine interactive play value — the conveyor and spinning brushes keep kids engaged long after the build is done. It's a solid, well-made set that earns its 4.9 rating.

**Skip it if** you're looking for a complex build or motorized features. This is a playset first, and it excels at that — but it's not a challenging build or a tech showcase.
