If your kids have discovered Ninja Warrior on TV, you already know what's coming: they're going to climb something. The question is whether that something is a bookshelf, a doorframe, or a tree in your backyard. The TOPNEW 12 and 20 Ninja Tree Climbing Holds are designed to give you a controlled answer to that question — and at $38.99 for a set that includes both 12 and 20 holds, it's priced like a no-brainer. But is it actually tree-safe, kid-safe, and durable enough to earn a permanent spot in your yard? Here's the real story from 229 buyers who've already put these to the test.

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting two separate sets bundled together: a 12-hold set and a 20-hold set, for 32 total climbing holds. Each hold is molded resin, roughly palm-sized, with a textured surface that mimics real climbing gym grips. The bundle includes stainless steel screws and wall anchors for mounting. As shown in the product image, the holds come in a mix of bright colors — red, yellow, blue, green — which makes them easy for kids to spot against dark bark. The combined set gives you enough to create a simple vertical route or two zigzagging paths up a medium-sized tree.

**Performance and build quality**

The grip texture is the standout feature here. Multiple reviewers note that the holds stay grippy even when wet — one parent wrote, "My kids climb after rain and I've never seen a slip. The texture is aggressive enough that even muddy sneakers get traction." That's not marketing fluff; the resin has a rough, sandpaper-like finish that genuinely helps small hands and feet find purchase.

Mounting is straightforward: pre-drill pilot holes, screw in the stainless hardware, and you're done. The included screws work fine on typical backyard trees like maple or birch. However, if you have a thick-bark species like oak or pine, several reviewers recommend swapping in longer deck screws for a more secure bite. One buyer noted, "The screws are decent but short for our oak. I replaced them with 3-inch deck screws and now it's rock solid."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The most repeated compliment is how quickly kids take to it. Parents describe children as young as 3 independently navigating the holds with supervision, while older kids (8-12) create their own routes and time themselves. One reviewer summed it up: "My 6-year-old and 9-year-old fight over whose turn it is. Best $39 I've spent on outdoor toys." Another praised the weight capacity: "I'm 180 lbs and I tested the bottom holds myself. They didn't budge."

**Where it falls short**

The honest tradeoff: 32 holds sounds like a lot, but on a tall tree, it's barely enough for one solid route. You'll likely space them 12-18 inches apart, which means the set covers maybe 12-15 feet of trunk height — fine for younger kids, but older climbers will want more holds for a complex course. Additionally, the screws included are the weak link for rough-bark trees. Budget for aftermarket hardware if you're not mounting on smooth bark. Finally, there's no carrying case or storage bag, so loose holds will clutter a garage shelf between seasons.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for parents of energetic kids aged 3-10 who want a safe, structured climbing outlet without building a permanent playset. It's also a great fit for families who already have a sturdy tree and want a budget-friendly activity that gets kids outdoors. Skip it if you have a very tall tree (you'll need to buy additional holds) or if you're looking for a full ninja obstacle course — this is strictly a climbing wall, not a full rig.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these damage my tree?**  
A: No, not if you install them correctly. Pre-drill pilot holes and the screws won't split bark. The holds are designed to be removable, and minor holes heal over. Just don't overtighten.

**Q: What's the weight limit?**  
A: TOPNEW doesn't publish an official limit, but buyers report adult testing (up to 180 lbs) without failure. For safety, supervise kids and don't let adults use the upper holds.

**Q: How many holds do I need for a 10-foot tree?**  
A: At 12-inch spacing, you'll use about 10 holds per vertical line. The 32-hold bundle covers two parallel lines or one zigzag route. Buy a second set if you want a denser course.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a durable, grippy climbing set that turns a backyard tree into an instant adventure zone for kids 3-10. The texture, hardware quality (with minor upgrades), and price-to-value ratio make it a clear winner.

**Skip it if:** Your tree has thick, deeply furrowed bark (budget for replacement screws) or you need a full ninja course rather than a single climbing route.
