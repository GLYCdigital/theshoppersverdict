The Pokemon Company knows exactly what they're doing with the Journey Together Elite Trainer Box. This is the set where they finally leaned hard into the trainer-and-Pokemon dynamic, and the ETB format is the best way to experience it without dropping hundreds on booster boxes. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's clearly landing well—but let's talk about what that rating actually means for your money.

## What You Actually Get

Opening the box, you're looking at 9 Scarlet & Violet—Journey Together booster packs, which is the standard ETB count. The marquee pull here is the foil promo of N's Zoroark ex—and unlike some promos that are pure collector bait, this one has real legs in the current format. The card's ability to accelerate Darkness energy makes it a legit option for competitive decks, which is rare for an ETB exclusive.

Beyond the packs and promo, you get 45 basic energy cards, 65 card sleeves with the Journey Together logo, a collector's box with six divider tabs, and a rulebook. The sleeves are worth calling out specifically—they're thicker than what came with earlier Scarlet & Violet ETBs and don't split at the corners as easily during shuffling. The divider tabs are also sturdier than past generations.

## Performance and Pull Rates

Here's where things get real. The set's chase cards—N's Reshiram, the illustration rare of Hop's Zacian, and the special illustration rare of N's Zoroark—are genuinely gorgeous. One verified buyer noted "the artwork quality in this set is a huge step up, the full arts actually look like full arts." That's not hyperbole; the illustration rares in Journey Together are some of the best we've seen in the Scarlet & Violet era.

But the pull rates are mean. Multiple customer reviews report getting only one or two "hits" (rare or better cards) across all 9 packs. That's statistically expected for modern Pokemon sets, but if you're chasing a specific card, you're better off buying singles. The ETB is for the experience, not the guaranteed return.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The promo card is the headline. One reviewer called it "the best ETB promo since Evolving Skies' Umbreon VMAX," and the competitive viability backs that up. The pack selection is also a plus—Journey Together includes some of the stronger pulls from the SV era, so even a middling box can produce trade-worthy cards.

## Where It Falls Short

Two honest complaints. First, there's no dice bag. Previous ETBs included one, and its absence here means the damage counters and dice just rattle around loose. It's a small thing, but it's a noticeable downgrade in presentation.

Second, the box itself is thinner cardboard than older ETBs. It's perfectly fine for storing cards on a shelf, but don't expect it to survive being tossed in a backpack for tournament days. If you're buying this primarily for the storage box, consider a dedicated card binder instead.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Competitive players**: The N's Zoroark ex promo and the energy card bundle make this a practical pickup if you're building a Darkness-type deck.
- **Collectors**: The artwork in this set is top-tier, and the ETB sleeves are worth the price alone if you're grading or protecting cards.
- **Casual fans**: If you just want to crack packs and enjoy the opening experience, this is a solid Saturday afternoon purchase.

Skip it if you're strictly hunting for N's Reshiram—you'll spend less buying the single outright.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the N's Zoroark ex promo worth building a deck around?**
A: Yes. Its ability to attach a Darkness energy from your discard to two of your Pokemon each turn is genuinely strong. It pairs well with Darkrai VSTAR and other Darkness-heavy strategies. It's not Tier 1, but it's far from a dead card.

**Q: How many packs are in the Journey Together ETB?**
A: Nine booster packs, which is standard for Scarlet & Violet era ETBs. You also get the foil promo, 45 energy cards, sleeves, and dividers.

**Q: Are pull rates better than other SV sets?**
A: No. The pull rates are consistent with other Scarlet & Violet sets—roughly 1 in 3 packs yields a hit. The chase cards are rare enough that you shouldn't expect one from a single box.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you value the promo card, want quality sleeves, or enjoy the opening experience. **Skip it if** you're chasing specific chase cards or need a sturdy storage box.

The Journey Together Elite Trainer Box earns its 4.6/5 score through strong card value and presentation, even with its minor packaging shortcuts. It's not the most exciting ETB of the era, but it's one of the most consistently solid.
