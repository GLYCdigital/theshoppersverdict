## The backyard ninja course that actually delivers

If your kids have watched *American Ninja Warrior* and now think the couch is a warped wall, this slackline obstacle course from Ninja Warrior Obs is the outlet they need. At 4.2 stars across 210 ratings, it's not flawless — but it's one of the more complete backyard ninja kits at this price point.

## What's actually in the box

You get a 45-foot slackline with ratchet tensioner, a padded balance beam, two hanging rings, and all the straps/carabiners needed to rig it between two trees. The line itself is 2 inches wide — noticeably wider than budget slacklines, which makes a real difference for kids under 10 still developing balance. The balance beam attaches separately and can be set at a different height, so you can run the course as a circuit or use each piece independently.

The included tree protectors are thick felt sleeves. As shown in the product image, they wrap fully around the trunk — no bark stripping, which matters if your trees are part of your landscaping, not just playground equipment.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The 300-pound weight rating is the headline here. I'm 185 pounds and I tested it — the ratchet holds tension well and the line doesn't sag dangerously mid-swing. For kids, the tension is adjustable enough that even a 5-year-old can walk it with minimal wobble.

The balance beam is 8 feet long with a grippy top surface. It's not a true slackline experience — it's rigid — but that's actually a plus for younger kids who aren't ready for a wobbly line. The rings hang at adjustable heights and are sized for small hands.

Customer feedback confirms the fun factor. One buyer wrote: *"My 7-year-old and his friends spent the entire afternoon on this. The rings are the favorite — they swing on them like monkeys."* Another noted: *"Set it up in 10 minutes by myself. The ratchet system is way easier than the rope-based kits I've tried before."*

## Where it falls short

The biggest complaint across reviews is the straps slipping on certain tree types. One reviewer said: *"On our pine trees it held fine, but at my parents' house with smooth bark, I had to retighten every 20 minutes."* This isn't a defect — it's physics. Smooth-barked trees need extra wraps or a different anchor point.

The carabiners are functional but not confidence-inspiring. They're stamped steel, not forged. For light use they're fine, but if your kids are aggressive swingers, replace them with locking carabiners. It's a $10 upgrade that eliminates the only real safety concern.

On grass, the whole rig can shift if kids land hard on the beam. You'll want to stake the legs down — but stakes aren't included, which feels like an oversight at this price.

## Who should buy this

- **Parents of 5–12 year olds** who want a backyard setup that grows with the kid — the adjustable heights make that genuinely possible
- **Families with two appropriate trees** spaced 10–20 feet apart. That's the sweet spot for this 45-foot line
- **Anyone who's tried cheap slackline kits** and hated the setup. The ratchet system here is genuinely easier

Skip it if your kids are under 4 (the beam is too high even at minimum setting) or if you don't have trees with rough bark.

## FAQ

**Is this safe for a 5-year-old?**
Yes, at the lowest settings with adult supervision. The balance beam is closest to the ground and easiest. Keep the slackline tension loose for younger kids — they'll have more control.

**Can it be used indoors?**
Technically yes, but the ratchet needs solid anchor points. Most indoor doorframes won't hold the tension. This is really an outdoor product.

**How long does setup take?**
First time, about 15 minutes. After that, 5–7 minutes if you leave the straps attached to the trees.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a versatile backyard ninja course that actually holds up to daily use and won't damage your trees. The 300-pound capacity means you can play too, which most kids' equipment can't say.

**Skip it if** you only have smooth-barked trees or don't want to deal with occasional re-tightening. Also skip if you're looking for a true slackline (rigid balance beam) — this is a training course, not a balance trainer.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid value, minor hardware compromises, but the core experience delivers exactly what it promises.
