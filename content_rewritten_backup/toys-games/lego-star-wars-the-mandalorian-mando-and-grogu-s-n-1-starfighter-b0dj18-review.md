**LEGO Star Wars: The Mandalorian Mando and Grogu's N-1 Starfighter Review**

---

**The Build: 291 Pieces of Mandalorian Magic**

The first thing you notice when the box arrives is that this isn't a massive, multi-hour build. At 291 pieces, LEGO Star Wars: The Mandalorian Mando and Grogu's N-1 Starfighter (set 75431) is an accessible, satisfying build that most adults will finish in under 90 minutes and kids in an afternoon. It's priced and sized as a mid-tier set, and it hits that sweet spot: detailed enough to display, sturdy enough to play with.

---

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the N-1 Starfighter itself, which is recognizably the gleaming chrome-and-silver ship from *The Book of Boba Fett* and *The Mandalorian* Season 3. The build captures the iconic long nose cone, the twin rear engines, and that distinctive pointed silhouette. Along with the ship, you get three minifigures:

- **The Mandalorian (Din Djarin)** – with his beskar armor, jetpack, and a printed cape
- **Grogu** – the adorable baby Yoda, with his pram that fits perfectly beside the ship
- **A Peli Motto minifigure** – a nice bonus for fans of the character, complete with her mechanic's overalls

The set includes a small display stand, which is genuinely useful for showing off the ship's profile. The sticker sheet is manageable—maybe a dozen stickers total, mostly for the cockpit console and engine detailing. No one enjoys stickers, but this set doesn't abuse them.

---

**Performance and Build Quality: Where It Shines and Where It Doesn't**

**The positives:**

The N-1 Starfighter's design is genuinely clever. The cockpit opens for Din Djarin, and there's a dedicated spot where Grogu can sit—a feature kids will absolutely love. The spring-loaded shooters are integrated cleanly into the forward engine nacelles, so they don't ruin the sleek look. The landing gear folds away neatly, and the ship sits flat on the stand without wobbling.

The minifigures are the real draw here. Grogu is adorable, with his big ears and expressive face print. Din Djarin's helmet print is crisp, and the beskar armor details are sharp. Peli Motto is a welcome addition—she's only appeared in a handful of sets, so she's a nice collectible bonus.

**The honest downsides:**

At 291 pieces, this is not a display centerpiece. It's noticeably smaller than the previous N-1 Starfighter from 2022 (set 75325), which had over 400 pieces and included a larger, more detailed ship. If you already own that set, this one is a downgrade in scale. The ship is also not minifigure-scale—it's scaled down to fit the price point, which means the proportions are slightly stubby compared to the on-screen ship. Purists will notice.

The build itself is straightforward. If you're a veteran LEGO builder, don't expect any challenging or innovative techniques. This is a solid, by-the-numbers build that prioritizes playability over complexity.

**What Buyers Are Saying**

Grogu in the cockpit sold it for my son."* Another echoed the playability factor: *"My 7-year-old built this in about an hour with minimal help. ---

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Parents of kids ages 6–10** who want a Star Wars set that's buildable without frustration and playable without falling apart. This is a home run for that group.
- **LEGO Star Wars collectors** who want the updated Peli Motto minifigure and a compact N-1 Starfighter for a shelf. It's a solid addition, not a must-have.
- **Budget-conscious Mandalorian fans** who missed the 2022 version and want a good-looking ship without the premium price tag.

**Skip it if** you already own the 2022 N-1 Starfighter (75325), or if you're expecting a minifigure-scale ship. This one is smaller and simpler.

---

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this set suitable for a 5-year-old?**
A: It's rated for ages 9+, but a confident 6-year-old can handle it with supervision. The smaller pieces—like the antenna and engine nozzles—can be tricky for younger kids. If your child is under 6, expect to help with the final assembly steps.

**Q: Does Grogu actually fit in the ship?**
A: Yes. There's a dedicated open spot near the cockpit where Grogu sits, and he fits snugly without falling out during play. It's one of the best features of the set.

**Q: How does this compare to the 2022 N-1 Starfighter?**
A: The 2022 version (75325) was bigger, more detailed, and included more minifigures, but it also cost significantly more. This 2025 set is a more affordable, compact alternative that still looks great on a shelf. If you want maximum detail, buy the older set. If you want a budget-friendly version, this is your pick.

---

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, affordable Mandalorian-themed set that delivers strong play value and a great minifigure trio. It's an excellent gift for young Star Wars fans or a low-commitment addition to a collection.

**Skip it if** you're a hardcore collector who already owns the larger N-1 Starfighter, or if you're looking for a challenging build. This one is designed for accessibility, not complexity.

At the end of the day, this is a well-executed, honest set that knows exactly what it is: a fun, compact tribute to the Mandalorian's iconic ship. For the price, it's hard to argue with 4.8 stars from 240 owners.
