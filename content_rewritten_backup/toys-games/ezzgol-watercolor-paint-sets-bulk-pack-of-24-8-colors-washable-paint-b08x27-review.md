If you're a teacher, camp counselor, or party parent who's tired of rationing art supplies, the Ezzgol Watercolor Paint Sets Bulk Pack of 24 solves a very specific problem: you need 24 kids painting simultaneously without a meltdown over who gets the "good" palette. This is a quantity-first product, and it delivers exactly what the box promises.

**What you actually get**

Twenty-four individual cardboard palettes, each holding eight standard colors: red, yellow, blue, green, orange, purple, brown, and black. Each palette measures roughly 6.5 x 4.5 inches — small enough to fit in a standard pencil case or a 5x7 party favor bag. The paint blocks are pre-formed and dry, so there's no leaking or mess in transit. A thin wooden brush is tucked into each palette's built-in slot. The packaging is straightforward: a bulk cardboard box with the palettes stacked flat, which keeps shipping damage minimal.

**Performance and real-world use**

The washable formula is the headline feature here, and it earns its keep. Verified buyer feedback consistently mentions that the paint cleans up with just warm water and soap — one reviewer wrote, "My 3-year-old painted her entire arm and face, and it came off in the bath without scrubbing." That's the kind of reassurance parents need before handing these to a preschooler.

Color payoff is where you'll notice the tradeoff for washability. The pigments are translucent and build slowly. On standard white copy paper, a single wash gives you a pastel tint; you'll need two or three passes for saturated color. One reviewer noted, "These are great for teaching technique, but don't expect watercolor masterpieces from a first grader." On thicker watercolor paper the results improve, but this is not an artist-grade set — it's a mass-activity tool.

The included brushes are the weakest link. They're about 4 inches long, thin-handled, and the bristles splay after a few uses. For a classroom setting where each child gets one palette, they work fine for basic painting. For any real artistic effort, buy a 24-pack of decent round brushes separately — it's a $10 upgrade that transforms the experience.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dominant theme in reviews is the sheer convenience. One teacher said, "I bought these for my kindergarten class and they lasted the entire semester. Each kid gets their own set, no sharing, no fighting." Another buyer used them for a birthday party craft station: "I put one in each goodie bag and the kids painted at the table for 45 minutes. Best party decision ever." The durability of the cardboard palette also gets repeated mentions — kids drop these, step on them, and they hold up without crumbling.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the brush quality and light pigment, there are two honest limitations. First, the color names aren't printed on the palette, which matters if you're teaching color theory. Second, the black and brown blocks are noticeably smaller than the brighter colors — a design choice that becomes annoying if your students go heavy on dark shades and run out halfway through a project. Also, the paint blocks are shallow; heavy-handed kids can scrape through to the cardboard base within a few sessions.

**Who should buy this**

This is ideal for: daycare centers and preschool classrooms (ages 3-6), Sunday school craft tables, birthday party favor craft stations, camp activity bins, and scout troop badge workshops. It's also a solid budget pick for a homeschool co-op with multiple families. Skip it if you're shopping for a single child who's serious about watercolor — you'd be better off with a single higher-quality pan set like the Prang or Sakura Koi.

**FAQ**

*Are these safe for toddlers who might put them in their mouths?* The paint is non-toxic and labeled washable, but let's be clear: non-toxic doesn't mean edible, and the cardboard palette can be a choking hazard if torn. For under-3s, supervise closely or choose a finger-paint alternative.

*How many uses do you get per palette?* With normal school-kid usage (one or two paintings per session), a palette lasts 6-8 sessions before the lighter colors run dry. Heavy users will go through them faster.

*Do the colors mix well on paper?* They blend acceptably but not beautifully — wet-on-wet techniques get muddy quickly. This is a starter set, not a technique tool.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need bulk, washable, low-stakes paint for groups of kids and your priority is cleanup ease over artistic vibrancy. **Skip it if** you want rich color payoff, quality brushes, or a product built for an aspiring young artist.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Ezzgol bulk pack nails its niche. It's not the best paint you'll ever buy, but it's the best value for the specific chaos of group kid activities.
