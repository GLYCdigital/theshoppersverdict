**Meta Description:** HyperX Cloud III wired gaming headset review: 53mm drivers, 10Hz–21kHz sound, comfort tested on PC, PS5 & Xbox. Is it worth it? Find out.

---

Let’s cut through the noise. The HyperX Cloud series has been the default recommendation for budget-conscious gamers for nearly a decade. The Cloud III arrives with a bigger price tag than its predecessor and some bold claims about audio precision. After 229 ratings holding a 4.6-star average, the question isn’t whether this headset is good—it’s whether it’s *the* headset for *you*.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the Cloud III headset, a detachable 10mm noise-cancelling microphone, a 3.5mm audio cable, a USB-C to USB-A adapter, and a USB-C to 3.5mm cable for console compatibility. Notably, there’s no carry pouch—a small miss at this price point. The headset itself is built around an aluminum fork and a fully adjustable steel headband, wrapped in the signature HyperX leatherette and memory foam.

## Performance: The 53mm Driver Difference

The headline spec here is the 53mm angled drivers, a step up from the 50mm units in the Cloud II. In practice, this translates to a wider soundstage and cleaner separation. Explosions in *Call of Duty* have a satisfying low-end thump without muddying footsteps—the directional audio is genuinely precise. The frequency response of 10Hz–21kHz extends slightly beyond human hearing range, but it gives the headset headroom for high-res audio on PC.

The detachable mic is a clear improvement. It uses a 10mm condenser element with noise cancellation, and in Discord tests, my voice came through warmer and less tinny than the Cloud II’s. It’s not broadcast-studio quality, but it’s firmly in the “good enough for raids and ranked” tier.

## Comfort: The Long-Haul Champion

This is where HyperX has historically dominated, and the Cloud III continues that streak. At 320g, it’s slightly heavier than the Cloud II (309g), but the weight distribution is better—the redesigned headband padding spreads pressure evenly. I wore these for a four-hour *Elden Ring* session and forgot they were on. The ear cups are large enough to fit over glasses without pinching, and the leatherette retains the Reddit-favorite “cloud” feel.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star rating isn’t an accident. Reviewers frequently cite three things:

- **Build quality:** “The aluminum frame feels like it’ll survive a drop down the stairs,” one verified buyer notes. Another writes, “My Cloud II lasted 5 years. This feels even more solid.”
- **Cross-platform versatility:** The included USB-C adapter works seamlessly on PS5, Switch, and PC. Xbox Series X/S users need the 3.5mm cable, which works fine but doesn’t enable the DTS Headphone:X spatial audio.
- **Sound clarity:** “I can hear reloads and footsteps I missed with my old headset,” says a 5-star review. Another adds, “The bass is punchy but not overpowering—music sounds great too.”

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires naming the flaws.

First, the DTS:X spatial audio is Windows-only. Console players get stereo sound, which is a letdown for the price. Second, the leatherette ear cups, while comfortable, get warm during long sessions—there’s no breathable fabric option included. Third, the “21kHz” frequency response is marketing math; most listeners won’t perceive a difference from the Cloud II’s 18kHz ceiling. Finally, the lack of a carry case feels cheap for a headset at this tier.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The multi-platform gamer:** If you play on PC, PS5, and Switch, the included adapters make this a one-headset solution.
- **The comfort-first player:** Long sessions without ear fatigue are this headset’s superpower.
- **The upgrade skeptic:** If you’re coming from a Cloud II, the better mic and sturdier build justify the jump.

**Skip it if:** You’re a competitive console player needing Dolby Atmos (you’ll want a dedicated wireless option), or you prefer open-back soundstage for single-player immersion.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the HyperX Cloud III work on Xbox Series X/S?**
Yes, but only via the 3.5mm cable connection. You won’t get DTS:X spatial audio on Xbox—that’s Windows-only.

**Q: Is the microphone detachable?**
Yes, it pops off cleanly. The 3.5mm jack on the headset also works for passive audio without power.

**Q: How does it compare to the Cloud II?**
Better mic, sturdier aluminum frame, and slightly wider soundstage. The comfort is similar, but the Cloud III is heavier by 11g—largely unnoticeable.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a durable, cross-platform wired headset with excellent comfort and clear directional audio for under $100.
**Skip it if:** You need spatial audio on console or prefer a wireless setup for freedom of movement.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A worthy successor that fixes the Cloud II’s weak points, despite a few console-specific compromises.
