Some crossover products feel like cash grabs. This one doesn't. The Magic: The Gathering Final Fantasy Game Booster Box brings two of gaming's most beloved franchises together, and the result is surprisingly respectful to both source materials. But is it worth your money? Let's break down what you actually get.

## What's in the Box

This is a standard Game Booster Box — 30 packs, 15 cards per pack, 450 cards total. You're getting roughly 90 commons, 120 uncommons, 30+ rares, and a solid shot at mythics across the set. As shown in the product image, the box features striking Final Fantasy key art that stands out on a shelf.

Each pack includes 1-2 foils, and here's the good news: the foil treatment on this set is genuinely improved. The borderless anime-style treatments on characters like Cloud Strife, Sephiroth, and Yuna are some of the best-looking cards MTG has printed in the Universes Beyond line.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The cardstock is where this set takes a hit. Multiple buyers have noted the cards feel thinner than standard MTG sets. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "The cards bend easier than my other MTG boxes. Double-sleeve them if you're playing with them."

That said, the printing quality is sharp. Colors pop, text is legible, and the alternate art treatments are worth chasing. For collectors, this is a display-worthy set. For players, you'll want to invest in quality sleeves.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The nostalgia factor is doing heavy lifting here, and it works. Reviewers across the board mention the art direction as the standout feature. One buyer wrote: "The Sephiroth alt-art alone is worth the price of admission. It's frame-worthy."

The draft experience also gets consistent praise. The set mechanics blend Final Fantasy job classes with MTG's color identity in ways that feel intuitive rather than forced. Drafting this set with friends is genuinely fun — the synergies are discoverable, and there's enough depth to keep experienced players engaged.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest truth: pull rates are inconsistent. While you're guaranteed a certain number of rares per box, mythic foil rates vary significantly. Some buyers report multiple mythics in a single box; others get mostly bulk rares. One reviewer noted: "Opened two boxes — one was incredible, the other was average. It's a gamble."

The cardstock issue deserves repeating. This is a premium-priced product, and the thinner stock feels like a cost-cutting measure that undermines the premium presentation.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You're a Final Fantasy fan looking to collect gorgeous alternate-art cards
- You draft with friends regularly and want a fresh, thematic experience
- You're a Magic player who enjoys crossover mechanics done right

**Skip it if:**
- You're chasing specific high-value cards for resale — buy singles instead
- Cardstock quality is a dealbreaker for you
- You're on a budget and want maximum playable value per dollar

## FAQ

**Is this box worth more sealed or opened?**
Sealed boxes tend to appreciate, but this set's value is primarily in the art treatments. If you're not drafting, consider buying a booster box to hold, and grab singles for play.

**How does this compare to other MTG Universes Beyond sets?**
It's better than the Fallout and Doctor Who sets in terms of art quality, but the thinner cardstock puts it slightly below the Lord of the Rings set in overall build quality.

**Can I play these cards in Standard?**
No. These are legal in Commander, Legacy, and Vintage — the casual and eternal formats.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you want gorgeous, playable crossover cards and enjoy the draft experience. **Skip it if** you're purely chasing value or can't tolerate thinner cardstock.

This is a collector's set first, a player's set second. The art direction justifies the price for most buyers — just go in knowing the physical quality has a few compromises. If you're on the fence, wait for a sale and grab it at a discount. At full price, you're paying for the nostalgia, and honestly? That's okay.
