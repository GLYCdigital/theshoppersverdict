If you've seen *Wicked* — or even just the trailers — you know Glinda's "Popular" number is the moment. Mattel's Singing Glinda doll tries to bottle that theatrical magic into a posable fashion doll, and for the most part, it works. But there are a few compromises you should know about before you hit add-to-cart.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the 11.5-inch Glinda doll, her signature glittering crown, a wand with a molded star tip, and the singing feature built into her torso. The dress is the showstopper here — a layered tulle skirt in that pale pink that reads instantly as "Glinda," with a metallic bodice that catches light nicely. As shown in the product image, the costume detail is genuinely impressive for a mass-market doll. No extra outfits, no brush, no stand — this is a focused package, not a playset.

## The Singing Feature: Does It Deliver?

Press the button on her back and you get a clear audio clip from the film — not a cheap approximation or a generic "la la la." The speaker quality is surprisingly decent for a doll at this price; it's not going to fill a room, but it's audible and distortion-free at normal play distances. The audio clip runs about 20 seconds, which is enough for the "Popular" hook without becoming repetitive.

One thing that frustrated several Amazon reviewers: the doll requires 3x AG13 button batteries, and they are **not included**. Nothing kills a gift moment faster than a doll that can't sing on Christmas morning. Budget an extra few dollars and grab batteries ahead of time.

## Build Quality and Articulation

This is where Mattel actually stepped up. Glinda has 13 points of articulation — including wrist, elbow, and ankle joints that many fashion dolls in this range skip entirely. She can hold poses, gesture dramatically, and actually sit without looking like a plank. The joints are snug without being stiff, which means kids can pose her without frustration.

The tradeoff? Her dress is **permanently attached** at the torso. You can't remove it, which kills costume-change play. And her heels are molded into the feet, so you can't swap shoes with other Wicked dolls or standard fashion doll footwear. If your kid's play style is all about wardrobe changes, this is a real limitation.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings isn't an accident. Parents consistently highlight the movie accuracy — one reviewer noted, "She looks exactly like Ariana Grande's Glinda, my daughter recognized her immediately." Others mention the sturdiness: "Survived being dropped down the stairs twice and still sings perfectly." The articulation gets frequent shoutouts too, with collectors noting it's "rare to get this much poseability at this price."

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the battery and costume issues, the doll's head is a bit heavy relative to her neck joint. She can look down dramatically, but holding a head-tilted-up pose requires patience. Also, if you're buying for a child under 5, the tiny crown and wand are choking hazards — this is firmly a 6+ product despite the movie's broad appeal.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Wicked superfans** who want a display-quality collector piece that also survives play
- **Parents of kids 6-12** who loved the movie and want a doll that isn't just another princess in a box
- **Doll collectors** looking for movie-accurate pieces with better-than-average articulation

Skip it if you're buying for a preschooler, or if your kid's core play loop is dressing and undressing dolls — this Glinda is for posing and performing, not wardrobe rotation.

## FAQ

**Does she sing the full song?**
No — it's a 20-second clip of "Popular," not the full track. It's the most recognizable portion, but don't expect a complete musical number.

**Can she stand on her own?**
Yes, but she's wobbly. The tulle skirt provides a bit of a base, but the molded heels make balance tricky on uneven surfaces. A doll stand helps if she's for display.

**Is this doll compatible with other Mattel fashion doll clothes?**
No. The permanently attached dress and molded shoes mean she's essentially locked into her Glinda look. Accessories like the crown and wand are removable, but clothing is not.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a movie-accurate, poseable Glinda that actually sings and looks fantastic on a shelf or in a playroom. The articulation and costume detail punch above the price point.

**Skip it if** you need interchangeable outfits, can't be bothered with buying separate batteries, or have a child under 6 who'll want to eat the crown.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A strong, character-accurate doll with real play value, held back only by the permanent costume and the battery oversight. For Wicked fans, it's worth the shelf space.
