Most card games in this price range fall into one of two camps: they're either so simple you're bored after three rounds, or so complex you need a 45-minute rules explanation before anyone touches a card. Scout from Oink Games sits in a sweet spot that's genuinely rare — it's a hand-management game that takes two minutes to explain but keeps your brain working for the full 15–20 minutes of play.

## What's Actually in the Tiny Box

Oink Games is famous for their compact packaging, and Scout delivers on that promise. The box measures about 4.1 x 2.8 x 1.1 inches — smaller than a paperback novel. Inside you get a 45-card deck, a rulebook, and a scoring pad. That's the entire footprint. For comparison, it's roughly the same size as Oink's other hits like Fake Art and A Fake Artist Goes to New York.

The cards themselves are standard poker-size with a linen finish that shuffles well. The artwork is minimalist — colored suits with numbers from 1 to 10. It's not going to win any beauty contests, but the clean design means you can read your hand at a glance, even in dim bar lighting.

## How the Game Actually Plays

Here's the core twist that makes Scout stand out: you don't arrange your hand. Cards are dealt in a random order, and you can only play cards from the front or back of your hand. The middle cards are "scouted" — you can take them, but only by playing cards from your hand first.

This sounds simple, but it creates a delicious tension. Do you play that strong card now to score points, or hold it to unlock better cards buried in your hand? The "scout" action — where you steal a card from another player's played set — adds a layer of aggression that keeps everyone engaged even when it's not their turn.

The game plays in three rounds, and each round you're trying to score points by having the highest-value sets. You can also "go out" by playing all your cards, which instantly ends the round and gives you bonus points. That creates a push-your-luck element: do you race to finish, or build a bigger score and risk someone else ending the round first?

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews at 4.8 stars with 240 ratings are remarkably consistent in what they highlight. One reviewer puts it well: "I've played this with my gaming group, my parents, and my kids. All three groups got it immediately and all three groups had fun." That's the universal appeal — it's light enough for casual players but has enough depth for experienced gamers.

Another common thread in the feedback: the "aha moment" when players realize how the scout mechanic works. As one five-star reviewer notes, "The first round is everyone figuring out the hand-order trick. By round two, people are trash-talking and stealing cards from each other." That progression from confusion to competitive chaos happens within a single session, which is exactly what you want from a filler game.

Portability gets mentioned constantly. Multiple reviewers describe bringing Scout to restaurants, on flights, and to game nights "just in case." The box genuinely disappears into a pocket, and the game fits on a tiny café table.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. The artwork is functional but forgettable — if your group cares about table presence and beautiful illustrations, Scout will disappoint. It looks like a prototype, not a polished product.

At five players, the game can drag. Each player's turn involves checking both ends of their hand, considering the current played cards, and weighing scout options. With AP-prone players, a single round can stretch to 10 minutes. That's fine for the first round, but by round three, people start checking their phones.

The scoring pad is a minor annoyance — it's a single sheet with a grid for tracking scores across multiple games. You'll need to photocopy it or just use a phone app after a few sessions. It's a small thing, but it shows where Oink saved money.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Travel gamers**: If you want a real game that fits in a shirt pocket, this is the one.
- **Mixed-ability groups**: It works with kids (around 8+), casual gamers, and serious hobbyists at the same table.
- **Filler game collectors**: If you need something to play between heavier games, Scout slots in perfectly at 15–20 minutes.

Skip it if you're looking for a deep, strategy-heavy experience that will occupy a full game night. This is a light-to-medium filler, not a main event.

## FAQ

**How many players does Scout support?**
Two to five players. The two-player game is more tactical and feels different from the multiplayer chaos — both work, but the sweet spot is three to four players.

**How long does a game take?**
A full game (three rounds) runs 15–20 minutes. It's designed to be played multiple times in a session.

**Is Scout available in English?**
Yes, the Oink Games English edition is widely available on Amazon and at game retailers. The rules are clear and well-translated.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a portable, clever card game that works across different groups and skill levels. It's one of the best small-box games I've played.

**Skip it if** you need beautiful artwork, prefer deeper strategy, or routinely play with five people who take their time.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Scout earns its award nomination through smart design, genuine innovation in the hand-management genre, and a price point that feels fair. The tiny box and fast playtime make it an easy recommendation for almost any game collection.
