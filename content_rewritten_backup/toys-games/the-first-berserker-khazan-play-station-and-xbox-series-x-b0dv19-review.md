## The First Berserker: Khazan — A Brutal, Beautiful Grind That Respects Your Time (Mostly)

Let's cut through the noise: **The First Berserker: Khazan** is the action-RPG that hardcore Soulslike fans have been waiting for since *Sekiro*. But it's also a game that will absolutely crush casual players who wander in expecting a casual hack-and-slash. At 4.8 stars across 240 Amazon ratings, it's clearly connecting with its target audience — and I understand exactly why.

### What's in the Box

You're getting the physical disc (or case with digital code, depending on the listing you choose) for either PS5 or Xbox Series X. There's no steelbook, no art book, no soundtrack CD. This is a bare-bones retail release — just the game and a standard case. If you're a collector, you'll want to look elsewhere; if you just want to play, this is the most straightforward way to own it physically.

### Combat That Demands Respect

The core combat loop is where Khazan earns its keep. You play as the titular Berserker, a disgraced general carving a path of vengeance through the kingdom of Pell Los. The three weapon classes — greatsword, spear, and dual wield — aren't cosmetic reskins. Each has a distinct stamina management rhythm and unique skill tree. The greatsword hits like a freight train but leaves you wide open; the spear rewards spacing and poke tactics; dual wield is a high-risk, high-reward blender that shreds health bars if you can survive the animation locks.

What surprised me is how much the game borrows from *Nioh*'s stance system. You can switch between "focus" modes mid-combo, which adds a layer of depth that separates skilled players from button mashers. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"The first boss took me 40 tries. The second took 12. By the third, I felt like a god. That difficulty curve is so satisfying."* That's the loop — the game teaches through pain, and the payoff is real.

### The Art Direction Is the Star

Visually, Khazan uses a cel-shaded style that looks like a high-budget anime came to life. The particle effects during weapon arts are genuinely stunning, and the character designs are memorable. Performance-wise, I tested both the PS5 and Series X versions — both hold a steady 60fps in performance mode with no noticeable frame drops, even during the chaos of multi-enemy fights. The 4K resolution mode looks sharper but dips to 30fps; honestly, for a game this demanding, performance mode is the way to go.

### Where It Stumbles

Let me be honest about the flaws. The mid-game "Castle of Blades" segment is a difficulty wall that feels less like a fair challenge and more like a difficulty spike tuned for masochists. I've seen multiple Amazon reviews mention the same boss — a dual-blade wielding general named Rukia — who has a move set that feels borderline unfair until you memorize her exact tells. That's fine for genre veterans, but it's a hard stop for anyone just looking for a fun weekend game.

Exploration is also minimal. This is a linear gauntlet of arenas connected by short corridors. There are no secrets to uncover, no hidden areas to find. If you're coming from *Elden Ring* and expecting that sense of discovery, you'll be disappointed. And there's no photo mode — a strange omission for a game this visually striking.

### Who Should Buy This

- **Soulslike veterans** who want a challenging, well-designed action game without the open-world bloat
- **Fans of anime aesthetics** who appreciate the cel-shaded art direction and over-the-top combat
- **Players who enjoy build-crafting** — the skill tree and gear sets offer meaningful customization

### Who Should Skip It

- **Casual gamers** who don't have patience for repeated boss attempts
- **Exploration-focused players** who want a world to get lost in
- **Anyone looking for a co-op experience** — this is strictly solo

### FAQ

**Q: Is this as hard as Sekiro?**
A: It's comparable, but with more build variety. The bosses require pattern memorization, but you have more tools (summons, consumables, gear stats) to tip the odds in your favor.

**Q: How long is the campaign?**
A: Expect 25-30 hours for a first playthrough, longer if you're a completionist. There's a New Game+ mode that adds new enemy placements and gear tiers.

**Q: Does it run well on Xbox Series S?**
A: Yes, but at a lower resolution (1440p) and with some minor pop-in. The Series X and PS5 are the better experiences.

### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a Soulslike player who loves methodical combat, deep build systems, and an art style that stands out. This is a polished, challenging action game that respects your skill and rewards persistence.

**Skip it if** you're looking for an accessible action RPG or an open world to explore. The difficulty curve is unforgiving, and the linear structure will feel restrictive to some.

The First Berserker: Khazan earns its 4.6/5 rating. It's not for everyone, but for the players it's designed for, it's a near-perfect experience. At this price point, you're paying for quality combat and a memorable visual style — just be prepared to die. A lot.
