Let's be honest about what this is: a 906-piece LEGO set that looks like your grandfather's radio, but with a working FM tuner hidden inside. If that sentence sounds intriguing, you're the target audience. If it sounds pointless, you can probably stop reading — but you'd be missing one of the more clever adult LEGO sets to drop recently.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains 906 pieces, a 94-page instruction booklet, and zero batteries (more on that later). The finished model measures about 10.5 inches wide, 5.5 inches tall, and 3.5 inches deep — roughly the footprint of a small bookshelf speaker. The build itself takes most people 3-4 hours spread over a couple of evenings.

The design nails the 1950s tabletop radio aesthetic. The tan-and-brown color scheme, the curved speaker grill, the cream-colored dial face — it looks like it belongs on a mid-century credenza, not a toy shelf. The printed dial (no stickers here, thankfully) shows AM/FM frequencies and adds a level of authenticity that printed pieces just can't match.

## The Clever Part: It Actually Works

Here's where this set surprises. The tuning dial physically connects to an internal FM tuner module. Turn the knob, and the needle moves across the dial while the frequency changes on the actual radio. There are also two preset buttons on top that let you jump directly to your saved stations. The speaker is decent for what it is — it's not a Sonos, but it's clear enough for talk radio or background music in a small room.

The mechanical linkage between the dial and tuner is genuinely impressive engineering. Watching the needle track your hand movements as you tune is satisfying in a way that's hard to explain until you try it. It's the kind of detail that makes you appreciate the design process behind this set.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 240 ratings (4.8 stars), a few themes dominate. One reviewer called it "the most fun I've had building LEGO in years — the working tuner is a gimmick, but it's a brilliant one." Another noted: "I bought this for my dad's 70th birthday. He spent the whole evening showing us how it picked up stations better than he expected."

The nostalgia factor is real. Multiple reviewers mention buying it for older relatives who grew up with actual radios like this, and several note it's become a conversation piece in their living rooms.

## Where It Falls Short

The FM-only limitation is the biggest gap. In 2026, most people stream their audio. There's no Bluetooth, no aux input, no way to connect your phone. If you don't listen to FM radio, the audio function is essentially a novelty you'll show guests once and then ignore.

The batteries are also a minor annoyance. Three AAA batteries aren't included, and you'll need to buy them separately. It's a small thing, but it's the kind of detail that feels nickel-and-dimey on a set at this price point.

Finally, the dial is genuinely fun to turn, but the preset buttons are a bit stiff. They work, but they don't have the satisfying click you'd hope for.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a LEGO adult fan who appreciates mechanical cleverness. You have a mid-century modern shelf that needs a conversation piece. You're looking for a gift for a dad or grandpa who grew up with physical radio dials — this is essentially engineered nostalgia.

**Skip it if:** You want a purely decorative piece (there are cheaper display models). You never listen to FM radio and won't care about the tuner gimmick. You're looking for a LEGO set with a more complex or challenging build — this one is more about clever design than difficulty.

## FAQ

**Q: Can you actually listen to music through it?**
A: Yes, FM radio only. The speaker is clear but not powerful — fine for talk radio or background music in a small room.

**Q: How long does the build take?**
A: Most people report 3-4 hours total. The pieces are well-organized into numbered bags, so you can easily split it across multiple evenings.

**Q: Does it come with batteries?**
A: No. You'll need 3 AAA batteries. Factor that into your budget.

## The Verdict

This is a 4.6/5 star product. It's not perfect — the FM-only limitation and battery requirement are real tradeoffs. But the working tuner mechanism, the excellent printed details, and the sheer charm of the finished piece make it one of the more memorable LEGO sets in recent memory. If you appreciate clever design and have any affection for vintage audio, this earns its shelf space.

**Buy it if** you want a functional conversation piece that doubles as a thoughtful gift. **Skip it if** you need Bluetooth or want a more challenging build. Everyone else — this one's worth your money.
