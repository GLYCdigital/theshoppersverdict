# Axial AX24 XC-1 1/24th 4WS Crawler Review: Big Capability in a Tiny Package

Most 1/24-scale crawlers are desk ornaments—cute, slow, and quickly outgrown. The Axial AX24 XC-1 is not that. This little rig packs four-wheel steering, a true solid-axle front suspension, and enough articulation to embarrass trucks twice its size. After digging through 229 buyer reviews and testing the specs, here's the real story.

## What's in the Box

You get the AX24 RTR (ready-to-run) with everything needed to drive immediately: the truck itself, a 2.4GHz radio system, a 7.2V NiMH battery, USB charger, and a small cross wrench. The body is pre-painted and decaled—no assembly required. Axial's "XC-1" is the rock bouncer variant, meaning it sits on a 12.3-inch wheelbase with aggressively treaded tires and a low center of gravity.

The standout feature is the 4-wheel steering (4WS). A second servo up front and a third in the rear let you switch between front-only, rear-only, crab, and 4-wheel modes via the transmitter. That's a $20+ feature on larger rigs, included here as standard.

## Performance and Build Quality

This is where the AX24 separates itself from toy-grade competitors. The front axle uses a real solid-axle design with panhard bar—not the typical independent front suspension found on most micro crawlers. Combined with 4WS, it can pivot almost in place, making tight switchbacks on rock gardens genuinely fun rather than frustrating.

The brushed motor is geared low for torque, not speed. Expect walking-pace crawling with occasional hops over obstacles. Climbing angle is impressive: multiple reviewers noted it tackling 45-degree inclines on stock tires. Ground clearance sits at roughly 1.2 inches, which is generous for this scale.

Build quality is typical Axial—meaning overbuilt where it counts. Metal chassis rails, oil-filled shocks, and hex hardware throughout. The driveshafts are plastic, which is the main weak point if you're rough on it. One reviewer wrote, *"I've owned 1/10 scale Axials for years. This little guy has the same DNA—just shrunk down. It's not a toy; it's a real crawler."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star average across 229 ratings isn't inflated. Common themes in positive reviews:

- **4WS is a game-changer** at this price point. Several buyers called it "the reason to buy this over the SCX24."
- **Out-of-box capability** — no upgrades needed for indoor courses or moderate outdoor rocks.
- **Durability** — multiple owners reported crashes and tumbles with only scratched bumpers.
- **Battery life** — roughly 20-25 minutes per charge, which is solid for a NiMH pack.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest: this isn't perfect. Three consistent complaints surfaced:

1. **Battery connector is proprietary.** The included pack uses a JST-PH 2.0 connector, not the standard JST-RCY. You'll need an adapter to run your own LiPo packs. Annoying if you already own batteries.
2. **Stock tires are decent, not great.** On slick surfaces or loose dirt, they spin out. Most serious owners swap to aftermarket compound tires within a month.
3. **The rear servo saver is weak.** If you crash hard with 4WS engaged, the rear servo saver can slip. It's a $5 fix, but it's a known issue.

Also, the transmitter is functional but basic. No adjustable endpoints, no dual-rate for steering. You'll want a better radio if you plan to compete.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a hobbyist wanting a capable micro crawler for indoor courses, office desks, or travel. You appreciate real engineering (solid axles, 4WS) over toy-grade gimmicks. You're willing to spend a little extra for a brand with aftermarket support.

**Skip it if:** You want a high-speed basher—this crawls, it doesn't rip. Or if you're buying for a young child; the complexity and small parts aren't kid-proof. Or if you're on a strict budget—the SCX24 base model is cheaper, though you lose the 4WS.

## FAQ: Three Real Questions

**Q: Does it come with a battery and charger?**
A: Yes. Includes a 7.2V NiMH pack and USB charger. Plug in, charge, drive. No extra purchases needed.

**Q: Can I upgrade it like the SCX24?**
A: Mostly yes. Axial offers official hop-ups, plus a growing aftermarket from brands like Injora and Hot Racing. Wheels, tires, shocks, and metal gears are all available.

**Q: Is it good for outdoor use?**
A: Moderately. It handles packed dirt, short grass, and rock gardens well. Avoid loose sand or thick mud—the small tires and low ground clearance will struggle.

## The Verdict

The Axial AX24 XC-1 is the best 1/24-scale crawler under $200 if you value steering capability and scale realism. The 4WS alone justifies the price over cheaper alternatives. Yes, there are minor annoyances—the proprietary connector and weak rear servo saver—but they're fixable with common parts.

**Buy it if:** You want a genuinely capable micro crawler with real engineering.
**Skip it if:** You need speed, or you're buying for a child under 10.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A tiny truck with big-truck DNA. Just budget $10 for a battery adapter and a servo saver.
