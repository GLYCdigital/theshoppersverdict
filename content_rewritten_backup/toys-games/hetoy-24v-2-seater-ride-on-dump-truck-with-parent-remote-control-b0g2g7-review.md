If you've got two kids under 8 and a yard with any kind of slope, the Hetoy 24V 2-Seater Ride-On Dump Truck is one of the few budget-adjacent options that won't embarrass itself on grass. Most 12V ride-ons in this price class are glorified sidewalk cruisers — this one's got actual torque. But it's not without compromises, and some of them are significant.

## What You Actually Get

The box is heavy — roughly 55 pounds — and what's inside is a full-size (60" long, 31" wide) two-seater dump truck. The 24V system is two 12V batteries wired in series, driving two 550-series motors through independent gearboxes. That's the same architecture you'd find in trucks costing $150 more.

Included in the box: the truck body (pre-assembled in two halves), four wheels, the dump bed, two seats with seatbelts, a 2.4GHz parent remote, a 24V charger, and a manual that's... not great. The hardware is bagged but not labeled clearly, and several reviewers note the illustrated steps don't match the actual bolt sizes. Budget 2–3 hours for assembly if you're handy, longer if you're not. As one verified buyer put it: *"Took me almost 3 hours. The instructions are a joke, but the truck itself is solid once it's together."*

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature is the 24V system, and it delivers. On flat pavement, this thing moves at a claimed 5 mph — honestly, that feels right. More importantly, it handles a moderate grass slope with two kids aboard. The suspension is basic coil springs on all four wheels, so it's not a plush ride, but it absorbs bumps without rattling apart.

The dump bed is a genuine highlight. It's a rigid plastic tub with a metal hinge and a manual latch — no electric lift, but kids don't care. They load it with sand, rocks, or their own weight, and the bed holds up. The bed tilts back about 45 degrees and dumps cleanly.

The parent remote is where things get complicated. It operates on 2.4GHz (so no interference with Bluetooth or Wi-Fi), but it's not a full override. It can stop the truck, steer left/right, and control forward/reverse — but the steering is digital, not proportional. That means the front wheels snap to full lock rather than turning gradually, which makes remote-controlled driving jerky. The range is roughly 30 feet — fine for a driveway, not enough for a large yard. Most parents will use it as an emergency brake, which it does reliably.

## What Buyers Praise

The two-seater capacity is the most common reason people buy this over single-rider models. One parent wrote: *"My 4-year-old and 6-year-old ride together daily. It's the only toy they don't fight over."* Another noted the battery can handle a full hour of mixed driving on flat ground with both kids aboard — that's about typical for 24V systems.

The build quality is praised more than expected at this price point. The plastic body is thick (about 4mm in high-impact areas), the wheels are rubber over plastic hubs, and the seatbelts are real — not decorative.

## Where It Falls Short

The battery life is the biggest practical complaint. You get 45–60 minutes of runtime per charge, and the charge time is 8–10 hours. That means no mid-day recharges. If your kids want to ride after school and again after dinner, you're out of luck unless you buy a second battery (which Hetoy sells separately for about $50).

The remote control range is genuinely limiting. If your driveway is longer than 30 feet, the remote loses signal. And as mentioned, the non-proportional steering makes remote driving feel like a video game with lag — it's a safety override, not a real driving experience.

Assembly is the other sore spot. Several reviews mention stripped screws or holes that don't align perfectly. It's not impossible, but it's not a 30-minute job, either.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have two kids aged 3–7 who will ride together, you have a grassy yard with mild slopes, and you're comfortable with a 2-hour assembly project. The 24V power and dual-motor setup make this genuinely capable for the price.

**Skip it if:** You need all-day battery life, you want a remote that can actually drive the truck smoothly, or you're expecting a plug-and-play experience out of the box.

## FAQ

**Q: How fast does it actually go?**
A: Claimed 5 mph, realistically 3.5–4.5 mph depending on surface and rider weight. On flat pavement with one kid, it hits the high end. On grass with two kids, expect closer to 3 mph.

**Q: Can the remote completely override the kid's controls?**
A: Yes. The remote takes priority when engaged. But it's not a proportional system — it's on/off steering. Use it for emergency stops or straight-line guidance, not precise maneuvering.

**Q: Does it work on gravel or dirt?**
A: It handles packed dirt fine. Loose gravel will bog it down, especially with two riders. Stick to pavement, grass, and hard-packed surfaces.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5**

The Hetoy 24V is a solid mid-tier ride-on that punches above its weight in power and capacity. The dual-motor 24V system, genuine two-seater design, and durable dump bed make it a legitimate value. But the short battery life, mediocre remote, and frustrating assembly keep it from being a slam-dunk recommendation. If you're willing to spend an evening with a wrench and plan charging around your kids' schedule, this is a capable, fun truck that will last a few years. Just know what you're signing up for.
