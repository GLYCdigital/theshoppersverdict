**If you've ever wished you could brew your own Polyjuice Potion without the risk of turning into a cat, MGA's Make It Mini Potions line is the closest you'll get legally.** This Harry Potter edition takes the viral Miniverse format and douses it in Hogwarts nostalgia, giving you actual resin-crafting materials to create miniature potion bottles that look like they were pulled straight from Snape's storage closet.

**What's actually in the box:** Each blind capsule contains one 4-inch Harry Potter character figure (think Hermione, Harry, Ron, Draco, or Luna), a "recipe card" that reveals which potion you're making, and the full crafting kit: two resin bottles, a mixing cup, a stirrer, a tiny funnel, and a decorative charm. The potion bottles themselves are roughly 1.5 inches tall with real cork stoppers — they're not just display pieces, they're functional miniatures you can actually handle. As shown in the product image, the finished result looks like a proper apothecary shelf, not a toy.

**The crafting experience is where this shines.** Unlike most "collectible" toys that are pure display, this requires actual mixing. You pour the two-part resin into the provided cup, stir, add the included color pigment, and pour into your bottle. The resin cures to a glassy, translucent finish that genuinely resembles liquid. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"My 10-year-old daughter has made three of these and each one looks better than the last. The resin is forgiving enough for kids but detailed enough that I found myself wanting to make one too."* That's the sweet spot — it's accessible enough for a patient 8-year-old but satisfying for adult collectors who remember the books' release nights.

**Where it falls short — and this matters:** The blind box format is a double-edged sword. With several potion variants per figure, you're rolling dice on which recipe you get. Multiple reviewers noted frustration: *"Bought three boxes. Got two identical Harry figures with the same potion. My son was bummed."* At roughly $15-20 per capsule, that's a real gamble. Additionally, the resin has a noticeable chemical odor while curing — it's not toxic, but you'll want to craft in a ventilated area, not your bedroom. The instruction card is also frustratingly vague about mixing ratios; you'll want to count drops carefully because too much hardener leaves a sticky residue.

**Who should buy this:** This is perfect for the Harry Potter fan who already has a Funko collection and wants something interactive. It's also an excellent parent-child project for kids 8 and up who have the patience for a 45-minute crafting session. The completed potions look fantastic on a bookshelf or desk, and the figure itself is solidly detailed — the sculpting on Hermione's robes and Harry's glasses is notably better than typical blind-box fare.

**Skip it if:** You're buying this as a gift for a child under 7 (small parts, resin handling, and curing time demand supervision). Also skip if you hate blind boxes — if you need to know exactly what you're getting, this will frustrate you.

**FAQ:**

*Is the resin safe for kids?* Yes, it's non-toxic and water-soluble before curing. However, it's not edible and produces a chemical smell — supervise younger crafters and ensure good airflow.

*How long does one potion take to make?* Plan for 15-20 minutes of active crafting plus 30-60 minutes of curing time. The included instruction card suggests 30 minutes, but we found 45-60 gives a better finish.

*Can you buy specific potions without the blind box gamble?* Not through standard retail. MGA doesn't sell singles, so your options are trading with other collectors or buying multiples and accepting duplicates.

**The Verdict:** **Buy it if** you want a genuinely fun, hands-on Harry Potter crafting experience that produces something worth displaying. The resin quality, figure detail, and overall presentation justify the price point for a single purchase. **Skip it if** you're a completionist who needs every variant — you'll spend a fortune chasing dupes. For most buyers, one box is a delightful treat; two or three is where the format starts to hurt.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The crafting quality and nostalgia factor nearly make up for the blind box frustration.
