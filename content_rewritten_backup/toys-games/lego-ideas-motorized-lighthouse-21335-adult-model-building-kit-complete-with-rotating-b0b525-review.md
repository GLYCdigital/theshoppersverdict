## The $400 LEGO Set That Actually Does Something

Most LEGO sets sit there. You build them, you admire them, they gather dust. The LEGO Ideas Motorized Lighthouse 21335 is different — it *does* something. That rotating beacon isn't a static brick. It's a real motor spinning a real light, visible from across the room. That alone justifies a second look, even at the eye-watering $399.99 price tag.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get 2,065 pieces, three minifigures (the lighthouse keeper, his dog, and a sailor), a rowboat, and — critically — the motor module and light brick. The build splits into three clear phases: the rocky base with crashing waves, the lighthouse tower itself, and the keeper's cottage attached to the side. As shown in the product image, the waves aren't flat blue plates — they're built at angles with white crests, giving the base real motion. The cottage interior has a fireplace, a bed, and a ladder up to the light room. It's a lot of detail for a set that's primarily a display piece.

## Performance: Does the Motor Actually Work?

Here's where this set earns its keep. The beacon rotates smoothly — not jerky, not stuttering. There's a small control dial on the base that adjusts the light's intensity from a dim glow to a bright beam. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"The rotating light is mesmerizing. My kids fight over who gets to turn it on at night."* That's the real-world reaction you're buying here. The motor runs quietly, and the batteries (6 AAA, not included) last a surprisingly long time — several evenings of use before needing a swap.

The build itself is satisfying. The angled rockwork forces you to think differently than a standard box build, and the tile-covered waves are a clever technique. Plan for six to eight hours total. It's not a weekend-killer, but it's not a quick afternoon either.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8/5 rating across 240 reviews isn't accidental. Beyond the motor, buyers repeatedly mention the *presence* of the finished model. One reviewer noted: *"It looks like a diorama, not a toy. The waves and rocks make it feel like a scene frozen in time."* The minifigures add charm — the keeper's dog has a little bone, and the sailor's rowboat is nicely detailed. For display purposes, this is one of the best Ideas sets LEGO has produced.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the price. At $399.99, you're paying roughly 19 cents per piece — that's premium territory, and the set doesn't have the sheer bulk of a UCS Star Wars or a Modular Building. It stands about 12 inches tall, so it won't dominate a shelf. Second, the light is warm white only. No color options, no flashing modes. It's a lighthouse, so that's realistic, but if you wanted something flashier, this isn't it. Third, the batteries are a hidden cost — six AAA batteries add up over time, and the motor doesn't work without them. There's no USB-C option.

One more thing: the battery compartment is on the underside of the base. To change batteries, you have to flip the whole model over, which is awkward if it's on a high shelf. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

This set is for the adult LEGO fan who wants a *living* piece of decor. If you're a collector who already has the big-ticket Star Wars or Architecture sets, this adds something different to your shelf. It's also ideal for someone who enjoys the build process as much as the finished product — the techniques are genuinely interesting. If you're buying for a child under 12, skip it. The motor and small pieces are fragile, and the price is too high for rough play.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
A: With 6 AAA batteries, expect 10-15 hours of continuous use. If you run it for an hour each evening, that's roughly two weeks per set. Use quality alkaline batteries; cheap ones drain faster.

**Q: Is the light bright enough to see in daylight?**
A: It's visible but not glaring. In a brightly lit room, you'll see the beam clearly when it faces you. In low light or darkness, it's genuinely impressive — that's where it shines.

**Q: Can you motorize it without the official motor?**
A: No. The motor and light brick are custom-designed for this set. You can't swap in a standard Power Functions motor. If you want the rotation, you need this set's specific components.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're an adult LEGO enthusiast who wants a display piece that actually functions, and you're willing to pay for that novelty. It's a genuinely unique set — there's nothing else in the Ideas line quite like it.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget, or you prioritize piece count and sheer size over interactivity. At 12 inches, it won't be the centerpiece of your collection, and the price per piece is steep.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The motorized lighthouse is a brilliant, functional design that elevates a solid build into something memorable. The price and battery quirks hold it back from a perfect score, but if you want a LEGO set that does more than sit there, this is the one.
