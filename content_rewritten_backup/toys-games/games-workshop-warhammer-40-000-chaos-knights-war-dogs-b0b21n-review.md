If you're eyeing Chaos Knights as your next 40K army, the War Dogs box is where you should start—not just because it's the logical point entry, but because this kit quietly delivers more flexibility per dollar than almost anything else in the range. With a 4.6-star average across 229 Amazon ratings, it's clearly earning its keep. Here's the real breakdown.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get one plastic War Dog kit—one miniature, but don't let that fool you. This sprue is packed. The kit builds any of the three War Dog variants: the Executioner (long-range anti-infantry), the Stalker (close-combat hunter), or the Brigand (balanced chaos war machine). Each has distinct weapon options, including daemonbreath meltaguns, havoc launchers, and the iconic war glaive. As shown in the product image, the detail work on the carapace and leg plating is genuinely impressive—those Chaos stars and riveted panels are crisp, not mushy.

**Build Quality and Real-World Assembly**

Games Workshop's plastic quality here is excellent. The parts fit together with minimal gap-filling, and the detail is sharp enough that even a mediocre painter will get a decent result. That said, this is not a beginner kit. You're dealing with dozens of small components—torso segments, leg pistons, weapon arms—and the instruction booklet occasionally assumes you've built a Knight before. Take your time with the leg assembly; getting the hip joints aligned properly matters more than you think.

The biggest practical issue? **Magnetizing is almost mandatory** if you want to switch between variants. The kit is designed to swap weapons, but no magnets are included. If you glue everything in place, you're locking yourself into one playstyle. Most experienced hobbyists recommend buying 3mm x 1mm neodymium magnets and drilling the weapon mounts—it's worth the extra effort.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The Amazon reviews are overwhelmingly positive, and the pattern is clear. One buyer wrote, "The detail is insane for the price point—way better than the old metal models." Another noted, "This kit is a joy to assemble if you take your time. The flexibility to run it as any of the three variants is a massive plus." People also repeatedly mention the value: for roughly the cost of a single full Knight, you can field three War Dogs, which is a much more flexible army core in most games.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honestly, the cons are minor but real. The fixed pose is the most common complaint—your War Dog will look the same as everyone else's unless you do some serious green-stuff work or kitbashing. The leg articulation is limited, so dynamic action poses are hard to achieve. Also, if you're new to the hobby, the sheer number of small parts can be overwhelming. And again, the magnet issue: expect to spend an extra $10–15 on magnets and a drill if you want full flexibility.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **New Chaos Knights players**: This is your entry point. Fielding three War Dogs gives you a legal, playable force without dropping $170 on a single Knight.
- **Experienced hobbyists** who enjoy magnetizing and customizing: The kit is a canvas.
- **Competitive players** wanting to run a "dog spam" list: War Dogs are currently strong in the meta for their points cost.

**Skip it if** you're a pure painter who hates sub-assemblies, or if you want a centerpiece model—buy a full Knight instead.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many War Dogs come in this box?**
A: One. This is a single-model kit, not a squad box. You need three for a full unit.

**Q: Can I build all three variants from one box?**
A: Yes, but not at the same time. The sprues contain all weapon options, so you choose which variant to assemble. To run all three, buy three boxes (or magnetize).

**Q: Is this kit suitable for beginners?**
A: It's challenging. The parts count is high and assembly requires patience. If you've only built Space Marines or Necrons, start with something simpler first.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're starting a Chaos Knights army, want a flexible kit that rewards careful building, or need cheap, effective heavy support for your Chaos forces. **Skip it if** you're a beginner who wants a quick, low-stress build, or if you're looking for a single impressive centerpiece model.

This is one of the best-value kits Games Workshop currently sells for the Chaos range. At 4.6/5 stars, the community agrees. Just budget for magnets and patience.
