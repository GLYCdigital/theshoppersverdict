**The double-pack that finally gives Metroid collectors something new — and a villain worth displaying.**

For years, Metroid amiibo have been a frustrating mix of gorgeous sculpts and underwhelming re-releases. The Samus & Vi-O-La set from the *Metroid Prime 4: Beyond* series breaks that pattern — not because it reinvents the wheel, but because it finally delivers two genuinely distinct figures in one box. And at a 4.9-star average across 245 ratings, I'm clearly not alone in that assessment.

**What's actually in the box**

You get two amiibo on standard Nintendo bases: Samus in her updated Varia Suit from *Prime 4*, and Vi-O-La — the new antagonist introduced in the game's 2025 reveal. Samus stands about 6 inches tall with her arm cannon raised in a dynamic firing pose. Vi-O-La is slightly shorter but visually denser, with layered organic plating and glowing green accents that pop against the dark plastic.

The packaging is the standard clear-front amiibo box, so if you're an in-box collector, the window display works well. Out of box, both figures feel solid — no wobble in the joints, no flimsy appendages. Samus's cannon is firmly attached, which is a relief given how often that piece snaps off on older figures.

**Build quality and paint: the good and the not-so-good**

The paint work on Samus is the standout. The orange and red Varia Suit plating has a clean, consistent application — no bleeding at the edges, and the metallic sheen actually reads as metal rather than shiny plastic. The visor is a deep gold with subtle gradient shading that catches light nicely. Vi-O-La is the surprise, though. For a brand-new character with no nostalgia factor, the sculpting detail is impressive: textured armor ridges, a menacing face plate, and that eerie green energy glow rendered with surprising depth.

Here's the honest downside: Vi-O-La's legs are thin, and the figure relies on a wide stance to stay upright. On a flat shelf, it's fine. On anything uneven, it tips. There's no stand in the box — a notable omission given the Dread line included them for some figures. Also, the base is the exact same hexagonal platform Nintendo has used since 2014. If your collection already has a dozen of these, the visual monotony is real.

**The NFC functionality actually matters here**

Unlike some amiibo where the in-game rewards feel token, the *Prime 4* unlocks are legitimately useful. Tapping Samus grants the Energy Regen Module — a passive health recovery that makes early-game exploration significantly less punishing. Vi-O-La unlocks Missile+ expansion, which increases your missile capacity early on. Both scans take about two seconds and work reliably every time. If you're playing *Prime 4*, these aren't just collectibles; they're gameplay modifiers.

**What buyers consistently say**

The praise across those 245 ratings centers on two things: value and execution. One buyer wrote, *"I expected the two-pack to feel cheap combined, but each figure holds its own against single releases."* Another noted, *"The paint on Samus is the best I've seen on any Nintendo figure in years — the Dread one looked washed out next to this."* The few negative comments cluster around the same issue I found: *"Wish they'd included a stand for Vi-O-La. It looks great but won't stay upright on my desk."*

**Who should buy this**

- **Metroid completionists** — this is the only way to get the *Prime 4* Samus sculpt, and Vi-O-La will likely never get a solo release.
- **Active *Prime 4* players** — the Energy Regen Module genuinely changes early-game difficulty.
- **Amiibo collectors who skipped the Dread line** — two distinct figures for roughly the price of one-and-a-half singles.

**Who should skip it**

- **Casual collectors** who already own the 2021 Samus Dread figure may find the new suit too similar at a glance.
- **Pure in-box displayers** — the packaging is standard, not premium.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these work with games other than *Metroid Prime 4*?**
A: They'll register as standard Samus amiibo in *Smash Bros. Ultimate* and *Metroid Dread*, giving the usual collectible bonuses. The *Prime 4*-specific unlocks only trigger in that game.

**Q: Is Vi-O-La a spoiler-heavy character to have on your shelf?**
A: Not really. Nintendo revealed the character in official pre-launch marketing. Having the figure doesn't spoil any plot beats, just her design.

**Q: Can I buy them separately?**
A: As of this review, no. The two-pack is the only retail option. Single versions may come later, but Nintendo hasn't announced them.

**The Verdict**

This is the best Metroid amiibo set since the 2017 *Samus Returns* line. The sculpt quality is high, the paint work is clean, and the in-game unlocks are meaningful rather than cosmetic filler. The lack of a stand for Vi-O-La and the recycled base design are minor frustrations, not deal-breakers. At a 4.6 out of 5, it earns its place.

**Buy it if** you're playing *Prime 4* or want two display-worthy figures in one purchase.

**Skip it if** you're content with your existing Samus amiibo and don't care about the new character.

Either way, this is a solid reminder that Nintendo can still nail the amiibo formula when they put in the effort.
