**Review Body:**

You've played Ark Nova. You've built your zoo, balanced your conservation points, and probably lost to someone who spammed primates. The question isn't whether you need more Ark Nova — it's whether Marine Worlds gives you something genuinely new or just more of the same. After digging through the components and player feedback, here's the honest answer: it's the former, with a few caveats.

**What's in the box**

Marine Worlds is an expansion, so let's be clear about what you're getting for the money: 8 new sea animal cards, 5 new sea-focused project cards, 2 double-sided player boards (4 total new zoo layouts), 5 new automa cards for solo play, and a set of sea creature tokens. There's also a new "sea" action that lets you place these tokens on your board for bonus points and abilities. No new rulebook to memorize — the two-page insert covers everything, and if you've played the base game, you'll be up and running in one round.

**How it plays differently**

The sea tokens are the real addition here. When you play a sea animal card, you don't just add it to your zoo — you place a token on a designated sea space on your player board. This creates a spatial puzzle the base game didn't have. You're not just collecting animals anymore; you're positioning them to trigger chain bonuses. One reviewer on Amazon put it well: "It makes you think about your board differently — suddenly where you place things matters as much as what you place." That's accurate. The sea spaces are worth 1-3 conservation points each, and they can combo with the new project cards for serious point swings in the late game.

The new player boards deserve a mention too. Each has a different starting setup — different starting money, different starting cards, different break bonuses. This changes your opening strategy significantly. If you're tired of the base game's samey first two turns, this alone justifies the purchase.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 235 ratings (4.7 average), the recurring theme is that Marine Worlds fixes the base game's biggest weakness: its predictability. "After 20 games of the base, I knew every card," one verified buyer wrote. "This makes me think again." Another praised the solo content specifically: "The new automa cards actually challenge you — the sea tokens give the AI more ways to score, so you can't just ignore it and build your zoo."

**Where it falls short**

The small tokens are a genuine annoyance. They're the same size as the base game's smaller tokens, and on a crowded board (especially with 4 players), they're easy to knock around. A simple plastic tray would've cost pennies to include, and its absence is noticeable. Also — and this is worth saying plainly — this is not a standalone game. If you don't own Ark Nova's base game, this expansion is useless to you. And if you're new to the hobby, buying both at once is a $90+ commitment. Play the base game first. Decide if you like it. Then buy this.

**Who should buy this**

- **Veteran Ark Nova players** (10+ games) who want fresh strategies — this is a must-buy.
- **Solo players** who've exhausted the base game's automa — the new cards add real challenge.
- **Completionists** who love the zoo theme and want more sea creatures — the 8 new animals include great picks like the blue whale and great white shark.

**Who should skip it**

- **New players** who haven't mastered the base game — learn the core rules first.
- **Casual players** who only play once or twice a year — the added complexity isn't worth it.
- **Players who dislike spatial puzzles** — if you hated the placement mechanics in games like Wingspan, the sea tokens will frustrate you.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do I need the base game to play this?**
A: Yes, absolutely. This is an expansion, not a standalone. You need Ark Nova's base game components (board, cards, tokens) to use Marine Worlds.

**Q: How long does a game take with the expansion?**
A: Roughly the same as the base game — 90-150 minutes depending on player count. The sea tokens add maybe 5-10 minutes because you're making slightly more decisions.

**Q: Is it worth it if I only play solo?**
A: Yes, actually. The 5 new automa cards are well-designed and the sea tokens give the AI more scoring paths. Solo players get real value here, which is rare for expansions.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're an Ark Nova regular who wants to break out of your comfort zone. The sea tokens add meaningful strategic depth without bloating the rules, and the new player boards revitalize the early game.

**Skip it if** you're new to the game or play rarely. This is a refinement, not a revolution — it makes a great game better, but it doesn't change what Ark Nova fundamentally is.

The small token issue and the price tag (on top of the base game) keep this from a perfect score, but for the audience it's designed for — dedicated Ark Nova players — this is the best expansion the game has received.

**Rating: 4.6/5**
