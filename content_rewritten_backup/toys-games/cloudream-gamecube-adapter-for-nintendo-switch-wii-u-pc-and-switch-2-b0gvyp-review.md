## Performance: Where It Counts

The dual-cable design isn't a gimmick. Single-cable adapters often suffer from voltage drop, which manifests as occasional dropped inputs or slight input lag during chaotic 8-player matches. The CLOUDREAM's separate power line keeps polling stable at 125Hz on Switch, which is the same rate as the official Nintendo adapter. In practice, that means wavedashes, SHFFLs, and ledge trumping register exactly when you press the button, not a frame later.

On PC, the story is slightly more complicated. The adapter uses the standard ZR-COM protocol, which Windows doesn't natively recognize. You'll need to download the Zadig driver and configure it in Dolphin or a third-party tool like JoyToKey. It works reliably once set up — but it's not the frictionless experience Switch users get.
