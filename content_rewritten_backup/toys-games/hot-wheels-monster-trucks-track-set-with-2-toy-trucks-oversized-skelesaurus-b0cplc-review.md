The first thing you notice about the Hot Wheels Monster Trucks Oversized Skelesaurus set is the sheer presence of it. This isn’t a flimsy fold-out mat with a plastic loop. The centerpiece is a large, articulated Skelesaurus skeleton—think dinosaur meets monster truck—that stands about two feet tall when assembled. Its jaw opens wide enough to swallow a standard Hot Wheels car, and its curved spine doubles as the launch ramp. That design choice is the smartest thing about this set: the ramp isn’t a separate piece that gets lost under the couch. It’s structurally part of the toy.

**What’s in the Box**

You get the Skelesaurus skeleton, a track section that connects to its tail, two fully decorated monster trucks, and a handful of crushable plastic cars designed to be run over. The trucks are the bigger "Monster Jam"-style bodies, not the standard 1:64 scale—they’re chunky and easy for small hands to grip. The two included trucks are different designs, which matters because kids will fight over the cooler one otherwise. The crush cars are thin plastic, clearly meant to be destroyed, and there are enough of them that losing a few won’t end the fun.

**Real-World Performance**

Set this up on a hard floor and the physics work. You push a truck down the tail track, it launches off the Skelesaurus spine ramp, and it comes down on the crush cars with a satisfying *crunch*. The trucks are heavy enough to actually flatten the plastic cars, which is the core loop kids will repeat for longer than you expect. The jaw mechanism also opens and closes, so you can feed it a car and have it "eat" the wreckage—a small touch, but it adds play value beyond the ramp.

One thing buyers consistently mention is durability. I saw multiple reviews praising how the trucks hold up to aggressive play. As one parent put it, "My son has thrown these down the stairs and they still roll straight." That tracks with what I’d expect from Hot Wheels Monster Trucks—the axles are metal, the bodies are thick plastic, and they’re built to be crashed.

**Where It Falls Short**

Assembly is the main friction point. The Skelesaurus comes in several pieces that snap together, and it’s not intuitive. Expect 15–20 minutes of adult involvement, and check that every joint clicks fully—a loose connection will wobble during launches. The track-to-tail connection is also the weak point. If a truck lands off-center on the ramp, the impact can knock the track piece loose. It’s a quick fix, but it interrupts the flow of play for younger kids who can’t reattach it themselves.

Also note the size. This is not a compact toy. The full assembled footprint is roughly three feet long, so it needs dedicated floor space. If you’re tight on room, this isn’t the set for you.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is a strong pick for kids aged 4 to 8 who are already into monster trucks or dinosaurs. The combination of those two obsessions is a near-guaranteed hit. It’s also a good choice if you’re tired of buying track sets that require separate truck purchases—two trucks are included here, which is rare at this price point. For parents, the durability means it’ll survive the inevitable drops and crashes that come with the territory.

Skip it if your child is under 3—the small crush cars are a choking hazard, and the ramp requires a bit of hand-eye coordination to use properly. Also skip it if you don’t have the floor space or patience for assembly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this set work with regular Hot Wheels cars?**
A: Yes, standard 1:64 Hot Wheels cars fit on the track and can be launched off the ramp. They’re lighter than the included monster trucks, so they’ll fly further but won’t crush the plastic cars as effectively.

**Q: Are the crushable cars reusable?**
A: Somewhat. They flatten permanently, but the plastic is soft enough that you can bend most of them back into a roughly drivable shape. Expect them to get more mangled with each use—that’s the point.

**Q: How much space do I need to set this up?**
A: Clear about three feet of length and two feet of width on a hard floor. Carpet will reduce launch distance and make the trucks bounce less, so hard flooring is better.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, crash-focused monster truck set that includes two trucks and a unique dinosaur-ramp gimmick that actually works. The build quality justifies the price, and the play value extends well beyond the initial novelty.

**Skip it if** you’re short on space, buying for a toddler, or want a set your child can assemble independently.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The assembly hassle and track connection quirk keep it from a perfect score, but the durability, included trucks, and clever design make this one of the better monster truck sets at this price point.
