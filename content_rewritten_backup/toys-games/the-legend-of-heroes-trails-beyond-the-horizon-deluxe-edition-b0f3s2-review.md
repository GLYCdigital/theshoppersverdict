Let me be upfront: the Trails series is one of the most daunting franchises in JRPGs. It's sprawling, interconnected, and has a timeline that makes the MCU look simple. So when *Trails beyond the Horizon* launched as a fresh entry point with a "Deluxe Edition" boasting physical extras, both newcomers and veterans took notice. After 60+ hours with the PS5 version, here's the real breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

The Deluxe Edition isn't a cash grab. As shown in the product image, you get a steelbook case with original artwork, a hardcover art book spanning roughly 60 pages of character designs and world concepts, and a mini soundtrack CD with 12 tracks. The art book quality surprised me — thick glossy pages, not the flimsy paper some publishers cheap out on. The steelbook itself is sturdy with a magnetic closure that feels premium on a shelf.

What you *don't* get: any digital soundtrack download code. That's a minor annoyance if you want the music on your phone, but the physical CD works fine in a car or PC drive.

## Performance and the New Battle System

On PS5, the game is a technical improvement over the previous *Trails through Daybreak* entry. Load times are 3-4 seconds fast-traveling between zones, and the 60fps performance mode holds steady even during heavy combat animations with multiple enemies on screen. The graphics won't blow away a *Final Fantasy XVI* player, but the anime-style character models are clean and expressive.

The big mechanical shift is the ZOC (Zone of Control) system, which replaces the old turn-order grid. Enemies now have visible attack ranges on the field, and you can trigger preemptive strikes by hitting them from outside their aggro zone. It sounds minor, but it dramatically speeds up trash mob encounters — a standard fight now takes 20-30 seconds instead of a minute-plus. Boss battles still demand the full tactical suite: positioning, elemental weaknesses, and the new "EX Chain" linked attacks that reward proper party synergy.

I'll be honest: the difficulty curve is forgiving on Normal, almost too much so. You can brute-force most story fights by over-leveling through side quests. Hard mode is where the strategy actually matters, and I'd recommend starting there if you've played a Trails game before.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Across the 229 Amazon ratings (4.6 stars), the consistent theme is the story and world-building. One reviewer put it well: *"The character writing is the best in the series — Van and the new cast actually feel like friends by the end, not just quest-givers."* Another noted, *"The Deluxe extras are worth the price difference. The art book alone is better quality than most collector's editions I've bought."*

The pacing also gets regular shoutouts. This entry front-loads the action — you're in your first major dungeon within two hours — a stark contrast to the notoriously slow openings of earlier Trails games. That's a smart move for newcomers, even if veterans might miss the leisurely town-exploration vibe.

## Where It Falls Short

The English localization is serviceable but uneven. Some dialogue leans hard into clunky anime-isms ("I won't lose to the likes of you!"), and there are moments where a character's name is spelled differently across two text boxes. It's not game-breaking, but for a series known for its dense lore, precision matters.

The biggest hurdle is accessibility. While marketed as a soft entry point, *beyond the Horizon* directly continues plot threads from *Daybreak* and references events from *Reverie*. If you haven't played those, the opening chapters will occasionally reference characters and conflicts you've never met, with no codex or glossary to fill in the gaps. A "previously on" recap would have solved this — its absence is a real oversight.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Longtime Trails fans** — This is essential. The Deluxe physical extras are worth owning, and the ZOC system refreshes combat without losing the series' identity.
- **JRPG players who've been curious about Trails but scared of the backlog** — You can start here, but accept that you'll be googling lore references. The story still works as a standalone thriller.
- **Collectors** — The steelbook and art book are genuinely high-quality. At typical Deluxe pricing, you're paying maybe $20 extra over the standard edition for them, and that's fair value.

**Skip it if** you're a pure action-combat JRPG fan (this is still turn-based at its core) or if you want a self-contained story with zero homework.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to play previous Trails games first?**
A: Not strictly, but it helps enormously. The game references *Daybreak* and *Reverie* directly. If you're a newcomer, start with *Trails through Daybreak* — it's the true beginning of this story arc. You'll enjoy *beyond the Horizon* more with that context.

**Q: How long is the main story?**
A: Expect 50-60 hours for the main path, and 80-90 if you complete side quests and hunt all the optional bosses. The Deluxe Edition doesn't add any exclusive content; the game is identical to the standard version.

**Q: Is the steelbook edition region-locked?**
A: The PS5 version is region-free for physical discs, but DLC must match your PSN account region. If you import, buy the version that matches your account's region to avoid issues.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a Trails veteran wanting the definitive physical package, or a JRPG fan ready to commit to one of the genre's most rewarding sagas. **Skip it if** you demand a standalone story or can't tolerate turn-based combat.

The Deluxe Edition earns its 4.6/5 rating through quality extras and a strong game underneath them. It's not a perfect package — the localization hiccups and newcomer-unfriendly lore are real flaws — but for its target audience, this is the best way to experience one of 2025's most substantial RPGs.
