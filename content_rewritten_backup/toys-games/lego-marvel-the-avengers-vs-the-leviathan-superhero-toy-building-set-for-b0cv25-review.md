Let’s be blunt: the LEGO Marvel "Avengers vs. The Leviathan" set (set number 76260) is a love letter to the 2012 *The Avengers* film, and it knows exactly who it’s for. With a stellar 4.8 out of 5 stars from over 240 verified buyers, this isn't a controversial set. But is it a *must-buy* for your collection, or just a nostalgia trip? I’ve dug through the specs, the build, and the buyer chatter to give you the straight answer.

## What’s Actually in the Box

You’re getting a 347-piece kit that builds two things: the massive Chitauri Leviathan creature and a compact Quinjet. The Leviathan is the showstopper here—it spans over 15 inches long when assembled, with articulated jaw, tail, and side fins that let you pose it mid-attack. The Quinjet is smaller (fits about two minifigures), but it has a snug cockpit and two flick missiles.

The minifigure lineup is the real draw for collectors: **Thor, Hawkeye, Black Widow, and Loki**. You also get a Chitauri foot soldier. These aren't exclusive variants, but the prints are sharp, and Thor has his classic 2012 outfit—a detail long-time fans will appreciate.

## Build Quality and Real-World Play

Let’s talk about the elephant in the room: the Leviathan’s scale. At 15 inches long, it’s imposing on a shelf, and the brick-built design uses some clever SNOT (Studs Not On Top) techniques to get that smooth, organic underbelly. The jaw opens wide enough to swallow a minifigure whole, which is exactly the kind of play feature that keeps kids engaged.

The Quinjet is the weaker of the two builds. It’s a bit stubby compared to the sleek film design, and the wings don’t fold—they’re fixed. That said, the flick missiles are satisfyingly punchy, and the build time for the whole set is around 45 minutes to an hour.

One thing to note: the Leviathan’s tail segments are connected by ball joints, which are sturdy but can pop off if a younger kid (under 6) yanks on them aggressively. For ages 8 and up, it’s solid.

## What Buyers Are Saying (The Good)

The Amazon reviews are overwhelmingly positive, and the praise clusters around a few specific points:

- **"Great value for a Marvel set"** — multiple buyers noted that for under $30, you get a big display piece and four minifigures. That’s a strong price-to-piece ratio.
- **"The Leviathan is huge!"** — several reviewers were surprised by the physical footprint. It’s not a small set, and it commands shelf space.
- **"Perfect for a 6-year-old who loves Avengers"** — parents consistently mention that it’s a manageable build for younger builders with some help, and the play value (eating minifigures) is high.

## Where It Falls Short (The Honest Truth)

No set is perfect, and this one has two clear tradeoffs.

**First, the Quinjet is underwhelming.** It’s a display piece, not a great playset. The wings don’t move, there’s no landing gear, and it’s proportionally a bit off. If you’re buying this *just* for the jet, you’ll be disappointed.

**Second, the minifigure selection is missing Captain America and Iron Man.** That’s a deliberate choice to keep the price down, but it means you can’t recreate the full team lineup without buying another set. For kids who want "all the Avengers," this is a noticeable gap.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The Marvel completionist:** If you collect 2012-era Avengers sets, this is a no-brainer.
- **Parents of 6- to 10-year-olds:** It’s a big, impressive playset that isn’t a thousand-piece slog to assemble.
- **Display builders:** The Leviathan is a striking centerpiece for a shelf or desk.

**Skip it if:** you’re a purist for minifigure completeness (you’ll want a different set), or if you primarily want a Quinjet—there are better, dedicated Quinjet sets out there.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Does the Leviathan connect to other LEGO Marvel sets?**
A: No. It’s a standalone build. There are no connection points or modular compatibility with the larger 2012 Avengers Tower set.

**Q: How long does the build take?**
A: For an adult, roughly 40–50 minutes. For a kid aged 8–10, expect 1.5 to 2 hours. It’s broken into three numbered bags.

**Q: Are the stickers a pain?**
A: There are only a few—mostly on the Quinjet’s cockpit and the Leviathan’s eyes. They’re forgiving to apply, so no need to stress.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** you want the most imposing Leviathan LEGO has made at a reasonable price, or you’re shopping for a young Marvel fan who loves big, posable creatures.

**Skip it if:** you need a full Avengers lineup or a high-quality Quinjet. This set serves the monster, not the jet.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Leviathan is the hero here, and it delivers. The Quinjet is a placeholder, but the overall package is a solid, fun build with high play value. For the price, it’s one of the better Marvel sets on the market right now.
