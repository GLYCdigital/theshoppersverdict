# Magic: The Gathering – Final Fantasy Play Booster Box: The Crossover You Didn’t Know You Needed

When Wizards of the Coast announced a *Final Fantasy* crossover for Magic: The Gathering, the internet collectively lost its mind. And after cracking open a Play Booster Box, I get it. This isn’t just a lazy reskin with a few iconic characters slapped on cards — it’s a genuinely thoughtful fusion of two beloved franchises that rewards both longtime MTG players and Final Fantasy fans diving in for the first time.

## What You Actually Get

The Final Fantasy Play Booster Box contains 30 Play Boosters, each with 14 cards. That’s 420 cards total, including:

- **1–4 Rare or Mythic Rare slots** per pack (with a chance at a special showcase treatment)
- **Full-art basic lands** themed around Final Fantasy regions
- **Double-sided tokens** featuring classic FF summons and monsters
- **A Collector Booster sample pack** in some boxes (check the packaging — it’s not guaranteed)

As shown in the product image, the box itself is sturdy with gorgeous cover art depicting a mashup of iconic characters. The pack wrappers feature distinct FF job class art, which is a nice touch for collectors.

## Performance and Build Quality

Let’s talk about the cards themselves. The cardstock quality is noticeably thicker than standard MTG printing from a few years ago — these won’t curl or warp as easily. The foils are vibrant without the “Pringle” bend that plagued earlier sets. Print registration is clean; I didn’t spot any misaligned text or blurry art across the full box.

Gameplay-wise, the set mechanics integrate Final Fantasy flavor into MTG’s framework seamlessly. The “Job Class” mechanic lets you build around character archetypes — a Black Mage deck plays differently from a Dragoon deck, and the synergy is genuinely fun to discover. It’s not just a collector’s item; these cards are actually playable in Standard and Commander formats.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this box has honest drawbacks:

- **No guaranteed chase cards** — You might open a box and get zero of the marquee characters (Cloud, Sephiroth, Tifa). That’s a real financial risk if you’re hunting specific cards.
- **Limited long-term value** — This is a Standard-legal set, meaning these cards rotate out of competitive play in about two years. If you’re investing purely for resale, you’re gambling on the casual market holding value.
- **Not a complete set** — 30 packs won’t get you a full collection. You’ll need multiple boxes or singles purchases to complete a playset of everything.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You’re a Final Fantasy fan wanting a gateway into Magic — this is the most accessible crossover set they’ve made
- You’re a Commander player — the legendary creatures and job class synergies are excellent for casual play
- You’re a collector who values art — the showcase treatments rival anything from recent Universes Beyond sets

**Skip it if:**
- You’re a competitive player solely focused on tournament staples
- You’re on a tight budget — at this price point, buying singles is more economical if you want specific cards
- You’re hoping for a guaranteed profit — sealed boxes are a gamble, not an investment

## FAQ: What Shoppers Ask

**Q: Is this box worth it for resale value?**
A: Short answer: Maybe. The set is popular, and sealed boxes historically gain value over time. But the Standard rotation is a headwind. If you’re flipping immediately, you might break even or take a small loss after fees. Hold it for 3–5 years if you want appreciation.

**Q: Can I play these cards in Commander?**
A: Absolutely. The legendary creatures and job class mechanics are designed with Commander in mind. Many cards are currently staples in casual EDH decks.

**Q: How do the pull rates compare to a standard MTG set?**
A: Better. The mythic slot appears in roughly 1 in 3 packs here versus 1 in 7 in standard sets. You’re getting more bang for your buck on rare pulls.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5 stars**

**Buy it if** you want a premium unboxing experience with strong art, generous pull rates, and genuinely fun gameplay. **Skip it if** you’re a competitive player chasing specific staples or a budget-conscious collector who prefers purchasing singles.

This is one of the strongest crossover sets Magic has produced. The Final Fantasy theme isn’t window dressing — it’s baked into the mechanics, the art, and the overall feel. At this price point, you’re paying a premium for the license, but you’re getting a quality product that delivers on its promise. Whether you’re a longtime Planeswalker or a chocobo-loving newcomer, this box is worth the trip to your local game store.
