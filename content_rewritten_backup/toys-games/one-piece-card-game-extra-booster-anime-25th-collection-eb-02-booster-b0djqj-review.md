**The One Piece Card Game Extra Booster Anime 25th Collection EB-0 isn't a typical booster. It's a love letter to the anime's 25-year history — and a calculated move by Bandai to keep the competitive scene affordable.**

If you've opened any One Piece Card Game product in the last year, you know the pattern: a new set drops, meta staples spike, and casual fans get priced out. EB-0 flips that script. Every card in this set is a reprint with new anime-inspired artwork, from the East Blue saga's Luffy moments to the manga's current Egghead arc.

**What's Actually in the Box**

A sealed box contains 24 packs, each with 32 cards: 4 commons, 4 uncommons, 2 foils (guaranteed), and 1 rare or better. The 4.3-star average across 215 Amazon ratings reflects mostly positive reception, but the variance is real. One buyer noted, "Got 3 alt-art Don!! cards in one box — felt like a huge win." Another grumbled about "pulling the same three commons in six consecutive packs."

**Real-World Performance and Build Quality**

Here's where I'll be more critical than the marketing suggests. The card stock is noticeably thinner than previous sets like OP-08 or OP-09. After a few weeks of deck shuffling, I noticed slight curling on foil cards — something that's become a known issue with this print run. If you're a sleeve-everything player, you'll be fine. If you're a raw collector, factor in the cost of quality sleeves.

The artwork is the headline. Bandai used actual anime frames and key animation stills for the first time, and it shows. The Luffy vs. Lucci (EG-03) alternate art is genuinely striking, with the kind of dynamic composition that looks great in a binder. The reprint of OP01-R Law is the other chase card — it's been hovering around $15-20 in the secondary market, making this set a cost-effective way to grab a playset for competitive decks.

**What Buyers Keep Praising**

The consistent feedback highlights two things: the guaranteed foil rate and the art selection. "Two foils per pack feels generous compared to standard boosters — I pulled the alt-art Nami in my first box," wrote one verified buyer. Another praised the set's accessibility: "As a returning player, being able to pick up staple cards with fresh art without paying premium prices was the whole reason I bought two boxes."

**Where It Falls Short**

Let me be direct: this is not a set for everyone. If you're a collector hunting new game mechanics or exclusive card numbers, skip this. There's nothing here that'll change the meta — it's all reprints. The 32-card pack structure also means you'll see a lot of duplicate commons across a full box, which hurts the opening experience. And that thinner card stock is a genuine quality concern that Bandai should address in future print runs.

**Who Should Actually Buy This**

- **Competitive players** building meta decks on a budget — this is your cheapest route to getting playsets of key staples with new art.
- **Anime fans** who want display-worthy cards without hunting singles — the art alone justifies the price.
- **Casual collectors** who enjoy opening packs for fun rather than flipping value.

Skip it if you're a sealed-collector looking for long-term investment value or a player expecting new cards to refresh the format.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are the cards tournament-legal?**
A: Yes. All reprints are legal in standard One Piece Card Game formats. The card numbers have an EB-0 prefix, but game effects are identical to their original printings.

**Q: How many alternate arts are in a full box?**
A: Typically 3-4 alt-arts per box, including at least one alt-art Don!! card. That's better than most standard sets, which average 2-3.

**Q: Is this a good entry point for new players?**
A: Solid — but buy singles instead if you're building a specific deck. A box gives you variety, but the random commons won't get you a complete competitive list.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want premium art, need cheap reprints of meta staples, or enjoy the excitement of guaranteed foils per pack.

**Skip it if:** New mechanics, investment potential, or premium card stock are dealbreakers for you.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A generous, fan-pleasing set that delivers on art and affordability, held back only by thin card stock and a complete lack of new cards. For the price, it's a genuinely fun opening experience — just manage your expectations.
