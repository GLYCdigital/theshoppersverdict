**The tiny pen that writes like its big brother — but has one tradeoff you should know about.**

The Pilot G2 is a legend. It's the pen you've borrowed from coworkers, found in waiting rooms, and probably own three of right now. So when Pilot shrunk it down to a mini version, the question isn't *whether* it writes well — it's whether the smaller size ruins what made the original great.

**What's in the box**

You get a single retractable pen measuring about 4.5 inches when closed and roughly 5.25 inches posted. That's noticeably shorter than the standard G2's 5.5-inch closed length. The body is slim, with the same rubberized grip zone and a metal pocket clip. It uses the exact same 0.7mm fine point gel ink as the full-size version — which matters more than you'd think.

**Performance and build**

The writing experience is identical to a standard G2. Same smooth, dark black gel ink that dries quickly enough for lefties. Same 0.7mm line width that's fine enough for small planner boxes but bold enough for signatures. The retractable mechanism clicks firmly with no wobble. The clip is genuinely useful — I clipped it to a shirt pocket, a notebook cover, and a pencil case without any bending.

Here's the catch: posted, the pen's balance shifts noticeably toward the clip end. The short barrel means your fingers sit closer to the writing tip, which gives you more control but less leverage. For quick notes, grocery lists, and signing receipts, it's perfect. For writing a three-page letter, your hand will fatigue faster than with the full-size version.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't inflated. Buyers repeatedly mention the same wins:

- *"Perfect size for my planner — doesn't stick out of the pocket"*
- *"Same great G2 ink, just more portable"*
- *"The clip is solid, not flimsy like other minis"*

The portability angle is the real selling point. It fits in a jean's coin pocket, a passport holder, a small makeup bag, or the pen loop of a compact planner. For anyone who carries a bag smaller than a tote, this is the answer.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest issue isn't the size — it's the refill situation. Pilot doesn't sell the mini G2 refill separately. When the ink runs out, you're buying a whole new pen. At roughly $3-4 per pen, that's not a dealbreaker, but it's wasteful compared to the standard G2's refillable design.

The other downside: if you have larger hands, this pen will feel cramped. The grip zone is about 2.5 inches from the tip, which works fine for most adult hands, but anyone who wears XL gloves will find it uncomfortable for extended writing.

**Who should buy this**

- **Planner enthusiasts** who need a pen that lives in their planner without adding bulk
- **Pocket-carriers** who want a dependable pen in a coin pocket or small bag
- **Students** who need a backup pen that fits in a pencil case without rattling around
- **Anyone who likes the G2 ink** but wishes the standard size was more portable

**Skip it if** you have large hands, write long-form content regularly, or want a pen you can refill instead of replace.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it use the same ink as the full-size G2?**
Yes. Same 0.7mm gel ink, same smooth performance. Only the barrel length differs.

**Q: Will it fit in a standard pen loop?**
Most standard pen loops, yes. It's slim enough for most planners and notebooks.

**Q: How long does the ink last compared to a full-size G2?**
Shorter, because the cartridge is proportionally smaller. Expect roughly 60-70% of the full-size writing life.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want the G2's legendary writing performance in a genuinely pocketable size. **Skip it if** you need a long-writing pen or prefer refillable options.

The Pilot G2 Mini isn't a gimmick — it's a practical adaptation of a classic. The balance tradeoff is real, but for its intended use — quick notes, planners, on-the-go writing — it's hard to beat. At 4.6/5, it earns its rating.
