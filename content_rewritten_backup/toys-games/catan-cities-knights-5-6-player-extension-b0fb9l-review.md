**Catan Cities & Knights 5-6 Player Extension Review: Does It Fix the Wait?**
---

**The Short Version**

If you've played Catan with five or six people, you know the pain: the board feels cramped, resources move at a crawl, and the game drags. This extension doesn't just add seats—it rebalances the entire experience. But here's the catch: it's not a standalone game. You need both the base *Catan* and the *Cities & Knights* expansion already. If you have those, this is a no-brainer. If you're new, buy the base game first.

---

**What's Actually in the Box**

The extension includes:

- 2 complete color sets (red and white) with 4 settlements, 4 cities, 4 roads each
- 2 more dice (one yellow, one red—why? I'll explain below)
- A full set of *Cities & Knights* components: progress cards, city walls, metropolis pieces, merchant tokens, and the barbarian ship
- 2 additional "event" cards for the barbarian attack sequence
- The all-important "Building Costs" and "Development Cards" reference sheets scaled for 5-6 players

Everything matches the quality of the base game—thick cardboard, crisp printing, and colors that don't fade into each other. The red and white pieces are distinct enough that even colorblind players can tell them apart, which is more than I can say for some other expansions.

---

**How It Plays: The 5-6 Player Difference**

Here's the thing about *Cities & Knights* with 5-6 players: it's not just "more of the same." The game fundamentally changes.

**The Critical Rule Change:** In the standard 5-6 player extension, you play on a *larger* board. Not here. The board stays the same size. Instead, the game introduces a "special case" phase: after each player's turn, the next player in rotation gets a brief "build phase" where they can build roads, settlements, or buy development cards—but *not* roll dice or trade.

This keeps the game moving. With six players, you're not sitting idle for 15 minutes waiting for the dice to come back around. You're constantly making micro-decisions. It's a clever fix, but it adds a layer of complexity that casual groups might find fiddly.

**The Barbarian Threat Scales:** With more players, the barbarian ship gets scarier. The event cards are shuffled differently, and the barbarian strength increases faster. In our six-player games, the first barbarian attack usually happens by turn 3-4, not turn 5-6. This forces early investment in knights—which means fewer resources for city upgrades. It's a tighter, more aggressive game.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**It's Expensive for What You Get.** The price point is steep for two color sets and some cards. If you already own the 5-6 player extension for the *base* game, you'll see a lot of redundant components. The dice, for instance—you get two new dice, but you'll only use one. The other is a spare. That feels like padding.

**The Learning Curve Doubles.** *Cities & Knights* is already the most complex Catan expansion. Adding the 5-6 player build phase on top makes it significantly harder to teach. If your group has casual gamers, expect a 45-minute rules explanation before the first real turn. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"This is not a gateway game. It's for people who already know Catan backwards and forwards."*

**The "Special Case" Phase Can Be Gamed.** The build phase between turns lets players react to trades and robber placements in real-time. Experienced players will use this to block opponents' strategies mid-turn. It's a double-edged sword: it adds depth, but it can feel punitive if you're on the receiving end.

**Who Should Buy This**

**Buy it if:**
- You regularly play with 5-6 people and own *Cities & Knights* already
- Your group is comfortable with complex rules and doesn't mind a longer game (expect 90-120 minutes per session)
- You enjoy aggressive, tactical play where every turn matters

**Skip it if:**
- You're a casual Catan player who plays once every few months
- Your group prefers the simpler base game without the barbarian mechanic
- You're on a budget—consider the base 5-6 player extension first if you don't own it

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Do I need the base *Catan* and *Cities & Knights* to use this?**
A: Yes. This is an expansion of an expansion. You need the base game, the *Cities & Knights* expansion, and this 5-6 player extension. It won't work standalone.

**Q: Does this make the game longer?**
A: Yes, but not as much as you'd think. The build phase between turns keeps the pace up. Expect 15-20 extra minutes compared to a 4-player game, not 30-40.

**Q: Is it worth the money if I already have the 5-6 player base extension?**
A: The components are different—this one is specifically for *Cities & Knights* rules. You can't substitute the base extension's pieces. If you want 5-6 player *Cities & Knights*, you need this.

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 4.6/5 (★★★★★)**

This is a well-crafted extension that solves a real problem—the downtime issue—with an elegant mechanic. The component quality is excellent, and the game balance holds up at 5-6 players. The downsides are the price point and the steep learning curve.

**Buy it if:** You're a committed Catan group that plays regularly with 5-6 people and wants the full *Cities & Knights* experience.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual player or your group isn't ready for the complexity. Stick with the base game and its simpler 5-6 player extension.

For what it is—a niche expansion for dedicated fans—it's the best option on the market. There's simply no competition at this level of quality.
