Let me be direct: Sony's PS VR 2 is a fantastic headset with one glaring flaw — the stock comfort system. After 45 minutes, that front-heavy design starts punishing your forehead. The Globular Cluster CMP2 is the most popular aftermarket fix, and it's earned its 4.7-star rating from 235 buyers. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The CMP2 kit ships with two complete pad sets (a breathable fabric option and a smooth leatherette option), a top strap, a rear counterweight pouch with weighted inserts, and all the mounting hardware. You get genuine choice here — the fabric pads are cooler for sweat-inducing games like *Beat Saber*, while the leatherette feels more premium for cinematic titles. That's not typical for third-party mods, which usually force one material on you.

## Performance in Real Use

The headline feature is the counterweight system. The PS VR 2's center of gravity sits too far forward, which is why your forehead takes the abuse. The CMP2's rear pouch holds ~200g of adjustable weight that shifts the balance point backward. The difference is immediate — the headset stops wanting to tilt forward, and the pressure on your brow bone drops significantly.

The top strap is the unsung hero. It anchors the halo at the correct vertical height, which directly prevents the lens assembly from sliding down your face. That's what stabilizes the "sweet spot" — the crisp focal zone that drifts out of alignment with the stock strap during fast head movement. One Amazon reviewer put it well: *"I didn't realize how much I was subconsciously holding my head still until I played with this mod. Now I can look around naturally without the image blurring."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, the sweet spot stability — users report the image stays sharp through intense gameplay. Second, the pressure relief — multiple reviewers mention being able to play for hours without the "VR face" headache. Third, the material quality — the pads are dense, well-stitched, and don't compress into flatness after a month of use.

## Where It Falls Short

Installation is the biggest hurdle. You're attaching hook-and-loop strips to a $550 headset, and it takes careful alignment to get the pads positioned correctly. Plan for 15-20 minutes and a steady hand. The counterweight also makes the headset more awkward to store — it won't sit flat in the stock case without removing the pouch. And honestly, if the PS VR 2's halo just doesn't match your head shape, this mod improves things but won't magically make it perfect.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Long-session players** who game for 2+ hours and feel forehead fatigue — this is the single best upgrade you can make
- **Sweet spot perfectionists** who hate re-adjusting the headset mid-game
- **Owners with larger heads** who find the stock pads too narrow and pressure-pointy

Skip it if you only play in 20-minute bursts or if the stock headset already fits you comfortably — it's $40 you don't need to spend.

## FAQ

**Will this void my PS VR 2 warranty?**
No. The mod uses adhesive hook-and-loop strips that attach to the existing pads and frame. It fully removes without leaving residue, so your warranty stays intact.

**Does the counterweight make the headset feel heavy?**
You'll notice the added bulk initially, but it redistributes weight rather than adding net pressure. Most users report the total perceived weight feels *lighter* because it's no longer concentrated on your forehead.

**Can I use this with glasses?**
Yes — the pads actually create slightly more room around the eye area than the stock foam, which helps glasses wearers. Just make sure you're using the thinner pad set for the front.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You play PS VR 2 for more than 30 minutes at a time and want the image to stay sharper while your face stays happier.

**Skip it if:** Your stock fit is already comfortable and you're not experiencing sweet spot drift.

This is one of those rare third-party accessories that genuinely fixes a design flaw rather than just adding cosmetic flair. At 4.6/5, it's not perfect — installation and the storage bulk are real compromises — but for the majority of PS VR 2 owners, it's the difference between a headset you tolerate and one you actually enjoy.
