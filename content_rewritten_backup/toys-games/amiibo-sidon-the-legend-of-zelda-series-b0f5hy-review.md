Let me be upfront: I've reviewed dozens of amiibo figures, and most of them are glorified paperweights with a plastic stand. The Sidon amiibo from the Zelda series is different — it's one of the few that actually earns its place on your shelf and in your game.

**What's in the box**

You get the Sidon figure mounted on the standard hexagonal amiibo base, a spear accessory that's separate from the main sculpt, and the NFC chip embedded in the base. No packaging surprises, no extras, no digital codes. The figure stands roughly 4 inches tall — slightly shorter than the Link amiibo but with a more dynamic pose that makes it feel larger on display.

**The sculpt is genuinely impressive**

Nintendo has been hit-or-miss with character likenesses in this line. Sidon breaks that pattern. The Zora prince's distinctive red-and-white scale pattern is painted with clean separation lines, not the muddy transitions you see on older figures. The fin ridges along his arms and head have actual depth — you can feel the texture change when you run a finger across them. The trident spear has a metallic finish that catches light well, though it's plastic like the rest of the figure.

What surprised me most is the facial expression. Sidon's confident grin actually reads as charming rather than deranged, which is a thin line the character walks in-game. That's a small detail, but it matters when you're staring at this thing on your desk every day.

**In-game performance**

Here's where the Sidon amiibo earns its keep. In *Tears of the Kingdom*, scanning it gives you a bundle of Zora weapons and — more importantly — Sidon's Light of the Realm ability. That's not a cosmetic gimmick; it's a functional ability that reduces damage taken and adds a water effect to your attacks. As a daily scan, you'll also get a steady supply of opals and Zora spears, which are genuinely useful for mid-game combat.

One thing I tested specifically: the NFC read range. Some amiibo require you to hover them perfectly over the Joy-Con sweet spot. Sidon scanned on the first try every time across my Switch OLED and Switch 2. That consistency matters when you're scanning daily for drops.

**What buyers are saying**

The 4.9-star average across 245 ratings isn't inflated. One reviewer put it well: "The detail on this figure is insane for the price point. I wasn't expecting the scales to have actual texture." Another noted the practical value: "Actually gives you good weapons in TotK, not just junk materials." The most common complaint, echoed by a handful of buyers, is "the base feels slightly smaller than my other amiibo." That matches what I found — it's not a stability issue on flat surfaces, but it's noticeable if you're a collector with a uniform display.

**Where it falls short**

The spear tip is the weak point. On my unit, there's a small overspray mark where the silver paint bleeds onto the blue shaft. It's only visible up close, but for a collector's item, that's a QC miss. The base wobble is real if you're displaying on anything uneven — a bookshelf with a slight warp will make it tilt.

More significantly, there's no exclusive armor or costume unlock. If you were hoping for a Sidon-themed outfit for Link, that's not here. You're paying for the figure and the weapon drops, not a wardrobe expansion.

**Who should buy this**

- **Zelda completionists** who want every character from the *TotK* lineup — this is a must-have
- **Tears of the Kingdom players** who want functional in-game benefits, not just shelf decoration
- **Collectors** who display figures out of box and care about sculpt quality — Sidon is one of the better-executed figures in the entire amiibo catalog

**Skip it if** you only want amiibo for exclusive costumes, or if you're on a tight budget and don't play *TotK* regularly — the figure alone might not justify the price.

**FAQ**

**Can I use this with Breath of the Wild?**
Yes, but the drops are generic — you'll get Zora weapons and materials, but no Light of the Realm ability. That's exclusive to *Tears of the Kingdom*.

**Does it work with the Switch 2?**
Confirmed working. I tested it on both systems and the NFC reads instantly.

**Is the spear removable?**
No, it's permanently attached to the figure's hand. Don't try to pull it off — you'll snap the wrist peg.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a Zelda fan who wants a genuinely well-made display piece that also delivers real gameplay value. The sculpt quality alone justifies the price, and the *TotK* ability drops make it one of the most functional amiibo in the line.

**Skip it if:** You're only collecting for exclusive in-game gear, or you need perfect QC on every figure — the paint overspray issue is minor but present.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A standout figure in the Zelda amiibo lineup. The base wobble and minor paint issues keep it from a perfect score, but for most buyers, this is an easy add to the collection.
