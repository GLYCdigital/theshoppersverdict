**The Arcade1Up Pac-Man Head-to-Head Arcade Table is the closest you'll get to a real cocktail cabinet without spending $2,000 on a vintage machine.** It's a proper 31.5-inch-tall table with a 17-inch screen lying flat between two players. This is not a toy — it's a piece of furniture that happens to play video games.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the table cabinet, two stools, a riser base, and all required hardware. The 17-inch LCD display runs at the original 4:3 aspect ratio, which matters more than most shoppers realize. Arcade games were designed for square-ish screens; widescreen versions stretch Pac-Man into a bloated yellow balloon. Arcade1Up got this right.

The control deck features genuine arcade-style joysticks and six action buttons per player — not the mushy rubber pads you find on $50 mini-arcades. The buttons have satisfying microswitch clicks. The riser adds 10 inches of height, turning the table into a bar-height setup for standing play or tall stools.

## Real-World Performance

**The 12-game library is the headline.** You get Pac-Man, Pac-Man Plus, Super Pac-Man, Galaga, Galaxian, Dig Dug, Rally-X, New Rally-X, Mappy, King & Balloon, and two others. Every game runs at 60fps with accurate sound emulation. The joysticks are responsive — no noticeable input lag when dodging ghosts in tight corridors.

Battery-free operation (plug-in only) means consistent performance, but it also means you're tethered to an outlet. The unit draws about 30 watts, so it's not a power hog.

## What Buyers Praise

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings reflects genuine satisfaction. One verified buyer wrote: *"My kids (9 and 12) and I played for 3 hours the first night. It's built like a real arcade cabinet, not a toy."* Multiple reviewers highlight the two-player experience as the differentiator. Unlike a standard upright cabinet where one person watches, the head-to-head layout puts both players in the action simultaneously.

## Where It Falls Short

**Assembly is the most consistent complaint.** The wire harness routing for player two's controls runs through tight channels, and the manual's diagrams are small and occasionally ambiguous. Set aside 90 minutes and have a Phillips screwdriver handy.

The single mono speaker is genuinely underwhelming. At maximum volume, the iconic Pac-Man "waka-waka" is audible but thin — no bass, and it struggles against a running TV or dishwasher. Headphone users are out of luck; there's no jack.

The included stools are the weakest component. They're stable enough, but the seat height suits kids and average-height adults. If you're over 6 feet, you'll want to buy taller stools separately.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you want a genuine two-player arcade experience in a living room or game room, especially if you're nostalgic for the 1980s cocktail-table format. It's ideal for families with kids aged 8 and up, or for couples who want a head-to-head gaming option that isn't another screen on the couch.

**Skip it if** you're a solo player (an upright cabinet is cheaper and takes less floor space), or if you need modern HDMI-out and wireless audio — this unit has neither.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it plug in or run on batteries?**
A: AC adapter only. No battery option. You'll need to be within 6 feet of an outlet.

**Q: Can I add more games later?**
A: No. The game list is fixed at 12 titles. There's no USB port or SD card slot for adding ROMs.

**Q: Is it real wood or particleboard?**
A: It's laminated MDF (medium-density fiberboard) with printed graphics. It's sturdy and looks good, but it's not solid wood — don't expect heirloom furniture quality.
