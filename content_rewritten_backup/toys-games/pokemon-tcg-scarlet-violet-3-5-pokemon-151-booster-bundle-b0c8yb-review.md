# Pokémon TCG Scarlet & Violet 3.5 Pokémon 151 Booster Bundle Review

If you grew up with the original 151 Pokémon, this is the set you've been waiting 25 years for. The Pokémon 151 Booster Bundle is the purest way to chase that nostalgia — six packs, no extra accessories, just the cards. At a street price hovering around $30–35, you're paying roughly $5–6 per pack, which undercuts the $6.99 MSRP of individual packs. That's the math that makes this bundle the smart play for anyone planning to open more than a few packs.

## What You Actually Get

The bundle is deceptively simple: a black and blue cardboard box containing six booster packs of Scarlet & Violet 3.5 — the Pokémon 151 expansion. Each pack contains 10 cards, so you're looking at 60 cards total. No promo card, no coin, no booklet. The bundle exists purely for pack-opening efficiency, and that's exactly what it delivers.

## The Set Worth Chasing

Pokémon 151 is special because it covers the entire original Kanto Pokédex — all 151 creatures from Bulbasaur through Mew. The set includes 207 cards in total, but the real treasures are the special illustration rares. You've got the starter trio (Bulbasaur, Charmander, Squirtle) and their evolutions with full-art treatments, plus the legendary birds, Mewtwo, and Mew. The crown jewel is the Special Illustration Rare Charizard ex, which regularly sells for $150+ in near-mint condition.

What makes this set particularly fun to open is the pull-rate structure. The set was designed with an unusually high number of illustration and special illustration rares — roughly 36 in the main set. That means roughly one in every three to four packs yields something visually stunning. Compare that to other Scarlet & Violet sets where you might go six-plus packs between hits, and the 151 bundle feels like a much more satisfying rip.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.5-star rating across 225 Amazon ratings reflects a generally thrilled customer base. One reviewer put it simply: "Best set Pokémon has released in years. The art on the illustration rares is incredible — worth buying multiple bundles just to chase the Charizard." Another noted the practical value: "Cheaper than buying packs individually. I got two hits out of six packs and was happy with that."

The recurring theme is that buyers appreciate the set's design and the bundle's value proposition. The nostalgia factor is real — this set resonates with adults who grew up in the late '90s and are now in their 30s with disposable income.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, there's no guaranteed hit. You can absolutely open six packs and get nothing but reverse holos. It happens. The odds on 151 are better than most modern sets, but they're still odds.

Second, the card quality is thinner than vintage Pokémon cards. The 151 set uses the same card stock as other Scarlet & Violet-era products, which means cards can show edge wear and surface scuffs if you're not careful. If you pull a chase card, double-sleeve it immediately.

Third, the $30–35 price point is a bit deceptive. It's great value per pack, but you could spend $120 on four bundles and still not pull the Charizard. This is gambling-adjacent — set a budget and stick to it.

## Who Should Buy This

The Pokémon 151 Booster Bundle is for three specific buyer types:

- **Returning collectors** (ages 25–40) who stopped playing after the original Game Boy games and want to reconnect with the franchise. This set is unapologetically aimed at you.
- **Investor-adjacent collectors** looking for sealed product with long-term appreciation potential. 151 is one of the most popular modern sets, and sealed bundles are likely to hold value.
- **Gift-givers** buying for kids aged 8–12 who are just getting into Pokémon. The set is beginner-friendly — no complicated mechanics, just iconic Pokémon.

If you're a competitive player looking for meta-relevant cards, skip this. The 151 set has almost no competitive staples. This is a collector's set, pure and simple.

## FAQ

**How many cards are in the Pokémon 151 set?**
The set contains 207 cards, including secret rares. The base collection is 165 cards — the original 151 Pokémon plus 14 additional cards featuring alternate forms and trainer cards.

**Are the pull rates better than other modern Pokémon sets?**
Yes. 151 has approximately 36 illustration and special illustration rare cards, which means you have a roughly 1-in-4 chance per pack of hitting something visually stunning. Most Scarlet & Violet-era sets have pull rates closer to 1-in-6 for comparable cards.

**Is the booster bundle better value than an Elite Trainer Box?**
For pure pack-opening, yes. The booster bundle gives you six packs at around $5–6 per pack. The Elite Trainer Box gives you eight packs plus accessories (dice, sleeves, condition markers) but runs $50–60 — roughly $7 per pack. If you only want cards, get the bundle.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're chasing nostalgia, collecting the Kanto starters, or want a sealed product with strong long-term value. The bundle is the smartest way to open this set.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget and can't afford to open six packs with no guaranteed hit. Or if you're a competitive player looking for playable cards — this isn't that set.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Pokémon 151 Booster Bundle delivers exactly what it promises: efficient access to one of the best-designed Pokémon sets in years. It's not perfect — card quality is mediocre and there's no guaranteed hit — but for the price per pack and the quality of the set itself, it's an easy recommendation for any collector with fond memories of the original 151.
