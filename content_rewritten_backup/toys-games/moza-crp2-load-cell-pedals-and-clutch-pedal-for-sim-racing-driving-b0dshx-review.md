# MOZA CRP2 Load Cell Pedals and Clutch Pedal for Sim Racing D: The Serious Sim Racer's Upgrade

Let's be blunt: if you're still braking with a potentiometer-based pedal set, you're leaving lap time on the table. The MOZA CRP2 pedals aren't a casual accessory — they're a genuine competitive tool. But they also demand patience, setup time, and a willingness to learn. Here's what you're actually getting for your money.

## What's in the Box

MOZA ships the CRP2 as a complete three-pedal set: brake with load cell, throttle, and clutch. The package includes the pedal plate, all three pedals, a USB connection cable, and mounting hardware for both rigs and floor setups. You'll also find the necessary Allen keys for adjustment — a nice touch that saves a trip to the hardware store.

As shown in the product image, the pedals feature full aluminum construction with a brushed finish. The pedal faces are machined with grip texture, and the entire assembly feels substantial when you pick it up. This isn't a flimsy entry-level unit.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The star of the show is the 100kg load cell brake. Unlike traditional pedals that measure pedal travel, the CRP2 measures pressure. This means you develop muscle memory for braking force, not pedal position — which translates to more consistent braking zones lap after lap.

The throttle and clutch use hall effect sensors rather than load cells. They're smooth and precise, but they don't offer the same pressure-based feedback as the brake. That's a tradeoff at this price point. The throttle especially feels slightly less responsive than I'd like in quick trail-braking transitions.

The pedal plate itself is rigid — no flex even under heavy braking. Mounting to a rig is straightforward, though the included hardware works best with MOZA's ecosystem. If you're using a different brand's cockpit, check bolt patterns before ordering.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews isn't accidental. Owners repeatedly highlight the build quality and braking feel. One verified buyer put it simply: "These pedals feel like they belong in a real car, not a gaming setup." Another noted, "The load cell brake completely changed how I approach corners — I'm consistently 2-3 seconds faster per lap after a week of practice."

The adjustability gets frequent mentions too. You can reposition pedal faces, change spacing, and adjust angle to suit your seating position. That flexibility matters for taller or shorter drivers who struggle with fixed-geometry pedals.

## Where It Falls Short

Setup is the biggest hurdle. The MOZA Pit House software is required for calibration, and the learning curve is real. Expect 30-60 minutes of trial and error before your braking feels natural. Multiple reviewers mentioned this — one described it as "frustrating at first, but worth the effort once dialed in."

The hall effect sensors on throttle and clutch are a cost-saving measure. If you're upgrading from high-end pedals like Heusinkveld or VRS, you'll notice the difference. The clutch bite point is adjustable, but it doesn't offer the tactile feedback of a dedicated load cell clutch.

Finally, these pedals are loud. The metal-on-metal construction transmits sound through rigs. If you're in an apartment with neighbors below, consider adding dampening material.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these pedals work with consoles?**
A: No. The CRP2 requires a PC for full functionality. If you're on PlayStation or Xbox, look at MOZA's console-compatible options instead.

**Q: Can I mount these to a desk or floor without a rig?**
A: Yes, but it's not ideal. The load cell brake needs firm resistance — a wheel stand or dedicated rig is strongly recommended.

**Q: How often do load cells need calibration?**
A: MOZA recommends recalibrating every few weeks or after significant temperature changes. It's a two-minute process once you know the software.
