The Lego Speed Champions 007 Aston Martin DB5 (76911) isn't just another brick-built car — it's a love letter to the most famous spy vehicle in cinema history. As shown in the product images, that iconic silver silhouette with the machine-gun headlights and ejector seat translates surprisingly well into brick form. But does it earn its shelf space next to the rest of your Speed Champions collection? Let's break it down.

**What's in the box**

You get 298 pieces, one James Bond minifigure in full tuxedo with a Walther PPK accessory, and the DB5 itself. The build uses the updated 8-stud-wide Speed Champions chassis, which is a significant upgrade over the older 6-stud cars — the proportions finally look like a real sports car rather than a chunky toy. The set includes printed pieces for the license plates (both BMT 214A and the rotating 4711-EA variant) and a clever gear mechanism under the hood that pops the passenger seat forward. No stickers for the bodywork — everything important is printed, which is a nice touch at this price point.

**Build experience and real-world play**

The build takes roughly 45 minutes to an hour. It's not a complex build, but there are a couple of clever techniques worth noting. The front fenders use curved slopes that capture the DB5's distinctive prow, and the rear haunches are built with stacked plates that give the car a surprisingly muscular stance. The ejector seat is the headline feature — flick a lever under the chassis and the seat launches forward with satisfying force. The rotating license plates work via a small knob on the underside, though it's fiddly to reach once the car is displayed.

In hand, the DB5 feels solid. The 8-stud width gives it real presence, and it rolls smoothly across surfaces. The Bond minifigure is excellent — double-sided head with a calm expression and sunglasses-wearing variant, crisp tuxedo printing with a red carnation, and a hair piece that sits perfectly. He fits snugly in the cockpit, though getting him in and out requires some careful maneuvering around the steering wheel.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Owners on Amazon are largely enthusiastic. One verified purchaser noted, "The ejector seat actually works and my son has launched Bond across the room at least twenty times." Another highlighted the display value: "This is the best-looking Speed Champions car I own. The silver bricks have a nice metallic sheen that photographs well." The minifigure quality gets consistent shout-outs, with multiple reviewers calling it "the definitive Bond minifig."

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. At 298 pieces, this is a smaller Speed Champions set — you're paying a premium for the Bond license. The fenders have visible gaps where the curved slopes meet the body panels, and the front end sits slightly higher than the real car's aggressive stance. The ejector seat mechanism, while fun, means the passenger seat can't be used for display with a second minifigure. And there's no Goldfinger or Oddjob included, which feels like a missed opportunity given the set's price point. One reviewer summed it up: "Great set, but I wish they'd thrown in a villain for the price."

**Who should buy this**

This is a target-rich environment for specific buyers. **James Bond completists** will want it for the minifigure alone. **Speed Champions collectors** should grab it to complete the 8-stud-wide lineup — it pairs beautifully with the 007 Aston Martin DB5 from the James Bond Aston Martin DB5 set if you want a garage scene. **Parents of Bond fans** (with parental supervision for the gun accessory) will find it a solid, non-electronic toy that encourages imaginative play. If you're purely a Lego technic or architecture fan with no attachment to the license, you can skip it — there are better value-for-piece-count sets elsewhere.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does the ejector seat actually work?* Yes — it's a spring-loaded mechanism that launches the seat forward with real force. Kids love it; just don't point it at anything fragile.

*Is this compatible with other Speed Champions sets?* Yes. It uses the standard 8-stud-wide chassis, so it matches the scale and build style of current Speed Champions releases. Display stands and road plates work without modification.

*Can you fit two minifigures in the car?* Only one. The passenger seat is occupied by the ejector mechanism, and there's no second seat mold included.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a Bond fan, a Speed Champions collector, or you want a genuinely fun, mechanically interesting build that displays beautifully.

**Skip it if:** You're on a strict per-piece budget, you want a more complex build, or you have no interest in the 007 license — the premium is real.

The Lego Speed Champions 007 Aston Martin DB5 earns a **4.6/5**. It's not the biggest or most complex Speed Champions set, but it's arguably the most characterful. The working ejector seat, excellent minifigure, and faithful silhouette make it a standout piece for any collection. Just know you're paying for the brand — and for Bond fans, that's worth it.
