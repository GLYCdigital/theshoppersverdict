# ARRMA RC Car Typhon GROM 4X4 223S BLX Brushless Small Scale Review

**The pocket rocket that punches way above its weight class — if you're ready for the speed.**

---

**What's in the box:** The ARRMA Typhon GROM 4X4 223S BLX arrives fully assembled and ready to run — just drop in a 2S LiPo battery (sold separately) and you're off. You get the buggy itself, a 2.4GHz radio transmitter, a Spektrum receiver, and the user manual. The body is already trimmed and painted, which saves beginners a frustrating hour with scissors and body mounts. At roughly 13 inches long and weighing just over 2 pounds, this is a true small-scale rig, not a toy-grade knockoff.

---

## The Real Story: Speed, Build, and Handling

The headline feature here is the brushless motor — a genuine 223S BLX system that pushes this little buggy to 40+ mph on 3S (or around 25 mph on 2S). That's **legitimately fast** for a vehicle this size. In practice, the acceleration is aggressive and the top end is genuinely thrilling for a small-scale car. The 4WD system delivers solid traction on dirt, gravel, and short grass, and the oil-filled shocks handle jumps surprisingly well for a vehicle in this price tier. The chassis is a proper aluminum-and-composite tub design, not a flimsy plastic tray, which gives it a reassuring rigidity when you're landing hard.

**Build quality note:** The Spektrum 223S ESC is a welcome upgrade over older ARRMA electronics — it's smoother at low throttle and has a decent punch on acceleration. Steering response is sharp, though the servo is the weak point. It's adequate for bashing but will struggle if you're running on high-grip surfaces with big tires.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs, because there are a few:

1. **Battery and charger are NOT included.** This is the biggest hidden cost. You'll need a 2S or 3S LiPo plus a charger, which adds $50–$90 to your total. Factor that in before you buy.
2. **The servo is the weak link.** At 40+ mph, the stock servo can feel vague and underpowered. Plan a $20–$30 upgrade if you're serious about racing or high-speed stability.
3. **Spare parts availability.** While ARRMA's parts support is generally good, small-scale GROM parts aren't as ubiquitous in local hobby shops as the bigger 1/8 and 1/10 platforms. Order online and keep a couple of common spares (wing mounts, shock shafts) on hand.

## Who Should Buy This

- **First-time RC buyers** who want real speed without jumping into a full-size 1/10 rig. The size makes it manageable in backyards and parks.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I really need a 3S battery for the full speed?**
A: Yes. On 2S, you'll see around 25 mph, which is still fun but nowhere near the 40+ mph claim. A 3S LiPo (around 5000mAh, 50C+) unlocks the full potential. Budget for one if speed is your goal.

**Q: Is this suitable for a child?**
A: With a 40 mph top speed, it's **not** a beginner toy for kids under 12. The brushless system is genuinely quick, and a hard crash at those speeds can cause injury or property damage. For younger kids, look at the brushed version instead.

**Q: How long does a charge last?**
A: Expect 10–15 minutes of hard bashing on a 2S battery, slightly less on 3S due to the higher power draw. Buy two batteries if you want a solid session — charging takes 45–60 minutes.

## The Verdict: Buy It or Skip It?

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely fast, durable small-scale basher that's ready to run out of the box and won't break the bank (just remember the battery/charger extra). The brushless system, solid chassis, and crash-tested durability make it one of the best values in its class.
