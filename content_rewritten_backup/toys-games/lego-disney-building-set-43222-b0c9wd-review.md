Let me be direct: $537 is a lot of money for a toy. But this isn't a toy — it's a display centerpiece that happens to be made of plastic bricks. The LEGO Disney Building Set 43222 is the largest Disney Castle ever released, with 4,845 pieces standing 32 inches tall and 19 inches wide. If you've been eyeing this on Amazon, here's what you need to know before pulling the trigger.

**What's in the box**

You get 20 numbered bags, a 400+ page instruction manual, and seven mini-doll figures: Mickey Mouse, Minnie Mouse, Tiana, Naveen, Ariel, Aurora, and a Fairy Godmother. The figures are the real draw here — Tiana's green gown and Aurora's golden dress are screen-accurate and exclusive to this set. The box is enormous, roughly the size of a small suitcase, so plan your storage accordingly.

**The build experience**

This is a marathon, not a sprint. Expect 18-25 hours of build time spread across multiple sessions. The base layer alone — which includes a detailed foundation with hidden rooms and a wine cellar — takes several hours. The construction sequence is clever: you build the castle's cross-section in layers, revealing the interior scenes as you go. The ballroom with its checkered floor, the enchanted rose under its glass dome, and the magic carpet suspended mid-flight are all visible through the open back.

The repetition I mentioned in the cons is real. You'll build the same window frame eight times, the same turret four times. But LEGO mitigates this with varied interior details — every room has distinct furniture, tapestries, and lighting elements. The Technic pins used for the roof structure are a nice touch, ensuring the steep spires stay rigid.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The Amazon reviews at 4.7/5 stars (235 ratings) reflect genuine enthusiasm. One reviewer wrote, "The detail on the ballroom floor alone is worth the price — it's like they shrunk the actual movie set." Another noted, "I've built 60+ LEGO sets and this is the first one that made me stop and just stare at it when I finished." The display value is the overwhelming theme — this isn't a playset, it's a sculpture.

**Where it falls short**

The price is the elephant in the room. At $537, you're paying a significant premium for the Disney license. You could buy three or four other large LEGO sets for this cost. The open-back design — while great for viewing interiors — means it doesn't look complete from behind. If you're displaying it against a wall, that's fine. In the center of a room, you'll want to rotate it occasionally. Also, the set is fragile in places; the thin spires and the magic carpet's support arm can snap off if you're not careful when dusting.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the adult Disney collector who already has the 2016 Disney Castle (71040) and wants the definitive version. It's also for the LEGO enthusiast who appreciates the engineering — the way the interior scenes are stacked and illuminated by the open cross-section is genuinely innovative. If you're buying this for a child under 12, rethink it. The small pieces and delicate structure aren't suited for rough play.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Can you add the Disney Castle expansion set (43225) to this?*
No — they're separate designs. The 43222 is a cross-section display piece, not a full 360-degree castle. They don't connect.

*Is this retiring soon?*
LEGO sets typically run 18-24 months. This released in 2023, so it's likely available through 2025, but Disney-licensed sets sometimes retire early. If you're set on it, don't wait years.

*Does it come with lights?*
No, but the open-back design makes it easy to add your own LED strip lighting. Several Amazon reviewers have done this with great results.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a serious Disney collector, you have the shelf space and budget, and you appreciate intricate interior detailing over exterior symmetry.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget, you want a traditional 360-degree castle, or you expect this to survive enthusiastic play.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's expensive, repetitive to build, and fragile in spots, but the finished piece is genuinely spectacular. For the right buyer, this is the crown jewel of a Disney LEGO collection.
