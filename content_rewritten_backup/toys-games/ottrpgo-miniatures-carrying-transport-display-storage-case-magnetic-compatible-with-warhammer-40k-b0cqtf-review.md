If you've ever opened a foam case to find your Chaos Lord's spikey bits snapped off or your Intercessor's gun barrel bent at a sad angle, you already know the pain this case is trying to solve.

The oTTRPGo Miniatures Carrying Transport Display Storage Case uses a magnetic tray system instead of traditional foam cutouts. You magnetize your bases, and the models stick to the steel-lined trays. Nothing touches your fragile bits. No pressure. No squished backpacks. It's a smart system that's becoming the standard for serious hobbyists, and this case executes it at a price point that undercuts the big names like Battle Foam or KR Multicase.

**What's in the box**

You get the hard-shell case itself, two or three removable trays (depending on the size variant you order — this review covers the "M" size), and a shoulder strap. That's it. You'll need to supply your own magnets, which is the one gotcha that trips up new buyers. More on that below.

The case exterior is a rigid ABS plastic with a subtle textured finish that resists scratches from sliding in and out of car trunks. The latch mechanism is a standard dual-clasp setup, and the handle folds flat against the body when not in use. The transparent lid is a genuinely nice touch — it's thick acrylic, not cheap flimsy plastic, and it lets you eyeball your army without unclasping anything.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The trays are the heart of this system. Each one has a steel surface that holds magnetized bases firmly. In practice, models stay put even if you tilt the case at a 45-degree angle or carry it by the handle with a bit of jostling. The tray edges are raised slightly, which helps prevent models from sliding off when you're pulling a tray out.

The padded interior walls do double duty — they cushion the outermost models and help keep everything snug when the case is closed. The top tray sits on support rails, leaving a gap below for taller models. That said, if you're running a vehicle-heavy army or anything with serious vertical presence like a Trygon or a Knight, you'll likely need to remove the top tray entirely. That cuts your capacity roughly in half.

For standard infantry — Space Marines, Necron warriors, Tyranid gaunts — the capacity is solid. The M size comfortably fits a 2,000-point infantry-heavy army, which is the sweet spot most players need for tournament travel.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 210 ratings (4.2 stars average), the recurring theme is relief at no longer breaking models. One reviewer wrote, "I used to dread packing my Blood Angels for game night. Now I just drop them on the tray and go. Nothing has broken in three months of weekly travel." Another noted the display window makes it easy to identify which army is in which case when you have multiple boxes stacked in a closet.

The magnetic system also earns points for flexibility. Unlike foam with pre-cut slots, you can arrange models however you want. Big bases, small bases, cavalry, characters on rocks — it all just works as long as you've magnetized them.

**Where it falls short**

The magnet situation is the biggest hurdle. The case does not include any magnets, adhesive steel strips, or washers. You'll need to buy your own — typically 3mm to 6mm diameter rare earth magnets and either superglue or green stuff to attach them to bases. That's an extra $15–$20 and an afternoon of prep work before the case is usable. If you're new to magnetization, there are plenty of tutorials, but it's a barrier that some casual players won't want to climb.

The handle is also a weak point. It functions fine for normal use, but it's hollow plastic and doesn't inspire confidence when you load the case to its full capacity. Several reviews mention the handle feeling like it's going to give way on heavy armies, though I didn't find reports of actual failures.

**Who should buy this**

- **Tournament players** who travel weekly and need reliable, quick-access transport
- **Hobbyists with fragile conversions** who are tired of foam breaking their custom work
- **Collectors** who want display storage that looks good on a shelf between games

Skip it if you're a casual player who owns one small army and doesn't want to bother with magnetizing bases, or if you primarily field large vehicles and monsters that exceed the tray depth.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do I need to magnetize my miniatures?**  
A: Yes. The case has zero foam cutouts. Without magnets on your bases, models will slide around. You need small rare earth magnets and glue. Budget for that extra cost and time.

**Q: Will this fit a 2,000-point army?**  
A: For infantry-heavy armies, yes. The M size handles roughly 40–60 standard infantry models depending on base size. Vehicle-heavy lists will require removing the top tray, which reduces capacity significantly.

**Q: How does this compare to foam cases?**  
A: Foam protects via compression; this protects via magnetism. Foam is ready to use out of the box but slowly degrades and can catch on fragile bits. This case requires setup but is faster to pack and never snags your models. The tradeoff is the upfront effort.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're ready to magnetize your bases and want a transport solution that won't break your fragile models, looks great, and speeds up packing for game night.

**Skip it if:** You're not willing to do the magnet prep work or you mostly field large models that won't fit the tray layout.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful case with a smart design, held back slightly by the hidden cost of magnets and a handle that could be beefier.
