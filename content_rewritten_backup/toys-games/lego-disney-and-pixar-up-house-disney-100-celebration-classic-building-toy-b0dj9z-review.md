**A Love Letter to the House That Flies**

Some LEGO sets are just builds. Others are emotional investments. The LEGO Disney and Pixar ‘Up’ House Disney 100 Celebration falls firmly into the second category—and it earns that distinction the hard way, through 1,942 pieces of meticulous design that capture the soul of a beloved film.

**What’s in the box**

You get the iconic Victorian house with its wraparound porch, the massive balloon lift system, and three minifigures: young Carl Fredricksen, young Ellie, and Russell in his Wilderness Explorer uniform. Notably absent? Dug and Charles Muntz. More on that in a moment.

The build itself is a masterclass in LEGO architecture. The house opens up like a dollhouse, revealing a living room with Carl’s worn armchair, a fireplace with a mantel, and tiny framed photos of Ellie—a detail that hit me harder than expected. The bedroom includes a small bed and nightstand. It’s compact but dense with personality.

**The balloon system: genius or gimmick?**

The roof’s balloon cluster is the headline act, and it delivers. Rather than one massive sheet of studs, LEGO splits the balloons into 11 color-coded sub-assemblies—red, orange, yellow, blue, and white in repeating patterns. This approach keeps the build interesting and creates a visually rich canopy that photographs beautifully. As shown in the product image, the balloons create a massive silhouette that dwarfs the house itself, which is exactly how it should look.

The tradeoff? Those balloons attach to the house via thin Technic pins and a central support frame. It holds fine for display, but kids who want to swoosh the house around will pop those connections repeatedly. This is a display piece, not a playset.

**What buyers consistently praise**

With a 4.9-star average across 245 ratings, the consensus is clear. One reviewer wrote, *“I bought this for my wife who grew up watching Up. She cried when she finished the fireplace. The detail is unreal.”* Another noted, *“The balloon build is repetitive but oddly meditative. I finished it in one evening.”* Several buyers specifically called out the minifigure quality—young Carl and Ellie have printed faces that capture the film’s animated style perfectly.

**Where it falls short**

Let’s be honest about the flaws. First, the missing characters. For a set celebrating Disney’s 100th anniversary, leaving out Dug—one of the most beloved Pixar characters ever—feels like a miss. You can buy Dug separately from other sets, but that’s an extra $15-20.

Second, the base is basic. Just a flat green plate with some studs and a small porch. The film’s Paradise Falls backdrop is nowhere to be found. For display purposes, you’ll want to invest in a custom base or build your own landscaping.

Third, the price. Expect to pay around $120-140 depending on the current Amazon listing. That’s steep for a set with no electronics, no moving features, and a fairly static build.

**Who should buy this**

- **Pixar completionists:** If you’re building a Disney LEGO collection, this is essential.
- **Display-focused adults:** The finished model is a conversation piece that looks fantastic on a shelf.
- **Gift-givers for kids 10+:** The build is engaging but not frustrating. Younger kids will struggle with the balloon assembly.

**Skip it if** you want a playset, want Dug included, or prefer LEGO sets with interactive functions like the Ideas line’s moving parts.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long does the build take?**  
A: Most adult builders report 3-4 hours. The balloon section alone takes about an hour.

**Q: Does the house actually “fly” via the balloons?**  
A: No. The balloons attach to the house, but there’s no mechanism to lift it. The connection is for display only.

**Q: Are there stickers or printed pieces?**  
A: Mostly printed pieces, which is excellent. There are a few stickers for the small details, but the minifigures and key elements are all printed.

**The Verdict**

The LEGO Up House Disney 100 Celebration is a beautifully crafted tribute to one of Pixar’s most emotional films. It’s not perfect—the missing characters and fragile balloon connections keep it from a perfect score—but for fans who want to build a piece of cinematic history, it’s a worthy investment.

**Buy it if** you want a stunning display piece with genuine emotional weight.  
**Skip it if** you need play features or plan to handle it roughly.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A must-build for Disney LEGO collectors, with minor compromises that don’t diminish the heart of the set.
