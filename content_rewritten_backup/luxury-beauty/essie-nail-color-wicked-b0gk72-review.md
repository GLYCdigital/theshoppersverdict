**The Vampy Red That Actually Lives Up to the Hype**

Some nail polishes look incredible in the bottle and turn into a streaky, patchy mess on your nails. essie's Wicked is not that polish. This deep burgundy — almost black-red in low light, clearly wine in sunlight — has earned its 4.6-star rating across 229 Amazon reviews for one simple reason: it delivers. As one buyer put it, *"This is the color I've been searching for — rich, dark, and somehow both elegant and edgy."* That's not marketing fluff; that's what this shade actually does.

**What You Actually Get**

The standard essie 0.46 fl oz bottle with the ProWide brush — the flat, fan-shaped applicator that's been essie's signature for years. No base coat, no top coat, no extras. Just the polish. But that brush is worth calling out specifically: it's wide enough to cover most nail beds in two strokes, which matters more than you'd think when you're doing your non-dominant hand at 11pm.

**Performance and Real-World Wear**

The formula is where Wicked separates itself from cheaper drugstore burgundies. It's dense and pigmented — genuinely one-coat coverage, though I'd still do two for that lacquered, glossy finish. The color shifts depending on light, which keeps it interesting: a near-black plum in dim interiors, a rich merlot in daylight. As another reviewer noted, *"Looks almost black until you step outside — then it's this gorgeous deep red."*

Wear time is solid for a non-gel polish. With a base and top coat, expect 5–7 days before tip wear becomes noticeable. Without them, you're looking at 3–4 days. That's standard for essie's core line, and it's consistent with what most buyers report.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The formula is thicker than other essie shades like Ballet Slippers or Fiji. That means it dries slower between coats — wait a full two minutes between layers or you'll drag the first coat. The deep pigment also stains. Skip a base coat and your nail beds will carry a faint reddish tint for days after removal. Neither is a dealbreaker, but both are real.

One more thing: this is a seasonal shade for most people. It's perfect for fall and winter, but if you're someone who rotates colors constantly, this might sit in your drawer half the year.

**Who Should Buy This**

Three buyer personas come to mind. The professional who wants a sophisticated, work-appropriate color that reads as polished rather than flashy. The fall/winter enthusiast who wants that classic oxblood or vampy look without spending on a luxury brand. And the person who's tried cheaper dark polishes that went on patchy — Wicked's formula quality justifies the slight price bump over drugstore options.

Skip it if you prefer jelly or sheer finishes, if you're impatient with dry times, or if you're looking for a bright red — this is decidedly dark, not festive.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**How many coats do I really need?**
Two for best results. One coat gives decent coverage but can look slightly uneven on longer nails. Two coats give that deep, glossy, professional finish.

**Does it chip quickly?**
With a good base and top coat, expect 5–7 days. Without them, 3–4 days. The thicker formula actually helps with chip resistance compared to thinner polishes.

**Is this true black or dark red?**
It's a dark burgundy — reads almost black in low light, clearly red in bright light or sunlight. If you want true black, look elsewhere. If you want a dark red with depth, this is it.

**The Verdict**

essie Wicked is one of those rare polishes that earns its reputation. The coverage is genuinely impressive, the color has depth that cheaper burgundies lack, and the brush makes application forgiving. The slow dry time and staining are real annoyances, but they're manageable with patience and a base coat.

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, sophisticated dark red that looks expensive and applies like a dream.

**Skip it if** you're impatient with dry times or prefer sheer, buildable formulas.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a shade this good forgives a lot of flaws, and most of those flaws are avoidable anyway.
