Let's be direct: most battery toothbrushes under $15 are barely better than a manual stick with a vibrating gimmick. The Oral-B Pro-Health Pulsar is different — but it's not without compromises. Here's what 229 buyers (averaging 4.6 stars) actually think after months of use.

**What you actually get**

This 2-pack ships with two blue handles, each fitted with soft, polished bristles. No charging base, no travel case, no battery included — just the brushes. You'll need one AA battery per handle, which will run roughly 3 months with twice-daily brushing. The "Pulsar" technology means the bristles themselves pulse (not the whole head vibrating like Sonicare models), which targets plaque at the gumline more effectively than manual brushing.

**Real-world performance**

The pulsing action is subtle but effective. Unlike sonic brushes that can feel aggressive, the Pulsar's soft bristles glide over sensitive gums while the micro-pulsations break up plaque. One buyer noted: *"I have receding gums and most electric brushes irritate them. This one actually feels soothing — my hygienist commented on how much cleaner my gumline looked at my last visit."* Multiple reviewers echo this — the soft bristles plus gentle pulse make it a solid pick for anyone with gum sensitivity.

The battery life claim holds up. With standard alkaline AAs, most users report 2-3 months before the motor noticeably slows. That's the catch: there's no power indicator. The brush just gets progressively weaker, so you might brush with a half-dead motor for a week without realizing it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- **Value**: Two brushes for under $12 (when not on sale) beats most single sonic units that cost $40-80.
- **Gum sensitivity**: The #1 recurring theme in reviews. Polished soft bristles don't shred gum tissue.
- **Simplicity**: No charging dock, no app, no brush head replacement costs. It just works.

**Where it falls short**

No timer is the biggest miss. The ADA recommends 2 minutes, and this brush gives you zero guidance. You'll need a separate timer or just develop the habit. Also, the brush head is permanently attached — once bristles splay (around 3 months), the entire handle goes in the recycling bin. That's more plastic waste than replaceable-head models. Finally, the AA battery requirement feels dated when most competitors offer rechargeable options.

**Who should buy this**

- **Budget-conscious brushers** who want electric benefits without the $50+ upfront cost
- **Sensitive-gum sufferers** who find sonic vibration too intense
- **Travelers** — grab one, toss in a AA, and you're covered for months without a charger
- **First-time electric users** testing the waters before committing to a premium model

**Skip it if** you need a timer, want replaceable heads, or expect sonic-level vibration. This is a gentle pulse, not a deep clean — which is precisely the point.

**FAQ**

**Q: How loud is it?**  
Quiet. Noticeably quieter than Sonicare units. You'll barely hear it over running water.

**Q: Does it actually remove more plaque?**  
Oral-B cites clinical data showing up to 2x more plaque removal vs. a manual brush. Real-world reviews support this, especially at the gumline.

**Q: Can I use it with braces or dental work?**  
Yes — the soft bristles and gentle pulse are safe. Several reviewers with braces confirmed no damage to brackets or wires.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want proven electric brushing benefits at a fraction of the cost, especially if your gums are sensitive. **Skip it if** you need a timer or prefer the deep vibration of sonic technology.

The Oral-B Pro-Health Pulsar earns its 4.6-star rating honestly. It's not flashy, but it delivers exactly what it promises: effective plaque removal with gentleness that pricier brushes often miss. At this price for a 2-pack, you can buy one for yourself and gift the second — no one will complain about a toothbrush that actually makes a difference.
