**The Short Version**

The MAYCHAO 15ML Jelly Gel Nail Polish is a solid entry in the transparent gel category, earning a 4.6/5 from 229 raters. It delivers the glass-nail look that's dominated salon feeds for two seasons, but it demands better-than-average prep work. Here's the full breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

One 15ML bottle of transparent jelly gel polish. That's it — no lamp, no base coat, no cleanser. The bottle itself is compact and sturdy, with a standard twist-off cap and a brush that's functional but not luxurious. At 15ML, you're getting about 20-25 full manicures if you apply thin coats properly. Compare that to the 18ML standard from brands like Gelish or CND, and you're sacrificing roughly 17% of product for a slightly lower price point.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The formula is where this polish earns its keep. It's a true jelly — translucent with a colored tint rather than an opaque cream. The self-leveling property is genuinely impressive: brush strokes disappear within about 20 seconds, which means fewer passes and less risk of disturbing the layer underneath. Viscosity sits in the sweet spot — thick enough to stay where you put it, thin enough to spread evenly without pooling at the cuticle.

In real-world testing, here's what you can expect:

- **One coat:** A subtle, sheer wash of color. Think "my nails but better."
- **Two coats:** A proper jelly look with visible translucency and depth.
- **Three coats:** The full glass-nail effect — glossy, dimensional, and almost candy-like.

Cure time under a 48W UV/LED lamp runs about 60 seconds per coat, which is standard for the category. Wear time across the 229 Amazon ratings consistently hits the 2-3 week mark without chipping, provided you cap the free edge properly.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The recurring themes in verified reviews point to the same strengths I found. One five-star reviewer noted, "Finally a jelly that doesn't look patchy — two coats and it's perfectly even." Another mentioned, "Removed cleanly with standard acetone wraps in about 9 minutes, no damage to my natural nails." The buildable coverage appears to be the standout feature — multiple reviewers mention the ability to dial in exactly how sheer or intense they want the color.

**Where It Falls Short**

The brush is the most common complaint. It's narrower than what you'll find on premium brands, which means wider nail beds require two passes per coat and careful attention to avoid uneven edges. If you're someone who struggles with precision application, this will test your patience.

The translucent formula also punishes poor prep. Any ridges, uneven base coat, or residual oil on the nail plate will show through. This isn't a product that hides mistakes — it amplifies them. Budget extra time for proper nail prep before your first coat.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **DIY enthusiasts** who already own a UV/LED lamp and want to expand their color library with a quality jelly option
- **Anyone chasing the glass-nail trend** without paying $40+ at a salon for the same look
- **Nail art hobbyists** who need a reliable transparent base for encapsulation or layering techniques

**Skip It If**

- You're a beginner who needs forgiving formulas — the narrow brush and translucent nature make this a frustrating first gel polish
- You prefer opaque, one-coat coverage
- You're looking for a full kit — this is just the color, not the whole system

**FAQ**

**Q: How many coats do I need for a full jelly effect?**
A: Two coats give you the classic jelly look. Three coats create a deeper, more saturated glass effect. Anything beyond three starts to look like a solid color rather than a jelly.

**Q: Will this work with any UV/LED lamp?**
A: Yes. It cures in 60 seconds under a 48W lamp. Weaker lamps may need up to 90 seconds per coat. Always check that the top layer is fully cured before touching anything.

**Q: Is it actually soak-off or do I need to file?**
A: It's genuinely soak-off. Wrap nails in acetone-soaked cotton for 8-10 minutes and the polish lifts cleanly. No aggressive filing needed to break the seal.
