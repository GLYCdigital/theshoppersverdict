# Colgate 09782 Toothpaste Review: The 240-Tube Bulk Buy That Makes Sense (and When It Doesn't)

Let's be honest — nobody wakes up dreaming about buying a case of 240 toothpaste tubes. But if you've ever paid $4 for a "travel-size" toothpaste at an airport newsstand, you already understand the pain this product solves. This is the unglamorous, practical workhorse of oral care logistics.

## What You're Actually Getting

This is Colgate's classic cavity protection formula in a 0.85oz (24g) tube. That's roughly 25-30 brushings per tube, or about two weeks of twice-daily use. The "unboxed" descriptor means these arrive as bare tubes — no individual cardboard boxes, no retail display packaging. The case contains 240 tubes total.

The formula itself is the familiar, minty, no-nonsense Colgate you've used for decades. Sodium fluoride at 0.24% (0.15% w/v fluoride ion) — the standard cavity-fighting concentration. Nothing fancy, nothing experimental. It does exactly what toothpaste should do.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The toothpaste itself performs identically to the full-size version. Good foam, clean rinse, that signature Colgate cooling sensation. No breakdown in quality just because it's mini-sized.

The real value here is cost-per-use. At bulk pricing, you're typically looking at roughly $0.30–$0.50 per tube depending on current Amazon pricing — versus $1.50–$3.00 for single travel tubes at retail. Over 240 tubes, that's serious savings if you actually use them all.

Buyers consistently cite three use cases: stocking emergency kits, outfitting office bathrooms, and prepping guest rooms. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I bought these for our vacation rental property — guests always forget toothpaste, and now we just restock from a drawer." Another mentioned using them for church donation drives and homeless shelter care packages.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's talk about the elephant in the room: 240 tubes is a *lot* of toothpaste. If you're buying for personal use, this will last you roughly 8-10 years. That's not a typo. Unless you have a business or organizational need, this quantity is overkill.

The unboxed packaging also means cosmetic imperfections. Multiple reviewers reported tubes arriving with slight dents or bent crimps from shipping. The toothpaste inside is fine, but if you're planning to hand these out individually, presentation matters and some tubes look a bit rough.

One more consideration: these tubes don't have the full ingredient label on every unit in some batches. If you have specific sensitivities or allergies, you'll want to check the case-level documentation before distributing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You manage a dental office, run a hotel or Airbnb, coordinate corporate wellness programs, volunteer with organizations serving unhoused populations, or simply want a decade of travel-size toothpaste at rock-bottom prices.

**Skip it if:** You're an individual who just wants a few travel tubes for an upcoming trip. You'll be better served buying a 3-pack of travel sizes at your local drugstore.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this TSA-compliant for carry-on luggage?**
A: Yes. At 0.85oz (24g), it's under the 3.4oz (100ml) limit. These are the exact size you want for airport security.

**Q: How long does one tube last?**
A: With twice-daily brushing, figure 12-15 days per tube. A family of four would use roughly 10-12 tubes per month.

**Q: Are these the same formula as full-size Colgate?**
A: Yes. Same active ingredients, same cavity protection. Only the tube size differs.
