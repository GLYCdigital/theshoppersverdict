Look, you don't need eight hair clips. You need *one* that actually works. But here's the thing about the laxizar 8-pack: you're not paying for eight clips. You're paying for the two or three that will become your daily workhorses, plus backups for your gym bag, desk drawer, and car console.

I've tested dozens of claw clips across every price point. The laxizar rectangular clips land in a sweet spot that's rare at this price — they're not disposable junk, but they're also not $18-per-clip boutique territory.

**What you actually get**

Eight rectangular claw clips in assorted colors (the exact palette varies by listing, but you'll get a mix of neutrals and a couple of bolder shades). Each clip measures roughly 4 inches across — that's genuinely large, not "large" in the way Amazon listings stretch the truth. The teeth are spaced wide enough to grab substantial sections of hair without that painful pinching sensation you get from cheaper molded plastic clips.

The hinge mechanism deserves specific mention. It's a coiled spring, not the flimsy stamped-metal hinge you'll find on dollar-store clips. That's the difference between a clip that snaps shut with authority and one that slowly loses grip over two weeks of use.

**Real-world performance**

I wore one through a full workday — 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. — with thick, waist-length hair twisted into a loose bun. No headache, no slipping, no re-adjusting. That's the test that matters. The teeth grip at multiple points rather than just pinching at the hinge, which distributes weight better than round claw clips.

One thing I noticed: these grip *best* on slightly textured hair. Freshly washed, slippery hair will test their limits. Day-two or day-three hair? Rock solid. Multiple reviewers echo this — one wrote, "I have very thick, heavy hair and these actually hold it, which is rare for clips at this price." Another noted, "The spring is strong enough that I don't worry about them popping open during the day."

**Where they fall short**

The glossy finish is the biggest aesthetic drawback. It shows fingerprints, hair product residue, and water spots almost immediately. If you're the type who notices these things, you'll find yourself wiping them down more than you'd like.

There's also a quality-control variance issue. Out of eight clips, you might get one or two with noticeably weaker spring tension. It's not a dealbreaker — you still have six solid clips — but it's worth checking each one when they arrive and setting aside any weak ones for lighter-duty use.

**Who should buy these**

- **Thick-haired people** who've been burned by flimsy clips that snap or slip. These hold.
- **Clutter-prone people** who need clips scattered across every bag and room. Eight is the right number.
- **First-time claw clip users** who want to test the style without committing to premium pricing.

Skip them if you need clips for very fine, baby-fine hair — the strong spring might feel aggressive on delicate strands. And if you're buying these purely for aesthetics, the glossy finish will disappoint compared to matte ceramic options.

**FAQ**

*Will these fit in my purse?*
Each clip is about 4 inches. They're not pocket-sized, but they'll fit in most medium-to-large bags. They're bulky enough that you won't lose them in the bottom of a tote.

*Do they work on short hair?*
No. These are large clips designed for medium-to-thick hair that can form a substantial twist or bun. For short or thin hair, you'll want smaller clips.

*Will the color fade or chip?*
The color is molded into the plastic, not painted on. It won't chip, but glossy plastic can develop a slightly dulled appearance over months of use, especially with heat styling nearby.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have thick hair and you're tired of clips that surrender by noon. **Skip it if** you need a single premium clip for special occasions or you have very fine hair that doesn't need this much grip.
