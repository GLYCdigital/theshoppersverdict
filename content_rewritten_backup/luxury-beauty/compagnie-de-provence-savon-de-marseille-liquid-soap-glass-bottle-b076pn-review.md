Let's cut to it: this is a $20+ hand soap in a glass bottle. You can buy a three-pack of Softsoap for that. So why do 220 Amazon reviewers average 4.4 stars? Because Compagnie de Provence isn't selling soap — it's selling the experience of washing your hands properly. And for a certain kind of buyer, that's worth real money.

**What you actually get**

The product is a 16.9 fl oz (500ml) liquid soap in a heavy glass pump bottle. The formula follows the traditional Savon de Marseille method: a base of 72% olive oil (the remaining 28% is water, soda, and salt — no palm oil, no synthetic surfactants). That's the same ratio that's been used in Provence since the 17th century. The pump mechanism is metal and plastic, and it's sturdy — no wobbly, cheap-feeling action. As shown in the product image, the bottle itself is clear glass with a minimalist label design; it looks like something you'd find in a boutique hotel bathroom, not under your kitchen sink.

**Performance and real-world use**

Here's where this soap earns its keep. The lather is rich but not sudsy-gimmicky. It rinses clean in about half the time of typical moisturizing soaps. The olive oil content means your hands genuinely feel softer after washing — not squeaky-clean-dry, not coated in lotion residue. One reviewer put it well: *"I have eczema on my hands and this is one of the only soaps that doesn't make it worse. My dermatologist asked what I use."* That tracks with the formula chemistry. Olive oil saponifies into a mild, glycerin-rich soap that respects the skin barrier.

The scent — I tested the verbena — is subtle and natural, like crushed lemon verbena leaves rather than a synthetic air freshener. It fades within minutes, which most buyers seem to prefer. The lavender version gets similar praise.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 220 ratings, three themes dominate. First, the glass bottle. Multiple reviewers mention refilling it with cheaper soap after the original runs out — the pump is that good. Second, the gentleness on sensitive skin. Third, the longevity. Despite the generous pump, a 16.9 oz bottle lasts most households 6–8 weeks of normal use.

**Where it falls short**

The pump is genuinely too generous. A single press delivers about 1.5 times what you need, and there's no way to adjust it. If you're not careful, you'll go through the bottle in a month. The price per ounce is the elephant in the room — this is a luxury item, and if you're looking for antibacterial action, it doesn't have it (that's fine for most everyday use, but worth knowing). Finally, the glass bottle, while beautiful, is a slip hazard in the shower if you use it there.

**Who should buy this**

- **The fragrance-sensitive** — anyone who hates overpowering hand soap scents
- **The skincare-conscious** — dry hands, eczema, frequent hand-washing (nurses, cooks, artists)
- **The aesthetic buyer** — you want your soap to look intentional on the counter

**Skip it if** you go through soap fast with a big family, or if antibacterial properties are a must.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this soap really made in Marseille?**
A: Yes, the brand is based in Provence and the soap is manufactured in France following the traditional Marseille method. The 72% olive oil content is the legal minimum to call it Savon de Marseille.

**Q: Does it work for body wash too?**
A: Yes, but be careful — the glass bottle is slippery when wet. Transfer to a plastic dispenser for shower use.

**Q: How does this compare to the original bar soap?**
A: The liquid version is more convenient and slightly milder due to added glycerin, but the bar is more economical per wash. The liquid is the better option for handwashing stations.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Buy it if you value skin comfort and countertop aesthetics over cost-per-wash. Skip it if you're shopping purely on price.**
