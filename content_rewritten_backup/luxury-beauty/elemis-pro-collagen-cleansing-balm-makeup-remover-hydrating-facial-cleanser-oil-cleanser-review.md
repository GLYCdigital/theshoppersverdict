**What's Actually in the Tub**

You get one 100g (3.5 oz) white tub with a pink lid. The balm itself is a solid, buttery texture that melts into a silky oil on contact with skin. The ingredient list is where things get interesting: padina pavonica (a Mediterranean algae known for its hydrating properties), optimega (a blend of essential fatty acids), and chia seed oil. **How It Performs: The 60-Second Test**

The real question: does it actually remove makeup? Yes — and impressively well. Warm a pea-sized amount between your fingers, massage it over dry skin, and watch waterproof mascara and long-wear foundation dissolve almost instantly. Add warm water, and it emulsifies into a milky cleanser that rinses clean without leaving that dreaded greasy film.

One Amazon reviewer put it perfectly: *"I've used this for years. It's the only thing that takes off my waterproof mascara without pulling out my lashes."* Another noted, *"My dry, sensitive skin has never felt so clean without that tight, squeaky feeling."*

**Where It Falls Short (Honestly)**

No product is perfect, and this balm has real tradeoffs. The most common complaint across reviews: eye irritation. If the balm gets into your eyes during rinsing, it stings — noticeably. You'll need to rinse with your eyes closed and be careful around the lash line. Another issue: the fragrance. It's light and pleasant (a subtle botanical scent), but if you have reactive skin, fragrance is always a gamble. Finally, the texture is temperature-sensitive — in cold bathrooms, the balm firms up and becomes hard to scoop. Warm it between your fingers first, or store it somewhere warmer.
