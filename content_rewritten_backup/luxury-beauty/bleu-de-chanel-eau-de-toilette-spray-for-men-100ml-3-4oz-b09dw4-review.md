The "blue fragrance" category didn't exist before this bottle. Chanel didn't just release a scent — they defined a genre that a dozen designers now imitate. But a decade later, does Bleu de Chanel EDT still hold up when you're dropping serious money on 100ml? Mostly yes, with caveats worth knowing before checkout.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the classic Chanel rectangular bottle — 100ml (3.4 oz) of clear glass with the magnetic cap that clicks shut with a satisfying snap. No box sleeve gimmicks, no samples, no travel atomizer. Just the bottle. The sprayer is excellent — fine mist, even coverage, no clogging. The magnetic cap is a nice touch that feels premium every time you use it.

**Performance and real-world wear**

The opening is a bright burst of citrus — grapefruit and lemon with a touch of mint. That lasts about 15 minutes before the mid emerges: ginger, nutmeg, and jasmine that give it that signature "clean but not soapy" character. The base is where Chanel earns its keep — sandalwood, cedar, and frankincense that sit close to skin with an amber warmth.

On my skin, I get 6–7 hours of wear. The first two hours project confidently — you'll notice it in a room. After that, it settles into a personal bubble that someone needs to be within arm's reach to smell. If you're an 8-hour workday person, expect to reapply after lunch. On clothes, it easily survives the day.

Here's the tradeoff: the EDT is the lightest of the three Bleu concentrations. The EDP adds more depth and lasts longer. The Parfum is densest. You buy the EDT for its versatility — it's the one that works in summer heat and office AC without choking anyone out.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews reflects a pattern. Compliments are the most common theme — multiple reviewers describe strangers asking what they're wearing. One buyer noted, "My wife bought this for me as a gift and now I can't wear anything else." Another praised the value: "Lasts all day at work and I only need two sprays."

The negatives cluster around two issues. First, longevity expectations — several reviewers report it fades faster than they'd like, especially compared to the EDP. Second, the price. This is a designer fragrance at designer pricing, and some reviewers felt the performance didn't justify the cost.

**Where it falls short**

Honest talk: this is one of the most-worn men's fragrances on the planet. That means you'll smell it in elevators, at gyms, and on your coworkers. Originality isn't its strong suit anymore. Also, the 100ml bottle is substantial — it's not travel-friendly, and the cap doesn't lock, so it can't go loose in a carry-on without leaking risk.

If you're a fragrance enthusiast with a curated collection, this might feel too safe. If you want one reliable scent that works everywhere, it's near-perfect.

**Who should buy this**

- The professional who needs one signature scent for meetings, dinners, and dates
- The gifting buyer — it's a recognized name with universal appeal
- Anyone new to fragrances wanting a safe blind buy with high compliment potential

**Skip it if** you want something unique, prefer beast-mode projection, or need all-day longevity from a single morning spray.

**FAQ**

**Q: How many sprays should I use?**
A: Three — one on each side of the neck, one on the chest. More than four sprays gets cloying because of the amber base.

**Q: Is this suitable for summer and winter?**
A: Yes. The citrus opening shines in heat, while the woody base holds its own in cooler weather. It's genuinely year-round.

**Q: How does it compare to the EDP?**
A: The EDP has stronger projection and lasts 2–3 hours longer but costs more. If longevity matters most, pay the difference. If versatility and value matter, the EDT is the smarter buy.
