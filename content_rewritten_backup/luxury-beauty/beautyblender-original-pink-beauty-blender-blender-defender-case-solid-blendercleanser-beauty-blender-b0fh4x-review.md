**The original pink sponge is still the gold standard — but this bundle makes you pay for convenience you might not need.**

If you've ever used a real Beautyblender, you know the difference immediately. The cheap drugstore knockoffs bounce like a stress ball and leave streaks. The genuine article — with its trademark "no skin left behind" density and that subtle egg shape — blends foundation into skin like it was airbrushed on. This listing pairs the cult-favorite Original Pink with two accessories: the Defender travel case and a solid version of the Blender Cleanser. At $30+ for the set, you're paying a premium. Here's what you actually get for it.

**What's in the box**

The bundle includes the Original Pink sponge (the classic, not the Pro or Micro version), a hard-shell Defender case with ventilation holes, and a 1.7-ounce solid Blender Cleanser bar. The case is the real differentiator here — it's a rigid plastic clamshell with a hinge that locks closed. The sponge sits in a cradle that keeps it suspended, so air circulates on all sides. That's the feature that prevents the moldy, mildewed sponge problem that plagues anyone who's ever thrown a damp Beautyblender into a Ziploc. The cleanser bar is fragrance-free, sulfate-free, and about the size of a travel soap — it'll last 2–3 months with daily use.

**Performance and build quality**

The sponge itself is identical to the standalone Original Pink you've been buying for a decade. Same latex-free, porous texture. Same bounce. Same ability to absorb water and triple in size. When you use it damp, it sheers out liquid foundation beautifully and doesn't soak up too much product. I tested it with a full-coverage matte foundation and a dewy tinted moisturizer — both applied streak-free in about 20 seconds. The pointed tip handles undereye concealer and the curved side works for cream blush.

The Defender case is genuinely useful if you travel. I tossed it in a carry-on with a damp sponge inside, and after 48 hours in a hot bag, there was zero odor and no mold spots. The ventilation holes work. But here's the catch: the case is roughly the size of a large egg or a small avocado. It won't lie flat in a normal makeup bag, and it's too bulky for a clutch. You'll dedicate a separate pouch to it.

The solid cleanser outperforms the liquid version in one way: it's easier to control the amount. You rub the damp sponge directly on the bar, then massage and rinse. Foundation and concealer came out completely in under a minute. I did notice the sponge's surface started to show slight pilling after about four weeks of daily washing with this bar — nothing catastrophic, but it wasn't as smooth as with the liquid cleanser.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews holds up. One buyer wrote, *"I've tried every dupe from Amazon and drugstore brands. Nothing blends like the real thing. The case makes it worth the splurge for travel."* Another noted, *"I was skeptical about the solid cleanser but it actually gets the sponge cleaner than my usual dish soap trick."* Multiple reviewers specifically highlighted the case's ability to keep the sponge from getting that musty smell — it's a niche problem, but if you've dealt with it, you know how gross it is.

**Where it falls short**

The most common complaint, and one I hit myself: the sponge wears out faster than previous generations. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"I've been using Beautyblender for 8 years. This one started tearing after 6 weeks. My old ones lasted 3 months."* The culprit seems to be the combination of the firmer cleanser bar and any vigorous scrubbing — you need to be gentle. Also, the Defender case has no drainage holes at the bottom, so if you close it with a dripping sponge, water pools in the cradle. Let it air-dry for 15 minutes before sealing it.

The price is the other issue. You can buy the sponge alone for about $20, and the case separately for $15. The bundle is only a slight discount, and if you already own a cleanser you like, you're paying for something you'll stash in a drawer.

**Who should buy this**

- **Frequent travelers** who wear makeup and hate replacing moldy sponges every trip — the Defender case pays for itself in reduced waste.
- **Beautyblender loyalists** who want the official cleanser to extend sponge life (as long as you wash gently).
- **Gift-givers** — this is the rare beauty bundle that feels complete and thoughtful without being a junk set.

**Skip it if** you're a casual user who only does makeup a few times a week. The standalone sponge is cheaper, and a $3 brush cleaner will do the job. The case is overkill for a nightstand routine.

**FAQ**

*Q: Can I use the Defender case with a wet sponge?*  
A: Yes, but let it air-dry for 10–15 minutes first. The cradle has ventilation, but no drainage — a dripping sponge will pool water at the bottom and defeat the anti-mold purpose.

*Q: Is the solid cleanser safe for the Pro or Micro versions?*  
A: Yes, it's the same formula as the liquid. Just use a lighter hand when rubbing — the Pro's denser texture is more prone to surface pilling.

*Q: How long does the sponge last with this cleaner?*  
A: With gentle washing and proper drying, 2–3 months. With harsh scrubbing, you'll see tears at week 6–8. Replace every 3 months max, regardless of appearance.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you travel with makeup, want to avoid mildewed sponges, and appreciate the convenience of a solid cleanser that doesn't spill in your bag. **Skip it if** you're on a budget or rarely leave the house with your makeup kit — the sponge alone is the better value.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — the sponge is still the best in its class, and the Defender case solves a real problem. But the bundle's premium price and the sponge's slightly shorter lifespan keep it from a perfect score.
