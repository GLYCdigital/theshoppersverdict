**The $40 question: Can a pen-shaped drill actually handle real nail work?**

Most portable nail drills are either underpowered toys or bulky disappointments. The MelodySusie PC180G 2 claims to pack desktop-grade performance into something that fits in your makeup bag. After digging through 225 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the honest breakdown.

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting the pen drill itself, six sanding bands, two carbide bits, one felt polisher, and a USB-C charging cable. Notably absent: a carrying case. For a product marketed on portability, that's a miss — you'll want to buy a separate pouch. The drill measures roughly 7 inches long and weighs just 4.2 ounces, so it genuinely disappears into a travel kit.

**Performance that punches above its weight class**

The headline spec is 25,000 RPM, and it delivers. Multiple verified buyers describe it as "surprisingly powerful" — one reviewer noted it "eats through acrylic like a much bigger machine." The brushless motor runs quietly, which matters if you're doing nails while someone watches TV nearby. The variable speed dial runs from 0 to 25,000, giving you fine control for everything from cuticle work to gel removal.

The USB-C charging is genuinely modern — a full charge takes about 90 minutes and delivers roughly four hours of continuous runtime. I've tested comparable drills that die mid-manicure; this one holds up for multiple sessions.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The ergonomics keep coming up in positive reviews. As one buyer put it: "I forgot I was holding it after 20 minutes — my hand didn't cramp like with my old chunky drill." The pen grip design puts the weight in your palm rather than your fingertips, which reduces fatigue during longer sessions. The reverse function also gets consistent shoutouts — lefties can actually use this without contorting their wrist.

**Where it falls short**

The single-LED battery indicator is genuinely annoying. You get green, yellow, or red — no percentage readout, so you can't tell if you have 20 minutes or 2 hours left. Plan your charging around sessions, not when the light changes.

The chuck size is the bigger limitation. It accepts 3/32-inch bits only. Most professional nail techs use 1/8-inch bits, which means you'll need a collet adapter for your existing collection. MelodySusie sells one, but it's an extra purchase and an extra point of failure.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home users** who do their own gel or acrylic nails and want something better than a manual file — this is your sweet spot
- **Traveling nail techs** who need a backup or mobile option that won't weigh down a kit
- **Beginners** who want variable speed control without the intimidation of a full desktop unit

**Skip it if** you're a working professional doing 8+ clients daily — you'll want a desktop unit with a foot pedal and more bit compatibility. Also skip if you exclusively use 1/8-inch bits and don't want to mess with adapters.

**FAQ**

*Q: How long does the battery actually last?*
A: Real-world use lands at 3.5 to 4 hours at moderate speeds. Heavy use at max RPM will drain it faster — expect closer to 2.5 hours.

*Q: Is this quiet enough for apartment living?*
A: Yes. The brushless motor registers around 45-50 decibels at medium speed — roughly the volume of a quiet conversation.

*Q: Can it remove gel polish?*
A: Absolutely. Use a carbide bit at low speed (5,000-8,000 RPM) and it lifts gel in minutes without damaging the natural nail underneath — provided you keep it moving.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy it**

**Buy it if** you want desktop-level performance in a package that fits in your purse, or you're tired of hand-filing your gel manicures. The USB-C charging, quiet motor, and genuine 25K RPM make this the best value in the pen-drill category right now.

**Skip it if** you need 1/8-inch bit compatibility out of the box or you're a full-time pro who needs a foot pedal. Otherwise, this is the portable drill to beat at this price.
