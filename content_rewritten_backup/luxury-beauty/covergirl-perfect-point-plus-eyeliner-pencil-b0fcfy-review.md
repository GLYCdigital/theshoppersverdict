If you've ever wrestled with a pencil liner that drags, snaps, or vanishes by lunch, the CoverGirl Perfect Point Plus is the $8 fix you didn't know you needed. This is the rare drugstore liner that behaves like a prestige product — provided you pick the right shade and manage your expectations.

**What's actually in the tube**  
You get 0.04 oz of product in a twist-up pencil (no sharpener needed — the cap has a built-in sharpener, which is genius and saves you from that crusty old sharpener in your drawer). The formula is enriched with jojoba oil and vitamin E, which explains the glide. As shown in the product image, the tip is fine enough for tight-lining but not so fine that it breaks under pressure.

**Performance: the good, the bad, the honest**  
The standout feature is the texture. It's creamy without being waxy — a single pass delivers opaque color, and you can build it for a smoky look before it sets (about 60 seconds). I tested it on the waterline during a 10-hour shift, and it held up through coffee, tears, and a regrettable nap. One reviewer noted, *"I've worn this through a full workday and a gym session — it's the only liner that doesn't end up under my eyes."* Another praised the staying power: *"Finally, a pencil that doesn't disappear by 3 PM."*

But here's the tradeoff: the "Black" shade is more of a charcoal. If you want true jet-black, go for "Black Onyx" — but note it's only available online, not in most stores. The tip also softens in warm weather, so if you live in a humid climate, keep it in a cool place or you'll be re-sharpening (via the built-in mechanism) more often than you'd like.

**Where it stumbles**  
The shade range is genuinely limiting — just four colors (Black, Black Onyx, Brown, and a muted Plum). No navy, no gray, no fun colors. And while the self-sharpening cap is convenient, the internal blade dulls after about three months, so you'll eventually need to buy a new pencil just for the sharpener. That's a clever way to sell refills, but it's also a hidden cost.

**Who should buy this**  
- **The tight-liner** who wants precise, no-fuss application without a brush  
- **The waterline warrior** who needs all-day wear and doesn't want to pay $25 for a Japanese import  
- **The minimalist** who wants one pencil that works for both a quick line and a smoky eye  

Skip it if you're a **glitter-obsessed** person (this is matte only) or if you need a **true black** for a dramatic wing — you'll end up layering it, and that defeats the point.

**FAQ: What shoppers actually ask**  
*Q: Does it smudge on oily lids?*  
A: It resists smudging on the waterline and lash line for 8+ hours, but on very oily lids, you'll want a primer. The formula sets to a semi-matte finish that doesn't budge once dry.

*Q: Is it safe for sensitive eyes?*  
A: Yes — ophthalmologist-tested, and several reviewers with contact lenses reported no irritation. The jojoba oil is gentle, and there's no fragrance.

*Q: How does it compare to the old Perfect Point (non-Plus)?*  
A: The Plus version is noticeably creamier and lasts longer. The old one dried out quickly; this one doesn't. Worth the slight price bump.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a reliable, budget-friendly pencil that handles the waterline like a champ and doesn't require a sharpener. **Skip it if** you demand a true black or a wider shade range — you'll be disappointed.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid workhorse with a few minor flaws. For the price, it outperforms most prestige liners, but the shade limitations and tip-softening keep it from perfection.
