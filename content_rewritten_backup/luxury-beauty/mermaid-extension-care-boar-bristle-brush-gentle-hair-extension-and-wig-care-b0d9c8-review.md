**Full Review**  

Let’s be blunt: most brushes marketed for extensions are just regular brushes with a premium price tag. The Mermaid Extension Care Boar Bristle Brush is different—and that difference matters if you’ve dropped serious money on tape-ins, wefts, or a quality wig.  

**What You Actually Get**  
The brush itself is compact: a wooden handle about 8 inches long, fitted with a cushioned pad and a blend of boar bristles and nylon pins. The nylon pins are longer and rounded at the tips, while the boar bristles are shorter and stiffer. That mix is the whole point—it lets you detangle the extension weft without pulling at the bonds, while the boar bristles smooth the cuticle of both your natural hair and the extension fiber. No box, no fancy pouch. Just the brush. Fine for the price, though some competitors include a cleaning comb.  

**Performance and Build Quality**  
Here’s where this brush earns its keep. The nylon pins are spaced wider than on a typical paddle brush, which means they glide between extension tracks rather than catching on them. The cushion has real give—it flexes with the contour of your scalp, so pressure distributes evenly instead of concentrating at the anchor points. That’s the difference between a tool that preserves your install and one that slowly pulls it loose.  

The boar bristles do what they’re supposed to: they lift sebum from your scalp and drag it down the shaft, which keeps both your natural hair and the extensions from looking dull or greasy at the roots. If you’re prone to oily scalps but dry ends—common with extensions—this brush genuinely helps.  

But there’s a catch. The bristles are firm enough for extension hair but not for thick, coarse natural hair. If you have dense 3C or 4C texture, this brush will just bounce off the surface. It’s a specialized tool, not an all-in-one.  

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**  
The 4.4-star rating holds up. Repeat buyers mention two things repeatedly: gentle detangling and reduced shedding. One reviewer noted, *“I was sure my tape-ins were slipping because I kept seeing hair in my brush—turns out it was breakage from my old brush. This one hasn’t pulled a single bond loose in three months.”* Another said, *“My wig stylist actually recommended this to me. It doesn’t snag the lace front like my old paddle brush did.”* That tracks with the design: the rounded pin tips are polished smooth, so they don’t micro-tear the cuticle of synthetic or human hair wigs.  

**Where It Falls Short**  
Honest downsides: The bristles are too soft for anyone with medium-to-thick natural hair, even if you’re using it just on your bio hair before blending with extensions. You’ll need a separate brush for that. Second, the handle is smooth wood with no grip texture. If you’re brushing in the shower or after applying a styling cream, it can slip. Third, there’s no cleaning brush included, and boar bristles trap oil and product residue. You’ll need to wash it weekly with warm water and mild soap, then dry it bristle-down.  

**Who Should Buy This**  
- **Tape-in and weft wearers:** The cushion and pin spacing are designed for your install.  
- **Wig users:** The gentle pins won’t snag lace fronts or mono tops.  
- **People with fine-to-medium natural hair** who want to distribute scalp oils without harsh brushing.  

Skip it if you have thick, coarse, or curly natural hair, or if you’re looking for a do-everything brush.  

**FAQ**  

*Can I use this on wet hair?*  
No. Boar bristles are brittle when wet, and wet extension bonds are more fragile. Brush dry hair only.  

*How do I clean it?*  
Remove hair strands after each use. Weekly, wash the bristles with warm water and a drop of clarifying shampoo, then dry it lying flat with the bristles facing down.  

*Will it damage my wig’s lace?*  
The nylon pins are rounded and the cushion compresses, so it won’t snag lace as long as you brush gently from the ends upward. Avoid pressing hard at the hairline.  

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you wear tape-ins, wefts, or wigs and want a brush that won’t pull at anchor points or snag lace. **Skip it if** you have thick natural hair or need a versatile everyday brush. At 4.2/5, this is a specialized tool that does one job exceptionally well—just don’t expect it to be your only brush.
