If you've got hair that laughs at regular conditioners, SheaMoisture's Jamaican Black Castor Oil Treatment Masque is the heavyweight you've been circling. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this isn't a quiet little hair mask — it's a treatment that makes promises and mostly keeps them. But it demands something from you in return: time, patience, and a tolerance for mint that could clear a sinus cavity.

**What you're actually getting**

The 12-ounce jar (as shown in the product image) contains a dense, butter-like masque that's closer to a hair butter than a typical conditioner. The texture is thick enough to hold a spoon upright — this is not a slap-it-on-and-rinse situation. The formula centers on Jamaican black castor oil, which is darker and more potent than regular castor oil because the beans are roasted before pressing. It's paired with shea butter, peppermint oil, and apple cider vinegar. No sulfates, no parabens, no mineral oil, no phthalates.

**How it performs in real use**

The first thing you'll notice is the tingle. Peppermint oil does real work here — within two minutes of application, your scalp starts buzzing. That's the stimulation effect, and it's genuinely pleasant if you're into that sensation. The masque needs to sit for at least 15 minutes, preferably under a shower cap with some body heat. When you rinse, the slip is surprisingly good for such a heavy formula — hair doesn't feel stripped, but it also doesn't feel weighed down.

The results are most dramatic on hair that's damaged, over-processed, or naturally textured. Multiple reviewers note that it "softened their curls without losing definition" and one put it plainly: "I have 4C hair and this is the only masque that makes my hair feel like I just left the salon." On fine or straight hair, however, it can be too much — you'll want to use a fraction of the recommended amount or skip it entirely.

**Where it stumbles**

The fragrance is the divide. The peppermint is intense — if you're sensitive to strong scents, this will linger in your hair for a full day. It's not a bad smell, it's just a lot. The texture also requires effort. You need to section your hair, work it through strand by strand, and be prepared to spend 20 minutes on application alone. And the jar packaging is genuinely annoying with wet hands — a pump or tube would be functionally better.

**Who should buy this**

- **Curlies and coilies** with dry, brittle, or heat-damaged hair — this is your weekly deep-conditioning treatment
- **Transitioning naturals** looking for slip during the awkward in-between phase
- **Scent enthusiasts** who genuinely enjoy bracing, mint-forward hair products

**Skip it if** your hair is fine, thin, or easily weighed down, or if you want a five-minute conditioning step. This is a treatment, not a maintenance product.

**FAQ**

*How often should I use this masque?*
Once a week is the sweet spot for most hair types. If your hair is severely damaged, twice a week for the first month is acceptable — just don't go beyond that or you'll over-moisturize and get limp, mushy curls.

*Can I use this on color-treated hair?*
Yes. The formula is sulfate-free and gentle enough for colored hair. The apple cider vinegar can slightly affect tone, so wait at least two weeks post-color before using.

*Does it actually stimulate hair growth?*
The peppermint oil increases blood flow to the scalp, which supports a healthy growth environment. It won't magically grow hair, but combined with the strengthening castor oil, it improves hair health at the root — which is what actually prevents breakage.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have thick, textured, or damaged hair that needs serious intervention. **Skip it if** you want quick conditioning or have fine hair that can't handle heavy butters.

This is a 4.6-star product for a reason: it delivers dramatic results for the people it's formulated for, and it's honest about its limitations. At its price point, it outperforms many salon brands that cost three times as much. Just be ready for the mint.
