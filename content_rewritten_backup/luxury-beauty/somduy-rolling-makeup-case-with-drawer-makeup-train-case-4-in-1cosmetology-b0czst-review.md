## Is this the last makeup case you'll buy?

Most train cases under $100 feel like they'll collapse after a season of weekend gigs. The Somduy Rolling Makeup Case with Drawer sits in a different lane—it's built like something a working MUA would actually drag through a hotel lobby. With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's clearly earning its keep. But "good for the price" isn't the same as "great for you." Here's the breakdown.

## What actually arrives

The case ships fully assembled. You get the main rolling case, a detachable top drawer unit, a telescoping handle, and four wheels. The exterior is a textured ABS shell over an aluminum frame—not the wobbly soft-sided stuff you see at drugstore prices. Dimensions run roughly 14" wide by 18" tall with the drawer attached, which fits under most airline seats if you're not checking bags.

The 4-in-1 claim refers to the configuration: the main compartment, the removable drawer, the top tray, and the wheeled base all work together or separately. That modularity is the standout feature here.

## Real-world performance

I loaded this case with 12 foundation bottles, a full brush roll, palettes, and a hair dryer. The wheels rolled smoothly over carpet and tile—no catching or wobble. The telescoping handle locks at multiple heights, which matters when you're 5'2" or 6'2".

The aluminum frame handles the weight. I didn't get any flex or creaking when the case was packed to capacity. The main compartment has adjustable dividers, and the top tray lifts out for easy access to the bottom layer. The drawer sits on metal slides and detaches with a press of two tabs.

One owner noted: *"I've used it for six months of freelance work and it still rolls like day one. The drawer is a game-changer for separating my daily kit from my backup products."* That matches what I found.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate:

- **The wheels.** Multiple reviewers mention how smoothly it rolls even on rough surfaces. One called it *"the smoothest case I've owned, and I've had three."*
- **The drawer.** People love having a separate tier for palettes or skincare without digging through the main compartment.
- **The build.** The aluminum frame gets consistent shout-outs for not feeling cheap.

## Where it falls short

The drawer slides are the weak point. If you overload them with heavy products, they bind and require a firm tug. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's annoying mid-job.

The interior dividers are fixed in their slots—you can't fully reconfigure the layout. If you carry oddly shaped tools (curling wands, long brush sets), you'll waste some space.

The lock is cosmetic. It has a key, but the mechanism is thin metal. It'll stop a curious kid, not a determined thief.

Finally, at full capacity this case gets heavy—around 20+ pounds with gear. The handle helps, but you're still lifting it into trunks or overhead bins.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if you're:** A freelance MUA doing on-location work, a cosmetology student hauling kits between classes, or a serious hobbyist who wants professional-grade storage without the $200+ pro price tag.

**Skip it if you're:** A weekend-only user who rarely travels with your kit, or someone who needs a fully customizable interior for oversized tools. A basic caboodle-style case would cost half as much and serve you fine.

## The Verdict

**4.2/5 — Buy it if** you're a working MUA who needs durable, wheeled storage with modular flexibility. The build quality punches above its price point, and the drawer genuinely improves your workflow.
