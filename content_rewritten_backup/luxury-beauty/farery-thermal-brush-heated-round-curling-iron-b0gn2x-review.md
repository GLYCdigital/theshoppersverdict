## Does This Heated Brush Actually Curl, or Is It Just a Warm Hairbrush?

The Farery Thermal Brush sits in that awkward middle ground between a traditional curling iron and a hot air brush. After digging through 215 ratings and the product specs, here's the real story: this is a heated round brush that works — but it demands a specific technique.

## What You're Actually Getting

The unit is a round barrel brush with built-in ceramic heating elements. The bristles are a mix of boar and nylon — boar for grip and shine, nylon for detangling. The barrel diameter runs about 1.25 inches, which is the sweet spot for medium-sized curls and volume at the roots.

As shown in the product image, the handle houses the temperature dial and an LED display. You get a swivel cord (essential, trust me) and a heat-resistant glove in the box. Nothing else — no carrying case, no clips. Keep that in mind if you travel.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the Farery either clicks or doesn't. The ceramic barrel heats evenly — no hot spots, which is rare at this price point. The temperature range spans 300°F to 410°F, and the dial lets you fine-tune. Fine hair should sit around 300–330°F. Thick or coarse hair needs 370–410°F. There's no preset button, but the manual dial is actually more precise than many digital controls.

The learning curve is real. Unlike a flat brush, the round barrel forces you to wrap sections around the cylinder. It's closer to using a curling iron than a paddle brush. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Takes a few tries to get the hang of wrapping hair around it without tangling. Once you do, it works great."* That sentiment shows up repeatedly — patience pays off.

For volume at the roots, this thing excels. The round shape lifts hair away from the scalp naturally, and the boar bristles grab fine strands without yanking. Styling time runs about 10–12 minutes for shoulder-length hair, which beats a traditional iron and separate brush routine.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Three themes dominate the positive reviews: heat consistency, reduced frizz, and time saved. Multiple buyers mention the ceramic surface makes hair shinier than their old metal-barrel irons. One user wrote: *"My hair actually looks healthier after using this. No more fried ends from my old iron."* The swivel cord also gets consistent shout-outs for not tangling mid-style.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint is the learning curve — and it's legitimate. If you're used to paddle brushes, expect a week of awkward results before you find your rhythm.

There's also no auto shut-off. For a tool that hits 410°F, that's a genuine safety miss. If you're the type to leave appliances plugged in, set a timer or buy a smart plug.

The barrel is fixed — no interchangeable sizes. You're getting one curl diameter, period. If you want tight ringlets or loose beach waves, this won't flex to either extreme.

Finally, the glove included is thin. It protects from brief contact but won't save you from sustained pressure against the barrel. Be careful near your ears and neck.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a two-in-one tool that cuts styling time, have medium-length to long hair, and are willing to practice the wrapping technique. It's ideal for fine to normal hair types that need volume without damage.

**Skip it if:** You have extremely thick or coily hair (the 1.25-inch barrel won't penetrate deeply enough), you're a complete styling novice who wants grab-and-go results, or you need multiple curl sizes from one tool.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Farery Thermal Brush work on wet hair?**
A: No. Hair must be completely dry. Using it on damp hair causes sizzling and potential damage. The ceramic plate needs dry hair to glide properly.

**Q: How long does the curl actually last?**
A: Expect 4–6 hours on fine hair, up to 8 hours on thicker textures — assuming you let each section cool completely before touching it. Hairspray extends that significantly.

**Q: Is this safe for color-treated hair?**
A: Yes, if you stay at or below 350°F. The even ceramic heat reduces the risk of hot spots that fade color faster. Start at 300°F and work up only if needed.

## The Verdict: 4.2/5

The Farery Thermal Brush earns its rating honestly. It's a solidly built, effective tool that delivers on its core promise — faster styling with less damage. The ceramic heating is genuinely good, the temperature control is precise, and the results speak for themselves once you learn the technique.

But the missing auto shut-off and the single barrel size keep it from being a no-brainer. If you're patient and understand what you're buying, this is a strong addition to your routine. If you want effortless, instant results, look elsewhere.

**Buy it if** you want a time-saving two-in-one and don't mind a week of practice. **Skip it if** safety features are non-negotiable or you need versatility in curl sizes.
