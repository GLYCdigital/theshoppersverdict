**Is this just overpriced scented water, or does the honey actually do something? I tested it for two weeks to find out.**

Gisou's Honey Infused Hair Perfume sits in an awkward category: it's not quite a traditional perfume, not quite a hair treatment. The brand — founded by influencer Negin Mirsalehi — leans hard into the honey heritage angle, and the packaging delivers. The amber glass bottle with gold accents and honeycomb detailing looks like it belongs on a vanity, not a drugstore shelf. But looks aside, what you're actually paying for matters more. At roughly $40 for 1.7 fluid ounces, this is a luxury impulse buy. Here's the honest breakdown before you add it to your cart.

**What's actually in the bottle**

The formula is mostly alcohol and water, but there's real substance mixed in. The star ingredients are honey extract (Gisou's proprietary blend sourced from Mirsalehi's family apiaries) and panthenol (pro-vitamin B5). Panthenol is the workhorse here — it's a humectant that draws moisture into the hair shaft and can help with frizz control. The honey extract is more about marketing and scent than measurable conditioning; at the concentration used in a spray fragrance, it's negligible for hair health. That's not a dealbreaker, but go in with realistic expectations.

The fragrance itself is the real draw. It opens with neroli and orange blossom, then settles into a warm, honeyed jasmine base. It's sophisticated — think honeyed black tea, not candy. Fans of Byredo's Bal d'Afrique or Jo Malone's Nectarine Blossom & Honey will recognize the vibe. It's distinctly feminine but not girly, and it layers beautifully over other scents.

**How it performs in real use**

I sprayed this on air-dried hair and also on heat-styled hair. The mist is lightweight — no sticky residue, no crunchy texture, and it doesn't stiffen hair the way some hairsprays do. It doubled as a light detangler on second-day hair, which was a pleasant surprise. One buyer echoed this: "I bought it for the scent but it actually makes my hair softer and easier to brush through."

Scent longevity is the biggest caveat. On clean, product-free hair, I got about three hours of noticeable projection before it faded to a skin-close scent that lingered another two hours. On hair with dry shampoo or styling products, it faded faster. This is a fragrance you'll need to reapply if you want all-day presence — and at this price point, that's a legitimate frustration. Another reviewer noted: "Gorgeous scent but I wish it lasted longer. By lunch it's gone."

The spray nozzle is also inconsistent. On my first bottle, the mist was fine. On the second, it dispensed a concentrated stream that left wet spots. It's a quality-control issue worth flagging — you may need to hold the bottle farther back and spray in sections.

**Who should buy this**

This is for someone who already owns a signature perfume and wants a complementary hair scent that doesn't compete. It's also great for anyone with dry or frizz-prone hair who wants a lightweight finishing mist that adds a touch of moisture and shine. If you're looking for a serious hair treatment with measurable benefits — this isn't it.

**Skip it if** you're on a budget, prefer long-lasting traditional perfume, or expect dramatic hair transformation. For those needs, a regular fragrance or a dedicated leave-in conditioner is a better value.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Can I use this on colored or treated hair?**
Yes. The formula is alcohol-based but doesn't contain sulfates or drying alcohols that strip color. Panthenol actually helps maintain moisture, which is beneficial for chemically treated hair.

**Q: How is it different from regular perfume?**
Regular perfume is designed for skin and often contains higher concentrations of fragrance oils that can dry out hair. This has a lighter concentration and includes conditioning agents, so it won't leave hair brittle or sticky.

**Q: Does it stain light-colored clothing?**
The formula is clear and alcohol-based, so it evaporates quickly without leaving yellow marks. Just avoid oversaturating one spot.

**The Verdict**

Gisou Honey Infused Hair Perfume is a well-crafted luxury product that delivers on scent quality and lightweight conditioning — but it's not a miracle worker. The fragrance is genuinely beautiful and distinctive, the bottle is gorgeous, and the added panthenol is a thoughtful touch. The price and modest longevity are the tradeoffs.

**Buy it if** you appreciate niche-quality scents, want a hair mist that doubles as a light conditioning spray, and don't mind reapplying. **Skip it if** you want a one-and-done fragrance with all-day staying power or need actual reparative hair treatment.

**Rating: 4.2/5 — Beautiful scent, honest execution, but priced for the experience, not the results.**
