Here's the thing about TRESemmé Rich Moisture: it's the rare drugstore line that refuses to act like one. The 2-count set delivers a full liter of product (two 28-ounce bottles) for a price that typically gets you a single 8-ounce tube of something fancy with a French name. That math alone gets shoppers' attention — but the 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews is what keeps it there.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains two full-size bottles: one Rich Moisture Shampoo, one Rich Moisture Conditioner. Both are formulated with vitamin B5 and aloe vera — two ingredients that actually do something for dry, parched hair rather than just looking good on the label. The shampoo is a clear, gel-like consistency that lathers well despite being sulfate-free. That's worth emphasizing because sulfate-free formulas often feel like they're doing nothing. This one cleanses without that stripped, squeaky sensation.

The conditioner is where the formula shines. It's thick — almost buttery — and a little goes further than you'd expect. On medium-length hair, a quarter-sized amount covers everything. It rinses clean without that slippery "did I get it all out?" residue that cheap conditioners leave behind.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this on color-treated, heat-styled hair that tends toward frizz by midday. After two weeks of consistent use: noticeably softer texture, fewer flyaways, and — surprisingly — my color held longer than with my usual salon brand. The biotin and vitamin B5 combo seems to genuinely reinforce the hair shaft rather than just coating it.

Buyer feedback mirrors this. One Amazon reviewer wrote: "I've spent $40+ on shampoo that doesn't work as well as this. My dry, frizzy hair actually looks healthy for once." Another noted: "This is the third time I've purchased this exact set. It's the only thing that doesn't make my scalp itch." The consistency of praise — hydration, softness, value — is striking across the review base.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires noting the tradeoffs. The fragrance is the most common complaint. It's a clean, salon-style scent that lingers in your hair for hours. If you're sensitive to fragrance or prefer unscented products, this will be a problem. One reviewer put it plainly: "Smells nice but strong. I could smell it even the next morning."

Also, the conditioner's richness can be too much for fine, straight hair. If your hair is thin or prone to oiliness at the roots, use it sparingly — concentrate on the mid-lengths and ends only. Applied full-head, you may end up with weighed-down, flat hair by day two.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Dry, curly, or coily hair types** — the moisture level here is genuinely restorative
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who don't want to sacrifice quality for price
- **Families** — two big bottles mean this lasts months even with daily use
- **Color-treated hair** — gentle enough to extend time between salon visits

Skip it if you have fine, limp hair or you're deeply fragrance-averse.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this shampoo really sulfate-free?**
A: Yes. It's formulated without sulfates, which is why it won't strip color or leave hair feeling squeaky-clean. You may need to double-lather if you use heavy styling products.

**Q: How long does the 2-pack typically last?**
A: For one person washing every other day, expect 2-3 months. The bottles are 28 ounces each — that's a substantial amount of product.

**Q: Does it work for itchy, sensitive scalps?**
A: Many reviewers report it helps. The aloe vera is soothing, and the lack of harsh sulfates reduces irritation. However, if you have diagnosed scalp conditions like psoriasis, consult a dermatologist first.
