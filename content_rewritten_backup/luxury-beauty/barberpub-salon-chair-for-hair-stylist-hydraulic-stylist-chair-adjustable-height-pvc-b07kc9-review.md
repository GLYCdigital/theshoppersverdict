Let me save you some scrolling: the BarberPub Salon Chair is the rare budget hydraulic chair that doesn't feel like it'll collapse mid-cut. But at 3.9 stars across 195 ratings, it's not the flawless workhorse some listings suggest. Here's what you actually need to know before you drop money on this thing.

## The Short Version

This is a no-frills hydraulic stylist chair built for function over comfort. You get the chair, a chrome base with five dual-wheel casters, and the gas lift cylinder. Assembly takes about 25 minutes if you're mechanically inclined—longer if you're not, because the instructions are basic line drawings with zero written guidance. One buyer noted, "The hardest part was getting the lift cylinder to seat into the base. It took two of us and some muscle."

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The frame is solid steel with a welded base plate, and the hydraulic lift is rated for 300 pounds. That's genuinely good for this price bracket. The lift action is smooth on the way down—no jarring drops—and the height range of roughly 19 to 24 inches covers most stylist needs, from cutting to shampooing.

The PVC upholstery is a double-edged sword. It wipes clean easily and won't absorb hair color or chemicals, which is exactly what you want in a salon chair. But it's not breathable. On warm days, clients will stick to the seat. And the cushion itself is dense with minimal give. Multiple reviewers mention the same thing: "Fine for a quick trim, but my elderly clients struggle with how hard the seat is."

The 360-degree swivel is smooth, and the casters roll well on tile and hardwood. On thick carpet, they drag a bit—something to keep in mind if your home studio is carpeted.

## Where It Falls Short

The scuffs issue is real. The black PVC shows every mark from shoes, belt buckles, and dropped tools. You'll be wiping it down constantly to keep it looking professional. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's annoying for a chair meant to look sharp in a client-facing space.

The seat depth is also shallow—about 17 inches. Taller clients or those with longer legs will find their knees at an awkward angle. This isn't a chair for extended services. If you're doing 90-minute color appointments, your client will be fidgeting by minute 30.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're a home-based stylist, a barber just starting out, or you need a spare chair for a second station. It's a solid value for the price, and the hydraulic mechanism is dependable.

**Skip it if** you run a high-volume salon where clients sit for extended periods, or if you regularly serve plus-sized clientele. The firm seat and shallow depth will generate complaints. Spend more on a chair with a thicker, contoured cushion.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the hydraulic lift reliable long-term?**
A: Yes, for the most part. The gas cylinder is the same type used in chairs costing twice as much. Reviewers report consistent performance over 6-12 months of daily use. The main failure point is the caster wheels, which can loosen over time—just tighten them periodically.

**Q: Can this chair be used for both cutting and shampooing?**
A: Technically yes, but it's not ideal. The height range works for both, but the lack of a footrest means clients will dangle their legs during shampooing. It's better as a dedicated cutting chair.

**Q: What's the actual weight capacity?**
A: The listing says 300 pounds, and that's accurate. The frame handles it fine. The concern is the seat comfort at that weight, not the structural integrity.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5** — The BarberPub Salon Chair delivers on the basics: solid hydraulics, easy-clean upholstery, and smooth swivel. But the firm cushion and shallow seat make it a compromise chair. For a home studio or backup station, it's a smart buy. For a busy professional salon, keep looking.

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, affordable hydraulic chair and your clients won't be parked in it for long stretches.

**Skip it if** comfort is your priority or you serve a diverse clientele with varying body types. Your clients will feel the difference—and so will your reviews.
