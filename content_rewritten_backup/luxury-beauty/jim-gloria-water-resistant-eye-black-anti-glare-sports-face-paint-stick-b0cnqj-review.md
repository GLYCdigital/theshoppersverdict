**The $8 solution that actually kills glare — but is it a one-hit wonder?**

You’ve seen pros swipe black under their eyes and assumed it’s pure intimidation. It’s not. That dark pigment exists to absorb sunlight before it bounces off your cheekbones and into your eyes — the difference between tracking a fly ball and losing it in the sun’s glare. The Jim&Gloria Water Resistant Eye Black stick does exactly that, and does it well. But at a compact size and with a single shade option, it’s not without compromises.

**What you actually get**

The package contains one twist-up stick, roughly the dimensions of a standard lip balm but slightly thicker. The product itself is a matte, wax-based pigment with a faint, neutral scent that disappears within seconds of application. There’s no applicator sponge, no mirror, no smudging tool — you twist, swipe twice under each eye, and rub lightly with a fingertip to blend. Total application time: under a minute.

**Performance: the real test**

I wore this through a 90-minute outdoor soccer session in 82-degree heat. The first thing you notice is the immediate reduction in squinting — the matte finish doesn't reflect light the way glossy sunscreens or sweat do. After 45 minutes of running, I checked for running or streaking. Nothing. The pigment stayed put along the orbital bone without creeping toward the eyes.

The water resistance claim holds up in practice. A light drizzle and heavy sweat didn't break it down. One buyer noted, "Played a full doubleheader in Florida humidity and it didn't budge. Better than the expensive brands I've tried." Another verified the removal process: "Comes off with just water — I don't need to scrub my face raw like with other eye blacks."

Where it stumbles: the pigment is a flat, true black with zero undertone flexibility. If you have very fair or very deep skin, it can look stark — almost war-paint-like — rather than the subtle gradient pros achieve with layered cream formulas.

**Build quality and longevity**

The stick mechanism is solid — no wobble, no cracking, and the product twists up smoothly. But here's the honest catch: it's small. At roughly 0.1 ounces of usable product, a daily athlete applying twice per session will burn through this in 3–4 weeks. That's not a dealbreaker at the price point, but it's worth knowing that frequent use means frequent repurchasing.

**Who should buy this**

- **Baseball and softball players** — the primary use case, and it delivers for infielders who spend hours tracking pop flies in direct sun.
- **Weekend runners and cyclists** — if you train outdoors at midday, the glare reduction is noticeable and the sweat resistance is reliable.
- **Lacrosse and football players** — the matte finish won't smear when helmets come off.

**Skip it if** you need a cream formula for precise artistic application, you're looking for a tinted option for lighter skin, or you play in extreme heat for 4+ hours daily — the small size will feel limiting.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it sting if it gets in your eyes?**
A: No — the formula is wax-based and non-irritating. If you rub your eyes and smear it, it's mildly uncomfortable but doesn't burn or blur vision.

**Q: Can you wear it over sunscreen?**
A: Yes, but apply sunscreen first and let it dry for 2–3 minutes. Applying the stick over wet sunscreen will reduce its staying power.

**Q: How do you remove it without staining?**
A: Warm water and a washcloth works in about 10 seconds. It does not stain skin or leave gray residue like some charcoal-based formulas.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a fast, no-mess eye black that survives sweat, performs under sun, and removes easily — all at a reasonable price.

**Skip it if** you need a larger quantity, a tinted formula, or a cream texture for layered application.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solidly built, functional product for athletes who value speed and reliability over cosmetics. The size and single shade keep it from a perfect score, but for the price, it's the best value eye black on Amazon right now.
