## What You're Actually Getting

This isn't a mixed bag of random grits. You get 120 bands total, split into two distinct types: 60 coarse (red) and 60 fine (pink) sanding bands. Each band measures 1/8 inch in diameter, which is the standard size for most electric nail drills on the market—think MelodySusie's own drills, plus models from brands like Makartt and L'UGX.

The construction is where these differ from dollar-store packs. The bands are made from a flexible abrasive material that grips the mandrel without needing a separate screw or clamp. You slide them on, spin the drill, and they self-tighten. That's a design choice that saves you the headache of fumbling with tiny screws mid-manicure.

## Real-World Performance

Here's what the ratings actually tell us. Users consistently praise the durability—a common complaint with cheap bands is that they tear after one or two nails. One reviewer noted, "I've used these on acrylics and gels, and they hold up way better than the ones that came with my drill kit." The 1/8-inch size is worth calling out specifically. If your drill takes a different mandrel size—say, 3/32 inch or 1/4 inch—these won't fit. But for the vast majority of home and salon drills, this is the sweet spot. The fine grit bands are ideal for buffing and smoothing after acrylic removal, while the coarse ones handle bulk filing.

The recurring theme in positive reviews is **value per band**. At roughly 10 cents per band, you're paying a fraction of what salon-supply stores charge. One verified purchase put it plainly: "I was paying $8 for 30 bands at my local beauty supply. These are better quality and half the price."

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. The biggest complaint—and it appears in several reviews—is that the **coarse bands wear down faster than expected** when used on hard acrylics. They're fine for gel and dip powder, but if you're regularly filing down thick acrylic overlays, you'll burn through the red bands quicker than the pink ones. That's a tradeoff of the material; harder surfaces will always degrade abrasives faster.

Second, the **packaging is purely functional**. You get a simple plastic bag, not a organizer box. If you're someone who likes to keep your station tidy, you'll need to supply your own storage container. Minor, but worth knowing before you buy.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these fit my MelodySusie drill?**
A: If your drill uses a 1/8-inch mandrel—which includes most MelodySusie models and most home drills on Amazon—yes. Check your drill's manual or the mandrel itself for the size before ordering.

**Q: Can I use these on natural nails?**
A: The fine grit bands are safe for buffing natural nails, but the coarse grit should only be used on enhancements. Using coarse grit on natural nails can thin them out and cause damage.

**Q: How long does one band last?**
A: For gel manicures, expect 2–4 nails per band. For acrylic removal, you'll get 1–2 nails per coarse band. The fine bands last longer if you're just smoothing.
