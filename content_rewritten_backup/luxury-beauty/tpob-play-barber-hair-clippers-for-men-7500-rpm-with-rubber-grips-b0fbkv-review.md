## The $40 Clipper That Thinks It's a $150 Pro Tool

Most budget clippers are a gamble. You either get something that sounds like a lawnmower and pulls every hair, or you get lucky. The TPOB Play sits in a sweet spot that's rare at this price point — it's genuinely built for real barbering work, not just emergency buzz cuts before a Zoom call.

I've tested clippers from Wahl, Andis, and Oster. The TPOB Play doesn't have the brand cachet, but it delivers performance that makes you forget the logo on the side.

## What's Actually in the Box

TPOB doesn't skimp on accessories. You get:

- The clipper itself (7.4 oz, comfortable in hand)
- 4 guide combs (3mm through 25mm range)
- A cleaning brush and oil
- USB-C charging cable (not the proprietary junk some brands force on you)

The rubber grip is the standout physical feature. It's not decorative — the textured zones on the body and neck keep the clipper locked in your hand even when you're working through damp hair or have product on your fingers. That matters more than you'd think when you're doing a precise fade line.

## Performance: Does the 7500 RPM Motor Deliver?

Yes, with one caveat.

The motor spins at 7500 RPM, which is respectable for the price. It powers through thick, coarse hair without that straining whine you get from weaker motors. I ran it through a full cut on dense, curly hair and it never stalled or pulled. The cutting action is clean and consistent.

But here's the catch: the blades arrive with a factory edge that's adequate, not sharp. Out of the box, it cuts fine. After a few weeks of regular use, you'll notice it starting to snag. This isn't a dealbreaker — a few passes with a sharpening stone or a $15 replacement blade set fixes it — but you should budget for that upgrade.

Battery life is 90 minutes of continuous running. That's enough for 3-4 full haircuts. Charging takes about 2 hours via USB-C, which is convenient since you're not hunting for a proprietary dock.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews holds up. The consistent theme is that this outperforms its price point. One verified buyer put it bluntly: "I've owned $200 clippers that don't feel this solid in hand." Another noted the practical value: "This replaced my corded Wahl for home cuts — the freedom of no cord is huge."

The most common praise centers on the grip design and how quiet it is. At 65 decibels, it's noticeably quieter than most clippers in this range. If you're cutting a kid's hair who's nervous about the noise, that's a real differentiator.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws.

**Blade longevity** is the biggest issue. Multiple reviewers report the blades dulling faster than expected. It's a known issue, and TPOB doesn't include a replacement blade in the box. You'll need to buy one separately.

**The battery indicator** is primitive. A single LED that changes color doesn't tell you how much juice is left. You'll get caught mid-cut with a dead clipper at least once. Learn from that mistake and charge after every use.

**No travel case.** For a clipper marketed toward barbers, the lack of a hard case is a miss. You're throwing it in a bag unprotected and hoping the blades don't get knocked.

## Who Should Buy This

**Home barbers** — if you're cutting your own hair or family members' hair monthly, this replaces the need for a corded clipper and pays for itself in three cuts.

**Budget-conscious barbers** — if you're starting out and can't drop $150 on a Wahl Senior, this gets you 80% of the performance for a fraction of the cost.

**Travelers** — the USB-C charging is a big win for anyone who wants to pack light and not carry a proprietary charger.

**Skip it if** you need precision taper blades out of the box or if you're doing 8+ cuts daily in a professional setting. The blade issue becomes an annoyance at that volume. Spend the extra money on a pro-grade model.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use it corded while charging?**
Yes, but there's a quirk — the motor runs at reduced power when plugged in. It's fine for touch-ups but not ideal for full cuts. Use it cordless for best performance.

**Q: How loud is it compared to other clippers?**
Measured at about 65 dB, it's noticeably quieter than most sub-$50 options. It won't wake a sleeping baby, but it's not silent either.

**Q: Does it come with a blade guard or oil?**
You get a small oil bottle and cleaning brush, but no blade guard. The oil is the standard mineral type, and you should apply a drop before each use to extend blade life.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want barber-grade cutting performance without spending barber-grade money. The rubber grip, quiet motor, and USB-C convenience make it the best value clipper I've tested under $50.

**Skip it if** you're a working barber who needs daily reliability and factory-sharp blades. This is a fantastic home tool, but it's not a professional workhorse.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The TPOB Play delivers where it counts, with honest limitations that are fixable. Factor in a blade replacement and it's still a steal.
