Let's cut through the noise: a $10 highlighter stick that actually delivers a believable, dewy glow instead of a glitter bomb. The CoverGirl Trublend Skin Enhancer Balm Stick in 320 Rose Beam has earned its 4.1-star average across 204 ratings, and after testing it against pricier cream highlighters, I understand why.

**What's in the stick**

You're getting a 0.21-ounce twist-up balm stick — compact enough for a makeup bag pocket. The formula is vegan and feels like a thick, emollient balm rather than a traditional powder or liquid highlighter. The Rose Beam shade is a soft, rosy champagne with a subtle pink shift. As shown in the product image, the stick itself looks almost sheer, but don't let that fool you — it builds quickly.

**Performance and real-world use**

The application is where this product shines. Swipe it directly onto cheekbones, then blend with a fingertip or damp sponge. The balm melts on contact and blends out to a sheer, skin-like glow that doesn't emphasize texture. I tested it over both matte powder foundation and dewy liquid foundation — it layers cleanly over both without lifting the base.

The finish is genuinely luminous rather than metallic. There's no visible glitter particle separation, just a soft sheen that reads as naturally hydrated skin. For a natural "no-makeup makeup" day, this is the kind of product that delivers.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the reviews, the overwhelming theme is that this performs like a premium product at a drugstore price. One reviewer noted, "I was skeptical at first, but this blends so smoothly and gives the most natural glow." Another mentioned, "I've paid five times more for highlighters that don't look this good on the skin." Multiple users specifically called out how well it works for mature skin — the creamy texture doesn't settle into fine lines the way powder highlighters can.

**Where it falls short**

Be honest with yourself about the downsides. The twist-up mechanism is a real issue — it dispenses more product than you need with a slight turn, and you can't twist it back down. If you're not careful, you'll have excess balm that needs blending or wiping off.

The Rose Beam shade is cool-toned. On deeper or warmer skin tones, it can read slightly ashy or frosty rather than glowing. If you're medium-deep or deeper, consider a shade like Rose Glow or a warmer champagne. Finally, longevity is average — on oily skin, expect noticeable fading around the four-hour mark without a setting spray.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the person who wants a natural, lit-from-within glow without paying Sephora prices. It's ideal for makeup minimalists who want one product that works for cheeks, brow bones, and even a subtle inner-corner eye highlight. It's also a smart pick for mature skin — the emollient formula works with the skin rather than sitting on top of it.

Skip it if you want a blinding, metallic highlight for photoshoot-level intensity, or if you have deep skin and want a shade that flatters your undertone without a cool cast.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this be used on bare skin without foundation?**
A: Yes. The balm blends into bare skin easily and gives a subtle, healthy sheen. Just apply and blend with your fingers.

**Q: Is it really vegan?**
A: Yes, CoverGirl markets this as a vegan formula, and the ingredient list confirms no animal-derived components.

**Q: Does it work as a blush topper?**
A: It can, but Rose Beam is more of a highlight than a blush. Use it sparingly on the high points of cheeks over your blush for a luminous effect.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a natural, easy-to-use cream highlighter that looks expensive but costs $9.97. It's a versatile, beginner-friendly formula that performs well above its price point.

**Skip it if** you need intense, long-wearing glam or if your skin tone leans deep and warm — the shade range is limited.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely good product with minor execution flaws, priced so fairly that the tradeoffs are easy to accept.
