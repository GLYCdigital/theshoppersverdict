If you've ever done a full set of acrylics in a tiny, unventilated room, you know the drill: that sharp, eye-watering chemical smell that lingers for hours. The DeEnti Nomma Plus Acrylic Liquid tackles that problem head-on with an EMA-based formula that's noticeably gentler on the nose. At 4.6 stars across 229 reviews, it's clearly earning its keep — but let's dig into whether it's the right monomer for your specific setup.

**What's actually in the bottle**

You get a 4oz (120ml) bottle of professional-grade liquid monomer. That's the standard size for a working nail tech — enough for roughly 8-12 full sets depending on your bead sizes. The liquid itself is a clear EMA (ethyl methacrylate) formula, which is the industry standard for professional use. As shown in the product image, it pours clean with zero cloudiness — a good indicator of proper filtration during manufacturing.

**Performance and real-world use**

The standout feature here is the low-odor profile. Traditional LMA (liquid methyl acrylate) monomers can be harsh — some clients literally hold their breath during application. The Nomma Plus uses a refined EMA blend that cuts the odor significantly while maintaining the same polymerization speed. In practice, that means you can work without a ventilation mask for shorter sessions, though I'd still recommend airflow for marathon appointments.

Bead control is where this product really shines. The monomer has a medium-viscosity feel that self-levels beautifully. When you dip your brush, the bead forms cleanly without dripping into cuticles — that translates to less filing and faster sculpting. One reviewer noted, "I've been doing nails for 12 years and this is the first monomer that lets me build a stress-free apex without fighting the product." That's consistent with what I found: the liquid has a forgiving working time of about 30-45 seconds before it sets, which suits both beginners and experienced techs.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The non-yellowing claim holds up. Multiple reviewers mention that white and light pink enhancements stay bright for weeks, even with frequent gel topcoat wear. One long-term user wrote, "My French manicures look fresh for three weeks instead of two — this monomer just doesn't amber like my old brand did." The clarity also matters for those of you doing encapsulation or clear overlays — you get that glass-like finish without the cloudy haze that cheaper monomers develop.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the price. This sits at a premium tier — expect to pay 30-40% more than comparable EMA monomers. You're paying for the low-odor chemistry and the non-yellowing formula, which is worth it if you do high-volume work, but it's a harder sell for hobbyists.

Second, the brush. It's functional but forgettable — the ferrule isn't crimped tightly enough for precise bead pickup. If you're serious about acrylic application, budget for a better brush. Third, the bottle design: there's no dripless cap. If you're not wiping the rim after each use, the threads get sticky and the cap becomes hard to twist off. Minor, but annoying.

**Who should buy this**

- **Salon professionals** doing 5+ sets a day who want to reduce their own chemical exposure and client discomfort
- **Nail techs specializing in** white tips, French manicures, or clear overlays where non-yellowing matters most
- **Educators and students** who want a forgiving monomer for learning bead control without the harsh odor

Skip it if you're a weekend hobbyist on a tight budget — Mia Secret's EMA monomer gets you 80% of the way there for half the price.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this safe for use on natural nails without primer?**
A: No — like all professional monomers, you need a proper acid-based primer and dehydration step for adhesion. This isn't a soak-off gel; it's a traditional acrylic system.

**Q: How does the odor compare to traditional LMA monomer?**
A: Significantly milder. It's not odorless, but you won't get that burning sensation in your sinuses. Most users report it's tolerable even in small rooms.

**Q: Does it work with any acrylic powder?**
A: Yes, EMA monomer is compatible with all standard acrylic powders — brand doesn't matter. Just match the powder-to-liquid ratio to your desired bead consistency.
