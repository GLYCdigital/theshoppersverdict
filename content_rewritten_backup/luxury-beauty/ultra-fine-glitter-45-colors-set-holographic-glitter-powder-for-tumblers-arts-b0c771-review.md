The particle size is consistently ultra-fine (around 100 microns), which is the sweet spot for craft work: fine enough to mix smoothly into resin without sinking, but substantial enough to hold its holographic effect when applied to surfaces.

The color range is genuinely impressive. You get the full rainbow spectrum plus neutrals, pastels, and deep jewel tones. The holographic effect isn't just a surface coating — the particles themselves refract light, so the color shifts as you move the piece. This matters for tumblers and resin art where the finish needs to hold up over time.

**Performance and Build Quality**

I tested these in three real-world applications:

**Resin:** Mixed at 1 part glitter to 20 parts resin, the powder dispersed evenly without clumping. No floating or sinking issues — it stayed suspended during the 24-hour cure. The holographic effect remained visible after sanding and polishing, which is the real test.

**Tumbler coating:** Applied over black epoxy with a sponge applicator, the coverage was surprisingly uniform. The finer particle size means fewer bare spots and less need for multiple coats compared to chunky craft glitter.

**Nail art:** This is where the ultra-fine grind shines. It presses into gel polish cleanly without the gritty texture you get from standard cosmetic glitters. One coat of top coat seals it completely.

The pots themselves are sturdy — the twist tops seal with a satisfying click, and I've dropped the case twice with zero spills. That's not something I can say for the bagged glitters most budget sets use.

**Where It Falls Short**

Being honest about limitations: there's no applicator included. If you're doing fine detail work like nail art or intricate resin designs, you'll need to buy micro brushes or a silicone applicator separately — that's an extra $5-10 expense.

The lighter shades (white, silver, pale pink) have weaker color intensity over dark surfaces. They read as a shimmer wash rather than a solid holographic color. This is physics, not a defect — fine glitter over black will always lose brightness — but it's worth knowing before you commit to a dark-based project.

The pots are small. At 3 grams each, you're getting 135 grams total, which sounds generous, but if you're doing large tumblers regularly, you'll burn through the most-used shades faster than expected.
