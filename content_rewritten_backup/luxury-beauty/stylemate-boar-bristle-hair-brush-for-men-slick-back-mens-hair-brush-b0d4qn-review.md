The first thing you notice is the bristle density. This isn't a sparse brush that skims the surface of your hair — the boar bristles are packed tight, which matters for the slick back technique. Boar bristles work by distributing your scalp's natural oils from root to tip, giving your hair that healthy, polished sheen without relying solely on product. The stiff bristles also provide genuine tension when you brush, which is what trains your hair to lay back flat against your scalp over time.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about limitations. If you're expecting this brush to give you that glossy, wet-look slick back straight out of the shower, you'll be disappointed. Boar bristles distribute oil — they don't create shine from nothing. You still need a styling product for that high-polish finish. The brush works best as part of a system: product in damp hair, then brush to distribute and set.

Also, the bristles are stiff out of the box. Several reviewers with sensitive scalps noted that the first week or two felt abrasive. It softens with use, but if you have a tender scalp, break it in gradually — a few gentle passes daily rather than aggressive brushing sessions.

## FAQ

**Will this brush work on short hair?**
Yes, but it's most effective on hair at least 2-3 inches long. Shorter hair won't have enough length for the bristles to grab and direct.

**How do I clean it?**
Remove loose hair with your fingers or a comb after each use. Once a week, wash the bristles with mild shampoo and warm water, then dry bristle-side down. Avoid soaking the wooden handle.

**Does it work with wet hair?**
It works best on damp or dry hair. Using it on soaking wet hair can strain the bristles and wooden handle over time.
