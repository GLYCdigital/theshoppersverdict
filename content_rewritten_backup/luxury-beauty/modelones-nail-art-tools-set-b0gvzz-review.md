Let's be direct: most nail art kits under $30 are landfill fodder. The Modelones set, however, punches well above its price point — with a few caveats worth knowing before you click buy.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 25-piece kit, but the math works in your favor. Most tools are dual-ended, meaning you're really getting 40+ functional tips. The breakdown includes:

- **Dotting tools** in five graduated sizes (0.5mm to 3mm) — the small end is genuinely tiny, which matters for fine floral work
- **Brushes** covering liners, detail, striping, and a fan brush for gradients
- **Carving/pick tools** for acrylic and gel work
- **A cuticle pusher** that's actually useful, not decorative

The stainless steel tips are the real story here. They resist acetone — I've soaked mine repeatedly without rust or pitting — and they haven't bent even during aggressive acrylic scraping. The wooden handles offer better tactile control than the slick plastic that plagues cheaper kits.

## Where It Shines

The dual-ended design isn't a gimmick. Having a 1mm dotter on one end and a fine liner on the other means you're not switching tools mid-design. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"I've bought three other sets trying to find one where the brushes actually hold a point. This is the first one that does."* That's consistent with what I found — the liner brushes maintain a sharp tip after multiple acetone cleansings.

For gel polish users, the carving tools are stiff enough to manipulate product without flexing, which is rare at this price. Beginners will appreciate the range; pros will appreciate that the set doesn't feel disposable.

## The Honest Shortfalls

**Brush ferrule quality is inconsistent.** Two of my brushes had loose metal collars where the bristles meet the handle. A drop of superglue fixed it, but you shouldn't have to fix a new product. Check yours immediately upon arrival.

**The case is cosmetic, not protective.** It's a basic vinyl zip pouch. Fine for a drawer, but don't toss this in a travel bag without padding — you'll get bent tips.

**The "25-piece" count inflates value.** Because most tools are dual-ended, you're getting fewer distinct implements than the number suggests. The variety is solid, but don't expect 25 unique tools.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Beginners** — the graduated sizes and brush variety let you experiment without investing in individual tools
- **DIY gel users** — the stiffness of the carving tools handles soak-off gel cleanly
- **Anyone replacing a worn-out kit** — the steel quality genuinely outlasts most competitors at this price

**Skip it if** you're a professional doing 10+ sets weekly. You'll want individual, replaceable brushes from pro supply houses. Also skip if you need a travel case — this pouch won't survive a checked bag.

## FAQ

**Q: Are the brushes suitable for gel polish?**
Yes. The synthetic bristles hold up to gel application and clean properly with monomer. Just wipe immediately after use — cured gel will ruin any brush.

**Q: Will the dotting tools scratch my nail plate?**
Not if used correctly. The tips are smooth and rounded. Pressing too hard can cause damage, but that's user error, not a tool flaw.

**Q: How do I clean the brushes?**
Use acetone or monomer immediately after use. Don't soak them — dip and wipe. Reshape the tips while damp and lay flat to dry.
