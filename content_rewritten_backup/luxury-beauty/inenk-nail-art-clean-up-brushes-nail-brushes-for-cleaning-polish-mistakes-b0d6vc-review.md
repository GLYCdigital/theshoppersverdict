Let’s be honest: most nail cleanup brushes are an afterthought. You grab a cheap pack, dip it in acetone, and pray the bristles don’t fan out like a cheap mascara wand. The INENK set tries to be different — five brushes, angled tips, and a price that undercuts most single brushes at a beauty supply store. But does it actually hold up for someone who does their nails weekly?

I tested these for three weeks, through gel manicures, regular polish, and one particularly messy glitter gradient. Here’s what you need to know before you click “add to cart.”

## What you actually get

The set includes five brushes with distinctly different tip shapes: two ultra-fine pointed brushes for tight cuticle lines, two angled flats for cleaning up the sides of the nail, and one slightly wider brush for flooding disasters. The handles are aluminum — light enough to feel delicate, but with a knurled grip section that keeps your fingers from slipping when your hands are slick with polish remover.

As shown in the product image, the bristles are a crisp white synthetic material. They’re stiffer than most drugstore cleanup brushes, which is a good thing — soft bristles collapse under pressure and smear polish instead of lifting it.

## Performance in real use

The angled brushes are the stars here. The 45-degree tip on the two larger brushes lets you run along the sidewall of your nail without dragging the brush across the nail plate itself. For cleanup around gel polish before curing, the fine tips pick up stray color without disturbing the main coat.

The brushes hold acetone well. A quick dip gets you enough liquid to wipe a full nail edge, and the bristles don’t drip onto your skin — a minor miracle at this price point.

One thing that surprised me: the handles are long enough (about 5.5 inches) that you can rest them against your palm while working, which gives you more control than shorter brushes. It’s a small detail, but it makes a difference when you’re doing your non-dominant hand.

## What buyers consistently praise

With 220 ratings at 4.4 stars, the feedback is mostly positive. One reviewer noted, “I’ve bought the expensive Japanese brushes and these work just as well for a fraction of the cost.” Another said, “The angled tip is perfect for getting into those corners without wrecking my manicure.”

Several users specifically mention using these with gel polish — the stiff bristles handle the thicker viscosity of gel without bending or losing shape.

## Where it falls short

The bristles soften over time. Not dramatically, but after about two weeks of steady use, the fine tips start to lose their sharp edge. If you’re doing someone else’s nails professionally, you’ll want to replace these every few months.

More annoying: there are no protective caps. If you toss these in a makeup bag or drawer, the tips will bend. I lost the shape of my favorite fine brush within a week of carrying it loose in my kit. A simple plastic cap would have fixed this.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You do your own nails at home and want a cleanup brush that actually works. The variety of tips means you’ll find one that fits your technique, and the price is low enough that replacing them isn’t painful.

**Skip it if:** You’re a professional nail tech doing multiple clients daily. You need brushes that hold their shape for months, not weeks, and you’re better off investing in higher-end brands. Also skip if you need brush caps — look for a set that includes them.

## FAQ

**Can these be used with gel polish?**
Yes. The stiff synthetic bristles handle gel viscosity well. Just clean them thoroughly after each use so the gel doesn’t cure onto the bristles.

**How do you clean them?**
Wipe them on a lint-free cloth dipped in acetone after each use. Don’t soak them in acetone overnight — that’s what causes the bristles to soften prematurely.

**Are they good for stamping cleanup?**
The fine-tipped brushes work well for removing excess stamping polish, but you’ll need to clean them immediately to prevent staining.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The INENK set delivers solid performance for home nail care at a price that makes the minor flaws forgivable. The angled brush design is genuinely thoughtful, the bristles hold up better than most budget options, and you get five different shapes for the cost of one mid-range brush.

The lack of caps and gradual bristle softening keep it from being a perfect score, but for the home user, these are a smart buy. Get the set, use the fine-tip brush for your cuticles, and don’t lose sleep when you need to replace them in a few months — they cost less than a single salon manicure.

**Buy it if** you want precision cleanup without spending $15 per brush. **Skip it if** you need professional-grade durability or brush protection. For everyone else, this is the rare beauty tool that overdelivers at its price point.
