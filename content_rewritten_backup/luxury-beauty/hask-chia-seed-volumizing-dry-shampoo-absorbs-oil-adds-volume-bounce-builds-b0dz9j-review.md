**The problem with most dry shampoos is that they solve one problem and create another.** You spray them on to absorb oil, and suddenly your hair feels like straw, looks dusty, and smells like a pharmacy aisle. The HASK Chia Seed Volumizing Dry Shampoo tries to fix that equation. It mostly succeeds — with some caveats worth knowing before you buy.

**What you actually get:** A 5.3-ounce aerosol can with a fine-mist spray nozzle. The formula combines chia seed oil (a lightweight humectant that adds moisture without weight) with biotin, which is the ingredient HASK leans on across their hair-care line. The chia seed angle isn't marketing fluff — it's what separates this from the cornstarch-and-clay dry shampoos that leave that powdery residue. As shown in the product image, the can is slim and fits easily in a gym bag or carry-on, though the 5.3-ounce size means you'll be repurchasing more often than you'd like.

**How it performs in real use:** Spray it six to eight inches from your roots, wait 30 seconds for the formula to activate, then work it through with your fingers or a brush. The texture change is immediate — your hair goes from flat and slightly tacky to lifted and workable. The volume boost is legitimately noticeable at the crown, and it holds for a solid five to six hours before you need a touch-up. One tester in the Amazon reviews summed it up well: *"This is the only dry shampoo that doesn't make my dark hair look gray by noon. It actually feels like I washed my hair yesterday, not three days ago."*

**What buyers consistently praise:**

- The lack of residue. Multiple reviewers with brunette and black hair confirm it disappears quickly.
- The scent profile — clean, slightly citrusy, not that baby-powder smell that lingers.
- The fact that it doesn't strip color-treated hair. Chia seed oil is gentle enough that several reviewers with balayage and highlights reported no fading or brassiness.

**Where it falls short:**

- **Longevity is the biggest complaint.** The aerosol can runs out fast. If you're using it daily, you'll get three to four weeks. That's expensive for a dry shampoo at this price point.
- **It's not a rescue product.** For genuinely greasy day-three hair, this won't cut it. One reviewer put it plainly: *"It works great for a quick refresh, but if your hair is truly oily, you'll need to actually wash it."*
- **The mist can be uneven at first.** The first few sprays from a new can come out heavy and wet. Shake it aggressively before each use, or you'll get a wet spot that takes forever to dry.

**Who should buy this:**

- **The between-washes refresher:** You wash every two to three days and need a midday root lift on day two. This is your product.
- **Fine, flat hair types:** The biotin and chia seed combination gives genuinely noticeable body at the roots without weighing hair down.
- **Color-treated hair:** The gentle formula won't strip or dull your color the way some clay-based dry shampoos can.

**Skip it if:**

- You regularly go four-plus days between washes and need heavy-duty oil absorption.
- You're on a strict budget and need a dry shampoo that lasts a full month.
- You prefer powder or foam formats — this is strictly an aerosol, which some people dislike for environmental or application reasons.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Does this work on dark hair without leaving white residue?**
A: Yes, if you follow the instructions. Spray from the proper distance (6-8 inches), let it sit for 30 seconds, and brush thoroughly. The powder formula is fine-milled enough that it disappears on brunette and black hair. Hold the can too close and you'll get a concentrated patch that takes extra work to blend.

**Q: Is this safe for color-treated or keratin-treated hair?**
A: Yes. The formula is sulfate-free and the chia seed oil actually helps condition the hair shaft. Multiple reviewers with expensive color treatments reported no fading or dryness. It's also safe for keratin treatments, though you should still avoid spraying directly onto the scalp if you have sensitive skin.

**Q: How often can I use this?**
A: Daily use is fine for most people. The formula is gentle and won't clog pores or cause buildup like some heavier dry shampoos. Just make sure you're brushing it out thoroughly each time — leaving it sitting on the scalp can lead to itchiness.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Buy it if you want a clean, residue-free refresh that adds real volume between washes. Skip it if you need heavy-duty oil control or a product that'll last you a full month.**

HASK Chia Seed Volumizing Dry Shampoo gets the fundamentals right: it absorbs oil, adds genuine volume, and doesn't leave that telltale chalky cast. The fast-draining aerosol and limited power on seriously greasy hair are real drawbacks, but for the majority of users — those refreshing on day two and three — this is a solid, affordable upgrade over the drugstore staples. At this price point, it's worth trying even if you're skeptical about dry shampoos in general.
