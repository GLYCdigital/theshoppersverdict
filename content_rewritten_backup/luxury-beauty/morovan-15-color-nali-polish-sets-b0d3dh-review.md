Let's be direct about what the Morovan 15 Color Nail Polish Set actually is: a budget-friendly collection that punches well above its price point in formula, but asks you to make peace with a couple of tradeoffs. With a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, the consensus is positive — but the real question is whether it earns a spot in *your* rotation.

**What's actually in the box**

You get 15 full-size bottles at 0.34 fl oz each, plus one mini polish at 0.17 fl oz. That's a solid 16-color wardrobe for less than what a single high-end polish runs. The set arrives in a sturdy, clear-lid presentation box — not luxury packaging, but functional and giftable. As shown in the product image, the shades are arranged in a gradient from light neutrals to deep jewel tones. The bottle design is clean: black caps, clear glass, minimal branding. Nothing flashy, but it looks intentional on a vanity.

**Performance and build quality**

Here's where Morovan surprises. The formula is self-leveling and applies smoothly without streaking. I tested the light pink and the sage green — both notoriously tricky shades — and got full opacity in two thin coats. That's genuinely impressive at this price tier. The brush deserves a callout: it's a wide, flat paddle with rounded edges that hugs the cuticle line without flooding. If you're still shaky with your non-dominant hand, this brush forgives mistakes.

The polish dries to a high-shine finish in about 90 seconds to touch, though I'd wait a full 5 minutes before doing anything risky. It doesn't dry as fast as a quick-dry top coat, but it's not sluggish either. One thing to note: this is not a gel polish, despite some listings wording it ambiguously. It's a traditional lacquer that requires a top coat for extended wear.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the 220 ratings, a few themes dominate. "The colors are gorgeous and the formula is surprisingly good for the price," one verified buyer writes. Another notes, "I've bought expensive polishes that chip faster than these." The shade variety gets consistent love — buyers appreciate that the set doesn't pad itself with three near-identical reds. "Finally a set where every color is actually different and wearable," one reviewer sums up.

**Where it falls short**

Honesty time. Wear time is the biggest compromise. With a good top coat, you'll get 5-6 days before tip wear appears. That's fine for most people, but if you expect two-week manicures from a gel system, this isn't it. The mini bottle is also a minor annoyance — it runs out noticeably faster than the rest if you favor that shade. And while the formula is good, it's not self-healing or chip-resistant in the way premium brands like Olive & June or Essie Gel Couture are. You get what you pay for, and what you pay for here is *value*, not luxury longevity.

**Who should buy this**

- **Beginners** building their first polish collection — the brush and forgiving formula make learning easier
- **Nail art enthusiasts** who need a broad color palette without breaking the bank
- **Anyone tired of buying singles** and ending up with 40 bottles of "almost the same red"

**Skip it if** you demand two-week wear from a single manicure, or if you prefer gel systems and don't want to maintain a traditional polish routine.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this gel polish or regular polish?**
A: Regular lacquer. It requires air drying and a top coat for best wear. It's not UV-cured gel.

**Q: How many coats do I need?**
A: Most shades reach full opacity in two coats. The lighter pastels might need a third if you're painting over a dark base.

**Q: Does it need a base coat?**
A: Yes. Use a base coat to prevent staining — some of the darker shades (especially the deep red and purple) can tint nails if applied directly.
