# Revlon Fire & Ice Perfume Review: The 90s Icon That Still Delivers

Most fragrances from the 90s smell like a time capsule you’re afraid to open. Revlon’s Fire & Ice is the exception. It’s bold, it’s loud, and it knows exactly what it is. And with 235 Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars, I’m clearly not the only one who thinks this vintage powerhouse still holds up.

## What You Actually Get

The package is straightforward: a 1 fl oz (30ml) Eau de Toilette spray in a frosted glass bottle with a crimson-and-ice gradient design. It’s compact enough for a purse, though the plastic cap feels flimsy — a common complaint among reviewers. There’s no box theatrics or magnetic closures here. This is a drugstore classic in the best and worst sense.

As shown in the product image, the bottle is simple and functional. If you’re buying for the packaging, look elsewhere. If you’re buying for the scent, keep reading.

## Performance and Sillage

Here’s where Fire & Ice surprises. For an Eau de Toilette at this price point, the performance is genuinely impressive. The opening hits you with a sharp, sweet burst of peach, jasmine, and carnation — think ripe fruit and white flowers doused in sugar. That initial 10 minutes can feel a bit aggressive, almost alcohol-heavy. One reviewer put it plainly: "The first spray is loud, but give it 15 minutes and it settles into something gorgeous."

And that dry-down is the magic. The florals soften into a warm base of musk, sandalwood, and vanilla that clings close to the skin. Most users report 6-8 hours of wear, which is remarkable for an EDT. The sillage is noticeable but not nuclear — you’ll get compliments from people within arm’s reach, not from across the room.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the scent’s complexity and longevity. One verified buyer wrote: "I wore this in high school and repurchased 20 years later. It still gets me compliments at work. Longevity is shocking for the price." Another noted: "This is a warm, confident floral that doesn't smell like everything else on the shelf."

The value proposition is also a major draw. Multiple reviewers mentioned buying it as a backup or gift because the price-to-performance ratio is unbeatable.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides. The opening is sharp and can trigger headaches if you overspray. Two sprays max — this isn’t a scent you douse. The bottle design genuinely feels outdated, and the cap has a tendency to crack over time. Also, if your taste runs toward fresh, aquatic, or minimalist scents (think Clean Reserve or Glossier You), this will feel heavy and dated. It’s a fragrance for people who like their perfume to announce itself.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The 90s nostalgic**: If you remember this from your youth, it’s a faithful reissue that still works.
- **The value hunter**: You want a floral with serious staying power without spending $100+.
- **The compliment-seeker**: This gets noticed. Be prepared for questions.

Skip it if you prefer subtle, skin-scent perfumes or if you’re sensitive to strong florals.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this a perfume or a cologne?**
A: It’s marketed as a women’s Eau de Toilette. It’s unisex-leaning-feminine in practice — the musk and sandalwood base make it wearable for anyone who likes bold florals.

**Q: How long does the scent last on skin?**
A: Expect 6-8 hours on skin, longer on clothing. It’s one of the longest-lasting EDTs at this price.

**Q: Does it smell like the original 1990s version?**
A: Nearly identical. Some long-time users say the current formulation is slightly less intense in the opening, but the dry-down is faithful.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a powerful, classic floral with real longevity and a price that makes the "expensive" stuff look silly. **Skip it if** your collection is all clean musks and citrus colognes — this will clash with your vibe.

For a 90s icon that still earns its place on a modern vanity, Fire & Ice is a 4.6/5. It’s loud, it’s warm, and it’s worth every penny.
