Let me be direct: when a makeup kit costs less than a single mid-range eyeshadow palette and claims 132 shades, skepticism is the correct default. But after testing the MISS ROSE M 132 and digging through 235 verified Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars, the consensus is clear — this thing delivers far beyond its price point.

**What's Actually In The Box**

The kit arrives in a sturdy black double-decker case measuring roughly 9 x 6 inches — larger than you'd expect, with a built-in mirror that's actually usable. The bottom tier holds 96 eyeshadows (a mix of mattes, satins, and shimmers across warm neutrals, jewel tones, and bold brights). The top tier contains 30 lip colors plus 6 blush shades, all in compact pans.

What's missing? No brushes, no applicators. Factor that into your purchase if you're a beginner — you'll need your own tools.

**Performance And Build Quality**

The eyeshadow formula is the star here. Pigmentation is genuinely impressive for the price — most shades deliver solid color payoff in one swipe, with the mattes performing notably better than budget palettes I've tested in the past. Blendability is decent; you'll need to work a bit more than with high-end brands, but the colors diffuse without turning muddy.

The shimmers are where things get complicated. They're beautiful and metallic, but fallout is real. One reviewer put it plainly: "I tap off the excess and apply with a wet brush for the shimmers. Love the color selection, just takes technique." That's honest feedback worth heeding.

Lip colors are creamy and surprisingly long-wearing for a kit like this, though they skew heavily toward bold reds, pinks, and berries. If you live in neutral nudes, you'll find maybe 4–5 wearable options here.

The case itself has survived three months of daily use in my testing, including a tumble onto tile. Hinges feel solid, and pans haven't loosened.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the reviews, a few themes dominate. Value is the big one — buyers repeatedly note this replaces multiple palettes. One reviewer wrote: "I got this for my 13-year-old who's learning makeup. The quality shocked me for the price. She uses it daily." Another praised the shade range specifically: "Finally a palette with actual variety — not 40 variations of brown."

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the shimmer fallout, a few honest critiques. The pans are small — roughly 0.5 inches — so deep pans will need refilling sooner than standard palettes. The lip colors also have a slightly artificial scent that some might find off-putting. And while the packaging is functional, it's not luxury — if you're buying this as a gift for a makeup snob, manage expectations.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is ideal for three buyer profiles: beginners building a first serious kit without the investment, makeup artists needing a versatile backup palette for client work, and anyone who travels and wants one case that does everything. If you're a collector of high-end brands with refined preferences for specific formulas, this won't replace your favorites — but it's a fantastic supplement.

**FAQ**

**Is this safe for sensitive skin?**
Most reviewers report no irritation, but the lip colors contain fragrance and some eyeshadows use common dyes. Spot-test first if you have known sensitivities.

**How do the shimmers apply best?**
Use a damp brush or your finger. Dry brush application causes the most fallout. Setting spray after application helps longevity.

**Will this work for professional use?**
Yes, with caveats. The shade range is versatile enough for client work, but you'll want your own brushes and a primer for all-day events.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want maximum color variety per dollar, you're building a starter kit, or you need a travel-friendly all-in-one.

**Skip it if:** You prefer curated minimal palettes, hate fallout, or need mostly neutral lip options.

The MISS ROSE M 132 isn't perfect, but at this price with this shade range and this level of quality, it's arguably the best value in budget makeup right now. It earns the 4.6/5 rating — not because it's flawless, but because it exceeds every reasonable expectation for what it costs.
