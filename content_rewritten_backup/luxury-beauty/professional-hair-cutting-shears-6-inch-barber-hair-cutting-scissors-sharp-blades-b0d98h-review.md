## What You Actually Get

The box contains the shears, a basic vinyl case, and a small oil cloth for maintenance. That's it—no finger rest, no extra screws, no frills. The shears themselves are 6 inches overall with a 2.5-inch cutting blade, made from what the listing describes as ice-tempered Japanese stainless steel. ## Performance: Where the Rubber Meets the Hair

The blade geometry is what separates these from drugstore scissors. The convex edge glides through damp hair without snagging, and the tension screw actually holds its setting—a common failure point on budget shears. One verified buyer noted, "I've been cutting hair for 12 years and these rival my $200 pair from the salon supply store. The sharpness is unreal for the price." Another mentioned they'd used them for six months of daily cuts "without needing a sharpening yet."

But let's be clear about what you're not getting. These aren't precision-ground to the exacting tolerances of a $300 Japanese hand-forged pair. For fine, detailed texturizing work or slide cutting on thick coarse hair, you'll feel the difference. The blade gap isn't perfectly uniform across the entire edge—minor, but noticeable to a trained hand.

## The Good, The Bad, The Honest

**What buyers consistently praise:** The sharpness out of the box, the ergonomic offset handles, and how well they hold an edge. The adjustment screw is a genuine standout—you can fine-tune the tension without hunting for a tiny wrench.

## FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Do these work on thick, coarse hair?**
A: Yes, for basic cutting. The blade is sharp enough for bulk removal, but fine texturizing on very thick hair takes more passes than premium shears. Keep them oiled and they'll handle most textures.

**Q: Can these be sharpened?**
A: Yes, and they hold up to sharpening well. A standard shear sharpening service can handle them, and the steel responds well to honing. Just don't expect them to last a decade of pro use.

**Q: Are they suitable for left-handed barbers?**
A: No. The offset handles are molded for right-hand use. Lefties will find them uncomfortable and imprecise. Look for ambidextrous or dedicated left-handed shears instead.
