# Does the $603 NuFACE Trinity+ Actually Sculpt Your Face? A Brutally Honest Review

You've seen the before-and-afters. The glowing cheeks. The lifted brows. The promise of a non-surgical facelift you can do while watching Netflix. The NuFACE Trinity+ is the most recognizable name in at-home microcurrent — but at $603, you're not buying a gadget. You're buying a commitment. Here's what you need to know before you click "buy."

## What's Actually in the Box

The kit arrives in a sleek Sandy Rose finish that looks more like a luxury cosmetic than a medical device. Inside you get:

- **The Trinity+ device** with the interchangeable ELE (Energizing Lift Electrode) attachment pre-installed
- **Full-size Gel Activator (2 oz)** — this is critical, not optional
- **Silk Creme moisturizer** — a nice post-treatment step
- Charging cradle, USB cable, and the instructional booklet

As shown in the product image, the Sandy Rose colorway is genuinely pretty — it won't look out of place on your bathroom counter. But here's the thing: the Trinity+ is a **system**, not a one-time purchase. The device has three attachment points, and while you get the standard ELE head, the Lip/Eye attachment and the Wrinkle Reducer are sold separately. That's where this kit gets expensive fast if you want the full experience.

## How It Actually Performs

The Trinity+ delivers 335 microamps of current — the same class of output used in professional microcurrent facials. The science is sound: low-level electrical stimulation contracts facial muscles, and over time, consistent use creates a toning effect similar to how a gym workout builds muscle.

**The 5-minute daily protocol is real, and it works — if you're consistent.** Most users report seeing subtle changes around week 3-4, with more noticeable lifting at the 60-day mark. The device has three intensity settings, and I'd recommend starting on level 1 for your first week. The sensation is a mild tingling — not painful, but definitely noticeable.

The included Gel Activator is **mandatory** — the device won't glide properly without it, and using it dry will cause micro-shocks. This is both a pro and a con: it forces you to use the correct technique, but it also means you're tethered to an ongoing consumable purchase.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides:

**1. The price is real.** At $603, this is competing with entry-level professional treatments. You need to be honest with yourself about whether you'll actually use it daily.

**2. The gel is a perpetual expense.** The 2 oz tube lasts roughly 6-8 weeks with daily use. At ~$30 per refill, that's an ongoing cost many don't factor in.

**3. The learning curve is steeper than expected.** The device requires specific upward motions and correct pressure. The instructional materials help, but there's a reason NuFACE has tutorial videos — it takes practice to get the technique right.

**4. Results are gradual.** This is not a quick fix. You're looking at 60+ days of consistent use before you'll see the kind of results shown in marketing images.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're already consistent with skincare routines, you're between the ages of 30-55 with early signs of laxity, and you're willing to commit 5 minutes daily for 60+ days. If you've tried creams and serums without satisfaction, this is the next logical step before considering injectables.

**Skip it if:** You're looking for instant results, you're not ready for an ongoing consumable expense, or you have a history of abandoning beauty devices after two weeks. Also skip if you have a pacemaker, are pregnant, or have metal implants in the treatment area — the FDA clearance doesn't override those contraindications.

## FAQ

**Q: How long until I see results?**
A: Most users notice subtle changes at 3-4 weeks, with meaningful lifting at 8-12 weeks of daily use. The clinical studies behind the technology used 60-day protocols.

**Q: Can I use a cheaper gel instead of the NuFACE Activator?**
A: Technically yes, but you need a conductive gel. Standard aloe vera won't work — the conductivity is too low. If you want to save money, look for ultrasound gels with similar conductivity ratings, but be aware that using the wrong gel can reduce effectiveness or cause discomfort.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
A: The device holds a charge for about 30 minutes of continuous use — roughly a week of daily 5-minute sessions. Charging takes about 3 hours via the included cradle.
