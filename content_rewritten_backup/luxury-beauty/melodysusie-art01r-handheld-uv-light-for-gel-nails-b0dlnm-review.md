**The MelodySusie ART01R isn't your typical nail lamp.** Most UV curers are bulky boxes that chain you to a desk. This one is a sleek, pen-style wand that fits in your palm — and after 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clearly filling a real gap for people who want gel nails without the stationary setup.

**What you actually get:** The box contains the ART01R wand itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a slim storage pouch. No wall adapter — you'll use your phone charger or laptop port. The device measures about 6 inches long and weighs 5.9 ounces, so it's genuinely pocketable. The curing window is a U-shaped opening that slides over your finger, with 36W of combined UV and LED output. That's not just marketing spec — it's enough power to cure standard gel polishes in 30 to 60 seconds per coat, which I verified against a few popular brands.

**Real-world performance:** The cordless freedom is the headline feature, and it delivers. I used it for a mid-week thumb repair without dragging out my full lamp setup. The single button cycles through a 60-second curing mode; press and hold for continuous operation. It's dead simple. One buyer noted, *"I was skeptical about a handheld curing, but this cured my gel just as fast as my full-size lamp."* Another echoed that sentiment: *"Perfect for travel — I did a complete set on a plane tray table."* That's the core value proposition: salon-quality curing without the footprint.

**Where it falls short — honestly:** The battery is the main compromise. You'll get roughly 40 minutes of cumulative curing time per charge. A full manicure with base, two color coats, and top coat on both hands eats about 25–30 minutes of that, so it's workable — but if you're doing builder gel or thicker applications, you might hit the low-battery warning mid-set. The lack of timer presets (10s/30s/60s) is also a minor annoyance. You're stuck with the single 60-second cycle or manual hold, which means overcuring thin coats if you're not paying attention. Finally, some thicker top coats needed a second pass — not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**Who should buy this:** Three personas come to mind. First, the frequent traveler who wants to maintain gel nails on the road without packing a bulky lamp. Second, the DIY manicurist who does touch-ups and single-nail fixes more often than full sets. Third, anyone with limited storage — this replaces a lamp that takes up a full drawer. Skip it if you're doing weekly full sets on yourself and others; a stationary 48W lamp will serve you better with faster, more consistent cures.

**FAQ:**

*Does it work with all gel polishes?* Most standard gel polishes will cure in 30–60 seconds. Very thick builder gels or certain hard gels may need a longer hold or two passes — that's true of any handheld unit.

*How long does the battery take to charge?* About 2 hours via USB-C for a full charge. There's an indicator light that turns green when ready.

*Is it safe for skin?* The U-shaped design only exposes the nail plate, and the unit auto-off after 60 seconds prevents accidental overexposure. Standard UV safety applies — don't stare into the light.

**The Verdict:** Buy it if you value portability and convenience over raw speed, and you're comfortable with the battery limitations. Skip it if you're a heavy user doing multiple full sets weekly — you'll want a plug-in lamp. At 4.6/5, this is a genuinely useful tool that does exactly what it promises, with honest tradeoffs.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Versatile, well-built, and genuinely portable. Just keep the charging cable nearby for marathon sessions.
