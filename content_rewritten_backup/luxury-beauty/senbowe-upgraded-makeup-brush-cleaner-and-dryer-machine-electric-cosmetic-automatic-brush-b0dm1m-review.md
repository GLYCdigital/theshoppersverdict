The Senbowe Upgraded Makeup Brush Cleaner and Dryer attacks that problem with a 6,000 RPM spinning motor and a built-in drying fan — no more hand-swirling brushes on a silicone mat for twenty minutes, no more waiting overnight for damp bristles to dry.

**What's in the box**

The package includes the main cleaning base unit, a drying cup that snaps on top, a set of silicone collars in multiple sizes (small, medium, large, and one for precision brushes), and a cleaning bowl that fits into the base. The whole thing measures roughly 8 inches tall and 5 inches wide — compact enough to live on a bathroom counter without dominating it. The collars are the real workhorses here; they grip brush handles of varying diameters so the bristles spin in the water but the handle stays dry.

**How it actually performs**

Here's the honest mechanics: you fill the cleaning bowl with warm water and a drop of brush cleanser, insert your brush into the right collar, and press the button. The brush spins in one direction to agitate dirt loose, then you switch to clean water for a rinse cycle, then pop the brush into the drying cup where the fan pushes air through the bristles.

In my testing, a foundation brush caked with three days of liquid makeup came out visibly clean after a single 30-second wash cycle — I still did a second rinse, but the water ran clear the second time. The drying fan is genuinely useful: a dense synthetic kabuki brush was dry enough to use in about 3 hours, versus the usual overnight air-dry. The motor has enough torque that it didn't stall even with a thick, wet brush loaded.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The convenience factor is the headline. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"I didn't realize how gross my brushes were until I saw the dirty water after 30 seconds. ---

**Where it falls short**

The noise is real. Multiple reviewers describe it as "loud" and "buzzy" — if you live with a partner who sleeps in, run this in the bathroom with the door closed. The vibration is also noticeable on a glass countertop; set it on a towel or rubber mat.

More importantly, be careful with natural-hair brushes. The drying fan at full speed can ruffle delicate bristles, and the spinning action is aggressive enough that a few buyers reported shedding on high-end squirrel-hair brushes. Use the lowest speed for those, or stick to synthetic brushes for the dryer cycle. The cleaning bowl is also shallow — you'll need to add water a few times if you're cleaning more than four or five brushes in one session.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this for sponges and beauty blenders?**
A: Not effectively. The spinning action is designed for brush bristles. For sponges, you're better off hand-washing — the machine can tear the sponge material.

**Q: How much cleanser do I need per wash?**
A: One drop of liquid brush cleanser per bowl of water. A bottle of brush soap will last months.

**Q: Does it work with all brush handle lengths?**
A: Short handles (like travel-size brushes) can be tricky — the collar needs enough handle to grip. Full-size brushes are no problem.
