Six lipsticks for the price of one mid-range tube. That's the pitch, and honestly, it's a good one. The firstfly Crystal Flower Jelly Lipstick set delivers exactly what the name promises: jelly-textured color with a dewy, glass-like finish. But is it worth your money, or are you just getting six tubes of tinted gloss? After digging through 210 ratings and testing the formula's claims, here's the real story.

**What's actually in the box**

You get six full-size lipsticks (roughly 3.5g each) in a range of pinks, corals, and reds. The packaging is slim and clear, letting you see the color without opening every tube — a small but genuinely useful touch. The floral cap design is pretty but underwhelming in person; it's lightweight plastic that won't survive a drop on tile. For the price per unit, that's an acceptable tradeoff, but don't expect luxury weight.

**Performance and wear**

The formula is where this set earns its keep. It glides on like a balm — no dragging, no tugging — and the jelly texture sits somewhere between a tinted lip balm and a gloss. The finish is glossy without being sticky, and it genuinely hydrates. One buyer put it plainly: "My lips feel softer after wearing this, not dried out like other lipsticks." That's backed by the ingredient list: shea butter and vitamin E do real work here.

Wear time is decent but not extraordinary. You'll get 3-4 hours before it fades to a stain, and it survives coffee, but not meals. It's not transfer-proof, so expect marks on your mug and mask. The color payoff is sheer-to-medium; the lighter shades need a few swipes for visible color, while the deeper reds build nicely. One reviewer noted, "The pink shades are more subtle than the photos suggest — you'll need to layer."

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 210 ratings, three themes stand out. First, value: "Six colors for under $20 is unbeatable." Second, hydration: multiple reviewers mention it outperforms pricier balms. Third, the shades are wearable — no duds in the set, just different intensities. The 4.2-star average holds up because most buyers feel they got more than they paid for.

**Where it falls short**

The cons are real. The packaging feels cheap — one reviewer called it "cute but flimsy." The lighter shades can settle into lip lines after a few hours, so exfoliate beforehand. And if you're looking for a bold, matte, long-wearing lipstick, this isn't it. The jelly formula is deliberately sheer and dewy, which is great for every day but not for events that demand staying power.

**Who should buy this**

This set is perfect for three types of shoppers: makeup minimalists who want variety without commitment, anyone with dry lips who hates the chalky feel of mattes, and gift-givers who want a practical, pretty present. Skip it if you need transfer-proof wear, prefer bold opaque color, or want packaging that looks premium on your vanity.

**FAQ**

*Is this lipstick actually long-lasting?* It lasts 3-4 hours with light eating and drinking. It fades gracefully to a tint rather than flaking, which is a plus, but it's not a 8-hour lipstick.

*Does it contain any harsh ingredients?* The formula is free of parabens and includes moisturizing agents like shea butter and vitamin E. It's gentle enough for sensitive lips.

*Can you build the color to full opacity?* Yes, but it takes 3-4 swipes for the deeper shades. The lighter pinks stay semi-sheer no matter how much you layer.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want six wearable, hydrating shades that feel like a balm and look like a gloss — at a price that makes experimentation painless.

**Skip it if** you need transfer-proof, high-pigment color with premium packaging. This is a budget-friendly everyday staple, not a luxury statement piece.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Great value, honest formula, and real hydration. The flimsy packaging and sheer payoff cost it a perfect score, but for the price, this set overdelivers.
