Cabinet hardware is the jewelry of a kitchen — and like jewelry, you notice the cheap stuff immediately. The Satanga Cabinet Handle Pulls sit firmly in the "surprisingly good for the price" category, but they're not without a quirk or two. After digging through 235 ratings and the physical specs, here's what actually matters.

**What you're getting**

The box contains the pulls themselves plus mounting screws. That's it — no paper template, no drill guide. The pulls are zinc alloy with a plated finish (brushed nickel and matte black are the popular options based on the product listing). Standard hole spacing is 3 inches on center for the smaller pulls, with larger sizes available depending on the variant you order. The screws included are sized for typical 3/4-inch cabinet faces, which covers most residential kitchen and bathroom cabinets.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

Zinc alloy is the right call at this price point — it's denser and more substantial than the hollow aluminum you find on bargain-bin pulls. Buyers consistently note the weight feels right in the hand. One reviewer put it plainly: *"These feel like the $8-per-handle ones at the big box store, not the $2 junk on Amazon."* The finish holds up to regular use, including wiping down with standard kitchen cleaners.

Installation is straightforward if you've mounted hardware before. The threads are clean, and the screws bite into wood without splitting — even in particle-board cabinet faces, which is where cheaper hardware often fails. Tighten with a screwdriver, not a drill. More than a few ratings mention stripping the screw heads when using a driver set to high torque. That's user error, but Satanga could have used beefier Phillips heads.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The consistency of the finish across multiple pieces gets frequent mention. If you're replacing hardware on a full kitchen, you're buying 10-20 of these, and color matching matters. One buyer noted: *"Ordered 16 in matte black — every single one matched. No weird variations between batches."* The anti-rust plating also holds up in humid environments like bathrooms, which is a common failure point for budget pulls.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of a drilling template is the biggest practical downside. If you're doing a full kitchen reno and your cabinets lack existing holes, you'll need to measure precisely — a misaligned handle is noticeable from across the room. Also, the included screws are standard Phillips, not the Torx or square-drive heads you see on premium hardware. They work fine, but they're more prone to cam-out if you rush.

**Who should buy these**

- **DIY renovators** updating a kitchen or bathroom on a budget — these deliver 90% of the feel of premium hardware at a fraction of the cost.
- **Landlords or flippers** needing consistent hardware across multiple units without overspending.
- **Anyone replacing existing pulls** where the hole spacing matches — swap is a 10-minute job per cabinet.

Skip these if you're outfitting a luxury custom kitchen where you want hand-finished solid brass, or if you need a specific vintage/artisan aesthetic. These are clean, modern, and utilitarian — not statement pieces.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these fit standard 3-inch hole spacing?**  
Yes, the standard variant uses 3-inch on-center spacing, which fits most pre-drilled cabinets. Measure your existing holes before ordering — some larger sizes use different spacing.

**Q: Will the finish fade or peel over time?**  
Based on buyer feedback, the plated finish holds up well for 12+ months of daily use. Avoid abrasive cleaners; a damp cloth is all you need.

**Q: What if I need a different screw length?**  
The included screws work for 3/4-inch faces. For thicker doors or drawer fronts with backing plates, pick up #8 x 1-inch screws at any hardware store — they're standard.

**Skip it if:** You need a drilling template for a from-scratch install, or you demand luxury-grade materials like solid brass.
