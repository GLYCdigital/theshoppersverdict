# Tresda 100W COB Rechargeable Work Light: Real Brightness, Real Battery, Real Tradeoffs

If you've ever squinted at a dark engine bay or tried to run a cord across a wet driveway, you know the drill: most rechargeable work lights are either too dim to matter or too bulky to use. The Tresda 100W COB sits in a sweet spot — bright enough to replace a corded halogen, but portable enough to toss in a tool bag. After 220 Amazon ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's clearly doing something right. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the light itself, a Type-C charging cable, a metal hook for hanging, and a small tripod stand that extends to about 5 feet. No wall charger — you'll use your phone charger or a USB port. The light measures roughly 8 x 5 x 2 inches and weighs about 2.4 pounds. The COB (chip-on-board) LED panel sits behind a durable aluminum housing with a built-in kickstand that folds flat.

**Three brightness modes** — 100%, 50%, and a low eco mode — plus a separate COB area light and a side-emitting strip for wider coverage. The controls are two simple buttons on the back: one for power/mode, one for the side light. No confusing multi-click sequences to memorize.

## Performance: The Numbers Match the Hype

At full 100W output, this thing genuinely pushes 7000 lumens. To put that in perspective: a typical 60W garage fluorescent tube puts out around 3000 lumens. The Tresda covers roughly a 20x20 foot area with usable light at max setting. Color temperature is a neutral 6500K daylight — harsh but excellent for seeing fine detail, less ideal for photography or warm ambient work.

The battery is a 100Wh lithium pack. Real-world runtime at full blast runs about 2 hours. Drop to 50% and you get roughly 4-5 hours — still plenty bright for most tasks. The eco mode stretches to 8+ hours but output drops to maybe 1500 lumens, which is fine for reading or close work.

Charging via Type-C takes about 3.5 hours from empty. The light has a battery indicator with four LEDs, so you're not guessing when it's about to die.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The pattern across reviews is remarkably consistent. One buyer wrote: *"Used it for a weekend engine swap in a dark garage — it lit the whole bay like daylight. My corded halogen went in the trash."* Another noted: *"Took it camping, dropped it on rocks, got rained on — still works perfectly."*

The IP65 waterproof rating is real, not marketing fluff. The housing has proper rubber gaskets, and the USB port is covered by a flap. I'd trust this in light rain or on a damp concrete floor without worry.

The tripod is a genuinely useful addition. It extends to a working height that puts the light at chest level when seated — perfect for under-dash work or wiring projects.

## Where It Falls Short

**Battery life at max is the biggest compromise.** Two hours at 7000 lumens is fine for a focused task, but if you're doing a full day of work, you'll need to plan charging breaks or carry a power bank. This isn't a flaw — it's physics — but it's worth knowing before you buy.

**The 2.4-pound weight** makes ceiling work uncomfortable. If you're hanging it overhead for extended periods, the metal hook helps, but your neck will feel it.

**The stand is a bit wobbly at full extension.** It's fine on flat surfaces, but on gravel or uneven ground, it wants to tip. The hook is your better option outdoors.

**No included wall charger** is mildly annoying, though most people have a Type-C brick lying around.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY mechanics** doing weekend repairs — this replaces a trouble light and a drop light in one unit
- **Electricians and plumbers** working in crawl spaces or attics where cords are a hazard
- **Campers and overlanders** who need reliable, water-resistant light for setup or repairs at night
- **Anyone doing interior painting or drywall** who needs even, shadow-free illumination

Skip it if you need all-day runtime at full brightness, or if you prioritize ultra-lightweight portability over output.

## FAQ

**Q: Can you use it while it's charging?**
A: Yes, but the light draws more power than the charger supplies at max brightness. You'll drain the battery slowly while plugged in at 100%, but 50% mode charges while running.

**Q: Is the 7000 lumen rating accurate?**
A: Independent tests and user feedback confirm it's close to real. The COB panel is efficient and the driver is decent. It's not a fake "100W equivalent" marketing number.

**Q: Does it come with a case?**
A: No. It ships in a cardboard box. The light itself is rugged, but you'll want a soft pouch or tool bag for transport.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a genuinely bright, cordless, weather-resistant work light that won't break the bank. The Tresda 100W delivers real 7000-lumen output with a usable battery, solid build quality, and IP65 durability that most competitors at this price don't offer.

**Skip it if** you need all-day runtime at max output, or if you're doing overhead work where 2.4 pounds becomes a burden. Also skip if you want a hard carrying case included.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong, honest performer with a couple of compromises you should know about. Well worth the money for most buyers.
