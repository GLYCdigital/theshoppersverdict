**A Real Shower for Your Clawfoot Tub — Without the Plumber**

If you own a clawfoot tub, you know the struggle: soaking is glorious, but rinsing off means a handheld sprayer that leaves shampoo in your eyes or a cold, awkward shuffle to a separate shower. The RX2300 Add-On Shower solves that with a wall-mounted fixed showerhead and a D-shaped rod that keeps water inside the tub. Here's the honest breakdown.

---

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains a chrome-finished showerhead, a 54-inch D-shaped curtain rod, a diverter valve that attaches to your tub's faucet spout, a metal hose, mounting hardware, and the curtain hooks. The rod's D-shape is the key design win — it follows the curve of the tub, giving you roughly 10 extra inches of elbow room at the foot end compared to a straight rod. That's not marketing speak; it's the difference between bumping your elbows on a shower curtain and having a functional space.

---

**Performance and Build Quality**

The diverter valve screws directly onto your existing faucet threads — no soldering, no cutting copper, no calling a plumber. Flip the lever and water routes from the tub spout to the showerhead. The chrome finish is polished and consistent, matching most vintage-style fixtures well. The hose is braided stainless steel, which resists kinking better than the rubber hoses you'll find on cheaper kits.

The showerhead itself is fixed and angled — it's not a handheld, so you won't be using it to wash the dog or rinse the tub walls. That's fine for most people, but worth knowing before you buy. The metal rod is sturdy, but the mounting flanges are plastic. They're not cheap-looking, but if you're the type who likes to hang wet towels on the rod, keep that habit in check — the plastic brackets are the weakest link.

---

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.1-star average with 204 ratings tells a story. The overwhelming majority of positive reviews center on one theme: **it actually works without a plumber**. One buyer put it plainly: *"I was dreading this project, but it took me 20 minutes. The diverter fit my faucet perfectly and the pressure is great."* Another noted the rod's real-world value: *"The D-shape makes the curtain feel like it's wrapping around you. No more cold air blasting in from the sides."* The included hardware is also a point of praise — buyers report the screws and anchors are usable, not the flimsy drywall anchors you often have to toss.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

Honesty time. The instructions are borderline useless — a single sheet with tiny, low-contrast graphics. You'll likely need to watch a YouTube video or figure out the diverter orientation by trial and error. Also, the mounting brackets are plastic, and several reviewers report the rod sagging slightly if the curtain is heavy or if it's used with a thick liner. And the showerhead is fixed, so if you're tall (over 6 feet), you may find the angle hits your chest rather than your shoulders — there's no swivel adjustment.

One more consideration: this is a shower *add-on*, not a full conversion. You'll still be standing in a tub, and if you have a vintage tub with non-standard faucet threads (some are 1/2-inch NPT, some are odd European sizes), the diverter may not thread on without an adapter. Check your faucet before ordering.

---

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Clawfoot tub owners** who want a real overhead shower without a full remodel
- **Renters** — the valve and rod can be removed without permanent damage (the rod brackets leave small screw holes, but that's it)
- **DIYers** who've watched a plumbing video or two and can handle a wrench

**Skip it if:** you're 6'2" or taller (the fixed head angle will annoy you), your faucet has unusual threads, or you want a handheld sprayer included.

---

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit any clawfoot tub faucet?**
A: Most standard 1/2-inch NPT faucet spouts, yes. If your faucet is older or imported with non-standard threads, you may need a thread adapter (about $8). Measure your spout threads before buying.

**Q: Does the showerhead have adjustable pressure?**
A: No. It's a fixed showerhead. The flow is limited only by your home's water pressure. If your pressure is low, expect a mediocre shower — this won't boost it.

**Q: Can I leave the shower curtain on the rod when not in use?**
A: Yes, but the plastic mounting brackets may sag under constant weight. It's best to pull the curtain open fully to distribute weight evenly.

---

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuine shower experience in your clawfoot tub without hiring a plumber or gutting your bathroom. **Skip it if** you're tall, your faucet has odd threads, or you expect handheld versatility.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, functional upgrade that delivers on its promise. The plastic brackets and poor instructions cost it half a star, but for the price, this is the most practical way to add a shower to a vintage tub that's otherwise stuck in the past.
