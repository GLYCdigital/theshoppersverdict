**SUNVIE Low Voltage Transformer Review: Two Independent Outputs, One Serious Workhorse**

Most low-voltage transformers are liars. They claim "dual output" but really just split one circuit into two fused terminals — same schedule, same load, same headache. The SUNVIE with 2 Independent Outputs doesn't play that game. As someone who's installed a dozen of these boxes for clients, this is the first one under $100 that actually delivers on the promise of separate control.

**What's actually in the box**  
You get the transformer unit itself (roughly 8 x 6 x 4 inches, weighing just under 5 pounds), a mounting bracket with stainless screws, and a clear instruction sheet that doesn't assume you're an electrician. The housing is die-cast aluminum with a powder-coated finish — not the thin stamped steel you see on budget units from big-box stores. The door has a rubber gasket and latches securely; rain won't get in even if you mount it sideways on a fence post, though I'd still keep it vertical for drainage.

The two outputs are labeled "Zone 1" and "Zone 2," each rated for 15V AC at 10 amps. That's 150 watts per zone, 300 total. Inside, each zone has its own resettable circuit breaker — not a fuse you have to hunt down at 9 PM when a landscape light shorts. The digital timer lives on the front panel behind a clear plastic shield, with buttons that are actually usable while wearing work gloves.

**Real-world performance**  
I tested this on a job with 22 path lights (Zone 1) and a small pond pump (Zone 2). Zone 1 ran from 7 PM to 11 PM; Zone 2 ran continuously. The transformer handled the load without overheating — after four hours, the case was warm but not hot, and the internal fan (yes, it has one) stayed quiet. The timer held its programming through two brief power blips during a thunderstorm, which is rare at this price point. The LCD display is bright enough to read in direct sunlight, and the buttons respond with a satisfying click.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Scrolling through the 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, a few themes pop. **Where it falls short**  
Honest tradeoffs exist. There's no photocell built in, so if you want automatic dusk-to-dawn operation, you'll need to pair it with an external sensor (about $15 on Amazon) or rely on the timer's fixed schedules. The terminal blocks are solid but tight — 12-gauge landscape wire seats fine, but 10-gauge requires patience and a flathead screwdriver with a narrow tip. One reviewer complained the mounting bracket holes are 4 inches apart, which doesn't match standard electrical boxes; you'll likely screw it directly to wood or masonry. Also, the included manual is dense — the zone programming requires holding the "Set" button for 3 seconds, which several users initially missed.

**Who should buy this**  
If you have two separate low-voltage runs — say, pathway lights and a water feature — and you're tired of flipping one switch to control both, this is your box. It's also ideal if you have loads over 100 watts per zone, because many competitors cap at 60W per channel. Electricians installing for clients will appreciate the sturdy build and resettable breakers. Skip it if you need photocell automation out of the box, or if you're wiring a single simple zone — a cheaper single-output unit will save you $30.

**FAQ**  
*Q: Can I use this with a smart home system?*  
A: Not directly. There's no Wi-Fi or Z-Wave. But you can wire a smart switch on the input side to control both zones, or add a smart plug to each output if you're handy with relays.

*Q: What's the max distance to my lights?*  
A: At 15V, keep runs under 100 feet with 12-gauge wire to avoid voltage drop. For longer runs, bump to 10-gauge or consider 12V systems.

*Q: Does it work with LED landscape lights?*  
A: Yes. The output is 15V AC, which most LED fixtures accept. Just verify your LEDs are rated for AC input — some cheap ones expect DC and will flicker.
