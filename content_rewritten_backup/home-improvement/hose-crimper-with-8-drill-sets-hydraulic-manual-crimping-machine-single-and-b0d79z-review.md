## Where It Falls Short

The honest downsides:

**No crimp diameter indicator.** This is the biggest frustration. You're working blind on each crimp — pump until it feels right, retract, check the fitting. For critical high-pressure applications, you'll want calipers and some test pieces to dial in your technique. Experienced users develop a feel, but newcomers will waste some fittings learning.

**Physical effort on larger hoses.** Crimping 3/4" and 1" fittings takes genuine muscle. The pump handle gets stiff, and if you're working outdoors in winter, cold hose rubber makes it worse. This is not a job for someone with shoulder issues.

**The case is cheap.** Thin plastic, basic latches. It'll hold the parts but won't survive being thrown around a work truck. Plan to upgrade your storage or add padding.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this crimp 2-wire and 4-wire hydraulic hose?**
A: Yes, within the die size range. The crimp force is sufficient for standard 2-wire and 4-wire spiral hose. For extreme high-pressure applications (over 5000 PSI), verify your fitting manufacturer's crimp spec against the final diameter you achieve.

**Q: How do I know which die size to use?**
A: Match the die to the hose's outer diameter — not the fitting size. A 1/2" hose with a thick cover might require the 5/8" die. Measure the hose OD with calipers before selecting. This is where most first-time users make mistakes.

**Q: Is the 8-die set complete, or will I need more?**
A: The set covers the most common sizes for agricultural and automotive hydraulic work. You'll likely need additional dies only for specialty hoses like 1/4" low-pressure return lines or oversized 1-1/4" industrial hose.
