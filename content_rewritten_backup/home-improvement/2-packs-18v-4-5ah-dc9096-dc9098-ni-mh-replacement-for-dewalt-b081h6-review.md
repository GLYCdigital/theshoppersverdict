## Real-World Performance: More Than Just a Number

The headline feature here is 4.5Ah — roughly double the capacity of the original 2.4Ah packs that shipped with most 18V tools back in the day. That's not marketing math. In practical terms, you're getting noticeably longer runtime between charges. Owners consistently report finishing jobs that previously required swapping batteries mid-task. One reviewer put it plainly: "These last longer than the original batteries I replaced."

The Ni-MH chemistry is worth understanding. Unlike the old Ni-Cad packs that suffered from memory effect, Ni-MH handles partial discharges better. However, Ni-MH self-discharges faster than Li-Ion when sitting on a shelf. If you grab a pack that's been idle for a month, expect it to be low. That's not a defect — it's the chemistry. Keep them topped off before use.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**No fuel gauge.** There's no LED indicator or charge level button on the pack. You'll know it's dying when the tool slows down. For a 4.5Ah pack, that's a minor inconvenience, but it's worth noting if you're used to modern Li-Ion packs with charge displays.

**Weight.** The higher capacity means more cells, and these packs are slightly heavier than the originals. In a drill, it's barely noticeable. In a saw held overhead all day, you'll feel it.

**Self-discharge.** This is the biggest gotcha for casual users. Ni-MH chemistry loses roughly 10-20% charge per month sitting idle. If you only use your tools every few weeks, you'll want a routine of topping off before starting a project. This isn't a flaw unique to this brand — it's inherent to the chemistry — but it's a real consideration.
