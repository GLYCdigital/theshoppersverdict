If you’re shopping for a benchtop band saw, the SKIL 2.8 Amp 9-Inch is probably on your radar. It sits in that awkward middle ground — not a toy, not a pro-grade machine. But after digging through 220 ratings and the spec sheet, here’s the honest breakdown of where this saw earns its keep and where it will frustrate you.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the band saw body, a 49-inch blade (already installed), a rip fence, a miter gauge, a hex wrench, and a blade guide block. Assembly takes about 20 minutes — the table bolts on, the fence rails slide into place, and you’re cutting. The cast aluminum table measures roughly 12 x 12 inches, which is adequate for small projects but cramped for anything wider than a 2x4.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature here is the 2-speed gearbox. At 1,520 FPM you get controlled cutting for curves and delicate work; flip the switch to 3,050 FPM and it rips through softwoods with decent speed. That’s genuinely useful — most sub-$200 band saws make you choose one speed and live with it.

The 2.8-amp motor is the limiting factor. On pine and plywood up to 2 inches thick, it cuts cleanly. Push it into white oak or maple at full depth, and you’ll hear the motor strain and watch feed rate drop to a crawl. Multiple owners note this. One reviewer summarized: “Great for small hobby projects, but don't expect it to replace a full-size saw.”

The cast iron frame provides stability — no excessive vibration at either speed when the blade is tracked properly. The tool-free blade tension release is a genuinely thoughtful touch. Swap the blade without hunting for wrenches, and the tracking adjustment holds well once set.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Owners love the value-to-feature ratio. The 2-speed capability, tool-free adjustments, and bevel capability get consistent shout-outs. As one verified buyer put it: “For the price, you get a lot. Cuts straight, handles curves well, and the fence is actually accurate — surprising for this price point.”

The miter gauge also earns mentions for being functional rather than flimsy, and the dust port connects to a standard 2.5-inch shop vac hose without adapters.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest headache: the 49-inch blade. That’s not the standard 59.5-inch length found on most 9-inch band saws. You’ll need to order replacements online or hunt specialty suppliers — your local hardware store likely won’t stock them. Factor in a $15-20 blade purchase when budgeting.

The stock blade is genuinely mediocre. It drifts on straight cuts and dulls quickly. Plan to replace it with a quality 1/4-inch or 3/8-inch blade for resawing or curves. That’s not unusual for budget tools, but it’s worth knowing upfront.

Also, the 2.8-amp motor means this is strictly a hobby saw. If you’re cutting hardwood stock thicker than 2 inches regularly, you’ll want something with more power. And at just 9 inches of throat depth, you’re limited to smaller workpieces — this won’t handle resawing a 6-inch board.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Hobbyist woodworkers** making small projects — cutting curves, shaping parts, trimming dowels
- **DIYers** who need occasional cuts and want a compact machine that fits on a shelf when not in use
- **Model builders and crafters** working with thin stock and softwoods

**Skip it if** you’re resawing hardwood, cutting thick stock regularly, or need a tool that runs all day on job sites.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I cut hardwood with this saw?**
A: Yes, but keep it under 2 inches thick and feed slowly. The 2.8-amp motor lacks torque for aggressive cuts in dense wood. Use a sharp, quality blade and let the saw do the work.

**Q: What size blade does it take, and where do I buy replacements?**
A: It uses a 49-inch blade. You won’t find that at most hardware stores. Order online — brands like Olson and Timber Wolf make compatible blades. Budget $15-20 per blade.

**Q: Does it come with a stand or legs?**
A: No. It’s benchtop-only. Factor in a sturdy work surface or purchase an optional stand. The saw weighs about 60 pounds, so it’s portable but not light.
