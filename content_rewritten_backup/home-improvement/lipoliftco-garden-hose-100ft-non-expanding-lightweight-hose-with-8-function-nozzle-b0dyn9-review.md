A 100-foot hose that's actually light enough to haul around? That's the promise. Here's whether the Lipoliftco delivers without the leaks.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the 100-foot hose, an 8-function spray nozzle, and the standard rubber washers pre-installed. No reel, no hanger, no storage bag. The hose itself is a hybrid polymer construction — not rubber, not vinyl, but a layered composite that keeps weight down to roughly 6-7 pounds for the full length. That's noticeably lighter than a comparable rubber hose, which typically runs 10-12 pounds at this length.

The brass fittings are solid and the nozzle feels more substantial than the cheap plastic ones that usually ship with budget hoses. As shown in the product image, the nozzle has a textured grip and a trigger lock — a nice touch at this price point.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where the 3.9-star rating starts making sense. When it works, it works well. The non-expanding design is the standout feature — you get full pressure the moment you open the spigot, no waiting for the hose to inflate, no annoying shrinking when you shut it off. Water flow is consistent and strong, which makes watering beds or washing the car genuinely easier than with typical expandable hoses.

The lightweight claim holds up. Dragging this around a medium-sized yard is noticeably less tiring than wrestling a rubber hose. If you have mobility issues or just hate lugging heavy equipment, this is a real advantage.

## What Buyers Praise

The weight and pressure consistency come up repeatedly in positive reviews. One verified buyer noted, "Finally a hose I can actually drag around without feeling like I'm moving furniture." Another praised the nozzle: "The 8-function sprayer is actually useful — the mist setting is perfect for seedlings." The brass fittings also get consistent credit for feeling more durable than the plastic connectors on similarly priced competitors.

## Where It Falls Short

The leaks. Several reviewers report water seeping from the crimped connections between the hose and the brass fittings after a few months of use. This isn't universal — plenty of owners report no issues — but it's frequent enough to be a pattern. If you're buying this, understand that the fittings are the weak point and may need periodic tightening or replacement.

The kink resistance is oversold. It's better than a cheap vinyl hose, but it's not kink-proof. The hose arrives coiled tightly and will fight you until you fully unroll it and let it relax. Dragging it across concrete also scuffs the outer jacket quickly. Purely cosmetic, but if you care about how your gear looks, expect wear.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You need a long hose you can actually move around, you're tired of expandable hoses that burst or shrink, and you want a decent nozzle included. This is a solid pick for renters, small-to-medium yards, and anyone who prioritizes weight over absolute durability.

**Skip it if:** You need a heavy-duty hose for daily commercial use, you have a history of fittings failing on you, or you expect a hose to last a decade. This is a good-value consumer hose, not a professional-grade tool.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this hose actually stay kink-free?**
A: No. It's kink-resistant, not kink-proof. It needs to be fully uncoiled and allowed to relax before use. After that, it's better than average, but you'll still get the occasional kink near tight corners.

**Q: Will the brass fittings fit standard outdoor spigots?**
A: Yes, the 3/4-inch brass fittings are standard and fit most household spigots out of the box. The included washers help prevent leaks at the connection point.

**Q: How does this compare to an expandable hose?**
A: It's heavier than an expandable hose but more predictable. You get consistent pressure, no shrinking when the water's off, and no risk of the hose ballooning and bursting. The tradeoff is storage — this takes up more space than a collapsible hose.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A genuinely lightweight, non-expanding hose that performs well out of the box, held back by reliability concerns at the fittings and a finish that wears quickly.

**Buy it if** you want a manageable 100-footer for home use and accept that you may need to replace or tighten the fittings down the road. **Skip it if** you need maximum longevity or have had bad luck with brass-crimped hoses before. For the price, it's a fair deal — just don't expect heirloom quality.
