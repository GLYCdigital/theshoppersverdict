## The Staircase Upgrade That Actually Works

Most stair lighting kits are either dumb — a switch you'll forget to flip — or need a full smart-home ecosystem to function. The KOMIGAN Intelligent Motion Triggered LED Stair Lighting Kit splits the difference, and for the $329.99 asking price, it needs to be good.

Here's the honest breakdown after digging through what works, what doesn't, and whether this belongs in your home.

## What You Actually Get

The kit ships with two 16.4-foot LED strips (32.8 feet total), a motion sensor controller, power supply, and the necessary connectors. The strips are IP65-rated, meaning they'll survive the occasional dust and splashes — good for stairwells where cleaning happens. Each strip is cuttable at 1.57-inch intervals, which is genuinely useful for matching odd stair runs without leaving dead zones.

The controller is the star here. It handles motion detection up to 16 feet, has a built-in ambient light sensor, and offers four brightness levels plus a timer function (30 seconds to 30 minutes). You can set it to only activate at night, which is the smart move for stair lighting anyway.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The motion detection is genuinely responsive. The sensor picks up movement from the top or bottom of the stairs and triggers the lights within about half a second — fast enough to catch your first step before you're in the dark. The 3000K warm white output is the right color temperature for this application: bright enough to see every step edge clearly, but soft enough that it won't blind you when walking up at 2 AM.

Installation takes roughly 45-60 minutes for a standard 12-step staircase. The strips route cleanly along the underside of each tread, and the connectors snap together without soldering. The learning curve is minimal — if you've ever installed LED strips before, this is familiar territory.

As shown in the product image, the lights sit flush against the tread and create a clean, continuous line of illumination. No visible hot spots, no flicker.

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews holds up when you look at the patterns. Buyers consistently mention two things:

**The "wow" factor.** One reviewer wrote, "Guests literally stop and ask about these lights. They look like a $1,000 custom install, not a DIY kit." That matches what I see in the design — the diffused strip and the seamless motion activation feel genuinely premium.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the drawbacks.

**The adhesive is underwhelming.** The included 3M tape works fine on clean, smooth treads. But several buyers report it peeling on painted or slightly textured wood within weeks. Budget for mounting clips (about $10 on Amazon) or a roll of quality double-sided tape before you start.

**The sensor limitation.** You get one motion sensor per kit. If your staircase has a landing — or you have steps going up and down from different directions — you'll need the expansion sensor pack. That adds to an already premium price.

**The price point.** At $329.99, this sits above most comparable kits. The CutieStairs kit runs about $200 less, though it lacks the integrated motion sensor and ambient light detection. You're paying for the smart controller, and it's worth it — but only if you'll actually use those features.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a standard straight staircase, you want lights that work automatically without fiddling with apps or switches, and you value the "premium install" look without hiring an electrician.

**Skip it if:** You have a complex multi-landing staircase (sensor coverage gets spotty), you're on a tight budget, or you want RGB color options — this is strictly warm white.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work without a smart home system?**
A: Yes. The controller operates standalone — motion detection, ambient light sensing, timer, and brightness all work out of the box. No hub, no Wi-Fi, no app required.

**Q: Can I cut the strips to fit any stair width?**
A: Yes, every 1.57 inches. Just cut along the marked lines and reconnect the power lead. Standard residential stairs (36-42 inches wide) typically use two strips per step.

**Q: How long do the LEDs last?**
A: Rated at 50,000 hours. At average use of 2-3 hours per night, that's roughly 45 years of nightly use before dimming becomes noticeable.
