The KOHLER PureWash M250 sits in a weird spot: it's priced like a premium bidet seat but stripped down to manual, non-electric basics. For some buyers, that's exactly the point — no outlet near the toilet, no complex wiring, no smart app nonsense. For others, it'll feel like paying luxury money for a plastic knob and a hose. Here's who should actually buy it.

**What's in the box**

You get the elongated seat assembly, a metal mounting bracket with bolts, the T-valve cold water adapter, a 3-foot supply hose, and a clearly written install manual. Note what's *not* included: the hot water T-adapter kit for the warm water feature. That's a $15–25 add-on you'll need to buy separately if you want anything other than cold water. The seat itself is compact — about 20.5 inches long, 14.2 inches wide — with a low-profile design that doesn't look like a hospital appliance.

**Installation: plan for an hour, not 20 minutes**

Mounting the seat is genuinely easy. The bracket slides into standard toilet bolt holes (5.5-inch spread fits nearly all American toilets), and the seat clicks on with a satisfying lock. The cold water hookup is straightforward: shut off the toilet supply, thread on the T-valve, connect the hose. But here's the catch — the warm water feature requires running a line from your sink's hot water supply under the vanity. That's a second shutoff valve, a T-adapter, and potentially drilling through cabinet or floor. If you're not handy with plumbing, budget for a plumber or just run it cold-only.

**Real-world performance**

The dual stainless steel nozzles — one for rear wash, one for front — extend and retract smoothly. Water pressure is adjustable via a dial on the right side of the seat, and you can control spray position with a slider. Users consistently praise the cleaning power: *"First bidet I've owned that actually feels like it gets you clean without a second pass of toilet paper,"* one verified reviewer noted. Another wrote, *"The self-cleaning wand feature is real — no residue, no spotting after months of use."*

The warm water mixing valve is the headline feature. It blends hot and cold mechanically, giving you a consistent temperature without waiting for a tank heater. But it's only as good as your hot water supply — if your sink takes 30 seconds to run warm, you'll get a cold blast first. Several reviewers flagged this: *"Warm water took way too long to arrive, so I just use cold now."*

**Where it falls short**

The M250's biggest problem is value. At its typical street price, you're in TOTO Washlet and Bio Bidet territory — electric units with heated seats, warm air dryers, and remote controls. The M250 offers none of that. The seat is room-temperature plastic. There's no dryer. The side knob controls work fine but feel dated. And the water pressure, while adjustable, tops out lower than electric competitors — one buyer called it *"adequate but not the power-wash I was hoping for."*

**Who should buy this**

- **The no-outlet household**: If your toilet isn't near an electrical outlet and you refuse to pay for an electrician, the M250 is a solid non-electric option.
- **The warm-water purist**: Anyone who's used a $30 cold-water bidet and wants temperature control without the cost of a tank-heated electric unit.
- **KOHLER loyalists**: If your bathroom is already KOHLER fixtures, the design language matches, and the Quiet-Close lid is genuinely nice — it drops silently, no slam.

**Skip it if** you want a heated seat, a dryer, or app controls — buy an electric unit instead. Also skip it if you have no hot water line accessible under the sink; the cold-only experience isn't worth this price.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this seat fit a standard toilet?**
A: Yes, it fits elongated bowls with the standard 5.5-inch bolt spread. It will *not* fit round bowls — measure your toilet from the seat bolt holes to the front rim first.

**Q: Can I use it without the warm water hookup?**
A: Absolutely. Just connect the cold water supply. You'll get cold washes only, but the seat functions perfectly otherwise. You can add the hot water line later.

**Q: Does it need electricity at all?**
A: No. It's 100% mechanical — water pressure from your supply line drives everything. If your water is shut off, it won't work, but there's zero power draw.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a reliable, non-electric bidet with genuine warm water capability and you're comfortable with the extra plumbing. The KOHLER name means the seat itself will outlast the house.

**Skip it if** you want modern bidet features — heated seats, dryers, remote controls — or if your bathroom lacks an accessible hot water line. You'll be overpaying for features you can't use.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A well-built, no-frills bidet that nails the basics but asks a premium price for a deliberately simple feature set.
