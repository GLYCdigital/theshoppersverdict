**Roman Tub Faucet, Solid Brass Bathtub Faucet Set with Valve — the honest breakdown**

Let's cut to it: most roman tub faucets in this price range are die-cast zinc with a brass-colored coating. This one isn't. The body, spout, and handle stems are actual solid brass — you can feel the weight difference the moment you lift it out of the box. That's the single biggest reason this faucet earns its 4.5-star average across 225 ratings.

## What's actually in the box

You get the complete set: the spout, two handles, the integrated 3-hole deck mount valve, and mounting hardware. The valve being included is a bigger deal than most shoppers realize — a separate rough-in valve from a brand like Moen or Delta runs $60–100 on its own. Here, it's built into the package. The spout measures a substantial 12 inches from the deck to the outlet, which clears the tub rim comfortably without splashing.

The finish is polished chrome over the brass base. It's not a PVD coating — so it'll show fingerprints and water spots more readily than a brushed nickel finish would. But it's also easier to touch up if it ever gets scratched.

## Real-world performance

Installation is straightforward if you have basic plumbing experience. The 8-inch center-to-center deck mount spacing is standard for roman tubs, so you won't need to drill new holes. The valve uses 1/2-inch NPT connections, which fits most existing supply lines.

One thing buyers consistently mention in their reviews: the handles feel solid when you turn them. No wobble, no cheap plastic click. The quarter-turn ceramic cartridges operate smoothly and don't drip — a couple of reviewers noted they've had theirs installed for over a year with zero leaks.

The flow rate is strong, too. At standard residential water pressure, you get a full, even stream from the spout without the aerator sputtering that plagues cheaper faucets.

## What buyers keep praising

A recurring theme in the feedback is the value proposition. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I paid $90 for a faucet that feels like it should have cost $200. The brass is thick — you can tell it's not going to corrode like my old one did."* Another mentioned the installation process: *"Took me about an hour as a DIYer. ## Where it falls short

Honest downsides? Three things.

**First**, the handles ship loose — you have to tighten the set screws yourself. It's a minor task, but the screws are small and easy to strip if you're heavy-handed. Use a properly sized Allen wrench and don't reef on them.

**Second**, there's no diverter option. This is strictly a tub-fill faucet. If you want to switch to a handheld shower, you'll need to look elsewhere.

**Third**, the chrome finish is a fingerprint magnet. If you have hard water, you'll be wiping this down regularly. It's a maintenance tradeoff for the classic look.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're remodeling a bathroom and want a solid, reliable roman tub faucet without paying designer-brand markup. You're comfortable with basic DIY installation. You value brass construction over cheap alternatives that corrode in 3–5 years.

**Skip it if:** You need a diverter for a shower combo. You want a matte or brushed finish that hides water spots. Or you're looking for a widespread faucet with handles more than 8 inches apart — this one won't fit.
