Let's be honest about what this WINGBO lamp actually is: a cleverly designed, old-school-looking portable light that solves a very specific problem. If you've got a corner that needs illumination but no outlet nearby — a bookshelf, a porch table, a bathroom vanity, a camping setup — this retro-styled cordless lamp earns its keep. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, buyers are mostly happy. Here's the real story.

**What you're actually getting**

The lamp measures roughly 11 inches tall with a weighted metal base and a glass shade that mimics vintage Edison bulbs. It runs on 3 C batteries (not included), which tuck into the base. The controls are touch-sensitive on the metal body: tap to turn on, tap again to cycle through three color temperatures — warm white, neutral, and cool daylight — and press-and-hold to dim steplessly. That's genuinely useful range for a lamp this size.

**How it performs in real use**

The warm setting (2700K-ish) is the star here. It throws a cozy, golden glow that makes a room feel intentional rather than just lit. One reviewer noted, *"I bought this for my screened porch and the warm light makes it feel like a fancy restaurant at night."* Another said, *"The dimming feature is what sold me — I can read at my desk or wind down in bed with the same lamp."* The build quality exceeds the price point: the metal base has heft, the glass shade isn't thin or cheap, and it's stable enough that a knock won't send it flying.

Battery life is the double-edged sword. At the lowest dim setting, you're looking at 100+ hours — that's months of nightly use for most people. Crank it to max brightness, and you'll get roughly 8-10 hours before swapping batteries. For occasional use, that's fine. For daily primary lighting, you'll want rechargeable C cells or a corded lamp instead.

**Where it falls short**

The touch controls are the most common complaint in the reviews. The sensor sits on the metal base and requires precise, deliberate taps. Brush it accidentally while reaching across the table and it'll cycle through modes or shut off. One buyer summed it up: *"Love the lamp, hate the controls. I've turned it off three times just trying to pick it up."* That's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Also, there's no rechargeable version of this lamp. Once those C batteries drain, you're buying more — or investing in rechargeables and a charger. For a product in 2026, the omission of even a basic USB-C port feels dated.

**Who should buy this lamp**

Three buyer profiles fit this lamp best:

1. **The porch/patio host** who wants warm ambient light without running extension cords.
2. **The renter** who can't install hardwired sconces and needs portable lighting that doesn't damage walls.
3. **The emergency-prep person** who wants a reliable, battery-powered light source that doesn't look like a camping lantern.

If you need a bright task light for reading or detailed work, skip this. It's an ambiance lamp first, a functional lamp second.

**FAQ: What shoppers actually ask**

**Q: How long do the batteries really last?**
A: At the lowest dim setting, you'll get 100+ hours. At max brightness, expect 8-10 hours. Most people run it mid-range and get 2-3 weeks of nightly use from one set of batteries.

**Q: Can I use rechargeable batteries?**
A: Yes. Standard C-cell rechargeables work fine. They'll save you money long-term, but the lamp itself has no built-in charging.

**Q: Is the light bright enough to read by?**
A: At full brightness on the cool setting, yes — but it's not a task lamp. You'll want it within arm's reach, and the light is softer than a dedicated reading light.

**The verdict: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you want a portable, cordless lamp with genuine vintage charm and useful dimming — especially for porches, bedrooms, or any spot where an outlet isn't convenient.

**Skip it if** you need bright task lighting, hate fussy touch controls, or don't want to manage disposable batteries.

At its price point, this is one of the better battery-powered table lamps you'll find. Just buy rechargeable C cells with it and you're set for years.
