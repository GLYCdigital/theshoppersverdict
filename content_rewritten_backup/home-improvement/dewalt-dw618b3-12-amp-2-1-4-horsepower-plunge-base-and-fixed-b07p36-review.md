If you're a serious woodworker who's ever owned a single-base router, you already know the frustration: you set up a dovetail jig, then need a chamfer bit, and suddenly you're unbolting, re-aligning, and re-zeroing. The DEWALT DW618B3 solves that with a clever two-base system. But is it the right router for your shop? I've spent time with this kit, and here's what actually matters.

## What's in the Box

The DW618B3 gives you the 2-1/4 HP motor, both the plunge base and fixed base, plus a 1/4-inch and 1/2-inch collet, a wrenches, a straight guide, and a carrying case. The motor itself features a 12-amp motor with electronic feedback that maintains speed under load — that's the same motor used in many of DEWALT's standalone routers. The kit also includes a sub-base for both bases, so you're ready for template work out of the box.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The motor housing is the star here. It's a quick-release system — press two levers, pull the motor, drop it into the other base. It takes about five seconds. That's not a marketing exaggeration; it's genuinely tool-free and secure when locked in.

In use, the plunge base is smooth. The plunge action has a consistent, controlled feel — no sticky spots or jerky movement. Depth adjustment is precise thanks to the micro-fine bit depth ring, which lets you dial in hundredths of an inch. For dovetail work, that accuracy matters.

The fixed base is where most people live for edge routing. It's got a through-the-base dust port, which is rare at this price point. Hook up a shop vac and you'll see maybe 80% of chips disappear. That's a huge win for keeping your workspace clean and seeing your cut line.

Power-wise, the 2-1/4 HP motor handles hard maple and even oak without bogging down, provided you're not taking aggressive passes. The electronic feedback keeps RPMs steady — I noticed less tear-out on figured wood compared to cheaper variable-speed models.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 235 ratings at 4.7 stars, a few themes pop up. One buyer said, "The quick-release motor is genius — I switch bases mid-project without even thinking about it." Another noted, "This replaced my old fixed-base router entirely. The dust collection is miles ahead of anything I've used."

Multiple reviewers highlight the smooth plunge action and the accuracy of the depth adjustment. Several mention they've had the router for years with zero issues — a good sign for longevity.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time. The plunge base is heavy — around 12 pounds combined with the motor. For overhead work like cutting dovetails on a vertical board, your arms will feel it. Dedicated plunge routers from other brands are lighter.

Bit changes require two wrenches. There's no spindle lock button like you'll find on newer competitors. It's a minor annoyance, but when you're switching bits frequently, it adds friction.

The carrying case is large and rigid. If you're tight on shop space, it'll eat up shelf room. Some buyers just toss the case and store the bases separately.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Cabinet makers and furniture builders** who need both fixed and plunge capabilities and value quick changeover
- **DIY enthusiasts** upgrading from a trim router who want one machine that does it all
- **Anyone who hates dust** — the fixed base's dust port is worth the price alone for clean shops

Skip it if you only do occasional edge routing and never plunge. A single fixed-base router will save you money and weight.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this for a router table?**
A: Yes. The fixed base mounts to most standard router table plates with the included screws. The through-the-base dust port works well in a table setup too.

**Q: Does it come with a carrying case?**
A: Yes, a hard-sided case that holds both bases, the motor, collets, wrenches, and the guide. It's sturdy but bulky.

**Q: What's the difference between 1/4-inch and 1/2-inch collets?**
A: The kit includes both. The 1/2-inch collet provides more stability for larger bits and reduces vibration; the 1/4-inch is for standard trim bits. Switch based on your bit shank size.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a versatile two-base router system that handles everything from dovetails to edge profiling without sacrificing dust control or precision. The quick-change motor and excellent dust collection justify the cost for serious woodworkers.

**Skip it if** you're a casual user who rarely plunges and don't mind a second router purchase later. You'll save money and weight with a single-base model.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The DW618B3 is one of the most versatile router kits available. It loses points for the heavy plunge base and two-wrench bit changes, but the power, precision, and dust collection make it a top-tier choice for anyone who works wood seriously.
