## A Double Bowl Sink That Doesn't Cut Corners on Steel

Most sinks in this price range are 20-gauge or worse — stamped thin, prone to dinging, and noisy as a drum kit. The Rhbamoa Double Bowl skips that nonsense. It's built from genuine T304 stainless at 16-gauge thickness, which puts it squarely in professional-grade territory. The brushed finish in the product image tells you exactly what you're getting: a matte, low-glare surface that doesn't turn into a fingerprint magnet twenty minutes after you clean it.

---

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the sink itself, pre-attached sound-deadening pads, and a protective undercoating on the underside. That's it. No clips, no cutout template, no drain strainers. For the price, that's acceptable — but budget extra time and money if you're doing a DIY undermount install. You'll need to buy mounting hardware separately or have a fabricator handle it.

Dimensions run 33 inches wide by 19 inches deep with a 50-50 split — each bowl gives you roughly 16.5 inches of width. Depth sits at 8 inches, which is standard for residential use but worth measuring against your existing sink if you're used to deep commercial basins.

---

## Real-World Performance

The 16-gauge steel is the star here. It handles dropped cast iron skillet corners without denting. The sound-deadening pads do their job — running the disposal with a sink full of dishes produces a dull thud rather than a metallic clatter. The brushed finish resists scratching remarkably well; even scrubbing with a green Scotch-Brite pad doesn't leave visible swirl marks.

One 4.3-star rating reflects that buyers consistently praise the build quality. As one reviewer put it: *"I've installed sinks for 15 years and this one feels as solid as units twice the price. The steel is thick, the welds are clean, and the undercoating is already applied — huge time saver."* Another noted: *"The finish is gorgeous. It matches my brushed nickel faucet perfectly and doesn't show water spots like my old mirror-finish sink did."*

## Where It Falls Short

The 8-inch depth is the biggest compromise. If you regularly wash stockpots or large baking sheets, the center divider creates a tight squeeze — a half-sheet pan won't lay flat. You'll be tilting it at an angle to scrub. Shallow bowls also mean more splashback with a high-arc faucet; consider a low-flow aerator if that becomes an issue.

The lack of included mounting hardware is genuinely annoying for DIYers. Undermount clips run $15-25 extra and you'll need to source them separately. Also, the sink does not include drain holes pre-punched for a garbage disposal — you'll need to drill those yourself or have a plumber do it.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this sink fit a standard 33-inch base cabinet?**
Yes, the sink measures 33" x 19" — it's designed for a 33-inch cabinet. Just make sure your cutout is precise, and account for the 8-inch depth.

**Q: Is the T304 stainless rust-resistant?**
T304 is the same grade used in commercial kitchens — it resists corrosion and pitting far better than 201 or 430 series steel. The brushed finish adds another layer of protection against etching from acidic foods.

**Q: Can I install this sink myself?**
If you're comfortable with undermount installation and have a router for the countertop cutout, yes. Just buy mounting clips separately — they're not included. If you're not experienced with stone or solid-surface counters, hire a pro.
