Let's be blunt: most faucets under $60 are disposable chrome-plated plastic. The AmirLans single-hole bathroom faucet isn't that. With a 4.6-star average from 229 ratings, this one's earning its place in bathrooms where people actually care about how the hardware feels.

## What's actually in the box

You get the faucet body, a matching pop-up drain assembly, pre-attached 16-inch supply lines, mounting hardware, and a plastic wrench for the nuts. The drain is solid metal, not the hollow zinc-alloy junk that strips out after a year. As shown in the product image, the brushed nickel finish is consistent across the faucet and drain — no mismatch between suppliers, which is surprisingly rare at this price point.

The faucet measures about 5.9 inches tall with a 4.7-inch spout reach. That's compact enough for a vessel sink or a standard vanity, but don't expect it to stretch across a deep counter.

## Real-world performance

The ceramic disc cartridge is the headline feature here, and it shows. The handle rotates with a smooth, positive stop — no grinding, no overshoot. One buyer noted, *"Handle turns effortlessly and doesn't drip even after six months of daily use."* That's the ceramic cartridge doing its job.

Water flow is steady at 1.2 GPM, which meets EPA WaterSense standards without feeling like you're being rationed. The aerator produces a clean, non-splashing stream that won't spray your mirror every morning.

Installation is where this faucet punches above its weight class. The supply lines come pre-attached with brass nuts — you don't need to wrestle with compression fittings under a sink. A homeowner with basic tools can handle this in under 30 minutes. One reviewer mentioned, *"Took me longer to unpack it than to install it. The pre-attached hoses are a game-changer."*

## Where it falls short

The brushed nickel finish is a double-edged sword. It hides fingerprints well but shows hard water spots more than chrome. If you have mineral-heavy water and don't wipe down fixtures regularly, you'll see buildup.

The pop-up drain's lift rod is functional but noticeably lighter gauge than the rest of the hardware. It works fine, but if you're aggressive with it, you might bend it. Be gentle during installation and daily use.

Also — and this is important — the deck plate for converting a 3-hole sink to single-hole is sold separately. The product photos make this easy to miss. Measure your sink before ordering.

## Who should buy this

- **DIY homeowners** replacing a leaky builder-grade faucet — the pre-attached lines make this a weekend morning project
- **Rental property owners** who want something durable that won't call back with complaints
- **Anyone with a single-hole sink** who wants metal construction without paying $150+ for a name brand

Skip it if you need a widespread faucet for a 3-hole sink, or if you want a matte black finish — this one's only available in brushed nickel.
