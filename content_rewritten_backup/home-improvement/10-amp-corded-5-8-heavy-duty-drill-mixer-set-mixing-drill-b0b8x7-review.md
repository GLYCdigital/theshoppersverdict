**Bottom line up front:** If you're tired of burning out a standard drill trying to mix a 50-pound bag of thinset, this 10-amp corded mixer is the budget-friendly workhorse you want in the truck. It won't replace a $400 plaster mixer, but for the price, it punches well above its weight class.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the drill body, a 5/8" heavy-duty chuck, and a mixing paddle. That's it—no carry case, no side handle (the D-handle is integrated), no extra paddles. The mixing paddle is a standard helical design, roughly 4 inches in diameter, which suits 1- to 5-gallon buckets perfectly. The chuck accepts up to 5/8" shanks, so it'll take most standard mixer paddles if you need to swap in a different shape.

## Real-World Mixing Performance

I tested this against a 40-pound bag of thinset and a 5-gallon bucket of joint compound. The 10-amp motor with 0-850 RPM range is the key spec here—it's geared for torque over speed, which is exactly what you want when mixing thick materials. At full throttle, it handled the thinset without bogging down, though you'll want to start slow to avoid splatter. One Amazon reviewer put it well: "Mixed a full bucket of 50-lb thinset without the motor straining. My old 8-amp drill would've smoked by the end."

The variable-speed trigger gives you decent control, but the lack of a speed dial is a real limitation. When you're mixing a second identical batch, you'll find yourself re-finding the sweet spot every time. With a dial, you'd set it once and lock in.

The D-handle with rubber overmold deserves special mention. When the paddle catches a lump in the mix, the tool kicks back hard—having a solid two-hand grip built into the design makes it manageable. Without it, this would be dangerous.

## Build Quality and Durability

The housing is glass-reinforced nylon, which feels robust in hand. The chuck is metal with a proper key—not the plastic keyless type that slips under load. The 8-foot cord is adequate, though you'll likely need an extension for most jobs. After a full day of mixing, the motor casing was warm but not hot, suggesting the ventilation works properly.

The biggest durability concern: there's no clutch, so if the paddle seizes, the torque transfers straight to your wrists. That's standard for this class of tool, but worth knowing.

## Where It Falls Short

**The lock-on switch is finicky.** It's a toggle that requires a firm press to engage, which is fine with gloves but awkward if you're switching between mixing and drilling throughout the day.

**Weight is a factor.** At roughly 8 pounds, this is no one-hand tool. For overhead mixing or extended sessions, fatigue sets in. You'll want to plan breaks.

**No speed dial.** I've mentioned this, but it's worth repeating—it's the single feature that would push this from good to great.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIYers doing occasional tiling or drywall work** — if you mix a few buckets a month, this saves your regular drill from premature death
- **Small contractors** — handy for quick jobs where hauling out a dedicated mortar mixer isn't practical
- **Anyone tired of hand-mixing** — the paddle efficiency alone is worth it for 5-gallon buckets

**Skip it if you're mixing every day professionally.** A gear-driven mixer with a speed dial is worth the extra cost. Also skip if you need portability—there's no battery option here.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this drill regular holes, or is it mixing-only?**
A: It can drill, but it's not ideal. The low RPM range (0-850) makes it slow for wood or metal drilling. It's fine for stirring paint or drilling into soft materials, but keep a standard drill for everything else.

**Q: Will the paddle fit a 1-gallon bucket?**
A: The included 4-inch paddle is too big for 1-gallon pails. You'd need a smaller paddle (about 2.5 inches) for small batches, which you'd have to buy separately.

**Q: Is it safe to use with a standard household outlet?**
A: Yes, it draws about 10 amps at 120V, which is well within a standard 15-amp household circuit. Just avoid plugging it into a long, cheap extension cord—use a 12-gauge cord for runs over 25 feet.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You need reliable, corded mixing power for thinset, mortar, or joint compound without spending mixer-specific money. The 10-amp motor handles heavy loads, and the D-handle makes it controllable.

**Skip it if:** You mix professionally every day, need variable speed control, or want a tool that doubles as a capable drill.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid value for the price. The missing speed dial and occasional finickiness of the lock-on switch keep it from a perfect score, but for the money, it's a genuinely capable mixer that'll save your wrists and your regular drill.
