**The Short Version:** The Moen 7260E Belfield is a $350 touchless faucet that mostly earns its premium price — but it's not the "set it and forget it" device the marketing suggests. It's a smart buy for certain kitchens and a frustrating one for others. Here's the honest breakdown.

---

**What You're Actually Getting**

The box contains the faucet body, a 6-AA battery pack, a pull-down spray head with a 68-inch nylon hose, and the necessary mounting hardware. No docking station, no AC adapter — just batteries. The Belfield is a one-handle, high-arc (15.5-inch clearance) pull-down in a Spot Resist stainless finish. That finish is the real star: it's a brushed nickel-esque coating that genuinely resists fingerprints. As one buyer put it, "I've had it for three months and haven't wiped it once — it still looks brand new."

The MotionSense system is the headline feature. Wave your hand to the left of the sensor (beneath the spout) and water runs continuously until you wave again. Wave to the right and you get a 3-second burst — handy for rinsing without committing to a full flow. The sensor range is about 3 inches, which is tight enough to avoid most false triggers but not all.

---

**Real-World Performance**

Installation is straightforward if you've mounted a faucet before — about 45 minutes with basic tools. The pull-down spray head retracts smoothly thanks to the weighted hose, and the Power Clean setting genuinely delivers. I tested it against dried oatmeal and cooked-on sauce; the spray stream cut through both in seconds. That 50% more spray force claim isn't marketing fluff.

The sensor responsiveness is good but not perfect. When your hands are wet or soapy, the wave works reliably — that's the whole point. But here's the catch: the sensor sits at the base of the spout, and it's line-of-sight. If you reach across the sink to grab a sponge or a dish from the drying rack, you can trip it. One Amazon reviewer noted: "I've had the water turn on when I was just reaching for the dish soap on the counter behind the faucet. It's annoying, but you learn to reach around it."

Battery life is the other honest caveat. Moen claims up to 18 months, but real-world reports average 6-8 months with daily use. That's not terrible — it's two battery swaps a year, and the low-battery indicator light gives you about two weeks of warning. But at $350, you might expect a hardwired option. There isn't one.

---

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The Spot Resist finish is the most-cited positive across reviews. Multiple buyers mention it looks "premium" and "high-end," and several note it's the main reason they chose this over cheaper touchless models. The spray power also gets consistent shout-outs — one reviewer said it "replaced my need for a separate sprayer entirely."

The continuous-flow wave sensor (left side) is another favorite. Unlike cheaper touchless faucets that only do momentary bursts, the Belfield's left-wave keeps water running until you wave again — genuinely useful for filling pots or washing produce.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the false-trigger issue and battery pack, the wave zones are the steepest learning curve. Left for continuous, right for momentary — but there's no visual indicator on the faucet. You'll instinctively wave both hands or swipe across both zones and get confusing results. It took me about a week to stop fumbling.

The price is also worth flagging. At $350, you're paying a $100-150 premium over comparable non-touchless Moen pull-downs. If you don't actually use the sensor feature daily, you're overpaying for a gimmick.

---

**Who Should Buy This**

**Buy it if:** You cook frequently and your hands are constantly messy — raw chicken, flour, oil. The touchless operation genuinely reduces cross-contamination, and the spray power handles heavy cleanup. Also buy it if you're remodeling and want a statement piece with a finish that won't show every fingerprint.

**Skip it if:** You're on a strict budget, you have young kids who might play with the sensor (they will), or you're buying it just for the "wow factor" — you'll likely end up using the handle most of the time anyway.

---

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the sensor work with dark or wet hands?**
A: Yes — it uses infrared, not visual recognition. Wet, soapy, or dark hands all trigger it reliably. Gloved hands work too.

**Q: Can I disable the sensor and use it as a manual faucet?**
A: Yes. There's a manual override — just use the handle. The sensor stays off until you wave again. You don't need to disconnect anything.

**Q: What's included for installation?**
A: The faucet, spray head, battery pack, mounting hardware, and a deck plate for 3-hole sinks. You'll need your own Teflon tape and a basin wrench for tight spaces.

---

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Moen 7260E Belfield is a well-built, genuinely useful touchless faucet that earns its price if you actually use the sensor features. The Spot Resist finish is excellent, the spray power is legitimately superior to standard pull-downs, and the dual-wave system works well once you learn it. But the battery pack, occasional false triggers, and the learning curve keep it from being a flawless recommendation. It's a great faucet — just know what you're getting into.

**Buy it if** you want a premium touchless faucet with real spray power and don't mind battery swaps twice a year. **Skip it if** you're buying it for the novelty or need a hardwired option.
