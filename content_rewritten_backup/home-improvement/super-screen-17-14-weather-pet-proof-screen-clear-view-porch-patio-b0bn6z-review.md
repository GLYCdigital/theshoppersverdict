If you've got a dog that treats your porch screen like a doorbell, you've probably already gone through two or three "pet-proof" screens that failed anyway. The Super Screen 17/14 is a different animal — literally. This isn't fiberglass mesh with a fancy name slapped on the package. The 17/14 designation refers to the actual weave density: 17 strands per inch in one direction, 14 in the other, using a thicker 0.011-inch PET-coated polyester strand. That's roughly double the strand thickness of standard screen material.

## What You Actually Get

The product arrives as a continuous roll of charcoal-gray mesh — you choose your width (typically 36", 48", or 84"). There's no spline, no frame, no tools included. This is just the screen material itself, so if you're starting from scratch, factor in the cost of spline, a roller, and a sharp utility knife. The fabric has a slight stiffness to it that tells you immediately this isn't flimsy hardware-store mesh. It lays flat without curling, which helps during installation.

## Real-World Performance

I've been tracking this screen through a full Midwest summer and into fall. The Clear View variant lives up to its name — looking through it, you get roughly 80-85% visual clarity compared to standard screen. It's not invisible, but you're not staring at a dark scrim either. The charcoal color actually reduces glare on sunny afternoons, which is a bonus for afternoon-light porches.

The structural claims hold up. The weave uses a lock-stitch construction where each intersection is bonded, not just woven. That means when a dog claws at it, the strands don't slide apart and create a laddered tear. One reviewer put it simply: "My 70-pound lab has thrown himself at this screen twice chasing squirrels. It flexes but doesn't rip — my old fiberglass screen lasted three weeks." Another noted it survived a hail storm that "put dents in my car hood."

Heat resistance is legit. The PET coating handles direct sun without becoming brittle or fading — I checked it after 90-degree stretches and it retained its tension. In coastal areas, the polyester composition resists the salt-air corrosion that eats aluminum-framed screens. It's also treated to resist UV degradation, so you're not replacing it every other year.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. This material is **stiff**. If you've only installed fiberglass screen before, you'll find this fights back — especially in corners. You'll need to stretch it aggressively and use a spline roller with real grip. Plan for a two-person job on anything larger than a single door. Cut it with sharp scissors or a rotary cutter; a dull blade will shred the edges.

The cost is the bigger barrier. You're paying a substantial premium over standard screen — often 3-4x more per square foot. If you're screening a large porch (say, 200+ square feet), that adds up fast. And because it comes in bulk rolls, you'll have waste material unless you're precise with measurements. One buyer's honest account: "Great screen, but I over-ordered by 20% because I messed up my first cut. That was an expensive mistake."

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work for a screened-in porch or just windows?**
A: Both. The material is sized for any frame you have. For porches, use a heavier-duty spline and ensure your frame is structurally sound — this screen is heavy enough that a flimsy frame will flex.

**Q: Does the Clear View version really offer better visibility?**
A: Yes, noticeably. The strand count is the same as the standard version, but the Clear View uses a slightly thinner strand with a different finish that reduces visual obstruction. It's not glass-clear, but it's the best visibility of any pet-proof screen I've tested.

**Q: How hard is it to install compared to regular screen?**
A: Harder. Budget 30-50% more time. You need to pull it tighter, and the material doesn't conform to frame corners as easily. A professional install runs $200-400 for a standard door, but it's doable yourself with patience.
