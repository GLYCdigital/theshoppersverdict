The Fluke 179 is the multimeter that electricians and HVAC techs buy when they're done replacing $40 meters every six months. It's not cheap, and it's not flashy. But after 235 Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars, the pattern is clear: this meter outlasts everything else on the truck. The question isn't whether it's good — it's whether you need this level of tool.

## What you actually get

The box includes the Fluke 179 meter, the TL71 silicone test leads with alligator clips, a built-in bead-wire Type K thermocouple probe, and a holster with a built-in test lead storage slot. The holster is worth noting — it's not just a rubber bumper. It has a magnetic hanging strap that wraps around conduit or a panel cover, which is genuinely useful when you're working alone.

The meter itself measures 6.4 x 3.6 x 2.0 inches and weighs 14.9 ounces with the holster. It's not the smallest meter on the market, but the ergonomics are right: the rotary dial clicks through 30 positions with a satisfying detent, and the buttons are raised enough to press with work gloves on.

## Real-world performance

The headline feature here is True-RMS measurement. If you only work on clean residential sine waves, a $60 meter handles that fine. But the moment you're troubleshooting a variable frequency drive, a dimmer switch, or any switching power supply, a non-TRMS meter reads garbage numbers. The Fluke 179 handles distorted waveforms accurately — that's the difference between finding a bad motor and chasing a ghost.

The built-in thermometer is the other big draw. The Type K input sits right on the front panel, and the included bead probe reads from -40°F up to 1000°F. For HVAC guys checking superheat and subcooling, this replaces a separate clamp thermometer. One reviewer noted, "I've used mine daily for three years checking duct temps and compressor lines — the thermocouple input is the reason I bought it and it hasn't missed a beat."

The backlight is a two-level amber LED that's plenty bright for dark crawlspaces and attics. It auto-off's after 20 minutes, which saves battery (one 9V lasts about 200 hours continuous), but it also means you'll get comfortable with the power button.

## What buyers consistently praise

The dominant theme across the reviews is longevity. Multiple buyers mention owning their 179 for 5-10 years with zero issues. One electrician wrote, "I dropped this from a 12-foot ladder onto concrete. The holster took the hit, meter still calibrated." That's the Fluke reputation in a nutshell.

Accuracy is the second pillar. The 0.09% basic DC accuracy spec is overkill for most field work, but the consistency is what matters. If you're doing motor winding resistance checks or verifying voltage drop, you can trust the number on the screen.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the drawbacks. The included TL71 leads are decent but not great — they're stiff in cold weather and the silicone insulation snags on sharp edges. Plan on upgrading to the TL175 leads with the rotating collar if you work in tight panels.

There's no LoZ (low-impedance) mode. That's the feature that lets you distinguish between real voltage and ghost voltage from induced coupling. The 179 will read 12V on a wire that's actually dead but sitting next to a live conductor. You'll learn to use the auto-range LoZ function on the Fluke 117 if that's a regular issue for you.

The price is the elephant in the room. This is a $400-class meter. If you're a homeowner changing outlets twice a year, this is overkill. If you're a professional whose meter is the difference between a correct diagnosis and a call-back, it pays for itself.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're an HVAC technician, commercial electrician, or industrial maintenance tech who works with variable frequency drives, motor controls, or refrigeration systems daily. The True-RMS accuracy and built-in thermocouple make it the go-to meter for these trades.

**Skip it if:** You're a weekend DIYer or work mainly on residential 120V circuits. A Fluke 117 or even a $50 Klein meter will handle that work. Save the money.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the Fluke 179 measure capacitance and frequency?**
A: Yes. It measures capacitance up to 10,000 µF and frequency up to 100 kHz. It also does diode and continuity testing with a beeper that's loud enough to hear in a noisy mechanical room.

**Q: Is this meter safe for CAT IV industrial work?**
A: Yes, it's rated CAT IV 600V and CAT III 1000V. That's the highest safety rating you'll find on a handheld multimeter. The input terminals are recessed to prevent accidental probe contact with live parts.

**Q: How do I calibrate the temperature probe?**
A: The thermocouple input is auto-calibrating to the meter's internal cold junction. You can zero it out using the relative (REL) button if you're working with small temperature differentials. Full calibration should be done by a certified lab annually if you're using it for critical HVAC charge verification.
