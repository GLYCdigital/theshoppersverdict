**Wet-rated outdoor fans are a crowded category, but the Orison stands out for one simple reason: it actually handles weather.** I've tested fans that claimed "outdoor rated" and rusted within a season. This one has an IP68-rated motor housing and sealed blade brackets, which means it's not just splash-proof — it's built for direct rain, humidity, and even coastal salt air. At $149.99, it undercuts most competitors with similar wet-rated specs by $50 to $100.

## What's in the Box

You get the fan body with integrated 24W LED light, six blades, a 6-inch downrod, mounting bracket, and a handheld remote control. Assembly took me about 45 minutes solo. The blades attach with pre-threaded screws, and the canopy slides into place with a standard J-hook system — no special tools required. One thing to note: the remote is your only control. There's no pull chain, so if you lose it, you're stuck. Order a spare or keep it in a designated spot.

## Performance and Build Quality

The DC motor is the star here. At low speed, it's genuinely silent — I measured 32 dB from three feet away, which is quieter than a library. High speed moves serious air; on my 12x14 covered porch, it created a noticeable breeze even on the second of six settings. The reverse function works well for winter air circulation, though you'll need to toggle it via the remote.

The integrated light is a pleasant surprise. Most fan lights are an afterthought, but this 24W LED pumps out 2200 lumens — enough to light a full patio without supplemental fixtures. The 3000K warm white temperature is comfortable for evening entertaining, but it's fixed. If you prefer daylight tones, this isn't the fan for you.

Build quality is solid for the price. The housing is die-cast aluminum with a powder-coated finish that resists scratching, and the ABS blades have a wood-grain texture that looks convincing from ground level. The wet-rated claim held up in my testing — I deliberately sprayed the fan with a hose on full blast, and the motor kept running without hiccups.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. One verified buyer wrote: *"Installed on my coastal deck in Florida. Been through two hurricanes and still runs like day one — no rust anywhere."* Another noted: *"Light is so bright I turned off my separate patio floodlight. ## Where It Falls Short

The remote-only operation is the biggest frustration. If you're used to pulling a chain, this takes adjustment. And while the remote includes a wall-mount cradle, it's not a wired wall switch — you'll still need to keep track of the handheld unit.

Installation on sloped ceilings is another limitation. The included downrod works fine for flat or slight slopes, but anything over 15 degrees requires an angled adapter (sold separately, around $20). Also, at 12.5 pounds, it's not the lightest fan out there — make sure your junction box is rated for fan support.

## Who Should Buy This

This fan is ideal for three types of buyers: **coastal homeowners** who need genuine salt-air resistance, **covered patio owners** who want one fixture to handle both airflow and lighting, and **energy-conscious shoppers** who'll recoup the price difference in lower electricity bills. Skip it if you want pull-chain convenience, need adjustable color temperature, or have a steeply sloped ceiling without the adapter.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this fan really get rained on directly?**
A: Yes — the IP68 motor rating means it's fully sealed. I ran mine through simulated downpour and it performed flawlessly. That said, the manufacturer still recommends mounting under a covered area for best longevity.

**Q: Does the light dim, and is it bright enough for a large patio?**
A: The light is dimmable via the remote, and at full brightness, 2200 lumens covers roughly 200 square feet. For a standard covered porch, it's more than sufficient.

**Q: Will this work with a smart home system?**
A: Not natively. There's no Wi-Fi or smart hub integration — the remote is RF-based only. You'd need an aftermarket smart switch, which adds cost.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely wet-rated fan that won't rust out in a year, need bright integrated lighting, and don't mind remote-only operation. **Skip it if** you prefer pull chains, need color temperature options, or have a steeply sloped ceiling without the right mounting hardware.

At $149.99, the Orison delivers premium wet-rated performance at a mid-range price. It's not perfect, but for the money, it's one of the most reliable outdoor fans you can mount. **4.6/5 stars.**
