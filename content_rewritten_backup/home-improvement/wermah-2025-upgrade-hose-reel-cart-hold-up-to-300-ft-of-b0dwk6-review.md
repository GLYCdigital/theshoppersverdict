If you've wrestled a 250-foot hose into a tangled mess one too many times, the WERMAH 2025 Upgrade Hose Reel Cart is aimed squarely at you. This is a serious piece of yard equipment, not a flimsy plastic turntable. It's built to hold a full 300 feet of 5/8-inch hose — the heaviest common residential size — and it does so without wobbling like a shopping cart with a bad wheel. At 4.2 stars across 210 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the reel cart frame, a 10-inch pneumatic tire set, the crank handle assembly, and all mounting hardware. You'll need your own hose and a standard wrench set. The frame is heavy-gauge steel with a powder-coated finish that resists rust better than the bare-metal carts I've tested. The handle is a folding design — a small detail that makes a big difference when you're trying to squeeze this into a shed corner. The unit weighs roughly 30 pounds empty, so it's light enough to maneuver but substantial enough that it won't blow over in a gust.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this cart with a 300-foot, 5/8-inch commercial-grade hose — the heaviest load it'll ever see. The pneumatic tires are the standout feature here. They're not the hard plastic wheels you find on cheaper carts; they actually absorb bumps and roll smoothly over uneven lawn. The ball bearings in the wheel hubs make a noticeable difference when you're pulling a fully loaded cart across a yard. The crank mechanism is geared smoothly, and the hose winds evenly without kinking — a common failure point on cheaper reels.

The swivel handle locks into three positions, which helps when you're navigating tight corners around flower beds or a parked car. The frame stayed stable even when I deliberately pulled the hose at sharp angles, which is where most carts in this price range tip over.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The 300-foot capacity is the recurring theme in positive reviews. One verified buyer wrote, *"Finally a cart that actually holds my full 300 ft hose. The tires make it easy to pull across my lawn, and the build is solid."* Another noted, *"I've had cheaper carts that bent after one season. ## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest friction point. Plan for 30 to 40 minutes, and pay close attention to the axle alignment — several reviewers mentioned that the wheels can bind if the axle isn't seated perfectly. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's not a five-minute snap-together job either.

Second, the cart ships without a hose. That's not unusual, but at this price point, some shoppers expect a bundled option. Budget an extra $40 to $60 for a quality 5/8-inch hose if you don't already own one.

Finally, the plastic hose guide — the small arm that feeds the hose onto the reel — feels slightly less robust than the rest of the metal frame. It works fine, but if you're rough with equipment, that's the first part that could crack.

## Who Should Buy This

This cart is for homeowners with large lots — think half an acre or more — who need a full 300 feet of reach and don't want to drag a hose around manually. It's also a strong pick for anyone who's burned through cheaper plastic reels and wants something that'll survive multiple seasons of outdoor storage. If you have a small yard and only need 100 feet, you're overpaying — a wall-mounted reel would serve you better.

## FAQ

**Will this hold a 5/8-inch hose or just a thinner one?**
It's specifically rated for 5/8-inch hose at 300 feet. If you use a 3/4-inch hose, drop down to about 200 feet to avoid overloading the reel.

**Can I leave it outside in winter?**
The powder coating helps, but no reel is fully weatherproof. Cover it or store it in a shed during harsh winters — the pneumatic tires will degrade faster than the metal frame.

**Does the crank handle get in the way when pulling the cart?**
No. It folds flat against the frame and locks, so it stays out of the way during transport.
