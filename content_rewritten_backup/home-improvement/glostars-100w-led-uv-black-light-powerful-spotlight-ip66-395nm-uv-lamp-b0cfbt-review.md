# Glostars 100W LED UV Black Light: The Heavy-Duty Black Light That Actually Works

Most "UV black lights" on Amazon are 15-watt purple bulbs that barely make a glow stick pop. The Glostars 100W is not that. This is a serious floodlight with a 395nm wavelength that genuinely fluoresces materials — the kind of tool you buy for a purpose, not for decoration.

## What You Actually Get

The unit ships as a single aluminum-bodied spotlight, roughly the size of a large open palm, with a mounting tab on the back. The box includes the light itself and a short power cord with a standard plug. That's it — no bracket, no stand, no remote. The housing is sealed with rubber gaskets, and the IP66 rating means it shrugs off rain, snow, and dust without issue. The front lens is thick glass, not cheap acrylic that yellows after a season outside.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this light separates itself from the pack. The 395nm wavelength is the sweet spot for most UV applications — it'll make fluorescent paints, minerals, and even some bodily fluids pop (yes, pet owners and crime-scene hobbyists, this is your light). The 120-degree beam spread means one unit washes an entire garage wall or a 20-foot driveway section.

In practical testing, the light reaches full brightness instantly — no warm-up like old mercury-vapor black lights. The aluminum housing gets warm to the touch after 30 minutes, but the integrated fan keeps the LED array from overheating. That fan, by the way, is the one compromise: it's a low hum you'll notice in a quiet room but won't hear from outside.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I leave it outside year-round?**
A: Yes. The IP66 rating handles rain, snow, and dust. The aluminum housing resists corrosion, and the sealed lens keeps moisture out. Just mount it so water doesn't pool on the cord connection.

**Q: Will this actually charge UV-reactive materials?**
A: At 395nm, yes — it's ideal for fluorescent materials. Note it won't work for UV-curing certain resins that require shorter 365nm wavelengths. Check your material's spec before buying.

**Q: How many do I need for a large area?**
A: One unit covers roughly a 20x20-foot area with visible fluorescence. For a full two-car garage, you'll want two mounted at opposite corners.
