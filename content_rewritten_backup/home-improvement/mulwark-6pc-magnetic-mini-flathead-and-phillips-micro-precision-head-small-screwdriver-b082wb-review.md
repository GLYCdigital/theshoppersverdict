If you fix your own glasses, open a laptop, or tinker with RC cars, you already know the pain: standard screwdrivers are too fat, and cheap precision sets bend or strip the first time you use them. The MulWark 6pc set aims straight at that gap — six micro drivers with magnetic tips that actually hold screws. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. Here's the full story after digging through real user feedback.

**What you get**  
Six drivers: three Phillips (#000, #0, #1) and three flathead (1.0mm, 1.5mm, 2.0mm). That's the exact spread you need for eyeglass hinge screws, MacBook bottom plates, drone arms, and jewelry clasps. Each driver is about 5.5 inches long, with a knurled metal shaft and a swivel cap on top that spins freely. The caps let you pinch the driver between thumb and index finger while applying controlled rotation — critical for not stripping tiny threads. No bits, no interchangeable handle, no bulky case. Just six dedicated drivers, which is honestly better for precision work because there's no slop from a bit holder.

**Performance and build**  
The magnetic pull is the headline feature, and it's genuinely strong. One reviewer who repairs watches noted, *"The magnet holds a #000 screw firmly even when the driver is pointed straight down — I've had $30 sets drop screws instantly."* That aligns with the composite material on the tips — it's not just a weak coating; the entire tip is magnetized. The shafts are machined steel, not stamped, so the tips fit snugly into Phillips heads without wobble. I found no reports of tip breakage, which is rare for sub-$15 micro drivers.

The swivel caps are the unsung hero. On a standard screwdriver, you twist your wrist and risk camming out. With these, you roll the cap between your fingers, giving you fine motor control. Multiple users mention using them for eyeglass adjustments without needing a magnifying lamp — that's a practical win.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
- **Magnet strength** — "These are the first micro drivers where the screw stays on the tip while I align it," wrote one 5-star reviewer.  
- **Tip durability** — Several users report months of daily use on electronics without wear.  
- **Ergonomic grip** — The knurled shaft gives traction even with slightly oily fingers.

**Where it falls short**  
No case is the biggest gripe. You get a clear plastic sleeve, but it's not a rigid organizer — drivers roll around loose in a toolbox. One reviewer said, *"I lost the 1.5mm flathead in a drawer within a week."* If you buy this, spend $3 on a canvas roll or magnetic strip. Also, the handles are slim (about 0.35 inches diameter). If you have large hands or need high torque on a stubborn screw, you'll find yourself choking up on the shaft. For precision work that's fine; for anything requiring oomph, it's not the tool.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Glasses wearers** who adjust their own frames — you'll use the 1.0mm flathead weekly.  
- **Laptop/phone repair hobbyists** doing battery swaps or screen replacements (the #000 Phillips is perfect for tri-point screws).  
- **RC and drone enthusiasts** who need to tighten tiny motor mounts without stripping.  
- **Jewelry makers** working with jump rings and clasps.

Skip it if you need a full 30-piece kit with a ratcheting handle — this is a focused, minimal set, not a do-everything system.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Are these magnetic enough to pick up screws from a flat surface?**  
A: Yes. The tips can lift a #000 screw vertically. For larger #1 Phillips screws, the magnet holds them but you'll want to guide them into place rather than expect a strong snap.

**Q: Will the flathead tips scratch my glasses lenses?**  
A: The tips are polished steel, not hardened cutting edges. As long as you use the correct size (1.0mm for most frames), you won't scratch. Avoid the 2.0mm on anything smaller than a watch back.

**Q: Do the swivel caps loosen over time?**  
A: No reports of that in 225 ratings. The caps are press-fit and stay snug. One user has had his for 18 months without wobble.
