## The 60-Yard Roll That Actually Lasts

Most painter's tape rolls tap out at 45 yards. This ScotchBlue gives you 60 — and that extra 15 yards matters when you're masking an entire room's trim. At 1.41 inches wide, it's the sweet spot for baseboards, door frames, and crown molding. The question is whether it holds up to the job. After testing across drywall, cured latex, and fresh paint, here's the honest picture.

## What's in the Box

You get one 1.41-inch by 60-yard roll of blue, medium-adhesion tape. That's it. No dispenser, no edge cutter. The cardboard core is standard, so it fits most tape dispensers if you own one. For a roll this size, expect it to handle roughly 1,500 linear feet of masking — enough for two to three average bedrooms.

## Performance You Can Feel

The tape's medium-adhesion formula is the real story. It grips firmly enough to hold on textured drywall and baseboards, but removes without leaving that sticky residue cheap tapes leave behind. On cured latex paint — paint that's been dry for at least a week — the line is razor sharp. I tested it on a window frame with semi-gloss trim and got zero bleed-through.

One note: this tape is designed for **smooth to slightly textured surfaces**. On rough brick or popcorn ceilings, the edges won't seal completely, and paint will seep under. That's not a flaw — it's physics. Use a high-adhesion tape for those jobs.

## Where It Falls Short

No tape is perfect, and this one has two honest weaknesses. First, removal timing matters. Pull it too early — before paint has cured 48 hours — and you'll lift fresh paint. Second, the tear-by-hand edges are never perfectly straight. If you're doing fine detail work, you'll want a razor blade or a tape dispenser with a cutter. Several reviewers mentioned this: "Tearing by hand leaves jagged edges, but a quick trim with a utility knife solves it."

## Who Should Buy This

## FAQ

**Q: How long can I leave this tape on before removing?**
Up to 14 days. Beyond that, the adhesive starts to bond more firmly and removal gets harder.

**Q: Will it damage my freshly painted walls?**
Not if you wait 48 hours for paint to cure before taping, and remove the tape while paint is dry. Pull at a 45-degree angle slowly.

**Q: Is this tape waterproof?**
It's moisture-resistant for occasional splashes, but not designed for prolonged water exposure. Keep it dry for best results.
