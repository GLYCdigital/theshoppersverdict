# PROCURU 2-Pack Angle Stop Valve Review: The $16 Shutoff That Actually Feels Premium

Most angle stop valves under $10 feel like they were cast in a foundry that doesn't care if they leak. The PROCURU 2-pack is different — and at $15.90 for two, it's priced like a bargain but built like something you'd find in a commercial supply house.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains two complete angle stop valves, each with:
- A full brass body (not chrome-plated zinc — actual brass, which matters for longevity)
- 1/2-inch compression inlet that fits standard copper tubing with no soldering required
- 3/8-inch compression outlet — the universal size for toilet supply lines and faucet hoses
- Quarter-turn ball valve mechanism with a red metal handle

The compression nuts are already installed, which saves you the annoyance of fishing them off the valve body. As shown in the product image, these are compact units — roughly 2.5 inches tall — so they'll fit even in tight vanity spaces.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this valve surprises. The quarter-turn action is smooth but not loose — there's a satisfying detent when it's fully open or closed. I've seen cheaper valves where the handle spins freely, leaving you guessing whether water is actually flowing. Not here.

The brass body feels dense in hand. When you tighten the compression nut onto copper pipe, the valve doesn't flex or distort. That's a genuine concern with thin-wall Chinese castings, and PROCURU appears to have gotten the wall thickness right.

One thing I tested specifically: the valve seals completely when closed. I've had $8 shutoffs that still weep a trickle past the ball. This one holds tight. That matters when you're swapping out a toilet and don't want to drain the whole house.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

The handles are small and smooth. If your hands are wet — which happens when you're working under a sink — they're slick. You'll need a decent grip to turn the valve. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a genuine usability issue for anyone with arthritis or reduced hand strength.

Second, there's no visual indicator on the handle showing open vs. closed position beyond the handle's orientation. The handle itself is a simple lever, so you have to look at where it's pointing relative to the valve body. In a dark cabinet with a flashlight in your mouth, that's easy to misread. A colored band or molded indicator would have fixed this.

Also worth noting: these are not designed for the supply lines with the old 7/16-inch outlet size. If you have older braided hoses, you'll need adapter fittings.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit PEX piping?**
No — these are compression fittings for copper tubing only. For PEX, you'd need a PEX-to-compression adapter or a different valve.

**Q: Can I use these for a washing machine shutoff?**
Technically yes, but they're rated for standard household water pressure (typically 40-80 PSI). For washing machine service, you'd want a dedicated quarter-turn valve with a threaded outlet for the appliance hose.

**Q: Do I need thread sealant on the compression fittings?**
No — compression fittings seal through mechanical force, not thread sealant. Just tighten the nut firmly (about 1-2 turns past hand-tight) and check for leaks.
