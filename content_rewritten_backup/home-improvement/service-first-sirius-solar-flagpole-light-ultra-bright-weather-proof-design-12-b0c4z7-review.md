**If you fly a flag at night, you already know the problem: either you leave a porch light on all evening (wasteful), or your flag disappears into the dark after sunset. The Service First Sirius Solar Flagpole Light aims to fix that with a 360-degree LED ring that clips onto your existing pole — no wiring, no electrician, no drilling.**

Here's what 220 Amazon reviewers at 4.4 stars actually think after living with it.

## What's in the Box

The package includes the light head itself (a ring-shaped housing about 6 inches in diameter), a mounting cradle that wraps around your flagpole, three Allen screws for securing it, and a small solar panel that sits on top of the light. That's it. No tools beyond the included hex key.

The build quality is respectable for the price point. The housing is UV-stabilized ABS plastic with a rubber gasket where the two halves meet. It's not military-grade, but it's clearly designed to survive a few seasons of rain, snow, and summer heat. The solar panel is a monocrystalline cell rated at 2.5W, which is on the higher end for this category.

## Real-World Performance

**The headline feature is the 360-degree illumination.** Unlike cheaper flagpole lights that use a single downward beam, the Sirius rings the pole with 48 individual LEDs arranged in a circle. This matters because it eliminates the "one side lit, other side shadowed" problem. Reviewers consistently note that the entire flag — even a 5x8 foot one — stays visible and evenly lit.

One buyer wrote: *"I've had three different solar flag lights over the years. This is the first one where my neighbor's flag actually looks good from every angle, not just head-on."*

**Installation is genuinely tool-free.** The cradle opens like a clamp, wraps around the pole, and tightens with the Allen bolts. It fits poles from 3/4 inch to 1 inch in diameter. The whole process takes about five minutes — you don't even need a ladder if your pole has a pulley system.

**The dusk-to-dawn sensor works as advertised.** It triggers at roughly the same light level as a standard streetlight sensor, and it shuts off at dawn. On a full day of direct sun, you get 8-12 hours of runtime. That's enough for most of the year, though winter days with shorter sunlight will leave you with a dimmer flag by 4 AM.

## What Buyers Praise

- **Brightness:** Multiple reviewers compared it favorably to wired 12V floodlights. The 360-degree spread is the standout feature.
- **Weather resistance:** Owners in Florida, Texas, and the Northeast report surviving hurricanes, ice storms, and 100°F summers without water ingress or cracking.
- **Zero ongoing cost:** Once it's up, it's up. No wiring, no batteries to replace annually (the internal 2200mAh Li-ion is rated for 500+ cycles).

One reviewer summed it up: *"I was skeptical about solar, but this thing outshines the $200 wired light I had before. Best $60 I've spent on the house."*

## Where It Falls Short

**Battery degradation is the #1 complaint.** After 12-18 months, several reviewers report runtime dropping to 4-6 hours. Replacing the battery requires removing the housing, which means taking the light off the pole and disassembling it. It's doable but fiddly — you'll need a small Phillips screwdriver and patience.

**The brightness fades over the evening.** The light is brightest for the first 2-3 hours after sunset, then gradually dims as the battery drains. By 3 AM, you're getting maybe 60% output. If you're a night owl who wants your flag lit at 2 AM, this will disappoint.

**The mounting cradle can slip on smooth poles.** If your flagpole is polished aluminum or has a powder-coated finish, the rubber grip pad inside the cradle can shift slightly in high winds. One reviewer solved this by wrapping electrical tape around the pole first. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a standard residential flagpole (up to 20 feet), want even 360-degree illumination without hiring an electrician, and don't mind replacing the battery every 18 months or so.

**Skip it if:** You need maximum brightness all night long, have a pole wider than 1 inch, or live in an area with less than 4 hours of direct winter sun — you'll be disappointed by the runtime.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work on a 20-foot flagpole?**
A: Yes. The light mounts at the top and illuminates the flag below it. The 48 LEDs provide enough output for flags up to 5x8 feet, even at that height. Just make sure your pole diameter is between 3/4 and 1 inch.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: On a full charge, expect 8-12 hours. Realistically, that means dusk to about 3-4 AM in summer, less in winter. The battery will degrade after a year or so of daily cycling.

**Q: Can you replace the battery?**
A: Yes, but it's not user-friendly. You'll need to remove the light from the pole, unscrew the housing, and replace the 18650 battery inside. It's a 20-minute job, not a 5-minute one.
