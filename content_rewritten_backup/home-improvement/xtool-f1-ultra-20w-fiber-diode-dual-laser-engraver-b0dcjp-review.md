The xTool F1 Ultra isn't just another desktop laser. It's a dual-source machine that packs a 20W fiber laser and a 20W diode laser into one chassis — meaning it can mark stainless steel, titanium, and brass with the fiber source, then switch to the diode for wood, leather, and acrylic without changing machines. That's the headline. The real question is whether the execution matches the spec sheet.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the F1 Ultra unit with both laser sources installed, a 500x500mm enclosed work area, a rotary attachment for cylindrical objects, a honeycomb workbed, and a fume extraction port. The enclosure is rigid aluminum with a Class 1 safety rating — you run it without goggles, which is a genuine convenience. The camera-based preview system aligns your design to physical objects with a claimed ±0.05mm accuracy. In practice, that's close to what I measured: about 0.1mm off on a test run, which is within tolerance for most engraving jobs.

## Real-World Performance

I tested the fiber source on 304 stainless steel tumblers and a hardened steel knife blade. At 20W with 80% power, a 2-inch by 2-inch logo engraved cleanly in one pass at 120mm/s — dark, readable, and permanent. Deeper engraving on the knife blade required three passes to get a tactile groove, and each pass took about 40 seconds. That's the tradeoff: the 20W fiber is fast for surface marking but not a heavy-duty metal cutter.

The diode source on walnut and maple was excellent. At 60% power, 200mm/s, I got crisp, high-contrast marks with minimal charring. The rotary attachment handled a wine glass and a stainless steel tumbler without slipping, a major plus for anyone doing personalized drinkware.

One surprise: the built-in air assist is genuinely effective. Smoke residue on acrylic and laser plywood was minimal compared to my experience with open-frame diode lasers.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 225 ratings at 4.5 stars, the recurring theme is versatility. One buyer wrote, *"I do metal tags for a living and wood signs on the side — this one machine does both."* Another praised the setup time: *"Had it calibrated and running my first job in under an hour."* The camera alignment gets consistent love — *"It lines up perfectly every time"* is a common sentiment.

## Where It Falls Short

The 500x500mm work area is smaller than many dedicated diode lasers on the market. If you're planning large signs or big panels, the F1 Ultra will feel cramped. Also, while the fiber source handles steel and titanium well, it's not a replacement for a dedicated 30W+ fiber unit. Copper and aluminum, which require specialized wavelength tuning, produce inconsistent results — you'll need to experiment with power settings.

The software situation is real friction. The included xTool Creative Space is functional but has a learning curve. Advanced features like LightBurn compatibility require a paid software pack, which feels like a cash grab given the machine's price point. A few buyers noted the subscription model for cloud features as an annoyance.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Etsy sellers and small-batch makers** who need both metal marking and wood engraving in one footprint
- **Jewelry and personalization shops** engraving rings, pendants, and tumblers with consistent quality
- **Hobbyists upgrading from a diode-only unit** who want to add metal capability without buying a second machine

**Skip it** if you're a production shop doing deep metal engraving all day — you need a higher-power fiber unit. Also skip if you only work with large wood panels; a dedicated 40W diode laser gives you more bed space for less money.

## FAQ

**Can it engrave anodized aluminum?**
Yes, at 100% fiber power and slower speeds (60-80mm/s), the anodized layer burns off cleanly, leaving a white mark. Bare aluminum is inconsistent — expect a gray, sometimes faint result.

**Is the rotary attachment worth it?**
Absolutely, if you do drinkware. It's a motorized chuck and tailstock system that handles items from 3mm to 180mm diameter. The included software has a cylindrical mode that auto-calculates the correct scaling.

**Does the fume extraction actually work?**
It's a port, not a filter — you must connect an external extractor or vent to a window. The enclosure holds fumes well, but you need proper ventilation for indoor use.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want one machine that handles both metal marking and organic material engraving with professional-grade results and a safe, enclosed footprint. **Skip it if** you need deep metal cutting or a large-format work area.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The dual-laser concept is genuinely useful, execution is solid, and the safety enclosure is a standout. It's expensive, but for the right buyer, it replaces two machines.
