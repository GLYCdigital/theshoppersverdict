If you're speccing a curbless shower or a large-format tile layout, the drain is the first thing you should design around — not the last thing you pick out at the hardware store. The Neodrain linear series gives you six length options from 24 to 60 inches, all built from 304 stainless steel with a brushed finish that holds up better than the cheap chrome-plated zinc you'll find in most big-box stores. At 4.7 stars across 235 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in real bathrooms. Here's what actually matters before you buy.

## What's in the Box

Each unit ships with the stainless steel channel body, the removable grate, a hair catcher basket, and adjustable leveling feet. You're getting the drain assembly itself — not the waterproofing membrane connector, which is sold separately. That's a bit annoying, but it's also standard practice for linear drains that need to interface with different waterproofing systems (liquid membrane, sheet membrane, or PVC liner). The grate is 1.8 inches deep on the channel, which is typical for a linear drain but worth measuring against your subfloor before you commit.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The grate is the star here. It's machined from solid 304 stainless — not stamped — so it doesn't flex or warp when you walk on it. The brushed finish hides water spots and soap scum far better than polished or painted surfaces. As one verified buyer put it: *"The grate sits completely flush with my tile. No lipped edges, no water pooling on the sides. It looks like part of the floor."* That flush fit isn't just cosmetic — it prevents water from sitting in the grout lines around a traditional round drain, which is where mold gets a foothold.

The drainage rate is strong. The 2-inch outlet handles the full flow of a rain shower head without backing up, and the hair catcher basket catches the bulk of debris before it reaches the P-trap. Several reviewers noted they clean the basket every two to three weeks instead of snaking the drain every few months. One reviewer summed it up: *"I have long hair and two kids. This thing catches everything. I just lift the grate, dump the basket, and I'm done."*

The adjustable feet are a genuine time-saver. You can level the drain to your tile flange height without shimming with mortar or cutting custom spacers. That said, the Phillips head screws on the grate are small — use a proper #2 bit and don't torque them down hard, or you'll strip the heads. Two reviewers mentioned this exact issue.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest gotcha is the depth. At 1.8 inches, this drain needs real space below the finished floor. If you're retrofitting a shower on a standard joist system without dropping the subfloor, you'll likely need to build up the surrounding floor or cut into the joists — which is a structural job, not a plumbing job. This is a design-for-it-from-the-start product, not a swap-in replacement for a round drain.

The separate waterproofing connector is also a point of friction. You'll need to know which membrane system you're using before you order, and the connector adds roughly $20–30 to the total cost. Some buyers didn't realize this until install day, which caused delays.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Tile installers and GCs** building curbless or low-profile showers where drainage slope matters
- **DIYers doing a full bathroom reno** with a pre-planned subfloor drop
- **Anyone using large-format tile** who wants a clean, grout-free drain line instead of a circular interruption

Skip it if you're doing a quick drain swap on an existing shower pan — this is a build-in product, not a retrofit.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this drain work with a Schluter-style foam shower pan?**  
A: Yes, but you need the Neodrain waterproofing connector kit to create a watertight seal between the drain flange and the pan membrane. The connector is sold separately.

**Q: Can I cut the grate to fit a custom shower width?**  
A: No. The grate is machined to spec. Order the closest standard length (24, 28, 32, 36, 48, or 60 inches) and adjust your tile layout around it.

**Q: What's the flow rate compared to a standard 2-inch round drain?**  
A: Comparable — the 2-inch outlet matches standard plumbing. The linear design actually improves surface drainage because it captures water across a wider plane, so you can use less slope in the pan.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're building a new shower or doing a full gut renovation and want a drain that looks like it belongs in a high-end spa, not a retrofit afterthought. The stainless build quality and flush-fit design justify the price.

**Skip it if** you're swapping a drain on an existing shower floor or you haven't planned for the 1.8-inch channel depth. This is a design-first product — it rewards preparation and punishes improvisation.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the separate waterproofing connector requirement and the fragile grate screws. Everything else is exactly what a premium linear drain should be. If you're in the middle of a tile shower build, this is the one to get.
