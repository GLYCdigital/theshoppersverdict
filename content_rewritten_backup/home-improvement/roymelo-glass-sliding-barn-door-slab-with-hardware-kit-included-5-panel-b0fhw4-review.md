**The ROYMELO Glass Sliding Barn Door — a 70-pound statement piece that will either elevate your hallway or test your patience during installation. There's no middle ground here.**

You're looking at this door because you want something that says "modern farmhouse" without the barn-door cliché of reclaimed wood planks. The ROYMELO's 5-panel tempered glass design delivers that. But before you hit buy, understand exactly what you're getting into — this isn't a weekend-afternoon swap for most DIYers.

---

**What's Actually in the Box**

The package includes the door slab itself, a steel track, two roller hangers, a floor guide, and all necessary mounting hardware. The glass panels are 5mm tempered — noticeably thicker and more substantial than the 3mm glass on budget competitors. The black steel frame is welded, not screwed together, which explains why the whole unit weighs in around 70 pounds.

One thing buyers consistently note in the 210 Amazon ratings: the packaging is solid. Multiple reviewers mention the door arriving without chips or cracks, which is no small feat for a glass product shipped via freight. I've seen worse packaging on $2,000 mirrors.

---

**Real-World Performance and Installation Reality**

Here's the honest truth about installation: the track must be mounted into wood studs or concrete. This is non-negotiable. The door's weight — roughly 70 pounds of glass and steel — will pull drywall anchors straight out of the wall within weeks. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"I had to return mine because I live in an apartment with hollow walls. Don't buy this unless you have solid mounting points."*

That said, if you have the right wall structure, the sliding mechanism is smooth. The rollers ride on a ball-bearing system that glides quietly — no grinding or sticking. The floor guide keeps the door aligned without rattling.

The glass itself is a double-edged sword. It looks stunning, letting light pass while providing visual privacy through its translucent finish. But it's a fingerprint magnet. If you have kids under 10 or a dog that leans on doors, budget for a glass cleaner in your weekly routine.

---

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The aesthetic is the runaway winner. Reviewers repeatedly describe the door as "a conversation piece" and "the focal point of the room." The industrial-modern look pairs well with both contemporary and transitional decor. One five-star review captured it: *"I've gotten more compliments on this door in two weeks than on anything else in my house."*

The included hardware also earns consistent praise. The track is thick-gauge steel with a powder-coated finish that resists scratching, and the rollers are adjustable for height — a small detail that makes leveling the door significantly easier.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the fingerprint issue, the lack of a soft-close mechanism is the most common complaint. At this price point, you'd reasonably expect a hydraulic dampener. Without it, the door can slam if released carelessly — and a 70-pound glass door slamming is not a subtle event. You can add a soft-close kit aftermarket for about $30, but it's an extra purchase you shouldn't have to make.

The installation instructions are also sparse. They include diagrams, but several reviewers note the text is minimal and the diagrams assume prior hardware experience. If you're not comfortable with a level and a stud finder, budget for professional installation — it'll cost $150–$250, but it's worth every penny for a door this heavy.

---

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Homeowners with solid wood studs** — you have the right wall structure for a secure install
- **Design-conscious renovators** who want the barn door look without the rustic wood aesthetic
- **Anyone replacing a swinging door** in a narrow hallway or closet where swing clearance is an issue

**Skip it if:**

- You're in a rental with drywall-only walls
- You have young kids who will grab and pull on the glass constantly
- You expect a silent, soft-close operation out of the box

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this door be installed on drywall with just anchors?**
A: No. The door weighs roughly 70 pounds. You need to mount the track into wood studs, concrete, or steel framing. Anchors alone will fail.

**Q: Is the glass truly privacy-grade?**
A: The translucent frosted finish obscures shapes and outlines, but it's not fully opaque. You'll see movement and silhouettes through it.

**Q: Does the hardware kit include a floor guide?**
A: Yes, a floor-mounted guide is included. It keeps the door tracking straight and minimizes sway.
