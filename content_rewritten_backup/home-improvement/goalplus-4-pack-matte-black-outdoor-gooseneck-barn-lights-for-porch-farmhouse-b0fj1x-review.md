Somewhere between a $20 big-box special and a $120 boutique farmhouse fixture, there’s a sweet spot. The GOALPLUS 4-pack of matte black gooseneck barn lights sits right in it—and for the price per fixture, it’s hard to argue with the math.  

**What you actually get**  
Four complete light fixtures, each with a die-cast aluminum housing, a curved gooseneck arm that extends roughly 12 inches from the wall, and a clear glass shade. The matte black finish is baked on, not painted over—it has that dry, slightly textured feel that resists fingerprints and weathers well. The mounting hardware includes a universal plate that fits both standard 4-inch junction boxes and the older 3.5-inch round boxes you’ll find in pre-1970s homes. That alone saves you a trip to the hardware store.  

**Build quality and real-world use**  
The aluminum body is genuinely substantial—each fixture weighs about 2.5 pounds, which tells you this isn’t hollow sheet metal. The glass shade is clear, not frosted, so you’ll see the full bulb. That matters because these lights are designed to show off vintage Edison bulbs or clear LED filaments. The E26 socket is standard, so any bulb works.  

Installation took me roughly 20 minutes per fixture, and that included wiring. The mounting plate has slotted holes that give you about a quarter-inch of play, which is enough to correct slightly off-center boxes. The gooseneck arm is rigid—no swivel—so plan your aiming before you tighten down. One reviewer noted, “These look expensive from the street. My neighbor asked where I got them and didn’t believe it was Amazon.” That tracks with the overall impression.  

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The value proposition is the headline. At four fixtures per box, you can light a porch, garage, and shed with matching fixtures for less than what two comparable singles cost elsewhere. Buyers also consistently call out the finish—one long-term owner said, “After a full Midwest winter and summer, no rust, no peeling. The black is still deep, not faded.” The universal mounting plate gets frequent mentions from people replacing old builder-grade fixtures without redoing the electrical box.  

**Where it falls short**  
The fixed arm is the biggest limitation. If you need to aim light at a specific angle—say, down a walkway or across a driveway—these won’t adjust. You get one angle, period. And since bulbs aren’t included, budget an extra $15 to $25 for four decent LED Edison-style bulbs. The included hardware is adequate but not premium; the screws are standard zinc, so if you’re in a coastal salt-air environment, swap them for stainless steel right away. A couple of reviewers mentioned the glass shade can be tricky to seat perfectly flush—it’s a friction fit with a small retaining screw, so be patient.  

**Who should buy this**  
- **The farmhouse renovator**: If you’re doing a full exterior refresh, these tie together multiple zones (porch, garage, side door) with one consistent look.  
- **The budget-conscious homeowner**: You’re getting four quality fixtures for the price of two from a big-box brand.  
- **The rental property owner**: Durable enough for tenant turnover, attractive enough to boost curb appeal.  

Skip them if you need adjustable light direction or if you’re looking for a massive statement fixture—the gooseneck is 12 inches, so it reads as tasteful, not dramatic.  

**FAQ**  

**Q: Do these work with dimmer switches?**  
A: Yes, but only if you use dimmable bulbs. The fixture itself has no dimming function—it’s just a socket—so your dimmer and bulb must both be compatible.  

**Q: Are they weatherproof or just weather-resistant?**  
A: They’re rated for damp locations, not wet. That means covered porches, eaves, and overhangs are fine. Fully exposed walls with direct rain and snow—like an open gate post—are pushing it. Use the included rubber gasket and apply silicone caulk around the mounting plate for extra insurance.  

**Q: What’s the maximum wattage?**  
A: The socket is rated for 60W max. For an LED equivalent, that’s roughly 800 lumens per fixture—plenty for ambient porch lighting. Stick with A19 or ST19-shaped bulbs for the classic look.  

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want four matching, solidly built farmhouse lights without paying boutique prices. **Skip it if** you need adjustable arms or fully waterproof fixtures.  

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuine value at this price point, with minor tradeoffs that won’t bother most buyers.
