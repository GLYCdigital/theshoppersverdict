The Delta Woodhurst 14 Series isn't the flashiest shower faucet on the market, but it's the one you'll likely forget about — and that's the highest compliment you can pay a plumbing fixture. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this matte black set has quietly become a favorite for bathroom remodels. After digging through the specs and what real buyers are saying, here's what you need to know before spending your money.

**What's Actually in the Box**

Delta ships this as a complete kit: the rough-in valve body, pressure-balancing cartridge, trim plate, handle, shower arm, shower head, and all mounting hardware. You're not hunting for missing pieces at the hardware store, which buyers consistently mention. One verified purchaser noted, "Everything I needed was in the box including the valve — saved me a trip and probably $60 buying parts separately."

The matte black finish is electroplated rather than powder-coated, which matters. It resists the greenish-blue corrosion that plagues cheap black fixtures in hard water areas. The shower head itself is a standard 6-inch round design with 60 silicone spray nozzles — those stay clear of mineral buildup because you can wipe them clean with a finger.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The heart of this system is Delta's Diamond Seal technology. Instead of rubber washers that degrade and cause drips, the cartridge uses a ceramic disc that's pressure-tested to last through 10 million uses. That's roughly 27 years of daily showers. The pressure-balancing valve is the real workhorse here — if someone flushes a toilet while you're in the shower, the water temperature doesn't swing violently. It holds steady within a couple degrees.

Installation is where opinions split. The valve body is solid brass, but the trim plate is plastic. It's not flimsy plastic — it's the same ABS used in automotive interiors — but if you're paying for a premium look, running your fingers over a plastic escutcheon can feel like a letdown. That said, several DIY reviewers praised the install process. As one put it: "I'm not a plumber and had this swapped out in an afternoon. The instructions were actually clear."

Where it falls short: the flow rate is capped at 1.75 gallons per minute. That's fine for everyday washing, but if you're coming from a 2.5 GPM head, the difference is noticeable. It's a softer spray, not a pounding massage. The handle also only rotates about 90 degrees. You can find the sweet spot, but it takes a bit more fiddling than a valve with a wider rotation arc.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The finish durability is the most common theme in positive reviews. Multiple buyers report months of daily use with zero spotting or fingerprints showing. The temperature stability gets frequent shout-outs — families with kids and multiple bathrooms especially appreciate it. And the install flexibility is a genuine plus: the valve body fits standard 1/2-inch copper or PEX plumbing without adapters.

**Honest Downsides**

Beyond the plastic trim and flow rate, there's the handle. It's a small lever that can be stiff for the first week until the cartridge seats. A few reviewers mentioned it loosens up but never becomes buttery smooth. Also, the matte black finish — while durable — will show any water spots if you have extremely hard water. You'll want a squeegee or periodic wipe-down.

**Who Should Buy This**

This set is ideal for the DIY homeowner on a mid-range budget who wants a reliable, good-looking fixture without paying Delta's premium for the top-tier Trinsic line. It's also a strong choice for rental properties or guest baths where you want durability over luxury. Skip it if you're designing a spa-level master bath with a rainfall ceiling mount — this isn't that product. It's a solid, workhorse shower valve that does its job without drama.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Does this include the valve, or just the trim?**
It includes the complete rough-in valve, cartridge, trim, handle, shower arm, and head. One box, everything needed.

**Will this fit my existing plumbing?**
The valve body uses standard 1/2-inch connections and fits a 3-1/2-inch center-to-center stud spacing, which is the standard for most U.S. homes. If you're replacing an old valve, you'll likely need access behind the wall.

**Is the matte black finish prone to chipping?**
The electroplated finish is baked on at the factory. It's much more durable than painted finishes, but like any dark finish, it can scratch if you drop a metal object on it. Normal use and cleaning won't damage it.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy It**

**Buy it if:** You want a reliable, leak-resistant shower valve with a modern matte black look, you're tackling the install yourself, and you value temperature stability over high-pressure flow.

**Skip it if:** You need a rainfall-style drenching experience, you insist on all-metal trim, or you're outfitting a luxury master bath where aesthetic refinement matters more than function.

For most homeowners, the Delta Woodhurst 14 Series is the quiet, competent choice that will serve you well for decades — and that's worth every penny.
