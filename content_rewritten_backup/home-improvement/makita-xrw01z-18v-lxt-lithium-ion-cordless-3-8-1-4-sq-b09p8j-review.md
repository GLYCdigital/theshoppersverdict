# The Makita XRW01Z: The Ratchet That Actually Earns Its Battery

Most cordless ratchets are toys. They spin fast, sound impressive, and then stall the moment they hit a torqued bolt. The Makita XRW01Z is not that. This is a real tool for real work — but it's not perfect, and you need to know where it fits before you drop your money.

## What You Actually Get

The XRW01Z is a bare tool. No battery, no charger, no case. What you do get is the ratchet head itself with both a 3/8-inch and 1/4-inch square drive — a nice touch since most manufacturers make you buy the head separately. The tool weighs 2.6 pounds with a battery attached, which is heavier than a manual ratchet but lighter than most other cordless options in this class. The rubberized grip runs the full length of the handle, and Makita's XPT (eXtreme Protection Technology) seals the electronics against dust and moisture.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this ratchet separates itself. The XRW01Z delivers 35 ft-lbs of torque at 2,200 RPM. Those numbers matter because they mean it can actually break loose caliper bracket bolts, strut nuts, and exhaust hangers — work that smaller 12V ratchets simply can't handle. The variable speed trigger is genuinely useful; you can feather it for delicate work like oil pan bolts, then mash it for stubborn fasteners.

I've seen these in professional shops for two years now, and the consensus holds: it's the most powerful compact ratchet Makita makes. The 18V platform means it shares batteries with drills, impacts, and saws — if you already own Makita, this is a no-brainer addition.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews tells a consistent story. One mechanic put it plainly: *"This thing has paid for itself in a week. I've used it on brake jobs, suspension work, and under dashes. The torque surprised me."* Multiple buyers note the twin LED lights are positioned correctly — they illuminate the fastener head, not the area around it, which is a small detail most manufacturers get wrong.

Another reviewer with a diesel truck called it *"the best tool I've bought in years for working on my own vehicles. It's saved my wrists on jobs that used to require a breaker bar."* That's the recurring theme: this ratchet saves physical effort without sacrificing power.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the limitations. The head is 3.1 inches long — noticeably bulkier than a standard manual ratchet. You'll still need a regular ratchet for tight spots like the back of an engine block or inside a transmission bell housing. The tool is also bare, so unless you're already invested in Makita's 18V LXT line, you're looking at another $100+ for a battery and charger.

One more thing: this is not a torque wrench. It's for speed and convenience. Final torque specs still require a click-type wrench. And while the 35 ft-lbs is respectable, it won't break free rusty lug nuts — that's still impact driver territory.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a home mechanic who does brake jobs, suspension work, or engine repairs on your own vehicles. You already own Makita 18V batteries. You want to save your wrists and time on repetitive fastener work.

**Skip it if:** You only do light assembly work, you're not already in the LXT ecosystem, or you need a ratchet for ultra-tight clearance spots where the bulkier head won't fit.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit between the frame and body on a truck?**
A: Probably not. The head is over 3 inches long. Use a manual ratchet for those tight spots and save this for open-access work.

**Q: Can it remove lug nuts?**
A: No. 35 ft-lbs won't touch torqued lug nuts. You need an impact wrench for that. This is for mid-torque fasteners only.

**Q: Does it come with a battery and charger?**
A: No, it's a bare tool. The "Z" in the model number means it ships without batteries. Budget accordingly.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Makita XRW01Z is the best cordless ratchet you can buy if you're already in the 18V LXT system. It delivers genuine torque, has a quality build, and the dual-head design adds real value. The bulkier head and bare-tool nature are honest tradeoffs, not dealbreakers.

**Buy it if** you're a serious DIYer or professional who's tired of hand-ratcheting caliper bolts. **Skip it if** you haven't invested in Makita batteries yet — the additional cost makes it a harder sell against 12V options. For everyone in between, this ratchet will earn its spot in your box within the first week.
