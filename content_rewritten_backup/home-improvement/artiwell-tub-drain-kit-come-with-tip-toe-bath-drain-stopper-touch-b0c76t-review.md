Let's be direct about the Artiwell Tub Drain Kit: it's a solid mid-tier replacement that solves the most annoying bathroom problem — a drain stopper that either won't seal or won't open. But before you click buy, understand what you're actually getting.

**What's in the box**

The kit includes the tip-toe drain stopper assembly, a matching overflow plate, two sets of mounting screws, and a rubber gasket. No plumber's putty, no Teflon tape — you won't need either if your current flange is clean. The main body is machined brass, not the zinc alloy you'll find in cheaper $15 kits. That matters because brass won't corrode when it meets acidic water or cleaning chemicals.

**The installation reality**

Here's where Artiwell earns its keep. The drain body fits both 1-1/4" and 1-1/2" openings — a thoughtful touch since many older tubs have non-standard sizing. As one verified buyer put it: *"Replaced my 20-year-old stopper in about 8 minutes. The old one was rusted solid, and this dropped right in with no adjustments."* You remove the old flange, clean the drain pipe mouth, seat the new gasket, and thread the body in by hand. The overflow plate covers the standard 6" x 6" opening, and the screws align properly on most tubs.

**How it performs daily**

The tip-toe mechanism works like it should: press down to seal, press again to release. The seal is snug when new — water stays put for a full bath with no slow drain. The brushed nickel finish is genuinely nicer than the photos suggest; it doesn't show every water spot the way polished chrome does. The spring tension is strong but not stiff, so even kids can operate it barefoot.

**Where it falls short**

Two honest complaints. First, the tip-toe plunger can stick if you have hard water. Buyers in mineral-heavy areas report needing to pop the cap off and wipe the shaft every few weeks. Second, the overflow plate screws are soft brass — overtorque them and you'll strip the heads. Hand-tighten only, and you're fine.

Also note: the kit doesn't include a hair catcher. If you've got long hair in the household, you'll want to add a basket strainer over the stopper, or you'll be cleaning clogs monthly.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY homeowners replacing a corroded or broken stopper** — this is a true 15-minute swap.
- **Landlords or property managers** — the brass body outlasts plastic alternatives, reducing callbacks.
- **Anyone with a standard US tub** — the dual-size fit covers almost everything built after 1980.

Skip it if you have a custom or European tub with non-standard drain sizing, or if you want a toe-touch (push-to-open) design instead of tip-toe. This is the classic foot-press style.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Will this fit my bathtub?**
Measure your drain opening first. If it's 1-1/2" or 1-1/4", you're good. The overflow plate matches standard 6" x 6" tubs. Custom tubs are the exception.

**Q: Does it come with plumber's putty?**
No. You shouldn't need it — the rubber gasket creates the seal. If your drain pipe mouth is rough or corroded, add a thin bead of silicone to be safe.

**Q: How do I clean the stopper mechanism?**
Pull the cap straight up, wipe the brass shaft with a vinegar-dampened cloth, and reinsert. Monthly maintenance prevents sticking.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a durable, easy-install stopper that won't rust out in two years, and you're comfortable with basic hand-tightening.

**Skip it if:** You need a hair catcher built-in, or you'd rather pay a plumber $150 to do what takes you 10 minutes.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Artiwell Tub Drain Kit is a genuine upgrade over builder-grade parts. It's not perfect (soft screws, hard-water sensitivity), but at this price, it's the best balance of durability and ease of installation you'll find. For most households, this is the last drain stopper you'll buy for a decade.
