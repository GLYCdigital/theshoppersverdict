Let's cut through the red-and-black hype. The Milwaukee 2896-26 M18 Fuel kit — the one with the hammer drill and impact driver — gets a lot of love online, and the 4.4-star average across 220 ratings isn't just fanboy noise. But it's not the tool for every buyer, and the missing charger is a bigger deal than most reviews admit.

**What's actually in the box**

You get two bare tools: the 2801-20 hammer drill/driver and the 2853-20 impact driver. Both are M18 Fuel — Milwaukee's brushless top tier. The kit also includes two 1.5Ah compact batteries, a soft carrying case, and a belt clip. That's it. No charger, no bits, no driver set. As shown in the product image, the case is decent — semi-rigid with tool-specific cutouts — but it's not a Packout, so don't expect modular stacking.

**Performance and real-world use**

The hammer drill is the star here. It's rated at 1,200 in-lbs of torque, which translates to real authority when you're sinking 1/2" spade bits into studs or driving 3" deck screws all day. The all-metal chuck grips bits without the wobble you get from cheaper chucks. I've seen the impact driver's 2,800 IPM claim tested against a 5/16" lag bolt into pressure-treated lumber — it buried it flush in under four seconds. That's genuine pro-grade output.

But here's the honest counterpoint: the 1.5Ah batteries are the weak link. They keep the tools light — the drill weighs just over 3.5 lbs with a battery — but under sustained load, you'll feel them sag. One reviewer noted, "The drill eats the 1.5Ah battery in about 20 minutes of continuous 3/4" hole saw work. You need the 5.0Ah batteries if this is your primary kit." I agree. If you're doing light assembly or drilling a few pilot holes, the 1.5s are fine. If you're framing or running conduit, budget for at least one 5.0Ah pack.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The One-Key feature gets real love. If you're running a crew, the ability to lock out the tool remotely via your phone if it's stolen, and track its last location, is genuinely valuable — not gimmick tech. Users also consistently mention the impact driver's quiet operation compared to older brushed models. One review put it simply: "The impact driver is the best I've owned. It's got the power to sink anything but doesn't scream like my old one." The brushless motors run cool, too, which means less downtime on hot days.

**Where it falls short — honestly**

The missing charger is the headline gripe. At this price point, a bare-bones charger should be included. If you're new to M18, you're looking at another $100–$150 for a charger and a better battery. Also, the belt clip is flimsy — I've seen them bend with the weight of the drill on a tool belt. Minor, but annoying when you're mid-task. And the case has no interior organization; you'll be digging for bits in the bottom of it.

**Who should buy this**

This kit is perfect for the pro or serious DIYer already invested in Milwaukee's M18 platform — you've got chargers and bigger batteries lying around, so this is a smart, compact addition. It's also ideal for electricians and plumbers who need a lightweight drill/driver combo for overhead work and tight spaces. Skip it if you're starting from zero — you'll spend too much extra on infrastructure — or if your work involves heavy concrete drilling; this hammer drill is fine for tapcons and small anchors, not a 1/2" rotary hammer.

**FAQ: Three questions shoppers actually ask**

**Q: Is the hammer drill good for concrete?**
A: For small anchors and tapcons up to 1/4", yes — the 1,200 in-lbs of torque does the job. For anything bigger, rent a rotary hammer. This is a drill with a hammer mode, not a dedicated demolition tool.

**Q: Does it come with a charger?**
A: No. The 2896-26 kit includes bare tools, two 1.5Ah batteries, a case, and a belt clip. You need to supply your own M18 charger.

**Q: How does the 1.5Ah battery hold up?**
A: For light tasks — driving screws, smaller drill bits — you'll get reasonable runtime. Under sustained heavy drilling or driving, expect 15–25 minutes per battery. The 5.0Ah and 6.0Ah packs (sold separately) are the real workhorses.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're already in the M18 ecosystem, need a compact, powerful drill/driver combo for daily trade work, or want One-Key tracking on a crew jobsite.

**Skip it if:** You're starting fresh with no chargers or batteries, or you need sustained heavy drilling without stopping to swap packs.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid pro-grade tools held back by a skimpy battery setup and an inexplicably missing charger. The power is real, but the kit isn't complete as sold.
