# DEWALT Door Lock Installation Kit Review: Precision Boring Without the Splintered Backs

If you've ever installed a door knob with a standard paddle bit, you know the drill: you bore through the face of the door, and when the bit punches through the backside, it chews up the wood, splinters the veneer, and leaves a mess you have to hide with the strike plate. The DEWALT Door Lock Installation Kit (model B000BOPE56) solves that problem with a C-clamp design that bites both sides of the door at once. It's a simple concept executed well, and it's the reason this kit holds a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings.

## What You Actually Get

The kit is refreshingly complete. In the molded plastic case you'll find:

- A **2-1/8"** bi-metal hole saw for the main bore (the standard for most residential deadbolts and handlesets)
- A **2-1/2"** hole saw for larger commercial or heavy-duty locksets
- A **1"** latch bore bit for the edge hole
- The C-clamp guide assembly with a built-in pilot drill

The clamp itself is the star. It's got a threaded rod running through the center, with a large knob on one end and a spring-loaded pilot bit on the other. You position the guide over your marked center point, tighten the knob until both faces bite into the wood, and drill. The saw cuts from one side while the clamp's backing plate supports the exit side, which prevents the wood from blowing out.

## Real-World Performance

I've used this kit on solid-core pine doors, hollow-core interior doors, and even a fiberglass entry door. The C-clamp design works exactly as advertised — the backside of every hole came out clean, with zero tear-out. That alone is worth the price of admission if you're doing finish carpentry where cosmetics matter.

The hole saws themselves are serviceable. They're bi-metal with carbide-tipped teeth, and they cut through pine and MDF quickly. On a solid-core oak door, you'll want to run a lower RPM and let the saw do the work — pushing hard just creates friction and heat. The 1" latch bit is a standard auger-style bit, and it handles the edge bore without issue.

One thing I noticed: the pilot drill bit that's built into the clamp is tough but not replaceable. On my third install, I hit a hidden nail, and the pilot bit dulled noticeably. Since it's an integral part of the assembly, that means the whole clamp becomes less effective. It still drills, but it wanders more on the initial bite.

## What Buyers Praise

The consensus on Amazon is clear: this kit works. One reviewer noted, *"I've installed a dozen locksets with this and it's still going strong. The C-clamp saves you from the dreaded splintering on the backside."* Another echoed that sentiment: *"Perfectly centered holes every time. Takes the guesswork out of a job that used to take me 20 minutes of fiddling."* The consistent theme is that the clamp makes the job more precise and dramatically reduces the risk of ruining a door.

## Where It Falls Short

The main complaint I see (and experienced) is the non-replaceable pilot bit. If you're a professional doing this daily, you'll wear it out within a year. For a homeowner doing a few doors, it's a non-issue.

Also, the clamp alignment takes a bit of finesse. If you don't center the guide perfectly on your mark, the hole saw will bind and walk off-center. It's not a flaw in the tool — it's operator error — but it's worth mentioning that it requires a steady hand and a sharp pencil mark.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners** replacing interior doors or installing new locksets — this is the tool that makes the job look professional.
- **Handymen and finish carpenters** who install multiple doors per month and want consistent, clean results.
- **Anyone who's ruined a door** with a paddle bit and wants to avoid that sinking feeling.

Skip it if you're a contractor who hammers through dozens of doors a week — you'll want a kit with replaceable pilot bits, like the Bosch or Milwaukee alternatives.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this kit work on metal or fiberglass doors?**
A: Yes, the hole saws will cut through fiberglass and thin-gauge steel, but go slow and use cutting oil. The C-clamp design works fine on these materials.

**Q: Does it come with a template or instructions?**
A: It includes a basic instruction sheet, but honestly, the process is intuitive: mark your center, clamp, drill. No fancy template needed.

**Q: Can I use my own drill with this kit?**
A: Yes, any standard 3/8" or 1/2" chuck drill works. The hole saws have a 1/2" shank, and the clamp assembly accepts the pilot bit from your drill.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want clean, splinter-free door hardware installs without buying a $200 jig. It's a solid, well-built kit that does one job exceptionally well.

**Skip it if** you're a high-volume pro who needs replaceable pilots and industrial-grade durability — you'll outgrow this quickly.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A reliable, time-saving tool that earns its place in any serious DIYer's kit. Just be gentle with that pilot bit.
