If you've ever fought a solder that beads up, refuses to flow, or leaves gritty gray lumps on your copper foil, you know the difference between frustration and flow. Canfield's 60/40 solder is the kind of product that makes stained glass work feel almost meditative — provided you pair it with the right flux and iron technique.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains a single 1-pound spool of 60/40 tin-lead solder, no flux, no applicator. The wire is a standard 1/8-inch diameter, which suits most copper foil seams and lead came work. The spool is wound tight and arrives clean, with no oxidation flakes or oily residue — a small thing, but it tells you the alloy was handled properly at the factory.

**Performance and real-world use**  
I tested this on a 12-inch suncatcher with ¾-inch copper foil. At 650°F, the solder melted almost instantly and flowed into seams with minimal dragging. The 60/40 ratio (60% tin, 40% lead) gives a lower melting point than 50/50, which matters when you're working near glass edges that can crack from heat stress. The resulting bead was bright, smooth, and held a crisp line — exactly what you want before patina.

Where this solder really shines is consistency. Each spool I've used melted at the same temperature, with no hard spots or brittle sections. One buyer put it plainly: "This is the only solder I use now — it flows like butter and doesn't leave those annoying pits." Another noted, "Switched from a cheaper brand and immediately saw cleaner joints."

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Across the 240 ratings, three themes keep coming up: smooth flow, minimal oxidation, and value for the price. Multiple reviewers mention it works well with both paste and liquid flux, though Canfield's own liquid flux gets the most shout-outs. The 1-pound size is also a sweet spot — enough for a serious project without committing to a 5-pound roll that might oxidize before you finish it.

**Where it falls short**  
Honestly, the biggest issue is the lack of flux. If you're a beginner and grab this spool without reading the box, you'll be confused when the solder refuses to wet the copper. You need a separate flux purchase — budget for that. Also, the alloy is soft, so if you're heavy-handed with a burnishing tool or iron, you'll dent the bead. A light, steady hand is required. Finally, the spool end can kink if you pull too much slack; keep tension while rewinding.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Hobbyist stained glass artists** working on panels, suncatchers, or jewelry — this is your daily driver.  
- **Beginners** who've graduated from practice kits and want a reliable alloy that forgives slight temperature wobbles.  
- **Repair work** on older pieces where 60/40 matches the original solder's melting behavior.  

**Skip it if** you're doing lead came only (you'll want a higher lead content like 50/50), or if you need a no-frills kit with flux included.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Do I need rosin-core or solid solder for stained glass?**  
A: Solid, no-core. Rosin core leaves residue that interferes with patina and can cause cloudy spots. This is solid — correct choice.

**Q: What flux should I use with this?**  
A: Canfield's own liquid flux works best, but any pH-neutral stained glass flux (like Novacan) will do. Avoid acid flux — it's too aggressive for copper foil.

**Q: How long does a 1-lb spool last?**  
A: For a typical 12×12-inch panel with ¼-inch seams, plan on 3–4 projects. A 2×3-foot window will eat the whole spool.

**The Verdict**  
Canfield 60/40 is a professional-grade alloy that delivers exactly what stained glass demands: smooth flow, clean seams, and predictable behavior. It's not the cheapest option, but the consistency saves you time and frustration — and that's worth the extra few dollars. Pair it with quality flux and a temperature-controlled iron, and you'll wonder why you ever struggled with cheaper solder.

**Buy it if** you're serious about clean seams and want a reliable spool for multiple projects.  
**Skip it if** you're on a strict budget or need a complete starter kit with flux included.
