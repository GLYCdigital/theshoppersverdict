If you've been burned by cheap solar lights that die after one rainy season, the Greluna Solar Post Lights deserve a closer look. With a 4.4-star average across 220 ratings, these aren't flash-in-the-pan gimmicks — they're a legitimately solid option for illuminating deck posts, fence caps, or mailbox posts without running wiring. Here's what you need to know before handing over your money.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes two complete post light units — each with the solar panel housing, LED lamp head, and the mounting bracket that fits standard 4x4 and 4x6 posts. The aluminum housing is a step above the plastic junk that dominates this category; it feels substantial in hand, and the tempered glass panel is less likely to yellow or crack than polycarbonate alternatives. You also get the mounting screws and a basic instruction sheet. Note: there's no remote control — operation is fully automatic or via the manual power button on the unit.

## Real-World Performance

The headline feature is the dusk-to-dawn sensor. Once you flip the switch to auto mode, the light takes over completely — no timers, no phone app, no daily button-pressing. Owners consistently confirm the sensor works as advertised: "I installed these on my porch posts in March and haven't touched them since. They come on at sunset and shut off at sunrise, every single day." That's the experience most buyers report.

The light output comes in two modes. High mode gives you a genuinely useful pool of light — enough to navigate steps or illuminate a house number clearly. Low mode extends runtime significantly, making it a better choice for lights mounted in shaded areas where solar gain is limited. In full sun, the monocrystalline panel charges the 1500mAh battery adequately for most of the year. The catch: during deep winter with short days and heavy overcast, high mode may only deliver 5–7 hours. If your winters are brutal and you need light until 5 AM, you'll be running on empty some mornings.

## Build Quality and Durability

This is where the Greluna earns its keep. The IP65 waterproof rating isn't marketing fluff — the gasketed seams and sealed battery compartment hold up to real weather. Multiple owners report surviving snow loads, coastal salt air, and direct rain exposure for a year or more without water intrusion or corrosion. One reviewer noted, "I've had cheap solar lights fail in months. These have been through two winters and still look and work like new." The aluminum housing also dissipates heat well, which extends LED lifespan.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The hands-off operation is the most-cited positive. People love installing these and forgetting them. The build quality is the second most common praise point — "These feel like a $50 light, not a bargain bin special" captures the sentiment. Brightness in high mode also gets consistent approval for a solar unit.

## Where It Falls Short

The mounting hardware is genuinely basic. The included screws are fine for wood posts, but if you're attaching to masonry, stucco, or metal, you'll need to buy appropriate anchors yourself — a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker. The battery capacity is the bigger limitation. On high mode in winter, you may get 6 hours or less in northern latitudes. Also, the solar panel is built into the light head — so if your post is under a porch overhang or dense tree cover, charging will suffer. There's no remote panel option here.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Fence and deck owners** with exposed posts in full or partial sun who want reliable, no-maintenance illumination.
- **House number or address markers** — high mode is bright enough to make your address visible from the street.
- **Anyone tired of cheap solar lights** that die in a season; these are built to last multiple years.
- **Not for you** if your posts are heavily shaded, you need all-night light in northern winters, or you expect floodlight-level brightness.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these work in freezing temperatures?**
Yes. The battery chemistry handles sub-freezing operation, though runtime drops in extreme cold. Owners in northern states report year-round function, just shorter duration in deep winter.

**Q: How long do they hold a charge in full sun?**
In direct summer sun, expect 8–10 hours on low mode and 6–8 hours on high. Partial shade cuts that roughly in half.

**Q: Can I replace the battery when it eventually wears out?**
Yes — the battery compartment is accessible with a screwdriver, and replacement 18650 batteries are inexpensive. This extends the product's life well beyond cheaper sealed units.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want durable, set-and-forget solar lighting for exposed posts and have at least 5–6 hours of direct sun. The build quality justifies the price point.

**Skip it if:** Your mounting location is shaded, you need guaranteed all-night operation through harsh winters, or you require brighter output for security purposes.
