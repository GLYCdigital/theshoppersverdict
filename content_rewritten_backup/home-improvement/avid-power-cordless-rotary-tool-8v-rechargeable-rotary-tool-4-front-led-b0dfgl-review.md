## Skip the Cord? Here's What You're Actually Getting

The AVID POWER 8V cordless rotary tool is the kind of purchase you make when you're tired of fighting with a 15-foot extension cord and a wall outlet for a five-minute sanding job. It's not a Dremel-killer — but at roughly half the price of a comparable corded model, it doesn't need to be. What it needs to do is handle light-to-medium tasks without drama, and for the most part, it does.

## What's in the Box

Open the package and you get the tool itself, a 36-piece accessory kit (grinding stones, sanding bands, cut-off wheels, polishing bits, and a couple of drill bits), a flex shaft attachment, a chuck key, and a USB-C charging cable. Notably absent: a wall adapter. That's fine if you've got a phone charger lying around, but if you don't, factor in a few extra dollars.

The tool weighs 1.2 pounds and measures about 9 inches long. It's balanced well enough for one-handed operation, and the rubberized grip actually helps when your hands get sweaty during longer sessions.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 8V motor spins between 8,000 and 30,000 RPM, controlled by a variable-speed dial on the side. That's a genuinely useful range — I've used it to polish a scratched watch crystal at low speed and cut through a copper pipe at full tilt. The LED light around the chuck is more than a gimmick; it illuminates the work area surprisingly well in dark corners.

Battery life is the tradeoff. AVID POWER claims 60 minutes of runtime, but real-world testing puts continuous heavy use at 20–30 minutes. For quick jobs — engraving a knife blade, sanding a small furniture repair, deburring a cut pipe — that's workable. For a full day of projects, you'll be tethered to the charging cable.

The chuck is a standard 1/8-inch collet, which covers most rotary tool bits. The catch: if you own Dremel-brand accessories with 3/32-inch shanks, you'll need an adapter. The included flex shaft is a nice bonus for detail work, though it adds bulk and reduces the already-limited torque.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 4.3-star average across 215 ratings reflects genuine satisfaction with a few consistent themes. One reviewer notes, *"I bought this for occasional hobby work and it's perfect. The variable speed is smooth and the battery lasts longer than I expected for small projects."* Another echoes the value proposition: *"Does everything my friend's Dremel does for half the price. The flex shaft is a game-changer for detail carving."*

The complaints cluster around two issues: runtime and chuck compatibility. As one buyer puts it, *"Great little tool, but it dies fast if you're cutting for more than 10 minutes straight."* Another warns, *"Make sure you have 1/8-inch bits — most of my Dremel stuff wouldn't fit without an adapter I didn't have."*

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the battery and chuck limitations, the motor lacks the torque of a corded unit. If you're trying to cut through thick metal or grind down substantial material, this tool will stall and complain. It's built for precision and light work, not demolition.

The USB-C charging is genuinely convenient, but the lack of an included adapter is an annoying nickel-and-dime move. And while the accessory kit is generous, the quality is what you'd expect from bundled bits — the sanding bands wear out quickly, and the cut-off wheels are thin and fragile.

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right tool for hobbyists, model builders, jewelry makers, and anyone doing light home repairs. It's also a solid first rotary tool for beginners who don't want to invest $80+ in a Dremel before knowing if they'll use it. Skip it if you're a professional or plan to do heavy cutting, grinding, or extended production work — you'll want a corded model with more torque.

## FAQ

**How long does the battery actually last?**
Expect 20–30 minutes of continuous use at moderate speeds. At the highest RPM setting, it drops to around 15 minutes. Charging via USB-C takes roughly 2 hours.

**Will my Dremel accessories fit?**
Only if they have 1/8-inch shanks. Dremel's standard 3/32-inch bits won't fit without a collet adapter, which is sold separately. Check your bit sizes before buying.

**Can I use this while it's charging?**
No. The tool is designed to operate only on battery power. You'll need to wait for a charge cycle to complete.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a lightweight, cordless rotary tool for hobby work, light sanding, polishing, or occasional cutting — and you want to avoid spending Dremel money. The variable speed control, LED light, and included flex shaft make it a genuinely useful addition to any toolbox.

**Skip it if** you're a professional, plan to do heavy-duty cutting or grinding, or need all-day battery life. This is a tool for the workbench, not the job site.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A capable budget performer with honest limitations. For the price, it's tough to beat.
