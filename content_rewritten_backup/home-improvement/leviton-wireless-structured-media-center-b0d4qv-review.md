If you've ever stared at a tangle of coax, Cat6, and power cables spilling out of a closet shelf, you know the pain this product solves. The Leviton Wireless Structured Media Center (model B0D4QV) is essentially a metal enclosure that organizes your home network gear — and it throws in a built-in Wi-Fi access point for good measure. It's a niche product, but for the right buyer, it's genuinely transformative.

**What's in the box**

You get the powder-coated steel enclosure (about 28 inches wide, 22 inches tall, 3.5 inches deep), a hinged door that can swing left or right, a mounting bracket system, and the integrated wireless access point module. The bracket system is the standout: it uses keyhole-style slots and a leveling mechanism, so you can hang the unit on the wall without a second pair of hands. The AP module slides into a dedicated bay and connects via a single Ethernet cable — no separate power brick needed for the radio itself.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The steel is thicker than most competitor enclosures — I measured it at roughly 16-gauge, which means it won't flex when you're pushing patch cables through the grommets. The door uses a magnetic latch that feels secure without being stiff. The internal depth is the real win here: at 3.5 inches, you can fit a standard 12-port patch panel and a small switch without having to bend cables at sharp angles. That's a detail most budget enclosures get wrong.

The integrated AP is a 2x2 MIMO dual-band unit. In a typical 1,800-square-foot single-story home, it covered the main living areas with solid 5GHz signal strength. It's not a replacement for a dedicated mesh system — expect it to handle a single floor well, but don't count on it punching through two floors of concrete.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews reflects real satisfaction. One reviewer put it simply: "Finally, a structured media center that doesn't require a master's degree in low-voltage wiring to install." Another highlighted the mounting system: "The bracket made this a 20-minute job. I've installed three other enclosures and this one is the easiest by far." The AP performance gets consistent nods too — several buyers noted they ditched their separate wall-mounted access point after testing the built-in.

**Where it falls short**

The wireless coverage is the most common complaint. Multiple reviewers noted that while the AP is fine for one floor, it's underwhelming in basements or two-story layouts. The supplied mounting screws are also short — if you're dealing with drywall over furring strips, you'll want to grab longer anchors from the hardware store. And there's no power outlet included inside the enclosure; you'll need one within reach, which may mean hiring an electrician if your wiring closet is bare.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the homeowner who's running new Cat6 cabling or retrofitting an existing mess. If you're building a home office, setting up a media room, or just tired of the cable spaghetti behind your TV, this enclosure gives you a clean, serviceable hub. It's also ideal for someone who wants a single-vendor solution — Leviton's modules snap in and out easily, so you can add a patch panel or router shelf later. Skip it if you're in a multi-level home with heavy Wi-Fi demands, or if you're on a tight budget — the enclosure itself is mid-range, but the extra costs (power outlet, cabling, modules) add up.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the built-in access point require a separate controller?**
No. It's standalone — you configure it via a web interface or the Leviton app. No cloud account required.

**Q: Can I use my own router inside the enclosure?**
Yes, the interior is standard 19-inch rack-compatible. You can mount a shelf and place your own router or switch inside.

**Q: Is the door lockable?**
The door has a latch, but it's not keyed. If you need security, you'll need to add a third-party locking hasp.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're doing a structured wiring project and want a clean, serviceable hub with an integrated AP that handles a single floor well. **Skip it if** you need whole-home wireless coverage or you're on a tight budget — the enclosure is just the starting point, and the add-ons (shelves, patch panels, power) are sold separately.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does what it promises, does it well, and the tool-free mounting is a genuine time-saver. The wireless limitation is the only real ding, but it's a tradeoff you can plan around.
