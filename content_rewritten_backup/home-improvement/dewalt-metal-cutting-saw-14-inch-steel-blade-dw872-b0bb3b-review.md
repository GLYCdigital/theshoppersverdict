If you cut steel for a living, you already know the drill: abrasive chop saws throw sparks like a cheap fireworks show, burn through wheels, and leave nasty burrs. The DEWALT DW872 takes a different route—it spins a carbide-tipped blade instead of a grinding wheel. It's a smarter concept, but is it a better tool for *your* shop? After digging through 195 ratings and the real gripes behind that 3.9-star average, here's the straight story.

## What's in the Box

You get the saw itself (model DW872), a 14-inch carbide-tipped steel-cutting blade, a 14-inch material clamp, and a hex wrench for adjustments. There's no stand included—this is a benchtop unit. At roughly 35 pounds, it's portable enough to haul to a job site, but you'll want a sturdy surface to bolt it to. The base has pre-drilled holes, so mounting is expected, not optional.

## Performance and Build Quality

This saw is a cold-cutting machine. Unlike abrasive saws that generate massive heat and friction, the DW872's carbide blade spins at a slower 1,450 RPM, which means the metal stays cool to the touch. That's a genuine advantage: no bluing, no heat-hardened edges, no warping. The cut quality is visibly smoother—closer to a miter saw finish than a chop saw gash.

The 5-horsepower motor handles solid steel up to 5/8-inch thick and 7-1/2-inch-wide beams. The miter capacity is a real workhorse feature: 0 to 45 degrees left and right, with positive stops at 90, 45, and 22.5 degrees. The integrated vise clamps material firmly, and the cast aluminum base keeps vibration manageable.

But here's the catch that shows up in buyer feedback repeatedly: **this saw demands patience and a delicate touch.** The blade is not designed for aggressive plunging. You need to let the saw do the work, using a steady, light feed rate. Force it, and you'll stall the motor or chip the carbide teeth. One reviewer noted, *"It cuts beautifully when you take your time, but if you try to muscle it like an abrasive saw, you'll regret it."* Another warned, *"The blade that comes with it is decent, but replacements run $60–$100, and they wear out faster than you'd hope on thicker stock."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be blunt about the downsides, because they're dealbreakers for some.

**Blade cost and fragility.** The factory blade is serviceable, but it's not meant for heavy, continuous production. You'll be shopping for a higher-end carbide blade (think Diablo or Freud) sooner than you'd like if you cut daily. That's a significant operating cost.

**No included stand.** At this price point, many competitors include a folding stand. DEWALT expects you to buy one separately, which adds $150+ to your total investment.

**The dust port is a joke.** There's a small port, but this saw throws fine metal shavings everywhere. You'll want a powerful shop vac or a dedicated dust collection setup. Several buyers mentioned the debris gets into the motor vents over time.

**The clamp can be finicky.** For thin or oddly-shaped stock, the standard vise doesn't grip as securely as it should. You may need to fabricate a custom hold-down for certain profiles.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a fabricator, metalworker, or serious DIYer who needs clean, burr-free, square cuts on a regular basis—especially if you work with stainless or aluminum, where abrasive wheels cause contamination. This is also a solid choice if you're tired of replacing abrasive discs every 20 cuts.

**Skip it if:** You only cut metal occasionally and just need something rough and ready. An abrasive chop saw at half the price will handle sporadic framing and angle iron work without the blade investment. Also skip it if you're a "push hard and let 'er rip" operator—this tool will punish you for that.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this saw for aluminum?**
Yes, but swap to a blade designed for non-ferrous metal. The included steel blade will load up and gum quickly on aluminum.

**Q: How long does the included blade last?**
Expect roughly 50–150 cuts on 1/4-inch steel, depending on feed rate and material. On thicker stock, it'll wear noticeably faster.

**Q: Does it replace a cold saw or band saw?**
It's a middle ground. It's cheaper than a true cold saw and faster than a band saw, but it won't match the blade life of either on heavy production work.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5**

The DEWALT DW872 is a smart, well-engineered tool with a serious identity problem. It delivers the clean, cool cuts it promises—when you respect its technique and budget for quality blades. But the hidden costs (stand, premium blades, dust management) and its intolerance for aggressive use make it a specialized tool, not a universal solution.

**Buy it if** you value cut quality and precision over speed, and you're prepared to invest in good blades. **Skip it if** you want a brute-force workhorse or you're on a tight budget. It's a great saw for the right owner—just make sure that's you.
