Let's be blunt: under-cabinet lighting is one of those upgrades that sounds simple until you're contorting under your sink trying to fish a hardwired transformer through a 2-inch gap. The maylit plug-in bars sidestep that entirely. No electrician, no junction boxes, no dimmer switch wiring — just stick, plug in, and flip the switch. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's clearly working for most buyers. But is it the right solution for *your* kitchen?

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes the LED bar itself, a 6-foot power cord, an AC adapter, adhesive mounting tape, and metal mounting brackets with screws. The unit is a slim aluminum extrusion — roughly 12 inches long and about 0.4 inches thick. It's not the thinnest on the market (some competitors hit 0.3 inches), but it's svelte enough to disappear under most cabinet overhangs.

The key differentiator: a physical switch on the light body itself. Many competing plug-in bars rely on a remote control or inline foot pedal. Those work — until the remote batteries die or the pedal slides under the stove. The on-unit switch is tactile and reliable. One buyer noted, "I appreciate that I don't need another remote cluttering my counter. The switch is right there."

**Performance and Build Quality**

Brightness is genuinely solid. The LEDs output a clean, even spread without the "spotlight effect" you get from puck lights. The diffuser lens does its job — no harsh dots or visible LED clusters when you're standing at the counter. Color rendering is decent for the price; whites look natural, and food prep areas are well-lit.

The three color temperatures are a real strength. A slide switch on the back lets you choose between warm (3000K), neutral (4000K), and daylight (6000K). Set it before mounting, because once it's stuck up there, you're not flipping it without pulling the unit down. Most buyers land on 4000K for kitchens — bright enough to work but not clinical.

Heat management is surprisingly good. After two hours of continuous use, the aluminum housing was warm but not hot to the touch. That's a genuine safety consideration for wood cabinets, and maylit handles it well.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

- **Ease of installation:** The adhesive tape holds well on smooth surfaces, and the brackets give you a screw-in option for textured or painted cabinets. Multiple reviewers mention a 10-minute install.
- **Low profile:** "These are so thin I forget they're there until I flip the switch," one verified buyer wrote. Another noted they fit under a cabinet with only a 0.5-inch overhang.
- **Reliability:** No flickering, no buzzing, no dead units out of the box — a common complaint with budget lighting brands.

**Where It Falls Short**

The cord is the biggest annoyance. Six feet sounds adequate, but it exits the side of the unit, not the back. If your outlet is on the wall behind the cabinet, you'll need to route the cord around the cabinet edge or use a power strip. Buyers with outlets directly above the cabinet (inside the upper cabinet) had the easiest time. Those with island cabinets or distant outlets may need an extension cord.

The adhesive is workable but not permanent. One reviewer reported a unit sagging after four months in a humid environment near the stove. Use the screws if you can — the brackets are designed for it, and it takes two extra minutes.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Renters** who can't hardwire and want to take the lights with them
- **DIY homeowners** who want task lighting without an electrician
- **Anyone with accessible outlets** above or beside their upper cabinets

**Skip them if** you have a long cabinet run with no nearby outlet, or if you need a fully integrated, hardwired solution with a wall dimmer.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these be dimmed?**
A: No. They're on/off only via the physical switch. No dimmer compatibility. If you need dimming, look at a hardwired system or a smart plug (though smart plugs won't dim these either — they're non-dimmable LEDs).

**Q: How many can I daisy-chain together?**
A: You can't chain these. Each unit needs its own outlet. Plan your layout around outlet proximity.

**Q: Do they work with a smart home system?**
A: Not directly. You could plug them into a smart plug for on/off voice control, but color temperature and brightness are set manually on the unit.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a no-headache, plug-in task light with solid brightness and a reliable physical switch, and you have an outlet within cord reach. **Skip it if** you need dimming, daisy-chaining, or a cordless battery option.

This is a genuinely good value for the price. It won't replace a professional hardwired system, but for most renters and DIYers, it's the smart, practical choice. **4.2/5 stars.**
