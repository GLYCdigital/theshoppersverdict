# Intermatic DT620 Review: The Heavy-Duty Timer That Actually Handles Real Loads

Most plug-in timers are toys. They're rated for 10 amps, feel like they'd melt if you plugged a space heater into them, and offer "7-day programming" that really means "same schedule every day." The Intermatic DT620 is not that. It's a legitimate piece of electrical equipment from a company that's been making commercial-grade timers since 1945 — and it shows.

## What You Actually Get

The DT620 is a chunky white plastic block with two grounded outlets on the face, a backlit LCD screen, and four rubberized buttons. It measures about 3.5 x 2.5 x 1.5 inches — noticeably larger than the typical plug-in timer. That's the price of the 15-amp, 1800-watt rating printed on the case. You get one grounded outlet that's switched by the timer and one that's always-on, which is a thoughtful touch for lamps or devices you don't want cut off.

The unit comes with a CR2032 battery pre-installed for program retention. No tools, no wiring, no wall plate needed — it's genuinely plug-and-play.

## Performance: Where It Excels and Where It Doesn't

The headline feature is the 15-amp capacity. Most competitors top out at 10 or 12 amps. That means the DT620 can handle a 1500-watt space heater, a window AC unit, or a bank of shop lights without thermal shutdown. This is the timer electricians and serious hobbyists buy for exactly that reason.

Programming is where the DT620 splits opinions. The interface is straightforward but dated: you set the current time, then cycle through days and set on/off times for each. It supports up to 8 on/off events per day, with independent schedules for all 7 days. That's genuinely useful if you run different schedules on weekends. But the four-button layout and small LCD mean you'll spend 10-15 minutes on initial setup, and you'll likely consult the manual for the first few weeks.

The battery backup works as advertised. Users report programs surviving power outages without issue — one reviewer noted, "Lost power for three days during a storm and the timer kept its schedule perfectly." That's a real advantage over mechanical timers that need resetting after every outage.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The heavy-duty rating is the most-cited reason for purchase. As one verified buyer put it: "Finally a timer that doesn't feel like it's going to melt when I run my heater through it." Another praised the independent day programming: "I work nights, so having different on/off times for Tuesdays and Wednesdays is essential. This is the only timer I found that does it."

The always-on second outlet also gets consistent mentions — users like being able to keep a router or phone charger live while the timer controls the main load.

## Where It Falls Short

The interface is the biggest complaint, and it's legitimate. The buttons are stiff, the LCD isn't backlit (despite what some listings claim), and navigating the menu system feels like using a calculator from 2005. Expect a learning curve.

The physical size is also a real issue. The DT620 is wide enough that it will block the second outlet on most duplex receptacles. If you need both outlets, you'll want a short extension cord or power strip.

There's also no random mode for security lighting, no sunrise/sunset calculation, and no Wi-Fi. For roughly the same money, a smart plug offers app control and voice commands — but most smart plugs top out at 10 amps and won't handle a heater.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The space heater user**: If you run a 1500-watt heater on a schedule, this is one of the few timers that's safely rated for it.
- **The aquarium or reptile keeper**: Constant, reliable on/off cycles for heat lamps and pumps — the battery backup is a lifesaver.
- **The "I want it to just work" buyer**: If you don't want to mess with Wi-Fi, apps, or cloud dependencies, this is rock-solid.
- **Skip it if**: You want app control, need a compact footprint, or plan to program complex schedules frequently.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the DT620 control a space heater safely?**
Yes — it's rated for 15 amps (1800 watts), which covers essentially all residential space heaters. Do not exceed that rating.

**Q: Does the battery backup preserve my settings during a power outage?**
Yes. The CR2032 battery maintains the program indefinitely. The clock and schedule survive outages; the timer just won't switch anything while it's unpowered.

**Q: How many on/off events can I set per day?**
Up to 8 per day, with independent schedules for each of the 7 days. That's 56 possible events total.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a timer that handles real electrical loads, requires independent day-by-day scheduling, and won't lose its program when the power blips. This is the workhorse timer for people who've melted cheaper units.

**Skip it if** you want app control, need a slim profile, or find the dated interface frustrating. For basic daily schedules, a simple mechanical timer or smart plug may serve you better.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The DT620 isn't flashy, but it's built for a job most timers can't handle. The clunky interface and bulky footprint are real tradeoffs, but for heavy-duty reliability, it's hard to beat at this price.
