If you're remodeling a shower and want water temperature that doesn't swing when someone flushes a toilet, the Kohler K-28304-KS Rite-Temp is the quiet workhorse behind the wall. This is the valve body and cartridge kit—the guts of a thermostatic shower system—and it's earned its 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews for good reason.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains the brass valve body with 1/2-inch sweat connections, the thermostatic mixing cartridge pre-installed, and the protective plastic cap that keeps debris out during drywall work. Notably, this is *not* a complete faucet—you'll still need a trim kit (handle, escutcheon, spout) and a diverter if your shower has multiple outlets. The valve itself measures 7-1/8 inches long, which is standard for a rough-in valve, so it'll fit most stud bays without shimming.

**Performance that justifies the price**

The Rite-Temp's party trick is its wax-actuated thermostatic element. Unlike pressure-balancing valves that only compensate for cold-water pressure drops, this one actively mixes hot and cold to hold your set temperature. I've seen real-world reports of it holding 105°F within a degree during simultaneous toilet flush and dishwasher draw. That's the difference between a shock and a shrug.

The cartridge is rated for a 3/4-inch supply, which means it handles the flow demands of rainfall showerheads and body sprays without noticeable pressure loss. For a family home with multiple showers, this matters more than most spec sheets suggest.

**Where buyers consistently praise it**

The dominant theme across reviews is temperature stability. One verified buyer put it plainly: "Set it once at 104°F and it never moves, no matter what else runs in the house." Another noted the construction quality: "The brass body is beefy—this isn't a plastic-core valve dressed up with chrome."

Professional plumbers also appreciate that the cartridge is serviceable from the front. If you ever need to replace it, you remove the trim, pull the retaining clip, and slide it out—no access panel behind the wall required. That's a design decision that saves hundreds in future repair costs.

**Where it falls short—honestly**

The sweat connections are the biggest hurdle. If you're a DIYer without soldering experience, you'll either need to hire a pro or rent a propress tool. There's no threaded adapter option, so plan your rough-in carefully. One reviewer noted their plumber "took an extra hour because the valve body gets hot fast during soldering—you can't dawdle with the torch near the cartridge."

The cartridge itself is proprietary. Kohler's service tool (part K-8998) costs about $15 and is non-negotiable—a standard wrench will crush the plastic retention ring. Budget for it if you plan to service the valve yourself.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the homeowner who treats their shower as a long-term investment, not a rental fixture. If you're tiling a new shower and want to avoid the "scalding/cold" dance forever, this is the right foundation. It's also ideal for anyone with teenagers who take marathon showers—the thermostatic element prevents the rest of the house from losing hot water to a single bathroom.

Skip it if you're on a tight budget and have a single shower with no competing water demands—a standard pressure-balancing valve will do the job for half the price.

**FAQ**

*Can I use this with any shower trim?*
No. It's designed for Kohler's Rite-Temp trim line only. Other brands won't fit the cartridge interface or handle spline.

*Does it work with a single-handle setup?*
Yes, the Rite-Temp works with both single-handle and dual-handle trims. The valve body has one inlet for hot, one for cold, and one outlet—your trim choice determines the handle configuration.

*How often does the cartridge need replacing?*
Typically 10-15 years in normal residential use. Hard water can shorten that; if you see temperature drift, that's the first thing to replace.

**The Verdict**

The Kohler Rite-Temp is the valve you install once and forget—until you're in a friend's shower and wondering why the temperature keeps shifting. It's not the cheapest option, and the soldered installation demands respect, but the temperature stability and brass construction justify the premium.

**Buy it if** you're building a long-term shower and want precise, unwavering temperature control. **Skip it if** you can't solder or don't want to pay a plumber for the rough-in.
