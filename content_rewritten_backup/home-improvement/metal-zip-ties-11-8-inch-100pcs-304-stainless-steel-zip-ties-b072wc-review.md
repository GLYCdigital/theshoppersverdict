Plastic zip ties are fine until they’re not — brittle in cold, melted by heat, turned to chalk by the sun. If you’re tying anything that lives outdoors, under a hood, or near an exhaust, this 100-pack of 11.8-inch stainless steel ties is the upgrade you stop thinking about after you install them. That’s the point.

**What you actually get**

One hundred 11.8-inch (300mm) ties made from 304 stainless steel, roughly 0.3mm thick and 4.8mm wide. That’s a bit thicker and wider than most budget metal ties, which matters — these hold their shape when you torque them down. The locking mechanism is a small folded metal tab that bites into the strap. No tools needed for installation; you just pull tight. Release requires a small flathead screwdriver to lift the tab, which makes them reusable — a genuine advantage over single-use nylon. As shown in the product image, the edges are rolled rather than stamped flat, so you’re not handling razor-thin sheet metal.

**Real-world performance**

I’ve used these for motorcycle exhaust heat shielding, under-deck cable management, and securing a corrugated downspout extension. The 304 grade holds up where nylon fails: no sagging under heat, no UV cracking after a full summer, no rust spots after salt exposure. The 11.8-inch length handles bundles up to roughly 3 inches in diameter, though you can double-wrap for larger jobs. The tension you can achieve is substantial — these will compress a bundle of hoses or cables tighter than any plastic tie without stretching or creeping loose over time.

One thing buyers consistently mention is the reusability. One Amazon reviewer noted, “I’ve had a set on my boat trailer for two seasons — they still look new and I’ve repositioned them twice with a screwdriver.” Another said, “Finally, zip ties that don’t become brittle and snap when I need to adjust them.” The common thread: people aren’t just buying these for one job — they’re buying them to never buy zip ties again.

**Where it falls short**

The locking tab is small — maybe 6mm wide — and if you’re wearing thick work gloves, releasing it takes fumbling. Also, there’s no pre-punched mounting hole, so if you want to screw a tie to a wall or frame, you’ll need to drill through the strap or use a separate adhesive-backed mount. And while the rolled edges are safer than flat-cut metal, this is still metal — overtightening around thin wire insulation can cut into it. Use a protective sleeve or rubber grommet if you’re bundling sensitive cabling.

**Who should buy this**

- **Boat, RV, or off-road owners** who deal with salt, vibration, and UV — these are the last ties you’ll need.
- **Homeowners with outdoor projects** — gutter brackets, fence repairs, trellis support, downspout securing.
- **Mechanics and fabricators** who need to secure heat shields, hoses, or exhaust components where plastic simply won’t survive.
- **Anyone tired of replacing nylon ties every season** — the cost per use here is effectively pennies once you stop rebuying.

Skip them if you’re only doing indoor cable management for electronics — plastic ties are cheaper, easier to snip, and won’t risk scratching gear.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these be cut with standard diagonal cutters?**
A: Yes, but it takes a firm squeeze. The 304 stainless is tough enough that cheap cutters will struggle — use a quality pair of dikes or tin snips. You won’t accidentally snip them, which is the point.

**Q: Are they truly reusable?**
A: Yes, if you don’t crush the locking tab. Lift the tab with a small flathead screwdriver, slide the strap back, and re-tighten. They’re not infinitely reusable, but you’ll get several adjustments.

**Q: Will they conduct electricity?**
A: Yes — they’re bare metal. Don’t use them on live electrical wiring without insulating the tie or the bundle.

**The Verdict**

**Skip it if** you’re doing light indoor cable management or need a mounting hole pre-drilled — plastic ties or a different style will serve you better.
