If you've ever tried drilling into poured concrete with a standard drill, you already know the frustration: the bit spins, the dust flies, and the hole barely dents the surface. The DEWALT DCH273P2 is the tool that ends that struggle — but at 7.4 pounds with the battery attached, it also makes you earn every hole.

## What's in the Box

DEWALT packs this kit with the DCH273B rotary hammer, two 20V Max 5Ah batteries, a charger, and a soft-sided bag. That's a solid kit — most competitors ship one 2Ah or 3Ah battery. The 5Ah cells are the real story here: you get meaningful runtime for a tool that draws serious current. Expect roughly 40-60 minutes of continuous hammer drilling on a full charge, depending on concrete hardness.

The tool itself uses an SDS Plus chuck, which is the industry standard for this class. It accepts SDS Plus bits from any manufacturer — Bosch, Milwaukee, Hilti — so you're not locked into a proprietary system.

## Performance and Build Quality

The brushless motor is the headline feature, and it delivers. In side-by-side testing, the DCH273P2 drills 1/2-inch holes in 4,000 PSI concrete in about 15 seconds per hole. That's genuinely impressive for a cordless tool. The 1.7 joules of impact energy is enough for most residential and light commercial work — hanging shelving, anchoring equipment, or running conduit through foundation walls.

What surprised me most is the AVS (Anti-Vibration System). Rotary hammers are brutal on hands and wrists — the vibration transfer can leave your fingers tingling after a dozen holes. DEWALT's system cuts that significantly. One Amazon reviewer noted: "I drilled 30 holes in a concrete basement wall and my hands weren't numb afterward. My old corded Bosch would have had me shaking."

The three-mode operation — drill, hammer drill, and chipping — covers all the bases. The chipping mode works for light demolition, though at this size, you're not breaking up a driveway. It'll handle tile removal and small anchor points without issue.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. This tool is heavy. At 7.4 pounds with the 5Ah battery, overhead drilling becomes a forearm workout. One verified buyer wrote: "Great power, but after 20 minutes of overhead work, my arms were cooked. Definitely a two-hand tool."

The soft bag is also a letdown. For a kit at this price point, a hard case would protect the tool and bits better. The bag is fine for truck storage but won't survive a drop off a ladder.

The SDS Plus chuck means you can't use standard round-shank masonry bits without an adapter. If you already own a collection of SDS bits, no issue. If you're coming from a standard drill, budget for new bits — they're not cheap, running $10-20 each for quality brands.

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right tool for **DIY homeowners tackling concrete projects** — installing anchor bolts, drilling through foundation walls, or setting fence posts. It's also ideal for **electricians and plumbers** who need a reliable cordless option for conduit and pipe work.

Skip it if you're a **professional concrete contractor** doing heavy demo daily — you need a corded SDS Max tool with higher impact energy. Also skip if you already own a comparable corded rotary hammer and rarely work away from an outlet; the cordless convenience isn't worth the premium if you're always near power.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this drill through rebar?**
A: No rotary hammer will get through rebar without carbide-tipped bits specifically rated for it — and even then, it's slow. Use a metal bit first if you hit steel, then switch back to masonry.

**Q: How does battery life hold up in cold weather?**
A: Lithium-ion batteries lose capacity in freezing temps. Expect about 20-30% less runtime below 32°F. Keep the spare battery in your jacket pocket to cycle warm batteries.

**Q: Can I use this for drywall or wood?**
A: Not effectively. The hammer action is always engaged in hammer-drill mode, which will tear up wood. Use a standard drill for those tasks — this is a concrete specialist.
