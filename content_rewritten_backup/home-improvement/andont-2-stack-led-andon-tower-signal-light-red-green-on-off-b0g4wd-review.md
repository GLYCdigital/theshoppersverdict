If you run a CNC machine, a 3D printer farm, or a small workshop, you already know the pain: you're across the room, and you can't tell if the machine is done, faulted, or still chewing through a 4-hour job. The ANDONT 2-Stack LED Andon Tower Signal Light exists to kill that problem with two glaringly obvious colors. At 4.1 stars across 204 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but it's not without quirks.

## What you actually get

The package contains the tower light itself (two stacked LED segments — red on top, green below), a black plastic base with a toggle switch, and a short pigtail of wires for hookup. There's no power supply included, which is worth knowing: this runs on 12-24V DC, so you'll need a wall adapter, a bench supply, or a PLC output to drive it. The unit measures roughly 2 inches in diameter and stands about 4.5 inches tall — compact enough to sit on a shelf or mount to a machine frame without dominating the space.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The housing is a polycarbonate blend that feels sturdy, not flimsy. The lens segments are clear and let the LEDs punch through with real authority. One buyer put it plainly: *"I can see this from my workbench 40 feet away in a bright garage — the green is unmistakable, and the red is even brighter."* That tracks with the spec: these are high-intensity LEDs, not the dim glow you get from cheap indicator lamps.

The IP54 rating is legitimate. In a dusty workshop or a garage where coolant mist might drift, this won't die on you. The wiring is straightforward — common, VCC, and a signal line per color — and several reviewers noted it played nicely with their Arduino or PLC setups without needing extra resistors or drivers. One user remarked, *"Took me 10 minutes to wire to my 24V CNC controller. Works exactly as advertised."*

## What buyers consistently praise

The brightness is the runaway winner. Multiple reviewers mention visibility at distance, in daylight, and through safety glasses. The second most-cited positive is ease of hookup — it's a simple 3-wire job that doesn't require an electrical engineering degree. And for the price, buyers generally feel it's a solid value compared to name-brand andon lights that cost two to three times more.

## Where it falls short

The biggest gap: no audible alert. If you want to know when a job is done while you're in another room, this light won't help — it's strictly visual. The toggle switch on the base is also a basic on/off for the whole unit, not a color selector, so if you want to switch between red and green independently, you'll need to wire it through your controller rather than relying on the switch. Finally, mounting is bare-bones: there are pre-drilled holes but no bracket or magnets, so plan to supply your own screws or adhesive.

## Who should buy this

This is ideal for the hobbyist machinist, the garage tinkerer, or the small-batch maker who needs a clear visual status indicator on a machine and doesn't want to overpay. It's also a good fit for a DIY home automation project where you want a clean, bright light without a huge footprint. If you need sound, a multi-color stack (3+ segments), or a pre-built magnetic mount, look elsewhere — those add cost and complexity this unit doesn't cover.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this run on 5V USB power?**
A: No. It needs 12-24V DC. Don't try 5V — the LEDs won't reach full brightness and it may not light at all.

**Q: Does the switch control each color separately?**
A: No. The toggle is a single on/off for the whole tower. Color control requires external wiring to the signal lines.

**Q: Is it weatherproof for outdoor use?**
A: Not really. IP54 handles dust and splashing water, but it's not rated for prolonged rain or submersion. Keep it under cover.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a bright, reliable, easy-to-wire visual indicator for a machine or project and don't need sound or multi-color stacks. **Skip it if** you require an audible alert, independent color switching via a physical switch, or a tool-free mounting solution.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, no-frills workhorse that does one job well. The lack of a buzzer and minimal mounting hardware keep it from a perfect score, but for the price, it's hard to beat for clear, distance-readable status signaling.
