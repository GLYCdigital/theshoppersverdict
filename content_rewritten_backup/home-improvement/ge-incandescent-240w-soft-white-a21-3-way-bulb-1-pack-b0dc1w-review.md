Let's be blunt: in 2026, buying an incandescent bulb feels almost subversive. But there are specific reasons people still seek out a 240W 3-way bulb, and GE's A21 version fills that niche better than most. If you have a floor lamp with a 3-way socket that's been collecting dust because LED replacements flicker or dim unevenly, this is likely the fix you've been hunting for.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains exactly one bulb — no adapters, no fancy packaging. It's a standard A21 shape (slightly larger than the typical A19), which matters because it won't fit under shallow lamp shades. The base is a standard medium E26 screw, so it works in any standard 3-way socket. As shown in the product image, it's a straightforward frosted glass bulb with a visible filament structure — nothing fancy, just honest engineering.

The 3-way functionality is the core feature: 50W, 100W, and 240W settings. That's a meaningful range. At 50W you get a dim, moody glow perfect for evening reading. At 100W it's a solid work light. Crank it to 240W and you're getting roughly 3,900 lumens — enough to light up a whole room from a single lamp.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The light quality is where this bulb justifies its existence. It's a true 2700K soft white, which means warm, amber-toned illumination that flatters faces and makes wood furniture glow. LED bulbs in the same color temperature often look slightly sterile by comparison. There's also zero warm-up delay — flip the switch and it's at full brightness instantly.

The 3-way switching is genuinely smooth. Some cheaper 3-way bulbs have noticeable flicker when stepping between settings. This GE unit transitions cleanly. One reviewer noted, "Finally a bulb that doesn't buzz on the middle setting like my last one did." That's a real advantage for sensitive spaces like bedrooms or home offices.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the 204 ratings, the recurring themes are reliability and light quality. One verified purchase wrote: "Works perfectly in my antique floor lamp. **Where It Falls Short**

Here's the honest tradeoff. At 240 watts, this bulb generates significant heat. One buyer warned: "It gets hot enough that I'm nervous leaving it on unattended in my lamp with a paper shade." That's a legitimate safety consideration — this bulb needs a fixture with good airflow. Don't use it in an enclosed globe fixture, and keep it away from fabric or paper lampshades.

The lifespan is another reality check. GE rates this bulb for roughly 750–1,000 hours at full power. If you're using it at 240W for 4 hours every evening, that's about 6 months before replacement. At the 50W setting, expect longer life, but frequent high-wattage use will burn it out faster.

**Who Should Buy This**

Three specific buyers should consider this: (1) Anyone with a 3-way floor or table lamp that needs genuine brightness range — LED 3-ways often have limited dimming steps. (2) People sensitive to LED flicker or harsh color rendering — this is smoother and warmer. (3) Vintage fixture owners whose sockets can't handle LED driver electronics.

Skip it if you're replacing a standard single-setting bulb (this won't work in a regular socket — it needs the 3-way mechanism). Also skip it if you're energy-cost conscious; running this at full power for 5 hours daily costs roughly $4–5 extra per month versus a comparable LED.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this in a regular (non-3-way) lamp socket?**
A: No. The bulb requires a 3-way socket to function. In a standard socket, it will only operate at the highest 240W setting — which defeats the purpose and wastes energy.

**Q: Is the 240W setting really that bright?**
A: Yes, it's roughly equivalent to a 60W LED floodlight. It's genuinely room-filling light from a single lamp. Have a dimmer switch handy if you want lower output.

**Q: How hot does it get?**
A: Hot enough to be a burn hazard near bare skin. The glass reaches over 200°F at full power. Always use it in a lamp with a metal or ceramic socket and adequate ventilation.
