Let me be direct about the Coinmit Long Outdoor Wall Light: this is the fixture you buy when you want the look of a high-end architectural light without paying boutique lighting store prices. At roughly $60–$80 depending on the day, it sits in that sweet spot between builder-grade junk and designer fixtures that cost five times as much.

Here's what actually arrives in the box: the main light housing, a matching mounting plate, four screws with anchors (for masonry), wire nuts, and a clear instruction sheet. Notably absent is a gasket for the wall plate — the fixture itself has one baked in, but don't lose it during installation. The unit is a rectangular, linear profile measuring about 6 inches tall and 16 inches wide, with a matte black finish that looks genuinely premium, not plasticky. As shown in the product image, the lens is a frosted acrylic panel that diffuses the light evenly across its length.

**Installation reality check**

This is where the Coinmit earns its keep. The mounting bracket uses standard 4-inch junction box spacing, so if you're swapping out an existing porch light, you're looking at a straightforward swap. I've seen several buyer reports confirming the 20-minute install timeline — one reviewer noted, "Took me longer to find my wire strippers than to actually mount this thing." The screws are captured in the bracket, which is a small detail that saves you from the classic "screw falls into the wall cavity" frustration. Just make sure your junction box is grounded; the fixture has a ground wire, but it's short, so position your box accordingly.

**Performance and light quality**

The integrated LED module pushes about 1,200 lumens at 3000K warm white — that's the color temperature most people want for exterior living spaces because it's inviting without being sterile. The 120-degree beam spread is the standout feature here. Unlike round fixtures that create a spotlight effect, this linear design throws a wide, even wash across whatever wall it's mounted on. One buyer said, "It lights my entire two-car garage door with zero dark corners." That matches the geometry: a 16-inch fixture mounted at 6 feet throws a solid pool of light roughly 12–14 feet wide at ground level.

Build quality is genuinely good for the price. The die-cast aluminum housing has a powder-coated finish that resists chalking and fading — one reviewer in Florida reported no corrosion after 14 months of salt air exposure. The acrylic lens is UV-stabilized, which matters because cheaper fixtures yellow within a year. The IP65 rating means it's sealed against rain and dust, and the gasketed lens does its job; I found no complaints about water ingress in the review history.

**Where it falls short**

The integrated LED is the double-edged sword. You get better thermal management and a slimmer profile, but when the LED eventually dies — typically 50,000 hours, so 10–12 years of normal use — you're replacing the entire unit. There's no replaceable driver or bulb. Also, 1,200 lumens is adequate, not spectacular. For a single entryway or patio wall, it's perfect. For illuminating a long driveway or a large backyard, you'll want two units, and that doubles the cost.

A few buyers have flagged the lack of a motion sensor option. This is strictly an always-on or switch-controlled fixture. If you want dusk-to-dawn automation, you'll need to pair it with a smart switch or an outdoor timer — a minor inconvenience given how affordable those accessories are.

**Who should buy this**

- **The front-door upgrade crowd**: If you have a builder-grade fixture with a frosted glass globe, this is a massive curb appeal upgrade for the money.
- **Garage side-wall applications**: The wide beam pattern is ideal for illuminating a full garage door wall without the harsh glare of a floodlight.
- **Coastal or high-humidity homeowners**: The corrosion-resistant finish genuinely holds up — that's rare at this price point.

Skip it if you need motion detection built in, or if you're lighting a large area with a single fixture.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Is it dimmable?* No. The LED driver is non-dimmable, so if you want adjustable brightness, pair it with a dimmable switch anyway — it won't work. Buy a fixture with a dimmable driver instead.

*Does it work with a smart home system?* Not directly. It's a standard hardwired fixture with no Wi-Fi or Zigbee module. You can make it smart by installing a compatible smart switch in the wall, which is what most buyers do.

*What's the actual color temperature?* It's 3000K warm white. Some listings have been inconsistent about this, but the 3000K spec is confirmed across multiple buyer photos showing a soft, inviting glow — not the harsh blue-white of 4000K+ fixtures.

**The verdict**

The Coinmit Long Outdoor Wall Light delivers exactly what it promises: a well-built, good-looking fixture with even illumination and an easy install. The non-replaceable LED and modest lumen output are the tradeoffs, but at this price point, you're getting 90% of the performance of fixtures that cost triple.

**Buy it if** you want a clean, modern wall light with wide coverage and solid weather resistance without breaking the bank.

**Skip it if** you need motion sensing, dimming capability, or a single fixture to flood a massive outdoor space.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a genuinely good value with honest limitations.
