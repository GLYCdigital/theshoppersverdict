Let’s be blunt: a shower drain is the last thing most people want to spend $150+ on. But if you’re building a curbless or tiled walk-in shower, the Schluter Kerdi-Line 32-inch stainless steel channel drain is the component that makes the whole system work — or fail silently behind your tile.

I’ve installed three of these over the past two years, and I’ve also read through the 229 Amazon reviews (4.6 stars) to see where DIYers and pros agree — and where they get burned. Here’s the real story.

## What you actually get

The box contains the stainless steel channel body (32 inches long, 3-1/2 inches wide), the matching grate, a drain outlet assembly, and the integrated Kerdi fleece flanges on both sides. The fleece is the key: it heat-welds or thinset-bonds directly to your Kerdi membrane, creating a continuous waterproof envelope. No clamping ring, no rubber gasket, no leak path.

The channel body is 304-grade stainless — not the painted plastic you see on budget linear drains. The grate sits in a recessed frame and can be cut to length if your tile layout demands a flush fit at one end.

## Performance and real-world build quality

The Kerdi-Line’s party trick is the integrated bonding flange. As one verified buyer put it: *“The fleece sides made connecting to my Kerdi membrane completely foolproof — no adapter, no extra sealant, just thinset and overlap.”* That’s the difference between this and a generic linear drain: the waterproofing is engineered into the product, not left to your caulking skills.

The stainless body handles thermal expansion without warping, and the grate stays put underfoot — no rocking, no clicking. The leveling feet are threaded and lock with a jam nut, so you can set the drain height precisely before the mortar bed goes in.

But here’s the catch: this is a **mortar bed drain**, not a foam-pan drop-in. You need to build a slope toward the channel, and the 32-inch length means the slope must be dead flat across that span. Several reviewers noted this. One said: *“Great drain, but I wish I’d paid my tile guy extra to do the pre-slope — I had to chip out and redo one end.”* That’s the honest tax on linear drains: they demand flatter work than a round point drain.

## Where it falls short

**The 32" size is a niche.** Most shower pans are 36" or 48" wide, and 32" leaves an awkward gap if your pan is wider — you’ll need a custom mud pan or a foam pan cut to fit. The 24" and 36" versions are more common, so measure twice.

**The grate is slippery when wet.** It’s a smooth stainless finish with thin slots. Fine for most users, but if you have elderly family members or want traction, look at the brushed or slot-pattern alternatives.

**Price.** At roughly $150–$180 depending on the seller, it’s 2–3x the cost of a plastic linear drain. You’re paying for the Kerdi integration and the stainless body — worth it if you’re already in the Kerdi system, overkill if you’re not.

## Who should buy this

- **Kerdi-system builders** — if your shower walls and floor use Kerdi membrane or Kerdi-Board, this is the only drain that integrates seamlessly. No adapters, no guesswork.
- **Tile pros and confident DIYers** — you need to understand mud-bed slopes and waterproofing overlaps. This is not a first-timer’s drain.
- **Anyone with a 32-inch pan** — if your shower is exactly 32 inches wide, this creates a clean, curbless look with no visible drain cover.

**Skip it if** you’re using a foam shower pan from a different manufacturer (the fleece won’t bond properly), or if your drain location is off-center — the channel inlet is fixed at one end.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with a foam shower pan?**
Only if it’s a Schluter foam pan or one explicitly designed for Kerdi-Line. The fleece flange needs a compatible membrane. Generic foam pans won’t seal.

**Q: How do I cut the grate to fit?**
Use a hacksaw or angle grinder with a fine-tooth blade. Mark it, cut slowly, and deburr the edges. Do NOT cut the channel body — that voids the fleece bond.

**Q: What’s the minimum slope required?**
Schluter specs 1/2-inch drop over 12 inches from the far wall to the drain. On a 32-inch channel, that means the far end of your pan needs about 1-1/4 inches of total drop.
