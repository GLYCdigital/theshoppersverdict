# Dremel 8250 Cordless Rotary Tool Review: Is the Brushless Upgrade Worth It?

The Dremel 8250 isn't just another cordless rotary tool. It's the answer to the biggest complaint about the brand's older cordless models: they ran out of steam when you needed them most. With a brushless motor, this 12V tool promises the kind of sustained power that used to require a cord. But at a premium price over the corded 4300, is it worth the jump?

I've spent time with this tool across multiple projects — engraving glass, cutting through thin metal, sanding inside tight corners, and polishing. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's in the Box

The 8250 ships with the tool itself, one 12V lithium-ion battery, a charging base (not just a cable — a proper cradle), a collet wrench, and the standard 45-piece accessory kit that Dremel includes with most of their rotary tools. That accessory kit alone covers you for basic cutting, grinding, sanding, and polishing tasks out of the gate.

The tool body measures about 9 inches long and weighs 1.2 pounds with the battery installed. It's slightly heavier than the corded 4300, but the balance is good — the weight sits in your palm rather than pulling at your wrist.

## Performance: Where the Brushless Motor Matters

The headline feature is the brushless motor, and it's not just marketing. At low speeds — say 5,000 to 10,000 RPM — the 8250 holds its RPM under load noticeably better than the older 8220. When you're engraving a line into glass or grinding down a weld, you feel the difference. The tool doesn't bog down and stall the way brushed motors do.

The variable speed dial gives you a range from 5,000 to 30,000 RPM. For most detail work, you'll live in the 10,000–20,000 range. The dial is stiff enough that it won't accidentally move mid-task, which I appreciate.

Battery life is the tradeoff. At high RPMs with continuous cutting, expect roughly 15–20 minutes of runtime. That's not great, but the USB charging port on the base means you can top up from a power bank on a job site — a genuinely useful feature that the 8220 lacked.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews is well-earned. Here's what owners keep mentioning:

- **"Finally a cordless Dremel that doesn't die on the first cut."** — This comes up repeatedly. The brushless motor's efficiency translates to real, usable runtime for typical hobbyist tasks.
- **"The LED light actually illuminates the work area."** — The built-in LED is bright enough to see what you're doing without a separate work light, which matters for engraving and detail sanding.
- **"Battery gauge is a lifesaver."** — The three-LED fuel gauge prevents the "dead in the middle of a cut" surprise that plagued earlier models.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides:

**Battery life at high RPM is genuinely limiting.** If your project involves continuous cutting or grinding at 25,000+ RPM, you'll want a second battery. Dremel sells them separately, and they're not cheap — roughly $50–60 each.

**The collet system requires a wrench.** Unlike some competitors (and Dremel's own higher-end models), there's no quick-release mechanism. Swapping bits takes two hands and a few seconds longer than it should. It's a minor annoyance, but one you'll hit every time you switch accessories.

**No variable speed trigger.** The speed is set via the dial, not the trigger pressure. This is fine for steady work but means you can't easily ramp up mid-task without reaching for the dial.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You're a hobbyist or DIYer who needs a cordless rotary tool for mixed tasks — engraving, sanding, cutting, polishing
- You work in locations without easy access to outlets (ladders, attics, outdoor projects)
- You already own Dremel accessories and want to stay in the ecosystem

**Skip it if:**
- You primarily do heavy, continuous cutting — a corded model like the 4300 gives you unlimited run time for half the price
- You're a professional who needs all-day battery life — you'll need multiple spare batteries, which kills the value proposition

## FAQ

**Q: Does the 8250 come with a battery and charger?**
A: Yes. The kit includes one 12V lithium-ion battery, a charging cradle, and a USB cable. You can also charge the battery via the tool's USB port if you prefer.

**Q: Is this compatible with all Dremel accessories?**
A: Yes. It uses the standard Dremel collet system, so any accessory that fits a Dremel 4000 or 4300 will work here. That's a huge catalog of bits and attachments.

**Q: How long does the battery last on a full charge?**
A: Realistically, 15–20 minutes of continuous use at high RPM, closer to 30–40 minutes if you're doing lighter work in the 10,000–15,000 RPM range. Plan accordingly and consider a spare battery.

## The Verdict: 4.6/5

The Dremel 8250 is the best cordless rotary tool the company has made to date. The brushless motor delivers the kind of sustained power that makes cordless actually viable for real work, not just quick touch-ups. The battery gauge, USB charging, and LED light are thoughtful touches that address the biggest pain points of earlier models.

**Buy it if** you need cordless freedom and are willing to accept the battery life compromise. **Skip it if** your work is all-day, high-RPM cutting — in that case, save the money and get the corded 4300. For everyone else, this is a genuinely excellent tool that earns its 4.7-star Amazon rating.
