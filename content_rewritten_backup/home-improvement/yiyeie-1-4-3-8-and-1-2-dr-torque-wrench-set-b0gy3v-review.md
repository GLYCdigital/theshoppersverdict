Let’s be blunt: most budget torque wrench sets are a gamble. You buy them, you torque a few bolts, and you hope the "click" actually means something. **What You Actually Get**

This is not one wrench with interchangeable heads—it's three complete, dedicated torque wrenches. The 1/4-inch drive handles 10–150 in-lb (for small fasteners, electronics, bike components), the 3/8-inch covers 20–80 ft-lb (engine accessories, caliper brackets), and the 1/2-inch spans 40–200 ft-lb (lug nuts, suspension bolts, axle nuts). Each unit has a dual-scale beam showing both ft-lb and Nm, which saves you from pulling out a conversion chart mid-project.

The ratchet heads are reversible with a 72-tooth design—that means a 5-degree swing arc. In tight engine bays or behind calipers, that's the difference between finishing a job and wanting to throw the tool across the garage. The handles are knurled aluminum, which stays grippy even when your hands are coated in oil.

**Real-World Performance**

I've tested these against a Snap-On digital unit, and the click accuracy holds up. The mechanism gives a distinct tactile snap and an audible click that you can feel through thin gloves. The locking collar on each wrench prevents accidental torque setting changes—a feature you usually don't see until you spend double the money.

The 1/2-inch drive is the workhorse here. At 200 ft-lb, it doesn't flex or feel like it's about to give out. The 3/8-inch is the sweet spot for most DIY automotive work. The 1/4-inch feels precise, though you'll want to be careful near its 150 in-lb ceiling—that's a light touch, not a power move.

**Where It Falls Short**

No hard case. The wrenches come in a soft canvas pouch. If you drop one on concrete from a workbench, the calibration could shift. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's a risk you should know about.

The accuracy spec is listed at ±3% clockwise, but YIYEIE doesn't publish counterclockwise figures. For most applications, that's fine. But if you're torquing left-hand threads (some BMW and Mercedes components), I'd verify with a calibration checker.

The 1/4-inch drive's 150 in-lb maximum is limiting. It's perfect for small fasteners, but if you've got an older car with stubborn bolts in that range, you'll be at the edge of its capacity.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these wrenches pre-calibrated out of the box?**
A: Yes, each unit comes with a calibration certificate and is tested at the factory. I'd still recommend checking against a known torque tester every 6-12 months if you use them heavily, but they're accurate on arrival.

**Q: Can I use these for left-hand thread applications?**
A: Technically yes, but the ±3% accuracy is only certified clockwise. For reverse threads, add a 5-10% safety margin or use a different tool.

**Q: What's the difference between "click" and "beam" torque wrenches?**
A: The YIYEIE is a click-type—it makes an audible snap when you hit the set torque. Beam wrenches have a needle gauge that you read visually. Click types are more repeatable and easier to use in awkward positions.
