If you're replacing a builder-grade tub faucet that's leaked for the last three years, the gotonovo Tub Shower Faucet Set looks like the sensible upgrade. It's priced like a budget option but spec'd with a brass valve body and a 6-mode shower head. After digging through 220 ratings and the build details, here's the honest picture.

## What's Actually in the Box

The kit includes the single-handle tub faucet body, a 6-inch brass shower arm, the 6-mode shower head, a diverter tub spout, handle, trim, and mounting hardware. You also get the rough-in valve body — that's the important part. Some budget sets make you buy the valve separately; this one ships complete. As shown in the product image, the chrome finish is bright and evenly applied, not the thin plating that peels after six months.

**Key spec check:** The valve is 1/2-inch IPS connections, standard for US plumbing. The tub spout is 6 inches with a diverter. The shower head is 4 inches wide. Nothing exotic — which is exactly why DIYers gravitate to this.

## Performance and Build Quality

The star here is the diverter. On cheap sets, the lever that switches water from tub spout to shower head is mushy and often leaves the shower head dripping for minutes after you turn it off. Multiple buyers note this one clicks positively and seals cleanly. One reviewer put it plainly: *"The diverter actually works. My last one dripped for two years and I just lived with it."*

The 6-mode shower head delivers distinct patterns, not marketing variations. The massage setting has real pulse, the mist setting is fine for rinsing shampoo, and the full spray covers a solid 6-inch radius. The head swivels at the ball joint with enough friction to hold position without drooping over time.

Finish quality is above average for the price point. The handle has a solid feel when you rotate it — no hollow click or binding. Temperature adjustment is smooth through the range.

## Where It Falls Short

The 8-inch shower arm is the most common physical complaint. If your existing stub-out sits low (say, 48 inches from the floor), the head lands around chest height for anyone 5'10" or taller. You can buy a longer arm separately for $15, but it's an extra trip to the hardware store.

The included mounting screws are genuinely soft. Two reviewers mentioned stripping them during install. Spend the $3 on quality stainless screws before you start — it's the kind of thing that separates a 30-minute install from a frustrating afternoon.

No pressure-balancing valve means if someone flushes a toilet while you're showering, you'll feel it. This is standard at this price point, but worth knowing before you buy.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the shower head have a pause button?**
A: No. The 6 modes are spray patterns, not a flow-stop feature. You'll use the handle or diverter to stop water.

**Q: Will this fit my existing shower arm?**
A: The included arm uses standard 1/2-inch NPT threads, so it will connect to any standard stub-out. If your existing arm is in good shape, you can reuse it — just check the finish matches.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: Yes, if you're comfortable with basic plumbing. You'll need to shut off water, remove the old valve, and sweat or thread the new connections. Budget 2-3 hours for a first-timer.
