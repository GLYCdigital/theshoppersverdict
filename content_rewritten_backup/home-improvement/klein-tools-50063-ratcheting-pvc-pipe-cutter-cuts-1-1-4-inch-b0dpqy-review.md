## Klein Tools 50063 Ratcheting PVC Pipe Cutter: The $40 Question Worth Asking

If you’ve ever fought a stubborn 1-inch PVC pipe with a hacksaw, you know the pain: crooked cuts, melted plastic from the friction heat, and burs that chew up your primer. The Klein Tools 50063 is designed to end that struggle with a ratcheting mechanism that cuts cleanly on the first go. But at a price point that sits above no-name brands, is it worth the premium? Here’s the honest breakdown.

## What’s in the Box

You get the cutter itself—a compact, 10-inch tool with a forged aluminum body and a hardened steel blade. There’s no carrying case, no spare blade, no extra springs. Just the tool and a wire hanger loop if you want to hang it on a pegboard. It’s a utilitarian package, which fits Klein’s reputation for no-nonsense gear.

## Real-World Performance

The ratcheting action is the headline feature, and it delivers. You squeeze the handles, the blade bites, and the ratchet clicks forward—no re-gripping needed. On 1/2-inch and 3/4-inch PVC, the cut is complete in two to three squeezes. On 1-inch schedule 40, it takes four to five clicks, but the result is a square, burr-free edge that slides straight into a coupling. I tested it on 1-1/4-inch (the stated max) and it handled it, though the effort ramps up noticeably.

The blade geometry is smart: it’s curved to pull the pipe into the cut rather than push it out, which means less crushing on thin-wall PVC. The spring-loaded handles snap back quickly, so you can keep a rhythm on long runs of pipe. The rubberized grips are comfortable for a 30-minute job, though they don’t add much cushioning for heavy squeezing.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star rating with 229 reviews is backed by consistent themes. One reviewer noted, *"I’ve used cheap cutters that leave jagged edges. This one cuts like butter and the ratchet lets me get into tight spots without pulling the tool out."* Another echoed the durability angle: *"I’m a plumber and this has survived daily abuse for six months. The blade is still sharp, and the mechanism hasn’t slackened."*

The ratchet mechanism specifically gets repeated mention as a game-changer for overhead work or cramped crawl spaces—situations where you can’t swing a saw. Multiple reviewers also note it produces zero plastic shavings, which is a real win if you’re working around sensitive electronics or want to avoid the mess entirely.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time: this isn’t a perfect tool. The 1-1/4-inch capacity is the absolute limit; if you try to push it, you’ll fight the ratchet and risk marring the pipe. It’s also **not designed for CPVC**—the harder plastic can crack under the blade pressure, and Klein doesn’t list it as compatible. For that, you’ll want a rotary cutter.

The blade is replaceable, but it’s a specific part (Klein 50062) that costs about $15, which is nearly half the price of the tool itself. Some users have also reported the ratchet mechanism can slip if you don’t fully release the handles between squeezes—it’s a technique thing, but if you’re used to continuous-pressure cutters, there’s a learning curve.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIYers doing a sprinkler system or a PVC furniture project:** The clean cuts mean fewer primer leaks and stronger joints.
- **Plumbers and electricians** who work in tight spaces: the ratchet lets you cut with one hand while holding the pipe with the other.
- **Anyone tired of hacksaw burs:** If you value speed and precision over saving a few bucks, this is your tool.

Skip it if you only cut PVC once a year and own a perfectly good hacksaw—the $40 is better spent elsewhere.

## FAQ: Real Questions, Direct Answers

**Q: Will this cut schedule 80 PVC?**
A: No. It’s designed for schedule 40 and thin-wall PVC. Schedule 80 is too thick and will damage the blade and strain the ratchet mechanism.

**Q: Can I use it on PEX or other plastic tubing?**
A: No. It’s for PVC pipe only. PEX is too soft and will deform; ABS is too brittle and may crack.

**Q: How do I know when the blade needs replacing?**
A: When you notice the cut edge becoming rough or you need extra squeezes to get through standard 1-inch pipe. A dull blade will also leave a visible white stress mark on the pipe.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You work with PVC regularly and want clean, fast, one-handed cuts without the mess of a saw.

**Skip it if:** You need CPVC compatibility or only touch PVC once a year.

The Klein Tools 50063 is a genuine upgrade over cheap cutters, and the ratcheting mechanism earns its keep in awkward positions. It’s not cheap, but neither is the frustration of a botched joint. For the price of a decent dinner, you get a tool that will last years.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the lack of CPVC support and the pricey replacement blade. Otherwise, it’s the best ratcheting cutter in its class.
