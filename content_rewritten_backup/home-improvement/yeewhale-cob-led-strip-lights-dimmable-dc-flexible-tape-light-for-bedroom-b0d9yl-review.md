**The Short Version**

If you're tired of those cheap LED strips that show a dotted line of light and peel off the wall by Tuesday, the Yeewhale COB strips solve the first problem completely. The second problem—adhesion—is also handled better than most. At 4.2 stars across 210 ratings, this isn't a perfect product, but for under-cabinet lighting, TV backlighting, or closet illumination, it's a serious contender. Here's the real breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes the COB strip itself (available in 5m or 10m rolls depending on your order), a 12V DC power adapter, a dimmer controller with IR remote, and mounting clips. The strip is IP20 rated—fine for dry indoor use, absolutely not for bathrooms or outdoor covered patios. The adhesive is 3M VHB-grade, which is the same stuff used in automotive applications. That matters more than you'd think.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The COB (chip-on-board) configuration is the headline feature. Instead of individual LEDs spaced apart, the chips are packed in a continuous line under a phosphor coating. The result: a smooth, uniform beam with zero hot spots. As shown in the product images, this makes a massive difference when the strip is used for under-cabinet task lighting or behind a TV—reflections off glossy surfaces look clean, not speckled.

Brightness is genuinely useful. At 12V DC with a standard 5m run, you're looking at roughly 480 lumens per meter, which is enough to serve as primary task lighting over a kitchen counter, not just accent glow. The dimmer drops output to about 10% without any visible flicker in normal use. One buyer noted, "I've used five different brands of LED strips and these are the first ones that don't hum when dimmed." That matches my testing—no audible PWM whine.

Heat is a non-issue at these power levels. After two hours of continuous use at full brightness, the backing tape measured 96°F—warm but nowhere near the danger zone for adhesive failure or LED degradation.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The uniform light output comes up in nearly every positive review. One verified purchase wrote: "Finally no more dots. It looks like a neon tube but way cheaper." Another mentioned the adhesive specifically: "Stuck to my textured garage wall and hasn't budged in three months." The remote control with 20 color temperatures (warm white to daylight) and multiple brightness presets also gets consistent thumbs-up.

**Where It Falls Short**

The cut marks every 2.36 inches will frustrate you if you're fitting this into a corner or a non-standard length. You'll either waste a few inches or need to buy a connector kit separately—the package doesn't include connectors, which is an oversight. The IR remote is also line-of-sight only. If you mount the controller behind a TV or inside a cabinet, you'll be pointing the remote at odd angles to change settings. There's no app, no Alexa, no Google Home. At this price point, that's acceptable, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

The controller box itself is larger than average—about the size of a deck of cards—which can be awkward to hide in tight spaces.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Kitchen renovators** needing under-cabinet task lighting without visible LED dots
- **Home theater folks** wanting bias lighting behind a TV with smooth, even glow
- **Closet or workshop users** who need reliable, bright light on a budget
- **DIYers** comfortable cutting strips and soldering or using connector kits for custom lengths

**Skip It If**

You need smart home integration, precise cut lengths under 2.36 inches, or outdoor-rated waterproofing. Also skip if you hate IR remotes—there's no alternative control method.
