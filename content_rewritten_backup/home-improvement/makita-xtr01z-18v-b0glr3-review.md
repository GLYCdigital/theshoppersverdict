The Makita XTR01Z is the kind of tool that makes you wonder why more manufacturers don't build cordless routers this well. It's compact, it's capable, and it doesn't feel like a compromise just because it happens to run on batteries. But here's the thing — this isn't a bare-bones budget pick, and it's not for everyone.

## What You're Actually Getting

This is the bare tool. No battery, no charger, no case. Just the router, a 1/4-inch collet cone wrench, and a guide base. If you're already in the Makita 18V LXT ecosystem, that's fine — you've got batteries on the shelf. If you're not, factor in another $100-150 for a battery and charger before you compare prices with corded options. The XTR01Z weighs just 3.5 pounds with a 5.0Ah battery attached, and the body measures roughly 4.9 inches tall — small enough to palm comfortably.

## Build and Real-World Performance

The motor spins at 30,000 RPM and delivers 1-1/4 horsepower equivalent. That's plenty for trim work — rounding over edges, flush trimming, chamfering. In tests on oak and maple, the constant speed control holds RPM noticeably better than cheaper cordless routers. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"I've used it to route 100+ feet of edge profiles in red oak without bogging down. My old corded router would have been fighting me the whole time."*

The base-mounted depth adjustment is the standout feature here. You reach through the base to adjust depth with a knurled ring, which means you don't have to reach around the motor housing while the tool is on the work. Combined with the dual LED lights that have a 20-second afterglow, you can actually see what you're cutting. That sounds basic, but it's rare in this class.

Battery life is honest but not amazing — expect roughly 25-35 minutes of continuous routing on a 5.0Ah battery, depending on the cut. Intermittent work gets you a full day. If you're doing production runs, keep a second battery on rotation.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews is backed by consistent themes. Owners love the power-to-size ratio — it's genuinely surprising how much torque this little thing produces. Reviewers also repeatedly mention the dust extraction port, which actually works when connected to a vac. One buyer noted: *"I was skeptical about cordless routing, but this thing has replaced my corded trim router entirely. The dust port keeps the work area almost clean, which I never expected."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. There's no soft-start function. At full throttle, the router kicks when you pull the trigger — it's not dangerous, but it's a jolt you'll notice, especially on delicate work. The base plate is plastic, and while it's rigid enough for trim work, it flexes noticeably if you lean into heavy edge profiling with a large bit. If you're planning to use this as a poor man's plunge router, you'll be disappointed. It's a trim router, full stop.

Also, the tool-free bit change everyone wants? Doesn't exist here. You'll need that included wrench, which is fine but feels a generation behind.

## Who Should Buy This

**The Makita LXT loyalist** — if you've got 18V batteries sitting around, this is a no-brainer addition to your kit. **The cabinet maker or finish carpenter** who does trim work on site and needs something that fits in a toolbox drawer. **The DIYer who already owns Makita tools** and wants to graduate from a laminate trimmer.

**Skip it if** you're starting fresh with no batteries, or if you mostly route thick hardwood stock — a corded 2-horsepower router will serve you better at a similar price. And if your work involves deep plunge cuts, this isn't your tool.

## FAQ

**Does it come with a battery?**
No. The XTR01Z is a bare tool. You'll need a Makita 18V LXT battery and charger — either the 2.0Ah minimum, though a 5.0Ah is recommended for longer runtimes.

**Will this handle hardwood like oak or maple?**
Yes, for trim work — edge profiling, chamfers, flush trimming. Take lighter passes (1/8-inch or less) in dense wood, and it'll cut cleanly without bogging. Don't attempt deep cuts in one pass.

**Can it accept standard router bits?**
Yes, any 1/4-inch shank bit. It also includes a guide base for template work, and you can add an offset base or edge guide if needed.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're already in the Makita LXT ecosystem and need a genuinely capable cordless trim router that will handle site work without dragging a cord. The constant speed control, base-mounted depth adjustment, and effective dust port make it a legitimate workhorse, not a novelty.

**Skip it if** you're starting fresh with cordless tools or primarily need a router for heavy stock removal. In those cases, a corded router offers more power for less money, and the lack of soft-start on this unit will frustrate precision work.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect, but for what it's designed to do, it's about as good as cordless trim routing gets right now.
