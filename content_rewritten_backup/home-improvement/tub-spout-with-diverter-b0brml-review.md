If you're staring at a corroded, leaky tub spout that's been on the wall since the Reagan administration, this replacement is worth a serious look. The Tub Spout with Diverter (B0BRML6F63) sits in that sweet spot of affordable, functional, and genuinely easy to install. But as with any hardware purchase, there are a few things you need to know before you break out the plumber's tape.

## What You Actually Get

The package is refreshingly straightforward: the spout itself, a diverter lever assembly, an Allen wrench, and a threaded adapter. The spout measures roughly 7 inches from wall to tip—slightly longer than the standard 6-inch fixture, which matters if you have a deep tub. The polished chrome finish is bright and even, with no visible machining marks. The diverter lever has a satisfying, positive click when you pull it up to redirect water to your showerhead.

Installation is genuinely tool-free for most setups. The spout threads directly onto your existing 1/2-inch male pipe stub-out. For slip-fit connections, the included adapter and Allen wrench handle the set screw. Most buyers report being done in under 15 minutes, and one reviewer noted, "I'm not handy at all and had this swapped out before my shower water even got hot."

## Where It Shines

The build quality punches above its price point. This is solid brass, not the zinc alloy or plastic that plagues budget fixtures. The chrome plating has held up well in the reviews I analyzed—no peeling, no clouding after months of use. The diverter mechanism is where this spout really earns its keep. It engages with authority, and water pressure to the showerhead stays consistent without the annoying "weeping" you get from cheaper diverters that half-engage.

Buyers consistently praise two things: the finish and the fit. A verified purchase mentioned, "Matches my Delta faucet perfectly, looks like it cost three times as much." Another reviewer with a clawfoot tub specifically noted the spout's reach was long enough to clear the tub edge—a common complaint with shorter spouts.

## The Honest Downsides

Here's where you need to pay attention. The diverter stem length is fixed and on the shorter side. If your existing plumbing stub-out extends more than 3/4 inch past the finished wall, the lever won't fully engage the shower position. This isn't a dealbreaker—you can trim the pipe—but it's an extra step that isn't mentioned in the product description. One reviewer flagged this directly: "Had to cut my pipe back 1/4 inch to get the diverter to seat properly."

Second, there's no flow restrictor inside. When you flip from tub fill to shower mode, water pressure spikes abruptly. It's not dangerous, but it can be startling if you're not expecting it, and it's worth noting if you have sensitive plumbing.

## Who Should Buy This

This spout is ideal for three specific buyers: the DIY homeowner replacing a corroded fixture, the landlord updating a rental without breaking the bank, and anyone matching a chrome bathroom suite on a budget. It's less ideal if you need a specific finish (only chrome is offered here) or if you have unusually deep rough-in plumbing that would require significant modification.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my existing shower setup?**
A: It fits standard 1/2-inch male pipe threads. For slip-fit connections, the included adapter handles it. Measure your stub-out length before ordering—if it extends more than 3/4 inch past the wall, plan on trimming it.

**Q: Does the diverter work for both tub and shower use?**
A: Yes. Pull up for shower, push down for tub fill. The mechanism has a positive detent, so it won't drift between positions.

**Q: Is this compatible with a handheld shower wand?**
A: The diverter itself is standard, but you'll need an additional adapter to convert the showerhead outlet for handheld use. The spout is designed for a fixed showerhead.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a solid, good-looking brass spout with a reliable diverter at a reasonable price, and you're comfortable with basic DIY installation.

**Skip it if** your plumbing stub-out is unusually deep, you need a non-chrome finish, or you're looking for a spout with integrated flow control.

The Tub Spout with Diverter delivers exactly what it promises: a durable, functional fixture that looks far more expensive than it is. The 4.2-star rating holds up—the two minor installation quirks are the only things keeping it from a perfect score. For most buyers, this is a 20-minute upgrade that'll last for years.
