Cabinet hardware is the jewelry of a kitchen. Get it right and the whole room looks intentional. Get it wrong and you're staring at a mismatch every time you open a drawer. The Goo-Ki Antique Brass zinc alloy pulls sit firmly in the "get it right" category — with a few caveats worth knowing before you add them to your cart.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting the pulls themselves — a set of two, four, or six depending on the listing you choose — plus the mounting screws. The screws are the right length for standard cabinet construction: short enough for 1/2-inch drawer fronts, long enough for 3/4-inch doors. No wall anchors, no templates, no fancy extras. It's a bare-bones package, which is fine for hardware this simple.

Each pull measures 5 inches overall with a 3-inch (76mm) hole spacing. That's the industry standard for US cabinets, so most of you won't need to drill new holes. If you're replacing old pulls, measure your existing spacing first — if it's not 3 inches, you'll be filling and drilling.

## Build Quality and the Finish Question

The zinc alloy body has a satisfying heft. These aren't hollow, flimsy pieces that flex when you grip them. As shown in the product image, the antique brass finish has a subtle brushed texture that hides fingerprints better than polished brass would. That's a practical win for high-traffic kitchens.

The finish is where you need to pay attention. This is an antique brass tone — warm, slightly darkened, with hints of bronze. It does *not* match bright, shiny polished brass. If you're mixing these with existing brass fixtures, the difference will be noticeable. If you're replacing all your hardware at once, that's not an issue.

Buyer feedback consistently praises the finish durability. One reviewer noted, "I've had these on my kitchen cabinets for six months and there's zero chipping or fading despite daily use." Another mentioned the "rich, deep color" in photos looked even better in person. That's the kind of consistency you want when buying hardware sight-unseen.

## Where They Fall Short

The Phillips-head screws are the weakest link. They work fine, but Phillips drives cam out under torque — a Torx or hex head would be more forgiving for DIYers who don't own a proper screwdriver set. You'll want a #2 Phillips with a good grip to avoid stripping.

The antique finish, while attractive, is specific. If you're the type who might want to swap hardware styles in a few years, know that these pulls commit you to the antique brass aesthetic. They won't blend neutrally with other finishes.

## Who Should Buy These

**The full-kitchen renovator.** If you're doing a complete refresh and want consistent antique brass hardware across all your cabinets and drawers, these deliver uniform color and quality.

**The landlord or flipper.** At the price point, these look more expensive than they cost. That's a good equation for rental properties or homes you plan to sell.

**The standard-spacing homeowner.** If your cabinets have 3-inch hole spacing, this is a true screw-in-and-go upgrade with no drilling.

**Skip these if** you're trying to match existing polished brass fixtures, or if you need non-standard hole spacing (2-3/4 inches, 4 inches, etc.).

## FAQ — Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Do these fit soft-close drawer slides?**
Yes. The pulls attach to the drawer front, not the slide mechanism. They work with any slide type.

**Q: Will the finish match other Goo-Ki products?**
Generally yes — Goo-Ki maintains consistent finishes across their antique brass line. But finishes can vary slightly between production batches, so order all your hardware at once.

**Q: Are these solid brass or just brass-colored?**
They're zinc alloy with a brass finish — not solid brass. That's expected at this price point. The alloy is durable and rust-resistant, but if you need solid brass for a historic restoration, look elsewhere.
