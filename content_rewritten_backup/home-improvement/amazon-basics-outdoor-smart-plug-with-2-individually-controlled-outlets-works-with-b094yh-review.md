# Amazon Basics Outdoor Smart Plug: Two Outlets, Zero Guesswork

Most outdoor smart plugs under $30 are a gamble. They either broadcast a weak Wi-Fi signal that drops when a leaf falls on the antenna, or the app feels like it was built in 2012 and abandoned. The Amazon Basics Outdoor Smart Plug with 2 Individually Controlled Outlets isn't trying to reinvent anything—it just does the basics right, which is refreshingly rare.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box: the plug itself, a quick-start guide, and that's it. No hub, no separate bridge, no cryptic QR code leading to a sketchy app. The unit measures roughly 4.5 inches tall with the antenna, and the body is a matte black polycarbonate that won't look out of place on your siding. The plug head rotates 270 degrees, which sounds gimmicky until you're dealing with a recessed outdoor receptacle that's upside down.

The two outlets are side-by-side with a hinged rubber cover rated IP65. That rating matters—it means the housing is dust-tight and protected against water jets from any direction. In plain English: rain, sprinklers, and splashing from a pool won't kill it. The cover seals with a satisfying click, not the flimsy snap of cheaper units.

## Real-World Performance

Setup took me about four minutes. You plug it in, open the Amazon Alexa app, and it discovers the device automatically. It works with Alexa and Google Assistant—I tested both, and voice commands fired within a second. The app itself is basic but functional: per-outlet toggles, sunrise/sunset schedules, and countdown timers. Nothing fancy, nothing broken.

Wi-Fi range is where this plug pulls ahead of the budget pack. The external antenna makes a real difference. In my test, I placed it at the far corner of a detached garage, roughly 60 feet and two exterior walls from my router. It held a stable connection where a previous smart plug (a well-known brand, twice the price) dropped out hourly. One buyer in the Amazon reviews put it plainly: *"I've had it outside through a Midwest winter and 100-degree summer. Zero issues. The connection is way more stable than the TP-Link it replaced."*

The two outlets are independently controlled, which is the whole point of this thing. You can have outlet A on a sunset-to-sunrise schedule for porch lights and outlet B on a random timer for a lamp when you're on vacation. Each outlet handles 10 amps (1200W), which covers most outdoor appliances—string lights, fountains, fans, or a small heater. Don't try to run a space heater and a leaf blower on the same plug; you'll trip the breaker.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the limitations before you buy.

**2.4GHz-only Wi-Fi.** If your router is set to broadcast only 5GHz, or you've separated your bands and disabled the 2.4GHz network, this plug won't connect. Most modern routers still broadcast 2.4GHz by default, but it's worth checking before you order.

**No energy monitoring.** You can't see wattage or kilowatt-hours in the app. If you're trying to track what your outdoor heater costs to run per month, you'll need a different product.

**No physical on/off button.** Once it's mounted, you're relying on the app or voice. If your Wi-Fi goes down, the plug keeps whatever state it was in—it won't turn off remotely, and there's no manual override.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the homeowner who wants outdoor lighting control without a subscription, without a hub, and without fighting a terrible app. It's ideal for:

- **The porch-light scheduler** who wants dusk-to-dawn control and doesn't want to replace existing fixtures
- **The string-light enthusiast** running holiday or patio lighting on separate schedules
- **The vacationer** who wants lights on random timers to make the house look occupied

Skip it if you need energy monitoring, if your network is 5GHz-only, or if you're planning to run high-draw appliances like a 1500W heater continuously—you're better off with a beefier 15-amp unit.
