**Bottom line:** These Aqraou solar lights deliver genuine 1200-lumen brightness and IP66 weather protection at a price that undercuts most competitors — but the battery performance in winter will test your patience.

If you've shopped solar lighting before, you know the category is full of weak 200-lumen pucks that barely illuminate a mailbox. The Aqraou units are different. As shown in the product image, each unit packs a large 1200-lumen LED panel rated at 120° beam spread — enough to light a two-car driveway or a 30-foot stretch of fence line. The build quality matches the specs: aluminum alloy housing, tempered glass lens, and silicone-gasketed seams that justify the IP66 waterproof rating.

**What you get in the box:** Two complete light units, two mounting brackets, all stainless steel hardware, and a basic instruction sheet. Each light measures roughly 10 × 6 × 2 inches. The solar panel is separate from the LED head on a 15-foot cable, which is a deliberate design choice — you can mount the panel on your roof and point the light where you actually need it. That flexibility is rare at this price point.

**Real-world performance:** During summer months with 7+ hours of direct sun, these lights run the full 10-hour cycle from dusk to dawn. The motion sensor (120° detection range, adjustable up to 30 feet) brightens from dim standby to full output when triggered. The remote control works from 20 feet away and lets you set 3-hour, 5-hour, or 8-hour timers. One buyer noted: *"I've had mine for 6 months through two snowstorms and a hurricane — they never flickered once. My neighbor's $80 Ring floodlight died, but these keep going."*

**Where they fall short:** Honest talk — winter performance is mediocre. When daylight drops below 4 hours, expect 4–5 hours of light, not the claimed 10. The motion sensor also has a noticeable 5-second delay between trigger and full brightness. And the included remote feels cheap; the buttons require a firm press. A few reviewers reported the mounting bracket screws stripping if over-tightened, so use a hand driver, not a drill.

**Who should buy these:** Homeowners needing reliable lighting for driveways, backyards, or detached garages without running electrical lines. Renters who can't hardwire lights will appreciate the tool-free installation — two screws per bracket and you're done. They also work well for rural properties where power access is impractical.

**Skip them if:** You live in the Pacific Northwest or anywhere with long cloudy stretches, and you need guaranteed all-night illumination. Or if you want floodlight-level intensity for security — these are bright, but they're not replacing a wired 2000-lumen security light.

**FAQ:**

**Q: How long do these last on a full charge?**  
A: In summer, 9–10 hours in motion-activated mode. In winter with partial sun, expect 4–5 hours. The battery is a rechargeable 18650 lithium cell.

**Q: Can these be mounted on a pole?**  
A: Yes — the bracket fits poles 2–4 inches in diameter. Just note the solar panel cable needs to reach, so plan your routing.

**Q: Do they work on cloudy days?**  
A: They charge, but at reduced capacity. One full day of overcast skies gives you roughly 3–4 hours of light. Two consecutive cloudy days means dim output.

**The Verdict:** **Buy it if** you need dependable, bright solar lighting for a driveway or yard and can live with reduced winter runtime. **Skip it if** you require year-round all-night performance in low-sun climates.
