If you've owned a DeWalt DW735 for any length of time, you already know the stock straight-knife cutterhead is the weak link. It's loud enough to require hearing protection, dulls quickly on plywood, and leaves snipe on your best boards. The FINDBUYTOOL 13-inch helical cutterhead exists to fix all three. The question is whether a $200-ish aftermarket upgrade actually delivers. After digging through 225 ratings and owner reports, here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the assembled helical cutterhead — a machined aluminum body about 13 inches wide — with 40 pre-installed carbide inserts arranged in a spiral pattern. Each insert is a square, so you get four cutting edges per insert. The package includes the replacement bearing and the necessary mounting hardware for the DW735, DW735X, and DW735XE. Note that this is not a complete planer conversion kit for other brands — it's specifically machined for DeWalt's 735 series.

**Performance and real-world use**

The dominant feedback from owners is that this head transforms the planer's personality. The spiral insert geometry shears the wood rather than chopping it, which produces a noticeably smoother finish — several reviewers noted they could skip sanding on cherry and maple after running boards through. The noise reduction is the second most praised benefit. One long-term owner put it plainly: *"I can actually talk to my wife while the planer is running now. Night and day difference."* That's consistent with the physics — helical heads distribute cutting forces across more inserts, reducing the percussive bang of straight knives.

The carbide inserts handle figured wood and even light passes on plywood without the tear-out you'd expect from the stock head. When an edge dulls, you rotate the insert 90 degrees — no sharpening, no resetting knives, no aftermarket blade alignment jigs. That's a genuine cost saving over time, since replacement inserts run about a dollar each in bulk.

**Where it falls short**

The most consistent criticism is installation difficulty. This is not a 20-minute swap. You'll need to remove the planer's gearbox, carefully set the bearing depth, and torque each of the 40 inserts to spec — usually around 15-18 inch-pounds, though the included instructions are sparse. One reviewer summarized the experience: *"Took me two hours and a YouTube video. Doable, but don't rush it."* A smaller number of buyers reported receiving a unit with a slightly loose insert seat or a minor burr on the body — worth inspecting all inserts before you install. If one is loose, it'll chatter and leave a groove pattern in your work.

**Who should buy this**

This upgrade is for the woodworker who uses their DW735 at least a few times a month and values finish quality and hearing preservation. If you're a hobbyist who planes a dozen boards a year, the stock head is probably fine and the installation hassle isn't worth it. But if you're building furniture, doing production runs, or just hate the stock head's noise, this is the single best upgrade you can make to a 735.

**FAQ**

**Will this fit my DW735X?**
Yes. The FINDBUYTOOL head is machined for the DW735, DW735X, and DW735XE — they all share the same cutterhead interface. The included bearing and hardware match the stock configuration.

**Do I need to replace the inserts often?**
No. Each carbide insert has four edges. Under normal hobbyist use, you'll get months per edge. When all four are dull, you replace the insert — roughly $40 for a full set of 40, compared to the cost of sharpening straight knives repeatedly.

**Does it eliminate snipe?**
It reduces snipe significantly because the spiral cut puts less downward pressure on the board ends, but it doesn't eliminate it entirely. You'll still get minor snipe on long boards unless you use a snipe-cutting jig or feed boards end-to-end.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you own a DW735 and want dramatically quieter operation, better finish quality, and an end to knife sharpening. The installation is a weekend-morning project, not a quick swap, but the payoff is immediate.

**Skip it if** you're not comfortable disassembling your planer's gearbox, or if you only plane a few boards per year — the stock head will do fine for that workload.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The machining quality and carbide insert system are excellent for the price, and the noise reduction alone justifies the upgrade for frequent users. The installation learning curve and occasional QC inconsistency on insert seats keep it from a perfect score.
