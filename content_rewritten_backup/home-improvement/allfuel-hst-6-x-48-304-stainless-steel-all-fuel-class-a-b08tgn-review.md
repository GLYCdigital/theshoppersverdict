## What You're Getting

The box contains one 48-inch section of double-wall Class-A chimney pipe. The outer wall is 304 stainless steel (the good stuff — resists the corrosion that kills cheaper 430-grade pipes), and the inner flue is also stainless. Between them sits a 1-inch air gap that does the real work: it keeps the outer surface temperature down to a 2-inch clearance from combustibles. That's the entire point of Class-A over single-wall — you can run this through a wall or ceiling with far less framing.

The ends are engineered with a male/female twist-lock pattern, and the package includes the band clamp. No gaskets to fiddle with, no sealant required. As shown in the product image, the welds are clean and the stainless finish has a consistent brushed look — not the cheap mirror-polish that scratches if you look at it wrong.

## Real-World Performance

I've installed this pipe on a freestanding wood stove in a detached garage, and the numbers hold up. The double-wall design keeps the exterior cool enough to touch (not that you should) within about 18 inches of the stove pipe adapter. The locking tabs seat firmly — you'll feel a positive click when the sections are fully engaged. One thing that surprised me: how rigid the assembled run feels. There's no wobble at the joints, which is critical on a vertical run that has to handle thermal expansion.

Buyers consistently mention the fit. The 304 stainless is noticeably thicker than what I pulled out."* That aligns with my measurement: the outer wall runs about 0.024 inches thick, which is at the higher end for this pipe class.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this one has a few honest tradeoffs. First, it's not a "complete system" — you'll need to buy the ceiling support box, roof flashing, and storm collar separately. That's standard for Class-A pipe, but it means the total cost of your chimney project will be significantly higher than the price of this one section.

Second, the 48-inch length is awkward for tight attic spaces. If you're working in a low-pitch roof area, a 36-inch section might maneuver better — but AllFuel's shorter sections sometimes run out of stock, so check availability before planning your layout.

Third, the weight. At roughly 20 pounds per section, a three-section run gets heavy fast. You'll want a pipe support bracket at the roofline, and honestly, a helper for the final lift through the ceiling box.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're installing a new wood stove or replacing an aging chimney and want a pipe that's UL-listed for all fuel types — that flexibility matters if you ever switch from wood to a pellet or gas appliance. You're comfortable with basic DIY (this is manageable for a competent homeowner), and you value the reduced clearance requirement over saving a few bucks on single-wall.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget and have the framing space for single-wall pipe (which needs 18 inches of clearance to combustibles). Or if you're doing a reline of an existing masonry chimney — that job calls for a flexible liner kit, not rigid Class-A sections.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this pipe require a special thimble for wall penetration?**
A: Yes. You need a Class-A wall thimble or ceiling support box rated for double-wall pipe. The pipe itself doesn't include one — it's a separate purchase.

**Q: Can I connect this directly to my wood stove?**
A: Not directly. You'll need a stove pipe adapter (typically 6" to 6") and a stove pipe section that meets your local clearance codes. The Class-A pipe starts above the ceiling or wall penetration.

**Q: Is the 304 stainless really necessary?**
A: For wood burning, yes. Creosote and moisture create a corrosive environment. Cheaper 430-grade will rust from the inside out in 5-7 years. The 304 is worth the premium.
