Most door reinforcement kits are a half-measure. They beef up the strike plate or add a longer hinge screw and call it a day. The EXPR Premium Door Install Kit takes a different approach — it wraps the door itself in steel. As shown in the product image, this isn't a discreet add-on. It's a visible, industrial-strength upgrade that secures both faces of the door with interlocking brackets. Here's what 229 Amazon reviewers at a 4.6 average actually think after installing it.

**What's in the box**

The kit ships with two heavy-gauge steel brackets — one for each side of the door — plus a full set of Grade 8 mounting bolts, washers, and a drill guide template. Unlike cheaper kits that expect you to eyeball hole placement, the included guide keeps your drill bit perpendicular to the door face. That matters more than you'd think. A slightly angled hole weakens the bolt's hold and can crack the door skin. The hardware is all zinc-plated, so rust isn't a concern even in humid climates.

**Real-world performance**

Installation takes about 30 minutes if you're comfortable with a drill. The kit is designed for standard 1-3/8\" and 1-3/4\" residential doors — which covers most interior and exterior doors in American homes. Once mounted, the brackets create a continuous steel channel around the door's edge. This distributes force across both sides rather than concentrating it on the hinge screws or strike plate.

Buyers consistently highlight the security upgrade. One reviewer wrote, "I installed these after a break-in attempt bent my old hinges. The door now feels like it's part of a bank vault." Another noted, "The through-bolts make a huge difference — the door doesn't flex at all when I kick-test it." The kit also works well for rental properties or landlords looking to harden entry doors without replacing the entire unit.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest gotcha is the 1\" hole saw requirement. The kit doesn't include one, and if you don't own it, that's an extra $15-20 purchase. You can use a spade bit, but a hole saw gives a cleaner edge that the bracket caps cover properly.

Second, this is permanent. Once you drill through the door, there's no going back. If you're renting or might replace the door later, factor in the cost of a new door slab. Some reviewers also mentioned the brackets are visible and can look "industrial" — fine for a workshop or back door, but possibly overkill for a formal front entry.

**Who should buy this**

- **Homeowners with vulnerable entry doors** — if your door has hollow-core construction or weak hinges, this kit is the single most effective upgrade short of replacing the door.
- **Rental property owners** — a $40 kit is cheaper than a door replacement after a forced entry. Install it on all exterior doors.
- **Anyone living in high-crime areas** — the visual deterrent alone is worth it. Most criminals move on when they see steel brackets.

**Who should skip it**

- **Renters** — unless your landlord approves, you'll lose your security deposit over the holes.
- **People with metal or fiberglass doors** — the kit is designed for wood and composite doors. The drilling method won't work cleanly on steel.
- **DIY beginners** — if you've never used a hole saw, practice on scrap wood first. A botched hole ruins the door.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this kit work on interior doors?**
A: Yes, but it's overkill. Use it on exterior doors or rooms with valuables. Interior doors rarely need this level of reinforcement.

**Q: Will the brackets interfere with door closers or hinges?**
A: No. The brackets mount on the door face, not the hinge side. Standard door closers and hinges clear them without modification.

**Q: Can I paint the brackets to match my door?**
A: Yes. Lightly sand the zinc coating, apply a metal primer, then paint. Just avoid painting the mating surfaces where the brackets interlock.
