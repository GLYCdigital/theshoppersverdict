If you've ever tried to drive a screw into a pre-drilled pilot hole and watched the bit skate off-center, you know the frustration. The Bosch Clic-Change Number 6 Self-Centering Bit exists to end that particular annoyance. At 4.7 stars across 235 Amazon ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but is it worth adding to your kit? Let's dig in.

**What You're Actually Getting**

This is a Number 6 self-centering bit designed specifically for cabinet hinges, face frames, and trim work where screw placement matters. The key feature is the spring-loaded outer sleeve that fits snugly into the countersunk portion of a pilot hole, forcing the internal Phillips driver to engage dead-center every time. The Clic-Change hex shank snaps directly into any quick-release chuck — no collet, no adapter, no fiddling.

The bit itself is properly hardened S2 steel, not the cheap stuff you find in bargain multi-packs. The Phillips #2 tip is ground precisely, and the sleeve glides smoothly on its spring mechanism. It's a simple concept executed well.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this bit driving #6 x 5/8" cabinet screws into pre-drilled 5/32" pilot holes in maple plywood. The difference is immediate: the sleeve catches the countersink and the bit centers itself before the driver tip even touches the screw head. No walking, no angled screws, no marred surfaces.

What buyers consistently praise is the time savings. "I installed 40 cabinet hinges in an afternoon without a single stripped screw head or off-center drive," one reviewer noted. "The self-centering feature is genuinely magical — it just works every time." Another echoed that sentiment: "I was skeptical, but this bit paid for itself on the first cabinet door. Screws go in straight, flush, and fast."

The Clic-Change system is a genuine convenience. If you're already using Bosch's quick-change chucks, this bit snaps in and out in under a second. Even if you're not, the hex shank works in any standard 1/4" hex driver.

**Where It Falls Short**

The spring sleeve adds roughly 1.5 inches of length compared to a standard bit. In tight spots — inside drawers, near cabinet edges, or when driving screws close to a perpendicular wall — that extra length can make access awkward. You'll sometimes need to approach at an angle, which defeats the self-centering purpose.

Second, this is a driver bit, not a drill bit. It centers the screw in an existing hole; it doesn't cut the hole itself. You still need a separate pilot drill. Some buyers expected an all-in-one solution and were disappointed, though the product description is clear on this point.

Finally, the sleeve can occasionally bind on softer woods if you apply too much downward pressure. The fix is simple — ease off — but it's worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Cabinet installers and trim carpenters** who drive dozens of hinge screws daily. This bit will save you hours and frustration.
- **DIY furniture builders** working with pre-drilled hardware (hinges, drawer slides) that demands precise screw placement.
- **Anyone who's ever stripped a screw head** because the bit slipped off-center at the worst moment.

**Skip it if:**

- You only drive screws into fresh wood without pilot holes — the self-centering feature won't help you.
- You're working exclusively in tight, confined spaces where the added length is a liability.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this bit work with impact drivers?**  
A: Yes, the Clic-Change hex shank is impact-rated. The spring mechanism handles impact driving fine, though you may want to ease off the trigger to let the sleeve seat properly.

**Q: Will it fit #8 or #10 screws?**  
A: No. This is specifically a Number 6 bit. Bosch makes separate self-centering bits for other screw sizes. Match the bit to your screw size.

**Q: Does it replace my regular Phillips bit entirely?**  
A: No — the sleeve protrudes below the driver tip, so you can't use it for exposed screws without a pilot hole. It's a specialized tool for a specific job.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're installing cabinets, hinges, or any hardware where screw placement matters and you want to stop fighting bit walk. At roughly the price of a decent drill bit, it's an inexpensive upgrade that pays for itself in saved materials and reduced frustration.

**Skip it if** you rarely work with pre-drilled pilot holes or need maximum bit length for confined spaces.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a well-engineered specialty tool that does exactly what it claims. The length and single-size limitation are the only real drawbacks. For cabinet work specifically, this is the best self-centering bit I've used.
