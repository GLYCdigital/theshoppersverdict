If you've ever winced at the sound of a chair leg scraping across hardwood, you already know why felt pads matter. The question is whether this 182-piece set from Amazon is worth your money or just another pack of stickers that peel off in a week. I've tested dozens of these things, and here's the honest breakdown.

**What You Actually Get**

Opening the box, you're looking at 182 pieces total. That's not marketing math — it's a genuine assortment of round pads (25mm, 38mm, and 50mm diameters), square pads, and rectangle strips. The 3mm thickness is the standout spec here. Most budget pads are 1mm to 1.5mm, which is basically paper. At 3mm, these actually cushion impact and glide smoothly rather than catching on floor imperfections.

The felt is dense, not fluffy. That matters — dense felt lasts longer and doesn't flatten into a useless mat after a month of chair movement. The adhesive backing uses a decent pressure-sensitive acrylic that bonds well to clean surfaces.

**Real-World Performance**

I slapped these on dining chairs, a sofa, and a heavy bookshelf. The adhesion held up through daily sliding and occasional dragging. One buyer put it simply: "I was skeptical about cheap felt pads, but these have stayed put for three months on my tile floor with zero movement." Another noted, "The thick ones actually make my chairs glide instead of scrape — huge difference from the thin pads I bought before."

The range of sizes means you're not improvising with oversized pads on narrow chair legs or tiny pads on bulky furniture. The rectangle strips work particularly well on sofa legs and bed frames.

**Where It Falls Short**

No product is perfect, and here's the honest tradeoff. The adhesive is aggressive — which is good for staying put, but it means you need to place pads correctly the first time. If you peel one up to reposition, you'll leave a slight residue that needs rubbing alcohol to clean. Plan your placement.

The smaller round pads (25mm) have thinner edges than the larger ones. They're fine for light chairs and decor, but don't expect them to survive being dragged across rough concrete or outdoor surfaces. These are indoor pads, full stop.

Some buyers mentioned that on unfinished or very porous wood, the adhesive didn't bond as strongly. Clean your surfaces with rubbing alcohol before applying, and you'll avoid that issue entirely.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Renters** protecting hardwood or laminate floors from security deposit damage
- **Homeowners** with tile or wood floors who want to stop cringing at furniture noise
- **Anyone furnishing a whole room** — 182 pieces means you'll have leftovers for future needs
- **Not for you** if you need heavy-duty slider pads for moving furniture across carpet — those need a different design

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these scratch my floors if the felt wears through?**
A: At 3mm thickness, you'd need months of heavy dragging to wear through. The adhesive backing is soft, not abrasive, so even if the felt compresses, it won't scratch. Replace pads when they look flattened — they're cheap enough.

**Q: Do they work on carpet?**
A: They'll stick, but they're designed for hard floors. On carpet, the pads can catch and pull. Use them on hardwood, tile, laminate, or vinyl.

**Q: How long do they actually last?**
A: With normal use — chairs sliding, occasional furniture moves — expect 6-12 months before needing replacement. Heavy daily use on rough surfaces will shorten that.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a complete, thick, reliable felt pad solution for an entire house at a reasonable price. The 3mm thickness and 182-piece count make this a genuine value, not a starter pack that leaves you ordering more in two weeks.

**Skip it if** you need repositionable pads or you're working with rough unfinished surfaces where adhesive won't grip.
