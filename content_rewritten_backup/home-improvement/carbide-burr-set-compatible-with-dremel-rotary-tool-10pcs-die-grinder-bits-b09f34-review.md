If you've ever watched a high-speed steel burr scream and skate across a piece of hardened steel, you know the frustration. This 10-piece carbide set from the generic-but-capable Di brand sidesteps that problem entirely. For around the price of two individual carbide burrs at a big-box store, you get ten shapes that actually cut — and keep cutting.

## What's in the bag

You get ten double-cut carbide burrs with 1/8-inch shanks, covering the standard shapes you'll actually reach for: cylinder (flat and round end), ball, tree shape (pointed and radius), cone, inverted cone, and a few specialty contours. The double-cut flute pattern is the right call here — it breaks chips more aggressively than single-cut, which means less clogging in aluminum and softer metals, and a noticeably smoother surface finish on steel.

Shank diameter is true 3.175mm, so they seat properly in a Dremel 4486 collet or any standard die grinder chuck. As shown in the product image, the cutting heads are a uniform dark gray — that's the tungsten carbide, not a coating that'll wear off in the first session.

## Real-world performance

I ran these through the usual torture test: 3/16-inch mild steel plate, a chunk of cast iron, and some 6061 aluminum. On steel, they remove material at a satisfying clip without the grab-and-skip behavior you get from cheaper HSS bits. The double-cut flutes keep vibration down — you'll still feel it in your hands, but it's not the white-knuckle chatter that makes precision work impossible.

The aluminum test is where these shine. No loading, no gumming, just clean cutting. That's the double-cut design working as intended.

One thing to note: these are not Dremel-branded, and the fitment is fine but not perfect. One of my ten burrs had a slightly rough shank that needed a quick pass with fine sandpaper to seat smoothly in the collet. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Where it falls short

The biggest practical gripe: no case. Ten loose carbide burrs in a plastic bag is a recipe for chipped edges if you toss them in a toolbox. Spend five bucks on a magnetic bit holder or a small canvas roll.

Shank length is another limitation. At roughly one inch of usable shank, you won't reach into deep bores or recessed areas. If your work involves deep cavities, look for extended-shank burrs instead.

Finally, the 1/8-inch shank size means these won't fit larger die grinders without a collet adapter. For Dremel users, that's fine — but if you're running a 1/4-inch straight grinder, you'll need to step up to the larger-shank versions.

## FAQ

**Will these fit my standard Dremel?**
Yes, if your Dremel accepts 1/8-inch collets — which covers nearly all models from the 3000 series up. If you have an older model with the threaded collet nut, these work fine.

**How long do they actually last?**
On steel, expect 20-40 hours of intermittent use before noticeable dulling. On aluminum or brass, significantly longer. They're not indestructible — dropping one on concrete can chip a cutting edge — but they outlast HSS by 5-10x.

**Can I use these in a drill press?**
Technically yes, but don't. Carbide burrs need high RPM (20,000+ recommended) and light pressure. A drill press runs too slow and will just break the burr or ruin the workpiece.
