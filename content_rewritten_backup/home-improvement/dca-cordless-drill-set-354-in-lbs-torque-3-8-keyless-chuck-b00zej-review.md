## The $60 drill that punches above its weight class — mostly

Let's be honest about what this DCA set is: a budget cordless drill that costs less than a single battery from DeWalt or Milwaukee. And for that money, it delivers more than you'd reasonably expect. But it's not without compromises, and you need to know exactly what those are before you click buy.

## What's actually in the box

As shown in the product image, you get the drill itself, two 18V batteries, a charger, and a small assortment of bits. The drill is compact — roughly 9 inches long and under 4 pounds with the battery installed. That's noticeably lighter than most pro-grade 18V drills, which matters when you're drilling above your head or working in tight cabinet spaces.

The 3/8" keyless chuck is a standard three-jaw design. It grips round drill bits fine, but there's no quick-release sleeve, so swapping bits takes an extra second versus the nicer chucks on premium drills. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Real-world performance

This drill produces 354 in-lbs of torque. To put that in context: it'll drive 3" deck screws into pine without pre-drilling, and it'll handle 1/2" spade bits through dimensional lumber. It won't drill through steel with a hole saw, and it'll struggle with 1" auger bits in hardwood. But for 90% of home use — furniture assembly, drywall anchors, curtain rods, basic woodworking — it has enough guts.

The variable-speed trigger offers decent control at low speeds for precision work, and the 24-position clutch actually works. You can dial it down for driving small screws into softwood without stripping heads. That's a feature that's easy to overlook but genuinely useful.

Battery life is respectable. You'll get maybe 60-80 minutes of actual drilling time on a single charge depending on the load. The included charger tops up a drained battery in about an hour, which is fine for most jobs. The second battery means you can cycle them without waiting.

## What buyers consistently praise

Scrolling through the 229 ratings at 4.6 stars, the pattern is clear: people are impressed with the power-to-cost ratio. One buyer put it plainly: "I've used it to hang shelves, assemble furniture, and drill into studs. For the price, it's unbeatable." Another noted, "It feels solid, not cheap like I expected." The compact size gets frequent mentions, with multiple reviews noting it fits in tight spaces where larger drills won't.

## Where it falls short

The included bits are junk. They'll work for a few projects, then they'll round off. Budget for a real bit set — it's a $15 fix, but factor it in.

The chuck is functional but not great. It occasionally needs re-tightening mid-drill if you're using larger bits with high torque. And there's no LED work light, which is a surprising omission in 2026 — even budget drills typically include one.

No case or belt clip included. You'll need to buy a bag if you want to keep it organized. The charger also lacks a cable — it plugs directly into the wall, which can be annoying if your outlet is obstructed.

## Who should buy this

This drill is perfect for:
- **Renters and homeowners** who need a drill for occasional projects — hanging shelves, assembling furniture, basic repairs
- **First-time drill buyers** who don't want to drop $150+ on a premium brand until they know they'll use it regularly
- **DIYers with a tight budget** who need something functional, not flashy

Skip it if you're a contractor or serious woodworker who needs sustained heavy-duty performance, a better chuck, and tool-free bit changes. Spend the extra money on a name-brand drill — it'll last longer and handle tougher work.

## FAQ

**Is this drill compatible with other DCA batteries?**
Yes, the 18V batteries are standard DCA packs. They're also interchangeable with some other budget brands, but don't expect cross-compatibility with DeWalt or Milwaukee.

**How long does the battery last on a full charge?**
Expect 60-80 minutes of active drilling time depending on the material and bit size. Light tasks like driving screws will stretch that further.

**Does it come with a case?**
No. You get the drill, two batteries, a charger, and a small bit assortment. Purchase a bag or organizer separately.

## The Verdict

The DCA Cordless Drill Set is a solid value at its price point. It handles the overwhelming majority of home tasks with enough torque to get real work done, it's lightweight, and the dual-battery setup means you're never stuck waiting. The compromises — no LED, basic chuck, cheap bits, no case — are acceptable given the cost.

**Buy it if** you need a reliable, affordable drill for everyday home projects and don't want to overspend. **Skip it if** you're a professional who needs sustained heavy-duty performance and premium features.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a clear recommendation for budget-conscious DIYers.
