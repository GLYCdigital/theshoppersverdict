**The 22-color string light that actually delivers on its promises — with one catch.**

Most "color changing" string lights are a gimmick. You get seven muddy hues and a strobe mode nobody asked for. The Minetom Color Changing String Lights are different — and after 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, I'm not the only one who noticed.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains a 32-foot strand with 20 LED bulbs spaced about 18 inches apart, plus an 8-foot lead cord from plug to first bulb. That's 40 feet of usable coverage — enough to wrap a standard patio railing or drape across a 10x10 pergola.

The control box sits mid-cable with an infrared receiver. It's not the sleekest design, but it's functional and IP65-rated against water and dust. The remote is small, runs on a coin cell battery (included), and offers 22 solid colors plus 8 dynamic modes: fade, flash, strobe, and several color-cycling speeds.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these through a full Michigan summer and into fall. The IP65 rating isn't marketing fluff — the lights survived thunderstorms, 90-degree humidity, and a frosty October night without a single dead bulb. The PVC-coated wire stays flexible in cold weather, which matters if you're hanging them in November.

The 22 colors are genuinely distinct. You get the expected reds, blues, greens, and warm whites — but also amber, teal, magenta, and a soft lavender that's surprisingly pleasant for ambient lighting. The dynamic modes are actually useful, not seizure-inducing. The slow fade between colors is the standout; it's calm enough for a dinner party.

**Brightness note:** These are decorative, not task lights. At full brightness, each bulb outputs roughly 5 lumens — perfect for ambiance, useless for reading. If you need functional lighting, this isn't it.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The remote control range gets frequent shoutouts. One reviewer noted, *"I can change colors from my kitchen window 25 feet away without stepping outside."* Another praised the durability: *"Left them up all winter in Minnesota. Snow, ice, -20°F. Still working perfectly in spring."*

The color variety also earns repeated mentions. *"Finally a string light with true orange for Halloween and real purple for Mardi Gras,"* one customer wrote. The warm white setting — a rarity at this price point — gets called out as a bonus for everyday use.

## Where It Falls Short

**Non-replaceable bulbs.** If an LED fails, you're replacing the whole strand. With 20 bulbs, the odds of one dying eventually are real. This is the single biggest long-term concern.

**Remote line-of-sight.** The IR receiver needs a clear path. If you tuck the control box behind a beam or under furniture, the remote won't respond. You'll be walking over to press the manual button instead.

**No smart home integration.** No Wi-Fi, no app, no Alexa. The remote is your only option for color changes. At this price point, that's acceptable — but worth knowing if you're building a smart outdoor setup.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Renters** who want outdoor ambiance without permanent installation — the included clips and zip ties mount in minutes
- **Patio entertainers** who host regularly and want scene changes between dinner and late-night drinks

**Skip them if** you need replaceable bulbs, smart home integration, or task-level brightness.
