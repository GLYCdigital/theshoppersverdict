## The 300° Blind Spot Killer

Most solar security lights cheat. They claim "wide angle" but leave a shadowy corridor right below the fixture. The TECKNET 231 breaks that pattern with three independently adjustable panels that swing and rotate to cover a genuine 300-degree arc. If you've got a garage corner, a side gate, or a back door where two walls meet, this is the unit that actually eliminates the dark zones.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the main light unit with the three LED panels pre-attached, a separate solar panel on a 9-foot cable, mounting hardware, and a user manual. The solar panel is the detachable kind — which is smart, because it means you can mount the panel in direct sun while the light itself goes where you actually need illumination. The unit measures roughly 8.5 inches wide and 4.5 inches tall, so it's compact enough not to scream "security camera" at your neighbors.

## Real-World Performance

**Brightness:** 231 LED beads split across three panels. In full brightness mode, this throws out roughly 600 lumens — enough to light a standard two-car driveway and clearly identify a person at 30 feet. The light color is a cool daylight white (6000K), which is better for security than the warm yellow glow many budget units emit.

**Motion Detection:** The PIR sensor picks up movement at 26 feet and triggers in about half a second. There's a sensitivity dial on the side — crank it to max if you have a narrow walkway, back it off if you're on a busy street where passing cars would set it off constantly. The 20-second auto-dim to 20% brightness after activation is a thoughtful touch — full brightness on every trigger drains the battery fast.

**Battery & Solar:** Here's the honest tradeoff. The battery is a 2200mAh lithium pack, which gives you about 8-10 hours of full-brightness runtime on a full charge. But the solar panel is only 5.5W. In summer, that's fine — you'll get full charge by 2 PM. In winter, or if you're mounting under a covered porch, expect maybe 4-5 hours of full output before it dims to the lower setting. Buyers in the Amazon reviews confirm this: one noted *"works great May through October, but November through February I get about half the runtime."* That's physics, not a defect — a bigger panel would've made this a $60 light, not a budget-friendly one.

**Waterproofing:** The IP65 rating holds up. The housing is die-cast aluminum with a rubber gasket around the lens. It's survived rainstorms and snow without condensation forming inside the lens — a common failure point on cheaper units.

## Where It Falls Short

- **Mounting hardware:** The included plastic wall anchors work fine for siding or wood, but they're weak for masonry. If you're drilling into brick or stucco, grab metal anchors before you start.
- **Solar panel cable length:** The 9-foot cable sounds generous, but if your optimal sun spot is around a corner, you might need an extension. It uses a standard barrel connector, so extensions are available, but it's an extra purchase.
- **No dusk-to-dawn sensor:** The light only comes on when motion is detected. If you want ambient low-level lighting all night, this isn't the unit — it's strictly motion-activated.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a corner, entryway, or garage that needs wide-angle coverage and you have direct sun exposure for at least 5-6 hours a day. It's ideal for renters (no hardwiring) and DIYers who want a 30-minute install.

**Skip it if:** You live in a region with long, dark winters, or your mounting location is permanently shaded. In those cases, a hardwired motion light or a unit with a separate, larger solar panel will serve you better.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the panels be adjusted after mounting?**
A: Yes — each panel pivots vertically and horizontally. Loosen the thumb screws, reposition, and re-tighten. It takes about 10 seconds per panel.

**Q: How long does the battery last on a full charge?**
A: In full-brightness mode, expect 8-10 hours. In the dimmed 20% mode, it stretches to 20+ hours. Real-world runtime depends on how many times the motion sensor triggers.

**Q: Does it work in freezing temperatures?**
A: Yes, down to about -4°F. The lithium battery handles cold, but expect reduced capacity — that's true of all lithium batteries in winter.
