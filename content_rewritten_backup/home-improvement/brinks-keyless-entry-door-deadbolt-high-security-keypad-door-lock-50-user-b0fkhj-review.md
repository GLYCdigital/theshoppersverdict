**The BRINKS Keyless Entry Deadbolt Is a Workhorse — But It's Not a Smart Lock**

Most keypad deadbolts under $100 are plastic-bodied compromises that feel like toys. The BRINKS Keyless Entry Deadbolt is not that. This is a serious piece of hardware that prioritizes physical security over gimmicks. If you want a deadbolt that's genuinely harder to defeat than a standard key lock, and you don't need app control, this deserves a hard look.

**What's in the Box**

You get the exterior keypad assembly (zinc alloy, not flimsy plastic), the interior housing, a hardened steel deadbolt, two physical keys as a backup, and all mounting hardware. The included strike plate is reinforced — a detail many competitors skip. Installation takes about 20-30 minutes if you have a standard 2-1/8" bore hole. The instructions are clear, and the wiring harness connects with a simple plug, no soldering.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The keypad is responsive. Press a code, and the bolt throws home with a solid, mechanical *thunk* — not the mushy click you get from cheaper units. The backlighting is bright enough to see in pitch dark, and it activates on touch. Code entry accepts 4-12 digits, which is flexible: you can use a short code for family or a longer one for contractors you want to expire mentally.

The auto-lock feature is where this lock shines. It's adjustable from 10 to 99 seconds. Set it to 30 seconds, and you'll never wonder if you locked the door again. In testing, it fired consistently every single time.

One detail that matters: the lock has a **one-time use code** function. Need to let a dog walker in once? Generate a code, and it self-deletes after first use. That's a feature usually found on locks twice this price.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews is well earned. The recurring themes are battery life and build quality. One reviewer noted: *"I've had this for 8 months on a high-traffic door, and the battery indicator still shows full. My previous smart lock needed charging every 6 weeks."* Another wrote: *"This thing feels like it belongs on a commercial door. The zinc housing is heavy and solid."*

The physical key override is also a selling point. In an era where many keypad locks offer flimsy "emergency" keys, BRINKS includes a proper, machined key that works smoothly.

**Where It Falls Short**

This is not a smart lock. There's no Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth, no app, no integration with Alexa or Google Home. If you want to check lock status from your phone or get notified when the kids get home, this isn't the product. That's a deliberate tradeoff — fewer electronics means fewer failure points — but it's a hard limitation.

The keypad beeps on every press, and while you can disable the press tones, the lock/unlock confirmation beep **cannot be silenced**. If you're a light sleeper with the bedroom door near the front door, this will wake you up at 2 AM when someone comes home.

Installation is standard for a deadbolt but not universal. If your door has a non-standard bore hole or a metal door with odd prep, you'll need to drill or file. Renters should check with their landlord first.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Homeowners who want real security** without smart lock complexity. The ANSI Grade 2 rating means it's certified against forced entry, unlike many unrated competitors.
- **Landlords or property managers** managing multiple units — the 50-code capacity and one-time codes are perfect for turnover.
- **Anyone who's frustrated with smart lock battery drain** and just wants a reliable keypad that works for a year plus on one set of AAs.

**Skip it if** you need app connectivity, remote access, or voice assistant control. For that, look at the Schlage Encode or August Wi-Fi models, but expect to pay 2-3 times more.

**FAQ: Real Shopper Questions**

**Q: Does this work with a standard door prep?**
A: Yes, for 99% of residential doors. It needs a 2-1/8" bore hole and 1" edge bore, which is the US standard. If you're replacing an existing deadbolt, it's a direct swap.

**Q: How long do the batteries actually last?**
A: BRINKS claims up to 1 year. Real-world reviews average 8-10 months on a busy door. The low-battery warning is clear and gives you about 2 weeks of warning. The physical key works even if batteries are completely dead.

**Q: Can I use it with a smart doorbell or home security system?**
A: No. This is a standalone lock. It won't integrate with Ring, SimpliSafe, or any smart hub. It's purely mechanical-keypad operation.
