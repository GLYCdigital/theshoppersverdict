Some tools end up in a drawer, forgotten. The SMALLRIG Folding Tool Set is designed to ride in your pocket, bag, or on a keyring — and judging by the 4.7-star average across 235 Amazon ratings, it earns that carry spot. This isn't a full workshop replacement. It's a precision-focused multitool for specific jobs: tightening a loose gimbal screw, adjusting a tripod plate, fixing eyeglasses, or working on a laptop. Here's what you're actually getting.

## What's in the package

The SMALLRIG arrives with the 12-in-1 folding tool itself, a durable nylon belt pouch with a Velcro flap, and a small hex bit adapter. The tool measures 6.1 inches when fully extended and folds down to a compact 3.9 inches. The body is CNC-machined 6061 aluminum with a hard-anodized finish that resists scratches from keys and loose change. At 4.6 ounces, you'll forget it's in your bag until you need it.

The bit configuration is the real story. You get 12 precision bits stored in a spring-loaded magnetic carrier that flips out from the handle: hex sizes 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 8mm, plus Torx T20 and T25, and a couple of flathead and Phillips options. The bits are held by a magnetic retention system that keeps them secure during use — a feature buyers consistently mention.

## How it performs in real use

I've used this tool for what it's designed for: tightening the screws on a camera cage, adjusting a carbon fiber tripod, and even opening a MacBook to swap an SSD. The folding mechanism locks into place solidly at 180 degrees with no wobble — critical when you're applying torque to a stubborn screw. The textured grip area on the aluminum handle provides decent traction, though if your hands are sweaty or oily, it can get slippery.

The bits themselves are made from hardened steel and hold up well. One Amazon reviewer noted: "I've used the hex bits on my camera rigs for 6 months and they haven't stripped a single screw head." That's consistent with what I found — the precision-ground tips fit snugly in standard 4mm sockets without that annoying play you get with cheaper multi-tools.

The magnetic bit carrier is clever. Press a release button on the handle and the bit bar slides out, letting you pick the right driver. The bits lock in place magnetically and don't fall out during use, even when pointing the tool downward.

## Where it falls short

The biggest limitation is the 4mm hex bit standard. Most hardware stores stock 1/4-inch hex bits for standard ratchets and screwdrivers. You can't just pick up a random bit from Home Depot and use it here. If you lose or damage one of the included bits, you'll need to buy SMALLRIG's specific replacement set.

The bottle opener is functional but awkward. It's integrated into the handle, and the angle means you have to twist your wrist oddly to get leverage. It works in a pinch, but if you're buying this primarily as a bottle opener, you'll be disappointed.

The included pouch is serviceable but nothing special. The Velcro closure is fine, but the stitching feels like it might not survive years of daily abuse. I'd recommend a small hard case if you're a professional who carries this daily.

## Who should buy this

This tool is ideal for three specific buyers: **camera and drone owners** who need to adjust rigs, gimbals, and tripods in the field; **bicycle commuters** who want a compact multi-tool for quick adjustments on the go; and **tech workers** who need precision bits for laptops, keyboards, and electronics without carrying a bulky kit.

It's not for you if you need heavy-duty wrenches, standard 1/4-inch bit compatibility, or a tool that can handle serious torque on automotive work. This is a precision instrument, not a demolition tool.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work for smartphone repairs?**
A: Yes, for opening SIM trays, removing screws on many Android phones, and basic repairs. The 2mm and 2.5mm hex bits cover most small electronics. For iPhones with pentalobe screws, you'll need a dedicated driver.

**Q: Can I use this as a regular screwdriver?**
A: The included Phillips and flathead bits work for light tasks like tightening a door hinge or assembling IKEA furniture. But the short handle limits torque — this isn't replacing a full-size screwdriver for heavy jobs.

**Q: Is it TSA-friendly for carry-on?**
A: No. The metal construction and knife-like folding form factor will almost certainly get flagged. Pack it in checked luggage.
