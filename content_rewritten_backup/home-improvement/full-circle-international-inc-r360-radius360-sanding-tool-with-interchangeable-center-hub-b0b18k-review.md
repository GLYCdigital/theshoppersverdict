Sanding curved surfaces has always been the awkward stepchild of woodworking. You've got your orbital sander for flat panels, your detail sander for corners, and then... what? A rolled-up piece of paper and a prayer? The Full Circle International R360 Radius360 aims to close that gap, and with 235 ratings averaging 4.7 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in workshops. Here's what you need to know before adding it to your cart.

## What You Actually Get

The Radius360 is a hand sanding tool built around a flexible curved face that wraps around contours, coves, and rounded edges. The key feature is the **interchangeable center hub** — you can swap sanding surfaces mid-project without switching tools. The package includes the main body, a hook-and-loop attachment system, and enough starter sheets to get you through your first project. It's compact enough to toss in a tool bag, and the grip is shaped to sit comfortably in your palm for extended use.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature delivers. The Radius360's curved design wraps around quarter-round molding, cabinet edges, and even tight inside radii that would shred standard paper. The hook-and-loop backing holds sheets securely — no slipping mid-stroke, which matters when you're working toward a smooth finish on a visible edge.

The interchangeable hub system is genuinely useful. Pop off one grit, snap in another, and you're back to work in seconds. No peeling sticky-backed paper, no hunting for a different block. The body itself is injection-molded plastic, but it's rigid where it needs to be and flexible along the sanding face. It won't replace a power sander for large surfaces — that's not its job — but for the detail work that eats up your weekend, it's noticeably faster than wrestling with loose paper.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the reviews, two themes dominate. First, the ergonomics. One reviewer noted, *"I have arthritis in my hands and this is the only sanding tool I can use for more than 10 minutes without pain."* That's a meaningful endorsement from someone who's clearly tested alternatives.

Second, the contour capability. Another buyer wrote, *"Used it on stair spindles and it hugged the curves perfectly — something my orbital sander just couldn't do without gouging."* That's the exact use case this tool was designed for, and it's delivering.

## Where It Falls Short

The proprietary sheet requirement is the biggest friction point. The Radius360 uses its own replacement sheets — standard 1/4-sheet sandpaper won't fit the hook-and-loop pattern. You're locked into buying Full Circle's refills, which cost more than generic paper. If you're a casual DIYer who sands twice a year, that's a minor annoyance. If you're a pro burning through sheets weekly, factor that recurring cost into your decision.

Also, it's a hand tool. For flat surfaces, a random orbital sander will still be faster. This is a complement to your power tools, not a replacement for them.

## Who Should Buy This

**The furniture refinisher** — you're working with curved legs, carved details, and molding that power sanders can't touch safely. **The DIY trim installer** — you need clean edges on baseboards and casing without hand cramps. **The hobbyist woodworker** — you want a dedicated contour-sanding solution that lives in your top drawer, ready when you need it.

Skip it if you only sand flat plywood panels or if you're on a tight budget and can't justify the proprietary refills.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use regular sandpaper with this tool?**
A: No. The hook-and-loop system is designed for Radius360-specific sheets. Standard 1/4-sheet paper won't stay attached securely.

**Q: How does it handle really tight inside corners?**
A: The flexible face conforms to gentle curves and coves well, but for sharp 90-degree inside corners, you'll still want a detail sander or a folded piece of paper.

**Q: Is the interchangeable hub worth the extra cost?**
A: If you switch grits frequently — say, from 80 to 220 on the same piece — yes. It cuts the swap time to seconds. If you only use one grit per project, the standard block will do.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you do any amount of curved or contoured sanding and are tired of the loose-paper dance. The ergonomics, the quick-swap hub, and the effective contour performance justify the price for anyone who values their time and their hands.

**Skip it if** your sanding work is all flat surfaces or you're unwilling to commit to the proprietary refill system.
