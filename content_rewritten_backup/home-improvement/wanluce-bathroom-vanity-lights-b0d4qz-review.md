Let’s be blunt: most vanity lights under $100 are cheap stamped metal with plastic shades that yellow in a year. The Wanluce bathroom vanity light isn't that. But it's also not a premium designer fixture. Here's where it actually lands after digging through 220 ratings and the specs.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the light bar, a frosted glass shades (count matches the configuration you order — 3-light, 4-light, or 5-light), mounting hardware, wire nuts, and a hardwired LED driver. Note: this is a **hardwire-only fixture**. There's no plug-in cord option. If you're replacing an existing vanity light, you're fine. If you're adding a light where none exists, budget for an electrician.

The housing is aluminum with a brushed finish — not the hollow, tinny steel you see in this price band. The shades are actual glass, not acrylic. That matters because acrylic scratches when you wipe it down and discolors under heat. Glass doesn't.

## Performance and Real-World Use

**The dimming is the headline feature.** The integrated LED driver works with standard TRIAC dimmers. That's rare below $150. At 3000K color temperature, the light is warm but not yellow — good for makeup application and shaving without the clinical glare of 4000K+.

Brightness is solid: a 3-light bar puts out roughly 2400 lumens, which is adequate for a single-basin vanity. The 5-light version hits around 4000 lumens — that's enough to light a double vanity without supplementary ceiling fixtures.

The damp-rating is legitimate. The glass shades seat against silicone gaskets, and the wiring compartment has a sealed backing plate. This is safe for direct installation above a sink, not just in a powder room.

## What Buyers Praise

The consistent themes across reviews aren't about looks — they're about installation and reliability. One verified buyer wrote: "Took me 20 minutes to swap out my old 1990s fixture. The wires are clearly labeled and the bracket lines up with standard junction boxes." Another noted: "I've had mine six months near a steamy shower. No fogging inside the glass, no rust spots."

People also repeatedly mention the shade removal. You twist the glass off by hand — no set screws, no tools. That's a small thing that becomes a big thing when you're cleaning soap scum off the third shade in a row.

## Where It Falls Short

The mounting plate is the weak point. It's narrower than the fixture body, and it won't cover oversized or non-standard junction box cutouts. If your old light had a large round box or someone drywalled over a rectangular box, you'll need a patch plate or drywall repair before installing. Several reviewers mentioned this specifically.

Also, the included bulbs are LED boards, not replaceable bulbs. When the driver fails — and eventually it will, as all LED drivers do — you're replacing the whole fixture, not a $4 bulb. At this price point, that's an acceptable tradeoff, but it's worth knowing.

One more nitpick: the finish is fingerprint-prone. The brushed aluminum shows smudges easily, and you'll be wiping it more than you'd like in a bathroom environment.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're replacing an existing hardwired vanity light, want dimming without paying designer prices, or need a damp-rated fixture that won't rust in a steamy bathroom.

**Skip it if** you're a renter (no plug-in option), you have an unusual junction box that needs heavy patching, or you want a fixture with replaceable bulbs rather than integrated LEDs.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with a standard dimmer switch?**
A: Yes, but it needs a TRIAC/ELV dimmer. Standard toggle or basic rotary dimmers work fine. If you have a smart dimmer, check that it supports TRIAC dimming.

**Q: Is it really damp-rated, or just "dry location"?**
A: It's genuinely damp-rated. The gaskets and sealed driver are rated for humid environments. I'd still avoid direct spray from a showerhead, but steam and splashes are fine.

**Q: How hard is installation for a DIYer?**
A: If you're comfortable wiring a basic switch or outlet, this is straightforward. Black to black, white to white, ground to ground. The bracket aligns with standard 3.5-inch junction boxes. If you're not comfortable with wiring, hire someone — the damp-rating doesn't excuse bad connections.
