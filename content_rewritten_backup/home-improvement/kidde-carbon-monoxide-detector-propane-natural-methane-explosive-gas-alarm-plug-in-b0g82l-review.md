Most homes have a carbon monoxide detector. Most don't have an explosive gas detector. The Kidde plug-in unit that combines both is the kind of product that makes you wonder why it isn't standard equipment — especially if you cook with gas, heat with propane, or own a garage where a fuel leak could go unnoticed.

This isn't just another CO alarm. It's a single device that watches for carbon monoxide *and* explosive gases — propane, natural gas, and methane — which is a meaningful upgrade for anyone with gas appliances. Let's break down whether it earns a spot on your wall.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the detector unit itself, a battery backup (typically a 9V alkaline), and a quick-start guide. As shown in the product image, it's a compact plug-in unit with a digital display that cycles between CO concentration and gas readings. There's no mounting hardware included because it's designed to sit in an outlet — though the backup battery means it keeps functioning if the power goes out.

## Real-World Performance

Here's what matters: the sensor responds. Multiple buyers report the alarm going off during actual gas leaks. One reviewer described it catching a propane leak from a grill connection that had gone unnoticed for months. Another noted the voice alert — "Warning, carbon monoxide detected" — is a step up from the generic beeping of older detectors, especially for waking up heavy sleepers or alerting kids.

The digital display gives you real-time feedback, which is genuinely useful. You can watch the CO reading climb as you preheat the oven or see the gas level spike if a burner doesn't ignite. That's information, not just noise. The 85dB alarm is loud enough to hear from another floor, and it meets UL standards for both CO and gas detection.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dual-sensor capability is the runaway favorite. "I have a gas water heater and a gas stove," one verified buyer wrote, "and I was tired of needing two separate detectors. This does both." Another praised the backup battery: "Power went out during a storm and the detector kept working — that's when you realize the battery backup matters."

The voice alerts and digital display also get consistent mentions. People like knowing *why* the alarm is going off, not just that it's going off.

## Where It Falls Short

The plug-in form factor is the biggest gripe. It sits in the outlet and blocks the second socket — on a duplex outlet, you're losing a plug. You can use a short extension cord, but that's an extra purchase and a minor trip hazard.

False alarms are the second issue. The gas sensor is sensitive, which is good for safety but annoying when it reacts to cooking fumes, spray cleaners, or even a dusty furnace kicking on. Several reviewers report occasional nuisance alarms in kitchens. Mount it away from the stove and vents if possible.

Finally, there's no smart connectivity. If you want phone alerts or integration with a home automation system, this isn't the detector for you. It's a standalone unit, and the lack of interconnectivity with other Kidde alarms means only the one in the affected room will sound.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Gas stove and oven owners** — this is the primary audience. If you cook with natural gas, you're exposed to both CO and potential gas leaks. One unit covers both.
- **Propane users** — RV owners, grill enthusiasts, or anyone with a propane tank indoors or nearby. Propane is heavier than air, so placement matters — install it low (near the floor) for propane detection, higher for natural gas.
- **Anyone with a gas furnace or water heater** — these run silently in basements and closets. A leak can go undetected for months.

Skip it if you have an all-electric home with no gas appliances — you only need a standard CO detector, not the gas sensing feature.

## FAQ

**Q: Where should I mount this detector?**
A: For carbon monoxide, install at head height or higher (CO rises). For propane, install lower — propane is heavier than air and pools near the floor. Natural gas and methane rise, so upper placement works for those. Since this detects both, a middle-ground height (4-5 feet) is a reasonable compromise, but prioritize the gas you're most concerned about.

**Q: Does this replace my existing CO detector?**
A: Yes, if you're replacing a plug-in or battery CO alarm. It does everything a standard CO detector does, plus gas detection. Just make sure you have at least one CO detector on each floor of your home.

**Q: How long does the sensor last?**
A: The unit has a 10-year end-of-life indicator, which is standard. When it expires, the alarm will chirp periodically and display "END" — replace the whole unit at that point.
