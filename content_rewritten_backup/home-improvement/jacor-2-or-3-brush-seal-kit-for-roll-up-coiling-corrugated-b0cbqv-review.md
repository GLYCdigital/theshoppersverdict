If you've got a roll-up door that lets in drafts, dust, or the occasional mouse, you already know the problem: standard rubber seals don't conform to the corrugated ridges. The Jacor brush seal kit takes a different approach, and after digging through 235 Amazon ratings (4.7 stars average), it's clear this thing solves a real problem for a lot of people.

## What's Actually in the Kit

Jacor sends you aluminum mounting channels, the brush seal strips, and enough self-tapping screws to mount everything. The flexible polypropylene bristles sit inside the channel and can be trimmed to length with scissors or a utility knife if you need a custom fit. The kit is designed to handle both 2-inch and 3-inch gaps, which makes it more versatile than most single-purpose seals I've seen.

## Real-World Performance

The key advantage here is the bristle design. Unlike solid rubber that only seals where it physically touches, these bristles flex and fill irregular gaps. That matters a lot on corrugated roll-up doors where the surface is wavy by design. One buyer put it plainly: *"Seals the gap between my overhead door and the floor perfectly. Bugs and leaves don't get in anymore."* Another noted, *"Installed on a warehouse door that had a 1.5-inch gap at the bottom. The brush conformed to the uneven concrete better than the rubber seal I had before."*

The aluminum channel is sturdy and doesn't bend when you tighten the screws. Installation is straightforward: measure, cut, position, and drive the included screws. Most reviewers report finishing in under 20 minutes per door. That's faster than most foam or rubber retrofit kits I've reviewed.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

- **Flexibility over rigid seals** — multiple reviewers mention the bristles adapting to warped panels or uneven floors. This is the #1 reason people buy it.
- **Value for the price** — the kit covers a decent length and buyers repeatedly mention it's cheaper than buying two separate 2-inch and 3-inch kits.

## Where It Falls Short

The bristles are about 2 inches long. If your gap is wider than that, you'll need to stack two rows of channel, which doubles the cost and complicates installation. Also, the included screws are on the shorter side — around 1 inch. They work fine on standard 18-gauge steel tracks, but if you have thicker gauge doors or wood mounting surfaces, plan to grab longer screws from your hardware bin. One reviewer complained the screws stripped easily on a heavy commercial door, which is worth knowing before you start.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners with gap-ridden garage doors** — especially if your floor is uneven or your door panels have settled over time.
- **Workshop and shed owners** — if dust, leaves, or small critters are getting under a roll-up door, this is your fix.
- **Anyone with a 2- to 3-inch gap** — if you're within that range, this kit is essentially plug-and-play.

Skip it if you need to seal a gap wider than 3 inches or if you're installing on heavy-gauge commercial steel doors without upgrading the screws.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I cut the brush and channel to fit a shorter door?**
Yes. Both the aluminum channel and the brush strip cut easily with a hacksaw or tin snips. Just deburr the edges after cutting.

**Q: Does this work on the sides and top of a roll-up door, or just the bottom?**
The kit works anywhere you can mount the channel. For side and top gaps, you'll need to cut and miter the corners. It's more fiddly than bottom installation but doable.

**Q: Will this stop mice and rats?**
The bristles are dense enough to block most rodents, but determined pests can chew through polypropylene over time. It's a deterrent, not an impenetrable wall.
