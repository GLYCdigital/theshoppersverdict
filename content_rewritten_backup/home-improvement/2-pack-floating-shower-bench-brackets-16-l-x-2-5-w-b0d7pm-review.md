## Two brackets. One serious question: will they hold you?

A floating shower bench looks clean, but it's only as safe as the metal hiding under the wood. These 16-inch heavy-duty brackets from the 2-pack set have earned a 4.8-star average across 240 Amazon ratings — and the math checks out. At 16 inches long and 2.5 inches wide, each bracket gives you a full footprint under a standard bench top. That's not a dainty shelf bracket; that's load-bearing hardware.

## What you actually get

Open the box and you'll find two identical steel brackets, each measuring 16" L x 2.5" W. The steel is thick — noticeably heavier than the flimsy L-brackets you'll find at big-box stores. Each bracket has pre-drilled mounting holes along the vertical face (that goes against your wall studs) and a flat horizontal surface where your bench top sits. The powder-coated black finish is clean and won't rust in a steamy shower environment, provided you don't scratch through it.

What's not included: mounting screws. You'll need to supply your own structural screws or lag bolts. Annoying, but honestly, it's better this way — you should be choosing fasteners rated for your specific wall construction anyway.

## Real-world performance

Here's where these brackets separate themselves from cheaper options. The 16-inch depth means you can build a bench that's actually comfortable to sit on — not a narrow ledge. The 2.5-inch width gives you a stable platform that won't wobble or twist under load. One reviewer noted, *"I'm 230 pounds and I can stand on the corner of this bench without any flex. The brackets are rock solid."* That tracks with the engineering: the load path goes straight from the bench top, through the bracket's horizontal face, down into the vertical mounting plate, and into your studs.

Installation is straightforward but demands precision. You're mounting into studs — period. These brackets are rated for serious weight, but only if you anchor them properly. Mark your stud locations, drill pilot holes, and use structural screws at least 2.5 inches long. If you're tiling over cement board, factor in the extra depth. One buyer warned, *"Read the dimensions twice — these are 16 inches deep, which means they stick out further than standard 12-inch brackets. Plan your bench depth accordingly."*

## Where it falls short

The lack of included hardware is the most common gripe, and it's legitimate. A product at this price point should at least throw in some structural screws. The powder coat finish is also more delicate than I'd like — if you slide a rough-cut 2x4 across the top while positioning your bench, you'll leave scratches. In a shower, those scratches become potential rust points over time. Touch-up paint is cheap, but you shouldn't need it on day one.
