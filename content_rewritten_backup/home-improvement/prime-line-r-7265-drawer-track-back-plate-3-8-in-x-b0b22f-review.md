You don't buy a drawer track back plate because you're bored on a Tuesday. You buy it because a drawer is sagging, scraping, or completely detached, and you need a 15-minute fix, not a full cabinet rework. The Prime-Line R 7265 is that fix — if your track matches its dimensions.

This is a tiny, unglamorous piece of stamped steel: 3/8 inch wide, 1 inch tall, with the mounting hole pattern drilled to match standard side-mount drawer tracks found in millions of builder-grade cabinets. The plated finish isn't cosmetic vanity — it's what keeps this bracket from corroding when your dishwasher steam or humid basement air works against it. At this price point, you're not buying precision aerospace hardware, but you *are* buying a part that will outlive the cabinet it's screwed into.

**What's in the package:** Two back plates on a retail card, nothing else. No screws, no instructions. That's fine — if you're swapping a back plate, you already have the track installed and you know what you're doing. The lack of included hardware keeps the cost down, but it does mean a second trip to the hardware store if your screw jar is empty. Pick up #6 x 1/2-inch pan-head screws while you're there.

**Real-world fit:** The 3/8 x 1-inch footprint is the standard for many drawer slides from the 1990s through today — brands like Liberty, KV, and even some Blum-compatible systems use this pattern. Installation is straightforward: remove the old back plate, align the new one over the track's rear mounting holes, and screw it into the cabinet side. One reviewer noted it "fit perfectly on a 20-year-old kitchen cabinet" — a common experience, since this part was designed as a direct replacement for worn or broken OEM brackets.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The durability gets mentioned repeatedly. Multiple reviewers describe this as the "last time" they'll need to fix that drawer because the steel is thicker than the original stamped part it replaces. Another common thread: the two-pack saves the day. As one customer put it, "Broke one during installation, had the spare ready to go." That's the kind of practical redundancy you appreciate when you're mid-project and the hardware store is 20 minutes away.

**Where it falls short:** The screws omission is the biggest gripe — it's a small annoyance, but a real one. Also, this is a niche part. If your drawer track has a different back plate size (some use 1/2-inch or have a different hole spacing), this won't work, and you'll need to measure carefully before ordering. A few reviewers mentioned needing to enlarge the mounting holes slightly for their specific track — that's a 2-minute job with a round file, but worth knowing.

**Who should buy this:** The homeowner with a single sagging drawer who wants a permanent fix for under five bucks. The landlord maintaining rental units with builder-grade cabinets. The DIYer who just pulled out a broken bracket and needs the exact replacement without buying a whole new drawer slide set. Skip it if you have an unusual European soft-close system or if your track is bent — a back plate won't fix a damaged rail.

**FAQ:**

*Q: Does this come with screws?*  
A: No. You'll need #6 or #8 pan-head screws, 1/2 inch long. If you're mounting into particle board, use coarse-thread screws for better grip.

*Q: Will this fit my drawer slide?*  
A: Measure the back plate on your existing track first. It must be 3/8 inch wide and 1 inch tall with holes spaced to match. If your current plate is a different size, this won't work.

*Q: Is this metal or plastic?*  
A: It's stamped steel with a plated finish. It's stronger than most original equipment plates and won't crack under load.
