If you've ever dragged an air compressor up a ladder just to run a few pieces of trim, the RYOBI P322 ONE+ HP AirStrike is the tool that makes you wonder why you waited so long. This isn't a toy-grade cordless nailer — it's a legitimate finish-work tool that trades the compressor for a 18V battery and a clever internal air chamber that builds pressure on its own. The catch? It's a 4.2/5-star tool, which means there are real tradeoffs. Let's get into them.

**What's in the box**

The P322 ships as a bare tool — no battery, no charger. You're getting the nailer itself, a belt hook, a no-mar pad for the nose, and the manual. The 25° angled magazine holds 100 nails (18-gauge, 5/8" to 2" length), and the dry-fire lockout stops you from driving blanks when the mag runs dry. If you're already in the ONE+ ecosystem, this is a no-brainer addition. If you're not, factor in the cost of a battery and charger — that's the real price of entry.

**Performance and build quality**

The AirStrike system works by using the brushless motor to compress air in an internal chamber before releasing it to drive the nail. That means no compressor noise — it's a quick "thump" rather than a bang, which is genuinely pleasant on a jobsite. The brushless HP motor delivers consistent power through the 2" nail range, and I've seen it sink 1-1/4" brads flush into pine and poplar trim without needing a second pass.

The depth adjustment is mechanical via a wheel near the nose. It's functional, but it's stiff out of the box — several Amazon reviewers note it loosens up after a few dozen cycles. The same goes for the jam release: it's tool-free and works, but the first time you use it, you'll think you're going to break something. You're not.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The silence is the headline. One reviewer put it plainly: "No compressor, no hose, no noise. I can trim out a door at 6 AM without waking the kids." Another called the AirStrike "the best $200 I've spent on finish work."

The battery efficiency also gets love. With a 4Ah battery, you'll get several hundred nails per charge — enough for a full room of baseboard. The 25° angled magazine is another win, letting you get into tight corners that straight-mag nailers can't reach.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest complaint across the 210 ratings is battery dependency. With a 1.5Ah pack, the nailer will drive nails but noticeably slower — the air chamber takes longer to rebuild pressure. Several reviewers report the tool "sputters" with low-capacity batteries. This isn't a dealbreaker, but if you're buying this as your first cordless nailer, budget for at least a 2Ah battery.

Jams happen. Unlike a pneumatic nailer where you can feel the pressure, the AirStrike sometimes leaves you guessing whether a nail seated properly. The tool-free jam release is fine, but it's not as smooth as the one on the DeWalt or Milwaukee equivalents. And at 7.2 pounds with a 4Ah battery, it's not the lightest 18-gauge nailer on the market — you'll feel it on overhead work.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIYers finishing a basement or building furniture**: The compressor-free operation is a game-changer in a garage or spare room.
- **ONE+ ecosystem owners**: If you already have RYOBI batteries, the bare-tool price makes this a no-brainer.
- **Trim carpenters doing light-to-moderate work**: It won't replace a pneumatic for production work, but for punch lists and small jobs, it's perfect.

**Skip it if** you're a production trim carpenter running thousands of nails daily, or if you don't already own RYOBI batteries — the total cost of entry gets steep.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can it drive 2-inch nails into hardwood?**  
A: Yes, but you'll want a 4Ah battery for consistent power. With a 2Ah pack, you might need to slow down your cadence to let the chamber rebuild pressure.

**Q: Does it work in cold weather?**  
A: It's less affected by temperature than pneumatic systems, but the battery will drain faster in freezing conditions. Keep the battery warm and it's fine.

**Q: Is the AirStrike mechanism reliable long-term?**  
A: Generally yes — the brushless motor and sealed air chamber are low-wear components. The main failure point is the trigger assembly, which RYOBI covers under a 3-year warranty.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a quiet, compressor-free finish nailer for home projects or light professional work, and you're already invested in the ONE+ system. **Skip it if** you need a production tool or you're starting from scratch with no RYOBI batteries — the cost of entry is too high for occasional use.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful tool with a real learning curve and battery quirks. The silence alone is worth the price of admission.
