If you're installing a Level 2 EV charger, a welder, or a dryer in a new spot, you've hit the wall with the same problem: you need a dedicated 240-volt outlet, and you need it done right the first time. This NEMA 14-30R & 14-50R combo unit takes a smart approach — one rugged duplex-style plate that handles both the 30-amp and 50-amp standards. It's not the only option, but after digging through 235 ratings and the actual hardware specs, it's clear why this one sits at 4.7 stars.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the dual-receptacle wall plate pre-wired with both configurations: the 14-30R (four-prong, 30-amp, commonly used for dryers and some RV hookups) and the 14-50R (four-prong, 50-amp, the standard for Level 2 EV chargers and welders). You also get the mounting hardware and terminal screws. No weatherproof cover, no box — that's on you, and that's a real thing to budget for if your install is outdoors.

The construction is the standout here. The housing is industrial-grade nylon, not the brittle plastic you'll find on builder-grade receptacles. The terminals are rated for 8 AWG solid copper, which matters if you're running a long 50-amp circuit and need the thicker wire to keep voltage drop in check. As shown in the product image, the terminals are clearly labeled, and there's a grounding screw that's actually accessible once mounted — a small thing that saves real frustration.

## Real-World Performance

For the EV owner, this is the workhorse setup. You can hardwire a charger or plug in a portable unit on the 14-50 side, and keep the 14-30 available for a dryer or a secondary device. Buyers consistently mention the install being straightforward. One verified owner noted, "Wired it into a 50-amp breaker for my Ford Lightning's charger — solid connection, no heat after a full 32-amp charge session." Another said, "The terminal lugs are generous. I didn't have to jam the 6 AWG wire in like I've done with other receptacles."

The heat resistance is a big deal. Cheap 14-50 outlets are notorious for melting under sustained high-amperage draws. The nylon body and heavy-duty contacts on this unit handle the sustained load without that worry.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, there are two real complaints floating around. First, no cover included — for outdoor use (which is where most EV chargers live), you'll need to buy a weatherproof in-use cover separately, and those aren't universal. Measure your box before ordering. Second, the combo unit is thicker than a standard duplex receptacle. If you're mounting it in a tight 2-gang box behind a panel, you might have to do some finagling with the wire bends.

One buyer put it well: "Works great, but I wish the screws were magnetic. Dropped one behind the drywall and spent 20 minutes fishing it out." Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're installing a Level 2 EV charger and want the flexibility of a 50-amp plug plus a 30-amp outlet for a dryer or compressor. Ideal for DIYers with basic electrical experience (turn off the breaker, verify with a meter) or anyone who wants an electrician to do a clean, dual-purpose install without running two separate boxes.

**Skip it if:** You only need a single 50-amp outlet for a dedicated EV charger — a standalone 14-50R is cheaper and less bulky. Also skip if your install is outdoors and you don't want to deal with finding a compatible cover.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this safe for a Tesla or Ford F-150 Lightning at full amperage?**
A: Yes. The 14-50R side is rated for 50 amps continuous, and the industrial-grade contacts handle the sustained draw. Just make sure you use a 50-amp breaker and 6 AWG wire.

**Q: Can I replace an existing dryer outlet with this?**
A: Yes, if you have a 30-amp circuit, the 14-30R side works. But you can't use the 14-50 side on that circuit — you'll need a separate 50-amp feed for it.

**Q: Does it come with a cover?**
A: No. For indoor use, you're fine. For outdoor, buy a weatherproof in-use cover separately, and check the dimensions.
