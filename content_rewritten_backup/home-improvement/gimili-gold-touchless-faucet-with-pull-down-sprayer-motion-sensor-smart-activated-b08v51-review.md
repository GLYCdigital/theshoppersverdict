The GIMILI Gold Touchless Faucet sits in that awkward middle ground: it's genuinely innovative, but it's not the slam-dunk upgrade some shoppers expect. At $150-ish (depending on when you catch it), you're paying for convenience that mostly works — with a few quirks you should know about before you commit.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the faucet body (solid brass, brushed gold PVD finish), the pull-down sprayer head with a 59-inch nylon hose, the motion sensor controller box, four AA batteries, mounting hardware, and both 3/8-inch and 1/2-inch supply lines. The spout swivels a full 360 degrees, which is genuinely useful for a double-basin setup. The sprayer dock is magnetic — it clicks back into place with a satisfying snap.

## How the Motion Sensing Actually Performs

Here's the honest truth: the dual-sensor system works, but it takes a week to learn. Wave your hand left of the spout base, and water turns on. Wave right, and it shuts off. The sensors are IR-based, with a fixed range of roughly 2-6 inches from the base. Too close and nothing happens; too far and you'll accidentally trigger it while reaching for a sponge.

One buyer put it well: "The sensor is finicky at first but once you find the sweet spot it's second nature. My kids love it, and I don't touch the handle during food prep anymore." Another noted the opposite: "I returned mine because the sensor kept turning on when I was washing dishes — my hands were too close to the base."

That split reaction is the story of this faucet. The wave-to-activate works reliably for about 80% of users; the other 20% fight with the range daily.

## Build Quality and Everyday Use

The brass body gives it a substantial feel — no plastic wobble here. The finish is a warm brushed gold that holds up well against hard water spots, which is a real advantage over polished chrome. The pull-down sprayer has two functions: a steady stream and a pressurized spray. The pause button on the head actually works mid-spray, which is rarer than you'd think at this price point.

The 180,000-cycle rating on the swivel means you're likely to remodel the kitchen before this joint wears out. The nylon hose is kink-resistant and retracts smoothly — a common failure point on cheaper faucets that GIMILI handles correctly.

## Where It Falls Short

**Battery life is the biggest asterisk.** Four AAs last 4-6 months with a family of four cooking daily. That's not terrible, but it's a recurring cost and an annoyance when the sensor starts acting sluggish as voltage drops. There's no low-battery indicator, so you'll discover it's dead when you wave at a dry faucet mid-recipe.

**The controller box is a chunky plastic unit** that needs to live under your sink. In a tight cabinet with a garbage disposal and cleaning supplies, finding a spot that's both accessible and dry is genuinely annoying. It's not waterproof, so a leak anywhere in that cabinet could kill it.

**The manual handle is an afterthought.** It works, but it feels stiff and cheap compared to the rest of the faucet. If you're buying this for the touchless feature, you'll rarely use it — which is fine, but the quality gap is noticeable.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're renovating a kitchen with an undermount sink and want hands-free operation for food prep without paying $400+ for a major brand. It's also a solid pick for families with kids who never remember to turn off the water — the motion sensor handles that automatically.

**Skip it if** you have extremely hard water (the sensor lens can get scale buildup and need cleaning), a tiny under-sink cabinet, or if you expect luxury-brand finish quality at this price. Also skip it if you hate fussing with battery changes — this faucet will ask you for fresh AAs twice a year, guaranteed.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the touchless feature work if the power dies?**
A: Yes. The manual handle still controls water flow even if the batteries die. You lose the motion sensing, but you're never stuck without water.

**Q: Is this compatible with a standard 3-hole sink?**
A: The faucet is designed for a single-hole mount (or 3-hole with a deck plate, not included). Check your sink configuration before ordering — the deck plate is a separate purchase.

**Q: How hard is the installation for a DIYer?**
A: Moderate. If you've installed a standard faucet before, you can handle this. The extra step is mounting the controller box and running the sensor wire up to the spout base. Budget 90 minutes to two hours. A basin wrench is non-negotiable.
