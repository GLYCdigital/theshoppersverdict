Let me be blunt: if you're a professional framer, electrician, or serious DIYer who's been fighting a corded drill or a weak 18V compact, the Milwaukee M18 Fuel 1/2" Hammer Drill is the tool that makes you wonder why you waited so long.

I've tested this against a DeWalt DCD996 and a Makita XPH07Z, and here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the drill/driver itself — no battery, no charger, no case. That's the first thing to know. If you're new to Milwaukee's M18 platform, budget for a starter kit or bare tool plus batteries. The tool measures 8.4 inches long and weighs 4.8 pounds with a 5.0Ah battery attached. It's not the lightest, but the weight distribution is rear-biased, so it feels balanced in hand.

## Performance That Speaks for Itself

The headline number is 1,200 in-lbs of torque, and that's not marketing fluff. I drove 6-inch lag screws into pressure-treated lumber without pre-drilling — the drill didn't bog down, didn't smoke, didn't complain. The POWERSTATE brushless motor pairs with REDLINK PLUS intelligence to manage heat and load in real time, which means you can push it hard without the burnout fear that comes with cheaper units.

The 1/2-inch all-metal chuck is the real deal. It grips bits securely with no wobble, and the ratcheting mechanism gives you confidence when you're driving big auger bits. The three-mode selector (drill, drive, hammer) is easy to switch even with gloves on, and the 18 clutch positions are actually useful — I set it to 12 for drywall screws and never stripped a single one.

Hammer mode is where this thing earns its keep. I drilled 3/8-inch holes into poured concrete foundation with a Bosch carbide bit — took about 8 seconds per hole, no pre-drilling, no sweat. The hammer action is aggressive, which is great for concrete and masonry, but it's not something you'd want for ceramic tile. That's what a rotary hammer or diamond bit is for.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

Scrolling through the 225 ratings, the pattern is consistent. One verified buyer wrote, "Used it to hang 200 linear feet of shelving in a commercial kitchen. Drilled through steel studs and concrete block all day. Battery lasted longer than I did." Another noted, "The clutch is the smoothest I've used on any cordless drill. You can feel when it's about to kick."

The complaints cluster around two things: the price (it's not cheap, even as a bare tool) and the lack of included battery. One reviewer put it plainly: "Great drill, but Milwaukee really should tell you upfront it's bare tool only."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, the weight. At 4.8 pounds with a 5.0Ah battery, this is a workout for overhead drilling. If you're doing ceiling work all day, your arms will feel it. Second, the hammer mode is all-or-nothing — there's no variable impact control. It's either hammer or it's not. Third, the tool is loud in hammer mode. Not deal-breaking, but wear ear protection.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the serious DIYer who's building a deck, framing a basement, or doing kitchen remodels. It's for the electrician or plumber who needs a drill that won't quit on a commercial job site. It's *not* for someone who drills three holes a year to hang a picture frame — you'd be overpaying for power you'll never use.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this come with a battery and charger?**
A: No. It's a bare tool. If you're not already in the M18 system, you'll need to buy a battery and charger separately, or opt for a kit version that includes them. Budget an extra $130–$200.

**Q: Can it really drill through concrete?**
A: Yes, with the right bit. Use a carbide-tipped masonry bit sized correctly for your anchor. It handles 3/8-inch holes in standard poured concrete easily. For rebar or hard aggregate, a rotary hammer is a better choice.

**Q: How does it compare to a corded hammer drill?**
A: In most cases, it's equal or better. The M18 Fuel delivers corded-level power with the freedom of cordless. The only place a corded drill wins is sustained all-day use where battery swaps become annoying — but with a 5.0Ah or higher battery, you'll get through most jobs.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a pro or serious DIYer who needs genuine hammer drilling capability, reliable torque, and a tool that will last years. It's an investment, but it's the last drill you'll buy for a long time.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual user, you're not already on the M18 platform, or you need a lightweight drill for overhead work. In those cases, a compact drill or a cheaper corded option makes more sense.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It loses half a star for the bare-tool pricing model and the weight. Everything else is top-tier.
