## Real-World Performance

The variable-speed trigger deserves the praise it gets. There's no dead zone at low speeds — you can feather it down to a crawl for precision work, then bury it for full power. That's not universal in this price range, and it matters more than most specs suggest.

The 4.2-pound weight is worth noting. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're doing overhead work or extended use, you'll feel it. The rubber overmold grip helps, and one reviewer put it plainly: "The grip is a lifesaver. I ran it for two hours straight and my hands weren't cramping." That's the kind of feedback that tells you the ergonomics were actually tested, not just sketched on a whiteboard.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the tradeoffs.

**The battery is the big one.** The charger and battery are sold separately, which means if you don't already own compatible Delta batteries, you're looking at an additional $60–$90. That's a significant hidden cost. Factor it into your decision before checkout, not after.

**The weight** is a legitimate issue for some users. It's not excessive, but it's on the heavier side for the category. If you're doing delicate, overhead, or precision work all day, consider whether that matters to you.

**Missing accessories**: No belt clip, no bit holder. These are small things, but for the price point, they shouldn't be missing. You'll spend an extra $10–$15 to solve this yourself.

## Who Should Buy This

**Get it if**: You're a serious DIYer or professional who needs a tool that can handle daily use without falling apart. You value precision control and don't mind paying for durability. You already own Delta batteries, or you're willing to invest in the system.

**Skip it if**: You're a casual weekend user who needs a tool once a month, or you're on a tight budget — the battery cost will sting. If weight is a concern for your typical work, look at lighter brushless alternatives.
