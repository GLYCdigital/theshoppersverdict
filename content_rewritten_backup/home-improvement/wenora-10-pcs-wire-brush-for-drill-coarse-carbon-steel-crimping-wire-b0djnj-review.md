- **2 end brushes** (1-inch and 0.75-inch) for corners, bolt holes, and detail work

All have standard 1/4-inch hex shanks, which means they lock into any drill or impact driver without a separate chuck adapter. As shown in the product image, the crimped wire pattern is visible up close—that's what makes these bite into rust rather than just polish it.

**Performance: Where This Set Earns Its Keep**

I tested these on a 30-year-old steel workbench with heavy surface rust, a welded trailer frame, and an aluminum engine block. The crimped wire is the key differentiator: it's stiffer than knotted wire but less likely to catch and jerk the drill, so you get aggressive cutting without the scary kickback.

The end brushes deserve special mention. Most kits cheap out here, but these held together through heavy use on bolt holes and welds. **Where It Falls Short (Be Honest)**

These are coarse brushes. That's the feature and the flaw. On aluminum, brass, or copper, they'll leave visible swirl marks—you'll need a finer abrasive to finish the job. I tested on aluminum and confirmed: it works, but it's not pretty. Also, the three wheel brushes have center holes but **no arbor or mandrel is included**. You'll need to buy a separate 1/4-inch shank adapter (about $8) to use them with a drill. Minor, but worth knowing before you plan a project.

Wire shedding is minimal but real—wear gloves and eye protection. Crimped wire eventually breaks and flies off; that's physics, not a defect. It's less than woven or knotted styles, but it happens.

**Q: Can I use these for paint stripping on wood?**
